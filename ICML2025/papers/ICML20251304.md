## Problem

Existing neural scaling law extrapolation methods rely on point estimation without uncertainty quantification, making them unreliable for high-stakes decisions like whether to invest additional computational resources. While Bayesian alternatives such as MCMC exist, they struggle with chaotic real-world behaviors like double descent due to non-convex landscapes and the difficulty of specifying appropriate priors. This paper introduces the first Bayesian framework specifically designed for neural scaling law extrapolation that handles such complex behaviors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a context set of partial (x, y) observations from a scaling curve—where x is a scaling quantity (data size, model size, training tokens) and y is a performance metric (error rate, loss, perplexity)—along with target x positions for extrapolation. Each (x, y) pair and each target x* is treated as an individual token fed to the Transformer. The output is a full posterior predictive distribution discretized into 1,000 equal-probability bins, from which the median (for RMSLE) and confidence intervals (for uncertainty) are extracted. Input x-values are normalized to [0.01, 1] and y-values to [0, 1] before processing.

## Method

NSL-PFN is a 12-layer Transformer (4 heads, 512 hidden) meta-trained via PFN on 1.6M synthetic scaling curves sampled from a novel functional prior. The prior defines three function families—Down (single M3/M4 segment), Down-Down (2–4 segments with random breaks), and Down-Up-Down (3 segments with a BetaCDF upward middle segment for double descent)—each constructed by sampling parameters, re-normalizing with Norm, and adding Noise. Cutoff positions are restricted based on the direction of the last visible segment (anywhere for downward, only within the last segment for upward), encoding the domain intuition that upward trends will eventually reverse. The training objective combines standard target prediction with an auto-regressive context regression loss to improve fit quality near the cutoff. At inference, a single forward pass yields the full PPD, making it orders of magnitude faster than MCMC baselines while producing better-calibrated uncertainties.

## Results

On the image classification benchmark (IC, 72 curves), NSL-PFN achieves the best average RMSLE of 0.0280 and log-likelihood (LL) of 3.330, outperforming the best point-estimation method BNSL (RMSLE 0.0406) and the best MCMC variant MCMC(M₄) (RMSLE 0.0422, LL 3.024). On the language domain (NLP + Nano, 44 curves), NSL-PFN reaches 0.0194 RMSLE and 2.773 LL, again leading all baselines. On the challenging double descent (DD) dataset, NSL-PFN achieves 0.0335 RMSLE and 2.565 LL, substantially outperforming BNSL (0.0468 RMSLE) and LC-PFN (0.0706 RMSLE, 1.321 LL), demonstrating strong capability in predicting non-monotonic scaling behaviors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSL-PFN outperforms all point-estimation baselines (M₁–M₄, BNSL) by a wide margin on every dataset, with the largest gaps on double descent (DD) where BNSL achieves 0.0468 RMSLE versus NSL-PFN's 0.0335. Among MCMC variants (following Domhan et al. 2015), MCMC(M₄) is the strongest competitor on IC (0.0422 RMSLE) and NMT, but NSL-PFN still leads by ~33% in RMSLE on IC and by a clear LL margin (3.330 vs. 3.024). LC-PFN (Adriaensen et al. 2023), the closest Bayesian PFN baseline, is outperformed on all four datasets; the gap is most dramatic on DD (0.0706 vs. 0.0335 RMSLE) and ColPret (0.0289 vs. 0.0271 RMSLE), where LC-PFN's prior lacks awareness of broken/double-descent behaviors. BLR with NN basis functions and DKGP (Wilson et al. 2016) perform poorly across all datasets (e.g., BLR achieves 0.307 RMSLE on IC and −95.97 LL on DD), confirming that generic Bayesian regression methods struggle to capture the inductive biases of neural scaling laws. The only cases where NSL-PFN is slightly outperformed are NMT and LM, where MCMC(M₄) and M₄ respectively achieve marginally lower RMSLE, which the authors attribute to those scaling laws being too simple for the more complex prior to be beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN) framework of Müller et al. (2021), which showed that Transformers can perform amortized Bayesian inference via meta-learning on synthetic data. It extends the closely related LC-PFN (Adriaensen et al., 2023), which applied PFNs to learning curve extrapolation, by designing a functional prior specifically tailored to neural scaling laws—one that incorporates breaks, inflection points, and upward (double-descent) segments. The paper departs from the dominant point-estimation paradigm (M1–M4, BNSL) and from MCMC-based Bayesian approaches, which struggle with non-monotonic and multi-modal scaling behaviors, by leveraging the PFN's flexibility in defining arbitrary samplable priors and its single-forward-pass inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neural scaling law extrapolation underpins high-stakes resource allocation decisions (how much compute to spend, when to stop training, how much data to collect), yet prior methods provide only point estimates without uncertainty, making them unreliable for such decisions. NSL-PFN addresses this gap and demonstrates that a tailored prior covering complex behaviors (double descent, multiple breaks) yields both better accuracy and well-calibrated uncertainty, particularly in data-limited settings like Bayesian active learning. Open questions include whether the manually tuned prior hyperparameters can be fully automated, whether the approach generalizes to scaling dimensions beyond data size and model size (e.g., training compute, sequence length), and whether the slight performance degradation on very simple scaling laws (NMT, LM) can be resolved without sacrificing robustness on complex curves.
