## Problem

Standard inverse propensity score (IPS) estimators for off-policy evaluation and learning suffer from high variance, and existing variance-reduction variants (truncated IPS, power-mean, exponential smoothing, implicit exploration) either require bounded rewards or degrade under heavy-tailed weighted rewards and noisy/estimated propensity scores. No prior model-free estimator simultaneously provides theoretical guarantees under unbounded (heavy-tailed) weighted rewards, noisy propensity scores, and noisy rewards while remaining differentiable for policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a logged bandit feedback dataset of n i.i.d. tuples (x_i, a_i, p_i, r_i) — context, action, propensity score, and observed reward — together with a parametric target policy π_θ. It produces a scalar policy value estimate via the LSE operator and, in the off-policy learning setting, the learned policy π_θ̂ that maximizes this estimate over the policy set. A single tunable hyperparameter λ < 0 governs the bias-variance-robustness trade-off: as λ → 0⁻ the estimator recovers the standard IPS estimator, while more negative λ increases robustness to outliers at the cost of higher bias.

## Method

The LSE estimator is defined as (1/λ) log((1/n) Σ_i exp(λ r_i w_θ(a_i, x_i))) with λ < 0, where w_θ(a_i, x_i) = π_θ(a_i|x_i)/π_0(a_i|x_i) is the importance weight. The key mechanism is that for negative λ, abnormally large weighted-reward values are exponentially suppressed in the sum (exp(λz) → 0 as z → +∞), providing built-in robustness to outliers and heavy tails without explicit truncation. Crucially, the estimator is non-linear in the full set of weighted-reward samples, unlike all prior model-free estimators which are linear (weighted averages) in individual rewards, necessitating new concentration and regret analysis techniques. Under the assumption that the (1+ε)-th moment of the weighted reward is bounded for some ε ∈ [0,1] (allowing unbounded rewards), the paper derives a regret bound with convergence rate O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}), bias and variance bounds, and a robustness bound under distributional shift of the reward distribution quantified via total variation distance.

## Results

In off-policy learning on EMNIST, LSE achieves the highest accuracy in most scenarios, particularly under noisy propensity scores (b=0.01, τ=10: LSE 82.15% vs. PM 80.85%, ES 81.07%, IX 77.49%, BanditNet 27.02%) and noisy rewards (P_f=0.5, τ=10: LSE 88.71% vs. ES 84.42%, IX 83.25%). In off-policy evaluation under heavy-tailed weighted rewards (α=1.4), LSE achieves MSE of 0.670, dramatically outperforming PM (165.041), IPS-TR (133.691), and SNIPS (133.521). Under lighter-tailed conditions (α=1.1), LSE still attains the lowest MSE (0.009) among all estimators. The theoretical regret bound achieves a convergence rate of O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}), matching O(n^{-1/2}) when the second moment is bounded (ε=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSE outperforms all seven baselines in the majority of OPL scenarios, with the largest margins under noisy propensity scores (b=0.01, τ=10: LSE 82.15% vs. OS 21.70%, BanditNet 27.02%) and moderate reward noise (P_f=0.5, τ=10: LSE 88.71% vs. ES 84.42%, IX 83.25%). In clean settings (true propensity, no reward noise), PM slightly edges out LSE (89.19% vs. 88.49% at τ=1), and OS wins one noisy-reward scenario (89.09% vs. 88.71% at τ=10, P_f=0.5). In OPE under heavy-tailed weighted rewards (α=1.4), LSE's MSE of 0.670 is orders of magnitude lower than PM (165.041), IPS-TR (133.691), and SNIPS (133.521), while the LS estimator (Sakhi et al., 2024) is the closest competitor at 0.776. BanditNet consistently performs worst across all scenarios, dropping to 27.02% under noisy propensity scores. The LSE estimator is the only method that simultaneously handles heavy-tailed rewards, noisy propensity scores, and noisy rewards with theoretical guarantees, as summarized in Table 2 of the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the log-sum-exponential operator—previously used in multinomial regression, naive Bayes, and tilted empirical risk (Li et al., 2023; Calafiore et al., 2019)—as a non-linear estimator for off-policy evaluation and learning from logged bandit feedback. It departs from the entire family of linear model-free estimators (IPS, truncated IPS, power-mean, exponential smoothing, implicit exploration, optimistic shrinkage, logarithmic smoothing) by operating on the full set of weighted rewards through an exponential-log transform, which inherently down-weights large values when λ<0. The theoretical analysis extends the heavy-tailed bandit literature (Bubeck et al., 2013; Shao et al., 2018) from bounded (1+ε)-th moments of raw rewards to the weighted reward w_θ(A,X)·R, and borrows distributional-shift tools from the tilted empirical risk framework (Aminian et al., 2025) to handle noisy rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: prior model-free OPE/OPL estimators are all linear in the weighted reward and lack theoretical guarantees under heavy-tailed or noisy conditions, yet these conditions are common in finance, advertising, and medical applications. The O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}) regret rate is the first such bound for a model-free estimator under bounded (1+ε)-th moment of the weighted reward, and the non-linearity of LSE means existing concentration tools for linear estimators are inapplicable, requiring new proof techniques. Limitations include the finite policy set assumption (extending to infinite policy classes requires VC-dimension or PAC-Bayesian arguments), the restriction to negative λ, and modest empirical gains over the recent LS estimator in many scenarios. Open questions include extending to positive λ for imbalanced data, handling missing rewards, and adapting the analysis to full MDP/RL settings with dependent data.
