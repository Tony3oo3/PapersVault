## Problem

Current LLM evaluations often reduce complex cognitive capacities to single accuracy scores and are vulnerable to models achieving high performance through parameter scale or training-data memorization rather than genuine understanding. The paper argues that LLM evaluation lacks the rigorous experimental design principles—adversarial controls, systematic stimulus variation, failure-type analysis, and mechanism-behavior distinction—that animal cognition researchers have refined over decades for probing other 'black box' intelligences.

## Input / Output

Experiments present GPT-4o with text-based transitive inference tasks in two formats: single-trial queries listing ranked elements with ordinal operators (e.g., 'A > B > C, which is bigger, A or C?'), and multi-turn n-term trial sequences where the model makes binary choices and receives differential 'correct/incorrect' feedback across 60 training trials per iteration. Outputs are analyzed not just for aggregate accuracy but for structured failure patterns (serial position effects, symbolic distance effects) and robustness across stimulus manipulations including operator type, element type, and chain length.

## Method

The paper distills five evaluation principles from animal cognition—adversarial control design, stimulus robustness testing, failure-type analysis, mechanism-behavior distinction, and accommodating systemic limitations—and demonstrates them through two experiments on GPT-4o probing transitive inference. Experiment 1 varies ordinal operators ('bigger than' vs. '>') and element types (size-ranked animals, reverse-ranked animals, random strings) in single-trial queries, revealing that performance is highly contingent on specific prompt wording and exhibits a serial position effect. Experiment 2 removes the explicit ordinal operator entirely, using a trial-based n-term task where the model learns pairwise discriminations through differential reinforcement before being tested on novel non-adjacent pairs, dropping performance from 100% to 40% (chance). The key insight is that systematic removal of linguistic scaffolding exposes brittleness invisible to standard single-metric evaluations, and that the pattern of failures (not just the aggregate score) is the primary diagnostic signal.

## Results

GPT-4o achieves 100% accuracy on explicit transitive inference queries (A vs. C, B vs. D) and 97% on ranked animal-name lists with an ordinal operator. Performance drops to 92% with random strings (no transitive link) and 78% with reverse-ranked animals, but collapses to 40% (chance) in the n-term trial-based task where both transitive elements and the ordinal operator are removed. The paper also reports a Symbolic Distance Effect: accuracy is positively correlated with symbolic distance across all six conditions, mirroring findings in humans, pigeons, and macaques.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare GPT-4o against other LLMs or external baselines; the evaluation is a single-model study with systematically varied task conditions serving as internal controls. The key comparison is across six task configurations that progressively remove linguistic scaffolding: performance degrades monotonically from 100% (explicit operator + transitive elements) through 92% (operator only, random strings) and 78% (operator only, reverse-ranked animals) down to 40% (no operator, no transitive elements, trial-based n-term task). The 60-percentage-point drop between the most and least scaffolded conditions is the paper's central empirical finding, demonstrating that GPT-4o's apparent TI ability is highly contingent on surface-level linguistic cues rather than reflecting a robust, domain-general transitive inference mechanism. The authors frame this as evidence that without adversarial controls (P1) and stimulus variation (P2), a simple 'yes/no' evaluation would misleadingly conclude that GPT-4o possesses transitive inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a specific prior ML evaluation method but instead repurposes decades of experimental design principles from comparative psychology and animal cognition (match-to-sample paradigms, n-term tasks, adversarial control conditions, Morgan's Canon) and explicitly imports them into the LLM evaluation setting. It positions itself against the prevailing trend of creating novel benchmark tasks (Kosinski 2023, Chollet 2019, Momennejad et al. 2024) by arguing that the *principles* of experimental design matter more than any single task. The empirical case study on transitive inference draws on well-established animal TI paradigms (Roberts & Phelps 1994; D'amato & Colombo 1990) and adapts them to GPT-4o, while the broader motivation connects to recent work documenting LLM brittleness (McCoy et al. 2023; Lewis & Mitchell 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The five principles offer a concrete, actionable framework for designing more rigorous LLM evaluations that go beyond single-score benchmarks, and the case study demonstrates their value by showing GPT-4o's TI performance collapsing from 100% to chance (40%) once linguistic scaffolding is removed. However, the empirical evidence is limited to one model, one reasoning capability, and relatively small-scale experiments, leaving open whether the principles generalize to other capabilities (theory of mind, planning, analogical reasoning) or to other model families. A key open question is whether these adversarial, multi-condition evaluation protocols can be scaled and automated, and whether they would change the field's current conclusions about LLM reasoning abilities.
