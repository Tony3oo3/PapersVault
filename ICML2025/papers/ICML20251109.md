## Problem

Existing RL-based LLM alignment methods are complex and hard to optimize, while direct preference optimization (e.g., DPO) simplifies the process but remains limited to pairwise objectives. Despite striking structural parallels between LLM generation/reward models and IR's retriever/reranker paradigm, no systematic framework has mapped LLM alignment mechanisms to core IR principles, and proven IR techniques—retriever optimization objectives, hard negative mining, and candidate list construction—remain largely unexploited for alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prompt dataset and iteratively generates multiple candidate responses (e.g., 10 per prompt) from the current policy LLM at a chosen temperature. A reward model (cross-encoder) scores and ranks these candidates, which are then assembled into preference lists (positive/negative sets) for training. The output is an aligned LLM policy that produces higher-quality text responses; evaluation is performed via win-rate metrics on AlpacaEval2 and MixEval benchmarks using LLM-as-judge protocols.

## Method

LARPO reframes iterative LLM preference optimization as iterative retriever optimization, mapping the LLM to a bi-encoder retriever and the reward model to a cross-encoder reranker. Instead of DPO's pairwise Bradley-Terry objective, LARPO derives three alternative ranking losses from IR: a contrastive (InfoNCE-style) 1-vs-m objective, a LambdaRank pairwise-product objective, and a ListMLE sequential-softmax objective, all parameterized by the implicit reward γ(y|x) = β log π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x). The method incorporates hard negative mining by generating negatives at lower temperatures (yielding responses more similar to the positive), and candidate list construction that balances inclusiveness (generating 10 responses per prompt) with memorization (retaining responses from all previous iterations in the training pool). Across T iterations, the policy LLM generates candidates, the reward model ranks them, preference data is constructed, and the LLM is updated with the chosen ranking loss—mirroring the iterative retriever optimization loop from dense retrieval.

## Results

LARPO achieves 38.9% and 13.7% averaged relative improvements over the best baselines on AlpacaEval2 and MixEval-Hard respectively, using the same LLM-Blender reward model for fair comparison. On Mistral-7b-base, LARPO(LambdaRank) reaches 34.9 LC Win Rate and 37.2 Win Rate on AlpacaEval2, and 0.452 on MixEval-Hard, substantially outperforming the strongest offline baseline SimPO (21.5 LC WR, 20.8 WR, 0.347 MixEval-Hard). With a stronger FsfairX reward model, LARPO(Contrastive) further improves to 41.5 LC WR on AlpacaEval2 for Mistral-7b-base, a 25.8% additional gain over the LLM-Blender setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LARPO consistently outperforms all nine baselines across both Mistral-7b-base and Mistral-7b-instruct on every reported metric. Against the strongest offline baseline SimPO, LARPO(LambdaRank) improves AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate from 21.5 to 34.9 (+13.4 pts) and MixEval-Hard from 0.347 to 0.452 (+0.105) on Mistral-7b-base; on Mistral-7b-instruct, the LC WR gap narrows (32.1→32.9) but WR and MixEval-Hard still improve (34.8→38.9, 0.363→0.417). Against the strongest online baseline Iterative DPO, LARPO gains +16.0 LC WR on Mistral-7b-base (18.9→34.9) and +12.5 LC WR on Mistral-7b-instruct (20.4→32.9), with MixEval-Hard improving from 0.341→0.452 and 0.389→0.417 respectively. Among the three LARPO variants, LambdaRank achieves the best MixEval-Hard scores (0.452/0.417) while Contrastive leads on AlpacaEval2 LC WR for Mistral-7b-base (31.6 vs. 34.9 for LambdaRank) — the gap is small and model-dependent. All pairwise (DPO, IPO, KTO, RDPO) and contrastive (CPO, SLiC-HF, RRHF) offline baselines are clearly dominated, with LARPO exceeding the best offline method by 10+ points on AlpacaEval2 LC WR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematically maps LLM alignment onto the information retrieval retriever-reranker paradigm, where the LLM acts as a bi-encoder retriever and the reward model as a cross-encoder reranker. It builds directly on DPO's optimal solution (Rafailov et al., 2024) to derive three new preference optimization objectives—contrastive (InfoNCE), LambdaRank, and ListMLE—by importing ranking assumptions from IR. The iterative training framework extends iterative DPO (Xiong et al., 2024) by incorporating IR-specific techniques: hard negative mining via temperature-controlled sampling and candidate list construction with memorization of prior iterations. The key departure from prior preference optimization work is treating alignment as a retriever optimization problem rather than a purely preference-pairing problem, which justifies listwise objectives and structured negative selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IR lens provides a principled vocabulary and set of tools (hard negatives, candidate list size, listwise ranking) for diagnosing and improving LLM alignment, potentially accelerating future method design. The 38.9% and 13.7% relative improvements on AlpacaEval2 and MixEval-Hard are substantial, though the closest online baseline (Iterative DPO) shows more modest gaps, suggesting part of the gain comes from the iterative/online setup rather than the IR-specific objectives alone. Open questions include whether the analogy holds at scale (70B+ models), whether other IR techniques (query expansion, index pruning, multi-stage cascading) map productively, and whether the bi-encoder framing adequately captures autoregressive generation's sequential dependencies. The reliance on off-the-shelf reward models as 'rerankers' also raises questions about how the method behaves when the reward model is weak or misaligned with true quality.
