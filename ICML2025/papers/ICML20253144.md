## Problem

Existing protein-sequence-to-microscopy-image generators (CELL-E, CELL-E2) produce only highly blurred outputs that cannot resolve fine subcellular structures beyond the nucleus, severely limiting their utility for studying subcellular protein localization and interactions. No prior method enables high-fidelity bidirectional transformation between protein sequences and fluorescence microscopy images within a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a protein amino acid sequence (up to 2,048 residues, embedded via frozen ESM2) and one or more cell morphology reference images (nucleus, ER, and/or microtubules at 256×256) as conditional inputs, producing a 256×256 protein fluorescence image decoded from a 4×64×64 latent representation. For sequence generation, it takes a protein microscopy image and cell morphology image as inputs and outputs an amino acid sequence via iterative unmasking. The cell morphology images serve as shared conditioning in both directions, anchoring the generated content to a specific cellular context.

## Method

CELL-Diff unifies continuous diffusion (DDPM-style noise prediction for images) and discrete diffusion (OA-ARDM masked-value prediction for sequences) under a single transformer-based U-Net architecture, trained jointly with a combined loss L_I + λL_S. The U-Net processes concatenated protein and cell images through downsampling/upsampling residual-attention blocks with cross-attention to sequence features, while a bidirectional 8-layer transformer (8-head attention, 1280-dim) integrates ESM2 sequence embeddings with image latents. Time-step conditioning uses adaLN-Zero, and sampling employs DDIM with 100 steps for images and random-order unmasking for sequences. The model is pre-trained on HPA (88,483 samples) and fine-tuned on OpenCell (6,301 samples), with a VAE (4×64×64 latent) compressing images to reduce computational cost.

## Results

On the HPA dataset, CELL-Diff achieves an MSFR of 641 nm (vs. 1824 nm for CELL-E2), approaching the 640 nm resolvability of the original training data, with IoU of 0.484 and FID-O of 51.1 (vs. 166.4 for CELL-E2). Adding ER and microtubule morphology images as additional conditions further improves IoU to 0.635 and FID-O to 45.6. On OpenCell, CELL-Diff achieves MSFR of 529 nm (vs. 1165 nm for CELL-E2), IoU of 0.492, and FID-O of 68.9 (vs. 245.8 for CELL-E2), again approaching the 463 nm resolvability of the original data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CELL-E2 (Khwaja et al., 2024a), a bidirectional text-to-image transformer using VQGAN. CELL-Diff decisively outperforms CELL-E2 on every metric across both datasets. On HPA, CELL-Diff reduces MSFR from 1824 nm to 641 nm (a ~2.8× improvement in resolvability), increases IoU from 0.462 to 0.484, and cuts FID-O from 166.4 to 51.1 (a 3.3× reduction). On OpenCell, the gains are even more pronounced: MSFR drops from 1165 to 529 nm, and FID-O plummets from 245.8 to 68.9. The paper attributes CELL-E2's poor image quality to its reliance on VQGAN, which produces highly blurred outputs that cannot resolve fine subcellular structures beyond the nucleus. CELL-Diff's use of a continuous latent diffusion model with a VAE encoder/decoder instead preserves high-frequency detail, enabling discernment of cytoplasmic organelles and other fine structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CELL-Diff extends the protein-sequence-to-microscopy-image generation line initiated by CELL-E (Khwaja et al., 2024b) and its bidirectional successor CELL-E2 (Khwaja et al., 2024a), which relied on VQGAN tokenization and produced only coarse, blurred images resolvable at the nuclear level. The key methodological departure is replacing the VQGAN-based discrete image representation with a continuous latent diffusion model (following Stable Diffusion and DiT) for images, while using OA-ARDM discrete diffusion for sequences, all unified in a single transformer-based U-Net with cross-attention. Unlike general multi-modal diffusion frameworks (Unidiffuser, Transfusion) that depend on large pre-trained encoders like CLIP or VQGAN, CELL-Diff trains its own VAE on microscopy data, sidestepping the lack of robust pre-trained encoders for fluorescence images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving ~3× higher spatial resolvability (MSFR) over CELL-E2, CELL-Diff opens the door to studying subcellular protein localization beyond the nucleus (ER, microtubules, cytoskeleton) in silico, with creative applications like virtual multi-protein staining to circumvent the 4-channel fluorescence limit and computational screening of nuclear localization/export signals. However, validation is limited: only one baseline is compared, test sets are small (714 and 473 images), and the generated localization signals have not been experimentally confirmed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse protein datasets, generalizes across cell types and organisms, and whether the image-to-sequence direction produces biologically meaningful sequences beyond motif-level statistics.
