## Problem

The p-Wasserstein distance for p≥2 suffers from two practical limitations: sensitivity to noise (a δ-mass perturbation can inflate the distance by δ^{1/p}) and the absence of scalable approximation algorithms in general metric spaces. Existing relative approximation methods for the 1-Wasserstein distance rely on tree-metric embeddings and metric cost structures that break down for p≥2, where costs d(·,·)^p no longer satisfy the triangle inequality, leaving no known near-quadratic-time approximation algorithm for arbitrary metrics.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two discrete probability distributions μ and ν over point sets A and B with |A|+|B|=n, a ground metric d(·,·) of unit diameter, an integer parameter p≥2, and (for the RPW variant) an additive tolerance δ>0 and constant k>0. Probabilities are represented with log U bits, and the spread Δ (ratio of largest to smallest nonzero pairwise distance) enters the runtime. The relative-approximation algorithm outputs a transport plan σ whose p-Wasserstein cost is within O(log n) of optimal; the RPW algorithm outputs a δ-close estimate of the (p,k)-RPW distance along with a partial transport plan.

## Method

For the O(log n)-relative approximation, the paper constructs p independently sampled hierarchically well-separated trees (HSTs) and defines a tree distance d_T(a,b) as the minimum 2^{-lev_i(a,b)} across all p trees, which provably bounds the expected p-th power distortion by O((4 log n)^p d(a,b)^p). A novel dynamic weighted bichromatic closest pair data structure—maintaining max-heaps per cluster across all p trees and a global min-heap—accelerates each Dijkstra step in the primal-dual Hungarian algorithm to O(n log Δ log n), yielding an overall O(n² log U log Δ log n) runtime. For the (p,k)-RPW additive approximation, the paper lifts the problem into a scaled integer space (scaling masses by s₁=2pn/δ and costs by s₂=g^p δ/(2p)), runs j*=8p/δ² phases of the Gabow–Tarjan scaling algorithm with a modified stopping condition that halts when projected cost exceeds projected untransported mass, and projects the resulting partial plan back. By sweeping over O(1/δ) guess values g_i=iδ/4 and returning the smallest upper bound, the algorithm achieves O(n²/δ³) time independent of p, exploiting the fact that RPW only requires transporting (1−ε−δ) mass at cost ≤ε+δ.

## Results

The paper presents an O(log n)-relative approximation algorithm for the p-Wasserstein distance (p ≥ 2) running in O(n² log U log Δ log n) time, the first near-quadratic-time sub-linear approximation for p ≥ 2 in general metric spaces. For the noise-robust (p,k)-RPW distance, a δ-additive approximation is achieved in O(n²/δ³) time, with runtime independent of p. A hardness reduction shows that any δ-additive approximation for p-Wasserstein in o(n²/δ^p) time would yield a sub-n^2.5 algorithm for bipartite perfect matching, establishing a barrier for improving existing combinatorial approaches. Empirically, the approximation factor remains stable as n grows and does not degrade with increasing p, while the number of BCP data structure operations grows sub-n^1.5 in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against the LMR-RPW algorithm of Raghvendra et al. (2024) for computing the (p,k)-RPW distance. The paper's RPW algorithm outperforms LMR-RPW in runtime across all tested configurations, with the advantage growing as p increases (e.g., at p=4 the new algorithm is significantly faster) and as the additive error δ decreases (the gap widens for δ=0.04 vs. δ=0.1). The relative approximation algorithm is compared conceptually against prior 1-Wasserstein methods (Charikar 2002, Agarwal & Sharathkumar 2014, Sherman 2017), which the paper shows cannot extend to p ≥ 2 because the cost d(·,·)^p violates the triangle inequality, making the tree-metric embedding approach yield unbounded distortion. The paper's key contribution over these prior works is the use of p independent HSTs with minimum edge cost, which tames the noise sensitivity that breaks single-tree embeddings for p ≥ 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HST-based tree metric embedding approach of Charikar (2002) and Fakcharoenphol et al. (2003) from the 1-Wasserstein setting to p≥2, where the single-tree embedding fails because its distortion acts as noise that the p-Wasserstein cost amplifies. The key departure is constructing p independent HSTs and taking the minimum edge cost across them, which provably bounds the expected distortion to O((log n)^p) per pair. For the additive approximation side, the paper builds on the LMR algorithm (Lahn et al., 2019) and Gabow-Tarjan scaling (1989) to design a faster algorithm for the (p,k)-RPW distance introduced by Raghvendra et al. (2024), while also establishing a hardness reduction to bipartite perfect matching that no prior work had identified for p-Wasserstein additive approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first near-quadratic-time O(log n)-approximation algorithm for the p-Wasserstein distance (p≥2) in arbitrary metric spaces, resolving a major open problem in computational optimal transport. The reduction showing that additive approximation of p-Wasserstein requires Ω(n²/δ^p) time (matching known algorithms) provides a clear theoretical barrier and explains why Sinkhorn-type methods, whose runtime grows with p, are essentially optimal among combinatorial approaches. The (p,k)-RPW distance, approximable in O(n²/δ³) time independent of p, offers a practical alternative that is both noise-robust and scalable, directly addressing the two limitations that have hindered p-Wasserstein adoption in ML. Open questions include whether the O(log n) relative approximation factor can be improved, whether the hardness result extends to geometric settings, and whether the RPW distance can be integrated into differentiable OT pipelines.
