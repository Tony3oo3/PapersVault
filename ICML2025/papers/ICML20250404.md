## Problem

Distributional RL captures the full return distribution for richer decision-making, but its theoretical analysis has been hampered by the infinite-dimensionality of distributions, which prior works either ignored or addressed through discretization assumptions that introduce model misspecification and linear regret. A fundamental question remained unresolved: among all statistical functionals (including nonlinear ones like quantiles, medians, and max/min), which can be exactly learned via Bellman updates AND unbiasedly estimated from finite samples in an online setting?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250404/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a finite N-dimensional moment embedding space rather than the full infinite-dimensional distribution space, taking as input state-action pairs (s,a), observed rewards r, sampled next states s', and a user-specified function class F^N ⊆ {f: S×A → R^N}. It produces a greedy policy π^k per episode, N normalized moment estimates E[Z^n]/H^{n-1} of the return distribution (the normalization reduces the confidence region from O(H^N) to O(√N)), and an optimistic Q-function Q_h^k = min{(f̃_h)^{(1)} + b_h^k, H} used for exploration. The final guarantee is a regret bound of Õ(d_E H^{3/2} √K) under statistical functional Bellman completeness, requiring no discretization of rewards.

## Method

The paper introduces Bellman unbiasedness—a property requiring that the sketch of the expected (mixture) target distribution can be unbiasedly estimated from k sampled sketches—and proves via Lemma 3.5 that it is equivalent to the sketch being a homogeneous functional of finite degree. Combining this with Bellman closedness (Theorem 3.6), the authors show that moment functionals (and exponential-polynomial functionals in the discounted case) are the unique finite sketches satisfying both properties, even when nonlinear functionals like max/min are admitted. The proposed SF-LSVI algorithm performs N-moment least square regression at each step using the binomial theorem to compute normalized target moments, then adds an optimistic bonus via the width function of the confidence region for exploration. Two technical refinements yield the improved regret: (1) removing the β(F^N, 1/δ) dependency from the eluder-dimension argument so the bound depends only on the function class, and (2) defining the eluder dimension in the finite-dimensional F^N space rather than an infinite-dimensional distribution class, avoiding the model misspecification error that causes linear regret in prior distBC-based analyses.

## Results

SF-LSVI achieves a regret bound of Õ(d_E H^{3/2} √K) in episodic MDPs with general value function approximation, where d_E is the eluder dimension of the N-dimensional function class F^N, H is the horizon, and K is the number of episodes. In the linear MDP setting (d_E = Õ(d)), this reduces to Õ(√(d²H³K)), matching the known lower bound Ω(√(d²H³K)) from Zhou et al. (2021). The bound is √H tighter than the state-of-the-art distributional RL algorithm V-EST-LSR (Chen et al., 2024), which achieves Õ(d_E H² √K). Additionally, SF-LSVI is the first distributional RL method to operate in a finite-dimensional embedding space without requiring discretized rewards or incurring model misspecification error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF-LSVI outperforms both prior distributional RL baselines on the regret bound: it achieves Õ(d_E H^{3/2} √K) versus V-EST-LSR's Õ(d_E H² √K) (a √H improvement) and O-DISCO's Õ(poly(d_E H)√K). Beyond the tighter bound, SF-LSVI operates under strictly weaker structural assumptions: it requires only statistical functional Bellman completeness (matching N moments to the target) rather than full distributional Bellman completeness, and it does not require discretized rewards or Lipschitz continuity of risk measures. Both O-DISCO and V-EST-LSR work in an infinite-dimensional distribution space H ⊆ F^∞, which introduces unavoidable model misspecification error (leading to linear regret in the worst case), whereas SF-LSVI works in a finite-dimensional embedding space F^N with zero misspecification. The paper also proves that SF-LSVI is the first distributional RL algorithm that is both exactly learnable (Bellman closed) and unbiasedly estimable (Bellman unbiased) in an online setting, properties that neither baseline satisfies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bellman closedness framework of Rowland et al. (2019) by introducing Bellman unbiasedness as a complementary property, proving that moment functionals remain the unique solution even when nonlinear statistical functionals (e.g., max, min, variance) are admitted. It builds on the general value function approximation machinery of Wang et al. (2020) and Osband et al. (2019), generalizing eluder dimension to vector-valued function classes. Crucially, it departs from prior distRL theory (O-DISCO, V-EST-LSR) by working in a finite-dimensional moment embedding space rather than an infinite-dimensional distribution space, thereby eliminating the discretized-reward assumption and the associated model misspecification that causes linear regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably efficient online distRL algorithm with general function approximation that avoids discretization, achieving a regret bound √H better than the prior state-of-the-art (V-EST-LSR). The demonstration that distributional Bellman Completeness is inherently intractable (necessarily inducing linear regret) is a significant negative result that redirects the field toward statistical-functional-based structural assumptions. A key open question the authors highlight is whether regret can be reformulated in terms of moment discrepancies rather than expected return, which would more directly capture the sample-efficiency gains of distributional learning. The work is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
