## Problem

Static LLM benchmarks become outdated and vulnerable to data contamination as models are updated, and they lack a temporal dimension to track how model performance evolves over time. This paper asks: how do LLMs' future-event prediction abilities degrade as their pre-training data becomes stale, and can retrieval-augmented generation or direct access to relevant articles mitigate this degradation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250470/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a forecasting question (True/False or 4-option Multiple Choice) whose resolution date is the publication date of the source news article, ensuring the question is genuinely 'future' relative to that date. In the constrained open-book setting, the model additionally receives up to 5 BM25-retrieved articles (truncated to 512 words each) from a corpus spanning Jan 2019 to a configurable RAG cutoff date, capped at the day before the resolution date. Output is a single prediction (Yes/No or letter choice); refusals are counted as incorrect. Performance is reported as monthly accuracy with 5-month moving averages across the Jan 2020–Dec 2024 window.

## Method

Daily Oracle is constructed via a four-step LLM pipeline applied to 6 news articles per day (3 random + 3 from DBSCAN-identified hot topics on TF-IDF representations): article summarization, QA generation (2 TF + 2 MC per article using GPT-4/GPT-4o with few-shot prompts), misleading-choice generation for MC, and a 7-criteria LLM-based filtering step (scored 0–2 each, threshold ≥13/14) ensuring correctness, non-answerability before publication, no information leakage, objectivity, clear temporal reference, public interest, and non-obviousness. The evaluation framework tests 8 LLMs across three settings—closed-book, constrained open-book (RAG with a tunable cutoff date), and gold-article reading comprehension—to disentangle missing future knowledge from outdated internal representations. The key empirical finding is that performance degrades smoothly over time (avg. −21.55% on TF, −11.33% on MC from 2020 to 2024), with steeper drops after each model's knowledge cutoff, and that even the gold-article setting shows declining accuracy, indicating that continuous pre-training is needed beyond what RAG can provide.

## Results

On the Daily Oracle benchmark (Jan 2020–Dec 2024), all evaluated LLMs show significant performance degradation over time in the closed-book setting: average accuracy drops by 21.55% on TF questions (64.68% → 50.74%) and 11.33% on MC questions (58.30% → 51.69%). Claude-3.5-Sonnet achieves the highest overall accuracy (81.21% TF / 76.86% MC in 2020), while GPT-4 shows the steepest post-cutoff MC decline (−18.54% YoY vs. −4.23% pre-cutoff). In the gold article setting, most models approach ~90% accuracy, confirming question answerability, yet declining trends persist, indicating outdated internal representations beyond missing knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are the eight LLMs evaluated under identical conditions. Claude-3.5-Sonnet dominates across both TF and MC questions, achieving 81.21% TF and 76.86% MC in 2020, well ahead of GPT-4 (69.68% TF / 70.60% MC) and all open-weight models. GPT-4 shows the most dramatic post-cutoff MC degradation (−18.54% YoY), far exceeding its pre-cutoff decline (−4.23%), while GPT-3.5, Qwen-2-7B, and Llama-3-8B exhibit comparatively milder temporal declines. Mistral-7B and Mixtral-8x7B suffer the steepest TF drops (−7.75% and −10.78% average YoY), falling below the 50% random baseline by 2024 due to elevated refusal rates. Gemma-2-2B, despite the lowest absolute accuracy, shows the smallest average YoY decline (−1.04% TF, −1.98% MC), likely benefiting from its recent July 2024 knowledge cutoff. In the constrained open-book (RAG) setting, Llama-3-8B can actually perform worse than closed-book when the RAG cutoff precedes its knowledge cutoff, while extending the RAG cutoff beyond the knowledge cutoff yields significant gains—yet the overall downward trend persists across all RAG configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the automatic QA generation methodology of Zhang et al. (2024, TLB-forecast) and the Common Crawl News collection pipeline of Zou et al. (2022, AutoCast), but departs from both by making the benchmark continuously updated on a daily basis rather than a fixed, one-time dataset. It positions itself against a family of static or periodically updated forecasting benchmarks (ForecastQA, AutoCast, ForecastBench, FreshBench) and dynamic QA datasets (StreamingQA, RealTimeQA, FreshQA), arguing that none simultaneously provide daily updates, forecasting evaluation, and sufficient scale. The paper also extends the temporal generalization literature (Lazaridou et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2025) by replacing perplexity/BPC metrics with a downstream forecasting task that directly measures predictive accuracy over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLMs show a smooth, consistent ~21% accuracy decline on TF questions over five years—and that this decline persists even when the gold article containing the answer is provided—suggests the problem is not merely missing knowledge but outdated internal language representations, pointing toward a need for continuous pre-training. The benchmark itself is practically valuable as the first daily-updated forecasting evaluation that never becomes stale by construction. Key open questions include whether the degradation pattern holds for models with much longer post-cutoff horizons, whether continuous pre-training or more sophisticated RAG can fully arrest the decline, and whether LLM-generated questions introduce systematic biases that limit long-term benchmark reliability.
