## Problem

Existing memory-efficient optimization methods for LLMs (GaLore, BAdam, LoRA) reduce optimizer state by restricting each step's update to a low-rank subspace, discarding gradient information in the orthogonal complement. This per-step information loss is particularly harmful during pre-training, where exploring the full parameter space is critical for convergence. The paper asks whether full-rank gradient utilization is possible while keeping optimizer state memory low.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253247/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, FRUGAL consumes the full backpropagated gradient and a projection operator (SVD, random, or blockwise) that partitions the parameter space into state-full and state-free subspaces. Unlike GaLore or BAdam, which emit only low-rank weight deltas per step, FRUGAL produces full-dimensional parameter updates at every iteration—the state-free component is updated via signSGD with zero stored optimizer state. Optimizer state memory therefore scales with only a fraction ρ of Linear layer parameters (plus fixed non-Linear modules such as Embeddings, RMSNorms, and the Output layer), while the weight trajectory remains full-rank.

## Method

FRUGAL splits each gradient into two orthogonal components: a projected component in a state-full subspace updated with an advanced optimizer (e.g., AdamW), and a residual component in the complementary state-free subspace updated with a state-free method (e.g., signSGD). The key insight is that signSGD captures much of Adam's effectiveness for most transformer parameters due to its similarity to sign-based descent, with the critical exception being the output layer, which must retain AdamW to avoid severe performance degradation. The active subspace is periodically rotated every T steps to explore new directions, and optimizer states are reprojected or reset upon subspace changes to maintain consistency between projected gradients and stored moments. The framework is modular—supporting arbitrary combinations of state-full optimizers, state-free optimizers, and projection strategies—and the authors prove convergence guarantees for the SGDM+SGD special case that recover the best-known O(1/kα + Lασ²) rate under standard non-convex assumptions.

## Results

On C4 pre-training of LLaMA models, FRUGAL (ρ=0.25) achieves validation perplexity of 18.60 (130M), 14.79 (350M), and 12.32 (1B), closely matching full AdamW (18.13, 14.43, 12.02) while using only ~50% of the optimizer state memory (e.g., 0.52G vs 1.00G at 130M). Even at ρ=0.0 (no state-full Linear parameters), FRUGAL reaches 12.63 perplexity at 1B scale with just 0.98G of optimizer state, outperforming GaLore (13.69) and BAdam (13.75) at ρ=0.25. On GLUE fine-tuning of RoBERTa-base, FRUGAL achieves 86.7 average score, surpassing LoRA (86.1) and GaLore (85.8). On LLaMA 3.1-8B commonsense reasoning, FRUGAL attains 84.94 average accuracy with ρ=0 (zero optimizer state), beating LoRA (84.18) and GaLore (84.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRUGAL consistently outperforms GaLore and BAdam across all four LLaMA model sizes (60M–1B) at the same ρ=0.25 memory budget, closing the gap to full AdamW to within 0.3–0.5 perplexity points (e.g., 12.32 vs 12.02 at 1B). Remarkably, FRUGAL at ρ=0.0 (no state-full Linear parameters, only Embeddings/RMSNorms/Output with AdamW) still beats both GaLore and BAdam at ρ=0.25, achieving 12.63 vs 13.69/13.75 at 1B scale. On GLUE fine-tuning, FRUGAL (86.7 avg) surpasses LoRA (86.1) and GaLore (85.8), and on LLaMA 3.1-8B commonsense reasoning, FRUGAL with ρ=0 (84.94) beats both LoRA (84.18) and GaLore (84.65) while requiring zero optimizer state memory. Against concurrent methods, FRUGAL slightly outperforms AdaMeM (18.60 vs 18.99 at 130M), while Fira and LDAdam achieve marginally better perplexity in a modified setup (Table 21) but introduce 10% and 15% wall-clock slowdowns respectively, negating their per-iteration advantage in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRUGAL extends the gradient-projection lineage initiated by GaLore (SVD-based low-rank projection with optimizer state stored in the reduced space) and BAdam (blockwise coordinate descent), but departs fundamentally from both by updating the complementary subspace with state-free optimizers (signSGD/SGD) rather than freezing it. This yields full-rank parameter updates at every step, in contrast to GaLore, ReLoRA, and BAdam where each individual update remains low-rank. The theoretical analysis draws on Block Coordinate Descent convergence theory, and the practical choice of signSGD is motivated by recent findings (Kunstner et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024b) linking Adam's effectiveness in transformers to sign-based descent. Concurrent works (Fira, LDAdam, Adamem) share the gradient-splitting idea but differ in how they handle the residual and state management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRUGAL offers a simple, drop-in technique that reduces optimizer-state memory by up to ~75% while maintaining near-AdamW perplexity across model scales from 60M to 3B, making it directly applicable to practitioners constrained by GPU memory. The finding that only the Output layer requires an advanced optimizer while Embeddings, RMSNorms, and Linear layers tolerate signSGD provides actionable guidance for memory-efficient training configurations. Open questions include whether the signSGD finding generalizes to much larger models (beyond 3B) and non-LLaMA architectures, and whether the theoretical convergence guarantee (proved only for SGDM+SGD) extends to the practical AdamW+signSGD configuration. The method is also orthogonal to quantization and offloading techniques, suggesting further memory reductions are possible through composition.
