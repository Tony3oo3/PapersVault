## Problem

Existing one-shot post-training pruning metrics for LLMs (e.g., Wanda) rely on a simple product of weight magnitude and activation channel norm, which ignores two critical distributional properties: imbalanced weight magnitudes across input/output channels and the disproportionate inflation of channel norms by a small set of activation outliers. This causes pruning decisions to be biased—channels with large norms are over-preserved while outlier-free channels are over-pruned—leading to suboptimal sparsity patterns and significant performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices and calibration activations (128 randomly sampled 2048-token segments from C4, batch size 16) to compute per-layer pruning metrics. It outputs a binary pruning mask for each linear layer, supporting both unstructured sparsity (50%–80%) and semi-structured N:M patterns (4:8, 2:4). The learned power factors (θ₁, θ₂, θ₃) are per-linear-layer, and the mask is applied in a single forward pass from first to last layer, with subsequent layers receiving updated activations from the pruned preceding layer.

## Method

BaWA redefines the pruning metric as (|W_ij|/‖W_j‖₂^θ₁ + |W_ij|/‖W_i‖₂^θ₂) · ‖X_j‖₂^θ₃, where magnitude normalization divides by input and output channel weight norms to equalize pruning pressure across channels with different magnitude scales, and outlier regularization applies learnable power factors to suppress the disproportionate influence of activation and weight outliers on channel norms. The three power factors per layer are automatically optimized per transformer block (rather than per layer) using a zeroth-order gradient estimator that handles the non-differentiable topk in mask generation, minimizing the RMSNorm-squared output difference between the dense and pruned block. This block-wise formulation captures inter-layer interactions while keeping the search space at C^m·L/m instead of C^L, and the entire optimization completes in ~16 minutes for LLaMA2-70B on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On 2:4 sparsity, BaWA reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 2.49 on Mistral-7B (9.88 vs. Wanda's 12.37), 1.14 on LLaMA2-13B (7.13 vs. 8.27), and 0.32 on LLaMA2-70B (4.84 vs. 5.16). On 4:8 sparsity, BaWA achieves perplexity of 6.16 (LLaMA2-13B), 4.32 (LLaMA2-70B), 7.54 (Mistral-7B), and 5.74 (Qwen2-72B), all best among prune-only methods. Combining BaWA with ADMM-Iter weight reconstruction further improves results, e.g., 9.53 perplexity on Mistral-7B at 2:4 sparsity. On zero-shot tasks at 2:4 sparsity, BaWA improves average accuracy by 3.08% over Wanda on Mistral-7B (53.23% vs. 50.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaWA consistently outperforms Wanda (Sun et al., 2023) across all four models and both N:M sparsity patterns, with the largest gap on Mistral-7B at 2:4 sparsity (9.88 vs. 12.37 perplexity, a 2.49 reduction). Against SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023), BaWA as a prune-only metric is competitive or superior (e.g., 7.13 vs. 8.32 on LLaMA2-13B at 2:4), and BaWA+ADMM clearly surpasses SparseGPT in all settings. BaWA also beats the gradient-based GBLM (Das et al., 2023), the symbolic-combination RIA (Zhang et al., 2024), and the evolutionary Pruner-Zero (Dong et al.) on perplexity for both 4:8 and 2:4 sparsity. Among weight-reconstruction baselines, BaWA outperforms DSnoT (Zhang et al., 2023) and ADMM-Iter (Boza, 2024) as a standalone metric, and combining BaWA with ADMM-Iter yields the best overall results (e.g., 4.71 perplexity on LLaMA2-70B at 2:4). On zero-shot tasks, BaWA at 50% sparsity on LLaMA2-70B (67.81% avg.) slightly exceeds both Wanda (67.03%) and SparseGPT (67.28%), while at 2:4 sparsity on Mistral-7B BaWA (53.23%) leads Wanda (50.15%) and SparseGPT (50.88%) by 3+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

BaWA extends the one-shot post-training pruning paradigm initiated by Wanda (weight magnitude × activation norm) by identifying two specific failure modes of the symbolic-combination approach: imbalanced weight magnitudes across channels and disproportionate influence of activation outliers. Rather than proposing yet another fixed symbolic formula (as GBLM-Pruner or Pruner-Zero do), BaWA introduces learnable power factors for channel-wise normalization and outlier suppression, optimized via a zeroth-order gradient estimator inspired by MeZO. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to weight reconstruction techniques (SparseGPT, DSnoT, ADMM-Iter), serving as a drop-in metric replacement that composes with any downstream reconstruction strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BaWA provides a practical, low-overhead pruning metric (seconds without search, ~16 min with search for 70B models) that consistently improves perplexity and downstream accuracy across LLaMA, LLaMA2, Mistral, and Qwen2 families at both unstructured and N:M sparsity levels. Its modularity—being a metric rather than a full pipeline—means it can be adopted alongside future weight reconstruction advances without re-derivation. Open questions include whether the block-wise zeroth-order search is truly near-optimal versus a global formulation, whether the learned power factors transfer across model scales or architectures, and whether the approach extends naturally to structured pruning or quantization-aware settings.
