## Problem

Prior Riemannian optimistic online algorithms suffer from circular dependencies between step sizes and geometric constants (e.g., minimum curvature), and cannot enforce in-manifold constraints without strong assumptions that leave analyses incomplete. Furthermore, implicit optimistic algorithms had not been developed even in the Euclidean setting, and existing Riemannian min-max methods exhibit poor polynomial dependence on curvature-dependent geometric terms, failing to match Euclidean rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on Hadamard manifolds (complete, simply-connected, nonpositive sectional curvature) and consumes g-convex L-smooth loss and hint functions over a compact g-convex constraint set, with inexact solutions permitted under a time-decaying precision criterion ε_t. For the min-max application, the input is a g-convex g-concave L-smooth bi-function f(x,y) over product constraint sets, and the output is an approximate saddle point (x̃_T, ỹ_T) achieving duality gap ε in Õ(LR²/ε) or Õ(L/μ) gradient oracle calls, where R is the initial distance to the solution and μ is the strong g-convexity constant.

## Method

RIOD is a two-step implicit optimistic online algorithm that, instead of linearizing the loss, minimizes the full g-convex loss (or hint) plus a Riemannian proximal regularizer at each step, preserving g-convexity of the subproblems. The two-step structure (primary iterate x̃_t for actions, secondary iterate x_t for conservative updates) ensures the hint-correction term keeps subproblems g-convex, while the implicit formulation avoids the circular dependency between step size and domain diameter that plagues prior Riemannian methods. For min-max optimization, RIODA applies RIOD in parallel to both variables with appropriate sign flips, and implementing the implicit subproblems via Composite Riemannian Gradient Descent (CRGD) yields gradient oracle complexity Õ(LR²/ε) for μ=0 and Õ(L/μ) for μ>0, matching Euclidean rates up to logarithmic factors and removing explicit dependence on the geometric term ζ for the first time.

## Results

RIODA_CRGD achieves gradient oracle complexity of Õ(LR²/ε) for μ=0 and Õ(L/μ) for μ>0, matching the optimal Euclidean rates up to logarithmic factors for the first time on Hadamard manifolds. RIODA_PRGD improves over the prior best constrained method RAMMA by a factor of Õ(ζ_R^3.5), while RIODA_RGD improves over the prior unconstrained method [MRP24] by a factor of Õ(ζ_R²). The online algorithm RIOD achieves regret O(D²/η + ηV_T) on Hadamard manifolds with in-manifold constraints, matching the Euclidean OOMD bound and removing the geometric ζ dependence present in all prior Riemannian optimistic methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIODA_CRGD is the standout result: it achieves Õ(LR²/ε) and Õ(L/μ) rates that are independent of the geometric constant ζ (up to log factors), matching Euclidean-optimal complexity for the first time in the Riemannian min-max setting. Against RAMMA [MRC+23], the only prior method handling in-manifold constraints, RIODA_PRGD improves the rate by a factor of Õ(ζ_R^3.5) and additionally eliminates the need to know the initial distance R and Lipschitz constant G. Against [MRP24] (the prior best unconstrained method not requiring R), RIODA_RGD reduces the ζ dependence from ζ_R⁴ to ζ_R². Compared to [WYH+23] and [HWW+23], which have explicit ζ_R/δ_R factors and require knowledge of both R and G, RIODA_CRGD removes all geometric dependence and parameter knowledge requirements. Against [CJL+23], which achieves curvature-independent rates but only for μ>0, requires knowledge of R, and cannot handle constraints, RIODA_CRGD extends to μ=0, handles constraints, and does not require R. The online algorithm RIOD matches the Euclidean OOMD [RS13] regret bound O(D²/η + ηV_T) exactly, while prior Riemannian methods O-RCEG [HGA23] and ROOGD [WYH+23] carry additional ζ factors in their regret bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-step optimistic online mirror descent (OOMD) framework of Chiang et al. (2012) and Rakhlin & Sridharan (2013) to Hadamard manifolds via implicit (non-linearized) updates, building on the implicit online learning line of Chen & Orabona (2023) and Choi et al. (2023) in the Euclidean setting. It departs from prior Riemannian optimistic methods (Hu et al. 2023a; Wang et al. 2023b) by using full loss functions rather than linearized surrogates, which avoids the circular dependency between step sizes and geometric constants that plagued those analyses. For min-max optimization, RIODA is positioned against RCEG (Zhang et al. 2023), RAMMA (Martínez-Rubio et al. 2023), and RIPPA, with the key distinction being that the implicit update structure combined with Composite Riemannian Gradient Descent (CRGD) removes the explicit polynomial dependence on the curvature constant ζ that all prior methods carried.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-curvature-independent rates O~(LR²/ε) and O~(L/μ) for RIODA_CRGD represent the first time Riemannian min-max optimization matches Euclidean gradient oracle complexity up to logarithmic factors, resolving a gap that had persisted across all prior Riemannian min-max algorithms. The paper clarifies that this does not contradict known lower bounds because the geometry is implicitly captured through the condition number lower bound L/μ ≥ ζ_R in negatively curved spaces, suggesting that for well-conditioned problems the geometric penalty is genuinely negligible. Open questions include the computational cost of CRGD subproblems (which may be hard to solve), whether the implicit-update trick extends to non-Hadamard manifolds or non-smooth settings, and whether the inexactness criterion can be tightened to reduce the logarithmic ζ dependence. The practical impact is currently limited to structured problems (SPD matrices, hyperbolic spaces) where Riemannian formulations are natural, but the online-learning perspective may inform adaptive training for hyperbolic neural networks and distributionally robust optimization.
