## Problem

Conventional fairness metrics like Equalized Odds evaluate LLMs on discrete accuracy alone, overlooking how model uncertainty (e.g., high confidence in biased predictions) creates fairness disparities invisible to error-rate-based measures. Additionally, existing gender-occupation fairness datasets such as WinoBias are small (3,168 samples), syntactically templated, and no longer challenge modern LLMs that rely on semantic rather than syntactic cues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes minimal pairs of co-reference resolution sentences (identical except for the binary pronoun, e.g., 'his' vs. 'her') along with the model's next-word probability distribution over the two occupations. It produces a per-sample fairness score U(x_i) ∈ [0,1], a desirability score D(x_i) ∈ [-1,1] encoding both correctness and certainty on a unified linear scale, and an aggregate UCerF score U(X) = E[U(x_i)]. For type1 (ambiguous) tasks where no ground-truth answer exists, only perplexity is used as the performance signal, and the desired UCerF value remains 1 when the model is equally uncertain for both pronouns.

## Method

UCerF converts perplexity into a normalized certainty c(x_i) ∈ [0,1], then maps each sample to a desirability score D(x_i) ∈ [-1,1] on a Linear Scale of Behavior Preference (LSBP) where confidently-correct predictions score +c, confidently-incorrect score −c, and uncertain predictions fall in between. Per-sample fairness is computed as U(x_i) = 1 − ½|D(x_i^A) − D(x_i^B)| over minimal pairs, and the aggregate UCerF is the expectation over the dataset, yielding a continuous [0,1] score where 1 is perfectly fair. A group-wise reformulation replaces EO's TPR/FPR with True/False Positive Desirability (TPD/FPD), enabling application to datasets without minimal pairs. The companion SynthBias dataset (31,756 samples) is generated by GPT-4o with occupation pairs from BLS statistics, filtered by rule-based checks, and validated by crowd-sourced human annotators using a redefined type1/type2 split based on human ambiguity rather than syntactic cues.

## Results

On SynthBias (31,756 samples), UCerF rankings diverge sharply from Equalized Odds: Mistral-7B-Instruct drops from 4th (EO) to 8th (UCerF) on type2 tasks due to overconfidence in biased predictions, while Pythia-1B rises from 5th (EO) to 1st (UCerF) despite the lowest accuracy (0.645). Falcon-40B-Instruct achieves the best joint fairness-performance score (FP = 0.666) on SynthBias type2, combining 2nd-place accuracy (0.840) with 2nd-place UCerF (0.793). On WinoBias type1 tasks, Mistral-7B-Instruct—despite the lowest perplexity (1.261, i.e., most certain)—ranks 1st under UCerF because it treats both pronouns identically, while Pythia-12B (2nd-highest uncertainty) ranks 9th due to differential confidence across groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Equalized Odds (Hardt et al., 2016), UCerF produces substantially different model rankings: on SynthBias type2, Mistral-7B-Instruct falls from 4th (EO = 0.309) to 8th (UCerF = 0.678) because it is highly confident in biased predictions, while Pythia-1B rises from 5th (EO = 0.341) to 1st (UCerF = 0.815) due to uniformly cautious uncertainty. On WinoBias type1, the gap is even more dramatic—Mistral-7B-Instruct is the most certain model (perplexity 1.261, worst performance) yet ranks 1st under UCerF because its certainty is uniform across groups, whereas Pythia-12B (2nd-highest uncertainty) ranks 9th due to group-differential confidence. Against WinoBias (Zhao et al., 2018) as a dataset baseline, SynthBias is ~10× larger (31,756 vs. 3,168 samples), has 2.6× the vocabulary (5,321 vs. 2,012), and reveals model behaviors invisible on WinoBias: Llama-3-70B-Instruct drops from 3rd to 8th in UCerF ranking on type1, and Pythia-12B overtakes AmberChat in accuracy on SynthBias but not on WinoBias, confirming SynthBias as a more discriminative benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Equalized Odds framework (Hardt et al., 2016) by replacing discrete TPR/FPR with continuous desirability scores that incorporate model uncertainty (perplexity), creating a unified linear scale (LSBP) where correctness and confidence are jointly assessed. It departs from the closest prior work on uncertainty-aware fairness (Kuzucu et al., 2023), which only checked whether two groups share the same uncertainty level, by jointly modeling correctness and uncertainty within a single metric. On the dataset side, it supersedes WinoBias (Zhao et al., 2018) by generating a 10× larger, semantically diverse corpus via GPT-4o with human validation, addressing WinoBias's template rigidity and syntactic-cue focus that no longer challenges modern LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that high-accuracy models like Mistral-7B can appear fair under Equalized Odds while exhibiting severe uncertainty-driven bias (confidently wrong for one gender group) has direct implications for deploying LLMs in hiring and other high-stakes settings. SynthBias's stricter human-validated ambiguity reveals hidden biases in large models (e.g., Llama-3-70B dropping from 3rd to 8th on type1 tasks) that WinoBias's semantic shortcuts mask, making it a more rigorous stress test. Open questions include whether perplexity is the optimal uncertainty estimator for fairness (vs. sampling-based or distributional methods), whether the metric generalizes to generation tasks and non-binary gender, and whether the US-centric occupation stereotypes limit cross-cultural applicability.
