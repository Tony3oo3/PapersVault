## Problem

Existing mesh-based 3D analysis methods model only surface topology and geometry, ignoring the inherent thickness of real-world objects where opposing surfaces exhibit strong deformation correlations. Surface meshes lack internal edge connections between these opposing surfaces, forcing GNNs to rely on long multi-hop paths (≥6 steps) to capture thickness-related interactions. Additionally, incorporating spatial coordinate information while preserving E(3)-equivariance without the heavy computational cost of spherical harmonics or high-dimensional representations remains an open challenge for large-scale industrial meshes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a water-tight 3D surface mesh (~54K nodes, ~325K edges) and a vector of 8 experimental conditions (pack pressure, pack time, projected area, gate size, injection value, volume, melt temperature, mold temperature) as input, and produces a 3D deformation vector (Δx, Δy, Δz) per node. Thickness is not explicitly provided but is derived geometrically by projecting each node along its inward normal to find the closest opposing-surface node. The data-driven coordinate transformation (center-of-mass shift + PCA rotation + direction alignment) is applied before processing and inverted at output, so predictions are returned in the original coordinate system.

## Method

T-EMNN extends MGN's encode-process-decode pipeline with two core innovations. First, a data-driven E(3)-invariant coordinate system is constructed via PCA on centered node positions, with axis directions fixed by a bounding-box-to-centroid reference vector, enabling direct use of 3D coordinates as MLP inputs without spherical harmonics. Second, a thickness processor introduces virtual edges between each node and its thickness-paired node (closest opposing-surface node along the inward normal), with edge features encoding both the thickness distance and the dot product of paired normals. A learnable threshold τ (converging to ~5.68) combined with a sigmoid activation (α=3) gates these thickness edges, filtering out 'width' connections on broad surfaces while retaining true thickness interactions. The architecture processes surface edges and thickness edges in separate message-passing stages per layer, then the decoder concatenates the processed geometric embedding with the spatial embedding and condition embedding before inverse-transforming predictions back to the original coordinate frame.

## Results

On the Injection Molding Dataset, T-EMNN achieves RMSE 0.2132, MAE 0.0892, and R² 0.9228 in the in-distribution setting, and RMSE 0.2131, MAE 0.0918, R² 0.9513 in the out-of-distribution (rotated) setting. The best baseline (EMNN with data-driven coordinates) achieves RMSE 0.2210 and R² 0.9149 in-distribution, and RMSE 0.2210 and R² 0.9473 out-of-distribution. On the Deforming Plate dynamic task, T-EMNN reduces RMSE from 17.420×10⁻³ to 14.903×10⁻³ and improves R² from 0.7007 to 0.7579 over its ablated variant without thickness edges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-EMNN outperforms all three baseline architectures (MGN, EGNN, EMNN) across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. Against the strongest baseline (EMNN with data-driven coordinates, row k), T-EMNN reduces in-distribution RMSE from 0.2210 to 0.2132 (−3.5%) and improves R² from 0.9149 to 0.9228; in the out-of-distribution setting, RMSE drops from 0.2210 to 0.2131 and R² rises from 0.9473 to 0.9513. Without spatial information, all three GNN baselines fail catastrophically (R² near 0 or strongly negative), underscoring that local geometric features alone are insufficient for this task. When the authors add thickness edges to each baseline architecture (Fig. 8), all baselines improve, confirming that the thickness-aware message passing is the key differentiator. The data-driven coordinate system also enables E(3)-equivariance in models that originally lacked it (e.g., MGN), closing the OOD performance gap between original and rotated coordinate systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-EMNN extends the encode-process-decode framework of MGN (Pfaff et al., 2020) by adding a dedicated thickness processor that introduces virtual edges between opposing surface nodes, addressing a gap that prior mesh-based methods (MGN, EGNN, EMNN) leave unaddressed by modeling only surface topology. For E(3)-equivariance, it departs from the spherical-harmonics and higher-dimensional representation approaches (e.g., SE(3)-Transformer, EGNN) in favor of a lightweight PCA-based data-driven coordinate transformation that achieves invariance without the associated computational overhead. The paper also positions itself against methods that avoid spatial information entirely (relying solely on relative displacements or cross products), arguing that global spatial context is necessary for accurate field-variable prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The thickness-aware message-passing mechanism offers a practical way to capture coupled dynamics between opposing surfaces without the computational cost of volume meshes, which is directly relevant to industrial static analysis (e.g., injection molding) where FEM solvers are too slow for iterative design. The learnable thickness threshold that automatically distinguishes 'thickness' from 'width' is a neat inductive bias, though its generalizability across diverse geometries and multi-material settings remains untested. Open questions include whether the PCA-based coordinate system handles highly symmetric or degenerate shapes robustly, whether the single-material assumption limits applicability, and whether the approach scales to truly large-scale meshes beyond the ~54K-node industrial dataset used here.
