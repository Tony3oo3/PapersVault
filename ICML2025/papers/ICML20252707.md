## Problem

Most generalization analyses for graph learning models focus on feature or label distribution shifts, leaving the critical question of how models generalize when graph topologies themselves change between training and testing (topological distribution shift) largely unexplored. Common GNNs, interpretable as local diffusion processes, provably suffer exponentially growing generalization error under such shifts, and no principled architecture existed that could control this error while retaining access to observed structural information.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} and a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, producing node representations Z ∈ R^{N×d} that are decoded via task-specific heads (MLP for node-level, sum-pooling+MLP for graph-level, or concatenated-pair MLP for edge-level predictions). A key design choice is the hyperparameter β ∈ [0,1] that interpolates between pure global attention (β=0, ignoring topology) and full advection (β=1, emphasizing observed structure), allowing the model to adapt to settings where graph topology is informative or uninformative for the target label.

## Method

ADVDIFFORMER is derived from the advective diffusion PDE ∂Z/∂t = [C + βV − I]Z, where the non-local diffusion coupling matrix C is instantiated as a global attention network (capturing environment-invariant latent interactions) and the advection velocity V is the symmetric normalized adjacency D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2} (capturing environment-sensitive observed topology). The closed-form solution Z(T) = e^{-(I−C−βV)T}Z(0) is computed without numerical PDE integration: ADVDIFFORMER-I approximates the matrix exponential via Padé-Chebyshev rational fractions (solving multiple linear systems in parallel heads), while ADVDIFFORMER-S uses a finite geometric series truncated at order K, achieving O(N) feed-forward complexity by reordering matrix products. The central theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that, under a mild injectivity condition on the node-feature generator, the model can bound the variation of node representations to any polynomial order in ‖ΔÃ‖₂, in contrast to local diffusion models whose error bound grows as ‖ΔÃ‖₂·exp(‖ΔÃ‖₂T). This yields a provable generalization guarantee under topological shifts without requiring the conditional independence between labels and adjacency that non-local diffusion alone would need.

## Results

On Arxiv node classification, ADVDIFFORMER-S achieves the best accuracy across all three temporal test splits: 53.41% (2018), 51.53% (2019), and 49.64% (2020), outperforming the next-best method GRAND by 0.96–1.63 points. On Twitch (geographic split), ADVDIFFORMER-S attains the highest average ROC-AUC of 62.51, edging out GraphGPS (62.13) and GRAND (61.65). On DPPIN protein-interaction networks, ADVDIFFORMER-S achieves the best node-regression RMSE (0.574 test average, 0.644 test worst), while ADVDIFFORMER-I leads on edge regression (0.166 test average, 0.184 test worst).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADVDIFFORMER-S consistently outperforms all classical GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, SGC) by 1.5–3.5 accuracy points on Arxiv and 1.5–3.4 ROC-AUC points on Twitch. Among diffusion-based GNNs, it surpasses GRAND (the strongest diffusion baseline) by 0.96–1.63 points on Arxiv and 0.86 points on Twitch, while A-DGNs and CDE lag further behind by 2–4 points. Against graph Transformers, ADVDIFFORMER-S beats GraphGPS by 0.38–2.38 points on Arxiv and 0.38 points on Twitch; GraphTrans runs out of memory on Arxiv (169K nodes) but is competitive on Twitch (61.65 vs. 62.51). The closest competitor is the diffusion-based DIFFormer/DIFormer, which trails ADVDIFFORMER-S by 1.0–5.3 points on Arxiv and by 0.4 points on Twitch. On DPPIN, ADVDIFFORMER-S leads node regression by 0.06–0.07 RMSE over DIFFormer, while ADVDIFFORMER-I leads edge regression by 0.017–0.013 RMSE over DIFFormer, confirming that the advection term (local message passing) is particularly beneficial for edge-level tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of graph learning under a single PDE framework: local diffusion (GNNs, e.g., GRAND, GCN) and non-local diffusion (graph Transformers, e.g., DIFFormer, GraphGPS). The key architectural departure is the introduction of an advection term alongside the diffusion term, where the advection (local message passing via normalized adjacency) captures environment-sensitive topology while the diffusion (global attention) captures environment-invariant latent interactions. The implementation leverages the Padé-Chebyshev rational approximation (Gallopoulos & Saad, 1992) for efficient matrix exponential computation, extending a technique previously used in 3D shape analysis to graph learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable generalization guarantees for graph learning models under topological distribution shifts, showing that advective diffusion achieves polynomial-order error bounds while standard local diffusion (GNNs) suffers exponential degradation. The practical impact is demonstrated across diverse domains (citation networks, social networks, protein interactions, molecular screening), though empirical gains over strong baselines like GraphGPS and DIFFormer are modest (1-3% accuracy, small RMSE differences). Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear diffusion equations (left as future work), relaxing the injectivity assumption on the feature-generation function, and validating the data-generation hypothesis (graphon-based) on more complex real-world topological shifts beyond the synthetic SBM setting.
