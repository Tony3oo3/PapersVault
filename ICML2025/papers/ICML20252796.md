## Problem

Existing document-level relation extraction methods built on PLMs fail to model structural interactions between entities and relations, and struggle to infer implicit relations spanning multiple sentences. Furthermore, integrating knowledge graphs with language models introduces a semantic misalignment because PLMs are trained on unstructured text while KGs encode structured relational data, degrading extraction performance on complex cross-sentence tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a document segmented into sentences along with a pre-defined relation set, and produces structured (subject, relation, object) triplets with predicted relation types. During inference, the document is processed through two parallel retrieval channels: a graph-based channel that extracts a relevant subgraph from a pre-constructed DocKG (threshold τ_eq = 0.7, max 128 tokens) and a sentence-based channel that retrieves Top-5 semantically relevant sentences via BGE embeddings (max 512 tokens), both of which are concatenated into a single hybrid prompt fed to the LLM.

## Method

DocKS-RAG is a two-stage framework. In stage 1, it constructs a Document-level Knowledge Graph (DocKG) using GNNs (8 convolutional layers, 128-dim embeddings) to learn joint entity-relation representations, then retrieves a query-relevant subgraph via cosine similarity and converts it into natural-language prompts through a predefined entity-relation mapping function. In parallel, a Sentence-level Semantic RAG (SetRAG) module embeds all sentences with BGE, retrieves Top-K contextually relevant sentences, and appends them with their associated triplets to form a second prompt component. The two prompt components are concatenated into a hybrid prompt. In stage 2, LLaMA3-8B is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16, 3 epochs, lr 5e-5) on the hybrid prompts, enabling parameter-efficient adaptation that bridges the structural-semantic gap without modifying the full LLM parameter set.

## Results

DocKS-RAG achieves state-of-the-art performance on both benchmark datasets. On DocRED (test), it reaches 61.83 Ign-F1 and 63.59 F1, surpassing the previous best (KD-DocRE at 60.04/62.08) by approximately 1.8 and 1.5 points respectively. On Re-DocRED (test), it attains 74.32 Ign-F1 and 75.49 F1, outperforming KD-DocRE (73.64/74.55) by 0.68 and 0.94 points. The ablation study confirms that removing DocKG drops F1 by ~5.5 points, removing SetRAG drops it by ~8.6 points, and removing Hybrid-Prompt Tuning causes a catastrophic drop to ~21 F1 on DocRED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocKS-RAG outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset where they are compared. On DocRED (test F1), it leads KD-DocRE (62.08) by 1.51 points, SIRE (62.05) by 1.54, and SRLR (61.88) by 1.71; the gap over older PLM/graph methods like GAIN (61.24), HeterGSAN (59.45), and LSR (59.05) is even larger (2.3–4.5 points). On Re-DocRED (test F1), DocKS-RAG (75.49) surpasses KD-DocRE (74.55) by 0.94, DocuNet (74.48) by 1.01, KMGRE (74.46) by 1.03, and LMRC (72.93) by 2.56. The LLM-based AutoRE baseline reports only 53.84 F1 on Re-DocRED test, trailing DocKS-RAG by over 21 points, highlighting the benefit of combining structural graph knowledge with LLM-based prompt tuning. Notably, the closest competitor KD-DocRE is a PLM-based method with knowledge distillation, suggesting that the hybrid DocKG + SetRAG + LoRA pipeline provides a meaningful advantage over purely discriminative or generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads for document-level RE: (1) PLM-based methods that model entity representations but underutilize structural interactions between entities and relations, (2) graph-based methods (GAIN, HIN, HeterGSAN) that capture topology but suffer from semantic misalignment with language models and introduce noisy edges, and (3) recent LLM-based approaches (AutoRE, GAP, KG-RAG) that leverage strong semantic understanding yet largely ignore the heterogeneity between structured graph knowledge and unstructured text. DocKS-RAG carves new ground by repurposing GNNs to build a document-level knowledge graph, sentence-level RAG to retrieve contextual semantics, and LoRA-based hybrid prompt tuning to bridge the structural-semantic gap, rather than proposing a fundamentally new architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for injecting both structural and semantic priors into LLM-based RE via parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which is attractive given the compute constraints of full LLM fine-tuning. However, the ablation revealing that removing hybrid-prompt tuning collapses F1 from ~63 to ~21 on DocRED suggests the retrieval and knowledge-graph components contribute far less than the fine-tuning itself, raising questions about whether the added complexity of DocKG and SetRAG is justified over simpler prompt engineering. The evaluation is limited to a single LLM (LLaMA3-8B) and two datasets, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to larger models, other domains, or zero/few-shot settings. The authors themselves flag domain adaptation and scalability across LLM sizes as key future directions.
