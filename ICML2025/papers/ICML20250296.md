## Problem

Uniform layerwise pruning assigns the same sparsity rate to every layer of an LLM, causing severe performance degradation at high sparsity levels because it ignores the varying importance of different layers. While OWL introduced non-uniform sparsity based on outlier proportions, it relies on an empirically chosen threshold M that varies across model types and parameter scales, limiting adaptability and preventing optimal performance. DLP addresses this by automatically computing layerwise importance without any predefined hyperparameters tied to model architecture or size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250296/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices W and corresponding input activations X (obtained by forward-propagating 128 calibration samples of 2048 tokens from C4 through the model), along with a target global sparsity rate p and a deflation scale α that controls the spread of per-layer sparsity. It outputs a vector of per-layer sparsity rates R_j, each constrained to [p−α, p+α] with a global mean of p, which are then applied during actual pruning at the per-output (per-row) granularity within each layer rather than uniformly across all rows.

## Method

DLP computes per-weight importance scores using Wanda's metric A_ij^l = |W_ij^l|·||X_j^l||_2, then measures each layer's absolute unimportance S^l as the median of all scores in that layer—chosen for its robustness to the heavy-tailed outlier distributions characteristic of LLMs. These unimportance scores are inverted into a Relative Importance Distribution (RID) via I^l = 1 − S^l/ΣS^l, so that layers with lower median scores (less redundancy) receive higher importance. The RID is then linearly rescaled to [0, 2α] and used to assign per-layer sparsity as R_j = p + m − d_j, where m is the mean of the rescaled scores, guaranteeing the global average sparsity equals p while more important layers are pruned less. This eliminates the need for OWL's empirical outlier threshold M and adapts automatically to any model type or parameter scale.

## Results

At 70% unstructured sparsity on WikiText, DLP consistently achieves the lowest perplexity across all model sizes and pruning methods. For example, with Wanda on LLaMA1-7B, DLP reduces perplexity to 20.46 (vs. 24.46 for OWL and 86.38 for uniform), and with SparseGPT on LLaMA2-7B, it achieves 18.58 (vs. 19.71 for OWL and 27.84 for uniform). On zero-shot tasks, DLP improves average accuracy by up to 10.67% over uniform SparseGPT on LLaMA2-13B. On the DeepSparse CPU inference engine, DLP-pruned LLaMA2-7B achieves 2.8×–3.7× end-to-end speedup at 70%–90% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLP is compared against 13 baselines spanning uniform pruning (Magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda), non-uniform layerwise methods (OWL, Global, ER, ER-Plus, LAMP, AlphaPruning), structured pruning (LLM-Pruner), and PEFT methods (LISA, OwLore). DLP consistently outperforms uniform layerwise pruning across all sparsity levels ≥50%, with the gap widening sharply at 70%+ (e.g., Wanda on LLaMA1-7B: 20.46 vs. 86.38 perplexity). Against OWL, DLP achieves lower perplexity at 70% sparsity (e.g., 20.46 vs. 24.46 with Wanda on LLaMA1-7B; 17.76 vs. 19.08 with SparseGPT) and at 80% sparsity reduces perplexity by 56% over OWL. Among other layerwise methods (Global, ER, ER-Plus, LAMP, AlphaPruning), DLP is the best at sparsity ≥40%, with AlphaPruning being the closest competitor (24.00 vs. 20.46 at 70% with Wanda). For PEFT, DLP's RID-based layer sampling achieves 70.09% mean few-shot accuracy on LLaMA2-7B, outperforming LISA (65.21%) by 4.88% and OwLore (69.29%) by 0.8%. In structured pruning, replacing LLM-Pruner's uniform sparsity with DLP's non-uniform allocation reduces perplexity from 33,849.77 to 642.16 at 80% sparsity on WikiText.

## Relation to Prior Work

DLP builds directly on Wanda's weight-activation scoring metric (|W|·||X||₂) as the per-weight importance signal, and on OWL's core insight that non-uniform layerwise sparsity outperforms uniform allocation at high sparsity. The key departure from OWL is replacing its empirically tuned outlier threshold M with a median-based unimportance measure, which the authors argue is more robust because the median's insensitivity to extreme values better captures the central tendency of each layer's weight distribution. The method also extends the median-based pruning lineage from FPGM and LRMF (which used geometric medians for filter redundancy) to a simpler, lower-cost statistical median applied at the layer level in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLP offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for high-sparsity LLM pruning that avoids the per-model hyperparameter tuning required by OWL, and its compatibility with quantization, N:M sparsity, SVD, and PEFT makes it a broadly applicable layerwise allocation strategy. However, the deflation scale α still requires per-sparsity-level tuning (Table 19), partially undermining the 'no empirical values' claim, and the structured pruning gains, while large in absolute terms, remain less dramatic than the unstructured results. Open questions include whether the median-based importance signal generalizes to multimodal or non-Transformer architectures, and whether the per-layer (vs. per-block) allocation granularity is optimal for all model families.
