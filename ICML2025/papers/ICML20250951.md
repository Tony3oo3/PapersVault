## Problem

Heavy-tailed self-regularization (HT-SR) theory diagnoses neural network training quality by measuring the heavytailness of empirical spectral densities (ESDs) of weight matrices, but prior methods ignore the fact that the Marchenko-Pastur distribution governing ESD shape depends explicitly on the aspect ratio m/n. Weight matrices with large aspect ratios (e.g., 512×100 final classification layers) appear artificially less heavy-tailed, causing HT-SR metrics to misclassify well-trained layers as undertrained and leading to incorrect layer-wise learning rate or pruning ratio assignments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw weight matrices of each DNN layer—2D matrices for linear/Transformer layers and 4D tensors [C1, C2, kH, kW] for convolutional layers—and produces a single scalar PL_Alpha_Hill value per layer. For CNN layers, the kernel dimensions are flattened into l′ = kH×kW separate C1×C2 matrices, each independently subsampled, with HT metrics averaged across all resulting submatrices. The resulting per-layer metrics are then fed into downstream optimization routines (TempBalance for learning rates, AlphaPruning for sparsity ratios, TB_Sigmoid for SciML fine-tuning) to assign layer-wise hyperparameters.

## Method

FARMS (Fixed Aspect-Ratio Matrix Subsampling) eliminates aspect ratio bias by partitioning each weight matrix into overlapping submatrices of a fixed aspect ratio Q (default 1.0) via a sliding window, computing eigenvalues of the correlation matrix W_ij^T W_ij for each submatrix, and measuring heavytailness on the merged (averaged) ESD rather than the original full-matrix ESD. For 4D CNN weight tensors, the kernel dimensions are flattened to yield l′ two-dimensional C1×C2 matrices, each independently subsampled, and the resulting HT metrics are averaged rather than pooling all singular values into one ESD (ablation in Table 7 confirms this yields better performance). The Hill estimator is then applied to the averaged ESD to compute PL_Alpha_Hill, which is now invariant to the original matrix's aspect ratio. This normalization makes HT metrics directly comparable across heterogeneous layer sizes, enabling accurate layer-wise hyperparameter assignment without the heuristic layer-exclusion (LS) workaround that prior methods required for extreme-aspect-ratio layers.

## Results

On LLM pruning, FARMS reduces the WikiText perplexity of LLaMA-7B from 96.02 to 79.42 (17.3% reduction) at 0.8 sparsity with SparseGPT, and LLaMA-13B from 47.57 to 41.44 under the same setting. On CIFAR-100 image classification, integrating FARMS into TempBalance improves test accuracy across all architectures: ResNet-18 from 79.03% to 79.35%, ResNet-34 from 79.81% to 80.07%, VGG-16 from 74.87% to 75.16%, and VGG-19 from 73.89% to 74.03%, while also reducing standard deviation. In SciML fine-tuning on 2D Compressible Navier-Stokes, FARMS lowers L2RE by up to 5.66% (DPOT-Tiny: 1.078e-2 → 1.017e-2) compared to TB_Sigmoid at 100% data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AlphaPruning (Lu et al., 2024) for LLM pruning, FARMS consistently achieves lower WikiText perplexity across all sparsity ratios (0.7–0.85) and all three pruning backends (Magnitude, Wanda, SparseGPT) on both LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-13B; the largest gain is 17.3% perplexity reduction (96.02 → 79.42) for LLaMA-7B at 0.8 sparsity with SparseGPT, and FARMS also yields lower perplexity standard deviation in most settings. Against TempBalance (Zhou et al., 2024) for layer-wise learning-rate assignment on CIFAR-100, FARMS wins on all four architectures (ResNet-18/34, VGG-16/19) with 0.14–0.32 percentage-point accuracy gains and notably lower variance; critically, FARMS remains stable even when the 'layer selection' heuristic (which excludes extreme-aspect-ratio layers) is removed, whereas TempBalance without it degrades significantly (e.g., VGG-19 drops from 74.19% to 73.22%). Against TB_Sigmoid (Liu et al., 2024) for SciML fine-tuning, FARMS achieves lower L2RE at every data subsampling ratio (5%–100%) on both DPOT-Tiny and DPOT-Small, with the gap widening at higher data fractions (5.66% relative reduction at 100% data for DPOT-Tiny). In all three comparisons, FARMS is a drop-in replacement for the HT-metric computation step, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying optimization method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization (HT-SR) framework initiated by Martin & Mahoney (2021), which uses random matrix theory to diagnose DNN training quality via the heavy-tailedness of weight-matrix empirical spectral densities. It directly extends three recent HT-SR-based optimization methods—TempBalance for layer-wise learning rates (Zhou et al., 2024), AlphaPruning for LLM sparsity allocation (Lu et al., 2024), and TB_Sigmoid for SciML fine-tuning (Liu et al., 2024)—by replacing their whole-matrix heavytailness measurement with a fixed-aspect-ratio subsampling procedure (FARMS). The key conceptual departure is identifying that the Marchenko-Pastur dependence of ESD shape on the aspect ratio γ = m/n introduces a systematic, previously unacknowledged bias when comparing layers of heterogeneous sizes, and that this bias can be eliminated by averaging ESDs over submatrices of uniform aspect ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The aspect ratio bias identified here is a latent flaw in every existing HT-SR diagnostic and optimization pipeline, meaning that layer-wise hyperparameter assignments (learning rates, pruning ratios) have been systematically distorted for layers with extreme aspect ratios (e.g., final classification heads). FARMS is a drop-in replacement that requires no architectural changes and consistently improves or stabilizes performance across CV, NLP, and SciML domains, with the most dramatic gains at high sparsity in LLM pruning (17.3% perplexity reduction on LLaMA-7B). Open questions include whether formal theoretical guarantees can be established for the subsampling procedure's preservation of feature-spike structure, whether the optimal fixed aspect ratio Q and window size transfer across model families, and how the method behaves for extremely wide or deep architectures where the number of submatrices grows substantially.
