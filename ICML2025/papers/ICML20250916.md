## Problem

Capability evaluations must accurately estimate an AI system's true capabilities, yet previously latent capabilities are often elicited long after initial release, and developers may strategically hide capabilities (sandbagging). While prior work demonstrated that password-locked models can be elicited, it is unclear which elicitation techniques are effective against more robust hiding mechanisms, and how effectiveness varies across task types. This paper systematically evaluates prompting, activation steering, and fine-tuning techniques against two model organisms of hidden capabilities to identify which methods reliably recover true performance.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a base language model (Mistral 7B or Gemma 2 9B) and fine-tunes it via LoRA into a model organism whose capabilities are gated behind a password, then applies elicitation techniques (N-shot examples, response prefilling strings, contrastive steering vectors with tunable coefficients and target layers, or supervised/anti-refusal fine-tuning) to recover the hidden capability without the password. Outputs are accuracy scores on WMDP and MMLU (multiple-choice, 4 options) and pass-rate on APPS (open-ended code generation evaluated via BigCodeBench with all test cases required to pass), reported per technique, per model organism variant, and per number of shots or turns.

## Method

The paper introduces a novel model organism that combines password-locking (fine-tuning on paired correct-with-password / incorrect-without-password data) with circuit-breaking, an adversarial robustness technique that fine-tunes using two loss terms: one penalizing activation similarity to the original model on trigger inputs (those lacking the password) and one retaining similarity on non-trigger inputs, thereby altering internal representations in early-to-middle layers rather than just output tokens. Elicitation is then tested across three families: prompting (N-shot with 1–10 examples, response prefilling with task-specific affirmative strings, and multi-turn persistent requests), activation steering (concept steering via truthfulness contrast pairs and persona steering via contrasting instruction pairs with random suffixes, applied at selected layers with swept coefficients), and fine-tuning (supervised fine-tuning on task demonstrations and anti-refusal training on unrelated harmful-request/affirmative-response pairs). The key finding is that while prompting and steering recover MCQA capabilities from both model organisms, only fine-tuning-based methods (especially anti-refusal training, which requires no task-specific data) reliably elicit the hidden code-generation capability of the circuit-broken model, and combining prompting techniques (N-shot + prefilling) approaches fine-tuning performance for MCQA without requiring weight access.

## Results

On WMDP, N-shot prompting nearly fully elicits the password-locked Mistral-base model (58.1% vs. 58.4% with password) but only partially elicits the circuit-broken variant (33.8% vs. 58.7% with password). Combining N-shot with prefilling recovers full capability for both organisms (57.6% and 61.1% respectively). On APPS (code generation), no prompting or steering technique exceeds 7% for the circuit-broken model, while supervised fine-tuning and anti-refusal training both recover the full hidden capability (~35.8%). Anti-refusal training, which requires no task-specific demonstrations, matches or approaches SFT performance across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the password-locked model organism from Greenblatt et al. (2024). The paper demonstrates that password-locked models are not robust to simple prompting: N-shot prompting recovers 49.5–58.1% on WMDP (vs. 52.2–58.4% with the password), meaning the hidden capability is nearly fully exposed without the password. The novel circuit-broken model organism is substantially more robust: N-shot alone only reaches 33.8% (Mistral-base) and 30.3% (Mistral-instruct) on WMDP, and near-zero on MMLU for Mistral-instruct (4.4% at 10-shot). However, circuit-breaking is fragile to hyperparameters—Gemma-instruct's circuit-broken variant is actually *less* robust than its password-locked counterpart on MMLU (7.7% vs. 26.4% N-shot). On APPS, the gap is stark: no prompting or steering technique exceeds 7% for the circuit-broken model, while SFT and anti-refusal training both recover the full ~35.8% capability. The paper concludes that fine-tuning (particularly anti-refusal training, which needs no task-specific data) is the only reliable elicitation method for the more robust circuit-broken organism in the code-generation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-organism methodology for stress-testing capability elicitation (Hubinger et al., 2023; Greenblatt et al., 2024) by combining password-locking with circuit-breaking (Zou et al., 2024) to create a more robust model organism that resists simple prompting attacks. It departs from Greenblatt et al. (2024), who showed password-locked models are vulnerable to fine-tuning, by demonstrating that even basic N-shot prompting and prefilling can recover most hidden capabilities in the MCQA setting, motivating the need for the harder circuit-broken variant. The elicitation techniques evaluated (few-shot prompting, contrastive activation addition, anti-refusal training) are drawn from the broader jailbreaking, representation engineering, and safety-training literatures, but are here applied systematically to a controlled hidden-capability setting rather than to safety refusal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical guidance for AI safety evaluators: fine-tuning (especially anti-refusal training, which requires no task-specific demonstrations) is the most reliable elicitation method, and combining prompting techniques (N-shot + prefilling) is the best black-box alternative for MCQA. However, for code generation with the circuit-broken model, no prompting or steering method recovers the hidden capability, leaving a significant gap for evaluators without fine-tuning access. Open questions include whether circuit-breaking's fragility to hyperparameters reflects a fundamental limitation or a training-stability issue, whether more realistic model organisms (beyond backdoor-style password-locking) would yield different conclusions, and whether the results generalize to larger frontier models where inference-time compute may further obscure latent capabilities.
