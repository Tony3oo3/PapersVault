## Problem

Building robust, intervenable simulators for real-world decision-making (healthcare, logistics, epidemiology) remains difficult: purely data-driven world models fail under out-of-distribution interventions and sparse/fragmented data, while purely LLM-generated simulators lack empirical grounding and accumulate quantitative errors. No existing framework unifies LLM-driven structural reasoning with rigorous, gradient-free parameter calibration to produce simulators that are simultaneously causally plausible, empirically aligned, and amenable to system-level 'what if?' experimentation.

## Input / Output

G-Sim consumes a textual environment description plus domain knowledge K (manuals, causal constraints, known submodules) and observational data D consisting of fragmented, partially observed state-action trajectories that may be asynchronous, sparse, or unpaired across submodules. It outputs a compositional simulator as executable code with a structural configuration λ (active submodules, causal coupling rules) and calibrated numerical parameters ω, which together generate simulated state trajectories supporting discrete, continuous, and stochastic dynamics. The simulator is modular by design, enabling submodule-level interventions and targeted updates without rebuilding the full system.

## Method

G-Sim operates as an iterative refinement loop with three phases: (1) an LLM proposes a structural configuration λ (submodule templates and causal links) from domain knowledge, (2) numerical parameters ω are calibrated against data using either Gradient-Free Optimization (evolutionary strategies via EvoTorch minimizing a Wasserstein/MSE fitness) or Simulation-Based Inference (Neural Posterior Estimation for a full posterior over ω), both likelihood-free and gradient-free with respect to the simulator, and (3) a diagnostic function aggregates predictive discrepancies and domain-violation checks into natural-language feedback that guides the LLM to restructure in the next iteration. The key architectural insight is the explicit factorization of the parameter space into structural (λ) and numerical (ω) components, letting the LLM handle topology while data-driven methods handle calibration, with the two co-evolving over up to m iterations until convergence. This design accommodates non-differentiable, stochastic, and discrete simulators that would be intractable for standard gradient-based fitting.

## Results

G-Sim achieves the lowest test Wasserstein distance (lower is better) across all three benchmark environments. G-Sim-SBI (the SBI-calibrated variant) records 0.351±0.094 on COVID-19 SIR, 1.22±1.68 on Supply Chain, and 5.24±2.70 on Hospital Bed Scheduling. The G-Sim-ES variant is competitive on COVID-19 (0.405±0.060) and Supply Chain (1.55±1.39) but degrades on Hospital Beds (101±17.4), while the best data-driven baseline (Causal Transformer) reaches 3.30, 2.29, and 199 respectively. The performance gap is most dramatic on Hospital Bed Scheduling, where G-Sim-SBI outperforms the strongest baseline by a factor of ~38×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-Sim-SBI outperforms all five baselines on every environment. Against the strongest baseline, the Causal Transformer (3.30 / 2.29 / 199), G-Sim-SBI improves Wasserstein distance by ~9× on COVID-19, ~1.9× on Supply Chain, and ~38× on Hospital Beds. The equation-discovery methods (SINDy and Genetic Program) and the continuous-dynamics model (DyNODE) perform worst overall, with DyNODE reaching 65.1 on COVID-19 and 231 on Hospital Beds—over 180× worse than G-Sim-SBI. The RNN baseline (16.7 / 9.71 / 199) is intermediate, confirming that purely data-driven sequence models struggle with the discrete, stochastic, and partially-observed structure of these tasks. Notably, even the zero-shot LLM ablation (no calibration, no refinement) achieves 1.17 on COVID-19, already beating the Transformer, underscoring the value of LLM-proposed structure; however, full G-Sim with iterative refinement and SBI calibration narrows this to 0.351, demonstrating the critical contribution of empirical grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

G-Sim sits at the intersection of LLM-coded simulation (WorldCoder, OMNI-EPIC, GenSim) and simulation-based inference (Cranmer et al., 2020), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike WorldCoder, which generates deterministic environment code with only partial calibration, G-Sim explicitly handles stochastic, discrete, and non-differentiable simulators and offers a choice between gradient-free optimization (via EvoTorch) and full Bayesian posterior inference (via Neural Posterior Estimation). Unlike purely data-driven world models (DyNODE, causal Transformers, SINDy), G-Sim injects domain-knowledge-driven structural priors through an LLM in an iterative refinement loop, addressing the causal ill-posedness of extrapolation under sparse or fragmented observational data. The closest prior work is WorldCoder (Tang et al., 2024), but G-Sim extends it with principled parameter inference, support for stochastic processes, and a diagnostic-driven refinement mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G-Sim addresses a practical gap: building intervenable, causally-grounded simulators for high-stakes domains (epidemics, healthcare logistics, supply chains) where data is fragmented and purely data-driven or purely LLM-generated approaches each fail in complementary ways. The dramatic performance gap on the Hospital Bed Scheduling task (Wasserstein 5.24 vs. ~200 for all data-driven baselines) demonstrates that structural priors plus calibration are essential for complex, partially-observed, multi-entity systems. Key open questions include: (1) SBI posteriors within G-Sim do not capture structural uncertainty from the LLM search, leaving a gap in principled uncertainty quantification; (2) scalability to very high-dimensional systems remains untested; and (3) the LLM coverage assumption (that the model can propose a sufficiently good structure) is critical but hard to verify a priori.
