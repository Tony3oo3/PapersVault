## Problem

Existing Factored MDP (FMDP) methods for overcoming the curse of dimensionality in RL suffer from three critical limitations: they require the MDP to be perfectly factorizable into independent components, model-based algorithms have a significant gap between their sample complexity upper bound and the theoretical lower bound, and no provable model-free algorithm exists for FMDPs. This paper addresses all three by introducing approximate factorization (allowing imperfect decompositions), a graph-coloring-based synchronous sampling strategy that closes the sample complexity gap, and the first near-optimal model-free algorithm for factored MDPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an approximate factorization scheme ω = (ω^P, ω^R) that specifies how to partition the n-dimensional state space and m-dimensional action space into K_ω transition components and ℓ_ω reward components, along with generative model queries (the ability to query any state-action pair for a next-state sample and reward). The factorization scheme is a tunable hyperparameter: increasing the number of components reduces sample complexity exponentially but increases the model misspecification bias E_ω = γ(1-γ)^{-2}Δ_ω^P + (1-γ)^{-1}Δ_ω^R. The output is an ε-optimal Q-function (and hence a greedy optimal policy) with a sample complexity of Õ(Σ_{k∈[κ_p]}|X[Z_k^P]| · ε^{-2}(1-γ)^{-3} + Σ_{i∈[κ_r]}|X[Z_i^R]|), where κ_p and κ_r are instance-dependent parameters from the graph-coloring solution that can be much smaller than K_ω and ℓ_ω.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a graph-coloring-based cost-optimal synchronous sampling strategy that formulates the problem of grouping factorized components for joint sampling as a graph coloring problem: components whose scope sets are disjoint (exclusive scopes) can be sampled simultaneously, and the optimal grouping minimizes total sampling cost, with the number of groups κ_p corresponding to the chromatic number of the dependency graph. For the model-based algorithm, the method estimates low-dimensional transition kernels P̂_k for each component using the synchronous sampling sets, combines them multiplicatively to form P̂, and applies standard value iteration to the estimated model. For the model-free VRQL-AF algorithm, the method constructs a factored empirical Bellman operator that reuses samples across components via the synchronous sampling scheme, then applies a two-loop variance-reduced Q-iteration: an outer loop builds a low-variance reference Bellman operator by averaging N_τ factored operators, and an inner loop performs variance-reduced updates combining a fresh high-variance operator with the reference correction term. Both algorithms achieve the same problem-dependent sample complexity up to logarithmic factors, matching the FMDP lower bound when κ_p, κ_r = O(1).

## Results

The paper establishes near-minimax sample complexity guarantees for both model-based and model-free RL under approximate factorization. The model-based algorithm (Theorem 5.1) and VRQL-AF (Theorem 6.1) both achieve sample complexity of Õ(Σ_{k∈[κ_p]}|𝒳[Z_k^P]| · ε⁻²(1−γ)⁻³ + Σ_{i∈[κ_r]}|𝒳[Z_i^R]|), where κ_p and κ_r are instance-dependent parameters from graph coloring that can be much smaller than the total number of components K_ω. For perfectly factorizable FMDPs, this improves the prior best upper bound (Chen et al., 2020) by up to a factor of K_ω and matches the instance-dependent lower bound (Xu & Tewari, 2020) when κ_p, κ_r = O(1). Numerical experiments on a wind farm storage control problem (using California ISO electricity price data) corroborate the theoretical trade-off between factorization granularity and approximation bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head empirical comparisons against named baseline algorithms; instead, it provides theoretical sample complexity comparisons. Against the standard MDP minimax bound of Õ(|S||A|·ε⁻²(1−γ)⁻³) (Azar et al., 2012), the proposed methods replace the |S||A| dependence with Σ_{k∈[κ_p]}|𝒳[Z_k^P]|, which is exponentially smaller when the MDP decomposes into many small components (e.g., 10 disjoint components of equal size yield Õ((|S||A|)^{1/10})). Against the prior FMDP upper bound from Chen et al. (2020), the improvement is a factor of up to K_ω (the number of components), since κ_p can be much smaller than K_ω due to the graph-coloring-based synchronous sampling. Against the FMDP lower bound from Xu & Tewari (2020), the proposed algorithms are the first to match it in an instance-dependent manner when the dependence structure is sparse (κ_p, κ_r = O(1)). For the model-free setting, VRQL-AF is the first provable model-free algorithm for FMDPs with near-optimal sample complexity, filling a gap noted in the introduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Factored MDP (FMDP) framework of Osband & Van Roy (2014) by introducing approximate factorization, which relaxes the restrictive assumption of perfect decomposability to handle imperfectly factored models. The model-free algorithm (VRQL-AF) builds directly on the variance-reduced Q-learning framework of Wainwright (2019b) and Sidford et al. (2018, 2023), adapting their two-loop reference-based update to a factored empirical Bellman operator. The key methodological departure is the graph-coloring-based synchronous sampling strategy, which enables simultaneous estimation of multiple factorized components from shared samples—a technique absent from prior FMDP work that sampled one component per draw. The model-based algorithm extends the generative-model analysis of Azar et al. (2012) to the factored setting, while the sample complexity analysis draws on the instance-dependent lower bounds of Xu & Tewari (2020) and Chen et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap in FMDP theory by providing the first near-minimax sample complexity guarantees for both model-based and model-free algorithms, and the first provable model-free algorithm for FMDPs at all. The graph-coloring formulation of the sampling problem is a clean and general tool that could transfer to other structured RL settings. Key limitations include the reliance on the generative model (rather than Markov sampling), the assumption that the factorization structure ω is known a priori, and empirical validation limited to a single wind-farm application. Open questions include extending to Markov sampling under ergodicity conditions, automatically discovering the factorization structure from data, and incorporating function approximation for continuous or very large state spaces.
