## Problem

Continuous-time generative models (diffusion, flow matching, consistency models) require access to clean ground-truth data for training, making them inapplicable to inverse generation problems where only noisy observations are available. Existing unsupervised denoising methods (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, SURE-based) are restricted to independent, centered, or Gaussian noise and cannot handle complex noise distributions with spatial correlations, signal-dependent variances, or SDE-based transformations without closed-form transition densities.

## Input / Output

The method requires only noisy observations x₁ and a specified noise distribution model p(x₁|x₀) — no clean data is needed. The noise model can be any continuous distribution, including those defined by SDEs (e.g., Jacobi/Wright-Fisher processes) or signal-dependent Poisson-Gaussian models fitted from the data itself. The output is a point estimate of the clean signal x₀ obtained by evaluating the learned consistency function c_θ(x₁, 1) in a single forward pass (ICM) or by integrating the learned ODE (IFM), along with the recovered data distribution q(x₀).

## Method

Inverse Flow (IF) inverts the data requirement of continuous-time generative models by training a mapping from observed noisy x₁ to unobserved clean x₀. Inverse Flow Matching (IFM) learns an ODE vector field v_t^θ(x) by replacing the unobserved x₀ in the conditional flow matching objective with the ODE solution ODE_{1→0}^{v^θ}(x₁) (with stopgrad), while choosing the conditional ODE to match the noise distribution p(x₁|x₀) at time 1. Inverse Consistency Model (ICM) achieves the same goal with a simulation-free objective that requires no ODE solver, enabling 10× faster inference. A key enabling contribution is Generalized Consistency Training (GCT), which extends the consistency training-from-scratch objective from a single specific diffusion process to any conditional ODE or forward diffusion process, proven equivalent to consistency distillation (Theorem 2). The framework is theoretically grounded: Theorem 1 shows that at zero loss the recovered distribution q(x₀) equals the true p(x₀), and Lemma 1 provides an interpretable form where the learned vector field points toward the expected ground truth.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic image denoising benchmarks (BSDS500, Kodak, Set12), ICM achieves the highest PSNR across all three noise types. For Gaussian noise (σ=25), ICM reaches 28.16/29.08/29.19 dB, surpassing the supervised baseline (28.00/28.91/28.99 dB) and all unsupervised methods. For correlated noise, ICM achieves 27.64/28.53/28.46 dB, dramatically outperforming Noise2Self (21.03/21.56/21.58 dB) which assumes independent noise. For the non-centered Jacobi process SDE noise, ICM reaches 24.28/25.07/24.74 dB, again the best among all methods. On single-cell RNA-seq, ICM improves cell-type classification accuracy from 0.513 to 0.571 (adult mouse brain) and from 0.647 to 0.736 (mouse brain development).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICM outperforms all unsupervised baselines (BM3D, Noise2Void, Noise2Self) across every noise type and dataset. The advantage is most dramatic on correlated noise, where Noise2Self collapses to ~21 dB (vs. ICM's ~28 dB) because it assumes pixel-wise independence, and on the Jacobi process where Noise2Self and Noise2Void both assume centered noise that the SDE violates. ICM also beats the supervised denoiser on Gaussian and correlated noise (e.g., 29.08 vs. 28.91 on Kodak-Gaussian), which the authors attribute to overfitting in the supervised setting. In the appendix, ICM also surpasses SURE and G-SURE on BSDS500 and CelebA respectively. IFM yields marginally higher PSNR than ICM but at 2× training cost and 10× inference cost due to iterative ODE solving, making ICM the preferred practical choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the data requirement of continuous-time generative models (diffusion models, conditional flow matching, consistency models) to solve inverse generation problems: instead of learning a mapping from a simple prior to the data distribution, it learns a mapping from observed noisy data to the unobserved clean data distribution. The Inverse Flow Matching (IFM) objective is derived directly from the conditional flow matching objective by replacing the unobserved x₀ with an ODE solution from x₁, while the Inverse Consistency Model (ICM) builds on a newly introduced Generalized Consistency Training (GCT) that extends the consistency training-from-scratch formulation to arbitrary forward diffusion processes or conditional flows. This departs from prior denoising-without-ground-truth methods (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, Noise2Score, SURE-based approaches) that are restricted to specific noise assumptions (independence, centered noise, exponential family), by supporting nearly any continuous noise distribution including SDE-based transforms without closed-form conditional densities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework extends the practical applicability of denoising without ground truth to noise types that were previously intractable (correlated noise, Jacobi/Wright-Fisher processes, signal-dependent Poisson-Gaussian noise), with demonstrated utility in fluorescence microscopy and single-cell RNA-seq where clean data is fundamentally unavailable. The simulation-free ICM objective (no ODE solver at inference) makes the approach computationally attractive for large-scale scientific data. A key limitation is the requirement of prior knowledge of the noise distribution, and future work is needed to relax this assumption. The generalized consistency training objective also has independent value, enabling consistency models to be trained from scratch under arbitrary forward processes beyond the specific EDM formulation.
