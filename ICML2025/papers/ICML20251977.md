## Problem

In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) via Algorithm Distillation has been demonstrated only on toy, grid-based, single-domain tasks, leaving open whether reward-driven, inference-time self-correction can scale to cross-domain continuous control. Meanwhile, the dominant paradigm for cross-domain action models (e.g., JAT, Gato, Baku) relies on expert distillation and largely ignores reward signals, forgoing the 'reward is enough' principle that underpins adaptive, self-improving agents.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-episodic subsequences of stacked (previous action, previous reward, current observation) tokens, where observation and action dimensionalities vary across tasks (e.g., 4-dim actions for Meta-World vs. 52-dim for Bi-DexHands). Rather than a task identifier, the model receives only a dimensionality-based group identifier, making it task-agnostic. At inference, it begins with an empty context and iteratively appends each new (s, a, r) transition, using a sliding 8192-token window with ALiBi positional encodings to handle unbounded episode lengths.

## Method

Vintix is a 300M-parameter TinyLLama-based next-action prediction model trained via Algorithm Distillation on a cross-domain dataset of 87 continuous-control tasks. The key data-collection contribution is Continuous Noise Distillation, which extends AD^ε to continuous action spaces by computing each action as a linear interpolation between a uniform random sample and the demonstrator action, with the noise coefficient ε annealed along the trajectory according to a tunable power-law schedule. The architecture uses separate encoder/decoder MLP heads per observation-action dimensionality group, and stacks (a_{t-1}, r_{t-1}, o_t) into a single sequence token to triple the effective context window. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and ground-truth actions over 8192-token sequences, and at inference the model performs cold-start many-shot ICRL: it generates actions iteratively from an empty context, appending each environment transition to enable progressive self-correction toward demonstrator-level performance without any gradient updates.

## Results

On MuJoCo, Vintix achieves an average demonstrator-normalized score of ~0.97 after inference-time convergence, substantially outperforming JAT (~0.55 average) and REGENT (~0.35 average) across the 11 tasks. On Meta-World, Vintix reaches ~0.95 average normalized score, again exceeding both baselines on domain-level averages. The model exhibits progressive self-correction: starting from a suboptimal cold-start policy with an empty context, it iteratively improves over many shots (episodes) to near-demonstrator performance without any gradient updates. On parametric variations (unseen viscosity/gravity in MuJoCo, unseen setpoints in Industrial-Benchmark), Vintix still reaches near-demonstrator levels but with slower convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vintix is compared against JAT (Gallouédec et al., 2024) and REGENT (Sridhar et al., 2024), both cross-domain generalist agents trained on overlapping MuJoCo and Meta-World tasks. On MuJoCo, Vintix dominates both baselines on 10 of 11 tasks, with particularly large margins on Humanoid (0.97 vs. JAT 0.10, REGENT 0.02), HumanoidStandup (1.02 vs. 0.35, 0.26), and InvertedPendulum (1.00 vs. 0.24, 0.06); JAT wins only on InvertedDoublePendulum (0.93 vs. Vintix 0.65). On Meta-World, the picture is more mixed: Vintix wins on the majority of tasks (e.g., assembly 1.04 vs. 0.96/0.83, button-press 0.97 vs. 0.86/0.62), but REGENT and JAT substantially outperform Vintix on five harder manipulation tasks (bin-picking, box-close, door-lock, door-unlock, hand-insert), where Vintix scores near or below random while baselines reach 0.67–0.95. Overall, the paper reports that Vintix outperforms both baselines on domain-level averages in both MuJoCo and Meta-World, attributing the advantage to the self-corrective inference-time adaptation enabled by Algorithm Distillation training rather than expert-only distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Algorithm Distillation (AD) framework of Laskin et al. (2022) and the noise-distillation data collection procedure of Zisman et al. (2024a) from single-domain, discrete-action toy tasks to a cross-domain, continuous-control setting spanning four benchmarks (MuJoCo, Meta-World, Bi-DexHands, Industrial-Benchmark). The key methodological departures are a continuous-action extension of the AD^ε noise schedule (linear interpolation rather than epsilon-greedy switching) and a 300M-parameter TinyLLama-based transformer trained jointly across all domains with dimensionality-grouped encoders/decoders. Conceptually, the paper positions reward-centric ICRL as a competitive alternative to the dominant expert-distillation paradigm (JAT, REGENT, Gato) for building generalist action models, arguing that the policy-improvement structure in AD trajectories enables inference-time self-correction that pure imitation cannot replicate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that ICRL can extend beyond single-domain grid-based benchmarks to multi-domain continuous control, providing a concrete pathway toward the 'reward is enough' vision for generalist agents. The released 87-task cross-domain dataset and collection tooling lower the barrier for the community to build and evaluate ICRL models. However, significant limitations remain: the model struggles with truly unseen tasks (random-level performance on several), generalization to parametric variations shows degraded convergence, and the entire evaluation is confined to structured, low-dimensional state spaces. Open questions include whether scaling to more domains, larger task sets, and richer modalities (vision, language) will unlock robust cross-task generalization and self-improvement beyond demonstrator performance.
