## Problem

Existing symmetry discovery methods are restricted to linear symmetries in the observation space, while recent attempts at nonlinear symmetries (LaLiGAN, Ko et al., Shaw et al.) either hide the generators inside black-box networks or are limited to single-parameter groups. No prior method can simultaneously discover nonlinear symmetries, determine the exact number of infinitesimal generators (i.e., the dimension of the Lie algebra subspace), and provide their explicit closed-form expressions.

## Input / Output

The method takes spatiotemporal data samples from a PDE-governed dynamic system, a user-specified function library Θ(x,u) that may include nonlinear terms (e.g., up to second-order monomials), and a separately trained neural network that fits the PDE mapping. It produces the number of infinitesimal generators, their explicit differential-operator expressions with nonlinear coefficients, and a sparse orthogonal basis for the Lie algebra subspace. The symmetry discovery step is a plug-and-play post-processing procedure on the trained network, requiring no retraining.

## Method

LieNLSD parameterizes the infinitesimal group action as v = WΘ(x,u)·∇, where Θ is a manually specified function library and W is a coefficient matrix. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) proves that the n-th prolongation of v remains linear in W, so the infinitesimal criterion for PDE symmetries reduces to a homogeneous linear system C·vec(W) = 0. In practice, derivatives are estimated via central differences, the PDE Jacobian is obtained by automatic differentiation through the trained neural network, and the resulting linear system is solved via SVD to extract the null-space dimension (number of generators) and basis vectors. A final LADMAP-based sparsification step (Algorithm 2) rotates the SVD basis to maximize element-wise sparsity, yielding interpretable generator expressions. This algebraic pipeline contrasts with GAN-based or MLP-based competitors by requiring no adversarial training or black-box function approximation for the symmetry itself.

## Results

On the Grassmann distance metric (lower is better), LieNLSD achieves (9.20±1.83)×10⁻² on top quark tagging, (1.26±0.20)×10⁻² on Burgers' equation, (1.40±0.01)×10⁻² on the wave equation, and (8.62±1.31)×10⁻² on the Schrödinger equation, consistently outperforming LieGAN by 1–2 orders of magnitude while using 3–4× fewer parameters (81–97K vs. 265–321K). In the data augmentation application, FNO with LPSDA based on LieNLSD improves long rollout NMSE by over 20% compared to FNO without augmentation, reaching performance comparable to ground-truth symmetries (e.g., (5.99±0.04)×10⁻² vs. (6.01±0.20)×10⁻² on the heat equation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LieNLSD is compared against LieGAN (Yang et al., 2023) on symmetry discovery quality and Ko et al. (2024) on the data augmentation application. Against LieGAN, LieNLSD achieves substantially lower Grassmann distance on all four datasets (e.g., 0.0126 vs. 1.58 on Burgers', 0.0140 vs. 2.36 on the wave equation), while also using 3–4× fewer parameters and automatically determining the Lie algebra subspace dimension without manual specification. Against Ko et al. (2024) in the FNO data augmentation task, LieNLSD-based LPSDA achieves lower NMSE on all three PDEs (e.g., (5.99±0.04)×10⁻² vs. (7.33±1.07)×10⁻² on the heat equation), and additionally does not require the explicit PDE expression that Ko et al. need to compute their validity score. Qualitatively, LieNLSD uniquely discovers nonlinear symmetries (e.g., special conformal transformations for the wave equation) and provides explicit generator expressions, whereas both baselines are limited to linear or implicit representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry discovery literature from linear to nonlinear Lie point symmetries of differential equations. Prior methods (L-conv, LieGG, LieGAN) rely on Lie algebra representations that inherently restrict them to linear group actions, while recent nonlinear attempts (LaLiGAN, Ko et al., Shaw et al.) use black-box neural networks that cannot explicitly recover the Lie algebra subspace or determine its dimension. LieNLSD departs from both by specifying a user-defined function library Θ for the infinitesimal group action and proving that the prolongation formula remains linear in the coefficient matrix W, reducing the problem to a linear system solvable by SVD. The sparsification step borrows LADMAP from the low-rank matrix recovery literature to produce interpretable, sparse generator expressions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LieNLSD provides the first principled pipeline that simultaneously determines the dimension of the symmetry Lie algebra and outputs explicit nonlinear generator expressions, filling a gap between black-box latent-space methods and linear-only approaches. The >20% improvement in long-rollout accuracy for neural PDE solvers via symmetry-guided data augmentation demonstrates practical utility beyond pure discovery. Key limitations include the manual specification of the function library Θ (which bounds what nonlinearities can be found), the restriction to Lie point symmetries acting pointwise on X×U, and the reliance on a trained neural network whose Jacobian quality affects discovery accuracy. Future work on using discovered symmetries to guide conserved-quantity identification or governing-equation discovery could broaden the impact.
