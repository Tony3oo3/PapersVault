## Problem

Current VLMs optimize their vision encoder and connector solely through backpropagation of text-based next-token prediction loss, which traverses the entire LLM, causing significant gradient information loss over this long propagation chain. Additionally, textual supervision is inherently semantically sparse compared to the rich pixel-level information available in images, limiting the quality of learned visual features.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a text prompt and produces a text response, identical to standard LLaVA-1.5. During training, the vision encoder's intermediate features (8th, 16th, 24th layers) and the connector's output are additionally fed into a Multi-Adapter Diffusion model that reconstructs the original image, providing a pixel-level supervision signal. The diffusion model is entirely discarded at inference, so no extra parameters or compute are introduced at test time.

## Method

DS-VLM introduces a plug-and-play diffusion-based supervision channel that bypasses the LLM to directly optimize the vision encoder and connector. A Multi-Adapter Diffusion model, built on Stable Diffusion's U-Net, receives image-modality features (low/mid/high layer outputs from the vision encoder) and text-modality features (connector outputs) through separate linear adapters. An MOE Cross-Attention mechanism replaces the standard cross-attention in the U-Net, maintaining independent attention heads for each modality and layer, with a learned routing vector (softmax over pooled query projections) to weight and aggregate the outputs. The training objective combines the standard LLM next-token prediction loss with a perceptual reconstruction loss between the diffusion-generated image and the original, ensuring the visual features capture both semantic and structural details. The approach is validated across multiple vision encoders (CLIP-L, SigLIP-SO) and LLMs (Vicuna-7B/13B, Llama3-8B, Qwen2-7B) with consistent improvements over the LLaVA-1.5 baseline.

## Results

With Vicuna-7B and CLIP-L (0.5M+0.6M training data), DS-VLM achieves an average improvement of 1.4% over LLaVA-1.5 across ten benchmarks, with gains of +2.4 on MMBench (61.5 vs 59.1), +2.1 on MMMU (37.8 vs 35.7), and +2.5 on LLaVABench (64.3 vs 61.8). Scaling to Qwen2-7B, DS-VLM reaches 70.4 on MMBench, 44.8 on MMMU, and 1953 on MME, surpassing the LLaVA-1.5 baseline (70.9/43.6/1849) on most metrics. The method generalizes across visual encoders (CLIP-L, SigLIP-SO) and LLMs (Vicuna-7B/13B, Llama3-8B, Qwen2-7B), consistently outperforming the corresponding baseline on the majority of evaluation datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA-1.5 baseline (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), DS-VLM consistently wins on 8–10 out of 10 benchmarks across all tested configurations, with the largest gains on MMBench (+2.4), MMMU (+2.1), and LLaVABench (+2.5) for Vicuna-7B. Compared to earlier SoTA methods in Table 2, DS-VLM (Qwen2-7B) substantially outperforms MiniGPT-4 (20.8 MMB), Qwen-VL (32.9 MMB), VisualGLM (35.7 MMB), PandaGPT (34.5 MMB), and mPLUG-Owl2 (60.8 MMB) by wide margins on nearly all benchmarks. Against the more competitive Yi-VL (64.2 MMB), DS-VLM wins on MMB, MMS, MMMU, MV, OCRB, AI2D, LB, SQA, and MME, but loses on HallusionBench (29.5 vs 36.0). Against ShareGPT-4V (61.6 MMB), DS-VLM leads on MMB, MMS, MMMU, MV, AI2D, HB, SQA, and MME, but trails slightly on OCRBench (33.2 vs 37.1) and LLaVABench (66.4 vs 66.9). Overall, DS-VLM achieves the best or near-best results across all ten benchmarks while using only 0.5M+0.6M training data, less than several competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (frozen vision encoder + MLP connector + LLM) by introducing a training-time diffusion model as an additional supervision signal, departing from prior VLM training that relies exclusively on text-based backpropagation through the LLM. Conceptually, it repurposes the Stable Diffusion U-Net (Rombach et al., 2022) in the spirit of ControlNet and T2I-adapter, but instead of conditioning on external control signals, it conditions on the VLM's own intermediate and final visual features to create a short-path gradient channel from pixel space back to the vision encoder. The multi-level feature supervision draws on Yao et al. (2024)'s observation that intermediate encoder layers carry important semantic content, while the MOE cross-attention routing mechanism is a novel architectural choice for integrating heterogeneous modality features into the diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and well-motivated limitation in VLM training—gradient signal degradation over long propagation paths through large LLMs—by providing a direct pixel-level supervision channel that requires no additional inference cost. However, the practical gains are modest (roughly 1–2% average improvement across benchmarks), and the approach adds meaningful training compute (running a 50-step diffusion model per sample). Open questions include whether the benefits compound at larger model scales, whether the diffusion supervision transfers to video or 3D modalities, and whether the perceptual-loss-based reconstruction could be replaced by learned metrics that better align with downstream task performance.
