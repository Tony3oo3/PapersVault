## Problem

Wild Test-Time Adaptation (WTTA) methods rely on entropy minimization to adapt models under extreme data scarcity and multiple concurrent shifts, but this approach introduces conflicting optimization dynamics: semantically similar samples within a local region exhibit inconsistent predictions, amplifying noise and undermining adaptation efficiency. Prior WTTA work focused on sample selection criteria while overlooking this fundamental issue in the underlying optimization objective itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250955/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB test images processed by a pre-trained backbone (ResNet50-GN or ViT-Base-LN) and a small in-distribution source set (~500 samples) used solely to estimate the diagonal covariance Σ for region modeling. Adaptation updates only the affine parameters of normalization layers, and the output is a standard class label with a C-dimensional prediction probability vector. The key operational detail is that all region confidence computations occur in the backbone's latent feature space (dimension d) rather than pixel space, with the classifier's affine layer (A, b) providing the mapping from features to class probabilities.

## Method

ReCAP replaces vanilla entropy minimization with a Region Confidence objective that jointly optimizes a bias term (integrated entropy over the local region) and a variance term (KL divergence between the center sample's prediction and neighboring predictions). To handle the uncertain region scope, local regions are modeled as multivariate Gaussians N(z_t, τ·Σ) in feature space, where Σ is a diagonal covariance estimated from source data and τ controls the scope. The intractable integrals are converted into closed-form upper bounds via a finite-to-infinite asymptotic approximation: Lemma 4.1 bounds the finite-sample bias by a mean-probability product, and Lemma 4.2 (via Jensen's inequality on the log-softmax) bounds the negative log-likelihood under Gaussian sampling, yielding Regional Entropy (L_RE) and Regional Instability (L_RI) as efficient proxies requiring no additional forward passes. The final loss (Eq. 9) uses L_RE for both sample selection (threshold τ_RE) and weighting (exponential decay), combined with λ·L_RI for the variance term, achieving SOTA WTTA performance at only +5% overhead over Tent.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, batch size=1), ReCAP achieves 46.4% average accuracy with ResNet50 and 65.7% with ViT-Base, outperforming the previous SOTA DeYO (44.1% / 64.1%) by +2.3% and +1.6% respectively. Across all three wild scenarios (limited batch, mixed domain, imbalanced label shift), ReCAP delivers average gains of +2.0%, +1.1%, and +1.9% over prior methods on 15 corruption types. When combined with sample selection (ReCAP+DeYO), gains reach 48.9% (ResNet) and 65.9% (ViT) under batch size=1. Running time is 116s for 50,000 images, only +5% over Tent (110s), confirming real-time feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCAP consistently outperforms all six baselines across every wild scenario and both architectures. Against the strongest prior method DeYO, ReCAP gains +2.3% (ResNet) and +1.6% (ViT) under batch size=1, +2.0% and +0.2% under mixed domain, and +1.5% and +2.2% under imbalanced label shift. The gap over entropy-based methods (Tent, EATA) is substantially larger: ReCAP exceeds Tent by +24.9% (ResNet) and +18.0% (ViT) on average under batch size=1. DDA, which relies on a diffusion model, is both slower (13,277s vs 116s) and less accurate (35.1% vs 46.4% ResNet). Notably, ReCAP is orthogonal to sample selection: combining it with SAR or DeYO yields further gains of +11.0%/+3.1%/+5.9% (vs SAR) and +3.3%/+1.6%/+4.0% (vs DeYO) across the three wild scenarios, confirming that region confidence optimization and sample filtering address complementary failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm of entropy minimization (Tent, Wang et al. 2020) and the subsequent line of work on sample selection for Wild TTA (SAR, Niu et al. 2023; DeYO, Lee et al. 2024). Rather than improving which samples to select for entropy minimization, it challenges the optimization objective itself, arguing that per-sample entropy introduces conflicting local dynamics under wild distribution shifts. The method builds on the Wild TTA benchmark and adaptation framework from SAR (normalization-layer updates, selection/weighting structure) but replaces the entropy loss with a region-integrated confidence objective derived via probabilistic Gaussian modeling in feature space and finite-to-infinite asymptotic approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying local prediction inconsistency (not just individual uncertainty) as the core bottleneck in wild TTA, this work reframes how the field thinks about test-time optimization objectives. The closed-form proxy (Regional Entropy + Regional Instability) is practically attractive because it requires no additional forward passes or sampling, matching Tent's speed while delivering consistent gains across three wild scenarios and two architectures. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to classification tasks, a domain-wise (not class- or sample-wise) region shape, and the reliance on a small in-distribution set for variance estimation. Open questions include whether region confidence generalizes to segmentation or detection, and whether adaptive per-class region modeling could yield further gains.
