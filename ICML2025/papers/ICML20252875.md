## Problem

Deep graph clustering on real-world graphs faces two co-occurring challenges that have been studied in isolation: large scale (hundreds of millions of nodes) and missing node attributes. Existing scalable DGC methods rely on subgraph sampling that disrupts the sparse graph structure, while attribute-missing methods like AMGC require full-graph GNN propagation and suffer OOM on large graphs. The paper identifies that in highly sparse large graphs, structural information is the critical signal for clustering when attributes are missing, yet both sampling and message-passing degrade this signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected attributed graph G=(V,E,H) where a subset of nodes have entirely missing feature vectors (attribute-missing graph), and produces a 3D tensor of shape (K+1)×N×d containing K+1 complementary multi-view node representations: the original node features as view 0, plus K views each formed by averaging features of nodes at exactly k hops away. This is a one-time CPU-based preprocessing step (no GPU, no sampling) whose output can be concatenated and fed into any DGC method or treated as separate views for any MVC method to produce final cluster assignments.

## Method

CMV-ND replaces GNN propagation with a non-propagative structural encoding via two components: (1) Recursive Neighborhood Search (RNS), a BFS-like expansion that finds all nodes within k hops of each target node, and (2) Neighborhood Differential Strategy (NDS), which computes D^k(v) = N^k(v) \ N^{k-1}(v) to isolate nodes at exactly k hops, ensuring zero overlap between views. Each differential hop neighborhood's node features are averaged to form one view, yielding K+1 non-redundant views per node. The paper formalizes a redundancy ratio R_red showing that a k-layer GNN accesses i-hop neighbor features (k-i+1) times while CMV-ND accesses each exactly once, making the approach inherently more efficient. Because no propagation or sampling is involved, the method scales to graphs with millions of nodes using only O(n) memory and can be combined as a plug-and-play preprocessing with any existing DGC or MVC clustering algorithm.

## Results

On attribute-missing graphs (60% missing rate), CMV-ND combined with Dink-Net achieves the best clustering performance across all six datasets. Headline results include ACC 72.54% / F1 70.57% on Cora, ACC 77.75% / F1 72.03% on Amazon-Photo, and ACC 69.31% / F1 60.97% on Reddit. On large-scale graphs where AMGC and several baselines fail with out-of-memory errors, CMV-ND + Dink-Net remains feasible, achieving ACC 41.74% on ogbn-arXiv and ACC 35.91% on ogbn-products. The average ACC improvement from adding CMV-ND preprocessing is 13.8% on Cora and 5.8% on Amazon-Photo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMV-ND + Dink-Net outperforms all competing DGC baselines on every dataset where comparisons are possible. On Cora, it beats S3GC (72.54 vs. 70.34 ACC) and AMGC (72.54 vs. 69.26 ACC); on Amazon-Photo, it leads S3GC by 1.34 ACC points (77.75 vs. 76.41). A critical scalability finding is that AMGC, the previous SOTA for attribute-missing graph clustering, suffers OOM failures on all three large graphs (Reddit, ogbn-arXiv, ogbn-products), while CMV-ND + Dink-Net completes all runs with stable memory. CMV-ND also consistently improves every method it is applied to: across all datasets and metrics, adding CMV-ND either improves or maintains performance (e.g., K-means ACC jumps from 31.55% to 45.55% on Cora). Among MVC baselines (Table 2), DIMVC is the strongest, reaching 69.85% ACC on Amazon-Photo and 64.23% on Reddit, but still trails the DGC counterpart (Dink-Net + CMV-ND) by 7.9 and 5.1 ACC points respectively. On attribute-complete graphs (Appendix D.2), CMV-ND is non-intrusive—DGC methods show nearly identical performance with or without it—while K-means benefits dramatically (e.g., Cora ACC from 33.78% to 74.00%), confirming that CMV-ND effectively compensates for methods that do not natively exploit graph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: large-scale graph clustering (S3GC, Dink-Net) and attribute-missing graph clustering (AMGC). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the well-known concept of multi-hop neighborhoods from GNNs into a non-propagative, differential form that directly encodes structural information into complementary multi-view representations. The key departure from the standard GNN 'aggregate-encode-predict' pipeline is the elimination of message passing entirely, replacing it with a one-time BFS-based preprocessing that produces K+1 non-overlapping views. This also naturally bridges DGC and multi-view clustering (MVC), a connection the authors explicitly highlight as a contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but previously unexplored combined setting (large-scale + attribute-missing), and its plug-and-play design means any existing DGC or MVC method can benefit without architectural changes. A notable limitation is that the MVC implementation underperforms the DGC counterpart, and the authors acknowledge the need for a dedicated MVC method tailored to CMV-ND. Open questions include whether the O(nΔ^k) preprocessing cost remains acceptable for extremely dense or high-hop settings, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or temporal graphs where neighborhoods change over time.
