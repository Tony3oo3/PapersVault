## Problem

Controlling a soft inflatable exosuit to assist human walking with RL is uniquely difficult because no reliable simulator or dynamic model exists for the coupled human–pneumatic-actuator system, and existing offline-to-online RL methods depend on extensive simulations or large offline datasets. This paper tackles the gap of achieving safe, personalized, real-time exosuit control directly in the physical environment using only limited data from a single participant, without any simulator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253164/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The controller consumes a 5-dimensional state vector derived from IMU-based knee kinematics (stance/swing peak flexion timings t_A, t_C, phase durations d_A, d_C, and peak flexion angle θ_f), processed through the RIIV normalization that converts absolute gait timing into gait-cycle percentages and maps to [−1,1] using biomechanics literature bounds rather than raw min/max. EMG signals from three knee muscles (VL, BF, RF) are integrated per gait cycle to compute the effort cost term. The output is a 4-dimensional timing-and-duration vector (t_1, d_1, t_2, d_2) specifying when and how long to inflate the pneumatic actuator for stance extension and swing flexion, constrained to safe gait-phase windows (e.g., t_1 ∈ [0,20]%, t_2 ∈ [60,80]%) with a maximum pressure of 206.8 kPa.

## Method

The AIP (Adaptation from Imitating Policy) framework combines a data-centric offline phase with an online actor-critic phase. Offline, behavioral cloning is trained on 150 gait cycles from a single participant using MoCap ground truth, with the key innovation being the RIIV data-processing pipeline that reduces intra-person step-length variation (by normalizing to gait percentage) and inter-person variation (by mapping to literature-derived bounds), yielding significantly lower action divergence than standard min/max normalization. Online, the dHDP deterministic policy gradient algorithm—initialized by the offline BC policy—learns a personalized control policy in real time, minimizing a stage cost that jointly penalizes deviation from normative walking kinematics (safety) and integrated EMG effort (reduced muscle load). The deterministic (rather than stochastic) policy is chosen to avoid erratic actuation that could compromise human safety and comfort. The paper further provides theoretical analysis establishing learning convergence, solution optimality, and closed-loop dynamic stability under the stated safety constraints, and validates the full pipeline on five participants walking on a level and 7° incline treadmill.

## Results

Across all five participants, online AIP training reduced the stage cost from 0.94–1.42 (offline IL policy) to 0.35–0.49, and reduced evaluation EMG effort by 5–48% relative to unassisted baseline walking (mean reduction ~22.6%). Evaluation peak knee error decreased for every participant (e.g., P5: 1.12°→0.56°), confirming convergence to normative gait. The RIIV data-processing method reduced action divergence to ground-truth MoCap by 30–99% compared to the DIRECT normalization baseline (e.g., d_A: 0.259→0.0003), validating the data-centric approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other RL algorithms (PPO, SAC, DDPG) in the main results; instead, it compares its AIP pipeline against three internal baselines. (1) **RIIV vs. DIRECT normalization (offline):** RIIV achieves substantially lower action divergence to MoCap ground truth across all state variables (e.g., t_C: 0.21→0.02; d_A: 0.259→0.0003), confirming that the data-centric processing is critical for policy quality. (2) **Offline IL policy vs. online AIP (personalization):** The same offline policy yields inconsistent results at the start of online training (EMG effort ranges 0.55–0.96 across participants, with P1, P4, P5 above their no-exosuit baseline), but after online dHDP adaptation all five participants converge to EMG effort below baseline, demonstrating that personalization is necessary. (3) **Ablation on cost function:** Removing either the EMG-effort term or the kinematic-error term from the stage cost leads to failure—without the kinematic term, gait deviates from normative patterns and EMG rises; without the EMG term, the exosuit fails to reduce muscular strain. The full two-term cost is required for stable, effective human-robot co-adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dHDP deterministic policy gradient algorithm (Si & Wang, 2001) for online adaptation and Behavioral Cloning (Torabi et al., 2018) for offline policy initialization, combining them into the AIP framework. It deliberately departs from the dominant sim-to-real paradigm in robot RL by operating entirely in the physical environment without a simulator, and from algorithm-centric offline-to-online methods (pessimistic Q-ensembles, policy expansion) by adopting a data-centric approach (RIIV) that improves data quality rather than modifying the learning algorithm. The work also positions itself against human-in-the-loop optimization methods (Bayesian optimization, impedance control) that require robust dynamical models of the wearable device, which are unavailable for soft pneumatic exosuits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of online RL personalization for a soft exosuit controlled directly in the physical environment with limited data (150 gait cycles from one participant), addressing a gap where prior offline-to-online RL methods relied on extensive simulations or millions of offline samples. The finding that a simple deterministic policy gradient method (dHDP) suffices for safe, convergent real-time control of a pneumatic wearable robot challenges the field's assumption that complex stochastic algorithms (PPO, SAC) are necessary. Key limitations include single-leg control, only five healthy participants, and level-ground walking; open questions include bilateral coordination, application to impaired populations with different gait objectives, and generalization to variable speeds and complex terrains.
