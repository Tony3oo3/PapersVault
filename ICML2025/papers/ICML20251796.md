## Problem

Predicting the next state of a PDE-governed dynamical system from a short trajectory when the underlying PDE is unknown and varies across trajectories (multi-physics-agnostic prediction) remains challenging. Standard transformer approaches require learning an encoder-decoder pair, introducing reconstruction error and demanding vast training data, while classical solvers cannot adapt to unknown or varying dynamics. No prior method simultaneously handles all three levels of variability (PDE class, PDE coefficients, initial conditions) while preserving physical structure such as continuous-time evolution and spatial translation equivariance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a context of T successive past PDE state frames at native resolution (1D up to 1024 points, 2D up to 512×512, 3D up to 64×64×64) and produces two outputs: (1) a parameter vector θ (~200k values, intrinsic dimension 384) that fully populates a small U-Net operator network, and (2) the predicted next state u_{t+1} obtained by integrating that operator via adaptive Runge-Kutta. No retraining or gradient steps are needed at inference—prediction is a single forward pass through the hypernetwork followed by numerical integration. The 384-dimensional bottleneck is far smaller than any input frame, forcing the model to encode dynamics rather than memorize initial conditions.

## Method

DISCO decouples dynamics estimation from state prediction using a two-stage architecture: a large transformer hypernetwork (~120M parameters) with axial time-space attention processes the context and outputs parameters θ for a small U-Net operator network (~200k parameters, intrinsic dimension 384). The operator is integrated over [t, t+1] using a third-order adaptive Runge-Kutta solver (max 32 steps) with adjoint-based backpropagation for efficient gradient computation. The U-Net operator preserves spatial translation equivariance and continuous-time structure, while the 384-dimensional information bottleneck prevents overfitting to initial conditions. The hypernetwork's output is normalized per-layer and passed through a signed sigmoid to constrain parameter ranges, stabilizing training. End-to-end training minimizes NRMSE over the predicted next frame, and the model is pretrained jointly across 1D, 2D, and 3D PDE datasets with shared weights except for field-specific embeddings and dimension-specific final layers.

## Results

On PDEBench next-step prediction (NRMSE, lower is better), DISCO trained for 300 epochs achieves 0.0027 (Burgers), 0.00056 (SWE), 0.0047 (DiffRe2D), and 0.0033 (INS), outperforming MPP trained for 500 epochs on all four datasets. DISCO reaches SOTA-level performance in as few as 35 epochs (INS) versus 500 for MPP. On The Well (9 diverse 2D/3D datasets), DISCO achieves the best NRMSE at t+1 on 7/9 datasets and maintains a clear advantage through t+16 rollouts. On fine-tuning to an unseen Euler PDE, DISCO achieves 0.029 NRMSE after 20 epochs, beating MPP (0.032), Poseidon (0.052), U-Net (0.050), and FNO (0.067).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO outperforms MPP (the prior SOTA transformer) on 4 of 5 PDEBench datasets with 40% fewer training epochs (e.g., 0.0033 vs. 0.0056 NRMSE on INS), losing only on Compressible Navier-Stokes due to its coarser temporal resolution. On The Well's 9 diverse datasets, DISCO beats MPP at t+1 on 7/9 datasets and maintains a consistent advantage through t+16 rollouts (e.g., 0.0249 vs. 0.0522 on Shear Flow at t+1). The GEPS meta-learning baseline performs poorly across all datasets (NRMSE >1 on 4/9 Well datasets), confirming that gradient-based adaptation at inference is impractical for multi-physics-agnostic prediction at scale. On the unseen-Physics fine-tuning task, DISCO (0.029) beats MPP (0.032), Poseidon (0.052), from-scratch U-Net (0.050), and FNO (0.067), demonstrating that pretraining plus operator-based inductive bias yields better generalization than both transformer retraining and training fixed operators from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural PDE solvers (PDE-Net, FNO, neural ODEs) that learn fixed operators from data, (2) meta-learning frameworks for PDEs (GEPS/CODA) that adapt operators per context but require gradient-based retraining at inference, and (3) transformer-based in-context predictors (MPP, Poseidon, Zebra) that directly output next frames in a latent token space. The key departure is architectural: rather than having a transformer predict the next frame (requiring a learned decoder) or fine-tuning an operator at test time, DISCO uses a large transformer hypernetwork to generate the parameters of a small U-Net operator that is then integrated via adaptive Runge-Kutta. This decouples dynamics estimation from state prediction and inherits the translation-equivariance and continuous-time structure of classical finite-difference schemes, which neither pure transformers nor meta-learning frameworks naturally provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DISCO achieves SOTA next-step prediction on PDEBench in as few as 35 epochs versus 500 for MPP, and is the first multi-physics-agnostic predictor scaled to 3D data (64×64×64) and 512×512 2D resolutions. The 384-dimensional information bottleneck in the operator space forces the hypernetwork to encode PDE dynamics rather than memorize initial conditions, yielding clean generalization to unseen initial conditions (confirmed via UMAP clustering). Open questions include extending to time-dependent PDEs, non-uniform meshes (where graph neural network operators would replace the U-Net), multi-step rollout training, and whether spectral-method-based operators could further improve the inductive bias.
