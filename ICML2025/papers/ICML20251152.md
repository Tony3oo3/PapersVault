## Problem

Leveraging offline data to accelerate online bandit learning is non-trivial when the offline data is generated by heterogeneous users or environments with unobserved latent states. Prior latent bandit work (Hong et al., 2020; 2022) restricts to finitely many latent states, black-boxes the offline estimation step, and provides no end-to-end guarantees. This paper tackles the more general setting of uncountably many latent states in a linear contextual bandit, where reward parameters across users lie in a low-rank subspace of dimension d_K ≪ d_A, and provides the first minimax-optimal hybrid (offline-online) regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of N short unlabeled trajectories (each of length H, collected under a behavior policy π_b that does not depend on the latent state) and a feature map φ: X×A → R^{d_A}. It outputs a d_K-dimensional subspace projection matrix ŨŨ^⊤ with a data-dependent confidence bound Δ_off, and then drives online action selection. A key constraint is that the offline trajectories must be non-adaptive (contexts independent of each other and of θ) for the subspace to be identifiable; otherwise, the paper proves the latent subspace is fundamentally unrecoverable even with infinite data.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. Offline, SOLD splits each trajectory into two halves to obtain independent ridge-regression estimates β̂_{n,1}, β̂_{n,2}, forms symmetrized outer products, and corrects for regularization distortion via matrices D_{n,i} = I − μV_{n,i}^{-1}, then extracts the top d_K eigenvectors of D̄_{N,1}^{-1} M̄_N D̄_{N,2}^{-1} to estimate the latent subspace. Online, LOCAL-UCB sharpens optimism by solving a constrained optimization over the intersection of a d_K-dimensional confidence ellipsoid (within the learned subspace) and the standard d_A-dimensional LinUCB ellipsoid, with a non-convex constraint ‖Ũ^⊤U‖_F ≥ √(d_K − Δ_off²/2) ensuring the subspace is within the confidence set. For computational tractability, ProBALL-UCB relaxes this to a simpler switching rule: it uses the projected low-dimensional estimate with an inflated bonus (scaled by Δ_off) until the offline confidence ball provably contains the online confidence set, then falls back to standard LinUCB. The resulting regret is Õ(min(d_A√T, d_K√T(1+√(d_A T/(d_K N))))) for LOCAL-UCB, matching a novel lower bound for hybrid offline-online bandits.

## Results

The paper establishes that LOCAL-UCB achieves a regret bound of Õ(min(d_A√T, d_K√T(1+√(d_AT/d_KN)))), which is minimax optimal up to coverage constants (matching lower bound in Theorem 3). ProBALL-UCB, the computationally efficient variant, achieves Õ(min(Rd_A√T, Rd_K√T(1+(1/λ_A³λ_θ)(√(d_AT/d_KN)+√(d_A/SN)·Σκ_t²/S)))) regret. On the MovieLens-1M dataset (d_K=18, d_A=200, N=5000 offline trajectories, T=200 online steps), ProBALL-UCB with martingale Bernstein confidence bands outperforms LinUCB and all latent bandit baselines, approaching the performance of LinUCB with ground-truth low-dimensional features as offline sample size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProBALL-UCB consistently outperforms LinUCB (Li et al., 2010) in both simulation and MovieLens experiments, with the gap widening as tighter confidence bounds (martingale Bernstein > empirical Bernstein > Hoeffding) are used. Against the latent bandit baselines from Hong et al. (2020) — mUCB and TS — and Hong et al. (2022) — mmUCB, mmTS, and MixTS — ProBALL-UCB achieves lower cumulative regret overall. While mUCB and mmUCB show slightly better initial performance due to lower exploration, they suffer linear regret in the long run because discretizing continuous latent states into d_K clusters introduces model misspecification. ProBALL-UCB is guaranteed to perform no worse than LinUCB regardless of the hyperparameter τ and the confidence bound Δ_off, making it a safe drop-in replacement. The key empirical finding is that as the number of offline samples N increases, SOLD recovers a subspace nearly as good as the ground-truth low-rank structure, and ProBALL-UCB's regret approaches that of LinUCB run directly on the true low-dimensional features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent bandit framework of Hong et al. (2020, 2022) from finitely many discrete latent states to uncountably many continuous states, while simultaneously providing end-to-end guarantees for both the offline subspace estimation (SOLD, inspired by spectral methods in Kausik et al., 2023) and the online learning phase. It also departs from the hybrid offline-online RL line (Song et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2024), which assumes a single underlying model, by explicitly modeling the offline data as arising from a mixture of reward parameters in a low-rank subspace. The online algorithms build directly on LinUCB (Li et al., 2010), with LOCAL-UCB sharpening optimism via subspace constraints and ProBALL-UCB offering a computationally tractable relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that latent bandits are the canonical framework for stateless decision processes with unobserved heterogeneity (via a de Finetti theorem), giving practitioners a principled justification for the model. The first lower bound in a hybrid offline-online sequential decision-making setting provides a clear benchmark for future work. Key limitations include the need to tune the discount hyperparameter τ in ProBALL-UCB, the difficulty of choosing regularization μ in noisy settings, and the gap between LOCAL-UCB's minimax-optimal guarantee and ProBALL-UCB's slightly weaker bound. Open directions include instance-dependent lower bounds, extension to MDPs and general function approximation, and handling cases where the unconfoundedness assumption is violated.
