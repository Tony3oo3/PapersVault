## Problem

Existing SVG generative models either require direct supervision from vector data (limiting training data and demanding extensive pre-processing) or lack versatile conditioning such as text. No prior method could learn to generate vector graphics from natural language using only raster image supervision, which would unlock orders of magnitude more training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt (tokenized via BERT) and optionally a set of partial shape codes with their canvas positions as context for SVG auto-completion. It outputs a sequence of (position, shape code) pairs where positions are discretized onto a 256×256 grid and shape codes index into a 4,375-entry FSQ codebook; each code is decoded into cubic Bezier control points that form a connected path, with optional continuous predictions for stroke width and color. Input patches are 128×128 RGB crops centered on each shape, and the final SVG is assembled by placing each decoded path at its predicted canvas position.

## Method

GRIMOIRE is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Visual Shape Quantizer) uses a ResNet-18 encoder to map each 128×128 patch to discrete codes via Finite Scalar Quantization on a 5-dimensional hypercube (L=[7,5,5,5,5], yielding 4,375 codes), and a lightweight decoder that predicts cubic Bezier control points; training uses MSE reconstruction loss computed through the DiffVG differentiable rasterizer plus a novel geometric constraint that penalizes irregular inter-point distances among control points to prevent degenerate strokes. Stage 2 (Auto-Regressive Transformer) is a 12–16 layer causal Transformer decoder that models the joint distribution over BERT text embeddings, 256×256 position tokens, and shape codes, trained with standard cross-entropy. The key architectural insight is that raster supervision via differentiable rasterization replaces the need for SVG ground truth, and the FSQ mechanism eliminates the need to learn a codebook, while the two-stage decomposition cleanly separates shape appearance (VSQ) from spatial layout and text alignment (ART).

## Results

On the full FIGR-8 icon dataset, GRIMOIRE achieves a FID of 0.64 and CLIPScore of 29.00, substantially outperforming Im2Vec which cannot handle multi-class data. On single-class comparisons, GRIMOIRE achieves higher CLIPScore across all datasets (e.g., 30.60 vs 25.81 on Fonts-A, 32.24 vs 31.72 on FIGR-8-Star) while Im2Vec achieves lower FID on filled shapes due to the low-resolution ground truth. Against SDS-based methods, GRIMOIRE achieves a PSNR of 45.19 dB versus 28.68 (CLIPDraw), 36.62 (VectorFusion), and 34.53 (SVGDreamer), and is two orders of magnitude faster at inference (2.34 s vs 100–380 s per sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Im2Vec (Reddy et al., 2021), the primary raster-supervised baseline, GRIMOIRE wins on CLIPScore across all single-class comparisons (e.g., 30.60 vs 25.81 on Fonts-A, 32.24 vs 31.72 on FIGR-8-Star) and achieves far lower reconstruction MSE (0.004 vs 0.330 on FIGR-8), but Im2Vec achieves lower FID on filled MNIST shapes (2.22 vs 12.25), which the authors attribute to low-resolution ground truth biasing FID. Against the three SDS-based methods (CLIPDraw, VectorFusion, SVGDreamer), GRIMOIRE dominates on both quality (PSNR 45.19 vs 28.68–36.62) and speed (2.34 s vs 100–380 s per sample), while also producing outputs coherent with the target dataset distribution rather than generic artistic renderings. Against vector-supervised methods, GRIMOIRE outperforms DeepSVG (CLIP 29.00 vs 22.10, FID 0.64 vs 58.03) and Llama 3.2 fine-tuned with minimal pre-processing (CLIP 29.00 vs 25.45, FID 0.64 vs 38.93), but IconShop slightly edges out GRIMOIRE on CLIPScore (31.18 vs 29.00) and FID (0.40 vs 0.64) when trained with its tailored SVG tokenization pipeline on the same FIGR-8 subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRIMOIRE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: differentiable rasterization for vector graphics (DiffVG, Im2Vec) and discrete representation learning via vector quantization (VQ-VAE, FSQ). It departs from prior raster-supervised SVG models (Im2Vec, DiffVG-VAE/GAN) by introducing a codebook-based two-stage pipeline that decouples shape encoding from sequence modeling, and from vector-supervised LLM-based approaches (IconShop, StrokeNUWA, DeepSVG) by eliminating the need for SVG data entirely. The geometric loss and the use of FSQ's implicit hypercube codebook rather than a learned codebook are the paper's specific architectural contributions, while the auto-regressive Transformer with BERT text conditioning follows standard NLP-style sequence modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only raster images for supervision, GRIMOIRE unlocks orders of magnitude more training data for vector generative modeling, which is a practical bottleneck that has limited the field. The modular two-stage design (VSQ for shape encoding, ART for sequence modeling) makes it straightforward to extend to new SVG attributes (color, stroke width) without retraining the language model, a flexibility that rigid tokenization schemes in prior work lack. Limitations include reliance on post-processing (path clipping/interpolation) to connect fragmented strokes, modest dataset scale (MNIST, ~400K icons, ~2M font glyphs), and the fact that the codebook approach may cap expressiveness for highly complex or multi-layer scenes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-page vector documents, animation, or 3D, and whether the raster supervision signal is fundamentally sufficient for fine-grained vector editing tasks.
