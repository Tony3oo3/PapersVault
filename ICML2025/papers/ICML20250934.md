## Problem

Reinforcement learning in environments with asymmetric traversal costs (e.g., uphill vs. downhill energy expenditure, irreversible state transitions) is poorly addressed by existing methods. Prior quasimetric RL approaches like QRL learn a monolithic distance function that conflates path-independent and path-dependent costs, and they provide no formal safety guarantees against irreversible transitions. This paper tackles both gaps by explicitly decomposing asymmetric costs and introducing a Lyapunov-based safety mechanism with improved sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state observation vector and a goal state, encoding both through a two-layer feedforward network (128→64 hidden units with ReLU) into a 64-dimensional latent space. A learned transition model T_ψ predicts the next latent state from the current latent state and action, which is used to estimate the subsequent state's potential for the safety check. The output is a continuous or discrete action selected by the policy π_ω, along with a decomposed quasi-potential cost estimate d(s,g) = Φ(g) − Φ(s) + Ψ(s→g) that separates reversible potential differences from irreversible path-dependent residuals.

## Method

QPRL decomposes the asymmetric traversal cost into a path-independent state potential Φ(s) (analogous to gravitational potential) and a path-dependent residual Ψ(s→g) capturing irreversible or dissipative costs, enabling interpretable cost attribution that prior monolithic quasimetric methods lack. Training jointly optimizes three components: (1) a state encoder and transition model for latent dynamics, (2) the quasi-potential function trained to reconstruct observed costs while enforcing quasimetric axioms (non-negativity and triangle inequality) via a ReLU penalty term, and (3) a policy that minimizes expected quasi-potential cost subject to a Lyapunov safety constraint E[Φ(s′)] ≤ Φ(s) + ε, solved via primal-dual Lagrange multiplier updates. The key theoretical result is that this decomposition restores contraction of the asymmetric Bellman operator, yielding a Õ(√T) regret bound that improves over the Õ(T) bound of prior quasimetric RL. Empirically, disabling the Lyapunov safety layer increases irreversible constraint violations by approximately 4×, confirming its critical role.

## Results

QPRL achieves the highest success rate across all five asymmetric environments: 92.5% on Asymmetric GridWorld, 91.2% on FetchPush, 88.9% on LunarLander, and 85.3% on Maze2D, with a normalized return of −95.6 on MountainCar. The method improves sample complexity from Õ(T) to Õ(√T) and reduces irreversible constraint violations by approximately 4× compared to baselines. QPRL also demonstrates faster convergence, reaching 90% success on LunarLander within 250,000 steps, and achieves roughly 15% lower traversal cost than QRL on MountainCar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPRL consistently outperforms all four baselines across every environment. Against the closest competitor QRL (Wang et al., 2023), QPRL gains 5.2–7.2 percentage points in success rate (e.g., 92.5% vs. 87.3% on GridWorld) and improves MountainCar normalized return by ~12 points (−95.6 vs. −108.4), attributable to the explicit Φ/Ψ decomposition that QRL lacks. Against DDPG+HER (Lillicrap, 2015), QPRL leads by 10.6–13.7 percentage points, with the largest gap on FetchPush (91.2% vs. 73.8%), reflecting DDPG's inability to model direction-dependent costs. SAC+HER (Haarnoja et al., 2018) trails QPRL by 8.6–11.8 percentage points, and Contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) by 9.9–12.7 percentage points. Paired t-tests confirm all differences are statistically significant (p < 0.01, Cohen's d = 0.9–1.4), and the ablation removing the Lyapunov safety layer degrades success rate from 92.7% to 78.3% on FetchPush, highlighting the contribution of the safety mechanism beyond the quasimetric decomposition alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

QPRL extends the quasimetric RL framework of Wang et al. (2023), which learns a monolithic asymmetric distance d(s,g) for goal-reaching, by decomposing it into a path-independent potential Φ and a path-dependent residual Ψ. This decomposition draws on potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) and Lyapunov-based safe policy optimization (Perkins & Barto, 2002; Chow et al., 2018), repurposing the potential function as a Lyapunov certificate for safety. The key conceptual departure from QRL is the explicit separation of reversible (conservative) and irreversible (dissipative) cost components, enabling a modified Bellman equation that preserves contraction under unbounded asymmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition provides an interpretable and theoretically grounded way to handle irreversible transitions in robotic navigation and manipulation, where prior quasimetric methods treated all asymmetry monolithically. The Lyapunov safety layer offers a principled mechanism to prevent entry into irreversible states, addressing a gap in safe RL for asymmetric environments. However, the method assumes stationary environments, scales quadratically with state dimensionality, and the theoretical proof of the Õ(√T) sample complexity bound contains a contraction-factor-of-1 argument that does not actually establish strict contraction, leaving the theoretical guarantee questionable. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to multi-agent settings and non-stationary dynamics.
