## Problem

Prior spatial transcriptomics prediction methods factorize the joint distribution over spots, predicting each spot's gene expression independently and thus ignoring cell-cell interactions where neighboring genes regulate one another. Additionally, slide-level encoders using exhaustive attention over all spots (often exceeding 10,000) suffer from prohibitive memory and compute costs on gigapixel whole-slide images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250999/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an H&E-stained whole-slide image segmented into N spots, each with a 2D coordinate and a patch image. Spot images are encoded by a frozen pathology foundation model (UNI, Ciga, or Gigapath) to produce visual embeddings, which are then combined with coordinates and noisy gene expression samples in the denoiser. The output is the spatial gene expression vector (top 50 highly variable genes, log1p-normalized) for every spot on the slide, generated iteratively from a zero-inflated negative binomial prior over S=5 refinement steps.

## Method

STFlow reformulates gene expression regression as a flow matching generative task that models the joint distribution p(Y_0,…,Y_N | I_0,…,I_N) rather than factorized per-spot predictions. The denoiser is a frame-averaging Transformer that achieves E(2)-invariance by projecting pairwise direction vectors into PCA-extracted frames and averaging MLP-encoded representations across frames, eliminating sensitivity to coordinate scale and rotation. Spatial attention is restricted to k-nearest neighbors (k=8) for O(Nkd) efficiency, and the attention weight explicitly incorporates the gene expression difference (Y_t,i − Y_t,j) between spots, enabling the model to capture cell-cell regulatory interactions during each refinement step. Training minimizes MSE between the denoiser's prediction and ground-truth expression on linearly interpolated samples Y_t = t·Y + (1−t)·Y_0, where Y_0 is drawn from a ZINB prior chosen to match the sparsity and overdispersion observed in real ST data.

## Results

On HEST-1k (10 cancer types), STFlow achieves a mean Pearson correlation of 0.415 across the top 50 highly variable genes, outperforming the best slide-based baseline TRIPLEX (0.395) and the best spot-based baseline BLEEP (0.368). On STImage-1K4M (7 organs), STFlow reaches 0.293, again surpassing TRIPLEX (0.252) and BLEEP (0.232). This represents an 18% average relative improvement over the pathology foundation model UNI (0.344 on HEST, 0.245 on STImage). STFlow also achieves the highest correlation on all four clinical biomarker genes (GATA3: 0.860, ERBB2: 0.844, UBE2C: 0.772, VWF: 0.666).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STFlow consistently outperforms all nine baselines on both benchmarks. Among spot-based methods, BLEEP (0.368 HEST / 0.232 STImage) is the strongest, but STFlow still leads by 5.2 and 26.7% relative respectively; STNet and HisToGene underperform even the raw pathology foundation model UNI, likely due to the challenging patient-stratified split. Among slide-based methods, TRIPLEX (0.395 / 0.252) is the closest competitor, with STFlow improving by 2.0 and 16.3% relative on HEST and STImage. Gigapath-slide, which uses global attention over all spots, frequently hits out-of-memory errors and does not surpass its spot-level counterpart Gigapath, suggesting its pretrained objective is misaligned with spot-level prediction. On biomarker prediction (Table 2), STFlow achieves the highest Pearson correlation on all four genes (GATA3 0.860, ERBB2 0.844, UBE2C 0.772, VWF 0.666), beating TRIPLEX by 0.7–5.4 points. In the E(2)-invariant architecture comparison (Table 3), STFlow's frame-averaging denoiser outperforms both EGNN and E2CNN backbones across all three pathology foundation models, confirming the expressiveness advantage of FA-based spatial attention over distance-only or steerable-kernel encodings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: (1) flow matching as a generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022), which it repurposes from image/molecule generation to gene expression prediction, and (2) frame averaging for E(2)-invariant point cloud processing (Puny et al., 2021), which it adapts to a spatial attention mechanism for whole-slide images. It departs from prior spatial transcriptomics prediction methods (STNet, HisToGene, TRIPLEX, BLEEP) that either factorize the joint distribution across spots or encode coordinates as positional embeddings, by instead modeling the full joint distribution p(Y₀,…,Yₙ | I₀,…,Iₙ) through iterative denoising, thereby explicitly capturing cell-cell gene interactions. The use of frozen pathology foundation models (UNI, Gigapath, Ciga) as spot encoders follows the recent trend of leveraging large-scale pretrained histology features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STFlow addresses a practical bottleneck in spatial transcriptomics—low throughput and need for specialized equipment—by enabling whole-slide gene expression prediction from standard H&E images with orders-of-magnitude lower memory cost than prior slide-based methods. The 18% average improvement over pathology foundation models and consistent gains across 17 datasets suggest that joint-distribution modeling of cell-cell interactions is a meaningful inductive bias for this task. A key limitation is that ZINB prior hyperparameters are grid-searched rather than learned, and the authors note that a VAE-based distribution estimator could be a natural next step. Open questions include whether the flow matching formulation generalizes to single-cell resolution or multi-omic prediction, and whether the local k-NN attention captures sufficient long-range biology for very large slides.
