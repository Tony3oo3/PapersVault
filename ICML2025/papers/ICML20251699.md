## Problem

Many-shot jailbreaking (MSJ) exploits LLM long-context capabilities by prefixing a malicious prompt with hundreds of fabricated harmful Q-A exchanges, but existing methods sample demonstrations uniformly and do not modify them to strengthen the instruction-following pattern. This paper asks how to improve MSJ's attack success rate by reinforcing compliance behavior within the fabricated dialogue and by selecting more effective demonstrations for each target prompt's topic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a malicious target prompt (reformulated as a 'How can I...' question from AdvBench or HarmBench) and a pool of 2,400 malicious Q-A pairs from the ManyHarm dataset spanning 12 harmful topics. It produces a single long-context prompt of up to 256 shots where demonstrations are optionally modified with affirmation, refusal, and correction phrases, and the demonstration set is sampled from a topic-adaptive distribution. The target LLM's response is then evaluated for harmfulness using both a rule-based refusal-phrase check (ASR-R) and Llama-Guard-3 as an LLM judge (ASR-L).

## Method

PANDAS integrates three modifications to standard MSJ. First, Positive Affirmation (PA) inserts short acknowledgment phrases (e.g., 'Exactly the detail I needed! Thanks!') before subsequent malicious questions, reinforcing the instruction-following trajectory without adding extra demonstrations. Second, Negative Demonstration (ND) rewrites selected Q-A pairs by inserting a refusal phrase and a correction phrase between the question and answer, explicitly showing the model that refusal is followed by correction and compliance. Third, Adaptive Sampling (AS) uses Bayesian optimization to learn a topic-dependent probability distribution over the 12 harmful topics for selecting demonstrations, replacing the uniform random sampling used in prior MSJ work. The three components are independently effective and combine additively; PA and ND require no additional computational overhead, while AS is optimized once per target model and topic group.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, PANDAS achieves 94.62% ASR-L at 256 shots on AdvBench, compared to 86.15% for MSJ and 92.00% for i-MSJ on AdvBench50. On Qwen-2.5-7B, PANDAS reaches 19.81% ASR-L at 256 shots on AdvBench, nearly doubling MSJ's 12.31% and substantially outperforming i-MSJ (2.00% on AdvBench50). On GLM-4-9B, PANDAS achieves 34.42% ASR-L at 256 shots on AdvBench versus 25.19% for MSJ. PANDAS also improves ASR-R consistently, reaching 96.15% on Llama-3.1-8B/AdvBench at 256 shots versus 87.69% for MSJ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANDAS consistently outperforms both MSJ (Anil et al., 2024) and i-MSJ (Zheng et al., 2024) across all three evaluated models and all three datasets. Against MSJ, the gains are most dramatic on Qwen-2.5-7B, where PANDAS achieves 19.81% ASR-L at 256 shots on AdvBench versus only 12.31% for MSJ, and on GLM-4-9B where PANDAS reaches 60.75% versus 46.25% on HarmBench. Against i-MSJ, PANDAS wins on Llama-3.1-8B (94.00% vs. 92.00% ASR-L at 256 shots on AdvBench50) and by a large margin on Qwen-2.5-7B (22.00% vs. 2.00% on AdvBench50), where i-MSJ's random search appears to degrade performance. On GLM-4-9B, PANDAS also surpasses i-MSJ at 256 shots (34.00% vs. 20.00% on AdvBench50). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that each PANDAS component (PA, ND, AS) independently improves over MSJ, with PA providing the largest single-component gain (e.g., 93.27% vs. 85.19% ASR-L at 64 shots on AdvBench for Llama-3.1-8B).

## Relation to Prior Work

PANDAS builds directly on Many-shot Jailbreaking (MSJ) by Anil et al. (2024), which demonstrated that prefixing a malicious prompt with hundreds of fabricated compliant dialogues can bypass safety alignment in long-context LLMs. The three PANDAS techniques draw from distinct lineages: Positive Affirmation extends the instruction-following conflict mechanism identified by Wei et al. (2023a); Negative Demonstration adapts the 'learning from mistakes' paradigm in in-context learning (Zhang et al. 2024b; Gao & Das 2024) to the jailbreaking setting; and Adaptive Sampling applies Bayesian optimization to the demonstration-selection problem, moving beyond the uniform topic sampling used in MSJ. The paper also extends Zheng et al.'s (2024) few-shot improvement method (i-FSJ) to the many-shot regime as i-MSJ, serving as a stronger baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even models explicitly aligned against long-context vulnerabilities (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B) remain highly susceptible to improved many-shot attacks, with PANDAS exceeding 80% ASR at just 64 shots. The finding that jailbreaking effectiveness does not monotonically increase with shot count (peaking at 32 for GLM-4-9B) challenges the power-law scaling narrative from Anil et al. and suggests that targeted safety alignment may be partially effective at specific shot counts. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to ~8B open-source models, reliance on uncensored models for dataset generation, and no evaluation on proprietary systems. Open questions include whether fewer, better-selected demonstrations can achieve comparable ASR, and what model properties drive the observed cross-model transferability asymmetries.
