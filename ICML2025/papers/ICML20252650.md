## Problem

Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) enables adaptive embedding lengths but requires full backbone retraining and suffers significant accuracy drops at short lengths (e.g., >10% at 8 dimensions). The paper asks whether sparse coding can achieve the same adaptive-length goal as a lightweight post-hoc module on frozen embeddings, without sacrificing fidelity or incurring MRL's training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252650/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen dense embedding v ∈ R^d (e.g., 2048-d from ResNet-50, 512-d from ViT-B-16, or 4096-d from NV-Embed-V2) and produces a sparse vector in a higher-dimensional space R^h (h = 4d) where only K dimensions are non-zero (K ranges from 8 to 256). At inference, retrieval uses sparse matrix multiplication over these TopK-activated vectors, yielding O(K) per-query complexity. The same sparse representation serves classification (via 1-NN), text retrieval (NDCG@10), and cross-modal retrieval (Recall@5).

## Method

CSR trains a single lightweight TopK Sparse Autoencoder (tied encoder-decoder, ~1.1M parameters) on top of a frozen backbone. The encoder maps the dense embedding to a high-dimensional latent space and applies TopK selection to retain only the K largest activations, producing a non-negative sparse vector. Training combines two objectives: (1) a reconstruction loss (L2 between original and decoded embedding) augmented with an auxiliary dead-latent loss and multi-TopK regularization to prevent capacity collapse, and (2) a non-negative contrastive loss (NCL) that leverages the sparsity-induced non-negativity to encourage disentangled, identifiable features with theoretical guarantees. Because only the SAE module is trained (not the backbone), training takes minutes to hours versus MRL's full retraining, and the resulting sparse vectors enable GPU-accelerated sparse matrix multiplication for retrieval that is up to 69× faster than dense baselines at matched accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 1-NN accuracy, CSR (frozen RN50 backbone, k=32 active dims) achieves 70.15%, outperforming MRL (69.46%) and SAE (68.91%) at the same sparsity, while at k=8 the gap widens to 67.78% vs. MRL's 62.19%. On MTEB text tasks with NV-Embed-V2 (k=32), CSR reaches 89.86% on MTOPIntent classification and 71.17% NDCG@10 on SciFACT retrieval, surpassing all MRL-based baselines at 2–3× lower retrieval time. On multimodal retrieval (MS COCO, in-distribution, k=256), CSR achieves 71.41% I2T and 83.49% T2I Recall@5 with only 1.1M trainable parameters, versus 67.12%/77.53% for fully fine-tuned ViT-B-16-MRL (86M params). Training CSR takes ~30 minutes on a single GPU, orders of magnitude less than MRL's full retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSR consistently outperforms MRL and MRL-E at all active dimensions below 128 on ImageNet-1K 1-NN, with the largest margin at k=8 (67.78% vs. 62.19%, a 5.6-point gap) where MRL suffers severe degradation. Against post-hoc compression baselines (SVD, Rand-FS, Rand-LP), CSR achieves dramatically higher accuracy—e.g., at k=8, CSR reaches 67.78% while SVD only achieves 19.14% and Rand-FS 2.36%. On MTEB text tasks, CSR at k=32 (retrieval time 1.0×) beats all MRL-based text embedding models (Stella-1.5B-v5, Jina-V3, Nomic-Embed-V1.5, Gecko-Embed-004, Text-Embed-3-L, Arctic-Embed-L-V2, Potion-Base-2M) that operate at k=64 or k=256 with 2–3× higher retrieval time, achieving a 61× speedup at matched performance versus the full NV-Embed-V2 representation. In multimodal retrieval, CSR with only 1.1M trainable parameters surpasses fully fine-tuned ViT-B-16-MRL (86M params) by 4.6–10.3% on in-distribution Recall@5 and 3.2–9.2% on zero-shot, while SAE (without the contrastive loss) shows noticeably larger degradation, confirming the benefit of the NCL component. The only setting where CSR does not clearly win is at very high active dimensions (k≥512) on ImageNet, where MRL and CSR converge to similar accuracy, and in extreme multimodal sparsity (k=64) where dead latents narrow the gap to MRL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), previously developed for interpretability in language models (Cunningham et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024), as a compression mechanism for adaptive representation learning, positioning them as a direct alternative to Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is replacing MRL's dense truncation (shorter vectors) with sparse activation in a higher-dimensional space (fewer non-zero entries), combined with a non-negative contrastive loss (Wang et al., 2024) to improve feature identifiability. Unlike MRL, which requires full backbone retraining, CSR is a lightweight post-hoc module (2-layer MLP) applied to frozen pre-trained embeddings, making it a plug-and-play solution orthogonal to other acceleration techniques like quantization and pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: CSR achieves a 69× retrieval speedup on ImageNet 1-NN with near-full-accuracy performance, while requiring only ~30 minutes of single-GPU training versus the full retraining MRL demands. This makes adaptive embedding compression accessible for existing foundation models (CLIP, NV-Embed) without prohibitively expensive fine-tuning. A key open challenge remains the 'dead latents' problem in multimodal alignment spaces, where CSR's advantage over MRL narrows under extreme sparsity, suggesting that cross-modal representation geometry may require specialized sparsity mechanisms. The method's generality across vision, text, and multimodal tasks positions it as a broadly applicable compression layer for production retrieval systems.
