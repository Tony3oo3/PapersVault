## Problem

Federated graph learning (FGL) suffers from topology heterogeneity, where nodes of the same class in different clients connect to different neighbor classes, causing biased local GNN training. Existing FGL methods attempt to fix this by injecting global signals (pseudo graphs, distillation, contrastive augmentation), which inflate computation and communication without consistently reducing embedding divergence across clients. The paper asks whether removing superfluous local topology information, rather than adding global increments, is a more effective strategy.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private graph G_k = (V_k, E_k, X_k) with node features and an adjacency matrix; the edge evaluator consumes concatenated node-feature pairs to produce per-edge importance scores in [0,1], yielding soft (probabilistic) causal and biased adjacency masks rather than hard edge removals. The method outputs node class labels via the causal GNN, and only the causal GNN parameters are uploaded to the server for weighted averaging—the edge evaluator and biased GNN remain entirely local, avoiding any extra communication overhead.

## Method

FedATH introduces a per-client edge evaluator (an MLP) that assigns each edge a soft probability of belonging to a causal subgraph, splitting the local adjacency matrix into causal (A_k^c = A_k ⊙ Ω_k^c) and biased (A_k^b = A_k ⊙ Ω_k^b) components via Hadamard products. A dual-GNN architecture encodes the two subgraphs separately: the causal GNN is trained with cross-entropy to align its representations with ground-truth labels, while the biased GNN is trained with a negative-entropy loss to produce smooth, non-discriminative predictions. A Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) term maximizes statistical independence between the two representations, enforcing disentanglement. Only the causal GNN is aggregated at the server using standard FedAvg weighting, and a theoretical generalization bound shows that the soft edge weights reduce the Frobenius norm of the adjacency matrices, thereby tightening the H-divergence term and improving global model generalization.

## Results

FedATH achieves the best accuracy across all six datasets and all client counts (K=10, 15, 20). On Cora (K=10), it reaches 77.90% ACC, outperforming the strongest baseline FedPUB (75.35%) by 2.55 points. On the heterophilic Roman-empire dataset (K=10), FedATH achieves 48.18% ACC versus 41.59% for FedSage+, a margin of 6.59 points. On WikiCS (K=10), it scores 75.22% versus 72.30% for FedPUB, and on ogbn-arxiv (K=10) it reaches 42.21% versus 41.02% for FedSage+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedATH consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and client count. Against the conventional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, FedOPT, FedProto), the gains are substantial: on Cora K=10, FedATH leads FedAvg by 4.31 points and FedOPT/FedProto by 3.34 points, indicating that standard FL strategies are poorly suited to topology heterogeneity. Among the graph-oriented FGL baselines, FedPUB is the closest competitor on Cora (75.35% vs. 77.90%) and PubMed (82.67% vs. 84.06%), while FedSage+ is the strongest on ogbn-arxiv (41.02% vs. 42.21%) and Roman-empire (41.59% vs. 48.18%). The largest absolute margins appear on the heterophilic Roman-empire dataset, where FedATH exceeds FedSage+ by 6.59 points (K=10) and FedTAD by 9.17 points, suggesting that causal subgraph extraction is particularly effective when node neighborhoods are dominated by cross-class edges. On WikiCS, FedATH surpasses FedPUB by 2.92 points (K=10) and FGSSL by 4.59 points, further confirming the advantage of reducing superfluous topology over adding global increments.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedATH departs from the dominant paradigm in FGL that addresses topology heterogeneity by creating global increments (e.g., repairing missing neighbors in FedSage+, generating pseudo-graphs in FedTAD, or calibrating embeddings via global models in FGSSL). Instead, it repurposes the causal representation learning framework (SCM-based disentanglement, as in Fan et al. 2022 for single-graph GNN debiasing) to the federated setting, arguing that removing biased edges is more effective than adding global signals. The dual-GNN + HSIC disentanglement architecture is borrowed from causal representation learning, while the federated aggregation protocol (sharing only causal GNNs) is a novel communication-efficient design specific to this paper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to the 'add more global information' strategy that has dominated FGL, with a generalization bound (Corollary 5.2) showing that reducing adjacency matrix Frobenius norms directly tightens the H-divergence between clients. The consistent 2–7 point gains across six datasets and three client counts, plus the Wasserstein distance analysis showing existing SOTA methods fail to reduce embedding divergence, make a compelling case for the reduction-over-increment philosophy. Key limitations include the supervised setting (node labels required for the cross-entropy loss on causal subgraphs) and the reliance on a per-client edge evaluator whose quality depends on local data; the authors acknowledge unsupervised FGL as an important open direction.
