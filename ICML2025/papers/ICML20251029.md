## Problem

Existing post-training sparsification methods for LLMs either apply a uniform sparsity rate across all layers or rely on hand-crafted metrics and expensive search to allocate layer-wise sparsity, without a theoretical justification for why earlier layers should be sparser than later ones. The paper identifies a critical 'reconstruction error explosion' phenomenon: errors introduced by sparsifying earlier layers propagate and amplify through subsequent layers, causing the total reconstruction error to grow dramatically and severely degrading model accuracy. No prior work provided a principled, theoretically grounded derivation for the optimal layer-wise sparsity allocation pattern.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense LLM (e.g., LLaMA, LLaMA2, LLaMA3, OPT, Mistral, Mixtral) and a single target average sparsity rate S (e.g., 0.5, 0.7, 0.8). It produces a monotonically increasing arithmetic progression of per-layer sparsity rates, fully determined by one hyperparameter β (the common difference), which is found via grid search over a narrow bounded range (e.g., 0 < β ≤ 0.019 for a 32-layer model at S=0.7) with step size 0.002, requiring only 3–9 forward passes. The resulting per-layer rates are then fed into any existing post-training sparsification method (Wanda, SparseGPT, DSnoT, ALPS, etc.) to produce the final sparse model.

## Method

The paper first proves two key theorems: (1) increasing sparsity in layer i strictly increases that layer's reconstruction error, and (2) increased reconstruction error in layer i raises the lower bound of reconstruction error in layer i+1 (via the smallest singular value of the sparse weight matrix), establishing a cumulative 'reconstruction error explosion' across the network. From this, the authors derive that sparsity rates must be monotonically increasing from early to late layers to minimize total error. They instantiate this as a simple arithmetic progression s_i = S − β(L−1)/2 + β(i−1), reducing the entire allocation problem to selecting a single scalar β. A grid search over the provably narrow valid range of β (bounded by the constraint that all s_i ∈ [0,1]) finds the optimal value in 3–9 trials (~18 minutes for a 7B model). Theorem 3.5 further proves that any monotonically increasing sparsity scheme strictly outperforms any non-monotonic scheme in total reconstruction error, and empirical comparison with Bayesian search over all 32 layer-wise rates confirms the arithmetic progression is near-optimal.

## Results

On 70% sparse LLaMA2-7B pruned with Wanda, ATP reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 74.26 (uniform) to 22.16 and improves average zero-shot accuracy from 35.33% to 45.83% (+10.50%). Across all evaluated LLMs (LLaMA 7B–65B, LLaMA2 7B–70B, LLaMA3-8B), ATP consistently achieves the best perplexity and zero-shot accuracy among all layer-wise sparsity methods. ATP also delivers 2.63× and 2.23× inference speedups on CPU and GPU respectively, and requires only 18 minutes to determine layer-wise sparsity rates (vs. ~33 hours for Bayesian search).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATP outperforms all metric-based layer-wise sparsity methods (OWL, AlphaPruning, ALS) and the search-based DSA across every model and sparsity rate tested. On 70% sparse LLaMA2-7B (Wanda), ATP achieves 45.83% average zero-shot accuracy vs. 43.37% (AlphaPruning), 41.75% (OWL), 36.55% (DSA), and 35.33% (Uniform), with perplexity of 22.16 vs. 28.87, 30.38, 63.71, and 74.26 respectively. ATP also surpasses traditional CV sparsity baselines (Global, LAMP, ER, ER-Plus) by large margins on WikiText-2 perplexity (e.g., 20.16 vs. 84.52 for Uniform at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-7B). When integrated with other post-training sparsity methods (DSnoT, Pruner-Zero, ALPS), ATP consistently improves perplexity and accuracy (e.g., DSnoT: 77.83→24.31 perplexity; ALPS: 19.31→17.99). Compared to Bayesian search (1000 iterations, ~33 hours), ATP achieves nearly identical perplexity (22.16 vs. 22.10 at 70% sparsity) in just 18 minutes, confirming near-optimality with dramatically lower computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on post-training sparsity methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) that apply a uniform sparsity rate across all layers, and addresses the layer-wise sparsity allocation problem previously tackled by metric-based heuristics (OWL, AlphaPruning, ALS) and search-based approaches (DSA). The key departure is providing a theoretical derivation from reconstruction error propagation that reduces the allocation problem to a single hyperparameter (the common difference β of a monotonically increasing arithmetic progression), rather than relying on hand-crafted importance metrics or expensive evolutionary/Bayesian search. The paper positions itself as the first to offer a rigorous proof that monotonically increasing sparsity is strictly superior to any non-monotonic scheme under the same average sparsity budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically significant because it is a drop-in improvement for any existing post-training sparsity technique, requires only ~18 minutes of computation (vs. 33 hours for Bayesian search), and generalizes across architectures (LLaMA, OPT, Mistral, Mixtral), modalities (vision, multimodal), and compression paradigms (N:M sparsity, structured pruning, quantization). The theoretical near-optimality guarantee (Theorem 3.5) and empirical validation against Bayesian search provide strong confidence in the approach. Open questions include whether the arithmetic progression is truly optimal (the authors acknowledge it may not be), whether the reconstruction error explosion analysis fully captures the nonlinear dynamics of Transformer layers, and how the method performs under even higher sparsity rates where the performance gap to dense models remains substantial.
