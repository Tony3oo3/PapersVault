## Problem

Most generative models for crystal structures treat each atom independently without enforcing symmetry constraints, producing materials that lack the symmetrical characteristics of real crystals (e.g., GNoME concentrates 34% of generated materials in four space groups, one of which barely exists in nature). This paper addresses the gap by generating symmetry-based protostructure descriptions rather than unconstrained atomic coordinates, ensuring symmetry is respected by construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250583/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model first samples a space group from the empirical training distribution, which determines the number and type of Wyckoff positions available. It then iteratively denoises a discrete state vector where each variable is categorical: constrained positions (fixed points) hold a single variable indicating which element (or none) occupies them, while unconstrained positions (line/plane/volume) hold one variable per element type indicating the count (0 to P=54) of atoms of that type. The output is a protostructure—a space group plus Wyckoff occupancy pattern—without exact atomic coordinates, which must be realized in a separate downstream step.

## Method

WyckoffDiff uses D3PM discrete denoising diffusion over the protostructure representation, with a novel WyckoffGNN backbone that treats each Wyckoff position as a node in a fully connected graph. The GNN encodes positions via message-passing layers where messages are computed with an attention-like mechanism (two MLPs producing a scalar weight and a transformed neighbor vector), and decodes node embeddings into per-variable categorical distributions via separate MLPs for constrained and unconstrained positions. A key design choice is the limiting (prior) distribution: using the empirical marginal or a Dirac-at-zero (empty material) prior dramatically outperforms the uniform prior, since the data is highly sparse (most Wyckoff positions are unoccupied). Training optimizes cross-entropy between the true clean sample and the predicted distribution (as in DiGress), with a cosine schedule for the noise rate β_t.

## Results

On the WBM dataset, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros achieves the best Fréchet Wrenformer Distance (FWD) of 0.48±0.02, substantially outperforming DiffCSP++ (0.83±0.14), SymmCD (1.47±0.29), and CDVAE (41.9±2.69). Among novel materials only, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros also achieves the lowest FWD (4.34±0.56) versus DiffCSP++ (5.15±0.17) and SymmCD (4.53±0.65), while generating 119 novel materials per minute. In terms of structural novelty, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros produces 1175±80 unique and novel prototypes among 10,000 novel protostructures, compared to 527±39 for DiffCSP++ and 780±49 for SymmCD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDVAE, which does not incorporate any symmetry constraints, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros achieves a dramatically lower FWD (0.48 vs. 41.9), confirming that symmetry-aware generation is critical for matching the training distribution; CDVAE's 36% of generated materials fall in space group 1 versus only 0.3% in the training data. Against DiffCSP++, which uses pre-defined structural templates, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros wins on FWD (0.48 vs. 0.83) and novel FWD (4.34 vs. 5.15), and produces more than twice as many unique novel prototypes (1175 vs. 527), though DiffCSP++ achieves higher raw novelty (48.4% vs. 30.2%). Against SymmCD, which generates Wyckoff positions via binary symmetry matrices, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros again achieves the best FWD (0.48 vs. 1.47) and novel FWD (4.34 vs. 4.53), while SymmCD generates more novel materials per minute (115 vs. 119 is comparable) but produces fewer unique novel prototypes (780 vs. 1175). Overall, WYCKOFFDIFF-zeros is the best or near-best on every metric except raw novelty percentage, where its lower value is attributed to the model memorizing the training distribution over 1000 epochs; the paper argues that the novelty filter is a natural part of the generation pipeline, and the higher novel-materials-per-minute rate compensates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete diffusion framework of D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) and the cross-entropy training objective and marginal-prior idea from DiGress (Vignac et al., 2022), but applies them to a fundamentally new representation: crystal protostructures (space group + Wyckoff position occupancy) rather than full atomic coordinates. It departs from template-based symmetry-aware methods like DiffCSP++ (which restricts generation to pre-defined structural templates) and SymmCD (which generates representative orbits with binary symmetry matrices) by instead generating occupancy of *all* Wyckoff positions in a given space group via a novel message-passing GNN (WyckoffGNN) over a complete graph of positions. The protostructure representation itself draws on the AFLOW prototype labeling system and the coordinate-free coarse-graining idea of Goodall et al. (2022), but is the first to place it inside a discrete generative diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By separating symmetry (protostructure) from geometry (exact coordinates), WYCKOFFDIFF enables fast, symmetry-respecting generation that avoids the well-documented failure mode of coordinate-based models producing low-symmetry or physically implausible structures. The proposed Fréchet Wrenformer Distance (FWD) provides a symmetry-aware evaluation metric that could become a standard tool for comparing crystal generative models. Key limitations include the need for a separate realization step (Pyxtal + MACE relaxation) to obtain full geometries, the complete-graph assumption that limits scalability for space groups with many Wyckoff positions, and the proof-of-concept materials discovery being limited to 200 DFT-verified structures with a potential bias toward fluorides. Open questions include whether the protostructure-to-geometry pipeline can be made more reliable for complex multi-element systems and whether the approach extends to larger chemical spaces beyond the 100-element, 54-atom-per-position limits used here.
