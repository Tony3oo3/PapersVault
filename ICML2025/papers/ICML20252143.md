## Problem

Millions of abandoned oil and gas wells leak methane and toxic compounds, yet a significant fraction remain unrecorded, preventing remediation. Existing remote sensing datasets for well detection are small (500–10,000 wells), geographically limited, and typically contain only active wells, making them inadequate for identifying the abandoned and suspended wells that pose the greatest environmental risk. This paper introduces the first large-scale benchmark dataset (213,000+ wells) that encompasses all three well states and provides a systematic evaluation of detection and segmentation algorithms.

## Input / Output

The input consists of 4-band (RGB + Near-Infrared) PlanetScope satellite imagery at ~3 m/pixel resolution, cropped into 1050 m × 1050 m square patches and stored as numpy arrays in HDF5 format with all geographic metadata stripped for licensing compliance. Outputs include binary segmentation masks (90 m-diameter circles per well site, ~30 px after 256×256 resizing), COCO-format bounding box annotations, and multi-class segmentation maps distinguishing active, suspended, and abandoned states. The dataset maintains a 1:1 ratio of well-containing to well-free patches, with well density per patch ranging from 1 to 125.

## Method

The dataset is constructed from AER ST37 well records (637k raw entries reduced to ~213k after deduplication and expert quality control) paired with cloud-free PlanetScope imagery, using a two-level K-Means clustering algorithm (300 local clusters grouped into 30 geographic super-clusters) to produce train/validation/test splits that balance regional diversity while preventing spatial leakage. For benchmarking, the authors evaluate both CNN-based (U-Net, PAN, DeepLabV3+, RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN, FCOS, SSD Lite) and Transformer-based (SegFormer, UperNet, DETR) architectures under both binary segmentation and object detection framings, trained from scratch with ImageNet-initialized backbones adapted for 4-channel input. Key findings: UperNet with ConvNeXt backbone achieves the best segmentation (IoU 59.9, Recall 73.1), DETR leads in detection (mAP50:95 = 38.45), the NIR band yields consistent gains over RGB alone, and training on all three well types substantially outperforms training on active wells only (IoU 0.576 vs. 0.502).

## Results

For object detection, DETR (ResNet-50) achieves the best overall performance with mAP50 of 15.22±0.28 and mAP50:95 of 38.45±0.32, while SSD Lite (MobileNet) attains the highest IoU at 0.5 threshold (65.07±0.03). For binary segmentation, U-Net with EfficientNetB6 backbone achieves the best IoU (60.4±0.3) and F1-score (64.8±0.4), while UperNet with Swin Small backbone achieves the highest recall (73.1±0.1). Overall, segmentation outperforms detection, suggesting it is a more suitable framing for this task. Including near-infrared (NIR) bands consistently improves both detection and segmentation metrics over RGB-only inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among detection baselines, DETR [Carion et al., ECCV 2020] dominates on mAP metrics (mAP50=15.22, mAP50:95=38.45), outperforming FCOS [Tian et al., ICCV 2019] by ~57% on mAP50 and ~26% on mAP50:95, and Faster R-CNN [Ren et al., TPAMI 2017] by nearly 3× on mAP50. SSD Lite [Liu et al., ECCV 2016] achieves the highest IoU@0.5 (65.07) but trails DETR on mAP50:95 (25.14 vs. 38.45). RetinaNet [Lin et al., ICCV 2017] performs poorly on mAP (0.18/0.63), likely due to class imbalance in dense well patches. For segmentation, U-Net [Ronneberger et al., MICCAI 2015] with EfficientNetB6 backbone leads in IoU (60.4) and F1 (64.8), while UperNet [Xiao et al., ECCV 2018] with Swin Small achieves the best recall (73.1) at the cost of lower precision (80.6 vs. 90.2 for U-Net+ResNet50). SegFormer [Xie et al., NeurIPS 2021] and PAN [Li et al., BMVC 2018] / DeepLabV3+ [Chen et al., ECCV 2018] all cluster around IoU 57–58, confirming that architecture choice matters less than backbone capacity for this small-object task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale benchmark dataset for oil and gas well detection that includes abandoned and suspended wells, not just active ones. Prior datasets (NEPU-OWOD, Oil Well Dataset, Well Pad Dataset) are limited to 500–12,000 wells, restricted to small regions, and focus exclusively on active wells, making them insufficient for the abandoned-well identification problem. Methodologically, the paper repurposes standard object detection and segmentation architectures (U-Net, DETR, FCOS, Faster R-CNN, SSD, SegFormer, UperNet) as baselines, while contributing a hierarchical K-Means clustering algorithm for geographically diverse train/validation/test splitting. The use of multi-spectral PlanetScope imagery (RGB + Near-Infrared) at 3 m/px resolution distinguishes it from prior work that relied on Google Earth or Sentinel-2 data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Abandoned oil and gas wells are a major and poorly quantified source of methane emissions (equivalent to ~500,000 metric tons CO₂/year in Canada alone), and a significant fraction remain unrecorded in government databases. This dataset provides the first scalable benchmark to train and evaluate algorithms that can identify these hidden wells from satellite imagery, directly supporting climate mitigation efforts. Key open questions include: whether models trained on Alberta transfer to other high-density regions (Appalachian US, former Soviet states), how to handle high-density well clusters where performance degrades, and whether the modest baseline performance (IoU ~60% segmentation, mAP50 ~15% detection) can be substantially improved with better architectures or multi-temporal data. The finding that training on active wells alone is insufficient for detecting degraded abandoned wells underscores the importance of including all well states in training data.
