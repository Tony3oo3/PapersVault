## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) provide human-interpretable predictions but assume source and target data share the same distribution, leading to significant performance degradation under domain shifts (e.g., 80%→66% accuracy on CUB). Naively combining CBMs with domain adaptation fails because it enforces separate class-wise and concept-wise alignment and assumes perfect (uniform) alignment between source and target concepts, which is overly restrictive and harms performance. The paper addresses the open problem of making CBMs robust to domain shifts while preserving concept-level interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain images (with both class labels and binary concept annotations for K concepts) and unlabeled target-domain images as input. It produces both a Q-class label prediction and K binary concept probability predictions for each target image. Notably, no concept labels or class labels are required in the target domain, and the concept embeddings are constructed as weighted combinations of positive and negative semantic embeddings (rather than scalar concept values), following the Concept Embedding Model paradigm.

## Method

CUDA is a game-theoretic adversarial framework with four components: a concept embedding encoder E (producing positive/negative embeddings combined by predicted concept probabilities), a concept probability encoder E_prob, a domain discriminator D, and a label predictor F. The key innovation is a relaxed discriminator loss L̃_d = min{L_d, τ} that caps the domain classification loss at a threshold τ, intentionally allowing small domain-specific differences in concept distributions rather than enforcing perfect uniform alignment. This relaxation is theoretically grounded: Theorem 4.1 shows the optimal encoder achieves JSD(p_S^v ‖ p_T^v) = log 2 − τ, and the relaxation for concept predictions is guaranteed no greater than that for embeddings. The training alternates between minimizing the discriminator loss (Eqn. 4) and jointly minimizing prediction loss, concept loss, and the relaxed adversarial loss (Eqn. 5), with a generalization error bound (Theorem 3.1) showing target error is controlled by source error with ground-truth concepts, HΔH divergence, and concept prediction accuracy.

## Results

CUDA achieves the highest average classification accuracy across all three dataset families: 81.15% on Waterbirds (vs. 66.34% for CEM and 67.53% for CBM), 91.25% on MNIST digit adaptation (vs. 64.03% for CEM and 58.82% for CBM), and 78.65% on SkinCON/Fitzpatrick (vs. 73.67% for CEM and 73.89% for CBM). On the Waterbirds-2 task alone, CUDA reaches 92.90% class accuracy, a 22.6-point improvement over CEM (70.27%). CUDA also leads on concept-level metrics, achieving 94.63% concept accuracy and 84.97% concept F1 on Waterbirds-2, outperforming both CEM and CBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUDA dominates all nine baselines across every dataset family. Against concept-based methods, CUDA outperforms CEM by 14.8 points (81.15% vs. 66.34%) and CBM by 13.6 points on Waterbirds, and by 27.2 points (91.25% vs. 64.03%) on MNIST; PCBM and CONDA are particularly weak on domain adaptation, with CONDA collapsing to 23.82% average on Waterbirds and 12.48% on MNIST. Against state-of-the-art DA methods, CUDA surpasses the best DA baseline GH++ by 12.8 points on Waterbirds (81.15% vs. 68.33%) and 14.0 points on MNIST (91.25% vs. 77.29%), while on SkinCON the margin is smaller but still positive (78.65% vs. 77.11% for UTEP). A critical distinction is that DA methods (DANN, MCD, SRDC, UTEP, GH++) cannot predict concepts at all, so they offer no interpretability, whereas CUDA simultaneously achieves the highest classification accuracy and strong concept-level metrics (e.g., 97.20% concept F1 on MNIST→MNIST-M). The relaxed alignment mechanism is key: without it, uniform alignment causes significant performance drops, as shown by the KDE analysis in Fig. 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020) and Concept Embedding Models (Zarlenga et al., 2022) into the domain adaptation setting, where prior CBMs assumed identical source and target distributions. It draws on adversarial domain adaptation (DANN, Ganin et al., 2016) but departs from the standard uniform-alignment assumption by introducing a relaxed discriminator loss that tolerates bounded domain-specific concept differences. The theoretical contribution—a generalization error bound for CBMs involving the HΔH divergence in concept-embedding space and a concept-prediction error term—is the first of its kind, building on the Ben-David et al. (2006; 2010) framework but operating in the concept space rather than the input space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CUDA is the first framework to jointly provide concept-level interpretability and domain-shift robustness without requiring labeled concept data in the target domain, addressing a gap the authors identify as 'largely underexplored.' The relaxed alignment mechanism, formally characterized via Jensen-Shannon divergence in Theorem 4.1, offers a principled trade-off between domain invariance and concept fidelity that could inform other bottleneck or structured-representation adaptation settings. Limitations include the focus on binary classification (multi-class is deferred), evaluation on relatively small-scale datasets (MNIST, Waterbirds, SkinCON), and the reliance on a pretrained backbone for feature extraction. Open questions include whether the relaxation threshold τ can be learned adaptively, how the approach scales to open-vocabulary or foundation-model-based concept spaces, and whether the theoretical bounds tighten with larger hypothesis classes.
