## Problem

Existing textual unlearning methods for language models are assumed to erase the influence of specific training texts, but it remains unclear whether they truly achieve clean erasure or whether the unlearning mechanism itself introduces new privacy risks. Prior auditing methods rely on randomly selected samples that suffer from 'false memory' due to distributional overlap, and the privacy threats posed by having access to both pre- and post-unlearning models in deployment have been largely unexplored for text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For auditing (U-LiRA+), the method consumes mislabeled audit samples injected into a test model plus T shadow models to produce a TPR@0.1%FPR score per unlearning method. For TULA-MI, the adversary queries both the original and unlearned models with a target text, receiving either a single scalar score (strict case) or full logit vectors (relaxed case), and outputs a binary membership prediction. For TULA-DR, the adversary takes the weight difference Δθ = θ_original − θ_unlearn and the known unlearning algorithm, and outputs a reconstructed text by decoding optimized continuous embedding vectors back through the model's tokenizer.

## Method

The paper contributes three mechanisms. First, U-LiRA+ performs rigorous unlearning auditing by injecting mislabeled samples (which a model cannot generalize to without explicit training, providing 'zero memory' worst-case conditions) and applying per-sample likelihood ratio tests fitted via shadow models. Second, TULA-MI exploits the fact that unlearned samples undergo large confidence shifts between pre- and post-unlearning models while unseen samples do not, using either a simple threshold on score differences (strict) or a LightGBM classifier trained on shadow-model logit features including the difference vector l_ori − l_ul (relaxed). Third, TULA-DR reformulates discrete text reconstruction as continuous optimization in the token embedding space, minimizing the cosine distance between the unlearning gradient of a candidate (x̂, ŷ) on the original model and the observed weight difference Δθ, augmented with a sentence-norm regularization term and per-element boundary clipping derived from the vocabulary embedding table to accelerate convergence and prevent local optima.

## Results

U-LiRA+ auditing reveals that inexact unlearning methods (GA, KL, NPO, TaskVec) fail to erase unlearned texts: in the worst case, over 70% of unlearned samples remain detectable with TPR@0.1%FPR well above zero, whereas the SOTA U-LiRA baseline incorrectly reports near-zero TPR (e.g., KL on OPT-1.3b drops from 12.25% to 0.28% under U-LiRA but stays at 41.60% under U-LiRA+). In the white-box TULA-DR attack, Pythia-1.4b leaks over 80% of original words (R-1) and preserves semantic structure (R-2 up to 66.75%, R-L up to 81.35% for GA), while OPT-1.3b still leaks ~40% of words. In the strict black-box TULA-MI, the adversary correctly identifies 27+ unlearned samples with only 1 false positive (NTS@1FS = 27.55 for Retrain/CrossEntropy, N=64) on Pythia-1.4b.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U-LiRA (Hayes et al., 2024), the proposed U-LiRA+ auditing method dramatically changes the conclusion: where U-LiRA reports near-complete erasure (e.g., KL on OPT-1.3b drops TPR from 12.25% to 0.28%), U-LiRA+ reveals TPR remains at 41.60%, showing that existing auditing overestimates unlearning effectiveness by an order of magnitude. In the relaxed black-box MIA setting (Figure 4), TULA-MI outperforms both LiRA (Carlini et al., 2022) and ULeaks (Chen et al., 2021): with a false-positive rate below 0.1%, TULA-MI infers membership for over 18% of samples, while LiRA is hindered by textual redundancy and overlap that prevent reliable per-sample distribution fitting, and ULeaks (which uses a random forest on combined logit features) achieves lower TPR across all unlearning methods. The paper does not directly compare TULA-DR against Hu et al. (2024) on text, as that prior work targets image data; instead, TULA-DR extends the weight-difference cosine-similarity objective to the embedding space with sentence regularization and boundary clipping, achieving 80%+ word-level reconstruction on Pythia-1.4b where no prior textual baseline exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning auditing and privacy attack literature from the image domain to text/language models. U-LiRA+ directly builds on the LiRA likelihood-ratio auditing framework (Carlini et al., 2022) and its unlearning-specific variant U-LiRA (Hayes et al., 2024), but introduces mislabeled sample injection to eliminate the 'false memory' confound that prior auditing methods suffer from. TULA-DR adapts the weight-difference inversion attack of Hu et al. (2024) from image reconstruction to text via continuous embedding-space optimization, while TULA-MI extends the deployment-risk analysis of Chen et al. (2021) to the text setting with both strict and relaxed black-box formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard unlearning auditing methods overestimate erasure effectiveness by orders of magnitude (e.g., reporting 0.28% TPR when actual is 41.60%) calls into question the reliability of prior unlearning evaluations across the field. The deployment-risk angle (TULA) highlights a previously underappreciated concern: even perfectly executed unlearning creates a new attack surface by making the 'non-existence' of data observable through model comparisons. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic datasets, small models (1.3b–1.4b), and text classification tasks only; whether these findings transfer to large-scale LLMs, text generation, or real-world data remains open. The paper motivates the need for fundamentally new unlearning mechanisms rather than incremental improvements to existing inexact methods.
