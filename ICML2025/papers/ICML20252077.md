## Problem

Latent Space Optimization (LSO) for discrete black-box problems frequently produces invalid solutions (e.g., unparseable expressions or molecules failing quality filters) because unconstrained acquisition optimization drifts into out-of-distribution regions of the VAE latent space. Existing remedies such as decoder-uncertainty censoring (Notin et al., 2021) are non-differentiable, rely on Monte Carlo sampling over multiple models, and are computationally expensive, limiting their integration into standard LSO pipelines.

## Input / Output

LES takes a single latent vector z and a pretrained VAE decoder (GRU, LSTM, or Transformer) as input, requiring no encoder, no training data, and no architectural modifications. It produces a scalar score computed from the decoder's Jacobian (obtained via one backward pass) and the softmax output probabilities, with the final determinant evaluated via SVD on a d×d matrix where d is the latent dimension. In the LSO loop, this score is added to the acquisition function with a regularization weight λ, and the resulting optimized latent vector is decoded to yield a valid sequence.

## Method

LES is derived as the log-determinant of the change-of-variables term in the push-forward density of the decoder, under the assumption that the decoder logits form a Continuous Piecewise Affine (CPA) spline followed by a column-wise softmax. The CPA representation yields a closed-form expression for the Jacobian of the inverse mapping, so LES = log √det(Σᵢ (Aᵢ†)ᵀ Bᵢᵀ Bᵢ Aᵢ†), where Aᵢ† is the Moore-Penrose inverse of the CPA slope matrix and Bᵢ encodes the softmax Jacobian. A key computational trick exploits the inverse function theorem to replace the pseudo-inverse with a product of the decoder Jacobian and the softmax derivative, avoiding explicit matrix inversion and eliminating the need for the decoder Hessian. To integrate LES into LSO, the authors use a normalized gradient-ascent update that separately normalizes the acquisition-function gradient and the LES gradient before combining them with weight λ, preventing the typically much larger LES gradient from destabilizing optimization. The score is fully differentiable, parallelizable across a batch, and up to 85% faster than the Bayesian uncertainty baseline.

## Results

Across 30 LSO experiments (22 VAEs × 5 benchmark tasks), LES achieves the best average rank of 2.15 (Top-1) and 1.9 (Top-20), and is within one standard deviation of the best method in 19/30 and 22/30 runs respectively. For valid-solution rate, LES improves over non-regularized gradient ascent by 7%, over TuRBO by 24%, and over LSO(L-BFGS) by 36% on average. In the AUROC evaluation for identifying valid latent-space regions, LES scores 0.93 on average (18/22 VAEs best), outperforming the Bayesian uncertainty score (0.88) and the likelihood score (0.91), while being up to 85% faster to compute than the uncertainty score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LES outperforms all six baselines on both average rank and frequency of being within one standard deviation of the best. Against TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019), LES achieves an average rank of 2.15 vs. 5.7 (Top-1) and 1.9 vs. 6.1 (Top-20), with TuRBO never reaching within one standard deviation of the best in any of the 30 experiments. Against the Uncertainty Censoring method of Notin et al. (2021), LES improves the average rank from 4.17 to 2.15 (Top-1) and increases the within-1-std count from 5 to 19; however, UC achieves a marginally higher valid-solution rate (2% more on average), though this does not translate to better objective values. Against the default BoTorch L-BFGS approach (Balandat et al., 2020), LES dominates with an average rank of 2.15 vs. 6.17 (Top-1) and a valid-solution rate 36% higher. The Likelihood score (a simpler single-forward-pass baseline) is the closest competitor, achieving rank 2.76 vs. 2.15, but LES still wins in 19/30 vs. 16/30 within-1-std runs and is more robust across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Latent Space Optimization (LSO) framework of Gomez-Bombarelli et al. (2018) by introducing a differentiable regularization score derived from the change-of-variables formula applied to the Continuous Piecewise Affine (CPA) representation of VAE decoders, building on the CPA spline theory of Balestriero & Baraniuk (2018) and Humayun et al. (2022). The key departure from the closest prior work (Notin et al., 2021, who regularize LSO with Bayesian decoder uncertainty) is that LES is differentiable, requires no ensemble of models or Monte Carlo sampling, and can be computed in a single forward pass with automatic differentiation. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the Jacobian determinant of the decoder transformation—previously used in normalizing flows and density estimation—as a practical constraint for acquisition function optimization in discrete-sequence VAEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LES provides a drop-in, differentiable regularization for LSO that works with any pretrained VAE decoder (GRU, LSTM, Transformer) without architectural changes or retraining, addressing a practical bottleneck in molecular and expression optimization pipelines. The main limitation is the cubic scaling of the determinant computation with latent dimension, which becomes a bottleneck for large models (e.g., the 256-dim pretrained SELFIES VAE takes 7.4s per batch vs. 0.5s for smaller models), and the bijectivity assumption, while empirically validated, is not formally guaranteed. Open questions include developing fast approximations for the change-of-variables term to enable LES in large-scale generative models, and whether the single hyperparameter λ can be automatically tuned rather than set per-dataset.
