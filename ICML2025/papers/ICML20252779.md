## Problem

Classical contextual bandits assume the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA), where each unit's reward depends only on its own action. In real-world settings like advertising, pandemic response, and social networks, one unit's action affects others' rewards (interference), yet no prior work addresses this within the contextual bandits framework where agents evolve over time with changing contextual information.

## Input / Output

The method takes d-dimensional contextual feature vectors X_ti for each unit, a known normalized interference matrix W_t (N_t × N_t with entries in [-1,1] and unit diagonal), and the actions chosen by all units in the current round. It produces per-unit arm selections from K arms, a dK-dimensional regression coefficient vector β, and a doubly robust estimate of the optimal value function V^π*. Unusually, the reward model consumes a transformed covariate X̃_ti that is a linear combination of all units' covariates weighted by W_t and gated by the actions of every unit in the round, rather than relying solely on the individual unit's own features.

## Method

The paper models each unit's reward as a linear combination of a per-arm linear payoff function over all units' covariates, weighted by the interference matrix: R_ti = Σ_j W_t,ij · f(X_tj, A_tj) + η_ti. A key algebraic insight shows the cumulative expected reward decomposes into Σ_i ω_ti · f(X_ti, A_ti), where ω_ti = Σ_j W_t,ji is the interference weight, so the optimal action depends only on individual covariates and the sign of ω_ti. Three algorithms (LinEGWI, LinUCBWI, LinTSWI) extend classical exploration strategies using transformed covariates that encode the interference structure, with a clipping mechanism (rate p_t) that enforces minimum exploration to guarantee estimation consistency. The theory establishes sublinear regret O(√N̄_T log N̄_T) matching the minimax rate without interference, asymptotic normality of both β and the optimal value function via a specialized martingale difference sequence, and a doubly robust estimator for V^π* with explicit asymptotic variance.

## Results

The proposed LinCBWI algorithms (LinEGWI, LinUCBWI, LinTSWI) achieve a sublinear regret bound of O(√N̄_T log N̄_T), matching the minimax optimal rate of classical contextual bandits even in the presence of interference. Coverage probabilities for both the OLS estimator β̂ and the doubly robust optimal value estimator V̂^DR consistently hover around 95% across 1000 replications, validating the asymptotic normality results. On the MovieLens-1M synthetic evaluation, LinCBWI algorithms consistently outperform classical LinCB baselines in average reward under both reward generating processes (RGP1 and RGP2), with the gap to the oracle model disappearing under the linear RGP2. Computational overhead is modest: LinCBWI runs in O(d²K²N̄_T) versus O(d²KN̄_T) for classical LinCB, with actual runtimes remaining within a few seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LinCBWI (in its three variants: LinEGWI, LinUCBWI, LinTSWI) against classical linear contextual bandit algorithms (LinEG, LinUCB, LinTS) that ignore interference. When interference is present, all three LinCBWI variants achieve significantly lower average regret at a faster convergence rate than their classical counterparts (Figure 2). In the absence of interference (W_t = I), LinCBWI reduces to classical LinCB and delivers comparable performance, confirming graceful degradation. Against an oracle model that knows the true β, LinCBWI approaches optimal performance under the linear reward model (RGP2) but shows a small gap under the non-linear RGP1, attributable to model misspecification. In the sensitivity analysis, even with severe interference matrix misspecification (b=1 perturbation on a [-1,1]-normalized matrix), LinCBWI maintains near-zero average regret, outperforming classical LinCB. On MovieLens-1M data, LinCBWI consistently outperforms classical contextual bandits in average rating under both interference structures, validating the practical benefit of accounting for interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical linear contextual bandit (LinCB) framework to account for interference between units, drawing on the doubly robust policy evaluation machinery of Ye et al. (2023) and the online OLS tail-bound technique of Bastani & Bayati (2020), both adapted to handle the dependency structure induced by an interference matrix. It departs from the multi-agent bandits (MAMAB) literature (Verstraeten et al., Bargiacchi et al., Dubey et al., Agarwal et al., Jia et al.), which assumes a fixed set of agents and homogeneous actions, by allowing units to evolve over time with heterogeneous contextual features. It also differs from single-stage causal inference work on interference (Su et al., Leung, Viviano) by embedding the reward model within a sequential exploration-exploitation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the minimax-optimal regret rate O(√N_T log N_T) is preserved even under interference, and provides the first asymptotic normality results for both the OLS estimator and the optimal value function in an online setting with within-round dependencies. The novel clipping-rate mechanism that balances statistical consistency against regret minimization offers a practical design principle for any online learner where exploration must be maintained for valid inference. Open questions include deriving a matching regret lower bound under interference, handling unknown or learnable interference matrices (e.g., via low-rank factorization), and extending the linear payoff assumption to nonlinear models such as neural networks.
