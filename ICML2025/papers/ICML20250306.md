## Problem

The properties of a tokenizer's latent space that enable effective diffusion model training and generation remain under-explored. While VAEs have been the default tokenizer due to their smooth latent distributions, it is unclear whether variational constraints are actually necessary or whether a more discriminative, structured latent space from a plain autoencoder could suffice. This paper establishes that fewer Gaussian Mixture modes in the latent space directly reduce diffusion training loss and improve generation quality, both empirically and theoretically.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encodes 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images into a compact set of 128 latent tokens of dimension 32 (total 4096 values), far fewer than the 256–4096 tokens used by prior continuous tokenizers. For conditional generation, a class label from ImageNet's 1000 classes conditions the diffusion model, which operates on these 128 tokens with patch size 1 and 1D positional embeddings. The system outputs reconstructed images (for the tokenizer) or novel 256×256 / 512×512 images (for the diffusion model), with the 128-token bottleneck enabling 76× faster training and 31× higher inference throughput compared to 1024-token baselines.

## Method

MAETok is a plain 1D autoencoder (no KL or variational loss) with ViT-Base encoder and decoder that uses learnable latent tokens to aggregate image information into 128 codes. The key training innovation is mask modeling: 40–60% of image patch tokens are randomly masked at the encoder, and auxiliary shallow decoders (3 layers each) predict multiple proxy features—HOG, DINOv2, CLIP embeddings, and optionally BPE text indices—of the masked tokens from the unmasked ones, driving the encoder to learn a discriminative latent space with fewer GMM modes. A critical decoupling step follows: after mask-modeling training, the encoder is frozen to preserve its well-organized latent structure while only the pixel decoder is fine-tuned (with mask ratio linearly annealed to 0) to recover high reconstruction fidelity. Theoretically, the paper proves that for a K-mode GMM data distribution, DDPM requires O(K⁴) samples to achieve a given KL divergence, explaining why fewer modes yield lower diffusion loss and better generation under finite data.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MAETok+SiT-XL (675M params, 128 tokens, plain AE) achieves gFID 2.31 and IS 216.5 without CFG, and gFID 1.67 and IS 311.2 with CFG. On ImageNet 512×512, the same model achieves gFID 2.79 (w/o CFG) and 1.69 (w/ CFG), outperforming the 2B-parameter USiT-2B (gFID 1.72 w/ CFG) while using only 675M parameters. Scaling to MAETok+USiT-2B pushes 512×512 gFID to 1.65 (w/ CFG) and 1.72 (w/o CFG). The 128-token design yields 76× faster training and 31× higher inference throughput compared to 1024-token baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAETok+SiT-XL dramatically outperforms SiT-XL/2+REPA without CFG (gFID 2.31 vs. 8.30) and also with CFG (1.67 vs. 2.06), using only 128 tokens versus REPA's implicit 256-token VAE latent. At 512×512, MAETok+SiT-XL (675M) beats USiT-2B (2B params) on both w/o CFG (2.79 vs. 2.90) and w/ CFG (1.69 vs. 1.72) gFID, establishing a new SOTA. Against autoregressive baselines, MAETok+SiT-XL with CFG (1.67) is competitive with MaskBit (1.52), TexTok (1.46), and MAR-H (1.55), though these use 256 tokens or VAE-based tokenizers. Without CFG, MAETok+SiT-XL (2.31) slightly edges out MAR-H (2.35) and substantially beats LlamaGen-3B (no w/o CFG reported) and all VQ-based methods. The key differentiator is efficiency: MAETok uses 128 tokens (vs. 256–4096 for baselines) with a plain AE (no KL loss), enabling 76× faster training and 31× higher inference throughput while matching or exceeding generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAETok sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) the 1D tokenizer design with learnable latent tokens (TiTok, ImageFolder, SoftVQ-VAE), which the paper adopts as its architectural backbone, and (2) the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE, SimMIM, MaskFeat), which it repurposes not for pre-training a vision encoder but for learning a discriminative latent space inside a plain autoencoder. The key conceptual departure is from the VAE/representation-aligned VAE (REPA, VAVAE) lineage: rather than imposing a KL prior or aligning latents to a frozen teacher, MAETok argues that the *structure* of the latent distribution (fewer GMM modes, more separated clusters) is what governs diffusion training difficulty, and that mask modeling in a plain AE achieves this structure without any variational constraint. The theoretical analysis directly builds on Shah et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2023) for GMM sample-complexity bounds under the DDPM objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a plain AE with mask modeling outperforms VAEs and representation-aligned VAEs for diffusion generation challenges a widely held assumption that variational regularization is necessary for a 'good' latent space, and the O(K⁴) sample-complexity scaling with GMM modes gives a clean theoretical explanation. Practically, using only 128 tokens (vs. 1024+ for typical 512×512 tokenizers) yields 76× faster training and 31× higher inference throughput, which is a substantial efficiency win for large-scale generation. Open questions include whether the decoupling trick (freeze encoder, fine-tune decoder) generalizes to other architectures, why CFG tuning becomes unusually sensitive when the unconditional class already encodes semantics, and whether the GMM-mode analysis extends to non-Gaussian or higher-order latent structures beyond ImageNet-scale benchmarks.
