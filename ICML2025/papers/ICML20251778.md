## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses for LLMs either require a clean reference model or degrade generative quality, leaving a critical gap for practical deployment. Standard finetuning, pruning, and quantization fail to reliably eliminate backdoors embedded in generative LLMs, particularly under diverse attack strategies (sentiment steering, targeted refusal, code injection) and multi-trigger configurations. The paper addresses how to purge backdoors from LLMs using only a small clean dataset, without trigger knowledge or external reference models.

## Input / Output

CROW takes a backdoored LLM (fine-tuned via LoRA on a poisoned Alpaca subset of 500 instructions) and 100 clean Alpaca samples, along with two hyperparameters: perturbation magnitude ε (default 0.1) and consistency weighting factor α (task-dependent, e.g., 5.5 for sentiment steering, 11 for targeted refusal). The clean samples are embedded, adversarially perturbed at the embedding level, and fed through the full transformer stack to compute layer-wise hidden states. The output is a purified LLM whose parameters have been updated via LoRA fine-tuning, completing in under 4 minutes on a single A100-40GB GPU with no additional inference-time overhead.

## Method

CROW exploits the observation that backdoor triggers cause abrupt, layer-compounding disruptions in hidden-state transitions, while clean inputs produce smooth, near-isometric layer-to-layer mappings. The defense operates in two phases: (1) adversarial perturbation generation, where FGSM-style perturbations are applied to input embeddings using the gradient of a layer-wise cosine-similarity consistency loss, simulating trigger-induced instabilities; and (2) adversarial consistency training, where the model is fine-tuned to minimize a combined objective of standard language modeling loss plus α-weighted perturbed consistency loss computed over all consecutive layer pairs. Theoretically, this regularization constrains each layer's spectral norm (Lipschitz constant) near 1, capping the compounding amplification of small input perturbations across N layers so that trigger-induced deviations cannot grow exponentially in deeper layers. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requires no trigger patterns or clean reference model, and is validated across six attack types, five LLMs, and three task domains.

## Results

CROW reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) to below 5% across all evaluated settings, compared to 33–92% for undefended models. For example, on Llama-2-7B Sentiment Steering (BadNets), ASR drops from 65.00% to 0.53%, and on Llama-2-13B Targeted Refusal (BadNets), ASR drops from 91.50% to 2.50%. MT-Bench helpfulness scores are preserved or improved (e.g., Llama-2-7B Sentiment Steering average rises from 2.51 to 3.77), while pruning and quantization degrade scores to 2.21 and 2.37 respectively. The entire finetuning run completes in under 4 minutes on a single A100 GPU using only 100 clean samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROW outperforms all three standard baselines (finetuning, pruning, quantization) on ASR across every model–task–attack combination. Finetuning is the weakest baseline and sometimes *increases* ASR (e.g., 98.45% vs. 94.50% for BadNets Targeted Refusal on Llama-2-7B; 99.49% vs. 92.30% for BadNets Sentiment Steering on Mistral-7B), while CROW reduces the same cases to 19.63% and 0.96% respectively. Pruning and quantization also fail to reliably reduce ASR and frequently degrade MT-Bench helpfulness (e.g., pruning drops Llama-2-7B Sentiment Steering MT-Bench from 2.72 to 2.51, quantization to 2.33), whereas CROW improves it to 3.80. Against CleanGen (Li et al., 2024b), CROW achieves comparable or better ASR on Targeted Refusal (0.50–2.38% vs. 11–67% for CleanGen) and eliminates the need for a clean reference model at inference time, incurring only a one-time ~4-minute finetuning cost instead of per-query dual-model overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROW extends the layer-wise feature analysis (LFA) paradigm from Jebreel et al. (2023, 2024) and the Trigger-activated Change (TAC) concept from Zheng et al. (2022) by repurposing internal hidden-state divergence as a training-time regularization objective rather than an inference-time detection signal. The adversarial perturbation component draws directly on FGSM (Goodfellow et al., 2015), while the theoretical grounding in Lipschitz-constrained near-isometric transformations connects to Parseval networks (Cissé et al., 2017). Crucially, CROW departs from prior LLM backdoor defenses like CleanGen (Li et al., 2024b) and NAD (Li et al., 2021) that require a clean reference model at inference, instead eliminating backdoors in-model with only 100 clean samples and no trigger knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CROW offers a practically deployable defense (<4 min on a single A100, 100 clean samples, no reference model) that addresses a real gap: existing defenses either require clean reference models or degrade generative quality. The finding that standard clean finetuning can *increase* ASR (e.g., 94.5%→98.5% on Llama-2-7B Targeted Refusal) challenges the common assumption that clean finetuning is a safe default and highlights a subtle failure mode practitioners should be aware of. Open questions include robustness against adaptive adversaries who know about CROW's consistency constraint, applicability to model-replacement attacks, and whether the α hyperparameter can be made adaptive rather than task-specific.
