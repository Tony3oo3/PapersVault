## Problem

Current deep learning-based de novo peptide sequencing models each exhibit data-specific biases due to the heterogeneous noise distributions in MS/MS spectra, meaning no single model is universally optimal. While different models produce unique correct predictions on different spectra, no existing framework systematically leverages this complementarity. RankNovo addresses this gap by reframing de novo sequencing as a list-wise reranking problem over candidates from multiple base models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252022/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an MS/MS spectrum (filtered to m/z ∈ [50.5, 4500.0], truncated to top 300 peaks by intensity), precursor charge and mass, and a set of peptide sequence candidates (each ≤100 residues) generated by six base models via beam search (size 5). Candidates are padded to equal length and stacked into a Multiple Sequence Alignment matrix of shape [c, ℓ, d]. The model outputs a PMD scalar per candidate (from the CLS token) and a per-residue RMD vector, from which the best candidate is selected; both scores are trained with RMSE loss against ground-truth mass deviations.

## Method

RankNovo is a list-wise reranking framework that processes all peptide candidates simultaneously in a single forward pass, modeling them as a Multiple Sequence Alignment. The peptide track applies axial attention—alternating row-wise (within-candidate) and column-wise (across-candidates) self-attention—followed by cross-attention with spectrum features extracted by a separate Transformer encoder, achieving O(cℓ² + k²ℓ) complexity versus O(c²ℓ²) for full self-attention. The key architectural insight is that column-wise attention enables direct comparison of aligned residues across candidates, which is critical for discriminating sequences with only minor mass differences. Training supervision uses two novel mass-deviation metrics: PMD, a Needleman-Wunsch alignment score with mass-based substitution costs normalized by the expected gap penalty, and RMD, a per-residue prefix-mass deviation vector that captures b-ion-level structural differences. The joint loss is 0.5·L_PMD + 0.5·L_RMD (both RMSE), and the model (8 layers, 8 heads, d=512) is trained on 7 million spectra from MassIVE-KB where not all six base models agree on the correct answer.

## Results

On the 9-species-V1 benchmark, RankNovo achieves an average peptide recall of 0.660 and amino acid precision of 0.824, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ContraNovo (0.618 recall, 0.784 precision) by 4.3% and 4.1% respectively. On the 9-species-V2 benchmark, RankNovo reaches 0.781 peptide recall and 0.906 amino acid precision, outperforming its strongest base model ByNovo (0.758, 0.897) by 3.0% and 1.0%. The method also demonstrates zero-shot generalization: when trained on only the two weakest base models, peptide recall still rises from 0.586 (2 models) to 0.649 (6 models) as unseen models are added at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankNovo outperforms all six compared baselines on both peptide recall and amino acid precision across both benchmark datasets. Against the traditional dynamic-programming method PEAKS (Ma 2003), RankNovo improves peptide recall from 0.322 to 0.660 on 9-species-V1 — a 105% relative gain. The first deep learning method DeepNovo (Tran 2017) and the learning-to-rank approach PointNovo (Yang 2019) are substantially outperformed, with peptide recall gaps of 28.4 and 22.1 percentage points respectively. Among modern Transformer-based methods, Casanovo (Yilmaz 2022) achieves 0.448 recall, Casanovo V2 (Yilmaz 2023) reaches 0.567, and the previous SOTA ContraNovo (Jin 2024) attains 0.618 on 9-species-V1; RankNovo surpasses each by 21.2, 9.3, and 4.2 percentage points respectively. On 9-species-V2, RankNovo extends its lead over ContraNovo from 4.2 to 2.9 points in peptide recall (0.781 vs. 0.752) and over Casanovo V2 by 6.7 points (0.781 vs. 0.714), confirming consistent gains across dataset versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankNovo builds directly on ContraNovo's mass-centric peptide embedding (prefix/suffix mass encoding) and repurposes axial attention—previously established in protein structure prediction (AlphaFold2, MSA Transformer)—for the first time in peptide sequence modeling. Conceptually, it transplants the list-wise reranking paradigm from NLP (RankT5, BERT rerankers) into de novo peptide sequencing, but departs from NLP practice by replacing binary relevance labels with continuous mass-deviation metrics (PMD via Needleman-Wunsch alignment and RMD via b-ion prefix mass matching). The ensemble-of-weak-models philosophy echoes ensemble learning in general, but the specific formulation of treating multiple de novo model outputs as an MSA to be reranked is novel to this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RankNovo establishes that no single de novo sequencing model is uniformly best across all spectra, and that a learned meta-ranker can extract complementary strengths from diverse models—a paradigm shift from the single-model optimization that has dominated the field. The zero-shot generalization (trained on 2 models, applied to 6) suggests the reranker learns transferable principles of peptide-spectrum matching rather than memorizing model-specific biases. Key limitations include the proportional inference cost of collecting candidates from multiple base models, and the evaluation is confined to two benchmark datasets with a fixed set of base models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model ensembles, whether efficient candidate sampling (e.g., shared-weight models) can close the speed gap, and whether the PMD/RMD supervision transfers to other mass-spectrometry tasks.
