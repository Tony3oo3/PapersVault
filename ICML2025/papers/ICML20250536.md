## Problem

Existing in-context RL (ICRL) pretraining methods like Algorithm Distillation and DPT require either complete learning histories of RL algorithms or access to optimal action labels, making them impractical for real-world settings where only suboptimal historical trajectories are available. This paper addresses the harder problem of pretraining a transformer for ICRL using exclusively suboptimal behavioral data, where standard supervised pretraining degenerates into imitation learning and yields suboptimal policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250536/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At deployment, the pretrained GPT-2-based transformer receives a current state and a context dataset of offline trajectories (state, action, next-state, reward tuples) collected by an unknown suboptimal behavioral policy in an unseen task, and outputs an action distribution conditioned on both. The context trajectories may span multiple episodes and the task identity is never explicitly provided—the model must infer task-relevant information purely from the in-context sequence. For the advantage estimator sub-models, the input is a prefix of the trajectory (prior state-action pairs with discounted return-to-go as noisy labels), and the output is a scalar Q or V estimate.

## Method

DIT (Decision Importance Transformer) replaces DPT's supervised optimal-action prediction with a two-stage weighted pretraining procedure. First, two transformer sub-models (Q̂ and V̂) are trained in-context to estimate advantage functions A^b(s,a) = Q^b(s,a) − V^b(s) for state-action pairs in the pretraining data, using return-to-go as noisy regression targets plus Bellman-consistency regularization. Second, the main policy transformer is trained with a weighted maximum likelihood estimation (WMLE) loss where each transition is weighted by exp(Â(s,a)/η), upweighting high-advantage actions and downweighting low-advantage ones. Proposition 4.2 provides a policy improvement guarantee: the WMLE optimizer strictly outperforms the behavioral policy in expected cumulative reward when the advantage magnitudes are bounded. The key technical difficulty—estimating advantages without knowing which task each trajectory belongs to—is resolved by the in-context transformer architecture, which interpolates across trajectories from different tasks to produce per-trajectory advantage estimates.

## Results

On Linear Bandit problems (K=20, d=10, H=200), DIT achieves lower cumulative regret than theoretically optimal algorithms (UCB, Thompson Sampling) in the online setting, and lower suboptimality than TS and LCB in the offline setting with randomly generated behavioral policies. On Dark Room (10×10 grid, sparse reward), DIT initially lags behind DPT and AD in online testing but surpasses both within ~15 episodes and continues improving over 40 episodes. On Meta-World (reach-v2) and Half-Cheetah (velocity), DIT consistently outperforms PDT and BC across all online and offline testing scenarios, and outperforms AD despite using strictly less pretraining information (no learning histories). In the Miniworld ablation, DIT significantly outperforms unweighted BC, and in the online setting DIT gradually improves while a restricted DPT (oracle upper bound) struggles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIT outperforms Thompson Sampling and LCB in offline bandit evaluation (lower suboptimality) and outperforms UCB and TS in online bandit evaluation (lower cumulative regret), matching or exceeding theoretically optimal bandit algorithms without requiring task-specific tuning. In MDP environments, DIT outperforms SAC (trained from scratch) across Dark Room, Meta-World, and Half-Cheetah, demonstrating that in-context pretraining from suboptimal data yields better generalization than online RL from scratch. DIT consistently outperforms Algorithm Distillation (AD) in all MDP environments despite AD having access to complete RL learning histories, while DIT uses only suboptimal trajectories. DIT outperforms Prompt-DT (PDT) and Behavior Cloning (BC) in all MDP testing scenarios, with the gap over BC highlighting the effectiveness of the exponential reweighting. DIT is slightly outperformed by DPT in most settings, which is expected since DPT has access to optimal action labels during pretraining; however, the performance gap is small in Dark Room and Half-Cheetah, and in the Miniworld ablation DIT actually surpasses a restricted DPT in the online setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIT extends the Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT) framework by removing its reliance on optimal action labels, replacing them with in-trajectory state-action pairs weighted by an in-context advantage estimator. The weighted maximum likelihood objective is a direct generalization of advantage-weighted regression (Wang et al., 2018) from single-task offline RL to the multi-task ICRL setting, where the key technical challenge is estimating advantage functions in-context across unknown tasks without grouping trajectories by source. Unlike Algorithm Distillation, which requires complete RL learning histories, DIT operates on arbitrary suboptimal trajectories, making it applicable to the far more common scenario of historical data from non-expert policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for optimal action labels, DIT makes ICRL substantially more practical, as suboptimal trajectories from non-expert users are vastly easier to collect than expert demonstrations or oracle queries. The theoretical policy improvement guarantee (Proposition 4.2) provides a principled justification for the exponential reweighting, and the empirical results show DIT matching DPT performance across bandit and MDP benchmarks despite this data disadvantage. A key limitation is that DIT still requires behavioral policies with 'reasonable rewards'—it cannot recover near-optimal actions from purely random trajectories—leaving open the question of how close to random the data can be before the weighted pretraining framework breaks down.
