## Problem

In real-world crowdsourcing, each worker annotates only a small number of instances, making it difficult to accurately model individual worker characteristics. Existing worker-modeling-based label completion methods (e.g., WSLC) are therefore constrained by insufficient per-worker data, which misguides the completion process and limits downstream label aggregation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253015/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a highly sparse crowdsourced label matrix (85–90% missing entries, where -1 denotes unannotated) together with tabular instance attributes (nominal or numeric, 10–64 dimensions) as input. It produces a completed label matrix in which missing entries are filled with the most likely label each specific worker would have assigned, preserving per-worker annotation characteristics rather than converging all workers toward a common quality level. The completed matrix is then consumed by standard label aggregation methods (MV, GTIC, DEWSMV, MNLDP, AALI, LAGNN) to infer true instance labels.

## Method

TLLC constructs a source domain from high-confidence instances identified via confident learning (filtering instances whose initial aggregated-label confidence exceeds the class-average confidence) and per-worker target domains from each worker's annotated subset. A Siamese network with a small fully-connected encoder (128→64→2) is pretrained on the source domain using MSE loss on pairwise embedding distances, then fine-tuned separately on each worker's target domain via parameter-based transfer learning. For label completion, the transferred per-worker network computes embeddings for all instances; a missing label is filled with class c_q only if the instance's embedding distance to the c_q centroid is within that class's average intra-class distance and the worker has annotated more than 2Q instances. Theoretical analysis shows that source-domain filtering reduces the L¹ divergence term in the transfer learning generalization bound, and that the MSE-based Siamese training is robust to i.i.d. Gaussian label noise.

## Results

On the Income dataset (binary, 85% missing), TLLC improves aggregation accuracy across all six label aggregation methods by 3–10 percentage points over WSLC, with the largest gain for AALI (75.37% vs. 64.87%). On Leaves (multi-class, 88% missing), TLLC yields consistent 5–8 point improvements (e.g., MNLDP 69.79% vs. 64.58%, AALI 72.40% vs. 64.06%). On Music_genre (multi-class, 90% missing), TLLC remains competitive but does not clearly surpass WSLC (MNLDP 78.00% vs. 78.86%; LAGNN 78.57% vs. 77.86%), with no statistically significant difference in the Friedman/Nemenyi tests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is WSLC (Wu et al., 2024), the state-of-the-art worker-similarity-based label completion method. TLLC decisively outperforms WSLC on both Income and Leaves: on Income, every aggregation method gains at least 3 points (AALI benefits most, +10.5 points), and on Leaves the gains range from +3.4 to +8.3 points, with all differences confirmed significant by Friedman/Nemenyi post-hoc tests. On Music_genre, the advantage narrows to within 1 point and is not statistically significant; the authors attribute this to three adversarial workers who annotated many instances with very low quality, causing TLLC's per-worker fine-tuning to lock in their erroneous patterns. Overall, TLLC wins on 12 of 14 reported (dataset, method) pairs and ties or narrowly loses on the remaining two Music_genre entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work that introduces worker modeling into crowdsourcing label completion (Yang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024), which itself builds on earlier PMF- and tensor-based completion methods (Jung & Lease, 2012; Zhou & He, 2016). The key departure is the identification of a bottleneck—each worker annotates too few instances for reliable modeling—and the repurposing of homogeneous parameter-based transfer learning (pretrain-then-fine-tune Siamese networks) to overcome it. The source-domain construction draws on confident learning (Northcutt et al., 2021) to select high-confidence instances, while the per-worker fine-tuning step is analogous to standard domain adaptation but applied to individual annotators rather than data distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TLLC provides a theoretically grounded (three theorems bounding generalization error and noise robustness) and empirically validated recipe for improving label completion in highly sparse crowdsourced matrices, a common real-world setting. However, the paper honestly reveals a significant vulnerability: adversarial workers who annotate many instances with low quality can dominate the fine-tuning process and degrade performance, as seen on Music_genre. This opens an important question about how to make per-worker transfer learning robust to a small number of high-volume but unreliable annotators, which remains unresolved and is flagged as future work.
