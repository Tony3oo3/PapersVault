## Problem

CLIP aligns entire images with text captions via contrastive learning, but this global alignment is insufficient for multimodal LLMs that require fine-grained, region-level understanding for referring and grounding tasks. Two practical barriers prevent simply adding region-text supervision: no public region-text datasets exist at the scale (hundreds of millions to billions of images) needed for contrastive pre-training, and it remains under-explored how to jointly design the training objective, model architecture, and data pipeline for effective region-text alignment on top of CLIP.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 for ViT-B/16, 336×336 for ViT-L/14) and produces a standard CLIP image embedding for image-text tasks. For region-level tasks, it additionally accepts a spatial prompt (a bounding box encoded as four coordinates via sinusoidal positional encoding) or a text prompt (a regional caption encoded by the CLIP text encoder), and outputs a region-specific embedding extracted from the pre-pooling ViT features. A separate two-layer MLP head regresses bounding box coordinates from the text-conditioned region embedding for the grounding objective. The same ViT forward pass is reused across multiple prompts per image, so the primary computational overhead is the ViT encoding itself rather than the lightweight Prompter.

## Method

CLOC introduces the concept of promptable embeddings: the vision encoder produces image embeddings that can be transformed into region representations given spatial or textual hints. Architecturally, a lightweight one-layer single-head transformer encoder (the Prompter) takes the ViT image tokens (before CLIP's pooled projection) concatenated with positional encodings of the box coordinates and outputs a region embedding via a pooled projection layer. The training objective combines three losses: the original CLIP image-text contrastive loss, a symmetric region-text contrastive loss (analogous to CLIP but operating on region embeddings vs. regional captions, with M=4 regions sampled per image and similar-text negatives masked), and a grounding loss that regresses box coordinates from a text-prompted region embedding. To support large-scale training, the VESL pseudo-labeling pipeline re-captions images with visually-enriched descriptions (VeCap), extracts region phrase candidates via NER, and localizes them with the OWLv2 open-vocabulary detector, yielding a 2-billion-image region-text dataset. The entire system is trained from scratch on ~14B image examples, and the resulting encoder serves as a drop-in replacement for CLIP in MLLMs, with the Prompter optionally substituting for more complex visual samplers during fine-tuning.

## Results

On zero-shot region-level tasks, CLOC (ViT-B/16) achieves 71.1% mAcc on COCO region recognition and 28.5% on LVIS, capabilities entirely absent in CLIP. On image-level tasks, CLOC matches or exceeds the in-house CLIP baseline (e.g., 69.2 vs. 66.3 COCO-i2t retrieval) while adding region-text alignment. On Ferret-Bench (referring/grounding VQA), CLOC L/14 with the Prompter improves average score from 50.7 (CLIP) to 56.9 (+6.2). On open-vocabulary detection, CLOC-L achieves 50.8/53.6/38.1 mAP on COCO-Det/ODinW/LVIS-Det, surpassing GLIP-L (49.8/52.1/37.3) despite using only two simple MLP heads instead of GLIP's DyHead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenAI-CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), CLOC is a strict superset: it matches or exceeds image-level performance (e.g., 69.2 vs. 52.4 COCO-i2t) while unlocking region-level tasks that CLIP cannot perform at all (71.1% mAcc on COCO region recognition). Against Shikra (Chen et al., 2023) on referring/grounding benchmarks (Table 5), FERRET with CLOC L/14 achieves 81.7 average across 13 evaluation sets, outperforming Shikra with OpenAI-CLIP L/14 (76.5) by 5.2 points. Against Ferret-v2 (Zhang et al., 2024), which also uses OpenAI-CLIP L/14, CLOC-equipped FERRET improves the average from 80.8 to 81.7 (+0.9). Against GLIP (Li et al., 2022) on open-vocabulary detection (Table 7), CLOC-L consistently wins on all three benchmarks (COCO-Det 50.8 vs. 49.8, ODinW 53.6 vs. 52.1, LVIS-Det 38.1 vs. 37.3) despite using only two simple MLP heads rather than GLIP's DyHead detector, indicating superior encoder representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOC extends CLIP's image-text contrastive pre-training by adding a region-text contrastive objective, drawing the promptable-embedding formulation from SAM's prompt-conditioned mask prediction and repurposing it for embedding extraction rather than segmentation. For data, it builds on the VESL pipeline that combines VeCap's visually-enriched re-captioning with OWLv2's open-vocabulary detection, departing from prior pseudo-labeling approaches (Minderer et al., 2024) that relied on noisy alt-text n-grams. Unlike RegionCLIP and UMG-CLIP, which target dense vision tasks (detection, segmentation) or fine-tune a pre-trained CLIP, CLOC trains from scratch with a lightweight Prompter designed specifically as a drop-in module for MLLM vision backbones, positioning itself at the intersection of contrastive VL pre-training and fine-grained spatial understanding for language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLOC provides a practical drop-in replacement for CLIP that unlocks zero-shot region-level recognition and retrieval while maintaining image-level performance, directly benefiting the large ecosystem of MLLMs (Ferret, LLaVA, etc.) that rely on CLIP as their vision backbone. The 2B-image region-text dataset and the VESL pseudo-labeling pipeline are significant community resources that address a previously acknowledged data bottleneck. Open questions include whether the promptable-embedding formulation generalizes to richer prompt types (masks, points, multi-modal prompts), whether the from-scratch training requirement can be relaxed, and how the approach interacts with emerging encoder architectures beyond ViT. The finding that fine-tuning from a standard CLIP checkpoint is suboptimal compared to joint training from scratch suggests that region-text alignment is not a simple add-on but requires co-optimization with the base encoder.
