## Problem

Stochastic localization (SL) has proven theoretical guarantees for sampling in continuous domains and for specific discrete models like the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, but no general framework exists for applying SL to arbitrary binary quadratic distributions (BQDs) using standard discrete MCMC samplers. The core difficulty is that continuous-domain proof techniques relying on log-concavity and Hessian analysis break down in the binary setting, where the quadratic term from the Gaussian likelihood collapses to a constant (since x²=1 for x∈{-1,1}^N), leaving only a modified linear term whose sampling properties are hard to analyze.

## Input / Output

The method takes a BQD parameterized by an interaction matrix W, a bias vector b, and inverse temperature β, along with a chosen discrete MCMC sampler (Glauber dynamics, Metropolis-Hastings, GWG, PAS, or DMALA) and SL hyperparameters (α schedule, noise level σ, number of SDE iterations T, and Monte Carlo sample size n per iteration). At each SL iteration, the algorithm uses the discrete MCMC sampler to draw n samples from the current posterior q_t^α(x|y) and computes their empirical mean as the posterior expectation. The final output is the rescaled observation Y_T/α(t_T), which is a sample from the target BQD. A key structural detail is that the posterior at each SL step differs from the target distribution only in its linear (external field) term, making the sampling problem a family of BQDs with growing field strength.

## Method

The paper proves that under the SL observation process Y_t = α(t)X + σB_t with α(t)/(σ√t)→∞, the effective external field h_t = b + α(t)Y_t/(σ²t) grows to infinity almost surely, and once |h| ≥ 2β sup_i Σ_{k≠i}|W_ik| (Condition 4.1), the posterior distributions satisfy Poincaré inequalities with high probability. The proof constructs the Dobrushin interdependence matrix for each discrete MCMC sampler's transition kernel, bounds its entries using the large-field condition to show the spectral radius is strictly less than 1, and then applies Wu's (2006) spectral-gap theorem to obtain explicit Poincaré constants. This yields polynomial-time mixing guarantees for Glauber dynamics, classical Metropolis chains, single-site gradient-informed MH algorithms (covering GWG and PAS), and the full-dimensional DULA/DMALA. The physical intuition is that a strong external field makes the quadratic interaction term negligible, reducing the posterior to an approximately product of independent Bernoullis, which is trivially fast to sample.

## Results

On Maximum Independent Set (MIS) benchmarks, SL-PAS achieves the best objective values across all five datasets, e.g., 104.531 on ER R-0.05 (vs. 104.375 for PAS) and 419.707 on SATLIB (vs. 419.539 for PAS). On Maximum Clique, SL-PAS reaches 87.649% of the DISCS baseline on RB (vs. 87.544% for PAS), while all methods achieve 100% on TWITTER. On Maximum Cut, SL variants consistently outperform their MCMC counterparts, with SL-PAS showing the largest gains of up to 0.045% on BA-512 and 0.038% on BA-1024. All comparisons use identical 10,000 MCMC steps, with SL allocating steps via exponential decay across iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three discrete MCMC samplers (GWG, PAS, DMALA) against their SL-augmented counterparts under identical 10,000 MCMC step budgets. SL consistently wins or ties on every dataset across all three problem types (MIS, MaxClique, MaxCut), with the most notable gains on sparse graphs and larger instances. SL-PAS is the strongest overall variant, outperforming PAS by 0.15% on ER R-0.05 (104.531 vs. 104.375) and by 0.045% on MaxCut BA-512 (100.928 vs. 100.883). SL-GWG shows the largest relative improvement on DMALA's weakest datasets, e.g., +0.531 on MIS ER R-0.05 (103.594 vs. 103.063). On the TWITTER MaxClique instance, all methods (with and without SL) achieve the optimal 100%, indicating the problem is already easy. The improvements, while modest in absolute terms (typically <0.5%), are consistent across all 14 datasets, supporting the theoretical prediction that SL's growing external field simplifies posterior sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic localization (SL) sampling framework from continuous domains (SLIPS, Grenioux et al. 2024) and model-specific discrete applications (SK models via TAP free energy, El Alaoui et al. 2022) to general binary quadratic distributions using off-the-shelf discrete MCMC samplers. The key conceptual departure is that the continuous mechanism—duality of log-concavity—breaks down in the binary case because x²=1 collapses the quadratic term, so the paper instead proves that the SL-constructed external field diverges, rendering the posterior approximately product-like, and establishes Poincaré inequalities via the Dobrushin interdependence matrix machinery of Wu (2006). The theoretical analysis unifies Glauber dynamics, classical Metropolis chains, and gradient-informed proposals (GWG, PAS, DMALA/DULA) under a single spectral-radius framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general theoretical guarantee that SL improves mixing for discrete MCMC on BQDs, covering arbitrary interaction matrices W and fields b without model-specific structure. The practical impact is currently modest—improvements over already-near-optimal baselines are typically under 1%—but the framework provides a principled recipe (exponential decay of MCMC steps across SL iterations) that could become more impactful as baselines weaken on harder instances. Open questions include extending beyond binary variables to multi-state discrete spaces, establishing convergence guarantees for the full SL process (not just the posterior sampling step), and applying the framework to unknown-form distributions such as deep energy-based models.
