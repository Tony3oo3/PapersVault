## Problem

While many techniques can detect that a distribution shift exists between two data sources, localizing which specific features originate the shift remains challenging. Existing localization methods are either inaccurate (statistical test-based approaches like MB-SM, KNN-KS) or computationally prohibitive at scale (DataFix's iterative random forest training takes up to 13 hours on high-dimensional genomic data), making them impractical for the large, high-dimensional datasets common in biomedicine, e-commerce, and multi-sensor applications.

## Input / Output

FSL-Net takes two tabular datasets of the same feature dimensionality d but potentially different sample sizes (N and M), with all features normalized to [0,1]. It produces a d-dimensional probability vector where each entry is the estimated probability that the corresponding feature is corrupted; the final shifted-feature set is obtained by thresholding at 0.5. The network handles variable dimensionalities (8 to 198,473 features) and sample sizes (1,444 to 70,000 samples) without retraining, requiring only a single forward pass at inference.

## Method

FSL-Net comprises two subnetworks: a Statistical Descriptor Network that extracts three statistical functional maps from each dataset (non-parametric measures like means, histograms, and CDFs; a shallow Moment Extraction Network using 1D convolutions with ReLU to capture higher-order moments; and a deep Neural Embedding Network of residual convolutional blocks with Tanh activations for richer representations), and a Prediction Network that combines the two datasets' descriptors via a normalized squared difference (resembling MMD) and applies residual blocks to output per-feature corruption probabilities. Mean pooling over samples provides sample-order invariance, while convolutions across features with random feature shuffling during training approximate feature-order equivariance. The network is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy plus an auxiliary locality-enforcing loss on 1,350 diverse datasets with 8 simulated manipulation types, enabling generalization to previously unseen datasets and shift types without retraining.

## Results

FSL-Net achieves the highest average F-1 score across all evaluated methods on 12 benchmark datasets (8 to 198,473 features), matching or exceeding DataFix in localization accuracy while being approximately 36× faster on average (up to 136× on the high-dimensional Phenotypes dataset). On CIFAR-10, FSL-Net reaches an F-1 of 0.9565 versus DataFix's 0.8911, with a mean runtime of 0.032 h versus 0.295 h. On COIL-100, FSL-Net achieves 0.9720 F-1 (vs. DataFix 0.9805) with a mean runtime of 0.49 h versus 2.92 h. FSL-Net also significantly outperforms MB-SM, MB-KS, KNN-KS, and Deep-SM, despite those methods having access to the ground-truth number of corrupted features |C|.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DataFix (the primary feature-shift-localization baseline), FSL-Net matches or slightly exceeds F-1 accuracy on most datasets while being 36× faster on average and up to 136× faster on the 1,227-feature Phenotypes dataset (2 min vs. 13 h). FSL-Net wins on manipulation E10 (KNN classifier) where DataFix fails to detect the shift, but loses on E9 (KNN regressor) and E4 (subtle constant noise) where DataFix performs better. Against the four conditional-testing methods from Kulinski et al. (MB-SM, MB-KS, KNN-KS, Deep-SM), FSL-Net significantly outperforms all four in both F-1 score and runtime, even though those methods are given the ground-truth |C|—a condition rarely available in practice. Feature-selection baselines (MI, SelectKBest, MRMR, Fast-CMIM) are far less accurate: SelectKBest and MI are fast but exhibit poor localization, while MRMR and Fast-CMIM struggle with scalability and fail entirely on correlation-shift manipulations (E3, E8). FSL-Net's advantage is most pronounced on high-dimensional datasets (Phenotypes, Founders, Canine), where it outperforms all competing methods and completes inference in minutes rather than hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSL-Net builds directly on the feature shift localization task formalized by Kulinski et al. (2020) and Barrabés et al. (2023, DataFix), adopting their problem definition and evaluation benchmark. Conceptually, it departs from DataFix's iterative random-forest-based heuristic and from Kulinski et al.'s conditional hypothesis-testing methods (which require knowing the number of corrupted features) by replacing them with a single end-to-end trained neural network. The architecture draws on Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., 2017) for sample-order invariance and on MMD-inspired statistical descriptors (Gretton et al., 2012) for distribution comparison, while the Moment Extraction and Neural Embedding subnetworks learn generalized moments and richer representations via convolutional residual blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 36× average speedup over DataFix (up to 136× on the 1,227-feature Phenotypes dataset) makes feature shift localization practical for high-dimensional biomedical and e-commerce datasets where iterative methods are infeasible. The ability to generalize to previously unseen datasets and shift types without retraining addresses a key scalability bottleneck in data-centric AI pipelines. Limitations include difficulty detecting minimal perturbations (manipulations E4, E9) and the assumption that not all features are simultaneously shifted, which constrains applicability to scenarios where a fully corrupted query is indistinguishable from a naturally different distribution. Open questions include robustness to truly novel shift mechanisms beyond the 10 evaluation types, performance under extreme class imbalance in corrupted features, and whether the fixed threshold of 0.5 can be adapted per-dataset.
