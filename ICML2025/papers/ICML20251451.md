## Problem

Scaling Fourier Neural Operators to handle complex PDEs requires increasing the number of Fourier modes K, which inflates the kernel integral operator's parameter count as O(K^d) and makes direct hyperparameter tuning computationally infeasible at billion-parameter scales. Under standard parametrization, the optimal learning rate and other hyperparameters shift as K changes, so naively transferring configurations from small proxy models to large FNOs yields suboptimal performance. The paper asks whether a principled parametrization exists that stabilizes the hyperparameter landscape across Fourier mode scales.

## Input / Output

The μTransfer-FNO algorithm takes a hyperparameter search space Ξ, a small proxy FNO with K_proxy Fourier modes, and a target FNO with K* Fourier modes, and outputs a rescaled hyperparameter configuration (learning rate and initialization variance for the kernel integral operator R) ready for target-model training. The underlying FNO consumes discretized PDE fields (e.g., initial conditions on 1024-point 1D grids, 61×61 2D grids, or 64×64 2D spatial grids with 512 temporal steps) and produces solution fields at the same resolution. Notably, the derived scaling depends only on the PDE dimensionality d and the number of Fourier modes K, not on the spatial discretization N_j, preserving FNO's resolution-agnostic property.

## Method

The paper derives the first Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) for the FNO kernel integral operator by showing its spectral norm equals the maximum absolute value of K^d i.i.d. sub-Gaussian entries, which concentrates at Θ(b(K)√(d log K)) rather than the Θ(b(K)√m) or Θ(b(K)√L) scalings seen for width/depth in prior μP work. This yields the scaling b(K) = c(K) = Θ(1/√(d log K)) for initialization variance and Adam learning rate of R. The μTransfer-FNO algorithm (Algorithm 1) sweeps hyperparameters on a small proxy model, then rescales the learning rate by √(log K_proxy / log K*) and the initialization variance by (log K_proxy / log K*) before training the target model, enabling zero-shot transfer. The approach is validated on Burgers', Darcy Flow, and Navier-Stokes equations, and extends to Physics-Informed Neural Operators (PINO) and additional hyperparameters (batch size, Adam β₂), achieving lower test error on Navier-Stokes with only 0.30× the training compute of direct tuning.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes Equation (FNO-3D, ~1B parameters), μTransfer-FNO achieves a 5.34% L² relative test error using only 0.30× the total training compute of directly tuning the full model (5.69% error at 1× cost). On Darcy Flow (FNO-2D), μTransfer-FNO achieves 1.22% L² relative error versus 1.25% for direct tuning, at a 1.38× training cost overhead (attributed to the smaller cost gap between small and large models in this setting). The method also stabilizes the optimal learning rate across Fourier mode counts K (e.g., ~4.2×10⁻³ for FNO-2D and ~5.6×10⁻³ for PINO-2D), whereas under standard parametrization the optimal LR shifts substantially (e.g., from 1.8×10⁻³ at K=3 to 7.4×10⁻⁴ at K=24 for FNO-3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is naive direct hyperparameter tuning on the target (large) FNO model. On the Navier-Stokes Equation, μTransfer-FNO wins on both accuracy (5.34% vs. 5.69% L² relative error) and efficiency (0.30× training compute), because sweeping hyperparameters on a small proxy model allows a larger search space. On Darcy Flow, μTransfer-FNO also achieves slightly lower error (1.22% vs. 1.25%) but at a 1.38× training cost, since the computational gap between small and large models is modest in this 2D setting. The paper also compares standard parametrization versus μTransfer-FNO across learning rate, batch size, and Adam β₂ sweeps: under standard parametrization the optimal hyperparameters shift with K, while μTransfer-FNO keeps them stable (optimal batch size = 20, optimal β₂ = 0.98 across all K values). The method is further validated on Physics-Informed Neural Operators (PINO), where it similarly stabilizes the learning rate landscape, confirming generality beyond vanilla supervised FNO training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) framework from Yang & Hu (2021) and the μTransfer zero-shot hyperparameter transfer technique from Yang et al. (2022) to Fourier Neural Operators, specifically for scaling the number of Fourier modes K. While prior μP derivations for MLPs and Transformers rely on central limit theorem arguments over averages of random variables (yielding Θ(1/m) width or Θ(1/√L) depth scaling), the kernel integral operator in FNOs introduces a fundamentally different structure: its spectral norm is governed by the maximum of K^d sub-Gaussian random variables, producing the novel Θ(1/√(d log K)) scaling rate. The paper also demonstrates applicability to Physics-Informed Neural Operators (PINO), extending the transfer beyond standard supervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone scaling FNOs to billion-parameter regimes: hyperparameters tuned on small proxy models can be directly transferred without costly re-tuning, reducing training compute by up to 70% on Navier-Stokes while improving accuracy. The theoretical result enriches the μP framework with a new scaling regime tied to the spectral structure of the kernel integral operator, and the scaling rate's independence from discretization resolution preserves FNO's resolution-agnostic nature. A key limitation is that the result is specific to FNOs; extending to other neural operator variants (GNO, DeepONet, multiwavelet NO) or to joint scaling of K with width/depth remains open.
