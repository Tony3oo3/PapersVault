## Problem

While representation learning and clustering each contribute to unsupervised anomaly detection, their interdependencies remain under-explored due to the absence of a unified theoretical framework. Existing methods either address pairwise interactions in isolation or merely superimpose separate objectives without a guiding principle, preventing the mutual enhancement of these three components for a unified detection goal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} along with a user-specified outlier ratio l (default 1%) and cluster count K (default 10). It produces two forms of anomaly scores per sample: a scalar score o_i (inverse of the mixture generative probability) and a vector-sum score o_i^V that additionally encodes directional information between each sample and all K cluster prototypes in the learned d=128-dimensional embedding space. Cluster posterior probabilities τ_ik are also available as soft assignments.

## Method

UniCAD unifies representation learning, clustering, and anomaly detection by maximizing an anomaly-aware data likelihood built on a Student's-t mixture model (ν=1 for robustness) in a learned representation space. A learnable indicator function δ(x_i) is embedded in the likelihood to explicitly down-weight anomalous samples during both representation learning and cluster parameter estimation, breaking the circular dependency among the three tasks. The anomaly score is theoretically derived as the inverse of the generative probability, and a gravity-inspired vector-sum variant aggregates the directional 'forces' from all K clusters to capture subtle inter-cluster distinctions that scalar summation misses. Training alternates between SGD updates of the 2-layer MLP autoencoder (with a reconstruction loss to prevent shortcut solutions) and EM updates of the mixture parameters, with top-l outliers removed from the training set at each outer iteration.

## Results

On 30 tabular datasets from ADBench, UniCAD with the vector-sum anomaly score (o_i^V) achieves the best average AUC-ROC rank of 2.6, followed by the scalar score (o_i) at 3.7, outperforming all 17 baselines. The AUC-PR average rank is 4.74 (vector) and 6.31 (scalar). The vector-sum formulation consistently outperforms the scalar formulation, confirming that directional information across multiple clusters improves anomaly discrimination. On the AUC-PR metric, UniCAD (Vector) also achieves the top average rank of 4.74 versus 6.31 for the scalar variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCAD (Vector) achieves the best average AUC-ROC rank of 2.6 across 30 datasets, substantially ahead of the next-best baselines: IForest (5.1), CBLOF (5.7), DIF (5.8), and KMeans-- (6.0). On AUC-PR, UniCAD (Vector) also leads with rank 4.74, followed by IForest (7.29) and HBOS (8.19). UniCAD shows particularly large margins over cluster-based methods on datasets like wine (95.25 vs. 82.18 for DCOD, 27.36 for KMeans--) and Stamps (94.18 vs. 93.41 for DCOD), while it is slightly outperformed by DIF on backdoor (89.24 vs. 92.87) and by ECOD on breastw (98.56 vs. 99.17). The vector-sum score consistently outperforms the scalar score (rank 2.6 vs. 3.7 on AUC-ROC), validating the gravity-inspired directional aggregation. Among probability-based baselines, DAGMM (rank 8.7) and ECOD (6.4) are notably weaker, while representation-based DIF (5.8) and DeepSVDD (15.48) show highly variable performance across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCAD extends the deep clustering + anomaly detection paradigm established by DCOD (Song et al., 2021) and the autoencoder + mixture model approach of DAGMM (Zong et al., 2018), but departs from both by providing a single probabilistic objective—anomaly-aware data likelihood—that jointly governs representation learning, clustering, and anomaly scoring rather than superimposing separate losses. The EM updates for the Student's t mixture model are inherited from Peel & McLachlan (2000), while the anomaly indicator function δ(x_i) that attenuates outlier influence on the likelihood is a new mechanism not present in prior mixture-model anomaly detectors. The gravity-inspired vector-sum scoring (Eq. 9) is a novel re-interpretation of the standard scalar likelihood score, drawing an analogy to Newton's law of universal gravitation to justify aggregating per-cluster 'forces' as vectors rather than magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified likelihood framework offers a principled answer to the long-standing question of how representation learning, clustering, and anomaly detection should interact, replacing ad-hoc objective superposition with a single optimization target. However, the framework is validated exclusively on 30 tabular datasets with fixed hyperparameters (k=10, l=1%), leaving open whether the anomaly-aware likelihood and vector-sum scoring transfer to high-dimensional modalities (images, graphs, time series) where representation learning is most critical. The gravity analogy, while creative, remains largely metaphorical—the vector sum's advantage over the scalar sum is consistent but modest (e.g., average rank 2.6 vs. 3.7), and the paper does not provide a formal guarantee that vector aggregation is optimal. Future work could explore learned degrees of freedom, adaptive cluster counts, and extensions to non-tabular data.
