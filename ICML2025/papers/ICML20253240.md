## Problem

NTP-based LLMs struggle to accurately retrieve key information from long contexts, while MLM-based models excel at retrieval but fail at coherent generation. Prior attempts to unify both objectives rely on complex multi-objective pipelines or encoder-decoder architectures that are cumbersome to scale. The paper asks whether the retrieval benefits of masking can be injected into standard NTP without any architectural or computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253240/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text sequence and randomly replaces a fixed fraction of tokens (15% in pre-training, 10% in fine-tuning) with a [MASK] token before feeding it to a decoder-only Transformer. In fine-tuning, the input is duplicated: the original sequence is concatenated with its masked copy, and cross-entropy loss is computed only on masked tokens within the answer span. The output is identical to standard NTP — autoregressive next-token predictions — so no inference-time changes are required.

## Method

MEAP randomly masks a small fraction of input tokens and then performs standard causal next-token prediction on the masked sequence using an unmodified decoder-only Transformer, requiring no bidirectional attention or encoder-decoder architecture. The key mechanism is that masked tokens receive negligible attention scores, which implicitly penalizes attention at those positions and amplifies attention differences among the remaining non-masked tokens, producing a more distinguishable attention distribution. This sharper attention allows the model to concentrate on task-relevant signals while suppressing peripheral context, improving retrieval and long-context reasoning. For fine-tuning, the training sample is duplicated and masking is applied only to the copy, with loss restricted to masked answer tokens, preserving the original context intact. The approach incurs zero additional overhead during pre-training or inference and is fully compatible with existing LLM training pipelines.

## Results

On Needle-in-a-Haystack (32K context), MEAP achieves 98.2% accuracy at 200B training tokens versus 87.1% for NTP, and critically reaches 85.8% at just 60B tokens where NTP requires 200B for comparable performance. On Multi-Document QA (20 documents), MEAP yields up to +27.2 percentage points improvement over NTP at position 20. For commonsense reasoning (zero-shot, 200B tokens), MEAP scores 47.8 average versus NTP's 46.2, and in fine-tuning (Llama-3-8B on Alpaca), MEAP achieves 68.42 average versus 67.30 for NTP, with an 11.77% average gain on the fine-tuned Multi-Document QA task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Next-Token Prediction (NTP) with a decoder-only Transformer. MEAP consistently outperforms NTP across all evaluated tasks: on Needle-in-a-Haystack, MEAP gains +14.3 to +33 percentage points depending on training scale (80.2% vs 65.9% at 40B; 98.2% vs 87.1% at 200B). On Multi-Document QA, MEAP improves by +7.0 to +30.6 percentage points across positions and document counts. On commonsense reasoning benchmarks, MEAP achieves a modest but consistent +1.6% (pre-training) and +1.12 (fine-tuning) average gain, with the largest improvements on ARC-c (+2.5 pre-training, +1.54 fine-tuning) and WinoGrande (+2.21 fine-tuning). Cross-model generalizability experiments confirm MEAP's advantage holds across Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-14B, and Mistral-7B architectures, with the largest cross-model gains on Multi-Document QA (e.g., +8.91 average for Llama-3.2-3B, +1.62 for Qwen2.5-14B). MEAP also reduces contextual hallucination rates across XSum, MultiNews, and WikiSum when evaluated with DeepSeek-V3, Qwen-Plus, and GPT-4o as judges.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEAP sits at the intersection of two well-established pre-training paradigms: next-token prediction (NTP) for decoder-only LLMs (GPT, LLaMA) and masked language modeling (MLM) from BERT and its successors. It departs from prior unified training approaches like UniLM and UL2, which required complex multi-objective pipelines and encoder-decoder architectures, by simply injecting random masks into the NTP input while retaining the standard causal-attention decoder. The conceptual lineage is thus NTP as the backbone with MLM's denoising signal grafted in at the data level rather than the architecture level, making it a minimal modification to the dominant autoregressive training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: MEAP requires no architectural changes, no bidirectional attention, and no extra inference cost, making it drop-in compatible with existing LLM training and serving infrastructure. However, the pre-training evidence is confined to 1.1B-parameter models, and the fine-tuning experiments use 8B or smaller models, leaving open whether the 3× data-efficiency gain on retrieval tasks (60B vs. 200B tokens for comparable Needle-in-a-Haystack accuracy) persists at the 70B+ scale where attention-sink and lost-in-the-middle effects are most acute. An open question is whether the fixed random masking ratio (10–15%) is optimal across all context lengths and task types, or whether adaptive or span-based strategies (briefly ablated) could yield further gains without the simplicity advantage.
