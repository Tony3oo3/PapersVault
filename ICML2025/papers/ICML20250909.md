## Problem

When allocating scarce intervention resources across spatial sites, the best-possible-reach (BPR) metric measures how well a model's top-K recommendation captures future events compared to an oracle. Two open problems remain: (1) how to assign per-site numerical scores from a probabilistic model to maximize BPR (the natural per-site mean is provably suboptimal), and (2) how to train model parameters to improve BPR when the discrete top-K selection yields zero gradients almost everywhere, blocking standard gradient descent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical non-negative event counts y_t ∈ R^S across S spatial sites over T time periods, along with site-level features (geographic coordinates, temporal lags, sociological vulnerability indices) and a fixed intervention budget K. It produces two outputs: a ranking vector r ∈ R^S computed via a Monte Carlo ratio estimator E[y/(1·y)] (not the per-site mean), from which the top-K sites are selected for intervention, and full probabilistic forecasts p_φ(y) for all sites. The ranking is stochastic, requiring M i.i.d. samples from the model to estimate the expectation, and the top-K selection is implemented via a differentiable perturbed TOPKMASK operator.

## Method

The paper contributes a ratio estimator r*(φ) = E[y/(1·y)] for site ranking, justified by a tighter upper bound on BPR than the per-site mean, and a training procedure called DAML (Decision-aware ML) that maximizes log-likelihood subject to a BPR ≥ ε constraint solved via the penalty method. To compute gradients through the discrete top-K selection, the method combines a score-function (REINFORCE) estimator for the Monte Carlo expectation in the ratio estimator with a perturbed-optimizer (stochastic smoothing) approach that adds Gaussian noise σz to the ranking vector and averages J perturbed TOPKMASK evaluations to obtain a tractable Jacobian. The overall gradient is a product of three estimators: ∇_φ r_t (score function), ∇_r b_t (perturbed top-K Jacobian), and ∇_b L_t (automatic differentiation). This constrained objective navigates the Pareto frontier between likelihood and BPR, delivering high-quality top-K decisions without the likelihood collapse seen in direct BPR optimization.

## Results

On Cook County IL (K=100), DAML achieves BPR-100 of 0.80 with MAE 1.12 and RMSE 1.84, improving BPR over NLL-only training (0.78) while preserving forecast accuracy. On Aransas TX Cranes (K=50), DAML reaches BPR-50 of 0.39 versus 0.38 for NLL-only. Direct BPR optimization achieves the highest BPR (0.82 Cook County, 0.41 Cranes) but produces catastrophic error metrics (MAE 70.31 / 40495), confirming that BPR alone destroys forecast quality. The Pareto frontier in Fig. 3 shows DAML occupying the top-right region (high BPR, high likelihood) across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EpiGNN (CASTNet), DAML wins decisively on BPR: 0.80 vs. 0.32 on Cook County and 0.39 vs. 0.06 on Cranes, though EpiGNN retains the best RMSE on Cranes (0.70). Against Perturbed Gradient (PG, Huang & Gupta 2024), DAML matches BPR on Cook County (both 0.80) but dominates on error metrics (MAE 1.12 vs. 5.65; RMSE 1.84 vs. 17.03), and also wins on Cranes BPR (0.39 vs. 0.35) with far better error scores. Against SPO+ (Elmachtoub & Grigas 2022), DAML achieves higher BPR on both datasets (0.80 vs. 0.74 Cook County; 0.39 vs. 0.18 Cranes) while maintaining comparable MAE/RMSE. The paper notes that PG and SPO+ struggled to improve beyond random initialization on the sparse Cranes dataset, suggesting their surrogate losses are less effective in high-sparsity regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-focused learning literature (Mandi et al., 2024; Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022) to the specific BPR metric for where-to-intervene decisions, building directly on the authors' prior introduction of BPR for overdose forecasting (Heuton et al., 2022, 2024). The gradient mechanism for the non-differentiable top-K operator is adapted from Cordonnier et al.'s (CVPR 2021) differentiable patch selection for images, which itself builds on perturbed optimizer theory (Berthet et al., 2020; Abernethy et al., 2016). The key departure from prior decision-aware methods (SPO+, PG) is the constrained optimization formulation (DAML) that enforces a minimum BPR threshold while preserving likelihood, rather than using surrogate losses or additive combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end framework for data-driven top-K resource allocation in public health and conservation, with the DAML objective giving stakeholders a tunable knob (ε) to balance forecast quality against decision quality. Key limitations include computational constraints (S > 2000, T > 20 push commodity GPUs to their limit), a single model family tested per task, and the assumption that larger event counts always indicate better intervention targets. Open questions include scaling to larger problems, incorporating additional constraints (e.g., rural/urban balance as in Marshall et al., 2022), and whether the ratio estimator's advantage over the per-site mean generalizes beyond the toy demonstration.
