## Problem

LLMs suffer significant performance degradation under distribution shifts (domain-specific terminology, linguistic diversity) in real-world deployment, yet existing adaptation methods are limited: fine-tuning requires labeled data, TTA relies on entropy minimization that ignores autoregressive dependencies, and TTT assumes access to training data. This paper asks whether LLMs can be effectively adapted at test time using only unlabeled test inputs, without labeled data, without access to training corpora, and without catastrophic forgetting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only unlabeled test inputs x (no ground-truth outputs y are available), processing them in batches through the frozen base LLM to compute input perplexity scores. The output is a set of LoRA adapter weights ΔΘ = BA (with B initialized to zero and A with random Gaussian initialization) that are added to the frozen base parameters Θ, yielding an adapted model f_{Θ+ΔΘ}. The sample selection score S(x) is computed via a forward pass only (no backpropagation), and samples below a perplexity threshold P₀ are excluded from gradient updates entirely, reducing the number of backward passes by ~5% in offline and ~70% in online settings.

## Method

TLM formulates test-time learning as input perplexity minimization: min_Θ P(x;Θ), justified theoretically via a first-order Taylor expansion showing that the cross-gradient term [∇_Θ log P(x;Θ)]ᵀ ∇_Θ log P(y|x) is non-negative for 98.75% of semantically aligned QA pairs, guaranteeing that reducing input perplexity also reduces output perplexity. A Sample Efficient Learning Strategy assigns each test sample a perplexity-based weight S(x) = λ·exp[log P(x;Θ) − log P₀]·𝕀{P(x;Θ) > P₀}, which zeros out low-perplexity samples (already well-modeled) and up-weights high-perplexity ones (more informative for adaptation), reducing unnecessary backpropagation. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, only LoRA parameters ΔΘ are updated (rather than full parameters), which the paper shows empirically preserves general knowledge (e.g., GSM8K accuracy) far better than full-parameter updates. The full objective is min_{ΔΘ} S(x)·P(x; Θ+ΔΘ), optimized with AdamW and greedy decoding (temperature 0).

## Results

On the DomainBench (R-Lsum metric), TLM achieves at least a 20% relative improvement over the original LLMs across all four vertical domains (Geography, Agriculture, Medicine, Finance) and all four LLMs tested. For example, on Llama3-8B-Instruct, TLM improves Geography from 0.2450 to 0.3212, Agriculture from 0.0834 to 0.1319, Medicine from 0.1265 to 0.2372, and Finance from 0.2329 to 0.3242. On InstructionBench (R-Lsum), TLM similarly outperforms baselines, e.g., Alpaca-GPT4 improves from 0.3752 to 0.4274 on Llama3-8B-Instruct. On ReasoningBench (Exact Match), TLM also leads, e.g., GSM8K improves from 0.7610 to 0.8074 on Llama3-8B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLM consistently and substantially outperforms all three TTA baselines (Tent, EATA, COME) across every LLM and dataset. Tent, which relies on entropy minimization, frequently degrades performance dramatically—for instance, on Llama3-8B-Instruct it drops Agriculture from 0.0834 to 0.0067 and Dolly from 0.3671 to 0.0036—confirming the paper's argument that entropy minimization is ill-suited for autoregressive LLMs. EATA is the strongest baseline in some settings (e.g., Llama2-13B Geography at 0.2800 vs. TLM's 0.2668 is the one case where EATA edges out TLM on a single domain), but TLM still wins on the majority of cells and by large margins on the others (e.g., Llama3-8B Medicine: TLM 0.2372 vs. EATA 0.0127). COME, which conservatively minimizes entropy, also suffers severe degradation in many cases (e.g., Llama3-8B Geography drops to 0.0048, Dolly to 0.0700), and TLM outperforms it in nearly all comparisons. Overall, TLM is the only method that reliably improves over the original LLM across all 28 evaluation cells (4 LLMs × 7 datasets), while the baselines often perform worse than no adaptation at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm (entropy minimization, as in Tent, EATA, COME) by arguing that entropy-based objectives overlook autoregressive dependencies in LLMs and can actively degrade performance. It repurposes input perplexity—a standard language-modeling metric—as a self-supervised test-time optimization objective, justified by an empirical correlation between input and output perplexity trends. Unlike Test-Time Training methods (Hardt & Sun, 2024; Hübotter et al., 2024) that require access to training data or a knowledge base, TLM operates solely on unlabeled test inputs, and unlike fine-tuning, it requires no labeled examples. The combination of perplexity-based sample selection with LoRA for parameter-efficient updates is a novel assembly of existing components rather than a departure from a single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TLM offers a practical, label-free recipe for adapting LLMs to domain shifts that avoids the retrieval overhead of TTT and the data requirements of fine-tuning, making it relevant for deployment scenarios where only unlabeled user queries are available. The demonstration that standard entropy minimization substantially harms LLM performance (e.g., Tent dropping from 0.245 to 0.078 on Geography) is a useful caution for the TTA community. However, the theoretical justification rests on an empirically verified but unproven cross-gradient non-negativity assumption, experiments are limited to models up to 13B parameters, and the online-setting results show smaller gains, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier-scale models or truly dynamic streaming scenarios.
