## Problem

Current AI safety moderation systems rely on black-box neural classifiers that directly map input content to harmfulness labels, making their decisions difficult to interpret and impossible to steer toward context-specific safety standards (e.g., stricter moderation for children vs. adults). No existing framework explicitly models the causal chain from an AI behavior through potential actions to downstream effects on stakeholders, nor does it provide a transparent mechanism for adjusting safety judgments to reflect different community values or regulatory requirements.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single user prompt as text input and produces a semi-structured harm-benefit tree enumerating stakeholders, harmful/beneficial actions per stakeholder, and effects per action, each annotated with likelihood (Low/Medium/High), extent/severity (Minor/Significant/Substantial/Major), and immediacy (Immediate/Downstream) labels. These features are then aggregated via a 28-parameter weighted sum into a scalar harmfulness score, which is thresholded through a sigmoid to yield a binary safe/unsafe classification. The 28 weight parameters are fully interpretable and can be re-optimized to align with different safety label distributions, enabling top-down policy adjustments or bottom-up community-value alignment without retraining the tree-generating models.

## Method

SAFETYANALYST decomposes prompt safety classification into two stages: (1) harm-benefit tree generation and (2) transparent weighted aggregation. For tree generation, frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Llama-3.1-70B/405B, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) are prompted with chain-of-thought instructions to produce 18.5 million structured features across 19k prompts; this capability is then distilled into two lightweight Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct specialist models (one for harms, one for benefits) via QLoRA supervised fine-tuning, including adversarial prompt augmentation from WildJailbreak for jailbreak robustness. For aggregation, a separate white-box model computes harmfulness as a triple sum over stakeholders, actions, and effects of γ · W_Action · W_Likelihood · W_Extent · W_Immediacy, where the 28 parameters encode the relative importance of 16 harmful action categories (from the AIR 2024 taxonomy), likelihood and extent ratios, and discount factors for downstream and beneficial effects. These weights are aligned by minimizing negative log-sigmoid loss on 500 held-out labeled prompts, and can be re-optimized for any target safety preference distribution without touching the tree-generating models.

## Results

SAFETYANALYST achieves an average F1 of 81.2 across six prompt safety benchmarks, outperforming all dedicated LLM moderation systems (best baseline: WildGuard at 71.7) while remaining within 0.4 points of zero-shot GPT-4 (81.6). On the largest benchmark, SORRY-Bench (9,450 prompts), SAFETYANALYST scores 75.4 F1 versus 58.2 for WildGuard and 59.1 for LlamaGuard-3. The student model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) matches teacher LMs on WildJailbreak classification (F1=88.8 vs. GPT-4o's 91.8), confirming that knowledge distillation preserves safety-relevant reasoning at a fraction of the compute cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFETYANALYST (avg. F1=81.2) substantially outperforms all dedicated moderation systems: WildGuard (71.7, +9.5), LlamaGuard-3 (64.6, +16.6), LlamaGuard-2 (62.9, +18.3), ShieldGemma-27B (40.6, +40.6), and OpenAI Moderation API (41.1, +40.1). The largest margins appear on SORRY-Bench (75.4 vs. 58.2 for WildGuard) and AIR-Bench (88.9 vs. 87.6 for WildGuard), where the harm-benefit tree's explicit reasoning over diverse risk categories provides an advantage. SAFETYANALYST is marginally below zero-shot GPT-4 (81.2 vs. 81.6), with GPT-4's edge driven primarily by its ability to decode Atbash/Caesar-ciphered prompts on SORRY-Bench (78.2 vs. 75.4). On adversarial WildGuardTest prompts, WildGuard (85.5) and GPT-4 (81.6) still outperform SAFETYANALYST (79.6), indicating room for improvement on obfuscated attacks. Among the distilled student models, the 8B student achieves F1=88.8 on WildJailbreak, comparable to GPT-4o (91.8) and Llama-3.1-70B (88.1), while requiring only 1 H100 GPU versus 4 for Llama-70B.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFETYANALYST departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of LLM content moderation (WildGuard, LlamaGuard, ShieldGemma, Aegis-Guard), which trains black-box classifiers to map prompts directly to safety labels. Instead, it decomposes the problem into interpretable sub-tasks: chain-of-thought generation of structured harm-benefit trees (informed by the AIR 2024 risk taxonomy and moral-philosophy-based effect taxonomies) followed by a transparent 28-parameter weighted aggregation grounded in cost-benefit analysis principles (Arrow et al., 1996). The knowledge distillation step draws on symbolic knowledge distillation (West et al., 2021) to transfer frontier-LLM reasoning into a lightweight open-weight student, while the steerability mechanism connects to the emerging pluralistic alignment literature (Sorensen et al., 2024b; Kirk et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical gap in AI safety: existing moderation systems cannot explain *why* a prompt is flagged or be re-tuned to different community values without expensive retraining. SAFETYANALYST's 28 interpretable weights enable both top-down policy alignment and bottom-up community-value alignment, making it a practical tool for context-sensitive deployment (e.g., stricter standards for children's applications). Key open questions include whether the cost-benefit aggregation adequately handles incommensurable values and non-quantifiable harms, whether pluralistic alignment generalizes across diverse populations, and whether the 30× inference-time overhead (6.12s vs. 0.22s) is acceptable in production settings. The released 18.5M-feature dataset and taxonomies of harmful/beneficial effects provide foundational resources for future work.
