## Problem

Self-Attributing Neural Networks (SANNs) guarantee faithful linear explanations but suffer severe performance trade-offs: per-feature SANNs (e.g., NAM) have provable error lower bounds that grow exponentially with dimension under feature correlation, while existing group-based SANNs (BagNet, FRESH) rely on fixed or limited group structures that cannot adapt to data-specific correlations. No prior framework learns feature groups end-to-end without explicit group supervision while remaining model-agnostic.

## Input / Output

SOP takes an input x (image patches for ViT, text tokens for BERT, or pixel values for CNN) and a frozen pre-trained backbone h. It produces m binary group masks (each retaining the top 20% of features via attention thresholding), m group-level predictions from the backbone on masked inputs, and m sparse contribution scores via sparsemax cross-attention. The final output is the linear aggregation f(x) = Σ c_i · h(g_i ⊙ x), which simultaneously serves as both the prediction and the faithful explanation, with the group masks and scores constituting the interpretable decomposition.

## Method

SOP is a model-agnostic framework with three trainable components wrapped around a frozen backbone: (1) a Group Generator Γ that applies multi-headed self-attention over feature embeddings and thresholds each attention distribution to the top τ=20% features, yielding m dynamic binary masks per input; (2) a Backbone Predictor h that is any pre-trained differentiable model, applied m times on Hadamard-masked inputs g_i ⊙ x; and (3) a Group Selector θ that uses sparse cross-attention (sparsemax) with class-weight-initialized queries to assign sparse contribution scores to groups. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with only final prediction labels—no group supervision—using a scaling factor in the loss to handle gradient flow through binarized masks. Theoretically, the paper proves that per-feature SANNs incur exponential total insertion/deletion error for correlated features (binomials/monomials), while group-based SANNs with groups aligned to polynomial terms achieve zero error, motivating the learnable-group design.

## Results

SOP achieves the lowest error across all three tasks: 0.267±0.017 on ImageNet-S-ViT (vs. 0.306 for the best baseline SHAP-F), 0.025±0.002 MSE on CosmoGrid-CNN (vs. 0.028 for SHAP-F), and 0.366±0.021 on MultiRC-BERT (vs. 0.386 for FRESH). On semantic coherence, SOP attains the best IOU on ImageNet-S (0.630±0.006) and the best purity on CosmoGrid (0.647±0.011), while tying FRESH on MultiRC IOU (0.176). SOP is the only model-agnostic SANN in the comparison, requiring 20× forward passes (same budget as perturbation-based baselines) while outperforming all 14 baselines on error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOP outperforms all 14 baselines on error/MSE across all three tasks. On ImageNet-S, SOP (0.267 err) beats the strongest post-hoc baseline SHAP-F (0.306) by ~4 points and the best dedicated SANN FRESH (0.537) by ~27 points; on CosmoGrid, SOP (0.025 MSE) edges out SHAP-F (0.028); on MultiRC, SOP (0.366 err) beats FRESH (0.386) and all post-hoc methods. On semantic coherence (IOU/purity), SOP achieves 0.630 on ImageNet-S, far exceeding FRESH (0.464), FG-F (0.511), and BagNet (0.314), and 0.647 purity on CosmoGrid versus the best baseline FG-F (0.529). On MultiRC, SOP ties FRESH on IOU (both 0.176) but with a tighter variance (±0.008 vs ±0.016). Notably, SOP is the only model-agnostic method in the table, meaning it can wrap any pre-trained backbone, whereas XDNN, BagNet, FRESH, and BCos-F require specific architectures. On faithfulness metrics (Table 3), SOP achieves the best insertion score (0.930) on ImageNet, substantially ahead of SHAP-F (0.878) and LIME-F (0.859), though it trails on deletion (0.109) where methods using fewer features are favored.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Self-Attributing Neural Network (SANN) lineage initiated by Alvarez Melis & Jaakkola (2018) and instantiated by NAM (per-feature), BagNet (fixed patches), and FRESH (single attention-selected subset). It departs from prior SANNs in two key ways: (1) it provides the first formal lower-bound proof showing per-feature SANNs are fundamentally limited by correlated features, and (2) it introduces a model-agnostic framework (SOP) that learns feature groups end-to-end via attention, removing the architectural constraints (conv layers, specific attention patterns) that prior SANNs imposed on the backbone. Unlike post-hoc attribution methods converted to SANNs, SOP's groups are trained jointly with the prediction objective without any group-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical lower bound gives a principled explanation for why all prior SANNs suffered performance trade-offs, and the SOP framework offers a practical path to close that gap while preserving the linear-decomposition interpretability guarantee. The model-agnostic design means practitioners can wrap existing pre-trained ViTs, BERTs, or CNNs without re-architecting, which is a significant practical advantage over BagNet or FRESH. Open questions include whether the 20× forward-pass cost is acceptable at scale, whether learned groups remain semantically meaningful in domains without ground-truth annotations, and whether the framework extends to multi-class or multi-task settings beyond the single-logit formulation tested here.
