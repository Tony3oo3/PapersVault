## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning of vision-language models adapts external encoders or bridging layers but overlooks transformer-level attention alignment within the LLM core, which is critical for precise word-to-region grounding. The paper identifies that diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) learn substantially more concentrated and accurate text-vision attention maps than VLMs optimized solely for next-token prediction, yet this visual expertise has not been directly transferred to VLMs during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a text answer, operating within standard VLM benchmarks. During training, the key additional input is a set of pre-computed per-word attention maps extracted offline from Stable Diffusion v1.4 via a short inversion process (~20 s/image on a single V100), which serve as fixed alignment targets. The VLM's own cross-attention (or self-attention) weights are aggregated across heads and layers into per-word saliency grids of shape (N_text × √N_patch × √N_patch), then passed through a lightweight Aligner network to produce a single-channel map comparable to the DM's attention.

## Method

Lavender (Language-and-Vision fine-tuning with Diffusion Aligner) augments standard SFT with an attention alignment loss: the VLM's per-word attention is projected through a 3-layer convolutional Aligner network and matched to Stable Diffusion's cross-attention maps via MSE, yielding a total loss L_total = L_VLM + λ·L_att. To avoid overwriting pretrained knowledge, the method introduces parallel cross-attention parameters (W_Qd, W_Kd, W_Vd) in roughly 1/5 of the layers, so the VLM learns a new attention channel for alignment while preserving its original attention. The DM's attention is pre-computed once offline on the training set (no gradient flow through the DM), and the VLM is fine-tuned with either LoRA or full fine-tuning, optionally pretraining only the Aligner first to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only 0.13M image-text pairs and 8 GPUs for one day of training, and is compatible with both cross-attention VLMs (OpenFlamingo, Llama-3.2-11B) and self-attention-only VLMs (MiniCPM-V-2.5).

## Results

On Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct fine-tuned with only 0.13M samples, Lavender with LoRA achieves up to 30% relative improvement over autoregressive fine-tuning on 19/20 benchmarks (e.g., DocVQA 90.3 vs 86.0, TextVQA 88.1 vs 81.1, MMBench 81.3 vs 73.6). On the severely out-of-distribution WorldMedQA-V medical benchmark, Lavender boosts Llama-3.2-11B by 68%, narrowing the gap with large closed-source models from 43% to 10%. On self-attention-only MiniCPM-V-2.5, Lavender improves 16/20 tasks by up to 4% while limiting drops to −1%. Across 16 benchmarks, Lavender-Llama-3.2-11B surpasses small budget-constrained open-source models (≤8B) by up to 50% and matches certain closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) on TextVQA, POPE, and RealWorldQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against small budget-constrained open-source models (≤8B), Lavender-Llama-3.2-11B (LoRA) dominates: it outperforms LLaVA-1.5-7B [Liu et al., 2024a] by 62 points on DocVQA (90.3 vs 28.1) and 30 points on TextVQA (88.1 vs 58.2); it beats LLaVA-NeXt-7B [Liu et al., 2024b] by 16 points on DocVQA and 23 on TextVQA; it surpasses Cambrian-1-7B [Tong et al., 2024a] by 42 points on DocVQA and 11 on TextVQA; and it edges out Eagle-X5-7B [Shi et al., 2024] by 3.7 on DocVQA and 16.2 on TextVQA, though Eagle slightly leads on POPE (89.3 vs 88.1). Against large closed-source models, Lavender-Llama-3.2-11B (LoRA) wins on TextVQA (88.1 vs 77.4 GPT-4o, 78.7 Gemini 1.5 Pro, 74.1 Claude-3.5 Sonnet), POPE (88.1 vs 85.6 GPT-4o, 73.6 Claude-3.5), and RealWorldQA (74.1 vs 75.4 GPT-4o, 64.1 Gemini, 60.1 Claude), but trails on MMMU (46.3 vs 69.1/62.2/68.3), HallusionBench (41.3 vs 55.0/55.9/49.9), and InfoVQA (65.1 vs 79.2/81.0/74.3). The paper notes that the gap versus data-heavy SOTA models (Qwen2-VL-7B, InternVL2-8B) is largely attributable to their 38×–384× larger fine-tuning datasets (5M–50M vs 0.13M), and that Lavender's low data overlap with benchmarks (Figure 13) supports genuine generalisation rather than memorisation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lavender extends the standard VLM supervised fine-tuning paradigm (LLaVA, Flamingo, Llama 3.2-Vision) by introducing a secondary attention-alignment loss that uses Stable Diffusion's cross-attention maps as a supervisory target, rather than relying solely on autoregressive next-token prediction. It departs from prior work that integrates diffusion models into VLMs for generation (Transfusion, Metamorph) or as visual encoders (Wang et al., 2024c) by treating the DM purely as a frozen attention oracle. The Aligner network draws on Squeeze-and-Excitation design, and the attention aggregation options include attention flow (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020), but the core contribution is the Bayesian framing of DM attention as a proxy for the optimal word-to-region posterior and its use as a regulariser during SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical appeal lies in its data and compute efficiency—0.13M samples on 8 GPUs for one day—making it accessible to groups without large-scale paired image-text corpora, and its model-agnostic design (cross-attention and self-attention VLMs) broadens applicability. The 68% OOD medical QA boost and the proposal of 'diffusion feedback' as a scalable alternative to human vision feedback in RL post-training open interesting directions. However, evaluation is limited to ≤0.13M training samples (38×–384× less than SOTA), a single SD version (v1.4), and the self-attention-only case (MiniCPM) shows only modest gains, leaving open whether the approach scales to 50M-sample regimes and higher-resolution diffusion models.
