## Problem

Regression adjustment for variance reduction in randomized experiments has been well studied for the average treatment effect under both simple random sampling and covariate-adaptive randomization (CAR), but its extension to distributional treatment effects (DTE) under CAR designs—such as stratified block randomization and Efron's biased-coin design—remains unexplored. Existing DTE estimation methods assume simple random sampling, while the only prior work on regression adjustment under CAR for distributional parameters (Jiang et al., 2023) is restricted to continuous outcomes and quantile treatment effects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular RCT data consisting of observed outcomes Y, treatment indicators W (supporting multiple arms), stratum indicators S, and a vector of additional pre-treatment covariates X, along with a user-specified supervised learning algorithm and a set of evaluation points y ∈ Y at which the distribution is assessed. It outputs the full distributional treatment effect curve Δ^{DTE}(y) and probability treatment effect (bin-probability differences) across all evaluation points, each accompanied by pointwise confidence intervals constructed via the asymptotic Gaussian process limit or multiplier bootstrap. The framework handles both continuous and discrete (or mixed) outcomes without modification.

## Method

The estimator treats the conditional distribution function F_{Y(w)}(y|S=s,X=x) as a binary regression target E[1{Y(w)≤y}|S,X] and estimates it with any off-the-shelf supervised learner (gradient boosting, random forests, neural networks, etc.) at each evaluation point y. The resulting AIPW estimator combines an inverse-propensity-weighted residual term with the ML-predicted conditional distribution, yielding a doubly-robust, Neyman-orthogonal moment condition that is first-order insensitive to nuisance estimation errors. Cross-fitting (L-fold) is applied to decouple estimation and evaluation. The paper proves that the estimator converges to a Gaussian process in L^∞(Y) under CAR, derives the semiparametric efficiency bound for DTE under CAR, and shows the estimator attains this bound—implying no regular estimator can achieve smaller asymptotic variance. This extends the standard i.i.d. asymptotic framework to accommodate the cross-sectional dependence in treatment assignments induced by CAR.

## Results

In simulation studies (n=1,000, 1,000 replications), ML regression adjustment (gradient boosting) achieves up to 50% RMSE reduction over the empirical estimator, with linear regression also yielding consistent gains; 95% CI coverage ranges from 0.96–0.97 (linear) and 0.97–0.99 (ML). On the Attanasio et al. (2015) Mongolian microcredit experiment (n=611), regression adjustment reduces standard errors by 1–13% across revenue levels (average 7%), and specifically cuts the SE for the probability of zero revenue by ~10%, yielding a statistically significant 10-percentage-point decrease (SE = 4.6 pp). The estimator is proven to attain the semiparametric efficiency bound for DTE under CAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct head-to-head baseline is the QTE regression-adjusted estimator of Jiang et al. (2023), which targets quantile treatment effects under CAR. On continuous outcomes, the proposed DTE method achieves 6.4–12.5% RMSE reduction across all quantiles, substantially outperforming Jiang et al.'s 0.4–6.5% (and negative at Q0.1). On discrete outcomes, the proposed method yields 0.9–6.1% RMSE reduction at every quantile, whereas Jiang et al.'s estimator actually increases RMSE (negative reduction) at 7 of 9 quantiles. Computationally, the proposed method is ~18× faster for continuous outcomes (0.008 s vs. 0.148 s) and ~9× faster for discrete outcomes (0.015 s vs. 0.145 s). The paper also implicitly compares against the unadjusted empirical (IPW) estimator, showing that both linear and ML regression adjustment strictly reduce variance, with ML adjustment delivering the largest gains (up to 50% RMSE reduction in simulations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regression-adjustment-under-CAR line of work (Rafi 2023; Cytrynbaum 2024; Wang et al. 2023), which has so far focused on the ATE, to the richer target of distributional treatment effects. Methodologically, it transplants the DML/AIPW machinery of Chernozhukov et al. (2018) and Robins et al. (1994) into the CAR asymptotic framework of Bugni et al. (2019), where treatment assignments are conditionally independent given strata rather than i.i.d. The key departure from the closest prior work, Jiang et al. (2023), is that the latter targets quantile treatment effects with continuous outcomes, whereas this paper handles the full distribution function and accommodates discrete or mixed discrete-continuous outcomes via a distribution-regression formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap in the CAR literature by providing both the asymptotic distribution and the semiparametric efficiency bound for DTE estimators, showing that a simple AIPW-with-ML-regression estimator is semiparametrically efficient. The microcredit application illustrates a practically important point: the ATE was statistically insignificant, yet the distributional analysis reveals a significant 10-percentage-point reduction in the probability of zero revenue, suggesting the program acts as a downside-risk hedge rather than a broad productivity booster. Open questions include extending the framework to imperfect compliance and network interference, handling settings with many strata and modest sample sizes, and incorporating kernel mean embedding approaches for richer distributional characterization.
