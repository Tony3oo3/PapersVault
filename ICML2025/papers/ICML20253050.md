## Problem

In RLHF, the intermediate reward model is often inaccurate, particularly on tasks requiring complex reasoning, which degrades policy learning. The paper identifies that reward model reliability is non-uniform: it is more trustworthy at extreme reward values (high or low) but unreliable in the moderate reward region. The key question is how to exploit this non-uniformity to improve policy optimization without retraining the reward model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a code problem or math word problem) and generates N candidate responses per prompt during training. These responses are ranked by the reward model, and a filtered subset (e.g., the best and a random other, or the best and the worst) is selected for PPO gradient updates. At inference, the resulting policy produces a single text response (code or math solution) via greedy decoding, evaluated by pass@1 or answer correctness.

## Method

PF-PPO modifies only the sampling phase of standard PPO: for each prompt, N responses are generated from the unfiltered policy π_θ, ranked by the reward model, and a filtered policy μ_θ selects which response to use for the PPO update via a categorical distribution over rank positions. The key weight vectors are best-random (BR: 1/2 mass on the top-ranked, 1/2(N−1) spread over the rest) and best-worst (BW: 1/2 on top, 1/2 on bottom), which concentrate training signal in the reward regions where the Bradley-Terry reward model is most reliable. The authors use the coefficient of determination (R²) between reward-model scores and ground-truth correctness on filtered samples as a pre-training diagnostic to select the optimal filtering strategy. The PPO objective and KL penalty remain unchanged; the approximation is that samples collected by π_θ are treated as if collected by μ_θ, which the paper shows does not harm reward optimization.

## Results

On code generation, PF-PPO (BR) achieves pass@1 of 82.9 on HumanEval, 75.9 on MBPP, and 33.0 on the newly introduced LeetCode Contest benchmark, setting new SOTA for 7B-parameter models (beating Magicoder's 76.8 on HumanEval by +7.9% relative). PF-PPO (BW) achieves 82.4/76.2/30.4 on the same benchmarks. On math reasoning, PF-PPO (BR) consistently outperforms PPO-S across three reward model types (ORM, Oracle, CRM) on both Ape210K and CMATH, e.g., 86.2 vs 84.1 on Ape210K with ORM and 95.1 vs 92.3 on CMATH with ORM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF-PPO (BR) outperforms the strongest RL baseline PPO-S (78.1) by +4.8 on HumanEval and +7.8 on LeetCode, and beats the best SFT-based method BOND (80.8) by +2.1 on HumanEval and +3.0 on LeetCode. Against direct policy optimization methods (DPO 78.4, IPO 78.2, KTO 77.9, Iterative-DPO 78.1), PF-PPO (BR) leads by 4.5–5.0 points on HumanEval. Notably, PF-PPO (BoN) performs poorly (75.8 on HumanEval), confirming that best-of-N filtering is counterproductive in an RL setting where contrastive signal from lower-ranked samples is needed. On math reasoning (Table 4), PF-PPO (BR) beats PPO-S by +2.1 (Ape210K/ORM), +2.8 (CMATH/ORM), +1.7 (Ape210K/Oracle), and +1.1 (CMATH/CRM), demonstrating consistent gains across reward model types. The gap between PF-PPO and PPO-M (which uses all N responses without filtering) is +2.7 on HumanEval and +3.2 on LeetCode, isolating the benefit of reward-reliability filtering beyond simply sampling more responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

PF-PPO builds directly on the standard PPO-based RLHF pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2022) and borrows the conceptual spirit of reject sampling / best-of-N inference (Nakano et al., 2021; Cobbe et al., 2021), but transplants the filtering idea into the RL sampling phase rather than using supervised distillation losses as in RAFT, BOND, or vBoN. It departs from prior reward-model-reliability work (Wang et al., 2024; Coste et al., 2023) by not attempting to fix the reward model itself, instead designing a policy-optimization algorithm that is robust to its known inaccuracy in the moderate-reward region. The rank-based filtering family (BoN, BR, BW) is positioned as a principled generalization of threshold-based or reweighting-based alternatives, with the R² diagnostic providing a pre-training selection criterion absent from prior RLHF methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a minimal, drop-in modification to PPO's sampling step that consistently improves pass@1 across code and math benchmarks without increasing compute, making it attractive for practitioners already running RLHF pipelines. The R²-based strategy selection offers a cheap, pre-training diagnostic that could reduce the cost of hyperparameter search over filtering schemes. Open questions include whether R² remains a reliable proxy for final performance under different reward-model architectures or non-Bradley-Terry preference models, and whether the rank-based advantage over threshold-based strategies persists when reward distributions are heavily skewed. The self-constructed LeetCode Contest benchmark, while useful, limits external reproducibility of the largest reported gains.
