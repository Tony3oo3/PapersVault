## Problem

Uniform-state discrete diffusion models (USDMs) offer a natural self-correcting property during generation—tokens can be revised at every denoising step—yet they have historically underperformed both autoregressive models and masked diffusion models (MDMs). The design space for USDMs remained primitive (mean parameterization, slow ancestral sampling), while the rich toolkit of Gaussian diffusion (efficient parameterizations, fast samplers, distillation) was inaccessible because no theoretical bridge existed between the two settings.

## Input / Output

The model operates directly on one-hot token vectors in V (dimension K, the vocabulary size) rather than continuous embeddings, with a diffusion timestep t ∈ [0,1] as conditioning. During training, the denoising network accepts both continuous latents (tempered softmax of Gaussian noise) and discrete one-hot latents, switching between them via a curriculum. Sequences are processed with context lengths of 128 (LM1B, BERT tokenizer) or 1024 (OWT, GPT-2 tokenizer), and the 170M-parameter DiT produces a categorical distribution over the vocabulary at each position.

## Method

The paper establishes the Diffusion Duality: applying argmax to the latents of a Gaussian diffusion process yields exactly the marginals of a Uniform-state discrete diffusion process, with diffusion parameters related by a closed-form transformation T(α̃_t) involving an integral of Gaussian densities. This duality enables two key applications. First, a curriculum learning strategy reparameterizes the discrete NELBO in terms of Gaussian latents and relaxes argmax with a tempered softmax (annealing τ from 0.001 to 0), reducing gradient variance by an order of magnitude and doubling training speed. Second, Discrete Consistency Distillation (DCD) constructs deterministic discrete trajectories by projecting the optimal Gaussian PF-ODE through argmax, then distills the model via KL divergence between teacher and student outputs on adjacent trajectory points, reducing sampling from 1024 to 8 NFEs with minimal quality loss. A Rao-Blackwellized ELBO further reduces memory and variance by eliminating explicit one-hot materialization.

## Results

On LM1B (with sentence packing), Duo achieves a PPL of 33.7, outperforming the best prior USDM (UDLM, 36.7) and SEDD Uniform (40.3), and narrowing the gap to the best absorbing-state model MDLM (31.8) to under 2 points. On OWT, Duo reaches 25.2 PPL vs. MDLM's 23.2 and UDLM's 27.4. In zero-shot evaluation across 7 held-out datasets, Duo surpasses the autoregressive Transformer baseline on 3/7 (Lambada: 49.78 vs 51.28, Pubmed: 44.48 vs 49.01, Arxiv: 40.39 vs 41.73) and beats SEDD Absorbing on 4/7. After 5 rounds of Discrete Consistency Distillation, Duo reduces sampling from 1024 to 8 NFEs (128× speedup) while maintaining competitive Gen PPL, and outperforms MDLM+SDTT in the low-NFE regime (T≤32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Duo (USDM) decisively outperforms all prior uniform-state and Gaussian diffusion baselines: it beats UDLM by 1.4–2.2 PPL points across LM1B and OWT, SEDD Uniform by 10.4–4.5 points, and Plaid by 46.9 points at 100K steps on LM1B (43.0 vs 89.9). Against the strongest absorbing-state model MDLM, Duo still trails by 1.9 PPL on LM1B (33.7 vs 31.8) and 2.0 on OWT (25.2 vs 23.2), but surpasses it on 1/7 zero-shot datasets (PTB: 89.35 vs 95.26) and, critically, in the few-step generation regime where MDLM+SDTT degrades sharply at T≤32 NFEs. Duo also beats SEDD Absorbing on 4/7 zero-shot benchmarks (PTB, WikiText, Lambada, Pubmed) but loses on LM1B, AG News, and Arxiv. Versus the autoregressive Transformer, Duo loses on LM1B (33.7 vs 22.8) and OWT (25.2 vs 17.5) but wins on 3/7 zero-shot datasets (Lambada, Pubmed, Arxiv), making it the first diffusion LM to beat AR on any standard zero-shot PPL benchmarks. In distillation, Duo+DCD outperforms MDLM+SDTT at T≤32 NFEs due to USDMs' self-correcting property, while at higher NFEs (T≥64) MDLM still leads in Gen PPL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Uniform-state discrete diffusion (USDM) framework of Schiff et al. (2025) and the interpolating noise formulation of Sahoo et al. (2024a), but introduces a fundamentally new theoretical lens: the 'Diffusion Duality' showing that USDMs emerge from an underlying Gaussian diffusion via the argmax operator. This connection repurposes the tempered-softmax relaxation from Gumbel-softmax (Jang et al., 2017; Maddison et al., 2017) as a curriculum learning schedule, and adapts consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023) to the discrete setting by constructing deterministic trajectories in Gaussian space and projecting them via argmax. The key departure from prior discrete diffusion work (MDLM, SEDD, UDLM) is that those methods operate strictly in discrete space, whereas Duo expands the design space by incorporating continuous-space Gaussian diffusion as a training and distillation substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The duality provides a principled mechanism for transferring well-studied Gaussian diffusion techniques (curriculum learning, PF-ODE-based distillation) to discrete diffusion, which previously lacked these tools. The practical impact is a 2× training speedup and a 128× sampling speedup (1024→8 NFEs) with minimal quality loss, and USDMs outperform MDMs in the few-step generation regime where MDMs' inability to revise tokens causes incoherence. However, the gap with MDMs in perplexity at high NFEs persists (29.9 vs 27.0 on LM1B), experiments are limited to 170M-parameter models on LM1B/OWT, and the duality is specific to USDMs—it does not extend to MDMs due to their absorbing-state prior. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether the perplexity gap can be closed, and whether similar dualities exist for other discrete diffusion variants.
