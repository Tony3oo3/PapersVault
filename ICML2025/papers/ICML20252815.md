## Problem

Existing dimension reduction methods face a fundamental tension: subspace clustering handles heterogeneity but ignores the response variable, while sufficient dimension reduction (SDR) preserves regression information but assumes a single homogeneous subspace. In high-dimensional settings with latent sub-populations, neither approach alone suffices—subspace clustering loses regression-relevant information, and homogeneous SDR cannot capture cluster-specific relationships between Y and X.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252815/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a p-dimensional predictor vector X, a univariate response Y (continuous or discrete), and a user-specified q-dimensional fitting function f(·) that encodes the assumed functional form of the Y–X relationship. It jointly produces K cluster assignments, K cluster-specific subspace basis matrices (each of rank d_w ≤ q), a sparse set of selected variables via the group Lasso penalty, and mixture proportions. A key design choice is that subspace estimation operates in a reduced d-dimensional space (d = dim of the union of all central subspaces) rather than the full p-dimensional space, avoiding repeated inversion of p×p covariance matrices during EM iterations.

## Method

The mixPFC model is a generative mixture where, conditional on cluster w and response Y, the predictor follows X | (Y, W=w) ~ N(μ_w + Γ_w f(Y), Δ), with Γ_w encoding the cluster-specific regression relationship and f(·) introducing nonlinearity. The estimation procedure is a group Lasso penalized EM algorithm: the E-step computes posterior cluster probabilities using only reduced predictors β^T X (projected onto the central subspace), and the M-step solves a convex optimization over a p×q basis matrix with an L2,1 (group Lasso) penalty that enforces coordinate-independent variable selection. This formulation avoids the p×p matrix inversion that makes standard EM infeasible in high dimensions, reducing the per-iteration cost to O(nKpq + Kn p²). The paper establishes a non-asymptotic convergence rate showing geometric contraction to statistical precision √(sq³(log n)² log p / n) without requiring sample splitting, equal mixing proportions, or minimum angle conditions between subspaces.

## Results

On the CCLE real-data benchmark, mixPFC reduces prediction mean squared error (PMSE×100) to 8.9 on Nutlin-3 and 45.6 on AZD6244, roughly halving the error of the best homogeneous SDR baselines (SEAS-SIR: 18.7 and 108.8; LassoSIR: 18.2 and 83.2). In simulation, mixPFC achieves clustering error rates of 3–10% for K=2 and under 3% for K=3, compared to 40–75% for all unsupervised subspace clustering methods (RANSAC, LSA, LRSC, SSC, GPCA). On the AIS dataset, mixPFC recovers the male/female subpopulations with a 7.4% misclassification rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mixPFC dominates all five unsupervised subspace clustering baselines (RANSAC, LSA, LRSC, SSC, GPCA) on clustering error by a factor of 4–20×: e.g., in the hardest setting (M1, identical subspaces), mixPFC achieves 3.4% error while RANSAC, LSA, LRSC, SSC, and GPCA all hover near 48%. The sole exception is M2 with Δ=0.1I (orthogonal subspaces, tiny noise), where SSC reaches 4.9% and RANSAC 28.3%, comparable to or slightly below mixPFC's 16.4%. On the CCLE prediction task, mixPFC outperforms all four homogeneous SDR baselines: it cuts PMSE by ~52% vs SEAS-SIR (8.9 vs 18.7) and ~58% vs SEAS-SIR (45.6 vs 108.8) on AZD6244, while selecting fewer or comparable variables (31.5 vs 48.3 for Nutlin-3). K-means and hierarchical clustering (applied to X or X∘Y) perform near chance level (40–55% error) in most settings, confirming that simple supervised heuristics cannot replace the joint model-based framework. The authors note that SEAS-PFC fitted on mixPFC-estimated clusters performs nearly as well as fitting on true clusters, validating the end-to-end pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

mixPFC unifies two previously separate research threads: model-based sufficient dimension reduction (PFC, Cook & Forzani 2008; high-dimensional variants like LassoSIR and SEAS-PFC) and subspace clustering (RANSAC, SSC, GPCA). The key conceptual departure is making subspace clustering supervised by incorporating the response Y into the generative model, which eliminates the need for minimal-angle separation conditions between subspaces that are standard in unsupervised subspace clustering. On the algorithmic side, the group Lasso penalized EM extends the penalized EM framework for high-dimensional mixture linear regression (Wang et al., 2024) and high-dimensional GMM (Cai et al., 2019) to the more complex setting of multiple heterogeneous subspaces with response-dependent means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee for a high-dimensional heterogeneous SDR method, establishing that the penalized EM algorithm converges geometrically to statistical precision without sample splitting. The theory is currently limited to K=2 clusters and known covariance, leaving multi-cluster high-dimensional EM as an open problem that the authors acknowledge requires fundamentally new tools. Practically, the method offers a unified pipeline for clustering, subspace estimation, and variable selection in settings where data are expected to be heterogeneous (e.g., cancer genomics, sports science), and the CCLE results demonstrate substantial prediction error reduction over homogeneous SDR baselines. A limitation is that subspace estimation accuracy degrades under strong predictor correlation (AR(0.5)) and small noise (Δ=0.1I), where the isotropic variant mixPFC-ISO is preferable.
