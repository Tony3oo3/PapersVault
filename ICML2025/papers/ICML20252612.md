## Problem

Large generative models are routinely trained on heterogeneous multi-source data, yet the theoretical interaction among these sources remains unexplored. A fundamental question—whether training a single model on multiple sources provably outperforms training separate models per source—lacks a rigorous distribution-estimation analysis for conditional generative modeling.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n i.i.d. data-label pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from K distinct sources, where y_i ∈ [K] identifies the source and x_i lives in a source-specific data space (d-dimensional vectors for Gaussians, D-length token sequences over vocabulary M for ARMs, or D-dimensional continuous vectors in [0,1]^D for EBMs). The output is an estimated conditional distribution p̂_{X|Y} that maps each source label to a distribution over the data space; in the diffusion-model experiments this is instantiated as a class-conditional generator producing 256×256 RGB images evaluated by FID.

## Method

The paper introduces an ε-upper bracketing number for conditional distribution spaces (one-sided brackets uniform across all conditions y) and proves a general high-probability bound on the average TV estimation error of conditional MLE scaling as Õ(√(log N_[]/n)). The key mechanism is that multi-source training constrains the model to share a parameter ψ across all K sources while retaining source-specific parameters φ_k, which strictly reduces the bracketing number relative to single-source training where each source has its own ψ_k. Instantiating the theory for conditional Gaussians, autoregressive models (with shared MLP and per-source embedding rows), and energy-based models (with shared energy MLP and per-source condition embeddings) yields explicit error bounds whose multi-to-single-source ratio uniformly takes the form √(1−(K−1)/K)·β_sim, where β_sim ∈ [0,1] quantifies the proportion of shared parameters. This shows the advantage grows with both the number of sources K and the distributional similarity β_sim, validated by Gaussian simulations and class-conditional EDM2 diffusion experiments on ILSVRC2012.

## Results

On ILSVRC2012 with class-conditional EDM2 diffusion models at 256×256 resolution, multi-source training consistently achieves lower average FID than single-source training across all 8 experimental configurations (varying K∈{3,10}, similarity level Sim∈{1,2}, and per-class sample size N∈{500,1000}). The largest improvement is observed at N=500, Sim=2, K=10, where multi-source training reduces average FID from 30.54 to 28.75 (a ~5.9% relative gain). The theoretical error bound ratio between multi-source and single-source training takes the form √(1−(K−1)/K)·β_sim, which decreases with both the number of sources K and the distribution similarity β_sim, confirming that multi-source training is provably sharper when sources share parametric structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between multi-source training (a single model trained jointly on all K sources with shared parameters ψ) and single-source training (K separate models, each trained exclusively on its own source with independent parameters ψ_k). Multi-source training wins in all 8 experimental configurations, achieving lower average FID in every case. The relative advantage (measured as (FID_single − FID_multi)/FID_single) increases with the number of sources K: for fixed Sim and N, K=10 yields a larger FID improvement than K=3. The advantage also increases with distribution similarity: for fixed K and N, Sim=2 (more semantically related classes in ImageNet's hierarchy) produces a greater FID reduction than Sim=1. In the Gaussian simulation experiments, the empirical average TV error for multi-source training consistently falls below that of single-source training, and the scaling orders match the theoretical predictions of Õ(√((K−1)d₁+d)/n) vs. Õ(√(Kd/n)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical MLE estimation error bounds from empirical process theory (Wong & Shen, 1995; Geer, 2000) and the recent TV-distance analysis under realizability (Ge et al., 2024) to the conditional multi-source setting, introducing a novel one-sided upper bracketing number for conditional distribution spaces. The ARM and EBM instantiations build directly on the formulations of Uria et al. (2016) and Du & Mordatch (2019), respectively, while the diffusion model experiments follow EDM2 (Karras et al., 2024). The key departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of shared versus source-specific parameters as the mechanism through which multi-source training reduces distribution-space complexity, a connection not previously formalized in the generative modeling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical analysis showing *when* and *why* multi-source training provably outperforms single-source training for conditional generative models, providing practitioners with a principled quantity (β_sim) to reason about data-source similarity. The clean ratio form √(1−(K−1)/K)·β_sim unifies the analysis across Gaussian, autoregressive, and energy-based models, suggesting a general principle. Limitations include the realizable assumption, the restriction to MLP-based architectures (not full Transformers or convolutions), and the gap between the theoretical conditional-label setting and practical LLM training where conditions are implicit. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-realizable settings, characterizing optimal data-mixing weights, and understanding how the theory applies to score-based/diffusion models beyond the MLE perspective.
