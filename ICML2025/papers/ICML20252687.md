## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) are designed for text-based LLMs with discrete turns and do not address the unique challenges of real-time spoken dialogue: full-duplex interactions with overlapping speech, interruptions, interjections, and no explicit turn boundaries. No prior work has demonstrated how to align a speech-to-speech dialogue model using large-scale live user interaction data that captures both linguistic content and temporal dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in full-duplex mode, continuously receiving quantized user audio (8-level RVQ tokens at 12.5 Hz, 2048 codebook size) while simultaneously generating its own audio stream and a time-synchronized text stream (Inner Monologue). For alignment, each preference pair consists of a multistream context (user audio, model audio, model text) paired with a preferred and a losing response; user audio is resynthesized via TTS with original timestamps to preserve temporal structure while discarding speaker identity for privacy.

## Method

The paper introduces a pipeline that converts raw multi-turn spoken dialogues into 150K+ preference pairs by using Mistral Large 2 as an LLM judge to flag problematic turns along seven axes (helpfulness, safety, factual accuracy, instruction adherence, tone, interruption, unresponsiveness), then generating corrected preferred responses. The key adaptation to DPO-LN for spoken dialogue is restricting the preference optimization objective to text-token probabilities only (π^T(y|x) = π(T^y | x, A^y, A'^y)), since jointly optimizing over both text and audio token probabilities leads to unstable training. The dataset explicitly covers both content-related preferences (inaccurate or unsafe responses) and timing-related preferences (model interrupting the user or failing to respond in time), and the authors show that the mix of these types significantly affects the model's linguistic and temporal behavior. Training uses a 7B Temporal Transformer + 600M Depth Transformer architecture with DPO-LN at β=0.3, achieving +3.1% average QA accuracy and +6.9% safety improvement over the base model.

## Results

On spoken question answering (WebQA, LlamaQA, TriviaQA), Moshi-Aligned achieves an average QA score of 39.2, a +3.1 point improvement over the base Moshi-Instruct (36.1). On safety benchmarks (ALERT and XSTest), the aligned model reaches an average of 77.8, a +6.9 point gain over the base model (70.9). When transferring the same preference data to a different-voice model (M-Alt-Vox), safety improves by +11.0 points (66.2 → 77.2), though replay length increases considerably. Among offline alignment algorithms, DPO-LN achieves the best QA (39.2) and near-top safety (77.8) with a moderate replay length of 51.4 words.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Moshi-Aligned substantially outperforms the cascade-based baselines SpeechGPT (QA avg 14.3) and Spectron (WebQA 6.1, LlamaQA 22.9) on spoken question answering, reflecting the advantage of end-to-end full-duplex architecture over chain-of-modality pipelines. Against the 9B speech-text model of Zeng et al. (2025), Moshi-Aligned (39.2) surpasses the speech-in/speech-out configuration (31.0) by 8.2 points, though it trails the speech-in/text-out variant (45.3) by 6.1 points; however, Zeng et al. do not report safety metrics, whereas Moshi-Aligned achieves 77.8 average safety. Among alignment algorithms, DPO-LN (QA 39.2, Safety 77.8) outperforms standard DPO (36.2, 75.4) and SimPO (38.2, 73.1), while APO-Zero matches DPO-LN on QA (39.0) and safety (77.9) but exhibits a slightly higher replay length (54.1 vs. 51.4). The transfer experiment to M-Alt-Vox shows that preference data collected from one voice can still improve a different-voice model's safety by +11.0 points, though at the cost of a large replay-length increase (19.3 → 91.3), suggesting distributional mismatch when voices differ significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preference alignment paradigm (DPO, SimPO, APO-Zero) from text LLMs to the full-duplex spoken dialogue setting, building directly on Moshi's multistream autoregressive architecture and adapting the DPO objective to operate over text-token probabilities conditioned on audio context. It departs from prior speech alignment work (Cideron et al. 2024 for music, Zhang et al. 2024 for TTS quality, Lin et al. 2024 for speech continuation) by targeting conversational dynamics—interruptions, unresponsiveness, overlapping turns—rather than acoustic quality or single-turn continuation. The RLAIF-style pipeline using Mistral Large 2 as a judge to flag and correct problematic turns in raw multi-turn dialogues is a natural extension of text-based preference data construction, but the inclusion of timing-related preference pairs (model cutting the user, model staying silent) represents a genuinely new data dimension absent from text-centric alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that large-scale, privacy-preserving preference alignment can improve full-duplex spoken dialogue models on factual accuracy, safety, and conversational naturalness simultaneously, without storing user audio. The finding that data composition (timing vs. content pairs) materially affects both linguistic quality and temporal behavior (e.g., speech tempo) provides practical guidance for future spoken dialogue alignment efforts. Key open questions remain: whether offline alignment generalizes to long, evolving conversations (current data focuses on the first problematic reply), whether online RLHF can capture user-specific style, and whether the pipeline extends beyond Moshi to other full-duplex architectures. The reliance on TTS-resynthesized user audio (for privacy) introduces a distributional gap that limits transfer to mismatched voices, as shown in the M-Alt-Vox experiment.
