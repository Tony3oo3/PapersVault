## Problem

Web-crawled image-text datasets like DataComp-1B suffer from brief, noisy, and often misaligned captions, yet large-scale open-source recaptioned datasets at the billion-image level remain unavailable. While closed-source systems (DALL-E 3, SORA) rely on advanced captioning to improve training data, the community lacks a reproducible, publicly accessible pipeline to generate high-quality captions at comparable scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251418/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The recaptioning model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a detailed natural-language caption (up to 128 tokens) via greedy decoding, prompted with 'Please generate a detailed caption of this image. Please be as descriptive as possible.' The resulting Recap-DataComp-1B dataset pairs each of the ~1.3 billion images with both the original web-crawled caption and the new LLaVA-generated caption. For downstream CLIP training, the text encoder is configured for 128-token sequences to accommodate the longer recaptions; for DiT training, images are resized to 256×256 square resolution before latent extraction.

## Method

The captioner is built by replacing the LLM backbone of LLaVA-1.5 with LLaMA-3-8B while keeping the CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder frozen, followed by a two-stage instruction-tuning procedure (projection MLP pre-training on 558k pairs, then joint fine-tuning on 665k instruction data plus HQ-Edit pairs). This model is then deployed to recaption the entire DataComp-1B dataset (~1.3B images) in a single pass. A key design choice for downstream training is mixing original and recaptioned captions at a tunable ratio p: for CLIP, p=0.8 (80% original) balances classification accuracy against retrieval gains, while for DiT, p=0.1 (90% recaption) maximizes FID and CLIP-score improvements. The paper demonstrates that this simple recaption-and-mix strategy yields consistent gains across discriminative (CLIP retrieval, +3.1% average) and generative (DiT, −8.4 FID, +3.1% CLIP score) vision-language models without architectural changes.

## Results

Recap-CLIP L/14 trained on Recap-DataComp-1B achieves 79.3% zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy and outperforms the DataComp-1B baseline by an average of 5.6% on Flickr30K retrieval (94.1% I→T vs. 89.0%) and 8.4% on COCO retrieval (72.0% I→T vs. 63.3%). The scaled-up Recap-CLIP H/14 reaches 81.0% ImageNet-1K and 94.8% Flickr30K I→T, matching or exceeding SigLIP trained on 45B private samples. For text-to-image generation, DiT-B/4 trained entirely on recaptions (p=0) achieves FID 27.8 and CLIP Score 32.5 on COCO-30K, improving over the vanilla DataComp-1B baseline (p=1) by 8.4 FID points and 3.1% CLIP score, with a GPT-4V quality score of 2.53 vs. 1.40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct DataComp-1B CLIP baseline (same images, original captions), Recap-CLIP L/14 wins on all four retrieval metrics: Flickr30K I→T 94.1% vs. 89.0% (+5.1), COCO I→T 72.0% vs. 63.3% (+8.7), with ImageNet-1K essentially tied (79.3% vs. 79.2%). Against OpenCLIP trained on LAION-2B, Recap-CLIP L/14 achieves higher retrieval (Flickr I→T 94.1% vs. 89.5%, COCO I→T 72.0% vs. 63.4%) while using a smaller public dataset. Against SigLIP trained on the private WebLI-5B (45B samples), Recap-CLIP L/14 is competitive on retrieval (Flickr I→T 94.1% vs. 91.8%, COCO I→T 72.0% vs. 70.8%) but trails on ImageNet-1K (79.3% vs. 80.5%); the H/14 variant narrows this gap to 81.0% vs. 83.1% while matching or exceeding SigLIP on all retrieval tasks. Against DFN-5B CLIP (private, 729 patches), Recap-CLIP H/14 is close on retrieval (Flickr I→T 94.8% vs. 94.0%, COCO I→T 73.1% vs. 71.9%) but lags on ImageNet-1K (81.0% vs. 84.4%), reflecting the advantage of the larger 729-patch vision encoder. On Urban1K long-caption retrieval, Recap-CLIP-B/16 surpasses the non-recaption baseline by 31.8% (I→T) and 36.4% (T→I), and on VG-Attribute it gains 9.1%, approaching the 10% boost from dedicated NegCLIP fine-tuning without any task-specific adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recaptioning-for-training line initiated by ShareGPT4V (GPT-4V-based captions) and LaCLIP (LLM-based caption rewriting) by replacing the closed-source GPT-4V with the open-source LLaMA-3-8B inside the LLaVA-1.5 framework, and scaling execution to ~1.3 billion images from DataComp-1B. It follows the broader trend of using open-source multimodal models (LLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT) for caption generation, as explored by PixArt-α, LongCLIP, and MobileVLM-v2, but distinguishes itself through the LLaMA-3 backbone and billion-scale scope. For downstream evaluation, it builds on CLIP/CLIPA training recipes and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures, positioning Recap-DataComp-1B as a drop-in replacement for the original DataComp-1B captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Recap-DataComp-1B is a major publicly available resource that democratizes access to high-quality, billion-scale image-text pairs previously locked behind closed-source captioning pipelines (DALL-E 3, SORA). The consistent improvements across both discriminative (CLIP retrieval +3–5%) and generative (DiT FID −8.4, CLIP score +3.1%) models, validated at multiple model scales, provide strong evidence that caption quality is a first-order bottleneck for vision-language training. Open questions include whether the slight ImageNet classification degradation from long captions can be resolved without sacrificing retrieval gains, whether the captioner's inability to identify specific named entities limits downstream open-vocabulary tasks, and whether the optimal mixing ratio (p=0.8 for CLIP vs. p=0.1 for DiT) generalizes to other architectures and data regimes.
