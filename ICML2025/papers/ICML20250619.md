## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation (GDA) methods focus on aligning structural and attribute distributions between source and target graphs, but overlook homophily distribution discrepancies — the differing proportions of homophilic versus heterophilic node subgroups across domains. The paper demonstrates both empirically and theoretically (via a PAC-Bayes bound) that these homophily shifts significantly degrade target node classification accuracy, particularly in subgroups where the source-target homophily ratio divergence is largest.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source graph (adjacency matrix A^S, feature matrix X^S, label matrix Y^S) and an unlabeled target graph (A^T, X^T) in a fully unsupervised GDA setting, where both graphs' complete structure and node features are available but only the source carries labels. It outputs predicted class labels for all target graph nodes. The three filter branches (homophilic, full-pass, heterophilic) each process the raw node features through two GCN-like layers with graph-specific propagation matrices, and the final node embedding is the element-wise sum of the three branch outputs before classification.

## Method

HGDA decomposes graph signal propagation into three parallel branches: a homophilic filter using the normalized adjacency matrix Ã, a full-pass filter using the identity matrix I (= Ã + L̃), and a heterophilic filter using the normalized Laplacian L̃, each with a learnable scalar gate and per-layer weight matrix. The alignment loss applies KL divergence separately between source and target embeddings from each branch, directly corresponding to the four KL-divergence terms in the paper's PAC-Bayes generalization bound (homophilic signal AX, attribute signal X, heterophilic signal LX, and heterophily distribution shift). The total training objective combines this three-way alignment loss with a cross-entropy source classifier loss and a target entropy loss, weighted by hyperparameters α and β. This design ensures that homophilic, heterophilic, and attribute signals are captured and aligned independently, addressing the subgroup-level distribution shifts that monolithic GDA methods miss.

## Results

HGDA achieves state-of-the-art cross-network node classification accuracy across all six dataset families (Airport, ACM, Blog, Citation, Twitch, MAG). The method delivers average improvements of 1.10% over the best baseline on Airport, 6.50% on ACM, 1.40% on Citation, 2.20% on Blog, and a maximum of 8.49% on the large-scale MAG dataset. For example, on the ACM A3→A4 task, HGDA reaches 71.8% accuracy versus 61.9% for PA and 51.6% for JHGDA, and on MAG US→JP it achieves 53.1% versus 47.4% for PA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGDA outperforms all ten baselines across every task in all three main tables. Against the strongest recent competitor PA (Liu et al., 2024), HGDA gains 10.0 points on U→B (72.1 vs 62.1), 9.9 points on A3→A4 (71.8 vs 61.9), and 11.0 points on MAG US→CN (51.0 vs 40.0). JHGDA (Shi et al., 2023) is the second-best baseline on most Airport and Citation tasks but trails HGDA by 2.6–13.5 points depending on the task; on E→B, JHGDA actually reaches 0.740 while HGDA scores 0.721, the only instance where a baseline edges out the full model. SpecReg (You et al., 2022) and GRADE-N (Wu et al., 2023) are competitive on some Blog and Twitch subgroups but are consistently surpassed by HGDA on the ACM and MAG benchmarks where homophily divergence is most pronounced. The ablation variants (HGDA_L, HGDA_F, HGDA_H) each beat most baselines individually, confirming that any single filter branch already contributes meaningfully, while the full three-branch HGDA consistently achieves the top score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph domain adaptation (GDA) line of work (UDAGCN, JHGDA, SpecReg, PA) by identifying homophily distribution shift as a distinct and previously overlooked source of domain discrepancy. While prior GDA methods align global structural and attribute distributions, HGDA decomposes graph signals into homophilic (AX), attribute (X), and heterophilic (LX) components using mixed graph filters, drawing on the graph filter theory of Nt & Maehara (2019) that treats the adjacency matrix as a low-pass and the Laplacian as a high-pass filter. The theoretical grounding adapts the PAC-Bayes domain adaptation framework (Ma et al., 2021; Mao et al., 2024a) to the graph setting, introducing a KL-divergence-based bound that explicitly includes a heterophily distribution shift term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of homophily distribution shift as a separate, actionable factor in GDA provides a clearer diagnostic for why cross-network transfer fails on certain node subgroups, and the mixed-filter decomposition offers a practical, low-complexity mechanism to address it. However, the PAC-Bayes bound is quite loose (involving four KL divergence terms and constants that are hard to estimate), and the method's gains, while consistent across six dataset families, are moderate (1–8% average improvement). Open questions include whether the three-filter decomposition generalizes to multi-relational or temporal graphs, and whether the homophily alignment can be made adaptive per-node rather than global.
