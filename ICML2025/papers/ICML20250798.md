## Problem

Model-based architectures (MBAs) for inverse problems offer interpretability and strong reconstructions, but the accuracy-robustness tradeoff in their training remains underexplored. Adversarial training improves worst-case robustness at the cost of generalization accuracy, while input jittering achieves comparable robustness but with significant accuracy loss. Moreover, no prior work provides a formal theoretical analysis of both generalization and robustness risks for nonlinear MBAs under perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on corrupted measurements y = Ax + z with a known forward operator A, and produces a reconstructed signal x via a fixed number K of iterative gradient (or proximal gradient) updates. During training, independent zero-mean Gaussian noise w_k ~ N(0, σ²_{w_k}/n · I) is injected into the learned gradient f_θ(x_k) at each iteration k; during inference, all jittering noise is removed so the model behaves identically to a standard MBA. The framework is architecture-agnostic within MBAs, requiring only that the learned gradient network f_θ be twice differentiable and L-Lipschitz continuous.

## Method

SGD jittering modifies the standard loop-unrolling training by adding independently re-sampled zero-mean Gaussian noise to the neural-network gradient update at every unrolling iteration, effectively turning the lower-level reconstruction into a stochastic gradient descent process. Unlike input jittering or adversarial training, the noise is added to the gradient rather than to the measurement y, so the lower-level objective remains unbiased and identical to regular MSE training, preserving reconstruction accuracy. The paper proves (via iterative trajectory expansions) that minimizing the SGD jittering risk implicitly regularizes both the gradient and Hessian of f_θ with respect to intermediate reconstructions, yielding a smaller generalization risk (Theorem 7.4) and a smaller average-case robustness risk (Theorem 7.5) than MSE training. A convergence guarantee (Corollary 6.2) adapted from Garrigos & Gower (2023) ensures the noisy iterations still converge to the correct solution. The approach extends naturally to proximal gradient descent (SPGD jittering) by injecting noise into the input of the proximal network at each step, and is shown to be significantly faster than adversarial training while matching or exceeding its robustness.

## Results

On 4× accelerated single-coil knee MRI (fastMRI), SGD jittering achieves 28.22 dB PSNR / 0.607 SSIM on in-distribution data, 26.77 / 0.552 under adversarial attack (ε=1), and 30.36 / 0.788 on OOD tumor-cell data. On seismic deconvolution, SGD jittering reaches 35.10 / 0.928 (ID), 29.89 / 0.842 (adv. attack), and 34.93 / 0.927 (OOD). The method matches or exceeds MSE training on ID accuracy while improving OOD generalization by ~0.4 dB PSNR and ~0.01 SSIM, and narrows the adversarial-attack gap to AT by ~1 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard MSE training, SGD jittering matches in-distribution accuracy (28.22 vs. 28.21 dB PSNR) while improving OOD generalization by 0.44 dB PSNR (30.36 vs. 29.92) and adversarial robustness by 1.09 dB (26.77 vs. 25.68). Against Adversarial Training, SGD jittering trades ~0.4 dB of worst-case robustness (26.77 vs. 27.17) for substantially better ID accuracy (+0.54 dB) and OOD generalization (+2.62 dB), confirming that AT's robustness comes at a significant accuracy cost. Against Input Jittering, SGD jittering dominates on all three metrics, with the largest gap on adversarial attacks (26.77 vs. 25.05 dB, +1.72 dB) and OOD data (30.36 vs. 29.97 dB). The SPGD extension to proximal gradient descent shows the same pattern: best ID and OOD scores with competitive adversarial robustness, while AT consistently produces smoother but lower-resolution reconstructions that miss fine anatomical details in OOD tumor data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the loop unrolling (LU) model-based architecture framework (Monga et al., 2021) and adapts SGD convergence theory (Garrigos & Gower, 2023) to prove convergence of the proposed noisy iterative scheme. It departs from adversarial training (AT) and input jittering (Krainovic et al., 2023), both of which add perturbations to the measurement y and thereby introduce a systematic bias that degrades generalization. The key conceptual move is to inject noise into the gradient updates at each unrolled iteration rather than into the input, making the approach structurally analogous to layer-wise noise in recurrent networks (Lim et al., 2021) but applied to the iterative reconstruction steps of MBAs. The paper also introduces the first formal generalization risk definition specific to inverse problems under small test-time perturbations, going beyond the heuristic flat-minima narrative used in classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The accuracy-robustness tradeoff in inverse problem solvers is a critical bottleneck for safety-critical deployments (e.g., detecting small fractures in medical imaging while remaining stable under measurement noise). SGD jittering offers a drop-in, near-zero-overhead training modification that simultaneously improves both metrics, which is notable given that AT and input jittering each sacrifice one for the other. Limitations include theoretical analysis restricted to the denoising problem (identity forward model) and experiments on relatively small-scale tasks; the paper itself acknowledges that for IPs with unknown forward models where MBAs are unsuitable, alternative techniques remain needed. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear forward models, deeper unrolling, and whether the implicit Hessian regularization effect scales with network depth.
