## Problem

The classic Gonzalez algorithm for Euclidean k-CENTER runs in O(nkd) time, making it impractical for large k (e.g., k = n^c with 0 < c < 1), a regime increasingly relevant in applications like product quantization and record linkage. While subquadratic approximation algorithms exist for large k, achieving an O(1)-approximation in near-linear Õ(n) time remained open. This paper closes the gap by constructing small coresets that reduce the effective input size from n to k·o(n), enabling near-linear time O(1)-approximation when k = n^c.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point set P ⊆ R^d of n points and a target number of centers k, and produces a coreset S ⊆ P of size Õ(k·n^{1/α^{2/3}}) (Theorem 1.1) or k·polylog(n) (Theorem 1.2), such that any β-approximate k-CENTER solution on S is an (α+β)-approximation on P. Before hashing, a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform projects P into O(log n) dimensions. The coreset is then fed into an existing approximation algorithm (e.g., Eppstein et al. 2020) to produce the final set of k centers in R^d.

## Method

The core technique reduces coreset construction to finding a small (β·opt)-covering of P, then applies a newly designed consistent hashing based on a randomly-shifted grid. This hashing partitions R^d into unit cubes such that any ball of radius ℓ/β intersects only poly(d)·exp(O(d/β^{2/3})) cells in expectation, while each cell has diameter O(ℓ) and evaluation takes O(d) time—exponentially improving over prior exp(d)-time constructions. The algorithm iterates over geometrically increasing scale parameters τ, hashes all points at each scale, and returns one representative per non-empty cell once the total count drops below a threshold of O(k·t_β). A second coreset (Theorem 1.2) instead uses iterative random sampling combined with β-approximate nearest neighbor search to achieve a k·polylog(n)-sized coreset in Õ(nk^{1/α²}) time. For k = n^c, setting α = poly((1-c)^{-1}) yields a near-linear Õ(n)-time O(1)-approximation, removing the O(k) factor from Gonzalez's original runtime.

## Results

The paper achieves an O(1)-approximation for k-CENTER in near-linear Õ(n) time when k = n^c for any 0 < c < 1, by combining their O(α)-coreset of size Õ(k·n^{1/α^{2/3}}) (Theorem 1.1) with the Eppstein et al. (2020) approximation algorithm. In experiments on four real-world datasets (Kddcup, Covertype, Census, Fashion-MNIST) with k ≈ √n, the coreset achieves a 2×–4× speedup over running Gonzalez's algorithm on the full dataset while keeping clustering cost within 1.3× of the benchmark. Using only 5% of the full dataset, the coreset matches or beats all other baselines in clustering cost across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: (1) **benchmark** (Gonzalez on full data), (2) **uniform** (uniform random sampling coreset), (3) **low-dim** (Agarwal et al. 2004 coreset adapted without random shift), (4) **k-median** (Chen 2009 / Cohen-Addad et al. 2021 coreset designed for k-MEDIAN), and (5) **tree-embedding** (Indyk 2004 randomly-shifted grid tree embedding + Gonzalez). The proposed coreset consistently achieves the lowest clustering cost among uniform, low-dim, and tree-embedding baselines across all four datasets, and outperforms k-median on three of four datasets (losing only on Covertype, where k-MEDIAN and k-CENTER solutions are empirically similar). The tree-embedding baseline performs poorly everywhere because its O(log²n) distortion is insufficient for the max-distance k-CENTER objective. The low-dim baseline is closer in performance to the proposed method, and the gap is attributed to the random shift in the hash function, confirming its practical benefit even on real-world data. Overall, the coreset delivers a 2×–4× speedup over the full-data benchmark while maintaining comparable cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classic Gonzalez 2-approximation algorithm and the ratio-time trade-offs of Eppstein et al. (2020) by introducing a data-reduction (coreset) layer that shrinks the input from n to k·o(n) points before running any existing approximation algorithm. The key technical ingredient is a new consistent hashing construction (Lemma 3.2) that achieves the competitive parameter trade-off Λ = poly(d)·exp(O(d/β^{2/3})) in O(d) evaluation time, exponentially improving over the prior exp(d)-time constructions of Czumaj et al. (2023) and Filtser (2024). The coreset notion used here is a 'weak coreset' that preserves approximation ratio rather than exact cost, distinguishing it from the stronger (and prohibitively large) coresets of Agarwal et al. (2004) and Har-Peled & Mazumdar (2004).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper achieves a near-linear time O(1)-approximation for k-CENTER when k = n^c (0 < c < 1), a regime that includes many practical applications like product quantization and record linkage, and represents a concrete step toward the ideal goal of near-linear time for all k. The consistent hashing tool is positioned as a general-purpose primitive for algorithm design in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces, potentially useful beyond k-CENTER. However, the ultimate goal of near-linear time O(1)-approximation for k close to n remains open, and even the seemingly easier task of evaluating the cost of a given center set in near-linear time is unsolved. The 2–4× practical speedup is modest, and the coreset size still scales as k·n^{1/α^{2/3}}, which can be large for very large k.
