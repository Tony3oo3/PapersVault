## Problem

Existing multi-source domain adaptation methods rely on restrictive assumptions (independent latent variables, invariant label distributions) to establish identifiability of the target joint distribution, limiting real-world applicability. The paper identifies that learning representations of the label's full Markov blanket is underspecified for domain adaptation, since it cannot distinguish which components correspond to parents, children, or spouses of the label, preventing correct factorization of the joint distribution under general distribution shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251841/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images from multiple source domains and unlabeled RGB images from a single target domain, processed through a CNN backbone (ResNet-50 for OfficeHome, ResNet-18 for PACS) to extract feature representations. The output is a class label prediction, but the internal representation is structured: the latent space is partitioned into Z_pa, Z_ch, Z_sps (and Z_mb^C), and domain-specific parameters θ_Y^(u) and θ_ch^(u) are jointly estimated. The final classifier operates on the tuple (Z_pa, Z_sps, Z_ch, θ_Y^(u), θ_ch^(u)) rather than a single flat feature vector.

## Method

GAMA establishes that partitioning the label's Markov blanket into parents, children, and spouses (rather than treating it as a monolithic subspace) yields identifiability of the target conditional P^τ(Y|X) under a general structural equation model with domain-varying parameters. Practically, it employs three VAEs: a primary VAE mapping X→Z, a Gumbel-Softmax VAE modeling (θ_Y^(u), Z_pa)→Y, and a third VAE modeling (θ_ch^(u), Y, Z_sps)→Z_ch, with the total loss combining KL divergences, reconstruction losses, cross-entropy for Y, MSE for Z_ch, and a classification loss. The identifiability guarantee (Theorem 3) relies on a low-dimensional change assumption: conditional distributions P(Z_ch|Y,Z_sps) vary across domains along a low-dimensional manifold, enabling reconstruction of target-domain distributions from unlabeled target data via mixture weights learned from source domains.

## Results

On Office-Home, GAMA achieves an average accuracy of 77.3%, surpassing the strongest baseline iMSDA (76.2%) by 1.1 percentage points, with gains across all four target domains (Ar: 76.6, Cl: 62.6, Pr: 84.9, Rw: 84.9). On PACS, GAMA reaches 93.7% average accuracy, edging out iMSDA (93.5%) and GeNRT (92.3%), with a peak of 98.8% on the Photo domain. The method outperforms all 20 baselines on average across both benchmarks, with the largest margins over single-source methods like DAN (71.6% on Office-Home) and Source Only (68.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAMA outperforms the strongest multi-source baseline iMSDA (Kong et al., 2022) on average for both Office-Home (77.3% vs. 76.2%) and PACS (93.7% vs. 93.5%), winning on 3 of 4 Office-Home domains and 3 of 4 PACS domains (losing by 0.1% on PACS-Art). On Office-Home, GAMA also surpasses WADN (76.1%), SSD (75.4%), TFFN (75.1%), and CASR (74.7%) by 1.2–2.6 percentage points on average. On PACS, GAMA leads GeNRT (92.3%) by 1.4 points and T-SVDNet (91.3%) by 2.4 points, while single-source methods like MCD (87.0%), M3SDA (88.3%), and DANN (81.5%) trail by 2.7–12.2 points. The largest absolute gains appear on the more challenging domains: Office-Home-Art (+1.2 over iMSDA) and PACS-Sketch (+0.1 over iMSDA, +3.6 over T-SVDNet). Ablation results confirm that both the VAE components and the θ-estimation modules contribute meaningfully, with removing either causing a 1.0–1.4 point drop in Office-Home average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability-based domain adaptation line initiated by Kong et al. (2022) and Li et al. (2024b), which established subspace identifiability under restrictive assumptions (independent latent variables, invariant label distributions). The key departure is a conceptual reframe: rather than recovering the full Markov blanket of the label as a monolithic predictive set, the authors show it must be partitioned into parents, children, and spouses to achieve identifiability in a general setting where distributional shifts can occur anywhere. The proof machinery draws heavily on Zhang et al. (2024) for Markov network identifiability and on Stojanov et al. (2019) for the low-dimensional change principle that enables target-domain distribution recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first identifiability guarantee for multi-source domain adaptation in a truly general setting (no independence, no invariant marginals), which addresses a long-standing concern about the trustworthiness of representation-based adaptation methods. The practical GAMA framework achieves modest but consistent SOTA on Office-Home and PACS, validating the theory. However, the approach requires multiple source domains, a faithful DAG, and sufficient distributional variation across domains—assumptions that may not hold in many real-world deployments. Open questions include whether the intimate-neighbor structural assumption (Assumption 3) can be relaxed, whether the method scales to larger benchmarks (ImageNet-scale), and whether the VAE-based estimation of θ parameters remains stable with very few source domains.
