## Problem

Existing diffusion-based adversarial purification methods drown adversarial perturbations into isotropic noise via the forward process, but this indiscriminately destroys all frequency components of the image, causing excessive damage to semantic content. Prior frequency-domain analyses of adversarial perturbations lacked quantitative decomposition into amplitude and phase spectra separately, and no theoretical characterization existed for how the forward diffusion process disrupts each spectral component over time.

## Input / Output

The method takes an adversarial RGB image (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 256×256×3 for ImageNet) and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (EDM or guided-diffusion checkpoint) as input, producing a purified clean image of the same resolution. The purification is entirely training-agnostic: no classifier retraining or additional data is required, and the frequency-domain operations (DCT/iDCT) are applied per-channel at every reverse diffusion step. A key hyperparameter t* controls how far the forward process runs before the reverse process begins, trading off perturbation removal against semantic preservation.

## Method

FreqPure modifies the reverse diffusion process by performing DCT on the estimated clean image x_{0|t} at each time step and selectively preserving low-frequency spectral information from the adversarial input. For the amplitude spectrum, a circular mask H_A replaces the low-frequency components of the estimated image with those of the adversarial image, exploiting the finding that low-frequency amplitude is nearly unaffected by adversarial perturbations. For the phase spectrum, a projection operation constrains the estimated image's low-frequency phase to lie within a bounded range δ around the adversarial image's low-frequency phase, allowing gradual alignment with natural-image structure while retaining coarse-grained shape information. The modified spectra are combined via iDCT to form a refined x_{0|t}, which then guides the standard DDPM reverse-step sampling. Theoretically, the paper proves that the variance of both amplitude and phase differences between clean and noisy images increases monotonically with the diffusion time step t, confirming that the unmodified forward process progressively destroys all frequency components.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet-28-10 under ℓ∞ (ε=8/255), FreqPure achieves 92.19% standard accuracy and 77.35% robust accuracy under AutoAttack, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline (Bai et al., 77.08%) and REAP (70.47%). Under PGD, it reaches 59.39% robust accuracy, surpassing REAP (55.82%) by 3.57%. On ImageNet with ResNet-50 under ℓ∞ (ε=4/255), FreqPure achieves 59.77% robust accuracy, exceeding the next-best method (Zollicoffer et al., 56.54%) by 3.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPure outperforms all diffusion-based purification baselines on CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-10, ℓ∞): it beats Bai et al. (CGAP) by 0.27% on AutoAttack robust accuracy (77.35% vs 77.08%) and by 10.17% on PGD robust accuracy (59.39% vs 49.22%), and surpasses REAP (Lee & Kim) by 6.88% on AutoAttack (77.35% vs 70.47%). On the larger WRN-70-16 backbone, FreqPure leads on PGD robust accuracy (62.50% vs 56.88% for REAP) but trails Bai et al. slightly on AutoAttack (78.13% vs 79.10%). On ImageNet (ResNet-50, ℓ∞ ε=4/255), FreqPure achieves the highest robust accuracy at 59.77%, exceeding Zollicoffer et al. (56.54%) by 3.23% and Song et al. (MimicDiffusion, 51.14%) by 8.63%, while maintaining competitive standard accuracy (71.88%). Against adversarial training baselines, FreqPure's robust accuracy under AutoAttack (77.35%) substantially exceeds the best AT method (Gowal et al. 2021, 63.38%) on CIFAR-10, demonstrating the advantage of purification over training-based defenses. The ablation confirms both the amplitude spectrum exchange and phase spectrum projection modules contribute: removing either degrades AutoAttack robust accuracy from 77.35% to 76.17% or 77.35% respectively, with the full method achieving the best average (84.77%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion-based adversarial purification framework established by DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022) and REAP (Lee & Kim, 2023), adopting the same forward-then-reverse diffusion pipeline and the x_0|t estimation from the null-space model (Wang et al., 2023). The key departure is a frequency-domain decomposition: rather than treating the purified image holistically in pixel space, FreqPure selectively preserves low-frequency amplitude and phase components extracted from the adversarial input at each reverse step. The theoretical contribution formalizes why prior methods over-damage images by showing that the DDPM forward process monotonically increases variance in both amplitude and phase differences across all frequencies as t grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain perspective provides a principled design criterion for purification: retain what adversarial perturbations barely touch (low-frequency content and structure) while letting the diffusion model regenerate what they heavily corrupt (high-frequency details). This reframes purification from a pure generative task to a guided reconstruction with explicit priors. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model, the fixed hyperparameters (D_A, D_P, δ) that require manual tuning, and the theoretical analysis's dependence on the SNR >> 1 assumption. Open questions include whether the frequency thresholds can be learned adaptively per image, whether the approach extends to video or 3D data, and whether the modest standard-accuracy trade-off observed on larger backbones (WideResNet-70-16, ImageNet ResNet-50) can be eliminated.
