## Problem

Decoder-only autoregressive Transformers have been largely overlooked for time series forecasting (TSF), with most SOTA models relying on encoder-only Transformers, MLPs, or linear layers. Additionally, existing AR attention mechanisms—particularly gated linear attention with exponential decay—struggle to decouple short-term local fluctuations from stable long-term and seasonal patterns, which are the dominant temporal structures in TSF data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series S ∈ R^{L×C} split into a historical window S_I (length L_I) and a forecasting horizon S_P (length L_P), processed channel-independently with RevIN normalization. Input is tokenized into non-overlapping patches of size L_P (matching the forecast length), so each autoregressive step predicts the entire future horizon in one shot, avoiding the error accumulation that plagues iterative one-step AR forecasting. The output is the predicted future values Ŝ_P ∈ R^{L_P×C} per channel.

## Method

WAVE attention augments any existing AR attention mechanism with a moving-average (MA) term by introducing an indirect MA weight generation scheme. Rather than explicitly inverting the (I+Θ) matrix (which would restore O(N²) cost), it uses the AR residuals r_j = v_{j+1} − o_j^AR as value inputs to a linear RNN, exploiting the algebraic identity B = Θ(I+Θ)^{−1} so that the implicitly generated weights β_{t−1,j} = φ_q^MA(q_{t−1}^MA)·φ_k^MA(k_j^MA) produce valid MA outputs at O(N) cost. The activation functions φ_q^MA = −LeakyReLU(−q/√d) and φ_k^MA = σ(αk/√d) constrain β to [−1, 0], ensuring the implicit Θ decays away from the diagonal as desired for local smoothing. Weight sharing between AR and MA terms (sharing W_q, setting W_v to identity) keeps parameter count identical to the pure AR model. The approach is applied uniformly across five attention variants—softmax, linear, gated linear, element-wise linear, and fixed attention—consistently improving each over its AR counterpart on 12 TSF benchmarks.

## Results

On 12 TSF datasets with L_I=512 and L_P∈{12,24,48,96}, the best WAVE variant (Lin Attn+ARMA) achieves an average rank of 2.33 with 25 first-place rankings out of 48 sub-experiments, substantially outperforming the best pure AR model (Lin Attn, AvgRank 4.27) and all SOTA baselines. The best baseline, CATS, achieves AvgRank 5.94 with only 4 first-place rankings, while iTransformer (7.27), PatchTST (9.79), DLinear (11.25), FITS (12.04), and Enc-Former (12.81) trail further. Every WAVE variant consistently outperforms its corresponding pure AR counterpart across all 12 datasets, with the largest gains observed for gated linear attention (e.g., ETTm1: 0.407→0.260 MSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAVE Transformers (Lin Attn+ARMA, AvgRank 2.33) decisively outperform all six SOTA baselines: CATS (5.94), iTransformer (7.27), PatchTST (9.79), DLinear (11.25), FITS (12.04), and Enc-Former (12.81), winning 25 of 48 sub-experiments at first place versus at most 11 for PatchTST. Even the weakest WAVE variant (GLin Attn+ARMA, AvgRank 6.23) beats DLinear, FITS, and Enc-Former, and is competitive with CATS and iTransformer. The ARMA structure provides consistent gains over pure AR across every attention type (e.g., Lin Attn 4.27→Lin Attn+ARMA 2.33; Std Attn 7.96→Std Attn+ARMA 4.29), confirming the improvement is structural rather than due to added capacity. In the MEGA comparison (Table 6), WAVE outperforms MEGA on all 7 tested datasets (e.g., ETTm1: 0.222 vs. 0.412; PEMS03: 0.096 vs. 0.161), showing that decoupling MA into a separate term is more effective than applying EMA directly to AR weights. Linear attention variants (Lin Attn, ELin Attn) outperform softmax attention in TSF, aligning with prior findings that simpler non-normalized attention generalizes better on time-varying distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-dominant assumption in TSF—that encoder-only or linear models are necessary for competitive performance—can be challenged by a properly tokenized decoder-only Transformer, and that a 50-year-old statistical structure (ARMA) provides a principled, low-cost way to decouple short-term and long-term temporal effects in attention. The indirect MA weight generation is a practical engineering contribution that preserves O(N) complexity and parameter count. However, the channel-independent formulation leaves inter-series relationships unmodeled, and the paper does not explore whether WAVE attention transfers to other sequence modeling domains (NLP, audio) or scales to large pretrained models, leaving its broader applicability an open question.
