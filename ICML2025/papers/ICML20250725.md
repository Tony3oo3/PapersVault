## Problem

Existing world models rely heavily on substantial action-labeled data and costly training to achieve precise action controllability, making it difficult to adapt to novel environments with heterogeneous action spaces through limited interactions. This limits their applicability across broader domains where defining a unified action format is infeasible and collecting labels across many environments is prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The latent action autoencoder takes two consecutive 256×256 video frames and produces a 32-dimensional continuous latent action vector representing the transition between them. The autoregressive world model consumes up to 6 historical frames (encoded via SVD's image encoder) plus the latent action (concatenated with timestep and CLIP embeddings) to denoise and predict the next single frame. For action transfer, the system takes a demonstration video and an initial frame from a new context, extracting the latent action sequence from the demonstration and reusing it autoregressively to generate a 20-frame video in the target scene.

## Method

AdaWorld comprises two components: a 500M-parameter Transformer-based latent action autoencoder and a 1.5B-parameter SVD-based autoregressive world model. The autoencoder uses an information bottleneck design where a compact 32-dim latent action must suffice to predict the next frame given the previous one, forcing it to encode only the critical action while discarding context; a β-VAE objective (β=2×10⁻⁴) balances expressiveness against disentanglement. The world model, initialized from Stable Video Diffusion, is modified to denoise one frame at a time with the latent action injected into both timestep and CLIP conditioning, and maintains a short-term memory of historical frames with noise augmentation to reduce long-term drift. Action-aware pretraining on ~2000M frames from 1000+ diverse environments (Gym Retro, Procgen, Open X-Embodiment, Ego4D, SSv2, MiraData) enables the model to learn a unified action-conditioned prediction interface. Adaptation to new environments requires only extracting and averaging latent actions from ~100 labeled samples per action to initialize the control interface, followed by 800 finetuning steps—far more efficient than training from action-agnostic pretraining.

## Results

On world model adaptation (Table 2), after only 800 finetuning steps with 100 samples per action, AdaWorld achieves the best PSNR and LPIPS across all four unseen environments: 23.58/0.327 (Habitat), 21.59/0.457 (Minecraft), 22.92/0.335 (DMLab), and 21.60/0.436 (nuScenes). On action transfer (Table 1), AdaWorld achieves 70.5% human-judged success on LIBERO and 61.5% on SSv2, compared to 0% and 1% for the action-agnostic baseline. For visual planning in Procgen games (Table 3), AdaWorld with finetuning reaches 56.67% average success rate, substantially outperforming the action-agnostic baseline (26.00%) and Q-learning (27.17%), approaching the oracle upper bound of 80.67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaWorld consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the action-agnostic pretraining baseline (zeros as action conditions), AdaWorld gains +3.24 PSNR on Habitat, +2.15 PSNR on Minecraft, +1.96 PSNR on DMLab, and +0.74 PSNR on nuScenes, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS in all cases. The optical flow baseline (UniMatch flows downsampled to 16×16) is the strongest competitor but still trails AdaWorld by 1.09 PSNR on Habitat and 0.66 PSNR on nuScenes, indicating that learned latent actions capture more semantically meaningful action information than raw motion. The discrete latent action baseline (VQ-VAE with 8 codes, following Genie) is slightly worse than the flow baseline on most environments, confirming that the continuous latent action space provides superior expressiveness. In visual planning, AdaWorld without any finetuning (44.83% average) already surpasses the finetuned action-agnostic baseline (26.00%), and with 500 steps of finetuning reaches 56.67%, far exceeding Q-learning (27.17%). The method's generality is further validated by applying action-aware pretraining to iVideoGPT, which improves BAIR simulation PSNR from 16.59 to 17.40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of pretraining world models on action-agnostic videos by introducing action-aware pretraining with self-supervised latent actions extracted via a β-VAE information bottleneck. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Video Diffusion for the autoregressive world model and on Genie's spatiotemporal Transformer for the latent action encoder, but replaces Genie's 8-code discrete VQ-VAE with a continuous latent action space to enable transfer, composition, and flexible adaptation. The key conceptual departure is that action information is incorporated during pretraining rather than added post-hoc, making the resulting model inherently adaptable to new action spaces with minimal finetuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to transfer demonstrated actions across contexts without any training, and to adapt to new environments with as few as 100 samples per action, addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying world models across diverse domains. The continuous latent action space also enables novel capabilities like action composition and customizable control granularity that discrete approaches cannot support. However, the model does not operate at real-time frequency, struggles with long-horizon rollouts beyond the initial scene, and cannot generate genuinely novel content—limitations that constrain its use as a full interactive simulator. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences, and whether the SVD-based backbone can be replaced with more efficient architectures.
