## Problem

While Chain-of-Thought prompting has dramatically improved reasoning in LLMs, its effect on Large Multimodal Models remains uncharacterized: existing benchmarks only score final answers, leaving the intermediate reasoning process unexamined. No prior work systematically assesses whether CoT steps are logically valid and faithful, whether CoT helps or harms perception-heavy tasks, or whether the long reflective chains in o1-like models are actually efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image with a text question (multiple-choice or free-form) and the LMM's full CoT response. The evaluation pipeline additionally consumes human-annotated key steps (inference conclusions and image captions) and reference image captions per question. The output is a structured set of six scores: F1/precision/recall for quality, stability/efficacy for robustness, and relevance rate/reflection quality for efficiency, each computed per question and aggregated across 1,130 questions spanning six domains.

## Method

MME-CoT introduces a three-axis CoT evaluation suite that uses GPT-4o as an automated judge over human-annotated ground-truth key steps. For quality, recall measures the fraction of annotated key steps (inference conclusions and image captions) that appear in the model's response, while precision partitions the response into atomic steps and checks each for correctness against annotations, yielding an F1 score. For robustness, the same questions are answered under both a CoT prompt and a direct-answer prompt; stability is the accuracy difference on perception tasks (should be ≥ 0) and efficacy is the difference on reasoning tasks. For efficiency, relevance rate computes the fraction of generated steps that contribute to solving the question (scaled by a factor of 0.8 to amplify inter-model differences), and reflection quality identifies reflection steps via linguistic markers (e.g., 'Wait', 'Alternatively') and judges whether each validly corrects a prior error or introduces a new insight. The dataset itself comprises 1,130 questions across 17 subcategories in six domains, with 3,865 key-step annotations produced through a two-phase GPT-4o-then-human pipeline, and a two-stage model-plus-expert classification separating perception from reasoning tasks.

## Results

On MME-CoT, Kimi k1.5 achieves the highest CoT quality F1 score of 64.2, narrowly surpassing GPT-4o (64.0) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (63.8). Among open-source models, QVQ-72B leads with an F1 of 62.0, outperforming its base model Qwen2-VL-72B (56.2) by 5.8 points. For robustness, GPT-4o attains the highest average score (49.9), while most models exhibit negative stability scores, indicating CoT degrades perception performance (worst: InternVL2.5-8B at −6.8%). On efficiency, QVQ-72B and Virgo-72B achieve only ~60% reflection quality, meaning roughly 40% of their reflection steps fail to contribute to correct answers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kimi k1.5 achieves the best overall CoT quality (F1 = 64.2) with the highest precision (92.0), surpassing GPT-4o (F1 = 64.0, precision 85.4) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (F1 = 63.8). Among open-source models, QVQ-72B (F1 = 62.0) significantly outperforms Qwen2-VL-72B (F1 = 56.2) by 5.8 points, demonstrating that reflection-based fine-tuning substantially improves reasoning quality. GPT-4o leads in robustness (avg. 49.9) and recall (81.4), while Kimi k1.5 shows the best recall among reflection models (89.8). A critical finding is that most models suffer negative stability: InternVL2.5-8B drops 6.8% on perception tasks under CoT prompting, Qwen2-VL-72B drops 6.3%, and only InternVL2.5-MPO variants and LLaVA-OV-72B achieve non-negative stability. For reflection quality, QVQ-72B (61.7%) and Virgo-72B (60.6%) show that ~40% of reflection steps are ineffective, while Kimi k1.5 (72.2%) performs better but still has over 25% invalid reflections. LLaVA-CoT, Mulberry, and Virgo produce unreliable robustness scores because they ignore direct prompts and generate extended rationales regardless.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only CoT evaluation lineage (ROSCOE, ReCEval, LLM-Reasoners) into the multimodal domain, where prior benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MMMU-Pro, OlympiadBench) have focused exclusively on final-answer correctness while leaving the intermediate reasoning process unexamined. It departs from the outcome-oriented paradigm by introducing process-level metrics (step-level precision/recall, relevance rate, reflection quality) that require annotated key steps and reference image captions. The LLM-as-judge methodology (using GPT-4o for step matching and correctness verification) is repurposed from text reasoning evaluation to handle the interleaved visual-logical structure of multimodal CoT, where image caption steps and logical inference steps must be jointly assessed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CoT prompting actively degrades perception-task performance across most tested models (negative stability scores, worst at −6.8% for InternVL2.5-8B) challenges the prevailing assumption that step-by-step reasoning is universally beneficial and has direct implications for deployment strategies that default to CoT. The ~40% invalid reflection rate in o1-like models (QVQ, Virgo) and even 25%+ in Kimi k1.5 raises open questions about whether longer thinking with self-correction is a reliable path to improved accuracy or merely adds latency. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as the evaluation judge (mitigated by 86–98% human agreement), a moderate dataset size (1,130 questions), and evaluation restricted to a specific set of models; broader generalization to future architectures and video/3D modalities remains open.
