## Problem

While weight-only quantization of LLMs to 3–4 bits is well-solved, quantizing the KV cache and activations simultaneously has remained much harder, with state-of-the-art methods like SpinQuant relying on scalar (uniform) quantization that wastes bit budget on rarely-used grid points outside the typical Gaussian support. This paper addresses the gap by showing that nested lattice quantizers—proven information-theoretically optimal for approximate matrix multiplication—can be made practical and drop-in for LLM inference, closing the perplexity gap to near-zero at 4-bit full quantization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes weight matrices, activation vectors, and KV cache entries from transformer layers. Each input vector is L2-normalized, rotated by a fast Hadamard matrix, and partitioned into 8-dimensional blocks; each block is encoded as a Voronoi code index (log₂q bits per entry) plus a 2-bit scaling-coefficient index (k=4 betas, compressed via zstd to yield ~3.99 effective bits/entry). Dot products between two quantized vectors are computed directly from the code indices and beta values without full dequantization, enabling efficient GEMV during generation.

## Method

NestQuant replaces scalar uniform quantization with vector quantization based on the 8-dimensional Gosset lattice E8 using Voronoi codes (Conway–Sloane nested lattice codes), where the codebook is Λ ∩ qV_Λ and encoding/decoding complexity is independent of the rate R = log₂q. To handle the fact that real LLM vectors are not perfectly Gaussian and that small-dimension lattices suffer overload, the method applies (i) L2 normalization plus Hadamard rotation to Gaussianize inputs, and (ii) a multi-beta strategy that takes a union of k scaled Voronoi codebooks, with optimal β values found via dynamic programming over calibration data. A key innovation, QA-LDLQ, modifies the standard LDLQ weight-quantization procedure to account for activation quantization noise by replacing the Hessian H with H+J and the target weight W with W̃ = WH(H+J)⁻¹, which is critical for layers with high amplification ratios. The full pipeline is a post-training, calibration-only procedure (no fine-tuning) that serves as a drop-in replacement for any matrix multiplication step in a transformer.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B at 4-bit quantization, NestQuant (q=14, k=4) achieves a Wikitext2 perplexity of 6.6 in full quantization (weights + KV cache + activations), compared to 7.3 for SpinQuant and OstQuant, and 8.2 for QuaRot (unquantized baseline: 6.14). This represents a >55% reduction in perplexity gap versus the unquantized model. On zero-shot benchmarks, NestQuant achieves 0.71 average accuracy in full quantization, outperforming SpinQuant (0.68) and QuaRot (0.67). In the weight-only regime, NestQuant reaches 6.3 perplexity and 0.72 zero-shot average, matching or exceeding SpinQuant (6.5 ppl, 0.72 avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NestQuant consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every quantization regime. In full quantization (W4A4KV4) on Llama-3-8B, NestQuant achieves 6.6 Wikitext2 perplexity, beating SpinQuant (7.3) and OstQuant (7.3) by 0.7 and QuaRot (8.2) by 1.6. In the weight-only regime, NestQuant (6.3 ppl) surpasses SpinQuant (6.5) and GPTQ (7.2), and in weights+KV it beats SpinQuant (6.4 vs 6.6). On Llama-2-7B weight-only 4-bit, NestQuant (5.53) outperforms QuIP# (5.56) and QuaRot (5.60). For the 4-bit weights+KV regime on Llama-3-8B, NestQuant (6.37) substantially outperforms DuQuant (8.06) and OstQuant (7.24). Across model sizes up to 70B (Table 2), NestQuant maintains its lead, e.g., achieving 3.61 ppl on Llama-3-70B full quantization versus 4.01 for OstQuant and 6.66 for QuaRot.

## Relation to Prior Work

NestQuant operationalizes the information-theoretic result of Ordentlich & Polyanskiy (2024) showing that nested lattice quantizers achieve the optimal rate-distortion tradeoff for approximate matrix multiplication, by instantiating the construction with the 8-dimensional Gosset (E8) lattice and Conway-Sloane Voronoi codes. It departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM quantization (SpinQuant, QuaRot, OstQuant, GPTQ) which relies on scalar uniform quantization with cubic (ℓ∞) shaping, replacing it with vector quantization on a lattice Voronoi region that captures Gaussian mass more efficiently. The weight quantization mechanism extends the LDLQ algorithm from QuIP# with a QA-LDLQ correction that accounts for activation quantization noise, and the random Hadamard rotation for outlier smoothing follows the approach of QuaRot and SpinQuant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent superiority across model sizes (1B–70B) and quantization regimes, particularly the finding that 4-bit full quantization (W4A4KV4) outperforms prior 4-bit weight+activation-only quantization (W4A4KV16), suggests that the shaping gain from lattice quantization is large enough to overcome the additional distortion from KV-cache quantization. Limitations include the restriction to 8-dimensional blocks (the E8 lattice is the highest dimension with a known efficient closest-vector algorithm), the need for calibration data to compute Hessians and optimal β coefficients, and the computational overhead of vector quantization relative to scalar rounding. Open questions include whether higher-dimensional lattices with tractable decoding can further close the gap to the information-theoretic limit, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-Transformer architectures or to quantization of attention scores themselves.
