## Problem

Existing safety certificate techniques for stochastic control require either complete transition dynamics or fully observable states, but real systems contain latent variables that induce 'spurious' distribution shifts between offline and online observed statistics even when the underlying mechanistic dynamics are unchanged. This mismatch breaks data-driven safety verification and risk estimation, and no prior method handles this specific type of distribution shift while simultaneously guaranteeing long-term safety with persistent action feasibility. The paper is the first to integrate causal reinforcement learning into safety certificate design to address this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of observable state-action sequences (generated by an unknown behavioral policy that depends on unobserved latent variables), the current observable state X_t, and a nominal policy action U^n. It produces a myopic safe control action U_t obtained by solving a constrained optimization that minimizes deviation from U^n subject to the safety certificate constraint S(X_t, u, t) ≥ 0. The safety certificate itself is a scalar mapping S: X × U × Z → R evaluated in real time, and the guarantee is that the long-term safe probability P(C(X_t) ∩ ... ∩ C(X_H) | X_0) ≥ 1 − ε for all t, with the additional property that a feasible action always exists at every time step.

## Method

The key insight is to represent the long-term safe probability Ψ^π(x,t) as a marginalized value function V^π of an auxiliary MDP whose state augments the observable state with a countdown timer, and whose dynamics freeze the state upon safety violation (Proposition 3.1). The invariance condition E[V^π(Ŷ_{t+1})|Ŷ_t,U_t] − V^π(Ŷ_t) ≥ 0 is then shown to be equivalently checkable via the Q-function as S(X_t,U_t,t) = Q^π(Ŷ_t,U_t) − E_{U~π}[Q^π(Ŷ_t,U)] ≥ 0 (Lemma 3.3), avoiding the need to know the online transition kernel. Persistent feasibility follows because argmax_u Q^π(Ŷ_t,u) always satisfies the constraint (Theorem 3.4). To learn Q^π from offline data despite latent-variable confounding, the method applies frontdoor adjustment through an observable mediator variable, iteratively solving a Bellman-type regression (Algorithm 1 transforms the raw offline data into the auxiliary MDP format, and Algorithm 2 performs the offline Q-learning followed by real-time constrained control).

## Results

On a discrete driving scenario (H=10, ε=0.2, 100 simulations × 100 trajectories from X₀=[0,0]ᵀ), the proposed method maintains the long-term safe probability P̂π,π(C(X_t)∩…∩C(X_H)|X₀) above the 1−ε=0.8 threshold for all t∈{0,…,H}, satisfying the safety objective (6). The DTCBF baseline, evaluated using offline statistics, fails to meet this threshold even when its own safety condition is satisfied. The proposed method achieves this without access to the latent variable W (road slipperiness) or the ground truth transition dynamics, relying solely on an unbiased Q-function estimate from causal RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: the Discrete-Time Control Barrier Function (DTCBF) of Cosner et al. (RSS 2023), which evaluates its safety condition E[h(X_{t+1})|X_t,U_t] ≥ αh(X_t)+δ using the offline transition statistics. The proposed method clearly outperforms DTCBF on the long-term safety metric: it sustains the required 1−ε=0.8 safe probability across all time steps, while DTCBF's long-term safe probability drops below the threshold (visible in Figure 2). The root cause of DTCBF's failure is the spurious distribution shift: the behavioral policy in the offline data observes the latent road-slipperiness variable W and brakes harder on slippery roads, biasing the offline transition statistics relative to the online (marginalized) statistics. The proposed method avoids this by using a frontdoor-adjusted Q-function (via mediator variable M_t) that recovers the online transition kernel from offline data, thereby evaluating the safety certificate S(X_t,U_t,t) under the correct distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously isolated research threads: probabilistic safety certificates (building on the invariance-in-probability-space framework of Wang et al. 2022 and Jing & Nakahira 2022, and the value-function representation of long-term safe probability from Hoshino & Nakahira 2024) and causal reinforcement learning for confounded MDPs (specifically the mediator-variable/frontdoor-adjustment approach of Shi et al. 2024). The key departure from prior safe control work is the explicit treatment of 'spurious' distribution shifts—where observed statistics diverge between offline and online settings purely due to latent variables, even when the underlying mechanistic dynamics are unchanged. The key departure from prior causal RL work is the application of these techniques to safety certificate design with a provable persistent feasibility guarantee, rather than to policy evaluation or optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important vulnerability: standard data-driven safe control methods that conflate observed statistics with mechanistic dynamics can be silently broken by latent variables, a failure mode that has received little attention in the safe control literature. The framework is modular in principle (any causal RL method satisfying Assumption 2.1 can be plugged in), but the current instantiation requires a mediator variable (Assumption 3.5) and is demonstrated only on a discrete-state driving toy problem with a single myopic baseline (DTCBF), leaving open questions about scalability to continuous spaces, the availability of suitable mediators in real systems, and how the approach performs against stronger long-term safety baselines. The authors also flag a promising open direction: defining new measures of probabilistic reachability that are learnable from observed statistics without requiring complete system models.
