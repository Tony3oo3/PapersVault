## Problem

Existing time series foundation models are either deterministic (trained with MSE or quantile loss), rely on parametric density priors (e.g., Gaussian mixtures in Moirai), or discretize continuous values into tokens (Chronos). These approaches suffer from mode collapse on heterogeneous large-scale data, produce over-smooth predictions, and cannot generate diverse probable futures, limiting their reliability for real-world decision-making. Sundial addresses this gap as the first family of truly generative time series foundation models that learn flexible, non-parametric predictive distributions natively on continuous-valued series.

## Input / Output

The model accepts univariate time series of arbitrary length (up to 2880 time points) as a lookback window, which is divided into patches of size 16 and embedded via a shared MLP that concatenates each patch with a binary padding mask. During pre-training, the S3 format is used to handle multivariate data in a univariate paradigm, with per-variable normalization. At inference, the model generates multiple raw future patches (prediction length F=16 or 720, where F>P enables multi-patch prediction to reduce autoregressive steps); statistics such as median, quantiles, and prediction intervals are then estimated from a set of 20–100 independently sampled predictions, each produced by a K-step (K=50) Euler integration of the learned velocity field.

## Method

Sundial combines a decoder-only Transformer backbone (with RoPE, Pre-LN, FlashAttention, and KV Cache) with a novel TimeFlow Loss that replaces standard MSE or cross-entropy objectives. TimeFlow Loss is a conditional flow-matching objective: given the per-token representation h_i from the Transformer, a small FM-Net (MLP with AdaLN conditioning) predicts the velocity field u_t(y_i^(t) | h_i) that transports Gaussian noise toward the ground-truth patch y_i along a linear optimal-transport path, with t sampled uniformly from [0,1]. This allows the model to learn arbitrary predictive distributions per token without specifying any parametric prior or discretizing values. At inference, multiple predictions are generated by starting from different Gaussian noises and integrating the velocity field over K uniform steps, while the Transformer's lookback representation is computed once and shared across all samples, yielding millisecond-level inference. The family is pre-trained on TimeBench (1.032 trillion time points from real-world and synthetic sources) with multi-patch prediction to reduce autoregressive steps.

## Results

On Time-Series-Library (TSLib), Sundial Large achieves the best average MSE of 0.290 and MAE of 0.338 across six datasets and four prediction lengths, winning 16/24 first-place counts in both MSE and MAE. On GIFT-Eval, Sundial attains the best MASE (0.673) among all 97 configurations and second-best CRPS (0.472) behind only TimesFM (0.465), surpassing the best supervised model PatchTST (MASE 0.762, CRPS 0.496). On the FEV leaderboard, Sundial outperforms 70% of statistical and supervised deep models while achieving a 35× inference speedup over Chronos, with zero-shot predictions generated in a few milliseconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sundial Large outperforms the previous state-of-the-art Time-MoE Ultra (2.4B parameters) by 7.57% in average MSE and 4.71% in average MAE on TSLib, despite using roughly 5× fewer parameters. On GIFT-Eval, Sundial achieves the best MASE (0.673) among all 13 baselines including supervised models like PatchTST (0.762) and iTransformer (0.802), and is second only to TimesFM in CRPS (0.472 vs. 0.465). On the FEV leaderboard, Sundial ranks second among zero-shot foundation models behind Chronos but with a 35× faster inference speed, and surpasses all statistical methods (Auto ARIMA, Auto Theta) and most supervised deep models (DeepAR, TiDE, N-BEATS). Among training objectives, TimeFlow Loss yields the best zero-shot MSE (0.290 avg) and CRPS (0.5050 on GIFT-Eval), outperforming both diffusion-based (0.314 MSE, 0.5340 CRPS) and MSE-based (0.296 MSE, 0.6420 CRPS) alternatives. Chronos, which uses point-level discrete tokenization, is notably weaker on long-term forecasting (e.g., 0.555 MSE on ETTm1 vs. Sundial's 0.331), while Moirai's mixture-of-distributions prior still lags behind Sundial's generative approach on both point and probabilistic metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sundial builds on the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and adapts it to autoregressive time series forecasting via the proposed TimeFlow Loss, which conditions a small flow-matching network on Transformer representations. It departs from prior time series foundation models (TimesFM, Timer, Time-MoE, Moirai) that rely on deterministic MSE/quantile objectives or parametric density priors, and from Chronos which uses discrete tokenization, by operating natively on continuous-valued patch tokens without any prior distribution specification. The architecture also incorporates LLM techniques (RoPE, Pre-LN, FlashAttention, KV Cache) that were previously neglected in time series foundation model development, and adopts the S3 univariate pre-training format from Timer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sundial is the first family of truly generative time series foundation models, enabling probabilistic forecasting without parametric priors and mitigating mode collapse on heterogeneous large-scale data. The trillion-point TimeBench dataset and the demonstrated scaling behavior (15.38% loss reduction from Small to Large) provide a foundation for future time series pre-training research. Open questions include generalization to very high-frequency data, multivariate pre-training to exploit variate correlations, and more sophisticated sampling/post-processing strategies beyond naive Gaussian noise initialization. The millisecond-level inference speed makes probabilistic forecasting practically deployable in real-time decision-making pipelines.
