## Problem

The open-source ecosystem for LLM post-training data curation is in its infancy, with no large-scale public dataset of synthetically generated chat responses from diverse open-weight models available for comparative analysis. This gap prevents researchers from systematically studying how the choice of data generating model (DGM) affects downstream SFT performance, since industry labs' capabilities far outstrip those of academic labs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250660/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes real human chat prompts from WildChat-1M (collected via HuggingFace Spaces with explicit user consent) and generates multi-turn responses using 54 distinct open-weight DGMs (19 unique pretrained bases plus 35 post-trained variants, ranging from 0.5B to 104B parameters) via VLLM inference, yielding over 125 million chat transcripts. The resulting WILDCHAT-50M dataset is then curated into RE-WILD, a 3-source SFT mixture of ~366k conversations, which is used to fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B-Base. The final output is a family of SFT checkpoints evaluated across nine benchmarks spanning generalist chat, instruction following, and world knowledge.

## Method

Data collection runs on a 12×8 H100 cluster over ~2 months (~10,000 H100-hours), with each model distributed across up to 8 GPUs and context windows heuristically maximized per model (2,048–20,000 tokens); the largest models use FP8 quantization while others run in bfloat16. RE-WILD is a deliberately simple 3-source blend—WildChat-Q72 (246,750 conversations), MMLU Auxiliary Train (99,800), and Tulu 3 Persona Hub Algebra (20,000)—chosen heuristically for complementary skillsets (chat, world knowledge, math) and totaling only ~40% the size of Tulu-3's SFT mixture. SFT training uses a modified Axolotl framework with AdamW (lr 2e-5), single epoch, cosine schedule, 8-step gradient accumulation, and bf16 precision on a single 4×H100 node (~5.5 hours per 250k-conversation run). Key empirical findings include: DGM choice has large, unpredictable effects on downstream benchmarks with no single model dominating; blending multiple DGMs provides no benefit over the best single DGM; formal stylistic elements (Markdown/HTML tags) are strongly inherited during SFT (mean proportional frequency ~0.91–1.05); and SDQ on generalist chat is driven by domain-agnostic factors like comprehensiveness, structural clarity, and tone rather than domain-specific knowledge.

## Results

RE-WILD, an SFT data mixture built from WILDCHAT-50M, outperforms the Tulu-3 SFT mixture using only ~40% as many samples (250k vs. ~600k conversations). In the DGM comparison (Table 2), Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (Q72) produces the strongest downstream SFT model, achieving 6.86 MTBench, 41.00 AlpacaEval (length-controlled), and 0.65 MixEval, while the weakest DGM (Cohere-Command-R Plus) scores 6.05 MTBench and 13.44 AlpacaEval. No single DGM dominates all nine benchmarks: on three of nine, the best-performing model has fewer than 10B parameters, and parameter count is not a reliable predictor of synthetic data quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE-WILD outperforms all three baselines—Tulu-3 SFT, Magpie Align SFT, and Ultrachat—on aggregate across nine benchmarks, with particularly strong gains on generalist chat (MixEval, AlpacaEval, MTBench) and instruction following (IFEval). The key advantage over Tulu-3 is efficiency: RE-WILD uses only ~250k conversations (40% of Tulu-3's SFT mix size) while still achieving superior benchmark scores. Among the DGMs tested, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct is the strongest teacher for chat-quality benchmarks (41.00 AlpacaEval vs. 21.12 for Llama-3.1-8B and 13.44 for Cohere-Command-R+), but no DGM uniformly dominates: Qwen2-7B and Cohere-Command-R+ lead on BBH (0.49), and Llama-3.1-8B leads on IFEval (0.38) and MMLU Pro (0.33). Blending DGMs provides no benefit over using a single high-quality DGM, and fine-tuned models do not reliably inherit the specific benchmark strengths of their DGMs (agreement rate of 0.5, i.e., chance level).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends WildChat-1M (Zhao et al., 2024) by replacing its GPT-only responses with synthetic outputs from 50+ open-weight models, and adopts the human-in-the-loop SFT data curation methodology from Tulu 3 (Lambert et al., 2024). Rather than proposing a new post-training algorithm, it reframes the question around data-generating model (DGM) quality: which model's outputs make the best SFT targets? This positions the work as a large-scale empirical study of synthetic data quality (SDQ) that prior work has not conducted at this scale, filling a gap the authors identify between industry labs' capabilities and the open research ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WILDCHAT-50M is 50× larger than the next-largest public chat dataset and provides a critical resource for studying post-training data curation, while RE-WILD demonstrates that a simple 3-source mix with a well-chosen DGM can outperform the more complex Tulu-3 SFT blend using only 40% as many samples. Key open questions include whether these findings extend beyond SFT to preference-based post-training (DPO, RLHF), whether DGM diversity helps on smaller task-specific datasets, and how to systematically characterize what makes one DGM's outputs higher quality than another's. The paper's scope is limited to generalist chat benchmarks and does not evaluate specialized domains like coding or legal reasoning.
