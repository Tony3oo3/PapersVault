## Problem

Integrating spatial audio context (direction-of-arrival) into LLMs for speech understanding remains largely unexplored, especially for wearable devices. Existing spatial LLM work like BAT handles only non-verbal sounds with a median DoA error of 88.52°, while traditional spatial sensing requires large microphone arrays that are impractical for miniature wearables. No prior system jointly performs DoA estimation and speech ASR within an LLM framework using a monaural setup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251784/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes monaural speech audio captured through an Owlet microstructure (16 kHz, single channel) where the physical structure encodes angular information into the signal's frequency response. Two channels are used: the microstructure-covered microphone feeds the DoA encoder, and the external microphone feeds the Whisper speech encoder. For spatial ASR, inputs are 30-second clips producing 80-mel-bin spectrograms; for multi-DoA soundscaping, inputs are 8-second clips with 128-mel-bin spectrograms. Outputs are natural-language LLM responses containing DoA angles in degrees, speech transcriptions/summaries, speaker counts, and multi-DoA angle lists.

## Method

SING uses a lightweight CNN spatial encoder (3 conv blocks + FC, 16.3M params) to extract DoA embeddings from mel-spectrograms of the microstructure channel, and Whisper-large-v3 to produce linguistic embeddings pooled to [128, 1024]. A LLaVA-style linear projection layer maps the spatial embeddings into the LLM's input space, and the fused representations are aligned with LLaMA-3.2 3B via two-stage training: first freezing the LLM to train the projection, then LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, alpha 16) on task-specific prompts. For multi-DoA soundscaping, a number-of-speaker encoder selects among five dedicated DoA encoders (1–5 speakers), and their concatenated embeddings are similarly projected into the LLM. The entire pipeline is trained on a synthetic 400-hour dataset created by convolving LibriSpeech with one-time-calibrated Owlet impulse responses at 1° angular resolution across 360°.

## Results

SING achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 25.72° for Direction of Arrival (DoA) estimation using a single monaural microphone with microstructure sensing, a substantial improvement over BAT's 88.52° MAE. For spatially-aware ASR (DoA + speech transcription jointly), SING reports a word error rate (WER) of 5.3%, while without the DoA component it achieves 1.8% WER. In the multi-source soundscaping task (up to 5 simultaneous speakers), SING maintains MAE values between 17.08° and 28.11° across 1–5 sources, with a median DoA error as low as 13°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BAT (Zheng et al., 2024), SING reduces the DoA MAE from 88.52° to 25.72°—a 3.4× improvement—while also performing speech transcription, a capability BAT lacks entirely. Compared to SALMONN (Tang et al., 2023), SING's spatial ASR WER of 5.3% is higher than SALMONN's 2.2%, but this reflects the added complexity of jointly estimating DoA; when spatial features are removed, SING actually outperforms SALMONN (1.8% vs. 2.2% WER). In the multi-source DoA setting (Table 2), SING dominates both SELDNet (Adavanne et al., 2018; 90.03° MAE, single-source only, 4 microphones) and AudioMAE (Huang et al., 2022; 43.79° MAE, single-source only), achieving 25.72° MAE with one microphone and extending to 5 simultaneous sources with MAE as low as 17.08°. The MEEM metric further highlights SING's hardware efficiency: with only one microphone, its MEEM equals its MAE, whereas SELDNet's MEEM is 360.12 (4× its MAE) due to its 4-microphone array.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate research threads: the Owlet microstructure-based monaural spatial sensing hardware (Garg et al., 2021) and the LLaVA-style linear projection alignment paradigm for multimodal LLMs (Liu et al., 2024). It departs from BAT (Zheng et al., 2024), which introduced spatial reasoning in LLMs but only for non-verbal sounds with a high 88.52° MAE, and from speech LLMs like SALMONN that handle ASR without any spatial awareness. The key architectural contribution is fusing a lightweight CNN-based DoA encoder (16.3M params) with Whisper speech embeddings via a simple linear projection into LLaMA-3.2 3B, fine-tuned with LoRA for on-device deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly perform DoA estimation and speech ASR within an LLM using a single monaural microphone, opening a path toward spatially-aware wearable assistants (meeting summarization, AR navigation, accessibility). However, the entire evaluation relies on a synthetic dataset created by convolving LibriSpeech with calibrated impulse responses, leaving real-world robustness (moving speakers, complex rooms, overlapping speech) unvalidated. The WER degradation from 1.8% to 5.3% when spatial features are added, and the 25.72° MAE (still large for practical attribution in meetings), highlight open questions about the accuracy ceiling of microstructure-based monaural sensing and whether the LLM integration adds value beyond a standalone DoA+ASR pipeline.
