## Problem

Constrained LLM decoding enforces syntactic correctness by masking invalid tokens against a formal grammar, but prior empirical work (Tam et al., 2024) observed that this often reduces functional accuracy, suggesting a loss of reasoning capability. No prior work provided a theoretical explanation for why restrictive output grammars diminish LLM expressivity, nor a practical decoding strategy that simultaneously guarantees syntactic validity and preserves the model's reasoning power.

## Input / Output

CRANE takes a text prompt, a context-free output grammar G, a pluggable constrained decoding algorithm (CSD, e.g., IterGen or Syncode), and two delimiter tokens S1 and S2 as inputs. It produces a single output string of the form r·S1·a·S2, where r is an unconstrained reasoning chain and a is the final answer guaranteed to be in L(G). The constrained decoder is initialized with the augmented grammar G' = S1·G·S2, so constraints are only active within the S1–S2 window, leaving the reasoning prefix entirely unconstrained.

## Method

The paper first proves (Proposition 3.1) that under a restrictive grammar Gc with a finite output set, any constant-layer log-precision LLM is limited to the logspace-uniform TC0 circuit class, since the output length is bounded by a constant and each autoregressive step maps to a constant-depth threshold circuit. It then shows (Proposition 3.3) that augmenting G with a reasoning nonterminal R_M that accepts arbitrary-length sequences of Turing-machine configuration encodings restores full expressivity: the LLM can simulate t(n) steps of any TM M in t(n) autoregressive steps under constrained decoding with Ga → R_M G. The practical CRANE algorithm operationalizes this insight by using delimiter tokens S1 and S2 to adaptively toggle between unconstrained generation (reasoning) and constrained generation (final answer) at runtime. At each decoding step, CRANE checks whether S1 appears in the current generation window to enter constrained mode and whether the constrained window ends with S2 to revert to unconstrained mode, requiring no fine-tuning and working with any existing grammar-constrained decoder.

## Results

On GSM-Symbolic, CRANE consistently outperforms all baselines across 9 open-source LLMs (1.5B–32B parameters), achieving up to 10 percentage points improvement over the best baseline. For example, CRANE reaches 38% functional accuracy with Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (vs. 29% for both Constrained and Unconstrained CoT) and 46% with QwQ-32B (vs. 43% Unconstrained CoT, 38% Constrained). On FOLIO, CRANE achieves 46.31% accuracy with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming Constrained (39.41%) and Unconstrained CoT (32.02%). CRANE also achieves 100% syntactic parse rate on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, compared to 90% for Unconstrained CoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRANE is compared against three baselines: (1) Unconstrained CoT (Chain-of-Thought prompting, Wei et al. 2022), (2) Constrained generation via IterGen (Ugare et al. 2024) on GSM-Symbolic, and (3) Constrained generation via Syncode (Ugare et al. 2024) on FOLIO. CRANE wins on every model–benchmark combination tested. Against Unconstrained CoT, CRANE gains 3–10 percentage points in functional accuracy (e.g., 38% vs. 29% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, 46% vs. 43% on QwQ-32B) while also improving syntactic parse rates (e.g., 100% vs. 90% on Qwen2.5-1.5B). Against the Constrained baseline (IterGen/Syncode), CRANE achieves 5–18 percentage points higher accuracy (e.g., 31% vs. 13% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, 46.31% vs. 39.41% on FOLIO with Llama-3.1-8B), confirming that strict grammar enforcement from the first token degrades reasoning. The token counts for CRANE are comparable to Unconstrained CoT (within ~10–20% more tokens), indicating the reasoning-augmented approach does not incur prohibitive generation cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the expressivity results of Merrill & Sabharwal (2023, 2024), which show that constant-layer LLMs can simulate Turing machines given sufficient autoregressive steps, and on the empirical observation by Tam et al. (2024) that constrained decoding degrades functional correctness. It departs from prior constrained decoding work (IterGen, Syncode, Poesia et al.) by providing a formal circuit-complexity explanation for *why* restrictive output grammars limit LLM expressivity to TC⁰, and by proposing an augmented-grammar construction that provably preserves Turing-machine simulation under constrained decoding. The practical CRANE algorithm is positioned as a lightweight, decoder-agnostic wrapper that complements rather than replaces existing constrained decoding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives the first theoretical account of the expressivity loss under constrained decoding, moving beyond the empirical observations of Tam et al. (2024) to a complexity-theoretic argument that applies to any constant-layer LLM. The augmented-grammar insight and the simple delimiter-based CRANE algorithm offer a practical, cost-efficient path to combining syntactic correctness with full reasoning capability, with up to 10% accuracy gains on GSM-Symbolic and FOLIO across nine open-source models. Key limitations remain: the theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) is restricted to finite output languages, leaving open whether it extends to infinite grammars, and CRANE requires access to model logits, excluding closed-source APIs. The broader question of whether similar reasoning-augmentation strategies can be formalized for tasks without natural delimiter structures remains open.
