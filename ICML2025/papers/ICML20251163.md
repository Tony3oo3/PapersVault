## Problem

In settings where a group of identifiable human experts must collectively classify inputs (e.g., radiologists reading X-rays), querying all experts is costly, yet predicting their consensus using a pre-trained classifier is non-trivial because experts are correlated, have varying per-class accuracy, and the classifier may suffer distribution shift. Prior methods either treat experts as exchangeable and non-identifiable (deciding only how many to query, not which) or combine human and model outputs to predict an independent ground truth rather than the expert consensus itself. This paper addresses the under-explored problem of sequentially selecting which specific identifiable experts to query in order to predict their aggregate vote with minimal querying cost.

## Input / Output

For each input image x, the method consumes K-ary class probability vectors from M pre-trained classifiers (assumed zero-cost) and a growing set of hard categorical votes from a subset of H identifiable human experts, processed online in a sequential stream. It produces a posterior distribution over the consensus label (majority vote of all H experts), posterior samples for each individual unobserved expert's vote, and a per-example decision of which remaining expert to query next. The stopping criterion is a user-specified error threshold e on the model's estimated probability of mispredicting the consensus, allowing direct control over the cost-accuracy trade-off on a per-example basis.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical Bayesian model that places a joint multivariate normal prior over the additive-logistic-transformed logits of all agents (classifiers and experts), capturing correlations both within and across agents via a shared covariance matrix Σ with LKJ and half-normal priors. A global temperature parameter τ is introduced to temper the latent expert probability vectors, correcting miscalibration in the predictive distributions. Posterior inference over the global parameters (μ, Σ, τ) is performed via MCMC, updated online after each example. Given posterior samples, the method uses simulation-based inference (Algorithm 1) to draw joint samples of unobserved expert logits and votes, re-weighted by the likelihood of already-observed votes, yielding a posterior over the consensus. Expert selection (Algorithm 2) computes the expected entropy of the consensus posterior under each candidate expert's hypothetical vote and queries the expert that maximizes information gain, repeating until the estimated error falls below threshold e.

## Results

The proposed Bayesian method achieves 0% consensus prediction error using fewer expert queries than both baselines across all four datasets. On ChestX-ray, it requires only 2.55 queries on average to reach perfect accuracy, compared to 3.16 for INFEXP + ε-greedy; on Chaoyang, it needs 1.58 vs. 1.82. The confusion matrices + calibration baseline never reaches 0% error even when all experts are queried. Expected calibration error (ECE) remains below 1% across all datasets at all tested error thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against INFEXP + ε-greedy (Showalter et al., 2024), the proposed method consistently requires fewer queries to reach 0% consensus error: 2.55 vs. 3.16 on ChestX-ray and 1.58 vs. 1.82 on Chaoyang. On the simulated-expert datasets (CIFAR-10H and ImageNet-16H), the proposed method also reaches 0% error while INFEXP does not, even at an error threshold of 0. Against the confusion matrices + calibration baseline (Kerrigan et al., 2021), the advantage is more pronounced: the confusion-based method never achieves 0% error on any dataset, even when all experts are queried, because it does not explicitly model the consensus aggregation function. The proposed method's advantage stems from its joint latent correlation model over experts and classifiers, which enables it to identify which specific expert to query next (rather than how many), and from its Bayesian uncertainty estimates that naturally balance exploration and exploitation over time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian consensus prediction framework of Showalter et al. (2024, INFEXP), which treats human experts as exchangeable and non-identifiable, by introducing identifiable experts with a joint latent correlation structure modeled via a multivariate normal over logistic-normal logits. It also draws on the logistic-normal modeling tradition (Atchison & Shen, 1980; Blei & Lafferty, 2006) and the LKJ prior for covariance, while departing from prior human-AI combination methods (Steyvers et al., 2022; Kerrigan et al., 2021) that predict an external ground truth rather than the expert consensus itself. The key conceptual departure is treating expert votes as the target to be predicted (not a signal about some independent label), enabling a sequential querying policy that selects which specific expert to query next based on expected information gain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, online mechanism for reducing expert query costs in settings like radiology where consensus is the operational ground truth but querying all experts is prohibitively expensive. The demonstrated ability to adapt to distribution shifts (via increased exploration) and to support arbitrary aggregation functions (unanimity, any-positive) broadens its practical applicability. However, the method is limited by the quadratic growth of the covariance matrix with K and H, restricting it to small classification problems, and the MCMC-based inference is computationally expensive. Open questions include scaling to larger K/H with structured covariances, incorporating verbal confidence from experts, and extending beyond image classification to text or tabular data.
