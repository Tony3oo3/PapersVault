## Problem

Standard DP-SGD privacy guarantees assume unstructured data where batches are sampled from flat input-target pairs, but time series forecasting uses a fundamentally different batching procedure: selecting sequences, sampling contiguous subsequences, and splitting them into context-forecast windows. This structure means a single sensitive element can appear multiple times in a batch at different positions, so applying standard DP-SGD guarantees in a black-box manner either underestimates or overestimates actual privacy leakage. No prior work has derived sound and tight privacy bounds tailored to this structured, two-level subsampling scheme.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of N univariate time series of length L and produces probabilistic forecasts parameterized as elementwise t-distributions, along with differentially private model parameters. Each training batch is constructed via two-level subsampling: a top-level sample selects ⌊Λ/λ⌋ sequences, and a bottom-level sample draws λ contiguous subsequences of length L_C + L_F from each, which are then split into context windows (length L_C) and ground-truth forecast windows (length L_F). Privacy is guaranteed at the level of w contiguous time steps (event-level) or w arbitrary time steps (user-level) within a single sequence, with optional magnitude constraints (w,v)-privacy.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical analysis of how the structured two-level batching procedure amplifies differential privacy, using coupling-based subsampling analysis generalized from unstructured to structured settings. They derive tight dominating pairs (mixture-of-Gaussians distributions) for the per-step privacy profile, showing that the probability of a sensitive element leaking is the product of the top-level sampling probability ρ = ⌊Λ/λ⌋/N and the bottom-level probability r = (L_C + L_F)/(L − L_F + 1). A key trade-off emerges: increasing λ strengthens top-level amplification but weakens bottom-level amplification, and they prove λ = 1 is optimal under composition. They further show that adding Gaussian noise to context and forecast windows (online data augmentation) shifts probability mass toward zero-leakage outcomes, with the label-only-noise special case providing the first analysis of online label perturbation for self-supervised sequence training. The training procedure is standard DP-SGD (per-subsequence gradient clipping plus calibrated Gaussian noise), but the privacy accounting is entirely new, composing per-step guarantees via privacy loss distribution accounting over a full training run.

## Results

On the traffic dataset (δ=10⁻⁷), DeepAR trained with 1-event-level DP achieves a CRPS of 0.157 at ε=0.5, 0.145 at ε=1, and 0.142 at ε=2, compared to 0.124 without DP (ε=∞). All four deep learning architectures (SimpleFF, DeepAR, iTransformer, DLinear) outperform the non-DP Seasonal Naive baseline (CRPS 0.251) and AutoETS baseline (CRPS 0.407) even at the strongest privacy setting ε=0.5. On electricity and solar_10_minutes, similar patterns hold: at least one DP-trained model beats both traditional baselines across all tested ε values from 0.25 to 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DP-trained deep forecasting models against two non-DP traditional baselines: Seasonal Naive and AutoETS (from statsforecast). On traffic, DeepAR with ε=0.5 achieves CRPS 0.157, beating Seasonal Naive (0.251) by 37% and AutoETS (0.407) by 61%. Even the weakest DP model (iTransformer at ε=0.5, CRPS 0.211) still outperforms Seasonal Naive. The gap between DP and non-DP training is modest: DeepAR degrades from 0.124 (ε=∞) to 0.157 (ε=0.5), a ~26% increase in CRPS, while still dominating both traditional baselines. On electricity, DLinear with ε=0.5 (CRPS 0.064) matches AutoETS (0.064) and beats Seasonal Naive (0.070); on solar_10_minutes, DeepAR with ε=0.5 (CRPS 0.820) substantially outperforms Seasonal Naive (1.120) and AutoETS (6.494). The key takeaway is that the structured subsampling analysis enables sufficiently tight privacy bounds to train useful models under strong privacy budgets (ε≤1, δ=10⁻⁷) that would be infeasible with the looser black-box DP-SGD guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) from unstructured datasets to the structured batching procedure inherent in time series forecasting, where batches are formed by (1) selecting sequences, (2) sampling contiguous subsequences, and (3) splitting into context-forecast windows. It departs from prior private time series forecasting work (Mercier et al., 2021; Arcolezi et al., 2022) that applied DP-SGD guarantees in a black-box manner, showing those guarantees are incompatible with the structured sampling. The analytical toolkit builds on coupling-based subsampling analysis (Balle et al., 2018; Schuchardt et al., 2024), which the authors generalize from unstructured to structured (bi-level) subsampling schemes, and differentiates from bi-level subsampling for LLMs (Charles et al., 2024; Chua et al., 2024) by analyzing the interplay of randomness at both sampling levels rather than only one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide sound and tight privacy guarantees for DP-SGD adapted to time series forecasting, revealing that naive application of standard DP-SGD bounds can both under- and over-estimate privacy leakage. The practical finding that λ=1 (one subsequence per sequence) is optimal under composition, and that top-level sampling without replacement outperforms deterministic iteration despite more compositions, gives concrete guidance for practitioners. Open questions include tightening bounds for λ>1, analytically understanding why λ=1 dominates under composition (potentially via central limit theorems), and extending the framework to token/sentence-level private language modeling, where the structural analogy is immediate. The empirical validation on three standard benchmarks with four architectures confirms feasibility but is limited to relatively small datasets and moderate privacy budgets.
