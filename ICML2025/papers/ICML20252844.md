## Problem

Compositional LMMs (e.g., LLaVA) rely on separate vision encoders that produce highly compressed global semantics, limiting fine-grained visual perception. Native single-transformer LMMs (Fuyu, Chameleon, EVE) avoid this by fusing raw modalities early but are extremely resource-intensive and still trail compositional models by large margins. The paper asks whether a single-transformer LMM can be built efficiently—leveraging prior knowledge from pre-trained models—while closing the performance gap with compositional counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Visual input is an RGB image patchified into k×k windows and embedded via a single linear layer (no vision encoder), while text is tokenized and projected through the LLM's embedding matrix into a shared latent space. The model supports up to 672×672 resolution (2304 vision tokens per image) and a 6144-token context window, and can accept multiple interleaved images alongside text. Output is a standard auto-regressive language response generated token-by-token from the fused multi-modal hidden states.

## Method

HaploVL decomposes the single transformer into a pre-decoder (24 blocks, 1024-dim, initialized from CLIP-ViT-L) and a post-decoder (initialized from Llama-3-8B or Vicuna-7B), connected by a lightweight patch-embedding layer for vision and a linear text projector. The pre-decoder performs early fusion of raw vision and text embeddings with a mixed masking strategy (bidirectional within an image, causal across text and between images), allowing text embeddings to dynamically attend to relevant visual regions. Training uses a two-stage recipe: Stage 1 distills vision knowledge from a frozen CLIP teacher (cosine similarity loss) while teaching the pre-decoder to pass text through via a feature-matching loss and a novel current-token-prediction loss (targeting the current token rather than the next, with a learnable temperature to control logit magnitude); Stage 2 fully fine-tunes the entire model on visual instruction data with standard next-token prediction. This design inherits prior single-modal knowledge, reducing training data to ~1.2M samples versus 35M for EVE, while the early fusion of uncompressed vision tokens enables superior fine-grained perception.

## Results

HaploVL-8B (Llama-3-8B) achieves 75.1 on SEED-Bench, 73.6 on MMBench, 57.2 on MMStar, and 45.3 on MMVP, outperforming the strongest single-transformer baseline Emu3-8B by 15.1 points on MMBench (73.6 vs. 58.5) and 5.5 points on MMMU (37.4 vs. 31.6). The larger HaploVL-7B-Pro (Qwen2.5-7B) reaches 80.5 on MMBench and 50.1 on MMVP, nearly matching the compositional LLaVA-OneVision (80.8 and 50.1). On fine-grained perception, HaploVL-7B (same LLM and data as LLaVA-1.5) gains 3.4 points on MMVP (24.7 vs. 21.3) and 4.2 points on MMStar (34.5 vs. 30.3) over LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-transformer baselines, HaploVL-8B dominates: it outperforms Emu3-8B by 15.1 points on MMBench (73.6 vs. 58.5) and 5.5 on MMMU (37.4 vs. 31.6), surpasses EVE-7B by 24.1 on MMBench (73.6 vs. 49.5) and 20.8 on SEED-Bench (75.1 vs. 54.3), and exceeds Chameleon-30B by 36.0 on MMBench (73.6 vs. 37.6) despite being 4× smaller. Against compositional LMMs, HaploVL-7B (sharing Vicuna-7B and 665K data with LLaVA-1.5) wins on fine-grained benchmarks—MMVP 24.7 vs. 21.3 and MMStar 34.5 vs. 30.3—while LLaVA-1.5 retains an edge on general benchmarks like GQA (62.0 vs. 62.5) and POPE (85.9 vs. 85.4). HaploVL-8B beats LLaVA-1.6 on SEED (75.1 vs. 70.2), MMB (73.6 vs. 67.4), and MMVP (45.3 vs. 37.3). The strongest variant, HaploVL-7B-Pro (Qwen2.5-7B), nearly matches LLaVA-OneVision on MMBench (80.5 vs. 80.8) and MMMU (48.7 vs. 48.8), while leading on MMVP (50.1 vs. –) and MMStar (61.4 vs. 61.7). Fuyu-8B and InstructBLIP are substantially outperformed across all overlapping benchmarks, with HaploVL-8B scoring 73.6 vs. 10.7 on MMBench and 75.1 vs. 58.8 on SEED, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

HaploVL sits at the intersection of two lines of work: compositional LMMs (LLaVA, InstructBLIP) that pair a frozen vision encoder with an LLM via a projector, and single-transformer early-fusion models (Fuyu, Chameleon, EVE, Emu3) that discard the separate encoder entirely. Its key departure from prior single-transformer models is a two-stage training recipe that distills CLIP-ViT-L's vision knowledge into a pre-decoder and Llama/Vicuna's text knowledge into a post-decoder, rather than training from scratch or relying on a single distillation signal as EVE does. The current-token-prediction loss (targeting the current token rather than the next) is a small but deliberate modification to the standard next-token objective, designed to preserve the magnitude and direction of text embeddings during modal expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single-transformer LMM trained on only 1.2M samples can match or exceed compositional LMMs on fine-grained perception benchmarks (MMVP, MMStar-FP) while using a fraction of the data required by prior unified models (EVE uses 35M). This challenges the assumption that dedicated vision encoders producing compressed semantic features are necessary for fine-grained visual tasks, suggesting that early fusion of raw patch embeddings lets text queries dynamically attend to relevant image regions. However, HaploVL still trails the strongest compositional model (LLaVA-OneVision) on most benchmarks, and the authors attribute this to limited context length (6144) and resolution (up to 672×672 vs. 7290 tokens for LLaVA-OV). Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic resolution, longer contexts, and unified understanding-and-generation, and whether the distillation-based training recipe generalizes to other pre-trained backbones.
