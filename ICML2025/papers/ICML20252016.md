## Problem

Existing Schrödinger Bridge (SB) methods like I2SB assume a unique, reversible mapping between two distributions, but in blind face restoration a single HQ image can correspond to multiple degraded LQ images, violating this assumption and making a unified SB infeasible. This paper identifies this theoretical conflict formally (Corollary 4.2) and shows that prior diffusion-based BFR methods that rely on feature extractors to embed LQ guidance are inherently limited by the quality of that extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252016/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB face image (512×512) with unknown compound degradations (Gaussian blur, downsampling, noise, JPEG compression) and produces a high-quality restored face at the same resolution. Unlike guidance-based approaches that feed LQ features into a conditional network, P-I2SB uses the raw LQ image directly as one endpoint of the stochastic transport path. During inference, the reverse diffusion recovers a pseudo-HQ image, which is then converted to the final output via an inverse pseudo-hashing operation (e.g., channel slicing for Cat-I2SB, addition for Res-I2SB, or DDIM denoising for Noise-I2SB).

## Method

P-I2SB consists of two stages: a Pseudo-Hashing Module (PHM) that preprocesses HQ training images into pseudo-samples whose distribution structurally matches the LQ distribution (via channel concatenation, residual subtraction, or light noise addition tied to the LQ), and a Schrödinger Bridge Module (SBM) that learns the optimal transport path between the pseudo-HQ and LQ distributions using I2SB's forward/reverse SDE framework with a UNet noise predictor. The key insight is that by hashing the HQ endpoint to be distributionally similar to the LQ endpoint, the SB solution becomes both optimal and reversible, resolving the multi-to-one mapping conflict identified in Corollary 4.2. Training uses the standard SGM noise-prediction loss over the bridge interpolation, and inference runs the reverse diffusion from the LQ image to the pseudo-HQ endpoint followed by the inverse hash to recover the final restoration. This eliminates the need for external feature extractors or complex conditioning networks, directly leveraging the raw LQ image as the transport endpoint.

## Results

On CelebA-Test (3,000 images), P-I2SB (Cat-I2SB variant) achieves the best perceptual metrics: FID 13.91, NIQE 5.33, and LPIPS 0.2395, outperforming the next-best FlowIE (FID 14.25, LPIPS 0.2413) and DMDNet (NIQE 5.34). However, it trails FlowIE on pixel-wise metrics (SSIM 0.6581 vs. 0.6815; PSNR 25.41 vs. 26.00). On real-world datasets, P-I2SB achieves the best NIQE across all four benchmarks (LFW 5.21, CelebChild 5.64, WebPhoto 5.23, Wider 4.91) and the best FID on the most challenging Wider dataset (69.62 vs. PMRF's 83.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-I2SB achieves the best FID (13.91), NIQE (5.33), and LPIPS (0.2395) on CelebA-Test, narrowly beating FlowIE on FID (14.25) and LPIPS (0.2413) and DMDNet on NIQE (5.34). It trails FlowIE on SSIM (0.6581 vs. 0.6815) and PSNR (25.41 vs. 26.00), indicating a trade-off between perceptual quality and pixel-level fidelity. Among GAN-based methods, GFPGAN and GPEN score higher on SSIM/PSNR but are substantially worse on FID (20.7–22.5) and LPIPS (0.28–0.30). Codebook methods (CodeFormer, VQFR) and diffusion-guidance methods (DiffBIR, DR2, PGDiff) all fall behind P-I2SB on FID by 5–19 points. On real-world datasets, P-I2SB dominates NIQE across all four benchmarks and achieves the best FID on Wider (69.62 vs. PMRF 83.76), though DiffBIR leads on LFW FID (44.42 vs. 54.39) and PMRF edges ahead on WebPhoto FID (83.76 vs. 84.68). The vanilla I2SB baseline (FID 21.39, NIQE 6.36) is significantly outperformed, confirming that the pseudo-hashing correction is essential for blind tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on I2SB (Liu et al., 2023), an image-to-image Schrödinger Bridge framework, and departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-based blind face restoration (DiffBIR, PGDiff, DR2, DifFace) that relies on feature extractors and fusion modules to embed LQ image information as indirect guidance conditions. The key conceptual departure is using the raw LQ image as a direct endpoint of the SB process rather than as a conditioning signal, which requires resolving a theoretical infeasibility (Corollary 4.2) where a single HQ image maps to multiple LQ images, violating the uniqueness assumption of SB. The pseudo-hashing preprocessing (concatenation, residual, or noise-based) is the novel mechanism that restores feasibility without introducing additional network complexity, positioning the work at the intersection of optimal transport theory and practical image restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical identification of why vanilla I2SB is fundamentally infeasible for blind inverse problems (non-unique optimal transport) provides a principled explanation for the suboptimal performance of bridge-based methods in this setting, and the pseudo-hashing correction is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that could transfer to other image-to-image translation tasks. However, the approach remains limited by its reliance on synthetically generated degradation pairs during training, which leaves it vulnerable to real-world artifacts like watermarks, and the dominance of frontal faces in training data constrains generalization to extreme poses. Open questions include whether unsupervised or contrastive variants of pseudo-hashing can handle truly unknown degradation types, and whether the direct-endpoint formulation extends beyond face restoration to general blind image restoration.
