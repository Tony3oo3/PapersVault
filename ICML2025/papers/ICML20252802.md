## Problem

Prompt-based domain incremental learning methods (e.g., C-Prompt) learn domain-specific prompts independently, causing component-wise misalignment where shared knowledge (e.g., object parts) is randomly positioned across prompts. This misalignment leads to conflicting knowledge integration during cross-domain prompt fusion at inference, and simultaneously leaves generalizable knowledge from prior domains underutilized when adapting to new domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input, which are tokenized by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone into a sequence representation. Learnable prompt embeddings (L_p × D) are concatenated with the [cls] token and image tokens to form the final input to the transformer, and a shared classifier across all domains produces the class label prediction. No extra learnable parameters are introduced beyond the prompt set, keeping total and trainable parameters identical to the C-Prompt baseline.

## Method

KA-Prompt enforces component-wise knowledge alignment across domains through two complementary mechanisms. First, a Reusable Knowledge Mining module uses a greedy prompt search algorithm to select N_p historical prompts that collectively maximize coverage of new-domain-relevant knowledge, then initializes new prompts from this set to establish intrinsic cross-domain componential alignment. Second, an Aligning-guided New Prompt Learning scheme dynamically identifies the most relevant old prompts during training and fuses them with new prompts using similarity-based adaptive weights, applying a cross-entropy alignment loss that penalizes componential drift as new prompts evolve. The total training loss combines the standard classification loss on new prompts with a weighted alignment loss (λ=0.1), while at inference the same top-K prompt matching and fusion pipeline as C-Prompt is used over all accumulated domain prompts.

## Results

KA-Prompt achieves an average accuracy (Avg-ACC) of 71.30% across four DIL benchmarks (DomainNet, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-C, ImageNet-Mix), outperforming the state-of-the-art C-Prompt by 4.73% on average. Per-dataset improvements over C-Prompt are 4.25% on DomainNet (62.91 vs. 58.66), 4.08% on ImageNet-R (66.51 vs. 62.43), 5.59% on ImageNet-C (85.43 vs. 79.84), and 5.00% on ImageNet-Mix (70.35 vs. 65.35). The method introduces no additional learnable parameters compared to C-Prompt (3,381,851 trainable params) while incurring only a modest increase in GPU memory (19.28 GB vs. 11.99 GB) and batch time (0.72s vs. 0.42s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KA-Prompt outperforms all 10 baselines on every benchmark. Against the primary SOTA competitor C-Prompt (Liu et al., IJCV 2024), KA-Prompt gains 4.25–5.59% Avg-ACC per dataset, with the largest margin on ImageNet-C (+5.59%), indicating that componential alignment is most beneficial when domain shifts involve quality corruptions. Among prompt-based methods, KA-Prompt also surpasses CODA-Prompt by 7.75% and DualPrompt by 7.11% on average, while regularization-based methods (EWC, LwF) trail by 9.7–15.8%. The weakest baselines are ESN (36.37%) and S-Prompts (38.95%), which suffer severe catastrophic forgetting on later domains (e.g., S-Prompts drops to 24.25% on ImageNet-Mix). Notably, KA-Prompt's advantage grows as more domains are accumulated: on DomainNet, it leads C-Prompt by only ~1% on the first domain (Real) but by 6–10% on intermediate domains (Quickdraw, Painting, Sketch, Infograph), confirming that the alignment mechanism becomes increasingly valuable as the prompt pool expands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends C-Prompt (Liu et al., IJCV 2024), which introduced compositional prompting for DIL by learning domain-specific prompt sets independently and fusing top-K prompts at inference. The key departure is the diagnosis that independently learned prompts suffer from component-wise misalignment—knowledge components (e.g., object parts) are randomly positioned across domains, causing destructive interference during fusion. KA-Prompt addresses this through two mechanisms absent in prior prompt-based DIL methods: a greedy search that mines reusable knowledge from all historical domains to initialize new prompts with intrinsic alignment, and an online alignment loss that dynamically preserves componential consistency as prompts evolve during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.73% average improvement over C-Prompt across four DIL benchmarks is substantial and comes without additional learnable parameters, making the method a practical drop-in upgrade for prompt-based DIL pipelines. The shuffle-based validation (showing C-Prompt benefits from random reordering while KA-Prompt degrades) provides clean empirical evidence for the misalignment diagnosis. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16, a modest increase in training GPU memory (11.99→19.28 GB), and untested generalization to other backbones (CLIP, Mamba) or very long domain sequences where the greedy search cost may grow. Open questions include whether the alignment principle transfers to multi-modal prompt settings and whether the componential structure discovered by the model has interpretable semantic meaning.
