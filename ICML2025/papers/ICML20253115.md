## Problem

Multi-camera 3D object detection models trained on a single source domain (e.g., nuScenes) suffer significant performance degradation on unseen domains (adverse weather, lighting changes, sensor damage), yet existing domain generalization methods either require multiple source domains or rely on 2D augmentation techniques (strong geometric transforms, style transfer, adversarial generation) that are incompatible with BEV-based detection due to physical spatial constraints. This paper is the first to address single-domain generalization specifically for multi-camera 3D object detection, where only one annotated source is available.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six synchronized RGB camera images (e.g., 256×704 or 512×1408 resolution) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which are projected into explicit BEV feature maps via LSS or transformer-based view transforms. It outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via mAP and NDS. The FCVL framework is strictly a training-time augmentation and loss module: it introduces no additional inference latency, no extra parameters, and no storage overhead, making it a plug-and-play addition to existing detectors (BEVFormer, BEVDepth, BEVDet, Far3D, Sparse4D, BEVFusion).

## Method

FCVL combines two components. First, Fourier Hierarchical Augmentation (FHiAug) operates at two levels: at the image level, Frequency Jitter scales the Fourier amplitude by α~U(η,1) and phase by β~U(λ,1) to create diverse low-level statistics while preserving semantic structure; at the feature level, Amplitude Transfer computes channel-wise mean and standard deviation of the Fourier amplitude of intermediate features, estimates their batch-level variance for uncertainty, samples perturbed style statistics from Gaussian distributions, and replaces the original statistics before inverse Fourier transform. Second, the Fourier Cross-View Semantic Consistency Loss applies a triplet loss in the frequency domain (on phase components) between adjacent camera views, using cross-view instance binding to identify positive pairs (same object in neighboring views) and negative pairs (different categories), encouraging domain-invariant BEV features. The theoretical guarantee via augmentation graph theory shows FHiAug increases graph connectivity λ_{k+1} (tightening the generalization bound) while retaining phase congruency to avoid the label error α that plagues pixel-domain style transfer.

## Results

On nuScenes-C (8 OoD corruptions), FCVL achieves the best OoD average NDS across all three BEV frameworks: 0.3567 on BEVFormer (+5.39% over baseline), 0.2870 on BEVDepth (+5.05%), and 0.2677 on BEVDet (+6.07%). The largest gains appear in the worst-case corruptions: Snow improves by 10.87% (BEVDepth) and 12.50% (BEVDet), while Low Light improves by 10.19% and 12.50% respectively. On Argoverse 2 with Far3D, FCVL lifts OoD mAP from 0.0970 to 0.1346 (+2.18%), again outperforming all compared DG methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCVL outperforms all five compared DG methods on every framework and dataset. Against CPerb (Zhao et al., 2024), FCVL gains +5.05% OoD NDS on BEVFormer (0.3567 vs 0.3062) and +4.04% on BEVDet (0.2677 vs 0.2120). Against DSU (Li et al., 2022a), the advantage is +2.37% on BEVFormer and +4.41% on BEVDet. Against DAC-SC (Lee et al., 2023), FCVL leads by +0.86% on BEVFormer but by a wider +3.75% on BEVDet, where DAC-SC's pixel-domain style transfer struggles more. Against FACT (Xu et al., 2021), FCVL's advantage is largest on BEVDet (+5.79%) because FACT's cross-domain amplitude mixing is ineffective under single-source training. Against PD-BEV (Lu et al., 2025), the only BEV-specific DG baseline, FCVL improves OoD NDS by +4.02% on BEVDepth (0.2870 vs 0.2468). On Argoverse 2, FCVL also leads all baselines by a clear margin (0.1346 vs best competitor DSU at 0.1128).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of BEV-based multi-camera 3D detection (BEVFormer, BEVDepth, BEVDet, Far3D) and single-domain generalization (SDG), a setting previously explored only for 2D image classification. It departs from prior frequency-domain DG methods (FACT, DAC-SC) by operating in a hierarchical manner—jittering both amplitude and phase at the image level, then performing uncertainty-based amplitude transfer at the feature level—rather than mixing style statistics across domains. The cross-view semantic consistency loss, which applies triplet loss on Fourier phase components between adjacent camera views, repurposes the natural multi-view structure of driving data in a way that prior BEV detectors have not exploited for generalization. The theoretical grounding via augmentation graph connectivity (HaoChen et al., 2021) provides a formal justification absent from most empirical SDG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: autonomous driving deployments rarely have access to diverse annotated source domains, making single-source training the realistic constraint, and FCVL adds zero inference latency while being plug-and-play across architectures. The paper's ablations and multi-dataset evaluation (nuScenes-C, Argoverse 2) provide credible evidence, though the Snow domain remains a weak point (absolute NDS ~0.20–0.30) and several hyperparameters require tuning. Open questions include whether the cross-view consistency loss can be extended to non-adjacent views or to temporal consistency across frames, and whether the frequency-domain augmentation strategy transfers to other sensor modalities (LiDAR, radar) or to end-to-end driving pipelines.
