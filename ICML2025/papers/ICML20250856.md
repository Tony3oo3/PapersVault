## Problem

Unlearned text-to-image diffusion models, which have had target concepts (e.g., nudity) erased from their parameters, remain vulnerable to adversarial prompts that can regenerate the erased content. Existing defenses either require costly iterative adversarial erasing during training or apply uniform noise at inference that degrades generation quality for benign inputs. The paper addresses the gap of an inference-time defense that certifiably bounds adversarial impact while preserving both model utility and inference efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user text prompt (tokenized into a sequence of token embeddings) along with a set of positive/negative prompt pairs that define the target concept direction (e.g., 7,863 pairs from ViSU or as few as 100 self-generated pairs). At inference, it produces n noisy text encodings (n=13 by default) whose element-wise median serves as the conditional input to the U-Net, ultimately yielding a generated image. The defense operates entirely at the text-encoding stage rather than at each diffusion timestep, so the U-Net receives a single smoothed embedding vector rather than per-step denoising modifications.

## Method

The paper reformulates adversarial robustness of unlearned diffusion models as a robust regression problem, bounding the MSE between noise predictions for benign and adversarial inputs. It extends naive median smoothing (which uses isotropic Gaussian noise) to a generalized framework with anisotropic Gaussian noise (diagonal covariance Σ), proving a certified bound (Theorem 3.2) where the guaranteed region becomes an ellipsoid shaped by Σ. The key adaptive mechanism computes per-token relevance scores via differential text embeddings (local word-level and optional global prompt-level) and cosine similarity against a target concept direction, then sets each token's noise magnitude as σ₀·exp(k·sim), concentrating noise on concept-relevant tokens while leaving others nearly undisturbed. For efficiency, the median is computed over n parallel text-encoder forward passes at the encoding stage rather than requiring n noise predictions at every diffusion timestep, reducing overhead from O(n×T) to O(n) encoder calls.

## Results

On the ESD-unlearned model (nudity erased), Adaptive Median Smoothing achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 6.80% across six adversarial attacks, comparable to SLD-Max (5.89%) while preserving model utility (FID 7.365 vs. SLD-Max's 25.544). On the more vulnerable UCE-unlearned model, the method reduces average ASR from 40.34% (no defense) to 11.02%, outperforming both SLD-Max (27.73%) and the adversarial-erasing baseline RECE (14.36%), with FID of 5.343. Inference time is 10.32 s per image, faster than SLD (11.78 s) and only marginally slower than no defense (9.44 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLD (Schramowski et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves a far better robustness–utility trade-off: on ESD, SLD-Max reaches a slightly lower ASR (5.89% vs. 6.80%) but degrades FID from 7.16 to 25.54, whereas Ours keeps FID at 7.37; on UCE, Ours clearly dominates with 11.02% ASR vs. SLD-Max's 27.73% and FID 5.34 vs. 18.45. Against the adversarial-erasing baselines RACE (Kim et al., 2024) and RECE (Gong et al., 2024), Ours achieves lower ASR (6.80% vs. 14.16% for ESD; 11.02% vs. 14.36% for UCE) and better or comparable FID, while being training-free. Against Blacklist (George, 2020) and POSI (Wu et al., 2024), Ours substantially outperforms on ASR (e.g., 6.80% vs. 14.18%/14.65% on ESD; 11.02% vs. 30.63%/21.09% on UCE) with minimal FID impact. Against AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024b), a text-encoder fine-tuning method, the paper reports comparable ASR (1.62%) but notes that Ours is training-free and provides theoretical robustness guarantees, whereas AdvUnlearn requires computationally intensive fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the median smoothing framework from Chiang et al. (NeurIPS 2020), which provided certified robustness for regression via isotropic Gaussian noise, by generalizing it to anisotropic Gaussian noise and leveraging the 1-Lipschitz property under the ℓ₂^Σ norm from Eiras et al. (TMLR 2022). It departs from the dominant adversarial-erasing paradigm (RACE, RECE) that iteratively searches for and erases adversarial inputs during training, instead proposing a training-free inference-time defense. The key conceptual departure is the token-wise adaptive noise schedule, which uses differential text embeddings to allocate more noise to tokens relevant to the unlearned concept, rather than applying uniform isotropic noise across all dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.2) for an inference-time defense specifically targeting unlearned T2I diffusion models, bridging the gap between certified robustness theory and practical generative model safety. The practical finding that SLD-Max achieves the best robustness but degrades FID by 3.5× while the proposed method maintains near-original FID at comparable ASR is a useful trade-off result for practitioners. Open questions remain: the fixed σ₀ leads to documented failure cases, dynamic per-input noise scheduling is left for future work, and compatibility with text-encoder fine-tuning methods (e.g., AdvUnlearn) is limited. The approach also faces efficiency challenges when scaling to many unlearned concepts simultaneously.
