## Problem

Greedy decision tree methods (e.g., CART) scale well but are provably suboptimal, while globally optimal branch-and-bound methods (e.g., GOSDT) achieve better accuracy and sparsity but are exponentially slow, limiting them to shallow trees on small feature sets. The paper identifies that the optimality gap between greedy and optimal splits shrinks monotonically toward the leaves, meaning full optimization near the leaves yields only marginal returns — yet no prior method exploits this depth-dependent structure to trade off search precision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset with binary features (real-valued features are binarized via threshold guessing from a gradient-boosted classifier) and binary labels, along with a sparsity penalty λ, a depth budget d, and a lookahead depth d_l < d. SPLIT and LicketySPLIT output a single sparse decision tree of depth ≤ d; RESPLIT outputs an approximate Rashomon set of near-optimal trees, enabling scalable variable importance estimation via the Rashomon Importance Distribution. The λ parameter is renormalized per subproblem during post-processing to maintain proportionality to the global misclassification penalty.

## Method

SPLIT performs branch-and-bound with dynamic programming up to a user-specified lookahead depth d_l, then switches to greedy splitting beyond that depth. The key mechanism modifies the branch-and-bound bounds (Algorithm 1): at the lookahead depth, lower and upper bounds for each subproblem are both fixed to the loss of a greedy subtree trained on that subproblem, effectively collapsing the search space. After solving the prefix, post-processing replaces each greedy leaf subtree with a fully optimal GOSDT subtree (or a recursive LicketySPLIT call for polynomial-time O(nk²d²) runtime). Theoretically, the optimal lookahead depth minimizing runtime is d_l = (d−1)/2, yielding a speedup factor of O(k^{(d−1)/2} · (d/2)!) over globally optimal methods, and the algorithm is provably arbitrarily better than pure greedy in the worst case.

## Results

SPLIT achieves over 100× speedup over the state-of-the-art optimal method GOSDT with negligible loss in regularized test loss (e.g., ~10 s vs ~10³ s on Bike). LicketySPLIT, the polynomial-time variant with O(nk²d²) runtime, completes in under 1 second on most datasets while maintaining test loss within the optimal frontier. RESPLIT accelerates Rashomon set computation by 10–20× (e.g., 194 s vs 14,330 s on Bike) while preserving near-perfect correlation (τ ≥ 0.93) with full Rashomon-set variable importances. Across all 10 datasets, SPLIT and LicketySPLIT consistently occupy the bottom-left region of the test-loss-vs-sparsity frontier, producing trees with fewer leaves at comparable or lower test loss than all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GOSDT (McTavish et al., 2022), SPLIT is orders of magnitude faster (e.g., ~10 s vs ~10³ s on Bike) while achieving comparable regularized test loss; LicketySPLIT is even faster at ~1 s. Against greedy CART (Breiman, 1984), SPLIT and LicketySPLIT produce sparser trees at equal or lower test loss, and Theorem 6.5 proves SPLIT can be arbitrarily better than greedy in the worst case. Against Murtree (Demirović et al., 2022) and MAPTree (Sullivan et al., 2024), SPLIT is substantially faster and more responsive to sparsity-inducing hyperparameters, while MAPTree struggles to optimize for sparsity. Against TSDT (Chaouki et al., 2024), SPLIT achieves superior performance and sparsity on all tested datasets. Against Blossom (Demirović et al., 2023), LicketySPLIT is 10–100× faster, produces 4× fewer leaves, and often achieves lower test loss (e.g., 0.087 vs 0.090 on Spambase, 0.103 vs 0.105 on Bank). Against TreeFARMS (Xin et al., 2022) for Rashomon set computation, RESPLIT is 10–20× faster with near-perfect variable importance correlation (τ = 0.93–1.0). Against Balcan & Sharma (2024), SPLIT is over 600× faster (1 s vs 10 min) with negligible test error difference (31.9% vs 31.6% on COMPAS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GOSDT branch-and-bound framework (Lin et al., 2020; McTavish et al., 2022) for optimal sparse decision trees, but introduces a key conceptual departure: performing full dynamic-programming optimization only up to a shallow 'lookahead' depth, then switching to greedy splitting. This is motivated by the empirical observation that near-optimal trees in the Rashomon set are predominantly greedy near the leaves, a property the paper quantifies across multiple datasets. It also extends TreeFARMS (Xin et al., 2022) for Rashomon set computation via the RESPLIT variant, and departs from prior lookahead heuristics (Norton, 1989; Murthy & Salzberg, 1995) by optimizing a global sparsity-regularized objective rather than a local information-gain heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100×+ speedup over state-of-the-art optimal methods with negligible accuracy or sparsity loss makes near-optimal decision trees practical for datasets with hundreds of features and deeper trees, where GOSDT and Murtree become infeasible. The polynomial-time LicketySPLIT variant and the scalable RESPLIT algorithm open the door to Rashomon-set-based variable importance at depths and feature counts previously out of reach. Open questions include characterizing when subproblems exhibit large optimality gaps (which would invalidate the greedy-near-leaves assumption) and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-class or regression settings beyond the binary classification focus here.
