## Problem

Existing active learning methods for Gaussian Process Regression lack theoretical guarantees on prediction error for a target distribution, and specifying a single target distribution is often impractical. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating a distributionally robust active learning (DRAL) problem that minimizes the worst-case expected squared error over an ambiguity set of target distributions, and provides the first convergence guarantees for such a DR objective in the GPR setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252316/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a compact input domain X (discrete grid or continuous subset of R^d), a normalized GP kernel (SE, Matérn-ν, or linear), and an ambiguity set P defined as an L∞-norm ball of radius η around a reference distribution p_ref. At each of T sequential rounds it selects one input, observes a noisy scalar label, and ultimately returns the GP posterior mean μ_T(x) and posterior variance σ_T²(x) as the predictive model. The ambiguity set is solved via linear programming over discrete distributions, and the worst-case distribution p_t is recomputed at every iteration.

## Method

The key insight is that the worst-case expected squared error E_T is upper-bounded by a constant times max_{p∈P} E_{p(x*)}[σ_T²(x*)], reducing the DRAL problem to minimizing the worst-case expected posterior variance. Two algorithms are proposed: (1) DR random, which samples the next input from the worst-case distribution p_t = argmax_{p∈P} E_{p(x*)}[σ_{t-1}²(x*)], and (2) constrained DR variance reduction (CDR), which greedily minimizes E_{p_t(x*)}[σ_t²(x*|x)] subject to the constraint that σ_{t-1}²(x) ≥ E_{p_t(x*)}[σ_{t-1}²(x*)], ensuring the selected point is at least as uncertain as the average. The theoretical analysis establishes that both methods achieve max_{p∈P} E_{p(x*)}[σ_T²(x*)] = O(γ_T/T) (with an additional O(log(1/δ)/T) term for DR random), where γ_T is the maximum information gain, yielding a high-probability error bound of O(log(T/δ)·γ_T/T) for continuous domains under standard smoothness assumptions. The proofs leverage Lipschitz bounds on the GP posterior mean (newly derived in Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3), discretization arguments, and concentration inequalities from the kernelized bandit literature.

## Results

The paper evaluates the worst-case expected squared error E_T across synthetic (|X|=1331, T=400, SE and Matérn-5/2 kernels) and three real-world regression datasets (King County house sales, Red wine quality, Auto MPG) for ambiguity set sizes η ∈ {0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1}. The proposed DR random and constrained DR variance reduction (CDR variance reduction) methods consistently achieve the lowest E_T across all η values and both kernel types, while baselines (US, variance reduction, EPIG) deteriorate notably at small η. The theoretical guarantee established is E_T = O(log(T/δ)·γ_T/T) with high probability, matching the convergence rate of standard US and RS. Results are presented as convergence plots (Figures 1–3) over 20 random trials rather than a single numerical summary table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Uncertainty Sampling (US), Variance Reduction (Yu et al., 2006), Expected Predictive Information Gain (EPIG, Bickford Smith et al., 2023), and Random Sampling (RS). US and Variance Reduction perform competitively only when η is large (η=0.1), where the problem approaches worst-case error minimization, but deteriorate significantly at small η (η=0, 0.001) because they ignore the ambiguity set P entirely. EPIG is consistently inferior across all η values because it optimizes entropy O(log σ_t(x)) rather than the squared error O(σ_t²(x)) that governs E_T. The unconstrained DR variance reduction (an ablation without the X_t constraint) shows slightly better practical performance than CDR variance reduction, confirming that the constraint needed for theoretical analysis does not sacrifice much effectiveness. DR random, while theoretically guaranteed, is often inferior to CDR variance reduction due to the inherent randomness in sampling from p_t.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributionally robust active learning (DRAL) framework of Frogner et al. (2021), which was previously limited to heuristic, non-Bayesian models, to the Gaussian process regression setting with the first theoretical error guarantees. The theoretical analysis draws heavily on the kernelized bandit literature (Srinivas et al., 2010; Vakili et al., 2021a,b), particularly the maximum information gain and confidence bound machinery, to establish convergence of the worst-case expected squared error. The work also connects to the optimal experimental design tradition (V-optimality for GPR) and the broader distributionally robust learning framework (Chen et al., 2020), positioning DRAL as a natural extension of DRL to the sequential data acquisition setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide theoretical guarantees for DRAL in the GPR setting, showing that the worst-case expected error can be made arbitrarily small with a finite number of labels under mild conditions. Key open questions remain: the convergence rate optimality is not established, the experiments are limited to discrete L∞-ball ambiguity sets (continuous Wasserstein or KL balls are left for future work), and the greedy algorithm's constraint—necessary for the proof—is empirically suboptimal compared to its unconstrained variant. For practitioners using GPR-based active learning in safety-critical or distribution-shift-prone settings, this provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic acquisition functions.
