## Problem

While GNN-based methods can predict initial bases close to the optimal simplex basis, a closer basis does not always yield greater solver acceleration—especially when the predicted basis is infeasible, forcing costly Phase I recovery. The prior bipartite graph representation (Fan et al., 2023) encodes only the constraint matrix as edges, leaving objective coefficients and bounds as node features, and raw solver labels contain structural inconsistencies that limit achievable prediction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a general-form LP (constraint matrix A ∈ R^{m×n}, objective vector c, variable bounds l^x/u^x, constraint bounds l^s/u^s) and encodes it as a tripartite graph with primal variable nodes, dual variable/bound nodes, constraint nodes, and a global node. The output is a 3-class probability vector per node (basic, non-basic at lower bound, non-basic at upper bound); exactly m nodes with the highest 'basic' probability are selected as the initial basis, and remaining non-basic variables are assigned to their lower or upper bound based on the relative probabilities of classes 0 and 2.

## Method

The core architecture is a tripartite graph GNN where each primal variable is split into a primal node and a dual node (representing its upper/lower bounds), connected to a global node with edge weights equal to objective coefficients and bound values—thereby embedding the primal and dual LP structures simultaneously. Message passing proceeds in three phases: (1) all nodes aggregate to the global node with coefficient-weighted sums, (2) the global node broadcasts back, and (3) two rounds of bidirectional message passing between primal and dual/constraint nodes with duality-based masks zeroing out infinite-bound half-nodes. Training uses a composite loss combining standard 3-class cross-entropy, a binary cross-entropy term targeting basic-variable selection accuracy (penalizing false positives/negatives on the m basic slots), and a multi-level feasibility loss that weights feasible bases sampled along the solver's path (closer to optimal receive higher weight). Data preprocessing presolves instances to reduce label inconsistency and applies a knowledge-based mask forcing equality constraints to never be predicted as non-basic at the upper bound.

## Results

On the presolved Mirp1 dataset, the tripartiteBMP model reduces solver iterations to 32.12% of the default (vs. 61.89% for the bipartite SOTA) and solving time to 41.99% (vs. 73.31%). Across all four presolved datasets, the geometric mean iteration ratio is 59.81% (vs. 73.57% for bipartite) and the geometric mean time ratio is 73.43% (vs. 88.96%), representing roughly a 2× speedup over the SOTA in iterations and a 1.2× speedup in wall-clock time. On the unpresolved datasets, the geometric mean iteration ratio is 57.02% (vs. 67.67%) and time ratio is 72.66% (vs. 82.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the bipartite graph-based GNN from Fan et al. (ICML 2023), the prior SOTA for learned initial basis selection. The tripartiteBMP method outperforms the bipartite baseline on 3 out of 4 presolved datasets in both iterations and solving time, with the most dramatic gain on Mirp1 (32.12% vs. 61.89% iteration ratio; 41.99% vs. 73.31% time ratio). On Mirp2, both methods show only modest improvement over the default (86.16% vs. 91.11% iterations), and on Anonymous the presolved time actually increases slightly for both methods (118.8% vs. 124.9%), though the tripartite model still wins. In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 8), the tripartiteBMP model successfully accelerates the solver in 6 out of 12 train/test dataset combinations, compared to only 3 out of 12 for the bipartite model, and achieves a better geometric mean time ratio in every test-set column. The ablation (Table 9) confirms that each added component (basic-variable loss, multi-level feasibility loss, label preprocessing) contributes to the tripartite model's advantage, with the full BMP configuration achieving 2.46s geomean time vs. 2.93s for the tripartite model without any of these components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bipartite-graph GNN for LP initial basis selection introduced by Fan et al. (2023) by replacing it with a tripartite graph representation motivated by LP duality, where each primal variable and constraint is split into primal and dual half-nodes connected through a global node that encodes objective coefficients and bounds as edge weights. The GNN architecture (GraphConv) and the overall supervised-learning framing are inherited directly from Fan et al., but the authors add three new training components: a binary cross-entropy loss targeting basic-variable selection, a multi-level loss using feasible bases sampled along the solver's path, and a label-preprocessing step that resolves inconsistencies in equality-constraint labels. The tripartite structure itself draws on Ding et al. (2020) for MIP, but is here generalized to handle arbitrary general-form LP (mixed ≤, ≥, = constraints and free variables) without the problem-size inflation that standard-form conversion would cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical insight—that a predicted basis closer to optimal does not automatically yield faster solving, because infeasibility can trigger costly Phase I recovery—reframes the objective from pure prediction accuracy to solver acceleration, a distinction that prior work had not explicitly addressed. The 2× average speedup over the SOTA on presolved instances and the improved cross-dataset generalization (6/12 vs. 3/12 successful accelerations) make the approach more deployable in real pipelines. Open questions remain: whether the tripartite representation transfers to other LP/MIP tasks (branching, cutting, presolving), what additional factors beyond basis accuracy and feasibility govern warm-start quality, and whether the gains persist on commercial solvers (Gurobi, CPLEX) where the authors could not run experiments.
