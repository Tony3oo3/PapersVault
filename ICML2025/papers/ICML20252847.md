## Problem

While RL has emerged as a key method for training LLMs to generate long chains-of-thought, the conditions under which long CoTs actually emerge remain poorly understood, and RL training for this purpose is unstable and sensitive to design choices. This paper systematically investigates the underlying mechanics of long CoT generation—specifically the roles of SFT initialization, reward shaping, and verifiable data scaling—to provide practical guidance for training strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252847/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text queries (primarily math problems from the MATH training split, or web-extracted QA pairs from WebInstruct) and produces an extended chain-of-thought trace of up to 16,384 tokens followed by a final answer. The long CoT is not merely longer than a short CoT but exhibits structured reasoning behaviors including branching (exploring multiple solution paths), backtracking (reverting when a path fails), and error correction (detecting and fixing intermediate mistakes). Evaluation uses temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, and a 16K max output length.

## Method

The paper's core technical contributions are threefold. First, it shows that SFT on long CoT data (distilled from QwQ-32B-Preview via rejection sampling) raises the performance ceiling and makes subsequent RL improvements significantly easier compared to short CoT initialization. Second, it introduces a Cosine Reward function that conditions the outcome-based reward on generation length using a piecewise cosine schedule, enforcing ordering constraints (correct > wrong, shorter-correct > longer-correct, longer-wrong > shorter-wrong) to stabilize emergent CoT length growth, combined with an N-gram repetition penalty (applied per-token with a low discount factor) to prevent length reward hacking. Third, it demonstrates that noisy web-extracted data (WebInstruct-462k) can be incorporated into both SFT (via mixture with MATH) and RL (via model-based or rule-based verifiers with prompt filtration) to scale up verifiable reward signals, yielding substantial OOD gains on STEM benchmarks. All experiments use PPO with the OpenRLHF framework on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B base models.

## Results

On MATH-500, long CoT SFT (distilled from QwQ-32B-Preview) scales to over 70% accuracy, while short CoT SFT saturates below 55%. After RL with the Cosine Reward, the emergent long CoT pattern achieves 59.4% MATH-500, 4.0% AIME 2024, 25.2% TheoremQA, and 34.6% MMLU-Pro-1k (Llama-3.1-8B). Mixing 50% MATH with 50% WebInstruct data in SFT followed by RL yields the best average score of 32.1%, with a notable 42.0% on MMLU-Pro-1k. At the 32B scale (Qwen-2.5-32B, RL from base without SFT), the Cosine+Repetition reward achieves 81.8% MATH-500, 20.6% AIME 2024, 39.1% TheoremQA, and 45.5% MMLU-Pro-1k at step 80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study without external method baselines; comparisons are internal ablations. Long CoT SFT substantially outperforms short CoT SFT: on MATH-500, long CoT reaches 70%+ while short CoT plateaus under 55%, and RL further improves long CoT by 3%+ while yielding negligible gains for short CoT. Among long CoT data sources, emergent patterns (distilled from QwQ-32B-Preview) beat constructed patterns (via Action Prompting) by 15–50% relatively on OOD benchmarks (AIME 2024, MMLU-Pro-1k). For reward design, the Cosine Reward with repetition penalty stabilizes training and improves downstream accuracy over the Classic Reward at both 8B and 32B scales (e.g., Qwen-2.5-32B: 46.8 vs 45.1 average at step 80). For noisy verifiable data, the rule-based verifier outperforms the model-based verifier on filtered prompts (43.5 vs 42.2 on MMLU-Pro-1k), while the model-based verifier is better on unfiltered free-form data (40.4 vs 35.1). Mixing MATH and WebInstruct at 50/50 achieves the best overall average (32.1%), combining MATH's in-domain strength with WebInstruct's OOD diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the post-o1 wave of work on training LLMs to produce long chain-of-thought reasoning via RL with verifiable rewards, following the paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi K1.5. It departs from those efforts by conducting systematic ablations to disentangle the roles of SFT initialization, reward shaping, and data quality, rather than proposing a new end-to-end training recipe. The Cosine Reward (a piecewise cosine length-scaling reward with an N-gram repetition penalty) is a new reward design, and the use of noisy web-extracted data (WebInstruct) as a scalable proxy for verifiable signals is a novel data strategy not explored in prior long-CoT RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical playbook for practitioners attempting to train long-CoT models: SFT with emergent (not constructed) long-CoT patterns raises the performance ceiling and makes RL more effective, the Cosine Reward stabilizes otherwise unstable length scaling, and noisy web data with appropriate filtering can extend verifiable RL beyond the limited MATH dataset. Key limitations include evaluation primarily on 7–8B models in the math domain, infrastructure challenges preventing 32B+ scaling, and the observation that REINFORCE is harder to tune than PPO. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to non-mathematical reasoning, whether the 8K-over-16K context window result reflects a fundamental compute-scaling law, and how to address reward hacking at larger scales.
