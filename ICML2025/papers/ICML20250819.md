## Problem

Subspace-based OOD detection methods (e.g., Mahalanobis distance, VIM, Neco) estimate a static prior geometry from training data, but outlier features in the training set distort this geometry, making the distance insensitive to OOD samples that align with those high-variance directions. The paper asks how to correct this distortion in real time using local information from the incoming feature, without erasing essential ID structure.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a single L2-normalized feature vector from a pre-trained encoder (DenseNet, ViT, ResNet-50, Swin-B, DeiT, or DINO), together with pre-computed per-class means, the within-class covariance matrix Σ_R, and the residual-space basis matrix B (eigenvectors of the smallest eigenvalues of Σ_R). It outputs a scalar score s(f) = −min_i √(r_i^T M(f) r_i), where r_i = f − μ_i; a threshold λ then yields the ID/OOD decision. No retraining or access to the full training set is required at test time.

## Method

The key mechanism is to replace the static precision matrix Σ_R^{-1} in the Mahalanobis score with a dynamically adjusted matrix M(f) = (Σ_R − B^T a a^T B)^{-1}, where a = f^T B is the coefficient vector of the real-time feature projected into the residual space. This subtracts a rank-one outer product along the input's residual-space direction, contracting the covariance there and increasing distance sensitivity to deviations in that direction. The adjustment is confined to the residual space (smallest-eigenvalue eigenvectors) to avoid disturbing principal/semantic directions, and the within-class covariance (deviation from class means) is used instead of the full covariance so that ID features form a single dense manifold around zero. Theorem 4.2 proves that under the condition (s−1)² ≤ (p−1)(q−1)—which holds in practice because p, q ≫ 1 for compact class clusters—the modified quadratic form remains non-negative, guaranteeing a valid distance. All features are L2-normalized before adjustment to strengthen the neural-collapse property that pushes OOD energy into the residual space.

## Results

On the CIFAR benchmark, the method achieves average AUROC of 94.61 and FPR95 of 22.31 on DenseNet, and AUROC of 92.63 and FPR95 of 31.73 on WideResNet, reducing FPR95 by 9.9% and 19.6% respectively over the closest competitor FDBD. On the ImageNet-1k benchmark across four pre-trained models (ViT, ResNet-50, Swin-B, DeiT), the method achieves average AUROC of 88.69 and FPR95 of 49.72, outperforming all subspace and distance-based baselines. On the self-supervised DINO model, it reaches AUROC 91.65 and FPR95 38.23, surpassing Mahalanobis distance (91.57 / 39.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 16 baselines on both CIFAR and ImageNet benchmarks. The closest competitor is FDBD (Liu & Qin, 2023), a distance-based method; the proposed approach reduces FPR95 from 32.22 to 22.31 on DenseNet and from 51.32 to 31.73 on WideResNet, with the gap widening on CIFAR-100 where FDBD is more sensitive to class separability. Among subspace methods on ImageNet, VIM (Wang et al., 2022) achieves the best baseline AUROC of 87.21, while the proposed method reaches 88.69; Neco (Ammar et al., 2024) and WDiscOOD (Chen et al., 2023) trail further at 82.61 and 82.80 respectively. Logit-based methods (MSP, Energy, ODIN, maxLogit) and feature-manipulation methods (ReAct, DICE, LINE) are consistently outperformed by 5–15 AUROC points. On the DINO self-supervised model, the method marginally beats Mahalanobis (91.65 vs. 91.57 AUROC) and substantially outperforms SSD (51.5 AUROC) and Neco (46.97 AUROC). For near-OOD detection, the dynamic adjustment improves RMDS from 84.71 to 85.48 average AUROC, confirming generality across distance formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mahalanobis-distance framework for OOD detection (Lee et al., 2018) by making the covariance matrix input-dependent rather than static, and confines the adjustment to the residual space defined by VIM (Wang et al., 2022). Unlike prior subspace methods (VIM, Neco, WDiscOOD) that replace the covariance with a fixed projection matrix, this work introduces a rank-1 dynamic update Σ − BᵀaaᵀB that adapts per-sample using real-time features. The theoretical grounding draws on the Sherman-Morrison formula and neural collapse properties to ensure the modified matrix still induces a valid distance, positioning the contribution as a principled refinement of matrix-induced distance scores rather than a new distance family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a lightweight, test-time plug-and-play correction to a known weakness of Mahalanobis-based OOD scores: sensitivity to outlier-induced covariance distortion. Its demonstrated generality across CNNs, ViTs, and self-supervised DINO (where competing subspace methods like WDiscOOD produce NaN) makes it practically attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the O(n³) matrix inversion scales to very high-dimensional features in VLMs/LLMs (acknowledged by the authors as future work), and whether the residual-space restriction remains optimal when the between-class covariance dominates, as the ablation hints at a tension between DCM and RSP in poorly clustered settings.
