## Problem

Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across network widths, yet the theoretical reason why early-stage tuning on small proxy models or short training fractions reliably approximates globally optimal hyperparameters remains unexplained. In particular, no prior work rigorously quantifies how the loss landscape (macro-level) and individual weights (micro-level) converge at different rates with width, leaving the industry practice of early halting for hyperparameter selection without a principled error bound.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a neural network of width n parameterized under μP scaling rules (hidden weights N(0,1/n) with LR η₀, input weights N(0,1) with LR η₀n, output weights N(0,1/n²) with LR η₀/n), a time-varying learning rate schedule η(t), and SGD training dynamics over a fixed horizon T. It produces three quantitative outputs: an O(1/n) bound on the width-dependence of the expected loss landscape M(η;n), a tight Θ(1/√n) bound on the expected weight displacement ‖μ(t)‖, and a closed-form loss scaling law L(t) = L₀ + A·S₁(t)^{-α} − C·S₂(t) where S₁ and S₂ are integrated learning-rate descriptors that encode the empirically observed delay between LR changes and validation-loss response.

## Method

The paper formulates SGD under μP as a continuous-time SDE via Euler–Maruyama discretization, then applies Itô's formula to decompose the loss evolution into a drift term (−η‖∇L‖²) and a second-order Hessian–noise trace term. The critical technical step is showing that under μP the Lipschitz constant decays as L = O(n^{-1/2}), so the Hessian–covariance trace scales only as O(n) rather than O(n^{3/2}), making the Itô correction the same order as the drift and yielding the O(1/n) macro convergence. For the micro bound, they derive a differential inequality on E‖μ(t)‖² via Young's inequality and Grönwall's lemma for the upper bound, then establish a matching lower bound using a non-vanishing diffusion assumption and a Paley–Zygmund argument to upgrade L² to L¹. The framework is extended to time-varying learning rates by introducing a memory kernel q(τ) and linearizing the scalar ODE on a one-dimensional effective manifold, which naturally produces the −C·S₂(t) delay term observed empirically by Tissue et al. (2024).

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that under μP, the expected training loss landscape M(η) converges at rate O(1/n) with width, while individual weight deviations μ(t) = θ_t − θ_0 evolve at the slower rate Θ(1/√n). This yields a transfer error bound of O(1/min(n₁, n₂)) for hyperparameters (Proposition 4.8) and an O(1/n) bound on early-stage tuning error (Theorem 3.1). In the single-width empirical validation (n = 10,000, CIFAR-10, 300 epochs), the spread in training loss across learning rates drops below 5×10⁻⁴ by epoch 10, while squared weight drift ‖μ(t)‖² continues growing roughly linearly until ~epoch 180 (high η) or 250 (low η), with its standard deviation across η growing from 3.8×10⁻² to 1.1 over the first 120 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-to-method comparison table; the `compares_to` set is empty. The contribution is a first-principles proof of scale separation that complements, rather than competes with, existing frameworks. Relative to NTK analyses (Jacot et al., 2018), which linearize in the infinite-width limit and miss finite-width weight trajectories, the paper's SDE framework captures both the fast O(1/n) macro stabilization and the slow Θ(1/√n) micro drift simultaneously. Compared to mean-field approaches (Mei et al., 2019), which track individual weight fluctuations but do not explain rapid stabilization of aggregate quantities, the paper provides the missing macro-side convergence rate. Against the closest prior work (Xie et al., 2024), which derives width-agnostic SDE-based scaling laws for arbitrary non-convex problems, the paper imposes μP-specific width regularity and local convexity on the meta-objective to obtain the sharper O(1/n) vs. Θ(1/√n) separation — a narrower scope that the authors note still holds empirically across non-convex networks. The empirical appendix (single width, n = 10,000) serves as a qualitative sanity check rather than a quantitative benchmark against competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the μP parameterization framework (Yang et al., 2021; Yang & Hu, 2021) and the SDE-based analysis toolkit of Xie et al. (2024), but departs from both by imposing a finite-width, multiscale perspective that neither NTK (Jacot et al., 2018) nor mean-field (Mei et al., 2019) analyses provide. While μP established that hyperparameters transfer across widths empirically, and Xie et al. derived width-agnostic scaling laws for optimal learning rates, this paper is the first to rigorously prove a separation of convergence rates between macro-level descriptors (O(1/n)) and micro-level weights (Θ(1/√n)) under μP, filling the theoretical gap that left early-stage tuning as an unexplained industry heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/n) transfer error bound provides the first rigorous justification for the widely-used practice of tuning hyperparameters on small proxy models or early training steps, potentially reducing the cost of full-run searches in large-scale pipelines. The explanation of the learning-rate delay phenomenon (Tissue et al., 2024) as a natural consequence of the macro-micro gap adds mechanistic depth to an otherwise purely empirical observation. Key limitations include reliance on μP-specific scaling (η ∝ 1/n for hidden layers), local convexity of the meta-objective, and the absence of adaptive optimizers; open questions include whether a weakened separation persists in the α-scaled family between NTK and μP, and what minimal width is needed for reliable transfer.
