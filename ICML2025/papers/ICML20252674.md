## Problem

Existing DP-based prompt perturbation methods for LLM inference either rely on truncated adjacency lists that are vulnerable to k-nearest-neighbor attacks, or measure semantic similarity solely via token embedding distances that ignore contextual information (e.g., 'enjoyable' and 'unenjoyable' appear close in embedding space). This paper addresses the gap of performing efficient, context-aware prompt perturbation that achieves a better privacy-utility trade-off without requiring white-box model access.

## Input / Output

The mechanism operates entirely on the client side: it takes a tokenized text prompt, a small client-side language model (BERT-base or GPT-2-base, ~400 MB) to compute contextual logits, and a pre-computed token embedding table. It outputs a perturbed prompt of the same length where each sensitive token is replaced by a DP-sampled token from the full vocabulary, while 179 NLTK stopwords and 32 punctuation marks are retained unchanged. The perturbation runs in ~0.1–0.15 s per prompt with ~1–1.5 GB GPU memory, making it practical for real-time client-side use.

## Method

Cape introduces two key components. First, a hybrid utility function u(t_i, t_r) = L_r^{λ_L} · D(t_i, t_r)^{λ_D} that combines contextual logits from a client-side model (capturing surrounding context) with normalized Euclidean embedding distance (capturing semantic similarity), where λ_L and λ_D are tunable importance factors. Second, a bucketized exponential mechanism that partitions the large vocabulary (e.g., 30,522 for BERT) into N_b equal-width buckets by utility score, samples a bucket via standard EM, then uniformly selects a token within that bucket. This two-stage sampling mitigates the long-tail phenomenon where thousands of low-utility tokens collectively absorb most probability mass under standard EM. The mechanism is proven to satisfy (ε + ε')-LDP where ε' = ln(max_{i,j} |b_i|/|b_j|), and the full pipeline requires no modifications to the server-side model.

## Results

On SST-2 text classification, Cape (BERT) achieves 91.33% accuracy at ε=20, closely approaching the non-private baseline of 92.54%, while significantly outperforming InferDPT (79.70%) and SANTEXT+ (83.27%) at the same budget. On QNLI, Cape (BERT) reaches 75.84% at ε=20 versus 76.88% non-private. For open-ended text generation on Wikitext-103-v1, Cape (BERT) attains 58.02 coherence and 62.31 alignment at ε=20 (non-private: 59.78 / 65.05), substantially exceeding InferDPT (56.04 / 60.33) while matching CUSTEXT's utility with far stronger empirical privacy (CUSTEXT's privacy score is only ~40% even at ε=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SANTEXT (metric-LDP over full GloVe vocabulary), Cape (BERT) achieves substantially higher utility at small-to-moderate budgets: at ε=6 on SST-2, Cape scores 64.93% versus SANTEXT's 91.06% only after its utility jumps sharply (SANTEXT is near-random at ε=1 with 52.29%). Against CUSTEXT (static adjacency list of size 20), Cape matches or slightly trails on raw utility at large ε (91.33% vs. 92.20% on SST-2 at ε=20) but provides dramatically stronger empirical privacy—CUSTEXT's KNN privacy score is only ~40% even at ε=1 on Wikitext, whereas Cape maintains >90% privacy score at ε=1. Against InferDPT (dynamic adjacency list via Laplace noise), Cape dominates on both utility and privacy: at ε=20 on SST-2, Cape (BERT) reaches 91.33% versus InferDPT's 79.70%, a gap of over 11 percentage points, while also achieving higher privacy scores under both KNN and MTI attacks across all ε values. The BERT variant of Cape consistently outperforms the GPT-2 variant, attributed to BERT's bidirectional context capturing both preceding and following tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cape extends the differential-privacy-based prompt perturbation line initiated by SANTEXT and CUSTEXT, which relied solely on token embedding distance and truncated adjacency lists to manage the large NLP vocabulary. It departs from these by introducing a hybrid utility function that fuses contextual logits from a client-side LM with Euclidean token distance, and by replacing adjacency-list truncation with an equal-width bucketized variant of the Exponential Mechanism (McSherry & Talwar, 2007) to handle the long-tail probability mass in large sampling spaces. For open-ended generation, it adopts the extraction-model paradigm from InferDPT (Tong et al., 2023) but applies its own perturbation mechanism upstream. Unlike white-box approaches (TextObfuscator, DP-Forward) that require deploying shallow model layers on the client, Cape operates in a black-box setting with no server-side model modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cape offers a practical, drop-in privacy layer for LLM inference services that requires no server modification and runs in ~0.1–0.15 s per prompt, making it more deployable than cryptographic alternatives (CipherGPT, Ditto) that need 30+ seconds per token. However, the utility degradation at low privacy budgets (ε ≤ 3) remains substantial—Rouge-L F1 drops to ~38–40%—and the method still requires a ~400 MB client-side model, limiting applicability on resource-constrained devices. Open questions include whether the bucketized EM can be adapted to multi-token or span-level perturbation, whether the hybrid utility generalizes to non-English or code domains, and how to assign heterogeneous privacy budgets to different token categories (e.g., PII vs. generic words) without compounding the privacy loss.
