## Problem

KL-regularized RL is the dominant framework for LLM alignment (RLHF) and empirically achieves superior sample efficiency, yet prior theoretical analyses either reduce to standard RL yielding O(√T) regret or rely on strong coverage assumptions. The paper asks whether KL-regularized RL is provably more efficient than standard RL in the standard online setting without additional coverage conditions, and answers affirmatively by establishing the first logarithmic regret bounds.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes a context (or state) drawn from a fixed distribution, selects an action from a finite action space, and receives a scalar reward with 1-sub-Gaussian noise. The algorithm requires a finite reward (or value) function class with known cardinality, a known reference policy π_ref, and a KL-regularization parameter η > 0. The output at each step is a Gibbs-distribution policy π(a|x) ∝ π_ref(a|x)·exp(η·(R̂ + b)), where R̂ is the least-squares reward estimate and b is an eluder-dimension-based exploration bonus; the theoretical guarantee is a regret bound of O(η·d_R·log(N_R·T)) for bandits and O(η·H²·d_F·log(N_F⊕B·T·H)) for MDPs.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms—KL-UCB for contextual bandits and KL-LSVI-UCB for MDPs—both built on the optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty principle with eluder-dimension-based bonuses. The key analytical insight for bandits is a refined suboptimality decomposition that expresses the gap as a functional difference Δ(x, R̂+b) − Δ(x, R*) involving the log-partition function Z_R(x), rather than discarding the KL term as prior work does. A gradient analysis of Δ combined with the mean-value theorem and the uniform optimism event converts the regret into a sum of *squared* optimistic gaps, which the eluder dimension then bounds logarithmically in T. For MDPs, the paper introduces a novel policy decomposition: instead of the standard value-function telescoping that yields linear Bellman errors, it decomposes the suboptimality gap by concatenating the learned and optimal policies step-by-step, reducing each step to a bandit subproblem and obtaining squared Bellman errors, at the cost of an additional H² factor.

## Results

For KL-regularized contextual bandits, the proposed KL-UCB algorithm achieves a regret bound of O(η d_R log T · log(N_R)), which is logarithmic in the number of rounds T, improving upon the previous O(√T) rate. For KL-regularized MDPs, KL-LSVI-UCB achieves O(η H² d_F log T · log(N_{F⊕B})) regret, the first logarithmic-in-T bound in the literature for this setting. Both results hold without any coverage assumption, with sample complexities of Õ(η d_R · log(N_R)/ε) for bandits and Õ(η H² d_F · log(N_{F⊕B})/ε) for MDPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's KL-UCB algorithm improves upon Online Iterative GSHF1 (Xiong et al., 2024a) by replacing the polynomial-in-T sample complexity Õ(d²/ε²) with a logarithmic-in-T bound Õ(η d_R · log N_R / ε), while also eliminating the need for a coverage assumption that Two-Stage Mixed-Policy Sampling (Zhao et al., 2024) requires. For MDPs, KL-LSVI-UCB achieves a logarithmic-in-T regret O(η H² d_F log T · log N_{F⊕B}), in contrast to the √T-dependent regret Õ(√(d_R T log N_R) + H d_P log N_P) of Online Iterative M-GSHF2 (Xiong et al., 2024b) and the Õ(d_F log N_F / ε²) sample complexity of XPO (Xie et al., 2024). The key distinction is that prior methods either reduce to standard RL analysis (yielding √T rates) or rely on strong coverage conditions, whereas this work exploits the benign optimization landscape of KL-regularization to obtain the first logarithmic regret bounds in the standard online setting without coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LSVI-UCB framework (Zhang, 2022; Wang et al., 2020) and the eluder dimension analysis for general function approximation, but departs fundamentally from prior KL-regularized RL analyses (Xiong et al., 2024a; Xie et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024) that either reduce to standard RL regret bounds of O(√T) or require strong coverage assumptions. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the closed-form Gibbs distribution structure of the KL-regularized objective to express the suboptimality gap as a functional gap whose gradient yields squared (rather than linear) uncertainty terms, enabling logarithmic regret. For MDPs, the novel policy decomposition across time steps—decomposing the gap by concatenating the learned and optimal policies at each step—reduces the multi-step problem to repeated bandit gaps, a technique not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for the empirically observed superior sample efficiency of KL-regularized RLHF (e.g., 52B and 671B models improving with only tens of thousands of samples), showing that KL-regularization fundamentally changes the learning rate from polynomial to logarithmic in T. The policy decomposition technique for MDPs and the refined suboptimality analysis for bandits are of independent interest and may generalize to other regularized decision-making problems. A notable limitation is the H² dependence in the MDP regret bound, which the authors flag as future work; additionally, the analysis assumes finite function classes (or covering numbers), and the practical algorithms require solving the Gibbs distribution at each step, which may be intractable for very large action spaces.
