## Problem

Existing algorithms for online convex optimization with delayed feedback either ignore loss curvature (achieving only O(√d_tot) regret via DOGD) or, for strongly convex losses, achieve O(d_max ln T) regret that can be substantially worse than O(√d_tot) when even a single round suffers a Θ(T) delay. No prior work had investigated whether logarithmic regret is achievable for exp-concave losses under delayed feedback, leaving a significant gap in the literature.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner selects a decision x_t ∈ X ⊆ R^n and incurs loss f_t(x_t); the gradient g_t = ∇f_t(x_t) is only revealed at round t + d_t for an arbitrary unknown delay d_t ≥ 0. In the online linear regression variant, the feature vector z_t ∈ R^n is observed before the prediction but the label y_t arrives delayed. The algorithm must decide without knowing which past gradients are still pending, tracking only the set o_t of rounds whose feedback has been received by round t.

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms, each built on a 'cheating iterate' decomposition that splits regret into a standard FTRL term and a drift term measuring the distance between actual and oracle iterates. For strongly convex losses, a delayed FTRL variant regularizes over all past decisions (not just observed ones), yielding a diameter-free O(min{σ_max ln T, √d_tot}) bound via a stability lemma that bounds ‖x_t* − x_t‖ in terms of missing observations. For exp-concave losses, a delayed Online Newton Step uses a delay-adaptive learning rate η_t = min{a_t, b_t} + 1 where a_t tracks the d_max n ln T guarantee and b_t tracks the √d_tot guarantee, achieving the best of both without prior knowledge of delay parameters. For unconstrained online linear regression, a delayed Vovk-Azoury-Warmuth forecaster incorporates a clipping trick (scaling x_t so the predicted label stays within the range of observed labels) to prevent gradient blow-up on the unbounded domain, combined with the same adaptive tuning scheme.

## Results

The paper's headline contribution is a set of regret bounds for OCO with delayed feedback under curved losses. For strongly convex losses, Algorithm 1 (Delayed FTRL) achieves O((G²/λ)(ln T + min{σ_max ln T, √d_tot})), improving upon the O(d_max ln T) bound of prior work. For exp-concave losses, Algorithm 2 (Delayed ONS) achieves O(min{d_max n ln T, √d_tot} + n ln T), the first logarithmic regret guarantee for this class under delays. For unconstrained online linear regression, Algorithm 3 (Delayed VAW with clipping) achieves O(‖u‖²(min{d_max n ln T, √d_tot} + n ln T)) without prior knowledge of the comparator or data. Experiments over T=10,000 rounds with 20 trials under two delay regimes confirm consistent empirical improvements over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOGD-SC (Wan et al., 2022a) and SDMD-RSC (Wu et al., 2024), which both achieve only O(d_max ln T) regret for strongly convex losses, the proposed Delayed FTRL improves the delay-dependent term to min{σ_max ln T, √d_tot}, where σ_max ≤ d_max can be substantially smaller, and additionally recovers the O(√d_tot) guarantee of DOGD simultaneously. The bound is also independent of the domain diameter D, unlike both prior methods. Against DOGD (Quanrud & Khashabi, 2015), which achieves O(√d_tot + T) for general convex losses, the paper's algorithms strictly improve the regret for both strongly convex and exp-concave losses by exploiting curvature. Against BOLD-based reductions (Joulani et al., 2013), which run d_max+1 copies of a base algorithm, the proposed methods consistently outperform in experiments under both delay regimes (uniform small delays and rare large delays), particularly for exp-concave and OLR settings where no prior curvature-aware delayed algorithm existed. The paper also provides a refined analysis showing SDMD-RSC can achieve the same min{σ_max ln T, √d_tot} bound, explaining the close empirical performance observed in the strongly convex experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: curvature-exploiting OCO algorithms (Hazan et al. 2007's OGD/ONS for strongly convex and exp-concave losses; Vovk-Azoury-Warmuth forecaster for online linear regression) and delayed-feedback OCO (Quanrud & Khashabi 2015's DOGD; Wan et al. 2022a's DOGD-SC; Wu et al. 2024's SDMD-RSC). The key departure is that prior delayed-feedback work either ignored curvature entirely (DOGD) or only handled strong convexity with a d_max·ln T bound that can be worse than the general convex guarantee. The paper adapts FTRL, ONS, and VAW with delay-adaptive learning rates and (for OLR) a clipping trick to simultaneously achieve logarithmic and sqrt(total-delay) regret, a 'best-of-both-worlds' guarantee absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers an explicit open question from Wan et al. (2022a) by providing the first logarithmic-regret algorithm for exp-concave losses under delayed feedback, and extends the same guarantee to unconstrained online linear regression. The diameter-free bound for the strongly convex case (recovering the optimal O(G²/λ · ln T) in the no-delay limit) is a clean improvement over prior polynomial-in-D guarantees. An open question the authors flag is whether the σ_max·n·ln T term can replace d_max·n·ln T for exp-concave losses, which would tighten the guarantee further. For practitioners, the adaptive learning rate tuning requires no prior knowledge of delay statistics, making the algorithms directly deployable in settings like ad-click prediction where feedback latency is unpredictable.
