## Problem

Existing autoencoders for latent generative models (e.g., SD-VAE, VQ-GAN) produce latent representations that are not equivariant under basic spatial transformations like scaling and rotation, introducing unnecessary complexity into the latent manifold. This forces downstream generative models to learn nonlinear relationships that could otherwise be avoided, and the standard regularization-reconstruction tradeoff (e.g., KL weight) sets an upper bound on generative fidelity. The paper asks whether equivariance can be enforced to simplify the latent space without degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251244/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained autoencoders (continuous VAEs with 4–16 latent channels at 8× or 16× compression, or discrete VQ-GAN with 256 codebook entries at 16× compression) and fine-tunes them on RGB images (OpenImages for autoencoder fine-tuning, ImageNet 256×256 for generative model training). During training, spatial transformations (anisotropic scaling with factors in (0.25, 1) and rotations at 90°/180°/270°) are applied directly to the latent code before decoding, and the decoded result is compared against the correspondingly transformed input image. The output remains a standard latent representation (continuous Gaussian or discrete codebook indices) and a reconstructed image, but the latent space is now equivariant under the trained transformations.

## Method

EQ-VAE replaces the standard reconstruction target with an implicit equivariance constraint: instead of directly penalizing ‖τ∘E(x) − E(τ∘x)‖² (which causes mode collapse), it computes the reconstruction loss between the transformed input τ∘x and the decoding of the transformed latent D(τ∘E(x)), combined with the original GAN and KL terms. With probability p_α (default 0.5), the standard untransformed objective is used to preserve reconstruction capability; otherwise, a randomly sampled composition of anisotropic scaling and rotation is applied to the latent before decoding. The method requires no architectural changes and only 5 epochs of fine-tuning on a pre-trained autoencoder, and extends to discrete autoencoders by applying transformations to the continuous encoder features before quantization. Empirically, this reduces the intrinsic dimension of the latent manifold and accelerates convergence of downstream generative models (×7 speedup on DiT-XL/2, ×4 on REPA) while maintaining or improving reconstruction fidelity.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, EQ-VAE fine-tuned for just 5 epochs on SD-VAE yields a ×7 training speedup for DiT-XL/2 (reaching GFID 8.8 at 1.5M iterations vs. 9.6 at 7M without EQ-VAE) and a ×4 speedup for REPA (SiT-XL/2) (reaching GFID 5.9 at 1M vs. 4M iterations). At 400K iterations, DiT-XL/2 with EQ-VAE achieves GFID 14.5 vs. 19.5 baseline, and SiT-XL/2 improves from 17.2 to 16.1. With classifier-free guidance, DiT-XL/2 + EQ-VAE reaches GFID 2.37 in 300 epochs, matching the 1400-epoch DiT-XL/2 baseline (2.47), while REPA + EQ-VAE achieves 1.70 GFID at 200 epochs vs. 1.42 for standard REPA at 800 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQ-VAE consistently outperforms all baseline autoencoders on generative FID: SD-VAE (GFID 43.5→34.1 with DiT-B), SDXL-VAE (46.0→35.9), SD3-VAE (58.9→54.0), and VQ-GAN (6.8→5.9 with MaskGIT), while maintaining or slightly improving reconstruction quality (RFID). Against generative model baselines, EQ-VAE-regularized DiT-XL/2 achieves GFID 2.37 at 300 epochs with CFG, beating SD-DiT (3.23 at 480 epochs) and approaching MaskDiT (2.28 at 1600 epochs) with far less compute. REPA + EQ-VAE reaches 1.70 GFID at 200 epochs vs. 1.42 for standard REPA at 800 epochs, a ×4 compute reduction. For MaskGIT, EQ-VAE matches the 300-epoch baseline GFID (6.80) in only 130 epochs and surpasses both the original MaskGIT (6.19) and the PyTorch reproduction (6.80) at 300 epochs with 5.91 GFID. The paper also confirms via ablation (Table 6) that the gains come from equivariance regularization rather than additional training, as 5 extra epochs of standard fine-tuning yield negligible GFID change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022) and VQ-GAN (Esser et al., 2021), targeting the autoencoder stage that prior work has largely treated as a fixed preprocessing step. It departs from the dominant line of autoencoder improvements—increasing latent channels, raising compression ratios, or adding task-specific priors—by instead identifying a structural deficiency (lack of spatial equivariance) and correcting it through a simple implicit regularization objective. The closest prior work is Sinha & Dieng (2021), which enforces invariance (not equivariance) under spatial transformations, and Ryu (2024), which trains VQ-GANs for flip equivariance but does not study downstream generative impact. Unlike architectural approaches to equivariance (group-equivariant CNNs, steerable filters), EQ-VAE requires no structural changes and operates as a few-epoch fine-tuning of pre-trained autoencoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 5-epoch fine-tuning of SD-VAE yields a ×7 training speedup on DiT-XL/2 and ×4 on REPA, with no degradation in reconstruction quality, making this a low-cost, high-leverage intervention for anyone training latent generative models. The finding that the latent space's intrinsic dimension correlates with generative performance provides a useful diagnostic lens, and the compatibility with both continuous and discrete autoencoders broadens applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher resolutions (beyond 256×256), to continuous rotation angles (not just multiples of 90°), and whether a theoretical account of the equivariance–complexity–generative-performance link can be established. The paper is limited to ImageNet-scale experiments and does not explore text-to-image or video settings.
