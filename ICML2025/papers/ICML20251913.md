## Problem

Ordered top-K targeted adversarial attacks require perturbations that precisely control the model's ranking across multiple outputs, but prior methods like QuadAttacK optimize in the DNN's feature space and rely on backpropagation to map solutions back to image space, causing feature-image misalignment and suboptimal visual perturbations—especially for large K (>20) under limited iteration budgets. No existing approach directly learns ordered top-K attacks in the image space, leaving it unresolved what form adversarial perturbations actually take.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign RGB image of size 3×224×224 (pixel values in [0,1]) and a randomly sampled sequence of K ordered target classes (K ∈ {1,5,10,15,20,25,30}) from the 1000-class ImageNet label space, excluding the ground-truth label. It produces an adversarial perturbation δ and corresponding adversarial image x^adv = Clamp(x^benign + δ) that satisfies the ordered top-K logit ranking constraints, evaluated under ℓ₁, ℓ₂, and ℓ_∞ norms with a visual imperceptibility threshold of ℓ_∞ < 8/255 ≈ 0.0314.

## Method

RisingAttacK formulates the ordered top-K attack as a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) problem solved directly in image space. At each iteration, it linearizes the DNN via first-order Taylor expansion, applies a compact mapping G that condenses 1000 logits into d = K+5K+1 reordered logits (using SmoothMax for the remainder), and computes the SVD of the resulting adversarial Jacobian (d×D matrix). The optimization is then reduced to a low-dimensional QP over the d−1 right singular vectors of the Jacobian, solved efficiently via cvxpy, with the perturbation recovered as δ* = V_r · ε_r*. An alternating anchor strategy switches between the benign image (to minimize perturbation energy) and the current perturbed image (to rapidly reach the adversarial region), and an ℓ_∞ percentile projection (99.5th percentile) post-processes the solution to suppress outlier pixel values caused by linearization inaccuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, RisingAttacK consistently outperforms QuadAttacK across all ordered top-K levels (K=1,5,10,15,20,25,30) and all four models (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, ViT-B, DEiT-B). For ResNet-50 at K=30 with 60 iterations, RisingAttacK achieves 66.4% ASR with ℓ∞=0.0511, versus QuadAttacK's 20.8% ASR with ℓ∞=0.1349 (FoM=6.48). RisingAttacK produces visually imperceptible perturbations (ℓ∞ < 8/255) up to K=20 for ResNet-50 and DEiT-B, K=15 for ViT-B, and K=10 for DenseNet-121, while QuadAttacK fails entirely at K=30 with only 30 iterations on ResNet-50 and ViT-B. The proposed FoM metric (combining ASR ratios and ℓp-norm ratios) exceeds 2 in most settings, indicating RisingAttacK is holistically at least 2× better than QuadAttacK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is QuadAttacK (Paniagua et al., NeurIPS 2023), the prior state-of-the-art for ordered top-K attacks that operates in feature space and backpropagates to image space. RisingAttacK wins on the proposed FoM metric across all K values and all four models, with FoM values ranging from ~2.1 (Top-1, ResNet-50) to >1600 (Top-20, ViT-B with 30 iterations where QuadAttacK nearly fails). The most dramatic gains appear at large K: at K=30 on ResNet-50, RisingAttacK achieves 66.4% ASR vs. 20.8% for QuadAttacK while simultaneously reducing ℓ∞ from 0.135 to 0.051. At K=25 on ViT-B, QuadAttacK₃₀ fails entirely (0% ASR) while RisingAttacK₃₀ achieves 75.4% ASR. The one setting where QuadAttacK edges out RisingAttacK on raw ASR is Top-5 (e.g., 99.7% vs. 95.6% on ResNet-50), but RisingAttacK still produces perturbations with roughly 3× lower ℓ∞ norm, yielding a net FoM advantage. Overall, the image-space SQP formulation of RisingAttacK eliminates the feature-to-image misalignment that limits QuadAttacK, particularly as K grows beyond 15.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends QuadAttacK (Paniagua et al., 2023), which solved ordered top-K adversarial attacks via quadratic programming in the DNN's feature space and then backpropagated solutions to image space, by instead formulating the problem as a Sequential Quadratic Program (SQP) solved directly in image space. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the SVD of the adversarial Jacobian (the ranking-constrained logit-to-image Jacobian) to reduce the high-dimensional constrained optimization to a low-dimensional QP over the right singular vectors, yielding the theoretical result that optimal perturbations are linear combinations of these vectors. The ordered top-K attack formulation itself traces back to Zhang & Wu (2020), and the SQP framework is drawn from classical nonlinear optimization (Boggs & Tolle, 2000), but no prior work had applied this combination to adversarial attack generation in image space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit geometric characterization of ordered top-K adversarial perturbations as iteratively learned linear combinations of adversarial Jacobian singular vectors, which could inform defensive strategies by identifying vulnerable subspaces. Practically, it substantially extends the range of K for which visually imperceptible attacks succeed (up to K=20 for ResNet-50/DEiT-B vs. K≤10 for QuadAttacK) and eliminates the feature-to-image space misalignment that limited prior methods. However, the method is slower than QuadAttacK for K>1 due to Jacobian computation, and the evaluation compares against only a single baseline, leaving open whether the SQP approach generalizes to other attack formulations or scales efficiently to larger models and datasets.
