## Problem

Vision-Language Models struggle with complex multimodal reasoning despite LLMs making remarkable advances in reasoning, yet the mechanisms by which perception and reasoning are embedded within VLMs remain poorly understood. It is unclear whether reasoning capabilities from text-only LLMs can be transferred to VLMs without additional training, and how these two abilities interact within the model's parameter space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The merged model receives an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a text answer, operating identically to the original VLM at inference time. The merging procedure itself takes two model checkpoints sharing the same base LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3-8B): the VLM's language model weights and a reasoning-specialized LLM's weights, with only the language model component being modified while the vision tower and projector remain untouched. The key hyperparameter λ (set to 0.9) controls the balance between preserving the VLM's original multimodal capabilities and injecting the reasoning LLM's task vector.

## Method

The paper proposes cross-modality model merging via task arithmetic: it computes the VLM task vector τ_vlm = θ_vlm − θ_base and the reasoning LLM task vector τ_reason = θ_reason − θ_base, then forms the merged language model as θ'_vlm = θ_base + λ·τ_vlm + (1−λ)·τ_reason, requiring no additional training. This is applied to the language model component of VLMs (LLaVA-Next, Idefics2, InternVL2) using math-specialized LLMs (Dart-Math, MAmmoTH, Magpie, DeepSeek-R1-Distill) as reasoning sources. Beyond the merging recipe, the paper uses knockout analysis—progressively replacing layer-wise MLP and attention parameters with those from the base LLM or with uniform noise—to locate where perception and reasoning abilities reside in the parameter space. The key finding is that perception is concentrated in early layers while reasoning occupies middle-to-late layers, and after merging, reasoning ability is distributed across all layers while the perception layer distribution remains largely unchanged.

## Results

Merging LLaVA-Next-LLaMA3-8B with the Dart-Prop math task vector (λ=0.9) yields the strongest gains: +3.5 points on MathVista Math (25.4→28.9), +3.6 points on MathVerse Overall (20.1→23.7), and +3.6 points on MMStar Math (30.0→33.6). On MathVerse Text-Dominant, the improvement reaches 30% relative (25.9→30.7). Across all five math benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, Dynamath, MMStar), every math-specialized task vector improves over the baseline, while vision-dominant subtasks show minimal or slight degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six task vectors against the unmerged LLaVA baseline. Dart-Prop (math-specialized, difficulty-aware) is the clear winner, improving MathVista Math by +3.5 points and MathVerse Overall by +3.6 points, outperforming Dart-Uniform (+2.9/+3.5), DeepSeek-R1-Distill (+1.6/+1.1), and all general-domain vectors (MAmmoTH-1, MAmmoTH-2, Magpie-v0.3) which gain only 0–0.6 points on MathVista Math. General-purpose reasoning vectors (Magpie-v0.3, MAmmoTH-2) provide negligible gains, confirming that domain-specific math tuning is critical for effective transfer. On vision-dominant subtasks (MathVerse V-O, V-D), all merging variants show minimal improvement or slight degradation, indicating that the transferred reasoning ability does not address the perception bottleneck. The approach also generalizes to larger models: InternVL2-76B gains ~1 point across all benchmarks with Dart-Uniform, while Idefics2-8B shows limited benefit due to its extensive prior math fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2023) from same-modality merging (LLM+LLM) to cross-modality merging (LLM→VLM), targeting only the language model component while leaving the vision tower and projector untouched. Prior merging work (Yadav et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024b) focused on combining models of the same kind, and the few VLM merging efforts (REMEDY, Sakana AI) addressed multilingual or multitask transfer rather than reasoning capability injection. The paper further repurposes model merging as an interpretability instrument—using knockout analysis on merged vs. unmerged models—to localize perception and reasoning within the layer hierarchy, a use of merging that goes beyond prior performance-oriented applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, parameter-only recipe offers a low-cost way to boost VLM math reasoning (up to ~3.6 points on MathVista math subset) without retraining, which is practically appealing for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning. The layer-wise decomposition—perception concentrated in early layers, reasoning in middle-to-late layers, and merging distributing reasoning across all layers—provides a concrete architectural hypothesis for VLM design and future capability-specific interventions. However, gains are modest, limited to math/text-dominant tasks (vision-dominant tasks show no improvement or slight degradation), and the analysis is primarily conducted on LLaVA-8B, leaving open whether the layer-wise findings generalize to other architectures or non-math reasoning domains.
