## Problem

Safe RL methods either assume full state observability or rely on world models built solely from partial observations, which introduces severe computational and statistical challenges. While privileged information (e.g., simulator states, extra sensors) is available during training, existing approaches to integrating it into world models lack theoretical justification and exhibit low efficiency in leveraging it, leaving a gap in achieving safe, high-performance, and theoretically grounded agents under partial observability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251014/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 64×64 RGB image from an onboard camera as its sole observation at every time step, both during training and deployment. During training only, privileged information (hazard positions, velocity, acceleration, gyro readings, goal coordinates, robot rotation matrix, past actions, and Euler angles) is fed into auxiliary components. At deployment, all privileged components are disabled and the agent acts solely on the 64×64 image through the naive world model and actor, producing a discrete or continuous policy action.

## Method

PIGDreamer introduces the ACPOMDPs framework, which theoretically proves that granting the critic access to privileged underlying states reduces the value-function representation space from O(|A||Γ_{t-1}|^{|Z|}) to |S| and guarantees V_asym*(b) ≥ V_sym*(b), i.e., CPOMDPs systematically underestimate safety risks. Architecturally, it maintains two parallel RSSM-based world models (naive and privileged) within a DreamerV3 backbone: the naive model includes an oracle posterior that fuses observation and privileged embeddings into an enriched state s*, and a representation-alignment loss pulls the naive state s⁻ toward s*. Reward and cost predictors consume both s* and the privileged state s⁺ for accurate estimation, while the actor operates exclusively on s⁻. A 'Twisted Imagination' procedure synchronizes rollouts across both world models to generate coherent abstract trajectories for actor-critic training, and an Augmented Lagrangian penalty enforces safety constraints during policy optimization.

## Results

On the Guard benchmark, PIGDreamer achieves an average reward of 10.68 with a cost of 4.59, representing a 136% improvement over SafeDreamer (reward 4.52) while incurring only a 28% increase in training time (19.14h vs. 14.97h). On the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark, PIGDreamer attains an average reward of 14.5 with a cost of 6.0, substantially outperforming model-free baselines (CPO: 11.3 reward / 28.3 cost; FOCOPS: 8.9 / 38.8; PPO_Lag: 5.2 / 26.6; TRPO_Lag: 9.9 / 24.9). PIGDreamer also achieves near-zero-cost performance on Safety-Gymnasium tasks, meeting safety constraints at convergence while maximizing reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIGDreamer decisively outperforms SafeDreamer (its unprivileged variant) on the Guard benchmark, achieving 10.68 vs. 4.52 average reward (136% improvement) with only 28% more training time. Against other privileged RL methods, PIGDreamer dominates: Scaffolder (Lag) achieves only 3.38 reward with 69% longer training time, Informed-Dreamer (Lag) reaches 3.27 with 10% longer training, and Distill-Dreamer (Lag) suffers severe degradation (1.08 reward, 11.87 cost) due to the information gap in teacher-student distillation. On Safety-Gymnasium, PIGDreamer (14.5 reward, 6.0 cost) outperforms all model-free baselines—CPO (11.3/28.3), FOCOPS (8.9/38.8), PPO_Lag (5.2/26.6), and TRPO_Lag (9.9/24.9)—by a wide margin in both reward and safety. LAMBDA matches PIGDreamer in reward on some tasks but fails to satisfy safety constraints and does not produce a practical policy for PointButton1. Safe-SLAC performs worst among model-based methods, failing entirely on PointPush1 and RacecarGoal1 with significant constraint violations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamerV3 world-model framework (Hafner et al., 2023) and the SafeDreamer safe-RL variant (Huang et al., 2024) by introducing a dual-world-model architecture that asymmetrically incorporates privileged information during training. It departs from prior privileged-RL approaches—Scaffolder (Hu et al., 2024), Informed-Dreamer (Lambrechts et al., 2024), and TWIST (Yamada et al., 2023)—by formalizing the advantage of privileged information through the new ACPOMDP framework and by using representation alignment plus an asymmetric actor-critic structure rather than teacher-student distillation or auxiliary reconstruction objectives. The Lagrangian/Augmented Lagrangian constraint handling follows the standard safe-RL literature (Achiam et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021), while the 'Twisted Imagination' procedure for synchronized dual-model rollouts is a novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACPOMDP formulation provides the first clean theoretical justification (Theorem 3.3: V_asym ≥ V_sym) for why privileged information improves safe RL under partial observability, and the 136% performance gain with only 28% training overhead makes the approach practically attractive for sim-to-real deployment. A notable limitation is that privileged information does not uniformly help: in the PointButton1 task, even granting full privileged access yields no improvement, suggesting task-dependent information gain that remains unexplained. Open questions include which specific types of privileged sensors (hazards, proprioception, past actions) are most beneficial for which task structures, and whether the dual-world-model overhead can be further reduced without sacrificing the representation-alignment benefit.
