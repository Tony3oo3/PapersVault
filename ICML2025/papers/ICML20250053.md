## Problem

Computing log-determinants of large positive definite matrices (e.g., empirical NTK Gram matrices) faces a dual bottleneck: cubic computational cost and, more critically, the memory wall—matrices like the 500,000×500,000 NTK for CIFAR-10 require 2 TB of storage, exceeding available RAM. Existing stochastic approximations (SLQ, pseudo-NTK, block-diagonal) suffer 55–122% relative error on these ill-conditioned matrices, while exact computation has never been performed at this scale, leaving no reliable baseline.

## Input / Output

MEMDET consumes a dense matrix stored entirely on disk plus a user-specified memory budget in bytes, and outputs the exact log-determinant, determinant sign, and the sequence of log-determinants for every leading principal submatrix—requiring only 3–4 b×b blocks in RAM at any time. FLODANCE takes a small precomputed NTK Gram matrix subsample (e.g., 5,000 of 50,000 datapoints, yielding a 50,000×50,000 matrix), the total datapoint count n, output dimension d, a burn-in index n_0, and a Laurent-series truncation order q, and produces a single scalar estimate of the normalized log-determinant L_n for the full nd×nd matrix. The key constraint is that MEMDET's block size b is determined by the memory budget c via b ≈ m/n_b where n_b = ⌈2m√(β/c)⌉, so the same algorithm adapts to any hardware.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary algorithms. MEMDET performs a hierarchical block-wise LDL (or LU/Cholesky) decomposition where only small b×b blocks reside in memory; at each stage it computes an in-place factorization of the diagonal block, solves triangular systems for off-diagonal blocks, and forms Schur complements stored on a disk scratchpad. The block processing order is derived from Hamiltonian paths in a line graph to minimize redundant disk reads, keeping total FLOP count at O(m³) independent of block count. FLODANCE exploits a scaling-law result (Lemma 1 + Assumption 1) showing det(K_n)/det(K_{n-1}) ≈ C/n^ν, which implies the normalized log-determinant L_n is an affine function of log(n!)/n plus unknown constants c_0 and ν_i. A single MEMDET pass on a tiny subsample yields the sequence (L_{n_0}, …, L_{n_s}), from which these constants are recovered by ordinary least squares on a design matrix involving √(n−1) and −log(n!)/n^{i−1}·√(n−1) covariates; the fit is then extrapolated to the full n. A burn-in period (typically < 500 terms) discards early samples where small eigenvalues emerge abruptly, and a non-asymptotic Laurent correction ν(n) = ν_0 + Σ ν_i n^{−i} (q ≤ 10) captures finite-size deviations.

## Results

On a 500,000×500,000 NTK Gram matrix (ResNet50, CIFAR-10, n=50,000), FLODANCE with n_s=5000 and q=4 achieves 0.02% relative error in 1.5 hours at $4 cost, compared to 55% error for SLQ (1.8 days, $83) and 0% error for the exact MEMDET baseline (13.8 days, $601) — a ~100,000× speedup over the exact method. On smaller datasets (n=1000–2500), FLODANCE with a burn-in phase (n_0=300, n_s=500) achieves 1.3–2.9% relative error on CIFAR-10 models, outperforming 32-bit direct computation (3.7–6.8%) and 16-bit (12.4–17.1%), while SLQ and Pseudo-NTK approximations incur 75–122% error. The first exact computation of a full-precision NTK determinant at this scale (2 TB matrix) was achieved via MEMDET on an NVIDIA GH200 GPU over 244 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLODANCE decisively outperforms both baselines across all tested settings. Against SLQ (Ubaru et al., 2017), FLODANCE reduces relative error from 55% to 0.02% on the 500,000×500,000 matrix (Table 1) and from 81–101% to 1.3–2.9% on smaller CIFAR-10 datasets (Table 2), while also being orders of magnitude faster. Against the Pseudo-NTK approximation (Mohamadi et al., 2023), FLODANCE reduces error from 75–123% to 1.3–2.9% on CIFAR-10 models; on MNIST (MobileNet), FLODANCE still achieves 23.2% error versus 75.3% for Pseudo-NTK. Notably, FLODANCE with a burn-in phase also outperforms reducing numerical precision (32-bit: 3.7–6.8% error; 16-bit: 12.4–17.1%), demonstrating that the scaling-law extrapolation captures spectral information that mixed-precision arithmetic destroys. MEMDET, while not a baseline per se, provides the exact ground truth that makes these comparisons meaningful and is itself the first method to compute NTK determinants at this scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: out-of-core matrix factorization (Dongarra et al., 1998a) and neural scaling laws for kernel methods (Li et al., 2023). MEMDET extends the out-of-core LU/LDL/Cholesky framework specifically to log-determinant computation under arbitrary memory constraints, while FLODANCE introduces a novel theoretical bridge between GP test-error scaling laws and the ratio of successive Gram-matrix determinants. The paper departs from prior log-determinant approximation methods (SLQ, Taylor expansions) by addressing the memory bottleneck rather than merely the cubic compute cost, and from prior NTK approximations (pseudo-NTK, block-diagonal) by targeting the full eigenvalue spectrum rather than spectral-norm convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exact computation of an empirical NTK Gram-matrix determinant at 500,000×500,000 scale, and FLODANCE's ~100,000× speedup with 0.02% error makes NTK-based model selection (via interpolating information criteria) practically feasible for the first time. The theoretical contribution—a law of large numbers and CLT for normalized log-determinants under a power-law scaling assumption—provides a general framework applicable beyond NTKs to any kernel family with eigenvalue decay. Open questions include whether the observed burn-in phenomenon (sudden emergence of small eigenvalues shifting the regression fit) has a deeper structural explanation, whether the scaling-law assumption holds universally across architectures and datasets, and whether the approach extends to the even larger ImageNet-scale NTKs (13 exabytes) that remain intractable even for MEMDET.
