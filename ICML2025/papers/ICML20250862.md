## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks largely test surface-level visual perception or text-dominant reasoning, failing to assess whether MLLMs can perform integrated reasoning that genuinely requires back-and-forth between visual and textual information. The paper asks whether current MLLMs can reason organically across both modalities in a way that cannot be decomposed into independent text-only or single-pass visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250862/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs a text prompt with one or more images (93% of questions contain an image in the prompt, 10% contain multiple images, and 7% place images in the answer options). Questions are split 72/28 between multiple-choice (4 options) and free-form short-answer formats, with all ground-truth answers being short and easily checkable. The benchmark is designed so that neither text-only reasoning nor a single visual pass suffices; models must iteratively interpret visual content and apply domain-specific reasoning steps.

## Method

EMMA is constructed through a two-step curation pipeline. First, questions from existing benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MMMU, OlympiadBench, EXAMS-V, RAVEN) are filtered using an enhanced mechanism: images are captioned by GPT-4o, and if any of three MLLMs (Llama-3-70B, GPT-4o, Qwen2-72B) answers correctly in at least 5 of 10 trials given only the text plus captions, the question is discarded—stricter than MMMU-Pro's text-only filter. Second, 1,796 new questions are manually constructed with domain experts, including organic chemistry problems generated via RDKit from SMICRM and visualization coding tasks built from 47 seed matplotlib/seaborn charts with four design variations each. Every question is assigned fine-grained skill labels (e.g., 2D Transformation, 3D Field Simulation, Reaction Simulation Pro, Polar Coordinates) enabling per-skill error analysis. The paper then evaluates ten SoTA MLLMs under direct and CoT prompting, and probes test-time compute scaling via majority voting, Best-of-N, and tournament selection up to N=16.

## Results

On the balanced EMMA-mini subset (400 questions), the best-performing model, Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking-0121, achieves 48.00% overall accuracy, trailing human experts (77.75%) by 29.75 percentage points. On the full EMMA benchmark (2,788 questions), Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking-0121 leads with 42.50% overall, while the best non-reasoning model (Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct) scores 33.46%. Test-time compute scaling (majority voting, best-of-N, tournament) yields at most a 7.5% improvement for Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking, still leaving a 27% gap to human performance. CoT prompting improves closed-source models by 0.3–3.0% but degrades open-source models by up to 7.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All ten evaluated MLLMs fall substantially short of human expert performance (77.75%) on EMMA-mini. Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking-0121 achieves the highest score at 48.00%, followed by o1 at 45.75% and Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking-1219 at 43.50%; the best non-reasoning model, Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct, reaches only 37.25% under direct prompting. Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0 Flash) generally outperform open-source counterparts (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-Onevision, InternVL2/2.5) on the full EMMA benchmark, with the gap widening under CoT prompting. Notably, CoT prompting improves closed-source models by 0.3–3.0% but degrades the top open-source models: Qwen2-VL drops from 37.25% to 31.00% (−6.25%) and InternVL2.5-78B from 35.25% to 27.50% (−7.75%). On the coding subtask, o1 (53.00%) outperforms Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking-0121 (48.00%) by 5%, suggesting stronger code-visualization reasoning. Test-time compute scaling (up to N=16) provides only marginal gains of 2.5–7.5%, with tournament selection using Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking as reward model consistently outperforming best-of-N and majority voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMMA extends the multimodal reasoning benchmark lineage initiated by MMMU, MathVista, and MMMU-Pro by applying a stricter two-stage filtering pipeline: in addition to discarding questions solvable from text alone (as MMMU-Pro does), it also removes questions answerable from text plus GPT-4o-generated image captions, ensuring that remaining problems genuinely require visual reasoning beyond a single perceptual pass. The paper departs from spatial-cognition benchmarks (Ramakrishnan et al., 2024; Chollet, 2019) and visual-puzzle benchmarks by introducing domain-specific tasks—3D field simulation, chemical reaction simulation, and program-output visualization—where visual aids are integral rather than decorative. For test-time compute scaling, EMMA builds on the majority-voting, Best-of-N, and tournament-selection frameworks from Snell et al. (2024) and the o1 paper, applying them in a multimodal setting where reward models must also process visual content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMMA exposes a substantial and persistent gap between SoTA MLLMs (best: 48%) and human experts (77.75%) on tasks requiring integrated visual-textual reasoning, with the largest shortfalls in math (34% gap), chemistry (39%), and coding (32.5%). The finding that textual CoT prompting actively degrades open-source model performance on visual-centric tasks (e.g., 2D Transformation) challenges the prevailing assumption that more reasoning steps universally help, suggesting that current architectures lack a mechanism to translate verbalized reasoning into genuine visual simulation. Test-time compute scaling yields only 5–7.5% gains despite Pass@N reaching 74–81.5%, indicating that models can occasionally produce correct visual reasoning but cannot reliably identify or reproduce it. Open questions include whether new architectures (e.g., native visual CoT, iterative image manipulation) can close the gap, and whether the underrepresented physics section (156 questions) limits the benchmark's coverage of spatial-physical reasoning.
