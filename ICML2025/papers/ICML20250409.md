## Problem

Semi-dual Neural OT (SNOT) frameworks, which learn optimal transport maps via a GAN-like max-min objective, can converge to spurious solutions that fail to transport the source distribution to the target, even when a unique optimal transport map exists. No prior work identified the conditions under which the max-min solution is guaranteed to recover the true OT map, nor provided a practical remedy for the failure cases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples from a source distribution μ (grayscale digits at 32×32, faces at 64×64 or 128×128, or animal images at 64×64) and a target distribution ν, with no paired data required. During training, each source sample is perturbed by Gaussian or variance-preserving noise at a schedule-dependent level before being fed to the transport network. The output is a transported sample from ν (a colored digit, face, or cat image) and, in the stochastic setting, a full transport plan π*(y|x) that can map one source point to multiple target points (e.g., one grayscale digit to red, green, or blue variants).

## Method

The paper first proves (Thm 3.1) that if the source distribution μ assigns no mass to measurable sets of Hausdorff dimension ≤ d−1, the max-min solution of the SNOT objective uniquely recovers the optimal transport map, and then characterizes concrete failure cases (perpendicular, parallel, one-to-many, grid) where this condition is violated. To handle those cases, the OTP model smooths μ into an absolutely continuous μ_ε via Gaussian or variance-preserving convolution, guaranteeing the sufficient condition holds at every noise level, and then gradually anneals ε_k → 0 over training so that the learned plan converges (up to subsequence) to the true OT plan π* (Thm 4.1). Training follows an adversarial loop: a potential network V_φ (optionally an ICNN for c-concavity) is maximized while a transport network T_θ (UNet for images, MLP for synthetic data) is minimized over the noised source, with the noise schedule linearly or exponentially decreasing from σ_max to σ_min > 0. This smoothing-then-annealing strategy is the key mechanism that eliminates spurious solutions without modifying the cost function or the semi-dual objective itself.

## Results

On the Male-to-Female (128×128) image translation benchmark, OTP achieves a FID of 6.38, outperforming OTM (7.55), DIOTM (7.40), DSBM (37.8), and ASBM (16.08). On Wild-to-Cat (64×64), OTP reaches FID 9.66 vs. OTM (12.42) and DSBM (20+). On the synthetic spurious-solution failure cases, OTP dramatically reduces target distribution error D_target in high dimensions: at d=16 on the Perpendicular dataset, OTP achieves D_target=0.59 versus OTM (4.97) and OTM-s (4.85), and at d=64, OTP achieves 10.09 versus OTM (19.37) and OTM-s (18.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTP outperforms the standard SNOT model OTM (Fan et al., 2022) on FID across all three image translation benchmarks (4.75 vs. 6.42 on Male→Female 64×64; 9.66 vs. 12.42 on Wild→Cat; 6.38 vs. 7.55 on Male→Female 128×128), confirming that the spurious solution problem degrades OTM's target distribution accuracy. Against DIOTM (Choi et al., 2024a), OTP wins on FID for Wild→Cat (9.66 vs. 10.72) and Male→Female 128×128 (6.38 vs. 7.40) but trails slightly on Male→Female 64×64 (4.75 vs. 4.48). OTP dramatically surpasses the entropic OT baselines DSBM (Shi et al., 2024) and ASBM (Gushchin et al., 2024) on FID, e.g., 6.38 vs. 37.8 and 16.08 respectively on Male→Female 128×128. On the synthetic spurious-solution datasets, OTP consistently achieves the lowest D_target in high dimensions (d≥16), where both OTM and OTM-s suffer severe target distribution errors (e.g., D_target of 4.97 and 4.85 at d=16 vs. OTP's 0.59). Against the Wasserstein-2 benchmark baseline MMv2:R (Korotin et al., 2021b), OTP achieves comparable performance (e.g., L²-UVP of 3.7 vs. 2.8 at d=256), though the benchmark's Gaussian mixture source is already absolutely continuous, making it less favorable to OTP's noise-smoothing mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Semi-dual Neural OT (SNOT) adversarial framework established by Fan et al. (2022) and Rout et al. (2022), which parametrizes the Kantorovich potential and transport map as a GAN-like max-min problem. It departs from prior work by providing the first sufficient condition (a Hausdorff dimension constraint on the source distribution) under which the max-min solution provably recovers the true OT Map, going beyond the saddle-point analysis of Fan et al. (2023) and the weak-OT-specific analyses of Korotin et al. (2023a,b). The proposed OTP model draws a conceptual analogy to diffusion model noise schedules (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021), applying Gaussian smoothing to the source distribution and gradually annealing the noise level to satisfy the theoretical condition while converging to the original transport problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental theoretical gap in Neural OT by characterizing exactly when the widely-used SNOT framework produces spurious solutions, giving practitioners a clear diagnostic (source distribution geometry) and a simple fix (noise annealing). The extension from deterministic OT Maps to stochastic OT Plans is practically important for inherently multi-valued tasks like colorization and inpainting, where prior SNOT methods provably fail. Key limitations include the convergence guarantee holding only up to a subsequence, the condition being sufficient rather than necessary, and the practical requirement of a non-zero minimum noise level. Open questions include whether the Hausdorff dimension condition can be sharpened to a necessary-and-sufficient criterion and whether the annealing schedule can be made adaptive.
