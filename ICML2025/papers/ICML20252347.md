## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for solid mechanics rely on implicit modeling, where factors influencing deformation (loads, contact constraints) are not explicitly structured but learned indirectly through global or graph-based propagation. As the number of solids and contact pairs grows, these approaches suffer from information blending, ambiguity in physical interactions, and poor scalability, leaving a notable gap in systematically modeling complex multi-solid systems with diverse coupling patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D Lagrangian mesh points for each object in the system—deformable solids (with material properties like Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, stress, friction), rigid solids, and loads (encoded as concatenated current and next-step coordinates)—and produces predicted geometry, inner stress, and plastic equivalent strain (PEEQ) for each deformable solid. The architecture handles variable numbers of solids (1–3 deformable, 1–17 rigid, 1–16 loads, 1–18 contact pairs) and supports two inference modes: single-shot long-time prediction over many time steps and step-by-step autoregressive simulation.

## Method

Unisoma introduces an explicit modeling paradigm built around a deformation triplet (deformable solid, equivalent load, equivalent contact constraint) that directly encodes the physical factors driving deformation. Each object is independently embedded into Edge Augmented Physics-Aware Tokens via kNN-based mesh edges and slice aggregation, preserving local geometric structure while enabling global attention. The processor then (1) applies dedicated attention-based contact modules to each pair of potentially contacting solids, (2) uses an adaptive interaction allocation mechanism to compute per-solid equivalent loads and contact constraints via learned weighted aggregation, and (3) feeds the triplet into individual deformation modules that model each solid's response through attention. This per-object, per-contact-pair decomposition avoids the information confusion inherent in implicit methods, and the slice composition/decomposition framework provides a theoretical justification for the element-wise addition operations used to merge tokens from different objects.

## Results

On the complex multi-solid long-time prediction task (Bilateral Stamping), Unisoma achieves the best Relative L2 across all six targets, e.g., 0.0057 for metal geometry vs. 0.0083 for the strongest baseline Transolver, and 0.2265 for rubber PEEQ vs. 0.3276. On the even more challenging Unilateral Stamping (17 rigid solids, 16 loads, 18 contact pairs), Unisoma again leads on all targets, e.g., 0.0071 metal geometry vs. 0.0079 Transolver. On the autoregressive simulation task, the advantage is most dramatic in the multi-solid Cavity Extruding dataset, where Unisoma achieves 11.98×10⁻² RMSE-all versus 53.98×10⁻² for the best GNN baseline (HOOD), a ~4.5× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unisoma outperforms all 14 baselines on every target across all seven datasets. The strongest competitor is Transolver (which also uses slice tokens), and the gap between them widens with system complexity: on the simple Deforming Plate (1 deformable + 1 rigid solid), Unisoma leads by only 0.004 in Relative L2 (0.0892 vs. 0.0933), while on Unilateral Stamping (2 deformable + 17 rigid solids, 18 contact pairs) the margin grows to 0.0008 in geometry but 0.048 in PEEQ (0.2114 vs. 0.2693). On the autoregressive Cavity Extruding task (3 deformable solids, 4 rigid, 6 contacts), Unisoma's 11.98×10⁻² RMSE-all is roughly 4.5× better than the best GNN baseline HOOD (53.98×10⁻²) and 4.8× better than MGN (57.37×10⁻²), whereas on single-deformable-solid tasks like Cavity Grasping the margin over HOOD is more modest (9.50 vs. 12.43 ×10⁻³). Neural operator baselines (Geo-FNO, GINO, GNO, LSM) and Transformer baselines (Galerkin, Factformer, OFormer, ONO) are consistently outperformed, with the largest gaps appearing on stress and PEEQ targets in multi-solid settings. GNN-based methods (GraphSAGE, GraphUNet, MGN, HOOD, HCMT) struggle most on multi-solid autoregressive tasks due to their local message-passing radius, which limits long-range contact influence propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Transolver's (Wu et al., 2024) slice-token embedding for representing mesh points in a compressed latent space, but departs fundamentally by introducing an 'explicit modeling' paradigm for multi-solid systems. Where prior work—whether domain-wise (PINNs, Neural Operators like Geo-FNO, GINO) or graph-wise (MGN, HCMT, HOOD)—implicitly learns physical interactions through end-to-end training, Unisoma structures the model around the deformation triplet (deformable solid, equivalent load, equivalent contact constraint) with dedicated contact modules and an adaptive interaction allocation mechanism. The addition of mesh edges to the slice embedding (Edge Augmented Physics-Aware Tokens) addresses a local-information gap in Transolver's point-only aggregation, and the per-object embedding strategy enables flexible handling of variable numbers of solids without re-architecting the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit modeling paradigm offers a principled alternative to the dominant implicit-learning approach for multi-solid simulation, with the practical advantage of dramatically lower memory usage (~1 GiB vs. 16–23 GiB at ~50K mesh points) and stronger OOD generalization to unseen material parameters. The three newly introduced industrial-inspired datasets (Bilateral Stamping, Unilateral Stamping, Cavity Extruding) fill a gap in benchmark complexity, moving beyond the few-solid scenarios that have dominated prior evaluation. Open questions include whether the explicit modeling paradigm extends to fluid-solid coupling or other multi-physics settings, how it scales beyond ~20 contact pairs, and whether the per-object module design remains tractable for systems with hundreds of interacting bodies.
