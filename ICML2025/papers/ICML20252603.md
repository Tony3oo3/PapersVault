## Problem

Designing effective reward functions in multi-agent reinforcement learning is a major bottleneck: sparse or complex rewards cause agents to converge slowly or settle on suboptimal cooperative policies. While RLHF has been applied to single-agent settings and LLM alignment, no prior work integrates iterative, multi-phase human feedback of varying quality directly into the online MARL training loop to dynamically reshape per-agent reward functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes agent rollout trajectories (rendered as video clips sampled at 1 fps for human/VLM evaluation), free-form natural-language feedback from humans of mixed expertise, and structured environment state observations (e.g., 32-length vectors in Overcooked, dictionary-keyed features in GRF). It produces a weighted pool of per-agent reward functions combining the original task reward with LLM-generated shaped rewards from predefined templates, along with the resulting trained multi-agent policies. Human involvement is capped at five generations, and the LLM (GPT-4o) mediates all parsing and reward-template selection, so raw human text never directly enters the reward computation.

## Method

M3HF extends the Markov Game to a Multi-phase Human Feedback Markov Game (MHF-MG) where, at each of up to five generations, agents pause training, generate evaluation rollouts, and receive human feedback that an LLM parses into agent-specific and global instructions. The LLM then selects from a library of predefined reward templates (distance-based, action-based, status-based, composite) and parameterizes them against environment entities to produce a new per-agent reward function, which is added to a growing reward pool. A weight-decay schedule (alpha^(M-m)) progressively down-weights older reward components, while a performance-based adjustment rule increases or decreases the newest component's weight by beta depending on whether the updated policy improves on the original task reward. The final per-agent reward is a normalized weighted sum over the pool, and IPPO optimizes policies under this composite signal. The paper provides two theoretical results: a convergence guarantee for rollout-based performance estimation (Proposition 4.1) and a robustness bound showing that low-quality feedback causes at most a single bounded degradation delta independent of the number of generations (Proposition 4.2).

## Results

On Overcooked-B (Lettuce-Onion-Tomato salad), M³HF achieves an average return of 164.8±1.2 at iteration 400 (Generation 1), substantially outperforming IPPO (27.4±4.9 at iteration 1000) and the best intrinsic-reward baseline IRAT-rw_1 (94.9±10.7 at iteration 1000). Across all six Overcooked layout-recipe combinations (Figure 3), M³HF consistently surpasses Mac-based Baseline, IPPO, and MAPPO, converging to near-optimal performance in fewer than five feedback rounds on simpler recipes. In the GRF 5v5-HARD benchmark (Figure 6), M³HF outperforms all baselines on Win Rate, Goal Difference, Total Move, Good Shot, and Interception metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³HF (built on IPPO) outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against IPPO (De Witt et al., 2020), M³HF achieves 164.8±1.2 at iteration 400 versus IPPO's 27.4±4.9 at iteration 1000 on Overcooked-B, a roughly 6× improvement in average episode return. Against MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022), M³HF maintains a consistent advantage in all six Overcooked layout-recipe combinations and in GRF 5v5-HARD, with the gap widening in more complex layouts (C) and recipes (Lettuce-Onion-Tomato). Against the Mac-based Baseline (Xiao et al., 2022, averaging Mac-IACC and Mac-CAC), M³HF shows accelerated early learning and higher asymptotic performance. Against IRAT (Wang et al., 2022), the best intrinsic-reward variant (rw_1) reaches 94.9±10.7 only after 1000 iterations, while M³HF already exceeds 164.8 after just 400 iterations, demonstrating that observation-driven human feedback targets coordination failures more precisely than handcrafted intrinsic rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF paradigm from single-agent and offline settings (Christiano et al. 2017; PbMARL, Zhang et al. 2024) to an online, multi-phase, multi-agent context, formalizing the interaction as a Multi-phase Human Feedback Markov Game (MHF-MG). It draws on LLM-based reward design work (EUREKA, MOTIF, Code as Policies) but departs by using LLMs not to generate novel reward code from scratch, but to parse heterogeneous human utterances and select/parameterize from a fixed set of predefined reward templates. The weight-decay and performance-based adjustment mechanism for handling mixed-quality feedback is novel relative to prior RLHF approaches that assume a single quality tier or a single feedback round.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework democratizes MARL reward design by showing that non-expert human feedback, even when noisy or erroneous, can be safely integrated with only bounded performance degradation (Proposition 4.2), lowering the barrier for human-in-the-loop multi-agent training. However, the evaluation is confined to grid-based environments (Overcooked, GRF 5v5) with at most 5 feedback rounds and 2–3 agents, leaving open whether the template-based reward generation generalizes to continuous-control or high-dimensional multi-agent domains. The finding that current VLMs (Gemini-1.5-Pro) produce vague, non-actionable feedback compared to humans highlights a gap that limits full automation of the loop, and the reliance on a fixed template library constrains the expressiveness of the generated rewards.
