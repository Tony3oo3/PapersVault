## Problem

Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), the standard algorithm for elastic time series alignment, suffers from quadratic O(L²) computational cost and sensitivity to noise, making it impractical for long or high-throughput signals. While 2D keypoint detection and description (e.g., SIFT, SuperPoint) has long enabled efficient image registration, no equivalent self-supervised framework exists for 1D time series, where nonlinear temporal distortions are more complex than image homographies and labeled keypoints are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TimePoint takes two univariate 1D time series of arbitrary length (trained at L=512 but generalizing to L=2500+) and produces a sparse set of keypoint locations (selected via NMS with window size 5 and top-K confidence thresholding) paired with 256-dimensional L2-normalized descriptors at those locations. The encoder downsamples by a factor of 8 via strided WTConv blocks, while the descriptor decoder upsamples back to full temporal resolution, so descriptors are defined at every original timestep but only extracted at detected keypoints. The final alignment is a DTW path computed over the sparse descriptor sequences using a cosine-similarity cost, reducing complexity from O(L·L′) to O(L̃·L̃′) where L̃ ≪ L.

## Method

TimePoint uses a fully convolutional shared encoder built from Wavelet Transform Convolution (WTConv) layers (3-level decomposition, kernel size 3, stride-2 downsampling) that maintains a fixed ~200K parameter count regardless of input length, feeding two decoders: a keypoint detector (1×1 conv → 8-channel cell → reshape → sigmoid → NMS) and a descriptor generator (1×1 conv to 256-d → linear upsampling → L2 normalization). Training is self-supervised on the SynthAlign synthetic dataset, where CPAB diffeomorphisms (sampled from a zero-mean Gaussian smoothness prior over piecewise-affine velocity fields with 16 domain segments) warp signals to create paired examples with exact ground-truth correspondences. The loss combines binary cross-entropy for keypoint detection with a margin-based contrastive loss (positive margin m_p=1, negative margin m_n=0.1) computed only over matched keypoint descriptors, focusing training on salient regions. At inference, DTW operates on the sparse 256-d descriptors rather than raw scalar signals, yielding up to 100× speedup at 10% keypoint usage while typically improving alignment accuracy over full-signal DTW.

## Results

On 102 UCR datasets, TimePoint+DTW at 20% keypoints achieves 0.721 average 1-NN accuracy (vs. 0.706 for standard DTW) while reducing GPU runtime from 192 hours to 2.88 hours (≈65× speedup). With fine-tuning on real data, TP+DTW at 20% KPs reaches 0.790 accuracy. The Critical Difference Diagram confirms TP+DTW attains the statistically highest average rank at both 100% and 20% keypoint usage across all 102 datasets, outperforming DTW, SoftDTW (γ∈{0.1,1,10}), DTW-GI, and ShapeDTW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimePoint+DTW at 20% keypoints outperforms standard DTW (Sakoe & Chiba, 1978) by 1.5% in accuracy (0.721 vs. 0.706) while achieving a 65× runtime reduction (2.88 h vs. 192 h). Against SoftDTW (Cuturi & Blondel, 2017) at all tested γ values, TP+DTW at 20% KPs leads by 5.9–6.3% in accuracy (0.721 vs. 0.658–0.662) with comparable or lower runtime. In the Critical Difference Diagram across 102 UCR datasets, TP+DTW achieves the highest average rank with statistical significance at both 100% and 20% keypoint ratios, placing it above DTW-GI (Vayer et al., 2020) and all three ShapeDTW (Zhao & Itti, 2018) descriptor variants. Fine-tuning on real UCR data further widens the gap: TP+DTW at 20% KPs reaches 0.790, surpassing the best SoftDTW+fine-tuning result (0.752) by 3.8% while remaining 65× faster than full-length DTW.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimePoint adapts the 2D keypoint detection and description paradigm of SuperPoint (DeTone et al., 2018) to 1D time series, replacing homography-based augmentation with CPAB diffeomorphic warps (Freifeld et al., 2017) to model the richer nonlinear temporal distortions inherent in signals. The encoder leverages the recently proposed WTConv architecture (Finder et al., 2024) to achieve large receptive fields with a fixed parameter count, enabling variable-length inputs without the quadratic cost of transformers. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior DTW variants (SoftDTW, ShapeDTW, DTW-GI) that modify the alignment algorithm itself, instead learning a sparse intermediate representation that makes the alignment problem smaller and more robust. It also extends the diffeomorphic alignment line (DTAN and successors) by adding explicit keypoint detection and discriminative descriptors, which those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing DTW from O(L·L') to O(L̃·L̃') with learned keypoints yields up to 100× speedups while often improving accuracy, making large-scale time-series classification and retrieval feasible on GPU. The zero-shot generalization from a purely synthetic training set (SynthAlign) to 102 diverse real-world UCR datasets is a strong signal that the learned keypoints and descriptors capture transferable temporal structure. Open questions include extension to multivariate signals, handling of very short sequences where the fixed 8× downsampling may be excessive, and whether the CPAB prior adequately covers the full range of real-world temporal distortions. The approach also raises the question of whether similar sparse-representation strategies could accelerate other quadratic-cost sequence alignment problems.
