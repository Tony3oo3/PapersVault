## Problem

Upcycling—reusing a pretrained dense model's weights to initialize a Mixture-of-Experts model—has been adopted by several large-scale MoE training efforts, yet prior studies report contradictory conclusions about its effectiveness. No systematic scaling law framework existed to predict when upcycling outperforms from-scratch MoE training, how performance depends on the dense training budget (sunk cost) versus the upcycled training budget, or how model architecture choices interact with these factors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained dense transformer checkpoint (trained for D₁ tokens on a given corpus) and a target MoE architecture specification (number of experts n_expert, top-k routing n_TopK), then continues training the upcycled MoE for D₂ additional tokens. The output is a closed-form scaling law predicting cross-entropy loss as a function of D₁, D₂, and model size N₁, from which a critical threshold D* can be computed: below D*, upcycling is more token-efficient than from-scratch MoE training; above it, from-scratch training wins. The scaling law also predicts compute-optimal allocation of FLOPs between dense and upcycled stages.

## Method

The paper derives a multiplicative scaling law for upcycled MoE loss: L = A·D₁^(−α₁)·D₂^(−α₂+α₃·log D₁) + E, where the novel interaction term α₃·log D₁ captures how larger sunk cost D₁ reduces the effective scaling exponent for upcycled tokens D₂, causing diminishing returns. This form is validated by showing that the fitted scaling exponent for D₂ decreases logarithmically with D₁ (Equation 10), and that the scaling factor A follows a power law in D₁. A separate additive scaling law for model configuration, L = A·P^(−β₁) + F·N₂^(−β₂) + E (where P is sparsity and N₂ is active parameters), shows that upcycling benefits from increased sparsity and active parameters without trade-offs. Combining both yields a joint scaling law (Equation 12) that extrapolates well across model sizes up to 7B total parameters, enabling prediction of the threshold D* ≈ 4·(N₁/10⁹)^(−0.7+0.04·log(N₁/10⁹)) B tokens beyond which from-scratch MoE training becomes more efficient.

## Results

The paper derives a multiplicative scaling law for upcycled MoE loss: L = A·D₁^(−α₁)·D₂^(−α₂+α₃·log D₁) + E, with fitted exponents α₁=0.043, α₂=0.085, α₃=7.98×10⁻⁴ (Table 2), confirming that increasing upcycled tokens (D₂) helps more than increasing sunk cost (D₁), but the interaction term causes diminishing returns at high D₁. The critical threshold D* below which upcycling beats from-scratch MoE training is approximately 4B tokens for an 8×1B model (Equation 2), decreasing with model size. On standard NLP benchmarks, the Upcycled 8×1B model achieves the best average score (52.94) among all compared ~1B models, outperforming Pythia-1B (50.53) and the paper's own 1B dense model (49.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Upcycled 8×1B model (trained on ~37B SlimPajama tokens for the upcycling stage, following ~37B dense tokens) achieves the highest average benchmark score (52.94), beating Pythia-1B (50.53, trained on 100B Pile tokens) by ~2.4 points and the paper's own 1B dense model (49.98, 74B tokens) by ~3.0 points. Against Pythia-1B, the upcycled model wins on ARC-c (+4.5), ARC-e (+8.9), SciQ (+4.7), and Winogrande (+6.6), but loses on Lambada (−3.8), LogiQA (−1.8), and PIQA (−2.1). Against TinyLlama-1.1B (103B tokens), the upcycled model leads on ARC-c (+5.8), ARC-e (+11.2), and Winogrande (+4.5), though TinyLlama edges ahead on PIQA (+0.1). Notably, the upcycled model uses substantially fewer total training tokens (~74B combined) than both baselines (~100B+), suggesting upcycling provides a meaningful efficiency gain at matched model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Komatsuzaki et al.'s sparse upcycling method (reusing dense checkpoints to initialize MoE experts) by providing the first systematic scaling-law characterization of the technique. It builds on the Chinchilla scaling law framework (Hoffmann et al., 2022) and the broader power-law scaling literature (Kaplan et al., 2020), but introduces a novel interaction term (α₃ log D₁) in the exponent that captures the interplay between dense pretraining tokens and upcycled MoE tokens. The paper also connects to transfer-learning scaling laws (Mikami et al., 2022; Zhang et al.) that use multiplicative/additive forms, while noting that no prior work incorporates such an interaction term. It resolves contradictory empirical reports from Wei et al. (2024) and Muennighoff et al. (2024) by showing both can be correct under different sunk-cost and model-size regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete threshold (D*) for deciding when upcycling is more efficient than from-scratch MoE training, which is directly actionable as upcycling is being adopted in large-scale production models (Mixtral, Skywork, Sarashina). The interaction term reveals a counterintuitive diminishing-returns effect: more dense pretraining tokens make the upcycled MoE converge *slower* with additional tokens, a finding that explains prior contradictory results. Key limitations include the study being capped at 7B parameters and a single Mixtral-like architecture (8 experts, top-2), and the interaction term lacks theoretical justification. Open questions include extending the scaling laws to shared/fine-grained expert architectures, other modalities, and deriving a formal explanation for the log-interaction term.
