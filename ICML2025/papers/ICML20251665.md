## Problem

1D convolution offers linear parameter scaling and hardware pipelining advantages over 2D convolution, but naively flattening images into 1D streams (e.g., via raster scanning) destroys spatial locality essential for detecting edges, textures, and shapes. While Hilbert and Z-order curves improve locality for most pixels, they sacrifice pixels at quadrant boundaries, and no prior work has resolved this trade-off to enable a purely 1D CNN to match 2D CNN performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251665/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, SVHN) or 64×64 (ImageNet-64) and outputs a class label via a dense classification head. Unusually, the input image is first transformed by a read-only path traversal layer that reorders pixels into three parallel 1D streams following shifted Hilbert or Z-order curves, so all subsequent convolutions operate on 1D sequences rather than 2D feature maps. A custom CUDA kernel accelerates this traversal by up to 73× over a single-thread CPU implementation.

## Method

The paper introduces PathConv, a CNN built exclusively from 1D operators. Its key insight is a path-shifting method: by sampling an s×s window from a spatially expanded (s+p)×(s+p) Hilbert or Z-order curve at various offsets and rotations, sacrificed pixels at quadrant boundaries are relocated, and three such shifted paths (found via randomized rounding on a set-cover formulation) guarantee every pixel has shorter cumulative neighbor distances than raster scanning in at least one path. The architecture adapts depthwise separable convolution and inverted bottleneck designs to 1D, with a path-aware channel attention (PACA) module that computes dual-scale intra-path channel attention and inter-path MLP-based attention to mitigate convergence issues from processing distinct image regions concurrently. PathConvS/B achieve ResNet-18/50-level accuracy on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and ImageNet-64 using only ~1/3 the parameters at comparable FLOPs.

## Results

PathConv models achieve ResNet-level top-1 accuracy using only ~1/3 of the parameters (3.62M vs 11.68M for small, 7.82M vs 25.55M for base) at comparable FLOPs. On CIFAR-10, PathConvB-H achieves 93.97% (matching ResNet-50's 93.95%), while PathConvS-Z reaches 92.66% vs ResNet-18's 93.17%. On ImageNet-64, PathConvB-Z achieves 68.83%, slightly exceeding ResNet-50 (68.43%), and PathConvS-Z (62.73%) outperforms WRN-40-2 (61.23%) despite having fewer parameters. Raster-scan baselines (PathConv-R) degrade by 3.5–8% compared to the shifted-path variants, confirming the critical role of locality-preserving traversal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResNet-18, PathConvS (3.62M params, ~1/3 of ResNet-18's 11.68M) trails by 0.5–1.2% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, and by 0.7–1.2% on ImageNet-64, while matching FLOPs. Against ResNet-50, PathConvB (7.82M vs 25.55M params) essentially matches or slightly exceeds ResNet-50: +0.02% on CIFAR-10, −0.05% on SVHN, and +0.03%/+0.40% on ImageNet-64 (H/Z variants). Against WRN-40-2 (9.33M params), PathConvS-Z (3.76M) outperforms it on ImageNet-64 by 1.5% (62.73% vs 61.23%) with fewer parameters. Against the much larger WRN-40-5 (57.24M params), PathConvB is 1.5–1.9% lower on ImageNet-64, but WRN-40-5 uses 7× more parameters and 2.4× more FLOPs. The raster-scan ablation (PathConv-R) drops 3.5–8% below the shifted-path variants, confirming that the path-shifting method is the key differentiator enabling competitive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant strategies for incorporating 1D convolution into CNNs—factorized (separable) convolution and oriented kernels—by eliminating 2D operations entirely and processing flattened 1D pixel streams. The architectural macro design borrows from depthwise separable convolution (Chollet, 2017) and inverted bottlenecks (Sandler et al., 2018), while the path-aware channel attention module extends efficient channel attention (Wang et al., 2020) with dual-scale 1D convolutions and cross-path MLPs. The core novelty lies in the path-shifting method, which repositions 'sacrificed' pixels at (sub)quadrant boundaries of Hilbert and Z-order curves, and the proof that three shifted paths suffice to outperform raster scanning in locality preservation, solved via randomized rounding over a set-cover formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that a purely 1D CNN can match ResNet accuracy with one-third the parameters opens a path toward hardware-friendly, pipelined vision backbones with substantially lower end-to-end latency. However, all experiments are conducted on low-resolution datasets (32×32 and 64×64), which the authors acknowledge amplifies locality issues; whether the approach maintains its parameter-efficiency advantage at standard 224×224 or higher resolutions remains an open question. Additionally, the method has only been validated for image classification, leaving its applicability to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) unexplored. The finding that a single unshifted path (P=1) can occasionally outperform the three-path configuration on small datasets suggests the optimal path count may be dataset- and resolution-dependent, warranting further investigation.
