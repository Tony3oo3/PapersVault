## Problem

Tool-augmented LLMs embed tool-using capabilities directly into their parameters, but no mechanism exists to selectively remove specific tools when they become insecure, deprecated, or restricted. Unlike traditional sample-level unlearning, which targets individual data points, tool unlearning must erase functional 'skills' (the ability to invoke specific tools) while preserving all other tools and general LLM capabilities—a task with distinct evaluation challenges and no prior solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tool-augmented LLM checkpoint (e.g., ToolAlpaca-7B, ToolLLaMA-2-7B, Gorilla), the corresponding tool-free instruction-tuned base model f_0, a randomly initialized model f_R, and a partition of tools into forget set T_f and retain set T_r. It produces updated LLM parameters that no longer invoke T_f tools while maintaining T_r tool usage and general capabilities. Notably, the method can operate without access to original training data by substituting GPT-4-generated 'shadow samples' as pseudo-demonstrations, and supports sequential unlearning requests without retraining from scratch.

## Method

TOOLDELETE implements three properties: (1) Tool Knowledge Deletion, which fine-tunes the model so that for forget tools it produces responses matching the tool-free LLM f_0 (which never saw those tools), via SFT (replacing original responses with tool-free responses) or DPO (prioritizing tool-free responses over tool-augmented ones); (2) Tool Knowledge Retention, which re-exposes the model to demonstrations for retained tools T_r to prevent collateral forgetting; and (3) General Capability Retention, which applies task arithmetic by adding α(θ_0 − θ_R) to the optimized weights to restore foundational abilities without expensive retraining. For evaluation, the paper introduces LiRA-Tool, a knowledge-level adaptation of the Likelihood Ratio Attack that uses GPT-4-generated diverse shadow samples to probe whether tool knowledge has been truly removed, rather than relying on limited training-set prompts as in sample-level MIAs.

## Results

On ToolAlpaca (20% tools deleted), TOOLDELETE-DPO achieves 53.4% on the test set (T_T), 75.1% on retained tools (T_r), 28.7% on forget tools (T_f, lower is better), and 23.1% average general capability (T_G). Compared to the strongest baseline RETRAIN, TOOLDELETE-DPO improves by 1.3, 3.3, 9.8, and 1.8 absolute points on T_T, T_r, T_f, and T_G respectively. The method saves 74.8% of training time versus retraining, and the LoRA variant retains 95%+ of full-parameter performance while reducing compute cost by 81.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOOLDELETE-DPO outperforms all 9 baselines on the forget-tools metric (T_f), achieving 28.7% versus the next-best NPO at 30.1% and RETRAIN at 38.5%, indicating the most effective removal of tool knowledge. On retained tools (T_r), TOOLDELETE-DPO (75.1%) surpasses all baselines including ICUL (74.8%), which was the only method to beat TOOLDELETE-SFT (72.1%) on this metric. ICUL's strong T_r performance comes at a severe cost: it fails to unlearn tools (T_f = 58.3%, nearly at the original 75.7%) and catastrophically degrades general capability (T_G = 7.3% vs. 23.1% for TOOLDELETE-DPO). Among general unlearning methods, GRADASCENT achieves moderate T_f (34.6%) but severely damages T_T (33.3%) and T_r (51.4%), while SOUL-GradDiff is the strongest general baseline on T_G (22.7%) yet still trails TOOLDELETE-SFT by 0.9 points. The paper reports that across all three datasets (ToolAlpaca, ToolBench, API-Bench), TOOLDELETE outperforms general and LLM-specific unlearning algorithms by 12.5 and 9.1 points on forget and retain tools respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within machine unlearning by shifting the unit of deletion from individual training samples to entire tool-use capabilities in tool-augmented LLMs. Methodologically, it repurposes task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) for general capability preservation and adapts the Likelihood Ratio Attack (Carlini et al., 2022) into LiRA-Tool for knowledge-level evaluation, while the core unlearning mechanism is a straightforward SFT or DPO fine-tuning that substitutes tool-free responses for tool-dependent ones. Conceptually, it aligns more closely with concept erasure in diffusion models (Gandikota et al., 2023) and zero-shot unlearning (Chundawat et al., 2023) than with the sample-level LLM unlearning literature (ICUL, SGA, TAU, NPO, SOUL), which the paper demonstrates are poorly suited to this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: as tool-augmented LLMs are deployed in regulated domains (healthcare, finance), the inability to remove a specific tool's knowledge from model parameters creates security and compliance risks that prompt-level blocking cannot resolve. Key open questions include scalability beyond 7B models, robustness under adversarial re-learning attacks, and whether the task-arithmetic step generalizes to much larger parameter spaces. The finding that existing LLM-specific unlearning methods fail on tool unlearning (e.g., ICUL preserves retain tools but cannot actually forget the target tools) validates the need for task-specific formulations, though the methods themselves are relatively straightforward adaptations of SFT/DPO.
