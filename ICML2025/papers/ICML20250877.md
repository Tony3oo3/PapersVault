## Problem

Existing efficient fine-tuning methods for VLP models (e.g., DAS) remove redundancy only at the coarse block level, replacing entire transformer blocks while ignoring finer sub-layer redundancies (MHSA, MHCA, FFN individually). Additionally, these methods overlook data-level redundancy: image patches vastly outnumber text tokens, and many patches carry little semantic information, yet prior patch-selection strategies (e.g., EViT) focus on salient objects and discard background context essential for complex multimodal tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed at 224×224 resolution into 196 patches) paired with text queries, two images with a text description, or image/text retrieval queries. Before entering the VLP backbone, each image is first passed through a YOLOv8 detector to obtain bounding boxes that partition the image into foreground and background regions; patches are then scored by CLS-token attention from a pre-trained ViT and filtered separately within each region. The output is task-specific: a 3,129-way answer classification for VQA, a binary yes/no label for NLVR², or ranked image-text similarity scores for retrieval.

## Method

Double-Filter combines two complementary filtering mechanisms. The Image Patch Filter (IPF) uses YOLOv8 to segment an image into foreground (union of detected bounding boxes) and background, then retains a user-specified fraction of patches by selecting the top-scoring patches via CLS attention within each region, with the FG/BG allocation proportional to their spatial area ratio—preserving both salient objects and contextual background. The Fine-grained Architecture Layer Filter (ALF) encodes each transformer block's three sub-layers (MHSA, MHCA, FFN) as binary genes in a chromosome of length 3N, and applies an Adjustable Genetic Algorithm (AGA) with single- or two-point crossover and a constraint-enforcing mutation (flipping genes to maintain exactly L replaced layers) to search for the optimal subset of sub-layers to replace with lightweight adapters. The AGA fitness function is the negative mean loss on randomly sampled training and validation batches, and the best chromosome after G generations defines the final pruned architecture, which is then fine-tuned jointly with IPF for 10 epochs.

## Results

On METER, Double-Filter achieves 74.25% VQA accuracy, 80.12% NLVR accuracy, and 80.05/91.22 R@1 retrieval, while reducing FLOPs by 21.18G (VQA) and 12.51G (NLVR) compared to the full model — more than 2× the reduction of DAS (−11.16G). On ViLT, it reaches 68.37% VQA, 74.50% NLVR, and 61.18/79.39 R@1 with a 4.72G FLOPs reduction. Inference speed improves to 70.72 items/s (METER VQA) and 135.24 items/s (ViLT VQA), the fastest among all compared methods, with average performance degradation of only 0.30% (METER) and 0.55% (ViLT) relative to full tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAS (Wu et al., 2024b), the closest competitor in FLOPs reduction, Double-Filter achieves roughly 2× the computational savings (−21.18G vs. −11.16G on METER VQA) at a small accuracy cost (74.25 vs. 74.80 on VQA; 80.12 vs. 80.11 on NLVR, essentially tied). Compared to Adapter (Sung et al., 2022) and Scaled PA (He et al., 2022), Double-Filter trades 0.4–0.9% accuracy for a dramatic FLOPs reduction (−21.18G vs. +1.64G/+1.12G), making it the only method that simultaneously reduces both parameters and inference compute. LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) adds no FLOPs but underperforms Double-Filter on NLVR (78.82 vs. 80.12) and retrieval (79.86/92.60 vs. 80.05/91.22 on METER). Prompt-based methods (Shallow Prompt, Deep Prompt) are clearly inferior, lagging by 3–10 points in accuracy while also increasing FLOPs. In the patch-filtering ablation (Figure 3), IPF outperforms EViT (Liang et al., 2022) and random filtering, with the gap widening as the filtering ratio increases, confirming that foreground-background-aware selection preserves more contextual semantics than CLS-only ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on VLP model efficiency: (1) parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (adapters, LoRA, prompt tuning) that reduce trainable parameters but leave the inference architecture unchanged, and (2) architecture-level redundancy removal, most recently DAS (Wu et al., 2024b), which skips entire transformer blocks. Double-Filter departs from DAS by operating at a finer granularity—filtering individual sub-layers (MHSA, MHCA, FFN) rather than whole blocks—and from EViT (Liang et al., 2022) by introducing foreground/background separation via YOLO to preserve contextual semantics alongside salient objects. The genetic-algorithm formulation for sub-layer selection is a new mechanism not present in prior VLP pruning or PEFT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous treatment of data-level (patch) and architecture-level (sub-layer) redundancy is a practical advance for practitioners deploying VLP models under compute constraints, yielding ~2× FLOPs reduction over DAS with only 0.3–0.55% accuracy loss. However, the method's reliance on an external YOLO detector and a pre-trained ViT for patch scoring adds a fixed inference overhead and limits flexibility across architectures. The authors acknowledge that the genetic algorithm's search is computationally expensive and prone to local optima, and that the approach has only been validated on two VLP backbones (METER, ViLT) and three tasks, leaving open whether the fine-grained filtering strategy generalizes to newer, larger multimodal models (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo).
