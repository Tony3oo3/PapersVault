## Problem

Self-Explainable GNNs (SE-GNNs) are widely used for ante-hoc interpretability in graph classification, yet the formal properties of the subgraph explanations they produce have not been characterized. This paper shows that SE-GNNs implicitly optimize for Minimal Explanations (MEs), which can be uninformative (indistinguishable across different classifiers) and unfaithful (failing to capture all causes of a prediction), even though they coincide with Prime Implicant explanations for the important special case of motif-based tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph with node feature vectors (and optionally edge features) as input and produces a class label prediction alongside two complementary explanation modalities: an edge-induced subgraph from the SE-GNN channel (selected via per-edge relevance scores thresholded or top-k'd) and a set of inequality rules derived from the weights of a sparse linear model operating on sum-aggregated node features. The (B)LENs aggregation additionally outputs per-channel importance scores indicating which channel(s) drive each prediction, enabling adaptive reliance on topological or non-topological evidence.

## Method

Dual-Channel GNNs (DC-GNNs) pair a standard SE-GNN topological channel (e.g., GSAT or the proposed SMGNN, which combines GSAT's GIN-based explanation extractor with GISST's sparsity loss) with a non-relational rule channel implemented as a sparse linear classifier on sum-aggregated node features. The two channels are jointly trained and combined via (B)LENs, an extension of Logic Explained Networks that applies progressive temperature annealing to input activations (scaling the pre-sigmoid value from τ=1 down to τ=0.3) to avoid leakage while preserving differentiability and channel semantics. The SE-GNN channel extracts a minimal subgraph explanation via per-edge relevance scores, while the linear channel's weights are interpretable as inequality rules (e.g., 'number of red nodes ≥ 2'). The (B)LENs aggregation adaptively learns to rely on one or both channels per task, and the paper proves that for positive existential (motif-based) classifiers, the SE-GNN's Minimal Explanations coincide with Prime Implicant explanations, justifying the topological channel's role while the rule channel handles non-topological patterns.

## Results

On real-world benchmarks, DC-GSAT and DC-SMGNN match or surpass their SE-GNN counterparts: on AIDS, DC-GSAT achieves 99±03 F1 vs. GSAT's 97±02, and DC-SMGNN reaches 99±01 vs. SMGNN's 97±02. On Graph-SST2, both DC variants achieve 87% accuracy vs. 86% for the plain SE-GNNs. On synthetic datasets, DC-GNNs achieve perfect 100% accuracy on RedBlueNodes and TopoFeature (vs. 99% and 95–99% for baselines), while matching 93% on GOODMotif. For OOD generalization, DC-SMGNN achieves 99–100% on RedBlueNodes OOD splits and 93–98% on TopoFeature OOD splits, substantially outperforming SMGNN (85–97% and 55–75% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GIN (Xu et al., 2018), DC-GNNs match or slightly exceed accuracy on most datasets (e.g., DC-GSAT 94% vs. GIN 92% on MNIST75sp; DC-GSAT 87% vs. GIN 88% on Graph-SST2), confirming that the dual-channel architecture does not sacrifice performance. Against GISST (Lin et al., 2020), DC-GISST improves F1 on AIDS from 97 to 99 and accuracy on Graph-SST2 from 85 to 87, while matching on MUTAG and MNIST75sp. Against GSAT (Miao et al., 2022a), DC-GSAT consistently matches or outperforms: AIDS F1 improves from 97 to 99, Graph-SST2 accuracy from 86 to 87, while BBBP AUC is comparable (65 vs. 66). Against DIR (Wu et al., 2022), DC-DIR matches performance on all five real-world datasets (e.g., 96 F1 on AIDS, 83% on Graph-SST2), demonstrating the framework's generality beyond Sparsity/IB-based SE-GNNs. On synthetic datasets, DC-GNNs achieve perfect 100% accuracy on RedBlueNodes and TopoFeature, outperforming GSAT (99%) and SMGNN (95–99%), while matching on GOODMotif (93%). The key advantage is not raw accuracy but the ability to adaptively select the appropriate channel, yielding more focused and interpretable explanations without performance loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SE-GNN literature (GISST, GSAT, LRI, GMT) by formally characterizing what their training objectives actually optimize for—Minimal Explanations—and connecting them to established formal explainability concepts (Prime Implicant and faithful explanations) previously studied primarily for tabular and image data. Architecturally, it extends Logic Explained Networks (LENs) with progressive temperature annealing to create (B)LENs as an interpretable aggregation mechanism, and repurposes the GSAT explanation extractor under GISST's sparsity loss to form SMGNN. The key departure from prior SE-GNN work is the demonstration that subgraph explanations alone are insufficient for non-motif tasks, motivating the Dual-Channel GNN framework that pairs a topological SE-GNN channel with a non-relational white-box rule extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results—that SE-GNN explanations can be completely uninformative (identical MEs for distinct classifiers) and achieve zero faithfulness even on trivial tasks—serve as an important warning against blindly trusting ante-hoc explanations from popular SE-GNNs. The DC-GNN framework offers a practical path forward, but its current instantiation relies on a simple linear model, and the theoretical analysis is limited to Sparsity- and IB-based SE-GNNs. Open questions include whether more expressive interpretable channels (e.g., logic distillation, decision trees) can further improve DC-GNNs, whether the formal characterization extends to other SE-GNN families (prototype-based, invariant-rationale), and whether the mixed OOD results indicate a fundamental limitation of the adaptive channel-selection mechanism.
