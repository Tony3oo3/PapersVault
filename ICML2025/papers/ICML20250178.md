## Problem

Discrete diffusion and flow matching models for graph generation suffer from compounding denoising errors: the Markovian dependency between intermediate noisy states causes early denoising mistakes to accumulate and propagate, degrading the structural validity of generated graphs. While this issue was previously documented for mask diffusion, the paper demonstrates it also affects marginal and uniform noise distributions, explaining why these models struggle to consistently produce valid molecules or planar graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method starts from a noise sample drawn from a chosen distribution (mask or marginal) over all node and edge attributes of a graph, and iteratively denoises it over T steps to produce a complete graph. The denoiser is a GPS-style GNN that predicts the full clean graph from the noisy state, while the Critic (in the CID variant) is a separate post-hoc trained GNN that outputs a scalar corruption probability per element. Because the training objective matches standard discrete diffusion, the SID sampling procedure is training-free when applied to any pre-trained DDM or DFM denoiser; only the Critic requires additional training.

## Method

Simple Iterative Denoising (SID) replaces the Markovian noising process with a conditional independence assumption: each intermediate noisy state depends only on the clean data and the noise level α_t, not on previous noisy states. This collapses the denoising step into a two-stage operation—predict the clean instance from the noisy state, then re-noise that prediction to the target noise level—which the paper proves equivalent to corrector sampling with maximal corrector steps and which acts as a barrier to error propagation. Critical Iterative Denoising (CID) extends SID with a Critic GNN trained post-hoc to estimate per-element corruption probabilities by comparing the predicted clean sample against the data distribution (formalized via a GAN-like optimal critic theorem). The Critic adaptively increases re-noising for elements overrepresented under the data distribution and decreases it for underrepresented ones, steering generation toward the data manifold and reducing the number of denoising steps needed to reach high validity.

## Results

On ZINC250k, Mask CID achieves 99.97% molecule validity (without correction), 2.26 NSPDK, and 3.46 FCD, while Marginal SID reaches 99.50% validity, 2.06 NSPDK, and 2.01 FCD. On QM9, Mask CID attains 99.92% validity, 1.40 NSPDK, and 1.76 FCD. The method dramatically outperforms its own Discrete Diffusion baselines: Mask DDM drops to 8.96% validity on ZINC250k and 48.38% on QM9, while Marginal DDM reaches only 80.40% and 95.73% respectively. The Critic further reduces the NFE needed for high validity, with Mask CID exceeding 99% validity in just 32 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest external baseline DruM (Jo et al., 2024), Mask CID wins decisively on ZINC250k validity (99.97% vs. 98.65%) and Marginal SID wins on FCD (2.01 vs. 2.25), but DruM retains an edge on QM9 NPK (0.2 vs. 1.04) and FCD (0.11 vs. 0.50). DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) is outperformed on ZINC250k validity by both Marginal SID (99.50% vs. 94.99%) and Mask CID (99.97% vs. 94.99%), and on FCD by Marginal SID (2.01 vs. 3.48). GDSS (Jo et al., 2022) is the weakest baseline, trailing all proposed methods on every metric across both datasets. The most striking comparison is against the paper's own Discrete Diffusion baselines: Mask DDM collapses to 8.96% validity on ZINC250k while Mask CID reaches 99.97%, and Marginal DDM plateaus at 80.40% validity versus 99.50% for Marginal SID, confirming the compounding denoising error diagnosis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the discrete diffusion and discrete flow matching frameworks (Austin et al., 2021; Campbell et al., 2022; Gat et al., 2024; Vignac et al., 2023), showing that standard DFM with independent coupling is equivalent to continuous-time discrete diffusion. Its key departure is replacing the Markovian noising assumption with conditional independence of intermediate states given the clean data, which the authors prove is formally equivalent to maximal corrector sampling (Campbell et al., 2024; Lezama et al., 2023) but without the inefficiency of random re-noising. The Critic mechanism extends this by introducing element-wise adaptive re-noising probabilities, positioned as a probabilistic planner that contrasts with confidence-based unmasking strategies (Zhao et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2025) by operating on fully denoised predictions rather than selecting which elements to unmask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free—any pre-trained discrete diffusion denoiser can be immediately upgraded to SID without retraining—making it a drop-in improvement for the entire class of discrete denoising models. The dramatic validity gains (e.g., 9% → 99.97% on ZINC250k for mask-based models) and the Critic's ability to reach near-perfect validity in 32 NFE (vs. 500+ for baselines) have direct practical implications for molecular and graph generation pipelines. Open questions include scalability to large graphs (the current GNN architecture uses dense pairwise computations), whether the Critic's benefits generalize beyond molecular validity to other structural constraints, and applicability to other discrete modalities such as language or protein sequences.
