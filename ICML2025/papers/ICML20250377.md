## Problem

The canonical single-timescale actor-critic algorithm, where actor and critic are updated simultaneously with proportional step sizes, lacks a finite-time convergence analysis in continuous state-action spaces with Markovian sampling. Prior theoretical results either restrict to finite spaces, assume impractical i.i.d. sampling from unknown stationary or visitation distributions, or rely on simplified variants (double-loop, two-timescale) that decouple the actor and critic artificially. This paper closes the gap by establishing convergence for the most practical and widely deployed setting: continuous spaces, Markovian sampling, and the discounted reward criterion.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes continuous state observations and generates transition tuples (s, a, s') via two distinct Markovian sampling schemes: the critic samples from the original transition kernel P, while the actor samples from a modified kernel P̂ = γP + (1−γ)η that resets to the initial distribution η with probability 1−γ. The value function is approximated linearly via a bounded feature mapping φ(s) with ‖φ(·)‖ ≤ 1. The output is a finite-time convergence guarantee showing both the critic error E‖ω_t − ω*_t‖² and the actor gradient norm E‖∇J(θ_t)‖² converge at rate Õ(log²T / √T) + O(ε_app), yielding Õ(ε⁻²) sample complexity for an ε-approximate stationary point.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel Lyapunov analysis framework that handles the strong coupling between actor and critic in the single-timescale setting. The analysis proceeds in three steps: (1) an implicit upper bound on the critic error that retains a cross-term coupling the critic error to the actor gradient, (2) a symmetric implicit bound on the actor error coupling it back to the critic error, and (3) a carefully weighted Lyapunov function L_t = (2B/(1−γ))E‖Δ_t‖² + ((1−γ)/(2B))E‖∇J(θ_t)‖² whose telescoping sum yields simultaneous convergence of both errors. A key technical ingredient is proving that the actor's Markovian sampling under P̂ converges geometrically (at rate γ) to the discounted state visitation distribution, enabling accurate policy gradient estimation without i.i.d. sampling. Markovian noise from the time-inhomogeneous chains is controlled via auxiliary fixed-policy chains and a mixing-time argument, introducing the log²T factor. The step-size ratio c = α/β must satisfy c < λ/(4BL_c) to ensure the Lyapunov function contracts.

## Results

The paper establishes finite-time convergence of the single-sample, single-timescale actor-critic algorithm in continuous state-action spaces with Markovian sampling. Theorem 5.1 shows that both the critic error (‖Δ_t‖²) and the actor error (‖∇J(θ_t)‖²) converge at a rate of Õ(log²T / √T) + O(ε_app), where ε_app is the linear function approximation error. This yields a sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-2}) to obtain an ε-approximate stationary point, matching the best-known rates for single-timescale actor-critic under i.i.d. sampling but now under the more practical Markovian sampling assumption in continuous spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's theoretical contribution is compared against two prior single-timescale actor-critic analyses: Chen et al. (2021) and Olshevsky & Gharesifard (2023). Both prior works achieve the same O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity but under significantly stronger assumptions: they require i.i.d. sampling from the stationary distribution (for the critic) and from the discounted state visitation distribution (for the actor), both of which are infeasible in practice since these distributions are unknown during online learning. This paper closes both gaps simultaneously by (1) extending to continuous (infinite) state and action spaces, and (2) replacing i.i.d. sampling with Markovian sampling from the actual MDP dynamics. The cost is an additional log²T factor in the convergence rate (hence Õ(ε^{-2}) vs. O(ε^{-2})), which arises from the mixing time of the Markov chain. The paper also differs from Chen & Zhao (2024), which handles the simpler undiscounted time-average reward setting where the policy gradient depends only on the stationary distribution, whereas this paper addresses the more challenging discounted reward setting where the visitation distribution must be tracked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-timescale actor-critic convergence analysis line initiated by Chen et al. (2021) and Olshevsky & Gharesifard (2023), which were limited to finite action spaces with i.i.d. sampling from known stationary/visitation distributions, and Chen & Zhao (2024), which addressed the simpler undiscounted time-average reward setting. The key departure is handling continuous state-action spaces with Markovian sampling under the discounted reward criterion, where the policy gradient depends on the (generally unknown) discounted state visitation distribution rather than the stationary distribution. The novel Lyapunov analysis framework unifies the coupled actor-critic dynamics that prior decoupled analyses (double-loop, two-timescale) could not capture, while an operator-based approach generalizes key bounds from the tabular to the continuous setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes two significant gaps simultaneously: continuous state-action spaces and practical Markovian sampling, making the theoretical guarantees applicable to real-world continuous control tasks (robotics, drone racing) where i.i.d. sampling from unknown distributions is infeasible. The Õ(ε^{-2}) sample complexity matches prior results, confirming that the Markovian sampling overhead introduces only logarithmic factors. Open questions include whether the uniform ergodicity assumption (Assumption 4.2) can be relaxed, whether the analysis extends to nonlinear function approximation (neural networks), and whether the step-size ratio constraint c < λ/(4BL_c) is tight or merely an artifact of the Lyapunov construction.
