## Problem

3D full attention in video Diffusion Transformers dominates inference cost (800 of 945 seconds for a 5-second 720P video), yet existing sliding window attention implementations (NATTEN, CLEAR) fail to convert their FLOP reductions into wall-clock speedups because token-level sliding creates irregular 'mixed' attention blocks that waste GPU computation and incur heavy mask-evaluation overhead. The paper identifies that video DiTs exhibit strong 3D locality and prompt-agnostic head specialization, but no prior method exploits this structure in a hardware-efficient manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For video generation, STA is applied to pretrained DiTs (HunyuanVideo, Wan 2.1) that consume a text prompt and produce a 5-second 720P video (117 frames at 1280×768, corresponding to a VAE latent of shape 30×48×80 with ~115K tokens). For image super-resolution, it accelerates FLUX with SDEdit, taking a low-resolution image plus text prompt and outputting a 2K or 4K image. The method is plug-and-play: in training-free mode it requires only a one-time per-head window-size profiling pass over 16 prompts, while the fine-tuning variant needs just 8 hours on 8 H100s with 2,000 synthetic videos.

## Method

Sliding Tile Attention (STA) replaces token-level sliding with tile-level sliding: the video is partitioned into non-overlapping spatial-temporal cubes (tiles) whose volume equals the FlashAttention block size (B = T³), and all queries within a tile attend to the same set of key tiles within a shared local window, producing exclusively dense or empty attention blocks with zero mixed blocks. The optimized kernel adopts FA3's consumer-producer paradigm, where data warpgroups asynchronously load only the relevant KV tiles from HBM to SRAM and manage the inter-block sparsity mask, while compute warpgroups perform dense attention oblivious to the sparse pattern, achieving 58.79% MFU. A key empirical finding is head specialization—different attention heads require different window sizes but this pattern is prompt-agnostic—enabling a simple per-head window search (Algorithm 1) that balances sparsity and quality without retraining. For the fine-tuning variant, the model is adapted to a fixed high-sparsity window using a combined loss of attention distillation (matching intermediate layer outputs to the dense teacher), final-output alignment, and flow-matching data loss.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (720P, 5s, 115K tokens), STA with ThunderKittens achieves 58.79% MFU and 94.09% kernel efficiency, delivering a 10.45× attention speedup over FA3 at 91% sparsity. End-to-end, training-free STA reduces latency from 945s (FA3) to 501s (1.89× speedup) with 82.46% VBench (vs. 82.71% for full attention), while finetuned STA further reduces latency to 268s (3.53× speedup) with 82.62% VBench. In human evaluation on 200 MovieGen Bench prompts, STA-tf-1.89x achieves an 83.0% tie rate against the original HunyuanVideo, and STA-t-2.43x decisively outperforms Δ-DiT 1.8× with a 70.0% win rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STA dominates all sliding-window baselines on the efficiency axis: CLEAR (0.37×) and Tiled NATTEN (0.51×) paradoxically *increase* end-to-end latency relative to full FA3 attention due to mixed-block overhead, while STA achieves 1.79×–3.53× speedup at comparable or higher sparsity. On quality, STA (82.46% VBench training-free, 83.00% finetuned) matches or exceeds full attention (82.71%), whereas Swin degrades to 79.00%–77.53% training-free and further drops to 75.48% after finetuning, confirming that non-overlapping window partitioning violates 3D locality. Against the caching-based Δ-DiT, STA wins on both quality and speed: at 50 steps, STA achieves SSIM 87.67 vs. 72.86 and PSNR 28.76 vs. 18.09 while delivering 1.89× speedup vs. Δ-DiT's 1.36×. In human evaluation, STA-t-2.43× beats Δ-DiT 1.8× with a 70.0% win rate (vs. 11.0%), and STA-tf-1.89× ties the original HunyuanVideo 83.0% of the time. At the kernel level, STA's ThunderKittens implementation reaches 58.79% MFU (94.09% kernel efficiency), compared to Tiled NATTEN's 8.20% MFU (13.12%) and CLEAR's 5.15% MFU (8.24%), making STA the first 3D sliding-window attention to achieve wall-clock speedups proportional to sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

STA extends the FlashAttention tiling paradigm (Dao, 2024; Shah et al., 2024) from 1D token-level sliding to 3D tile-level sliding, directly modifying NATTEN's (Hassani et al., 2023) token-wise sliding window into a tile-wise mechanism. The key conceptual departure is a system-algorithm co-design insight: by aligning the sliding unit with the FlashAttention block size, STA eliminates the 'mixed blocks' that plague all prior 2D/3D SWA kernels, converting what was an irregular, mask-heavy computation into purely dense and empty blocks. The paper also positions itself against decomposed spatial-temporal attention (Singer et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023) and caching-based acceleration (Δ-DiT, Chen et al., 2024), arguing that full 3D locality is essential and that STA is complementary to step-reduction techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Attention accounts for ~85% of inference time in state-of-the-art video DiTs, making this bottleneck the primary barrier to practical deployment; STA's plug-and-play, training-free mode (1.89× speedup) and fine-tuned mode (3.53× speedup) directly address this with minimal quality loss. A key open question is whether STA composes effectively with other acceleration axes (caching, consistency distillation, quantized attention) that the authors note are orthogonal but leave unexplored. Limitations include the requirement that video and window dimensions be integer multiples of the tile size, evaluation on a single primary model (HunyuanVideo) plus one secondary (Wan 2.1), and the fine-tuning still requiring 8 H100s for 8 hours.
