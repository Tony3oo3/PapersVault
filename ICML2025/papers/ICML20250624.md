## Problem

Geometric Horizon Models (GHMs) learn generative models of the successor measure via temporal difference bootstrapping, but existing methods suffer from instability and rapidly growing inaccuracy beyond 20–50 step horizons. While flow matching and diffusion models are powerful generative tools, they cannot be directly applied to the off-policy TD setting because they require direct access to samples from the target distribution. The paper asks how to exploit the Bellman equation's structure within these iterative generative frameworks to achieve stable, high-fidelity long-horizon predictions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a state-action pair (s, a) and a policy π (or a Forward-Backward policy embedding w in the multi-policy setting) as conditioning inputs, and is trained on offline one-step transitions (S, A, S′) from the ExoRL dataset. It outputs samples from the successor measure m^π(·|s, a) by integrating a neural ODE (flow matching) or reverse SDE (diffusion) from a Gaussian prior, which are then used to compute value estimates Q^π(s, a) = (1−γ)⁻¹ E_{X∼m^π}[r(X)] or to select actions via Generalized Policy Improvement over 256 candidate policy embeddings. The effective horizon (1−γ)⁻¹ is a free parameter at inference, allowing the same trained model to make predictions at arbitrary horizons up to 100 steps.

## Method

The core contribution is TD²-CFM (and its diffusion analogue TD²-DD), which exploits the linearity of the Bellman operator to decompose the flow-matching loss into two independent terms: a standard conditional flow matching term for the one-step transition (weighted 1−γ) and a bootstrapped term where the regression target is the *previous iteration's* vector field v_t^(n) evaluated at the next state (weighted γ). This 'double temporal difference' design means the bootstrapped sample X_t is generated by the same vector field that serves as the regression target, eliminating an independent source of variance. Theoretically, all TD-Flow variants (TD-CFM, TD-CFM(C), TD²-CFM) implement the same Bellman-like operator B_t^π that is a γ-contraction in 1-Wasserstein distance, guaranteeing convergence to the Monte-Carlo probability path. Variance analysis (Theorems 2–3) shows TD²-CFM's gradient variance is strictly lower than TD-CFM and TD-CFM(C) by a term scaled by γ², explaining its superior stability at long horizons. Empirically, TD²-CFM achieves ~10× lower value-function MSE and ~4 orders of magnitude improvement at horizon 100 over naive TD-CFM, and enables ~30%+ planning gains via GPI over Forward-Backward baselines.

## Results

Across 19 tasks in 4 DeepMind Control Suite domains (Cheetah, Pointmass, Quadruped, Walker), TD²-CFM achieves the best or near-best performance on all three metrics (EMD, normalized NLL, MSE of value function). Averaged across domains, TD²-CFM reduces value-function MSE by ~10×, EMD by ~1.5×, and log-likelihood by ~3× compared to the non-coupled TD-CFM baseline. At an effective horizon of 100 (γ=0.99), TD²-based methods maintain accuracy while naive TD-CFM degrades by nearly four orders of magnitude in MSE(V). In GPI planning, TD²-CFM and TD-CFM(C) yield ~30%+ improvement over the zero-shot Forward-Backward policy, whereas FB-GPI actually degrades performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TD-Flow methods against three baselines: a GAN-based GHM (Janner et al., 2020), a β-VAE-based GHM (Thakoor et al., 2022), and Forward-Backward (FB) representation for planning (Touati & Ollivier, 2021). TD²-CFM decisively outperforms both GAN and VAE across all domains and metrics — for example, on Pointmass, TD²-CFM achieves MSE(V) of 8.74 versus 1257.26 (GAN) and 1821.89 (VAE), a >100× improvement. Among the TD-Flow variants, TD²-CFM consistently beats TD-CFM (the non-coupled version) by ~10× in MSE(V) and ~3× in NLL on average, with the gap widening dramatically at longer horizons (e.g., 40× MSE(V) reduction on Pointmass at γ=0.99). TD-CFM(C) with straight conditional paths performs comparably to TD²-CFM, confirming the theoretical variance equivalence, but degrades significantly with curved paths while TD²-CFM maintains or improves. For GPI planning, all TD-based GHMs yield ~30%+ improvement over the zero-shot FB policy, whereas FB-GPI (planning directly in FB embedding space) actually degrades performance on average across three of four domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Geometric Horizon Model (GHM) / γ-model framework (Janner et al., 2020; Thakoor et al., 2022) by replacing GAN, VAE, and normalizing-flow backbones with flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and denoising diffusion (Ho et al., 2020), while critically exploiting the linearity of the Bellman operator in both the sampling procedure and the regression objective (TD²). Prior TD-based GHM methods bootstrapped only in the sampling step; here, the previous iterate's vector field (or score) is also used as the regression target for the bootstrapped term, which the authors prove reduces gradient variance by a γ²-discounted 'computational variance' term. The work also subsumes and rigorously extends the preliminary TD²-DD derivation of Schramm & Boularias (2024) into a unified flow-matching framework with convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-reduction insight provides a principled explanation for why prior GHM methods degrade at long horizons and a general recipe (use the previous iterate's generative model as both sampler and target) that could transfer to other bootstrapped generative settings. Empirically, the 5× horizon improvement and ~30% GPI planning gains over Forward-Backward representations open a path toward reliable long-horizon decision-making with behavior foundation models. Key limitations include the iterative sampling cost of neural ODEs / reverse SDEs (mitigated but not eliminated by the reduced transport cost), and the fact that the horizon-scaling experiment is concentrated on a single Maze task. Open directions flagged by the authors include consistency models and self-distillation to reduce sampling steps, and extension to higher-dimensional observations.
