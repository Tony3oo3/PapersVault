## Problem

Diffusion Policy learns closed-loop manipulation from human demonstrations but generalizes poorly to novel 3D object arrangements, requiring expensive data collection to exhaust scene configurations. Existing equivariant diffusion policies are limited to SO(2) equivariance, single-object transformations requiring task-specific segmentation, or computationally heavy SO(3) irreducible representations with multi-stage inference. This paper seeks a lightweight, end-to-end SE(3)-equivariant diffusion policy that generalizes across multi-object 3D scenes without discretization error or per-task engineering.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a history of h states, each comprising a colored point cloud (1024 points, with RGB treated as degree-0 spherical coefficients) and the end-effector's 6DoF pose plus gripper aperture. For translation invariance, both observations and actions are canonicalized into the current gripper frame (relative action formulation), so the network never sees absolute workspace coordinates. The output is a trajectory of r=8 actions, each a 6DoF gripper pose and aperture (12 DoF for bi-manual), produced by K iterative denoising steps from Gaussian noise. In simulation, observations come from MimicGen's RGBD cameras; in physical experiments, from two overhead RealSense D415 cameras.

## Method

SDP embeds states, actions, and the denoising process in spherical Fourier space, representing each end-effector datum as a degree-1 vector (position), three degree-1 vectors (rotation matrix columns), and a scalar (aperture), i.e., ρ₁⁴ ⊕ ρ₀. A 5-layer EquiformerV2 encoder maps the point cloud to a multichannel spherical scene feature C, which conditions the denoiser via novel Spherical FiLM (SFiLM) layers that modulate each degree-l channel separately using 2l+1-dimensional scaling and offset vectors. The denoiser itself is a Spherical Denoising Temporal U-net (SDTU) with 1D temporal convolutions along the action horizon and mixing-channel convolutions over spherical Fourier coefficients, both proven SO(3)-equivariant via Schur's lemma. Translation invariance is achieved by the relative action formulation (canonicalizing to the gripper frame), while rotation equivariance is enforced structurally through Wigner D-matrix rotations of Fourier coefficients. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective (100 training steps, 8 inference steps via DDIM), requiring no segmentation or task-specific preprocessing.

## Results

On 4 MimicGen tasks with SE(3) initialization (random table tilt and object placement), SDP achieves average success rates of 63% (0°), 49% (±15°), and 35% (±30°), substantially outperforming EquiDiff (45/20/13%), DiffPo (18/9/7%), and EquiBot (2/2/1%). Across 12 standard MimicGen tasks with SE(2) initialization, SDP reaches 76% average success rate versus 64% for the best EquiDiff variant and 42% for DiffPo-C. On 5 physical robot tasks (single-arm and bi-manual), SDP achieves 78% average success rate, 61% higher than EquiDiff (17%) and 71% higher than DiffPo-C (7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDP outperforms all baselines on the SE(3) initialization tasks, with the gap widening as tilt increases: at ±30°, SDP (35% avg) is 2.7× EquiDiff (13%), 5× DiffPo (7%), and 35× EquiBot (1%). On the 12 standard MimicGen tasks, SDP (76%) beats the best EquiDiff variant (64%, Voxel/Abs), DiffPo-C (42%), DP3 (24%), ACT (21%), and BC-RNN (23%), winning on 10 of 12 tasks. On physical experiments, SDP (78%) dramatically outperforms EquiDiff (17%) and DiffPo-C (7%), with the largest gaps on bi-manual tasks where EquiDiff and DiffPo-C score 0%. EquiBot (Yang et al., 2024a) performs near-randomly on SE(3) tasks due to its degree-l=1 limitation and absolute action formulation. ET-SEED (Tie et al., 2024) was not included in success-rate comparisons due to unavailable code, but SDP is 66× faster in inference (0.44s vs 29.4s) and supports 32× larger batch sizes. SDP's advantage is most pronounced under increased 3D variation, confirming that continuous SE(3) equivariance provides stronger generalization than discrete C8⊂SO(2) equivariance (EquiDiff) or no equivariance (DiffPo).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) by embedding the entire denoising pipeline in spherical Fourier space to enforce continuous SE(3) equivariance, building on the theoretical result from Ryu et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2024b) that equivariant policies require equivariant denoising functions. It departs from prior equivariant diffusion policies in three key ways: replacing the discrete C8 ⊂ SO(2) equivariance of EquiDiff with continuous SO(3) via spherical harmonics, replacing the degree-l=1 Vector Neurons of EquiBot with higher-degree spherical Fourier coefficients for richer representations, and replacing the computationally heavy SO(3) irreps and two-stage inference of ET-SEED with a lightweight spherical temporal U-net. The point cloud encoder leverages EquiformerV2 (Liao et al., 2024), and the conditioning mechanism extends FiLM (Perez et al., 2018) into the spherical domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDP is the first end-to-end SE(3)-equivariant closed-loop diffusion policy that handles multi-object scenes without task-specific segmentation, achieving 10× data efficiency over the strongest equivariant baseline and 66× faster inference than ET-SEED while maintaining continuous (not discretized) equivariance. This makes equivariant manipulation policies practically deployable on real robots at near-real-time speeds. Key limitations include position-only control (no force awareness, causing protective stops in contact-rich tasks like Flip_Book) and sensitivity to point cloud resolution for fine-grained manipulation. Open directions include equivariant force-aware policies and more efficient point cloud encoders that capture fine geometric details.
