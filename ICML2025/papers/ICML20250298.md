## Problem

Existing Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT) methods treat catastrophic forgetting as a monolithic knowledge-loss problem, overlooking that forgetting in MLLMs decomposes into two distinct phenomena: superficial forgetting (response format/style deviation caused by single-format training bias) and essential forgetting (genuine knowledge loss). Because severe superficial forgetting obscures the model's true knowledge state, it must be addressed before essential forgetting can be reliably assessed or mitigated, yet no prior method explicitly separates or jointly tackles these two types.

## Input / Output

The method operates on image-text instruction pairs (RGB images paired with natural-language instructions) and produces task-specific outputs ranging from single letters (multiple choice), yes/no tokens, short phrases, multi-word explanations, to bounding box coordinates for grounding tasks. A key design choice is the Answer Style Diversification (ASD) paradigm, which reformats each task's training data into five standardized question types (yes/no, multiple-choice, short answer, brief explanation, detailed explanation) with corresponding response format prompts, so the model learns to produce all five output styles for the same underlying knowledge rather than locking into one format per task.

## Method

SEFE combines two complementary components. First, the ASD paradigm converts X% (default 20%) of each task's samples into four alternative question formats using MLLM-based rewriting and fixed rules, exposing the model to diverse answer styles during training to eliminate superficial forgetting. Second, RegLoRA extends LoRA by, after each task's LoRA is merged into the base model, identifying the top M% (default 2%) elements of the weight update matrix ΔW = B×A by absolute value and constructing a binary regularization mask at those positions; during all subsequent LoRA training, a Hadamard-product regularization loss penalizes the new LoRA's ΔW at every previously masked position, driving those elements toward zero while leaving the remaining ~98% of the update matrix unconstrained. This targeted constraint on dot products of A and B vectors (rather than on individual parameters) preserves prior knowledge with minimal restriction on new-task learning capacity.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 multimodal tasks, evaluated under Truth Alignment), SEFE achieves a Mean Final Accuracy (MFN) of 58.57%, Mean Average Accuracy (MAA) of 63.04%, and Backward Transfer (BWT) of −10.45%, outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, O-LoRA+ASD, reaches MFN 52.57%, MAA 61.63%, and BWT −13.02%. SEFE also attains the best per-task accuracy on ImageNet (83.10%), TextVQA (58.66%), and Grounding (16.75%), while remaining competitive on the remaining tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEFE outperforms all three PEFT baselines (LoRA, O-LoRA, LoTA) on every aggregate metric. Against LoRA, SEFE improves MFN by +16.98% (58.57 vs 41.59) and BWT by +18.17 points (−10.45 vs −28.62). Against O-LoRA, the strongest baseline without ASD, SEFE gains +6.70% in MFN and +13.48% in MAA. Against LoTA, SEFE leads by +20.99% in MFN and +12.58% in MAA. Crucially, even when the ASD paradigm is applied to all baselines (making them train on CoIN-ASD), SEFE still achieves the best MFN (58.57 vs 52.85 for FFT+ASD and 52.57 for O-LoRA+ASD), best MAA (63.04 vs 61.63 for O-LoRA+ASD), and best BWT (−10.45 vs −13.02 for O-LoRA+ASD), confirming that RegLoRA's regularization provides additional gains beyond superficial-forgetting mitigation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) into RegLoRA by adding a regularization loss on the top-M% elements of the weight update matrix ΔW, rather than constraining the A and B parameters directly. Conceptually, it departs from the standard continual learning taxonomy (regularization, replay, parameter isolation) by introducing a data-reconstruction paradigm (ASD) that rephrases training data into multiple answer formats, and by decomposing catastrophic forgetting in MCIT into superficial (style drift) and essential (knowledge loss) components. The method builds on the CoIN benchmark (Chen et al., 2024a) and positions itself against prior MCIT methods like O-LoRA, LoTA, EProj, and Model Tailor, which treat forgetting as a monolithic problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The superficial/essential forgetting decomposition provides a useful diagnostic lens for MCIT research, and the CoIN-ASD benchmark removes the style-bias confound that has obscured knowledge retention measurements in prior evaluations. The practical finding that reformatting only 10% of training data into alternative question types substantially mitigates superficial forgetting is a low-cost intervention likely to be adopted broadly. Limitations include evaluation on a single base model (LLaVA-1.5/Vicuna-7B), a fixed 8-task sequence, and dependence on a large MLLM (InternVL2-26B) for data transformation; whether the five-question-type taxonomy generalizes to all multimodal application domains remains open.
