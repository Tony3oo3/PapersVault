## Problem

Deep graph networks relying on synchronous message passing suffer from three interrelated pathologies—oversmoothing, oversquashing, and underreaching—that collectively prevent them from capturing long-range dependencies essential in scientific domains. Existing mitigations address these issues in isolation (e.g., rewiring for oversquashing, skip connections for oversmoothing) or require expensive manual depth selection, leaving no unified mechanism that simultaneously learns how deep to go and which messages to propagate.

## Input / Output

AMP consumes attributed directed graphs with d-dimensional node features and d'-dimensional edge attributes, and produces either per-node predictions or whole-graph predictions. Unusually, the final output is a weighted sum of readout heads from every layer (weighted by the learned depth distribution q(ℓ;λ)), rather than a single final-layer prediction. The message filters F_i ∈ [0,1]^{|V|×L×d} are computed by lightweight MLPs with sigmoidal activations, either from raw node features or from intermediate embeddings, making the filtering fully differentiable and task-adaptive.

## Method

AMP extends the variational inference framework of Nazaret & Blei (2022) for unbounded-depth networks to graphs by introducing a soft message filtering mechanism: at each layer, outgoing messages are element-wise multiplied by learned filters in [0,1], pruning irrelevant information without discrete operations. The network depth is modeled as a random variable with a learnable distribution (Poisson, discrete folded normal, or mixtures thereof) whose support is truncated at the 0.99-quantile, allowing the architecture to grow or shrink dynamically during training. Each layer carries its own readout head, so the prediction is a weighted mixture over all layers, and the ELBO—comprising a predictive term plus regularizers over parameters and depth—is optimized via standard backpropagation. Theoretically, the paper proves that filtering decreases the upper bound on Jacobian sensitivity (mitigating oversquashing) and that AMP can propagate a message unchanged between any two connected nodes, a capability impossible in classical synchronous message passing.

## Results

On the synthetic long-range datasets (Diameter, SSSP, Eccentricity), AMP achieves average relative error reductions of 63%, 72%, and 32% over its base architectures, with AMP_ADGN reaching the best SSSP score of −3.9579 log₁₀(MSE) and AMP_GIN the best Diameter score of −0.4874. On the chemical benchmarks, AMP_GCN attains 0.7161 AP on peptides-func and 0.2446 MAE on peptides-struct, placing it at or near state-of-the-art levels (IPR-MPNN: 0.7210 AP / 0.2422 MAE). All AMP variants consistently outperform their unfiltered base models across all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMP is combined with four base architectures (GCN, GIN, GatedGCN, ADGN) and consistently outperforms each on all five datasets. On the synthetic benchmarks, AMP_ADGN achieves the best SSSP score (−3.9579) compared to ADGN (−3.5448), and AMP_GIN dramatically improves Diameter from 0.2408 to −0.4874 (−81% relative error). On peptides-func, AMP_GCN (0.7161 AP) surpasses the re-evaluated GCN (0.6860), DRew_GCN (0.7150), and CO-GNN (0.6990), approaching IPR-MPNN (0.7210) and NBA_GCN (0.7207). On peptides-struct, AMP_GCN (0.2446 MAE) beats GCN (0.2460), DRew_GCN (0.2536), LASER (0.3043), and GRED (0.2584), and is competitive with IPR-MPNN (0.2422) and Graph ViT (0.2449). The paper also compares against GAT, GraphSAGE, GCNII, DGC, and GRAND on the synthetic tasks, where AMP variants dominate across the board, with the largest gains observed over GIN (which the prior work found best at only 1 layer, highlighting the benefit of learned adaptive depth).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational inference framework for unbounded-depth networks (Nazaret & Blei, 2022) from MLPs/CNNs to graph message passing, introducing a novel soft message filtering mechanism within the same ELBO. It also builds on the Jacobian sensitivity bounds of Di Giovanni et al. (2023) by proving an analogous bound that incorporates the filtering function, showing that filtering can decrease the sensitivity upper bound. Conceptually, AMP departs from the dominant rewiring and ODE-based approaches to oversquashing by arguing that selectively discarding messages (rather than adding edges or preserving all information) is a complementary and sometimes preferable strategy, and it unifies adaptive depth learning with message selection in a single differentiable framework rather than treating them as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMP provides a plug-and-play framework that can be applied to any message passing architecture (GCN, GIN, GatedGCN, ADGN) without modifying the underlying graph structure, offering a principled alternative to the growing zoo of rewiring and structural modification techniques. The theoretical result that filtering can reduce sensitivity while improving task performance challenges the widely held assumption that 'improving sensitivity mitigates oversquashing,' suggesting the oversquashing problem is more nuanced than a single metric captures. Limitations include the additional readout parameters per layer (O(|V|L) cost), the reliance on a first-order variational approximation for the parameter posterior, and the fact that the approach has only been validated on graphs up to ~150 nodes, leaving open whether it scales to the large graphs common in scientific applications.
