## Problem

Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) such as DPO and SimPO uniformly adjust all token probabilities during preference optimization, treating every token as equally relevant to human preference. However, not all tokens carry equal preference information—highly predictable tokens add little signal while a small subset of low-confidence tokens drives most of the alignment learning. Prior token-level selection methods require auxiliary models, extra forward passes, or external annotators, introducing computational overhead and scalability concerns that ConfPO eliminates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard preference dataset of (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) triples and a base or instruction-tuned language model, producing an aligned policy model. During training, the policy model's own per-token probabilities are computed in the same forward pass used for the loss—no additional inference, backward passes, or external models are invoked. The selection threshold τ is set dynamically per response as the arithmetic mean of that response's token probabilities, so the number of selected tokens adapts throughout training (typically 40–50% of total tokens).

## Method

ConfPO modifies SimPO's preference optimization objective by introducing a binary selection function s(y_i) = 1(π_θ(y_i|x, y_{<i}) ≤ τ) that restricts the reward sum to only those tokens whose predicted probability falls below the sequence-average confidence threshold τ. The key empirical finding is that token-level gradient norms in DAAs follow a long-tailed distribution and exhibit a strong negative Spearman correlation with token probability, meaning low-confidence tokens carry disproportionately large learning signals. A Shapley value decomposition of the gradient-norm-to-probability ratio confirms that the denominator (token probability) dominates the variation, providing theoretical grounding for using confidence as a proxy for gradient magnitude. By optimizing on fewer than half of all tokens, ConfPO improves alignment on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard across Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B (Base and Instruct) while simultaneously mitigating overoptimization through more efficient use of the KL divergence budget. The approach generalizes to DPO by applying the same confidence-based masking to the log-ratio terms in DPO's objective.

## Results

ConfPO consistently outperforms SimPO (its direct baseline) across all four model settings on both AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard. On Mistral-Base (7B), ConfPO achieves 28.9% LC / 26.7% WR on AlpacaEval 2 and 16.9% on Arena-Hard, versus SimPO's 27.1% / 23.4% / 13.8%. On Llama-3-Instruct (8B), ConfPO reaches 49.1% LC / 45.0% WR and 32.8% Arena-Hard, edging out SimPO (48.8% / 44.4% / 32.6%). Notably, ConfPO achieves these gains while optimizing fewer than 50% of tokens, and its overoptimization curve sits above SimPO's, indicating more efficient KL budget utilization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfPO outperforms SimPO (its primary baseline) on every metric across all four model settings, with the largest gains on Base models (e.g., +3.3 WR on Mistral-Base AlpacaEval 2, +7.0 Arena-Hard on Mistral-Base). Against DPO, ConfPO leads by 10.6 LC points on Mistral-Base and 5.3 LC on Llama-3-Instruct. ConfPO also dominates RRHF, SLiC-HF, IPO, KTO, and ORPO across all settings. Two near-ties exist: CPO marginally edges ConfPO on Arena-Hard for Mistral-Instruct (22.6 vs. 22.4), and R-DPO slightly leads on Arena-Hard for Llama-3-Instruct (33.1 vs. 32.8), though ConfPO wins on AlpacaEval 2 LC/WR in both cases. A random-token-selection ablation (ConfPO-rand) underperforms SimPO, confirming that the confidence-based selection criterion—not merely reducing token count—is responsible for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConfPO is a targeted extension of SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), which itself resolved the reference-model inconsistency and length bias in DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024b). It departs from prior token-level DAAs (SePO, T-REG, FIGA, TDPO) that require external models, multiple forward passes, or auxiliary training to identify important tokens, by using the policy model's own per-token confidence as a free proxy for gradient magnitude. The theoretical grounding via Shapley value decomposition of the log-probability gradient norm ratio provides a principled justification for why confidence (the denominator) dominates gradient magnitude, connecting psycholinguistic surprisal theory to preference optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ConfPO achieves consistent alignment improvements over SimPO across four model configurations with zero additional computational overhead, while also mitigating overoptimization (reward hacking) by using the KL budget more efficiently on fewer than 50% of tokens. The finding that training exclusively on high-confidence tokens yields negligible or negative alignment gains is a useful negative result that validates the selective approach. Open questions acknowledged by the authors include whether the method generalizes beyond SimPO and DPO to other DAAs, whether it scales to larger models, and whether a deeper theoretical characterization of the alignment mechanism is possible.
