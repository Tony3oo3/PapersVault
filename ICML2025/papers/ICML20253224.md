## Problem

Existing positional encodings in transformers impose rigid, fixed decay patterns on attention maps and are learned as dataset-level biases, limiting their ability to model long-range dependencies and adapt to instance-specific algorithmic structures. This rigidity constrains the class of algorithms transformers can represent (upper-bounded by TC^0) and weakens position-based addressing, which is critical for tasks like arithmetic reasoning and in-context retrieval where token position matters as much as token content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence of N tokens with feature dimension C, an attention mask M ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, and multi-dimensional positional embeddings E ∈ R^{N×M×L×R} (where the hidden dimension C is split into M blocks of size B=C/M, and each block carries L positional vectors of dimension R). It produces standard next-token predictions for language modeling, exact arithmetic results, and retrieved passkeys. A key design choice is that positional embeddings are updated layer-by-layer through interactions with token features, rather than being fixed at the input layer, and the entire architecture is compatible with Flash Attention for efficient inference.

## Method

TAPE extends conventional vectorized positional embeddings into multi-dimensional tensors and introduces two equivariant operations per transformer layer: (1) an O(R)-invariant attention mechanism where pre-softmax logits are computed as (W_Q x_i)_m^T φ(e_{i,m} e_{j,m}^T) (W_K x_j)_m, using pairwise inner products of positional embeddings to encode relative position information; and (2) an O(R)-equivariant position contextualization module that updates positional embeddings by (a) mixing them across tokens via the same attention weights and (b) applying a token-conditioned diagonal linear transformation (Eq. 8) that reshapes the flattened positional tensor while preserving orthogonal equivariance. By enforcing permutation and orthogonal group equivariance, TAPE provably achieves NC^1-complete circuit representational power (vs. TC^0 for standard transformers) and guarantees that positional encodings remain relative after layer-wise updates, enabling length generalization. RoPE is a special case (L=R=2) and serves as initialization, allowing TAPE to be plugged into pre-trained models as a parameter-efficient fine-tuning module with only W_1, W_2, and post-attention linear layers updated.

## Results

On the Addition Bucket 40 arithmetic task (trained to 40, tested to 80), TAPE achieves 32.82% average accuracy, a 21.6% relative improvement over the prior best (FIRE, 26.98%). On the SCROLLS long-context benchmark, TAPE leads on 5 of 7 tasks, with particularly large gains on QuALITY (11.60 EM vs. 0.04 for RoPE) and NarrativeQA (6.79 F1 vs. 1.77 for RoPE). For context window extension of Llama2 7B from 4k to 8k, TAPE reduces perplexity to 2.708 on Proof-pile and 7.063 on PG19 at 8192 tokens, outperforming LoRA, LongLoRA, and Theta Scaling across all lengths. TAPE also achieves near-perfect passkey retrieval accuracy up to 8k context, matching full-parameter Theta Scaling while using only parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPE outperforms RoPE on all seven SCROLLS tasks, with the most dramatic gains on QuALITY (11.60 vs. 0.04 EM) and NarrativeQA (6.79 vs. 1.77 F1), confirming RoPE's severe limitations on long-context retrieval. Against ALiBi, TAPE wins on 5 of 7 tasks but trails on GovReport (15.18 vs. 18.81 Rgm) and QAS (11.52 vs. 8.25 F1 is a win, but RandPE leads QAS at 13.44). TAPE dominates RandPE on 6 of 7 tasks, losing only on QAS F1 (11.52 vs. 13.44). Compared to FIRE, TAPE is superior on 6 of 7 tasks, with the largest margin on QuALITY (11.60 vs. 1.25 EM). Against xPos, the closest competitor, TAPE wins on 5 of 7 tasks but falls short on GovReport (15.18 vs. 16.38) and QAS (11.52 vs. 9.02 is actually a win; the loss is on GovReport only). For context window extension (Table 2), TAPE beats LoRA, LongLoRA, and Theta Scaling on both Proof-pile and PG19 at every context length from 1k to 8k, with the gap widening at longer lengths (e.g., 2.708 vs. 2.956 perplexity on Proof-pile at 8k). On arithmetic, TAPE's 32.82% average accuracy surpasses FIRE (26.98%), RoPE (26.32%), RandPE (26.56%), and NoPE (22.45%), with the advantage growing at longer operand lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPE positions itself at the intersection of equivariant geometric deep learning and transformer positional encoding, importing permutation and orthogonal group equivariance principles from graph/point-cloud networks into language models. It explicitly builds on RoPE as a special case (L=R=2) and extends it with layer-wise contextualization via attention and MLP updates, departing from the fixed-pattern paradigm of ALiBi, xPos, FIRE, and RandPE. The theoretical analysis is heavily inspired by concurrent work from Yang et al. (2025) on Path Attention with Householder matrices, which first established NC1-completeness for a related construction; TAPE connects this result to its geometric equivariance constraints. The paper also engages with the circuit-complexity line of Merrill & Sabharwal (2023) showing standard transformers are bounded by TC0, positioning TAPE as a mechanism to break that barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NC1-completeness result provides a clear theoretical separation from standard transformers (TC0), suggesting that context-aware positional encoding is not merely a practical trick but a fundamental expressivity upgrade for algorithmic reasoning. Practically, TAPE's drop-in PEFT design with negligible overhead (~14% FLOPS increase) and Flash Attention compatibility makes it immediately deployable in existing LLMs, as demonstrated by the Llama2 7B context extension to 8k with no passkey retrieval degradation. Key limitations include the exclusive focus on decoder-only architectures at limited training scale, and the arithmetic accuracy, while improved, remains low in absolute terms (~33%). Open questions include whether the equivariance constraints are necessary or merely sufficient, and whether the NC1 expressivity advantage persists at the scale of frontier models.
