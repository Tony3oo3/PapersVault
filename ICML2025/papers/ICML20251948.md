## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely on accurate concept labels during training, but in practice concept annotations are frequently noisy due to data augmentations, subjective labeling, and human error—degrading performance by up to 25%. Standard binary cross-entropy (BCE) training is inherently sensitive to this noise because it updates on every sample regardless of correctness, and no principled loss existed that could handle concept mislabeling while preserving the CBM's interpretability and intervenability guarantees.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (299×299 for CUB, standard resolutions for AwA2 and CelebA) and produces two outputs: a class label via a task predictor f_φ and a vector of binary concept predictions (e.g., 112 concepts for CUB, 85 for AwA2, 6 for CelebA) via a concept predictor π_θ. A key additional output is per-concept uncertainty, computed as the Bernoulli variance c(1−c), which the paper shows is more calibrated than BCE-based or ProbCBM-based uncertainty under occlusion and augmentation. The concept predictor is initialized from a ResNet-34 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-1K, and the full model is trained end-to-end.

## Method

The paper introduces the Concept Preference Optimization (CPO) loss, an online adaptation of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for CBMs. Rather than assuming empirical concept labels are correct (as BCE does), CPO assumes only a weaker preference: the empirical concept set is preferred over a negative concept set sampled from the policy π_θ itself. Under a uniform prior, CPO reduces to log-likelihood only when the sampled concept differs from the empirical one, and becomes a constant otherwise—meaning the policy receives no gradient update when it happens to sample the correct concept. The paper proves (Proposition 4.2) that CPO's expected gradient norm is upper-bounded by BCE's, and (Theorem 4.3) that under constant label noise, CPO's noisy gradient is closer in L2 distance to the optimal gradient than BCE's, because CPO's gradient is BCE's gradient weighted by the policy's confidence in the sampled concept. The paper further shows via a control-as-inference derivation that optimizing CPO is equivalent to learning the amortized posterior distribution over concepts π(c|x), which yields better-calibrated uncertainty estimates and more effective test-time interventions compared to BCE or latent-variable-based approaches like ProbCBM.

## Results

On the un-noised evaluation, CBM trained with L_CPO achieves 80.0% task accuracy and 0.952 concept AUC on CUB, 91.5% task accuracy and 0.971 concept AUC on AwA2, and 31.0% task accuracy on CelebA, consistently outperforming its BCE-trained counterpart across all three datasets. CEM trained with L_CPO further improves to 80.7% (CUB) and 91.7% (AwA2) task accuracy while matching or exceeding CEM-BCE on CelebA (35.2% vs 35.1%). Under 40% random concept label noise, CBM-CPO retains 78.0% task accuracy on CUB compared to 68.2% for CBM-BCE, demonstrating substantially greater noise robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard CBM-BCE baseline, L_CPO improves task accuracy by 4.7 points on CUB (80.0% vs 75.3%), 1.5 points on AwA2 (91.5% vs 90.0%), and 2.7 points on CelebA (31.0% vs 28.3%), while also achieving the best concept AUC on CUB (0.952 vs 0.937) and AwA2 (0.971 vs 0.959). Compared to CEM-BCE, CBM-CPO matches CUB task accuracy (80.0% vs 80.0%) and surpasses it on AwA2 (91.5% vs 88.9%), showing that the preference-optimization objective recovers the expressivity benefit of concept embeddings without additional parameters. Against ProbCBM (both sequential and joint), CBM-CPO leads on CUB task accuracy (80.0% vs 74.2%/76.6%) and concept AUC (0.952 vs 0.900/0.943), and on AwA2 task accuracy (91.5% vs 89.1%/86.0%). CoopCBM performs comparably to CBM-BCE and is outperformed by CBM-CPO on CUB (80.0% vs 76.0% task accuracy). Under 40% label noise, CBM-CPO retains 78.0% CUB task accuracy versus 68.2% for CBM-BCE and 66.7% for CEM-BCE, confirming the theoretical noise-robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from the LLM/RLHF literature into the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) training pipeline, replacing the standard binary cross-entropy concept loss with a preference-based objective (CPO). It departs from ProbCBMs and Stochastic CBMs, which amortize a posterior over an auxiliary latent variable, by showing that CPO directly approximates the posterior over the concepts themselves—a distinction made explicit through a control-as-inference derivation. The theoretical analysis builds on gradient-bounding results from offline RL (Kumar et al., 2022; Rashidinejad et al., 2021) but provides a CBM-specific proof that CPO's expected gradient norm is upper-bounded by BCE's, yielding a concrete noise-robustness guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CBMs are positioned as key tools for high-stakes interpretable AI (healthcare, law enforcement), yet their reliance on clean concept labels is a practical bottleneck that this paper addresses with a parameter-free loss swap. The finding that a simple CBM trained with CPO can match or exceed CEMs (which add substantial capacity) without extra parameters or compute is practically appealing. Open questions include whether the uniform-prior assumption limits real-world applicability, whether the results extend to non-binary or high-dimensional concept spaces, and whether the streaming-data prior mechanism generalizes beyond the single CUB experiment. The paper also notes that full RL (with learned reward functions) remains an unexplored but promising direction.
