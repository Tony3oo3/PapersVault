## Problem

Branch-length estimation in phylogenetics under the Cavender-Farris-Neyman model involves maximizing a non-concave log-likelihood that can admit exponentially many critical points and multiple global maximizers. Despite the empirical success of coordinate maximization algorithms (e.g., PHYML), no theoretical guarantee existed explaining why this algorithm converges to the correct parameter. This paper provides the first such guarantee, showing that deep inside the Kesten-Stigum reconstruction regime the empirical likelihood landscape is benign in a tree-independent neighborhood of the true parameter.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed binary phylogenetic tree T with known topology, m independent binary spin observations (±1) at the leaves, and an initial parameter estimate θ₀ within O(δ) of the true parameter in L₂ norm. It produces the estimated edge parameters θ̂ ∈ [0,1]^E (one per edge, where θ_e = 1 − 2p_e encodes the transition probability). The tree topology is assumed known, so optimization is only over the |E| edge parameters; the sample complexity is polynomial in |E| when the tree is balanced (diameter O(log n)) but exponential in the tree diameter in general.

## Method

The proof follows a three-step program: (1) the population Hessian is strongly concave and smooth in an L∞ box of universal size around θ* (from prior work); (2) the Hessian entries vary Lipschitz-continuously with the parameter, established by bounding third-order derivatives of the log-likelihood via explicit magnetization-based formulas; and (3) the per-sample Hessian has uniformly bounded spectral norm via Gershgorin's theorem. These ingredients feed into a novel uniform matrix Bernstein inequality (Lemma 4.5) that concentrates the empirical Hessian around its population expectation uniformly over the entire box, yielding strong concavity of the empirical landscape with high probability. The O(1/√m) statistical guarantee follows from a first-order Taylor expansion combined with Berry-Esseen, while the exponential convergence of cyclic coordinate maximization is established by extending the two-block linear convergence result of Beck & Tetruashvili to arbitrarily many blocks, with a local confinement argument ensuring iterates remain in the good region.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical guarantees for branch-length estimation under the CFN model deep inside the Kesten-Stigum regime. (1) With m ≥ (C₅/δ)^(diam(T)+8) log(ε⁻¹) samples (polynomial in tree size for balanced trees), the empirical log-likelihood is Θ(δ⁻¹)-strongly concave and Θ(δ⁻¹)-smooth on a universal L∞-box around θ* with probability ≥ 1−ε (Thm. 3.2). (2) The MLE is O(√(|E|/m) · log(|E|/ε))-consistent with the true parameter (Thm. 3.3). (3) Cyclic coordinate maximization converges to the MLE at an exponential rate (1 − C₆/C̃₆)^k where the contraction factor is a universal constant independent of tree size and δ (Thm. 3.4), meaning O(log ε⁻¹) iterations suffice to reach any desired tolerance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The contribution is the first rigorous guarantee explaining why coordinate maximization (as used in PHYML, RAxML, IQ-TREE) works well for branch-length estimation despite the non-concave likelihood landscape. The paper builds on Clancy et al. (2025a), which established strong concavity of the *population* Hessian in a box-neighborhood of θ*, by extending this to the *empirical* landscape via a uniform matrix Bernstein inequality (Lemma 4.5) and proving Lipschitz continuity of Hessian entries. The convergence rate for coordinate maximization extends the two-block result of Beck & Tetruashvili (2013) to arbitrarily many blocks (one per edge). No prior work had established polynomial sample complexity for obtaining a well-conditioned MLE landscape in this problem, nor a convergence guarantee for the coordinate maximization algorithm used in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the population likelihood landscape analysis of Clancy, Jr. et al. (2025a) to the finite-sample empirical setting, completing a three-step program: (1) strong concavity of the population Hessian, (2) Lipschitz continuity of Hessian entries, and (3) uniform spectral norm bounds on per-sample Hessians. It builds on the uniform matrix Bernstein inequality (extending Tropp, 2012) and the multi-block coordinate descent convergence framework (extending Beck & Tetruashvili, 2013 from two blocks to arbitrarily many). The recursive magnetization-based gradient computation from Clancy, Jr. et al. (2025b) serves as the computational backbone of the coordinate maximization algorithm analyzed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing open problem (dating to Felsenstein, 1981) by providing the first rigorous explanation for why coordinate maximization performs well in phylogenetic branch-length estimation despite the non-concave likelihood landscape with exponentially many critical points. The three-step methodological framework for analyzing non-concave MLE problems is potentially transferable to other latent tree or mixture model settings. Key limitations include the requirement that the initial estimate be O(δ)-close to the true parameter in L2 norm, and the sample complexity being exponential in tree diameter (polynomial only for balanced trees). The open question of whether the initialization requirement can be removed, and whether the framework extends beyond the Kesten-Stigum regime, are natural next steps.
