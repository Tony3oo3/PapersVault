## Problem

High-dimensional ridge regression theory and the GCV risk estimator assume i.i.d. training samples, but real data from time series, finance, and neuroscience exhibit sample-sample correlations that invalidate these assumptions. The paper shows that the standard GCV and all previously proposed corrections for correlated data are asymptotically biased in the proportional limit, leaving no principled way to estimate out-of-sample risk or tune the ridge parameter for correlated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250071/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the empirical covariance matrix Σ̂ (N×N), the in-sample risk R̂_in, the ridge parameter λ, the overparameterization ratio q=N/T, and the S-transform S_K of the sample-sample correlation matrix K. A key practical requirement is knowing (or estimating) the functional form of S_K, which for stationary time series can be obtained from the autocorrelation function with only O(T) parameters. The output is a scalar CorrGCV risk estimate that is asymptotically exact and concentrates over dataset draws, enabling direct optimization over λ to find the optimal ridge parameter.

## Method

The paper derives sharp high-dimensional asymptotics for ridge regression with correlated Gaussian samples using free probability and random matrix theory, specifically one- and two-point strong deterministic equivalents for Wishart matrices with anisotropic sample-sample correlations. The central result is the CorrGCV estimator, which corrects the standard GCV by a factor S(df₁)·df̃₁/(df̃₁−df̃₂) that accounts for the correlation structure through the S-transform of K and second-order degrees of freedom. The computation leverages autograd (e.g., JAX) to differentiate the self-consistent equation for the renormalized ridge κ with respect to λ, extracting the needed second-order spectral statistics without explicit matrix inversions. A surprising duality is uncovered between train-test covariate shift and a mismatch between covariate and noise correlations, showing that a GCV exists only when K=K′. The framework is further extended to correlated test points, yielding a multiplicative correction [1−ρ+κ̃²αᵀK(K+κ̃)⁻²α] that quantifies the over-optimism of near-horizon cross-validation in time series forecasting.

## Results

The paper provides sharp high-dimensional asymptotics for ridge regression with correlated samples, showing that the proposed CorrGCV estimator is asymptotically exact and unbiased in the proportional limit T,N→∞. For strongly correlated data (exponential correlation length ξ=10²), the ordinary GCV and previously proposed corrections (Naive GCV₁, Naive GCV₂) fail to predict out-of-sample risk, while CorrGCV matches the true risk across all ridge values. The paper further shows that correlations mollify the double-descent peak (effectively replacing λ→λS_K(1) near the interpolation threshold), and that testing on a correlated point at horizon τ yields risk approximately (1−ρ) times the uncorrelated-test-point risk, quantifying the over-optimism of near-horizon cross-validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CorrGCV against three previously proposed estimators for correlated data. Naive GCV₁ (Golub et al., 1979), which ignores sample-sample correlations entirely, and Naive GCV₂ (Altman, 1990), which accounts for noise correlations but not covariate correlations, both fail for strong correlations (ξ=10²): one underestimates and the other overestimates the true out-of-sample risk (Figure 1). The estimator of Carmack et al. (2012) is also shown to be asymptotically biased (Appendix G). In contrast, CorrGCV correctly predicts the out-of-sample risk in all tested settings (weak and strong correlations, exponential and power-law correlation structures) and is the only estimator that allows accurate identification of the optimal ridge parameter λ (Figure 2). For weak correlations (ξ=10⁻²), all estimators converge to the same value and agree with the true risk, confirming that the failure mode is specific to strong correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharp high-dimensional asymptotics for ridge regression (Hastie et al., 2022; Dobriban & Wager, 2018; Canatar et al., 2021b) from the i.i.d. setting to arbitrary sample-sample correlations, using the S-transform and deterministic equivalence machinery from free probability that the authors developed in Atanasov et al. (2024). It directly generalizes the classical GCV estimator (Golub et al., 1979) and shows that all previously proposed corrections for correlated data (Altman, 1990; Carmack et al., 2012; Lukas, 2010) are asymptotically biased, providing the first asymptotically exact estimator (CorrGCV) for this setting. The analysis also unifies covariate shift and noise-correlation mismatch under a single duality framework, extending prior out-of-distribution results (Patil et al., 2024; Canatar et al., 2021a) to the correlated-sample regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is directly applicable to time series regression in finance, climate, and neuroscience, where the i.i.d. assumption is fundamentally violated and standard cross-validation gives misleading risk estimates. The paper precisely quantifies how testing on near-horizon data overestimates long-term forecast accuracy, and shows that correlations mollify (but do not eliminate) the double-descent peak. Key limitations include the Gaussian data assumption (though universality is expected), the requirement to know or estimate the correlation structure K, and the absence of dimension-free finite-sample bounds. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian data, sample-dependent feature covariances, and establishing rigorous universality for non-independent samples.
