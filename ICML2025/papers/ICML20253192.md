## Problem

Pre-training data quality has traditionally been improved through human-crafted heuristic rules (document filtering, perplexity scoring) that are rigid and cannot address the unique characteristics of individual examples. While LLMs have been applied to data filtering or synthesis, filtering methods lack fine-grained string-level refinement and synthesis methods are computationally expensive and prone to hallucination. The paper asks whether small language models (as small as 0.3B parameters) can be adapted to perform sample-wise, fine-grained data refinement at pre-training scale.

## Input / Output

PROX takes a raw pre-training document (e.g., an HTML extract or textbook passage) as input and produces a Pythonic refinement program consisting of function calls from a fixed API (drop_doc, keep_doc, remove_lines, normalize, keep_chunk). The program is then executed by a deterministic Python executor to yield the refined document. The process operates in two sequential stages: first at the document level (keep or drop the entire document), then at the chunk level where documents are split into ~1,500-word chunks and fine-grained operations (line deletion by index, string replacement) are applied per chunk.

## Method

PROX formulates data refinement as a program synthesis task: a small base model (0.3B–1.7B, trained from scratch on ~26B tokens) is adapted via supervised fine-tuning on seed data annotated by LLaMA-3-70B-Instruct using additive scale scoring prompts (educational + format scores) for document-level decisions and few-shot prompting for chunk-level noise patterns (navigation bars, HTML links, footers). At inference, the adapted model generates function-call programs for each document or chunk, which a Python executor runs to produce the refined corpus. The key design choice is that models generate only function calls with parameters (not full implementations), keeping the program space simple enough for sub-1B models while remaining expressive enough to encode complex expert heuristics. This two-stage sequential approach—coarse document filtering followed by fine-grained chunk cleaning—achieves >80% F1 on document-level and >75% F1 on chunk-level tasks with <0.4% program error rates, and consistently improves downstream benchmark performance by 2–3% across five corpora and model sizes up to 1.7B.

## Results

On 10 general-domain benchmarks (ARC-C, ARC-E, CSQA, HellaSwag, MMLU, OBQA, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, SciQ), PROX-D+C achieves 44.6% average accuracy, outperforming the raw corpus (42.1%) by 2.5% and the best rule-based baseline (FineWeb rules, 42.8%) by 1.8%. On 8 benchmarks using C4 and Pythia architecture, PROX achieves 46.2% 0-shot and 47.5% 2-shot average, beating the strongest data-selection method MATES (44.0% / 45.0%) by 2.2% and 2.5% respectively. In math-domain continual pre-training, PROX-refined OpenWebMath boosts CodeLlama-7B by +20.3% (49.4% avg over 9 math tasks) using only 10B training tokens, approaching LLEMMA-7B's 50.9% which required 200B tokens (a 20× reduction in compute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROX-D+C (44.6% avg) outperforms all rule-based filtering baselines: Gopher rules (42.3%, +2.3%), C4 rules (42.6%, +2.0%), FineWeb rules (42.8%, +1.8%), and the combined Go+C4+Fw pipeline (42.3%, +2.3%). Against the strongest non-PROX baseline, FastText filtering from DCLM (43.3%), PROX leads by 1.3%. In the data-selection comparison on C4/Pythia, PROX (46.2% 0-shot) beats MATES (44.0%, +2.2%), QuRating (43.5%, +2.7%), DsDm (43.4%, +2.8%), and DSIR (42.5%, +3.7%), winning 7 out of 8 individual benchmarks. In math-domain continual pre-training, PROX on CodeLlama-7B reaches 49.4% with 10B tokens, approaching LLEMMA-7B (50.9%) which used 200B tokens, and surpassing InternLM2-Math-7B (48.7%, 125B tokens) with far fewer tokens. PROX-curated models also match or exceed LLM-synthesis approaches (InstructionLM-1.3B, Cosmo-1.8B) and pruning-based models (ShearedLLaMA-1.3B, TinyLlama-1.1B) while using substantially less total compute for both data refinement and training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROX carves out a distinct middle ground between two established paradigms for pre-training data quality: heuristic rule-based filtering (Gopher, C4, FineWeb) and LLM-based data selection or synthesis (MATES, QuRating, DSIR, InstructionLM, Cosmo). Rather than scoring documents for binary keep/drop decisions or generating entirely new text, PROX reframes refinement as a program-synthesis task where a small LM emits executable function calls (drop_doc, remove_lines, normalize) that a Python executor applies per-example. This borrows the function-calling interface from LLM agent research and the additive scoring prompts from FineWeb-Edu, but departs from both by producing fine-grained, interpretable transformations rather than opaque scores or hallucination-prone synthetic text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 0.3B refining model yields consistent 2–3% gains across five major corpora and three model scales, and in math-domain continual pre-training achieves LLEMMA-7B-level performance with 20× fewer training tokens (10B vs. 200B). The program-based formulation offers interpretability and verifiability that synthesis methods lack, and the low error rate (<0.4%) makes it deployable at scale with modest inference cost. Open questions include whether the fixed five-function program space can be expanded to capture more complex transformations (e.g., reordering, summarization), how the approach generalizes to multilingual or code-heavy corpora, and whether the seed-data annotation step (requiring a 70B LLM) can itself be automated or reduced.
