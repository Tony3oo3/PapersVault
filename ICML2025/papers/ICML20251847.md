## Problem

The minimax sample complexity of group distributionally robust optimization (GDRO) scales linearly with the number of groups K, but this worst-case bound relies on arbitrarily small risk gaps between groups for every hypothesis—a condition that rarely holds in practice. This paper introduces a novel (λ,β)-sparsity condition capturing the fact that, for most hypotheses, only a small subset of β groups have risks significantly exceeding the rest, and shows how to exploit this structure to reduce the K dependence in the leading sample complexity term.

## Input / Output

The learner queries a sampling oracle that returns i.i.d. samples from group distribution P_i upon request i ∈ [K], operating over a compact convex hypothesis set Θ ⊂ ℝⁿ with ℓ₂ diameter D and a G-Lipschitz loss bounded in [0,1]. The algorithm outputs an ε-optimal hypothesis θ̄ (the uniform average of T iterates) and, in adaptive variants, an estimate of the optimal sparsity parameter λ* that minimizes the sample complexity trade-off between the pre-sampling cost (∝ K/λ²) and the game-round cost (∝ β_λ/ε²). The key structural constraint is that the loss is real-valued in [0,1] and the hypothesis set is convex with bounded diameter.

## Method

The core technical contribution connects the two-player zero-sum game formulation of GDRO to sleeping bandits: the max-player's active group set Ŝ_θt (computed via a uniform convergence bound on m pre-collected samples per group) is time-varying and controlled by the min-player's current hypothesis, mirroring a non-oblivious adversary in sleeping bandits. By deploying SB-EXP3—a sleeping bandits algorithm with per-action regret bounds—for the max-player, the regret improves from O(√(K ln K · T)) to O(√(β_λ ln K · T)), reducing the leading-term sample complexity from Õ((D²G²+K)/ε²) to Õ((D²G²+β_λ)/ε²). The min-player uses stochastic mirror descent with time-varying learning rates η_{w,t} = D/(G√t). For adaptivity, SB-GDRO-A constructs a non-decreasing estimate ĝ(λ) of β_λ via cover-based evaluation and runs a bisection-like SolveOpt procedure to find λ* with only a ln(1/ε) multiplicative overhead. A dimension-free variant (SB-GDRO-DF) eliminates the n-dependent pre-sampling cost by reusing dominant sets across short episodes, exploiting Lipschitz stability of the min-player's regularized updates to keep risks within each episode close.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution reduces the leading-term dependence on the number of groups from Õ((D²G² + K)/ε²) to Õ((D²G² + β_λ)/ε²), where β_λ can be much smaller than K under the proposed (λ,β)-sparsity condition. A near-matching lower bound of Ω((D²G² + β)/ε²) is established. Empirically, on the Adult dataset (K=10 groups, ε=0.001), SB-GDRO-SA achieves an optimality gap below 4×10⁻⁵ well before 4×10⁵ samples, while SMD-GDRO fails to reach this threshold even after nearly 10⁶ samples. Notably, over 60% of SB-GDRO-SA's samples come from the female Amer-Indian-Eskimo group, which constitutes only 0.3% of the dataset, demonstrating the robustness focus of GDRO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SB-GDRO-SA (the semi-adaptive algorithm) against SMD-GDRO (Zhang et al., 2023), the only existing baseline with a near-optimal high-probability guarantee in the minimax regime. On the Adult dataset (K=10, ε=0.001, T=10⁶), SB-GDRO-SA clearly outperforms SMD-GDRO in sample efficiency: it reaches an optimality gap below 4×10⁻⁵ well before 4×10⁵ samples, whereas SMD-GDRO struggles to get under 4×10⁻⁵ even after nearly the full 10⁶ samples. The advantage stems from SB-GDRO-SA's ability to discover non-trivial (λ, ln K)-sparsity early (within ~3000 rounds) and then concentrate sampling on only the 2 highest-risk groups, while SMD-GDRO samples uniformly across all K groups. On the synthetic lower-bound environment (K=10, λ*=0.2, β_{λ*}=2), SB-GDRO-SA discovers the sparsity structure using roughly 3000 samples (final λ≈0.125≈0.5λ*), confirming the theoretical prediction that the sample complexity is dominated by the two-player game rounds rather than the λ-estimation phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-player zero-sum game framework for GDRO established by Nemirovski et al. (2009) and refined by Soma et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2023), which achieved near-minimax sample complexity of Õ((G²D²+K)/ε²). The key departure is introducing (λ,β)-sparsity—a structural condition ensuring only β ≤ K groups have significantly elevated risk at any hypothesis—and exploiting it via the sleeping bandits framework (Kleinberg et al., 2010; Nguyen & Mehta, 2024) to reduce the leading-term dependence from K ln K to β ln K. The min-player's stochastic mirror descent strategy follows Zhang et al. (2023), while the max-player's strategy is the novel contribution, replacing the fixed K-arm bandit with a time-varying active set of size at most β.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of sample complexity bounds for GDRO that provably improve upon the minimax rate, opening a path toward understanding how problem structure (beyond worst-case) affects learning efficiency in distributionally robust settings. The fundamental connection between per-action regret in sleeping bandits and the max-player's regret in the GDRO game is a reusable conceptual bridge. Open questions include whether the (λ,β)-sparsity condition can be relaxed to hold only locally near θ* (the paper's experiments suggest it does in practice, but a rigorous treatment requires high-probability last-iterate convergence for non-strongly convex stochastic games, which remains open), and whether the dimension-dependent uniform convergence cost can be further reduced. The practical impact is currently limited to linear models and small-scale experiments, though the framework is general.
