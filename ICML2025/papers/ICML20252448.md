## Problem

Masked diffusion models for language modeling, while simpler and effective, reintroduce the fundamental limitation of autoregressive models: once a token is filled in, it can never be revised, causing errors to propagate irreversibly. Additionally, only masked tokens carry a training signal, reducing effective batch size. No general theoretical framework existed for designing discrete diffusion processes with flexible noise types (e.g., combining masking with uniform noise) or for deriving the corresponding training objective.

## Input / Output

The model takes a partially noised 512-token text sequence (using the GPT2 tokenizer) where each token is independently in one of three states—original data, [MASK], or a uniformly random token—along with a continuous time step t ∈ (0,1), and outputs a per-token categorical distribution over the vocabulary predicting the clean token. During generation, the model starts from an all-mask sequence and iteratively denoises over T chosen steps; a separate self-correction loop then repeatedly resamples and commits single-token edits until convergence, producing a refined text sequence.

## Method

The paper introduces Generalized Interpolating Discrete Diffusion (GIDD), a family of discrete diffusion processes whose marginal forward transition is a linear interpolation q_t(z_t|x) = Cat(z_t; α_t·x + β_t·π_t) between the data and a time-varying mixing distribution π_t, with mixing rate α_t controlling the signal-to-noise ratio. The key theoretical contribution is a closed-form derivation of the CTMC forward rate, the cumulative transition matrix, and a novel continuous-time ELBO (Theorem 3.7) that decomposes into a KL-divergence term and a pointwise Itakura-Saito divergence term, both minimized iff the model matches the true marginal. For practical language modeling, the mixing schedule combines masking with a configurable fraction p_u of uniform noise, and a dynamic loss-weighting scheme (GIDD+) addresses the extreme weight growth at low/high noise levels. A fixed-point self-correction algorithm—resampling all tokens at a given temperature, then committing the single highest-likelihood change—enables the model to iteratively improve its own outputs, a capability absent in both autoregressive and mask-only diffusion models.

## Results

On OpenWebText, GIDD+ (p_u=0.0, 262B tokens) achieves a validation PPL of 22.29, outperforming the compute-matched MDM reimplementation (23.36) and MDLM (23.21), while remaining competitive with MD4 (21.80, trained on 524B tokens). On zero-shot benchmarks, the best GIDD+ model (p_u=0.0, 131B tokens) achieves 39.30% average accuracy, surpassing GPT2-small (38.77%) and MDM (38.25%), though still trailing the retrained Llama 110M baseline (41.04%). With self-correction applied, the GIDD+ BASE model (p_u=0.2) reduces generative PPL from 214 to 93.3 and improves self-accuracy from 62.0% to 73.5%, a capability absent in mask-only models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the compute-matched MDM reimplementation (262B tokens), GIDD+ (p_u=0.0) improves validation PPL from 23.36 to 22.29, a gain of ~1.07 PPL attributable to the reweighted training objective. GIDD+ also outperforms MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024) at the same 262B compute budget (22.29 vs. 23.21 PPL), though MD4 (Shi et al., 2024) reports a lower PPL of 21.80 at double the compute (524B tokens). On zero-shot benchmarks, GIDD+ (p_u=0.0, 131B) achieves 39.30% average accuracy, beating GPT2-small (38.77%) and MDM (38.25%), but the retrained Llama 110M autoregressive model still leads at 41.04%. The key differentiator is self-correction: hybrid-noise GIDD+ models (p_u > 0) significantly improve sample quality after self-correction (e.g., +16.5% clarity, +16.6% grammaticality per GPT-4o grading), while mask-only models and MDM actually degrade in quality when self-correction is applied, confirming that the ability to revise tokens is uniquely unlocked by the uniform noise component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes masked diffusion (MDM), which restricts the noising process to replacing tokens with a single [MASK] token, to the broader GIDD family where the mixing distribution π_t can be any time-varying probability vector. The theoretical foundation builds on the discrete diffusion ELBO of Austin et al. (2023) and its continuous-time extension by Campbell et al. (2022), from which the authors derive a closed-form ELBO for the general interpolating family. The practical motivation of combining masking with uniform noise draws from BERT's denoising objective, but the paper is the first to provide a principled diffusion ELBO and Markov chain construction for this hybrid noise in the generative (not representation-learning) setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that training with a small amount of uniform noise (which slightly worsens validation PPL) unlocks self-correction at generation time, allowing the model to iteratively improve its own outputs by up to 55% in generative PPL — a capability where autoregressive models have notoriously struggled. However, all experiments are limited to 110M–320M parameter models on OpenWebText, and the scaling analysis (extrapolated from ~10^20 FLOPs) suggesting uniform noise benefits grow with compute remains unverified at the 10^22 FLOPs scale where modern LLMs operate. An open question is whether the likelihood-vs-sample-quality gap (mask-only models win on PPL and benchmarks, hybrid models win on generation quality) persists or closes at larger scale, and whether the self-correction ability transfers to more complex generation tasks.
