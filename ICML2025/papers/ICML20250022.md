## Problem

While LLMs successfully perform in-context learning on scalar classification and language completion tasks, they consistently fail on structured sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) tasks beyond very short inputs. The paper identifies the root cause as the LLM's inability to discover the task-specific alignment between input and output tokens in-context, which is a prerequisite for induction heads to form informative keys for lookup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250022/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes k in-context input-output sequence pairs (with x drawn from a two-level PCFG and y generated from per-token PFAs conditioned on an alignment function parameterized by λ) followed by a test input sequence, and produces an auto-regressively generated output sequence. For ICA-Tune, the training input is a concatenated prompt of k+1 examples where the first k serve as in-context demonstrations, and the only trainable parameters are the Q, K, V attention matrices (via LoRA rank-16), leaving all other weights frozen. The alignment function A_τ(j) maps each y-token position to its relevant x-token position, with λ controlling the degree of monotonicity from identity (λ→∞) to near-random permutation (λ→0).

## Method

ICA-Tune fine-tunes a pre-trained LLM in ICL mode rather than standard per-example mode: each training batch is a single concatenated prompt of k+1 input-output pairs, and the loss is standard next-token prediction over the y tokens of all examples. Only the attention parameters (Q, K, V) are updated via LoRA, and in many cases fine-tuning a single middle layer suffices. The key mechanistic finding is that this procedure causes a three-stage induction circuit to emerge: lower layers establish inter-sequence boundaries and relative positions, middle layers (e.g., layers 14–16 in Llama-3-8B) learn the input-output alignment without any explicit alignment supervision, and higher layers (e.g., 17–19) form induction heads that use the aligned context as keys to look up matching y-tokens from in-context examples. Alignment accuracy rises abruptly before IC-Lookup accuracy jumps, confirming that alignment must form first. Compared to standard fine-tuning, ICA-Tune achieves near-perfect accuracy with ~50 examples versus 800+ for standard fine-tuning, and generalizes better to out-of-distribution inputs because it decomposes the task into alignment learning plus associative lookup rather than memorizing whole sequences.

## Results

On frontier LLMs (m=8, c=1), standard prompting yields only 20–51% accuracy (Llama-3.3-70B: 20%, GPT-4o: 36.25%, Claude-3.7-Sonnet: 51.25%), while pre-aligned prompting boosts accuracy to 82.5–100%, confirming alignment as the bottleneck. ICA-Tune achieves near-100% Seq2Seq accuracy with just 50 labeled in-context examples, whereas standard fine-tuning fails to reach that level even with 800 examples. ICA-Tune also converges faster and generalizes better to out-of-distribution (OOD) validation sets where input sequences are random permutations rather than CFG-sampled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between ICA-Tune (fine-tuning attention parameters in ICL mode) and standard per-example fine-tuning. ICA-Tune is dramatically more sample-efficient, reaching ~100% accuracy with 50 training examples while standard fine-tuning does not reach that level even with 800 examples. Mechanistically, ICA-Tune causes alignment accuracy to emerge abruptly in middle layers (layers 14–16 for Llama-3-8B) followed by IC-Lookup accuracy rising in higher layers (17–19), whereas standard fine-tuning produces no meaningful alignment signal in any layer. On OOD generalization (random-permutation inputs vs. CFG-sampled inputs), ICA-Tune converges to near-perfect accuracy on both sets, while standard fine-tuning shows a clear gap, indicating it overfits to surface x-sequence patterns rather than learning the decompositional alignment structure. The paper also contrasts standard prompting with oracle pre-aligned prompting across four frontier LLMs, showing that even the largest models (405B parameters) gain 60+ percentage points from alignment, ruling out model capacity as the limiting factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic ICL literature (induction heads, Olsson et al. 2022; Singh et al. 2024) from scalar classification and language-learning tasks to structured sequence-to-sequence tasks, where both input and output are variable-length token sequences. It draws on classical machine-translation alignment models (Brown et al. 1993; Dyer et al. 2013) to construct a formal synthetic task family with controllable alignment functions, and repurposes LoRA-based fine-tuning (Hu et al. 2021) in an ICL-mode objective. The key departure from prior ICL-on-Seq2Seq work (e.g., Roy et al. 2023 on semantic parsing) is the use of formal structured languages to isolate and diagnose the specific failure mode—alignment learning—rather than reporting aggregate accuracy on real datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unarticulated bottleneck for LLM ICL: the inability to discover task-specific input-output alignments in-context, which explains why Seq2Seq ICL degrades with sequence length even at frontier scale. ICA-Tune offers a practical, sample-efficient adaptation method that decomposes the problem into alignment learning (middle layers) and associative lookup (higher layers), with better OOD generalization than standard fine-tuning. Open questions include whether causal transformers with fixed embedding size can express arbitrary alignment learning at all (the paper conjectures not), whether pre-training objectives can be redesigned to support alignment ICL, and whether these findings transfer to real-world Seq2Seq tasks like translation or Text2SQL.
