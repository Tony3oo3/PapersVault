## Problem

LoRA-based fine-tuning underperforms full fine-tuning due to (1) static SVD initialization that cannot adaptively select relevant pre-trained knowledge segments per input, and (2) unaligned optimization where the low-rank constraint creates large gradient gaps, a problem exacerbated in LoRA MoE architectures where total rank is split among experts and SVD-based initialization introduces weight misalignment never addressed by prior zero-initialization methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained weight matrices (ViT-B/32 for 7 image classification datasets, LLaMA2-7B for dialogue/math/code generation, RoBERTa-large for 7 GLUE tasks), decomposing each target weight via SVD and distributing rank-r segments across E experts. At inference, the model consumes standard RGB images or text prompts and produces class labels, generated text, or entailment scores, with the MoE router dynamically selecting top-k experts per token to determine which SVD segment's prior is activated.

## Method

GOAT initializes each of E LoRA MoE experts with a distinct SVD segment of the pre-trained weight W_0 (B_0^i = √(1/sρ)·U_i·Σ_i^{1/2}, A_0^i = √(1/sρ)·Σ_i^{1/2}·V_i^T), letting the router adaptively select task-relevant priors per input. To resolve the weight misalignment introduced by non-zero SVD initialization, a residual term W_res = (s/E)ΣB_0^i A_0^i is subtracted so the equivalent weight equals W_0 at step zero. For gradient alignment, the paper derives a closed-form optimal scaling factor s = √(3nη/r) that makes the equivalent gradient of each low-rank expert approximate the full-rank counterpart's gradient, enabling LoRA MoE to track an upcycled full-fine-tuned MoE without modifying the architecture or training algorithm.

## Results

Across 25 datasets spanning 4 domains, GOAT achieves 81.49% average accuracy on image classification (99.07% of Full FT's 82.25%), 89.76% on NLU (surpassing Full FT's 89.47%), 82.73% on commonsense reasoning (beating ChatGPT by 7.42%), and 60.20 on GSM8K / 6.01 on MT-Bench for NLG. GOAT uses only 2.24% of parameters (vs. 100% for Full FT) while outperforming all LoRA and LoRA-MoE baselines. On NLU, GOAT narrows the gap with Full FT MoE to just 0.1% (89.76 vs. 89.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAT outperforms all single-LoRA baselines (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, rsLoRA, LoRA-Dash, NEAT, KaSA) and all LoRA-MoE baselines (MoLoRA, AdaMoLE, HydraLoRA) across all four domains. On image classification, GOAT (81.49) beats MoLoRA (80.26) by 1.23% and HydraLoRA (79.58) by 1.91%, while using the same 2.24% parameter budget as MoLoRA. On NLU, GOAT (89.76) surpasses Full FT (89.47) and beats MiLoRA (89.51) and MoLoRA (89.52), with only a 0.14% gap to Full FT MoE (89.90). On commonsense reasoning, GOAT (82.73) exceeds KaSA (81.53) by 1.20% and HydraLoRA (81.12) by 1.61%, and beats ChatGPT (77.01) by 5.72%. On NLG, GOAT achieves the best MT-Bench (6.01 vs. Full FT's 5.56) and GSM8K (60.20 vs. 59.36), though it trails Full FT on HumanEval (25.61 vs. 35.31), likely due to the inherent low-rank constraint on code generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAT sits at the intersection of two PEFT research threads: SVD-based initialization (PiSSA, MiLoRA, KaSA) and LoRA MoE architectures (MoLoRA, AdaMoLE, HydraLoRA). It departs from prior SVD methods by rejecting the static choice of principal or minor singular values in favor of an adaptive, input-conditioned selection via MoE routing over all SVD segments. It departs from prior LoRA MoE work by introducing SVD-structured initialization (rather than zero initialization) and deriving a theoretical scaling factor that aligns the low-rank expert gradients with those of an upcycled full-rank MoE, a connection not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple scaling factor (s = √(3nη/r)) combined with SVD-segmented expert initialization can close most of the LoRA-to-Full-FT gap without architectural changes, which is broadly applicable to any LoRA MoE setup. However, the theoretical scaling is derived under zero-initialization assumptions and only approximated for SVD initialization, and the alignment target is an upcycled MoE rather than true full-rank fine-tuning, leaving a residual gap. Open questions include whether the scaling generalizes to adaptive optimizers (Adam, AdamW), whether the SVD segment partition is truly input-adaptive or effectively a fixed multi-expert ensemble, and whether the approach extends to non-linear or multi-layer MoE configurations.
