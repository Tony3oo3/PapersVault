## Problem

While single-layer linear attention can be reinterpreted as a dynamic VAR model, existing multi-layer Transformer architectures introduce structural mismatches with the autoregressive forecasting objective—including mismatched loss targets, inconsistent residual streams, and unbalanced observation weighting—that obscure the underlying VAR structure and degrade both interpretability and generalization for time series forecasting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series of length L_I with C channels, partitioned into non-overlapping patches of size L_P. A distinctive ARX tokenization strategy creates two tokens per patch per series: an autoregressive token (projected from the target series' patch) and an exogenous token (a linear mix of all other series' patches), interleaved along the sequence dimension so each exogenous token precedes its corresponding target token. The output is the next L_P steps for each of the C series, projected back from the hidden dimension via a learned output matrix.

## Method

SAMoVAR reorganizes the standard linear Transformer by (1) removing the key projection matrix (replacing W_k with identity) to prevent numerical instability in multi-layer weight compositions, (2) computing query and value vectors for every layer directly from the original first-layer input rather than from intermediate layer outputs, with RMSNorm applied to control path magnitudes, and (3) aggregating outputs from all l attention layers to form a 'mixture of VAR' where the final weight at each lag is a sum of temporal influence paths with up to l−1 intermediate timesteps. A structural VAR component is added via a learnable LU-factorized invertible matrix D applied as a shared output projection across all layers. The MLP layers are placed before the attention stack (rather than interleaved) to learn optimal VAR observation representations, and a unified residual shortcut across all attention layers stabilizes training while preserving the VAR recurrence structure.

## Results

SAMoVAR achieves the best average rank (1.41) and 29 top-1 results out of 48 total (12 datasets × 4 prediction lengths) across all multivariate TSF benchmarks. On datasets with stable long-term patterns, improvements are particularly large: on PEMS04, SAMoVAR reaches 0.102 MSE versus 0.184 for iTransformer (44% reduction) and 0.256 for PatchTST; on Solar, it achieves 0.184 MSE versus 0.202 for DLinear. The closest competitor, CATS, achieves an average rank of 2.86 with 9 top-1 results, while the vanilla encoder Transformer (EncFormer) ranks last at 8.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMoVAR outperforms all five external baselines on the majority of datasets. Against CATS (the strongest competitor, AvgRank 2.86), SAMoVAR wins on 10 of 12 datasets, with the largest gaps on PEMS04 (0.102 vs 0.184 MSE, 44% lower) and PEMS08 (0.234 vs 0.359, 35% lower); CATS edges ahead only on ETTh2 (0.320 vs 0.324) and Traffic (0.385 vs 0.391). Against iTransformer (AvgRank 5.43), SAMoVAR wins on all 12 datasets, with particularly large margins on PEMS03 (0.150 vs 0.212) and PEMS04 (0.102 vs 0.171). FITS (AvgRank 5.20) and PatchTST (AvgRank 4.00) are consistently outperformed, with SAMoVAR achieving 0.184 vs 0.209 (FITS) and 0.184 vs 0.202 (PatchTST) on Solar. DLinear (AvgRank 5.82) and the encoder-only EncFormer (AvgRank 8.30) are the weakest baselines, with Encformer showing especially poor results on ETT and PEMS datasets (e.g., 0.906 MSE on ETTh1 vs SAMoVAR's 0.401). The paper's own LinTrans baseline (standard linear Transformer, AvgRank 3.41) is also consistently outperformed, confirming that the structural VAR alignment provides meaningful gains over unaligned linear attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the linear attention mechanism (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) through the lens of Vector Autoregression (Stock & Watson, 2001), going beyond the existing RNN and Fast Weight Programming perspectives to show that a single linear attention layer is inherently a dynamic VAR model. It then identifies specific structural misalignments in standard multi-layer Transformers (mismatched losses, inconsistent residual streams, unbalanced observation weighting) that break this VAR structure, and proposes SAMoVAR as an architectural fix that reorganizes MLP and attention layers with a key shortcut and no key projection. The ARX tokenization strategy combines PatchTST-style patching (Nie et al., 2022) with the VARX exogenous-variable formulation from CATS (Lu et al., 2024b), treating each series autoregressively while using other series as exogenous inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly aligning the Transformer architecture with the VAR generative process, SAMoVAR offers interpretable dynamic VAR weights and temporal influence paths that can be directly inspected, which is valuable for applied domains like economics and climate science where model transparency matters. The >30% MSE improvement on datasets with stable long-term patterns (Solar, PEMS) suggests that respecting the underlying data-generating process yields substantial gains over purely data-driven attention. Key open questions include whether SAMoVAR scales to foundation-model-level TSF tasks and whether the learned dynamic VAR structure transfers to general sequence modeling beyond time series, both of which the authors acknowledge as untested.
