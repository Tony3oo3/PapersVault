## Problem

Constrained Correlation Clustering, which adds must-link and cannot-link hard constraints to the standard problem, has a best-known approximation factor of 3, while its unconstrained counterpart has recently broken the 2 barrier. The paper shows that achieving a better-than-2 approximation for the constrained version reduces to efficiently solving the (exponentially large) Constrained Cluster LP, and provides a 1.92+ε approximation algorithm conditional on such an LP solver.

## Input / Output

The input is a complete unweighted undirected graph with edges labeled plus or minus, together with sets F (must-link pairs that must share a cluster) and H (cannot-link pairs that must be separated). The output is a valid vertex partition satisfying all hard constraints, with cost at most (1.92+ε)·OPT. The Constrained Cluster LP has a variable z_C for every non-empty subset C⊆V (exponentially many), and the algorithm assumes access to a solution with at most poly(n,1/ε) non-zero z_C values, running in n^{poly(1/ε)} time.

## Method

The algorithm combines LP-guided local search with a pivoting (mixing) procedure. It first runs a standard local search over clusters from the LP solution to obtain L, then a second local search with a penalty term on plus-edges already paid by L to obtain a structurally different L'. A sampling-based rounding of the LP solution (from Cao et al., 2024a) produces a third clustering I that closely mimics the fractional optimum. The key insight is that, unlike in the unconstrained setting where the optimal clustering is only used in the analysis, here the fractional optimum is algorithmically available, so the mixing step can be executed as part of the algorithm. A pivoting procedure assigns each node a triple label (cluster in L, cluster in L', cluster in I) and builds a new clustering P by grouping nodes sharing at least two label coordinates, guaranteeing that P pays for a minus edge only if at least one of the three clusterings does, and for a plus edge only if at least two do. The final output is the best of L, L', and P, yielding the 1.92+ε guarantee.

## Results

The paper's main result is a conditional theoretical guarantee: assuming polynomial-time solvability of the Constrained Cluster LP, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for Constrained Correlation Clustering with approximation factor 1.92+ε (Theorem 4.18). This improves upon the previous best-known approximation of 3 (van Zuylen & Williamson, 2009) for the same problem. The algorithm combines a local search guided by the LP solution with a penalized second local search and a pivoting/mixing procedure, achieving the better-than-2 barrier for the first time in the constrained setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or dataset evaluations, so there are no head-to-head numerical comparisons. In terms of approximation guarantees, the paper's 1.92+ε result for Constrained Correlation Clustering improves upon the prior state-of-the-art 3-approximation by van Zuylen and Williamson (2009). The approach builds on two key prior works: the Cluster LP and sampling-based rounding from Cao et al. (STOC 2024), which achieves 1.437 for unconstrained Correlation Clustering, and the two-phase local search with pivoting from Cohen-Addad et al. (STOC 2024), which achieves 1.847 for the unconstrained case. The paper's contribution is showing that these two techniques can be combined in the constrained setting, where the LP solution (rather than the true optimal) guides the local search, and the mixing argument must account for a fractional clustering rather than an integral one.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper unifies two previously separate approaches for unconstrained Correlation Clustering — the Cluster LP (Cao et al., STOC 2024) and the local-search/pivoting technique (Cohen-Addad et al., STOC 2024) — into a single framework for the constrained variant. The key conceptual departure is showing that a fractional LP solution, rather than the true optimal clustering, can guide the local search and enable the mixing argument to work algorithmically rather than only in the analysis. The constrained setting had previously been addressed only by van Zuylen & Williamson's 3-approximation, and the paper reduces the problem of breaking the 2 barrier to solving the (exponentially large) Constrained Cluster LP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is conditional: it proves that an efficient solver for the Constrained Cluster LP would immediately yield a 1.92-approximation, but does not provide such a solver. The central open question is whether this LP admits a polynomial-time solution, analogous to the unconstrained Cluster LP which has been solved in sublinear time. The inapproximability landscape for the constrained setting also remains unclear, and it is unknown whether stronger hardness results exist beyond those inherited from unconstrained Correlation Clustering.
