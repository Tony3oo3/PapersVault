## Problem

Existing graph invariant learning methods for OOD generalization rely on predefined or automatically generated environment labels to identify variant subgraphs, which are impractical or inaccurate for real-world graph data with severe bias. This paper tackles the unexplored problem of explicitly estimating the degree of spurious correlations from variant subgraphs without any environment annotations, and leveraging that estimation to capture truly invariant subgraphs for robust OOD predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single graph G = (X, A) with node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A as input, and outputs a graph-level class label. Internally, a learnable edge mask matrix M (values in (0,1) via Sigmoid) soft-decomposes the graph into an invariant subgraph G_I = (X, M⊙A) and a variant subgraph G_V = (X, (1−M)⊙A); the final prediction is made solely from the invariant subgraph, while the variant subgraph is used only to estimate spurious correlation strength for reweighting during training.

## Method

VIVACE consists of three mutually promoted modules. First, a GNN-based invariant subgraph generator produces a learnable edge mask that disentangles invariant and variant subgraphs in a global, dataset-shared manner. Second, a variant subgraph contrastive estimation module trains a variant encoder via self-supervised contrastive learning (InfoNCE-style) on augmented variant subgraphs, augmented with a variance regularizer over per-label-group contrastive losses that forces the encoder to ignore label-invariant patterns and capture only environment-variant information; a variant classifier is then trained with generalized cross-entropy (GCE) to fit the spurious correlations. Third, an inverse propensity weighting (IPW) module computes the propensity score p(y|G_V) from the ratio of variant and invariant prediction losses, and reweights the invariant subgraph cross-entropy loss so that samples with strong spurious correlation are down-weighted. The overall objective jointly optimizes all three losses, and a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that minimizing the variance term ensures the edge mask recovers the ground-truth invariant/variant decomposition.

## Results

VIVACE achieves the best performance across all five benchmark datasets. On the biased graph datasets (CMNIST, CFashion, CKuzushiji) with spurious correlation strength r=0.9, VIVACE attains 79.46%, 65.38%, and 48.15% accuracy respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline DisC by 1.32%, 0.05%, and 0.02%. On the OGB molecular datasets, VIVACE reaches 62.15% ROC-AUC on MOLSIDER (vs. 60.21% for DiffPool) and 78.11% ROC-AUC on MOLHIV (vs. 77.05% for DIR), demonstrating consistent gains under scaffold-based distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIVACE outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and bias level. The closest competitor is DisC (Fan et al., 2022), a disentangled causal substructure method; VIVACE edges ahead by 0.11–1.32% accuracy on the biased datasets (e.g., 79.46% vs. 78.14% on CMNIST r=0.9) and by 1.94% ROC-AUC on MOLSIDER (62.15% vs. 59.31%). DIR (Wu et al., 2022c), which relies on interventional environment generation, collapses to ~10% accuracy on all biased datasets, confirming its failure under severe bias. LDD (Lee et al., 2021) and the GNN backbones GCN/GIN show moderate performance but are consistently 5–25 points below VIVACE on the biased benchmarks. On the OGB molecular datasets where scaffold-based splits create natural distribution shifts, VIVACE achieves the top ROC-AUC on both MOLSIDER (62.15%) and MOLHIV (78.11%), surpassing the next-best methods (DiffPool at 60.21% and DIR at 77.05%) by 1.94 and 1.06 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIVACE extends the graph invariant subgraph learning line initiated by DisC (Fan et al., NeurIPS 2022) and DIR (Wu et al., ICLR 2022), which decompose input graphs into invariant and variant subgraphs via learnable edge masks. The key departure is that prior methods require predefined or automatically generated environment labels to identify variant patterns, whereas VIVACE replaces this dependency with a self-supervised contrastive objective whose variance term across label groups enforces that the variant encoder captures only environment-related information. The inverse propensity weighting mechanism for reweighting invariant predictions is adapted from causal inference literature (Qi et al., ECCV 2022; Jung et al., NeurIPS 2020), and the GCE loss for fitting spurious correlations follows Fan et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in graph OOD generalization: environment labels are typically unavailable or inaccurate in real-world biased datasets, yet most invariant learning methods depend on them. The variance contrastive estimation provides a label-free mechanism to estimate spurious correlation strength, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the optimal generator recovers ground-truth invariant/variant subgraphs strengthens the method's credibility. However, improvements over the strongest baseline (DisC) are modest (1–3% across datasets), and the method is limited to static, homogenous graphs, leaving open questions about extension to dynamic or heterogeneous graph settings and to scenarios where the invariance/sufficiency assumptions are only approximately satisfied.
