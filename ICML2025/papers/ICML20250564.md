## Problem

Existing EDL-based trusted multi-view classification methods rely on Dempster-Shafer theory for fusion, which is overly sensitive to conflicting views (noise, misalignment) and underestimates uncertainty for such instances because uncertainty depends only on total evidence and category count, ignoring inherent conflicts between belief masses. This leads to both misclassification and unreliable uncertainty estimates in real-world multi-view scenarios where views are often noisy or unaligned.

## Input / Output

The method accepts V views per sample (feature vectors of varying dimensionality or grayscale images, e.g., 3–6 views across 8 datasets) and produces a predicted class label along with a scalar decision uncertainty in [0,1]. The network logits are Lp-normalized (p=3) and passed through ReLU to yield fuzzy memberships in [0,1] per category; the final uncertainty is computed as the normalized Shannon entropy of the fused category credibility vector, so higher values indicate less reliable predictions.

## Method

FUML models classifier outputs as fuzzy memberships (possibility measure) and introduces category credibility as the arithmetic mean of possibility and necessity (1 minus the maximum membership of all other categories), capturing both within-class and between-class information. A tailored category credibility learning loss avoids the over-optimization trap of standard cross-entropy by computing necessity differently for matched vs. unmatched categories during training. For multi-view fusion, the Dual-reliable Multi-view Fusion (DRF) strategy assigns each view a weight proportional to exp((1−uncertainty)(1−conflict)), where conflict is measured by the mean cosine dissimilarity of its membership vector against all other views, enabling a single global fusion step that simultaneously down-weights noisy (high-uncertainty) and misaligned (high-conflict) views. Theoretical propositions prove that fusing a clean view with a conflicting view always increases the fused uncertainty relative to the clean view alone, guaranteeing more accurate uncertainty estimation under conflict.

## Results

On normal test sets, FUML achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all 8 datasets, with improvements of 0.20%–1.62% over the best baseline (e.g., 99.78% on Leaves100, 99.76% on MSRC-V1, 79.41% on Scene15). On conflicting test sets, the advantage widens substantially: FUML gains 4.83% on Scene15, 7.31% on LandUse, and 14.60% on Leaves100 over the second-best method, demonstrating superior robustness to noisy and misaligned views. For uncertainty estimation, FUML achieves an FPR95 of 0.510 on Fashion and 0.886 on LandUse, compared to 0.930/0.964 (ETMC) and 0.967/0.945 (ECML), confirming more accurate out-of-distribution detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUML outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset in both normal and conflicting settings. On normal test sets, the closest competitors are DCP-CG (best untrusted, e.g., 99.00% on HW, 98.19% on Leaves) and UIMC (best trusted on MSRC at 98.81%), with FUML leading by 0.20–1.62 percentage points. On conflicting test sets, the gap widens dramatically: ECML (the SOTA trusted method for conflicting views) drops from 94.71% to 84.00% on PIE and from 92.53% to 74.88% on Leaves, while FUML retains 96.18% and 94.44% respectively. The largest relative gains appear on LandUse (+7.31% over CCML's 52.48%) and Leaves (+14.60% over UIMC's 79.84%), where EDL-based methods severely underestimate uncertainty for conflicting instances. Untrusted methods (DFTMC, DCP, QMF, PDF) degrade most severely under conflict (e.g., DFTMC drops to 1.10% on Leaves), confirming that FUML's dual-reliable fusion and entropy-based uncertainty estimation provide both accuracy and reliability advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper explicitly departs from the dominant Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) + Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST) paradigm that underpins prior trusted multi-view classification methods (TMC, ETMC, UIMC, ECML, TMNR, CCML). Rather than modeling network outputs as Dirichlet evidence and fusing via sequential D-S combination, FUML re-frames the problem through Fuzzy Set Theory, treating outputs as fuzzy memberships with both possibility and necessity measures. The Dual-reliable Multi-view Fusion (DRF) strategy also contrasts with prior EDL-based fusion by performing a single global weighted aggregation informed by both view-specific uncertainty and inter-view conflict, rather than sequential pairwise evidence combination. The category credibility learning loss is a novel training objective designed to avoid the over-optimization of necessity that would arise from naively applying cross-entropy to credibility values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode of EDL-based TMVC: uncertainty is systematically underestimated for conflicting multi-view instances (FPR95 of 0.93–0.97 vs. 0.51–0.89 for FUML), which is critical for safety-sensitive deployments where the system must flag its own unreliability. The DRF strategy offers a practical, lightweight mechanism for robust multi-view fusion that practitioners can adopt without changing backbone architectures. Open questions include whether the fuzzy-set framing scales to very high-dimensional or continuous-output settings, how the method handles arbitrary view missing (which UIMC addresses but FUML does not), and whether the Lp-normalization + ReLU membership construction is optimal compared to learned membership functions. The paper also acknowledges data bias in open environments as a residual limitation.
