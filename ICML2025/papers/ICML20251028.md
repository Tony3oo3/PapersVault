## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for Vision-Language Models face a fundamental trade-off: prompt optimizers (e.g., TPT) achieve strong performance but require expensive full-network backpropagation and a secondary forward pass, while training-free visual optimizers (e.g., MTA) are efficient but suffer from unstable gains that degrade on unseen classes. No prior method simultaneously delivers state-of-the-art accuracy and lightweight inference for single-image TTA.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB test image, generates N augmented views via random cropping (N=8 for cross-dataset, N=64 for domain generalization), and additionally extracts spatial patch features (wh×d) from the ViT encoder. The output is a refined class probability distribution computed as the element-wise sum of CLIP's original logits, a global bias vector B_g ∈ R^{1×C}, and a spatial bias vector B_s ∈ R^{1×C}, both zero-initialized and learned in 5 gradient steps at the logit level. No secondary forward pass through the VLM is required after bias optimization.

## Method

GS-Bias introduces two zero-initialized, learnable bias vectors added directly to CLIP's output logits, bypassing full-network backpropagation. The global bias B_g is optimized by minimizing the entropy of the mean logit distribution across augmented views (with a confidence filter ρ), capturing image-level semantic consistency. The spatial bias B_s is optimized by first computing a category-aware relevance map M over all spatial patches, selecting the top-K most informative regions via Top-K selection, and then minimizing the entropy of the mean spatial-region logits over those K regions. Both biases are updated via lightweight gradient descent (learning rates α and β) solely at the logit output stage, and the final prediction is p_CLIP(y|x) + B_g + B_s. This design eliminates the need for prompt re-encoding, full ViT/text-encoder backpropagation, and a second inference pass, reducing memory to ~6.5% of TPT while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Results

On the 10-dataset Cross-Datasets Generalization benchmark (ViT-B/16), GS-Bias with prompt ensembling (BS=8) achieves 67.03% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming all baselines including MTA+E. (65.95%), DiffTPT (65.47%), and TPT (64.80%). On the Domain Generalization benchmark (ImageNet + 4 OOD variants), GS-Bias+E. reaches 64.52% average and 63.01% OOD average accuracy, surpassing MTA+E. (64.08% / 62.53%) and DiffTPT (62.28% / 60.52%). In terms of efficiency on ImageNet, GS-Bias+E. runs at 12.34 FPS with 1308 MiB memory, approximately 10× faster than TPT (1.38 FPS, 19997 MiB) while achieving higher accuracy (70.57% vs. 68.48%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-Bias+E. (BS=8) outperforms all six baselines on both benchmarks. Against the prompt-optimizer TPT (BS=64), GS-Bias gains +2.23% average accuracy on cross-datasets and +2.72% OOD accuracy on domain generalization, while using only 6.5% of TPT's GPU memory (1308 vs. 19997 MiB) and running ~10× faster (12.34 vs. 1.38 FPS). Against the diffusion-augmented DiffTPT (BS=128), GS-Bias leads by +1.56% on cross-datasets and +2.49% OOD on domain generalization, despite using 16× fewer augmented views. Against the visual-optimizer MTA+E. (BS=64), GS-Bias achieves +1.08% on cross-datasets and +0.48% OOD on domain generalization at nearly identical inference speed (12.34 vs. 12.55 FPS). Against the few-shot prompt-tuning methods CoOp and CoCoOp (trained on ImageNet with 16 views/class), GS-Bias improves by +3.15% and +2.40% on cross-datasets respectively, and by +3.73% and +3.10% in OOD accuracy on domain generalization, all without requiring any downstream training data. Notably, GS-Bias is the only method that consistently outperforms zero-shot CLIP+E. across all 10 cross-dataset benchmarks, whereas TPT degrades on Aircraft and MTA degrades on Pets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-Bias directly builds on TPT's entropy-minimization-over-augmented-views framework but relocates the optimization from the text-prompt input side to the logit output side, replacing learned prompt vectors with two lightweight additive biases (global and spatial). It also positions itself against the training-free visual optimizer MTA, which refines internal visual features via MeanShift but degrades on unseen classes. The spatial bias component draws on spatial-feature prompt tuning (GALOP, LoCoOp) but is the first to exploit region-level spatial consistency in a TTA setting, and the global bias is a direct logit-level analogue of TPT's prompt-level entropy minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is the 10× speedup and 6.5% memory footprint relative to TPT while matching or exceeding its accuracy, making TTA viable in memory-constrained deployment scenarios (edge devices, real-time systems). The spatial bias's consistent ~1% gain on cross-dataset benchmarks suggests that region-level semantic coherence is an underexploited signal in TTA. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameters (α, β, K) can be made sample-adaptive, and whether the logit-level optimization paradigm generalizes to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP.
