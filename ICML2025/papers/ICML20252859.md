## Problem

Safe offline RL algorithms struggle to guarantee safety constraints during testing because the infinite combinations of reward-related and cost-related states lead to out-of-distribution (OOD) observations that were never seen in the training dataset. Existing methods perform both safety assessment and decision-making on global observations, which limits generalization to unfamiliar state configurations. This paper asks whether decoupling observations into reward- and cost-specific representations can improve safety assessment accuracy and policy generalization without requiring model-based estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scalar state observation vector (e.g., 60 dimensions in Safety-Gymnasium, where dimensions encode agent self-status, reward-relevant sensor readings, and cost-relevant obstacle/destination distances) and produces a single action. The output is structured as a three-way safety routing: the cost-related value functions V_h^low and V_h^up determine whether the state is absolutely safe (deploy reward policy π_r), borderline (deploy tradeoff policy π_to), or unsafe (deploy cost policy π_h), and the final action is selected as the argmin of Q_h among a batch of diffusion-sampled candidates. Each scalar observation dimension is first expanded into a vector via a fixed Gaussian Fourier Encoder before being processed by an attention-based state encoder.

## Method

SDQC decouples the global state into reward-related z_θr(s) and cost-related z_θh(s) representations learned via Q-supervised contrastive learning: states whose optimal Q* values are similar across all in-support actions are pulled together in representation space, while dissimilar states are pushed apart, using a soft similarity measure Γ(s,s̃)=exp(−d(s,s̃)/η) where d is the sup-norm Q* gap over behavior-policy-generated actions. Training proceeds in three phases: (1) a diffusion behavior cloner generates in-support action sets A_β^s for each state; (2) reward and cost value functions (via IQL upper expectile regression and safety Bellman lower/upper expectile regression, respectively) are jointly optimized with the contrastive representation loss; (3) three weighted-regression diffusion policies (π_r, π_h, π_to) are trained conditioned on the decoupled representations. The key theoretical result is that the Q*-irrelevance representation is provably coarser than bisimulation (higher conditional entropy H(s|z)) while preserving the optimal policy, which provides a formal generalization advantage over model-based abstraction methods.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark, SDQC achieves the lowest normalized cost across both Safety-Gymnasium (0.17) and Bullet-Safety-Gym (0.08), substantially outperforming the closest competitor FISOR (1.25 and 0.10 respectively). SDQC achieves zero or near-zero cost violations in the majority of individual tasks (e.g., 0.00 on PointPush1, CarGoal2, CarPush1, AntRun, BallRun, CarCircle, AntCircle, BallCircle), while maintaining competitive normalized rewards (0.26 Safety-Gym avg, 0.39 Bullet-Gym avg). In generalization tests (Figure 3), SDQC is the only algorithm that guarantees no increase in cost when tested in unseen environments, with only a slight reward decay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDQC dominates on the safety metric (normalized cost) across all six baselines: it reduces Safety-Gymnasium average cost from 9.17 (BCQ-Lag), 9.05 (CPQ), 5.66 (COptiDICE), 4.93 (CDT), 2.10 (TREBI), and 1.25 (FISOR) down to 0.17, and Bullet-Gym average cost from 10.63, 5.28, 7.64, 2.09, 2.44, and 0.10 down to 0.08. The closest competitor, FISOR (the only other method targeting hard constraints), still incurs 7× higher cost on Safety-Gymnasium (1.25 vs 0.17) and slightly higher cost on Bullet-Gym (0.10 vs 0.08). On reward, SDQC trades off slightly against unsafe baselines: CDT achieves higher Safety-Gym reward (0.46 vs 0.26) and Bullet-Gym reward (0.63 vs 0.39), but at the cost of 4.93 and 2.09 normalized cost respectively (both unsafe). Among safe methods (cost < 1), SDQC matches or exceeds FISOR on reward in Bullet-Gym (0.39 vs 0.39) while achieving lower cost, and is competitive on Safety-Gym (0.26 vs 0.28) with dramatically lower cost. In generalization tests, all baselines show substantial cost increases or reward drops when tested in unseen environments, whereas SDQC is the only method that maintains zero cost increase with only slight reward decay.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDQC sits at the intersection of three research threads: in-sample offline RL (IQL's expectile regression for in-support value estimation), Hamilton-Jacobi reachability-based safety assessment (Fisac et al. 2019; FISOR/Zheng et al. 2024), and contrastive representation learning for RL (Agarwal et al. 2021). The key departure from FISOR, the closest prior work, is the introduction of state decoupling: rather than performing safety assessment and decision-making on the full observation, SDQC learns separate reward-related and cost-related representations via Q-supervised contrastive learning, then routes decisions through three specialized diffusion policies based on the safety verdict. Theoretically, the paper extends the bisimulation-vs-Q-irrelevance comparison from Givan et al. (2003) to infinite-horizon MDPs and the safety Bellman operator, proving that Q-based representations are strictly coarser while preserving optimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD problem during testing is a fundamental barrier to deploying safe offline RL in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, robot control), and SDQC's state-decoupling approach offers a principled mechanism to improve generalization without requiring online interaction. The generalization experiments—where SDQC is the only algorithm maintaining zero cost increase in unseen environments—are practically significant for real-world deployment where training cannot cover all sensor configurations. Open questions include whether the three-phase training pipeline (behavior cloner → joint value/representation optimization → policy extraction) scales to higher-dimensional or partially observable settings, whether the contrastive loss's reliance on a pre-trained generative model for in-support action sampling introduces additional failure modes, and whether the near-zero-violation guarantee holds under distributional shift beyond the tested Safety-Gymnasium/Bullet-Safety-Gym domains.
