## Problem

Zero-shot transfer RL lacks performance guarantees for the transferred policy, risking overly optimistic and potentially harmful actions in the target domain, and existing multi-domain methods (e.g., domain randomization, multi-task learning) cannot identify or down-weight dissimilar source domains, leading to negative transfer. No prior framework provides a provable lower bound on target performance while simultaneously mitigating the adverse effects of irrelevant source domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K source domain transition kernels P_k, a known upper bound Γ on the distributional distance D(P_0, P_k) (e.g., total variation or Wasserstein), and state observations from each local domain. It outputs a target-domain policy π* and a global Q-table, with the guarantee that the proxy value is a conservative lower bound on true target performance. In the distributed setting, local domains share only updated Q-table entries (not raw trajectories or MDP parameters) with a central server, and aggregation occurs every E local steps to reduce communication.

## Method

The paper introduces a pessimism principle for transfer learning: construct a conservative proxy f(π) ≤ V_{P_0}^π via robust RL, then transfer its optimal policy, yielding a sub-optimality gap bounded by the pessimism level ‖ζ‖. Two concrete proxies are built from (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty sets centered at each source kernel: (1) an Averaged Operator (AO) proxy whose Bellman operator is the arithmetic mean of local robust Bellman operators, and (2) a Minimal Pessimism (MP) proxy whose operator takes the pointwise maximum over local robust operators, which automatically up-weights the most informative source domain and avoids negative transfer. For distributed optimization, MDTL-Avg performs local stochastic updates followed by average aggregation, while MDTL-Max uses a multi-level Monte Carlo scheme to obtain an unbiased estimate of the max aggregation despite stochastic local operators. Both algorithms achieve partial linear speedup in K with convergence rate Õ(1/(TK) + (E−1)Γ/T), and the framework extends to model-free and function-approximation settings.

## Results

On the Recycling Robot problem, MDTL-Avg and MDTL-Max achieve a 195.03% performance improvement over non-robust baselines in the target domain and maintain a 15.95% improvement under model uncertainty (R_test=0.1). On the HPC Cluster Management problem, the methods achieve a 183.28% improvement in the target domain and 11.05% under uncertainty. On the real-world Dynamic Vehicle Routing Problem, MDTL-Max achieves the best Route Smoothness (52.46) and Route Stability (0.68), while both proposed methods tie for the lowest Route Distance (4.01), outperforming Non-robust DR (4.53) and Non-robust Single-learn (5.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MDTL-Avg and MDTL-Max against two external robust RL baselines: Adversarial Robust RL (Tessler et al., 2019) and Distributionally Robust RL (Panaganti & Kalathil, 2022). On the Recycling Robot problem, both proposed methods dominate across all five uncertainty levels: at R_test=0.01, MDTL achieves 134.45 versus 61.06 (Adversarial) and 53.86 (Distributionally Robust), a more than 2× advantage; even at the highest uncertainty (R_test=0.1), MDTL maintains 55.17 versus 49.58 and 50.70. The advantage is most pronounced at low uncertainty, where the transfer-based pessimistic proxy leverages source domain information more effectively than standalone robust RL. In addition, the paper compares against non-robust DR and non-robust maximal aggregation baselines, showing that the pessimism principle yields a 195.03% improvement in the target domain and avoids the overly optimistic (and potentially harmful) policies produced by non-robust methods. On the DVRP real-world benchmark, MDTL-Max outperforms Non-robust DR by 11.5% in Route Distance and 23.6% in Route Stability, confirming the practical advantage of the pessimistic framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pessimism principle from offline RL (Jin et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2022) into the transfer learning setting, using the robust MDP framework (Iyengar, 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui, 2004) as the mechanism for constructing conservative proxies of target-domain performance. It departs from domain randomization and multi-task learning, which optimize average or proximal objectives without lower-bound guarantees, by instead optimizing a pessimistic proxy whose optimality gap is provably bounded by the level of pessimism. The distributed algorithm design extends federated learning (Wang et al., 2024a) to the non-linear robust Bellman operator setting, and the multi-level Monte Carlo technique (Blanchet & Glynn, 2015) is adapted to achieve unbiased max-aggregation for the minimal pessimism variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide a provable lower-bound guarantee on zero-shot transfer RL performance, addressing a fundamental gap in prior methods that offer no connection between the optimized proxy and actual target-domain outcomes. The monotonic improvement property (less pessimism → smaller sub-optimality gap) gives practitioners a principled knob for tuning conservativeness, and the minimal pessimism principle offers an elegant, automatic mechanism for mitigating negative transfer without explicit domain selection. Key limitations include the requirement of a known domain-distance upper bound Γ (which may be overly conservative if loose), the restriction to tabular MDPs in experiments, and the added sample complexity of the MLMC module. Open questions include scaling to continuous/high-dimensional state spaces, tightening uncertainty sets with limited target data, and practical estimation of domain distances in real deployments.
