## Problem

Deep neural networks trained with empirical risk minimization develop spurious bias by relying on non-causal correlations (e.g., backgrounds) rather than core features, degrading performance on data lacking those correlations. Existing mitigation methods operate at the sample level and require external group-label annotations of spurious correlations, which are costly to obtain and may not reflect the specific bias a particular model has developed. This paper asks whether a model can self-identify and mitigate its own spurious bias by directly intervening in its internal decision process, without any external annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained ERM model (frozen feature extractor plus linear classifier), a held-out validation set for identifying biased dimensions, and the original training set for last-layer retraining. It produces a debiased model whose last layer has been retrained with identified biased dimensions zeroed out, along with the explicit set of biased dimension indices. The validation data is consumed only to extract latent embeddings and binary prediction outcomes (correct/incorrect) per class; it is not used to update weights, making the procedure analogous to model selection rather than training.

## Method

NeuronTune identifies 'biased dimensions' in the model's latent embedding space by computing, for each dimension i and class y, the difference δ_i^y = Med(activations on misclassified samples) − Med(activations on correctly predicted samples); dimensions with δ_i^y > 0 are flagged as biased. The key insight, proven theoretically, is that δ_i^y ≈ −2μγ^T w_spu,i, so a positive score selects exactly those neurons whose spurious-component weight has the wrong sign (i.e., the model relies on the spurious feature even when the correlation breaks). The model is then debiased by retraining only the last linear layer on class-balanced training data while zeroing out activations on all identified biased dimensions, effectively forcing the classifier to discover robust decision rules from the remaining core dimensions. A spuriousness fitness score (SFit) is used for model selection across iterations, and the theoretical analysis proves the resulting model is closer to the unbiased optimum than the original ERM model.

## Results

On Waterbirds, NeuronTune (unsupervised) achieves 92.2% WGA and 94.4% average accuracy (gap 2.2%), compared to ERM's 72.6% WGA and 97.3% accuracy (gap 24.7%). On CelebA, it reaches 83.1% WGA with 92.0% accuracy (gap 8.9%) versus ERM's 47.2% WGA and 95.6% accuracy (gap 48.4%). On the text datasets, NeuronTune attains 72.1% WGA on MultiNLI and 82.4% WGA on CivilComments, substantially outperforming ERM (67.9% and 57.4% respectively). On ImageNet-A (out-of-distribution), NeuronTune achieves 37.3% accuracy, the best among all compared methods, with the smallest accuracy gap (56.4%) to ImageNet-9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the unsupervised setting (no group labels), NeuronTune outperforms the strongest baseline BAM on WGA across all four datasets: Waterbirds (92.2% vs. 89.1%), CelebA (83.1% vs. 80.1%), MultiNLI (72.1% vs. 70.8%), and CivilComments (82.4% vs. 79.3%), while also achieving smaller accuracy gaps. Against semi-supervised methods that exploit validation group labels, NeuronTune† (using half the validation set for tuning) matches or exceeds DFR† on CelebA (87.3% vs. 87.0% WGA, gap 3.0 vs. 5.6) and is competitive on Waterbirds (92.5% vs. 92.4% WGA), though SELF† retains a slight edge on Waterbirds WGA (93.0% vs. 92.5%). On the out-of-distribution ImageNet-A benchmark, NeuronTune achieves the highest accuracy (37.3%) among all 15 compared methods, surpassing LWBC (36.0%), CaaM (32.8%), and ReBias (29.6%), while maintaining the smallest ImageNet-9/ImageNet-A accuracy gap (56.4% vs. 58.0% for LWBC). The only notable trade-off is a slight drop in in-distribution accuracy on ImageNet-9 (93.7% vs. 94.0% for LWBC), consistent with the expected WGA–average-accuracy trade-off across spurious bias mitigation methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuronTune extends the last-layer retraining line of work (Kirichenko et al., 2023; Qiu et al., 2023) by shifting the intervention from the sample level to the neuron level. While prior methods reweight or retrain on samples identified via group labels or misclassification heuristics, NeuronTune directly probes a model's latent embedding space to identify dimensions whose activations are disproportionately associated with mispredictions, then suppresses those dimensions during retraining. The theoretical framework builds on the two-layer linear model and data generation process from Ye et al. (2023), extending it with a formal neuron-selection principle (Proposition 4.1) and a provable guarantee that the tuned model is closer to the unbiased optimum (Theorem 4.3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuronTune offers a practical, annotation-free post-hoc tool for improving worst-group accuracy, which is valuable in standard ERM settings where group labels are unavailable. However, the method is limited to last-layer tuning and cannot correct spurious features already entangled in the frozen feature extractor, and the mild assumption β^T w_core,i ≈ γ^T w_spu,i may not hold in all architectures. Open questions include how to optimally choose the identification and tuning datasets, whether the approach extends to multi-layer intervention, and whether the neuron-level perspective can be combined with representation-level debiasing for stronger gains.
