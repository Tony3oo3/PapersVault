## Problem

The first best-of-both-worlds algorithm for online CMDPs (Stradi et al., 2024b) requires full feedback over the entire environment and solves a convex program over occupancy measures at every episode, making it impractical for real-world applications where only trajectory-level feedback is available. This paper addresses both limitations by providing the first best-of-both-worlds algorithm for CMDPs under bandit feedback, using a policy-optimization-based primal-dual approach that avoids convex optimization entirely.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives only bandit feedback: at each episode, the learner observes rewards and constraint violations solely along the realized trajectory (state-action pairs visited), not over the full state-action space. It does not require prior knowledge of the Slater's parameter ρ, which measures constraint feasibility margin. The output is a per-state action distribution (policy) updated each episode, accompanied by theoretical guarantees: Õ(√T) regret and violation under stochastic constraints, and Õ(√T) violation plus a ρ/(ρ+H) fraction of optimal reward under adversarial constraints.

## Method

The algorithm PDB-PS employs a primal-dual structure where the primal uses FS-PODB (Fixed-Share Policy Optimization with Dilated Bonus) and the dual uses projected online gradient descent on Lagrangian multipliers. The key mechanism is the fixed-share update in the primal: each policy is a convex combination of the standard exponential-weight update and the uniform distribution, which guarantees the no-interval regret property for the first time in adversarial MDPs with bandit feedback. This no-interval regret for both primal and dual enables a proof that Lagrangian multipliers are automatically bounded (by Λ = 112mH²/ρ²) without requiring knowledge of ρ, via a contradiction argument comparing interval regret bounds. An episode-dependent learning rate η_t ∝ 1/√(T·Ξ_t), where Ξ_t tracks the maximum observed loss range, ensures the primal regret scales linearly (not quadratically) in the Lagrangian multipliers. The dilated bonus term, inherited from Luo et al. (2021), provides optimism in the face of uncertainty for transition estimation, while the (1+1/H) factor in the bonus recursion incentivizes exploration in deeper layers.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline guarantees of PDB-PS are: (i) under stochastic constraints with Slater's condition (ρ ≥ T^{-1/8}H√(112m)), Õ(√T) regret and Õ(√T) constraint violation; (ii) under adversarial constraints with Slater's condition, Õ(√T) violation and a ρ/(ρ+H) fraction of the optimal reward; (iii) when Slater's condition is not satisfied, the bounds degrade to Õ(T^{3/4}) regret and violation in both stochastic and adversarial settings. Crucially, the algorithm requires no knowledge of the Slater's parameter ρ and avoids solving convex programs at each episode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is to Stradi et al. (2024b), the first best-of-both-worlds algorithm for CMDPs. PDB-PS matches the theoretical guarantees of Stradi et al. (2024b) (Õ(√T) regret/violation in stochastic settings, Õ(√T) violation in adversarial settings) but improves on two critical practical axes: it operates under bandit feedback (observing only trajectory-level rewards/costs) rather than full feedback, and it uses a policy optimization approach that avoids solving convex programs over occupancy measures at each episode. Additionally, PDB-PS achieves a result not available in Stradi et al. (2024b): sublinear regret and violation (Õ(√T)) in the adversarial setting when using a weaker baseline that requires constraint satisfaction at every round rather than on average. The paper also introduces the first no-interval-regret algorithm for adversarial MDPs with bandit feedback (FS-PODB), which is a technical contribution of independent interest beyond the CMDP setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the best-of-both-worlds framework of Stradi et al. (2024b) from full feedback to bandit feedback in episodic CMDPs, while replacing the occupancy-measure-based primal optimization with a policy optimization approach. The primal algorithm (FS-PODB) builds on the dilated-bonus policy optimization of Luo et al. (2021) for adversarial MDPs, augmented with the fixed-share update of Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2012) to achieve the no-interval-regret property. A key departure from prior work is the use of a policy-based (rather than occupancy-measure-based) primal space, which makes the fixed-share convex combination well-defined and avoids solving convex programs at each episode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to achieve optimal regret and violation bounds for CMDPs under bandit feedback while handling both stochastic and adversarial constraints without requiring knowledge of the Slater's parameter ρ, removing a significant practical barrier. The no-interval-reget guarantee for adversarial MDPs with bandit feedback resolves an open problem and may be of independent interest beyond constrained settings. Limitations include the loop-free MDP assumption, the dependence on the number of states and actions in the bounds, and the fact that in the adversarial setting with the standard baseline, only a fraction of optimal reward is guaranteed (though sublinear regret is recovered with a weaker per-episode-feasible baseline). Open questions include whether the policy-optimization approach extends to continuous action spaces or partially observable settings.
