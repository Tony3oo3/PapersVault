## Problem

Certifying optimality for k-sparse generalized linear models requires computing tight lower bounds at each branch-and-bound node, but existing approaches rely on interior-point or ADMM-based conic solvers that are computationally expensive, do not scale to large instances, and cannot be effectively parallelized on GPUs. The paper addresses this gap by developing a first-order method that computes these dual bounds 1–2 orders of magnitude faster while guaranteeing exact proximal evaluations needed for valid certificates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×p}, a label vector y ∈ R^n, and three hyperparameters: the cardinality bound k, the box constraint M (bounding |β_j|), and a small Tikhonov coefficient λ₂. At each BnB node it produces a safe lower bound on the optimal objective via weak duality, a sparse coefficient vector β with at most k nonzero entries, and an optimality certificate when the gap closes to zero. The lower bound is computed from the FISTA iterate's gradient ζ = ∇F(Xβ̂) through the conjugate expression −F*(−ζ̂) − G*(Xᵀζ̂), making it a valid certificate even before full convergence.

## Method

The paper reformulates the perspective relaxation of the k-sparse GLM as an unconstrained composite problem min_β f(Xβ,y) + 2λ₂g(β), where g is an implicit function defined by a cardinality-constrained minimization. The key insight is that the conjugate g*(α) admits the closed form TopSum_k(H_M(α)), and its proximal operator can be evaluated exactly in O(p log p) time via a customized PAVA that exploits the isotonic-regression structure revealed by sign- and magnitude-preserving properties of the proximal map. This replaces the expensive SOCP solve required by generic conic solvers with a simple sorting step plus linear-time block merging. FISTA is then applied with a value-based restart strategy (resetting momentum when the objective increases), where the restart criterion requires evaluating g(β) exactly via a majorization-based algorithm in O(p + p log k) time. The resulting method achieves empirical linear convergence and relies only on matrix-vector products, enabling straightforward GPU acceleration that provides an additional order-of-magnitude speedup on high-dimensional instances.

## Results

On the largest synthetic instances (n=16,000, p=16,000), the proposed FISTA+PAVA method certifies optimality for linear regression in 79 seconds and logistic regression in 626 seconds, while Gurobi and MOSEK both exceed the 1800-second time limit. On the DOROTHEA dataset (n=2,300, p=89,989), the method achieves a 0.0% optimality gap in 230 seconds versus MOSEK's 0.63% gap at 1814 seconds. For proximal operator evaluation at p=10^5, the customized PAVA completes in 0.01 seconds compared to several seconds or minutes for SOCP solvers, representing a ~2-order-of-magnitude speedup. GPU acceleration provides an additional ~10x speedup on high-dimensional instances (e.g., 19.52s CPU vs. 2.09s GPU at p=16,000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four state-of-the-art solvers: Gurobi, MOSEK, SCS, and Clarabel. For proximal operator evaluation (Figure 1), the customized PAVA achieves approximately 2 orders of magnitude speedup over all four SOCP solvers, which must solve a second-order cone program per call. For solving the perspective relaxation (Figure 2), the FISTA method outperforms MOSEK (the fastest conic solver) by over 1 order of magnitude, while Gurobi cannot handle the cardinality-constrained logistic regression problem at all. For end-to-end optimality certification (Table 2), the proposed method achieves 0.0% optimality gap on all four tested datasets within 41–626 seconds, whereas Gurobi either times out or leaves a 0.89% gap, and MOSEK either times out or leaves a 0.63% gap. SCS and Clarabel are only compared in the relaxation-solving experiments (Figures 1–2) and are consistently slower than MOSEK, making the proposed method's advantage even more pronounced. The method also uniquely supports GPU acceleration, reducing runtime by an additional order of magnitude on high-dimensional instances, a capability none of the baseline MIP/SOCP solvers offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of perspective-based convex relaxations for MIP (Ceria & Soares, 1999; Günlük & Linderoth, 2010), first-order optimization (FISTA, Beck & Teboulle, 2009), and isotonic regression (PAVA, Best & Chakravarti, 1990). It extends the k-support norm proximal operator results of Argyriou et al. (2012) and McDonald et al. (2016) by incorporating big-M box constraints through a dual-space analysis, and departs from prior BnB lower-bound methods (Hazimeh et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024) that relied on SOCP or ADMM solvers by replacing them with a customized PAVA achieving O(p log p) exact proximal evaluation. The restart strategy draws on O'Donoghue & Candes (2015) but is the first to achieve empirical linear convergence in the MIP lower-bound computation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–2 order-of-magnitude speedup in dual bound computation makes optimality certification feasible for large-scale sparse GLMs (n, p up to 16k) that previously exceeded time limits for commercial MIP solvers, with direct implications for high-stakes interpretable ML in healthcare and finance. GPU compatibility (additional ~10× speedup at p=16k) opens the door to even larger instances. Key limitations include the empirical (not proven) linear convergence rate, the dependence on the specific perspective relaxation structure, and the fact that the BnB framework omits advanced MIP heuristics like cutting planes, so real-world performance on harder instances remains to be validated.
