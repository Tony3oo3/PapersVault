## Problem

Deep generative models for tabular data (diffusion models, GANs, VAEs) are computationally intensive and require extensive tuning of many hyperparameters, making them impractical for many data-sharing applications. While lighter alternatives like adversarial random forests exist, there is no fully non-parametric, model-free method that handles mixed numeric-categorical data while remaining competitive with state-of-the-art deep generators in fidelity and utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular data matrix containing any combination of numeric and categorical variables, along with two hyperparameters: n_c (number of discretization levels, ranging from 1 to n, controlling the amount of restricted shuffling and thus the privacy-fidelity trade-off) and p (sampling proportion in [1/n, 0.5], controlling the subsample size for marginal generation). It outputs a synthetic dataset of the same size and column structure as the input, with new numeric values drawn from interpolated order statistics and categorical values drawn from the original level set. The n_c parameter provides a single, interpretable knob: n_c=1 gives maximum shuffling (full privacy, no association), while n_c=n gives no shuffling (full fidelity, no privacy).

## Method

TabSDS is a two-step non-parametric procedure. First, it generates uncorrelated synthetic marginal distributions for each numeric variable via an interpolated order statistics sampler: two random subsamples of size m are sorted, and for each rank position a value is drawn uniformly between the two corresponding order statistics (Theorem 1 proves the resulting distribution converges almost surely to the true marginal as m→∞). Second, it applies SJPPDS (sequential joint probability preserving data shuffling) to the real data to produce a partially shuffled matrix whose joint distribution approximates the original, then rank-matches the synthetic marginals to the shuffled matrix's rank structure to induce the correct inter-variable associations without altering the marginals. For categorical variables, an arbitrary numeric rank encoding is applied before SJPPDS and reversed afterward; for mixed data, numeric and rank-encoded categorical columns are concatenated before shuffling to preserve cross-type associations. The entire pipeline requires no model training, no parametric distributional assumptions, and only the single critical tuning parameter n_c.

## Results

Across 12 datasets, TabSDS achieves competitive fidelity (detection-test AUROC, lower is better), ranking 1st or 2nd in 9 of 12 datasets and intercalating top positions with DDPM and ARF. On utility (ML efficiency), TabSDS ranks 1st or 2nd in 9 of 12 datasets. The headline advantage is runtime: TabSDS completes in 1.2–23.0 seconds across all datasets, versus 97–2203 seconds for DDPM, 17–304 seconds for ARF, and 262–1887 seconds for TVAE — orders of magnitude faster than deep generative baselines and considerably faster than ARF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (the strongest deep generative baseline), TabSDS wins on fidelity in 7 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.690 vs 0.782 on Adult, 0.664 vs 0.954 on Mushroom) but loses in 4 datasets where DDPM's diffusion model captures joint structure better (e.g., 0.721 vs 0.647 on California Housing, 0.976 vs 0.802 on Electricity). On utility, the two are nearly tied, with TabSDS edging ahead in 8 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.849 vs 0.839 on Bank Marketing, 0.949 vs 0.947 on House 16H). Against ARF, TabSDS dominates on fidelity in 11 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.837 vs 0.981 on Abalone) and is 10–50× faster (e.g., 1.33 s vs 24.52 s on Abalone). Against TVAE and CTGAN, TabSDS wins on fidelity in all 12 datasets (margins of 0.1–0.3 AUROC) and is 20–100× faster. Against BayesNet, TabSDS wins on fidelity in 10 of 12 datasets and is comparable or faster in runtime. In Experiment Set 2, TabSDS also outperforms SMOTE on both fidelity and DCR-based privacy, while offering a tunable privacy–utility trade-off via the n_c parameter that SMOTE lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabSDS extends the SJPPDS data perturbation method (Chaibub Neto, 2024) from a numeric-only privacy-preserving shuffle into a full synthetic data generator for mixed tabular data, adding rank-based encoding for categorical variables and an interpolated order statistics sampler for generating new values. The rank-matching step that induces association structure into synthetic marginals is borrowed from Domingo-Ferrer and Muralidhar (2016). Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a lightweight, model-free alternative to the wave of deep generative approaches (diffusion models, GANs, VAEs) that have dominated recent tabular data synthesis, and contrasts with the closely related permutation-based method of Domingo-Ferrer et al. (2025) by avoiding parametric distribution assumptions and ontology-based semantic ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the orders-of-magnitude speedup over deep generative baselines (e.g., ~10s vs. ~1000s on mid-size datasets) combined with competitive fidelity and utility, making high-quality synthetic data generation accessible without GPU resources or extensive hyperparameter tuning. The single critical tuning parameter (n_c) provides interpretable, continuous control over the fidelity-privacy tradeoff, which is valuable for data-sharing applications. Key limitations include the inability to generate fair synthetic data (requiring causal modeling) and the lack of differential privacy guarantees, both identified as open directions. The paper also raises the question of whether hybrid approaches—using parametric models for marginals while recovering associations non-parametrically—could further improve performance.
