## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of the bias introduced by uniform-width binning in ECE estimation are limited to binary classification, leaving a significant gap for the multiclass setting common in practice. Additionally, parametric recalibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling, GP-based recalibration) lack theoretical guarantees that a low ECE on recalibration data will generalize to test data. This paper addresses both gaps by deriving the first PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for calibration error in multiclass classification.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the output probabilities of a pre-trained classifier f_w (a point on the K-simplex) evaluated on a held-out recalibration dataset S_re, along with the corresponding true labels. It produces a recalibrated probability vector η_v ∘ f_w(x) that maps the original simplex output to a new simplex output. The GP prior is constructed using M outputs of the pretrained model as inducing points, and the variational posterior is a multivariate Gaussian over the GP parameters, optimized via gradient-based variational inference.

## Method

The paper decomposes the total ECE bias into a binning bias (O(1/B)) and a finite-sample estimation bias, then derives a novel concentration inequality that handles the non-i.i.d. structure of binned ECE (where bins may be empty and the ECE is not a sum of i.i.d. terms). This yields a PAC-Bayes bound of the form KL(ρ‖π)/λ + B log 2/λ + 2λ²/n, from which the optimal bin size B = O(n^{1/3}) and an estimation bias of O(1/n^{1/3}) follow. Building on this bound, the proposed PAC-Bayes Recalibration (PBR) algorithm minimizes an objective combining the Brier score (a differentiable upper bound on ECE), a classification loss, and a KL regularization term α·KL(ρ̃‖π̃)/n_re, using a Gaussian process as the recalibration model with variational inference. This extends the GP recalibration of Wenger et al. (2020) by adding flexible KL regularization and a Brier score term grounded in the PAC-Bayes generalization guarantee.

## Results

On MNIST, PBR (Eq. 10) reduces ECE from .1428 (uncalibrated Random Forest) to .0057, and from .0164 (1-layer NN) to .0112, while maintaining comparable accuracy. On CIFAR-100, PBR (Eq. 10) achieves the lowest ECE for WRN-28-10-drop (.0349 vs .0568 uncalibrated) and ResNeXt-8x64d (.0310 vs .0401 uncalibrated). Temperature scaling achieves the best ECE on AlexNet (.0216) and DenseNet-BC-L190 (.0222), outperforming PBR in those cases. The paper also demonstrates a positive correlation between the KL divergence term in the PAC-Bayes bound and the observed ECE generalization gap, validating the theoretical framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GP-based recalibration (Wenger et al., 2020), PBR consistently achieves lower ECE across nearly all model-dataset combinations, with the most dramatic improvement on MNIST Random Forest (.0057 vs .0313) and CIFAR-100 WRN-28-10-drop (.0349 vs .0567). Notably, GP recalibration sometimes fails to improve over the uncalibrated model at all (AlexNet and DenseNet on CIFAR-100 show identical ECE), whereas PBR always provides some reduction. Against temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), the comparison is mixed: temperature scaling achieves the best ECE on AlexNet (.0216 vs .2548 for PBR) and DenseNet (.0222 vs .0618), but PBR wins on WRN-28-10-drop (.0349 vs .0374) and ResNeXt-8x64d (.0310 vs .0401). The authors attribute temperature scaling's occasional superiority to its simplicity and note that it lacks the KL regularization that prevents overfitting when n_re is small, making PBR more robust in low-data regimes. Overall, the paper recommends trying both methods and selecting based on validation performance, while emphasizing that PBR is the only approach with a PAC-Bayes generalization guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayes generalization framework (McAllester, 2003; Alquier et al., 2016) to the calibration metric, specifically the expected calibration error (ECE) in multiclass classification. It builds directly on Futami and Fujisawa (2024), who derived an O(1/n^{1/3}) total bias bound for ECE in binary classification using information-theoretic analysis, by generalizing to the multiclass setting and switching to PAC-Bayes theory to obtain an optimizable bound. The proposed PBR algorithm extends the GP-based recalibration of Wenger et al. (2020) by replacing the ELBO objective with a PAC-Bayes-derived objective that includes a tunable KL regularization coefficient and a Brier score term, while retaining the same GP model and inducing-point strategy. The paper also formally establishes the curse of dimensionality for binning-based estimation of the full K-class calibration error (CE_K), a phenomenon previously noted informally by Gruber and Buettner (2022) but without a formal rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic PAC-Bayes bounds for ECE generalization and estimation bias in multiclass classification, yielding a practical optimal bin size of B = O(n^{1/3}) that practitioners can adopt immediately. A key practical insight is that the O(1/n^{1/3}) convergence rate makes ECE evaluation inherently noisy at typical dataset sizes, which the authors demonstrate empirically and which calls into question prior recalibration comparisons that used fixed bin counts (e.g., B=100). The PBR algorithm offers a principled, generalization-aware recalibration method, though its empirical gains over GP-based recalibration are modest and it does not consistently outperform simple temperature scaling. Open questions include extending the analysis to uniform-mass binning (UMB), developing nonparametric estimators that avoid the slow binning convergence rate, and understanding when GP-based recalibration is genuinely beneficial versus when simpler methods suffice.
