## Problem

Model merging combines multiple task-specific fine-tuned models into a single multi-task model, yet a significant performance gap between the merged model and individual experts persists. The paper identifies that the skewed singular value spectrum of Task Arithmetic concentrates information in a few dominant directions, underrepresenting tasks whose principal directions are orthogonal to those common directions, and demonstrates that subspace alignment between task and merged matrices strongly correlates (ρ=0.94) with per-task performance improvement.

## Input / Output

The method operates layer-by-layer on weight update matrices (task matrices Δ_t = θ_t − θ_0) computed from pre-trained and fine-tuned checkpoints. It handles both fully fine-tuned models and LoRA-adapted models (where task matrices are reconstructed as ΔW_t = B_t A_t from rank-16 LoRA factors), and applies simple averaging to non-matrix parameters such as biases and layer normalization. The output is a single merged model θ_M = θ_0 + αΔ_M with a per-layer scaling factor α selected on a held-out validation set, requiring no additional training or access to original training data.

## Method

The core insight is that replacing the skewed singular value spectrum of the Task Arithmetic sum with a uniform (isotropic) one increases the effective rank k_M of the merged matrix, which in turn raises the Subspace Alignment Ratio (SAR) between each task matrix and the merged matrix — a quantity that strongly predicts per-task performance. Iso-C computes the SVD of Δ_TA = Σ_t Δ_t, sets all singular values to their mean σ̄, and reconstructs Δ_Iso-C = σ̄UV^⊤, yielding a flat spectrum that balances representation across tasks without altering the singular vectors. Iso-CTS extends this by retaining the top-k singular vectors from the common subspace, projecting each task matrix onto the orthogonal complement (Δ̄_t = Δ_t − U^{1:k}(U^{1:k})^⊤Δ_t) to extract task-specific directions, concatenating common and task-specific vectors into U_* and V_*, orthogonalizing via SVD-based whitening, and applying isotropic scaling with the mean of all retained singular values. This two-subspace design ensures tasks with dominant directions orthogonal to the common subspace are explicitly represented, yielding up to 2.8% absolute accuracy gains over Iso-C when merging 20 diverse tasks.

## Results

Iso-CTS achieves state-of-the-art model merging performance across all evaluated settings (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14; 8, 14, and 20 tasks). On the 20-task ViT-B/16 benchmark, Iso-CTS reaches 82.4% absolute accuracy (88.4% normalized accuracy improvement), outperforming the closest competitor TSV-M (80.6%, 86.5%) by 1.8 points and Task Arithmetic (65.8%, 70.8%) by 16.6 points. The advantage of Iso-CTS over Iso-C grows with task count: from a near-tie at 8 tasks (86.2% vs 86.3% on ViT-B/32) to a 2.8% absolute accuracy gap at 20 tasks. On NLP (T5-Large, 8 tasks), Iso-C achieves 75.6% accuracy, surpassing MaTS (72.5%) and RegMean (69.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Iso-CTS outperforms all 12 baselines across every setting. The closest competitor is TSV-M (Gargiulo et al., CVPR 2025), which also leverages SVD structure; Iso-CTS leads by 1.0–2.8% absolute accuracy depending on task count, with the gap widening as tasks increase (e.g., 1.0% at 8 tasks vs. 2.4% at 20 tasks on ViT-B/16). Iso-C alone already surpasses TSV-M on 8-task ViT-B/16 (90.6% vs. 89.0%) and ViT-L/14 (94.2% vs. 93.0%), but falls behind TSV-M at 20 tasks (79.6% vs. 80.6%), highlighting the importance of task-specific subspaces. Per-parameter baselines (Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic, TIES, Consensus TA) are consistently 5–17 points below Iso-CTS. In the LoRA setting (Table 2), Iso-CTS (85.3% on ViT-L/14) outperforms KnOTS-TIES (78.2%) by 7.1 points, despite KnOTS being specifically designed for LoRA merging. On NLP tasks (Table 3), Iso-C (75.6%) beats MaTS (72.5%) and RegMean (69.1%), while Iso-CTS (75.2%) is marginally below Iso-C, suggesting the common subspace suffices for 7–8 NLP tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Task Arithmetic's formulation of merging as summation of task matrices, but departs from the per-parameter interference-reduction paradigm (TIES, Model Breadcrumbs, Consensus Merging) by operating in the spectral domain via SVD. It is most closely related to the concurrent TSV work (Gargiulo et al., 2025), which also uses SVD structure for merging but focuses on orthogonalizing task subspaces to reduce interference; here, the authors instead flatten the singular value spectrum to increase alignment, and adopt TSV's whitening procedure for the orthogonalization step in Iso-CTS. The Subspace Alignment Ratio (SAR) metric is introduced as a new diagnostic that, unlike the pairwise cosine similarity previously used to justify Task Arithmetic, strongly correlates with per-task performance of the merged model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a practical merging technique (Iso-CTS) that achieves state-of-the-art results across vision and language tasks at multiple scales without additional training, and a diagnostic framework (SAR) that explains *why* certain merging strategies succeed or fail. The key open question is whether the common subspace, currently identified by Task Arithmetic's SVD, can be determined more effectively, as the authors acknowledge this is a potential bottleneck. Additionally, the methods could be further adapted to exploit the low-rank structure of LoRA adapters for improved efficiency, and the approach's behavior with very large numbers of highly diverse tasks remains to be fully characterized.
