## Problem

LLM-based robotic planners struggle with long-horizon, complex tasks in specialized environments due to insufficient hierarchical reasoning, lack of domain-specific knowledge, and the absence of formal correctness guarantees. While hierarchical planning and RAG each address part of this gap, the paper argues that a deeper neuro-symbolic integration combining hierarchical decomposition, knowledge-graph retrieval, and symbolic verification is required for reliable plan generation and execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251961/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language task description (either constructive, e.g., 'boil water in a pot and place it on a countertop', or goal-oriented, e.g., 'I want a pot with boiling water on a countertop') together with a dynamically retrieved subgraph from the environment's knowledge graph containing relevant object instances, their types, properties, and current states. The output is a sequence of atomic actions (the expanded plan) in PDDL syntax that can be directly executed by the robot, along with a macro action plan and symbolic verification feedback. The knowledge graph is updated in real time during execution with multi-modal observations (visual scene graphs and auditory labels), making the retrieval context dynamic across the planning horizon.

## Method

HVR integrates three components around a frozen LLM planner: (1) Hierarchical planning decomposes the task into macro actions (MAs), each expanded into an atomic action block (AA-block) via separate LLM prompts with in-context examples, then concatenated into the final expanded plan; (2) Knowledge Graph RAG retrieves a task-relevant subgraph (object instances, states, properties) to condition both MA generation and AA-block expansion, reducing hallucination and compensating for limited LLM context; (3) A PDDL-based Symbolic Validator checks pre- and post-conditions at both the macro and atomic levels, triggering LLM-based back-prompting corrections when violations are detected, and at runtime aligns expected world states (from PDDL simulation) with observed scene-graph triples to detect execution failures. The action parser extracts predicate-argument structures from LLM output, and an action mapper matches them to the finite action set via embedding-space cosine similarity. A reusable macro action library is built by transferring verified MAs from the dynamic KG into the static ontology, enabling knowledge transfer across agents.

## Results

HVR achieves 94.19% Plan Correctness (PC) with Gemini-1.5-flash and 59.66% with Phi-3-mini across 12 kitchen tasks in AI2Thor, substantially outperforming all five baselines. For Gemini, HVR reaches 100% PC on moderate-complexity tasks and 88.39% on high-complexity tasks, while the best baseline (HV) reaches only 93.06% and 77.48% respectively. Symbolic verification yields strong gains: Expanded Plan Verification (EPV) reaches 88.11% for HVR with Gemini, and Macro Plan Verification improves from 47.03% to 74.20% (Phi-3) and from 100% to 100% (Gemini) after correction. LLM-based planners still generate plans ~100–200% longer than ground truth, and simulator execution succeeds on only ~95% of correctly planned actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVR outperforms all five baselines (HV, HR, VR, R, LLM-only) on Plan Correctness for both LLMs. With Gemini, HVR (94.19% PC) beats the strongest baseline HV (85.27%) by ~9 points, while HR (49.01%), VR (28.50%), R (23.87%), and LLM-only (17.91%) trail far behind. With Phi-3, the gap narrows: HVR (59.66%) edges out HR (55.50%) by ~4 points, while HV drops to 18.91%, revealing that RAG is essential for smaller models. A key asymmetry emerges: for Gemini, hierarchical planning without RAG (HV) outperforms hierarchical planning with RAG (HR) by 36 points, because the larger context window makes retrieval redundant; for Phi-3, the reverse holds (HR 55.50% vs. HV 18.91%), confirming RAG compensates for limited reasoning capacity. On verification metrics, HV achieves 100% EPV and AABV with Gemini (matching or exceeding HVR), but HVR's advantage lies in combining all three components for the highest overall PC, especially on high-complexity tasks where the gap widens (88.39% vs. 77.48% for Gemini; 67.73% vs. 21.48% for Phi-3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads in LLM-based robotic planning: hierarchical decomposition (as in HiP and MLDT), retrieval-augmented generation over knowledge graphs (extending Lewis et al.'s RAG and Edge et al.'s Graph RAG to symbolic KGs), and PDDL-based symbolic verification (building on the LLM-Modulo framework and VAL). It directly extends the RECOVER framework (Cornelio & Diab, 2024) by adding hierarchical planning and KG-RAG on top of the existing neuro-symbolic failure-detection pipeline, and departs from prior verification work (CLAIRIFY, Raman et al.) by using the symbolic validator both for pre-execution plan correction and as a runtime failure detector that aligns expected PDDL states with observed scene-graph triples. The macro-action library for cross-agent knowledge transfer is a novel addition not present in prior LLM-planner work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical neuro-symbolic recipe for making LLM planners formally correct in safety-critical domains (surgical robots, autonomous vehicles, domestic assistance), where hallucination-free, verifiable plans are non-negotiable. Key open questions remain: the fixed ontology and linear plan structure limit generalization, error correction does not reason across planning levels, and the framework has only been validated in a single kitchen simulator. The finding that RAG compensates for small-model limitations while hierarchical planning drives gains for large models offers actionable guidance for practitioners choosing between model scale and architectural complexity. The introduced metrics (PC, LD, EPV, MPV, AABV) fill a gap in the evaluation landscape for LLM planners, which previously lacked standardized measures of plan minimality and formal correctness.
