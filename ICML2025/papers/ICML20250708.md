## Problem

Diffusion models require many iterative denoising steps, making inference expensive. Existing acceleration techniques—caching (which accumulates reuse errors over time steps) and post-training quantization (which suffers from large activation outliers and varying ranges across timesteps)—each introduce significant quality degradation at low bit-widths. No principled framework simultaneously reduces quantization error and prevents cross-step error accumulation in a training-free manner.

## Input / Output

MoDiff operates as a drop-in inference-time modification on pre-trained diffusion models (U-Net DDIM on CIFAR-10 32×32, LDM-4/LDM-8 on LSUN 256×256, Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO, DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet 256×256). It takes standard Gaussian noise and produces generated RGB images, with no architectural changes or retraining required. The method is sampler-agnostic (DDIM, DDPM, DPM-Solver, PLMS) and quantizer-agnostic (channel-wise, tensor-wise, Q-Diffusion), requiring only that linear/convolutional layers be bias-free and that a warm-up step at full precision initializes the cached state.

## Method

MoDiff reformulates each linear layer's computation at time step t as A(a_t) = A(a_t − a_{t+1}) + o_{t+1}, exploiting the linearity of the operator so that only the small temporal difference needs to be quantized rather than the full activation. This 'modulated quantization' dramatically shrinks the input range to the quantizer (over 10× smaller), enabling 3-bit activation precision where 8-bit was previously the practical limit. To prevent the quantization error from propagating and accumulating across the T diffusion steps, MoDiff introduces an error-compensated modulation: an intermediate variable â_t tracks the actual quantized activation, and the residual error e_t = a_t − â_t is fed back into the next step's quantized input, causing errors to decay exponentially rather than accumulate linearly. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 4.3–4.4) formalizes both the range-reduction benefit and the exponential error decay, and the framework subsumes heuristic caching (e.g., DeepCache) as a special case when the temporal difference falls below a threshold and is assigned a 0-bit representation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DDIM, 100 steps), LCQ+MoDiff reduces activation precision from 8 bits to 3 bits with sFID 4.92, matching full-precision performance (sFID 4.41) while cutting computation from 1636 to 153 GBops (>10× savings). On LSUN-Churches (LDM-8), Q-Diff+MoDiff at 8/4 bits achieves FID 3.97 and sFID 11.16, compared to Q-Diff's catastrophic FID 355.85 at the same precision. The method also generalizes to Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO (FID 13.21 vs. 71.38 at 8/6) and DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet (FID 54.74 vs. 200.26 at 8/6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDiff consistently and dramatically outperforms all five baselines at low activation bit-widths. Against Q-Diffusion, the improvement is most striking: on LSUN-Churches at 8/4 bits, Q-Diff collapses to FID 355.85 while Q-Diff+MoDiff achieves FID 3.97 (a ~90× reduction in FID); on CIFAR-10 at 8/3, Q-Diff yields FID 457.35 versus 5.01 for Q-Diff+MoDiff. Against LCQ (BRECQ-based channel-wise quantization), MoDiff similarly rescues generation quality: on LSUN-Churches at 8/4, LCQ gives FID 198.37 while LCQ+MoDiff reaches 34.02, and on CIFAR-10 at 8/3, LCQ+MoDiff maintains sFID 4.92 versus LCQ's 4.92 (both comparable, but Q-Diff+MoDiff is stronger). Against LLM.int8()-style tensor-wise quantization (LTQ) on Stable Diffusion/MS-COCO at 8/6, LTQ+MoDiff achieves FID 13.21 versus LTQ's 71.38. Against PTQ4DiT on ImageNet 256×256 (DiT-XL/2) at 8/6, PTQ4DiT+MoDiff reaches FID 54.74 versus PTQ4DiT's 200.26. Against MixDQ on SDXL-Turbo (few-step, 2 steps, 8/6), MixDQ+MoDiff improves FID from 318.68 to 193.17, though the absolute quality remains lower due to the extreme few-step regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoDiff unifies two previously separate lines of diffusion acceleration—caching (DeepCache, BlockCaching) and post-training quantization (Q-Diffusion, BRECQ/LCQ, LLM.int8/LTQ)—by observing that temporal differences between adjacent-step activations are an order of magnitude smaller in range than raw activations. This insight allows the paper to reformulate each linear layer's computation as a quantized update on the difference plus a cached previous output, subsuming heuristic caching as the 0-bit special case while providing a principled quantization target. The error-compensation mechanism, which tracks and feeds back quantization residuals across time steps, is novel and orthogonal to the specific PTQ algorithm used, positioning MoDiff as a drop-in wrapper rather than a competing quantization method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to push activation precision from 8 bits to 3 bits training-free, with over 10× computational savings on CIFAR-10, is a concrete efficiency win that could make diffusion sampling more accessible on edge or memory-constrained hardware. However, the gains diminish on higher-resolution LDMs (256×256), where 4-bit activation still degrades quality noticeably, and the method incurs additional memory for cached activations and outputs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion or autoregressive generation, whether hardware-aware implementations can realize the theoretical Bop savings, and whether the memory overhead can be further reduced through selective layer application.
