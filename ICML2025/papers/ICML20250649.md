## Problem

Existing targeted data selection methods rely on influence-based greedy heuristics that assume additive influence and work well only on unimodal target distributions. In complex, multimodal settings (e.g., motion prediction, multi-source driving data), these heuristics fail because dominant gradient feature directions bias distance estimation and the linear additive assumption cannot capture diverse target patterns. TAROT addresses both issues by reformulating selection as an optimal transport distribution-matching problem with a corrected distance metric.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a candidate dataset (e.g., 25k GTA5 images, 3M driving trajectories, or ~10M instruction-tuning samples), a smaller target dataset (e.g., 3k Cityscapes images, 32k nuScenes samples, or MMLU/BBH prompts), and a pretrained model whose gradients at multiple checkpoints are extracted, randomly projected, and whitened to form per-sample feature vectors. The output is a selected subset of the candidate data plus integer repetition weights derived from OT dual potentials, which determine how many times each selected sample appears per training epoch (scaled to a user-specified repetition factor R).

## Method

TAROT first computes a Whitened Feature Distance (WFD): gradient features from T checkpoints are randomly projected, centered, decorrelated via Cholesky whitening, and L2-normalized to unit hypersphere vectors, yielding a pairwise distance that mitigates dominant-feature bias. This WFD serves as the cost matrix for an OT problem between the target distribution and the selected subset, solved efficiently via the Sinkhorn algorithm. For selection, TAROT exploits the sparsity of the OT coupling (mass concentrates on nearest neighbors) and iteratively adds k-th nearest candidates from the pool; when the budget is exceeded, candidates are ranked by their OT dual potential to pick the most beneficial additions. A second scheme (OTM) uses k-fold cross-validation on the target set and stops adding data once the held-out OT distance increases, automatically determining an optimal selection ratio. Finally, OT potentials are scaled to positive integer weights that control per-sample repetition during downstream training.

## Results

On instruction tuning, TAROT-OTM selects less than 0.5% of the candidate data (0.09%–0.21%) while matching or surpassing 5%-selection baselines: for Qwen-2.5-7B it achieves 74.3 MMLU and 68.9 BBH, compared to 74.3/66.3 for 5% LESS. For LLaMA-3.1-8B, 5% TAROT reaches 66.0 MMLU and 65.0 BBH, outperforming 5% LESS (65.7/62.6) without requiring subtask labels. In semantic segmentation, TAROT achieves the highest mIoU using only ~24% of the GTA5 synthetic dataset, surpassing both LESS and DsDm at all selection ratios. In motion prediction, TAROT-OTM (selecting ~30% of multi-source data) outperforms training on the full dataset and achieves top-1 on the nuScenes leaderboard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRAK (Park et al., 2023), WFD consistently achieves higher LDS scores across all ensemble sizes on both CIFAR-10 and nuScenes, confirming that whitened feature distance is a more reliable influence estimator. Against LESS (Xia et al., 2024), TAROT wins on both MMLU (66.0 vs 65.7) and BBH (65.0 vs 62.6) for LLaMA-3.1-8B at 5% selection, and on BBH for Qwen-2.5-7B (66.9 vs 66.3); critically, TAROT-OTM with only 0.13% of data reaches 68.9 BBH, surpassing LESS's 5% result by 2.6 points. Against DsDm (Engstrom et al., 2024), TAROT outperforms on both semantic segmentation (highest mIoU at ~24% selection) and motion prediction (lower Brier-minFDE), with the gap widening at higher selection ratios where DsDm's additive influence assumption breaks down. Against TSDS (Liu et al., 2024), TAROT leads on MMLU (66.0 vs 65.2) and BBH (65.0 vs 63.1) for LLaMA, and on both metrics for Qwen (74.1 vs 73.9 MMLU; 66.9 vs 66.2 BBH). Notably, TAROT does not require subtask labels (unlike LESS) and demonstrates cross-model transferability: data selected by LLaMA-3.1-8B improves Qwen-2.5-7B's BBH from 63.8 (full data) to 68.7 (OTM-selected).

## Relation to Prior Work

TAROT extends the gradient-based influence estimation lineage (Pruthi et al., 2020; Park et al. 2023 TRAK) by introducing Whitened Feature Distance to correct for dominant-feature bias in high-dimensional gradient spaces, and repurposes the dataset-level OT distance framework of Alvarez-Melis & Fusi (2020) and Just et al. (2023) as a selection objective rather than a valuation metric. It departs from influence-based greedy heuristics (LESS, DsDm) that assume additive influence, and from the concurrent TSDS (Liu et al., 2024) which also frames selection as OT but retains the same unwhitened gradient distance and uses a slower continuous OT formulation. The key conceptual shift is treating targeted selection as empirical distribution matching with a greedy, deterministic subset algorithm that explicitly tracks OT distance for early stopping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OTM scheme's ability to identify that less than 0.5% of a large candidate pool suffices to match or exceed 5%-selection baselines on LLM instruction tuning offers a concrete, actionable path toward drastically reducing fine-tuning costs. The demonstrated transferability—data selected by a 1.5M-parameter model improving a 15M-parameter model, and cross-architecture transfer between LLaMA and Qwen—suggests the selected subsets capture task-relevant structure rather than model-specific artifacts. The authors acknowledge that TAROT may overfit on small or highly specific target distributions, and identify incorporating distribution diversification into the selection objective as a natural next step; broader questions about how WFD-based OT selection behaves under distribution shift or with very large candidate pools (beyond the ~1000k scale tested) remain open.
