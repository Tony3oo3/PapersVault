## Problem

Most uncertainty estimation methods for graph neural networks implicitly or explicitly rely on homophily—smoothing uncertainty across semantically similar neighbors—and deteriorate on heterophilic graphs where adjacent nodes have different semantics. No prior work addresses uncertainty quantification that deliberately avoids homophily assumptions, leaving a gap in reliable uncertainty for the increasingly studied heterophilic node classification setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250691/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a post-hoc, model-agnostic estimator applied to any trained MPNN backbone. It requires a single forward pass to extract node embeddings at every layer (z^(1) through z^(L)), applies PCA to each layer's embeddings to preserve 95% variance, concatenates all layer embeddings per node, and computes KNN distances (k=5) against the training set's concatenated embeddings. The output is a scalar epistemic uncertainty score per node, proportional to the squared L2 distance to its k nearest training neighbors in the joint embedding space. No retraining or architectural modification is needed.

## Method

The paper derives a Data Processing Equality (DPE) for MPNNs (Theorem 4.1) that decomposes the change in mutual information between successive layer embeddings into an information loss/recovery term Δ₋ and a novel information gain term Δ₊ from the expanding receptive field. Unlike the standard DPI for i.i.d. data, this equality permits information to increase with depth when neighbors carry semantically distinct information. The authors further define information-based homophily (Definition 4.3) and prove (Proposition 4.2) that the attainable information gain Δ₊ decreases as homophily increases, explaining why each layer's embedding in heterophilic graphs captures unique, non-redundant information about the target. This motivates Joint Latent Density Estimation (JLDE): a KNN-based density estimator computed over the concatenation of all layer embeddings rather than a single layer, which the authors show empirically outperforms single-layer, first-and-last-layer, and per-layer-averaged density estimates on heterophilic graphs while remaining competitive on homophilic ones.

## Results

On the heterophilic Roman Empire dataset, JLDE achieves epistemic AUC-ROC of 76.9 (LoC), 93.8 (Near-Features), and 100.0 (Far-Features), outperforming the best baseline (Ensemble: 76.2, 78.2, 86.9) by up to 13.1 points. On Amazon Ratings, JLDE scores 61.2, 65.4, and 99.2, again beating all baselines (best: Ensemble at 57.9, 63.6, 94.4). On the homophilic CoraML, JLDE (80.8, 54.9, 95.7) is competitive with model-agnostic methods but trails homophily-exploiting estimators like SGCN (87.2) on LoC, while still achieving the best Far-Features score (95.7 vs. 78.4 for Ensemble).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JLDE decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on both heterophilic datasets (Roman Empire and Amazon Ratings) across all three distribution shifts, with the largest margins on Far-Features (100.0 vs. 92.0 for EBM on Roman Empire; 99.2 vs. 94.4 for EBM on Amazon Ratings). On Roman Empire Near-Features, JLDE (93.8) beats the next-best GPRGNN (87.9) by 5.9 points and the Ensemble (78.2) by 15.6 points. On the homophilic CoraML, JLDE is competitive with model-agnostic methods (Ensemble, MCD, EBM) but trails homophily-exploiting estimators like SGCN (87.2 vs. 80.8 on LoC) and FAGCN (76.0 vs. 54.9 on Near-Features), confirming that its advantage is specifically in heterophilic settings. Notably, JLDE achieves the best Far-Features score on CoraML (95.7) without any graph-diffusion post-processing, whereas GEBM and GNN-Safe—which rely on homophilic smoothing—drop to 53.3 and 51.0 respectively on the same shift. The rank analysis (Table 2) confirms JLDE ranks 1st or 2nd across all four backbone architectures (Res-GCN, Res-GAT-Sep, Res-GATE, GPRGNN) on heterophilic data, while GEBM dominates on homophilic Chameleon and Squirrel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck framework of Tishby & Zaslavsky (2015) from i.i.d. data to message-passing neural networks, deriving a novel Data Processing Equality (Theorem 4.1) that accounts for the ego-graph structure and shows information about the target can increase with depth in heterophilic settings. It departs from prior graph uncertainty methods (GPN, GEBM, GNN-Safe) that implicitly or explicitly rely on homophilic smoothing kernels, and from Benkert et al. (2024) who empirically motivate joint layer consideration for i.i.d. data but lack a graph-specific theoretical justification. The proposed JLDE method is a deliberately simple post-hoc KNN density estimator on concatenated layer embeddings, contrasting with training-time approaches like evidential learning, Bayesian weight sampling, or ensemble methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly target epistemic uncertainty estimation on heterophilic graphs, filling a gap where all prior graph uncertainty methods assume or exploit homophily. The MPNN DPI provides a principled theoretical foundation for why joint layer consideration is necessary under heterophily, and the simple KNN realization demonstrates that this principle yields state-of-the-art results across multiple backbones and distribution shifts without architecture-specific modifications. Limitations include the focus on transductive node classification, the use of a basic KNN density estimator (the authors conjecture more sophisticated models would improve results), and no treatment of aleatoric uncertainty or calibration. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic framework extends to regression, link prediction, or inductive settings, and whether the joint density principle can be exploited with learned density models rather than KNN.
