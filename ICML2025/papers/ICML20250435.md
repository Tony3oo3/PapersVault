## Problem

Probabilistic time series forecasting must capture multimodal conditional distributions over future trajectories, but existing approaches face a trade-off: parametric autoregressive models (DeepAR) are flexible but limited by their distribution family, while generative models like diffusion (TimeGrad) and normalizing flows (TempFlow) are computationally expensive at inference and lack a mechanism to guarantee diverse hypotheses in a single forward pass. The paper asks whether one can produce a small set of diverse, representative futures efficiently, with a principled connection to optimal quantization of the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The model takes a context window of past multivariate observations (and optional covariates) and produces K distinct future trajectories of fixed horizon in a single forward pass, along with K learned scores in [0,1] that estimate the probability of each hypothesis being the closest match to the realized future. The K trajectories are generated by autoregressive unrolling of a shared LSTM backbone through K separate linear prediction heads, so all hypotheses share the same hidden-state computation and differ only in their final projection. Scores are computed by averaging per-timestep score-head outputs over the prediction horizon, and at inference the hypotheses can be resampled proportionally to their scores for metric computation.

## Method

TimeMCL adapts the Winner-Takes-All (WTA) loss from Multiple Choice Learning to time series: a shared two-layer LSTM encodes the context, and K linear heads each predict the full future trajectory. During training, for each sample the head with the lowest negative log-likelihood is designated the 'winner,' and gradients are backpropagated only through that head, inducing a Voronoi tessellation of the trajectory space. The paper proves that at convergence each head's output equals the conditional mean of the target distribution restricted to its Voronoi cell, making TimeMCL a conditional functional quantizer analogous to K-Means over future paths. Two relaxation variants—Relaxed-WTA (backpropagating a small fraction ε to non-winner heads) and annealed MCL (softmin weighting with a decaying temperature)—mitigate under-trained heads. Additional score heads trained with binary cross-entropy learn to predict the probability of each head winning, enabling calibrated hypothesis selection at inference.

## Results

On the Distortion metric with K=16 hypotheses, TimeMCL (Relaxed) achieves the best scores on SOLAR (280.03), TRAFFIC (0.68), and TAXI (187.81), outperforming the strongest baselines Tactis2 and TimeGrad by 22%, 19%, and 10% respectively. In terms of computational efficiency, TimeMCL requires only 8.83×10⁶ FLOPs and 1.12 s inference time, compared to 3.05×10⁹ FLOPs and 241.57 s for TimeGrad—a 347× reduction in FLOPs. TimeMCL also produces significantly smoother trajectories (Total Variation), e.g., 0.031 on EXCHANGE vs. 0.237 for Tactis2 and 0.606 for TimeGrad, confirming its theoretical interpretation as a conditional functional quantizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeMCL (Relaxed) achieves the best Distortion on three of six datasets (SOLAR, TRAFFIC, TAXI), beating Tactis2 by 22% on SOLAR (280.03 vs. 358.01) and by 10% on TAXI (187.81 vs. 243.63). On EXCHANGE, Tactis2 retains a narrow edge (0.030 vs. 0.035), and on WIKIPEDIA Tactis2 is also slightly ahead (254675 vs. 261950). TimeGrad, while competitive on distortion, incurs 347× more FLOPs (3.05×10⁹ vs. 8.83×10⁶) and 216× longer inference time (241.57 s vs. 1.12 s) than TimeMCL. DeepAR is consistently the weakest neural baseline across all datasets, and TempFlow (RNN) sits in the middle, outperformed by TimeMCL on every dataset. On the Total Variation smoothness metric, TimeMCL (Relaxed) achieves the lowest values on 5 of 6 datasets, e.g., 709.86 on TAXI vs. 4147.2 for TempFlow and 4232.16 for TimeGrad, validating the theoretical prediction that WTA-trained heads produce conditional means rather than noisy samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeMCL adapts the Multiple Choice Learning (MCL) framework with Winner-Takes-All (WTA) loss—originally developed for ambiguous computer vision tasks (Guzman-Rivera et al., 2012; Lee et al., 2016; Rupprecht et al., 2017)—to multivariate probabilistic time series forecasting. The theoretical foundation draws directly on Letzelter et al. (2024), who showed WTA learners are geometry-aware conditional density estimators, and Letzelter et al. (2023) for the scoring mechanism. The paper departs from the dominant probabilistic forecasting paradigms (parametric autoregressive models like DeepAR, diffusion-based TimeGrad, normalizing-flow TempFlow, and copula-based Tactis2) by reframing the problem as conditional functional quantization rather than distribution fitting or iterative generative sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TimeMCL offers a single-forward-pass alternative to computationally expensive generative forecasters (TimeGrad requires ~350× more FLOPs), while providing a provable quantization interpretation that explains why its predictions are smooth and representative. Key limitations include the need to predefine the number of hypotheses, sensitivity to data scaling (mean-based scaling can underutilize heads), and the absence of a mechanism to dynamically add or remove hypotheses without retraining. Open directions include exploring transformer backbones, advanced normalization (Z-score, reversible instance normalization), and whether the quantization perspective transfers to other sequential prediction tasks.
