## Problem

Apers & de Wolf (2020) posed the open question of whether quantum speedups extend from graph sparsification to the more general hypergraph setting. All known classical hypergraph sparsification algorithms require Ω(m) time (linear in the number of hyperedges), with the state-of-the-art running in Õ(mr) time. This paper provides the first quantum algorithm for hypergraph sparsification, breaking the linear-in-m barrier.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes quantum oracle query access to a weighted hypergraph H=(V,E,w) with n vertices, m hyperedges, and rank r, where the oracle provides hyperedge size, vertex membership, and weight queries via unitaries O_H^size, O_H^vtx, and O_H^wt. It outputs an explicit description of an ε-spectral sparsifier with O(n log n log r / ε²) hyperedges, along with an intermediate data structure providing Õ(r)-time quantum queries to hyperedge leverage score overestimates. The input is consumed entirely through quantum oracle queries rather than classical adjacency structures, and the sparsifier is a reweighted sub-hypergraph preserving the energy Q_H(x) within a (1±ε) factor for all x∈ℝ^V.

## Method

The algorithm quantumizes the iterative leverage score overestimate framework of Jambulapati et al. (2023) by decomposing the computation into operations on sparse underlying graphs (star subgraphs with r−1 edges per hyperedge rather than complete cliques). In each of O(log r) rounds, the quantum graph sparsification algorithm of Apers & de Wolf (2020) reduces the underlying graph to Õ(n) edges, after which effective resistances are queried via a quantum data structure and edge weights are updated proportionally to leverage scores. Sampling is performed using Hamoudi's 'preparing many copies of a quantum state' technique to draw Õ(n/ε²) hyperedges in Õ(r√(mn)/ε) time without explicitly computing the normalization constant, which is instead estimated via quantum sum estimation. Correctness is established through the chaining argument of Lee (2023), which bounds the expected spectral error using Talagrand's generic chaining method applied to the max-norm structure of hypergraph energy.

## Results

The proposed quantum algorithm produces an ε-spectral sparsifier of size O(n log n log r / ε²) in time Õ(r√(mn)/ε), matching the sparsifier size of the best classical algorithm (Jambulapati et al., 2023) while achieving sublinear time in the number of hyperedges m. For constant rank r, this matches the quantum lower bound of Õ(√(mn)/ε) established by Apers & de Wolf (2020). For dense hypergraphs with m ∈ Ω(n^r), the algorithm achieves a near-quadratic speedup, reducing time from Õ(rn^r) classically to Õ(rn^((r+1)/2)) quantumly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's quantum algorithm matches the sparsifier size of the state-of-the-art classical algorithms (Jambulapati et al. 2023 and Lee 2023, both achieving O(n log n log r / ε²)) while reducing time complexity from Õ(mr) to Õ(r√(mn)/ε), a sublinear improvement in m. Compared to earlier classical baselines, the improvement is more dramatic: Soma & Yoshida (2019) required Õ(mnr + n³/ε²) time with a much larger O(n³ log n / ε²) sparsifier, and Bansal et al. (2019) needed Õ(mr² + r³n/ε²) time. The Kapralov et al. (2022) result achieved the same near-linear sparsifier size but in Õ(mr + poly(n)) time, still linear in m. For constant rank r, the quantum algorithm's Õ(√(mn)/ε) time matches the quantum lower bound from Apers & de Wolf (2020), while the classical lower bound is Ω(m), confirming a genuine quantum speedup. The paper also shows that for dense hypergraphs (m ∈ Ω(n^r)), the speedup becomes near-quadratic in n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper quantumizes the classical hypergraph sparsification framework of Jambulapati et al. (2023), which computes group leverage score overestimates via an iterative John-ellipsoid-style procedure. The key technical insight is that the leverage score overestimate computation can be decomposed into a sequence of sparse underlying graphs, each of which is sparsified using the quantum graph sparsification subroutine of Apers & de Wolf (2020). The sampling and reweighting stages are handled via Hamoudi's (2022) multi-copy quantum state preparation and quantum sum estimation, while correctness is established through the chaining argument of Lee (2023). The work directly answers an open problem posed by Apers & de Wolf (2020) about whether quantum speedups for graph sparsification extend to hypergraphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantum algorithm for hypergraph sparsification, breaking the linear-in-m barrier of classical algorithms and achieving sublinear time in the number of hyperedges for constant rank, matching the known quantum lower bound. It immediately implies quantum speedups for hypergraph mincut and s-t mincut approximation. Open questions include whether the runtime is tight for all ε, whether the approach extends to directed or online hypergraph sparsification, and whether similar quantum speedups exist for related hypergraph problems like k-cut or spectral diffusion. The practical impact is currently limited by the absence of fault-tolerant quantum hardware and the QRAM assumption.
