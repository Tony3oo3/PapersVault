## Problem

Existing flow-based generative models for geometric graphs (diffusion, flow-matching) employ fixed, noise-independent graph neural network architectures throughout the entire noising process, using constant-radius or fixed k-NN connectivity regardless of noise level. This static approach limits expressiveness because the optimal information aggregation structure fundamentally changes as noise increases—local structure becomes less informative while global structure becomes more relevant. The paper asks: how should graph neural network connectivity and resolution adapt to the noise level to optimally recover the underlying signal?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251914/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noised geometric graph G_t = (X_t, R, E) at noise level t ∈ [0,1] and outputs a velocity field v_θ(G_t, t) that guides the denoising process. For 3D shape generation (ModelNet40), inputs are 400 point positions in R³ and outputs are 3D velocity vectors per point. For spatiotemporal transcriptomics, inputs are 100 cells with 3-gene expression features and 2D positions (temporal × spatial), outputting gene expression velocities. For image generation (ImageNet 256×256), inputs are 32×32 autoencoder latents patchified into variable-size grids, outputting latent-space velocities. The noise level t directly conditions the graph topology, not just the network weights.

## Method

The paper introduces Noise-Conditioned Graph Networks (NCGNs), where the GNN architecture itself is parameterized by the noise level t, and develops Dynamic Message Passing (DMP) as a concrete instantiation. DMP simultaneously adapts two structural parameters: (1) the connectivity range r_t (kNN radius), which increases with noise, and (2) the graph resolution s_t (number of supernodes via voxel clustering), which decreases with noise, following an exponential adaptive schedule that maintains linear time complexity (r_t · s_t = r_1 · N). At each GNN layer, the original graph is coarsened to s_t supernodes, additional kNN edges are constructed at range r_t, message passing (GCN or GAT) is performed on the coarse graph, and results are uncoarsened back to original nodes via a gated mechanism. The theoretical justification comes from a mutual information analysis (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2) proving that optimal aggregation radius increases as SNR decreases, and a positional noise analysis (Proposition 3.3) showing expected inter-node distances grow with noise, both motivating the dynamic range and resolution design.

## Results

On ModelNet40 3D shape generation, DMP achieves a 16.15% average reduction in Wasserstein distance (W₂) compared to noise-independent baselines, reaching 4.215 (GCN) and 4.263 (GAT) versus 4.315 and 4.741 for the strongest fixed-range baseline. On ImageNet 256×256, the modified DiT-DMP reduces FID from 84.051 to 63.983 (a 23.9% improvement) while maintaining identical computational complexity. On the spatiotemporal transcriptomics dataset, DMP achieves the lowest W₂ distance on GCN across all six biologically-inspired tasks, with the largest gains on unconditional generation (0.812 vs. 0.880 for fully connected).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet, DiT-DMP decisively outperforms the baseline DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) across all five metrics: FID drops from 84.051 to 63.983, SFID from 18.787 to 12.823, IS rises from 16.735 to 24.681, Precision from 0.296 to 0.446, and Recall from 0.394 to 0.426, all at the same computational cost. On ModelNet40, DMP beats the strongest noise-independent baseline (long-short range) by 2.3% (GCN) and 10.2% (GAT) in W₂ distance, and substantially outperforms fixed KNN (23–28% improvement) and random edges (51–52% improvement). On the spatiotemporal transcriptomics dataset, DMP (GCN) achieves the best W₂ on all six tasks, though the margin over the fully connected baseline is modest (e.g., 0.812 vs. 0.880 on unconditional generation), while on GAT the differences are smaller and sometimes in favor of the fully connected baseline. The key finding is that DMP's advantage is most pronounced when the fixed-range baseline cannot simultaneously capture both local and global structure, confirming that noise-adaptive connectivity is the source of the gain rather than simply having more edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow-based generative modeling of geometric graphs (diffusion, flow-matching) by introducing Noise-Conditioned Graph Networks (NCGNs), which depart from the standard practice of using a fixed GNN architecture (constant kNN radius, full resolution) across all noise levels. The theoretical contribution builds on information-theoretic analysis of Gaussianized graphs to prove that optimal aggregation radius increases with noise, providing formal justification for adaptive connectivity. The practical instantiation, Dynamic Message Passing (DMP), draws on coarse-graining/pooling ideas and is demonstrated by modifying DiT with FlexiViT's dynamic patch sizing and neighborhood attention, showing that existing state-of-the-art architectures can be adapted with minimal code changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for adapting GNN architectures to noise level in generative models, with the practical advantage of maintaining linear-time complexity and requiring only two code changes to integrate into existing models like DiT. However, the adaptive schedule is currently hand-designed (exponential interpolation) rather than learned, and the method still suffers from oversmoothing at high layer counts, limiting its depth. Open questions include whether range and resolution parameters can be learned end-to-end, whether other GNN components (layer count, width, message passing type) should also be noise-conditioned, and whether the benefits generalize to larger-scale or more complex geometric domains beyond the relatively small datasets tested here.
