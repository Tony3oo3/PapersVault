## Problem

Existing certified unlearning methods for non-convex tasks require the loss function to be smooth and often demand knowledge of the smoothness constant, effectively excluding practical deep neural networks. No prior approach provides formal (ε,δ)-unlearning guarantees for arbitrary non-convex objectives without such restrictive assumptions, leaving a gap between theoretical certification and real-world deployment where GDPR-style 'right to be forgotten' requests must be handled efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a trained model (from any optimizer—SGD, Adam, momentum-SGD, etc.), a forget set specifying which training examples to erase, and the full training dataset. It outputs an unlearned model that satisfies a formal (ε,δ)-unlearning guarantee relative to a certifying algorithm that never saw the forget data. Notably, the certifying algorithm is purely a theoretical construct requiring no additional computation in practice, and the unlearning phase itself uses only SGD with constant step size, while the initial training and post-unlearning fine-tuning can use any optimizer or schedule.

## Method

The core insight is to frame each noisy fine-tuning step on retained data as a stochastic post-processing operation that amplifies privacy (and hence unlearning) progressively, extending Feldman et al.'s privacy amplification by iteration from convex to non-convex settings. Two variants are proposed: gradient clipping, which clips per-step gradients to a bounded norm before adding Gaussian noise (analyzed via Rényi divergence and a shift-reduction lemma), and model clipping, which clips the full parameter vector after each update before noise injection (analyzed via hockey-stick divergence contraction coefficients from Asoodeh et al.). In the gradient clipping variant, the required noise per iteration scales as O(C₀²/T + γ²TC₁²), so increasing iterations T amortizes the initial model's influence; adding L2 regularization further yields exponential decay of the initial clipping radius C₀. The method requires no smoothness, convexity, or boundedness assumptions on the loss function, making it the first certified unlearning approach applicable to arbitrary deep neural networks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Gradient Clipping and Model Clipping reduce the epochs needed to reach 40% accuracy from 18 (retrain from scratch) to 10, a 44% compute saving, while Output Perturbation requires 12 epochs. At 30% accuracy, Model Clipping needs only 3 epochs versus 6 for retraining (50% faster). In the transfer learning setting with a frozen ResNet-18 backbone, Gradient Clipping reaches 85% accuracy on CIFAR-10 in 9 epochs (10% faster than retrain, 47% faster than DP-SGD), and 60% on CIFAR-100 in 32 epochs, while DP-SGD never exceeds 20% within a 50-epoch budget despite using a much weaker privacy target (ε=50 vs. ε=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD with group privacy (Abadi et al., 2016), the proposed Gradient Clipping method is substantially faster: on CIFAR-10 transfer learning it reaches 85% accuracy in 9 epochs versus DP-SGD's ~17 epochs (47% faster), and on CIFAR-100 it achieves 60% in 32 epochs while DP-SGD never exceeds 20% within 50 epochs, even though DP-SGD uses a far weaker privacy budget (ε=50 vs. ε=1). Against the Langevin Diffusion approach of Chourasia & Shah (2023), the paper's method removes the smoothness and boundedness assumptions entirely; their noise variance has no closed-form expression and requires knowledge of the smoothness constant, whereas Gradient Clipping needs only clipping thresholds. Against Rewind-to-Delete (Mu & Klabjan, 2024), the proposed method avoids the exponential-in-smoothness noise scaling and the requirement for noisy training; Table 1 shows their noise variance grows exponentially with the smoothness constant while Gradient Clipping scales linearly in γC₁C₀. Against the simpler Output Perturbation baseline, both Gradient and Model Clipping consistently save 20–50% of compute across all accuracy targets, with the advantage being largest at lower compute budgets where retraining from scratch struggles most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes noisy fine-tuning on retained data as a stochastic post-processing step, connecting machine unlearning to the differential privacy literature on privacy amplification by iteration (Feldman et al., 2018) and amplification by mixing (Balle et al., 2019; Asoodeh et al., 2020). It departs from prior certified unlearning methods (Chourasia & Shah, 2023; Chien et al., 2024; Mu & Klabjan, 2024) by eliminating the smoothness assumption on the loss function and the need to know the smoothness constant, and from convex-only methods (Guo et al., 2020; Sekhari et al., 2021) by handling arbitrary non-convex objectives. The key conceptual move is using gradient clipping to bound sensitivity without requiring any structural properties of the loss, making the framework applicable to deep neural networks for the first time in the certified unlearning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first certified unlearning method that provides formal (ε,δ)-guarantees for arbitrary non-convex tasks without smoothness or unique-minimizer assumptions, filling a significant gap between theory and the practical need to unlearn from deep networks under GDPR-style requirements. The 20–50% compute savings over retraining from scratch and the dramatic gap versus DP-SGD (which never exceeds 20% accuracy on CIFAR-100 within budget) are practically relevant, though the experiments use small networks (4k–20k parameters) and the method is restricted to SGD during the unlearning phase. Open questions include whether the curse of dimensionality inherent in the Gaussian mechanism limits scalability to modern large-scale models, and whether the framework can be extended to adaptive optimizers or more complex architectures.
