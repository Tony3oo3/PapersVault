## Problem

One-step text-to-image generators offer extreme inference efficiency but have not been aligned with human preferences, as prior distillation work only matches reference distributions without incorporating reward signals. Existing RLHF approaches for one-step models rely on KL divergence regularization, which exhibits mode-seeking behavior and causes collapse to over-saturated, painting-like outputs that score high on reward but lack diversity and realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251671/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in one forward pass (no iterative denoising). The 2.6B-parameter generator is initialized from DMD2-SDXL-1step and post-trained without any image data—only text prompts from LAION-AESTHETIC (aesthetic score > 6.25) are used. At inference, it requires only 2.22 seconds per 10 images versus 118.64 seconds for FLUX-dev-50step, using 29.3% of the GPU memory.

## Method

Diff-Instruct* (DI*) frames preference alignment as online RLHF but replaces the standard KL divergence regularization with a general score-based divergence, which provably preserves sample diversity. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) shows that the intractable score-divergence objective admits a strictly equivalent tractable loss whose gradient can be computed via an assistant diffusion model that approximates the one-step model's score function. Training alternates between updating this assistant diffusion on student-generated samples and updating the one-step generator with a combined loss of explicit human reward (PickScore), an implicit classifier-free-guidance reward (derived in Theorem 3.2), and the score-based regularization term using a pseudo-Huber distance. Two CFG integration strategies are proposed—implicit CFG reward and CFG-enhanced reference diffusion—with the latter empirically yielding better results. The resulting 2.6B DI*-SDXL-1step model outperforms the 12B FLUX-dev-50step model on ImageReward, PickScore, and CLIPScore on Parti prompts while using only 1.88% of the inference time.

## Results

On the Parti prompts benchmark, the 2.6B DI*-SDXL-1step model (long training) achieves ImageReward 1.140, PickScore 0.2331, Aesthetic Score 5.83, and CLIP Score 32.75, outperforming the 12B FLUX-dev-50step model (ImageReward 1.132, PickScore 0.2317) while using only 1.88% of the inference time (2.22 s vs. 118.64 s per 10 images) and 29.3% of GPU memory. On HPSv2.1, DI*-SDXL-1step sets a new open-source record of 31.19 average (32.26 with DDPO reference), surpassing FLUX-dev (30.70) and SD3.5-Large (30.12). On COCO-2014-30K, DI* is on par with FLUX-dev and SD3.5-Large in PickScore and FID while remaining orders of magnitude faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DI*-SDXL-1step (long training) beats the 12B FLUX-dev-50step model on all four Parti metrics (ImageReward 1.140 vs 1.132, PickScore 0.2331 vs 0.2317, AES 5.83 vs 5.90, CLIP 32.75 vs 31.70) while running 53× faster (2.22 s vs 118.64 s per 10 images). Against the 8B SD3.5-Large-28step, DI* wins on ImageReward (1.140 vs 1.133), PickScore (0.2331 vs 0.2306), and AES (5.83 vs 5.70) with comparable CLIP scores. Among one-step baselines, DI* outperforms DMD2 (PickScore 0.2331 vs 0.2249), Diff-Instruct (0.2331 vs 0.2253), SIM (0.2331 vs 0.2273), and Diff-Instruct++ (0.2331 vs 0.2260), confirming that score-based divergence regularization yields better alignment than KL-based RLHF. On COCO-2014-30K, DI* is roughly on par with FLUX-dev and SD3.5-Large in PickScore (0.2335 vs 0.2333 vs 0.2305) but trails slightly on ImageReward (0.983 vs 1.065), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on challenging, concept-rich Parti prompts. The DDPO-reference variant further improves all metrics (ImageReward 1.210, PickScore 0.2342, HPSv2.1 32.26), demonstrating compatibility with pre-aligned diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diff-Instruct++ (DI++) framework for RLHF-based preference alignment of one-step text-to-image models by replacing the KL divergence regularization with a general score-based divergence, drawing on the Score Implicit Matching (SIM) theory that established the diversity-preserving properties of such divergences in diffusion distillation. The base one-step model is initialized from DMD2-SDXL-1step, and the reference diffusion is SDXL, following the same architecture. A second contribution formalizes classifier-free guidance (CFG) as an implicit reward within the alignment objective (Theorem 3.2), offering two practical integration strategies. The key conceptual departure from DI++ is the regularization choice: where DI++ uses KL (mode-seeking, prone to collapse), DI* uses score-based divergences (diversity-preserving), with a pseudo-Huber distance for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2.6B one-step model matching or exceeding 12B FLUX-dev on human preference metrics at 1.88% inference time opens the door to real-time, high-quality text-to-image generation on edge devices. The finding that KL divergence causes reward hacking (collapse to over-saturated painting-like images) while score-based divergences maintain diversity is a useful diagnostic for anyone applying RLHF to generative models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-step/few-step models, whether reward models can be eliminated (as in DPO-style training), and whether the one-step architecture's known weaknesses (faces, hands) can be addressed without sacrificing efficiency.
