## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees, but its extension to multi-output regression remains underexplored. Existing multivariate methods either produce rigid hyperrectangular regions that ignore output dependencies (M-CP, CopulaCPTS), incur high computational costs from density evaluation or repeated sampling (C-HDR, PCP, HD-PCP), or fail to achieve asymptotic conditional coverage (DR-CP, PCP, ST-DQR). No unified framework exists to compare these methods under identical conditions, and no conformity score simultaneously achieves conditional coverage, computational efficiency, and compatibility with arbitrary generative models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a feature vector x ∈ R^p (p ranges from 1 to 279 across datasets), a trained base predictor (MQF² normalizing flow, Distributional Random Forests, or a hypernetwork-parameterized GMM), and a calibration set of 2048 points. It outputs a multivariate prediction region R̂(x) ⊆ R^d (d = 2–16 for tabular data, d = 3072 for 32×32×3 CIFAR-10 images) with a guaranteed marginal coverage of 1−α. The region geometry varies by method: hyperrectangles (M-CP), density superlevel sets (DR-CP, C-HDR), unions of d-balls (PCP, C-PCP), or quantile regions mapped from latent space (L-CP).

## Method

The paper introduces two novel classes of conformity scores that generalize univariate counterparts to multivariate outputs while ensuring asymptotic conditional coverage. CDF-based scores transform any base conformity score s_W through its conditional CDF: s_CDF(x,y) = F_{W|X=x}(s_W(x,y)), which by the probability integral transform yields a score distributed as U(0,1) conditional on X=x, guaranteeing conditional coverage as |D_cal|→∞. A key special case, C-PCP, applies this transform to PCP's nearest-sample distance score, eliminating the need for density estimation and making it compatible with any generative model (diffusion models, GANs). Latent-based scores (L-CP) instead perform conformalization directly in the latent space of a conditional invertible generative model Q: Z×X→Y, defining s_L-CP(x,y) = ‖Q^{-1}(y;x)‖, which generalizes distributional conformal prediction to multivariate outputs without requiring directional quantile regression or grid construction. The paper further proves that sample-based methods (PCP, HD-PCP, C-PCP) are equivalent to density-based methods (DR-CP, C-HDR) under a max-of-Gaussians density, unifying the two families. All nine methods are implemented in a shared codebase and evaluated across 13 tabular datasets and CIFAR-10 using conditional coverage (WSC, CEC-X, CEC-Z), region size, and computation time.

## Results

Across 13 tabular datasets (7,207–50,000 instances, 2–16 output dimensions) and CIFAR-10 (d=3072), the proposed C-PCP and L-CP methods achieve the best conditional coverage (WSC closest to 1−α), alongside C-HDR, while DR-CP, PCP, HD-PCP, and STDQR are the least conditionally calibrated. On the unimodal toy benchmark (Table 2), C-PCP attains WSC=0.822 and CEC-X=0.046, L-CP attains WSC=0.811 and CEC-X=0.054, versus DR-CP (WSC=0.710) and PCP (WSC=0.713). C-HDR yields the smallest median region size (6.80) among ACC-achieving methods, while DR-CP has the smallest mean region size. Computationally, M-CP and CopulaCPTS are fastest (~0.03–1.04 s), L-CP and DR-CP follow (~0.02 s), and sampling-based methods (C-HDR, C-PCP, PCP) require 100–200× more time (~1.7–3.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against M-CP (Zhou et al., ICML 2024) and CopulaCPTS (Sun & Yu, ICLR 2024), the proposed C-PCP and L-CP achieve substantially better conditional coverage (WSC 0.81–0.82 vs. 0.80–0.81) and produce non-rectangular regions that adapt to output dependencies, at the cost of higher computation time. Compared to DR-CP (Sadinle et al., JASA 2019), which minimizes mean region size but fails to achieve asymptotic conditional coverage (WSC=0.710 vs. 0.822 for C-PCP), the CDF-based scores trade a modest increase in median region size for correct conditional calibration. Against PCP and HD-PCP (Wang et al., AISTATS 2023), C-PCP improves conditional coverage (WSC 0.822 vs. 0.713/0.758) by adapting the ball radius per instance, while retaining the advantage of not requiring a predictive density. Versus STDQR (Feldman et al., JMLR 2023), L-CP achieves ACC without directional quantile regression or grid construction, reducing computation from ~1.77 s to ~0.02 s per test point. C-HDR (Izbicki et al., JMLR 2022) remains the strongest baseline for median region size among ACC methods (6.80 vs. 8.24 for L-CP), but requires both density evaluation and Monte Carlo sampling, making it ~160× slower than L-CP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several threads in conformal prediction for multi-output regression: it generalizes the HPD-split score (Izbicki et al., 2020/2022) to any conformity score via a CDF-based transformation, extends distributional conformal prediction (Chernozhukov et al., 2021) to multivariate outputs through a latent-space formulation, and builds on PCP (Wang et al., 2023b) to propose C-PCP, which avoids density estimation. The latent-based score (L-CP) departs from Feldman et al. (2023) by eliminating directional quantile regression and grid construction, performing conformalization directly in the latent space of an invertible generative model. A key conceptual contribution is showing that sample-based methods (PCP, HD-PCP, C-PCP) are special cases of density-based methods (DR-CP, C-HDR) under a max-density construction, unifying two previously separate families of approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-output conformal prediction remains underexplored relative to the univariate case, and this paper provides the first systematic comparison of nine methods under a unified framework across 13 datasets and three base predictors, giving practitioners clear guidance on trade-offs between conditional coverage, region size, and computational cost. The C-PCP score is particularly notable for its compatibility with any generative model (including diffusion models and GANs) without requiring an explicit density, broadening the applicability of conformal methods. Open questions include the performance of CDF-based scores in low-data regimes where the conditional distribution of the conformity score is hard to estimate, and the extension to complex unstructured outputs (images, text, graphs) where the current tabular evaluation does not fully apply.
