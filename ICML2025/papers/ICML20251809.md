## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability methods proliferate rapidly, but new methods are typically evaluated via ad hoc comparisons on single tasks or models with non-standardized metrics, making it impossible to determine whether a new method represents genuine progress. No large-scale benchmark exists to systematically compare circuit localization or causal variable localization methods across a principled suite of models, tasks, and evaluation protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a neural language model (Llama-3.1 8B, Gemma-2 2B, Qwen-2.5 0.5B, or GPT-2 Small) and a task dataset with fixed counterfactual input pairs (IOI, two-digit Arithmetic, MCQA, ARC), and evaluates either a circuit localization method or a featurizer-plus-feature-selection method. For circuits, it outputs CPR (area under the faithfulness curve, higher is better) and CMD (area between the curve and 1, lower is best) marginalized over circuit size, plus AUROC on a semi-synthetic InterpBench model. For causal variables, it outputs interchange intervention accuracy (proportion of matching outputs between high-level and low-level interventions) or MSE for IOI. A private test set is evaluated via API submission to prevent overfitting to public splits.

## Method

MIB defines two evaluation tracks. The circuit localization track introduces two metrics—CPR and CMD—that split the overloaded 'faithfulness' concept into performance-recovery and behavior-matching objectives, each computed as an area under/between the faithfulness curve across 10 circuit sizes to marginalize over the threshold hyperparameter; circuit size is measured via a weighted edge count that normalizes across granularities (neurons, submodules, edges). The causal variable localization track uses interchange interventions to measure whether a high-level causal variable and its aligned low-level features produce identical outputs under counterfactual swaps, evaluating featurizers (identity, PCA, SAE via GemmaScope/LlamaScope) and selection methods (DBM, DAS) across four tasks with task-specific causal models. Counterfactual inputs are fixed per instance to ensure consistent evaluation, and the benchmark spans four tasks chosen to cover diverse reasoning types, difficulty levels, and answer formats, with two of them (IOI, Arithmetic) previously well-studied and two (MCQA, ARC) chosen precisely because they had not been.

## Results

On the circuit localization track, EAP-IG-inputs with counterfactual ablations achieves the best CMD scores across most task-model combinations (e.g., 0.01 on IOI/Llama-3.1, 0.00 on Arithmetic/Llama-3.1, 0.06 on MCQA/Gemma-2), with EAP(CF) and EAP-IG-activations(CF) close behind. On the causal variable localization track, DAS achieves the highest interchange intervention accuracy (e.g., 95% mean / 97% best-layer on MCQA/Gemma-2 for O_Answer; 77% / 93% for X_Order), while SAE features fail to outperform standard hidden-vector dimensions. On the two-digit addition task, all methods perform poorly (DAS best at 56% mean IIA for Gemma-2), suggesting the hypothesized carry-the-one variable may not exist in a linearly accessible form.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On circuit localization, EAP-IG-inputs(CF) [Hanna et al., 2024] generally achieves the best CMD, outperforming exact activation patching EActP(CF) [Vig et al., 2020] on Qwen-2.5 (0.02 vs. 0.49 on IOI) and InterpBench (0.71 vs. 0.28 AUROC), despite EActP computing exact indirect effects. EAP(CF) [Nanda, 2023] is highly competitive and sometimes ties or beats EAP-IG-inputs (e.g., 0.09 vs. 0.14 on MCQA/Llama-3.1; 0.18 vs. 0.22 on ARC(C)/Llama-3.1). EAP-IG-activations(CF) [Marks et al., 2025] matches EAP-IG-inputs on CMD but degrades on harder tasks (0.37 vs. 0.22 on ARC(C)/Llama-3.1). IFR [Ferrando & Voita, 2024] consistently underperforms all attribution methods (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.01 on IOI/Llama-3.1) while still beating random (0.74). UGS [Li & Janson, 2024] is competitive on CMD for IOI (0.03) but was only tractable on a subset of tasks. On causal variable localization, DAS [Geiger et al., 2024b] dominates all baselines (e.g., 95% vs. 84% for DBM [Cao et al., 2020] on MCQA/Gemma-2 O_Answer). Critically, DBM+SAE [Bricken et al., 2023] does not outperform DBM on standard dimensions (e.g., 51% vs. 63% on MCQA/Gemma-2 X_Order), and DBM+PCA [Tigges et al., 2023] is sometimes worse than the full-vector baseline, challenging the assumption that SAE or PCA features are superior units of analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIB unifies two previously separate evaluation threads in mechanistic interpretability: circuit discovery (building on Wang et al. 2023, Conmy et al. 2023, and the faithfulness formulation from Marks et al. 2025) and causal variable localization (building on the causal abstraction framework of Geiger et al. 2021/2024a and DAS from Geiger et al. 2024b). Prior benchmarks such as InterpBench, RAVEL, CausalGym, and SAEBench each cover only a narrow slice—either a single task, a single method family, or a single model—whereas MIB spans four tasks, four models, and both localization paradigms simultaneously. The paper also draws on attribution patching (Nanda 2023; Syed et al. 2024), mask-based pruning (Li & Janson 2024), and SAE featurizers (GemmaScope, LlamaScope) as baselines, positioning itself as the first large-scale cross-method comparison in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIB provides the first standardized, multi-task, multi-model evaluation framework for MI localization methods, replacing the ad hoc comparisons that have dominated the field and enabling meaningful progress tracking via public leaderboards. The finding that SAE features do not outperform standard neurons for causal variable localization challenges a widely held assumption in the community and suggests that the featurization problem may be harder than dictionary learning alone implies. Open questions remain: the carry-the-one variable was not convincingly localized in arithmetic (suggesting the hypothesized causal model may be wrong), IOI baselines were only run on GPT-2 heads identified by Wang et al., and no exhaustive hyperparameter searches were performed, leaving room for future submissions to substantially improve baseline scores.
