## Problem

Traditional text-based person re-identification assumes witnesses provide complete, single-shot descriptions, but real-world descriptions are often partial and vague. No prior work has explored interactive, dialogue-based person re-identification where a system iteratively refines its understanding by asking targeted questions to the witness, making the task fundamentally different from static text-to-image retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an initial coarse text description (typically 6–34 words simulating a witness's first impression), a gallery of person images, and the accumulated dialogue history of prior Q&A rounds. The Questioner receives a concatenated input of 4 selected candidate images (each reduced to 49 tokens via 2×2 mean pooling), the dialogue context, and a sampled instruction prompt. It outputs a single targeted question per round (descriptive, yes/no, or multiple-choice), while the Retriever produces a ranked list over the gallery. The Answerer, an LLM with access only to fine-grained text descriptions (not images), generates witness responses, mirroring how real witnesses recall details rather than view photos.

## Method

LLaVA-ReID is a selective multi-image questioner built on LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B-ov (fine-tuned with QLoRA at r=128) that addresses two challenges: which candidate images to show and which question to ask. For image selection, a 5-layer Transformer selector takes top-k candidate embeddings and the dialogue embedding, produces per-candidate weights via a linear layer, and selects c=4 most representative candidates using Gumbel-top-k relaxation for differentiable training. For question selection, a looking-forward strategy evaluates each remaining dataset question by simulating its answer, appending it to the dialogue, and measuring the resulting retrieval rank of the target person; the question yielding the highest rank improvement becomes the supervision signal, avoiding the O(t!) cost of enumerating all permutations. The Retriever is a CLIP dual-encoder trained with IRRA on fine-grained descriptions, and the entire pipeline iterates for up to 5 rounds, with the selector's candidate pool shrinking as rounds progress (k=⌈200/t⌉).

## Results

On Interactive-PEDES after 5 rounds of interaction, LLaVA-ReID achieves R@1=73.20, R@5=90.62, R@10=95.95, mAP=53.31, and the lowest BRI of 0.719, improving R@1 by 37.34% over the initial description alone (35.86). When integrated into existing T-ReID frameworks (IRRA and RDE), LLaVA-ReID yields an average R@1 improvement of 5.19% across CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, and RSTPReid benchmarks, with the largest gains on ICFG-PEDES (e.g., RDE+LLaVA-ReID reaches R@1=69.98 vs. 67.68 for RDE alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-ReID outperforms all three interactive retrieval baselines on every metric. Against SimIRV (Liang & Albanie, 2023), a heuristic object-attribute questioner, LLaVA-ReID gains +11.93% R@1 and +6.78% mAP at Round 5. Against ChatIR (Levy et al., 2023), which uses few-shot LLM prompting, the improvement is +9.34% R@1 and +5.26% mAP. Against the strongest baseline PlugIR (Lee et al., 2024), which employs k-means candidate selection and iterative question filtering, LLaVA-ReID achieves +7.76% R@1 and +3.42% mAP at Round 5, while also reducing BRI from 0.849 to 0.719, indicating more efficient refinement per interaction round. On the T-ReID integration experiments (Table 2), LLaVA-ReID consistently boosts IRRA (Jiang & Ye, 2023) and RDE (Qin et al., 2024) by 4–5% R@1 across CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, and RSTPReid, surpassing standalone T-ReID methods including CFine (Yan et al., 2023), APTM (Yang et al., 2023), and AUL (Li et al., 2024) on all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from text-based person ReID (T-ReID), which assumes a static, single-shot description, by formulating re-identification as a multi-round dialogue process. It also extends the interactive cross-modal retrieval line (SimIRV, ChatIR, PlugIR) from general image/video retrieval to the domain-specific, fine-grained setting of person identification, where distinguishing subtle clothing and accessory differences is critical. Architecturally, it builds on LLaVA-OneVision as a multi-image LMM questioner and CLIP as a dual-encoder retriever, but introduces a learned hard-pass selector (using Gumbel-top-k relaxation) and a looking-forward supervision strategy that are absent from prior interactive retrieval methods, which typically rely on top-k or k-means candidate selection and image-to-text captioning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Inter-ReID and the Interactive-PEDES dataset provides the first dedicated benchmark for studying dialogue-based person retrieval, a setting that more faithfully mirrors real-world witness scenarios than single-shot T-ReID. The finding that even human-annotated fine-grained descriptions (ICFG-PEDES) still benefit from interactive refinement underscores a persistent gap between available descriptions and what is needed for reliable identification. Key limitations include the reliance on GPT-4o-generated dialogues (introducing potential annotation bias), an LLM-based Answerer that may not capture real witness uncertainty, and the retriever's limited capacity to handle incremental dialogue-based descriptions. Open questions include whether the looking-forward strategy generalizes to open-ended (non-closed-set) question generation and how the system performs with genuinely uncooperative or uncertain human witnesses.
