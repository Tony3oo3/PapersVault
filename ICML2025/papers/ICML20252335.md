## Problem

Existing d-rectangular distributionally robust MDP (d-DRMDP) algorithms for offline robust RL suffer from two key limitations: theoretically, results are restricted to TV divergence or require hard-to-verify regularity assumptions for KL, with no results for χ²; practically, they rely on dual optimization oracles whose cost scales with feature dimension d and horizon H. The paper asks whether one can design provably efficient offline robust RL algorithms under general f-divergence models while preserving the linear structure of transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K i.i.d. trajectories collected from the nominal environment by a behavior policy, a known simplex-constrained feature mapping φ(s,a) ∈ ℝᵈ (entries non-negative, summing to 1), and a regularization parameter λ that controls robustness. The nominal transition kernels must admit a linear structure P⁰(·|s,a) = ⟨φ(s,a), μ⁰⟩. The output is a deterministic stationary robust policy π̂ along with estimated robust Q-functions and value functions at each time step, with the policy constructed greedily from the estimated Q-functions.

## Method

The paper introduces the d-rectangular linear RRMDP (d-RRMDP), which replaces the d-rectangular uncertainty set constraint of d-DRMDPs with an f-divergence regularization penalty on the transition kernels, preserving the linear structure. The key insight is that this regularization yields closed-form dual solutions for TV (a simple clipping operation) and KL (a log-transform), eliminating the need for the expensive dual optimization oracle required by DRPVI and DRVI-L. The R2PVI algorithm performs backward value iteration: at each step h, it estimates the robust Q-function parameter w_h^λ via ridge regression on the dual formulation, constructs a pessimism penalty Γ_h based on the inverse of the feature covariance matrix, and clips the resulting Q-estimate. For χ²-divergence, the duality involves a variance term requiring an element-wise optimization over a dual variable, but the overall structure remains tractable. The paper proves instance-dependent suboptimality bounds of the form β·sup_{P∈U^λ(P⁰)} Σ_h E^{π*,P}[Σ_i ‖φ_i(s,a)1_i‖_{Λ_h^{-1}}] and matching information-theoretic lower bounds, establishing near-optimality.

## Results

R2PVI achieves instance-independent suboptimality bounds of Õ(d²H²K^{-1/2}) for TV-divergence, Õ(√λ·e^{H/λ}·d²H^{3/2}K^{-1/2}) for KL-divergence, and Õ(d²H³(1+λ^{-1})K^{-1/2}) for χ²-divergence under a regularized partial coverage assumption. The TV bound matches P2MPO, the KL bound matches DRVI-L but without requiring an additional regularity assumption on the KL dual variable, and the χ² bound is the first theoretical result for d-rectangular structured regularization. Computationally, R2PVI runs at the same speed as the non-robust PEVI baseline and is significantly faster than DRPVI and DRVI-L, thanks to closed-form duality solutions for TV and KL divergences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PEVI (Jin et al., 2021), the non-robust baseline, R2PVI underperforms when environment perturbations are small but clearly outperforms it under significant dynamics shifts, confirming the value of robustness. Against DRPVI (Liu & Xu, 2024b) for TV-divergence d-DRMDPs, R2PVI achieves comparable robustness (matching average reward ranges for appropriately chosen λ vs. ρ) while eliminating the need for a dual optimization oracle, resulting in substantially lower computation time that scales comparably to PEVI rather than DRPVI. Against DRVI-L (Ma et al., 2022) for KL-divergence d-DRMDPs, R2PVI matches the suboptimality bound in d and H dependence but removes the need for an additional regularity assumption on the KL dual variable (which can be extremely small, making √β·e^{H/β} ≫ √λ·e^{H/λ}), and also avoids a value-shift technique. The χ²-divergence result has no prior d-rectangular comparison, representing a first. Overall, R2PVI wins on computational efficiency across all baselines while matching or improving theoretical guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research: the d-rectangular linear DRMDP framework (Ma et al., 2022; Liu & Xu, 2024b) that introduces latent structures into transition kernels, and the Robust Regularized MDP (RRMDP) framework (Yang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024) that replaces uncertainty set constraints with f-divergence regularization penalties. The key conceptual move is transplanting the d-rectangular structure into the RRMDP setting, yielding the d-RRMDP, which inherits the linear structure of d-DRMDPs while gaining the computational advantages of the regularization-based formulation. The pessimism principle from Jin et al. (2021) is adapted to handle distribution shift in the offline setting, and the closed-form dualities for TV and KL divergences (building on Zhang et al., 2024 for KL) eliminate the need for the expensive dual optimization oracles required by prior d-DRMDP algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap by providing the first suboptimality bounds and information-theoretic lower bounds for d-rectangular robust RL with χ² divergence, and by removing the hard-to-verify KL dual variable regularity assumption required by prior d-DRMDP results. The computational simplification—closed-form dualities for TV and KL under the RRMDP framework—makes robust offline RL more tractable for high-dimensional features and long horizons. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale simulated environments (5-state linear MDP, 20-step American Put Option), leaving open whether the efficiency gains and robustness properties hold in larger, more realistic settings. The authors also note that tightening the upper and lower bounds to characterize the fundamental hardness of d-RRMDPs remains an open question.
