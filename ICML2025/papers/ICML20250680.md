## Problem

While the EM algorithm is widely used to fit Mixture of Experts models, its convergence properties for MoE have lacked a unified theoretical framework. Prior work by Kunstner et al. (2021) established that EM equals mirror descent for models where the complete data distribution belongs to an exponential family, but this did not extend to MoE, where only the conditional distribution of (y,z) given x is an exponential family. This paper closes that gap by proving EM for MoE is equivalent to projected mirror descent and deriving explicit convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The method operates on feature vectors x drawn from a unit spherical Gaussian in R^d paired with target y, where a latent discrete variable z ∈ [k] routes x to one of k experts. In the Fashion MNIST experiment, 28×28 grayscale images are randomly inverted with probability 1/2 to create a two-component mixture, and the model outputs a binary class prediction. The learned parameters consist of expert weights β ∈ R^{s×k} and gating weights w ∈ R^{d×k}, with the synthetic experiments using d=10 and the image experiments using a 2×28×28 gating layer and two 10×28×28 logistic experts.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 4.1) shows that for general MoE where p(y,z|x) belongs to an exponential family, the EM update is exactly equivalent to a projected mirror descent step on the conditional log-likelihood with unit step size and KL divergence as the Bregman regularizer, where the projection is an expectation-moment projection onto the parameter space. For the special case of symmetric 2-expert mixtures (SymMoLinE and SymMoLogE), the projection disappears and EM reduces to plain mirror descent with an explicitly characterized strictly convex mirror map A(θ). Building on this equivalence, the paper derives three convergence regimes: O(1/T) stationarity, O(1/T) sub-linear convergence to true parameters under local average-convexity, and (1−α)^T linear convergence under local α-strong average-convexity relative to A. For the 2-expert case, sufficient conditions for linear convergence are further characterized via the eigenvalues of the Missing Information Matrix and the signal-to-noise ratio of the generative model.

## Results

On the Fashion MNIST 2-component MoLogE task, EM achieves 78.5% test accuracy with 0.827 cross-entropy loss after 100 iterations, substantially outperforming Gradient EM (66.0%, 1.29) and Gradient Descent (62.4%, 1.30). On the synthetic SymMoLinE benchmark, EM requires significantly fewer iterations to recover the true parameters and minimize the objective compared to both Gradient EM and GD, with all three methods exhibiting linear convergence. Paired t-tests confirm the differences are statistically significant (T-statistic ≥ 17 on Fashion MNIST, ≥ 22 on synthetic data; p-value ≈ 0.000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three optimization strategies for fitting MoE: full EM (solving each sub-problem to convergence), Gradient EM (one gradient step per sub-problem), and standard Gradient Descent (one gradient step on the joint negative log-likelihood). EM dominates both baselines on every metric and dataset: on Fashion MNIST it achieves 78.5% accuracy versus 66.0% (Gradient EM) and 62.4% (GD), a gap of 12.5 and 16.1 percentage points respectively. On the synthetic SymMoLinE task, EM converges to the true parameters in markedly fewer iterations than either baseline, while Gradient EM also consistently outperforms GD. The advantage of EM is attributed to its equivalence to Mirror Descent with a KL divergence regularizer, which better matches the geometry of the latent-variable likelihood than the Euclidean regularization implicit in GD. All reported differences are statistically significant (paired t-test, p-value ≈ 0.000).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EM-as-Mirror-Descent equivalence established by Kunstner et al. (2021) from generative models with exponential-family complete-data distributions to MoE, where only the conditional distribution p(y,z|x) needs to be an exponential family. It also builds on the relative smoothness framework of Lu et al. (2018) to derive convergence guarantees, and departs from Jordan & Xu (1995), who proved EM convergence for MoE under Hessian-based (GD-like) conditions that are more restrictive than the relative-convexity conditions naturally arising from the MD perspective. For the symmetric 2-expert special cases, the paper shows the projection step vanishes entirely, yielding a pure MD equivalence even though the complete-data distribution is provably not an exponential family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MD lens provides a unified and more natural set of sufficient conditions for EM convergence in MoE (relative convexity/smoothness rather than Hessian eigenvalue bounds), and connects convergence rates directly to the Missing Information Matrix and SNR of the generative model. Key open questions include extending the clean MD equivalence to k≥3 experts (where the reparameterization becomes non-linear), scaling the analysis to stochastic/mini-batch EM for large-scale MoE, and whether the projected MD structure can inform better initialization strategies. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale settings (Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10 with 2 experts), so practical impact on modern sparse MoE training remains to be demonstrated.
