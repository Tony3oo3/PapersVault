## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve state-of-the-art video generation quality but suffer from prohibitive latency due to O(n²) self-attention over long video token sequences. Existing acceleration strategies either require expensive fine-tuning (step distillation) or are tightly coupled to specific architectures (feature caching), while naive token merging (ToMe) applied to video DiTs causes visible distortions and pixelation because it applies uniform reductions across all attention features, blocks, and timesteps without accounting for their varying sensitivities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252292/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and plug-and-play: it takes a text prompt and operates on pre-trained video DiT weights (CogVideoX-2B/5B, Mochi-1, HunyuanVideo, FastVideo-Hunyuan, AnimateDiff) without any retraining or architectural modification. It produces generated videos at the model's native specifications (e.g., 49 frames at 480×720 for CogVideoX, 129 frames at 544×960 for HunyuanVideo, 65 frames at 720×1280 for FastVideo-Hunyuan). The only additional inputs are lightweight hyperparameters (similarity thresholds and reduction rates per feature) tuned via ~10 single-inference iterations per model.

## Method

AsymRnR decouples the sequence-length reduction between Q and K&V after their respective projections, allowing each feature to minimize its own covariate shift independently (justified via a KL-divergence nearest-neighbor estimator). A reduction scheduling mechanism adaptively assigns reduction rates across blocks and denoising timesteps by thresholding pre-registered, context-agnostic similarity distributions, concentrating computation where redundancy is low. A matching cache reuses Bipartite Soft Matching results across s consecutive denoising steps, cutting matching overhead by a factor of 1/s. The method uses (negative) Euclidean distance rather than cosine similarity for token matching and directly discards redundant tokens rather than merging them, both of which improve quality and efficiency over the ToMe baseline.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo, AsymRnR reduces latency from 913s to 703s (30% faster) while *improving* VBench from 0.8157 to 0.8240. On Mochi-1, it achieves a 13% speedup (202s→178s) with VBench improving from 0.7911 to 0.7996. On the step-distilled FastVideo-Hunyuan (6-step sampling), AsymRnR delivers a 24% speedup (159s→128s) with VBench 0.8140. On CogVideoX-2B, AsymRnR outperforms ToMe at comparable latency, achieving VBench 0.7917 vs. 0.7825 and LPIPS 0.5511 vs. 0.5562.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (Bolya & Hoffman, 2023) on CogVideoX-2B, AsymRnR achieves a higher VBench score (0.7917 vs. 0.7825) and lower LPIPS (0.5511 vs. 0.5562) at comparable FLOPs (~10.3 vs. ~10.5 ×10¹⁵) and latency (~122s vs. ~124s), indicating better quality preservation at the same speed. Critically, ToMe is incompatible with CogVideoX-5B, Mochi-1, HunyuanVideo, and FastVideo-Hunyuan due to its inability to handle 3D rotary position embeddings (ROPE), so no direct comparison is possible on those models. Against the feature-caching method PAB (Zhao et al., 2024) on HunyuanVideo, AsymRnR is orthogonal and complementary: PAB alone yields a 1.28× speedup (913s→716s, VBench 0.8153), while stacking AsymRnR on top of PAB achieves a combined 1.71× speedup (913s→535s) with only a marginal VBench drop to 0.8140. On the step-distilled FastVideo-Hunyuan, AsymRnR provides an additional 24% speedup over the already-accelerated 6-step baseline, demonstrating compatibility with distillation pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Token Merging (ToMe) line of work (Bolya et al., 2023; Bolya & Hoffman, 2023) from symmetric sequence reduction on the input H to an asymmetric design that independently reduces Q and K&V after their projection, motivated by the observation that these features exhibit different redundancy patterns across blocks and timesteps. It departs from prior token reduction methods in three key ways: (1) a KL-divergence-based theoretical framework justifying the matching criterion and favoring Euclidean over cosine similarity, (2) an adaptive reduction scheduling that allocates budget based on pre-estimated per-block, per-timestep similarity, and (3) a matching cache that amortizes the BSM computation across denoising steps. Unlike distillation or feature-caching approaches, AsymRnR is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and compatible with 3D rotary position embeddings that block symmetric pre-projection reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AsymRnR offers a practical, plug-and-play 10–30% speedup for state-of-the-art video DiTs (CogVideoX, Mochi-1, HunyuanVideo, FastVideo) without retraining, and it stacks additively with orthogonal accelerators like PAB (reaching 1.71× combined) and step distillation. The finding that asymmetric reduction can match or even slightly exceed baseline VBench scores on larger models (Mochi-1, HunyuanVideo) while cutting FLOPs is practically valuable for production video generation pipelines. Open questions include whether the per-model hyperparameter tuning (similarity thresholds, reduction rates) can be automated or learned, whether the SymRnR + AsymRnR combination explored in ablations can be made beneficial rather than detrimental, and whether the approach transfers to image DiTs where sequence lengths are shorter and gains are marginal.
