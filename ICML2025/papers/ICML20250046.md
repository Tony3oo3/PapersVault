## Problem

Achieving both differential privacy and Byzantine robustness in federated learning typically requires a fully trusted central server (central DP), while the no-trust local DP model incurs a fundamental utility penalty that scales linearly with the number of workers. This paper identifies an intermediate threat model—secret-based local DP (SecLDP)—where each pair of workers shares a randomness seed unknown to others, and provides the first analysis of this model in adversarial distributed learning with an untrusted server and colluding malicious workers.

## Input / Output

Each of n workers holds a local dataset of m grayscale images (28×28 for MNIST, 28×28 for Fashion-MNIST) and a set of pairwise shared randomness seeds established via a one-time public-key exchange before training begins. Workers compute clipped mini-batch gradients, inject correlated and independent Gaussian noise, maintain local momentum, and transmit momentum vectors to the untrusted server. The server outputs a trained model parameter vector θ̂ (431,080 parameters for the CNN used in experiments), uniformly sampled from all iterates to satisfy DP composition.

## Method

CAFCOR extends distributed SGD with three key components. First, each honest worker perturbs its clipped gradient with independent Gaussian noise (σ_ind) plus pairwise-canceling correlated Gaussian noise (σ_cor) shared with every other worker, where v_ij = −v_ji ensures correlated terms cancel in aggregate, reducing effective noise seen by the server. Second, workers maintain local momentum before transmission, smoothing stochastic and noise-induced fluctuations. Third, the server applies CAF (Covariance-bound Agnostic Filter), a novel robust aggregation that iteratively down-weights vectors contributing most to variance along the worst eigendirection, tracking the weighted mean with the smallest top eigenvalue until total weight drops below n−2f. Unlike prior robust estimators (e.g., Diakonikolas et al.), CAF requires no bound on honest-input covariance, only an upper bound f on corrupt workers, and runs in polynomial time O(f(nd²+d³)) or O(fnd log d) with a power-method variant. The privacy analysis leverages the exact Rényi divergence of the correlated noise scheme (via the Laplacian matrix of the complete graph and Sherman-Morrison inversion), yielding a privacy-utility trade-off of Õ((f+1)d/n²ε²) that bridges LDP and CDP.

## Results

On MNIST with f=5 malicious workers (ALIE attack, n=100), CAFCOR under SecLDP achieves 83% accuracy, matching the adversary-free DSGD-CDP baseline exactly, while DSGD-LDP is significantly lower. On Fashion-MNIST under the same setting, CAFCOR-SecLDP reaches 72% accuracy, again matching DSGD-CDP. In the robust aggregation comparison (Figure 2, CDP model, example-level DP with ε=13.7), CAF achieves ~90% accuracy under Label Flipping on both datasets (nearly indistinguishable from adversary-free DSGD) and ~80% under Sign Flipping, while the best standard robust aggregation (CWTM, CWMED, or GM) drops to just above 50% under Label Flipping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFCOR (with CAF aggregation) clearly outperforms all four standard robust baselines across every configuration tested. Under Label Flipping on both MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, CAF reaches ~90% accuracy (matching adversary-free DSGD), while CWTM, CWMED, and GM drop to just above 50%, and Meamed performs moderately on MNIST but struggles on Fashion-MNIST. Under Sign Flipping, CAF achieves ~80% accuracy, while Multi-Krum deteriorates significantly on MNIST and Meamed struggles on both datasets. The only near-tie is Multi-Krum matching CAFCOR on Fashion-MNIST under Label Flipping, but MK's advantage vanishes under Sign Flipping and on MNIST. The paper also shows that replacing CAF with trimmed mean in the full CAFCOR pipeline degrades the theoretical convergence rate from Õ((f+1)d/n²ε²) to Õ(fd/nε²), an n-fold worsening, confirming CAF's critical role in the privacy-utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: correlated-noise privacy mechanisms (Allouah et al., 2024), high-dimensional robust mean estimation via iterative filtering (Diakonikolas et al., 2017), and the privacy-robustness-utility trilemma in distributed learning (Allouah et al., 2023c). It departs from prior work by introducing the first analysis of the SecLDP threat model in an adversarial setting with an untrusted server and colluding malicious workers, and by designing the CAF aggregation, which adapts the Diakonikolas-style iterative filter but removes the need for a covariance bound on honest inputs—a requirement that prior robust estimators treated as essential. The correlated noise scheme leverages pairwise secret sharing (Shamir, 1979) in a way that prior decentralized DP work (Sabater et al., 2022; Allouah et al., 2024) had not combined with Byzantine robustness or utility guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a significantly weaker trust assumption—pairwise shared randomness among workers—suffices to bridge the large utility gap between local DP and central DP in federated learning, which is practically important for cross-silo settings where no single entity can be fully trusted. The CAF aggregation's ability to satisfy the high-dimensional robustness criterion (bounding error via max eigenvalue rather than trace) without covariance assumptions makes it broadly applicable. However, empirical validation is limited to MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with relatively large privacy budgets (ε ≈ 26–40), leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex vision tasks or tighter privacy regimes. The authors explicitly flag as an open question whether CAFCOR achieves the optimal utility under SecLDP, and whether the quadratic one-time seed-exchange cost is acceptable in large-scale deployments.
