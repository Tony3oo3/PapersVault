## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs (e.g., LLM-Pruner, SlimGPT, LoRAP) require tens of thousands of annotated instruction samples and hours of GPU time for LoRA-based retraining to recover performance after pruning. This makes them prohibitively expensive in resource-constrained settings, particularly for domain-specific pruning where annotating large instruction datasets from scratch is impractical.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B/13B, LLaMA-2, Vicuna), only 256 calibration text samples (128 tokens each, drawn from Bookcorpus/Alpaca or C4/Alpaca), and a target sparsity ratio (20%–50%). It outputs a structurally pruned model with reduced MHA head dimensions and FFN intermediate widths, plus per-layer low-rank calibration matrices (rank r ≈ 3% of d) inserted into the residual stream. Unlike baselines requiring 50K samples and 1–3 hours of retraining, Olica completes pruning in ~7 minutes on a single A100 with 3 GB peak memory.

## Method

Olica's core insight is that the MHA layer depends on two matrix products (W_q W_k^T and W_v W_o^T), which can be treated as unified entities and compressed via PCA without retraining. Orthogonal Neuron Decomposition (OND) applies SVD to these products, retaining eigenvectors selected by activation-weighted importance scores rather than magnitude alone, ensuring the surviving neurons carry distinct information. A Fast-OND variant exploits the empirically similar singular-value distributions of W_v vs. W_o (and W_q vs. W_k) to perform SVD on only one factor, reducing complexity from O(h·d³) to O(d³/h). For FFN pruning, neurons are removed by activation-weighted importance, and a linear calibration step reconstructs residual errors via the closed-form ridge-regression solution, further compressed to two low-rank matrices (2dr parameters) via SVD. Layer selection for calibration uses the Multiple Correlation Coefficient (MC²) to target only those FFN layers whose errors are linearly recoverable, keeping extra parameters under ~1% of model size.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B, Olica achieves the best average accuracy and lowest PPL across all tested sparsity ratios without any retraining: at 20% SR, 64.54% avg accuracy and 15.35 PPL; at 25% SR, 63.54% and 16.69; at 33% SR, 61.21% and 19.83. For LLaMA-13B at 20% SR, Olica reaches 67.67% avg accuracy and 13.68 PPL, outperforming all baselines. The method also leads on LLaMA-2-7B (30% SR: 61.14% avg, 18.54 PPL) and Vicuna-7B (20% SR: 64.88% avg, 20.23 PPL), while requiring only 256 calibration samples, 3 GB peak GPU memory, and ~7 minutes runtime versus hours and tens of thousands of samples for retraining-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Olica consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on both PPL and average accuracy without requiring LoRA fine-tuning. Against LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., 2023), Olica achieves 4.88 PPL lower and 4.87% higher average accuracy at 25% SR on LLaMA-7B (16.69 vs. 20.57 PPL; 63.54% vs. 58.67%), while LLM-Pruner requires 50K samples and 3 hours of retraining. Against SlimGPT (Ling et al., 2024), Olica leads by 2.42 PPL and 1.07% accuracy at 25% SR, and the gap widens at 33% SR (19.83 vs. 24.55 PPL; 61.21% vs. 60.37%). Against LoRAP (Li et al., 2024), the closest competitor, Olica achieves 0.75 PPL lower and 0.97% higher accuracy at 25% SR, and 1.83 PPL lower at 33% SR. Against Compresso (Guo et al., 2023) and FALP (An et al., 2024), Olica leads by 2+ PPL and 4–5% accuracy at 20–25% SR. Against LLM-Surgeon (van der Ouderaa et al., 2024) on LLaMA-2-7B at 30% SR, Olica achieves 2.71 PPL lower and 4.58% higher accuracy (18.54 vs. 21.25 PPL; 61.14% vs. 56.56%). Against DISP-LLM (Gao et al., 2024) and SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024), Olica leads by 4–7% average accuracy. In the ablation (Table 5), Fast-OND also outperforms Wanda (Sun et al., 2024) by 0.6 PPL and 0.86% accuracy at 33% SR, confirming the advantage of the orthogonal decomposition over direct activation-weighted neuron removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage structured pruning paradigm (prune then LoRA-retrain) established by LLM-Pruner, LoRAP, and SlimGPT, which all require tens of thousands of annotated samples and hours of GPU time for retraining. Conceptually, Olica builds on Wanda's activation-weighted importance scoring for neuron selection and on the PCA/SVD-based compression ideas from SliceGPT, but applies them to the MHA layer in a novel way: treating W_q W_k^T and W_v W_o^T as unified entities rather than pruning individual weight matrices. The linear calibration component draws on ridge regression and low-rank approximation, but its application to reconstruct FFN residual errors without iterative optimization is the key methodological departure from prior work that relied on gradient-based fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: pruning a 7B model in 7 minutes with 3GB of GPU memory and 256 calibration samples, versus hours and 30GB for prior methods, makes structured pruning accessible in resource-constrained and domain-specific settings where annotating large instruction datasets is infeasible. However, performance gains over the best retraining-based baselines (SlimGPT, LoRAP) are modest (1–3 points in average accuracy), and at 50% sparsity all methods degrade significantly, suggesting a fundamental tolerance limit. Open questions include whether the unified-entity PCA approach generalizes to architectures without standard MHA (e.g., grouped-query attention, sliding-window attention), whether the RoPE workaround of applying separate PCA to W_q and W_k is suboptimal, and whether the linear calibration strategy can handle deeper error accumulation in very deep models.
