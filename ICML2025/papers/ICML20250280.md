## Problem

Multimodal fusion models frequently exhibit modality collapse, relying on only a subset of available modalities while ignoring the rest, yet prior work offered only empirical observations and a priori conjectures (e.g., conflicting gradients) without a bottom-up learning-theoretic explanation. This paper develops a mechanistic theory showing that collapse arises from cross-modal polysemantic entanglements in the fusion head, driven by the low-rank simplicity bias of SGD, and proposes both an implicit remedy (cross-modal knowledge distillation) and an explicit algorithm (Explicit Basis Reallocation) to prevent it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on multimodal samples where each modality is independently encoded before fusion: for MIMIC-IV, six modalities (clinical notes, lab values, demographics, diagnosis, procedure, medications) are fused to predict mortality or readmission; for avMNIST, a digit image and its corresponding audio clip are fused to predict the digit label (0–9). All architectural modifications are confined to the unimodal encoder level, leaving the fusion operator untouched, which makes the approach agnostic to the choice of fusion strategy. After training with EBR, the learned latent encodings g_i(x_i) additionally yield a feature-to-basis mapping across modalities that identifies substitution candidates for handling missing modalities at inference time.

## Method

The paper first proves that as the number of modalities grows, the fraction of cross-modal polysemantic neurons in the fusion head increases quadratically (Lemma 1), and that the low-rank simplicity bias of SGD (Lemma 2) forces the fusion head to parameterize these neurons polysemantically (Theorem 2), entangling noisy features of one modality with predictive features of another and suppressing the latter's contribution to loss reduction (Theorem 1). Cross-modal knowledge distillation from surviving to collapsing modalities implicitly frees the rank bottleneck by aligning modality representations under a distance metric, encouraging monosemanticity and enabling independent denoising (Theorem 3). Building on this, Explicit Basis Reallocation (EBR) adds a lightweight encoder-decoder pair h_i · h_i^{-1} on top of each modality encoder and a modality-discriminator network ψ, trained adversarially: ψ is updated to maximize modality discrimination loss while the encoder g_i is updated to minimize it, explicitly bringing all modalities within an ε-neighborhood and reallocating basis dimensions so that noisy features occupy dedicated, denoisable directions. The resulting EBR latents are then used to rank modalities by similarity to a reference modality, enabling principled substitution of missing modalities at test time via h_i^{-1}(g_j(x_j)).

## Results

On MIMIC-IV (averaged across missingness rates of 0.1–0.7), EBR achieves 0.8533 AUC-ROC and 0.4277 AUC-PRC for Mortality prediction, and 0.7030 AUC-ROC and 0.4290 AUC-PRC for Readmission prediction, outperforming the strongest baseline MUSE (ICLR'24) by approximately 3% in AUC-ROC on both tasks. On avMNIST with 95% audio missingness, EBR reaches 95.30% accuracy versus 94.21% for MUSE. EBR also consistently improves upon each fusion strategy it is plugged into (e.g., MUSE+EBR reaches 0.9380 AUC-ROC vs. 0.9201 for vanilla MUSE on Mortality).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBR outperforms all ten baselines across both MIMIC-IV and avMNIST. The closest competitor is MUSE (ICLR'24), which EBR surpasses by ~3% AUC-ROC on MIMIC-IV Mortality (0.8533 vs. 0.8236) and Readmission (0.7030 vs. 0.6781), and by ~1.1% accuracy on avMNIST at 95% audio missingness (95.30% vs. 94.21%). M3Care (SIGKDD'22) and MT (CVPR'22) trail MUSE by 1–3% AUC-ROC on MIMIC-IV, while SMIL (AAAI'21), ShaSpec (CVPR'23), Grape (NeurIPS'20), and CM-AE (ICML'11) are further behind. On avMNIST, EBR also beats ShaSpec (95.30% vs. 93.33%), SMIL (95.30% vs. 92.89%), GAN (95.30% vs. 89.11%), Full2miss (95.30% vs. 90.00%), and Autoencoder (95.30% vs. 89.78%) at 95% audio missingness. Notably, EBR is fusion-agnostic: when plugged into Grape, M3Care, or MUSE as a backbone replacement, it yields consistent 1–2% AUC-ROC gains over each vanilla model (Table 2), confirming that the benefit comes from the encoder-level basis reallocation rather than a specific fusion architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground by being the first to apply polysemanticity (Scherlis et al., 2022; Elhage et al., 2022) and low-rank simplicity bias (Huh et al., 2023; Radhakrishnan et al., 2024) tools—previously restricted to unimodal analysis—to explain modality collapse in multimodal fusion. Prior work (Javaloy et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2024) documented the phenomenon empirically and offered hypotheses like conflicting gradients or distribution-fusion interactions, but provided no bottom-up learning-theoretic mechanism. The paper also repurposes cross-modal knowledge distillation (Tian et al., 2020) not as a training trick but as a theoretical instrument that implicitly frees rank bottlenecks, and derives from this analysis the Explicit Basis Reallocation (EBR) algorithm, which is agnostic to the choice of fusion operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying polysemantic entanglement of noisy and predictive features as the mechanistic root of modality collapse, the paper reframes a widely observed but poorly understood failure mode and offers a principled, fusion-agnostic remedy (EBR) that achieves SOTA on missing-modality benchmarks in healthcare (MIMIC-IV) and audio-visual (avMNIST). Open questions include extending the theoretical bounds to settings where features carry unequal conditional mutual information, formalizing the conjectured saddle-to-convex loss landscape transition under EBR, and validating the framework on larger-scale or more heterogeneous multimodal settings. The identifiability assumption (up to equivariances) limits the generality of the substitution strategy for missing modalities, and the practical applicability of EBR to very high-dimensional or continuous modalities remains untested.
