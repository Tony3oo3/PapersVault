## Problem

State-of-the-art non-parametric regression methods such as Trend Filtering achieve optimal statistical rates only when a regularization hyperparameter is tuned with oracle knowledge of the ground truth's total variation. Online learning algorithms can adapt to smoothness without such tuning, but their output is a non-smooth sequence of point predictions rather than a proper regression function, making them unsuitable for inference. The paper asks whether the instance-dependent adaptivity of online methods can be injected into an inherently offline spline-fitting procedure to yield a parameter-free estimator that is both a smooth function and statistically optimal.

## Input / Output

The method takes n sorted (typically equally spaced) covariates x_i = i/n, noisy responses y_i = f(x_i) + ε_i with ε_i ~ N(0, σ²), and the noise variance σ² as explicit input. It outputs a continuous linear spline function f̂(x) together with the adaptive knot set K ⊂ {x_1, …, x_n}; the spline is the least-squares projection of Y onto the space of linear splines with knots in K. Unlike standard regression splines, the number and locations of knots are data-driven and scale as Õ(n^{1/5} TV_1[f]^{2/5}), and the algorithm requires no user-specified regularization parameter.

## Method

AKORN reduces adaptive knot selection to optimal online denoising. It runs ADDLE (Adaptive Denoising with Linear Experts), an extension of Aligator that applies Follow-the-Leading-History (FLH) expert aggregation over online linear regression experts, in both forward and backward passes over the data. During each pass, a knot is placed and the online learner restarts whenever the cumulative squared distance between ADDLE's predictions and a local linear fit exceeds a threshold proportional to σ² log(n²/δ), effectively detecting where the ground truth ceases to be locally linear. The forward and backward knot sets K_f and K_b are combined with crossover points of two preliminary piecewise-linear fits to form the final knot set K, onto which a standard least-squares linear spline is projected. The key theoretical contribution is a fictitious-estimator argument that decouples the statistical dependence between the data-adaptive knots and the responses, yielding the instance-dependent rate Õ(n^{-4/5} TV_1[f]^{2/5}) without requiring TV_1[f] to be bounded by a known constant.

## Results

On the Doppler function (n=1000), AKORN achieves MSE of 0.0039 at σ=0.3, compared to 0.0023 for Oracle Trend Filtering and 0.003 for DoF Trend Filtering, while Wavelets lags at 0.024. In terms of empirical convergence rates, AKORN achieves a rate of −1 on piecewise-linear functions (matching the parametric rate), −0.91 on the spatially heterogeneous Doppler function (close to the theoretical −4/5), and −0.7 on the Jump function, substantially outperforming Oracle Trend Filtering's −0.37 on the latter. At higher noise (σ=0.5), AKORN's MSE (0.007) matches DoF Trend Filtering exactly and is within 23% of Oracle Trend Filtering (0.0057).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Oracle Trend Filtering (Tibshirani, 2014), which is given oracle knowledge of the ground truth's smoothness for hyperparameter tuning, AKORN is competitive on standard functions (Doppler MSE within ~70% at σ=0.3) but substantially outperforms it on the pathological Jump function, achieving an empirical rate of −0.7 versus Oracle TF's −0.37. Against DoF Trend Filtering (Tibshirani & Taylor, 2012), which uses Stein's Unbiased Risk Estimator for data-driven parameter selection, AKORN is slightly behind at low noise (0.0008 vs 0.0006 at σ=0.1) but matches it exactly at σ=0.5 (both 0.007), and does so without any hyperparameter tuning. Against Wavelet shrinkage (Donoho & Johnstone, 1998), the only other parameter-free method with optimal TV₁ guarantees, AKORN is a clear winner: at σ=0.3, AKORN's MSE is 0.0039 versus 0.024 for Wavelets, a roughly 6× improvement. Overall, AKORN's key advantage is that it matches or beats methods requiring oracle or heuristic parameter selection while remaining fully parameter-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

AKORN extends the Aligator algorithm (Baby et al., 2021) from TV_0 online denoising to TV_1 via ADDLE, which uses Follow-the-Leading-History (Hazan et al., 2006) with online linear regression experts. The key conceptual departure is from prior online-to-offline reductions: rather than simply interpolating point predictions (which performs poorly offline), AKORN uses forward and backward online passes to adaptively select knots for a regression spline, producing a continuous function. This positions AKORN as the first knot-selection algorithm with provable statistical guarantees, filling a gap between the well-studied Trend Filtering (which requires oracle-tuned hyperparameters) and the online learning literature (which produces non-smooth prediction sequences).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AKORN provides the first parameter-free method achieving the optimal instance-dependent rate Õ(n^{-4/5} TV_1[f]^{2/5}) for TV_1 regression without requiring knowledge of the ground truth's smoothness, and it empirically matches or exceeds Oracle Trend Filtering on standard test functions. Two main limitations remain: the method only handles TV_1 (extending to TV_k for k>1 would require generalizing the spline existence lemma to higher-degree splines), and the O(n²) runtime is slower than Trend Filtering's O(n^{3/2} log(1/ε)). The broader implication is that online learning's adaptivity machinery can substitute for oracle hyperparameter tuning in offline statistical problems, potentially opening a new class of parameter-free estimators.
