## Problem

While deep CNNs and ICA both learn Gabor-like filters from natural images, the precise sample complexity required by the two main ICA algorithms—FastICA and SGD—to escape the search phase in high dimensions was not rigorously established. This paper fills that gap by proving sharp algorithmic thresholds: FastICA requires n ≳ d⁴ samples to recover a single non-Gaussian direction, while smoothed SGD can reach the optimal computational threshold of n ≳ d².

## Input / Output

The method operates on centered and whitened d-dimensional data vectors (64×64 ImageNet patches or synthetic spiked cumulant model points), where whitening removes all second-order structure so that only higher-order non-Gaussianity along a hidden spike direction v remains. The output is a unit vector w ∈ S^{d-1} that maximizes the non-Gaussianity (measured by a contrast function such as −exp(−s²/2) or excess kurtosis s⁴−3) of the projections s = w·x, yielding Gabor-like filters or independent components. In the synthetic setting, the goal is to recover the planted spike v from n i.i.d. samples drawn from a noisy ICA model with Rademacher latent variables.

## Method

The core technical tool is rewriting the non-Gaussian population expectation as a Gaussian expectation weighted by a likelihood ratio ℓ(v·x), then expanding both the contrast function G and ℓ in Hermite polynomial series to express the loss as a power series in the overlap α = w·v. This reveals an information exponent k* = 4 for the whitened ICA model, governing the sample complexity. For FastICA, a one-step (giant-step) analysis shows the regularisation term E[G″(w·x)]w is essential for escaping mediocrity, but the gradient still fails to concentrate until n ≳ d⁴ (Theorem 2). For SGD, the paper shows vanilla online spherical SGD achieves cubic sample complexity n ≳ d³, and then proves that smoothing the loss with a large-λ operator (following Biroli et al. and Damian et al.) reduces this to the optimal quadratic n ≳ d², but only when the contrast function's own information exponent k₂* matches the loss's k₁* = 4—making the fourth-order Hermite polynomial (excess kurtosis) the optimal data-dependent contrast function.

## Results

The paper establishes sharp sample complexity thresholds for recovering a single non-Gaussian direction in d-dimensional inputs. FastICA requires n ≳ d⁴ samples (Theorem 2), while vanilla online SGD requires n ≳ d³. Smoothed online SGD achieves the optimal computational threshold of n ≳ d² if and only if the information exponents of the loss and contrast function match (k₁* = k₂* = 4), as demonstrated with the excess kurtosis contrast G(s) = s⁴ − 3. On ImageNet patches, FastICA exhibits a clear search phase at linear sample complexity (n = 2d) but recovers a non-Gaussian direction at quadratic complexity (n = 2d²), faster than the d⁴ bound predicted for the synthetic spiked model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between FastICA and SGD as algorithms for ICA. Vanilla online SGD outperforms FastICA by one order of magnitude in sample complexity: SGD recovers the spike at n ≳ d³ while FastICA requires n ≳ d⁴ (Theorem 2), a gap confirmed experimentally in Figures 2 and 3. The paper then shows that smoothing the loss landscape (à la Biroli et al. 2020; Damian et al. 2023) closes the remaining statistical-to-computational gap, reducing SGD's sample complexity from d³ to the optimal d², but only when the contrast function's information exponent matches the loss's (k₂* = k₁* = 4). Critically, the standard contrast functions used in practice (G(s) = −exp(−s²/2) or log cosh(as)/a) have k₂* = 2 ≠ 4, so they do not benefit from smoothing and remain at cubic complexity. The excess kurtosis G(s) = s⁴ − 3, the most classical non-Gaussianity measure, is the optimal contrast function achieving d². On real ImageNet data, FastICA recovers a direction at n = 2d² (quadratic), faster than its d⁴ synthetic bound, which the authors attribute to the strong finite-size non-Gaussianity of natural image patches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work on high-dimensional learning dynamics (Ben Arous et al. 2021, Damian et al. 2023, Dandi et al. 2024) from supervised settings with Gaussian inputs to the unsupervised ICA problem with explicitly non-Gaussian inputs, using likelihood ratio techniques and Hermite polynomial expansions. It builds on the classical observation that ICA recovers Gabor-like filters similar to CNN first layers (Olshausen & Field, Bell & Sejnowski, Hyvärinen & Oja) and on Auddy & Yuan (2024), who established fundamental statistical and computational limits for ICA but did not analyze the specific algorithms used in practice. The smoothing approach from Biroli et al. (2020) and Damian et al. (2023) for tensor PCA and single-index models is generalized here to the mismatched case where the contrast function and the data distribution have different information exponents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that FastICA requires n ≳ d⁴ samples to escape the search phase (versus the d² fundamental limit) provides a rigorous explanation for its well-known practical failures in high dimensions, and the identification of the regularisation term as essential for signal recovery (not merely for convergence speed) is a conceptual insight into the algorithm's structure. The result that smoothing the loss with an optimally tuned contrast function (the excess kurtosis, the most classical non-Gaussianity measure) closes the statistical-to-computational gap for SGD suggests a principled path to efficient ICA. Open questions include extending the analysis to multiple non-Gaussian directions, understanding whether the strong non-Gaussianity of real images (which empirically accelerates FastICA beyond theoretical predictions) can be exploited algorithmically, and developing synthetic models that produce localized oriented filters.
