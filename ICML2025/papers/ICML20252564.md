## Problem

Causal discovery from high-dimensional spatiotemporal gridded data is hindered by scalability issues, spatial correlations between proximate points that mask true causal links, and the fact that existing two-stage approaches perform dimensionality reduction independently of causal structure. Prior causal representation learning methods either ignore spatial structure or impose restrictive assumptions (no instantaneous effects, single-parent decoding, sufficient variability) that are hard to validate in practice.

## Input / Output

SPACY takes N samples of L-dimensional time series (T timesteps each) arranged on a K-dimensional spatial grid (K=2 in practice, coordinates scaled to [0,1]^K), with grid sizes up to 250×250 (L=62,500). It produces D-dimensional latent time series (D≪L, typically 10–30), a temporal causal DAG with adjacency matrix of shape (τ+1)×D×D encoding both lagged and instantaneous edges, and spatial factors F∈R^{L×D} parameterized by RBF kernel centers and scales. A multivariate extension handles V variates on the same grid with variate-specific spatial factors.

## Method

SPACY is an end-to-end variational inference framework that jointly learns latent time series, a causal DAG, and spatial factors by optimizing an ELBO. Spatial factors are parameterized by RBF kernels (center ρ_d and scale γ_d per latent dimension) that aggregate spatially proximate grid points, mapping the L-dimensional observation to a D-dimensional latent space via X_ℓ^(t) = g_ℓ([FZ]_ℓ^(t)) + ε_ℓ^(t), where g_ℓ is a shared MLP with grid-point embeddings. The latent SCM uses Rhino's architecture (MLPs for structural equations, conditional spline flows for history-dependent noise), while the causal graph is sampled via Gumbel-Softmax with a continuous DAG constraint. The key theoretical contribution is an identifiability proof for a continuous spatial domain: by leveraging the overdetermined structure (L≫D) and real-analytic properties of the spatial factor functions, the latent processes are shown to be recoverable up to permutation and scaling without assumptions on instantaneous effects, sparsity, or sufficient variability.

## Results

On synthetic spatiotemporal data (100×100 grids, T=100, N=100 samples), SPACY consistently achieves the highest orientation F1 score and mean correlation coefficient (MCC) across all latent dimensions D ∈ {10, 20, 30} and all SCM/mapping configurations (linear/nonlinear). In nonlinear settings, SPACY's advantage over baselines widens substantially as D increases, while LEAP, TDRL, and Linear-Response show sharp F1 degradation. SPACY scales to 250×250 grids with moderate runtime growth, maintaining strong F1 scores where Mapped-PCMCI (fastest) underperforms and Linear-Response scales poorly in runtime. On the Global Climate Dataset, SPACY qualitatively recovers known teleconnection patterns (MJO, NAO, AAO) with spatially confined, interpretable factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPACY outperforms all five baselines (Mapped-PCMCI, Linear-Response, LEAP, TDRL, CDSD) on F1 score across every synthetic configuration. On linear SCM datasets, SPACY is only slightly ahead of CDSD and Mapped-PCMCI, but in nonlinear settings the gap becomes pronounced, with LEAP, TDRL, and Linear-Response dropping sharply as D grows from 10 to 30. For latent recovery (MCC), SPACY matches or exceeds CDSD and Mapped-PCMCI, while LEAP, TDRL, and Linear-Response consistently trail. In scalability tests (30×30 to 250×250), Mapped-PCMCI is the fastest in runtime but underperforms in causal discovery; LEAP, TDRL, and CDSD incur similar or higher computational costs than SPACY without matching its F1; Linear-Response scales poorly in runtime. On the climate dataset, SPACY produces spatially localized, interpretable factors corresponding to known oscillation patterns, whereas Mapped-PCMCI yields broadly distributed, harder-to-interpret components.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACY sits at the intersection of causal representation learning (LEAP, TDRL, CDSD) and spatiotemporal causal discovery (Mapped-PCMCI, Linear-Response), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike two-stage approaches that perform dimensionality reduction independently of causal structure, SPACY jointly infers latent time series and the causal DAG via variational inference, using RBF spatial kernels to aggregate proximate grid points. For the latent SCM, it builds directly on Rhino (Gong et al., 2023), inheriting its support for instantaneous edges and history-dependent noise via conditional spline flows. The identifiability theory extends prior temporal identifiability results (Yao et al., 2022a,b; Lachapelle et al., 2024) to a continuous spatial domain, replacing restrictive temporal assumptions (no instantaneous effects, sufficient variability, sparsity) with the overdetermined structure of spatially redundant observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPACY addresses a practically critical gap: causal discovery from high-dimensional gridded data (climate, neuroscience, epidemiology) where existing methods either fail to scale or produce uninterpretable latent factors. The identifiability guarantee under minimal assumptions—leveraging spatial redundancy rather than temporal regularity—is a genuine theoretical advance that could inform future latent-variable causal models. Limitations include the isotropic RBF kernel (which simplifies anisotropic atmospheric dynamics), the continuous-domain assumption in the theory (practically mitigated by L >> D), and reliance on Rhino's own identifiability conditions for the causal graph. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-stationary latent processes, higher-dimensional grids (K > 2), and whether the kernel choice can be learned adaptively per region.
