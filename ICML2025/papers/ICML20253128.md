## Problem

Evaluating LVLMs across hundreds of benchmarks is prohibitively expensive in both compute and wall-clock time, yet prior efficiency methods (e.g., TinyBenchmarks, LMMs-Eval) only reduce cost within a single benchmark and ignore the strong cross-model and cross-task correlations in performance. This paper asks whether observed scores from other models or tasks can be leveraged to predict unknown scores, thereby avoiding unnecessary evaluations entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse M×N×S performance tensor (108 models × 176 datasets × 6 metrics) where only a subset of entries are observed, augmented with structured model profiles (vision encoder type, LLM parameter count, model family) and dataset profiles (one-hot cluster assignments derived from text or image embeddings). It outputs predicted scores for every unobserved entry, per-entry uncertainty estimates (standard deviation across 100 MCMC posterior samples), and a priority ranking of model-dataset pairs that guides which evaluations to run next to most efficiently reduce overall prediction error.

## Method

The core approach formulates performance prediction as matrix completion via PMF, decomposing the score matrix into low-rank latent factors U (models) and V (datasets), and uses NUTS-based MCMC to sample the full posterior over scores rather than a single point estimate. Three enhancements target the sparse-data regime where standard PMF collapses to predicting global averages: (1) a Probabilistic Tensor Factorization (PTF) that models multiple metrics jointly by applying a shared latent dot product through a metric-specific linear transformation (weight vector w and bias b); (2) Bayesian PMF replacing fixed Gaussian priors with LKJ correlation priors on the latent factor covariance and Exponential priors on variance; and (3) Constrained PMF that adds profile-driven features (H·Y for models, G·X for datasets) to the latent vectors. The MCMC posterior simultaneously enables active evaluation: unobserved entries are ranked by prediction uncertainty, the highest-uncertainty pairs are evaluated first, and the model is re-fitted iteratively as new observations arrive, yielding a practical loop for budget-constrained model assessment.

## Results

On a 108×176 performance matrix of LVLMs across 36 benchmarks, PMF with MCMC achieves 0.192 overall RMSE at 20% observed data, nearly halving the error of the Global Mean baseline (0.367) and Mean of Means (0.319). At 80% test ratio, PMF still outperforms baselines (0.258 vs. 0.373 and 0.329), but at 95% test ratio it degrades below both baselines (0.462 vs. 0.458 and 0.385). Against deep learning matrix completion, PMF achieves 0.193 RMSE at 20% test ratio versus 0.225 for DMF, and 0.317 versus 0.561 for DMF at 80% test ratio. The enhanced PTF model further improves sparse-data prediction, reaching 0.205 overall RMSE at 20% test ratio across six metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMF consistently outperforms both simple baselines (Global Mean and Mean of Means) across all test ratios from 20% to 90%, with the largest gap at 20% observed data where PMF achieves 0.192 RMSE versus 0.367 and 0.319 respectively — a roughly 48% and 40% reduction in error. At 95% test ratio (only 5% data observed), PMF degrades below both baselines, confirming the known sparse-data limitation of matrix factorization. Against deep learning-based matrix completion, PMF substantially outperforms DMF at both 20% (0.193 vs. 0.225 RMSE) and 80% (0.317 vs. 0.561) test ratios, with the gap widening dramatically as data becomes sparser. GCMC is only evaluated on accuracy scores (due to its discrete-classification design) and achieves 0.187 RMSE at 20% test ratio, slightly below PMF's 0.074 on the accuracy subset but without coverage of BART/BERT scores. The authors attribute PMF's advantage over deep learning methods to the relatively small dataset size (108 models, 176 datasets, ~19K samples) where neural networks are more data-hungry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (PMF, Mnih & Salakhutdinov, 2007) and its Bayesian variant (Salakhutdinov & Mnih, 2008) from recommender systems to the novel domain of cross-model, cross-task LVLM performance prediction. It departs from prior evaluation-efficiency work (TinyBenchmarks, LMMs-Eval) that predicts a single model's performance using a coreset of the same benchmark, instead exploiting correlations across different models and tasks. The active-evaluation component is a natural extension of MCMC-based uncertainty quantification, and the enhancements (tensor factorization for multi-metric scoring, LKJ priors, Constrained PMF with model/dataset profiles) are adaptations of existing techniques to address the specific sparsity challenges of the LVLM evaluation matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-cost way to reduce the substantial computational burden of LVLM evaluation (estimated ~20 hours per 7B model on 50 benchmarks), and the constructed 108×176 performance matrix is a valuable community resource. The paper demonstrates complementarity with coreset methods (Table 4), suggesting a combined pipeline could further reduce evaluation costs. Key limitations remain: PMF degrades below baseline performance when fewer than ~10% entries are observed, generalization to entirely new models or datasets without any prior scores is weak, and the linear-metric assumption in PTF can hurt performance for non-linearly related metrics like BART/BERT scores. Open questions include better encoding of dataset-level information and extending the framework to handle models with zero prior performance data.
