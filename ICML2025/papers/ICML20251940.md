## Problem

Group equivariant neural networks encode classical group symmetries but are invalid for data living in non-commutative geometry, since the C*-algebra of continuous functions on any compact matrix group is commutative and cannot capture the non-commutative algebraic structure of such spaces. This paper addresses the gap by deriving a new class of equivariant neural networks whose inductive bias encodes symmetries described by compact matrix quantum groups, the natural generalisation of compact matrix groups to the non-commutative setting.

## Input / Output

The network consumes vectors in tensor product representation spaces (C^n)^{⊗k}, where the specific representation is determined by a two-coloured word w specifying tensor products and complex conjugations of the fundamental representation u of the quantum group. The learnable parameters are weight matrices of size n^l × n^k; for easy quantum groups these are constrained to lie in the span of matrices D_π derived from two-coloured partition diagrams in a specified category. Unlike C*-algebra-based neural networks that use function-valued parameters, all layer spaces and weights here remain complex-valued, so standard backpropagation applies.

## Method

The paper derives the existence of compact matrix quantum group equivariant neural networks via Woronowicz's Tannaka-Krein duality, which establishes a bijection between compact matrix quantum groups and their two-coloured representation categories. Each layer composes a learnable linear equivariant map (an intertwiner between representation spaces) with a fixed equivariant non-linear activation. For 'easy' compact matrix quantum groups defined by two-coloured categories of partitions, the paper constructs a functor mapping partition diagrams d_π to concrete matrices D_π, proving that equivariant weight matrices are exactly linear combinations of these D_π. This yields a practical algorithm: enumerate the partition diagrams in the category, compute their associated matrices, and parameterize the weight as a weighted sum. As a corollary, the framework recovers prior characterisations for known groups and provides new ones for the hyperoctahedral group H_n, bistochastic group B_n, and unitary group U(n), which had not appeared in the machine learning literature.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are: (1) a proof of existence of compact matrix quantum group equivariant neural networks via Woronowicz-Tannaka-Krein duality (Theorem 5.8 + Definition 6.1), and (2) a full characterisation of equivariant weight matrices for easy compact matrix quantum groups as linear combinations of partition-diagram matrices D_π (Theorem 7.10, Eq. 23). As a corollary, the paper recovers known weight-matrix characterisations for compact matrix groups and provides new ones for the hyperoctahedral group H_n, the bistochastic group B_n, and the unitary group U(n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper performs no empirical comparisons, as it is entirely theoretical. However, it provides a unifying framework that subsumes prior group-equivariant neural network results: by showing compact matrix groups are a special case of compact matrix quantum groups (Theorem 3.4 + Corollary 6.3), it recovers the weight-matrix characterisations previously obtained by Ravanbakhsh et al. (2017), Maron et al. (2019a), Pearce-Crump (2023a; 2024), and Godfrey et al. (2023). Additionally, it yields new characterisations for three compact matrix groups (H_n, B_n, U(n)) that had not appeared in the machine learning literature before. The paper explicitly notes that practical implementation and empirical validation remain future work, as the characterisation currently covers only the linear layers and not the equivariant non-linear activation functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant neural network literature (Ravanbakhsh et al. 2017; Maron et al. 2019; Pearce-Crump 2023) from classical compact matrix groups to compact matrix quantum groups, using Woronowicz's Tannaka-Krein duality (1988) to prove existence and Banica & Speicher's (2009) partition-based classification to characterise weight matrices. It departs from prior C*-algebra ML work (Hashimoto et al. 2022, 2024) that generalises layer spaces to function-valued objects, instead keeping complex-valued spaces and generalising the symmetry structure itself. The paper also recovers known weight-matrix characterisations for groups like S_n and O(n) while producing new ones for H_n, B_n, and U(n) that had not appeared in the ML literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a principled path to equivariant ML for data living in non-commutative geometries (free probability, particle physics, statistical mechanics), where classical group symmetries are provably inadequate. However, the contribution is entirely theoretical: no non-linear equivariant layers are characterised, no implementation is provided, and no empirical validation is attempted, leaving the practical utility of these networks untested. A key open question is whether the partition-based weight-matrix characterisation extends to equivariant non-linear activations, which would be necessary for actual network construction. The new characterisations for H_n, B_n, and U(n) are a useful byproduct for the classical equivariant NN community.
