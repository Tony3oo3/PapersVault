## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in latent diffusion models causes severe color distortion and oversaturation at high guidance weights because linear extrapolation of the score function inevitably amplifies sample norms in the latent space. The paper provides a theoretical framework identifying norm amplification and anomalous diffusion as the root causes, and asks whether guidance can be reformulated to enhance text-image alignment without inflating sample magnitudes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251108/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within an existing latent diffusion pipeline (e.g., Stable Diffusion v3.5 with 38-dimensional latent space or SD v2.1), taking a text prompt and Gaussian noise as input and producing a decoded RGB image. ADG is a drop-in replacement for the CFG step at each denoising iteration: it computes the conditional and unconditional x̂₀ estimates from the same noise prediction network, then applies an angular rotation instead of a linear combination before feeding the result into any standard sampler (DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver). It also extends naturally to flow-matching models by substituting the velocity field for the score function.

## Method

ADG replaces CFG's linear extrapolation x̂₀^(c) + (ω−1)(x̂₀^(c) − x̂₀^(∅)) with a geometric rotation in the angular domain. At each denoising step, it computes the angle γ between the conditional and unconditional x̂₀ vectors, then rotates x̂₀^(c) by (ω−1)γ (capped at π/3 to prevent pathological directions) around the axis defined by the component of x̂₀^(c) orthogonal to x̂₀^(∅). Proposition 4.1 guarantees the resulting norm is bounded by √2·‖x̂₀^(c)‖, preventing the unbounded norm growth that causes oversaturation. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.2–3.3) shows that CFG's linear amplification pushes samples toward the outer boundary of the latent distribution (surface classes in a GMM), inducing anomalous diffusion where updates point toward lower-density regions; ADG avoids this by preserving magnitude while only adjusting direction, which is sufficient for semantic alignment given the approximately spherical structure of VAE latent spaces.

## Results

On COCO10k with SD v3.5 (d=38, 10 NFE), ADG achieves a best CLIP score of 0.324, ImageReward of 0.970, and FID of 16.6, consistently outperforming CFG (best: CLIP 0.318, IR 0.843, FID 16.6) and CFG++ (best: CLIP 0.315, IR 0.574, FID 17.1) across all guidance weights. The most striking advantage appears at high guidance weights: at ω=10, ADG maintains IR=0.970 while CFG collapses to IR=−0.142 and CFG++ to IR=−1.509. ADG also demonstrates a wide stable operating range, with performance degrading gracefully from ω=2 to ω=15, whereas baselines exhibit sharp quality drops beyond their narrow optimal points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADG decisively outperforms CFG at high guidance weights: at ω=10, ADG achieves IR=0.970 vs. CFG's −0.142, and at ω=15, ADG maintains IR=0.940 while CFG drops to −0.659 with FID degrading from 16.8 to 20.3. Against CFG++, ADG dominates across all guidance weights, with CFG++ showing severe degradation (IR=−1.680 at ω=15) while ADG remains stable. The closest competitor is APG, which achieves a marginally better FID (16.1 vs. 16.6 at best) and comparable IR at ω=6 (0.964 vs. 0.964), but ADG surpasses APG on CLIP (0.324 vs. 0.319) and IR (0.970 vs. 0.966) at best performance, and maintains more consistent quality across the full ω range. Overall, ADG's key advantage is its wide stable operating range: it delivers strong results from ω=2 through ω=15, while all baselines exhibit a narrow optimal window followed by rapid degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) framework of Ho & Salimans (2021), which remains the standard guidance mechanism in latent diffusion text-to-image models. Prior improvements—CFG++, APG, PCG, and ReCFG—all operate within the linear domain, modifying the weighted combination of conditional and unconditional score functions. The key conceptual departure here is identifying norm amplification as the root cause of CFG's high-weight failures and replacing linear extrapolation with an angular-domain rotation of the predicted clean sample x̂₀, a geometric reinterpretation not explored in prior guidance literature. The theoretical analysis builds on and generalizes the Gaussian mixture analyses of Chidambaram et al., Wu et al., Bradley & Nakkiran, and Xia et al. by introducing the notion of 'surface classes' in high-dimensional multi-component settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since CFG is embedded in virtually every production text-to-image system (Stable Diffusion, FLUX, etc.), a drop-in replacement that eliminates oversaturation at high guidance weights while preserving or improving text alignment has immediate practical value. The angular-domain perspective offers a cleaner geometric intuition for why guidance degrades, and the norm bound (Proposition 4.1) provides a principled constraint absent in prior methods. Limitations include the heuristic nature of the π/3 angle threshold, the restriction to latent-space diffusion (unclear applicability to image-domain diffusion), and the absence of formal convergence guarantees. Open questions include whether the angular formulation reveals deeper structure about the latent manifold, and whether it composes well with other guidance strategies (e.g., multi-condition or reward-based guidance).
