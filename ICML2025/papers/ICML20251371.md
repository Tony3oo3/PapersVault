## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods derive supervision from similarity, pseudo labels, or reconstruction, but overlook the per-sample training dynamics during unsupervised optimization. This paper identifies that sample representation stability across consecutive epochs strongly correlates with clustering accuracy and network memorization: unstable samples are consistently mispredicted and tend to be atypical or rare, yet no prior method exploits this signal to guide training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR, 96×96 for STL-10/ImageNet-10/ImageNet-Dogs, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K), augmenting each sample into two views fed to separate network branches. It outputs cluster labels via K-means over the final representations, but critically also produces per-sample stability scores (cosine similarity between current and previous-epoch embeddings) that are used as training-time supervision signals rather than post-hoc diagnostics.

## Method

LFSS introduces a predecessor network f_p that is a direct copy of the target network from the previous epoch, enabling measurement of sample stability as the cosine similarity between f_o(x) and f_p(x). Building on a BYOL-style asymmetric architecture (online, target, and predecessor networks), the method constructs three loss terms: an instance-level NT-Xent variant that treats same-sample representations across epochs as positive pairs (penalizing excessive representation drift), a cluster-level contrastive loss that computes K-means centers excluding the top-δ most unstable samples to yield more discriminative prototypes, and a self-distillation loss with Gaussian noise on the predictor input for robustness. The predecessor network is stop-gradiented and updated by direct copying from the target network each epoch, while the target network uses momentum updates from the online network. A warm-up phase (η epochs) trains with only the instance-level and self-distillation losses before introducing the cluster-level loss once cluster assignments stabilize.

## Results

LFSS achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance across all five benchmark datasets. On CIFAR-10, it reaches 93.4% ACC, 87.2% NMI, and 86.6% ARI, surpassing the previous best (ProPos) by 1.4%, 1.6%, and 2.5% respectively. On the fine-grained ImageNet-Dogs dataset, LFSS attains 69.1% ACC and 61.7% NMI, improving over ProPos by 5.2% ACC and 6.9% NMI. Compared to the BYOL baseline, LFSS yields gains of 5.4%–18.6% across all metrics and datasets, with the largest jump on CIFAR-20 (ACC: 49.3% → 58.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFSS outperforms all 15 compared methods on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor ProPos (TPAMI 2023), LFSS gains +1.4% ACC on CIFAR-10 (93.4 vs 92.0), +6.9% ACC on CIFAR-20 (58.7 vs 51.8), +2.9% ACC on STL-10 (86.1 vs 83.2), +4.2% ACC on ImageNet-10 (93.2 vs 89.0), and +5.2% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs (69.1 vs 63.9). Versus the BYOL self-distillation baseline, improvements range from 5.4% (CIFAR-10 ACC) to 18.6% (CIFAR-20 ARI), confirming that the sample-stability supervision signals provide substantial gains over pure self-distillation. Among the contrastive-learning-based clustering methods, LFSS also surpasses CoNR (NeurIPS 2023) by 8.1% ACC on CIFAR-10 and 6.9% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs, and IDFD (ICLR 2021) by 11.9% ACC on CIFAR-10. On the large-scale Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), LFSS achieves 26.8% ACC and 43.5% NMI, outperforming ProPos (25.6% ACC, 40.5% NMI) and BYOL (19.9% ACC, 36.5% NMI). Significance tests (t-test at 5% level) confirm that LFSS's improvements over IDFD, ProPos, and CoNR are statistically significant across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BYOL self-distillation framework (Grill et al., 2020) for its asymmetric target/online network architecture and on SimCLR's NT-Xent contrastive loss (Chen et al., 2020), which it extends to create an instance-level stability loss. It departs from prior deep clustering supervision signals (pseudo-labels, neighbor relationships, reconstruction) by introducing sample stability—the cosine similarity of a sample's representation across consecutive epochs—as a novel guiding signal, drawing a conceptual bridge to the network memorization literature (Feldman, Arpit) that had previously been explored only in supervised settings. The predecessor network, which stores the previous epoch's weights to compute stability, is a new architectural component not present in BYOL or SimCLR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence linking per-sample representation stability to both clustering accuracy and memorization difficulty in unsupervised training, offering a diagnostic lens for understanding why certain samples remain hard to cluster. The practical impact is that sample stability serves as a lightweight, plug-and-play supervision signal that improves multiple existing frameworks (BYOL, MoCo, SimCLR) without requiring architectural redesign. Open questions include whether the stability signal generalizes to non-image modalities or other unsupervised tasks beyond clustering, whether the requirement to know the number of clusters K limits applicability, and whether the predecessor network's memory overhead becomes prohibitive at very large scale.
