## Problem

Rectified-flow-based diffusion transformers (e.g., FLUX, OpenSora) produce high-quality generations but fail at inversion and reconstruction because the vanilla Euler-based ODE solver accumulates significant per-timestep errors. These accumulated errors severely degrade downstream inversion-based tasks such as image and video editing, where structural fidelity to the source is critical. No prior work had identified or addressed the ODE-solving precision as the root cause of poor RF inversion.

## Input / Output

For inversion/reconstruction, the method takes a source image (1024×1024) or video and a detailed text prompt (generated by GPT-4o), inverts the latent to noise space, then reconstructs it. For editing, it takes a source image/video plus a target text prompt and outputs a structurally preserved edited result. The derivative estimation in RF-Solver requires two network forward passes per timestep, so fair comparisons use matched NFE (e.g., 10 NFE for T2I, 50 NFE for inversion). The method is entirely training-free and plugs into any pre-trained rectified-flow model.

## Method

RF-Solver derives the exact integral form of the rectified flow ODE via the variation of constants method, then approximates the nonlinear velocity field with a second-order Taylor expansion around each timestep. The first-order derivative v_θ^(1) is estimated numerically by stepping forward a small Δt=0.01 and computing the finite difference of two network predictions, reducing per-step error from O(h²) to O(h³). RF-Edit extends this by storing Value features from self-attention layers in the last M transformer blocks during the final n inversion timesteps, then replacing the corresponding Value features during denoising (converting self-attention to cross-attention) to anchor structural information. For FLUX, features are shared in the last 19 single blocks; for OpenSora, in the spatial attention of the last 14 DiT blocks. The entire pipeline requires no training, fine-tuning, or optimization beyond the standard forward passes.

## Results

On image inversion-reconstruction (MS-COCO), RF-Solver reduces MSE from 0.0268 (vanilla RF) to 0.0092 and improves SSIM from 0.7626 to 0.9276, outperforming the Heun solver (MSE 0.0117, SSIM 0.8924). For image editing, RF-Edit achieves the best CLIP Score of 33.66 and a competitive LPIPS of 0.149 across 300+ images. On video editing (VBench), RF-Edit attains the highest scores on all four metrics: Subject Consistency 0.9501, Motion Smoothness 0.9712, Aesthetic Quality 0.6796, and Imaging Quality 0.7207. For text-to-image generation, RF-Solver achieves FID 23.98 and CLIP Score 31.09, surpassing both the vanilla RF sampler (FID 25.33) and DPM-Solver++ (FID 24.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RF-Edit outperforms all six image editing baselines on CLIP Score, with the largest margin over P2P (33.66 vs. 30.70) and SDEdit (33.66 vs. 31.61), and a clear lead over the closest competitor RF-Inv (33.66 vs. 33.02). On LPIPS, PnP achieves the lowest value (0.080) but at the cost of the worst CLIP Score (30.58), as it fails on structural edits; RF-Edit's LPIPS of 0.149 is second-best while maintaining the highest prompt alignment. For video editing, RF-Edit dominates all five baselines (FateZero, Flatten, COVE, RAVE, TokenFlow) on every VBench metric, with the most notable gaps over FateZero (AQ: 0.6796 vs. 0.6092) and RAVE (SC: 0.9501 vs. 0.9292). In inversion-reconstruction, RF-Solver reduces image MSE by 66% relative to vanilla RF (0.0092 vs. 0.0268) and by 21% over the Heun solver (0.0092 vs. 0.0117). For T2I generation, RF-Solver beats DPM-Solver++ on both FID (23.98 vs. 24.63) and CLIP Score (31.09 vs. 30.62), confirming that the Taylor-expansion-based ODE solver generalizes beyond inversion to improve sampling quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rectified Flow ODE framework (Liu et al., 2022a) and the DDIM inversion paradigm (Song et al., 2021a), but departs from the standard first-order Euler discretization used in FLUX and OpenSora by deriving a closed-form second-order Taylor expansion solution for the RF ODE. For editing, it extends the attention-sharing lineage of P2P (Hertz et al., 2022) and PnP (Tumanyan et al., 2023) to the DiT architecture of RF-based models, replacing the Value feature in self-attention during denoising with features cached from inversion. Unlike RF-prior (Yang et al., 2024b) which requires optimization, or Rout et al. (2024b) which augments the vector field, RF-Solver directly corrects the discretization error in the original vector field without any additional training or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously underappreciated failure mode of RF-based models: the first-order Euler solver accumulates O(h²) error per step that compounds catastrophically during inversion, and a simple second-order Taylor fix reduces this to O(h³). This is a general, training-free technique applicable to any RF-based DiT (demonstrated on FLUX, OpenSora, and HunyuanVideo), making it immediately practical for the growing ecosystem of RF models. Limitations include the 2× NFE cost from the extra forward pass for derivative estimation, and the finding that higher-order expansions do not help under fixed NFE budgets, suggesting diminishing returns. An open question is whether the feature-sharing mechanism can be made adaptive (e.g., per-region or per-timestep) rather than relying on a fixed number of sharing steps.
