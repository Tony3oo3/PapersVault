## Problem

Whether LLM-like scaling laws in data size, model parameters, and compute transfer to driving behavior models has remained unexplored, as existing work is constrained to ~2M training samples and sub-500M parameter models. This paper systematically scales an autoregressive driving model to 1.4B parameters and 120M training segments (~50× more data than prior open-source datasets) to characterize these scaling properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes target agent history states, nearby agent history states, and vectorized map polylines, all normalized to an agent-centric coordinate frame and tokenized via a PointNet-like encoder. Output is a sequence of discrete Verlet action distributions (second-order position differences) rolled out autoregressively over a prediction horizon; at inference, multiple trajectories are sampled in batch and subsampled to a fixed number of modes via K-Means clustering.

## Method

DriveGPT uses an encoder-decoder transformer where the encoder fuses all input modalities into scene embedding tokens via self-attention, and the decoder follows an LLM-style architecture that cross-attends agent position embeddings with scene tokens to predict a discrete distribution over Verlet actions at each timestep. Training uses teacher forcing with a single cross-entropy loss over the action space, and the key empirical finding is that the autoregressive decoder scales more effectively than a one-shot decoder beyond 8M parameters, while data size—not model size—is the primary bottleneck: model scaling yields diminishing returns until training data exceeds ~21M samples. The paper further validates that pretraining on the 120M internal dataset before fine-tuning on the smaller WOMD benchmark yields up to 3% additional gains in geometric metrics.

## Results

On the WOMD test set (averaged over vehicle, pedestrian, cyclist and 3s/5s/8s horizons), DriveGPT-Finetune achieves the best minADE (0.5240) and minFDE (1.0538) among all non-ensemble methods, and the second-best miss rate (0.1202) behind only the MotionLM ensemble (0.1058). Pretraining on the 120M internal dataset yields up to 3% additional gains over training on WOMD alone. However, DriveGPT reports lower soft mAP (0.3857) than all baselines, which the authors attribute to accumulated probability noise in the autoregressive decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveGPT-Finetune outperforms all seven baselines on minADE and minFDE without any ensembling, beating the closest non-ensemble competitor HPTR by 0.0325 in minADE (0.5240 vs 0.5565) and 0.0855 in minFDE (1.0538 vs 1.1393). Against the two ensemble baselines, DriveGPT still wins on both geometric metrics: it improves over Wayformer† by 0.0214 in minADE and 0.0742 in minFDE, and over MotionLM† by 0.0269 in minADE and 0.0661 in minFDE. On miss rate, DriveGPT (0.1202) is competitive with Wayformer† (0.1228) and ControlMTR (0.1282) but trails the MotionLM ensemble (0.1058) by 1.4 percentage points. The consistent weakness is soft mAP: DriveGPT scores 0.3857 versus 0.4572 for ControlMTR and 0.4507 for MotionLM†, a gap the authors trace to compounding action-probability errors over the autoregressive rollout. Pretraining on the 120M internal dataset provides a small but consistent boost (e.g., minADE 0.5240 vs 0.5279) despite a large distribution shift to WOMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveGPT extends the autoregressive motion prediction paradigm introduced by MotionLM (Seff et al., 2023), which itself builds on the Verlet action representation from R2P2 (Rhinehart et al., 2018) and the PointNet-style vectorized scene encoding from VectorNet (Gao et al., 2020). The key departure is scale: prior behavior models were constrained to a few million parameters and at most a couple million training segments, whereas DriveGPT pushes to 1.4B parameters and 120M segments, explicitly testing whether LLM-style scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) transfer to driving. The encoder-decoder transformer architecture is standard; the contribution is the systematic empirical study of data, model, and compute scaling in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic scaling study for driving behavior models spanning three orders of magnitude in both data and parameters, providing practitioners with concrete guidance that data remains the primary bottleneck and that model scaling only becomes effective beyond ~21M samples. The closed-loop deployment at under 50ms latency demonstrates practical viability, and the WOMD results confirm generalizability beyond the internal dataset. A notable limitation is the degraded soft mAP from accumulated probability noise in autoregressive decoding, which the authors flag as an open problem. Whether further data scaling (beyond 120M) continues to yield gains, and how to fix probability calibration in autoregressive driving models, remain important open questions.
