## Problem

Current state-of-the-art ODE solvers for fast diffusion sampling (DPM-Solver++, UniPC) rely on Adams-like multi-step methods that use t-only Lagrange interpolation to estimate the velocity field within each integration interval. The paper argues this t-only interpolation is suboptimal because the velocity field v(x,t) also depends on x, and that the specific interpolation form is ultimately reducible to a set of coefficients that can be learned. This leaves a gap: no prior work has jointly optimized both the solver coefficients and the timestep schedule in a data-driven, differentiable manner tailored to a specific pre-trained model.

## Input / Output

The method requires no changes to the pre-trained diffusion model's parameters; it only searches for a small set of solver coefficients (N×N lower-triangular matrix, e.g., 55 parameters for 10 NFE) and N-1 timestep deltas. At inference, the input is standard Gaussian noise (plus an optional text prompt for text-to-image models), and the output is a generated image at the model's native resolution (256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024). The searched solver is model- and scheduler-specific: a solver searched on FlowDCN-B/2 with rectified-flow scheduling transfers across rectified-flow models (SiT-XL/2, FlowDCN-XL/2) at different resolutions, while a solver searched on DiT-XL/2 with DDPM/VP scheduling applies to other DDPM models sharing the same β_min and β_max (e.g., PixArt-Σ).

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that any universal interpolation function P(x_t, t, x_j, t_j) can be pre-integrated and its x-dependent part replaced by its expectation E_{x_i}[C_j(x_i)], reducing the solver to a linear combination x_{i+1} ≈ x_i + Σ_j v_j · c_i^j · (t_{i+1} - t_i) with constant coefficients c_i^j. This defines a compact search space of {c_i^j} and {t_i}. Timesteps are parameterized as softmax-normalized deltas (ensuring they sum to 1), and coefficients are parameterized with the implicit constraint Σ_k c_k^i = 1 (diagonal = 1 − sum of off-diagonals), initialized to mimic Euler. The differentiable search minimizes an MSE loss between the N-step solver trajectory and an L-step Euler reference trajectory (plus Huber loss on the final sample), trained with the Lion optimizer at lr=0.01 over ~50,000 generated samples in roughly 30 minutes on 8×H20 GPUs. The searched solver is then hardcoded and applied at inference with zero additional parameters beyond the coefficient table.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the searched solver achieves 2.33 FID with DiT-XL/2 (DDPM/VP) and 2.40 FID with SiT-XL/2 (Rectified Flow) under only 10 NFE, substantially outperforming DPM-Solver++ (3.23 FID) and UniPC (3.04 FID) at the same step count. On ImageNet 512×512, FlowDCN-XL/2 reaches 2.77 FID and DiT-XL/2 reaches 3.64 FID under 10 NFE. The searched solver also outperforms the distillation-based FlowTurbo at comparable NFE while requiring only 55 learned parameters versus 2.9×10⁷ additional parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The searched solver dominates all traditional multi-step solvers across every NFE setting. On DiT-XL/2-R256 at 10 NFE, it achieves 2.33 FID versus 3.23 for DPM-Solver++ (uniform-t), 3.04 for UniPC (uniform-t), and 2.74 for UniPC with optimized timesteps (Xue et al., 2024) — a gap of 0.41–0.90 FID. The advantage is even more pronounced at low step counts: at 5 NFE the searched solver reaches 7.40 FID while DPM-Solver++ and UniPC remain above 23 FID. On SiT-XL/2-R256 (Rectified Flow), the searched solver at 10×2 NFE-CFG achieves 2.40 FID, outperforming FlowTurbo (2.69 FID at 14×2 NFE-CFG) with fewer steps and 55 parameters versus 2.9×10⁷. Against Adam4 (2.27 FID at 16×2 NFE-CFG), the searched solver matches or exceeds performance at fewer NFE (2.24 FID at 15×2). The solver also generalizes across architectures and resolutions: a solver searched on FlowDCN-B/2 (256×256) transfers to SiT-XL/2 and FlowDCN-XL/2 at both 256×256 and 512×512, consistently beating handcrafted solvers by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of solver-based fast diffusion sampling initiated by DPM-Solver++ and UniPC, which rely on Adams-Bashforth-like multi-step methods with Lagrange interpolation. It builds directly on Xue et al.'s (CVPR 2024) observation that handcrafted timesteps are suboptimal, but departs by showing that the specific interpolation function form is inconsequential and can be reduced to a set of pre-integrated coefficients. The key conceptual departure is jointly searching over both timesteps and solver coefficients via a differentiable objective, rather than fixing the solver structure (e.g., Adams-Bashforth order) and only optimizing the schedule. For the DDPM/VP extension, it leverages the semi-linear structure and exponential integrator framework from Zhang & Chen (ICLR 2023) and Lu et al. (2023), but reformulates the interpolation to decouple coefficients from the exponential integral for tractable search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The searched solver is a drop-in replacement requiring no model retraining, no additional parameters at inference (only 21–55 scalars), and transfers across architectures, resolutions, and model sizes (e.g., a solver searched on FlowDCN-B/2 improves SiT-XL/2 and FlowDCN-XL/2 at both 256×256 and 512×512). This makes it broadly applicable to any pretrained diffusion model with a matching noise schedule, which is a significant practical advantage over distillation-based methods that alter model weights. Key limitations include degraded performance under large CFG values (attributed to the naive solver structure lacking predictor-corrector components) and a noted mismatch between reconstruction error and FID, suggesting the search objective may not fully capture perceptual quality. Open questions include whether incorporating predictor-corrector structures or training with CFG would close the remaining gap, and whether the approach extends to other noise schedules beyond DDPM/VP and Rectified Flow.
