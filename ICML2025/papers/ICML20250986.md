## Problem

Large Video Models (LVMs) built on LLMs frequently produce hallucinated content that deviates from actual video content, and existing video DPO methods like Hound-DPO only perform coarse-grained, instance-level alignment between the full video and the response text. This ignores the two dimensions that make video fundamentally different from images: dynamic temporal reasoning (event ordering, action sequences) and fine-grained spatial-object grounding (object locations, spatial relationships), leading to suboptimal preference alignment and persistent hallucinations.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a video clip and a text prompt (question or captioning instruction) and produces a free-form text response. For training, VistaDPO-7k provides each sample with a chosen response, two types of rejected responses (relevant-but-incorrect with spatial/temporal inconsistencies, and entirely irrelevant hallucinated content), plus fine-grained spatial-temporal groundings: keyframe timestamps, bounding boxes of relevant objects, and event-relevant clip segments. Video-level non-preferred samples are drawn from unrelated videos in the mini-batch, and object-level non-preferred samples are constructed by masking or spatially displacing key regions within the keyframe.

## Method

VistaDPO introduces a three-level hierarchical preference optimization loss that extends standard DPO to jointly model spatial and temporal video semantics. At the Instance level, it combines response-level DPO (with weighted relevant and irrelevant rejected responses) and a novel video-level DPO term that contrasts the chosen video against an unrelated video to reduce over-reliance on language. At the Temporal level, clip-level DPO aligns event-relevant video segments against event-irrelevant segments, capturing temporal dynamics. At the Perceptive level, object-level DPO uses ROI-masked keyframes as spatial negatives, and a token-level loss (adapted from TDPO) applies sequential KL divergence to align individual token preferences. The overall loss is a weighted sum of all six sub-losses (L_DPO_v, L_DPO_r, L_DPO_c, L_DPO_o, L_DPO_t), trained for 3 epochs on 7B LVMs (Video-LLaVA, PLLaVA) with β=0.1, λ=0.4, μ=0.2, ρ=0.1.

## Results

On VideoHallucer, VistaDPO achieves 57.8% Overall accuracy on PLLaVA (vs. 38.1% base, 36.2% Hound-DPO) and 54.3% on Video-LLaVA (vs. 17.8% base, 29.5% Hound-DPO). On EventHallusion, VistaDPO reaches 12.7% Overall on PLLaVA (vs. 6.1% base) and 12.1% on Video-LLaVA (vs. 7.6% base). On Video QA, VistaDPO on Video-LLaVA achieves 85.3% MSVD, 76.9% MSR-VTT, 74.1% TGIF, and 55.0% ActivityNet, yielding average improvements of 26.42% over PLLaVA and 53.92% over Video-LLaVA across all hallucination benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VistaDPO consistently outperforms Hound-DPO (the primary video DPO baseline) across all benchmarks: on VideoHallucer Overall, VistaDPO achieves 57.8% vs. 36.2% on PLLaVA and 54.3% vs. 29.5% on Video-LLaVA; on EventHallusion Overall, 12.7% vs. 9.8% and 12.1% vs. 9.5% respectively. Notably, Hound-DPO degrades the base model's Basic accuracy (75.1→69.3 on PLLaVA) and descriptive capability, while VistaDPO improves both simultaneously. On Video QA, VistaDPO on Video-LLaVA reaches 85.3% MSVD and 76.9% MSR-VTT, surpassing Hound-DPO (80.7%, 70.2%) and all other compared LVMs (VideoChatGPT 64.9%, LLaMA-Adapter 54.9%, Video-LLaMA 51.6%). On captioning, VistaDPO preserves or improves all five dimensions (Correctness, Detail, Context, Temporal, Consistency) while Hound-DPO shows degradation in Detail and Consistency. On MVBench, VistaDPO achieves 49.3% average on PLLaVA and 46.3% on Video-LLaVA, outperforming Hound-DPO (45.3% and 43.3%) and all other baselines including VideoChat2 (51.1% but on a different architecture).

## Relation to Prior Work

VistaDPO extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) from text-only and image-text settings to video-language alignment, building directly on Hound-DPO (Zhang et al., 2024b), the first video-specific DPO method. The key departure is the introduction of hierarchical spatial-temporal preference optimization across three levels (instance, temporal, perceptive), where Hound-DPO operated only at the coarse instance level. The token-level component is adapted from TDPO (Zeng et al., 2024), and the video-level preference optimization (comparing different videos as preferred/non-preferred) is a novel addition not present in prior multimodal DPO work. The accompanying VistaDPO-7k dataset with explicit spatial-temporal grounding (timestamps, keyframes, bounding boxes) distinguishes it from prior video DPO datasets that lacked such fine-grained annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: video LLMs produce hallucinated content that deviates from actual video, and prior DPO approaches failed to capture the temporal and spatial structure of videos. The VistaDPO-7k dataset, with its spatial-temporal grounding annotations, is a valuable resource for the community that goes beyond what Hound-DPO provided. However, the method is validated only on two 7B base models (Video-LLaVA and PLLaVA), and the authors acknowledge limitations on long-duration videos with complex temporal dependencies. Open questions include whether the hierarchical approach scales to larger models, whether the 7.2K dataset size is sufficient for broader generalization, and whether the specific negative sample construction strategies (e.g., ROI Move, Reverse) transfer to other video domains.
