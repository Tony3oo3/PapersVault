## Problem

Low-rank approximation (LRA) reduces ViT parameter counts but causes significant accuracy drops due to information loss during SVD-based matrix decomposition, and existing LRA studies largely ignore quantization—a critical step for practical mobile/edge deployment. When LRA and quantization are combined, the paper observes large activation outliers in specific channels and tokens that cause severe additional accuracy degradation, highlighting the need for an LRA-aware quantization strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification) and produces the same outputs as the original ViT model—class labels for classification, bounding boxes and segmentation masks for detection/segmentation, or keypoints for pose estimation. The key distinction is that the output model is a compressed INT8, low-rank representation (e.g., Swin-B reduced from 352 MB FP32 to 60 MB INT8) designed for inference on mobile (Android Cortex-X3) and edge (NVIDIA Jetson Xavier) hardware, with 1.5×–3.2× latency reduction.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, RB-LRA decomposes each FC layer via SVD and adds a reparameterizable residual branch (Ṽ, Û) that compensates for discarded singular values; the branch is initialized via Weight Reconstruction (WR), which reuses the discarded SVD components (S_del·V_del) to minimize information loss, then fine-tuned with encoder block-level knowledge distillation from the original model. At inference, the residual branch is merged into the main branch via reparameterization, yielding a single low-rank matrix with no extra parameters. Second, WADS (Weight-Aware Distribution Scaling) addresses LRA-induced activation outliers by jointly optimizing a per-channel scaling vector that minimizes both activation quantization error and weight quantization error, applied selectively to layers whose weight quantization loss is below average. Per-token activation quantization is used instead of per-layer to handle token-level outliers, and the full pipeline is validated on DeiT and Swin models with INT8 post-training quantization.

## Results

On ImageNet, Swin-B with RB-LRA+KD achieves 83.44% top-1 accuracy (only −0.03% vs. the 83.47% FP32 baseline) while reducing parameters by 31.8% and GFLOPs by 30.4%. DeiT-B with RB-LRA+KD reaches 81.12% (−0.73%) with a 45.7% parameter reduction. After applying the proposed WADS INT8 quantization, Swin-B retains 82.97% (−0.47% from RB-LRA) and DeiT-B retains 80.56% (−0.56%), outperforming naive INT8 PTQ by 0.83% and 0.94% respectively. On mobile (Android) and edge (Jetson Xavier) devices, the full RB-LRA+WADS pipeline delivers 1.9×–3.2× and 1.5×–2.5× inference speedups over the FP32 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the LRA front, RB-LRA+KD clearly outperforms both PELA (Guo et al., 2024) and AAFM+GFM (Yu & Wu, 2023): on Swin-B it achieves 83.44% (−0.03%) versus PELA's 82.50% (−0.97%) and AAFM+GFM's 82.99% (−0.48%); on DeiT-B it reaches 81.12% (−0.73%) versus PELA's 81.00% (−0.85%). For quantization, WADS beats all compared PTQ methods on every model: on Swin-B it scores 82.97% (−0.47%) versus SmoothQuant 82.76% (−0.68%), QADS 82.37% (−1.07%), and NaivePTQ 82.14% (−1.30%); on DeiT-B it scores 80.56% (−0.56%) versus RepQ-ViT 80.37% (−0.75%), SmoothQuant 80.26% (−0.86%), and QADS 79.82% (−1.30%). In the combined LRA+quantization setting (Table 3), the full framework achieves 82.97% at 60.1 MB, surpassing PELA (82.50% at 248.8 MB) and AAFM+GFM (82.68% at 240.8 MB) while being roughly 4× smaller. Against INT4-only quantization methods on DeiT-B (Table 10), RB-LRA+WADS at 44.4 MB / 80.56% (−1.29%) outperforms IGQ-ViT at 43.3 MB / 79.23% (−2.62%) and AdaLog at 78.03% (−3.82%), demonstrating that combining 8-bit quantization with structural LRA compression is more effective than aggressive 4-bit quantization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SVD-based low-rank approximation (LRA) for transformer compression by introducing a reparameterizable residual branch (RB-LRA) that compensates for the information loss inherent in matrix decomposition, departing from prior LRA methods (PELA, AAFM+GFM) that rely solely on knowledge distillation to recover accuracy. The quantization component builds directly on QADS (Kim et al., 2024), extending its activation scaling loss to jointly account for weight quantization error (WADS), and introduces per-token quantization granularity motivated by LRA-specific outlier patterns. The key conceptual departure is treating LRA and quantization as a coupled problem rather than independent compression steps, since prior LRA studies largely ignored quantization effects and prior quantization methods did not account for LRA-induced distributional shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical integration of LRA with INT8 quantization for ViT deployment on mobile and edge devices addresses a real gap: using these techniques naively together causes severe accuracy degradation that neither technique alone produces. The observation that LRA creates structured outliers in specific channels and tokens—requiring specialized quantization handling—provides actionable insight for practitioners combining compression methods. Limitations include the non-trivial fine-tuning cost (~6 hours for DeiT-B), the requirement for training data access, and evaluation primarily on ImageNet-scale classification; open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (e.g., LLM-scale transformers) and whether per-token quantization overhead is acceptable across all target hardware.
