## Problem

Long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) models traditionally employ thousands to millions of parameters to capture extended temporal dependencies, incurring substantial computational and memory costs. However, unlike high-dimensional images or text, time series data exhibit strong temporal pattern similarity and approximate low-rank structures, raising the question of whether such large parameter budgets are truly necessary. This paper asks whether an ultra-minimal model can achieve competitive LTSF performance by explicitly exploiting these structural properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251031/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a univariate historical time series of length T (typically 720) and a segment length P (set to the dataset's natural period, e.g., P=24 for hourly data) as input, and produces a forecast of length L (96–720, extendable to 1800). For multivariate series, channel independence decomposes the problem into independent univariate tasks. The prediction is generated at segment-level (N′ segments of length P) and then flattened, meaning the output granularity is tied to the chosen P rather than individual time steps.

## Method

TimeBase consists of two extremely small linear layers operating on a segmented representation. First, the 1D input is divided into N non-overlapping segments of length P to form a 2D matrix X_his ∈ R^{N×P}; a linear layer (BasisExtract) then compresses this into R basis components X_basis ∈ R^{R×P} (R ≪ min(N,P)), exploiting the low-rank structure of long-term time series. A second linear layer (SegmentForecast) maps the basis components to N′ future segments, which are flattened to yield the final forecast. An orthogonal regularization loss penalizes the off-diagonal entries of the Gram matrix of X_basis, encouraging the learned basis to capture diverse, uncorrelated temporal patterns. The total parameter count scales as O(aT + bL) rather than O(TL), yielding only 0.39K parameters for T=L=720. Additionally, TimeBase can be inserted as a plug-and-play basis-extraction front-end into any patch-based model (e.g., PatchTST), reducing MACs by 77–93% without architectural redesign.

## Results

On 17 normal-scale LTSF benchmarks (input length 720, horizons 96–720), TimeBase achieves Top-2 average MSE/MAE on 16 of 17 datasets, with only ETTh2 as a near-tie exception. It uses just 0.39K parameters and 2.77M MACs, reducing MACs by 4.58× and parameters by 2.56× versus the prior lightest model SparseTSF (1.0K params, 12.71M MACs), and by 120× in MACs versus DLinear. On the four LARGEST large-scale datasets (up to 8,600 variables), TimeBase ranks within Top-2 across all horizons while requiring only 3.51M–74.30M MACs, compared to billions for iTransformer and PatchTST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeBase (0.39K params) outperforms the prior lightest model SparseTSF (1.0K params) on 15 of 17 datasets in MSE, with the sole exception being ETTh2 (0.347 vs 0.344) and a near-tie on Solar (both 0.216); it also reduces MACs by 4.58× (2.77M vs 12.71M). Against DLinear (1.04M params), TimeBase achieves an average 8.82% MSE reduction and 7.64% MAE reduction, winning on 14 of 17 datasets. Versus the heavier Transformer-based models (PatchTST 8.69M, iTransformer 5.47M, TimeMixer 5.58M), TimeBase secures the best MSE on 23 of 34 averaged metrics and second-best on 6, while using 100–22,000× fewer parameters. On the four LARGEST large-scale datasets (CA, GLA, GBA, SD), TimeBase matches or beats iTransformer, DLinear, and PatchTST on MAE at every horizon (e.g., CA-720: 0.386 vs 0.441/0.386/0.439) while consuming 100–1000× fewer MACs. As a plug-and-play module inserted before PatchTST's encoder, TimeBase reduces MACs by 77.74%–93.03% and parameters by 56.95%–91.43% while maintaining or slightly improving MSE/MAE on 43 of 56 metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeBase extends the lineage of lightweight linear forecasting models (DLinear → FITS → SparseTSF) by introducing a fundamentally different inductive bias: rather than operating at the point level or frequency level, it exploits the approximate low-rank structure of long-term time series to extract a small set of temporal basis components and perform segment-level forecasting. It borrows the channel-independence principle and non-overlapping patching from PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023), but replaces the heavy transformer encoder with two tiny linear layers (basis extraction + segment forecast) plus an orthogonal regularization on the learned basis. The plug-and-play integration into patch-based pipelines is a novel application that no prior lightweight model (SparseTSF, FITS, DLinear) offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TimeBase's plug-and-play basis extraction reduces PatchTST's MACs by 77–93% while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, offering a drop-in efficiency boost for any patch-based LTSF model without architectural redesign. The linear parameter scaling O(aT + bL) versus the quadratic O(TL) of DLinear becomes increasingly important as look-back windows and horizons grow toward ultra-long sequences. Open questions include whether the low-rank assumption degrades for highly non-stationary or multi-scale series, how the choice of segment length P interacts with irregular sampling, and whether the orthogonal constraint generalizes beyond the modest gains observed on some datasets. The paper also raises the broader question of whether pre-trained large LTSF backbones can be distilled into basis-component form for edge deployment.
