## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks against diffusion models rely on accessing the internal U-net to extract noise predictions at intermediate denoising steps, making them inapplicable to proprietary models (e.g., DALL-E 2, Stable Diffusion APIs) that expose only a black-box image-to-image variation endpoint. This paper addresses the gap by designing an MIA that operates solely through the publicly available variation API, without any access to the denoising network's parameters or intermediate outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target RGB image and a diffusion step parameter t, then calls the variation API n times (default n=10) to produce n reconstructed images. The output is a binary membership label determined by comparing the averaged reconstruction to the original image via a distance function D, which can be SSIM (for Stable Diffusion), L2 norm (for Diffusion Transformer), or a ResNet-18 classifier trained on pixel-wise absolute differences (for DDIM). The diffusion step t is a free hyperparameter; experiments show performance is stable across moderate values (e.g., t=100–300 for DDIM, t=10 for Stable Diffusion).

## Method

REDIFFUSE is built on the insight that for training-set images, a well-trained diffusion model yields an unbiased noise prediction (formalized via a full-rank Jacobian assumption on the denoiser), so the reconstruction error is a zero-mean random variable whose magnitude shrinks with averaging. The algorithm independently calls the variation API n times on the target image, averages the n outputs to obtain x̂, and classifies the image as a member if D(x, x̂) falls below a threshold τ. Theorem 4.2 provides a Cramér-Chernoff concentration bound showing the reconstruction error decays exponentially in n for member images, while non-member images retain a systematic bias that does not average out. The approach generalizes directly to Stable Diffusion (by wrapping the encoder-decoder around the latent-space variation) and Diffusion Transformer (by substituting the ViT-based denoiser for the U-net), and is validated on DALL-E 2's live API using famous paintings as presumed members.

## Results

On DDIM models, REDIFFUSE achieves AUC of 0.96 (CIFAR-10), 0.98 (CIFAR-100), and 0.96 (STL-10), with corresponding ASR values of 0.91, 0.93, and 0.90. On Diffusion Transformer (ImageNet 256×256), REDIFFUSE reaches AUC 0.97 and ASR 0.94, substantially outperforming the best baseline (PIA at AUC 0.91). On Stable Diffusion (LAION-5B), REDIFFUSE achieves AUC 0.81 and ASR 0.75, outperforming all baselines by approximately 10% in AUC. Crucially, REDIFFUSE requires no access to the model's internal U-Net or Transformer, operating solely through the variation API.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REDIFFUSE outperforms all three baseline families (Loss, SecMI, PIA/PIAN) across every dataset and model architecture tested, while being the only method that does not require access to the model's internal U-Net or Transformer. On CIFAR-100 with DDIM, REDIFFUSE achieves AUC 0.98 vs. 0.96 for SecMI and PIA, and a notable 0.91 for PIAN; on CIFAR-10, it edges out SecMI and PIA (0.96 vs. 0.95 AUC). The advantage is most pronounced on the Diffusion Transformer (ImageNet 256×256), where REDIFFUSE reaches AUC 0.97 and TP 47.3, compared to PIA's 0.91 AUC and 6.8 TP, and PIAN's 0.67 AUC. On Stable Diffusion (LAION-5B), REDIFFUSE achieves AUC 0.81 vs. 0.70–0.73 for the best baselines, a roughly 10-point improvement. The key practical distinction is that all baselines require white-box or grey-box access to intermediate noise predictions from the U-Net, making them inapplicable to proprietary API-only models, whereas REDIFFUSE operates purely through the publicly available variation API.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) line for diffusion models initiated by Hu & Pang (2023) and refined by Duan et al. (2023) and Kong et al. (2023), all of which require access to the U-net's intermediate noise predictions. The key departure is REDIFFUSE, which replaces the need for internal model access with repeated calls to the publicly available image-to-image variation API, leveraging the theoretical observation that well-trained models produce unbiased noise predictions on training data. The paper also extends MIA to the Diffusion Transformer architecture for the first time, adapting both prior baselines and its own method to the ViT-based denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: commercial diffusion models (DALL-E 2, Midjourney, etc.) expose only API endpoints, rendering all prior MIA methods inapplicable for real-world copyright enforcement. The DALL-E 2 experiment, while underpowered (30 assumed-member paintings, non-members from a different generator), demonstrates feasibility in a genuine black-box setting. Key limitations include sensitivity to the diffusion step parameter (accuracy degrades at high t) and the reliance on a moderate step that may not be available in all APIs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-noise regimes, video diffusion, and whether the averaging-based detection can be made robust to adversarial obfuscation of the target image.
