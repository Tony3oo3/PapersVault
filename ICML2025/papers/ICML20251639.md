## Problem

Training diffusion models on web-scale data raises copyright and memorization concerns, motivating training exclusively on noisy (corrupted) samples. While deconvolution theory guarantees that the original distribution is identifiable from noisy observations, the optimal convergence rate is O(log n)^{-2}, making it practically infeasible to collect enough noisy samples for high-quality generation. Moreover, the existing consistency-loss approach (TweedieDiff) fails to produce competitive results when training from scratch, even with pretraining, leaving a gap between theory and practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams: a large set of noisy images (each pixel corrupted by independent Gaussian noise N(0, σ_ζ²) after rescaling to [-1,1]) and a small pretraining set of clean images (as few as 50, or 4–10% of the dataset). Experiments use CIFAR-10 at 32×32 and CelebA at 64×64. The output is a trained EDM-style denoiser D_φ that generates clean images by solving the backward SDE from pure noise; the denoiser also produces intermediate 'denoised' samples at each SFBD iteration that serve as training targets for the next round.

## Method

SFBD alternates between two steps iteratively: (1) Backward Sampling — solving the backward SDE from the corruption noise level ζ down to 0 using the current denoiser's score estimate, producing a set of denoised samples E_k from the noisy data; and (2) Denoiser Update — fine-tuning the denoiser on E_k via the standard score-matching (denoising) loss. The key insight is that this loop is equivalent to enforcing the consistency constraint across all time steps without requiring sampling from p_{r|s} or access to ground-truth scores, thereby bridging the gap between the theoretical consistency framework and practical implementation. Pretraining on a small clean set is essential: it initializes the denoiser close to the true score field, shrinking the convergence constant M_0 in the theoretical guarantee (Prop 2) and reducing the number of iterations needed. The algorithm uses the EDM architecture with 2nd-order Heun sampling for the backward SDE, and clean pretraining images can be mixed into E_k to accelerate convergence when they share the same distribution as the noisy data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32) with σ_ζ=0.2 noisy images and 50 clean pretraining images, SFBD achieves FID 13.53, outperforming all noisy-data baselines except TweedieDiff+ (FID 8.05). On CelebA (64×64) under the same noise level, SFBD reaches FID 6.49, matching DDIM trained entirely on clean data (FID 6.53). With increased clean data (4% and 10% of CIFAR-10), SFBD improves to FID 6.31 and 3.58 respectively, surpassing DDIM trained on 10% clean images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFBD substantially outperforms all prior noisy-data training methods: SURE-Score (FID 132.61), EMDiff (86.47), and TweedieDiff (65.21 with pretraining) on CIFAR-10, and TweedieDiff (58.52) on CelebA. On CelebA, SFBD (FID 6.49) nearly matches DDIM trained on fully clean data (6.53), a remarkable result given it only sees corrupted inputs. The closest competitor is TweedieDiff+ (FID 8.05 on CIFAR-10), which uses a simplified consistency loss; the paper notes TweedieDiff+ edges out SFBD when only 50 clean pretraining images are available, but SFBD surpasses it as more clean data is introduced (Fig. 1). Against clean-data baselines (DDPM 4.04, DDIM 4.16, EDM 1.97 on CIFAR-10), SFBD with 4% clean data (FID 6.31) and 10% clean data (FID 3.58) approaches or exceeds DDIM's clean-data performance, demonstrating that a small fraction of clean pretraining is sufficient to recover most of the quality lost to noise corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion model training and classical density deconvolution theory. It builds directly on the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) for architecture and sampling, and on the score-based SDE formulation (Song et al., 2021b) for the forward/backward process. The key departure is from Daras et al. (2024), whose consistency-loss approach for training on noisy data is shown here to be theoretically sound but practically infeasible from scratch due to an O(log n)^{-2} convergence rate inherited from deconvolution theory. SFBD replaces the consistency loss with an iterative backward-sampling-and-denoiser-update loop, and Prop 3 shows this is equivalent to the consistency constraint at r=0, bridging the gap between theory and implementation that plagues the original approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(log n)^{-2} rate is a fundamental negative result: it proves that training a high-quality diffusion model purely from noisy samples requires an infeasible number of samples, explaining why prior consistency-loss methods only worked in fine-tuning regimes. The practical prescription—pretrain on a small clean set (as few as 50 images) then iterate SFBD—gives practitioners a concrete, privacy-preserving recipe for training on sensitive data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video generation, how it behaves at the very high noise levels (σ_ζ > 1) needed for strong privacy guarantees on large datasets, and whether the pretraining requirement can be further relaxed. The privacy analysis (Appendix F) is encouraging but relies on a single similarity metric and two datasets.
