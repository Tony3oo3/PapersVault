## Problem

State-of-the-art non-autoregressive (NAR) generative models, such as discrete diffusion language models (SEDD, MDLM), achieve strong unconditional perplexity comparable to or better than GPT-2, yet suffer severe performance degradation on conditional generation tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as an inability to generalize to conditional queries (i.e., different mask patterns) unseen during training, since existing NAR architectures rely solely on global contextual features that are highly sensitive to changes in the <MASK> token distribution.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text sequence of length T (up to 1024 tokens) in which a subset of positions is provided as observed context while the remaining positions are replaced with a <MASK> token. It supports two generation paradigms: contextual autoregressive (CAR) generation, where the model autoregressively decodes remaining tokens given all preceding tokens plus an arbitrary context subset, and arbitrary-context (AC) generation, where each masked token is predicted independently given the full context set. The output is a per-token categorical distribution over the vocabulary, and the sparse attention design (at most N_max attended tokens per position per layer) keeps inference linear in sequence length, enabling KV-caching during autoregressive decoding.

## Method

Tracformer introduces an encoder-decoder architecture built around a Multi-Scope Self-Attention (MSSA) encoder where the context window of each embedding grows exponentially (2^l) with layer index l, implemented via sparse attention masks that attend to only N_max tokens per position, yielding O(L·T·N_max) encoder cost. The decoder consists of cross-attention-only Transformer blocks that progressively attend from global (large-scope) to local (small-scope) encoder features, with carefully designed masks ensuring each decoder position only receives information from variables in the given context. For bidirectional context, separate prefix and suffix encoders (with shared parameters) capture preceding and succeeding information, and the decoder cross-attends to both with direction-specific masks. The key insight is that local features are invariant to mask-token changes outside their context window, so the multi-scope hierarchy provides robustness to unseen conditional queries while the topmost layers retain global expressiveness. Training uses a span-masking strategy (Geometric or DLogistic span lengths) with a mixed objective combining CAR and AR losses for the large-scale OpenWebText experiments.

## Results

On zero-shot conditional perplexity (lower is better), Tracformer (109M) achieves 29.38 on WikiText103 and 41.98 on 1BW with mask range [0.25, 0.75], outperforming SEDD (37.83 / 53.72) and MDLM (30.36 / 55.19) by 8.45 and 11.74 points respectively. On unconditional perplexity, Tracformer is competitive: it achieves 51.34 on 1BW (beating MDLM's 67.94 and SEDD's 79.29) but trails MDLM on WikiText103 (43.27 vs. 37.01). In small-scale architecture comparisons, Tracformer consistently achieves lower conditional perplexity than BART, BERT, and BERT-bidir across all mask ratios and span lengths on WikiText103, LAMBADA, and 1BW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tracformer (109M) is compared against five SoTA diffusion/AR models at 169M–124M scale and two NAR architectures (BART, BERT) at similar scale in small-scale experiments. Against SEDD and MDLM (both 169M), Tracformer wins on conditional perplexity in 5 out of 6 mask ranges on both WikiText103 and 1BW, with the largest gap on 1BW (up to 15.82 points lower PPL vs. SEDD). The one exception is the [0.2, 0.3] & [0.4, 0.6] range on WikiText103, where MDLM (22.97) slightly edges Tracformer (24.31). On unconditional perplexity, Tracformer trails MDLM on WikiText103 (43.27 vs. 37.01) and PTB (166.10 vs. 96.40) but surpasses both SEDD and MDLM on 1BW (51.34 vs. 79.29 / 67.94). In small-scale architecture comparisons, Tracformer consistently outperforms BART (138M) on CAR conditional perplexity across all mask ratios, and outperforms both BERT (149M) and BERT-bidir (170M) on AC generation, with the gap widening as mask ratio increases. D3PM and PLAID are included only as unconditional baselines in Table 4, where both are substantially worse than Tracformer across all five datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of NAR generation architectures and query generalization. It builds conceptually on BERT's masked-token paradigm and BART's encoder-decoder design, but departs from both by identifying that their reliance on global (full-attention) features makes them brittle to unseen conditional queries. The multi-scope sparse attention mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that repurposes the idea of hierarchical receptive fields (analogous to U-Net or dilated convolutions) within a Transformer, while the encoder-decoder cross-attention design that progressively refines from global to local context is a new structural choice not present in prior NAR models like SEDD or MDLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's key diagnostic finding—that SoTA diffusion language models with superior unconditional perplexity actually underperform GPT-2 on conditional generation, and that their log-likelihoods violate the chain rule across different query orderings—challenges a working assumption in the NAR generation community and highlights a critical evaluation gap. The multi-scope attention architecture offers a practical, linear-complexity solution that could serve as a backbone for future diffusion-based NAR models. However, scalability remains unproven (only GPT-2 base scale at 109M parameters), and the paper acknowledges that larger-scale validation is needed to confirm whether the architectural advantage persists at the scale where diffusion models have shown their strongest unconditional results.
