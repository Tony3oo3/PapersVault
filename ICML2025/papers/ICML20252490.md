## Problem

Existing RAG systems handle complex multihop questions by decomposing queries and retrieving chunks independently, without awareness of what knowledge is actually available in the knowledge base. This decoupling leads to suboptimal sub-question generation, retrieval failures due to semantic misalignment between query phrasing and corpus formulation, and reasoning errors that compound across hops. The paper argues that question decomposition must be knowledge-aware and that retrieval must be tightly coupled with decomposition to resolve domain-specific complex questions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a complex multihop question and a pre-processed atomic knowledge base, where each document chunk is paired with multiple LLM-generated atomic question tags (questions answerable by that chunk). Retrieval operates on these atomic tags via cosine similarity (text-embedding-ada-002, threshold δ=0.5, top-k=4) rather than on raw chunks. The output is a final answer with a reasoning rationale, produced after up to N=5 iterative decomposition-retrieval-selection rounds that progressively accumulate relevant chunks into context. The method is zero-shot: no demonstrations or task-specific training are used at inference time.

## Method

KAR³-RAG introduces a knowledge-aware dual rewriting mechanism that rewrites both the query side (decomposing into multiple atomic question proposals) and the document side (tagging each chunk with atomic questions) into a shared question-based representation, bridging the semantic gap between queries and corpus. Four components operate iteratively: a Knowledge Atomizer pre-processes chunks into atomic question tags; a Query Proposer generates multiple atomic query proposals conditioned on the original question and accumulated context; an Atomic Retriever fetches top-k (atomic tag, chunk) pairs per proposal; and an Atomic Selector uses an LLM to pick the single most useful pair (or None to terminate). The 'propose multiple, then select one' design enables a dynamic decomposition path that adapts to retrieved knowledge, avoiding the error-prone single-path commitment of chain methods and the combinatorial cost of tree methods. The entire pipeline is zero-shot and model-agnostic, demonstrated with both GPT-4 and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct.

## Results

With GPT-4, KAR³ achieves F1 scores of 76.48 (HotpotQA), 75.00 (2Wiki), and 57.86 (MuSiQue), with corresponding Accuracy scores of 88.00%, 82.20%, and 62.60%. With Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, the method reaches F1 of 75.27, 72.79, and 50.68, with Accuracy of 88.20%, 81.00%, and 59.70%. The largest gain over the second-best method is +10.1 F1 (20.4%) on MuSiQue with Llama 3, while on HotpotQA the margin is +1.2 F1 (1.6%) over Iter-RetGen with GPT-4. On legal benchmarks, KAR³ reaches 90.12% Accuracy on LawBench (Statute Recitation) and 98.59% on Open Australian Legal QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAR³ outperforms all eight baselines across all three open-domain multihop datasets with both GPT-4 and Llama 3. The closest competitor is Iter-RetGen, which KAR³ surpasses by +1.2 F1 on HotpotQA (76.48 vs. 75.27) and +5.4 F1 on MuSiQue (57.86 vs. 52.48) with GPT-4; with Llama 3, the gap widens to +3.0 F1 on HotpotQA and +7.5 F1 on MuSiQue. ProbTree is the second-closest on 2Wiki (69.42 F1 vs. KAR³'s 75.00), while Self-Ask w/ Retrieval and IRCoT trail by 5–10 F1 points depending on the dataset. Zero-Shot CoT and Naive RAG are substantially outperformed (by 20–35 F1 points), confirming that both external retrieval and iterative decomposition are necessary. In the extended comparison (Table 6), SearChain and GraphRAG (both local and global modes) perform poorly on F1 and EM (often below 15 F1), though GraphRAG Local achieves high Accuracy on HotpotQA (89.00%) due to verbose outputs that echo the query. On legal benchmarks, KAR³ achieves 90.12% Accuracy on LawBench Statute Recitation versus 16.60% for GraphRAG Local and 1.23% for Zero-Shot CoT, and 98.59% on Open Australian Legal QA versus 88.27% for GraphRAG Local.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAR³-RAG extends the iterative retrieval-reasoning lineage of Self-Ask, IRCoT, and Iter-RetGen by introducing a knowledge-aware decomposition mechanism where the decomposition path is dynamically shaped by what is actually retrievable, rather than following a fixed chain or tree structure. Its key conceptual departure is the 'dual rewriting' strategy: both document chunks and queries are atomized into question-form tags, creating an intermediate retrieval layer that bridges the semantic gap between query phrasing and corpus schema. This contrasts with prior RAG work that rewrites only the query side, and with knowledge-graph approaches (GraphRAG, KG-based MHQA) that impose rigid triple structures at high construction cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that coupling decomposition with retrieval through atomic alignment yields consistent gains across three multihop benchmarks and two model families, with the largest improvement (20.4%) on the hardest dataset (MuSiQue). However, the approach's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the underlying LLM's reasoning quality—GPT-3.5 experiments show it fails to outperform simpler baselines—limiting its applicability to resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the query proposer can be made learnable to reduce inference cost, whether in-context demonstrations can further boost decomposition quality, and whether the atomic tagging preprocessing scales economically to very large corpora.
