## Problem

Text-guided image-to-image diffusion models can be misused to spread misinformation, infringe copyrights, and evade content tracing, yet no method exists to reliably retrieve the original image of a translated query. Existing similarity-based retrieval approaches fail to generalize across different diffusion models because each model introduces distinct visual characteristics, causing a trained embedder to drop from 87.1% mAP on its training model to as low as 30.5% on an unseen one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252559/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single generated RGB image (query) produced by any of seven diffusion models via SDEdit-style text-guided translation, and matches it against a reference database of 1,000,000 original images. Both query and reference images are resized to 256×256 before being encoded by the VAE encoder, producing 4,096-dimensional latent vectors (for SD2's VAE). The output is a ranked list of reference images by similarity, evaluated via mAP and Top-1 Accuracy against the true origin.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that a linear transformation matrix W exists such that W-projected VAE embeddings of a generated image and its origin become arbitrarily close (Theorem 1), and that this W generalizes across diffusion models with different VAE architectures and parameters (Theorem 2, supported by near-unity cosine similarity of singular value vectors). In practice, W is learned via gradient descent on a CosFace metric loss over triplets of (generated, origin, negative) VAE-encoded embeddings, using only the SD2 VAE encoder for all models. This single linear layer applied to VAE latents achieves 86.6% average mAP on unseen models—surpassing the best similarity-based baseline by 31.6% mAP—while being 8.6× faster to train and 875× faster to match than feature-map-based generalization methods.

## Results

On the OriPID test set, the proposed method (VAE embeddings with linear transformation) achieves 88.8% mAP and 86.6% Acc on the seen model (Stable Diffusion 2), and 86.6% mAP and 84.5% Acc averaged across six unseen diffusion models. Per-model unseen mAP ranges from 81.5% (SDXL) to 90.3% (Kolors). On the real-world SEED-Data-Edit dataset with manually edited images, the method achieves 86.6% mAP and 85.5% Acc, outperforming all competing methods. Training takes only 0.17 hours on 8 A100 GPUs, and matching speed is 875× faster than the best domain-generalization baseline (QAConv-GS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 20 publicly available models (supervised, self-supervised, vision-language, and image copy detection) fail dramatically on OriPID, with the best being AnyPattern at only 29.1% mAP and 25.7% Acc, highlighting that no off-the-shelf embedding can handle diffusion-model-generated translations. Among trained similarity-based baselines, CosFace achieves the best seen performance (87.1% mAP) but drops to 52.2% mAP on unseen models; the proposed method surpasses it by +34.4% mAP on unseen data. The strongest generalizable baseline, QAConv-GS, reaches 75.8% unseen mAP but is still 10.8% below the proposed method, and suffers from 875× slower matching speed (464 vs. 0.53 in 10⁻¹⁰ s/pair). On the manual-editing SEED-Data-Edit benchmark, the proposed method (86.6% mAP) outperforms all baselines by at least 8.3% mAP over the next best (TransMatcher at 78.3%). The method also trains 8.6× faster than the nearest competitor (0.17 h vs. 1.47 h for QAConv-GS), making it the only approach that simultaneously achieves top accuracy, strong generalization, and practical efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining the ID² task (origin identification for diffusion-translated images) as distinct from Image Copy Detection, which handles manually-designed transformations rather than prompt-guided diffusion edits. Methodologically, it repurposes the VAE encoder already embedded in diffusion models as a feature extractor, then proves that a single learned linear transformation on VAE embeddings suffices for cross-model retrieval—departing from the standard paradigm of training deep embedding networks. The metric learning framework (CosFace) and the SDEdit-based generation pipeline are borrowed from prior work, but the theoretical existence and generalizability proofs for the linear transformation are novel contributions not present in ICD or AI-generated content detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a timely and practically urgent security gap: as diffusion models proliferate, there is no standardized way to trace a translated image back to its origin for misinformation correction, copyright enforcement, or content tracing. The OriPID dataset (2M training pairs, 35K test queries across 7 models) becomes the first benchmark for this task, and the finding that a 0.17-hour-trained linear matrix outperforms all deep embedding models by over 30% mAP offers a highly efficient, deployable solution. Key limitations remain: the method fails on CLIP-based paradigms (IP-Adapter), struggles with hard negatives due to VAE compression, and the theoretical guarantee (Hypothesis 1) for VAE-encoded but non-SDEdit paradigms is still unproven. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion, multi-turn editing chains, and adversarially crafted queries designed to evade retrieval.
