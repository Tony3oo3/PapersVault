## Problem

Robotic planning for long-horizon, multi-stage tasks faces a fundamental trilemma: fine-grained video prediction is computationally expensive and accumulates errors, while forward subgoal planning is efficient but drifts off-task due to compounding inaccuracies. No existing method simultaneously achieves computational efficiency, long-horizon consistency, and on-task prediction accuracy for real-time deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251564/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (256×256 in simulation; three Logitech C922PRO camera views in real-world), a natural-language instruction, and proprioception. It produces action chunks of fixed length 6, ensembled via exponential weighting over overlapping sequences. Planning operates entirely in a frozen latent space (DecisionNCE or SigLIP), so the subgoal sequence is a set of low-dimensional latent vectors rather than pixel images, and the goal-fusion module compresses the entire (sub)goal sequence into a single token before conditioning the diffusion policy.

## Method

LBP introduces a backward planning scheme in latent space with three components: (1) a goal predictor f_g (two-layer MLP) that grounds the language instruction into a final latent goal z_g from the current observation; (2) a unified subgoal predictor f_w that recursively generates intermediate subgoals from the final goal toward the current state, governed by a planning coefficient λ (default 0.5) that controls temporal sparsity, trained with both ground-truth supervision and self-prediction consistency to reduce compounding error; and (3) a Perceiver-style cross-attention goal-fusion module that adaptively compresses the asymmetric coarse-to-fine subgoal sequence into a single context token for the policy. The low-level policy is a diffusion policy (25 denoising steps) with a shared ResNet-34 backbone, FiLM language conditioning, and action chunking. The key architectural insight is that predicting subgoals in reverse temporal order (from the grounded final goal backward) keeps every predicted subgoal aligned with task completion, fundamentally mitigating the off-task drift inherent in forward planning while requiring only lightweight MLPs rather than heavy generative models.

## Results

On the LIBERO-LONG benchmark (10 long-horizon manipulation tasks), LBP with DecisionNCE latent space achieves 88.6% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline Seer (78.6%) by 10 percentage points. LBP with SigLIP latent space also achieves 85.0%, demonstrating flexibility across different latent representations. In real-world experiments on four multi-stage tasks (Stack 3/4 cups, Move cups, Shift cups), LBP consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest margins appearing at later stages (e.g., 67.1 vs. 41.8 avg. score on Shift cups vs. LCBC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBP (DecisionNCE, 88.6%) decisively outperforms all six baselines on LIBERO-LONG. The closest competitor is Seer (78.6%), a video-prediction-based inverse dynamics model, which LBP surpasses by 10 points; SuSIE (76.3%), an image-editing diffusion subgoal planner, trails by 12.3 points. Among non-planning baselines, MPI (77.3%) and MVP (68.2%) are mid-tier, while OpenVLA (54.0%) and MTACT (41.0%) perform substantially worse. In real-world experiments, LBP wins on all four tasks with the largest gaps at later stages: on Shift cups (5 stages), LBP scores 67.1 vs. LCBC 41.8, GLCBC 36.1, and SuSIE 17.7, confirming that backward planning's advantage grows with task horizon. The paper also shows that LBP's lightweight MLP-based planner matches or exceeds heavy generative models (Seer, SuSIE) while being far more computationally efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of robotic planning: video-prediction planners (UniPi, HiP, Seer) that generate full pixel-level future frames, and forward subgoal planners (SuSIE, HiQL, Nair & Finn) that predict intermediate goals autoregressively from the current state. LBP's key conceptual departure is reversing the temporal direction of subgoal prediction—starting from a grounded final latent goal and recursively predicting intermediate subgoals backward toward the current state—inspired by coarse-to-fine paradigms in NLP and vision. The low-level policy builds directly on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) with FiLM-conditioned ResNet-34 features, while the high-level planner uses only two-layer MLPs in DecisionNCE or SigLIP latent space, a stark contrast to the diffusion-based generative models used by competing planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LBP demonstrates that the field's trend toward increasingly complex generative models for planning is not necessary: lightweight MLPs with a backward planning scheme can outperform diffusion-based video planners on long-horizon tasks while enabling real-time deployment. The practical impact is significant for roboticists seeking efficient, on-task planning without heavy inference costs. Open questions include whether the backward scheme generalizes to tasks with highly non-deterministic dynamics, how to automatically select the number of subgoals and the planning coefficient λ, and whether richer latent spaces (beyond frozen encoders) could further improve subgoal quality. The paper also leaves open the integration of key-frame detection for adaptive subgoal selection.
