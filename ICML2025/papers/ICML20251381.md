## Problem

Existing OOD benchmarks like ImageNet-C are no longer truly out-of-distribution for models trained on web-scale datasets such as LAION, since common corruptions (blur, noise, JPEG artifacts) already appear abundantly in those training sets. This makes it impossible to distinguish genuine OOD robustness from mere exposure to similar distortions during training, as evidenced by saturating model scores on ImageNet-era benchmarks. The paper addresses this gap by introducing LAION-C, a new benchmark with novel synthetic distortions specifically designed to remain OOD even for web-scale training data.

## Input / Output

Each input is an RGB image from the ImageNet validation set, corrupted with one of six synthetic distortions (Mosaic, Glitched, Vertical Lines, Geometric Shapes, Stickers, Luminance Checkerboard) at one of five intensity levels, yielding 131,040 total images. The classification task is simplified to 16 superclasses (e.g., ball, bird, cat, dog, fish, primate, snake) to enable human psychophysical evaluation, with 273 images per superclass. Outputs include per-model top-1 accuracy broken down by distortion type and intensity level, overall LAION-C accuracy, and error consistency (Cohen's κ) scores quantifying agreement between model and human error patterns.

## Method

The paper designs six fully synthetic distortions targeting different aspects of visual processing—texture handling, color perception, edge detection, amodal completion, and local lighting adaptation—selected to be exotic enough to have negligible occurrence in web-scale datasets while still testing feature extraction relevant to robust object recognition. Intensity levels are calibrated so that either humans or a reference ViT-B model reach chance performance at the highest level, ensuring a gradual difficulty gradient from near-perfect to chance. OOD-ness is verified through three complementary measures: qualitative search in LAION-400M, model performance divergence (LAION-C yields ~27% standard deviation across models vs ~10% for other OOD datasets), and FID scores (~70 for LAION vs LAION-C vs ~40 for LAION vs ImageNet-C). The evaluation suite spans 58 vision models (ViT, ConvNeXt, EVA, ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, etc.) plus GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro, alongside a controlled psychophysical experiment with 19 participants (11,400 trials) to establish human baselines and compute error consistency.

## Results

On LAION-C, the best model (EVA-G-P14-560-M30M-IN22K) achieves 67.5% top-1 accuracy averaged across all six distortion types and five intensity levels, while GPT-4o reaches 54.1% and Gemini 1.5 Pro reaches 50.2%. The best human observer scores 55.2% and the average human observer 47.0%, indicating that top models now match or exceed human performance on this truly OOD benchmark. In contrast, models maintain above 60% accuracy on ImageNet-C, confirming that LAION-C poses a substantially harder challenge (FID ≈ 70 vs. ≈ 40 relative to LAION-400M, and ~27% vs. ~10% standard deviation in model performance). Fine-tuning a ViT-H model on LAION-C corruptions raises accuracy from e.g. 45.2% to 80.6% on Mosaic, confirming the dataset retains sufficient signal to be solvable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019), the long-standing OOD benchmark. LAION-C is shown to be substantially more OOD than ImageNet-C relative to web-scale training data: the FID between LAION-400M and LAION-C is ≈ 70 versus ≈ 40 for ImageNet-C, and model performance on ImageNet-C plateaus above 60% while LAION-C yields a ~27% standard deviation across models (vs. ~10% on other OOD datasets). On LAION-C, the best vision models (EVA-G at 67.5%, ViT-L-CLIP at 57.8%) now match or exceed the best human observer (55.2%), marking a paradigm shift from the ImageNet era when humans vastly outperformed models. However, humans still outperform all models on Mosaic (58.0% vs. 53.6% best) and Glitched (78.7% vs. 78.2% best) distortions, while models dominate on Stickers (53.4% vs. 35.7%), Geometric Shapes (81.0% vs. 40.4%), and Luminance Checkerboard (97.2% vs. 63.4%). Error consistency analysis (κ ranging 0.15–0.45) reveals that despite matching human accuracy, models use fundamentally different strategies, suggesting super-human rather than human-like robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the corruption-based OOD benchmarking paradigm established by ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) by arguing that its corruptions (blur, noise, JPEG artifacts) are no longer truly out-of-distribution for models trained on web-scale datasets like LAION-400M. It draws on the psychophysical comparison framework of Geirhos et al. (2018) for human-machine evaluation and the error consistency metric of Geirhos et al. (2020b) to quantify behavioral alignment, while adapting distortion concepts from Kaufmann et al. (2019) into more intense synthetic corruptions. The key conceptual departure is the explicit requirement that test distortions must be OOD even for web-scale training distributions, shifting the benchmarking target from ImageNet-trained models to foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAION-C addresses a critical practical gap: as ImageNet-C scores saturate for modern models, the field lacks a reliable OOD benchmark that truly challenges web-scale vision models, making it difficult to measure genuine robustness progress. The finding that the best models now match or outperform the best human observers on OOD distortions—while simultaneously showing low error consistency (κ ≈ 0.15–0.45)—suggests a paradigm shift where super-human performance is achieved through non-human strategies (e.g., exploiting background cues under occlusion). Open questions include why specific architectures underperform on particular distortions, what the true performance ceiling is without fine-tuning on the corruptions, and whether LAION-C's synthetic nature limits its ecological validity for real-world deployment scenarios.
