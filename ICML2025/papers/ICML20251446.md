## Problem

RL-based embodied agents are lightweight and deployable but only handle single simple tasks, while LLM-based agents generalize well but require 1000B+ parameters and slow multi-token generation. Additionally, the paper mathematically proves that in long-horizon embodied exploration, classic RL reward signals (and thus GAE values) vanish to zero, making it impossible to train LLM-style policies via standard PPO without human-engineered dense rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251446/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LARM takes a task description, text observation (inventory list, historical actions, surrounding blocks), and a real-time RGB image as input, all encoded into token sequences via CLIP and concatenated with a single learnable skill token. Rather than generating a multi-token sentence, the model outputs a single skill token selected by feature matching against skill descriptions, alongside a scalar state value estimate from a separate critic head. This single-token action output enables 0.58 s per inference on an RTX 4090, meeting real-time scheduling requirements.

## Method

LARM is built on TinyLLaVA-3.1B with frozen decoder weights and a trainable LoRA module, pre-trained on a 34 GB Minecraft-related webpage dataset to inject domain knowledge. The key training innovation is Referee RL: the paper proves that under sparse terminal rewards, the critic converges so that all TD-errors vanish except the last, and the GAE value decays to zero as (γλ)^(T-t) → 0, nullifying the PPO actor objective. To fix this, a GPT-4-based referee evaluates each (task, state, action, next-state) tuple and returns one of four discrete auxiliary rewards (correct+positive, correct+neutral, incorrect+neutral, incorrect+negative), providing dense per-step feedback that prevents GAE vanishment. The actor and critic are parametrized by the same LARM backbone with two separate prediction heads, and the full policy is optimized end-to-end via PPO with the combined environment + referee reward.

## Results

On MineDojo, LARM achieves the highest success rate across all 14 tasks with a single model, ranging from 0.93 (Harvest stick) to 0.27 (Harvest iron sword), consistently outperforming the best prior method by 5–28 percentage points. On Mineflayer, LARM achieves 30/30 on wooden, stone, and iron swords, 28/30 on diamond sword, and 16/30 on enchanted diamond sword — the first method to ever complete this achievement (all prior methods score 0/3). LARM runs at 0.58 seconds per inference on a single RTX 4090 GPU, meeting real-time scheduling requirements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MineDojo, LARM outperforms all four baselines (MineAgent, Plan4MC, LLaMA-Rider, RL-GPT) on every task, with the largest margins on longer-horizon tasks: 0.60 vs. 0.47 (Plan4MC) on Harvest iron ingot and 0.37 vs. 0.20 (Plan4MC) on Harvest bucket. On Mineflayer, LARM ties AutoGPT, Voyager, and STEVE on the three simpler achievements (wooden/stone/iron swords, all 100% success) but decisively wins on diamond sword (28/30 vs. STEVE's 3/3, Voyager's 1/3, AutoGPT's 0/3) and enchanted diamond sword (16/30 vs. 0/3 for all three baselines). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the referee RL with GPT-4 (ER+AR4) is critical: replacing it with environment reward alone (ER) drops Harvest iron sword from 0.27 to 0.00, and using a lightweight LLaVA-7B as referee (ER+LAR) yields only 0.00 on the same task, validating that only a giant LLM can serve as an effective referee.

## Relation to Prior Work

LARM sits at the intersection of two lines of work on Minecraft embodied agents: RL-based methods (MineAgent, Plan4MC) that are lightweight but task-specialized, and LLM-based methods (Voyager, STEVE, LLaMA-Rider) that generalize well but require giant models or multi-step prompting at inference. Architecturally, it builds on TinyLLaVA as a frozen backbone with LoRA adapters, and on PPO as the optimization framework. The key conceptual departure is 'referee RL': rather than using an LLM as the policy or as a planner, the paper uses GPT-4 as an external reward oracle during training of a lightweight end-to-end policy, and provides a mathematical analysis showing why standard PPO rewards vanish over long horizons. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first work that directly optimizes an LLM-style embodied policy through online RL interaction with the environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sub-5B model can be trained to complete diverse open-world Minecraft tasks with a single policy, achieving the first enchanted diamond equipment harvest and outperforming prior methods that use task-specific networks. However, the approach is tightly coupled to GPT-4 as the referee, and the ablation showing >10% referee noise causes significant performance degradation raises practical concerns about cost, latency, and reliability for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the referee can be replaced by cheaper or learned models, whether the reward vanishment analysis generalizes beyond Minecraft's discrete skill space, and whether the framework transfers to continuous-control or real-robotic settings.
