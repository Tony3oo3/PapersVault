## Problem

Traditional one-shot subset selection relies on information extractors (IEs) pre-trained on the target dataset, introducing costly dataset dependency that limits scalability. While foundation models (FMs) offer a dataset-agnostic alternative, prior work showed they do not consistently outperform traditional IEs, leaving unclear when and why FMs help. This paper systematically investigates FM effectiveness across dataset characteristics and proposes a multi-FM method that exploits complementary strengths of different FMs for fine-grained subset selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fine-grained image dataset with class labels and a pre-specified sampling rate p (budget). Images are resized to 224×224 and passed through two frozen FMs (CLIP-ViT-L/14 and DINOv2-ViT-S/14) to extract unaligned feature vectors of different dimensions (768-d for DINOv2, 768-d for CLIP). The output is a ranked list of sample importance scores from which the top-M samples (M = p×N) are selected as the training subset; no fine-tuning of the FMs is performed at any stage.

## Method

RAM-APL fuses features from multiple FMs via two complementary metrics that operate in model-agnostic spaces. RAnking Mean (RAM) computes, for each FM, the Euclidean distance from each sample to its class centroid, ranks samples within each class, and averages the normalized ranks across all FMs—mapping unaligned feature spaces into a unified distance-ranking space that captures intra-class representativeness. Accuracy of Pseudo-class Labels (APL) assigns each sample a pseudo-class by nearest centroid in each FM's space, checks agreement with the ground-truth label, and averages the binary correctness across FMs—capturing inter-class confusion. The final importance score is a weighted linear combination Score = W₁·RAM + W₂·(1−APL), where W₁ and W₂ are modulated by the sampling rate p via a sigmoid function (W₁ = α + (1−α)/(1+e^{β(p−0.5)})), prioritizing intra-class similarity at low budgets and gradually incorporating inter-class difficulty as p increases. Samples with the lowest scores are selected, and the key design insight is that ranking-based fusion eliminates the need for cross-model feature alignment while jointly addressing the large intra-class variance and small inter-class separation characteristic of fine-grained datasets.

## Results

RAM-APL achieves state-of-the-art one-shot subset selection on three fine-grained datasets, with average accuracy improvements over Random of +3.74% on Oxford-IIIT Pet, +4.44% on Food-101, and +6.40% on CUB-200-2011 across all sampling rates (1%–70%). On Pet at 50% sampling, RAM-APL reaches 47.5% top-1 accuracy versus 43.6% for the strongest baseline (Graph Cut) and 42.6% for Random. On CUB-200-2011 at 10% sampling, RAM-APL achieves 42.6% accuracy compared to 36.1% for GC and 32.8% for MIN. The method uses CLIP-ViT-L/14 and DINOv2-ViT-S/14 as dual foundation-model feature extractors without any fine-tuning on target datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAM-APL outperforms all 12 baselines at every sampling rate on all three fine-grained datasets (Pet, Food-101, CUB-200-2011). The strongest competitor is Graph Cut (GC), which achieves +1.52%, +3.04%, and +2.78% average improvement over Random on Pet, Food-101, and CUB-200-2011 respectively, compared to RAM-APL's +3.74%, +4.44%, and +6.40%. Geometry-based methods (Herding, KCG, CD) and decision-boundary methods (Margin, Cal) consistently underperform even Random selection at low sampling rates, with Herding showing −2.44% on Pet and −2.16% on Food-101. Training-dynamic methods (Forgetting, GraNd) are competitive at high sampling rates (e.g., GraNd reaches 58.6% on Pet at 70%) but fall well behind RAM-APL at low budgets (e.g., GraNd: 4.9% vs RAM-APL: 6.5% at 1% on Pet). Moderate_DS (MDS) and MIN, which also use class-center distance, are outperformed by RAM-APL's multi-model fusion, confirming that combining CLIP and DINOv2 features via the RAM-APL scoring function yields superior selection over any single-model or single-metric approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot coreset selection literature (Sener & Savarese 2017, Iyer et al. 2021, Xia et al. 2023) by replacing the traditional dataset-dependent information extractor with foundation models, following the initial exploration by Xie et al. (2023) and Killamsetty et al. (2023). However, it departs from those prior single-FM studies in two key ways: (1) it provides a systematic, multi-dataset empirical analysis showing that FM advantage is conditional on dataset granularity and noise level, and (2) it introduces a multi-FM pipeline (RAM-APL) that fuses unaligned features from multiple FMs via a ranking-based alignment (RAM) and pseudo-class label accuracy (APL), rather than relying on a single pre-selected FM. The method draws on geometry-based selection (intra-class distance to class centers) and decision-boundary-inspired signals (inter-class confusion) but unifies them through a sampling-rate-dependent weighting scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FMs consistently outperform traditional IEs on fine-grained datasets but lose their edge on coarse-grained noisy datasets provides practitioners with clear guidance on when to adopt FM-based selection pipelines, and the multi-FM consensus approach eliminates the need to identify a single optimal FM per task. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification on a handful of fine-grained benchmarks, reliance on two specific FMs (CLIP and DINOv2) for the main results, and the fact that the method still requires full-dataset feature extraction (albeit without task-specific training). Open questions include whether the multi-FM strategy generalizes to other modalities (video, audio, text), whether adaptive or iterative selection could further exploit FM features, and whether the ranking-based fusion can be replaced by learned alignment for even better performance.
