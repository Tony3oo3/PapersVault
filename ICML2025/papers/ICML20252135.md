## Problem

Schema matching across heterogeneous databases remains largely manual because prior ML approaches either require extensive labeled data (often 50%+ of attribute pairs) or, in the LLM setting, suffer from poor zero-shot accuracy and O(n²) scalability from binary classification over the full Cartesian product. The paper addresses this by formulating schema matching as information retrieval and introducing a zero-shot, self-improving compositional LLM program that requires no labeled demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only schema-level information—attribute names, descriptions, data types, and table metadata—from both source and target schemas, with no access to raw data values. It outputs a ranked list of candidate target attributes per source query with per-candidate confidence scores (0–100), and can abstain by returning an empty match when no suitable correspondence exists. The retrieval formulation means each source attribute is mapped to at most one target attribute (m:1), making accuracy@1 equivalent to F1-score.

## Method

Matchmaker is a three-stage compositional LLM program: (1) multi-vector document creation encoding target schema tables with ColBERTv2 token-level embeddings, (2) diverse candidate generation combining semantic retrieval (top-k via MaxSim late interaction) with LLM reasoning-based candidates (Chain-of-Thought over schema hierarchy), followed by LLM refinement to a smaller candidate set, and (3) confidence scoring structured as a multiple-choice question with a 'None of the above' abstention option, where the LLM assigns a 0–100 relevance score per candidate. The key novelty is a zero-shot self-improvement loop: the unoptimized program is run on a set of evaluation queries (easy and challenging), full execution traces are captured, an LLM evaluator scores each final output (0–5), and the highest-scoring intermediate input-output pairs are bootstrapped as synthetic in-context examples injected into each stage's prompt—eliminating the need for any human-labeled demonstrations while systematically guiding the LLM's multi-stage reasoning.

## Results

Matchmaker achieves 62.20% accuracy@1 on MIMIC-OMOP and 70.20% on Synthea-OMOP, outperforming the closest retrieval-based baseline ReMatch (42.50% and 50.50%) by approximately 20 percentage points on both datasets. On MIMIC-OMOP, Matchmaker also leads on accuracy@3 (68.80% vs. 63.80%) and is competitive on accuracy@5 (71.10% vs. 72.90%). On Synthea-OMOP, Matchmaker dominates across all k values (70.20/78.60/80.90 vs. 50.50/58.10/74.30 for ReMatch). The largest relative gains over supervised baselines (SMAT, LLM-DP, Jellyfish) exceed 50 percentage points on MIMIC-OMOP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Matchmaker decisively outperforms ReMatch (the closest retrieval-based method) on accuracy@1 by ~20 points on both MIMIC-OMOP (62.20 vs. 42.50) and Synthea-OMOP (70.20 vs. 50.50), though ReMatch edges out Matchmaker on MIMIC accuracy@5 (72.90 vs. 71.10). Against the supervised attention model SMAT, Matchmaker's advantage is dramatic: 62.20% vs. 10.85% (50-50 split) on MIMIC and 70.20% vs. 44.88% on Synthea, highlighting the benefit of the information-retrieval formulation over binary classification. LLM-DP (zero-shot GPT-4 prompting) achieves only 29.59% on MIMIC and 41.44% on Synthea, well below Matchmaker. The fine-tuned Jellyfish models (13B and 7B), despite being trained on the same MIMIC/Synthea data, score only 15.36% and 35.17% respectively, underscoring that task-specific fine-tuning does not compensate for the lack of compositional reasoning. On the simpler non-healthcare datasets (Magellan, WikiData), Matchmaker matches or exceeds ReMatch (1.00 vs. 1.00 on Magellan; 0.95 vs. 0.84 on WikiData), confirming generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary-classification formulation of schema matching (used by SMAT, LLM-DP, and Jellyfish) by recasting it as an information-retrieval problem with candidate generation, refinement, and confidence scoring. It builds on DSPy's bootstrapping paradigm for selecting in-context examples, but extends it to a multi-stage compositional LLM program where intermediate traces are captured and scored by an LLM-as-a-judge to produce synthetic demonstrations for each component. The closest prior work, ReMatch, uses a single retrieval-then-rank LLM call; Matchmaker differentiates itself through diverse candidate generation (semantic retrieval plus CoT reasoning), a refinement stage, MCQ-based confidence scoring with abstention, and the zero-shot self-improvement loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Schema matching is a critical upstream bottleneck for creating ML-ready, interoperable datasets in healthcare, finance, and e-commerce, where manual mapping can take hundreds of expert-hours. The zero-shot self-improvement mechanism—using synthetic in-context examples selected via an LLM evaluator—is potentially transferable to other compositional LLM programs that lack labeled demonstrations. However, the paper acknowledges that Matchmaker is only one component of the broader data harmonization pipeline and is best deployed with human-in-the-loop deferral rather than as a fully autonomous solution. Open questions include whether the self-improvement loop generalizes to non-healthcare schemas with different structural complexity, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge evaluator introduces systematic biases that compound across optimization rounds.
