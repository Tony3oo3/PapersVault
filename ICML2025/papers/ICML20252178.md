## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction strategies for LLMs cannot simultaneously satisfy two critical requirements for long-context generation: robust long-range dependency capture (needing retention of many past tokens) and continuous generation without out-of-memory errors (needing bounded cache size). Recency-based methods like StreamingLLM enforce O(1) memory but degrade accuracy sharply, while retrieval-based methods like Quest maintain full O(T) caches and eventually OOM on long sequences.

## Input / Output

LaCache operates on the 2D KV cache (layers × token positions) of a transformer LLM during autoregressive decoding. It takes the full or partially-evicted KV cache as input and produces a compressed, ladder-shaped KV cache that fits within a user-specified token budget (e.g., 256 or 512 tokens), which is then used for continued text generation. The method is training-free, requires no attention-map access, and is directly compatible with FlashAttention-based inference kernels.

## Method

LaCache introduces a ladder-shaped KV cache pattern that stores KV pairs both sequentially (left-to-right within each layer) and across layers (shallow-to-deep): early tokens are preserved in shallow layers while progressively later tokens are retained in deeper layers, forming a stepwise structure that extends the effective context span under a fixed storage budget. Two hyperparameters control the geometry: span S (number of consecutive layers that retain the same token) and overlap O (number of tokens preserved per layer), which together balance information retention against storage efficiency. To support infinite-length generation, LaCache adds an iterative compaction mechanism: when the cache reaches capacity, the ladder pattern is re-applied to the already-compacted cache, discarding KV states outside the new ladder boundary and allocating freed space to incoming tokens. This recursive compression naturally applies stronger compression to older tokens and weaker compression to newer ones, aligning with recency-based intuition while maintaining the cross-layer coverage that distinguishes LaCache from flat sliding-window approaches.

## Results

On LongBench (21 tasks, Llama2-7B-Chat, 50% KV cache budget), LaCache achieves an average score of 27.34 vs. StreamingLLM's 26.56 and full-cache 29.08, reducing the performance gap from 2.52 to 1.74 points. On Needle-In-A-Haystack (Llama3.2-3B-Instruct-128k, 50% budget), LaCache reaches 99.16% accuracy versus StreamingLLM's 54.54%. On RULER (LongChat-7b-v1.5-32k, 50% budget), LaCache scores 50.88% average across 13 tasks vs. StreamingLLM's 44.82%. On Wikitext-2 (Llama2-7B-Chat, 512-token cache), LaCache degrades perplexity by only ~5% relative to full cache, compared to ~35% for StreamingLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StreamingLLM (the primary recency-based baseline), LaCache wins on every benchmark: lower perplexity on Wikitext-2 and PG19, higher LongBench scores (e.g., 27.34 vs. 26.56 average at 50% budget on Llama2-7B-Chat), nearly double the Needle-In-A-Haystack accuracy (99.16% vs. 54.54%), and a 6-point gain on RULER (50.88% vs. 44.82%). Against importance-based methods (H2O, TOVA, PyramidInfer, SnapKV), LaCache achieves a superior score-throughput trade-off on LongBench: these methods maintain competitive accuracy but suffer from low throughput because they require explicit attention maps, making them incompatible with FlashAttention, whereas LaCache is attention-free and integrates seamlessly with FlashAttention for higher real-device throughput. Against the full KV cache, LaCache at 50% budget retains most of the full-cache performance (e.g., 27.34 vs. 29.08 on LongBench; ~5% PPL degradation vs. ~35% for StreamingLLM on Wikitext-2), and its iterative compaction enables continuous generation up to 600K tokens on PG19 where the full cache OOMs at 160K.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaCache carves new ground in KV cache eviction by introducing a cross-layer organizational principle: rather than all layers caching the same set of tokens (as in StreamingLLM) or using attention maps to select important tokens (as in H2O, TOVA, SnapKV), it distributes different token subsets across different layers in a ladder-shaped pattern. This departs from both recency-based methods (which sacrifice accuracy for O(1) memory) and retrieval/importance-based methods (which sacrifice FlashAttention compatibility for accuracy). The iterative compaction mechanism extends this pattern to support infinite-length generation, a capability that prior methods like Quest (O(T) memory) cannot provide without OOM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaCache addresses a core practical bottleneck in deploying long-context LLMs: the linear memory growth of KV caches that causes OOM on extended sequences. Its training-free, FlashAttention-compatible design makes it immediately deployable without retraining or modifying inference frameworks, which is a significant practical advantage over attention-map-based eviction methods. Open questions include whether the heuristic ladder pattern is truly optimal (the authors acknowledge alternative patterns may exist), whether fine-tuning the compression schedule could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or multimodal settings.
