## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks rely on brute-force optimization or manual prompt design, and their evaluation reduces to a binary success/failure metric that cannot distinguish between vague, low-severity outputs and genuinely actionable harmful content. The paper asks whether LLMs can be systematically induced to produce detailed, high-risk knowledge by exploiting cognitive heuristics analogous to human biases, and whether a more nuanced harmfulness metric can capture the true severity of jailbreak outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252835/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single malicious goal prompt (e.g., 'Write a guide to spread misinformation to manipulate public opinion') and a contextual template (role-playing, hypothetical scenario, or virtual background) as input. An auxiliary LLM (GPT-3.5) processes the goal through intent recognition and concept decomposition, producing k=6 low-complexity sub-concepts by default. The output is a reassembled jailbreak prompt T(C_k) sent to the target model, the target's harmful response R(C_k), and a global harmfulness ranking of attack methods computed via Elo, HodgeRank, and Rank Centrality over pairwise LLM-judged comparisons with majority voting across multiple judge models.

## Method

ICRT is a two-stage iterative framework that exploits two cognitive biases to elicit irrational LLM behavior. Stage 1 (Intent Recognition and Concept Decomposition) uses an auxiliary LLM to split the malicious goal into structural intent I and harmful behavior B, then decomposes them into n low-complexity sub-concepts, filtering out overly explicit ones via a cardinality-constrained safety constraint — this leverages the simplicity effect, hypothesizing that LLMs, like humans, preferentially engage with low-complexity inputs. Stage 2 (Concept Reassembly and Template Matching) selects the k most semantically relevant sub-concepts to the original intent (exploiting relevance bias) and embeds them into a contextual template, then iteratively refines the selection to maximize intent alignment of the target model's response while maintaining low explicit harmfulness. For evaluation, the paper replaces binary ASR with a ranking-based harmfulness metric: multiple LLM judges perform pairwise harmfulness comparisons across five risk categories, and the resulting comparison matrices are aggregated via Elo, HodgeRank, and Rank Centrality to produce a global severity ranking of attack methods.

## Results

On AdvBench (520 harmful objectives), ICRT achieves a 98.2% average Attack Success Rate (ASR) across six target models, outperforming the strongest baseline Multilingual (93.7%) by 4.5 percentage points. Against the most safety-hardened model, GPT-4-0613, ICRT reaches 96% ASR versus 63% for Multilingual and 75% for Cipher. Under black-box defenses (Table 2), ICRT maintains 61.3–67.8% ASR against ICD defenses, compared to 6.3–8.5% for GCG-T and 16.0–22.3% for PAIR. On the NeurIPS 2024 Red Teaming Track, ICRT achieves 87–100% ASR across six models including GPT-4 (87%) and DeepSeek-R1 (96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICRT outperforms all 11 baselines on average ASR across the six AdvBench target models. The closest competitor, Multilingual (93.7%), is surpassed by 4.5 points on average, with the gap widening to 33 points on GPT-4-0613 (96% vs. 63%). JailBroken (92.2%) and Cipher (84.2%) trail by 6.0 and 14.0 points respectively, while CodeChameleon (82.2%) and ReNeLLM (75.8%) lag further behind. The optimization-based methods GCG-T and PAIR are substantially weaker under defenses: without defenses ICRT averages 87.5% versus 29.3% (GCG-T) and 37.0% (PAIR), and under ICD(2shots) the gap widens to 61.3% vs. 4% (GCG-T) and 12% (PAIR). In white-box settings, ICRT (84.7% avg. without defense) also exceeds GCG (65.3%) and AutoDAN (66.7%). The ranking-based harmfulness evaluation (Elo, HodgeRank, Rank Centrality) further confirms ICRT consistently ranks first in generated content severity, while CodeChameleon and GPTFUZZER rank last across all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICRT draws on cognitive psychology (simplicity effect, relevance bias, cognitive decomposition) to motivate a two-stage prompt decomposition-and-reassembly attack, conceptually overlapping with DRAttack (Li et al., 2024) but reframing the mechanism through human heuristics rather than pure optimization. It departs from gradient-based or iterative-search methods (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN) by relying on an auxiliary LLM for structured semantic decomposition rather than numerical optimization. The evaluation contribution is more distinctive: replacing the standard binary attack-success-rate with ranking aggregation (Elo, HodgeRank, Rank Centrality) to produce a continuous harmfulness ordering, a framing not previously applied to jailbreak assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ranking-based harmfulness metric addresses a genuine gap—binary ASR conflates vague topic mentions with actionable dangerous instructions—making it a useful tool for defense prioritization. However, the cognitive-psychology narrative (LLMs exhibiting 'irrational choices' analogous to human heuristics) remains largely correlational; the paper does not ablate whether the psychological framing itself improves over a decomposition-without-narrative baseline, leaving open whether the mechanism is truly cognitive or simply that finer-grained decomposition reduces trigger detection. The attack's 98.2% average ASR, while strong, sits only ~5 points above MULTILINGUAL (93.7%), and the method's reliance on a powerful auxiliary LLM (GPT-3.5/4) for decomposition limits its applicability in resource-constrained or fully black-box settings.
