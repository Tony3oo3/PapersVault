## Problem

Transformers struggle to generalize beyond their pre-training context length because expanding the attention window disrupts attention distributions and incurs quadratic computational costs. Sliding-window approaches achieve extrapolation but sacrifice access to distant information. The paper tackles the open challenge of simultaneously achieving length generalization and random access to long-range context with a fixed-size attention window.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250968/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text token sequence divided into fixed-size chunks (S=64 tokens each) with a special landmark token appended to each chunk. It produces standard autoregressive next-token predictions, but internally retrieves top-k past chunks per chunk (k=8) to form a dynamic context of 512 tokens. During inference, hidden states of past chunks are offloaded to CPU memory and reloaded only when retrieved, reducing GPU memory from O(NLd) to O(Ld/S + (N/2)KSd + NWd).

## Method

The core contribution is Grouped Cross-Attention (GCA), which makes chunk retrieval differentiable by using softmaxed relevance scores as fusion weights over per-chunk Cross-Attention outputs, rather than using scores only for chunk selection as in prior Chunked Cross-Attention. This allows autoregressive loss gradients to back-propagate into the relevance scores, enabling the retriever to learn 'causal retrieval'—selecting past chunks that best predict the next chunk rather than merely semantically similar ones. The full architecture (DRT) splits N layers into lower standard Transformer layers and upper layers with GCA, further divided into G groups for iterative multi-hop retrieval. Training employs Gumbel top-k sampling to balance exploration and exploitation, and a hardware-aware Triton implementation (FlashGCA) achieves linear complexity O(G·L²/S² + (N/2)·L·K·S + N·L·W) by combining sliding-window self-attention with chunk-wise retrieval.

## Results

On PG19 and ArXiv-math (350M params, 16K eval length), DRT retrieval×2 achieves the best perplexity of 13.16 and 2.65 respectively, outperforming all baselines including Landmark Attention (13.21 / 2.70) and RPT (13.52 / 2.92). In the Single NIAH test, DRT maintains 100% accuracy up to 16M tokens — 1000× the 16K pre-training length — while Landmark Attention drops to 0% beyond 32K. In the 2-hop NIAH test, DRT retrieval×2 reaches 94.11% at 256K context, whereas Landmark Attention fails completely (0%) beyond 16K. Training overhead is minimal at only 1.24× the base LM's wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRT retrieval×2 outperforms Landmark Attention on perplexity (13.16 vs. 13.33 PG19 test) and dramatically on NIAH: DRT sustains 100% Single NIAH accuracy from 32K through 16M tokens, while Landmark Attention collapses to 0% at 64K and beyond. Against RPT_contriever, DRT achieves lower perplexity (13.16 vs. 13.49) at less than half the training time (1.24× vs. 2.5×), and far exceeds it on NIAH (100% vs. 2.86% at 64K). Block Recurrent Transformer is the weakest baseline, with the highest perplexity (14.56 PG19 test) and 0% NIAH beyond 64K. Compared to the 7B reference models, DRT (128M) matches Yarn-base on Single NIAH up to 64K and surpasses it at 128K (100% vs. 42.32%), while far exceeding CEPE which drops to 55.45% at 16K. In 2-hop NIAH, DRT retrieval×2 (94.11% at 256K) substantially outperforms Landmark Attention (0% at 64K+), confirming that chunk-level causal retrieval generalizes to multi-hop retrieval scenarios where token-level retrieval fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the chunk-based retrieval architecture of RPT (Rubin & Berant, 2024) and the landmark-token + grouped-softmax mechanism of Landmark Attention (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023), while adopting the chunked cross-attention structure from RETRO (Borgeaud et al., 2022). The key departure is making the retriever end-to-end learnable: instead of using an off-the-shelf or distillation-trained retriever whose scores are excluded from the loss, GCA uses softmaxed relevance scores as fusion weights in a per-chunk cross-attention, so gradients from the auto-regressive loss flow back to the retrieval scores. This 'causal retrieval' objective—retrieving past chunks that best predict the *next chunk* rather than the next token—distinguishes it from both RPT's reference-LM distillation and Landmark Attention's per-token, per-layer retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCA offers a concrete, hardware-efficient (linear-complexity, Triton-based) mechanism for long-context modeling that achieves 1000× length generalization with perfect passkey retrieval at 16M tokens, a first reported in the field. The conceptual insight that chunk-level retrieval generalizes to much larger search spaces than token-level retrieval (explaining why Landmark Attention fails to extrapolate while DRT succeeds) may reshape how retrieval-augmented attention is designed. Limitations include evaluation at relatively small scale (128M–350M parameters) and modest perplexity gains over strong baselines at matched context lengths; open questions include whether the 1000× generalization holds at multi-billion-parameter scale and whether self-supervised causal retrieval can extend to information outside the context window.
