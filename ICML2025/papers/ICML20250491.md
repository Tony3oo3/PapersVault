## Problem

Existing synthetic data generation research for medical tasks predominantly focuses on improving generative models or loss functions, while neglecting how to effectively handle and refine the generated data itself. The data-centric profiling approach of Hansen et al. (2023) was limited to classification, leaving a gap for regression tasks where hard-to-learn samples—those predicted with low confidence or misclassified with high confidence—degrade model reliability in data-scarce medical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real tabular medical dataset (split 67/33 train/test), a generative model from a pool of seven (CTGAN, TVAE, NFlow, Bayesian Network, DDPM, Tabformer, GREAT), and a profiling framework (CleanLab or DataIQReg) selected via a label-flipping optimization procedure that evaluates F1 scores across six flipping proportions and six thresholds. The output is a refined synthetic tabular dataset approximately 10% smaller than the original, with hard-profiled samples removed, suitable for both train-on-synthetic/test-on-real evaluation and data augmentation at varying proportions (0.1–1.0).

## Method

Profile2Gen is a three-stage pipeline: (1) Preprocessing profiles the training data using the selected framework to partition samples into easy, hard, and ambiguous subsets; (2) Generation trains a separate generative model on each subset independently, so that each model learns a narrower conditional distribution p(x|c) rather than the full p(x), reducing mode collapse and improving minority-group representation; (3) Postprocessing combines all synthetic subsets, re-profiles the union, and removes samples classified as hard, mitigating distribution poisoning. The framework selection itself is data-driven: labels are randomly flipped at proportion τ, and the framework (CleanLab vs. DataIQReg) achieving the highest average F1 score across flipping rates and thresholds is chosen. A key contribution is DataIQReg, a regression adaptation of DataIQ that replaces NLLLoss with MSE, converts categorical targets to continuous floats, and remaps classification metrics (correctness→RMSE, entropy→R², mutual information→Pearson correlation) to their regression equivalents.

## Results

Across ~18,000 experiments on six medical regression datasets using seven generative models and twelve AutoGluon predictors, Profile2Gen reduces RMSE by 4.8% on average compared to the Traditional (no-profiling) baseline in augmentation tasks, and in TSTR (train-on-synthetic, test-on-real) achieves up to 1.2% lower RMSE than models trained exclusively on real data. The Kruskal-Wallis test confirms significant performance differences in 96% of TSTR cases and 75% of augmentation cases. Profile2Gen synthetic datasets are approximately 10% smaller than the originals due to hard-sample removal, yet maintain or improve predictive performance. Adding 22.7% synthetic data preserves baseline performance, while 68.2% marks a critical degradation threshold for most models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Profile2Gen is compared against two baselines: **Traditional** (generative models trained on the full dataset without any profiling) and **PreProcess** (profiling applied only at the preprocessing stage, with no postprocessing refinement). In augmentation tasks, Profile2Gen significantly outperforms Traditional in 79% of dataset-model combinations and reduces average RMSE by 4.8%. Against PreProcess, Profile2Gen is more consistent across models and datasets, though PreProcess occasionally achieves lower RMSE in specific scenarios (notably the Diabetes dataset with certain generative models). Against the Real-data baseline (train and test on real data), Profile2Gen achieves statistically comparable performance in most TSTR cases, with significant differences in only 60% of augmentation experiments, and in some configurations (e.g., Plasma dataset with NFlow, TVAE, CTGAN) actually surpasses real-data performance. Traditional methods fail to improve predictor performance in 80% of augmentation cases, often increasing RMSE relative to the real-data baseline, underscoring the necessity of the profiling and refinement steps that Profile2Gen introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the data-centric synthetic data generation pipeline of Hansen et al. (2023), originally designed for classification, to the regression setting, and extends the DataIQ profiling framework (Seedat et al., 2022) to handle continuous targets via a new DataIQReg variant. The key methodological departure is the addition of a postprocessing stage that iteratively removes hard-profiled samples from the synthetic output, a step absent in both the Hansen et al. pipeline and the PreProcess baseline. Conceptually, the work positions itself against the dominant model-centric paradigm in synthetic data generation (improving GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) by arguing that data quality profiling and refinement matter more than generative model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that a smaller (~10% reduced), profile-refined synthetic dataset can match or modestly outperform both unrefined synthetic data and, in some cases, real data for medical regression — a practically relevant finding for domains where real data is scarce and privacy-constrained. However, the evaluation is limited to six small OpenML tabular datasets (some of questionable medical relevance, e.g., Body Fat), and the authors themselves note the approach is not a one-size-fits-all solution with inconsistent gains across model-dataset combinations. Open questions include scalability to larger and more heterogeneous medical datasets, whether the hard-sample removal strategy transfers to classification or multimodal settings, and how the generalization-diversity trade-off quantified via Wasserstein distance maps to downstream clinical utility.
