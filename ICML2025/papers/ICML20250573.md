## Problem

Fairness-motivated RL often requires satisfying constraints over intersectional demographic groups (e.g., all 2^d combinations of race, gender, income), but existing methods either enumerate constraints explicitly (infeasible for large d), are restricted to tabular MDPs, or lack oracle-efficient reductions that separate the RL problem from the combinatorial group-optimization problem. This paper asks: can we solve multi-objective RL with exponentially many intersecting group constraints in an oracle-efficient manner for both tabular and large state-space MDPs?

## Input / Output

The method takes an episodic MDP (S, A, P, r, μ) whose states carry d-dimensional feature vectors, a collection of group functions G: X → {0,1} (potentially exponentially large, e.g., all boolean conjunctions over d attributes), a scalar reward r(s,a) ∈ [0,1], and hyperparameters C (Lagrangian norm bound) and ε (best-response approximation error). The output is a distribution D over policies that is minimax-optimal in expectation, paired with Lagrangian weights λ ∈ Λ, together forming a ν-approximate saddle point of the game. Crucially, the algorithm never enumerates G explicitly: the learner only needs a standard RL best-response oracle on a scalarized MDP, and the regulator only needs a linear (or contextual) optimization oracle over G.

## Method

The paper formulates the constrained RL problem as a repeated zero-sum game between a learner (selecting policies) and a regulator (selecting the worst-off group), connected via a Lagrangian relaxation that scalarizes the reward as r_λ(s,a) = r(s,a) + Σ_g λ^g(r(s,a)g(s,a) − α/H). The learner's best response reduces to standard single-objective RL on the modified MDP M_λ, while the regulator's best response reduces to a batch linear optimization over G. Three algorithmic instantiations are provided: (1) FTPL for tabular MDPs, achieving ν-approximate equilibrium in poly(1/γ, 1/δ, |S|, log|G|) rounds; (2) Contextual FTPL for large state spaces where G has a polynomial-sized separator set (as boolean conjunctions do), achieving poly(1/γ, 1/δ, d, log|G|) rounds; and (3) FairFictRL (Fictitious Play) for arbitrary group structures, which converges asymptotically without polynomial-time guarantees but is simpler to implement. An appendix further addresses error cancellations by replacing linear constraints with max{0, h^g(D)} ≤ 0, preserving convexity while preventing slack in some policies from masking violations in others.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, providing oracle-efficient algorithms (FTPL-based for tabular MDPs, contextual FTPL for large state spaces with separator sets, and Fictitious Play for general groups) that converge to ν-approximate minimax equilibria in poly(1/γ, 1/δ, |S|, log|G|) or poly(1/γ, 1/δ, d, log|G|) iterations respectively. Experimentally, on a Barabasi-Albert graph MDP with three degree-based groups and α/H = 0.04, FairFictRL converges within a few iterations to a policy where all groups achieve at least 0.04 average reward, while the non-fair optimal policy achieves at most 0.3 average reward by concentrating on Group 2 nodes. The Pareto frontier analysis (Figure 3) shows that increasing the fairness constraint α progressively equalizes per-group rewards at the cost of decreasing total cumulative reward V^tot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal head-to-head comparison against other fairness-in-RL methods (e.g., Wen et al. 2021, Satija et al. 2023, Calvo-Fullana et al. 2023). The only implicit comparison is between the unconstrained optimal policy (which achieves at most 0.3 average reward by exploiting Group 2 nodes) and the FairFictRL output (which ensures all groups meet the 0.04 threshold with a more evenly distributed occupancy). The paper positions its contribution as algorithmic/theoretical — handling exponentially many intersectional constraints via oracle-efficient reductions — rather than as an empirical benchmark against competing fairness methods. The authors note that their Fictitious Play approach (Algorithm 4) is used in experiments despite weaker (asymptotic-only) guarantees compared to the FTPL-based algorithms, and that it 'works quite well' empirically on the tested graph MDP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the oracle-efficient reduction framework from fair classification (Agarwal et al., 2018; Kearns et al., 2018) to the RL setting, where the learner's problem reduces to standard scalarized RL and the regulator's problem reduces to linear optimization over group functions. For large state spaces, it leverages the contextual FTPL and separator-set machinery from Dudik et al. (2020) and Syrgkanis et al. (2016) to handle exponentially many intersectional constraints without enumeration. It departs from prior fair RL work (Wen et al., 2021; Satija et al., 2023; Cousins et al., 2024) by providing guarantees beyond the tabular setting and by treating the constraint set as an oracle rather than an explicit enumeration, and from constrained RL work (Calvo-Fullana et al., 2023; Miryoosefi et al., 2019) by avoiding the need to enumerate or explicitly represent all constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle-efficient formulation is practically important because intersectional fairness constraints grow exponentially with the number of demographic attributes, making explicit enumeration infeasible; reducing the problem to a standard RL oracle plus a linear optimization oracle makes the approach scalable in principle. However, the empirical validation is limited to a small Barabasi-Albert graph MDP with only three groups, and the practical applicability hinges on the availability of efficient best-response and optimization oracles. Open questions include extending beyond boolean conjunction group structures to infinite function classes, obtaining polynomial convergence bounds for the fictitious play variant, and applying the framework to realistic RLHF or resource-allocation settings with fine-grained subgroup data.
