## Problem

Existing DiGNNs fail to fully exploit the concealed correlations between directed topology and node profiles at the data level, resulting in sub-optimal representations and model performance. While directed edges provide richer structural patterns than undirected ones, prior work has not systematically decoded this 'digraph data knowledge' through a data-centric lens, leaving a gap between the information available in directed graphs and what current architectures can extract.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asymmetric adjacency matrix, node feature vectors (size f), and partial node labels (semi-supervised setting). Beyond standard node embeddings and class predictions, EDEN produces a Hierarchical Knowledge Tree (HKT) as an intermediate data structure that encodes multi-level structural knowledge. The framework is dual-use: it operates as a standalone data-centric DiGNN or as a model-agnostic hot-and-plug online KD module that wraps around any existing (Di)GNN, with the HKT construction decoupled from model training to reduce deployment overhead.

## Method

EDEN constructs a coarse-grained HKT by minimizing directed structural entropy (Shannon entropy over in/out-degree distributions) via a greedy partition-tree algorithm, then refines it through GAN-based mutual information neural estimation that scores intra-partition and inter-partition node representativeness. Knowledge distillation proceeds hierarchically: parent nodes (teachers) generate partition-level representations via affinity-weighted aggregation, and child nodes (students) receive personalized transfers through learned Q_parent and Q_child operators, with a Frobenius-norm KD loss driving mutual evolution. For prediction, a task-adaptive tree-based random walk (sampling siblings for link tasks, parents/children for node tasks) concatenates multi-level representations before an MLP head. The theoretical foundation derives MI lower bounds via Donsker-Varadhan and f-divergence representations (Theorems 3.1–3.3), and a lightweight Monte Carlo variant replaces exhaustive greedy search for scalability on million-node graphs.

## Results

EDEN achieves SOTA node classification accuracy across all 7 directed graph datasets, with headline results of 84.6% on CoraML, 81.4% on WikiCS, 81.3% on Tolokers, and 69.7% on Arxiv. On link-level tasks (Table 2), EDEN leads on all 6 datasets, e.g., 90.84% AUC on CoraML Existence and 94.93% on Tolokers Existence. The paper reports average improvements of 2.78% over the best baseline on node-level tasks and 2.24% on link-level tasks. As a plug-and-play KD module (Table 4), EDEN boosts existing DiGNNs by up to 4.96% (e.g., Dir-GNN from 82.6% to 85.9% on CoraML).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDEN consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and task. On node classification (Table 3), the strongest competitors are HoloNet (spectral, ICLR 2024) and D-HYPR (CIKM 2022), which are typically second-best; EDEN surpasses them by 1.9–2.2% (e.g., 81.4% vs 79.2% on WikiCS, 81.3% vs 79.4% on Tolokers). On link-level tasks (Tables 1–2), EDEN beats the best directed baselines (Dir-GNN, MagNet, HoloNet) by 1.5–3.5% AUC, e.g., 93.3% vs 92.2% on Slashdot Direction and 94.93% vs 93.84% on Tolokers Existence. Undirected baselines (GCN, GAT, GCNII, GATv2, AGT) trail directed methods by 2–5% on most datasets, confirming the importance of edge directionality. As a hot-and-plug module (Table 4), EDEN adds 2.5–4.7% to existing DiGNNs such as Dir-GNN (+4.68%), HoloNet (+4.46%), and DIMPA (+4.32%), with the largest gains on heterophilic datasets (PPI, Flickr). MGC (complex-valued GCN) and NSTE are competitive on Slashdot/WikiTalk but do not appear in the broader 6-dataset link-level comparison, limiting their generalizability assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDEN sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) directed structural entropy from information theory (Li & Pan, 2016), which the paper adapts as a quantification metric for hierarchical partition tree construction; (2) mutual information neural estimation (MINE, Belghazi et al., 2018) and its GAN-based f-divergence variant (Nowozin et al., 2016), repurposed here not for contrastive learning but for refining partition boundaries via node-profile affinity; and (3) hierarchical encoding theory (Clauset et al., 2008), extended from undirected community detection to directed graphs with in/out-degree asymmetry. The key departure from prior DiGNN work (Dir-GNN, MagNet, HoloNet, D-HYPR) is a deliberate shift from model-centric architecture design to data-centric knowledge extraction: rather than designing new message-passing operators for directed edges, EDEN constructs a Hierarchical Knowledge Tree (HKT) that encodes topological and profile knowledge, then distills it into any backbone GNN as supervised signal. This positions EDEN as orthogonal to—and composable with—existing DiGNNs rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that data-level quality (captured via structural entropy and MI) is an underexplored bottleneck for digraph learning, and that hierarchical KD can break through it without modifying the backbone model. The plug-and-play nature (up to 4.96% improvement on existing DiGNNs) makes it practically attractive for practitioners who cannot re-architect their models. However, the multi-step pipeline (greedy tree construction → MI-based refinement → KD loss → random-walk prediction) introduces significant algorithmic complexity, and the authors acknowledge scalability challenges at billion-node scale. Open questions include whether the HKT construction can be parallelized via graph partitioning, whether the Monte Carlo approximation in the lightweight variant loses too much signal on highly heterogeneous graphs, and whether the framework's gains persist when the backbone GNN is already very strong (e.g., large-scale transformers).
