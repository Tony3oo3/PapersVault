## Problem

Self-supervised learning on heterogeneous graphs typically relies on contrastive strategies that require carefully designed graph augmentations and explicit positive/negative sample selection, both of which are problematic for heterogeneous graphs due to their discrete structure and the vagueness of 'maximize/minimize similarity' objectives. Additionally, aggregating semantic information across diverse meta-paths while preserving the graph's overall topology remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251590/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous graph with node features of heterogeneous dimensions (projected into a unified latent space via type-specific linear maps), edge adjacency matrices, and a set of predefined meta-paths. It produces node embeddings that are directly usable for downstream classification and clustering without any graph augmentation or explicit sample-pair construction. The training signal is entirely derived from the optimal transport plans computed between meta-path subgraphs and an aggregated view, eliminating the need for labeled data or augmentation strategies.

## Method

HGOT decomposes the heterogeneous graph into meta-path-specific homogeneous subgraphs (branch views) and fuses them into a central aggregated view via attention-based node aggregation and logical-OR edge fusion. It then computes a fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport plan between each branch view and the aggregated view in graph space (combining node-feature Wasserstein cost and edge-structure GW cost with a trade-off parameter σ), and a corresponding Wasserstein transport plan between the backbone encoder's node representations of the same views. The training loss aligns these two transport plans (matching loss) and additionally corrects the representation-space cost matrix to preserve implicit structural relationships (implicit structure loss), forcing the encoder to learn representations whose pairwise matching geometry mirrors that of the original graph.

## Results

On the node classification task, HGOT achieves the best Micro-F1 across all four datasets: 95.66% on DBLP, 94.49% on ACM, 62.71% on IMDB, and 77.58% on Yelp. Compared to the strongest baselines (HGBER-V, HGMAE, HEAD), HGOT improves Micro-F1 by approximately 2.2–3.0 percentage points per dataset, and by roughly 34% over GraphSAGE on ACM (60.72% → 94.49%). In node clustering, HGOT also leads on most datasets, e.g., 93.41% ACC on DBLP and 55.80% ACC on IMDB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGOT outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset for both Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 in node classification. Against the strongest heterogeneous SSL baselines, the gains are: +2.89 Micro-F1 over HGBER-D on DBLP (95.66 vs 92.93), +2.15 over HGBER-V on ACM (94.49 vs 92.34), +2.58 over HGMAE on IMDB (62.71 vs 60.13), and +2.97 over HEAD on Yelp (77.58 vs 74.61). The margin over homogeneous graph methods is substantially larger — e.g., HGOT improves over GraphSAGE by ~34% on ACM (94.49 vs 60.72) and over GAE by ~29% on ACM (94.49 vs 65.71). Among contrastive SSL baselines, HeCo (92.03 on DBLP) and HGCML (91.11 on DBLP) trail HGOT by 3.6 and 4.6 Micro-F1 points respectively, confirming that the optimal-transport alignment strategy captures matching information that contrastive objectives miss. In node clustering, HGOT also leads on DBLP (93.41 ACC), IMDB (55.80 ACC), and Yelp (66.23 ACC), though HGMAE edges it slightly on ACM ACC (89.83 vs 89.77).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant contrastive self-supervised learning paradigm for heterogeneous graphs (HeCo, HGCML, HGBER) by eliminating graph augmentation and explicit positive/negative sample selection entirely. Instead, it repurposes the Gromov-Wasserstein and Wasserstein distance machinery from the optimal transport literature (Mémoli 2011; Vayer et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2019) to define a transport plan between meta-path subgraph views and an aggregated view, then aligns that plan with the corresponding plan in representation space. The backbone encoder (HAN) and meta-path decomposition follow standard heterogeneous GNN practice, so the novelty is concentrated in the self-supervised objective rather than the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for hand-crafted augmentations and sample-pair selection, HGOT offers a cleaner and arguably more principled self-supervised signal for heterogeneous graphs, which is practically valuable given the difficulty of designing label-consistent perturbations on discrete heterogeneous structures. The >6% average node-classification gain over strong baselines (HGMAE, HGBER) across four datasets is a meaningful margin. However, the finding that node-only matching (σ=1) outperforms the full node+edge Gromov-Wasserstein formulation raises the question of whether the edge-structure transport term is truly necessary or merely a regularizer, and the cubic complexity of the GW solver limits scalability to very large heterogeneous networks. Open questions include whether the OT-alignment objective transfers to other graph types (homogeneous, temporal) and whether the approach remains effective when the number of meta-paths grows large.
