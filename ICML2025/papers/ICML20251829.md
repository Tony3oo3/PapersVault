## Problem

Certifying a federated learning model's performance on an unseen target network using only source-network measurements has lacked rigorous theoretical guarantees. Prior robust evaluation schemes either require centralizing all private client data (violating privacy) or introduce non-vanishing ε-dependent generalization gaps that inflate certificates regardless of the model's inherent robustness. No prior work provided minimax-optimal, privacy-preserving bounds for meta-distributionally robust evaluation in federated settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251829/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server never accesses raw client data; instead, it queries each of K clients for local loss values (ordinary or adversarial at specified radii) via a private communication protocol, with the total number of queries per client growing at most polynomially. The meta-distributional shift bound ε is specified in either f-divergence (e.g., KL, chi-square) or Wasserstein distance between the source and target meta-distributions, and the confidence level δ controls the probability guarantee. The output consists of certified upper bounds on the average loss and the risk CDF, with the CDF bound holding uniformly over all thresholds λ ∈ ℝ simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel adversarial extension of the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz (DKW) inequality (Lemma B.2) and a corresponding robust Glivenko-Cantelli theorem (Corollary B.3), which bound the gap between empirical and true CDFs under f-divergence meta-shifts. For f-divergence shifts, the method reweights client loss values via importance sampling coefficients α_k solved through a server-side convex program that optimizes over the worst-case density ratio within the ε-ball. For Wasserstein shifts, a quasi-convex program allocates per-client robustness radii ρ_k, requiring clients to compute local adversarial losses at multiple radii via stochastic gradient descent. All bounds are asymptotically minimax optimal with generalization gaps that vanish as K and min_k n_k / log K grow, and the entire procedure is computable in polynomial time without accessing private data.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation on four datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, EMNIST, ImageNet) using a simulated federated network of 1,000 nodes with 1,000 local samples per node. The key experimental finding is that the proposed DKW-based risk CDF bounds and f-divergence/Wasserstein robust certificates are tight: the certified upper bounds closely track the true risk CDF of unseen target clients across all tested shift scenarios (feature, label, and combined affine shifts). For f-divergence shifts on CIFAR-10 (resolution and color-based meta-distributions), both Chi-Square and KL-divergence certificates tightly capture the target network's risk CDF. No single headline accuracy or loss number is reported, as the contribution is the certification framework itself rather than a trained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in a head-to-head benchmark sense; it is a theoretical contribution that introduces the first certifiable robust evaluation framework for federated learning under meta-distributional shifts. The closest conceptual baselines discussed are: (1) the naive MMD-based bound (Equation 16), which the paper shows suffers from a non-vanishing ε-dependent gap that does not shrink as K or n_k grow, whereas the proposed bounds have vanishing generalization gaps of rate Õ(min{K, min_k n_k/log K})^{-1/2}; and (2) standard (non-robust) DKW bounds, which the paper extends to the adversarial meta-distributional setting. The paper also notes that prior robust evaluation schemes (e.g., Reisizadeh et al. 2020, Liu et al. 2024) require aggregating all private data centrally for collective adversarial manipulation, violating privacy, while the proposed method operates entirely through federated loss queries. Empirically, the paper validates tightness by showing the certified bounds (dotted curves in Figures 4, 6, 7) closely envelop the true risk CDF of unseen clients, confirming the theoretical guarantees are not overly conservative in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical DKW inequality and Glivenko-Cantelli theorem to adversarial meta-distributional settings, building on the foundational statistical tools of Dvoretzky et al. (1956) and Talagrand (1987). It also draws on the Wasserstein DRO framework of Kuhn et al. (2019) and the adversarial risk formulation of Sinha et al. (2018), but lifts these from single-distribution robustness to the meta-distribution level (a distribution over client distributions). The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to provide theoretical bounds for meta-distributionally robust evaluation in federated settings, departing from prior MMD-based approaches that introduce non-vanishing ε-dependent gaps and from centralized robust evaluation schemes that violate client privacy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first polynomial-time computable, privacy-preserving certificates for federated model evaluation under meta-distributional shifts, with vanishing generalization gaps as network size and per-client samples grow. The novel robust DKW inequality (Lemma B.2) and robust Glivenko-Cantelli theorem (Corollary B.3) are clean theoretical contributions that could inform future work on distributional robustness beyond FL. Key limitations include: the Wasserstein case only covers average risk (not the full CDF), no formal differential privacy certificates are provided, and the practical validity of the bounded meta-shift assumption (ε) remains an open question for real deployments. Extending the Wasserstein CDF bounds and developing information-theoretic privacy guarantees for the query protocol are identified as future directions.
