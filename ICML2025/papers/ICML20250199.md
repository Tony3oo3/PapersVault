## Problem

Existing automated fact-checking systems are predominantly text-only, lack explainability, rely on static parametric knowledge, or address only isolated subtasks (evidence retrieval, summarization, OOC detection). No prior method can simultaneously process multimodal claims and multimodal evidence while performing end-to-end, explainable verification with dynamic evidence retrieval. Additionally, widely used benchmarks (AVERITEC, VERITE, MOCHEG) suffer from data leakage because their claims fall within the pretraining window of current MLLMs, inflating apparent performance.

## Input / Output

DEFAME accepts a text claim or a text+image claim as input, processing interleaved text-image sequences where images are Base64-encoded and referenced by identifier, preserving their original positional context. The system produces three outputs: a benchmark-specific verdict label (e.g., Supported/Refuted/Misleading/NEI), a detailed structured fact-check report documenting every retrieval action, summarized evidence, step-by-step reasoning, and source hyperlinks, and a concise one-paragraph justification summary appended to the report for end-user readability. The pipeline is constrained to a maximum of 32 images per scraped page and truncates inputs exceeding the backbone MLLM's context window.

## Method

DEFAME is a zero-shot, six-stage MLLM agent pipeline that functions as a dynamic multi-step RAG system, requiring no fine-tuning. Each iteration cycles through: (1) a planning stage where the MLLM selects targeted actions from four external tools (web search via Serper API, image search, reverse image search via Google Vision API, and geolocation via GeoCLIP); (2) execution of the selected tools with temporal leakage prevention (results restricted to pre-claim-date sources); (3) abstractive summarization of retrieved evidence into a running fact-check report; (4) a develop stage for step-by-step multimodal reasoning over the accumulated evidence; and (5) verdict prediction, which loops back to planning if the model returns NEI (up to three iterations). A final justification stage distills the full report into a concise, linked summary. The key architectural insight is that every MLLM call receives the current state of the fact-check report as in-context input, enabling iterative deepening and multi-hop reasoning without any task-specific training. The framework is backbone-agnostic, tested with GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, LLaVA-OneVision (7B), and Llama 4 Scout.

## Results

DEFAME achieves 70.5% accuracy on AVERITEC, 59.2% on MOCHEG, and 83.9% True-vs-False accuracy on VERITE, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. On the newly introduced CLAIMREVIEW2024+ (claims post-dating GPT-4o's knowledge cutoff), DEFAME reaches 69.7% accuracy, dramatically outperforming the GPT-4o baseline (35.2%) and GPT-4o CoT (31.4%). The system also wins the AVERITEC shared task, ranking first among 21 submitted systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFAME outperforms all six prior SOTA methods on their respective benchmarks. On AVERITEC, it beats DEBERTA (Cao et al., 2023) by +4.9 pp (70.5 vs 65.6) and CFR (Sriram et al., 2024) by +10.5 pp (70.5 vs 60.0). On MOCHEG, it surpasses META SUM (Chen et al., 2024) by +10.6 pp (59.2 vs 48.6) and LVLM4FV (Tahmasebi et al., 2024) by +14.1 pp (59.2 vs 45.1). On VERITE, DEFAME dominates CHASMA (Papadopoulos et al., 2024b) across all three splits (T/F: 83.9 vs 52.1; T/MC: 83.3 vs 59.3; T/OOC: 78.4 vs 74.4) and AITR (Papadopoulos et al., 2024a) on T/F (+25.9 pp) and T/MC (+31.5 pp), though AITR retains a narrow edge on T/OOC (82.7 vs 78.4). The most striking gap appears on CLAIMREVIEW2024+, where DEFAME (69.7%) more than doubles the GPT-4o baseline (35.2%) and GPT-4o CoT (31.4%), demonstrating that retrieval-based evidence grounding mitigates the temporal knowledge cutoff that cripples parametric-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEFAME positions itself as the first end-to-end automated fact-checking system that natively processes both multimodal claims and multimodal evidence with dynamic retrieval, unifying a fragmented landscape where prior work addressed isolated subtasks (evidence retrieval, OOC detection, chart verification) or restricted to a single modality. Conceptually it extends the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) into a multi-step, planning-driven agent loop, and its closest relative RAGAR (Khaliq et al., 2024) differs in that RAGAR converts claim images to text descriptions and retrieves only textual evidence. The paper departs from MLLM-only approaches (which rely on static parametric knowledge) by grounding every verdict in externally retrieved, temporally-constrained evidence, and from trained multimodal classifiers (HAMMER, AITR, CHASMA) by being entirely zero-shot and training-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CLAIMREVIEW2024+ directly addresses a widely acknowledged but under-addressed problem: data leakage in AFC benchmarks where GPT-4o's pretraining likely overlaps with publicly fact-checked claims. The temporal generalizability result—GPT-4o dropping from ~79% to ~35% on post-cutoff claims while DEFAME holds near ~70%—provides concrete evidence that retrieval-based grounding is necessary for real-time fact-checking, challenging the assumption that increasingly capable MLLMs can substitute for external evidence. Open questions include scalability to millions of daily social-media claims (cost is ~$0.13/claim), source credibility assessment beyond search-engine ranking, and whether the six-stage pipeline generalizes to non-English or non-web evidence sources. The paper also acknowledges that output quality still falls short of professional human fact-checkers, positioning DEFAME as an augmentation tool rather than a replacement.
