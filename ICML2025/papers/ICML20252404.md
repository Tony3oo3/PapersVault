## Problem

The dominant ML paradigm assumes that scaling data and model sizes will improve out-of-distribution performance, including compositional generalization. However, it remains unclear whether vision models actually develop compositional understanding through scaling, or whether the inherent combinatorial sparsity of large datasets (where most attribute-object combinations are never observed) prevents this. This paper asks: under what specific data conditions do vision models achieve compositional generalization, and is mere data scale sufficient?

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (128×128 for synthetic datasets, variable for MPI3D) explicitly labeled with a pair of concept values (e.g., color and shape) during training, while additional unlabeled dimensions (position, orientation, background) vary to provide augmentation. The model outputs two simultaneous concept predictions via separate linear heads on a shared backbone. For pretrained model evaluation, frozen features are probed with linear or two-layer MLP classifiers. The (n,k) framework controls the concept space: n values per concept dimension and k observed training combinations per concept value, with the remaining (n−k)·n combinations held out for OOD testing.

## Method

The paper introduces an (n,k) experimental framework that systematically varies concept diversity (n) and combination coverage (k) to isolate causal effects on compositional generalization. Models (ResNet-50 trained from scratch; DINOv1, DINOv2, CLIP evaluated via probing) are trained on balanced subsets where each concept value appears in exactly k training combinations, with oracle model selection and multi-head classification to encourage feature reuse. The key empirical finding is a three-phase learning dynamic: spurious features at low diversity (<10% combinations), discriminative but nonlinearly-factored features at moderate diversity, and strongly linear (R²>0.8) and orthogonal (cosine similarity<0.1) representations only at high diversity (>75%). Theoretically, the paper proves (Proposition 4.1) that when representations are perfectly linearly factored, just k=2 combinations per concept value suffice for a linear classifier to generalize perfectly to all unseen combinations, by showing the 2n×2n system of equations for recovering individual concept vectors is full rank.

## Results

In basic compositional settings (n=3, k=2), all from-scratch models achieve near-100% in-distribution accuracy but suffer 60–80% accuracy drops on unseen concept combinations, and scaling training data by 4× does not close this gap. Increasing concept diversity (more values n or more combinations k) is the critical driver: at >75% combination coverage, models achieve R² > 0.8 linearity, cosine similarity < 0.1 orthogonality, and >90% zero-shot OOD accuracy. Proposition 4.1 proves that under perfect linear factorization, just k=2 combinations per concept value suffice for perfect generalization to all (n−2)·n unseen combinations. Pretrained models (CLIP, DINOv2) achieve above-random but imperfect compositional accuracy, with DINOv2 ViT-L reaching 95% non-linear probe accuracy at 93% combination coverage on FSprites.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four pretrained models—ResNet-50 ImageNet, DINOv1 ResNet-50, DINOv2 ViT-L/14, and CLIP ViT-L/14—against a from-scratch ResNet-50 baseline on compositional generalization. DINOv2 ViT-L consistently outperforms all other models, reaching 95% non-linear probe accuracy at 93% combination coverage on FSprites, compared to 92% for DINOv1 ResNet-50, 84% for CLIP, and 82% for ResNet-50 ImageNet. CLIP shows a clear specialization for color-based concepts (highest accuracy on CMNIST color-digit and Shapes3D object-hue), while DINOv2 excels on shape-based concepts (scale, shape, orientation). Non-linear probes (two-layer MLP) generally outperform linear probes, with the largest gap for DINO models (e.g., 0.80→0.92 for DINO ResNet-50 at 93%), suggesting their representations encode compositional information in a partially non-linear manner. For ResNet-50 ImageNet and CLIP, the linear/non-linear gap is smaller, indicating more linearly separable representations. Under the ideal linear factorization protocol (Proposition 4.1), all pretrained models achieve >90% accuracy on at least one concept per dataset, but none achieve the perfect generalization predicted by the theory, confirming only partial linearity in their representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the linearly factored embeddings framework of Trager et al. (2023), adopting their definition and extending it with a theoretical proof (Proposition 4.1) showing that k=2 combinations per concept value suffice for perfect generalization under ideal linear factorization. It departs from the dominant scaling paradigm (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.) by demonstrating that combinatorial diversity, not raw data volume, drives compositional generalization, and contrasts with model-centric approaches (object-centric learning, soft prompting, feature alignment) by showing that standard architectures like ResNet-50 can develop compositional structure purely through data design. The (n,k) framework positions this work as a controlled causal study, complementing prior correlational observations of linearity in VLMs (Stein et al., Park et al.) with systematic ablations over data properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 4x more in-distribution data fails to close the ID-OOD gap while increasing concept diversity does, challenges the default assumption in the field that scaling is a universal remedy and has direct practical implications for dataset construction (prioritize combinatorial coverage over sample count). The three-phase learning dynamics (spurious → discriminative → linearly factored) provide a concrete diagnostic for when models have or haven't acquired compositional structure. Key limitations include the restriction to two concepts and simplified synthetic/semi-synthetic datasets; the linear independence assumption in Proposition 4.1 may break down at scale, and whether these findings transfer to multi-concept, real-world scenes remains open. The partial linearity observed in CLIP and DINOv2 suggests current pretraining is close but not sufficient, pointing toward targeted data curation as a practical path forward.
