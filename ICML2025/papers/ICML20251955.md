## Problem

Existing diffusion distillation methods (e.g., consistency models) produce fast generators that support only one or a few sampling steps, discarding the iterative refinement capability that makes diffusion models flexible for test-time compute scaling and zero-shot controllable sampling. The paper asks whether a generative model can be distilled that enables fast one-step generation while preserving the ability to improve sample quality through multi-step sampling and to perform asymptotically exact probabilistic inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251955/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The generative denoiser takes a noisy observation y_σ at an arbitrary noise level σ (not just pure Gaussian noise) plus a random latent z ~ N(0,I), and outputs a clean sample x_0 approximating the denoising posterior q(x_0|y_σ). For class-conditional generation on ImageNet (64×64 or 512×512), a class label is additionally provided. For inverse problem solving via PnP-GD, an energy function E(·) and a noise annealing schedule are supplied, and the output is a sample from the unnormalized target π(x_0) ∝ q_data(x_0) exp(−E(x_0)/β). The model operates at any noise level, enabling both one-step generation from pure noise (high σ) and multi-step refinement (annealing σ from high to near-zero).

## Method

The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that the conditional score ∇log q(x_t|y_σ) has a closed-form expression in terms of the unconditional score, via an 'effective noise level' σ_eff = (σ^{-2}+t^{-2})^{-1/2} and effective observation y_{σ_eff} = (σ^{-2}y_σ + t^{-2}x_t)/(σ^{-2}+t^{-2}), allowing the pretrained teacher score model to estimate the data score without retraining. Training extends VSD by conditioning on noisy data (NCVSD): the reverse KL between the generator's diffused output p_θ(x_t|y_σ) and the true diffused posterior q(x_t|y_σ) is minimized, with the model score estimated by a separate conditional score model D_φ trained online. The generator G_θ is initialized from the teacher, with stochasticity injected by adding scaled noise to y_σ (γ=0.414) and the original conditions passed through a ControlNet-like branch; an auxiliary adversarial loss (JSD with a discriminator) is added to surpass the teacher's quality ceiling. Multi-step sampling uses a DDIM-like latent variable construction that preserves the marginals p(x_i)=q(x_i|y_σ), and the PnP-GD sampler replaces the expensive reverse-diffusion prior step in the Split Gibbs Sampler with a single generative denoiser call, reducing NFEs from ~1000–2500 to 50.

## Results

On ImageNet-512×512, 4-step NCVSD-L achieves FID 1.76, surpassing the teacher EDM2-XXL (FID 1.81) and 2-step sCD-XXL (FID 1.88) while using only 4 NFEs versus 126 for the teacher. On ImageNet-64×64, 4-step NCVSD-L reaches FID 1.53, beating 2-step sCD-XL (FID 1.66). For inverse problems on FFHQ-256×256, PnP-GD achieves the best LPIPS across all five tasks (e.g., 0.128 on inpainting, 0.151 on super-resolution) using only 50 NFEs, compared to 1000+ NFEs for DAPS and DPS, representing roughly a 20× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against teacher diffusion models (EDM2), NCVSD-L at 4 NFEs (FID 1.76) outperforms the much larger EDM2-XXL (FID 1.81) and EDM2-L (FID 1.88) on ImageNet-512×512, while requiring only 4 NFEs versus 126. Against consistency models, NCVSD-L at 4 steps beats sCD-XXL at 2 steps (1.76 vs. 1.88) and sCD-XL at 2 steps (1.76 vs. 1.93), and NCVSD-M at 4 steps matches sCD-XL at 2 steps (1.92 vs. 1.93), all with far fewer training iterations (32k×3 vs. 200k). Against the original CD and ECM consistency models, NCVSD-S at 4 steps (FID 2.00) already surpasses ECM-XL at 2 steps (FID 1.96) and CD at 2 steps (FID 4.70) on ImageNet-64×64. For inverse problems, PnP-GD achieves the best LPIPS on all five tasks (inpainting 0.128, Gaussian deblur 0.155, motion deblur 0.160, super-resolution 0.151, phase retrieval 0.186) with only 50 NFEs, outperforming DAPS (1000 NFE), DPS (1000 NFE), PnP-DM (2483 NFE), DWT-Var (99 NFE), IIGDM (99 NFE), DiffPIR (100 NFE), and DDRM (100 NFE) on perceptual quality. The PSNR trade-off is acknowledged: PnP-GD sacrifices some PSNR (e.g., 27.06 vs. DAPS's 29.19 on Gaussian deblur) in favor of higher perceptual fidelity, consistent with the known distortion-perception tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

NCVSD extends Variational Score Distillation (VSD) by conditioning the distillation objective on noisy data, enabling the learned model to approximate the full denoising posterior rather than a single-point MMSE estimate. The key theoretical enabler is Proposition 1, which shows the unconditional score function implicitly characterizes the conditional score of denoising posteriors via an effective noise-level transformation, reducing the data-score estimation to a single call to a pretrained score model. For multi-step sampling, the paper adapts the DDIM latent-variable framework, and for probabilistic inference it builds on the Split Gibbs Sampler, replacing the expensive reverse-diffusion prior step of PnP-DM with a one-step generative denoiser call. The parameterization reuses EDM2's preconditioning and introduces a ControlNet-style architecture for injecting the noisy-data condition, while an auxiliary adversarial loss (as in DMD and SDSD) breaks the teacher's performance ceiling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative denoiser concept offers a practical middle ground between one-step GAN distillation (fast but no iterative refinement) and full diffusion sampling (flexible but slow), with the appealing property that test-time compute can be scaled via multi-step sampling. The 20× NFE reduction for nonlinear inverse problems (50 vs. 1000+) while matching or beating LPIPS is practically significant for real-time imaging applications. Key limitations include the dependence on a pretrained teacher (no from-scratch training), the fragility of adversarial training requiring careful tuning, and the lack of validation at the largest scales (EDM2-XXL). Open questions include whether the noise-conditional score estimator can be integrated with other distillation objectives, whether generative denoisers can be pretrained without a teacher, and whether the approach extends to text-to-image or 3D generation at scale.
