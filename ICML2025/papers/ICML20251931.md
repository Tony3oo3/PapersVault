## Problem

While global upper bounds on the number of linear regions in ReLU networks are well studied, the local density of linear regions near the data distribution and its relationship to key learning phenomena—representation learning, adversarial robustness, and implicit optimization biases—remained theoretically unexplored. Empirical observations showed that the number of linear regions near data drops sharply in the terminal phase of training (associated with grokking and improved robustness), but no formal measure or theoretical framework connected this local geometric structure to the learned function's properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully-connected ReLU network with parameters θ = (W_i, β_i), an input data distribution p with compact support Ω ⊂ R^{n0}, and a noise scale σ for Gaussian perturbations of the biases. It produces a scalar local complexity value LC(N_θ, p) defined as the expected (n0−1)-dimensional volume density of the nonlinear locus over the data, along with explicit upper and lower bounds relating LC to the local rank (expected Jacobian rank of intermediate-layer pre-activations), the total variation E[‖∇_x N(x)‖], and the representation cost R(N_θ). The local complexity is defined as an expectation over noisy biases b_i = β_i + δ_i, making it robust to small parameter perturbations rather than depending on a single parameter configuration.

## Method

The paper defines local complexity as the limit of the expected (n0−1)-dimensional volume of the nonlinear locus B_{N_θ̃} intersected with a ball B_ε(x), normalized by the ball volume, then averaged over x ~ p. The key technical result (Theorem 2) applies the generalized co-area formula to the pre-activation functions z_i(x), yielding an explicit formula: LC = Σ_i E[‖∇z_i(x)‖ · ρ_{b_i}(z_i(x)) · 1_{z_i good at x}], where the 'good' indicator restricts to neurons whose activation path reaches the output. From this formula, the paper derives bounds connecting LC to local rank via Jacobian singular value decomposition (Theorem 5), to total variation via Lipschitz constants of subnetworks (Theorem 7), and to representation cost via parameter norms (Proposition 8). The optimization connection leverages the kernel-to-rich regime dichotomy of Lyu et al. (2024) and the rank-minimization implicit bias of Timor et al. (2023) to show that weight decay implicitly drives LC toward lower values in the rich phase of training.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper that introduces the local complexity (LC) measure and establishes several key bounds: (1) LC is bounded above and below by the sum of expected gradient norms across all neurons (Corollary 3), (2) the sum of local ranks across layers is bounded by LC (Theorem 5), (3) the expected total variation (TV) of the network provides a lower bound on LC (Theorem 7), and (4) the representation cost of the network upper-bounds LC (Proposition 8). Empirically, on MNIST (4-layer MLP, 200 neurons/layer), the paper demonstrates a sharp drop in both LC and TV in the terminal phase of training, which coincides with increased adversarial robustness (Figure 3). On a Gaussian-to-Gaussian mapping task, both LC and local rank exhibit a sudden late-training drop, supporting the theoretical link between representation learning and linear region simplification (Figure 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods or baselines, as it introduces a new theoretical framework rather than a new algorithm. The empirical experiments serve as validation of the theoretical predictions rather than benchmarking. The MNIST experiment (Figure 3) shows that a 4-layer MLP trained with 2× He initialization exhibits a pronounced drop in expected total variation and local complexity in the terminal phase, mirroring the empirical observations of Humayun et al. (2024b) on grokking and linear region simplification. The Gaussian mapping experiment (Figure 2) validates the Theorem 5 prediction that LC and local rank are tightly coupled, with both quantities dropping simultaneously late in training. The weight decay ablation (Figure 13) confirms Corollary 10's prediction that larger weight decay leads to lower local complexity, though the paper notes the bound does not exploit data-distribution dependence and could be tightened.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on counting and bounding linear regions in ReLU networks (Montúfar et al., 2014; Hanin & Rolnick, 2019a) by shifting from global region counts to a *local* density measure over the input data distribution. The proof technique adapts the co-area formula approach of Hanin & Rolnick (2019a) but introduces a bias-noise regularization and a 'good at x' condition to make the measure well-defined and data-dependent. For the optimization connection, the paper leverages the kernel-to-rich regime dichotomy of Lyu et al. (2024) and the rank-minimization implicit bias result of Timor et al. (2023) to bound local complexity in terms of weight norms, providing a theoretical explanation for the empirical grokking observations of Humayun et al. (2024b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical framework that connects three previously separate threads—linear region geometry, representation learning (via local rank), and adversarial robustness (via total variation)—under a single measure (local complexity). This offers a concrete mechanism for why networks that learn low-dimensional feature manifolds during grokking also become more robust. However, the bounds are loose (involving products of Lipschitz constants and exponential terms in depth), the empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments (MNIST subset, Gaussian-to-Gaussian mapping), and the training dynamics of local complexity remain an open problem. Extending the analysis to general piecewise-linear activations and deriving generalization bounds from local complexity are natural next steps.
