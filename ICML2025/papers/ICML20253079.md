## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for SAM adjust trainable parameters of individual modules in isolation, overlooking the cross-module relational structure that encodes domain-invariant knowledge from pre-training. Task-specific tuning tends to override or suppress these universal visual features, degrading generalization in specialized domains. The paper asks how to extract domain-invariant relations from the pretrained SAM and faithfully transfer them to the fine-tuned student without propagating pseudo-invariant (domain-specific) information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with geometric prompts (boxes or points) and outputs dense segmentation masks, operating within the standard SAM encoder-decoder pipeline. During training, prompts are randomly sampled from noised ground-truth boxes and points at a 1:1 ratio; during evaluation, ground-truth boxes serve as prompts to minimize randomness. The information-theoretic losses operate on intermediate representations: the image encoder output (B×H×W×D) and mask decoder tokens (B×N×D), from which normalized Gram matrices are constructed for mutual information computation.

## Method

InfoSAM introduces an attention-based relation module that computes cross-attention between the mask decoder tokens (queries) and image encoder embeddings (keys), producing a compressed relational representation r. Two information-theoretic losses govern training: (1) a relation compression loss L_r that minimizes the multivariate mutual information I_α(z_i^T, z_m^T; r^T) via an information bottleneck, forcing the module to discard pseudo-invariant information while retaining domain-invariant structure; and (2) a relation distillation loss L_d that maximizes I_α(r^T; r^S) between teacher and student relations. Both losses use matrix-based Rényi's α-entropy with α=2, enabling efficient computation via Frobenius norms of Gram matrices (O(n²) instead of O(n³) eigenvalue decomposition). The total objective combines a standard structure loss (weighted IoU + BCE) with λ₁·L_r + λ₂·L_d, and the relation module parameters are shared between teacher and student.

## Results

On SAM2 (Hiera-B+ backbone), InfoSAM achieves Sα=94.5±0.17 on Kvasir (polyp), IoU=77.3±0.14 on Leaf (agriculture), and IoU=61.3±0.05 on Road (remote sensing), outperforming the best PEFT baseline (Adapter: 94.4/76.8/60.9) and all distillation methods. On SAM (ViT-B backbone), the full method (Adapter + L_r + L_d) reaches Sα=94.4, IoU=75.6 on Leaf, and IoU=61.4 on Road, improving over the Adapter-only baseline by +1.0/+1.2/+0.9 respectively. The most dramatic gain is over TinySAM on the Road dataset, where InfoSAM achieves 61.3 IoU versus TinySAM's 23.9 IoU, demonstrating the benefit of relation compression when the teacher performs poorly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoSAM consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines (BitFit, AdaptFormer, LoRA, Adapter, HQSAM, SU-SAM, ConvLoRA-SAM) across all four domains and eight datasets, with the largest margins on remote sensing (Road) where it gains +2.1 IoU over the best PEFT method (Adapter). Among distillation baselines, InfoSAM surpasses PKT (+3.5 IoU on Road), VID (+0.2 IoU on Road), ReviewKD (+5.4 IoU on Road), MobileSAM (+9.0 IoU on Road), and TinySAM (+37.4 IoU on Road). The catastrophic failure of TinySAM on Road (23.9 vs. 61.3 IoU) is attributed to its full-stage feature mimicry forcing the student to replicate the teacher's poor zero-shot performance, whereas InfoSAM's relation compression selectively transfers only domain-invariant structural knowledge. On the SAM (ViT-B) backbone, InfoSAM also achieves the best results in camouflaged object segmentation (COD10K, CHAMELEON, CAMO) and polyp segmentation (Kvasir, CVC-612), confirming that the gains are not backbone-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of SAM and knowledge distillation. It departs from existing PEFT methods (SAM-Adapter, SU-SAM, Conv-LoRA, BLO-SAM) that adjust trainable parameters module-by-module while ignoring cross-module relational structure, and from existing SAM distillation methods (MobileSAM, TinySAM) that align paired feature maps without relational guidance. The key conceptual departure is formulating PEFT as an information bottleneck problem: rather than directly aligning teacher-student features, the paper extracts compressed domain-invariant relations from the frozen teacher via an attention-based module and transfers them through mutual information maximization, using matrix-based Rényi's entropy (Gong et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2019) as the computational substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, structure-agnostic framework for preserving domain-invariant knowledge during SAM fine-tuning, which is practically relevant given SAM's widespread adoption across medical, agricultural, and remote-sensing domains. A notable finding is that most conventional distillation methods (TinySAM, MobileSAM, PKT, ReviewKD) actually degrade PEFT performance relative to fine-tuning without distillation, suggesting that naive feature alignment is counterproductive when the teacher is already strong. Open questions include whether the information bottleneck formulation generalizes to larger backbones (ViT-H, ViT-G), whether the relation module's domain-invariance holds under more extreme domain shifts, and whether the modest 1–2% gains scale with dataset size or task difficulty.
