## Problem

Policy gradient methods suffer from high gradient variance and poor sample complexity, and while tree search has achieved state-of-the-art results in value-based RL, it has never been integrated into the policy parameterization itself. This paper asks how to design a differentiable policy that incorporates multi-step planning directly into the softmax, and how the choice of tree-expansion policy governs the resulting gradient variance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an environment state (Atari frame), a forward model (exact GPU simulator or learned approximation), and neural network parameters θ as input. It produces a differentiable action distribution over discrete actions by expanding a tree of depth d from the current state, applying W_θ to all leaf states, and accumulating discounted rewards along each trajectory to form the policy logits. The gradient of this policy with respect to θ is computed analytically (no sampling) and fed into a standard PG loss (PPO).

## Method

SoftTreeMax replaces the standard softmax logit θ(s,a) with the expected score of all trajectories of horizon d starting from (s,a) under a behavior policy π_b, yielding two variants: C-SoftTreeMax (exponentiate the expected cumulative reward) and E-SoftTreeMax (expectation of exponentiated reward, for risk-sensitive settings). The key theoretical result shows that the policy gradient variance decays as |λ₂(P^{π_b})|^{2(d−1)}, where λ₂ is the second eigenvalue of the transition matrix induced by π_b, meaning the expansion policy should be chosen to make induced transitions as state-independent as possible. The gradient is non-uniformly scaled along different eigenvectors of P^{π_b} rather than uniformly shrunk, preserving learning signal while eliminating variance. In practice, the method performs exhaustive parallel GPU tree expansion (breadth-first, pruned to width 1024) with a uniform behavior policy, applies a shared neural network to all leaf states, and integrates the resulting differentiable policy into PPO, reducing gradient variance by three orders of magnitude over distributed PPO on Atari.

## Results

On multiple Atari games, SoftTreeMax (single GPU worker) outperforms distributed PPO (256 GPU workers) by up to 5x in cumulative reward while reducing gradient variance by three orders of magnitude. The method is evaluated at tree depths d=1 through 8, with each game exhibiting a different optimal depth (sweet spot) that balances the variance decay against the computational cost of deeper trees. Reward and gradient variance are strongly negatively correlated across all games, confirming the theoretical prediction that lower variance leads to better performance. All results are averaged over five seeds, with values taken after convergence (average over the last 20% of a one-week run).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SoftTreeMax against a single baseline: distributed PPO with 256 parallel GPU workers (Dalton et al., 2020). SoftTreeMax wins on both primary metrics in all tested Atari games: it achieves up to 5x higher reward and reduces gradient variance by three orders of magnitude, despite using only a single worker compared to PPO's 256. This comparison is particularly striking because it isolates the benefit of tree expansion and planning within the policy, rather than simply scaling compute. The non-monotone performance as a function of depth (reward first increases then decreases) reflects the trade-off between deeper planning (better variance reduction) and fewer environment steps per unit time. The paper does not compare against other variance-reduction techniques (e.g., GAE, baseline subtraction) or other planning-based methods (e.g., MuZero), limiting the scope of the empirical comparison to the PPO baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftTreeMax sits at the intersection of two major RL families: policy gradient methods (Sutton et al., 1999; Schulman et al., 2017) and tree search/planning (Silver et al., 2016; Dalal et al., 2021). It generalizes the standard softmax policy by replacing per-state-action logits with multi-step discounted trajectory scores computed via exhaustive tree expansion under a behavior policy, making it fundamentally different from n-step TD methods (which modify the value target) and from MCTS/UCT (which select a single best trajectory). The GPU-based parallel tree expansion implementation builds directly on the parallel tree search framework of Dalal et al. (2021), while the theoretical analysis introduces a novel connection between PG gradient variance and the spectral properties (second eigenvalue) of the behavior policy's induced transition matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to prove that PG gradient variance decays exponentially with tree depth at a rate governed by the second eigenvalue of the expansion policy's transition matrix, and the first to bound gradient bias under an approximate forward model. The practical demonstration that a single-worker SoftTreeMax agent outperforms a 256-worker distributed PPO baseline (up to 5× reward, 3 orders of magnitude lower variance) is striking and suggests that planning-in-the-policy can be far more sample-efficient than brute-force parallelism. Key open questions include the optimal tree depth trade-off (variance decays as |λ₂|^(d-1) but bias grows as d·γ^(-d)), whether variance-optimal expansion aligns with UCT-style regret guarantees, and extension to continuous action spaces and learned (rather than exact) forward models.
