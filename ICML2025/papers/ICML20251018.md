## Problem

LLMs are not perfectly aligned with human populations, yet they are increasingly used to simulate survey responses for cost and speed. The critical unresolved question is how many synthetic samples to generate: too many overfit the (potentially biased) synthetic distribution, while too few yield uninformative confidence sets. No prior method provides a principled, data-driven way to select the simulation sample size while guaranteeing valid coverage for the true human population parameter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real human responses to m similar survey questions (calibration data), LLM-simulated responses to those same m questions plus the target question (up to budget K), and a black-box confidence set construction procedure C that maps any dataset to a confidence set in R^d. It outputs a single integer k̂ ∈ {0, 1, …, K} and the corresponding confidence set S^syn(k̂) for the target population parameter. Notably, the true population parameters are never directly observed; instead, real-data confidence sets serve as proxies, and the procedure C is entirely agnostic—any valid or invalid confidence set construction can be plugged in.

## Method

The core mechanism is an adaptive 'first up-crossing' criterion for sample size selection. For each candidate k, synthetic confidence sets S_j^syn(k) are built from the first k LLM responses to each of m calibration questions, and real-data confidence sets S_j are built from actual human responses. The miscoverage proxy L(k) measures the fraction of real-data confidence sets not contained in the corresponding synthetic confidence sets. The selected k̂ is the largest k such that L(i) ≤ γα for all i ≤ k, where γ is the confidence level of the real-data sets. This yields a coverage guarantee of at least 1 − α − γ⁻¹√(1/2m) with no assumptions on LLM fidelity or the quality of C. The selected k̂ additionally quantifies the LLM's effective representational capacity: a larger k̂ means the LLM can faithfully simulate a larger human population, while a small k̂ signals substantial distribution shift.

## Results

The method achieves valid coverage (miscoverage ≤ α) across all 8 LLMs and both datasets, verified by hypothesis tests where all p-values exceed 0.05 significance. The selected simulation sample size k̂ reveals substantial heterogeneity in LLM simulation fidelity: on OpinionQA, GPT-4o achieves the highest k̂ (54.1 at α=0.05, rising to 91.9 at α=0.50), while the Random baseline plateaus at ~20. On EEDI, k̂ values are dramatically lower (single digits for most LLMs), with DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o performing best, suggesting LLMs simulate subjective opinions more faithfully than mathematical problem-solving.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only explicit baseline is a Random response generator that selects answers uniformly at random. On OpinionQA, all 8 LLMs clearly outperform Random (k̂ ≈ 20), with GPT-4o achieving roughly 2× the simulation sample size of Random at α=0.05 (54.1 vs. 17.1). On EEDI, the picture is more nuanced: only DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o appear to outperform the Random baseline, while most other LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku, Mistral-7B) achieve k̂ values in the single digits (4–8), comparable to or below Random (~7). Among LLMs, GPT-4o dominates on OpinionQA (k̂ up to 92) while DeepSeek-V3 leads on EEDI, and LLMs uniformly achieve higher k̂ on OpinionQA than EEDI, indicating greater fidelity in simulating subjective social opinions than mathematical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two literatures: LLM-based human simulation (Aher et al., 2023; Horton, 2023) and model-free statistical inference (conformal prediction, prediction-powered inference). Prior work on LLM simulation has focused on measuring misalignment via integral probability metrics or Turing-type tests, but has not provided methods for constructing valid confidence sets from imperfect simulations. The paper departs from prediction-powered inference and conformal methods in a key way: the population-level labels are not directly observable, and the infinite family of candidate point predictions (one per sample size k) makes standard calibration inapplicable. The adaptive sample size selection criterion is the paper's core conceptual contribution, bridging the gap between these two bodies of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first principled, model-free framework for converting LLM-simulated survey responses into statistically valid confidence sets, with the selected sample size k̂ serving as an interpretable measure of LLM fidelity. The practical impact is significant for social science, economics, and education researchers who increasingly rely on LLM simulations for cost-efficient experiments. Key limitations include average-case (not instance-wise) coverage guarantees, and the method does not minimize confidence set size. Open questions include whether debiasing or prompt engineering can improve the effective k̂, and whether the framework extends to sequential or adaptive survey designs.
