## Problem

Fine-tuning aligned LLMs on user-uploaded data can compromise safety alignment, but existing defenses (data augmentation, weight projection) rely on static hyperparameters that fail to adapt to the diverse sizes, types, and safety-degradation profiles of real-world fine-tuning datasets. This rigidity causes either insufficient protection against large harmful datasets or unnecessary utility loss on benign ones, with no mechanism to estimate dataset-specific safety degradation and adjust defense strength accordingly.

## Input / Output

The method takes the original aligned model weights, the delta parameters from standard fine-tuning (ΔW_sft = W_sft − W_orig), and a precomputed Hessian inverse of the safety loss (computed once on a fixed safety dataset before any fine-tuning requests arrive). It produces updated model weights W_sd = W_orig + M ⊙ ΔW_sft + C, where M is a per-layer binary mask selecting a subset of delta parameters and C is a safety compensation vector applied to the unselected positions. The optimization is decomposed layer-wise, operating on individual linear transformation weight matrices (Q, K, V, O projections and MLP up/down projections) rather than the full parameter space, with a block-wise memory-efficient implementation.

## Method

Safe Delta operates in two stages after standard fine-tuning. First, it estimates per-parameter safety degradation via a Hessian-based quadratic approximation (δL_safe^m = (δw_m)²/(2[H⁻¹]_mm)) and utility improvement (δL_util^m = −(δw_m)²), then greedily selects delta parameters in descending order of the utility-safety ratio r_m = 2[H⁻¹]_mm until cumulative safety loss reaches a layer-specific threshold. Second, it applies a safety compensation vector derived from Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) Lagrangian optimization (Theorem 4.1), which computes the optimal adjustment of unselected weights to minimize residual safety loss while preserving the utility gains of selected deltas. The key efficiency insight is that the safety Hessian depends only on the original aligned parameters and safety examples, enabling a single precomputation of H⁻¹ reused across all fine-tuning requests, while the utility objective is approximated by parameter distance to avoid prohibitively expensive per-request Hessian recomputation.

## Results

On harmful fine-tuning (PureBad), Safe Delta reduces ASR from 95.76% (fine-tuned) to 3.33% and harmfulness score from 4.82 to 1.13, while retaining competitive utility (MT-Bench 6.18, MMLU 44.61%). On Identity Shift, it achieves an even lower ASR of 0.91% (vs. 88.48% fine-tuned). On benign fine-tuning, Safe Delta achieves the highest utility among all defense methods—0.489 Rouge-1 F1 on Dirty Summary and 0.334 accuracy on Math—while keeping ASR at 5.15% and 3.33% respectively, closely matching the original model's safety level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safe Delta dominates all four baselines on safety metrics across both harmful datasets. Against SafeLoRA and Resta (weight-modification methods), the gap is dramatic: on PureBad, Safe Delta achieves 3.33% ASR versus 84.24% (SafeLoRA) and 89.39% (Resta); on Identity Shift, 0.91% versus 47.27% and 34.85%. Against data-based methods, Safe Delta also wins on safety (3.33% vs. 37.82% for SafeInstr, 8.79% for BEA on PureBad) while preserving higher utility (MT-Bench 6.18 vs. 5.92 and 5.87). On benign fine-tuning (Table 3), Safe Delta achieves the highest utility on both Dirty Summary (0.489 vs. 0.478 for Resta, 0.475 for SafeLoRA, 0.470 for BEA) and Math (0.334 vs. 0.314 for Resta, 0.310 for SafeLoRA, 0.308 for BEA), while maintaining ASR comparable to or better than all baselines—effectively matching the fine-tuned model's utility without the safety degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Safe Delta builds directly on the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework for second-order parameter sensitivity analysis, repurposing the Lagrangian-based weight compensation derivation to construct a safety compensation vector that counteracts fine-tuning-induced safety degradation. It departs from the two dominant families of fine-tuning defenses—data augmentation (SafeInstr, BEA) and static weight modification (SafeLoRA, Resta)—by replacing their fixed hyperparameters with a per-parameter, dataset-adaptive selection strategy. The motivation for per-parameter contribution estimation draws on attribution methods (SHAP, Integrated Gradients), but the key conceptual shift is formulating the safety-utility trade-off as a knapsack-like selection problem over delta parameters rather than a global projection or data-mixing ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically urgent gap: fine-tuning API providers (e.g., OpenAI) need a single defense that works across arbitrarily diverse user-uploaded datasets without per-request hyperparameter tuning, which existing methods cannot provide. The one-time Hessian precomputation makes the approach deployment-friendly, and the consistent performance across four dataset types, three model architectures, and LoRA/full fine-tuning validates its generality. Open questions include vulnerability to adversarially crafted fine-tuning data designed to evade the greedy selection, the suboptimality of the greedy (vs. exact) selection, and extension to multimodal LLMs where the Hessian would need to incorporate image inputs.
