## Problem

Implicit bias in nonlinear neural networks has been characterized primarily through parameter-dependent metrics such as weight norms and output-space margins, but these are not invariant to reparameterization and show poor correlation with generalization in deep networks. The paper asks whether a function-level, reparameterization-invariant geometric quantity can serve as a principled measure of implicit bias that predicts generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained classifier and a set of training data points. It randomly samples pairs (for 1D) or triplets (for 2D) of training points to form line segments or triangles in the input space, evaluates the network on a grid of 200 (1D) or 30×30 (2D) points within each subspace, and counts connected components sharing the same predicted label via BFS. Averaging over 100 random subspaces yields a scalar region count that is then correlated with the generalization gap (training error minus test error).

## Method

The paper defines region count as the number of maximally connected regions in a low-dimensional input subspace where the network assigns the same label, extending the fragmentation score of Somepalli et al. (2022) from fixed 2D planes to arbitrary-dimensional subspaces. Empirically, across nine CNN architectures (ResNet, VGG, MobileNet, EfficientNet, etc.) on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet, region count correlates with the generalization gap at r ≥ 0.93, and larger learning rates or smaller batch sizes consistently reduce region count. Theoretically, for a two-layer ReLU network trained by gradient descent at the edge of stability (where the Hessian's largest eigenvalue scales as 1/η), the paper proves the average region count is upper-bounded by O(N/(r²η)), establishing that large learning rate implicitly biases the optimizer toward simpler decision boundaries with fewer regions.

## Results

The headline finding is a strong correlation between region count and generalization gap: 0.98 per individual network (ResNet18, EfficientNetB0, SeNet18) and 0.93 pooled across all three on CIFAR-10. This correlation remains robust across 9 architectures, 3 datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), and counting dimensions 2–5, with most entries in Table 2 exceeding 0.90. Theoretically, for two-layer ReLU networks under the edge-of-stability assumption, the average region count is upper-bounded by O(N/(r²η)), explaining why larger learning rates η induce simpler decision boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new training method to benchmark against competing classifiers; instead, it introduces region count as an implicit-bias metric and compares it against existing parameter-dependent measures. Norm-based (Frobenius distance from initialization) and margin-based (output-space margin) indicators show low and inconsistent correlation with the generalization gap on ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (Figure 2), whereas region count achieves 0.98 correlation under the same protocol. Region count is also invariant to reparametrization (e.g., scaling W₁ by 2 and W₂ by 1/2 preserves the function but changes the norm), a property that norm and margin metrics lack. The closest prior work is the fragmentation score of Somepalli et al. (2022), which counts decision regions only on 2D planes defined by triplets of training points; the present paper generalizes this to arbitrary-dimensional subspaces (1D–5D) and provides the first theoretical bound linking region count to learning rate via the edge-of-stability condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fragmentation score introduced by Somepalli et al. (2022), which counted decision regions on 2D planes defined by training triplets, to arbitrary-dimensional input subspaces and reframes it as a general implicit bias metric. It deliberately departs from the dominant parameter-dependent characterizations of implicit bias (L2 norm, output-space margin, sharpness) by proposing a function-dependent, reparameterization-invariant measure grounded in the geometry of decision boundaries. The theoretical analysis connects region count to the edge-of-stability phenomenon, bridging the implicit bias literature with the growing body of work on training dynamics at large learning rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reparameterization-invariant diagnostic for generalization that correlates at 0.93–0.98 with the generalization gap across nine architectures and three datasets, offering a concrete geometric interpretation of why hyperparameters like large learning rate and small batch size improve generalization. However, the theoretical guarantee is restricted to two-layer ReLU networks under an edge-of-stability assumption, and the N-dependent bound is acknowledged as loose; extending the theory to deep, overparameterized networks remains open. The authors also note that applying region count to non-classification tasks (e.g., language generation) and leveraging it for architecture design or regularization are promising but unexplored directions.
