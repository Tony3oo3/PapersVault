## Problem

Permutation symmetry, the well-studied parameter-space symmetry for MLPs, is inherently discrete and cannot fully characterize the equivalence set of transformer self-attention layers, which rely on continuous matrix products of queries, keys, and values. This limitation means existing parameter-matching methods (e.g., Git Re-Basin) are suboptimal for transformers, leaving a gap in how to bring end models closer in parameter space before fusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes two (or more) fine-tuned transformer models of identical architecture and operates on their per-head, per-layer weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O) and bias vectors in self-attention layers, as well as the two-layer FFN weight matrices. It produces rotation-matched parameters for the source model (the target/anchor model remains unchanged), which are then fed into any standard fusion method (simple averaging, Fisher, RegMean, OT-based). The matching is computed independently for each attention head and each FFN module, making it embarrassingly parallelizable, and the entire matching step adds less than 5% overhead relative to the fusion step itself.

## Method

The paper identifies a novel rotation symmetry for self-attention layers: applying an orthogonal rotation R to the query matrix and R^T to the key matrix preserves the QK^T product (and analogously for V and O), extending the discrete permutation symmetry of FFNs to a continuous domain. Parameter matching is then formulated as an Orthogonal Procrustes problem over the rotation matrices, yielding a closed-form solution via SVD (an adaptation of the Kabsch algorithm): R_1 = UV^T where UΣV^T is the SVD of the summed cross-product matrix of corresponding parameters. For FFN modules, matching reduces to a linear assignment problem solved by the Hungarian algorithm. A rescaling symmetry step (scaling Q by a and K by 1/a) is applied sequentially after rotation matching to further reduce inter-model distance. The entire procedure is a plug-and-play pre-processing module that can be inserted before any existing model fusion method without modifying the fusion algorithm itself.

## Results

On NLP tasks (Table 1), parameter matching (+match) consistently improves model fusion across all baselines, with the largest gains on DeBERTa-large: NER-CoNLL F1 improves from 10.80 to 23.30 (Simple), 12.53 to 18.33 (Fisher), and 10.80 to 14.06 (RegMean). On ViT/CIFAR-10 (Table 2), matching yields consistent but smaller improvements, e.g., Simple fusion accuracy rises from 7.60% to 10.19% and RegMean from 14.24% to 15.35%. The matching module adds negligible overhead (<5% of Fisher/RegMean merging time), taking only 1.6–3.5 seconds per model pair.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Simple (direct averaging, Wortsman et al. 2022), +match delivers the largest relative gains, particularly on DeBERTa-large where NER-CoNLL F1 more than doubles (10.80→23.30) and NER F1 improves by 3.77 points (27.54→31.31), confirming that reducing parameter distance benefits naive averaging most. Against Fisher-weighted averaging (Matena & Raffel 2022), improvements are modest but consistent (e.g., NER DeBERTa: 33.20→33.83; NER-CoNLL DeBERTa: 12.53→18.33), as Fisher already performs data-dependent weighted alignment. Against RegMean (Jin et al. 2023), +match yields notable gains on DeBERTa (NER: 27.54→31.31; NER-CoNLL: 10.80→14.06) but minimal changes on RoBERTa, suggesting larger models benefit more from continuous rotation alignment. Against OT-Fusion variants (Imfeld et al. 2024) on ViT/CIFAR-10, +match provides small but consistent improvements (e.g., OT-ACTS: 61.15→61.25; OT-WTS: 57.11→57.17), with the paper noting OT-Fusion's matching is restricted to permutation operations on FFN only. Against Git Re-Basin (Ainsworth et al. 2023), the paper's rotation-symmetry matching reduces Euclidean distance between end models more effectively than permutation-based matching (Figure 4), attributed to the continuous nature of rotation symmetry enabling smoother alignment in self-attention layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter matching framework of Git Re-Basin (Ainsworth et al., 2023) from discrete permutation symmetry to continuous rotation symmetry, specifically for transformer self-attention layers. While feedforward networks in transformers already inherit permutation symmetry from MLPs, the Q·K^T product in attention (lacking element-wise activation) admits orthogonal rotation matrices that preserve functionality, generalizing the discrete permutation group to the continuous orthogonal group. The closed-form solution leverages the Kabsch/Orthogonal Procrustes framework, replacing the NP-hard linear assignment problem of permutation-based matching with a tractable eigendecomposition. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play module composable with existing fusion strategies (Simple, Fisher, RegMean, OT-Fusion) rather than a standalone fusion algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous nature of rotation symmetry enables smoother, more precise parameter alignment than discrete permutations, which is particularly important for the high-dimensional parameter spaces of large transformers where models tend to be more spread out. The finding that FFN-only matching (effective for MLPs/CNNs) is insufficient for transformers, while attention matching contributes more significantly, refines understanding of where parameter alignment matters most in transformer architectures. Open questions include extending the closed-form solution to multi-model matching (where global optimality is not preserved), characterizing the full symmetry group of transformers beyond rotation/permutation/rescaling, and deriving a jointly optimal solution for rotation and rescaling rather than the current sequential approximation.
