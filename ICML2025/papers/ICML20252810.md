## Problem

Tang et al. (2024) empirically showed that limiting the vocabulary space during DP synthetic demonstration generation improves DP-ICL accuracy, but the theoretical basis for this heuristic remained unexplored. More fundamentally, the Gaussian noise required for differential privacy degrades the LLM's ability to infer the ground-truth concept underlying the demonstrations, and no principled method existed to counteract this degradation beyond the ad-hoc vocabulary restriction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252810/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task instruction, a set of private demonstrations partitioned into M disjoint subsets, an LLM, and DP parameters (ε, δ) as input. At each of T sequential token-generation steps, it computes next-token probabilities conditioned on both a public prompt (instruction + tokens generated so far) and each private prompt subset, then selects one synthetic token. The output is a set of DP synthetic demonstrations (typically 4 per task) that replace the original private demonstrations in the ICL prompt, enabling unlimited downstream queries without additional privacy cost thanks to DP's post-processing immunity.

## Method

The paper interprets ICL as implicit Bayesian inference via an HMM formulation and extends it to DP-ICL by modeling Gaussian noise as perturbations to emission probabilities, deriving Theorem 2 which shows the LLM infers the ground-truth concept θ* only if the expected log-likelihood ratio between p(O|θ*) and p(O|θ) exceeds a threshold inflated by a noise error term Ω(T²G(σ)log|V|) and an estimation error term. Motivated by the insight that enlarging this divergence helps, PTA modifies the next-token probability at each step via p_v ∝ p_base(v) · (p(v|S_priv)/p(v|S_pub))^α, amplifying tokens distinctive to the ground-truth concept while a KL regularization term preserves natural token flow. The private-to-public ratio serves as a tractable proxy for the intractable concept-level likelihood ratio, and because public prompts incur no additional privacy cost under DP post-processing immunity, PTA achieves the same (ε,δ)-DP guarantee as the Tang et al. baseline without requiring extra noise.

## Results

On 4-shot DP-ICL, PTA (P1) achieves 93.99% accuracy on GINC and 87.88% on AGNews at ε=1, outperforming the Tang et al. baseline (B1) by +3.19 and +4.14 points respectively. At ε=8, PTA reaches 96.61% on GINC and 86.48% on AGNews, again surpassing B1 (94.63% and 83.62%). The non-private 4-shot ICL reference (R0) at ε=∞ achieves 99.02% on GINC and 87.82% on AGNews, showing PTA recovers most of the accuracy lost to DP noise. PTA is the only method that loses to the baseline on DBPedia at ε=1 (82.06% vs. 85.72%), where the ablation variant PA3 (without KL regularization) performs best at 86.56%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Tang et al. (2024), which generates DP synthetic demonstrations by adding Gaussian noise to next-token probabilities with vocabulary space limitation. PTA consistently outperforms this baseline on GINC (+3.19 at ε=1, +1.98 at ε=8), AGNews (+4.14 at ε=1, +2.86 at ε=8), and TREC (+1.24 at ε=1, +1.60 at ε=8). The sole exception is DBPedia at ε=1, where the baseline (85.72%) and its Top-p variant (86.10%) both exceed PTA (82.06%), suggesting the KL regularization in PTA may over-constrain adaptation on this particular dataset under high noise. The Top-p variant of the baseline (BA2) is consistently weaker than B1 on GINC and TREC, indicating that adaptive vocabulary limiting without amplification does not compensate for the lack of concept alignment. Overall, PTA narrows the gap to the non-private reference (R0) most effectively at ε=1, recovering 93.99% vs. 99.02% on GINC and 87.88% vs. 87.82% on AGNews.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian analysis of ICL from Xie et al. (2022), which interprets in-context learning as implicit inference over a latent concept via an HMM, to the differentially private setting by incorporating Gaussian noise into the emission probability model. It also builds directly on Tang et al. (2024), who introduced DP synthetic demonstrations by adding noise to next-token probabilities and empirically limited vocabulary space; here, the authors provide the missing theoretical justification for that vocabulary limitation (via the noise-error term in Theorem 2) and go further by proposing PTA, which explicitly maximizes the concept-divergence term that Tang et al.'s method leaves unoptimized. The method family sits at the intersection of differential privacy mechanisms (Gaussian mechanism on the probability simplex) and Bayesian concept inference, with the KL-regularized optimization in Eq. (11) drawing on standard information-theoretic tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers the first principled theoretical account of why DP-ICL degrades in accuracy and what levers (vocabulary size, concept divergence) can recover it, which should guide future DP-ICL design beyond ad-hoc heuristics. However, the HMM formulation does not fully capture the autoregressive feedback of generated tokens, and the experiments rely on relatively small models (GPT-2 variants, 7B LLMs) with modest accuracy gains (1–5 points over baseline), leaving open whether PTA's benefits persist at larger scale or with more complex tasks. The ablation revealing that removing the KL regularization (PA3) can outperform the full method on DBPedia at ε=1 suggests the regularization may be overly conservative in some regimes, pointing to adaptive or task-specific tuning as a natural next step. Integration with concurrent adaptive-noise-variance methods (Gao et al., 2025) is identified as a promising but unexplored direction.
