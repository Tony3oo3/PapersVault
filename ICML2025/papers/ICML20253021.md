## Problem

Large-scale kernel methods face a memory bottleneck: state-of-the-art approaches like Falkon require O(M²) storage for preconditioners (e.g., 55 GB for M=1.2×10⁵ on HIGGS), making them unaffordable for most users. Additionally, existing scalable kernel methods focus almost exclusively on kernel ridge regression, leaving classification models such as kernel logistic regression and SVM without efficient large-scale solvers.

## Input / Output

Joker takes standard supervised learning inputs—feature vectors of dimension 9 to 3072 (tabular data or flattened RGB images) paired with scalar regression targets or class labels—and produces predictions (regression values or class probabilities/labels) via a kernel machine. A key design constraint is that the entire pipeline must fit within consumer-grade hardware (single 10 GB GPU, 64 GB RAM), which drives the choice of randomized features over Nyström approximation and the use of single-precision arithmetic. Multi-class problems are handled via one-versus-rest decomposition.

## Method

Joker formulates a unified dual optimization problem (via Fenchel conjugates of the loss) that covers KRR, Huber regression, L₂-SVC, SVR, and KLR under a single box-constrained quadratic-plus-separable structure. The core solver, DBCD-TR, performs block coordinate descent on the dual, where each block subproblem is solved by a trust-region procedure using a truncated CG-Steihaug step that naturally handles both box constraints and non-smooth losses without explicit step-size tuning. For scalability, inexact Joker replaces exact kernel evaluations with Random Fourier Features, reducing per-iteration cost from O(nd|B|) to O(Md|B|) and eliminating the O(M²) preconditioner storage that plagues Nyström-based methods. The dual formulation is better conditioned than the primal (linear vs. quadratic dependence on the kernel matrix), and the trust region mechanism provides implicit step-size control that stabilizes convergence even for ill-conditioned losses like logistic.

## Results

On the HIGGS dataset (11M samples), Joker-KRR achieves 80.41% AUC and 81.94% accuracy in 1.0 hour using only 1.9 GB GPU memory, matching or exceeding Falkon (80.38% AUC, 80.90% ACC, 9.9 GB) while saving ~80% memory. On CIFAR-5M (5M samples, 3072 dims), Joker-SVM reaches 74.47% accuracy and Joker-KLR reaches 74.88% accuracy, substantially outperforming Falkon (68.24%) and EigenPro3 (72.94%). Across all five datasets, Joker methods consistently use the least memory (0.7–5.9 GB vs. 6–10 GB for baselines) while training 5–36× faster than EigenPro3 and ThunderSVM. ThunderSVM times out (>1 week) on HIGGS and CIFAR-5M entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Falkon (the fastest baseline), Joker matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on HIGGS (81.94% vs 80.90% ACC for KRR; 82.40% vs 80.90% for SVM) while using 1.9 GB vs 9.9 GB memory (~80% savings), at the cost of ~2× longer training time (1.0 h vs 31 min). On CIFAR-5M, Joker-SVM (74.47%) and Joker-KLR (74.88%) substantially outperform Falkon (68.24%) by 6+ points in accuracy. Against EigenPro3, Joker achieves comparable or better accuracy on all datasets while training 5–36× faster (e.g., 1.0 h vs 18 h on HIGGS; 2.0 h vs 80 h on CIFAR-5M) with similar or lower memory. Against ThunderSVM, Joker dominates on every metric: ThunderSVM times out (>1 week) on HIGGS and CIFAR-5M, and where it does complete (MSD, HEPC, SUSY), Joker is 5–30× faster with lower memory and equal or better accuracy. LogFalkon is competitive on SUSY/HIGGS AUC but requires 9.9 GB memory and does not support multi-class (inapplicable on CIFAR-5M).

## Relation to Prior Work

Joker extends the dual coordinate descent lineage (Hsieh et al. 2008; Shalev-Shwartz & Zhang 2013) from linear models to kernel machines, and builds on the BCD convergence theory of Nutini et al. (2022) and Rathore et al. (2024), which had previously been applied only to KRR. It departs from the dominant Nyström-based paradigm (Falkon, LogFalkon) by substituting Random Fourier Features, thereby eliminating the O(M²) Cholesky preconditioner that has been the primary memory bottleneck. The trust-region subproblem solver (DBCD-TR) repurposes the Steihaug truncated CG method for the constrained dual, filling a gap where projected Newton methods struggled with step-size tuning in ill-conditioned kernel settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing peak memory from >50 GB to ~2 GB on HIGGS while matching or exceeding Falkon's accuracy, Joker makes large-scale kernel learning accessible on consumer hardware (single RTX 3080), a practical barrier that has limited adoption of kernel methods in the deep learning era. The unified dual framework covering KRR, KLR, SVM, SVR, and Huber regression addresses a genuine gap: prior large-scale kernel work was almost exclusively KRR-focused, leaving classification models to slow SMO-based solvers (ThunderSVM) or memory-hungry Newton methods (LogFalkon). Open questions include the convergence rate of DBCD-TR (only linear is known), extension to equality-constrained duals (multi-class SVM, softmax), and whether the RFF approximation quality degrades for non-shift-invariant kernels at very large M.
