## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of benign overfitting in two-layer neural networks (Cao et al., 2022; Kou et al., 2023) predicted a sharp phase transition to harmful overfitting when data noise exceeds a threshold, yet such harmful overfitting is rarely observed in practice and memorization of atypical data empirically improves generalization on long-tailed distributions. This discrepancy arises because existing feature-noise models assume homogeneous noise across all classes, overlooking the heterogeneous, class-dependent noise structures present in real-world datasets.

## Input / Output

The theoretical model takes each sample as a pair of d-dimensional patches: a feature patch containing a class-specific vector u_y and a noise patch generated as A_y ζ where A_y is a class-dependent matrix and ζ has i.i.d. sub-Gaussian coordinates. The trained two-layer ReLU CNN outputs a K-dimensional logit vector for classification. Beyond predictions, the analysis yields closed-form test loss bounds parameterized by the signal-to-noise ratio and a novel noise correlation ratio, plus a training-free influence score defined as the change in the Frobenius norm of the class covariance matrix upon removing a single sample.

## Method

The paper refines the feature-noise data model by assigning each class k its own noise covariance structure via a matrix A_k, so that noise patches are class-dependent rather than i.i.d. across classes. They analyze the full training dynamics of a two-layer ReLU CNN under gradient descent with cross-entropy loss, proving a two-stage convergence: exponential loss decrease in Stage 1 followed by O(1/T) convergence in Stage 2. The key technical challenge is handling the random activation states and magnitudes of neurons triggered by noise patches (unlike stable feature activations), which they resolve by analyzing singular value distributions of the per-class weight matrices W_j^{(T)} A_j and showing that at least a constant fraction of neurons activate on any noise patch with high probability. The main theorem establishes a generalized phase transition in two dimensions: the signal-to-noise ratio governs explicit feature-based classification, while the noise correlation ratio (|S_k|‖A_k^T A_k‖_F² / |S_j|‖A_k^T A_j‖_F²) governs implicit noise-based classification of long-tailed data, with longer-tailed data (larger threshold L) benefiting more from memorization. The analysis also yields a training-free influence score based on the change in class covariance Frobenius norm, which identifies visually atypical samples whose memorization is critical for generalization.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) establishes a generalized phase transition between benign and harmful overfitting governed by two ratios: the signal-to-noise ratio |S_k|²‖u_k‖⁴/(|S_j|‖A_kᵀA_j‖_F²) and the noise correlation ratio |S_k|‖A_kᵀA_k‖_F²/(|S_j|‖A_kᵀA_j‖_F²). When the signal-to-noise ratio is large, test loss decays exponentially in that ratio; when the noise correlation ratio is large, long-tailed data test loss decays exponentially in L²·(noise correlation ratio), where L parameterizes the tail. Empirically, on synthetic data (K=5, d=1000, m=100), test accuracy reaches near-optimal levels even when feature strength approaches zero, provided the noise correlation ratio Γ is sufficiently large (Γ up to 1400). On MNIST, removing a fraction of high-influence-score data causes a significantly larger accuracy drop than removing the same fraction of low-influence-score data across LeNet, ResNet-18, and ResNet-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; its primary comparison is theoretical. Previous works (Kou et al., 2023; Cao et al., 2022) assumed homogeneous noise across classes and predicted a sharp phase transition to harmful overfitting when noise magnitude exceeds a threshold. The present paper shows that this prediction fails for long-tailed data because it ignores class-dependent noise heterogeneity: under their homogeneous model (A_k A_kᵀ = I − Σu_k u_kᵀ/‖u_k‖²), the noise correlation ratio is uniform and the benign-overfitting-via-noise regime (Statement 2(b)) does not emerge. The paper's heterogeneous model recovers the prior results as a special case (Remark 3.5) while additionally explaining why memorization of atypical data improves generalization (Feldman & Zhang, 2020) and why subsampling majority classes reduces minority error (Sagawa et al., 2020). The proposed training-free influence score (Eq. 10, based on the change in class covariance Frobenius norm) is contrasted with influence-estimation-based metrics (Feldman & Zhang, 2020) and curvature-based metrics (Garg & Roy, 2023), which require training or storing multiple models; the new metric requires only a single forward pass over the dataset to compute per-class covariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the feature-noise data model introduced by Allen-Zhu & Li (2020) and analyzed for benign overfitting by Cao et al. (2022) and Kou et al. (2023), which assumed homogeneous Gaussian noise shared across all classes. The key departure is incorporating class-dependent noise matrices A_y, which breaks the symmetry that led prior work to predict a sharp phase transition to harmful overfitting whenever noise magnitude exceeds a threshold. This refinement is motivated by empirical observations from Feldman & Zhang (2020) and Garg & Roy (2023) that neural networks selectively memorize atypical, data-specific information and that such memorization improves generalization on long-tailed distributions—behavior the homogeneous-noise theory cannot explain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental discrepancy between theoretical predictions (noise memorization is always harmful) and empirical reality (memorization of atypical data aids generalization) in overparameterized networks, providing the first theoretical mechanism—implicit feature learning from class-dependent noise—that explains why. The training-free influence score (based on covariance matrix Frobenius norm change) offers a practical, lightweight alternative to influence-function or curvature-based metrics for identifying high-value training samples. Limitations include the restriction to two-layer ReLU CNNs and a specific structured data model; whether the heterogeneous-noise mechanism generalizes to deeper architectures, transformer-based models, or unstructured natural images remains open. The counterintuitive finding that increasing majority-class data can degrade minority-class accuracy also raises open questions about optimal data collection strategies for imbalanced classification.
