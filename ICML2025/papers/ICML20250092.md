## Problem

Current behavior cloning methods for robot manipulation improve generalization by scaling datasets and adding modalities, but they ignore whether the learned latent representations contain significant redundant information and lack a theoretical foundation to guide the learning process. This paper asks how to formally characterize and reduce such redundancy in a principled, information-theoretic way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250092/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images, robot proprioceptive states (e.g., gripper positions), and optional language instructions as input. Each modality is encoded by a separate feature extractor (ResNet, ViT, or pre-trained encoders like R3M/VC-1), and the resulting features are concatenated into a single multimodal input vector X. The output is a continuous robot action vector predicted via an MLP policy head from the fused latent representation Z, trained with MSE loss.

## Method

The core contribution is applying the Information Bottleneck principle to BC by augmenting the standard MSE loss with a mutual information penalty β·I(X,Z), estimated via MINE (a two-layer MLP discriminator). Rather than constraining each modality separately as in prior IB work, the bottleneck is applied at the concatenated multimodal feature level, enabling unified redundancy reduction across all modalities and better compatibility with frozen pre-trained encoders. The paper categorizes BC architectures into spatial fusion (MLP/CNN) and temporal fusion (RNN/Temporal Transformer) to identify which fusion strategy suits which task complexity. Theoretical analysis provides generalization error bounds (Theorems 4.1–4.2) showing that minimizing I(X,Z) tightens the bound, and a novel theorem (Theorem 4.3) proves that applying the bottleneck at the intermediate feature X is equivalent to applying it at the original input O up to a small constant δ. The Lagrange multiplier β (typically 1e-4 to 1e-2) controls the compression–prediction trade-off, with β=0 recovering vanilla BC.

## Results

On the single-task CortexBench (14 tasks, 4 simulators), BC+IB consistently outperforms vanilla BC across all six backbones, with average success rates improving from 73.40% to 78.11% (ResNet), 47.69% to 51.00% (Voltron), and 57.04% to 59.28% (VC-1). On the multi-task LIBERO benchmark (40 tasks, 4 suites), improvements are larger: BC-MLP+IB jumps from 16.79% to 25.71% average success rate (+8.92%), BC-VILT+IB from 48.21% to 53.79% (+5.58%), and BC-RNN+IB from 15.38% to 20.46% (+5.08%). The largest single-task gain is ResNet+IB on DMControl (+10.01%), while on LIBERO-Goal, BC-VILT+IB reaches 83.83% vs. 76.17% for vanilla BC-VILT. Real-world experiments on a UR5 arm confirm consistent gains in both single-task (Pick/Put) and language-conditioned multi-task settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BC+IB outperforms every vanilla BC baseline it is paired with, across all backbones and both benchmarks, with no case of degradation. On CortexBench, the gains range from +1.31% (R3M) to +4.71% (ResNet) in average success rate, with the largest per-task improvement being +10.01% on DMControl (ResNet+IB: 84.94% vs. 74.93%). On LIBERO, improvements are more pronounced due to higher data redundancy in multi-task, language-conditioned settings: BC-MLP+IB gains +8.92% average, BC-VILT+IB gains +5.58% (reaching 83.83% on LIBERO-Goal vs. 76.17%), and BC-RNN+IB gains +5.08%. The paper also validates IB in real-world settings, where VC-1+IB outperforms VC-1 on Pick and Put tasks, and CogAct+IB consistently beats CogAct on language-conditioned multi-task manipulation including unseen object-bowl combinations. A Diffusion Policy head experiment on LIBERO-Long (DP: 78.0% → DP+IB: 84.0%) further confirms that IB benefits extend to more expressive policy architectures. The only setting with minimal gain is TriFinger, which the authors attribute to inherently low visual redundancy in that simple scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 1999) from its prior applications in reinforcement learning and copycat-mitigation imitation learning to the specific setting of multimodal behavior cloning for robot manipulation. It departs from conventional IB formulations that constrain individual modalities by instead applying the bottleneck to a concatenated multimodal input X, enabling unified redundancy reduction across RGB, proprioception, and language. The theoretical analysis adapts generalization bounds from Shwartz-Ziv et al. (2019) and Kawaguchi et al. (2023), with a new theorem (Theorem 4.3) establishing that optimizing I(X;Z) at an intermediate feature level is approximately equivalent to optimizing I(O;Z) at the raw input level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study quantifying and mitigating latent representation redundancy in BC for robot manipulation, providing both empirical evidence (consistent 2–10% gains across six baselines, two benchmarks, and real-world robots) and theoretical justification via tightened generalization bounds. The practical takeaway is that IB is a lightweight, drop-in regularizer (a two-layer MINE estimator plus one hyperparameter β) that requires no architectural changes, making it immediately adoptable. Key open questions remain: scalability to large VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, RT-2), compatibility with alternative policy heads (diffusion, autoregressive), and robustness under domain shift — all acknowledged as limitations by the authors.
