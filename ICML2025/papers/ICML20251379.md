## Problem

The literature on LM preference optimization has produced conflicting conclusions about whether on-policy or off-policy data is superior, with prior comparisons failing to control for the alignment algorithm or to account for task-type dependencies. This paper asks two questions: under what circumstances do on- and off-policy data offer different strengths, and can their complementarity be leveraged for more efficient alignment?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an SFT language model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Tulu-3-8B-SFT) and two sources of pairwise preference data: on-policy pairs generated by sampling N responses from the SFT model per prompt and labeling them with a reward model (Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B), and off-policy pairs from existing datasets (UltraFeedback or HelpSteer2). The output is a single aligned policy trained for one epoch on the same total number of (y_w, y_l) pairs as all baselines, ensuring a data-constrained comparison. The on-policy data requires additional compute for generation and reward-model scoring, while off-policy data is directly available.

## Method

SIMPLEMIX combines on- and off-policy preference pairs by sampling each (y_w, y_l) pair with equal probability (0.5/0.5) from either the on-policy source (responses sampled from π_SFT, labeled by a reward model) or the off-policy dataset, then optimizes the standard DPO loss without any modification. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that on-policy data improves reasoning tasks with objective answers (math, coding) while off-policy data improves open-ended tasks (creative writing, personal recommendations), making a balanced mixture strictly better than either source alone. Ablations confirm that the 0.5/0.5 ratio is optimal, that simply increasing sampling diversity (via higher temperature or iterative prompting) degrades quality and underperforms SIMPLEMIX, and that filtering off-policy data by reward-model quality before mixing yields further gains. Despite its simplicity, SIMPLEMIX outperforms more complex hybrid methods—HyPO (which adds an online KL regularization term) and DPO-Mix-P (which uses interpolated sampling between π_θ and π_SFT)—by an average of 3.05% on Alpaca Eval 2.0.

## Results

SIMPLEMIX achieves the best Alpaca Eval 2.0 length-controlled win rate across both SFT models and both preference datasets: 20.64% (Tulu-3-8B-SFT, UltraFeedback) and 29.41% (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, UltraFeedback), improving over the best single-source baseline by 2.5–6.4 points. On the 8-benchmark average score, SIMPLEMIX reaches 63.06 (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, UltraFeedback) and 61.22 (Tulu-3-8B-SFT, HelpSteer2), outperforming all baselines. The paper reports an average 6.03% improvement over on-policy and off-policy DPO on Alpaca Eval 2.0, and a 3.05% improvement over the more complex hybrid methods HyPO and DPO-Mix-P.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIMPLEMIX consistently outperforms all four baselines across both models and both datasets. Against Off-policy DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), SIMPLEMIX gains +5.4 to +6.4 points on Alpaca Eval 2.0 LC (e.g., 20.64 vs. 14.23 for Tulu-3-8B-SFT on UltraFeedback). Against On-policy DPO (Guo et al., 2024), the gains are +2.1 to +5.9 points (e.g., 29.41 vs. 27.28 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on UltraFeedback). Against the more complex hybrid HyPO (Song et al., 2024b), SIMPLEMIX leads by +2.5 to +3.0 points on Alpaca LC (20.64 vs. 18.11; 29.41 vs. 27.91), and against DPO-Mix-P (Shi et al., 2024) by +2.1 to +3.4 points (20.64 vs. 17.25; 29.41 vs. 27.79). On the 8-benchmark average score, SIMPLEMIX also leads all baselines, with the largest margin over DPO-Mix-P on Tulu-3-8B-SFT (61.14 vs. 60.70 on UltraFeedback). The key finding is that the performance gap between on- and off-policy DPO alone is minimal (≤1 point), yet simply mixing them at 50/50 yields a substantial and consistent improvement over either source in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as its optimization objective, keeping the loss function fixed while varying only the data source. It positions itself against the ongoing on-policy vs. off-policy debate (Tajwar et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Ivison et al., 2024) by controlling the algorithm and breaking performance down by task type, which prior comparisons did not do. Against existing hybrid methods that combine on- and off-policy data through complex mechanisms—HyPO's KL-regularized online objective (Song et al., 2024b) and DPO-Mix-P's interpolated samplers (Shi et al., 2024)—SIMPLEMIX departs by simply sampling pairs from both sources with equal probability, arguing that the data mixture itself, not the loss modification, is what matters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a 50/50 on/off-policy mix consistently outperforms both single-source DPO and more complex hybrid methods—gives alignment practitioners a low-cost recipe that avoids the compute overhead of iterative on-policy sampling while still capturing its benefits on reasoning tasks. The task-dependent complementarity finding (on-policy for math/coding, off-policy for creative writing/recommendation) provides actionable guidance for data curation in production pipelines. Key limitations include evaluation only on 8B models, reliance on LLM-as-a-judge benchmarks susceptible to gaming, and the open question of how fine-grained 'on-policyness' of individual examples affects performance, which the authors leave for future work.
