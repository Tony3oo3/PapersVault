## Problem

Differentially private mechanisms for causal inference add noise to user-level outcomes, inflating the variance of treatment effect estimators and creating a fundamental privacy-variance trade-off. Existing user-level DP mechanisms (e.g., UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP) ignore any available non-sensitive cluster structure in the data, missing an opportunity to reduce the variance penalty. This paper asks whether leveraging cluster information can tighten the privacy-variance trade-off without weakening privacy guarantees.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes individual discrete outcomes from a finite space Y of cardinality K, binary treatment assignments, non-sensitive cluster memberships, and three tunable privacy parameters (truncation threshold γ ∈ [0, 1/K], Laplace noise scale σ, and resampling probability λ). It outputs privatized user-level responses along with the response randomization matrix Q (and its inverse) so that third parties can compute an unbiased stratified ATE estimator themselves. Unlike aggregation-based baselines that release only a noisy summary, the output preserves user-level granularity, enabling advertisers to run proprietary covariate-adjustment or sub-segment analyses on the privatized data.

## Method

CLUSTER-DP processes each cluster and treatment group independently: it computes the empirical response distribution, adds Laplace noise at scale σ/n_{a,c}, truncates probabilities to [γ, 1], renormalizes, and then with probability λ replaces each unit's true outcome with a draw from this transformed distribution. The key theoretical insight is that the (ε,δ)-label DP guarantee (Theorem 3.1) is agnostic to cluster quality, while the variance gap over the non-private estimator (Theorem 3.5) decomposes into a term proportional to cluster homogeneity φ_a (average intra-cluster outcome variance) plus a mechanism-dependent term. Thus, more homogeneous clusters yield strictly lower variance at identical privacy loss. The resulting estimator debiases privatized responses via the inverse of the rank-one-perturbed randomization matrix Q, and is shown to be unbiased and consistent for the finite-sample ATE. The mechanism generalizes UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP (recovered at γ = 1/K) and CLUSTER-FREE-DP (single-cluster special case), and empirically outperforms both on synthetic Gaussian-mixture and semi-synthetic YouTube social-network data.

## Results

The paper's main results are presented as privacy-variance trade-off curves rather than a single numeric table. At a fixed privacy loss of ε=0.2 and δ=10⁻⁴, CLUSTER-DP achieves significantly lower estimator variance than both CLUSTER-FREE-DP and UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP (stratified and unstratified) across the range of truncation parameters γ ∈ [0.1/K, 1/K]. On the YouTube social network dataset (22,179 users across 50 communities, K=8 outcome levels), CLUSTER-DP again dominates CLUSTER-FREE-DP in the privacy-variance trade-off, approaching the variance of the non-private stratified estimator at moderate privacy budgets. The theoretical variance gap (Theorem 3.5) is validated empirically: the gap scales with cluster homogeneity φ_a, and the bound is confirmed to be tight up to a mechanism-specific second term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CLUSTER-DP against four baselines: (1) UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP (stratified and unstratified), which samples from a uniform distribution over outcomes; (2) CLUSTER-FREE-DP, the special case where all units belong to one cluster; (3) Noisy Horvitz-Thompson, which adds Laplace noise to the ATE estimate; and (4) Noisy Histogram, which adds Laplace noise to per-cluster response frequencies. CLUSTER-DP outperforms UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP and CLUSTER-FREE-DP on the privacy-variance trade-off in all three experiments (Gaussian mixture simulation, clustering-quality ablation, and YouTube data), with the advantage growing as cluster homogeneity increases (higher β). Against the aggregation-based baselines (Noisy Horvitz-Thompson and Noisy Histogram), CLUSTER-DP achieves lower finite-sample conditional bias under one-shot communication when n ≫ K and n ≫ C, because user-level noise is averaged over n users rather than over K outcomes or C clusters. The UNIFORM-PRIOR-DP mechanism is shown to be a strict special case of CLUSTER-DP (recovered when γ=1/K and σ→∞), and the Noisy Horvitz-Thompson mechanism has a smaller variance gap than Noisy Histogram for the same ε, but both are outperformed by the user-level CLUSTER-DP approach in the relevant regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the add-noise-truncate-renormalize pipeline from Esfandiari et al. (2022) on label-DP via clustering, repurposing it from classification excess-risk reduction to causal effect estimation under privacy constraints. It generalizes the binary-outcome DP mechanism of Kancharla & Kang (2021) to arbitrary discrete outcome spaces and introduces a clustering-aware variant (CLUSTER-DP) that no prior DP-causal-inference work (Guha & Reiter 2024, Ohnishi & Awan 2023, Lee et al. 2019) addresses. The key conceptual departure is treating cluster structure as a non-sensitive, variance-reducing signal while keeping privacy guarantees entirely agnostic to clustering quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between privacy (independent of clustering) and variance (controlled by cluster homogeneity φ_a) gives practitioners a practical knob: better clusters yield tighter ATE estimates at zero additional privacy cost. This is directly relevant to advertising platforms that must release user-level privatized data for advertiser-side analysis. Limitations include the requirement for a known non-sensitive cluster structure, finite (or binned) outcome spaces, and the assumption of a trusted central curator. Open questions include how to handle continuous outcomes without lossy binning, how to privatize the cluster structure itself when it is sensitive, and whether the variance bound tightens further with adaptive or learned clusterings.
