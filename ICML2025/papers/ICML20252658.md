## Problem

Diffusion and flow-matching models, while powerful at sampling from complex continuous distributions, collapse to hallucination when the joint distribution over spatially distinct variables encodes high-level logical constraints (e.g., Sudoku rules). The paper identifies that the degree and order of sequentialization during generation are critical but unexplored degrees of freedom for continuous spatial reasoning, and that standard parallel denoising is fundamentally insufficient for tasks with complex inter-variable dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of image patches where observed patches are at noise level t=0 and unobserved patches start at t=1; the partial observation is encoded purely through per-patch noise levels rather than a separate mask channel. Patches are 28×28 (Sudoku, 252×252 image), 4×4 (Even Pixels, 32×32), or 8×8 (Counting Polygons/Stars, 128×128). The output is a fully denoised image where all patches reach t=0, with the generation order and overlap between patches' denoising intervals controlled at inference time without retraining.

## Method

SRMs parameterize a noise-prediction UNet (or DiT) that jointly denoises multiple spatial variables, each with its own noise level t_i, enabling a continuum from fully parallel to fully autoregressive generation. During training, a novel Uniform t̄ sampling strategy first draws a uniform mean noise level then recursively allocates per-patch levels via a sum-constrained vector algorithm, fixing the Bates-distribution mismatch of Diffusion Forcing's independent uniform sampling. The network additionally predicts per-patch uncertainty σ_θ, which at inference drives a task-agnostic greedy ordering that denoises the most certain patches first. A graph-based ordering variant propagates certainty through a task-specific adjacency matrix. The framework is agnostic to denoiser architecture and noise schedule, and the key finding is that predicted-uncertainty ordering with no overlap improves hard Sudoku accuracy from <1% to >50% using the same trained model.

## Results

On the MNIST Sudoku benchmark (hard difficulty, 55–81 masked cells), SRM with predicted-order autoregressive sampling achieves 51.6% accuracy, compared to 0.8% for a standard DDPM diffusion model and 67.2% for a discrete greedy oracle. On the Even Pixels task, SRM with predicted order and overlap reaches 51.8% accuracy (pixel-count error 0.534) versus 25.0% for the diffusion baseline. On Counting Polygons/Stars FFHQ, SRM with sequential sampling improves star accuracy from 7.0% to 15.0% and star-point consistency from 54.4% to 88.8%, while also improving polygon accuracy from 13.2% to 18.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a standard DDPM diffusion model (with inpainting conditioning for Sudoku) that uses fully parallel denoising of all patches. On MNIST Sudoku, SRM with predicted-order autoregressive sampling dramatically outperforms the diffusion baseline on hard instances (51.6% vs. 0.8% accuracy) and medium instances (75.4% vs. 53.6%), while matching it on easy instances (99.8% vs. 99.4%). On Even Pixels, SRM with sequentialization and overlap more than doubles the diffusion baseline's accuracy (51.8% vs. 25.0%), though SRM with parallel sampling actually underperforms the baseline (5.4% vs. 25.0%), showing that the benefit is specific to sequential strategies. On Counting Polygons/Stars FFHQ, SRM with sequential sampling wins on both accuracy and consistency for stars (15.0% vs. 7.0% accuracy; 88.8% vs. 54.4% consistency) and on polygon accuracy (18.6% vs. 13.2%), while the diffusion baseline retains a slight edge on polygon consistency (99.0% vs. 96.4–99.8%). Overall, the paper demonstrates that the advantage of SRMs over standard diffusion is task-dependent and strongly tied to the degree and order of sequentialization rather than the underlying denoising architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms—parallel denoising (DDPM/DDIM/rectified flow) and spatially autoregressive generation (MAR, Diffusion Forcing)—into a single framework (SRM) where each spatial variable carries its own noise level. It directly extends Diffusion Forcing's sequential generation to arbitrary spatial settings without a canonical order, and corrects its training-time noise-level sampling (which produces a Bates-distributed mean rather than the uniform mean seen at inference). Conceptually, the sequentialization strategy draws an explicit analogy to chain-of-thought prompting in LLMs, while the uncertainty-based ordering mechanism is inspired by belief propagation in probabilistic graphical models, though without requiring Markov assumptions or predefined distribution families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantification of hallucination in continuous spatial generative models and demonstrates that sampling strategy alone—without retraining—can improve reasoning accuracy from under 1% to over 50% on hard Sudoku, a gap that is well-evidenced across architectures (UNet, DiT), noise schedules (linear, cosine), and stochasticity settings. The finding that the denoising network itself can predict a near-optimal generation order via learned uncertainty is a promising direction for task-agnostic spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether backtracking (re-noising previously committed variables) can close the remaining gap to oracle solvers, how to define a general difficulty metric for distributions, and whether these strategies transfer to higher-resolution, more open-ended spatial reasoning tasks beyond the relatively constrained benchmarks introduced here.
