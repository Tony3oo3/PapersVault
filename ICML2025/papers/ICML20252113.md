## Problem

When neural networks are implemented directly in hardware (optical, analog in-memory), backpropagation fails because detailed internal information such as activation derivatives and layer-wise inputs cannot be accurately obtained. Existing zeroth-order optimization methods sample perturbations from N(0,I) or one-hot coordinate vectors, both of which ignore the strong parameter correlations present in deep networks. This paper addresses the missing piece: designing a perturbation distribution that accounts for parameter correlations via the Fisher information matrix while retaining multiple non-zero elements per perturbation vector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252113/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset of (input, target) pairs and a black-box neural network from which only loss values ℓ_D(θ) can be queried—no gradient or internal activations are accessible. Parameters are partitioned into blocks of at most N_max elements, and the FIM is estimated in a black-box manner via coordinate-wise finite differences of the network output (N_b queries per block, updated every T_ud iterations). The output is the trained parameter vector, with the method consuming Q+1 queries per gradient step plus periodic Jacobian estimation queries, and is validated on RGB images (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR10) and sequence tasks (Equalization, Copying memory) with CNN, MLP-Mixer, and MZI-based optical architectures.

## Method

The core contribution is sampling perturbations u from N(0, Σ) where Σ = (λ_P·I + λ_F·F_θ)^(−1), derived by maximizing the entropy of the perturbation distribution under both parameter space discrepancy (PSD) and function space discrepancy (FSD) regularization—the FSD term, weighted by the FIM, is what makes these 'natural' perturbations distinct from standard N(0,I) sampling. The FIM is computed in a black-box manner: the output FIM F_y is obtained analytically from the known output distribution (identity for regression, softmax-based for classification), while the Jacobian J_{yθ} is estimated via coordinate-wise finite differences of the network output. To handle large networks, parameters are partitioned into blocks of size ≤ N_max, making the FIM block diagonal so only small N_b × N_b inversions are needed. A theoretical bound (Theorem 3.2) shows the ZO gradient approximation by natural perturbations approximates the natural gradient with error O(μL·((3+N)/λ_P)^(3/2)), and experiments across five tasks and three architecture families demonstrate consistent superiority over standard ZO and coordinate-wise ZO baselines.

## Results

The proposed ZO-NP method achieves the best test accuracy across all five tasks: 97.8% on MNIST, 85.8% on FashionMNIST, 99.3% R² on Equalization, 99.3% on Copying memory, and 62.5% on CIFAR-10. The gains over the strongest existing baseline (ZO-co) are most pronounced on FashionMNIST (+4.4 pp) and Equalization (+4.9 pp), while on CIFAR-10 the improvement is +2.4 pp over ZO-co. All improvements are statistically significant per a one-sided Mann-Whitney U test at p=0.008.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZO-NP consistently outperforms both existing ZO baselines on all five tasks. Against ZO-I (standard ZO with N(0,I) perturbations, Nesterov & Spokoiny 2017), ZO-NP gains range from +1.2 pp (MNIST) to +4.9 pp (Equalization). Against ZO-co (coordinate-wise one-hot perturbations, Lian et al. 2016), the gains are +1.2 pp (MNIST), +4.4 pp (FashionMNIST), +4.9 pp (Equalization), +0.8 pp (Copying memory), and +2.4 pp (CIFAR-10). In Appendix D.2, ZO-NP also outperforms CMA-ES (Hansen 2016) on MNIST, FashionMNIST, and Equalization, where CMA-ES was feasible due to small parameter counts (N ≤ 816). The advantage is largest on tasks with strong parameter correlation (optical MZI-based networks and FashionMNIST), confirming that the FIM-based covariance design is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends zeroth-order (ZO) optimization for black-box neural network training by introducing the concept of natural perturbations, which applies the Fisher information matrix (FIM)-based regularization from natural gradient descent to the design of the perturbation sampling distribution rather than to the gradient vector itself. It builds directly on the authors' prior work (Sawada et al., 2024; 2025) on ZO optimization of optical neural networks, advancing it with a black-box FIM computation method, a principled entropy-maximization criterion, and a block coordinate approach that enables handling of CNNs and RNNs. The paper explicitly differentiates its approach from natural evolution strategies (NES), which also use the FIM but in a fundamentally different way (iteratively updating the sampling distribution's parameters via natural gradient rather than using the FIM directly as the covariance structure).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: training neural networks implemented in hardware (optical, analog in-memory) where backpropagation is infeasible due to inaccessible internal information. The block coordinate FIM approach makes the method tractable for moderately sized networks, and the consistent outperformance across five diverse tasks (image classification, regression, sequence memory) with statistical significance is encouraging. However, the practical limitation to roughly one million parameters (due to FIM update frequency and memory overhead) constrains near-term applicability, and the authors identify stronger FIM approximations as the key open problem for scaling to LLM fine-tuning. The method's relevance is somewhat niche, targeting hardware-implemented networks rather than standard GPU training.
