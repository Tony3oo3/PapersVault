## Problem

Geometric GNNs enforce E(3)-equivariance for 3D physical system modeling but cannot leverage external domain knowledge or task-specific instructions, while LLMs possess rich scientific knowledge yet lack guaranteed equivariance when processing continuous 3D spatial data. The paper addresses the non-trivial challenge of integrating LLMs with geometric GNNs without compromising the essential symmetry constraints required for physical predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251334/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a geometric graph with 3D coordinate trajectories (T×N×3) and invariant node features (atom types or amino acid types) as input, augmented by a task-specific text prompt containing E(3)-invariant statistics (distance distributions, PCA projections) rather than absolute coordinates. It produces future 3D coordinates as equivariant outputs and amino acid sequences or invariant node features as invariant outputs. Critically, the LLM never receives equivariant coordinate data—only invariant features and text—ensuring the equivariance constraint is preserved by architectural design rather than post-hoc correction.

## Method

EquiLLM comprises four modules: a geometry-aware prompt (task description, object features, and E(3)-invariant statistics), a domain-specific equivariant encoder (e.g., ESTAG or MEAN), a frozen LLM (GPT-2), and a one-layer EGNN equivariant adapter. The key architectural insight is a strict separation of roles: the equivariant encoder produces both invariant features H' and equivariant coordinates X', but only H' (projected to LLM dimension) is fed into the LLM alongside the prompt, while X' bypasses the LLM entirely via a skip connection to the adapter. The adapter then fuses the LLM's invariant output with the equivariant coordinates through EGNN message passing, where scalar MLP outputs multiply inter-node displacement vectors to preserve directional information. The LLM is fully frozen during training, and only the projector, encoder, and adapter are learned, keeping additional parameters minimal. A formal proof in the appendix establishes that node features remain invariant and coordinates remain equivariant under arbitrary E(3) transformations.

## Results

On the MD17 molecular dynamics benchmark (predicting 10 future frames from 10 input frames), EquiLLM(PCA) achieves the lowest MSE across all eight molecules, with improvements of 5.41%–42.76% over the ESTAG equivariant encoder baseline (e.g., Uracil: 0.940 vs. 1.852 ×10⁻³). On the Human Motion Capture dataset, EquiLLM attains SOTA MSE across all six prediction-length settings (Walk and Basketball, R=10/15/20), improving 5.63%–36.11% over ESTAG. On the RAbD antibody design task, EquiLLM achieves the best AAR (38.97%) and RMSD (1.73 Å), with a TM-score of 0.9830 that is marginally below GeoAB's 0.9836.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiLLM outperforms all equivariant GNN baselines (ST_EGNN, ST_TFN, ST_SE(3)-Tr., Equiformer) and non-equivariant GNNs (ST_GNN, STGCN) on every molecule in MD17 and every setting in Human Motion, with the largest gains over ESTAG reaching 42.76% (Uracil) and 36.11% (Basketball R=20). Direct LLM baselines (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-flash, DeepSeek-V3) performing coordinate prediction from raw 3D inputs are dramatically worse—GPT-4o-mini achieves 13.070 MSE on Aspirin versus EquiLLM's 1.931—and even adding canonicalization (SVD-based rotation/translation invariance) only narrows the gap to 11.783. On antibody design, EquiLLM surpasses MEAN (36.77%→38.97% AAR) and GeoAB (36.43%→38.97% AAR, 1.79→1.73 RMSD), though GeoAB retains a marginal TM-score advantage (0.9836 vs. 0.9830) due to its explicit bond-angle/torsion-angle constraints. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that removing the prompt, object feature description, or statistical information each degrades performance, validating that the LLM's contribution is specifically activated by the geometry-aware prompting design.

## Relation to Prior Work

EquiLLM builds on the geometric GNN lineage (EGNN, ESTAG, MEAN) for E(3)-equivariant 3D modeling and the growing body of LLM+GNN work (Prot2Text, MoleculeSTM, MolCA) that fuses language models with graph encoders for scientific tasks. It explicitly departs from two prior directions: (1) CrystalLLM-style approaches that feed 3D coordinates directly into LLMs (breaking equivariance), and (2) LLaVA-style multimodal adaptation where a GNN simply replaces the image encoder (also breaking equivariance since the LLM receives both invariant and equivariant features). The key architectural departure is a strict invariant/equivariant pathway separation: only invariant features pass through the frozen LLM, while equivariant coordinates bypass it via a skip connection into a lightweight EGNN adapter, preserving E(3) symmetry by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to demonstrate that a frozen LLM can meaningfully augment equivariant geometric models without compromising symmetry, adding only two projection layers and one EGNN layer of trainable parameters. However, the practical impact is tempered by the use of GPT-2 (a small LLM) and the moderate 5–42% improvement over the encoder alone, leaving open whether larger LLMs or better prompting strategies would yield substantially larger gains. The exploratory finding that canonicalization plus LLM fine-tuning improves performance by 100× on a single small molecule suggests the LLM's role in 3D prediction is more nuanced than the frozen-LLM paradigm captures, and the paper itself flags optimal prompting and broader task coverage as key future directions.
