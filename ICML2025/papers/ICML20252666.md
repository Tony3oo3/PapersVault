## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods suffer significant detection performance drops under even slight text modifications (paraphrasing, copy-paste) and introduce logit biases that degrade LLM performance on downstream tasks like translation and code generation. The root cause is the separate training of encoder and decoder rather than joint end-to-end optimization, which prevents achieving an optimal tradeoff between text quality and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252666/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During generation, the encoder consumes a sliding context window of W=10 preceding tokens and the current LLM logits to produce watermark logits over the top-k=20 candidate tokens, which are added to the original logits with strength δ before Gumbel-Softmax sampling. During detection, the decoder (LSTM + MLP head) takes a full text sequence and outputs a binary watermark score. For cross-LLM inference, a converter transforms embeddings from the target LLM's tokenizer space back to the training LLM's (OPT-1.3B) embedding space, with the context window dilated by 2× to accommodate tokenizer differences.

## Method

The core contribution is a jointly optimized encoder-decoder pipeline where the encoder (MLP) learns context-aware watermark logits and the decoder (LSTM) learns robust detection, trained end-to-end with two objectives: cross-entropy detection loss and cosine-distance semantic loss, balanced via MGDA multi-objective optimization. To resolve non-differentiability, the paper introduces an online prompting technique that keeps all operations in the embedding domain: the on-the-fly LLM is dynamically prompted to perform text paraphrasing (simulating user edits) and to extract semantic embeddings (replacing an external semantic model), both by concatenating prompt embeddings with generated text embeddings. Gumbel-Softmax sampling replaces discrete token selection to enable gradient flow through the generation process. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (detection-only → joint objectives → activate noise layer for paraphrasing), and a lightweight converter enables zero-shot transfer to unseen LLMs without retraining.

## Results

On Llama2-7B, the proposed method achieves an average F1 of 0.975 across all editing conditions (CL, SS, CP, PA), outperforming the strongest distortion-free baseline DiPmark by 17.2% on average and by 37–39% under paraphrasing. Text quality is preserved with a PPL of 7.730 (vs. 7.681 for DiPmark and 7.565 for Unbiased), while downstream tasks show the best BLEU score of 31.062 (MT) and a competitive pass@1 of 34.0 (code generation). The method generalizes zero-shot to Llama3-8B, Llama3.2-3B, and Mixtral-7B with F1 ≥ 0.99 on clean samples and average F1 of 0.969 under edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW, the proposed method wins on paraphrasing robustness (0.916 vs. 0.858 F1 on Llama2-7B) and PPL (7.730 vs. 8.658), and dominates on downstream tasks (BLEU 31.062 vs. 26.325; pass@1 34.0 vs. 22.0). Against Unigram, the method achieves a 12% F1 gain under copy-paste on Llama2-7B (0.978 vs. 0.873) and reduces PPL by 17% (7.730 vs. 9.275), while Unigram retains a slight edge on clean-sample F1. Against the distortion-free baselines Unbiased and DiPmark, the method dramatically outperforms on paraphrasing (0.916 vs. 0.684/0.670) and copy-paste (0.978 vs. 0.912/0.915), at the cost of a marginal PPL increase (~0.1–0.2) and a 2-point pass@1 gap (34.0 vs. 36.0). Against SWEET, SIR, and TSW (Appendix Table 6), the method achieves the highest average F1 of 0.969, surpassing the second-best Unigram (0.933) by 3.9%, while maintaining the lowest PPL among all robust methods (7.730 vs. 8.466–8.658). Overall, the method consistently wins on robustness across all seven baselines, matches or nearly matches distortion-free methods on PPL, and leads on machine translation BLEU, while trailing by 2 points on code generation pass@1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KGW logits-based watermarking paradigm by replacing the fixed hash-based green/red list partition with a trainable encoder network, following the direction set by SIR and TSW. Its key departure is the joint end-to-end optimization of the encoder and decoder, which prior methods (UPV, SIR, TSW) train separately, and the introduction of an online prompting technique that uses the on-the-fly LLM as a differentiable surrogate for non-differentiable operations (text editing, semantic embedding extraction). The Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization for differentiable token sampling and MGDA for multi-objective balancing are borrowed from the broader variational inference and multi-task learning literature, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end formulation with online prompting offers a general recipe for handling non-differentiable modules in generative pipelines, potentially applicable beyond watermarking. The cross-LLM converter enables a single trained model to watermark and detect across diverse LLMs without retraining, which is practically important for deployment. However, training still requires ~5 days on a single A6000 GPU, and the converter introduces a 60% generation-time overhead for cross-LLM use (e.g., Llama2-7B), limiting real-time applicability. Open questions include robustness against adaptive adversaries who know the encoder architecture, scalability to very large LLMs (70B+), and whether the online prompting technique generalizes to other non-differentiable operations in broader ML pipelines.
