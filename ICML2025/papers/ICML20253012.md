## Problem

SageAttention accelerates attention by quantizing QK^T to INT8 and using FP16 accumulators for PV, but INT8 matmul is only half as fast as INT4, and FP16 accumulators provide speedup only on RTX 4090/3090 GPUs. Moving to INT4 for QK^T and FP8 for PV introduces severe accuracy challenges: INT4's narrow range [-7,+7] causes many non-outlier elements to quantize to zero, and the tensor-core FP8 accumulator is actually FP22 (13 mantissa bits), silently degrading PV precision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Q, K, V matrices in FP16 of shape N×d (d typically 64 or 128) and produces the standard attention output O of shape N×d. Before the main kernel, Q and K undergo smoothing (subtracting token-wise means) and per-thread INT4 quantization, while V undergoes per-channel FP8 (E4M3) quantization; P̃ is quantized to FP8 with a fixed scale of 1/448 since its values lie in [0,1]. A GEMV correction term ΔS = q̄_i·γ(K_j)^T is computed in a fused preprocessing kernel and added back on-chip after the INT4 matmul, so the only off-chip reads are the original FP16 Q and K.

## Method

SageAttention2 quantizes Q and K to INT4 at per-thread granularity, where quantization groups are defined by the mma.m16n8k64 PTX instruction layout so that each GPU thread holds exactly one Q scale and one K scale, eliminating the dequantization overhead of per-token quantization while achieving near-per-token accuracy. To handle INT4's limited range, the method smooths both Q and K by subtracting their token-wise means (extending SageAttention's K-only smoothing), then corrects the QK^T result with a GEMV term ΔS computed in a fused preprocessing pass. For the PV matmul, P̃ and V are quantized to FP8 E4M3 (per-channel for V), and a two-level accumulation strategy is introduced: the FP22 tensor-core result R_ij is accumulated into a separate FP32 register O_ij after each block, confining the 13-mantissa-bit truncation error to a single b_k-token block rather than the full sequence. An optional V-smoothing step (subtracting the channel mean and adding it to the final output) further improves FP22 precision when V exhibits channel-wise bias.

## Results

SageAttention2-4b achieves ~3× kernel speedup over FlashAttention2 and ~4.5× over xformers on RTX4090 (481 TOPS peak), while SageAttention2-8b matches FlashAttention3(fp8) speed on Hopper GPUs. On Llama3.1, SageAttn2-8b incurs negligible loss (WikiText ppl 6.019 vs 6.013 full-precision; MMLU 0.634 vs 0.635), whereas HadmdAttn degrades WikiText to 7.872 and MMLU to 0.500. On CogvideoX(1.5-5B), SageAttn2-8b preserves VQA-a at 69.49 (vs 70.23 full-precision), while HadmdAttn collapses to 8.99 and FlashAttn3-fp8 to 6.53. End-to-end generation latency is reduced by 1.8× on CogvideoX(1.5-5B) and up to 2× on Mochi with no visible quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SageAttention2 decisively outperforms both HadmdAttn (Quarot) and SmoothAttn (QServe) across all models: on Llama3.1, HadmdAttn drops MMLU from 0.635 to 0.500 and WikiText ppl from 6.013 to 7.872, while SmoothAttn degrades MMLU to 0.541; on CogvideoX, both baselines collapse VQA-a below 9 (vs 70.23 full-precision), whereas SageAttn2-8b retains 69.49. Against SageAttention (the direct predecessor), SageAttn2-8b matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on text models (LongBench 49.59 vs 49.55) while being ~1.5× faster, and critically, SageAttention fails entirely on CogvideoX(1.5-5B) (marked X) while SageAttn2-8b succeeds. Compared to FlashAttention3(fp8) on Hopper GPUs, SageAttn2-8b matches kernel speed but achieves substantially better accuracy: on CogvideoX, FlashAttn3-fp8 drops VQA-a to 6.53 while SageAttn2-8b maintains 69.49; on Llama-3-262k InfiniBench, SageAttn2-8b scores 43.06 average vs 41.53 for FlashAttn3-fp8. Against FlashAttention2 and xformers (full-precision baselines), SageAttention2 trades negligible accuracy loss for 3× and 4.5× kernel speedup respectively, with end-to-end generation latency reduced by 1.5–2× on video models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to SageAttention (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025), which first demonstrated that INT8 per-block quantization of Q,K combined with K-smoothing and FP16-accumulator PV could accelerate attention ~2× over FlashAttention2 with negligible end-to-end loss. SageAttention2 departs from its predecessor in three key ways: it pushes Q,K quantization from INT8 to INT4 using a novel per-thread granularity aligned with PTX mma.m16n8k64 tensor-core layout, it extends smoothing from K alone to Q+K by exploiting the strong token-similarity structure in attention, and it replaces the FP16-accumulator PV (which only speeds up on RTX 4090/3090) with FP8 E4M3 matmul plus a two-level FP32 accumulation strategy. The overall tiling and online-softmax framework remains inherited from FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that the mma.f32.f8.f8.f32 tensor-core instruction on Ada and Hopper GPUs actually uses a 22-bit (not 32-bit) accumulator is a hardware-level finding with broad implications for any FP8 matmul workload, not just attention. Practically, the ~3× kernel speedup over FlashAttention2 and ~4.5× over xformers, with plug-and-play integration and near-zero end-to-end metric loss across language, image, and video generation models, makes this immediately deployable. Limitations include the need for a separate INT8 variant (SageAttn2-8b) on Hopper GPUs lacking native INT4 tensor cores, and the 4-bit variant still shows measurable (though small) degradation on video-generation metrics. Open questions include whether the per-thread quantization and two-level accumulation ideas transfer to emerging hardware (e.g., Blackwell) and whether training-time quantization-aware fine-tuning could further close the remaining 4-bit accuracy gap.
