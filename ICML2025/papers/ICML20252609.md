## Problem

Estimating the 3D orientation of open-world objects from a single monocular image remains largely unsolved: existing datasets (ObjectNet3D, Omni3D) cover only limited categories and domains, and even state-of-the-art VLMs like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro perform near chance on basic orientation questions. The core obstacle is the scarcity of orientation annotations for arbitrary objects, making it difficult to train generalizable models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image containing one object (rendered at 512×512 during training; real images at arbitrary resolution) and produces three continuous angles—polar θ (0–180°), azimuth φ (0–360°), and camera rotation δ (0–360°)—along with a scalar orientation confidence in [0,1]. Internally, the network outputs probability distributions over 180 bins (polar) and 360 bins (azimuth, rotation); the predicted angle is the argmax of each distribution, and objects with confidence below 0.5 are flagged as lacking a meaningful front face. For multi-object scenes, a segmentation model first isolates each object before individual orientation estimation.

## Method

The key data contribution is a 2M-image synthetic dataset built by rendering 55K canonical-pose 3D models from Objaverse at 40 random viewpoints each, with front-face labels assigned via a VLM-based pipeline augmented by SIFT/SSIM symmetry checks to reduce hallucinations. The core architectural insight replaces direct angle regression (which fails to converge) with probability distribution fitting: ground-truth angles are converted into discretized Gaussian (polar) or circular Gaussian (azimuth, rotation) target distributions, and the DINOv2-initialized ViT encoder with linear heads is trained via cross-entropy to match these distributions, naturally capturing adjacent-angle correlations. Synthetic-to-real transfer is improved through DINOv2 pre-training for real-world visual priors and random cropping augmentation to simulate occlusion. At inference, the argmax of each predicted distribution yields the orientation angles, and a binary confidence head gates whether the object has a meaningful orientation at all.

## Results

On six unseen real-image benchmarks (SUN RGB-D, KITTI, nuScenes, Objectron, Pascal3D+, Pix3D), Orient Anything (ViT-L) achieves zero-shot azimuth absolute errors of 30–55°, dramatically outperforming the fully-supervised Cube RCNN (89–123°) and the few-shot FSDetView. On Pascal3D+ and Pix3D, it reaches 55.0% and 66.0% Acc@30° respectively, surpassing FSDetView (46.0% and 49.0%). On the in-domain rendered benchmark, ViT-L achieves 73.94% azimuth Acc@22.5° and 86.75% polar Acc@5°, compared to only ~20% for GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro. On the out-of-domain COCO real-image benchmark, it attains 72.44% horizontal direction recognition accuracy versus 45.78% (GPT-4o) and 31.95% (Gemini-1.5-pro).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cube RCNN (fully-supervised, trained on indoor/street data), Orient Anything (ViT-L) reduces azimuth absolute error by roughly 2× across all four RGB-D/street benchmarks (e.g., 42.98° vs. 93.58° on SUN RGB-D; 30.09° vs. 122.99° on Objectron) and polar error by 4–5×, despite being zero-shot. On Pascal3D+ and Pix3D, Orient Anything outperforms the few-shot FSDetView by 9 and 17 percentage points in Acc@30° respectively. Against the VLMs GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro on the rendered-image benchmark, Orient Anything achieves 73.94% azimuth Acc@22.5° versus ~20% for both VLMs (near random-guess level of 12.5%), and 86.75% polar Acc@5° versus 17.56% and 16.31%. On the real-image COCO benchmark, Orient Anything reaches 72.44% horizontal direction recognition, exceeding GPT-4o (45.78%) by 26.7 points and Gemini-1.5-pro (31.95%) by 40.5 points. On ImageNet3D (200 categories), the fine-tuned ViT-B achieves 71.3% average Acc@30°, surpassing the fine-tuned ImageNet3D-DINOv2-B baseline (64.0%) by 7.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior 3D orientation estimation work (ObjectNet3D, Omni3D/Cube R-CNN) that was restricted to specific domains (indoor, street) and limited object categories, by formulating the first open-world, zero-shot orientation estimation problem. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes DINOv2 as a task-agnostic visual backbone and introduces a novel learning objective—fitting circular Gaussian probability distributions over angles—that replaces the direct regression or discrete classification used in prior pose estimation. The data pipeline is also novel: instead of relying on manual 3D annotations, it leverages Objaverse 3D assets rendered from random viewpoints, with VLM-assisted front-face identification and symmetry analysis to generate 2M precisely labeled images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ori-Bench reveals that even GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro perform near-randomly on basic spatial orientation questions (15–27% accuracy on spatial reasoning), exposing a fundamental gap in current VLMs that this work addresses with a dedicated tool. The 2M-image rendered dataset and the probability-distribution-fitting formulation provide a reusable foundation for downstream tasks including spatial understanding, generation scoring, and 3D asset orientation voting. Key limitations include the single-object focus (multi-object scenes require external segmentation), the absence of depth-aware 3D relationships in the LLM pipeline, and a residual synthetic-to-real gap that data augmentation only partially closes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the distribution-fitting objective generalizes to full 6-DoF pose, and how to integrate orientation estimation natively into VLM training rather than as a bolt-on module.
