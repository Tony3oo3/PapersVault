## Problem

Defending GNNs against adversarial attacks requires balancing accuracy and robustness, but adversarial training intertwines these conflicting objectives in a single classifier, capping achievable robustness at the cost of clean accuracy. Existing adversarial purification methods either rely on fixed heuristics (Jaccard pruning, SVD) or classifier-derived edge representations, lacking a dedicated, data-driven training process that adapts to diverse graph structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251319/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The purifier takes a perturbed adjacency matrix with continuous edge weights (produced by edge injection, masking, and reweighting) along with the node feature matrix, and outputs a purified adjacency matrix in continuous form—passed to the GNN classifier without discretization. The multi-step purification iteratively refines the adjacency matrix over up to 5 steps, terminating when the relative change falls below a threshold τ=1/1000. The purified graph is then fed to any standard GNN classifier (GCN, GAT, GPRGNN, APPNP) for node classification, making the purifier a plug-and-play module independent of the downstream classifier.

## Method

GPR-GAE is a graph auto-encoder whose node encoder applies K+1 distinct Generalized PageRank filters, each with learnable coefficients γ that selectively weight m-hop neighborhood contributions, and concatenates their outputs into a multi-scale node representation. Edge encoding concatenates paired node embeddings through an activation and linear projection, while edge decoding computes bidirectional sigmoid link scores averaged for undirected graphs. Training is self-supervised: the model reconstructs the clean adjacency matrix from randomly perturbed versions (edge injection at ratio p, masking at ratio q, and reweighting with factor η) using a restoration loss on positive/negative edges plus a symmetry loss enforcing direction-invariant predictions. At test time, a multi-step purification process iteratively updates the adjacency matrix via A^(t+1) = A^(t) + α·(Â^(t) − A^(t)), which is proven to converge linearly to a fixed point under a local Lipschitz condition (L<1) induced by the self-supervised objective. The purifier is trained entirely independently of the classifier, fully decoupling the robustness objective from the accuracy objective.

## Results

On Cora under adaptive PRBCD attacks with ε=0.5, GPR-GAE paired with GPRGNN (vanilla) achieves 64.2% test accuracy, outperforming the strongest adversarial training baseline (GPRGNN trained with PRBCD) by 15.7 percentage points (48.5%). Under the most severe LRBCD ε=0.5 attacks, GPR-GAE_GAT.Vanilla retains 67.1% accuracy versus 50.5% for adversarially trained GAT. On non-adaptive transfer attacks (Table 2), GPR-GAE_GCN.Vanilla achieves 66.4% on Citeseer and 72.0% on Pubmed under PRBCD ε=0.5, surpassing GOOD-AT (56.6% and 68.9%) and SVD-GCN (46.7% and 61.4%), while also scaling to OGB-arXiv where SVD-GCN and GOOD-AT run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPR-GAE decisively outperforms all adversarial training baselines under strong attacks: on Cora with PRBCD ε=0.5, GPR-GAE_GPRGNN.Vanilla (64.2%) exceeds adversarially trained GPRGNN (48.5%) by 15.7 points, and GPR-GAE_APPNP.Self (65.2%) exceeds adversarially trained APPNP (37.8%) by 27.4 points. Against robust GNNs, GPR-GAE also dominates under heavy perturbations: at PRBCD ε=0.5, GPR-GAE_GPRGNN.Vanilla (64.2%) surpasses SoftMedianGDC (48.2%) and EvenNet (35.6%) by 16 and 28.6 points respectively, while maintaining comparable clean accuracy. In non-adaptive transfer attack settings (Table 2), GPR-GAE_GCN.Vanilla outperforms all prior purification methods—GOOD-AT (56.6% vs 66.4% on Citeseer ε=0.5), SVD-GCN (46.7% vs 66.4%), and Jaccard-GCN (40.6% vs 66.4%)—and is the only method that scales to OGB-arXiv without OOM. The key advantage is minimal clean-accuracy trade-off: GPR-GAE preserves within 0.9 points of the base classifier's clean accuracy, whereas adversarially trained GPRGNN sacrifices 1.6 points relative to its self-trained counterpart. GPR-GAE's plug-and-play nature means it works with any GNN backbone (GCN, GAT, GPRGNN, APPNP) without requiring retraining of the classifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Generalized PageRank (GPR) filter from GPRGNN (Chien et al., 2021) — originally designed as a flexible propagation mechanism within a single classifier — into a multi-filter graph auto-encoder that serves as an independent purifier. It departs from adversarial training, which the authors formally decompose (Theorem 3.1) to show that accuracy and robustness objectives are inherently coupled in a single model, and from prior purification methods (Jaccard-GCN, SVD-GCN, GOOD-AT) that rely on fixed heuristics or classifier-derived edge representations. The key conceptual shift is training the purifier in a fully self-supervised manner (reconstructing clean graphs from perturbed ones) so that it is architecturally and optimizationally independent from the downstream classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design — where GPR-GAE can be attached to any GNN classifier without retraining — offers a practical path to robustness that avoids the accuracy-robustness trade-off inherent in adversarial training. The convergence guarantee for multi-step purification (Theorem 3.3) provides theoretical grounding that iterative refinement will stabilize rather than oscillate, a property absent from prior single-step purification methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to feature-level attacks (the paper focuses on structural/edge perturbations), how it behaves under adaptive attacks at scale (gradient-based attacks become memory-prohibitive against GPR-GAE, limiting evaluation to transfer attacks on large graphs), and whether the self-supervised training budget (p=1.5 edge injection) generalizes to graphs with very different density or community structure.
