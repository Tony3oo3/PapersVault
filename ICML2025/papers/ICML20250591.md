## Problem

Maximum coverage, a classic NP-hard problem with applications in information retrieval, influence maximization, and sensor placement, had no algorithm for the turnstile streaming model where items can be both inserted and deleted from subsets one-by-one. Prior streaming algorithms only supported insertion-only or set-arrival models, and dynamic-model algorithms required superlinear space or worse approximation guarantees. The paper also addresses the practical need for real-time re-identification risk monitoring in fingerprinting, where prior work required O(nd) space and O(knd) time.

## Input / Output

The input is an n×d matrix A consumed as a stream of updates of the form (i, j, ±c) that add or subtract c from entry A_ij, along with a cardinality constraint k and (for targeted fingerprinting) a target user u. The algorithm must process each update in poly log n time using sublinear space, then output k subsets (or k features) achieving a (1−1/e−ε) relative approximation to the optimal coverage or re-identification risk. For general fingerprinting, the output is k features that separate the most user pairs, with the function value estimated via a novel sketch for the complement of the p-th frequency moment n^p − F_p.

## Method

The core algorithm (Max-Coverage-LS) is a one-pass linear sketch that implements the Bateni et al. (2017) subsampling framework in a streaming setting: it runs log n parallel subsampling rates, hashes rows into buckets, and within each bucket uses L0 sketches and CountSketch structures at log(rd) subsampling levels to recover a capped number of nonzero entries per row, building a smaller universe A* of Õ(d/ε³) entries. L0 sketches for each column of A are maintained to evaluate the final greedy solution's coverage on the original matrix and to select the best subsampling rate. For targeted fingerprinting, a simple reduction subtracts the target user's row from all other rows before applying the maximum coverage sketch. For general fingerprinting, the paper introduces a novel linear sketch (p-Tuples-Sketch) for estimating n^p − F_p with error relative to the complement, and instantiates a general submodular maximization framework (Theorem 1.3) that achieves (1−1/e−ε) approximation by querying k independent linear sketches with γ = ε/k, yielding Õ(dk³/ε²) space and Õ(k³/ε²) update time.

## Results

On the US Census Data (1990) dataset (2.46M users, 195 features), the general fingerprinting algorithm achieves a 210x speedup over Gulyás et al. (2016) with 55,000-row L₀ sketches, while maintaining an accuracy ratio (proportion of separated user pairs) that never drops below 70% and reaches ~99% with 400,000 rows. For targeted fingerprinting on the same dataset, the algorithm runs in 1.06s vs. 52.6s for the baseline (49x speedup) at k=7, with accuracy converging to the baseline as k grows. On the smaller Adult dataset (32.5K users, 80 features), general fingerprinting achieves a 44x speedup (~0.8s vs. 35.3s) with accuracy ratios of 80–99% depending on sketch size. As a dimensionality reduction technique on the Wine dataset, selecting 3–5 features via general fingerprinting yields ~68% k-means clustering accuracy (vs. 71% with all 13 features) while being 2.1–3.2x faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the deterministic O(nd)-space, O(knd)-time algorithm of Gulyás et al. (2016), which achieves a 1−1/e approximation for both targeted and general fingerprinting. The proposed streaming algorithms win decisively on runtime: up to 49x faster for targeted fingerprinting (US Census, k=7) and up to 210x faster for general fingerprinting (US Census, 55K-row sketch). On accuracy, the proposed methods trade a small ε-factor in approximation for the speedup: targeted fingerprinting accuracy converges to the baseline as subsampling probability increases (minimum 84% on Adult, 92% on US Census), while general fingerprinting maintains an accuracy ratio of 80–99% on Adult and 70–99% on US Census depending on sketch size. The paper notes the baseline is expected to always achieve slightly better error due to the theoretical ε loss, but the practical gap is small. No other baselines are compared; the paper positions its contribution as the first turnstile streaming algorithm for these problems rather than a competitive accuracy comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the insertion-only streaming algorithms for maximum coverage (Bateni et al., 2017; McGregor & Vu, 2019) to the turnstile model, where arbitrary insertions and deletions of items from subsets are supported. The core subsampling framework from Bateni et al. is adapted into a linear sketch, and the submodular maximization framework from Horel & Singer (2024) is leveraged to handle the general fingerprinting variant. For fingerprinting specifically, the paper departs from Gulyás et al. (2016), which required O(nd) space and O(knd) time, by reducing the problem to streaming maximum coverage and a novel n^p − F_p frequency-moment-complement sketch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first one-pass turnstile streaming algorithm for maximum coverage with poly log n update time and near-tight (1−1/e−ε) approximation, opening the door to real-time monitoring of re-identification risk as datasets evolve. The n^p − F_p sketch is a result of independent interest for frequency-moment estimation where the error guarantee is on the complement rather than the moment itself. Limitations include the one-pass restriction, the dependence on k hidden in polylog factors, and the fact that the general fingerprinting algorithm's O(dk³/ε²) space may still be large for very large k. Open questions include whether multi-pass or two-pass variants can improve the space-approximation tradeoff, and whether the linear sketch framework extends to other submodular objectives beyond fingerprinting.
