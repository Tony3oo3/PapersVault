## Problem

Sinkhorn's algorithm is the standard solver for the static Schrödinger bridge and entropic optimal transport, but all existing linear convergence guarantees are restricted to the KL divergence f(x) = x log x. No convergence theory exists for the natural generalization to arbitrary strictly convex divergence functionals, despite the generalized problem arising in entropic optimal transport, random graph/matrix theory, and contingency table enumeration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a nonnegative weight matrix W (assumed to factor as W = C ⊙ uv^T for a positive matrix C and positive vectors u, v), a pair of margin vectors (r, c) with matching total mass, and a twice-differentiable strictly convex divergence f with domain (A, B). It produces the unique optimal transport matrix Z^{r,c} whose entries lie in (A, B) and satisfy the W-weighted margin constraints, along with the (shift-invariant) row and column potential functions (α, β) whose direct-sum α⊕β is unique and characterizes the solution via Z_{ij} = ψ'(α(i)+β(j)).

## Method

The paper establishes a Kantorovich dual for the generalized SSB problem whose objective is the convex conjugate ψ of f evaluated entrywise on the direct-sum potential α⊕β, and shows the dual's first-order conditions are exactly the Schrödinger system. The Generalized Sinkhorn Algorithm (GSA) alternates between solving for the column potential given the row potential and vice versa, with each sub-problem having a unique solution by strict concavity. The key convergence proof shows each Sinkhorn update is L^∞-non-expanding (via the implicit function theorem revealing row-stochastic Jacobians), confining iterates to a compact set where ψ is strongly convex and smooth. Within this set, the algorithm is a contraction in the weighted norm ‖·‖_{uv^T} on direct-sum potentials, yielding a linear rate governed by the condition number κ = C_max/C_min and the ratio (σ_−/σ_+)⁴ of the strong-convexity-to-smoothness parameters of ψ. Dimension-independent convergence follows from a priori potential bounds under linear margin constraints s ≤ r(i)/W_{i·} ≤ t, with separate theorems for bounded and unbounded domains of f.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.6) establishes that the generalized Sinkhorn algorithm converges linearly with rate (1 − κ⁻²σ₋⁴/σ₊⁴)^(k−k₀) on the dual objective gap, where κ is the condition number of the cost matrix C and σ± are the strong convexity/smoothness parameters of the convex conjugate ψ on the tame interval. For the unbounded-domain case (e.g., EOT with f(x)=x log x), Theorem 2.8 gives dimension-independent potential bounds of O(‖c‖∞/ε) + O(log(t/s)), ensuring dimension-independent linear convergence. Numerical experiments on 100×100 Gaussian marginals with L² cost and ε=10 demonstrate linear convergence across six f-divergences (KL, Jeffreys, Jensen-Shannon, Neyman χ², Binomial, Squared Hellinger), with convergence rates varying by divergence choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing algorithms in the standard sense; rather, it generalizes and subsumes several prior convergence results. Franklin & Lorenz (1989) established linear convergence of the classical Sinkhorn algorithm in Hilbert projective metric for KL divergence only; this work extends that to arbitrary strictly convex f-divergences with arbitrary weight matrices. Carlier (2022) proved linear convergence of multi-marginal Sinkhorn in Euclidean metric for EOT; the present result generalizes this to the full f-divergence setting. Lyu & Mukherjee (2024) established linear convergence for the generalized typical table problem (uniform weight W=11ᵀ); this paper removes the uniform-weight restriction via Assumption 2.2 (W=C⊙uvᵀ). Marino & Gerolin (2020) and Carlier (2022) gave potential bounds for EOT depending only on ‖c‖∞/ε; Theorem 2.8 here provides analogous bounds for general divergences but with an additional O(log(t/s)) margin-dependence term, since the analysis does not assume the special structure uvᵀ=rcᵀ. The numerical experiments qualitatively confirm that different divergences yield different convergence speeds and transport map geometries, suggesting that divergence choice is a meaningful design parameter beyond the standard KL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical static Schrödinger bridge (SSB) and entropic optimal transport (EOT) problems from the KL divergence f(x) = x log x to arbitrary strictly convex f-divergences, and from uniform weight matrices to general rank-one-modulated weights W = C ⊙ uv^T. The convergence analysis builds directly on Lyu & Mukherjee (2024) for the generalized typical table problem and on Carlier (2022) for multi-marginal Sinkhorn convergence, both of which were restricted to specific divergence structures. The key departure is that all prior linear convergence results for Sinkhorn-type algorithms (Franklin & Lorenz 1989, Ruschendorf 1995, Chen et al. 2016, Marino & Gerolin 2020, Carlier 2022) concern only the KL divergence; this paper is the first to establish such rates for arbitrary divergences and general weight matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-independent linear convergence guarantee (Theorems 2.7–2.8) is practically important for large-scale generalized SSB computations, as it ensures the contraction rate does not degrade with problem size under mild margin conditions. The numerical experiments reveal that different f-divergences produce qualitatively different transport maps and convergence rates, suggesting that divergence choice is a meaningful design parameter in EOT formulations beyond the default KL. Open questions include whether the centering argument extends to the more general Luo–Tseng coordinate descent framework, and whether the a priori tameness bounds can be sharpened to conditions as tight as Barvinok margins for contingency tables.
