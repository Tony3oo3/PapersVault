## Problem

No single pre-trained VLM dominates across all downstream visual tasks, yet evaluating every candidate model on a target task is computationally infeasible. Existing model-selection methods either target single-modal architectures or rely on large-scale benchmark performance (e.g., ImageNet) that poorly predicts performance on domain-specific tasks. This paper formalizes VLM selection and reuse as a distinct problem and proposes a target-task-independent labeling mechanism to make selection efficient and scalable.

## Input / Output

At submission time, each anonymous VLM is pre-tested on a fixed semantic graph of 9,055 WordNet synset nodes (each with up to 75 representative images drawn from five ImageNet-family sample datasets) to produce a persistent model label; no target-task data is needed. At query time, the system receives only lightweight task metadata—class names, domain type, and task type—which are expanded into GPT-4-generated captions for matching against the semantic graph. The output is a per-class ensemble of up to k VLMs and, for each test image, a single predicted class label via weighted softmax over the ensemble's cosine-similarity scores.

## Method

MLL operates in three stages. (1) Model labeling: a semantic graph built from WordNet hypernym/hyponym relations is populated with representative images and definition-based captions; each VLM's image and text encoders score every node, yielding a per-node similarity vector that constitutes the model's label. (2) Model selection: GPT-4 generates expanded captions for target-task classes, and cosine similarity between caption embeddings (via text-embedding-3-large) produces a sparse transfer matrix linking semantic-graph nodes to task classes; per-node precision is propagated through this matrix to estimate per-class performance, and a reuse metric blends class-specific accuracy (weight α) with overall accuracy. (3) Model reuse: the top-k models per class form an ensemble whose predictions are combined using entropy-based weights that down-weight overconfident (potentially erroneous) models, and the highest-confidence class across all classes is returned as the final prediction.

## Results

On the MLL benchmark of 17 downstream zero-shot visual tasks, the proposed MLL paradigm achieves an average accuracy of 0.6620 with single-model selection (k=1) and 0.6639 with 3-model ensemble (k=3), outperforming the ImageNet Baseline (INB: 0.6434 / 0.6498) and ModelGPT/LOVM (0.6367 / 0.6480). The largest gains appear on FER2013 (0.4933 vs. 0.2859 for INB, +10.7 pp) and RESISC45 (0.6437 vs. 0.6139, +3.0 pp), while the method underperforms INB on PCam (0.5003 vs. 0.5332) and ModelGPT on Rendered-SST2 (0.5206 vs. 0.5800). Ablation shows α=0.7 is optimal and k=1–3 provides the best accuracy-to-compute trade-off (k=3 costs 2.23× the inference time of k=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MLL against two baselines: INB (selecting the single best ImageNet-accuracy model from the hub) and ModelGPT/LOVM (Zohar et al., 2023, which trains a linear predictor on text-only reuse metrics). MLL outperforms INB on 15 of 17 tasks, with the most dramatic win on FER2013 (+10.7 pp, 0.4933 vs. 0.2859) and consistent 1–3 pp gains on CIFAR-100, DTD, GTSRB, RESISC45, and UCF101; it loses only on PCam (−3.3 pp) where the limited class count makes a single strong model preferable. Against ModelGPT, MLL wins on 14 of 17 tasks, with the largest margin again on FER2013 (+9.2 pp) and notable gains on MNIST (+24.5 pp, where ModelGPT's text-only signal fails on digit images); it loses on Rendered-SST2 (−6.0 pp) where ModelGPT's text-based evaluation aligns well with the OCR task. The paper notes that ModelGPT largely mirrors INB's selections, indicating that text-only model selection struggles under domain shift, whereas MLL's semantic-graph-based labels capture visual-domain specificity more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM selection problem introduced by LOVM/ModelGPT (Zohar et al., 2023), which relied on text-only evaluation and ground-truth performance on large-scale datasets like ImageNet. It also draws conceptual inspiration from the Learnware paradigm (Zhou & Tan, 2022), which uses model specifications for selection, but departs from it by eliminating the need for access to training data—a critical constraint for VLMs whose pre-training corpora are inaccessible. The key departure is the introduction of a task-independent semantic graph (built from WordNet) as a universal evaluation substrate, combined with GPT-4-generated captions to bridge semantic nodes to arbitrary task classes, and an entropy-weighted ensemble reuse mechanism that addresses VLM overconfidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open-source VLM hubs like open-clip grow beyond 100 models, the inability to efficiently select and combine the right models for a given task becomes a practical bottleneck; this paper provides both a concrete paradigm and a 49-model/17-dataset benchmark to advance the field. However, the current scope is limited to zero-shot visual classification with VLMs only, and the reliance on GPT-4 for caption generation introduces a dependency on a proprietary model. Open questions include whether the semantic graph approach generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether it extends to non-VLM architectures, and whether the modest 1–2% gains over baselines persist as model hubs scale to hundreds of models with greater architectural diversity.
