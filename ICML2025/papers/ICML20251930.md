## Problem

Existing analyses of sequence model memory rely on qualitative visualizations (e.g., attention maps) or crude capacity metrics like cache size, which fail to capture how much of a model's available memory is actually realized during training. This gap obscures critical differences between architectures with comparable computational budgets and prevents principled guidance on initialization, regularization, and distillation. The paper asks: can we derive a single quantitative, interpretable metric that measures effective memory utilization across the broad class of causal sequence models?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251930/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained sequence model (any LIV or linear system: attention, SSM, convolution, recurrence) together with an input sequence, from which the linear operator T ∈ R^{dℓ×dℓ} is extracted. For each sequence index i, the submatrix H_i = T_{i:,i-1} is isolated and its singular values are computed via SVD; for SISO models this decomposes into d independent ℓ×ℓ SVDs, reducing complexity from O((dℓ)³) to O(dℓ³). The output is a 4D tensor ESS ∈ R^{m×d×b×ℓ} (layers × channels × batch × sequence index), which can be marginalized into average ESS or total ESS, and further analyzed as a state-modulation profile over sequence position or as a compressibility estimate for distillation.

## Method

The paper unifies diverse sequence models under the LIV abstraction (y = f_T(u)·u), then defines ESS as the rank of the operator submatrix H_i = T_{i:,i-1}, grounded in minimal realization theory: Theorem 3.2 proves this rank equals the minimal state size required to realize the causal operator as a recurrence. Two computational variants are provided—tolerance-ESS (count singular values above a threshold τ, interpretable via Eckart-Young-Mirsky as the minimal state size for approximation error ≤ τ) and entropy-ESS (exponentiated spectral entropy of normalized singular values, giving a continuous effective rank). The key empirical finding is that ESS/kv correlates strongly with MQAR accuracy across the task-model space, far outperforming TSS/kv as a predictor, and that ESS reveals two distinct failure modes (state saturation and state collapse) that TSS alone cannot distinguish. Applications include diagnosing the S6 featurizer's poor ESS-TSS scaling at initialization, using ESS as a distillation compressibility heuristic, adding an ESS-based regularizer (λ‖A−I‖_F) to counteract state collapse, and characterizing state modulation in LLMs where ESS dips at EOS tokens, with SA > GLA > WLA > LA in modulation strength.

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that ESS/kv exhibits a significantly higher correlation with MQAR accuracy than TSS/kv across the entire task-model space (Figures 3, 5), establishing ESS as a superior proxy for memory utilization over raw capacity. On MQAR, S6 (Mamba) achieves accuracy ≈ 0 across TSS 16–256, while GLA shows monotonic accuracy improvements correlated with both TSS and ESS increases (Figure 8b). In the state modulation analysis on 1B-parameter language models, a clear hierarchy emerges: SA > GLA > WLA > LA, with SA showing modulation at tolerance 1e-2 and LA showing none (Figure 42). This hierarchy closely mirrors bigram recall perplexity performance, where SA, GLA, and WLA show similar standard perplexity but diverge significantly on recall (Figure 9b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new architecture but rather a diagnostic metric (ESS) applied to existing models, so 'winning' or 'losing' is reframed as which architectures exhibit favorable ESS properties. GLA (Yang et al., ICML 2024) is the strongest recurrent baseline: it shows monotonic ESS-TSS scaling at initialization, achieves meaningful MQAR accuracy, and exhibits moderate state modulation (tolerance 1e-1), outperforming S6/Mamba (Gu & Dao, COLM 2024) which fails entirely on MQAR (accuracy ≈ 0) due to poor ESS-TSS scaling caused by the arange term exponentially suppressing new states. Softmax Attention (Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017) achieves the highest ESS and most pronounced state modulation (tolerance 1e-2), explaining its superior bigram recall perplexity. Linear Attention (Katharopoulos et al., ICML 2020) has the highest state utilization (ESS/TSS) among recurrent models and avoids state collapse, but shows zero state modulation, explaining its known weakness on complex language recall tasks. WLA sits between GLA and LA: it shows minimal modulation (tolerance 1.0) and suffers state collapse at high kv (2^7), unlike LA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical minimal realization theory (DeWilde & van der Veen, 1998; Akaike, 1974) from linear time-invariant systems to the broader class of input-varying linear operators (LIVs), which encompasses modern sequence models like attention, SSMs, and gated convolutions. It departs from prior memory analysis in sequence models—which has relied on qualitative visualizations (attention maps) or crude capacity proxies (cache size, model size)—by introducing a quantitative, input-dependent metric (ESS) grounded in the rank of operator submatrices. The empirical validation builds on the MAD synthetic-task framework (Poli et al.) and the model-order reduction literature (Massaroli et al., 2023; Bick et al., 2024), while the state modulation analysis connects to the bigram recall perplexity metric of Arora et al. (2024a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESS provides a unified, architecture-agnostic lens for diagnosing why models with similar memory capacity perform differently, with immediate practical applications: it reveals a fundamental initialization flaw in S6/Mamba (the arange term suppresses new states), informs distillation by predicting compressibility, and explains the linear-attention language gap via the absence of state modulation. Open questions include how ESS behaves for non-SISO models (e.g., S5), whether it captures all relevant memory dynamics in very deep or hybrid architectures, and whether the state modulation hierarchy holds as models scale beyond 1B parameters. The computational cost of SVD-based ESS (O(dℓ³)) also limits its use as a real-time training signal for very long sequences.
