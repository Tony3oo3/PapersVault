## Problem

Approximate second-order optimizers (AdaHessian, Sophia-H, Shampoo) consistently converge to significantly sharper minima than SGD, which correlates with their poorer generalization performance. While sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) has been shown to improve first-order methods, it has not been adapted to second-order frameworks, and naively applying it causes numerical instability in diagonal Hessian estimates as curvature is driven toward zero.

## Input / Output

SASSHA consumes standard model parameters, mini-batch training data (32×32 CIFAR images, 224×224 ImageNet images, or tokenized text sequences up to 512 tokens), and a differentiable loss function. Each iteration requires 2 gradient computations and 1 Hessian-vector product (reduced to 0.1 HVP on average via lazy updates at k=10), yielding a total cost of ~2.3 GC-equivalents—comparable to SAM's 2 GCs but augmented with second-order preconditioning. The output is a trained model with flatter minima, evaluated via validation accuracy, perplexity, or GLUE scores.

## Method

SASSHA integrates three mechanisms into a unified second-order update. First, it applies a SAM-style perturbation ε* = ρ∇f/||∇f|| before computing both the gradient and the diagonal Hessian (via Hutchinson's method), so that the second-order Taylor expansion is centered at the worst-case point in the ρ-ball. Second, it stabilizes the Hessian estimate by taking the element-wise square root of the absolute diagonal (|H̃|^{1/2}), which smoothly inflates near-zero curvature entries without introducing threshold hyperparameters, while the absolute value prevents convergence to saddle points. Third, it lazily updates the Hessian every k steps (default k=10) with an exponential moving average, a strategy to which SASSHA is unusually robust because sharpness minimization biases the trajectory toward regions of low Hessian sensitivity. The final update is x_{t+1} = x_t − η_t D̄_t^{−1} m̄_t − η_t λ x_t, where D̄_t is the bias-corrected square-rooted Hessian EMA and m̄_t is the bias-corrected gradient momentum evaluated at the perturbed point.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-32, SASSHA achieves 72.14% validation accuracy, outperforming SGD (69.32%), SAM-SGD (71.99%), and all second-order baselines (AdaHessian 68.06%, Sophia-H 67.76%, Shampoo 64.08%). On ImageNet with ViT-s-32, SASSHA reaches 69.20% top-1 accuracy, surpassing SAM-AdamW (68.31%) and AdamW (66.46%). For language pretraining on Wikitext-2 (GPT1-mini), SASSHA achieves the lowest perplexity of 122.40, substantially below Sophia-H (157.60) and SAM-AdamW (158.06). SASSHA also demonstrates superior robustness to label noise, retaining 53.98% accuracy at 60% noise on CIFAR-100 versus 51.93% for SAM-SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASSHA outperforms all approximate second-order baselines (AdaHessian, Sophia-H, Shampoo) by a wide margin across every setting, with gaps of 1–8% accuracy on image classification and up to 35 perplexity points on Wikitext-2. Against first-order baselines, SASSHA surpasses SGD by 0.3–3% and AdamW by 0.3–2.7% on image tasks, and by 52.66 perplexity points on GPT1-mini pretraining. Notably, SASSHA also beats SAM (both SAM-SGD and SAM-AdamW variants) in most configurations, with the largest gains on Transformer-based architectures: 69.20% vs. 68.31% on ImageNet ViT-s-32 and 83.54% vs. 83.14% on CIFAR-100 WRN-28-10. The paper attributes this advantage over SAM to SASSHA's second-order preconditioning addressing the block heterogeneity inherent in Transformers, where the Hessian spectrum varies significantly across layers. On GLUE finetuning with SqueezeBERT, SASSHA is competitive with Sophia-H and outperforms AdamW on most tasks (e.g., 72.08% RTE accuracy vs. 68.95% for AdamW), though it trails Sophia-H slightly on QQP F1 (87.85 vs. 87.60) and MNLI matched (81.71 vs. 82.36).

## Relation to Prior Work

SASSHA sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads: sharpness-aware minimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) for improving generalization via flat minima, and approximate second-order optimization (AdaHessian, Sophia-H, Shampoo) for accelerating convergence via curvature information. The authors explicitly note that combining these—applying sharpness minimization within a second-order framework—had not been previously explored. The key technical departure from both parent lines is the stabilization machinery (square-rooted absolute-value preconditioning) needed to prevent numerical divergence when sharpness reduction drives Hessian entries toward zero, plus the observation that this same mechanism makes lazy Hessian reuse far more robust than in prior second-order methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-evidenced explanation for why practical second-order optimizers generalize worse than SGD (they find sharper minima) and a principled fix that closes the gap while retaining second-order speed benefits. The counterintuitive result that SASSHA outperforms SAM—despite SAM being the 'natural' sharpness-aware baseline—suggests that second-order preconditioning and sharpness minimization are complementary rather than redundant, which could reshape how practitioners think about combining these techniques. Open questions include convergence rates, generalization bounds, implicit bias analysis, and whether the approach scales to frontier-scale models and datasets, all of which the authors acknowledge as future work.
