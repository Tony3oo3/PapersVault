## Problem

Combinatorial semi-bandits with semi-bandit feedback (observing only selected arms) and full information (observing all arms) are well understood, but many real applications—multi-platform advertising, recommendation—exhibit intermediate information structures where observing a selected arm also reveals rewards of semantically related arms. This paper formalizes such structures via a directed feedback graph G and asks: what is the minimax regret as a function of the graph's independence number α, and what algorithmic ingredients are needed to achieve it?

## Input / Output

At each of T rounds, the learner selects a binary vector v ∈ {0,1}^K with ‖v‖₁ = S (the combinatorial budget) and observes the rewards of all out-neighbors of the selected arms in a strongly observable directed graph G = ([K], E), i.e., {r_a^t : ∃a with v_a=1 and (a,i)∈E}. The algorithm outputs a sequence of S-arm decisions and achieves regret Õ(S√T + √(αST)), where α is the independence number of G. The bound interpolates between Õ(S√T) when G is complete (α=1, full information) and Õ(√(KST)) when G has only self-loops (α=K, semi-bandit).

## Method

The proposed algorithm OSMD-G performs Online Stochastic Mirror Descent in a dual space defined by negative entropy, using unbiased reward estimators constructed from the graph feedback. The critical technical ingredient is a sampling subroutine (randomized swap rounding from Chekuri et al., 2009) that realizes the convexified action x^t in expectation while simultaneously guaranteeing negative correlations Cov(v_i^t, v_j^t) ≤ 0 for all i≠j. These negative correlations allow the second moment of the number of arms observing a given node to decompose into a squared-mean term plus a sum of individual variances, eliminating O(K²) cross-correlation terms and reducing the regret factor from Sα to S+α. The paper further proves that the mean condition alone (sufficient for both semi-bandit and full-information settings) is provably suboptimal under general graph feedback, establishing that negative correlations are a genuinely new requirement for near-optimal performance in this intermediate regime.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax regret for adversarial combinatorial semi-bandits with a strongly observable feedback graph G is Θ̃(S√T + √(αST)), where S is the combinatorial budget and α is the independence number of G. This bound is tight: the proposed algorithm OSMD-G (using randomized swap rounding for negative-correlation sampling) achieves the upper bound, and a matching lower bound is proven. The result interpolates between the known full-information regret Θ̃(S√T) (when α=1, i.e., G is complete) and the semi-bandit regret Θ̃(√(KST)) (when α=K, i.e., G has only self-loops). A key negative result shows that OSMD without the negative-correlation requirement is provably suboptimal, suffering Ω(S√(αT)) regret in the worst case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between regret bounds under different feedback structures and algorithmic variants. The new general-graph bound Θ̃(S√T + √(αST)) recovers the full-information bound Θ̃(S√T) of Koolen et al. (2010) when G is complete and the semi-bandit bound Θ̃(√(KST)) of Audibert et al. (2014) when G has only self-loops, confirming it as the correct interpolation. The critical algorithmic comparison is between OSMD-G with negative-correlation sampling (achieving Θ̃(S√T + √(αST))) and vanilla OSMD-G with only the mean condition (provably suboptimal at Ω(S√(αT)) per Theorem 3.4). For general decision subsets A₀ ⊊ A where negative correlations are infeasible, the regret degrades to Θ̃(S√(αT)), which is fundamentally worse than the full-set result. The paper also shows that for combinatorial semi-bandits with general capacity (each arm selectable up to nₐ times), the graph-feedback approach yields Θ̃(√(KST)) rather than the naive Θ̃(√(NST)) from ignoring the copy structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: combinatorial semi-bandits (Audibert et al., 2014; Koolen et al., 2010), where a learner selects S arms and observes their individual rewards, and feedback graph bandits (Alon et al., 2015; Mannor & Shamir, 2011), where the learner observes rewards of neighboring arms in a directed graph. The algorithm OSMD-G extends the standard Online Stochastic Mirror Descent framework by incorporating a randomized swap rounding subroutine (Chekuri et al., 2009) that enforces negative correlations among sampled arms—a requirement that is absent in both the full-information and semi-bandit settings. The paper also departs from prior work by proving that the standard OSMD (which only requires mean-matching of the convexified action) is provably suboptimal under general graph feedback, a failure mode not present in either extreme case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete minimax regret characterization Θ̃(S√T + √(αST)) for combinatorial semi-bandits with general strongly observable feedback graphs, cleanly interpolating between the known full-information and semi-bandit bounds. The identification of negative correlations as a necessary (not merely sufficient) technical ingredient for near-optimal regret is a conceptual advance that may inform future algorithm design in partially observed combinatorial settings. Open questions include extending results to weakly observable graphs (where the regret scales as δ^{1/3}T^{2/3} for S=1), deriving problem-dependent bounds that exploit the graph structure, and characterizing the optimal regret for general decision subsets where negative correlations may be infeasible.
