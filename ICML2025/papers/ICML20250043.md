## Problem

Existing differentiable solvers for the Maximum Independent Set problem either rely on training data (suffering from poor out-of-distribution generalization) or use quadratic formulations that do not exploit the duality between MIS and Maximum Clique. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a new quadratic objective that incorporates a clique term from the complement graph, with full theoretical characterization of its local minimizers and stationary points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250043/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single undirected graph G=(V,E) specified by its adjacency matrix, along with a small set of hyperparameters (edge penalty γ, clique penalty γ', step size α, momentum β, iterations T, and initialization spread η). It outputs a binary vector identifying the vertices in the maximum independent set. Notably, no training data or labeled graphs are required, and the per-iteration computational cost is O(mn) via a single matrix-vector product, with total runtime scaling only with the number of nodes n rather than edges m.

## Method

The core formulation minimizes f(x) = -e_n^T x + (γ/2)x^T A_G x - (γ'/2)x^T A_G' x over [0,1]^n, where the third term (informed by the complement graph's adjacency matrix) encourages selecting non-adjacent vertex pairs and discourages sparsity. Theoretical results establish that γ ≥ 1 + γ'k (k = MIS size) is necessary and sufficient for all MaxIS vectors to be local minimizers, and that all local minimizers are binary under γ > 1 + γ'Δ(G'). To navigate the non-convex landscape, the method runs M parallel projected momentum-based gradient descent trajectories initialized from a degree-weighted Gaussian distribution, then applies an efficient single-step projected gradient check to verify MaxIS validity. This dataless, parameter-efficient approach avoids the out-of-distribution tuning and (un)labeled data dependencies of learning-based methods while achieving competitive or superior MIS sizes.

## Results

On the ER benchmark (128 graphs, 700–800 nodes, p=0.15), pCQO-MIS achieves an average MIS size of 45.109, outperforming ReduMIS (44.87), iSCO (44.57), and all learning-based methods (best: DIMES at 42.06). On SATLIB (500 graphs, up to 1,347 nodes), pCQO-MIS reaches 425.148, slightly below the exact solvers (425.96) but above all learning-based and sampling baselines. The MC term contributes approximately 4 nodes of improvement on ER and 11 on SATLIB over the ablated pQO-MIS variant (γ′=0). On dense GNM graphs (n=2000, m≈n²/4), pCQO-MIS solves in under 4 minutes while ReduMIS requires ~350 minutes, demonstrating node-only scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ER graphs, pCQO-MIS (45.11) beats ReduMIS (44.87) by 0.24 nodes and iSCO (44.57) by 0.54 nodes, while also outperforming all learning-based methods by 3+ nodes (best learning: DIMES at 42.06). On SATLIB, pCQO-MIS (425.15) falls just short of the exact solvers CP-SAT and Gurobi (425.96) and ReduMIS (425.96), but exceeds DIFUSCO-RL+S (425.13) and all other learning/sampling baselines. The ablation pQO-MIS without the MC term (412.89 on SATLIB, 40.40 on ER) is substantially worse, confirming the MC term's critical role. On dense GNM graphs (n=2000), pCQO-MIS completes in <4 min versus ~350 min for ReduMIS, while exact solvers (Gurobi, CP-SAT) are infeasible beyond n=500. On DIMACS graphs, pCQO-MIS matches the optimal solution on 49/61 graphs within 30 seconds, compared to 58/61 for ReduMIS. On BA graphs, pCQO-MIS matches ReduMIS in 6/9 cases but underperforms by 3–5 nodes on two denser instances (n=1000, q=15 and q=50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QUBO-based continuous relaxation for MIS (Pardalos & Rodgers, 1992) by incorporating a Maximum Clique term derived from the complement graph, building on the characterization results of Mahdavi Pajouh et al. (2013) that link binary local minimizers to MaxISs. It departs from data-driven learning-based solvers (GCN, DIMES, DIFUSCO, LwD) that require per-dataset training, and from prior dataless differentiable methods (Alkhouri et al. 2022's ReLU-based objective, Schuetz et al. 2022's PI-GNN) by proposing a purely quadratic objective with explicit theoretical guarantees on local minimizer structure. The optimization strategy—parallel degree-based initializations with projected momentum gradient descent—differs from the single-trajectory approaches in prior dataless methods and from the graph-reduction-heavy pipelines of ReduMIS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that pCQO-MIS's runtime scales with the number of nodes rather than edges, giving it a decisive advantage on dense graphs where ILP solvers and ReduMIS degrade substantially (e.g., <4 min vs. ~350 min at n=2000). However, the method still requires per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (γ, γ', α, β, T, η), remains NP-hard in the worst case, and underperforms ReduMIS on sparse graphs where graph reductions are highly effective. Open questions include whether the MC term can be generalized to other COPs with complement-structure duality, whether the hyperparameter sensitivity can be reduced, and whether the parallel MGD strategy can be further accelerated for very large dense instances.
