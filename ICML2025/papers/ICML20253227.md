## Problem

Auto-regressive PDE foundation models suffer from a shortcut problem where the network learns to copy previous frames rather than capture true dynamics, causing severe error accumulation on long trajectories and out-of-distribution data. Existing physics-informed approaches either require known PDE symbols as conditioning priors (limiting generalization to unseen equations) or apply fixed physics constraints that overfit to specific PDE families, leaving a gap for universal, data-driven physical supervision that works without prior knowledge of the governing equation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T_in=10 initial PDE solution frames on a 2D spatiotemporal grid and iteratively predicts T_ar future frames (T_ar=1 or 10) via a neural operator. For the PDE discovery component, the predicted sequence is spatially downsampled to 1/4 of grid points and temporally truncated to the most recent T_C=3 frames to reduce computational cost, while the ground-truth input sequence is kept at full resolution to ensure accurate equation identification. Outputs include both the predicted solution fields and the sparse coefficient matrix Λ encoding the discovered governing PDE structure.

## Method

PITA couples auto-regressive prediction with SINDy-style sparse regression in a self-supervised loop: the governing equation is first discovered from ground-truth frames, then rediscovered from the predicted sequence, and the discrepancy between the two coefficient matrices serves as a consistency loss that penalizes dynamics deviation. Three losses—data (nRMSE between prediction and ground truth), physics (residual of the discovered PDE applied to predicted data), and consistency (L2 distance between ground-truth and predicted coefficient matrices)—are balanced via Kendall-style uncertainty-based multi-task weighting. Because the l0 sparsity penalty renders joint optimization NP-hard, PITA uses alternating direction optimization: sparse regression updates Λ given fixed θ, then gradient descent updates θ and the uncertainty parameters given fixed Λ, iterating until convergence. This design requires no known PDE symbols, making it applicable to any time-dependent PDE family.

## Results

On short-trajectory prediction (10 steps), PITA achieves SOTA nRMSE across 77.78% of tasks for medium/large models, with an average 13.53% reduction over auto-regressive baselines (e.g., DPOT-L on FNO-NS-1e-3: 0.00139 vs. 0.00298, a 53.4% improvement). On long-trajectory prediction, PITA reduces total accumulated error by 30.22% on average, with the largest gain of 63.81% (DPOT-S on FNO-NS-1e-4: 0.00854 vs. 0.02370). For out-of-distribution generalization to the Burgers equation, PITA achieves a 37.8% average error reduction over auto-regressive finetuning across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPOT (Hao et al., 2024), PITA wins on the majority of dataset-model combinations: for short trajectories, PITA achieves an average 13.53% nRMSE reduction across medium/large models (e.g., DPOT-L on FNO-NS-1e-3: 0.00139 vs. 0.00298), and for long trajectories the average improvement reaches 30.22% (e.g., DPOT-S on FNO-NS-1e-4: 0.00854 vs. 0.02370, a 63.8% reduction). PITA does lose on a few short-trajectory cases (e.g., DPOT-Ti on PDEBench-DR: 0.02119 vs. 0.01326), but the long-trajectory advantage is consistent across all DPOT sizes. Against MPP (McCabe et al., 2023), PITA shows modest but consistent gains on available datasets (e.g., PDEBench-SWE: 0.00605 vs. 0.00645 for Ti; 0.00274 vs. 0.00281 for S), with the largest improvement on PDEBench-DR long trajectory (0.03704 vs. 0.04258 for S). Against the single-family FNO baseline (Li et al., 2020), PITA delivers the most dramatic improvements, particularly on PDEBench-CNS datasets where auto-regressive FNO-M suffers severe degradation (e.g., PDEBench-CNS-(1,0.1): 0.10947 vs. 0.27849, a 60.7% reduction), confirming that physics-informed alignment is especially beneficial when the base model lacks multi-PDE pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

PITA sits at the intersection of three research threads: data-driven PDE discovery via sparse regression (SINDy, Rudy et al. 2017), physics-informed training with alternating direction optimization (Chen et al. 2021), and uncertainty-weighted multi-task learning (Kendall et al. 2018). It departs from prior physics-informed PDE solvers (PINNs, PROSE, UniSolver) that require known governing equations as priors, instead discovering the PDE structure entirely from observation data in a self-supervised manner. The key conceptual departure from auto-regressive PDE foundation models (DPOT, MPP) is the 'temporal alignment' mechanism: comparing PDE coefficients discovered from ground-truth sequences versus those from predicted sequences to generate a physics-consistency signal that penalizes shortcut-driven error accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PITA offers a plug-and-play training strategy for existing auto-regressive PDE foundation models that addresses the well-documented shortcut problem without requiring known PDE equations, making it applicable to real-world scenarios where governing physics is partially or fully unknown. The finding that pretraining on PDE datasets does not transfer well to out-of-distribution tasks (Appendix E.4) is an important caution for the foundation-model-for-science community. Open questions include scalability to 3D PDEs, sensitivity to library completeness and observational noise, and whether the computational overhead (~23–42% extra training time) remains acceptable at larger scales. The approach also opens a path toward integrating other inverse-problem techniques (parameter inference, initial state recovery) into forward PDE prediction.
