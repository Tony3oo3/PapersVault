## Problem

Weight-only quantization of LLMs faces two intertwined challenges: current hardware lacks native support for mixed-precision GEMM (mpGEMM), forcing inefficient dequantization-based inference, and the highly non-uniform weight distributions in LLMs make standard uniform quantization suboptimal. Existing non-uniform quantization methods rely on heuristic mappings (power-exponent functions, clustering with ad-hoc distance metrics) that lack principled optimization and generalization guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253134/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained linear layer's weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} and calibration activations X ∈ R^{n×p} (p = b×s tokens from C4, typically 128 sequences × 2048 tokens for LLaMA models). It outputs a per-channel codebook T ∈ R^{m×2^N} of 2^N real-valued entries per output channel and a low-bit query matrix Q ∈ {0,…,2^N−1}^{m×n} that indexes into the codebook. The additional storage over basic per-channel uniform quantization is less than 0.2% for typical model dimensions, since min{m,n} ≫ 2^N.

## Method

GANQ formulates layer-wise LUT-based non-uniform quantization as a mixed-integer quadratic program minimizing the Frobenius norm of the layer output error, which decomposes into m independent row-wise subproblems amenable to GPU-parallel execution. An alternating direction optimization framework solves each subproblem: the codebook update T_i has a closed-form solution via the Moore-Penrose inverse of a small 2^N × 2^N matrix, while the discrete assignment S_i is solved by a back-substitution procedure that exploits the Cholesky factorization of XX^T. Specifically, the lower-triangular structure of L allows each column of S_i to be determined sequentially from right to left, where each step reduces to a nearest-neighbor lookup in the current codebook. This GPU-adaptive design quantizes a 7B model in approximately one hour on a single RTX 4090, and the framework is directly compatible with existing LUT-based inference kernels and outlier-handling techniques like sparse-dense weight decomposition.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity (lower is better), GANQ consistently achieves the lowest values across all OPT and LLaMA models at both 4-bit and 3-bit quantization. Notably, at 4-bit on OPT-2.7B, GANQ reaches 12.33 perplexity, surpassing the FP16 baseline (12.47); at 3-bit on OPT-6.7B, GANQ achieves 11.39 versus 15.11 for GPTQ and 13.47 for OmniQuant. On zero-shot tasks (LLaMA-2-7B, 4-bit), GANQ attains 64.23% mean accuracy, nearly matching FP16 (64.47%), while at 3-bit it maintains 62.22% versus 58.66% for OmniQuant. Deployed on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, GANQ achieves up to 2.57× inference speedup over FP16 with peak memory reduced to 3.30 GB (LLaMA-7B, 3-bit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GANQ outperforms all four baselines across every model and bit-width configuration. Against GPTQ, the gap is most pronounced at 3-bit: on OPT-6.7B, GANQ achieves 11.39 perplexity versus GPTQ's 15.11 (a 3.72-point reduction), and on LLaMA-2-7B, 6.25 versus 9.46. Against OmniQuant, GANQ leads by 0.78 perplexity points on OPT-6.7B at 3-bit (11.39 vs. 13.47) and by 0.83 on LLaMA-2-7B (6.25 vs. 7.08); OmniQuant also fails to quantize LLaMA-3-8B on a single RTX 4090. In the outlier-handling setting (Table 5), GANQ* (with 0.5% sparse outliers) beats AWQ(g128) by 0.37 perplexity on OPT-125M at 4-bit (28.16 vs. 29.09) and SqueezeLLM by 0.35 on OPT-125M (28.16 vs. 28.51), while matching SqueezeLLM on LLaMA-2-7B (5.57 vs. 5.57 at 4-bit). GANQ also demonstrates superior robustness on LLaMA-3.2 models, where GPTQ collapses (perplexity >4000 on 3B-Instruct at 4-bit) and RTN fails on 1B-Instruct, while GANQ maintains stable performance (11.99 on 3B-Instruct, 14.36 on 1B-Instruct at 4-bit).

## Relation to Prior Work

GANQ positions itself at the intersection of two prior threads: LUT-based mpGEMM inference kernels (SqueezeLLM, LUT Tensor Core, Guo et al.) that eliminate dequantization overhead, and non-uniform quantization methods (NUPES, k-means-based codebook learning) that adapt to non-Gaussian weight distributions. It departs from both by formulating the LUT codebook design as a principled layer-wise mixed-integer quadratic program that minimizes output error, rather than relying on heuristic distance metrics or manually designed mappings. Algorithmically, it introduces a GPU-adaptive alternating direction scheme with Cholesky-based back-substitution to solve the resulting combinatorial subproblem, which is distinct from the gradient-based optimization used by OmniQuant or the sensitivity-based clustering used by SqueezeLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GANQ offers a practical, training-free quantization pipeline that runs in ~1 hour on a single RTX 4090 and plugs directly into existing LUT-based inference kernels, making it immediately deployable for on-device LLM serving. The 2.57× speedup at 3-bit with sub-4 GB peak memory is a meaningful efficiency win for single-GPU deployment. Open questions include whether the sub-optimal back-substitution can be tightened (e.g., via local search or iterative refinement), how the method scales to 70B+ models where calibration data and Hessian computation become more expensive, and whether emerging hardware with native mpGEMM support would reduce the relative advantage of LUT-based approaches. The paper does not explore weight-activation quantization or dynamic quantization, leaving those as natural extensions.
