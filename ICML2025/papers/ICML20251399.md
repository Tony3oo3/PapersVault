## Problem

Existing VQ-VAE-based skill learning for robot manipulation suffers from codebook collapse, where the straight-through estimator assigns identical gradients to all embeddings quantized to the same code, causing most codebook vectors to go unused. Additionally, prior discrete-skill approaches fail to model causal dependencies between skill abstractions, making it difficult to generate coherent, temporally consistent actions for long-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a 10-timestep window of multimodal observations (RGB images, proprioceptive states) concatenated with a language instruction, encoded via ResNet-18, an MLP, and CLIP-base respectively. It outputs a pair of hierarchical discrete skill codes (2 levels × 16 codes each, covering 8 timesteps per abstraction) and a continuous action sequence produced by decoding the selected codebook vectors and adding a learned offset refinement.

## Method

STAR is a two-stage imitation learning framework. Stage 1 trains RaRSQ, which replaces the straight-through estimator in residual VQ-VAE with a rotation-based gradient mechanism: at each quantization depth, a rotation matrix aligns the current residual to its nearest codebook vector, and during backpropagation this rotation preserves relative angular relationships so that points within the same quantization region receive geometrically distinct gradient updates, preventing codebook collapse. Stage 2 freezes RaRSQ and trains a Causal Skill Transformer (CST) that autoregressively predicts skill codes from coarse to fine levels (modeling inter-code dependencies) and appends an offset prediction head (borrowed from BeT) to refine the discretized actions into continuous control signals. Training uses reconstruction + commitment losses for RaRSQ and cross-entropy skill prediction + L2 action refinement losses for CST, with nucleus sampling at inference.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, STAR achieves 93.6% overall success rate, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art QueST (81.5%) by 12.1 percentage points. The improvement is most pronounced on long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long: 88.5% vs. 69.1%, +19.4%) and complex manipulation (LIBERO-Goal: 95.0% vs. 76.7%, +18.3%). On MetaWorld MT50, STAR achieves 92.7% average success rate across 50 tasks, outperforming existing methods by 2.1%–5.4%. In real-world experiments, STAR achieves 60% complete task success on sequential object placement and 30% on drawer manipulation, compared to 40%/10% (VQ-BeT) and 40%/0% (QueST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all seven baselines across every LIBERO task suite. Against the closest discrete LVM competitor QueST (81.5% overall), STAR gains 12.1 points, with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (+19.4%) and LIBERO-Goal (+18.3%), where long-horizon skill composition is critical. VQ-BeT (76.8%) trails by 16.8 points overall, with particularly large gaps on LIBERO-Goal (95.0% vs. 61.3%) and LIBERO-Long (88.5% vs. 59.7%), reflecting its codebook collapse and lack of temporal dependency modeling. Among end-to-end imitation methods, Diffusion Policy (62.6%) and ACT (66.8%) are the weakest, losing 31.0 and 26.8 points respectively, while ResNet-T (67.3%) also falls well behind. Notably, STAR surpasses the large-scale VLA models Octo (75.1%) and OpenVLA (76.5%) by 18.5 and 17.1 points respectively, despite using far less training data, suggesting that structured skill abstractions provide a more efficient representation than scaling model size alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR combines three previously independent ideas into a unified framework for robot skill learning: the rotation trick from Fifty et al. (2024) for gradient-preserving quantization, multi-level residual VQ-VAE structure from Lee et al. (2022), and the BeT offset prediction mechanism from Shafiullah et al. (2022) for bridging discrete-to-continuous action gaps. It departs from VQ-BeT (Lee et al., 2024) and QueST (Mete et al., 2024), which share the motivation of discrete latent skills for transformer-based policies, by arguing that the straight-through estimator's uniform gradient assignment is the root cause of codebook collapse and that geometric relationships between embeddings must be preserved during backpropagation. The causal skill transformer extends the autoregressive prediction paradigm by explicitly modeling hierarchical dependencies between residual quantization levels, going beyond the independent code prediction used in prior discrete-skill approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-motivated fix for codebook collapse in discrete skill learning—a persistent practical problem that limits the expressiveness of VQ-based robot policies—by showing that rotation-augmented gradients maintain full codebook utilization (16/16 vs. 7/16 codes). The 12% improvement over the prior SOTA (QueST) on LIBERO and successful real-world validation on an ALOHA robot suggest the approach is practically relevant for multi-task manipulation. However, the method requires manually tuned codebook sizes and quantization depths, depends on expert demonstrations, and the two-level residual structure (D=2) has not been stress-tested at greater depths, leaving open whether the rotation mechanism scales to deeper hierarchies or larger codebooks without additional regularization.
