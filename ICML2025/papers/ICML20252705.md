## Problem

Existing iterative CoT methods suffer from two critical failures: over-reasoning, where correct early predictions are overturned by subsequent iterations, and high similarity between consecutive reasoning paths that prevents further uncertainty reduction. Additionally, generic prompts like 'Let's think step by step' fail to capture task-specific nuances, leading to suboptimal initial reasoning. The paper addresses these gaps by reframing CoT reasoning through the lens of self-training as an entropy minimization process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question text concatenated with a task-specific prompt as input and produces N diverse reasoning paths (sampled via self-consistency) along with their corresponding answer predictions. In iterative rounds, the input is augmented with previous reasoning paths and predictions, and a new divergence-encouraging prompt p* is appended. The final output is a single answer prediction, selected either when semantic entropy drops below a threshold δ or via majority voting across all iterations if the maximum iteration count T is reached.

## Method

The framework comprises two modules grounded in a theoretical analogy between self-training and CoT as iterative entropy minimization. The Task-Specific Prompt (TSP) module uses the LLM to generate m candidate prompts tailored to the task, evaluates each by mean semantic entropy on a held-out question set, and selects the prompt minimizing entropy to produce high-quality initial reasoning. The Adaptive Reasoning Iteration (ARI) module computes semantic entropy of predictions at each iteration; if entropy ≤ δ, it halts and outputs the current prediction (mitigating over-reasoning). If entropy > δ, it introduces a new prompt p* that explicitly instructs the LLM to re-evaluate from alternative perspectives, then measures Jaccard similarity between new and previous reasoning paths, resampling until diversity exceeds a threshold τ. If entropy remains above δ after T iterations, majority voting across all iterations yields the final answer, balancing accuracy against computational cost.

## Results

On 10 zero-shot reasoning datasets spanning arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic categories, the full ST-CoT framework (TSP+ARI) achieves 83.7% overall average accuracy, a +4.2% improvement over the Zero-Shot-CoT+SC baseline (79.5%). The largest gains appear on arithmetic datasets (e.g., AQuA: 70.1% vs. 63.0%, +7.1%; AddSub: 88.4% vs. 81.3%, +7.1%) and symbolic reasoning (LastLetter: 77.2% vs. 66.2%, +11.0%). In few-shot settings on MultiArith and GSM8K, adding ARI to Few-Shot-CoT+SC raises average accuracy from 85.5% to 87.4% (+1.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full ST-CoT method (TSP+ARI) outperforms all four baselines on the overall average: Zero-Shot-CoT+SC (79.5%) by +4.2%, RE2+SC (79.2%) by +4.5%, RE2 (74.4%) by +9.3%, and Contrastive-CoT+SC (82.5% on the two few-shot datasets) by +1.2% in the few-shot setting. The ARI module alone contributes the largest single gain (+3.8% over SC), particularly on arithmetic (AQuA +6.3%, AddSub +5.8%) and symbolic (LastLetter +9.6%) tasks, while TSP adds a smaller but consistent +1.9%. On commonsense datasets (STQA, CSQA), gains are modest (+0.8% and +1.4%), suggesting the method's benefits are most pronounced for tasks requiring multi-step logical decomposition rather than world-knowledge retrieval. In the few-shot comparison (Table 2), ARI applied to Few-Shot-CoT+SC improves GSM8K by +3.7% (73.7%→77.4%) and MultiArith by +0.1%, confirming ARI's plug-and-play applicability across prompting paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes chain-of-thought reasoning through the lens of self-training, drawing a formal analogy between iterative pseudo-label refinement (Frei et al., 2022) and iterative reasoning-path correction in LLMs. It builds directly on zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022) and self-consistency sampling (Wang et al., 2022) as its operational backbone, but departs from prior CoT methods by introducing a task-specific prompt search guided by semantic entropy minimization and an adaptive iteration module that uses entropy thresholds to halt over-reasoning and a Jaccard-based diversity constraint to prevent redundant reasoning paths. The theoretical contribution—a Gaussian mixture model analysis showing that self-training entropy dynamics decompose into four spatial regions—provides a formal scaffold that the authors then heuristically transfer to the CoT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of over-reasoning (correct early predictions degrading under further iteration) and the entropy-based stopping criterion offer a practical, plug-and-play mechanism applicable to both zero-shot and few-shot CoT pipelines, which is valuable for practitioners already using self-consistency. However, the gains are modest (4.2% over SC overall, near-zero on commonsense tasks), the theoretical analysis is confined to a simplified 2D GMM that does not directly model LLM generation, and all experiments use a single model (GPT-3.5-turbo), leaving open whether the entropy dynamics and adaptive stopping generalize to larger or differently-architected models. A key open question is whether the semantic-entropy threshold δ can be set adaptively per question rather than globally, and whether the Jaccard-based diversity constraint scales to longer, multi-step reasoning chains.
