## Problem

Deep learning models for coastal process emulation have been hindered by the absence of a large-scale, high-resolution dataset that captures multiple interacting physical processes simultaneously. Existing datasets are limited to small spatial domains, coarse resolutions (>1 km), short time spans, or single-process scenarios, making it impossible to train and benchmark models for realistic, multi-physics coastal forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252794/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D ocean state on a non-uniform 898×598×12 mesh (depth treated as channels, yielding 2D spatial patches), lateral boundary conditions along four domain edges, 8 meteorological forcing fields, 3 river inflow vectors, static physical features (bathymetry, Coriolis parameter, curvilinear grid metrics), and a scalar lead time. It outputs the temporal difference ΔX for 7 ocean variables (u, v, w, temperature, salinity, salinity diffusion coefficient, free surface elevation) at the requested lead time, which is added back to the initial state to recover the absolute prediction. The non-uniform mesh with resolution varying from ~1 m in estuarine inlets to ~300 m offshore is a key structural constraint distinguishing this from regular-grid weather datasets.

## Method

The paper proposes a customized ViT-Base that departs from standard patch embedding in three ways: (1) each ocean and meteorological variable is independently tokenized and then aggregated across the variable dimension via a single cross-attention layer with a learnable query, preserving vertical relationships and integrating external forcings; (2) static physical features (bathymetry, grid size, Coriolis) are patchified and fused into the positional embedding through cross-attention, encoding geophysical context; (3) lead time, boundary conditions, and river inflows are encoded into a conditioning vector applied via adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) in every attention and feed-forward block. Training predicts temporal differences ΔX with randomly sampled lead times from {0.5, 3, 12} hours, followed by a second-stage fine-tuning with K=4 autoregressive rollout steps to reduce error accumulation. At inference, a greedy strategy selects the largest available lead-time step that fits the remaining horizon, achieving a >70× speedup over ROMS for 72-hour forecasts.

## Results

The ViT-Base model (hidden dim 768, depth 12, 12 heads, patch size 4) achieves a >70× speedup over ROMS for a 72-hour forecast, reducing runtime from 2,477 seconds (512 CPU cores, AMD EPYC 7742) to 34.14 seconds (single A100 GPU). Prediction quality is reported via RMSE, MAE, and Pearson correlation (r) across lead times of 0.5–72 hours for variables including velocity (u, v, w), temperature, salinity, and free surface elevation; errors increase smoothly with lead time while correlation decreases. In the scaling test, ViT-Base consistently outperforms ViT-Small and ViT-Tiny, with the performance gap widening at longer lead times (48–72 hours). The ablation study confirms that removing meteorological forcings, boundary conditions, static physical features, or river inflow each degrade specific variables (e.g., boundary conditions most affect velocity, river inflow most affects salinity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other deep learning surrogate models; instead, the ViT model is validated against ROMS numerical simulations as ground truth. The primary quantitative comparison is computational: the ViT model achieves a >70× speedup (34.14 s vs. 2,477 s for a 72-hour forecast), making it a practical surrogate for operational coastal forecasting. The scaling test (ViT-Tiny vs. ViT-Small vs. ViT-Base) shows that larger models yield lower RMSE across all ocean variables, with the advantage growing at longer lead times, suggesting that further scaling (e.g., ViT-Large) could improve accuracy. The ablation study serves as an internal comparison, demonstrating that each input modality (meteorological forcings, boundary conditions, static features, river inflow) contributes meaningfully to prediction quality, with boundary conditions being most critical for velocity and river inflow most critical for salinity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the architectural and training toolkit developed for global weather forecasting (ViT-based patch embedding, adaLN conditioning, multi-step autoregressive rollout from FourCastNet/GraphCast, greedy inference from Pangu-Weather) to the fundamentally different problem of high-resolution regional coastal ocean emulation on a non-uniform 3D mesh. It departs from prior coastal ML work (James et al. 2018, Wei & Davison 2022, Shen et al. 2024), which targeted single processes (waves, sea level, SST) on small or low-resolution datasets, by formulating a unified multi-variable, multi-process emulation task. The physics-aware positional embedding incorporating bathymetry and grid metrics is a modest but domain-specific extension of standard ViT positional encoding, motivated by the irregular terrain-following coordinate system of ROMS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoastalBench is the first open, decade-long, sub-100m-resolution coastal circulation dataset with 22 variables on a 6-million-cell 3D mesh, filling a critical gap that has constrained progress in deep learning for coastal oceanography. The demonstrated 70× speedup over ROMS (2,477 s → 34 s for a 72-h forecast) is practically significant for operational forecasting and ensemble applications. Key open questions include whether the purely data-driven approach can be augmented with physics constraints to avoid unphysical predictions, whether the model generalizes beyond the single Charlotte Harbor domain, and whether larger architectures (ViT-Large/Huge) or longer autoregressive rollouts would meaningfully reduce the error accumulation observed at 48–72 h lead times.
