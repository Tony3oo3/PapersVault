## Problem

Prior theoretical work on linear reinforcement learning has focused almost exclusively on single-objective settings, leaving multi-objective RL (MORL) without provable regret guarantees. In the lexicographic reward structure—where objectives are optimized hierarchically—the Bellman optimality equation fails, invalidating standard greedy action-selection strategies like LSVI-UCB. This paper provides the first MORL algorithm with rigorous regret bounds in the linear MOMDP setting.

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment states x and step indices h, mapped through a feature function φ: S×A → R^d with ||φ(x,a)|| ≤ 1, and produces an action a at each step. The output is a lexicographically optimal policy over m hierarchically ordered objectives, with performance characterized by per-objective regret bounds that scale with the trade-off parameter λ via the factor Λ^i(λ) = 1 + λ + ... + λ^{i-1}. The algorithm operates online over K episodes of H steps, updating Q-function estimates via least-squares regression after each episode.

## Method

LLRL replaces direct Q-maximization with a multi-stage action refinement process using geometrically decaying confidence thresholds 2^{-s}. At each stage, the Lexicographic Action Elimination (LAE) subroutine sequentially eliminates suboptimal actions by objective priority, retaining actions whose estimated Q-values fall within a confidence band scaled by (2 + 4λ + ... + 4λ^{i-1})·β_k·C. This scaling is specifically designed to guarantee that the confidence interval of the lexicographically optimal action overlaps with that of the most promising action at each objective level, preventing its premature elimination. Policy updates follow a standard LSVI-style backward pass: the covariance matrix U_h is updated with observed feature vectors, surrogate rewards combine immediate rewards with bootstrapped Q-values, and parameters are estimated via ridge regression. The key theoretical insight is that all objectives share a common confidence term β_k||φ(x,a)||_{U_k,h}, and the geometric tightening of thresholds across stages drives the value function gap to zero, yielding the regret bound Õ(Λ^i(λ)·√(d²H⁴K)).

## Results

The paper establishes the first provable regret guarantee for multi-objective linear RL with lexicographic rewards. The proposed LLRL algorithm achieves a regret bound of Õ(Λ^i(λ)·√(d²H⁴K)) for any objective i ∈ [m], where Λ^i(λ) = 1 + λ + … + λ^(i-1) quantifies the trade-off among conflicting objectives. In the misspecified setting, the bound extends to Õ(Λ^i(λ)·(√(d²H⁴K) + εdH²K)). For the highest-priority objective (i=1), Λ^1(λ)=1, recovering a d² dependence that matches the near-optimal single-objective bound of He et al. (2023) in terms of d and K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is purely theoretical and compares regret bounds rather than empirical performance. Against Jin et al. (2020), LLRL improves the feature-dimension dependence from d³ to d² (for i=1) while simultaneously handling m objectives, at the cost of a multiplicative Λ^i(λ) factor for lower-priority objectives. Against Zanette et al. (2020), the d-dependence improves from d⁴ to d². Against He et al. (2023), LLRL matches the d² dependence for the first objective but has a slightly worse H-dependence (H⁴ vs. H³); the authors note this gap stems from using a simpler policy update and anticipate closing it by incorporating the LSVI-UCB++ framework. In the misspecified setting, LLRL matches the εdH²K approximation-error term of Jin et al. (2020) while improving the d-dependence. Overall, LLRL is the first algorithm to provide multi-objective regret guarantees in the linear MDP setting, trading a modest H-dependence penalty for the ability to handle hierarchical objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: single-objective linear RL (Jin et al. 2020, He et al. 2023) and lexicographic multi-objective RL (Skalse et al. 2022, Gabor et al. 1998). While prior linear RL work provides regret guarantees via LSVI-UCB-style algorithms, it is restricted to a single reward function; prior lexicographic MORL work is largely empirical without formal performance bounds. The key technical departure is that the Bellman optimality equation fails under lexicographic rewards (Proposition 2), invalidating the greedy argmax action selection that underpins LSVI-UCB and LSVI-UCB++. The paper replaces this with a multi-stage Lexicographic Action Elimination (LAE) procedure using geometrically decaying confidence thresholds, which is the core algorithmic novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable regret guarantees for MORL, filling a significant theoretical gap that has limited principled algorithm design in the multi-objective setting. The regret bound's dependence on the trade-off parameter λ through Λ^i(λ) = 1 + λ + … + λ^{i-1} gives a clean quantitative characterization of how much regret lower-priority objectives incur, which is practically informative for system designers. Key open questions include whether comparable bounds hold without Assumption 1 (which bounds inter-objective trade-offs), whether the H-dependence can be tightened to match the near-optimal O~(√(d²H³K)) of He et al. 2023, and whether the framework extends to non-lexicographic preference structures. The linear MDP assumption, while enabling tractable analysis, limits direct applicability to complex real-world environments.
