## Problem

Length generalization—the ability of LLMs to perform well on sequences longer than those seen during training—remains a fundamental challenge. Prior work has focused on input-side factors such as positional encodings and data structure, but the paper identifies a largely overlooked issue: the model's output distributions become misaligned across sequences of different lengths, and this output-space discrepancy is a primary driver of poor generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250555/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on text sequences of length l_train (e.g., 4k or 8k tokens) and produces next-token probability distributions over the vocabulary at each position. Unusually, the training procedure simultaneously processes two overlapping suffixes of a single longer sequence (of length l_train + l_extra), where the overlap region is used to compute the misalignment loss between the two output distributions. The regularization coefficient α (typically 0.1–0.3) balances the standard cross-entropy loss against the misalignment penalty.

## Method

The paper introduces a long-short misalignment metric L_misalign that measures divergence between model outputs on two suffixes of the same sequence with slightly different lengths, using symmetric cross-entropy (SCE) as the divergence measure. This metric is incorporated as a regularization term added to the standard cross-entropy training loss, encouraging the model to produce consistent output distributions regardless of input length. An efficient implementation requires only two forward passes per step by sampling an extra length l_extra and computing the misalignment loss over the overlapping token positions. Theoretical analysis (via linear attention models) shows that the generalization error is upper-bounded by a weighted sum of training loss and misalignment, with the misalignment coefficient growing as test length increases, explaining why this term becomes increasingly critical for long contexts. Empirically, the approach improves LongBench-E scores and reduces perplexity on 8k/16k validation sets across multiple base models (Llama2-7B) and adaptation strategies (CLEX, LongQLora, EABF), while also mitigating the 'lost-in-the-middle' phenomenon on BABILong.

## Results

On the classical length generalization setting (4k training, 8k+ testing) with CLEX adjustment on RedPajama-Book, adding 0.1·L_misalign to the standard cross-entropy loss improves LongBench-E from 24.7 to 26.6 (+1.9) and reduces 8k perplexity from 6.12 to 5.88 after 200 steps. On PG19 under the same setting, the improvement is larger: LongBench-E rises from 22.5 to 25.3 (+2.8). On BABILong (16K input), the method achieves a 2.2% absolute gain over baseline, and on the 50%-depth fact retrieval subtask, accuracy jumps from 30% to 38%, demonstrating mitigation of the 'lost-in-the-middle' phenomenon. The proposed L_misalign metric correlates with long-context performance (|r|=0.85) substantially more strongly than standard training loss (|r|=0.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing long-context extension methods (e.g., YARN, PI, EABF) as standalone alternatives; instead, it adds the proposed L_misalign regularization on top of existing positional-encoding adjustment strategies (CLEX, LongQLora, EABF) and compares against the same strategy trained with only standard cross-entropy loss. Across all settings, the +0.1·L_misalign variant consistently outperforms the baseline: on RedPajama-Book (4k training) it gains +1.9 LongBench-E points and −0.24 PPL at 200 steps; on PG19 it gains +2.8 LongBench-E points; with LongQLora (8k training) it gains +2.4 LongBench-E points; and on BABILong it gains 2.0–2.2% absolute accuracy at 8K–16K lengths. The +0.5·L_misalign variant shows degraded performance, confirming that over-regularization harms next-token prediction. Under matched computation time (Appendix F, Table 9), the gains persist, indicating the ~3–5% per-step overhead does not erode the benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant input-side focus (positional encodings, data structures, task design) in length generalization research by shifting attention to the model's output distribution. It builds on the symmetric cross-entropy loss (Wang et al., 2019) as a divergence measure, extends the autoregressive modeling theory of Zhang et al. (2024a) to derive generalization guarantees, and uses the linear attention framework of Ahn et al. (2024) for tractable theoretical analysis of synthetic tasks. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'long-short alignment'—consistency of output distributions across input lengths—as a previously overlooked bottleneck, demonstrated through the contrast between mean prediction (stable output support) and length prediction (shifting output support) in synthetic tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the long-short misalignment metric correlates more strongly with long-context performance (r=0.85) than training loss (r=0.62) offers a practical diagnostic tool and a new optimization target for long-context modeling. The proposed regularization term is computationally cheap (~3-5% overhead) and composes with existing long-context adaptation methods (CLEX, LongQLora, EABF), making it broadly applicable. However, limitations include evaluation on a single model family (Llama2-7B) in a fine-tuning setting, reliance on linear attention for theoretical guarantees, and modest absolute gains (1-3% on LongBench-E). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to pre-training from scratch, whether it helps at larger scales, and whether the linear attention analysis captures the behavior of full dot-product attention in practice.
