## Problem

Despite the empirical success of foundation models, there is no systematic characterization of what representations these models actually learn or why they transfer to tasks unrelated to their training objective. The paper addresses this by asking: given a learning paradigm, what is the precise statistical estimand that the encoder is targeting, and when is that target optimal for downstream tasks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252978/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data point X from an input space and a context variable A (labels, augmented views, graph neighbors, or pretrained features) specified through a joint distribution P+(x,a), and produces a d-dimensional embedding Φ(x) that spans the top-d singular functions of the induced expectation operator. A secondary output is the context usefulness score τ, computed solely from the top-d₀ singular values of the dual kernel, which requires no downstream task labels and can be estimated in O(m³) time from a random subset of m = Θ(d₀ log d₀) samples.

## Method

The paper establishes the contexture theory, proving that the optimal d-dimensional encoder for a context P+ recovers the span of the top-d left singular functions of the expectation operator T_{P+}: L²(P_A) → L²(P_X), which is equivalent to the top-d eigenfunctions of the dual kernel operator T_{k_X+} = T_{P+}T_{P+}*. The key unification is showing that supervised learning (via class-balanced MSE), self-supervised learning (via spectral contrastive or non-contrastive losses with orthonormality constraints), and graph-based manifold learning all provably learn this contexture. Optimality is characterized through a compatibility measure ρ(f, P+) that quantifies how well a downstream task f aligns with the context, with the worst-case approximation error over compatible tasks bounded by (s₁² − (1−ε)²)/(s₁² − s_{d+1}²). The paper further shows that once the model is large enough to approximate the top-d singular functions, further scaling yields diminishing returns, and proposes a task-agnostic metric τ_d combining the spectral gap and cumulative explained variance to evaluate context quality without downstream task knowledge.

## Results

On the abalone dataset, large neural networks (width 512, depth 3) achieve CCA alignment of up to 0.9 and mutual KNN alignment above 0.8 with the exact top-d eigenfunctions of T_{k_X^+}, confirming that scaling brings representations close to the contexture. The proposed task-agnostic context usefulness metric τ shows a median Pearson correlation of 0.587 and median distance correlation of 0.659 with actual encoder performance (err_{d*}) across 28 OpenML datasets and 4 context types (140 contexts total). The metric successfully tracks actual prediction error for weak and moderate association levels but fails to track error under strong association, where τ_d decreases monotonically with d while actual error increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not benchmark against competing representation learning methods; instead, it validates its proposed context usefulness metric τ against ground-truth encoder performance. The metric is evaluated across four context families (RBF kernels, KNN, RBF_mask, KNN_mask) with 35 association levels each, totaling 140 contexts per dataset. The metric performs well on most datasets (median Pearson 0.587, median distance correlation 0.659), with particularly strong correlations on pumadyn32nh (0.938), grid_stability (0.925), and kings_county (0.842). However, it fails on brazilian_houses (Pearson −0.290) and fifa (Pearson −0.349), where the correlation is negative, and on abalone (Pearson 0.028) where the linear correlation is near zero despite a moderate distance correlation of 0.470. The paper attributes these failures to the specific structure of the downstream task variance across eigenfunctions, noting that no task-agnostic metric can universally work for all contexts and tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the spectral analysis of self-supervised learning (HaoChen et al., 2021; Johnson et al., 2023) with supervised learning and manifold learning under a single 'contexture' framework, showing that all three paradigms learn the top singular functions of an expectation operator induced by a context variable. It extends the positive-pair kernel formalism from contrastive learning to a general setting where the context can be labels, augmented views, graph neighbors, or pretrained features. The paper also connects to the neural collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al., 2020) and the platonic representation hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024), providing a concrete mathematical object (the top-d singular functions) that scaling is hypothesized to converge toward, and arguing that this convergence explains diminishing returns in scaling laws.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication—that once a model is large enough to approximate the contexture, further scaling yields diminishing returns and improvement requires better contexts—reframes the scaling debate from 'bigger models' to 'better pretraining signals,' with direct consequences for how the field should invest compute. The proposed task-agnostic context evaluation metric τ offers a concrete tool for practitioners to select among pretraining strategies without downstream labels. Key open problems include handling distribution shift between pretraining and downstream tasks, incorporating architectural inductive biases (e.g., translation invariance in CNNs), and understanding how the edge-of-stability optimization dynamics (Cohen et al., 2021) affect whether trained models actually reach the theoretical minimizers.
