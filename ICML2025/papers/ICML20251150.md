## Problem

Most large-scale implementations of categorical temporal-difference learning use a KL divergence loss, yet prior convergence theory (Rowland et al., 2018) only covers the Cramér-distance-based variant, creating a significant theory-practice gap. This paper addresses whether KL-CTD converges, characterizes its asymptotic behaviour, and derives practical guidance for hyperparameter selection that is absent from the existing Cramér-based analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251150/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes individual MDP transitions (x, R, x') generated under a fixed policy and maintains per-state logit vectors φ(x) ∈ ℝ^m that are mapped through softmax to categorical probabilities over m fixed support locations z₁,…,z_m. Continuous returns are projected onto the discrete support via a two-hot (Cramér) projection before entering the KL gradient. The final value estimate at each state is the inner product of the categorical probabilities with the support vector z, so the output is simultaneously a full return distribution and a scalar value.

## Method

The paper first shows that the expected KL-CTD update is linear in probability space and equals (T^π − I)p^φ, connecting it directly to the categorical distributional Bellman operator, whose unique fixed point gives exact mean estimates at convergence. Because the unpreconditioned KL objective is not a Lyapunov function for these dynamics, the authors introduce PKL-CTD, which preconditions the gradient with C^T C (where C is the lower-triangular matrix encoding the Cramér norm), and prove almost-sure convergence via a weighted-KL Lyapunov function combined with the Robbins-Siegmund theorem. In the asymptotic regime, they derive closed-form limiting variances for categorical Monte Carlo and a Lyapunov-equation characterisation for PKL-CTD, showing that the categorical projection adds at most (b−a)²/(4(m−1)²) variance and that the optimal learning rate should scale as √m to transfer across different numbers of atoms.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves almost-sure convergence of the preconditioned KL-CTD (PKL-CTD) algorithm under Robbins-Munro step sizes (Theorem 5.5), and derives exact asymptotic variance expressions for CMC, PCMC, TD, and PKL-CTD value estimates. Empirically, on a motivating 2-state MDP, KL-CTD achieves substantially lower value MSE than both TD and Cramer-CTD (Figure 2). In the tabular experimental suite (Figure 7), PKL-CTD performs competitively across all non-deterministic environments (Garnet, Dirichlet transitions with Gaussian/t₂ rewards), while TD with a large learning rate remains best in the fully deterministic Cycle environment. The paper also validates that scaling the learning rate by √m transfers optimal hyperparameters across different numbers of categorical atoms (Figure 6), and that the asymptotic variance predictions match empirical histograms after ~10⁴ iterations (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical TD learning, KL-CTD and PKL-CTD show clear advantages in stochastic environments: in the tabular suite (Figure 7), categorical methods achieve lower value MSE in Garnet and Dirichlet environments with noisy rewards, while TD with a large learning rate dominates in the deterministic Cycle environment. Against Cramer-CTD (Rowland et al., 2018), the paper demonstrates that KL-CTD exhibits fundamentally different learning dynamics — the two do not overlap as TD and Cramer-CTD do (confirmed in Figure 2, where TD and Cramer-CTD curves are perfectly overlapped per Lyle et al., 2019), and KL-CTD can achieve lower value MSE on certain MDPs. The key practical finding is that PKL-CTD (the paper's preconditioned variant) performs well across all non-deterministic environments, suggesting the convergence-motivated preconditioning provides robustness that vanilla KL-CTD lacks. The asymptotic variance analysis (Propositions 6.1, 6.4, 6.5) shows that CMC's limiting variance is strictly smaller than MC's by a factor involving J(p̃)², while the Cramér projection adds at most (b−a)²/(4(m−1)²) to the variance, giving practitioners concrete guidance on support size and atom count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper closes a theory-practice gap in categorical distributional RL: while Bellemare et al. (2017) introduced the KL-loss CTD update that underpins large-scale agents like Rainbow, prior convergence theory (Rowland et al., 2018; Boeck & Heitzinger, 2022) analyzed only the Cramér-distance variant, which Lyle et al. (2019) showed is equivalent to classical TD in the tabular setting. The paper departs from this lineage by studying the KL-loss variant directly, revealing that it is not a stochastic gradient descent method and that KL divergence is not a Lyapunov function for its dynamics. The key methodological contribution—preconditioning with the C^T C matrix drawn from the Cramér-distance contraction analysis—bridges the two theoretical frameworks and yields a convergent algorithm (PKL-CTD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence guarantee for PKL-CTD and the asymptotic variance comparisons between TD, CMC, and PKL-CTD provide the first rigorous theoretical grounding for the KL-loss categorical algorithms that are the workhorse of modern distributional RL. Practical insights—particularly the sqrt(m) learning-rate scaling and the finding that KL-CTD outperforms TD in high-reward-noise environments—give practitioners actionable guidance. Open questions include finite-sample analysis, extension to function approximation (where the tabular contraction structure is lost), and whether the preconditioning benefit persists in the deep RL regime where the C-matrix structure is not directly available.
