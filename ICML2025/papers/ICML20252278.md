## Problem

Decoding directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) in a principled manner remains challenging because nodes admit exponentially many topological orders, causing existing autoregressive and sequential-decoding methods to rely on arbitrary orderings that break one-to-one correspondence, determinism, and validity guarantees. While graph grammars offer formal language structure, their decoding capability lags far behind modern sequence models due to the absence of a rigorous, faithful mapping between the space of DAGs and the space of sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of node-labeled DAGs (with optional edge labels) and induces a context-free edNCE grammar from them, producing for each DAG a unique, position-free sequence of production-rule tokens (each token encoding a subgraph plus neighborhood connection instructions). For generation and optimization, a latent vector is decoded autoregressively into a rule sequence, which is then expanded into a DAG via deterministic, stateless rule application. The sequential representation is lossless: concatenating the decoded rules exactly reconstructs the input graph, and the grammar's context-free nature means each rule applies independently without tracking intermediate graph state.

## Method

DIGGED induces a grammar through iterative lossless compression guided by Minimum Description Length: it mines frequent subgraphs (via Subdue), solves a maximum-clique problem to find the largest set of motif occurrences compatible with a shared instruction set, selects the rule maximizing compression gain, and finally disambiguates via a hitting-set formulation to ensure each DAG has exactly one derivation. The resulting grammar is context-free with linear parse trees, guaranteeing one-to-one, onto, deterministic, valid, and stateless mapping. Within a VAE framework, a DAGNN GNN encoder (or Transformer encoder) maps the DAG to a latent space, while a causal-attention Transformer decoder autoregressively generates rule tokens; constrained decoding enforces domain-specific validity predicates at each step (e.g., op-amp stability constraints in circuits). The key differentiator is that the sequential representation is a learned, data-driven 'change-of-basis' rather than an arbitrary node ordering, making it inherently compositional and amenable to modern sequence-modeling techniques.

## Results

On the CKT circuit design benchmark, DIGGED (GNN) achieves 100% valid DAGs and 100% valid circuits, with a Bayesian Optimization FoM of 310.26, nearly 10× the next-best baseline CktGNN (33.44). On ENAS and BN, DIGGED achieves 100% validity and ~98–99% uniqueness, exceeding the second-best methods (DeepGMG at 46.37% and 57.27%) by over 50 percentage points. For predictive performance on CKT, DIGGED (GNN) achieves 0.627 RMSE and 0.787 Pearson r on FoM, a 26.5% RMSE reduction and 60% Pearson r improvement over CktGNN (0.854 RMSE, 0.491 r).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIGGED decisively outperforms all autoregressive (AG) and sequential decoding (SD) baselines on validity and uniqueness. On CKT, DIGGED (GNN) achieves 100% valid DAGs and circuits versus 80–99% for all baselines, and its BO FoM of 310.26 exceeds CktGNN (33.44) by nearly 10×, even surpassing the best design in CktBench301 (197.23). On ENAS and BN, DIGGED's ~98–99% uniqueness dwarfs the 27–57% achieved by D-VAE, S-VAE, GraphRNN, GCN, and DeepGMG, directly reflecting its one-to-one grammar guarantee. On CKT predictive performance, DIGGED (GNN) beats CktGNN by 26.5% in RMSE (0.627 vs. 0.854) and 60% in Pearson r (0.787 vs. 0.491) on FoM. The one area where DIGGED trails is novelty on CKT (78.80% vs. CktGNN's 92.29%), and on ENAS predictive accuracy it slightly underperforms DAGNN (0.912 vs. 0.882 RMSE), which the authors attribute to the large rule vocabulary (7504 rules) making dictionary learning more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIGGED bridges two previously disconnected research threads: formal graph grammars (edNCE, node-replacement grammars) and modern sequence-based generative modeling (Transformers, VAEs). Prior graph generative models (D-VAE, GraphRNN, CktGNN) decode graphs autoregressively using arbitrary node orderings, while graph grammar work (Jin et al., Guo et al., Sun et al.) demonstrated the value of substructure-based abstractions but lacked a rigorous, one-to-one mapping to sequences. This paper departs from both by using MDL-driven grammar induction (building on Subdue's frequent subgraph mining) to produce a deterministic, lossless sequential representation, then applying standard Transformer autoencoder training on top. The disambiguation step, formulated as a hitting-set problem, is a novel algorithmic contribution that addresses the undecidability of grammar ambiguity in a data-driven way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-to-one, deterministic, and valid guarantees mean that graph generation reduces cleanly to sequence generation, opening the door to directly importing NLP techniques (pretraining, scaling laws, constrained decoding) into structured data domains. The 100% validity at both syntax and semantic levels for circuit design, combined with novel designs exceeding CktBench301's best, demonstrates practical value for EDA. However, the method's reliance on multiple NP-hard subproblems (subgraph isomorphism, max clique, hitting set) limits scalability, and the finding that a GNN encoder outperforms the Transformer encoder suggests the sequence-learning narrative is not yet fully realized. Open questions include whether proper encoder pretraining (e.g., masked language modeling over rule sequences) can close this gap, and how the approach extends to variable-size or cyclic graphs.
