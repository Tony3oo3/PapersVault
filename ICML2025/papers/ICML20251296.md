## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks in cooperative MARL target only a single agent per attack, ignoring the interdependencies among agents that respond to protect the targeted one. This leaves robust policies trained under such attacks vulnerable to coordinated multi-agent disruptions. The paper asks whether a coordinated attack that sequentially targets an initial agent and then the agents adjusting their behavior to defend it can be significantly more destructive, and how to train policies robust against such coordinated threats.

## Input / Output

The Wolfpack attacker consumes the global state s_t, the joint action a_t, and the remaining attack budget k_t to produce a modified joint action ã_t that minimizes Q^tot for targeted agents. The follow-up agent selection operates on individual Q-value vectors Q^j(τ^j, ·) before and after the initial attack, transforming them via Softmax and computing KL divergences to pick the m agents with the largest policy shifts. The planner Transformer ingests replay-buffer trajectories (states, observations, actions) and outputs predicted Q-value reductions ΔQ^WP over a 20-step horizon, which are converted into attack-step probabilities via a temperature-scaled softmax.

## Method

The Wolfpack adversarial attack is a two-phase coordinated strategy: an initial attack on a randomly chosen agent i (minimizing Q^tot over its action space), followed by t_WP steps of attacks on a follow-up group of m agents selected by maximizing the sum of KL divergences between Softmax(Q^j) and Softmax(Q̃^j) after the initial attack's TD update. A Transformer-based planner predicts future Q-value reductions over a 20-step window and uses a temperature T to balance targeting critical steps versus maintaining diversity in attack timing. The WALL defense framework trains robust CTDE policies (QMIX, VDN, or QPLEX) by performing standard TD learning on the LPA-Dec-POMDP induced by the Wolfpack attacker, with the planner Transformer and Q-difference Transformer trained separately via MSE losses on replay data. The key insight is that attacking agents whose policies shift most in response to the initial disruption prevents the cooperative 'backup' behavior that single-agent attacks inadvertently train, yielding a 59.3% win-rate drop for Vanilla QMIX in SMAC versus 29.1% for EGA.

## Results

On SMAC (6 scenarios), the Wolfpack adversarial attack reduces Vanilla QMIX mean win rate from 98.7% (natural) to 39.4%, a 59.3% drop, far exceeding EGA (29.1% drop) and Random Attack (22.4% drop for RANDOM-trained policies). The proposed WALL framework achieves 93.4% mean win rate under the Wolfpack attack, outperforming the next-best baseline ROMANCE (59.1%) by 34.3 percentage points. On MPE (3 predator-prey scenarios), the Wolfpack attack drops Vanilla QMIX mean cumulative reward from 455.4 to 367.6, while WALL maintains 489.5. Under natural (no-attack) conditions, WALL also achieves the highest performance (99.4% SMAC mean win rate; 560.1 MPE mean reward), indicating superior policy quality even without adversarial pressure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WALL dominates all six baselines across every attack type and both benchmark suites. Under the Wolfpack attack on SMAC, WALL (93.4% mean win rate) outperforms ROMANCE (59.1%), RAP (58.5%), RANDOM (63.5%), RARL (52.5%), and ERNIE (50.5%) by 24–43 percentage points, with Vanilla QMIX collapsing to 39.4%. Under EGA attacks, WALL (93.6%) still leads ROMANCE (87.5%) by 6.1 points and RAP (81.5%) by 12.1 points. Under Random Attack, WALL (95.9%) beats RANDOM (89.4%) and ROMANCE (87.8%) by 6.5 and 8.1 points respectively. Notably, WALL also achieves the best natural-condition performance (99.4% vs. RANDOM's 99.3% and ERNIE's 99.1%), showing that adversarial training against the coordinated Wolfpack attack does not sacrifice clean performance. The same pattern holds for VDN and QPLEX backbones (Appendix E.1), where WALL achieves 91.0% and 93.4% mean win rates under Wolfpack attack versus 57.9% (ROMANCE/VDN) and 55.2% (ROMANCE/QPLEX).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LPA-Dec-POMDP adversarial MARL framework introduced by Yuan et al. (ROMANCE/EGA, AAAI 2023), which formalized single-agent-targeted adversarial attacks in cooperative settings. The key conceptual departure is shifting from independent single-agent attacks to a *coordinated* multi-agent attack (Wolfpack) that sequentially targets an initial agent and then the follow-up agents that respond to assist it, using KL-divergence on Q-value distributions for follow-up selection and a Transformer-based planner for critical step identification. The defense (WALL) is built atop standard CTDE value-decomposition algorithms (QMIX, VDN, QPLEX), making it a drop-in robustness layer rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Wolfpack attack exposes a structural vulnerability in cooperative MARL that single-agent attacks cannot: by disrupting the *response* mechanism (agents that heal, guard, or reposition), it breaks the coordination loop that robust policies rely on for recovery. WALL's ability to outperform attack-specific baselines (RANDOM, ROMANCE) under *all* attack types suggests that training against coordinated attacks induces a more general form of robustness. Limitations include the ~30% additional training cost from the Transformer planner, sensitivity to hyperparameters (m, T, K_WP), and evaluation restricted to discrete-action, fully cooperative settings—open questions include scalability to continuous action spaces, partially cooperative (mixed-motive) settings, and real-world deployment where attack budgets are unbounded.
