## Problem

Existing CLIP-based continual learning methods (prompt-based approaches and MoE-Adapters) partition parameters across tasks, requiring an auxiliary parameter-selection step at inference to determine which prompts or adapters to activate for a given image. This selection is error-prone, leading to misassignments that degrade classification accuracy. Additionally, these methods struggle with forward forgetting—degradation of pre-trained zero-shot knowledge on unseen tasks—because fine-tuning shifts the shared representation space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder to produce a fixed-dimensional embedding. At inference, the model must classify the image among all seen and unseen classes without any task-identity hint (X-TAIL setting). The final prediction for seen classes is a linear combination of LADA logits and fine-tuned text-feature logits, while unseen classes rely solely on vanilla CLIP text features, making the output a single class label from the union of all encountered and unencountered classes.

## Method

LADA appends a compact set of learnable label-specific memory vectors (initialized via k-means clustering of per-class CLIP features) after the frozen image encoder, generating label-specific features through inner products between the CLIP embedding and each class's memory matrix. A fixed nearest-neighbor-style classifier converts these features into logits, and during training only the current task's memory vectors are updated while all previous ones remain frozen, preventing backward forgetting. Distribution-preserved training fits a Gaussian Mixture Model to each previous class's features and augments prototypes with scaled Gaussian noise to better preserve the original feature distribution and mitigate forward forgetting. The text encoder is simultaneously fine-tuned via AdaptFormer to produce optimized text-feature classifiers, and at inference the final logits for seen classes are a linear weighting of LADA and text-feature logits, eliminating any need for parameter selection or an external zero-shot CLIP selector.

## Results

On the X-TAIL 16-shot benchmark (10 datasets, 1,100 classes), LADA achieves 61.5% Transfer, 72.7% Average, and 83.1% Last accuracy, surpassing the previous best method (Dual-RAIL) by 2.5%, 1.4%, and 0.8% respectively. In the more challenging full-shot setting, LADA achieves 61.9% Transfer, 75.2% Average, and 86.9% Last accuracy, outperforming Primal-RAIL by 2.9%, 2.4%, and 2.9%. Notably, LADA exceeds vanilla zero-shot CLIP (57.7% average) on the Transfer metric for several tasks (Flowers, Food, SUN397), demonstrating that it not only mitigates but actively reverses forward forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LADA outperforms all five baselines across both 16-shot and full-shot settings on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method, Dual-RAIL (Xu et al., 2024), LADA gains 1.4% in Average (72.7 vs 71.3) and 0.8% in Last (83.1 vs 82.3) in 16-shot; in full-shot, LADA beats Primal-RAIL by 2.4% Average (75.2 vs 72.8) and 2.9% Last (86.9 vs 84.0). Against MoE-Adapters (Yu et al., 2024), which also uses parameter partitioning, LADA improves Average by 9.7% (72.7 vs 63.0) and Last by 12.6% (83.1 vs 70.5) in 16-shot, highlighting the cost of the parameter-selection step. Against ZSCL (Zheng et al., 2023), which distills from vanilla CLIP to reduce forward forgetting, LADA gains 12.7% Average and 19.7% Last in 16-shot, and 10.7% Average and 14.8% Last in full-shot. The older baselines LwF and WiSE-FT are substantially outperformed (by 19–21% in Average), confirming that regularization-only and weight-interpolation approaches are insufficient for the cross-domain X-TAIL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LADA departs from the dominant CLIP continual learning paradigm of partitioning learnable parameters (prompts or adapters) across tasks, which necessitates an auxiliary selection step at inference. Instead, it repurposes the idea of class prototypes—previously used only for replay or regularization—into a set of label-specific memory vectors appended after the frozen image encoder, generating discriminative features via inner products. The text-encoder fine-tuning backbone (using AdaptFormer) and the Gaussian Mixture Model for distribution-preserved distillation are borrowed from prior continual learning and prototype-based methods, but their combination with the label-specific adapter is novel. The work is evaluated on the X-TAIL benchmark (Xu et al., 2024), positioning it within the cross-domain task-agnostic incremental learning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the parameter-selection step that causes misassignment errors in prompt-based and MoE-adapter methods, LADA offers a cleaner and more scalable architecture for continual CLIP adaptation, with only a small number of new vectors added per class and no gradient flow through the backbone. The observation that Transfer accuracy on some tasks (Flowers, Food, SUN397) exceeds vanilla zero-shot CLIP suggests continual learning can actively improve generalization rather than merely preserve it, though this finding is limited to the X-TAIL benchmark and ViT-B/16. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of tasks or larger backbones, whether the k-means initialization remains effective with very few samples per class, and whether the method extends to non-classification tasks such as detection or segmentation.
