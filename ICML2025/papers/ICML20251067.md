## Problem

Offline RL policies are typically fixed after training and rely on conservatism to avoid high epistemic uncertainty, limiting their adaptivity and generalization to out-of-distribution states or shifted dynamics. Existing model-based planning methods (e.g., LOOP) address uncertainty only heuristically via ensemble variance penalties, without explicitly modeling the agent's epistemic uncertainty over environment dynamics. The paper asks: how can one effectively incorporate epistemic uncertainty into test-time MB planning to enhance any offline-learned policy?

## Input / Output

At each test-time step, RefPlan consumes the agent's accumulated state-action-reward history τ_{:t} (a sequence of continuous state vectors, action vectors, and scalar rewards), a pretrained prior policy π_p (from any offline RL algorithm such as CQL, MOPO, or MAPLE), a pretrained VAE dynamics model (GRU encoder q_φ and 20-model ensemble decoder p̂_ψ), and a value function V_φ for long-horizon bootstrapping. It outputs the posterior mean over the optimal H-step action sequence, computed by importance-weighted sampling of N̄ trajectories under n̄ latent belief samples, with the first action executed in the environment before re-planning at the next step.

## Method

RefPlan unifies epistemic uncertainty modeling and MB planning in a single doubly Bayesian framework inspired by control-as-inference. The 'Reflect' stage uses a VAE (GRU encoder + ensemble decoder) trained via ELBO to infer a posterior belief m_t ~ N(μ_t, σ_t²) over environment dynamics from real-time observations, with a two-stage training procedure: first VAE training via ELBO, then freezing the encoder and fine-tuning the decoder via MLE for accurate next-state prediction. The 'Plan' stage recasts trajectory optimization as posterior inference p(τ|O), where the offline-trained policy π_p serves as the action prior and the learned model p̂_ψ provides state transitions; the posterior expectation over optimal actions is estimated by importance sampling with softmax-weighted returns. Crucially, RefPlan marginalizes over the latent belief m_t via Monte Carlo (n̄ samples), so the final plan averages over diverse plausible dynamics scenarios rather than committing to a single model, directly addressing epistemic uncertainty in a principled Bayesian manner.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d × 5 dataset configs), RefPlan outperforms LOOP in 10/15 tasks with CQL, 7/15 with EDAC, 12/15 with MOPO, 9/15 with COMBO, and 12/15 with MAPLE as prior policies. On average, RefPlan improves prior policy performance by 11.6% versus LOOP's 5.3%. Under OOD initialization (trained on ME, tested from R states), RefPlan achieves the largest gains in Walker2d: +42.5% for CQL (65.0→92.6), +31.4% for MAPLE (66.8→87.8), and +16.4% for COMBO (62.5→72.8). RLiable analysis confirms statistically significant improvements over LOOP with non-overlapping confidence intervals across all four aggregate metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOOP (Sikchi et al., 2021) is the primary competing MB planning method; RefPlan outperforms it in the majority of tasks across all five prior policies, with an average improvement of 11.6% vs. LOOP's 5.3% over the original prior policy. The gap is most pronounced for MOPO (12/15 tasks won) and MAPLE (12/15), where LOOP's KL-regularized optimization without explicit epistemic uncertainty modeling struggles under high uncertainty. For CQL, RefPlan wins 10/15 tasks with notable gains in Hopper-M (66.9→85.1 vs. LOOP's 73.9) and Walker2d-MR (63.5→93.6 vs. LOOP's 81.9). EDAC shows the smallest differential (7/15), as EDAC's value ensemble already provides some uncertainty awareness, leaving less room for RefPlan's Bayesian marginalization to add value. A controlled experiment matching LOOP's sampling budget to RefPlan's maximum (16×) confirms that RefPlan's advantage is not merely computational but stems from its explicit epistemic uncertainty modeling. Under OOD initialization (Table 1), RefPlan consistently reduces performance degradation compared to both the raw prior and LOOP, with the largest relative gains in Walker2d (up to +42.5% for CQL). Under changing dynamics (Table 4), RefPlan achieves the best returns across all five HalfCheetah variations, with the largest margin in the steep slope task (6238 vs. LOOP's 3245 vs. MAPLE's 2123).

## Relation to Prior Work

RefPlan synthesizes three established threads: the control-as-inference framework (Levine, 2018) for recasting planning as posterior estimation, VariBAD-style variational inference (Zintgraf et al., 2020; Dorfman et al., 2021) for approximating a belief over environment dynamics, and the epistemic POMDP view of offline RL (Ghosh et al., 2021, 2022). Its key departure from LOOP (Sikchi et al., 2021) is replacing the heuristic ensemble-variance penalty with an explicit latent-variable model of epistemic uncertainty that is marginalized out during planning, and its departure from VariBAD is the two-stage training procedure that retains the decoder for test-time planning rather than discarding it. The authors emphasize that the novelty lies in the unification of these components into a single probabilistic framework where the offline-trained policy serves as an action prior in the control-as-inference PGM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefPlan offers a plug-and-play test-time planning module that can enhance any offline RL policy, providing a principled mechanism for handling the epistemic uncertainty that plagues offline settings with limited data coverage. The consistent improvements across five prior policies, three environments, and five dataset configurations, validated with RLiable statistical tests, make it a practical tool for deployment scenarios where OOD states or dynamics shifts are expected. However, the evaluation is confined to MuJoCo locomotion tasks, performance still degrades notably under certain dynamics changes (hill, gentle slope), and the computational cost of marginalizing over latent samples at every step limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or partially observable settings, and whether the two-stage VAE training can be made more sample-efficient.
