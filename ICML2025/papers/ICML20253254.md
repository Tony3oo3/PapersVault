## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) suffer from visual hallucinations because the autoregressive text decoder progressively loses reliance on visual tokens as generation proceeds, creating a modality imbalance akin to 'amnesia' about the input image. Existing mitigation strategies—contrastive decoding (VCD, ICD) and attention intervention (OPERA)—either require multi-round inference that doubles latency, introduce noise into output distributions, or degrade general-purpose performance, leaving a gap for a single-pass, training-free method that improves both hallucination fidelity and general capability simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input, processed through the MLLM's existing vision encoder and MLP/Q-Former to produce dimension-aligned visual tokens z_v ∈ R^{d×N_v} (e.g., 256 tokens for ViT-L/14) that are concatenated with the text embedding. During autoregressive decoding, the method operates entirely within the text decoder's FFN layers, modifying hidden states in-place without altering the input pipeline or requiring any additional forward passes. The output is a standard autoregressive textual response, with the only change being that at triggered layers the FFN output is a convex blend of the original FFN computation and a visual re-retrieval term, controlled by an injection ratio α ∈ [0,1].

## Method

MemVR reformulates each FFN layer as a key-value memory (following Geva et al., 2021) and introduces Visual Retracing (VR): when the entropy-based uncertainty of the next-token prediction at a candidate layer exceeds a threshold γ (default 0.75), the hidden state x is used as a query to retrieve visual evidence from the pre-computed visual tokens via a simple inner-product retrieval Δ(z_v|x) = Σ φ(⟨x, z_{v,i}⟩)·z_{v,i}, and the FFN output is replaced by αΔ + (1−α)FFN^(l)(x). This 'look-twice' mechanism is triggered at most once per decoding step (dynamic strategy selects the first layer exceeding γ; a static variant fixes the layer via validation), requiring no extra parameters, no multi-round inference, and negligible compute since N_v ≪ D (e.g., 256 vs. 11008 in LLaMA-7B). The theoretical analysis shows via the Data Processing Inequality that visual information in hidden states monotonically decreases with depth, and that re-injection increases mutual information I(x̂; z_v), reduces conditional entropy H(y|x̂), and optimizes the Information Bottleneck objective, providing a principled guarantee that the mechanism suppresses hallucination while preserving general capability.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, MemVR achieves an average accuracy of 86.93% on MSCOCO (+7.1% over LLaVA-1.5-7B) and 86.21% on A-OKVQA (+7.1%), substantially outperforming all baselines. On CHAIR, MemVR reduces CHAIR_S from 50.0 to 46.6 (−6.8%) and CHAIR_I from 15.4 to 13.0 (−15.6%). On the MME general benchmark, MemVR increases the total score by +32.0 points (1864.6→1896.7) while simultaneously improving hallucination sub-scores. Inference latency is only 68.32 ms/token (×1.04 over greedy), compared to 144.62 ms/token for VCD (×2.20) and 240.59 ms/token for OPERA (×3.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemVR consistently outperforms all three baselines across every hallucination and general benchmark. Against OPERA (attention intervention), MemVR gains +2.7% average POPE accuracy on MSCOCO (86.93 vs 84.21), improves CHAIR average by 1.4 points (29.8 vs 31.2), and boosts MME total score by +112 points (1896.7 vs 1784.3), while running 3.5× faster (68.32 vs 240.59 ms/token). Against VCD (visual contrastive decoding), MemVR leads by +4.7% POPE accuracy on MSCOCO (86.93 vs 82.27), +2.0 on CHAIR average (29.8 vs 31.8), and +1.9 on MME (1896.7 vs 1872.9), at roughly half the inference latency (68.32 vs 144.62 ms/token). Against ICD (instruction contrastive decoding), the margins are largest: MemVR achieves +4.0% POPE accuracy on MSCOCO (86.93 vs 82.96), +6.5 on CHAIR average (29.8 vs 36.3), and +302 points on MME (1896.7 vs 1594.8), with ICD actually degrading LLaVA-Bench from 64.8 to 56.9 while MemVR improves it to 65.2. Notably, MemVR is the only method that simultaneously improves both hallucination metrics and general-purpose benchmarks, whereas VCD and ICD degrade general performance (e.g., MM-Vet drops from 31.1 to 30.2 and 25.9 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Contrastive Decoding (CD) paradigm (VCD, ICD, HALC, VORD) for hallucination mitigation in MLLMs, arguing that CD's reliance on perturbed inputs and multi-round logit comparison introduces noise and doubles inference cost. Instead, it repurposes the FFN-as-key-value-memory reformulation (Geva et al., 2021; Jie et al., 2024) to treat visual tokens as supplementary evidence re-injected at a single middle layer when entropy-based uncertainty exceeds a threshold, drawing on the Data Processing Inequality and Information Bottleneck framework for theoretical grounding. The approach also builds on the observation from attention-intervention methods (OPERA, PAI) that deeper layers progressively lose visual information, but addresses it through hidden-state modification rather than attention matrix manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MemVR offers a plug-and-play, training-free decoding strategy with near-zero overhead (1.04× latency) that simultaneously improves hallucination metrics and general benchmarks — a combination that CD-based methods fail to achieve, as they often degrade general performance (e.g., ICD drops LLaVA-Bench by 7.9 points). The practical significance is high for deployment in safety-critical domains where inference latency is constrained. Open questions include the sensitivity of the threshold γ and injection ratio α to model architecture and data distribution, the limited failure analysis on celebrity/scene/landmark sub-tasks, and whether the framework extends cleanly to non-visual modalities (audio, spatial) as the authors speculate.
