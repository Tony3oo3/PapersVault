## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D panoptic segmentation methods (LCPS, PanopticFusionNet) suffer from two critical flaws: they apply data augmentation only to the LiDAR side, causing misalignment between LiDAR and image inputs that forces the model to rely predominantly on LiDAR; and they use convolution-based prediction heads followed by post-processing clustering rather than directly predicting panoptic results, limiting global context utilization and inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251903/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud of N points (each with 3D Cartesian coordinates and reflection intensity) and K multi-view RGB images resized to 640×360. The point cloud is discretized into cylindrical voxels of resolution [480×360×32] in polar coordinates, with the polar range set to [-50m, 50m]×[0, 2π]×[-5m, 3m] for nuScenes. The output is per-point panoptic segmentation: both a semantic class label and an instance ID for every LiDAR point, predicted directly by a transformer decoder without any post-processing clustering step.

## Method

IAL is a transformer-based framework with three core components. First, PieAug performs modality-synchronized augmentation by pairing each cylindrical voxel with its projected image bounding rectangle and applying instance pasting or scene swapping (along height or angle axes) simultaneously to both modalities, generalizing prior LiDAR-only augmentation techniques. Second, the Geometric-guided Token Fusion (GTF) module aligns features by projecting all physical points within each voxel to the image plane and averaging their 2D features, then fusing with a Scale-aware Position Embedding (SPE) that encodes both the voxel centroid (via mixed Cartesian/polar encoding) and its scale (via L2 norms of eight extreme corner points), producing concatenated multi-modal tokens. Third, the Prior-based Query Generation (PQG) module initializes instance queries from three sources: geometric-prior queries from a LiDAR center heatmap with NMS sampling, texture-prior queries from GroundingDINO+SAM 2D masks lifted into 3D frustums and clustered via DBSCAN, and learnable no-prior queries for instances lacking reliable priors from either modality. The 3D encoder is Cylinder3D and the 2D encoder is SwiftNet (ResNet-18); all tokens and queries are processed by a multi-layer transformer decoder that directly outputs instance masks and semantic labels.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, IAL (LiDAR+Camera) achieves 82.3% PQ, 84.7% PQ†, 89.7% RQ, and 91.5% SQ, outperforming the previous best multi-modal method LCPS (79.8% PQ) by 2.5% and Panoptic-FusionNet (77.2% PQ) by 5.1%. On the nuScenes test set, IAL achieves 82.0% PQ, ranking first on most metrics. On SemanticKITTI validation, IAL reaches 63.1% PQ, surpassing LCPS (59.0% PQ) by 4.1% despite having only two front-view cameras available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among multi-modal baselines, IAL outperforms LCPS by 2.5% PQ (82.3 vs. 79.8) and 3.0% PQ^th (85.3 vs. 82.3) on nuScenes val, and by 4.1% PQ (63.1 vs. 59.0) on SemanticKITTI val. IAL surpasses Panoptic-FusionNet by a larger margin of 5.1% PQ (82.3 vs. 77.2) on nuScenes val, with particularly strong gains in PQ^th (+7.8%) and mIoU (+7.2%). On the nuScenes test set, IAL (82.0% PQ) also beats 4DFormer (78.0% PQ) by 4.0%. Among LiDAR-only methods, IAL's LiDAR branch (77.0% PQ) edges out P3Former (75.9%) by 1.1% and CenterLPS (76.4%) by 0.6%, while the full multi-modal model gains an additional 5.3% over its own LiDAR branch, confirming the value of image assistance. The only baseline IAL's LiDAR branch does not match is LidarMultiNet (81.4% PQ on test set), which uses additional temporal frames and detection annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on P3Former's transformer-decoder architecture for LiDAR panoptic segmentation, extending it from a single-modality to a multi-modal setting. It departs from the two prior multi-modal panoptic segmentation methods (LCPS and PanopticFusionNet) by addressing their two core weaknesses: modality misalignment during data augmentation and reliance on post-processing clustering for instance prediction. The design draws conceptual inspiration from multi-modal 3D object detection (TransFusion, CMT) for the query-based transformer decoder, and generalizes existing LiDAR augmentation techniques (PolarMix, LaserMix) into a unified multi-modal framework (PieAug) that synchronizes point-cloud and image transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-modal 3D panoptic segmentation remains underexplored relative to detection and semantic segmentation, and this paper provides the first framework that simultaneously resolves augmentation misalignment and eliminates post-processing, making it a more principled baseline for the field. The PieAug strategy is positioned as a general multi-modal augmentation tool applicable beyond panoptic segmentation, and the PQG module's use of pre-trained 2D models (GroundingDINO, SAM) as texture priors is practical but also a stated limitation—task-specific query extraction could yield further gains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to indoor or non-driving scenes, whether the reliance on large pre-trained 2D models introduces fragility under distribution shift, and whether the transformer decoder's global context advantage scales to denser or longer-range point clouds.
