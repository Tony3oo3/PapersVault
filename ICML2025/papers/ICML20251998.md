## Problem

Self-supervised learning models exhibit strong OOD generalization but still rely on spurious correlations (e.g., background texture, style) that vary across environments, degrading performance on unseen tasks. The paper identifies that the causal relationship between the anchor sample and unobserved confounding variables changes across mini-batch tasks, making it impossible to eliminate spurious factors with a single unified causal criterion. This leaves SSL models unable to faithfully learn the true task distribution, limiting their worst-case OOD performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB image pairs produced by any D-SSL (e.g., SimCLR, BYOL) or G-SSL (e.g., MAE) augmentation pipeline, along with a pre-trained regularized latent variable model that encodes the joint distribution over the positive sample, anchor, and spurious latent variable. It outputs structured mini-batches of size a+1 where all pairs share a matching balancing score (propensity score over the latent variable), effectively enforcing the Post-Intervention Distribution constraint. The resulting SSL feature representations are then used unchanged for downstream linear probing, semi-supervised fine-tuning, object detection, or few-shot transfer.

## Method

The proposed approach is a two-stage mini-batch sampling strategy grounded in structural causal models. In stage one, a regularized latent variable model (a VAE with an exponential-family prior parameterized by task-specific sufficient statistics) is trained via a modified ELBO to recover the joint distribution p(x⁺, x^label, s) up to an equivalence relation, with identifiability guaranteed under mild conditions (Theorem 4.3). In stage two, a propensity score is computed from the learned conditional p(s|x^label) for each pair, and Algorithm 1 constructs mini-batches by greedily matching pairs with identical or closest balancing scores, thereby enforcing the Post-Intervention Distribution where the spurious variable s is independent of the anchor. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) proves that SSL models trained exclusively on PID-satisfying batches achieve minimax-optimal worst-case risk across all environments in the distribution set, unifying the explanation for both contrastive and generative SSL.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (linear probing, ResNet-50), the proposed PID sampling strategy improves all SSL backbones by 1.2–3.2% top-1 accuracy, with VICRegL+Ours reaching 78.20% and DINO+Ours reaching 77.47%, surpassing all SOTA baselines. On the full ImageNet benchmark (1000 epochs), SwAV+Ours achieves 77.42% top-1, the best result in the table. On few-shot learning (miniImageNet 5-way 1-shot), improvements exceed 5% (e.g., SwAV+Ours: 49.15% vs. SwAV: 44.39%), and on semi-supervised ImageNet (1% labels, 1000 epochs), BYOL+Ours gains 2.6% top-1 over BYOL (58.9% vs. 56.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed PID sampling strategy is evaluated as a plug-in module on nine SSL backbones (SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, SwAV, DINO, RELICv2, VICRegL, BYOL) and consistently improves every baseline across all four task categories. On unsupervised ImageNet-100 linear probing, gains range from +1.2% (SwAV: 75.78→76.99) to +3.2% (SimCLR: 70.15→73.32) top-1, with VICRegL+Ours (78.20%) and DINO+Ours (77.47%) setting new state-of-the-art. On semi-supervised ImageNet (1% labels, 1000 epochs), BYOL+Ours achieves 58.9% top-1 vs. BYOL's 56.3% (+2.6%), and MoCo+Ours reaches 71.2% at 10% labels vs. MoCo's 68.4% (+2.8%). On transfer learning (COCO detection), MoCo+Ours improves AP from 39.3 to 42.1 (+2.8) and SimSiam+Ours from 39.2 to 42.5 (+3.3). The largest relative gains appear in few-shot learning: on miniImageNet 5-way 1-shot, SwAV+Ours jumps from 44.39% to 49.15% (+4.76%) and SimCLR+Ours from 42.32% to 47.14% (+4.82%), confirming that PID-constrained batching is most beneficial under data-scarce OOD conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal analysis of SSL established by Zimmermann et al. (2021) and Von Kügelgen et al. (2021), who showed that SSL inverts the data generation process and that augmentations can isolate content from style, by reframing the OOD generalization problem through mini-batch construction as task distribution learning. It repurposes the propensity score and balancing score matching framework from causal inference (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1981) and Pearl's do-operator/backdoor adjustment (2009) to construct a novel Post-Intervention Distribution (PID) constraint on mini-batches, combined with a regularized VAE to model the spurious latent variable. Unlike prior OOD-SSL methods (Ni et al., 2021; Bai et al., 2023; Qiang et al., 2024) that rely on inductive biases without theoretical guarantees, this work provides a minimax optimality theorem (Theorem 3.4) linking PID-satisfying batches to optimal worst-case OOD performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PID-based sampling strategy offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for random mini-batch sampling that consistently improves OOD generalization by 2–5% across unsupervised, semi-supervised, transfer, and few-shot tasks, addressing a gap where prior causal-SSL work focused on in-distribution representation learning. However, the O(D²·n·k) complexity of the matching phase and the need to train an additional RLVM limit scalability to very large datasets, and the BYOL+Ours result at 200 epochs in Table 2 actually shows a performance *decrease* (46.5 vs. 54.8), raising questions about robustness under limited training. Open questions include whether the exponential family assumption (Assumption 4.1) and invertibility condition (Assumption 3.3) hold broadly, and whether the approach extends to generative SSL (MAE) with the same theoretical guarantees, since the paper acknowledges that backdoor adjustment alone cannot explain G-SSL improvements.
