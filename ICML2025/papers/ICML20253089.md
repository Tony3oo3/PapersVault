## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for multi-label learning rely on Lipschitz continuity of base loss functions, leaving the impact of smoothness (bounded second derivative) completely unexplored despite smooth losses like squared loss being widely used in practice. Additionally, the fastest achievable convergence rate via standard Rademacher complexity is O~(1/√n) with a √c dependency on the number of labels, and no general framework existed to eliminate this label-count dependency or improve the rate.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a general vector-valued function class F with bounded components (|f_j| ≤ B), a γ-smooth base loss function, and a dataset of n i.i.d. examples, and produces high-probability generalization bounds relating expected risk to empirical risk for Hamming, Subset, Ranking losses, and Macro-Averaged AUC. The bounds are expressed in terms of the worst-case Rademacher complexity of the scalar-valued projection function class P(F), which is tractable and independent of c up to logarithmic factors. For Macro-Averaged AUC, the bounds additionally depend on r₀ = n·min_j min{p_j, 1−p_j}, capturing the effect of label-wise class imbalance.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a novel vector-contraction inequality for smooth base losses: by applying the smoothness inequality (f(t)−f(r))² ≤ 6γ(f(t)+f(r))(t−r)², the ℓ₂ covering number of the loss function space is bounded by the ℓ∞ covering number of the projection function class P(F) at radius ε/√(12γM), which eliminates the √c factor inherent in ℓ₂-Lipschitz analysis. This covering-number relationship is then combined with fat-shattering dimension bounds and a refined Dudley entropy integral to yield O~(1/√n) generalization bounds with no dependency on c. For faster rates, the authors develop local vector-contraction inequalities for the local loss function space L^r (restricted to functions with small empirical risk) and exploit sub-root function fixed-point arguments to derive O~(1/n) bounds. For Macro-Averaged AUC, U-process techniques with disjoint positive-negative sample pairs are used under both ℓ∞-Lipschitz and smooth conditions to obtain O~(1/√r₀) bounds independent of c.

## Results

The paper derives tight generalization bounds for multi-label learning with smooth base losses that are independent of the number of labels c (up to logarithmic terms), achieving O~(1/√n) convergence via standard Rademacher complexity (Theorem 4.4) and O~(1/n) convergence via local Rademacher complexity (Theorem 5.5). For Macro-Averaged AUC, the paper obtains O~(1/√r₀) bounds with no dependency on c under both Lipschitz (Theorem 6.3) and smooth (Theorem 6.7) base loss conditions, where r₀ = n·min{minⱼ pⱼ, minⱼ(1−pⱼ)} captures class-imbalance. These results improve prior O~(√c/√n) bounds (Zhang & Zhang, 2024a) by a √c factor and improve O(c/√n) bounds (Wu & Zhu, 2020) by a c/√c factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical bounds strictly improve upon all prior multi-label generalization results. Compared to Wu & Zhu (2020) and Wu et al. (2021a), which yield O(c/√n) and O(√c/√n) bounds respectively for ℓ₂ Lipschitz losses, the new smooth-loss bounds eliminate the c-dependency entirely (up to log terms), a √c improvement over the best prior decoupled result. Compared to Zhang & Zhang (2024a), which achieves O~(√c/√n) for ℓ₂ Lipschitz losses, the paper identifies a missing √c factor in their proof and shows that smoothness of the base loss eliminates this factor, yielding O~(1/√n). For Macro-Averaged AUC, the O~(1/√r₀) bound improves the O~(√c/r₀) bound in Zhang & Zhang (2024a) by a √c factor, making the bound less likely to be vacuous under class-imbalance. The local Rademacher complexity analysis further accelerates the convergence rate from O~(1/√n) to O~(1/n), matching the rate of Xu et al. (2016) but without requiring trace-norm regularization or exponential singular-value decay assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vector-contraction inequality framework for multi-label learning generalization bounds, building directly on Maurer's (2016) typical vector-contraction inequality and the subsequent improvements by Wu & Zhu (2020), Wu et al. (2021a), and Zhang & Zhang (2024a) that progressively reduced the dependency on the number of labels c from linear to square-root for ℓ₂ Lipschitz losses. The key departure is exploiting the smoothness (bounded second derivative) of base loss functions—previously unexplored in this context—combined with the projection function class to eliminate the √c factor entirely, yielding bounds independent of c up to logarithmic terms. The paper also leverages the local Rademacher complexity framework of Bartlett et al. (2005) and the smoothness inequality of Srebro et al. (2010) to derive faster Õ(1/n) convergence rates, and identifies a technical gap in Zhang & Zhang (2024a) where a √c factor in the covering number radius was overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These results provide the first general theoretical guarantees for multi-label learning methods using smooth base losses (e.g., squared loss), which are widely employed in practice but previously lacked any generalization analysis. The Õ(1/n) rate via local Rademacher complexity is the fastest known for general multi-label learning without strong structural assumptions (e.g., exponential singular value decay or trace norm regularization), and the c-independent bounds for Macro-Averaged AUC address a practically important metric. Open questions include extending the analysis to unbounded losses like cross-entropy, incorporating label correlations explicitly, and establishing matching lower bounds to confirm optimality. The paper is purely theoretical with no experimental validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
