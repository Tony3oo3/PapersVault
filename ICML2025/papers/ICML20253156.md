## Problem

Masked Language Models introduce [MASK] tokens into the context during pre-training, which causes two intertwined issues: the presence of unreal tokens absent from real text, and the corruption of context semantics that makes the remaining context ambiguous. Prior work (e.g., ELECTRA, MAE-based approaches) addressed the unreal-token problem but largely overlooked the corrupted-semantics problem. Through a novel Repeated MLM experiment that decouples the two factors, this paper demonstrates that corrupted semantics has a substantially greater negative impact on MLM performance than unreal tokens, manifesting as increased prediction entropy (semantic multimodality).

## Input / Output

The model takes a tokenized sequence (English text or SMILES) in which each [MASK] token is replaced by k duplicated [MASK] embeddings, producing an expanded input sequence longer than the original. During pre-training, the output is a probability distribution over the vocabulary for each expanded state, supervised via a dynamic-programming alignment loss that marginalizes over all valid state-to-token assignments. During fine-tuning, the model produces standard task outputs (classification labels for GLUE/MoleculeNet, span predictions for SQuAD 2.0) using the same expanded-state architecture with 2D positional encoding to distinguish clones.

## Method

ExLM enhances the MLM context through two mechanisms. First, States Expansion duplicates each [MASK] embedding into k copies and applies 2D Rotary Position Embedding (with the second dimension indexing the clone) so the Transformer encoder produces k distinct hidden states per masked position, enlarging the semantic space available for reconstruction. Second, Dependency Capture computes a transition matrix E via an attention-like operation over the hidden states, constrained to an upper-triangular (DAG) structure, explicitly modeling semantic dependencies among the expanded states. Training uses a States Alignment objective: a dynamic-programming algorithm (adapted from DA-Transformer) efficiently computes the marginal log-likelihood of the target tokens over all valid paths through the DAG in O(M×L²) time, providing supervision to both the per-state emission probabilities and the transition matrix without requiring a fixed one-to-one state-to-token assignment.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, ExLM achieves a mean score of 86.2, outperforming RoBERTa* (85.2, same pre-training data), TUPE (84.9), and BERT (83.1). On SQuAD 2.0, ExLM reaches 82.0 EM / 84.6 F1, surpassing RoBERTa* (78.3/81.5) and UniLMv2 (80.9/83.6). On the MoleculeNet molecular property benchmark, ExLM achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 76.1 across 7 tasks, beating the strongest baselines Mole-BERT (73.4) and GraphMVP (73.3), and significantly outperforming the same-architecture SMILES-BERT* (73.2). ExLM LARGE further improves to 86.9 GLUE mean and 82.6/85.0 SQuAD 2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExLM outperforms all 13 baselines across both text and molecular benchmarks. On GLUE, ExLM (86.2 mean) beats RoBERTa* (85.2, trained on identical pre-training data), TUPE (84.9), BERT (83.1), and XLNet/UniLMv2 (which report only partial GLUE scores); the largest margin is on RTE where ExLM scores 78.8 vs. RoBERTa*'s 75.5 and BERT's 68.6. On SQuAD 2.0, ExLM (82.0 EM / 84.6 F1) surpasses UniLMv2 (80.9/83.6) by ~1.1 EM and RoBERTa* (78.3/81.5) by ~3.7 EM. On the MoleculeNet benchmark, ExLM (76.1 mean ROC-AUC) wins on 5 of 7 tasks and leads the mean, outperforming Mole-BERT (73.4), GraphMVP (73.3), SMILES-BERT* (73.2, same architecture and data), GROVER (71.5), 3D InfoMax (71.8), Attentive FP (72.1), D-MPNN (74.2), and N-GramRF; the largest molecular gain is on ClinTox (91.6 vs. 89.8 for SMILES-BERT*) and ToxCast (66.9 vs. 65.5 for D-MPNN). The comparison against SMILES-BERT* is particularly informative since it isolates the contribution of ExLM's context-enhancement mechanism from architectural or data differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BERT/MLM pre-training framework by rethinking how [MASK] tokens are handled in the input context. It builds on DA-Transformer's DAG-based transition matrix and dynamic programming alignment for state-to-token assignment, and adapts RoPE into a 2D variant to distinguish expanded [MASK] states. Conceptually, it departs from prior [MASK]-focused work (ELECTRA, Liao et al., Meng et al.) that addressed the 'unreal token' problem, by identifying and isolating the 'corrupted semantics' problem as a distinct and more impactful factor through a novel Repeated MLM experimental design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that corrupted semantics dominates over unreal tokens in degrading MLM performance redirects attention in the field toward context quality rather than token realism, potentially informing future pre-training objective design. The ExLM approach is modality-agnostic (validated on both text and SMILES), suggesting broad applicability to other sequence domains like proteins. Open questions include whether the optimal number of expanded states (k=4) and the DP alignment overhead scale gracefully to larger models and longer sequences, and whether the artificial redundancy in the Repeated MLM experiment fully captures real-world semantic corruption patterns.
