## Problem

State-of-the-art model-based RL algorithms like DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 rely on holistic, unstructured latent representations of the environment, which limits interpretability and makes it difficult for behavior models to reason about individual objects and their interactions. No prior method learns policies purely inside imagined rollouts of an object-centric world model trained directly from pixel inputs, leaving the potential of structured, object-centric representations for MBRL largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251802/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames from MuJoCo-simulated robotic environments (a Sawyer arm on a table with colored blocks/spheres) along with 4-dimensional continuous action vectors [a_x, a_y, a_z, a_grip] ∈ [-1,1]⁴. It produces continuous robot actions parameterized as a Gaussian (mean and std), predicted future object slots (N slots of dimension 128 per frame), reconstructed RGB frames, and reward/value estimates computed as expectations over K exponentially spaced bins via a categorical head. The SAVi encoder-decoder is pretrained on ~1M random frames before joint training begins.

## Method

SOLD combines three components: (1) a SAVi encoder-decoder pretrained on random episodes that decomposes each frame into N permutation-equivariant object slots via iterative Slot Attention; (2) an action-conditional dynamics model based on OCVP-Seq that autoregressively predicts future slots using decoupled temporal and relational attention, trained with a hybrid loss combining joint-embedding distance to ground-truth slots and pixel reconstruction; and (3) a Slot Aggregation Transformer (SAT) backbone with causal attention, ALiBi positional biases, and learnable register tokens that processes slot histories to produce reward, value, and action predictions. The actor and critic are trained entirely on imagined rollouts (horizon T=15) within the structured latent space, following the Dreamer framework with bootstrapped λ-returns and entropy regularization. Critically, the SAVi encoder-decoder is fine-tuned (not frozen) during joint training, enabling it to represent object configurations absent from random pre-training data, such as lifted blocks in Pick tasks.

## Results

On the Specific task suite (requiring primarily robotic control), SOLD achieves an average success rate of 89.4%, outperforming DreamerV3 (81.7%) and TD-MPC2 (84.2%). On the more challenging Distinct task suite (requiring complex relational reasoning), SOLD achieves 74.6% average success, more than doubling DreamerV3 (33.1%) and TD-MPC2 (17.3%). On non-object-centric generalization tasks, SOLD reaches 100% success on MetaWorld's Button-Press and Hammer, and returns of 497 and 645 on DM-Control's Cartpole-Balance and Finger-Spin, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLD is compared against two state-of-the-art MBRL baselines (DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2) and a non-object-centric ablation (w/o OCE) that replaces the SAVi encoder-decoder with a standard CNN autoencoder. On the Specific tasks, SOLD wins on average (89.4% vs. 81.7% and 84.2%) but the margins are modest: it loses to DreamerV3 on Push (82.8% vs. 97.1%) and Pick (85.8% vs. 96.7%), while decisively winning on Reach-Rel (91.1% vs. 45.6% and 79.1%). On the Distinct tasks, which demand identifying the odd-one-out among multiple objects, SOLD dominates all baselines by a wide margin, achieving 74.6% average versus 33.1% (DreamerV3) and 17.3% (TD-MPC2), with the gap reaching 60+ percentage points on individual tasks like Reach-Distinct (91.8% vs. 14.6%). The w/o OCE ablation confirms that the object-centric structure is critical: it collapses to 6.9% average on Distinct tasks, showing that holistic representations cannot capture the required relational reasoning. On non-object-centric generalization tasks (MetaWorld, DM-Control), SOLD achieves 100% success on Button-Press and Hammer, demonstrating that the framework is not limited to naturally object-centric scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLD sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: object-centric video prediction (SAVi, OCVP, SlotFormer) and model-based RL with latent imagination (Dreamer, TD-MPC2). Prior object-centric prediction methods model future slot dynamics but stop at forecasting; prior MBRL methods train policies inside imagined rollouts but use holistic, unstructured latent states. SOLD is the first to close this gap by training actor-critic networks entirely within imagined rollouts of an action-conditional, object-centric dynamics model trained from raw pixels, and introduces the Slot Aggregation Transformer as a dedicated backbone for aggregating slot histories into scalar predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that structured, object-centric inductive biases in the world model's latent space yield substantial gains on tasks requiring relational reasoning (more than 2× over DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 on Distinct variants), and that this benefit persists even when the non-object-centric baseline is given 5× more parameters. The fine-tuning of the SAVi encoder during RL training addresses a practical limitation of prior object-centric RL that relied on frozen encoders, and the generalization to non-object-centric environments (Cartpole, Finger-Spin) suggests the approach is not brittle. Open questions include scaling to real-world, high-stochasticity settings and whether the object-centric decomposition remains meaningful in visually complex, unstructured scenes.
