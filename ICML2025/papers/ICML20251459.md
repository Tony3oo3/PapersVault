## Problem

Existing point cloud explanation methods (e.g., Critical Points, PC Saliency Map) fail to simultaneously satisfy faithfulness (causal influence on model predictions) and conceptual cohesion (semantically meaningful structures aligned with human perception). Pooling-based methods ignore the downstream classifier, while gradient-based methods introduce a biased centroid-singularity prior that concentrates critical points at spatial corners. The paper formalizes the problem of extracting interpretable critical subsets that meet both criteria for arbitrary point cloud architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen trained point cloud model and its intermediate feature z = F^{1:l}(x) at a chosen layer l (with N' points and D channels), plus the original input x ∈ R^{3×N}. It produces a per-point critical score map s(x) ∈ [0,1]^N via a learnable attention bottleneck, from which interpretable critical subsets and multi-level 3D concept decompositions can be derived. For hierarchical models where N' < N, scores are propagated back to all N original points via KNN-based spatial interpolation weighted by L2 distance.

## Method

InfoCons reformulates the Information Bottleneck objective for critical point selection as max I(C,y) − βI(x,C), where C = m⊙x is a masked subset. The key innovation (Deep InfoCons, Eq. 6) replaces simple feature zeroing with Gaussian noise injection: for points deemed unimportant (mask→0), their features are replaced by ε ~ N(μ_z, σ_z²) sampled from the feature distribution, with a stop-gradient on (1−m̂)·ε to decouple neighbor entanglement while preserving coarse neighborhood information. A non-linear channel-wise attention bottleneck f(m̂|z;θ) (Eq. 7) learns the selective mask, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy (lower-bounding I(C,y)) plus β·KL(m̂‖q(m̂)) (upper-bounding I(x,C)). This design addresses the entanglement problem in hierarchical and self-attention models where point features are highly correlated with neighbors, making simple point-wise masking ineffective.

## Results

On ModelNet40 with DGCNN, InfoCons (20 iterations) achieves the second-best point-drop attack effectiveness (OA = 63.70% after dropping 500 most critical points), behind only LIME3D (45.22%) but at 1/15th the computational cost (0.29s vs. 4.54s per point cloud). In single-pass mode, InfoCons (73.50% OA) outperforms CP++ (75.08%) and PCSAM (89.87%), while matching CP++ in efficiency (both require one forward pass, 0.01s). When integrated into SageMix data augmentation, InfoCons improves DGCNN test OA from 92.79% to 93.19%, and when used in SI-Adv adversarial attacks, it increases ASR from 99.76% to 99.80% while reducing Chamfer distance from 5.58 to 5.47.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CP++ (Levi & Gilboa, 2023), InfoCons (1-pass) achieves a lower OA of 73.50% vs. 75.08%, indicating more effective critical point identification, and the gap widens substantially with iterations (63.70% vs. 75.08% at 20 iterations). Against PCSAM (Zheng et al., 2019), InfoCons dominates in both single-pass (73.50% vs. 89.87%) and multi-iteration settings (63.70% vs. 79.86%), while also avoiding PCSAM's biased centroid-singularity prior that causes spatially concentrated explanations. Against LIME3D (Tan & Kotthaus, 2022), LIME3D achieves the strongest drop attack (45.22% OA at 1000 queries) but at 4.54s per point cloud versus InfoCons's 0.29s at 20 iterations, making InfoCons 15× faster with only a moderate effectiveness gap. In the SageMix data augmentation application, InfoCons improves DGCNN test OA by +0.40% over the original gradient-based SageMix (93.19% vs. 92.79%). In the SI-Adv adversarial attack, InfoCons-guided perturbations achieve marginally higher ASR (99.80% vs. 99.76%) with lower Chamfer distance (5.47 vs. 5.58) and Hausdorff distance (6.55 vs. 6.70), confirming more effective sensitive-region identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) framework for attribution (Schulz et al., 2020; Alemi et al., 2016) from 2D pixel-level explanations to 3D point cloud critical subset selection, reformulating the IB objective as a tradeoff between mutual information with the label (faithfulness) and mutual information with the input (conceptual cohesion). It departs from existing point cloud explanation methods—maxpooling-based Critical Points, gradient-based PC Saliency Maps, and black-box LIME3D—by introducing a learnable attention bottleneck that produces a soft, continuous score map rather than relying on encoder activations, spherical-coordinate gradients, or iterative point-dropping queries. The key conceptual departure is formalizing the critical subset as a 'concept' that must simultaneously be causally faithful to the full model (encoder + classifier) and semantically coherent with human perceptual priors, addressing limitations the authors identify in all prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap in 3D interpretability, where explanation methods lag significantly behind 2D images and language, and provides a unified IB-based framework that generalizes across non-hierarchical, hierarchical, and self-attention point cloud architectures. The practical integration into SageMix (data augmentation) and SI-Adv (adversarial attacks) demonstrates that the critical score maps are not merely diagnostic but can actively improve downstream pipelines. However, the method requires training a 2.4M-parameter attention bottleneck per model, is sensitive to hyperparameters (β, intermediate layer l), and exhibits failure cases (e.g., PCT), leaving open questions about scalability to larger real-world scenes and robustness under domain shift. The modest quantitative gains over strong baselines (e.g., +0.4% OA for SageMix, +0.04% ASR for SI-Adv) suggest the primary value is in interpretability quality rather than performance improvement.
