key_contribution: Longtail Guidance (LTG), a training-free diffusion sampling technique that uses a differentiable Epistemic Head to compute model-based longtail signals from an existing production model and biases latent diffusion denoising toward generating in-distribution synthetic data that is specifically rare or hard for that model, yielding up to 6.8 points average accuracy improvement over prior synthetic data generation methods at 4× synthetic data efficiency.
limitations: The primary computational limitation is the expensive VAE decoding step required for differentiable terminal state estimation, making LTG ~6× slower than unguided diffusion (1.01 vs 6.32 images/second). Additionally, generalization gains saturate after approximately 200 fine-tuning epochs, and the evaluation is limited to image classification on 7 natural image datasets, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other modalities or task types.

surprise_factor: The conceptual surprise is that a production model trained exclusively on clean (data, label) pairs can effectively guide diffusion without ever seeing intermediate noisy states — the paper's FID analysis (Figure 15) convincingly shows decoded terminal states are dramatically closer to real data than raw intermediate states, explaining why this works. The 4× synthetic data efficiency over GIF (achieving equal or better accuracy at ~25% of the data volume) is a notable practical result, though the absolute accuracy gains, while significant, are in the context of a well-studied task with strong baselines.

## Problem

Deployed predictive models inevitably encounter rare or disproportionately hard inputs (longtail cases), but current practice is reactive: deploy, discover failures, collect more data, retrain. Existing synthetic data generation methods (GIF, Dream-ID) condition on foundation models like CLIP rather than the specific deployed model, and standard classifier guidance requires retraining the predictive model on intermediate noisy diffusion states it will never see in production. The paper asks: can we proactively generate in-distribution training data that is specifically challenging to an existing production model, without modifying either the diffusion model or the predictive model?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained predictive model (ViT-B, ResNet-50, or ResNet-34) augmented with an Epistemic Head (K=3–5 duplicated classification heads), a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.4), and a generic text prompt ('a photo of [Class]'). It produces 512×512 synthetic RGB images that are in-distribution but exhibit higher longtail signal (lower correct-class probability, lower accuracy) from the predictive model's perspective. A secondary output is Longtail Introspection: VLM-generated text descriptions and novel keywords identifying what the model struggles with, usable to prompt future data collection or generation.

## Method

The core contribution is Longtail Guidance (LTG), a diffusion sampling technique that at each denoising step estimates the terminal latent state ẑ_t⁰ via the DDIM prediction, decodes it to data space through the VAE, computes a model-based longtail signal f_φ^lt from the production model, and biases the denoising estimate in latent space toward higher longtail signal (Eq. 11). The key insight is that the decoded terminal state is dramatically closer in distribution to real training data than intermediate noisy states (quantified via FID), so the production model—trained only on clean data—can effectively guide generation without any retraining on intermediate diffusion states. The longtail signal is provided by an Epistemic Head: K duplicated classification heads trained with an oracle loss (gradient flows only through the best-performing head per example) and a stop-grad on the base model, yielding a differentiable single-forward-pass epistemic uncertainty that distinguishes rare inputs (high epistemic) from ambiguous ones (high aleatoric). The entire pipeline requires no retraining of either the diffusion model or the predictive model, and a single guidance weight can be fixed across all classes, datasets, and fine-tuning epochs.

## Results

On the Dataset Expansion task (Table 1), LTG(Epistemic) achieves 82.4% average top-1 accuracy across Caltech101, Stanford Cars, Oxford Flowers, and Oxford Pets, outperforming the strongest baseline GIF-SD (75.6%) by 6.8 points and prompt-tuned Stable Diffusion (59.9%) by 22.5 points. On the in-distribution synthetic data generation task (Table 2), LTG(Epistemic) reaches 64.64% average top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-100/ImageNet-A/ImageNet-V2, surpassing Dream-ID (60.33%) by 4.3 points. On ImageNet-LT (Table 3), LTG(Epistemic) improves the Few-class split from 40.4% (LiVT baseline) to 50.0% (+9.6 points, +24% relative), while overall accuracy rises from 60.6% to 61.9%. The paper reports that LTG achieves these gains at more than 4× the synthetic data efficiency of GIF, matching GIF's performance at only ~24.5% of the synthetic data volume.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTG(Epistemic) outperforms all synthetic data generation baselines across every dataset in Table 1: it beats GIF-SD (75.6% avg) by 6.8 points, GIF-DALLE (67.7%) by 14.7 points, DALL-E2 (63.8%) by 18.6 points, and prompt-tuned Stable Diffusion (59.9%) by 22.5 points. Against data augmentation baselines (RandAugment 58.2%, GridMask 49.6%, Cutout 48.5%), LTG leads by 24+ points on average. On the ImageNet-100 in-distribution task (Table 2), LTG(Epistemic) at 64.64% avg surpasses Dream-ID (60.33%) by 4.3 points and all data augmentation methods (58.65–59.52%) by 5+ points, with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (22.09% vs 12.13% for Dream-ID, a >80% relative improvement). On ImageNet-LT (Table 3), LTG(Epistemic) improves the Few-class split from 40.4% (LiVT) to 50.0% (+9.6 pts), substantially outperforming baseline Stable Diffusion fine-tuning (44.3%, +3.9 pts) and all prior longtail methods in the table (best prior: TADE at 43.5%). The Epistemic Head signal provides mild but consistent gains over Energy and Entropy signals (82.4% vs 81.5%/81.7% avg in Table 1; 64.64% vs 63.75%/64.47% in Table 2), at negligible additional compute cost (<2% training/inference overhead). Notably, LTG requires no prompt tuning (using generic 'a photo of [Class]' prompts) whereas GIF and Dream-ID rely on 40 hand-crafted prompts per class, yet still achieves superior results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) by showing that guidance can be computed on decoded terminal state estimates rather than intermediate noisy states, eliminating the need to retrain the predictive model on diffusion-specific inputs. It departs from prior synthetic data generation approaches (GIF, Dream-ID) that reason in CLIP embedding space by instead conditioning directly on the production model's own uncertainty signals, and from Universal Guidance (Bansal et al., 2023) by using a single-pass gradient rather than recurrent/backward sampling. The Epistemic Head draws on LoRA-style parameter freezing and Hydra-style multi-head ensembling to provide a differentiable, single-forward-pass epistemic uncertainty signal without altering base model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LTG offers a practical, training-free mechanism for production ML teams to proactively mine high-value synthetic data from an existing deployed model, shifting longtail mitigation from reactive to proactive. The key limitation is computational cost: the VAE decoding step makes LTG ~6× slower than unguided diffusion (1.01 vs 6.32 img/s), and gains saturate after ~200 fine-tuning epochs. Open questions include whether higher-capacity generative models extend the useful mining window, whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities (e.g., LiDAR, point clouds), and whether the Epistemic Head's oracle-loss training strategy generalizes to continuous-output or multi-task predictive models.
