## Problem

Most theoretical results for follow-the-regularized-leader (FTRL) dynamics in games assume perfect, deterministic payoff observations, a level of precision rarely justified in real-world decision-making. This paper asks how noise, randomness, and uncertainty fundamentally alter the long-run behavior of regularized learning, revealing that the answer is a universal principle: 'uncertainty favors extremes,' with every player reaching an almost-pure strategy in finite time regardless of game structure or noise level.

## Input / Output

The model takes a finite normal-form game (payoff functions over action profiles), state-dependent diffusion matrices σ_i: X → R^{n_i×m} that can encode correlated noise across strategies and players, an initial mixed strategy profile, and decomposable regularizer kernels θ_i. It produces explicit hitting-time bounds (e.g., E[τ_{i,ε}] ≲ e^λ/λ with λ scaling as Θ((1+σ²_max)/σ²_min · θ''(ε/(n_i−1))²)), characterizations of stochastically asymptotically stable sets, and limit-set properties. The noise is modeled as a general continuous square-integrable martingale rather than i.i.d. shocks, allowing correlated and state-dependent perturbations that capture common sources of randomness (e.g., shared coin tosses in Matching Pennies).

## Method

The paper formulates a stochastic FTRL (S-FTRL) as an Itô SDE where score variables Y_i evolve with a drift given by payoffs and a martingale noise term, with strategies recovered via regularized best-response maps Q_i. The key technical step is deriving the explicit SDE for strategy dynamics (Proposition 1) by combining Itô's lemma with KKT conditions on the regularizer, yielding three components: the deterministic FTRL drift, a martingale term, and a second-order Itô correction that induces a measurable bias. Theorem 1 proves finite-time reachability of pure-strategy neighborhoods via a Lyapunov function of the form φ(x) = n−1+e^λ − Σ_α e^{λx_α}, bounding the infinitesimal generator's action outside ε-neighborhoods. Theorem 2 establishes that a span of pure strategies is stochastically asymptotically stable if and only if it is closed under better replies, a result that fails for other stochastic replicator models (SRD-AS, SRD-PI) due to their different Itô corrections. For recurrent games (harmonic games, zero-sum with interior equilibria), Theorem 4 shows the Bregman-energy constant of motion acquires a positive drift under noise, causing mean escape toward the boundary in finite time.

## Results

The paper establishes that under stochastic FTRL (S-FTRL), every player reaches an ε-neighborhood of a pure strategy in finite time with E[τ_{i,ε}] ≲ e^λ/λ, where λ = Θ((1+σ²_{i,max})/σ²_{i,min} · θ''(ε/(n_i−1))²) (Theorem 1). The only possible limits of the dynamics are pure Nash equilibria, and a span of pure strategies is stochastically asymptotically stable if and only if it is closed under better replies (Theorems 2–3). In harmonic games (including two-player zero-sum games with fully mixed equilibria), the Bregman energy diverges: E[H(X(t))] → ∞ with E[τ_M] ≤ 2(M−H(x))/(σ²_min·ε(M)), and the process is transient in the interior of the strategy space (Theorems 4–5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contrasts S-FTRL with three alternative models: (i) the deterministic FTRL (σ=0), where orbits in harmonic games are Poincaré recurrent and bounded away from the boundary; (ii) the stochastic replicator dynamics with aggregate shocks (SRD-AS, Fudenberg & Harris [21]), where any pure strategy can be stochastically asymptotically stable depending on the noise profile, violating the 'closed under better replies' characterization; and (iii) the stochastic replicator dynamics of pairwise imitation (SRD-PI, Foster & Young [20]), where all pure strategies become stochastically asymptotically stable for sufficiently high noise. The critical distinction is the Itô correction term (14c) in S-FTRL, which is 'just right' to preserve the strategic stability characterization (Theorem 2) while still driving trajectories toward the boundary. In zero-sum games, S-FTRL exhibits a mean drift toward the boundary without converging to it with probability 1 (resembling Brownian motion), whereas SRD-AS and SRD-PI converge to the boundary with a nonzero drift even under small perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic replicator dynamics of Mertikopoulos & Moustakas [41] and the uncorrelated-noise model of Bravo & Mertikopoulos [13] to the full FTRL family with arbitrary regularizers and general correlated diffusion matrices. It departs conceptually from the stochastic replicator models of Foster & Young [20] and Fudenberg & Harris [21], which share the same deterministic limit but differ in their Itô correction terms; the authors show this difference is critical, as only the S-FTRL Itô correction preserves the 'closed under better replies' stability criterion. The analysis builds on the deterministic FTRL framework of Mertikopoulos & Sandholm [42] and the harmonic game recurrence results of Legacci et al. [34].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that mixed Nash equilibria are fundamentally fragile under any non-degenerate noise: the only possible limits of stochastic FTRL are pure Nash equilibria, and in games without pure equilibria the dynamics cannot converge at all. This has direct implications for predicting the long-run behavior of learning algorithms in multi-agent systems, where payoff observations are inherently noisy. Open questions include the vanishing-learning-rate regime (where the authors conjecture recurrence may be restored), continuous action spaces, and a discrete-time analysis that would require substantially different tools. The conjecture that all stable attractors must lie on the boundary of the strategy space remains unproven.
