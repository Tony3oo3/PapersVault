## Problem

Existing mechanical metamaterial design models handle at most two of the three fundamental modalities (3D topology, density condition, mechanical property), leaving no unified framework capable of performing topology generation, property prediction, and condition confirmation simultaneously. Additionally, no benchmark exists that covers all three tasks with appropriate datasets and evaluation metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any two of the three modalities as known context and generates the third: 3D topology (a graph with node coordinates, adjacency matrix, and three lattice vectors), relative density (a scalar), or mechanical properties (a vector of Young's modulus, shear modulus, Poisson's ratio). All modalities are first discretized into a shared token sequence via modality-specific encoders and a VQ-VAE codebook, so the diffusion backbone operates on a fixed-length token vector where known tokens are frozen and only unknown tokens are denoised. The dataset contains 15,000 samples (500 topologies × 3 densities × 10 rotations) with properties computed via finite-element homogenization.

## Method

UNIMATE consists of two modules. The modality alignment module maps each modality through a dedicated encoder (GCN for topology, MLP for density and property) into a shared discrete latent space via a VQ-VAE codebook, then aligns the three token distributions using a novel tripartite optimal transport (TOT) objective solved by a generalized tripartite Sinkhorn algorithm that computes a 3D transport plan. The synergetic generation module is a score-based diffusion model with a transformer backbone that performs partially frozen denoising: at each step, all tokens pass through the transformer, but the output positions corresponding to known context tokens are overwritten with their original values, so only unknown tokens evolve. Training jointly optimizes reconstruction, codebook rounding, tripartite Wasserstein alignment, and diffusion denoising losses, while inference initializes unknown tokens from the high-probability regions of the learned transport plan rather than pure noise.

## Results

On the UniMate dataset, UNIMATE achieves F_qua=2.74 and F_cond=7.81 on topology generation, NRMSE_pp=2.44 on property prediction, and NRMSE_cc=4.43 on condition confirmation. These represent improvements of 80.2%, 5.1%, and 50.2% over the respective second-best baselines (SyMat, MACE+VE, and uniTruss). UNIMATE is the only model evaluated on all three tasks simultaneously, while baselines are limited to one or two tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIMATE outperforms all six baselines across every task it is evaluated on. In topology generation, UNIMATE's F_qua of 2.74 dramatically surpasses SyMat (16.94) and CDVAE (19.23), an 80.2% improvement over the best baseline. For property prediction, UNIMATE (NRMSE_pp=2.44) edges out MACE+VE (2.57) by 5.1% and uniTruss (2.71) by 10.0%, while Equiformer (5.31) and ViSNet (3.12) trail further behind. In condition confirmation, UNIMATE (NRMSE_cc=4.43) more than halves the error of the best baseline uniTruss (8.89), a 50.2% improvement, and substantially outperforms MACE+VE (9.09), ViSNet (10.43), and Equiformer (38.05). Notably, only uniTruss is evaluated on all three tasks among the baselines; the generation-oriented models (CDVAE, SyMat) cannot perform prediction tasks, and the prediction-oriented models (Equiformer, ViSNet, MACE+VE) were forced to predict density for condition confirmation despite not being designed for that input configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of work—VQ-VAE-style discrete latent encoding, score-based diffusion for generative modeling, and optimal transport for distribution alignment—into a single framework for mechanical metamaterial design. It departs from prior generation models (CDVAE, SyMat) that handle only topology synthesis, and from prediction models (Equiformer, ViSNet, MACE-ve) that only estimate properties from structure, by introducing a tripartite optimal transport (generalizing the classical two-way Sinkhorn algorithm) to align all three modalities in a shared discrete space. The partially frozen diffusion mechanism, where known tokens are held fixed during denoising, is a novel adaptation of score-based diffusion for multi-modality conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIMATE is the first model to treat topology, density, and property as a unified trinity where any one modality can be the generation target, and it provides a new benchmark dataset (15,000 samples) with task-specific metrics that the field previously lacked. However, the dataset is built from only 500 base topologies with 3 density levels each and restricted to cubic unit cells, limiting generalizability to more complex geometries or continuous density ranges. Open questions include whether the tripartite OT alignment scales to more than three modalities or higher-dimensional property vectors, and whether the 80% topology generation gap over baselines persists when compared against metamaterial-specific generators rather than crystal-structure models adapted to the task.
