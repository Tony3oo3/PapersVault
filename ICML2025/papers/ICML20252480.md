## Problem

Existing learning-based methods for molecular ground-state conformation prediction (e.g., ConfOpt) use empirically designed neural architectures whose feedforward computations do not correspond to minimizing a physically meaningful energy function, resulting in limited interpretability and sub-optimal accuracy. This paper bridges the gap between slow energy-based simulation and fast but uninterpretable learning-based approaches by grounding the model architecture in Wasserstein gradient flow theory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252480/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D molecular graph (atom types and bond connectivity) together with an initial low-quality 3D conformation generated by RDKit as input, and produces the predicted ground-state 3D conformation as per-atom 3D coordinates. The decoder predicts a residual displacement from the RDKit starting conformation via a weighted average of inter-atomic displacement vectors, ensuring SE(3)-equivariance throughout the pipeline. Training uses a multi-task loss combining interatomic distance MAE (primary) and Procrustes-aligned coordinate RMSD (auxiliary).

## Method

WGFormer is an SE(3)-Transformer whose attention mechanism is built on the Sinkhorn-scaling algorithm with adjusted query, key, and value matrices, operating within an auto-encoding framework (30 WGFormer encoder layers + MLP decoder). The key theoretical insight is that when the Sinkhorn iteration count M→∞, each attention head corresponds to an entropic optimal transport problem, making the stacked layers implement Wasserstein gradient flows that minimize a physically interpretable energy function on latent mixture models of atoms—specifically, an energy balancing expected atomic distances (preventing atom collapse) and coupling entropy (encouraging dense inter-atomic relations). The relational representation R is updated alongside atom-level representations X in each layer, and the value matrix incorporates cross-head parameter sharing via a shared W W^T matrix. The model is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss (distance MAE + coordinate RMSD) and achieves state-of-the-art C-RMSD while being over 50% faster than ConfOpt baselines due to its simpler simultaneous update architecture.

## Results

On Molecule3D (random-split), WGFormer achieves C-RMSD of 0.664 and D-MAE of 0.392 on the test set, outperforming the best 3D baseline ConfOpt-ThreeAtom (C-RMSD 0.736, D-MAE 0.430) by ~10% in C-RMSD. On QM9, WGFormer reaches C-RMSD 0.206 and D-MAE 0.227, improving over ConfOpt-ThreeAtom (C-RMSD 0.246, D-MAE 0.244) by ~16% in C-RMSD. WGFormer also reduces per-molecule inference time by over 50% compared to ConfOpt variants, achieving 0.150 s/mol on QM9 versus 14–18 s/mol for generative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WGFormer outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against the strongest 2D baseline GTMGC, WGFormer reduces C-RMSD from 0.713 to 0.664 (Molecule3D random) and from 0.367 to 0.206 (QM9), a 15–44% improvement. Against the strongest 3D baseline ConfOpt-ThreeAtom, WGFormer lowers C-RMSD from 0.736 to 0.664 (Molecule3D random, ~10% gain) and from 0.246 to 0.206 (QM9, ~16% gain), while also achieving lower D-MAE and D-RMSE. The weaker 2D baselines (GINE, GATv2, GPS) and 3D baselines (SE(3)-Transformer, EGNN, ConfOpt-TwoAtom) are all surpassed by larger margins. In efficiency comparisons, WGFormer runs at 0.150 s/mol on QM9 versus 14.4–18.1 s/mol for generative models (TorsionDiff, GeoDiff) and 48.1 s/mol for ConfOpt on DRUGS, making it over 100× faster than ConfOpt while simultaneously achieving better accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Sinkformer architecture (Sander et al., 2022), which introduced doubly stochastic attention via Sinkhorn scaling, and the Uni-Mol (Zhou et al., 2022) pipeline for 3D molecular representation learning. It departs from prior conformation optimization methods like ConfOpt (Guan et al., 2022), which use empirically designed Transformer backbones to predict gradient fields without guaranteeing minimization of a physically meaningful energy. The key conceptual contribution is reinterpreting the modified Transformer (with QKV adjustment and Sinkhorn attention) as a Wasserstein gradient flow that minimizes an entropic optimal transport energy over latent atomic mixture models, providing a principled theoretical foundation absent in prior empirical architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding the architecture in Wasserstein gradient flows, the paper offers a rare combination of strong empirical performance and physical interpretability for molecular conformation prediction, bridging the gap between expensive energy-based simulation and black-box learning methods. The demonstrated correlation (Pearson 0.885) between latent energy minimization and actual potential energy reduction validates the theoretical framework empirically. Open questions include whether the Wasserstein gradient flow perspective generalizes to other molecular tasks (e.g., excited-state conformations, reaction pathways), whether the theoretical guarantees extend to non-Dirac measures with finite M, and whether the approach scales to larger biomolecular systems beyond small molecules.
