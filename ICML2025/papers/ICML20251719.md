## Problem

Deep learning models for time series forecasting treat data as abstract patterns without explicitly modeling the underlying dynamical systems that generate them. Empirical Dynamic Modeling (EDM), grounded in Takens' theorem, can recover nonlinear dynamics from observations but suffers from three critical limitations: it assumes noise-free measurements, models each sequence independently, and requires the forecasting horizon to be much shorter than the lookback window. This paper addresses all three gaps by integrating EDM's dynamical-systems rigor with the scalability and noise-robustness of deep learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series lookback window of length T (set to 2H in main experiments) with D variates and produces H-step future predictions for each variate. The input is first time-delayed by δ_T steps to form embedding vectors of dimension δ_T, which are then projected into a higher-dimensional latent space (M ≫ δ_T) before kernel regression. For multivariate series, a single shared model is applied channel-wise, and the output is a D×H matrix of predicted values.

## Method

DeepEDM is a fully differentiable, stackable architecture that reinterprets EDM's Simplex projection through a learned latent space. A base MLP generates a coarse H-step forecast, which is concatenated with the lookback window and time-delayed by δ_T steps; a shared linear encoder then projects each delay vector into a latent space of dimension M. In this latent space, a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression—implemented as softmax attention with temperature τ—computes a weighted average over all lookback points to predict the time-delayed future, replacing EDM's non-differentiable K-nearest-neighbor selection with a soft, differentiable analogue. A lightweight MLP decoder reconstructs and denoises the final H-step forecast. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining pointwise error (L_err) and first-order temporal-difference error (L_td), with an adaptive balancing coefficient λ that up-weights temporal fidelity when sign changes between true and predicted differences are frequent.

## Results

On the main multivariate forecasting benchmark (10 datasets, H∈{48,96,144,192}), DeepEDM achieves the best MSE or MAE on 36 out of 80 metrics, compared to 5 for the next-best dynamical-systems method (Koopa) and 11 for the strongest deep learning model (CycleNet). On the M4 univariate benchmark, DeepEDM attains the best weighted-average SMAPE (11.695) and OWA (0.841), outperforming all dynamical-modeling baselines (Koopa: 11.863 SMAPE, 0.858 OWA). In the generalization-to-unseen-sequence setting, DeepEDM ranks first in 39 out of 48 MSE/MAE configurations across ETT, Exchange, and Weather. On synthetic chaotic Lorenz data with σ_noise=2.5, DeepEDM maintains low MSE while EDM with Simplex degrades sharply, confirming robustness to measurement noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepEDM outperforms all 16 baselines on the majority of metrics: it wins 36 out of 80 MSE/MAE entries in the main multivariate table, versus 5 for Koopa (the closest dynamical-systems competitor) and 11 for CycleNet (the strongest pure deep-learning model). Against Transformer-based methods (iTransformer, PatchTST), DeepEDM consistently achieves lower MSE and MAE across ETT, Weather, Traffic, and ILI, with the gap widening at longer horizons (H=192). Against MLP-based methods (TimeMixer, DLinear, FITS), DeepEDM's advantage is most pronounced on high-dimensional datasets like Traffic and ECL, where its kernel-regression mechanism captures nonlinear interactions that linear/MLP models miss. The notable exception is the Exchange (Stocks) dataset, where the Naïve baseline (last-value prediction) outperforms all learned models, a finding the authors attribute to the non-stationary nature of stock data. On the M4 univariate benchmark, DeepEDM beats all dynamical-modeling baselines (Koopa, KNF) on every subset and achieves the best weighted-average SMAPE and OWA overall. In the cross-sequence generalization experiment (Table 2), DeepEDM leads in 39/48 settings, outperforming Koopa, iTransformer, and PatchTST when trained on disjoint channels.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepEDM bridges two previously separate lines of work: classical Empirical Dynamic Modeling (EDM) rooted in Takens' theorem, which reconstructs system dynamics from time-delayed embeddings, and modern deep learning architectures for time series forecasting (Transformers, MLPs, Koopman-based predictors). The key conceptual departure from EDM is replacing the non-differentiable K-nearest-neighbor simplex projection with a fully differentiable kernel regression implemented via softmax attention, and wrapping it in a learned encoder-decoder that provides noise robustness and cross-sequence generalization. The paper also draws an explicit structural analogy to Transformer blocks, arguing that time-delayed embeddings are a special case of local patching and that the encoder–attention–decoder pipeline mirrors self-attention with distinct query/key/value definitions, thereby providing a dynamical-systems interpretation for why patching and attention work in models like PatchTST and iTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing three concrete limitations of EDM (noise sensitivity, per-sequence modeling, short-horizon constraint) while retaining its theoretical grounding, yielding a model that is both interpretable and competitive with the strongest deep baselines across 10+ real-world benchmarks. The noise-robustness experiment (Table 4) is particularly valuable: the learned latent kernel preserves neighborhood structure far better than raw time-delayed embeddings under Gaussian noise, which is the regime most real-world sensors operate in. Open questions include principled joint selection of embedding dimension δ_T and delay interval τ, extension to the more flexible S-map variant of EDM, and whether the Transformer-EDM connection can be made into a formal equivalence that guides architecture design beyond post-hoc interpretation.
