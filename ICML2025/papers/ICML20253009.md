## Problem

Existing generalized-smooth bilevel optimization methods (Hao et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024b) only handle generalized-smooth upper-level objectives while still requiring standard smoothness for the lower-level, and restrict the lower-level to (strongly) convex. This paper fills the gap by studying bilevel optimization where both upper- and lower-level objectives are generalized-smooth and the lower-level is nonconvex (weakly convex), a setting that arises in tasks like language model learning and distributionally robust optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253009/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two generalized-smooth objective functions f(x,y) and g(x,y) (with g weakly convex in y) along with an initial point (x⁰, y⁰, θ⁰), and produces a stationary solution (x, y) of the bilevel problem. A key practical distinction is that the algorithm is entirely Hessian/Jacobian-free, requiring only first-order gradient evaluations at each iteration, and operates in a single-loop fashion. The stochastic variant S-PNGBiO additionally draws three independent minibatch samples per iteration for the upper-level, lower-level, and Moreau-envelope subproblem gradients.

## Method

PNGBiO reformulates the nonconvex lower-level via a Moreau envelope v_γ(x,y) = min_θ g(x,θ) + (1/2γ)‖θ−y‖², then solves the resulting constrained problem through an increasing penalty parameter c_t = c̲(t+1)^{1/4}. At each single-loop iteration, it first performs a gradient step on θ to approximate the Moreau-envelope minimizer, then updates x and y using normalized gradient descent (dividing each gradient direction by its ℓ₂ norm), which unifies feature scales and stabilizes convergence under generalized smoothness. The key differentiator from the closely related MEHA method (Liu et al., 2024) is this normalized gradient step, which is essential for handling the unbounded Lipschitz constants inherent in generalized-smooth objectives. The deterministic method achieves O(1/T^{1/4}) convergence to a stationary point (gradient complexity O(ε⁻⁴)), while the stochastic S-PNGBiO achieves O(1/T^{1/6}) with batch size B_t = O(T^{1/2}) (gradient complexity O(ε⁻⁹)).

## Results

Across all three experimental tasks (data hyper-cleaning, hyper-parameter learning, and meta learning), PNGBiO/S-PNGBiO achieves higher test accuracy and lower test loss than all five baselines. On data hyper-cleaning and hyper-parameter learning (MNIST and FashionMNIST), PNGBiO reaches the best final accuracy and lowest loss, though its convergence speed is slower than F2SA due to the absence of momentum acceleration. On meta learning (CIFAR-10), S-PNGBiO achieves the fastest convergence rate and highest test accuracy within a fixed 1800-second CPU runtime, demonstrating robust generalization. The paper reports results as convergence curves (loss and accuracy vs. iterations/time) rather than a single numerical summary table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNGBiO outperforms BOME (Liu et al., 2022), PBGD (Shen & Chen, 2023), and MEHA (Liu et al., 2024) on both final accuracy and loss across all three tasks (data hyper-cleaning, hyper-parameter learning, and meta learning). Against F2SA (Kwon et al., 2023), PNGBiO achieves a better final test accuracy and lower loss, but F2SA converges faster in wall-clock iterations because it employs a momentum acceleration technique absent in PNGBiO. SLIP (Gong et al., 2024b) is outperformed by PNGBiO on the MNIST and FashionMNIST tasks, but is omitted from the CIFAR-10 meta learning experiment due to its high computational cost of computing Hessian/Jacobian matrices. Overall, PNGBiO/S-PNGBiO is the only method that simultaneously handles generalized-smooth upper- and lower-level objectives with a nonconvex lower-level, while remaining Hessian/Jacobian-free and single-loop, giving it a practical advantage over baselines that require standard smoothness or convexity assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) generalized-smooth bilevel optimization (Hao et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024b), which relaxes the upper-level smoothness but still requires a standard-smooth, (strongly) convex lower-level, and (2) nonconvex bilevel optimization via Moreau envelope reformulation (Liu et al., 2024, MEHA), which handles nonconvex lower-levels but assumes standard smoothness for both objectives. The key departure is simultaneously applying generalized smoothness to both the upper- and lower-level objectives while retaining a nonconvex (weakly convex) lower-level, and replacing standard gradient steps with normalized gradient descent to exploit the generalized smoothness structure. The method is also Hessian/Jacobian-free and single-loop, distinguishing it from earlier penalty-based or value-function approaches that require second-order information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contribution fills a concrete gap: machine learning tasks such as language model training and distributionally robust optimization naturally violate standard smoothness, yet no prior bilevel method handled generalized-smooth objectives at both levels with a nonconvex lower-level. The O(1/T^{1/4}) and O(1/T^{1/6}) convergence guarantees provide the first theoretical foundation for this setting. However, the experiments are limited to small-scale problems (logistic regression for hyper-cleaning/hyper-parameter learning, shallow networks on CIFAR-10 for meta-learning), and the authors acknowledge that momentum-based baselines like F2SA converge faster in practice. Open questions include whether the normalized-gradient approach scales to large-scale deep learning bilevel problems and whether the weakly-convex lower-level assumption can be further relaxed.
