## Problem

Multi-label class-incremental learning (MLCIL) faces three simultaneous challenges that existing methods fail to jointly address: label absence (samples only carry labels for the current phase, making prior-phase classes appear as negatives), class imbalance (co-occurrence patterns bias the model toward frequent classes), and privacy constraints (no access to historical samples). While analytic continual learning (ACL) offers closed-form, exemplar-free updates, prior ACL methods are limited to single-label CIL and cannot handle the incomplete-label and imbalanced-distribution structure of multi-label data.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (MS-COCO 2014: 120k images, 80 classes; PASCAL VOC 2007: 10k images, 20 classes) and produces multi-hot class prediction vectors over the cumulative label space. During training, each sample's label vector is incomplete—only the current phase's classes are annotated, while all previously and future-seen classes are masked. The model is evaluated on the joint test set spanning all phases up to t, requiring correct multi-label predictions across the full accumulated class set.

## Method

L3A combines a frozen TResNetM backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k) with a random-projection buffer layer (ReLU(X·W_rand), 8192-dim) and two key modules. The pseudo-label (PL) module feeds current-phase samples through the previous phase's classifier to generate binary pseudo-labels for historical classes (threshold η=0.7), augmenting the incomplete label set so the analytic solution sees a complete representation. The weighted analytic classifier (WAC) solves a ridge-regression objective with sample-specific weights ω_{t,i} = mean of 1/√f^(k) over active classes, then updates the classifier and autocorrelation matrix R_t recursively via a closed-form formula (Theorem 3.1) that is equivalent to joint training over all phases without storing any historical samples. This eliminates backpropagation, task-recency bias, and the need for replay buffers.

## Results

On MS-COCO B0-C10, L3A (exemplar-free) achieves 81.5% average mAP and 77.6% last mAP, surpassing the best exemplar-free method CSC (78.0/72.8) by 3.5/4.8 points and the best replay-based method CSC-R (79.6/74.8) by 1.9/2.8 points. On PASCAL VOC B0-C4, L3A reaches 96.7% average mAP and 94.1% last mAP, exceeding the upper-bound (94.7) on average and outperforming the SOTA exemplar-free method MULTI-LANE (93.5/88.8) by 3.2/5.3 points. L3A uses only 29.4M parameters (TResNet-M) yet outperforms prompt-based methods using ViT-B/16 (86.0M parameters) by 3.1 points in last mAP on MS-COCO B0-C10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L3A outperforms all 16 compared baselines across both MS-COCO and PASCAL VOC protocols. Among exemplar-free MLCIL methods, L3A surpasses CSC (78.0/72.8) by 3.5/4.8 points in Avg/Last mAP on MS-COCO B0-C10, and KRT (74.6/65.9) by 6.9/11.7 points. Against the strongest replay-based MLCIL method CSC-R (20/class: 79.6/74.8), L3A still leads by 1.9/2.8 points without storing any historical samples. On PASCAL VOC B0-C4, L3A (96.7/94.1) exceeds MULTI-LANE (93.5/88.8) by 3.2/5.3 points and CSC-R (2/class: 92.4/87.8) by 4.3/6.3 points. In the prompt-based comparison (Table 2), L3A with TResNet-M (29.4M params) beats MULTI-LANE with ViT-B/16 (86.0M params) by 3.1/2.2 points in Last mAP on B0-C10/B40-C10, and outperforms L2P (73.4/68.0) and CODA-P (74.0/65.4) by 8.1/9.6 and 7.5/12.2 points respectively. Classical CIL baselines (iCaRL, BiC, ER, TPCIL, PODNet, DER++, oEWC, LWF) all trail L3A by 10–50+ points in Last mAP, confirming that single-label CIL strategies are insufficient for the multi-label setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

L3A extends the Analytic Continual Learning (ACL) paradigm introduced by ACIL (Zhuang et al., 2022), which demonstrated that recursive least-squares closed-form solutions can replace backpropagation for exemplar-free class-incremental learning. The key departure is adapting this single-label framework to multi-label CIL, where two additional challenges arise: label absence (addressed via a pseudo-label module inspired by semi-supervised learning) and class imbalance (addressed via a weighted ridge-regression classifier with sample-specific inverse-frequency weights). Unlike prior MLCIL methods that rely on replay buffers (KRT, CSC, PRS, OCDM) or prompt-tuning of large pretrained models (MULTI-LANE, L2P), L3A is fully exemplar-free and operates on a frozen CNN backbone with a linear analytic head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Exemplar-free MLCIL is practically important in privacy-sensitive deployment where historical samples cannot be stored, and L3A's closed-form update avoids the task-recency bias inherent in backpropagation-based continual learning. The method's strong results (outperforming both replay-based and prompt-based SOTA by 2–5% mAP) suggest that analytic learning is a viable and efficient alternative to gradient-based approaches for multi-label incremental settings. Open questions include scalability to very large class spaces (beyond 80 classes), sensitivity to backbone quality (the method relies on a frozen TResNetM), and whether the pseudo-label quality degrades over many phases as classifier errors compound.
