## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized multimodal densities is difficult because the reverse KL divergence is non-convex in Wasserstein space, causing gradient-flow-based methods (CNFs, Langevin dynamics) to collapse to a subset of modes or converge extremely slowly. Existing remedies like importance sampling or sequential Monte Carlo produce non-iid samples or require knowledge of the current approximation's density, which is unavailable for most MCMC samplers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250907/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized target density g(x) (no normalizing constant) and an initial standard Gaussian as the latent distribution. Beyond generating iid samples, it uniquely provides pointwise density evaluations of the generated distribution at arbitrary points in R^d and an estimate of the log-normalizing constant log(Z_g), both obtained as byproducts of the iterative procedure without additional computation.

## Method

The approach alternates between two types of steps: (1) neural JKO steps, where a continuous normalizing flow (neural ODE) is trained to approximate the Wasserstein proximal mapping of the reverse KL functional with an OT-regularization on the velocity field, performing local transport of samples; and (2) importance-based rejection steps, where each sample is accepted with probability min{1, g(x)/(c·p(x))} and rejected samples are resampled from the current model, performing non-local mass redistribution. The key design choice is that the rejection proposal is the model's own current density, avoiding the curse of dimensionality of classical rejection sampling. Density values are propagated through both step types (via the change-of-variables formula for CNFs and an explicit formula from Theorem 4.2 for rejection), enabling density evaluation and log(Z) estimation. Theoretically, the paper proves strong convergence of the sequence of velocity fields to the Wasserstein gradient flow velocity field and monotone decrease of the reverse KL through rejection steps.

## Results

On the energy distance metric, Neural JKO IC achieves the best result across all 9 test distributions, with particularly dramatic improvements on multimodal targets: 1.2×10⁻⁵ on shifted8Modes (vs. 1.3×10⁻¹ for plain Neural JKO), 3.4×10⁻⁵ on shifted8Peaky, and 6.0×10⁻⁴ on GMM-100. For shifted8Modes, shifted8Peaky, and GMM-d, the energy distance is within the same order of magnitude as the reference sampling error (2.4–2.6×10⁻⁵), meaning the generated distribution is statistically indistinguishable from the ground truth. On the log-normalizing constant estimation (Table 2), Neural JKO IC also achieves the closest values to the ground truth (0) across all distributions, e.g., +5.1×10⁻⁶ on shifted8Modes vs. −3.4×10⁻¹ for Neural JKO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural JKO IC outperforms all four baselines (MALA, HMC, DDS, CRAFT) and the ablation (plain Neural JKO without rejection steps) on every single test distribution in the energy distance metric. The most dramatic gains appear on multimodal targets: on shifted8Modes, the method achieves 1.2×10⁻⁵ versus 1.3×10⁻¹ for plain Neural JKO (a ~10,000× improvement), showing that the importance-based rejection steps are critical for correcting wrong mode weights. HMC is the strongest classical baseline (e.g., 4.1×10⁻⁵ on shifted8Modes) but still underperforms Neural JKO IC by a factor of ~3 on that distribution and by orders of magnitude on GMM-100 (3.7×10⁻² vs. 6.0×10⁻⁴). CRAFT (annealed flow transport) degrades severely on high-mode-count GMMs, reaching 2.8×10¹ on GMM-100, while Neural JKO IC maintains 6.0×10⁻⁴. On the log-normalizing constant estimation, Neural JKO IC is the only method that consistently achieves values within 10⁻² of the ground truth (0) across all distributions, whereas CRAFT and DDS show large negative biases (e.g., −9.4 on GMM-200 for DDS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) with OT-Flow regularization (Onken et al., 2021), the Jordan-Kinderlehrer-Otto (JKO) minimizing-movements scheme for Wasserstein gradient flows (Jordan et al., 1998), and recent neural JKO approaches that approximate JKO steps with generative models (Vidal et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024). The key departure is the introduction of importance-based rejection-resampling steps that alternate with the local CNF-driven JKO steps, using the model's own density as the proposal distribution. This design avoids the curse of dimensionality that plagues classical rejection sampling and the non-iid sample issue of sequential Monte Carlo, while providing a non-local correction mechanism that the purely local Wasserstein gradient flow cannot supply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a fundamental limitation of Wasserstein gradient flow-based samplers: mode collapse and incorrect mass allocation in multimodal targets, where the reverse KL is non-convex. By achieving energy distances at the level of the sampling error (i.e., statistically indistinguishable from ground truth) on highly multimodal distributions up to d=1600, and by providing density evaluations alongside samples, the approach enables downstream tasks like log-normalizing-constant estimation and adaptive stopping. The main open limitation is the exponential runtime growth with the number of rejection steps (base 1+r), which the authors acknowledge and plan to address by replacing CNF-based resampling with diffusion models. The theoretical contribution—strong convergence of the neural JKO velocity fields to the Wasserstein gradient flow velocity—provides a rigorous foundation that prior neural JKO papers lacked.
