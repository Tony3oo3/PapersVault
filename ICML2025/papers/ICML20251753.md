## Problem

When bilevel optimization problems are solved by unrolling iterative solvers (e.g., FISTA, proximal gradient with line search), the update mapping changes at every iteration, yet prior convergence guarantees for the resulting derivative iterates either required the update mapping to converge pointwise (Beck, 1994) or provided no rate at all. This paper closes that gap by establishing explicit linear convergence rates for automatic differentiation of time-varying iterative processes, including cases where the update mapping has no pointwise limit.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parametric optimization problem min_x F(x,u) = f(x,u) + g(x,u) where f is C²-smooth and g is partly smooth relative to a C²-manifold, along with a time-varying step size sequence (α_k) and optional extrapolation sequence (β_k). It produces the derivative iterates D x^(k)(u) of the solution mapping ψ(u) together with an explicit rate bound of the form ξ₁(ρ+δ)^(k−K) + ξ₂(k−K)·max(ρ+δ, q_x)^(k−K), where ρ is the spectral radius of the derivative of the update map at the solution and q_x is the linear convergence rate of the primal iterates. The key structural property is that the derivative error decays at the same rate as the primal error.

## Method

The core mechanism rewrites the forward-mode AD recursion (DR_k) as a linear iterative process e^(k+1) = B_k e^(k) + C_k e^(k) + d^(k) and applies a perturbed-contraction analysis (Theorem A.6) to obtain explicit rates. Two new assumptions replace Beck's pointwise-convergence requirement: (i) the sequence of affine maps X ↦ D_x A_k(x*,u*)X + D_u A_k(x*,u*) shares a common fixed-point X*, and (ii) D A_k(x^(k),u*) − D A_k(x*,u*) → 0 (satisfied by equicontinuity). For partly smooth problems, the authors combine the partly-smooth IFT (Lewis, 2002) with activity identification results (Liang et al., 2014, 2017) to show the update mappings of PGD and APG are C¹-smooth near the iterates after a finite number of steps, then invoke the general rate theorem. The final result for both PGD with variable step size and APG/FISTA is that the derivative iterates converge linearly at rate max(ρ+δ, q_x), mirroring the primal convergence rate.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it establishes that the derivative iterates D x^(k)(u*) generated by unrolled automatic differentiation converge to Dψ(u*) at a linear rate of max(ρ+δ, q_x), where ρ is the spectral radius of the update mapping's Jacobian and q_x is the convergence rate of the original iterates. Numerical experiments (Figure 1) on lasso regression (50 random datasets), ℓ₂-regularized logistic regression (50 MADELON instances), and multinomial logistic regression (CIFAR-10) confirm that the derivative error decays at the same rate as the original algorithm error for PGD with fixed and variable step sizes and for APG (FISTA) with β_k=(k−1)/(k+5). The linear convergence of AD for PGD with non-converging variable step sizes and for APG is a new result not covered by prior work (Beck 1994; Mehmood & Ochs 2022).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method baseline comparisons; the 'compared' quantities are the original iterates x^(k)(u*) and their derivative iterates D x^(k)(u*) under the same algorithm. The key comparison is against prior theoretical guarantees: Beck (1994) proved convergence of AD for time-varying maps but required pointwise convergence of D A_k to D A and provided no rate; Mehmood & Ochs (2022) established convergence of AD for APG but required converging step-size and extrapolation sequences and gave no rate. This paper strengthens both by (i) adding a convergence rate guarantee to Beck's setting under a mild additional assumption (Assumption 2.1(ii)), and (ii) extending to the case where A_k has no pointwise limit, thereby covering PGD with non-converging variable step sizes (e.g., line-search) and providing the first linear rate guarantee for AD of APG. The numerical experiments in Figure 1 visually confirm that the derivative error tracks the original error across all five algorithm variants (PGD fixed, PGD_{k_1}, PGD_{k_2}, PGD_{k_3}, APG) on all three problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical line of work on automatic differentiation of iterative processes (Fischer 1991, Gilbert 1992, Beck 1994) by removing the requirement that the time-varying update mapping A_k converge pointwise to a fixed A, and by providing explicit convergence rate guarantees that Beck (1994) could not deliver. It builds directly on the partly smooth framework of Lewis (2002) and the activity identification / linear convergence results of Liang et al. (2014, 2017) to differentiate PGD and APG update mappings, while strengthening the convergence results of Mehmood & Ochs (2022) and Riis (2020), which were limited to converging step-size sequences via the Beck (1994) framework. The paper also connects to the broader bilevel optimization and implicit differentiation literature (Lorraine et al. 2020, Blondel et al. 2022) by providing the theoretical underpinning for unrolled AD as an alternative to implicit differentiation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable theoretical gap: practitioners have long used unrolled AD through FISTA and PGD with variable step sizes (e.g., from line search) in hyperparameter optimization and meta-learning, but lacked convergence rate guarantees for the resulting derivative iterates in the non-converging-step-size regime. The result that the derivative convergence rate mirrors the primal convergence rate provides a clean, actionable criterion for choosing the number of unrolling steps. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic settings (partially addressed by Iutzeler et al. 2024), higher-order methods, and non-convex partly smooth objectives, as well as understanding the practical impact of the 'late-starting' heuristic when the initial iterate is far from the solution.
