## Problem

Consistency training approximates the true velocity field with a single-sample Monte Carlo estimate, introducing a discrepancy with consistency distillation that the authors prove persists even in the continuous-time limit. The standard independent coupling (IC) between data and noise does not resolve this gap, and it is unclear how to improve the one-sample velocity estimate without access to the true diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a noise vector z and a noise level σ_t and produces a generated RGB image in a single forward pass. During training, the method operates on intermediate noisy points x_t = x_* + σ_t z at various resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, 64×64 for CelebA, LSUN Church, and FFHQ). The key unusual aspect is that the consistency model itself is used during training to predict an endpoint from an intermediate point, and this predicted endpoint is re-coupled with the same noise vector to form the training pairs, creating a self-referential training loop.

## Method

The paper introduces generator-augmented coupling (GC): given an intermediate point x_t = x_* + σ_t z sampled under IC, the (stop-gradient) consistency model f̂ predicts an endpoint x̂_t = f̂(x_t, σ_t), which is then coupled with the same noise z to define new training pairs x̃_t = x̂_t + σ_t z and x̃_{t+1} = x̂_t + σ_{t+1} z. Theoretically, GC reduces the proxy regularizer R̃_t (measuring the gap between the velocity field and its one-sample estimator) asymptotically faster than IC (Theorem 2), and decreases the data-noise transport cost for both small and large t (Corollaries 1–2). Practically, the authors propose a joint learning strategy where a single model is trained from scratch on a mixture of IC and GC trajectories controlled by a parameter μ, with loss L_GC-μ(θ) = μ·L_GC(θ) + (1−μ)·L_CT(θ). This one-stage approach avoids the need for a separately pre-trained endpoint predictor and yields faster convergence and lower FID than both standard IC and minibatch-OT baselines across four image datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, iCT-GC (μ=0.5) achieves a FID of 5.95, substantially outperforming iCT-IC (7.42) and iCT-OT (6.75), while also improving KID (0.26 vs 0.44 and 0.36) and IS (9.10 vs 8.76 and 8.86). On CelebA 64×64, GC achieves FID 11.74 versus 15.82 (IC) and 13.63 (OT). On ImageNet 32×32, GC reaches FID 13.99 compared to 14.89 (IC) and 14.13 (OT). In the ECT fine-tuning setting, GC (μ=0.3) consistently improves FID over IC across CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and ImageNet 64×64 in both short (4k) and long (100k) training regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iCT-IC (Song & Dhariwal, 2024), GC wins decisively on all four datasets in the iCT setting, with the largest FID gap on CelebA (11.74 vs 15.82, a 26% relative improvement) and CIFAR-10 (5.95 vs 7.42). Against iCT-OT (Pooladian et al., 2023; Dou et al., 2024), GC outperforms on CIFAR-10 (5.95 vs 6.75), ImageNet 32×32 (13.99 vs 14.13), and CelebA (11.74 vs 13.63), but slightly trails on LSUN Church FID (9.88 vs 9.71) while still achieving a higher IS (2.14 vs 2.00). In the ECT fine-tuning setting (Geng et al., 2024), GC (μ=0.3) consistently beats ECT-IC on CIFAR-10 (3.74 vs 4.11 FID at 100k steps), FFHQ (8.51 vs 9.68), and ImageNet 64×64 short training (10.31 vs 10.82), though it underperforms on ImageNet 64×64 long training (6.39 vs 5.84). The paper also shows that GC accelerates convergence relative to both IC and OT baselines, reaching comparable FID scores in fewer training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency model framework (Song et al., 2023; Song and Dhariwal, 2024) by identifying and exploiting a theoretical gap between consistency training and consistency distillation that persists even in the continuous-time limit, contrary to prior suggestions. It departs from the independent coupling (IC) paradigm shared by both consistency training and flow matching, and from minibatch optimal transport approaches (Pooladian et al., 2023; Dou et al., 2024) that approximate deterministic couplings at prohibitive cubic cost. The key conceptual move is using the consistency model itself as a proxy for the true diffusion flow to construct generator-augmented couplings (GC), creating a self-referential training loop that avoids external OT solvers while still reducing the variance of the velocity field estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that consistency training and distillation objectives are fundamentally different in continuous time (Theorem 1) corrects a working assumption in the field and provides a principled target (the regularizer R(θ)) for improving training. Practically, the joint learning strategy (mixing IC and GC with factor μ) is a drop-in modification requiring only one extra forward pass, making it easy to adopt in existing consistency model pipelines. Limitations include the dependence on the model's own quality for GC construction (μ=1 degrades performance), the assumption of an ideal predictor in theoretical guarantees, and evaluation restricted to unconditional generation at moderate resolutions. Open questions include whether GC benefits scale to large conditional models (e.g., text-to-image) and whether the optimal μ is dataset- and architecture-dependent.
