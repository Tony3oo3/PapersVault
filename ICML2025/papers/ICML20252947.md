## Problem

Text-based CAD editing—modifying an existing CAD model according to a natural-language instruction—remains unexplored. Prior work either generates design variations without text-based control or generates CAD from text without conditioning on an existing model, and the required triplet training data (original CAD, instruction, edited CAD) does not naturally exist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252947/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form textual editing instruction and an original CAD model encoded as a tokenized Sketch-and-Extrude (SE) operation sequence (following the FlexCAD tokenization), and produces the edited CAD model in the same SE sequence format. Due to the long-tail distribution in DeepCAD, training is restricted to models with at most 3 SE pairs, and the maximum token length is 1024. The task is formulated as a sequence-to-sequence generation problem where the input concatenates the instruction with the original CAD sequence.

## Method

CAD-Editor decomposes text-based CAD editing into a locate-then-infill pipeline: a locating stage autoregressively generates a masked CAD sequence (inserting <mask> tokens at regions to modify), and an infilling stage fills those masked regions with concrete edits, both using Llama 3-8B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA. Training data is synthesized automatically: Hnc-CAD generates paired CAD models via design variation, and LVLMs (GPT-4o for rendered images, LLaMA-3-70B for SE sequences) produce editing instructions through a stepwise captioning strategy (describe → identify differences → compress). Ground-truth masks for the locating stage are derived via the Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) algorithm between original and edited sequences. A selective dataset, where human annotators pick the best model-generated candidate, is used for a second LoRA fine-tuning pass on the infilling stage to further improve instruction alignment and generation quality.

## Results

On the DeepCAD test set, CAD-Editor achieves a Valid Ratio of 95.6%, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: Text2CAD at 84.8%). It also attains the best JSD of 0.65 (×10², lower is better), the lowest Chamfer Distance of 1.18 (×10²), the highest D-CLIP score of 0.11 (×10²), and the highest human evaluation score of 43.2. In the scaling experiment (Appendix E), CAD-Editor consistently outperforms baselines across SE lengths 1–5 and generalizes to the Fusion 360 Gallery dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Editor outperforms all five baseline categories on every metric where overlap exists. Against the most direct competitors—GPT-4o-Basic and GPT-4o-IC (foundation models prompted for CAD editing)—CAD-Editor improves VR by 11.1 and 11.1 points respectively, reduces JSD from 1.10/0.70 to 0.65, lowers CD from 2.30/1.55 to 1.18, and flips D-CLIP from negative (−1.08/−0.11) to positive (0.11), indicating that the generated edits actually align with the textual instruction rather than deviating from it. The human evaluation gap is especially stark: CAD-Editor scores 43.2 versus 15.6 for GPT-4o-IC and 7.22 for GPT-4o-Basic. Against design-variation baselines (SkexGen, Hnc-CAD, FlexCAD) and the text-to-CAD baseline (Text2CAD), CAD-Editor achieves the highest VR (95.6% vs. 74.3–84.8%) and the lowest JSD (0.65 vs. 1.72–2.39), while also being the only method that supports text-guided editing. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the locate-then-infill decomposition and the selective human-curated dataset each contribute meaningful gains over a direct seq2seq fine-tuning approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task—text-based CAD editing—by combining two existing threads: design variation generation (Hnc-CAD, SkexGen, FlexCAD), which modifies CAD models without text-based control, and text-based CAD generation (Text2CAD, CAD Translator), which creates models from text but ignores existing designs as constraints. It adopts the SE-operation sequence tokenization from FlexCAD (Zhang et al., 2024b) and repurposes Hnc-CAD's auto-completion as a data synthesis engine, while introducing a locate-then-infill decomposition over a fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B backbone. The stepwise captioning strategy for LVLM-based instruction generation extends prior LLM/LVLM data synthesis work (Khan et al., 2024b) from single-model captioning to pairwise difference summarization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing text-based CAD editing and providing an end-to-end pipeline (data synthesis + model), this work opens a practical path toward natural-language-driven CAD modification, potentially lowering the barrier for non-expert designers. Key limitations include reliance on costly LVLMs for data synthesis, restriction to Sketch-and-Extrude representations with at most 3 SE pairs in the main experiments, and a test set synthesized from the same distribution as training data, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to truly novel editing scenarios. Future work on more complex CAD structures, cost-efficient data pipelines, and benchmarks reflecting real user workflows would strengthen the contribution.
