## Problem

In bandits with general long-term constraints (beyond resource-consumption/packing), standard primal-dual frameworks relying on no-regret guarantees fail to achieve sublinear constraint violations, and existing remedies require a priori knowledge of Slater's parameter ρ. This paper demonstrates that no-regret is fundamentally insufficient for general constraints and identifies the stronger property—weak adaptivity—that enables constraint satisfaction without knowing ρ.

## Input / Output

The framework is modular: the primal algorithm Alg_P receives bandit feedback (utility of only the chosen action) while the dual algorithm Alg_D receives full feedback over the Lagrangian. The primal must be 2-scale-free and weakly adaptive; the dual is OGD on ℝ≥0^m with a learning rate set by the primal's regret bound. In the contextual variant, the primal additionally observes a context z_t before acting and uses m+1 online regression oracles to estimate reward and constraint functions, with performance governed by their cumulative ℓ2-error.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-bounding lemma: when the primal is 2-scale-free and weakly adaptive (o(T) regret on every sub-interval) and the dual is OGD with a carefully chosen learning rate, the ℓ1-norm of dual variables is automatically bounded by 13m/ρ without explicit projection or knowledge of ρ. The proof proceeds by contradiction—exceeding the threshold forces the primal's scale-free regret bound to imply large cumulative utility over the interval where multipliers are large, contradicting the dual's OGD regret bound. This yields best-of-both-worlds guarantees: sublinear regret against the best feasible randomized strategy in the stochastic setting, and a tight competitive ratio of ρ/(1+ρ) in the adversarial setting, with sublinear constraint violations in both. For concrete instantiations, EXP3-SIX serves as the primal for discrete bandits, while the Foster–Rakhlin reduction with regression oracles provides the required 2-scale-free weakly adaptive primal for contextual bandits with linear constraints.

## Results

The paper establishes best-of-both-worlds guarantees for bandits with m general long-term constraints without requiring knowledge of Slater's parameter ρ. In the adversarial setting, the framework achieves a competitive ratio of ρ_Adv/(1+ρ_Adv) against the best unconstrained strategy in hindsight (Theorem 7.2), which is shown to be optimal by a matching lower bound (Theorem 4.2). In the stochastic setting, it guarantees sublinear regret Õ((m/ρ_Stoc)² · R̄^P_{T,δ}(X)) against the best feasible randomized strategy (Theorem 7.3). For the concrete multi-armed bandit instantiation with EXP3-SIX, violations are Õ(m²/ρ · √(KT)) and the reward gap is Õ(m²/ρ² · √(KT)) in both settings (Corollary 8.2). For contextual bandits with linear constraints, the first no-α-regret guarantees under adversarial contexts are Õ(m³/ρ · Err · √(KT)) (Corollary 8.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's framework strictly improves upon prior LagrangeBwK-based approaches in three key dimensions. Compared to Immorlica et al. (2022) and Castiglioni et al. (2022a), which require explicit projection onto [0, 1/ρ] and thus a priori knowledge of Slater's parameter, this work eliminates that requirement entirely through the self-bounding lemma (Lemma 6.2), where dual variables are automatically bounded by 13m/ρ via the interaction of weakly adaptive primal and dual algorithms. Compared to Castiglioni et al. (2022b), which handles the stochastic setting but requires an initial T^{1/2}-round estimation phase to estimate ρ, this paper achieves the same stochastic guarantees without any estimation overhead. Compared to Castiglioni et al. (2023), which applies the weakly adaptive technique only to the specific case of one budget and one ROI constraint, this work generalizes to m arbitrary constraints. For contextual bandits with linear constraints (CBwLC), the paper provides the first adversarial-context guarantees (Corollary 8.5), extending Slivkins et al. (2023b) and Han et al. (2023) which only handle stochastic or nonstationary contexts and require knowledge of ρ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the LagrangeBwK primal-dual framework (Immorlica et al., 2022) from packing/knapsack constraints to arbitrary long-term constraints, and extends the weakly-adaptive regret technique of Castiglioni et al. (2023) (which handled one budget plus one ROI constraint) to m general constraints. The key conceptual departure is showing that the standard no-regret property, which suffices for BwK, is provably insufficient for general constraints (via a clean counterexample), and that weakly adaptive regret is the correct primitive. The self-bounding lemma eliminates the need for a priori knowledge of Slater's parameter ρ, which prior frameworks (Balseiro et al., 2022; Castiglioni et al., 2022a) required for explicit projection of dual variables. For the contextual bandit application, the paper extends the Foster-Rakhlin (2020) regression-oracle reduction to satisfy the 2-scale-free weakly-adaptive requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regret guarantee for adversarial bandits with general (non-packing) constraints, and the first no-α-regret guarantee for contextual bandits with linear constraints under adversarial contexts. The conceptual result that no-regret is insufficient and weakly-adaptive regret is necessary challenges a standard assumption in the primal-dual online learning literature and provides a clean diagnostic for when Lagrangian frameworks may silently fail. A key limitation is the requirement for 2-scale-free primal algorithms, which are harder to construct than 1-scale-free ones; the authors note that 1-scale-free algorithms would yield tighter bounds (1/ρ instead of ρ/(1+ρ)) but are less abundant. Open questions include characterizing which algorithms are 2-scale-free, extending to non-stationary or partially adversarial settings, and whether the self-bounding property can be leveraged in other constrained online optimization problems.
