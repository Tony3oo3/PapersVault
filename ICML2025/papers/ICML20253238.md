## Problem

Existing camera-based 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods face two key limitations: (1) temporal fusion via attention mechanisms incurs quadratic computational complexity that becomes prohibitive at the large spatial resolutions required for dense voxel prediction, and (2) the 'Internal Occupancy Vacancy' (IOV) problem, where the centers of large foreground objects (buses, trucks, construction vehicles) are systematically predicted as empty voxels due to semantic ambiguity in voxel-wise classification that ignores instance-level aggregativity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253238/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4 frames of 6 surround-camera RGB images (1600×896 for the base model, 800×448 for the small model) sampled at a frame interval of 2 (covering a 3-second time span). It outputs a dense 3D semantic occupancy volume at 200×200×16 resolution (0.4m voxel size, spanning ±40m in X/Y and -1m to 5.4m in Z) with 17 semantic classes, and can additionally project predictions onto LiDAR point clouds for semantic segmentation evaluation.

## Method

CSV-Occ introduces two core mechanisms. First, the Cross State Space Module (Cross SSM) extends the Mamba SSM to accept two input sequences simultaneously—current-frame and historical-frame 3D voxel features—by deriving that the SSM's C variable plays an analogous role to the query tensor in cross-attention, enabling multi-sequence interaction with linear complexity in a cascaded k-step architecture. Second, the Voting-based Enhancement Mechanism (VEM) operates in two stages: Relative Central Regression (RCR) uses an MLP to predict the 3D offset from each foreground voxel to its instance's center voxel, and the Parallel Interaction Strategy (PIS) then performs cross-attention between voting voxels and their voted center (with feature-descriptor sorting to order interactions), updating all foreground voxel features from coarse to fine. The full pipeline is: ResNet-101/50 + FPN → deformable-attention-based Time-based View Transformation with cascaded Cross SSM → 3D deconvolution upsampling → coarse occupancy head → VEM refinement → final occupancy head, trained with focal + Lovász + scene-class affinity losses plus an MSE loss on the center offsets.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes validation set, CSV-Occ-B (ResNet-101, 4 frames) achieves a state-of-the-art mIoU of 44.93 for 3D semantic occupancy prediction, outperforming the previous best OctreeOcc (44.02) by 0.91 points. The smaller CSV-Occ-S variant (ResNet-50) reaches 42.37 mIoU, competitive with larger-backbone methods like RB-OCC (42.24) and PanoFormer (42.13). When projected onto LiDAR points for semantic segmentation on nuScenes, CSV-Occ-B achieves 73.4 mIoU, surpassing all camera-based baselines (best prior: PanoOcc R101 at 71.6) and approaching LiDAR-native methods such as RPVNet (77.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSV-Occ-B (4F) achieves the highest mIoU of 44.93 on Occ3D-nuScenes, beating the closest competitor OctreeOcc (4F, 44.02) by 0.91 points and RB-OCC (8F, 42.24) by 2.69 points, while using only 4 frames versus 8 for RB-OCC. The gains are most pronounced on small/medium foreground classes: pedestrian (39.14 vs. 30.65 for OctreeOcc), bicycle (32.76 vs. 29.93), and traffic cone (33.49 vs. 29.99), attributed to the Voting-based Enhancement Mechanism addressing Internal Occupancy Vacancy. On LiDAR semantic segmentation (nuScenes), CSV-Occ-B reaches 73.4 mIoU, outperforming PanoOcc R101 (71.6), TPVFormer (68.9), and BEVFormer (56.2) among camera-based methods, though it remains below LiDAR-native approaches like RPVNet (77.6) and Cylinder3D++ (76.1). The smaller CSV-Occ-S (R50, 42.37 mIoU) matches or slightly exceeds RB-OCC (42.24) and PanoFormer (42.13) despite using a lighter backbone and fewer frames, demonstrating parameter efficiency. In the instance-level panoptic segmentation comparison (Table 9), CSV-Occ-Instance (PQ 48.3) underperforms PanoOcc (PQ 62.1), which benefits from explicit 3D bounding box supervision that CSV-Occ deliberately excludes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BEVFormer paradigm (learnable voxel queries + deformable attention for multi-camera 3D perception) by replacing its quadratic-complexity temporal self-attention (TSA) with a Cross State Space Module derived from Mamba's selective SSM formulation. The key conceptual departure is showing that the SSM's output projection matrix C can be obtained by mapping a *different* input sequence (the current frame's voxel features), enabling multi-sequence interaction with linear complexity. The Voting-based Enhancement Mechanism is a novel addition not directly inherited from prior occupancy methods, which treat voxels independently and ignore instance-level aggregativity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Internal Occupancy Vacancy (IOV) as a distinct failure mode that worsens with more input frames is a useful diagnostic for the occupancy prediction community, and the VEM mechanism provides a principled coarse-to-fine remedy. The linear-complexity Cross SSM is practically important for scaling occupancy prediction to higher spatial resolutions where attention-based temporal fusion becomes prohibitive. However, the paper acknowledges that VEM degrades background class performance due to training confusion, and instance-level occupancy prediction (PQ 48.3) remains substantially below PanoOcc (62.1), leaving open the question of how to achieve instance-aware occupancy without explicit bounding box supervision.
