## Problem

Existing tuning-free diffusion model watermarks embed watermarks in only a single domain (spatial or frequency) of the initial Gaussian noise, making them highly vulnerable to even minor image manipulations—e.g., Gaussian Shading drops from 100% to 64% detection accuracy with just a 3° rotation. This fragility limits practical deployment where images routinely undergo rotation, cropping, compression, and adversarial attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251363/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For injection, the method takes a randomly sampled Gaussian noise map z_T ∈ R^{4×64×64} (corresponding to 512×512 pixel images in Stable Diffusion's latent space) and an l-bit binary watermark ω (l < 4×64×64, typically 256 bits), producing a watermarked image via standard LDM generation. For detection, it takes a potentially manipulated image, performs DDIM inversion (50 steps, guidance scale 0, empty prompt) to estimate the original noise, and outputs a fused detection score r ∈ R along with the estimated l-bit watermark. The GNR component operates on the binary signal map (signs of the estimated noise) rather than the full noise values.

## Method

GaussMarker injects two watermarks sequentially into the Gaussian noise: a multi-bit spatial-domain watermark via sign-flipping guided by a ChaCha20-shuffled up-sampled signal map, and a zero-bit frequency-domain watermark as a ring-like Fourier feature masked by a circle of radius r_f=4. Detection extracts separate scores from each domain and fuses them with a 2-layer MLP trained on 200 samples. The key robustness component is the Gaussian Noise Restorer (GNR), a 30M-parameter UNet trained model-independently (without any diffusion model) to restore the binary signal map after transformations (rotation ±180°, cropping 70–100%, sign flipping). GNR exploits the observation that the sign pattern of the estimated noise maintains geometric invariance under image transformations during DDIM inversion, and is trained with binary cross-entropy plus negative-sample learning to avoid the shortcut of always outputting the watermarked pattern. This design achieves >99% TPR@1%FPR and bit accuracy across eight distortions and four advanced attacks on three Stable Diffusion versions.

## Results

On SD V2.1, GaussMarker achieves an average TPR@1%FPR of 0.997 and bit accuracy of 0.990 across eight image distortions (Clean, Rotate, JPEG, C&S, R. Drop, Blur, S. Noise, G. Noise, Bright), surpassing the best baseline Stable Signature by 4.6% in TPR and 11.5% in bit accuracy. This performance is consistent across SD V2.0 (0.998/0.991) and SD V1.4 (0.998/0.991). Under four advanced attacks (VAE compression, diffusion regeneration, UnMarker), GaussMarker maintains TPR@1%FPR of 1.000 on VAE-1/VAE-2/UnMarker and 0.667–0.853 on diffusion regeneration, while all other tuning-free baselines drop below 0.25 on regeneration and UnMarker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussMarker outperforms all seven baselines on average TPR@1%FPR and bit accuracy across all three SD versions. Against the strongest tuning-based baseline, Stable Signature (Fernandez et al., 2023), GaussMarker gains 4.6% TPR and 11.5% bit accuracy on average, and further pulls ahead under advanced attacks where Stable Signature drops to 0.312 TPR on VAE-1 compression versus GaussMarker's 1.000. Among tuning-free methods, RingID (Ci et al., 2024) achieves the highest TPR on SD V2.1 (0.883 avg) but is inconsistent across SD versions and drops to 0.044 under UnMarker; GaussMarker maintains 0.997+ TPR uniformly. Gaussian Shading (Yang et al., 2024) and PRC (Gunn et al., 2024) are catastrophically vulnerable to rotation (TPR drops to 0.018 and 0.010 respectively at 75°), while GaussMarker retains 0.997. Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023) fails under C&S (0.048 TPR) and LatentTracer (Wang et al., 2024) is essentially non-functional on SD V1.4 (0.000 TPR even on clean images). The classic DwtDetSvd (Cox et al., 2007) baseline achieves near-zero TPR under most distortions, confirming that post-processing watermarks are inadequate for diffusion model provenance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tuning-free diffusion model watermarking line initiated by Tree-Ring (frequency-domain) and Gaussian Shading/PRC (spatial-domain), which each embed watermarks in only one domain of the initial Gaussian noise. Its key departure is a pipelined dual-domain injector that sequentially embeds a multi-bit spatial watermark and a zero-bit ring-shaped frequency watermark, inspired by classical spatial+frequency image watermarking (Shih & Wu, 2003). A second departure is the model-independent Gaussian Noise Restorer (GNR), a small UNet trained without any LDM to restore signal maps after rotation/cropping, which prior tuning-free methods lack entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With governments (EU AI Act, California AB-3211, US Executive Order) mandating provenance watermarks on generative AI outputs, a tuning-free method that requires no model fine-tuning and a single model-agnostic GNR is practically attractive for deployment across multiple SD versions. However, the method still relies on DDIM inversion (ODE-based samplers only) and cannot protect released model weights, and the regeneration attack via a separate diffusion model still degrades TPR to ~0.7–0.85, leaving a meaningful gap for the strongest adversarial setting. Open questions include whether the dual-domain editing can be made visually lossless and whether GNR-style restoration generalizes to non-affine manipulations.
