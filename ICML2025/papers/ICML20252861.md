## Problem

Dynamic data selection reduces training costs but inherently limits data diversity, capping generalization at relatively high selection ratios. Data augmentation can restore diversity, yet existing selection methods prioritize representative or challenging samples without considering augmentation suitability, and applying augmentation indiscriminately to complex or noisy samples can introduce ambiguity. No prior work unifies these two techniques into a single training framework that selects samples specifically suited for augmentation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images paired with text class labels, using a task-specific CNN (e.g., ResNet-18/50) for online density estimation via HNSW nearest-neighbor queries and a frozen CLIP model with lightweight MLP adapters for precomputed semantic consistency scores. The output is a dynamically pruned training subset (at a user-specified selection ratio of 20%–90%) where each retained sample receives a single TrivialAugment transformation, and the resulting augmented subset is used to train the target classifier. The density distribution updates every epoch while the consistency distribution is computed once before training begins.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a joint distribution p_sel(x) = p_ρ(x) · p_con(x), where p_ρ is a dynamically evolving density score (mean ℓ₂ distance to k HNSW neighbors, Min-Max normalized) and p_con is a static semantic consistency score (cosine similarity between CLIP image and text embeddings, also Min-Max normalized). Samples with high joint scores—i.e., low-density yet semantically consistent—are selected and augmented with a single lightweight TrivialAugment operation, filling intra-cluster gaps and sharpening decision boundaries without amplifying noise. The HNSW graph supports O(log n) online updates, keeping per-epoch overhead negligible, while the CLIP adapters are fine-tuned for 15 epochs with InfoNCE loss before training starts. This design ensures augmentation is applied to meaningful sparse regions rather than outliers, yielding lossless performance at 50% data on ImageNet-1k and superior robustness under 20% label noise.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18, the method achieves 95.5%/78.9% top-1 accuracy at 50% selection ratio, matching or exceeding full-dataset performance (95.6%/78.2%). On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-50, it reaches 44.9% at 30% selection, nearly matching the full-dataset 45.0%. On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50 at 60% selection, the method achieves 76.9% top-1 accuracy (vs. 76.4% full dataset) while reducing training cost by ~40% (88.2 vs. 140.0 GPU-hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and selection ratio. The closest competitor is InfoBatch (Qin et al., 2024), which the method surpasses by +0.4% on CIFAR-10 (95.5 vs. 95.1 at 50%), +0.8% on CIFAR-100 (78.9 vs. 78.1 at 50%), and +2.7% on Tiny-ImageNet (44.9 vs. 42.2 at 30%). Among static selection methods, MoSo (Tan et al., 2024) is the strongest, yet the proposed method still leads by +5.3% on CIFAR-100 at 50% (78.9 vs. 73.6) and +3.7% on Tiny-ImageNet at 30% (44.9 vs. 31.2). Dynamic baselines UCB and ε-Greedy (Raju et al., 2021) trail by 0.8–1.1% on CIFAR-10 and are not reported on Tiny-ImageNet. Notably, the method exceeds full-dataset accuracy on CIFAR-100 (78.9% vs. 78.2%) and Tiny-ImageNet at 50–70% selection (47.0–49.4% vs. 45.0%), and on ImageNet-1k at 60% selection (76.9% vs. 76.4%), indicating that the joint density–consistency selection with targeted augmentation actively improves generalization rather than merely preserving it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dynamic data pruning (InfoBatch, UCB, ε-Greedy) and data augmentation (TrivialAugment, AutoAugment), two lines of work that have been developed largely in parallel. It departs from prior selection methods that prioritize representative or challenging samples by instead targeting samples specifically suited for augmentation, using a joint distribution of local density (estimated via HNSW) and CLIP-based semantic consistency (with lightweight adapters). The conceptual departure is that augmentation is no longer applied uniformly or independently of selection; rather, the selection criterion is explicitly designed to identify where augmentation will be most beneficial (sparse, semantically coherent regions) while filtering out noisy or ambiguous low-density outliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 50% training cost reduction on ImageNet-1k with lossless performance, and consistent gains across ResNet, ViT, and Swin architectures, makes this directly relevant to practitioners training large-scale models. The robustness to label noise and corruption (4% improvement over baselines) addresses a real-world pain point. However, the method's reliance on a pretrained CLIP model limits applicability to domains where such multimodal priors are unavailable or mismatched (e.g., medical imaging), and the evaluation is confined to image classification, leaving open whether the density-plus-consistency selection principle transfers to detection, segmentation, or other tasks.
