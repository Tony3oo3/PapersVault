## Problem

Grokking — the sudden transition from memorization to perfect generalization in overparameterized networks — has been demonstrated only on small synthetic tasks like modular arithmetic. Real-world knowledge graphs are too sparse to naturally provide the high ratio of multi-hop (inferred) to single-hop (atomic) facts required to trigger grokking, and no prior work has shown how to extend this phenomenon to large-scale factual reasoning. This paper bridges that gap by asking whether targeted data augmentation can push real-world multi-hop QA into the regime where Transformers form internal generalizing circuits.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-hop questions over a Wikipedia-derived knowledge graph in either structured form (short triplets like 'City -- country -- France') or unstructured form (full Wikipedia paragraphs), and produces a single factual answer. A key detail is that the training corpus is not the raw dataset: it is an augmented version where LLM-generated synthetic atomic and inferred facts have been injected to raise the per-relation inferred-to-atomic ratio φ_r above the grokking threshold (~3.6), and some of these synthetic facts are intentionally or accidentally factually incorrect. Evaluation is split into in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) splits, where OOD queries involve entity-relation combinations never co-occurring in training.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. First, an LLM-based augmentation procedure (using GPT-4o and o1-mini) systematically generates new atomic facts and multi-hop inferred questions for the 2WikiMultiHopQA comparison and composition tasks, expanding the dataset from roughly 120–200 atomic facts to 800–1,000 atomic facts paired with 5,000–8,000 inferred facts, thereby pushing φ_r to 6.25–8.0. Second, an 8-layer GPT-2-style Transformer (768 hidden, 12 heads) is trained from scratch with AdamW and weight decay 1 for up to ~300k steps until the characteristic late-phase OOD accuracy jump appears. The paper provides theoretical bounds (Lemma 1, Lemma 2) showing that without augmentation, φ_{n,r} is asymptotically capped at b_r^{n−1}, making it impossible for sparse real-world KGs to reach the grokking threshold. A surprising empirical finding is that even factually incorrect synthetic data strengthens emergent reasoning circuits, because it forces the model to exploit relational structure rather than memorize surface patterns.

## Results

On the structured 2WikiMultiHopQA comparison task, the grokked GPT-2-small (124M parameters) achieves 100% in-distribution (ID) and 96% out-of-distribution (OOD) accuracy, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (87%) and o1-mini (88%). On the compositional task, the grokked model reaches 93% ID accuracy but only 7% OOD, indicating that longer multi-hop chains remain challenging. Averaged across both tasks, the grokked model attains 97% ID and 52% OOD accuracy, compared to 56% and 60% for GPT-4o and o1-mini respectively (which lack a clear ID/OOD split due to extensive pre-training on Wikipedia text).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the grokked GPT-2-small against two strong pretrained baselines: GPT-4o and o1-mini. On the comparison task, the grokked model decisively wins, reaching 96% OOD accuracy versus 87% for GPT-4o and 88% for o1-mini — a gap of roughly 8–9 percentage points. On the compositional task, the grokked model achieves 93% ID accuracy (far exceeding GPT-4o's 25% and o1-mini's 32%), but its OOD performance (7%) remains weak, suggesting that 2–3 hop chains are harder to internalize than single-attribute comparisons. In aggregate, the grokked model's 97% ID accuracy surpasses both baselines, while its 52% OOD average is slightly below o1-mini's 60% (though the latter's OOD split is not well-defined). The key takeaway is that a 124M-parameter model trained from scratch with targeted data augmentation can match or exceed frontier models on structured multi-hop factual reasoning, particularly in the comparison setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grokking phenomenon—originally demonstrated on synthetic algorithmic tasks like modular arithmetic (Power et al., 2022)—to real-world factual knowledge graphs, using the inferred-to-atomic ratio thresholds identified by Wang et al. (2024) as a design target for data augmentation. It departs from prior multi-hop QA approaches (retrieval-based, GNN-based, chain-of-thought) by pursuing purely implicit reasoning: the Transformer is expected to form internal generalizing circuits during prolonged training rather than relying on external scaffolding. The key methodological departure is the use of LLM-generated synthetic data (including intentionally incorrect facts) to raise the φ ratio above the grokking threshold, a strategy not explored in either the grokking or the knowledge-graph-completion literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that grokking-based circuit formation can be induced on real-world Wikipedia-derived data, suggesting that data distribution engineering (rather than model scale) may be the critical lever for multi-hop reasoning. However, the results are narrow: grokking emerges only for structured comparison queries, while compositional tasks and unstructured paragraphs show little to no OOD improvement, and the comparison with GPT-4o/o1-mini is confounded by their pretraining exposure to Wikipedia text. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer reasoning chains, domain-specific corpora, and whether the counterintuitive benefit of factually incorrect synthetic data can be harnessed without introducing harmful factual drift in high-stakes applications.
