## Problem

Existing adversarially robust conformal prediction methods either inflate prediction sets excessively to preserve coverage, are tailored to specific attack types, or suffer from poor computational efficiency. No prior work directly optimizes prediction set size under adversarial conditions in an attack-agnostic manner, leaving a gap between maintaining statistical coverage guarantees and producing practically useful (compact) prediction sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250106/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes normalized RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 84×84 for mini-ImageNet) as input and produces a prediction set — a subset of class labels defined as all classes k whose logit f_k(x) exceeds a threshold τ. The threshold τ is computed as the (1−α)-quantile of true-class logits on a held-out calibration set, yielding a marginal coverage guarantee of at least 1−α (set to 90% in experiments). Unlike standard classifiers that output a single label, the output is a set-valued prediction whose size reflects model uncertainty.

## Method

The paper introduces OPSA, an attack that maximizes a soft prediction set size M_T = Σ_k σ((f_k(x+ε) − f_y(x+ε))/T) by treating the true-class logit as the internal reference threshold, making it agnostic to the defender's significance level α. The perturbation is optimized via projected gradient ascent (Adam) within an ℓ∞ ball of radius r. For defense, OPSA-AT formulates a bi-objective loss combining a classification term (sigmoid-approximated indicator for true-class inclusion minus count of wrongly included classes) and a size term (soft set size), weighted by λ. During training, each mini-batch is split into training and calibration subsets: OPSA generates adversarial examples for the training subset, while the exact (1−α)-quantile threshold τ is computed from the calibration subset, and both are updated iteratively. This hard-quantile calibration (as opposed to sigmoid-approximated thresholds in prior conformal training) combined with the OPSA-specific adversarial examples yields a model that simultaneously minimizes prediction set size and maintains coverage under diverse attacks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Table 1), OPSA-AT achieves the smallest average prediction set size of 3.11±0.03 under the OPSA attack, compared to 3.37 for OPSA-ST, 4.02 for TRADES, and 7.34 for BETA-AT, while maintaining coverage at 89.50% (target 90%) and the lowest SSCV of 0.03. On CIFAR-100 (Table 2), OPSA-AT reduces size to 32.07±0.25 under OPSA attack versus 32.30 for TRADES/OPSA-ST and 75.25 for BETA-AT, with SSCV of 0.03. As an attack, OPSA consistently produces the largest prediction set sizes across all defense models (e.g., 7.29 under FGSM, 5.40 under MART, 7.34 under BETA-AT on CIFAR-10), confirming it as the most effective uncertainty-inducing attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a defense, OPSA-AT achieves the smallest prediction set size across all attacks on CIFAR-10: 3.11 under OPSA (vs. 3.37 OPSA-ST, 4.02 TRADES, 5.40 MART, 7.34 BETA-AT), 2.50 under FGSM (vs. 2.56 OPSA-ST, 3.20 BETA-AT), and 1.90 under Square (vs. 1.96 OPSA-ST, 2.16 TRADES). On CIFAR-100, OPSA-AT similarly leads with size 32.07 under OPSA attack versus 32.30 for TRADES and 75.25 for BETA-AT, and 17.00 under BETA attack versus 20.38 for TRADES. As an attack, OPSA produces the largest prediction set sizes against most defenses on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 7.29 under FGSM vs. 7.06 for PGD⁴⁰; 5.40 under MART vs. 5.19 for PGD⁴⁰; 7.34 under BETA-AT vs. 7.71 for Auto), confirming its superiority in inducing uncertainty. The only exceptions are BETA-AT (where Square and Auto slightly exceed OPSA) and OPSA-ST (where PGD⁴⁰ marginally exceeds OPSA at 3.46 vs. 3.37). Coverage remains near the 90% target for all methods, and OPSA-AT consistently achieves the lowest or near-lowest SSCV, indicating the most stable coverage across prediction set sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005; Sadinle et al., 2019) by integrating adversarial training in a way that prior robust-CP methods (ARCP, PRCP, VRCP) did not: rather than post-hoc adjusting thresholds or using randomized smoothing to preserve coverage, it trains the classifier directly against a purpose-built uncertainty-maximizing attack (OPSA). The soft set-size objective M_T is borrowed from conformal training (Stutz et al., 2021), but the paper departs from that line by using a hard quantile for the threshold and by framing the defense as a bi-objective (coverage + size) optimization rather than a single sigmoid-approximated loss. The min-max adversarial training structure follows Madry et al. (2017), while the attack-agnostic formulation contrasts with prior CP-specific adversarial training (ATUR, Luo et al. 2024) that was tied to particular attack families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attack-agnostic design of OPSA-AT addresses a practical gap: safety-critical deployments face unknown attack vectors, yet most robust-CP methods are calibrated to specific perturbation types or norms. The consistent finding that OPSA inflates prediction sets more than FGSM, PGD, Auto-Attack, or BETA across all baseline defenses suggests a useful stress-test for conformal classifiers. However, the evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 and mini-ImageNet with ResNet backbones, the BETA-AT comparison is partially confounded (it uses a similar training-time attack), and the paper acknowledges that optimal mini-batch sizes (500–1000) were not fully explored due to hardware limits. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet or vision transformers, whether it generalizes to non-THR non-conformity scores, and whether the bi-objective formulation admits tighter theoretical guarantees beyond the standard CP exchangeability assumption.
