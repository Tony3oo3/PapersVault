## Problem

While scaling Transformer-based generators has consistently improved image and video generation, the visual tokenizers that compress pixels into latent space have rarely been the focus of scaling efforts. It remains unclear how specific auto-encoder design choices—bottleneck size, encoder capacity, and decoder capacity—differentially affect reconstruction fidelity versus downstream generative performance, and whether scaling the tokenizer yields the same gains as scaling the generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250861/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViTok tokenizes RGB images (256p or 512p) and 16-frame video clips (128p or 256p at 8 fps) into continuous latent codes via a 3D convolution with spatial-temporal stride q×p×p, producing a latent tensor of shape (T/q)×(H/p)×(W/p)×c where the total dimensionality E = L×c controls the compression ratio. Unlike discrete VQ-VAE tokenizers, the noised latents z+ε can be fed directly into a DiT generator without additional patchification. For generation, class labels condition a DiT-L or DiT-XL model that denoises in the ViTok latent space, with quality measured via gFID/gIS over 50k samples.

## Method

ViTok is an asymmetric Vision Transformer auto-encoder that replaces CNN backbones with a Llama-enhanced ViT (SwiGLU activations, 3D Axial RoPE), pairing a compact encoder with a larger decoder. Training follows a two-stage protocol: Stage 1 optimizes MSE + LPIPS + KL losses for stable auto-encoding, then Stage 2 freezes the encoder and fine-tunes the decoder with a StyleGAN discriminator to improve perceptual quality. The central empirical finding is that the total number of floating points in the latent code (E) is the dominant bottleneck for reconstruction, but generation performance is non-monotonic in E—each patch size has an optimal E, and excessive channel width c degrades generation. Scaling the encoder provides no reconstruction benefit and can harm generation, while scaling the decoder improves reconstruction but offers minimal generation gains, indicating that scaling efforts should concentrate on the generator rather than the tokenizer.

## Results

ViTok S-B/16 achieves state-of-the-art 256p image reconstruction with rFID of 0.50 on ImageNet-1K and 3.94 on COCO-2017, outperforming SD-VAE (0.78/4.63) and SDXL-VAE (0.68/4.07) while using only 34.8 GFLOPs versus 162.2 for SD-VAE. At 512p, ViTok achieves rFID 0.18 on ImageNet-1K, matching SD-VAE (0.19) with 4x fewer FLOPs. For 128p video reconstruction on UCF-101, ViTok S-B/4x8 achieves SOTA rFVD of 8 (vs. 22.0 for MAGViT and 20 for LARP-L-Long) with rPSNR of 30.11 and rSSIM of 0.923. In class-conditional video generation, ViTok achieves gFVD of 27, substantially outperforming MAGViTv2 (58), LARP (57), and W.A.L.T (46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViTok S-B/16 clearly outperforms all CNN-based continuous tokenizers on rFID: 0.50 vs. 0.78 (SD-VAE), 0.68 (SDXL-VAE), 0.81 (OAI), and 2.02 (Cosmos-CI) on ImageNet-1K 256p, while using 2–5× fewer FLOPs (34.8 vs. 162.2 GFLOPs for SD-VAE). However, ViTok trades off some pixel-level fidelity, scoring lower on rPSNR (24.36 vs. 25.08 for SD-VAE, 26.04 for SDXL-VAE) and rSSIM (0.747 vs. 0.834 for SDXL-VAE), consistent with the paper's Finding 4 that GAN fine-tuning shifts the decoder toward generative quality at the cost of compression fidelity. For video reconstruction on UCF-101 128p, ViTok S-B/4x8 achieves rFVD of 8, dramatically beating MAGViT (22.0), LARP-L-Long (20), and MAGViTv2 (16), while also leading in rPSNR (30.11) and rSSIM (0.923). In class-conditional image generation, ViTok is competitive but not dominant: gFID of 2.45 at 256p is slightly behind DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and TiTok-B (2.48), and 3.41 at 512p trails DiT-XL/2 (3.04), confirming the paper's central finding that scaling the tokenizer alone does not improve generation. For video generation, ViTok achieves a clear SOTA gFVD of 27, roughly halving the error of the next-best methods (W.A.L.T at 46, LARP at 57, MAGViTv2 at 58).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE/VQGAN auto-encoding paradigm (Kingma & Welling, 2013; Esser et al., 2021) by replacing the standard CNN backbone with a Vision Transformer enhanced with Llama-style components (SwiGLU, 3D Axial RoPE), building on ViViT for video and MAE for architecture design. While prior work (TiTok, LARP, MAGViT) has explored ViT-based tokenizers, this paper's primary contribution is a systematic empirical study of how scaling the encoder, decoder, and bottleneck independently affects reconstruction versus generation—a question that has not been rigorously addressed. The tokenizer is paired with Diffusion Transformers (DiT) for generation, following the latent diffusion framework established by Rombach et al. (2022) and Peebles & Xie (2023), but the paper's focus on tokenizer scaling rather than generator scaling represents a deliberate departure from the dominant research direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway—that scaling the auto-encoder tokenizer does not improve downstream generative performance, and that compute is better invested in the generator—provides clear guidance for practitioners allocating training resources. The identification of E (total latent floating points) as the dominant reconstruction bottleneck, independent of architecture size or FLOPs, offers a simple design principle for tokenizer configuration. However, the study is limited to class-conditional generation on relatively small datasets (ImageNet-1K, UCF-101), leaving open whether these scaling conclusions hold for text-conditional or open-domain generation where the generator's modeling complexity is substantially higher. The finding that the decoder can act as a partial generator (Finding 4) raises interesting questions about the boundary between tokenization and generation that remain unresolved.
