## Problem

Computational methods for designing RNA sequences that bind to specific proteins typically require either large collections of known interacting RNA sequences for each target protein or accurate RNA structure predictions, severely limiting their applicability to novel or poorly characterized targets. This paper addresses the gap by developing a generative approach that produces protein-binding RNA sequences using only the target protein's sequence and 3D structure, without protein-specific experimental data or RNA structural information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251217/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein 3D structure (obtained via AlphaFold2, with pLDDT > 70% required) and its amino acid sequence as input, and generates variable-length RNA sequences (up to 50 nucleotides, mean ~45.6) as output. The protein structure is consumed through rigid backbone frames (C, Cα, N atoms per residue) fed into a geometric attention mechanism, while the protein sequence is tokenized over 20 standard amino acids. The generated RNA is produced bidirectionally from a central anchor point rather than sequentially from a fixed start, and no RNA secondary structure is required at any stage.

## Method

The core contribution is Bidirectional Anchored Generation (BAnG), a novel factorization of the joint sequence distribution that generates tokens outward from a central anchor position, alternating between left and right directions, rather than sequentially from a sequence end. Two special anchor tokens (<ancl>, <ancr>) are inserted before the anchor position to provide a fixed starting context and prevent information leakage between the two generation directions. The RNA-BAnG model is a transformer with separate protein and nucleotide modules (10 blocks each, 14.5M parameters), where the protein module uses unmasked self-attention plus Geometric Attention (adapted from AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention) to encode 3D structural information, and the nucleotide module uses a custom bidirectional attention mask that restricts each token's representation to only previously generated tokens in the BAnG factorization. Training is two-stage: first on 3M standalone RNA sequences from RNAcentral to learn general sequence patterns, then on 123K protein-nucleotide interaction pairs from PDB/PPI3D to learn protein conditioning, using cross-entropy loss with top-k (k=4) sampling at inference.

## Results

On the synthetic DoubleBind task, BAnG achieves 0.97 motif recovery, substantially outperforming autoregressive (0.53), IAnG (0.54), and iterative ESM3-style methods (0.04–0.05). On the biological RNAcompete evaluation (71 samples), RNA-BAnG generates more high-affinity sequences than random for 56/71 samples, with an area under the threshold-dependent performance curve of 0.57 (vs. 0.39 for random and 0.88 for the positive experimental set). Generated sequences exhibit high diversity (0.93 ± 0.13) and novelty (0.99 ± 0.01) relative to training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM3-style iterative generation (Hayes et al., 2024), BAnG achieves a decisive advantage on the DoubleBind task (0.97 vs. 0.54 for IAnG variants and 0.04–0.05 for vanilla iterative methods), with the gap driven by the train/inference mismatch in demasking-based approaches. Against GenerRNA (Zhao et al., 2024), RNA-BAnG produces a higher proportion of high-affinity sequences across all six directly comparable RNA Compendium samples (e.g., 0.91 vs. 0.64 for ELAVL1, 0.74 vs. 0.59 for SRSF1 sample 107), and does so without requiring protein-specific fine-tuning data. Against RNAFLOW (Nori & Jin, 2024), RNA-BAnG outperforms on the majority of test samples; RNAFLOW exhibited a severe G/C nucleotide bias (62%/32%) and was approximately 50× slower, generating high-affinity sequences for only a couple of proteins. Overall, RNA-BAnG wins on all three comparisons, with the largest margins over the iterative ESM3 baselines on the synthetic task and over RNAFLOW on the biological evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in conditional RNA generation, where most prior methods (VAEs, LSTMs, transformers, diffusion/flow-matching models) require extensive protein-specific experimental RNA data or RNA structure prediction tools. RNA-BAnG instead leverages AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention for geometric protein representation and introduces a novel bidirectional anchored generation (BAnG) factorization that generates sequences outward from a binding-motif anchor rather than from sequence ends. The generative strategy is conceptually distinct from both standard autoregressive (left-to-right) and iterative masked (ESM3-style) approaches, and the transformer architecture with RoPE self-attention and sinusoidal cross-attention positions is tailored to this bidirectional factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for protein-specific RNA interaction data and RNA structure prediction, RNA-BAnG substantially broadens the set of proteins for which computational RNA design is feasible, moving beyond the narrow set of well-characterized targets. However, the evaluation relies on DeepCLIP as a proxy scorer rather than experimental binding assays, and the test set is limited to 71 samples from RNAcompete, leaving the true biological performance unvalidated. Open questions include whether the bidirectional anchored generation principle transfers to other biomolecular design tasks, whether integrating experimental feedback (e.g., active learning loops) can close the gap between predicted and actual affinity, and whether the method scales to longer or structured RNAs beyond the 50-nucleotide limit tested here.
