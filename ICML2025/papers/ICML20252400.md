## Problem

Existing reward-guided generation in diffusion models performs a single denoising pass from fully noised to clean states, leaving no mechanism to correct errors introduced by approximate value functions at high noise levels. This is particularly problematic for masked diffusion models where demasked tokens are permanently locked in, and for tasks with hard constraints where inaccurate value functions cause systematic constraint violations. The paper asks whether iterative refinement—repeatedly noising and reward-guided denoising—can overcome these fundamental limitations of single-shot guidance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained discrete diffusion model (EvoDiff for proteins, CNN-based masked diffusion for DNA), a black-box or differentiable reward function, and optional hard constraints (e.g., cell-type activity thresholds), and produces optimized sequences entirely at test-time without any model fine-tuning. For protein design, rewards are computed via ESMFold structure prediction (cRMSD, ss-match, globularity, symmetry); for DNA design, rewards come from Enformer-based activity oracles across multiple cell lines. The algorithm operates on batches of N sequences with L importance-sampling candidates per step, using a partial noise level K (set to 10% of total steps T) to balance exploration against value-function accuracy.

## Method

RERD alternates two steps per iteration: (1) partial noising from the current design to noise level K using the forward diffusion policy, and (2) reward-guided denoising from K back to 0. The denoising step combines local importance sampling at each intermediate step k=K,…,1—where L candidates are drawn from the pre-trained policy and weighted by exp(r(x̂₀(z))/α)—with a global SMC-style resampling at k=1 that selects among the batch based on final rewards. This hybrid IS+SMC strategy avoids the diversity collapse of pure SMC (resampling at every step) while enabling elimination of poor samples that pure IS would retain, and bears structural resemblance to evolutionary algorithms (mutation + selection). Theoretically, the output distribution is guaranteed to be p^(α) ∝ exp(r(·)/α) p^pre(·), and the small noise level K ensures value-function approximations remain accurate by avoiding the high-noise regime where they degrade. For constrained tasks, the algorithm initializes from feasible designs and the small K prevents deviation from the constraint region, enabling effective hard-constraint satisfaction that single-shot methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On protein design (Table 1), RERD achieves a median cRMSD of 1.68 Å (P95: 0.96 Å), dramatically outperforming SMC (8.9/5.1), SVDD (8.2/4.6), and GA (6.3/3.01). For secondary-structure matching (ss-match), RERD reaches P50=0.86 and P95=0.96, compared to 0.63–0.70 for baselines. On cell-type-specific DNA design (Table 2), RERD achieves median activity scores of 7.9 (HepG2), 7.4 (K562), and 5.5 (SKNSH), roughly 3–6× higher than the best single-shot baselines (SVDD: 2.3, 1.3, 1.7; GA: 2.3, 2.2, 1.9), while maintaining comparable per-residue log-likelihood.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RERD consistently outperforms all three baselines across every task and metric. Against SVDD (single-shot derivative-free guidance), RERD improves cRMSD from 8.2 to 1.68 Å (P50) and ss-match from 0.66 to 0.86 (P50), while maintaining similar log-likelihood. Against SMC (single-shot SMC-based guidance), the gap is even larger: cRMSD drops from 8.9 to 1.68 and DNA activity in HepG2 rises from 1.2 to 7.9. Against GA (genetic algorithm with diffusion-based mutation), RERD wins on cRMSD (1.68 vs. 6.3), ss-match (0.86 vs. 0.70), and all three DNA cell-type tasks (e.g., 7.9 vs. 2.3 in HepG2), though GA is competitive on globularity (−1.35 vs. −1.29) and symmetry (0.21 vs. 0.34). The advantage is most pronounced on tasks with hard structural constraints (cRMSD) and multi-constraint objectives (cell-type-specific DNA), where single-shot methods struggle due to value-function approximation errors at high noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-guided generation framework for diffusion models (classifier guidance, SVDD's importance-sampling decoding, SMC-based conditional sampling) by introducing an iterative refinement loop that alternates partial noising with reward-guided denoising. Conceptually, it bridges the single-shot guidance literature with evolutionary algorithms (mutation via noising + selection via resampling) and iterative refinement in masked language models, but is the first to formalize this specifically within the diffusion model framework. The theoretical analysis builds on the soft optimal policy formulation from RL/SMC literature, showing that the iterative procedure provably samples from the desired tilted distribution exp(r/α)·p_pre.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative refinement approach addresses a fundamental practical limitation of single-shot guidance: errors in value function approximation at high noise levels are irreversible, especially in masked diffusion where demasked tokens are frozen. This is particularly impactful for tasks with hard constraints (cell-type-specific DNA, structural targets) where single-shot methods frequently fail to satisfy feasibility. Open questions include the optimal noise level K and number of iterations S (currently heuristic), computational cost scaling with S, and whether the framework extends to continuous diffusion or small-molecule design as the authors note as future work.
