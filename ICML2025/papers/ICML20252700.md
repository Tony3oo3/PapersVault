## Problem

In reinforcement learning with delayed observations, existing belief-based methods recursively forecast the unobserved state step-by-step, causing approximation errors to compound exponentially with delay length. This fundamental limitation severely degrades performance in environments with long delays, while augmentation-based alternatives suffer from the curse of dimensionality as the augmented state space grows with Δ. The paper asks whether belief forecasting can be reformulated to avoid recursive error accumulation entirely.

## Input / Output

DFBT consumes a window of Δ timesteps reformulated into Δ tokens of the form {s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-Δ+i}, r_{t-Δ+i}}_{i=0}^{Δ-1}, combining the last observable state, the intervening actions, and the corresponding reward signals. It simultaneously outputs the full predicted state sequence {ŝ_{t-Δ+1}, ..., ŝ_t} in a single forward pass rather than Δ sequential predictions. These predicted states then feed into the SAC critic for N-step bootstrapping (default N=8), where the critic targets are computed on the DFBT-predicted states rather than the true (unavailable) states.

## Method

DFBT reframes belief forecasting as a sequence modeling problem: a 10-layer transformer with 4 attention heads and 256 hidden dimension processes the Δ-token input and autoregressively predicts all Δ unobserved states in parallel, trained with a negative log-likelihood (or MSE for deterministic environments) loss on offline D4RL trajectories. The key architectural insight is that the attention mechanism captures long-range temporal dependencies across the delay window in a single pass, eliminating the recursive chain that causes compounding errors. Theoretically, the performance degeneration bound for DFBT is |I^direct| ≤ |I^true| + L_V·ε_direct, whereas recursive methods incur |I^recursive| ≤ |I^true| + L_V·((1−L_P^Δ)/(1−L_P))·ε_P, showing the error grows only linearly (or is bounded) rather than exponentially with Δ. DFBT-SAC integrates the pre-trained DFBT with Soft Actor-Critic, using multi-step bootstrapping on DFBT-predicted states to improve sample efficiency, and is trained in two stages: offline belief learning followed by online policy optimization in the reconstructed delay-free environment.

## Results

On MuJoCo with deterministic 128-step delays, DFBT-SAC achieves normalized returns of 0.41 (HalfCheetah-v2), 0.20 (Hopper-v2), and 0.40 (Walker2d-v2), substantially outperforming the best augmentation-based baseline (ADRL: 0.14, 0.07, 0.08) and the best belief-based baseline (D-Dreamer: 0.15, 0.09, 0.08). Under stochastic U(1,128) delays, DFBT-SAC outperforms the second-best baseline by approximately 144% (HalfCheetah-v2: 0.39 vs 0.16), 180% (Hopper-v2: 0.14 vs 0.05), and 100% (Walker2d-v2: 0.30 vs 0.15). DFBT also maintains a stable inference speed of ~4 ms across all delay lengths, while recursive methods (DATS, D-Dreamer) degrade to 15–27 ms at 128 delays.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFBT-SAC is compared against three augmentation-based methods (A-SAC, BPQL, ADRL) and three belief-based methods (DATS, D-Dreamer, D-SAC). Against all belief-based baselines, DFBT-SAC wins decisively at every delay setting: at 128 deterministic delays it achieves 0.41 vs 0.15 (D-Dreamer, best belief baseline) on HalfCheetah-v2, a 2.7× improvement. Against augmentation-based methods, DFBT-SAC is competitive at short delays (8 steps: 0.35 vs ADRL's 0.44 on HalfCheetah-v2) but pulls ahead sharply as delays grow to 32 and 128, where augmentation-based methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality (e.g., 0.41 vs 0.14 for ADRL at 128 delays). Under stochastic U(1,128) delays, the gap widens further: DFBT-SAC reaches 0.39 on HalfCheetah-v2 while the best baseline (DATS/D-Dreamer) only reaches 0.16. The single-step variant DFBT-SAC(1) confirms that multi-step bootstrapping (N=8) provides additional gains, e.g., 0.41 vs 0.40 on HalfCheetah-v2 at 128 delays.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant recursive belief-forecasting paradigm in delayed RL (DATS, D-Dreamer, D-SAC), which applies an approximate dynamics function Δ times sequentially and suffers exponential error accumulation. Instead, it reframes belief estimation as a sequence-modeling problem solvable by a Transformer (DFBT) that predicts all Δ unobserved states in parallel, drawing conceptual inspiration from decision transformers and trajectory transformers. The RL component builds directly on Soft Actor-Critic, augmented with multi-step bootstrapping on the DFBT-predicted states, while the theoretical analysis extends standard Lipschitz-continuity bounds from model-based RL to compare recursive versus direct forecasting error propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why existing belief-based methods degrade at long delays (exponential compounding) and shows that direct forecasting removes this exponential factor, yielding a delay-independent error bound. Practically, DFBT-SAC maintains strong performance at 128-step delays where all recursive baselines collapse, and its inference cost stays constant (~4 ms) regardless of delay length. Key open questions remain: the belief is trained offline and frozen (or fine-tuned), so online belief learning and the sample complexity of direct forecasting are left for future work, and all experiments are confined to MuJoCo continuous-control tasks.
