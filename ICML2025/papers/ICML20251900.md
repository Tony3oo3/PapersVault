## Problem

Recent deep architectures for hierarchical clustering (e.g., DeepECT, TreeVAE) suffer from two critical limitations: they fail to scale to datasets with hundreds or thousands of clusters due to high computational demands, and they produce leaf-level clusterings that are substantially worse than those of dedicated non-hierarchical models. This paper asks whether a simple post-hoc procedure on pre-trained flat models can outperform these specialized deep approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the raw logits (an N×K matrix) from any pre-trained flat clustering model or supervised classifier, requiring no access to internal representations, no fine-tuning, and no pairwise distance computation. It outputs a full dendrogram with K leaves constructed in K−1 agglomerative merging steps, executable on a CPU in under a minute even for ImageNet-scale (1,000-class) datasets. The same procedure applies identically to unsupervised clustering logits and supervised classifier logits, making it applicable to black-box or API-based models.

## Method

L2H is a lightweight agglomerative algorithm that builds a hierarchy purely from logits via a masked-softmax mechanism. At each of K−1 iterations, it first scores every current group by aggregating the predicted probabilities of its member data points (using a chosen aggregation function Λ, e.g., mean per cluster), then selects the lowest-scoring group G* for merging. In the second stage, it recomputes cluster assignments for data points in G* using a masked softmax that zeros out all clusters in G*, effectively asking 'where would these points go if their current clusters were unavailable?'; the group G† receiving the highest average reassigned probability is merged with G*. This avoids pairwise distance computation entirely and leverages the logit geometry of the pre-trained model as a proxy for cluster relatedness, retaining the backbone's leaf-level clustering quality by construction.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, L2H-TURTLE achieves NMI 0.985, ARI 0.989, and Dendrogram Purity 0.988, substantially outperforming all deep hierarchical baselines (TreeVAE: NMI 0.414, DP 0.341; DeepECT: NMI 0.006, DP 0.101). On CIFAR-100, L2H-TURTLE reaches NMI 0.917 and DP 0.803, while TreeVAE (limited to 20 leaves) achieves only NMI 0.199 and DP 0.103. On Food-101, L2H-TEMI attains NMI 0.917 and DP 0.801 versus TreeVAE's NMI 0.114 and DP 0.016. The L2H algorithm itself runs in under 0.01 minutes on a CPU for all three datasets, compared to 364 minutes for TreeVAE on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2H dominates all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against agglomerative clustering (scikit-learn, Ward's linkage on PCA embeddings), L2H-TURTLE improves NMI from 0.074 to 0.985 on CIFAR-10 and from 0.223 to 0.917 on CIFAR-100. Against HypHC (hyperbolic hierarchical clustering), the gap is even wider: L2H-TURTLE achieves DP 0.988 vs. 0.104 on CIFAR-10. DeepECT is the weakest baseline, producing only 2–3 leaves (degenerate shallow trees) and near-chance NMI (0.006 on CIFAR-10, 0.003 on Food-101). TreeVAE is the strongest deep hierarchical competitor but is limited to 20 leaves on CIFAR-100/Food-101 due to computational constraints, yielding NMI 0.199 and DP 0.103 on CIFAR-100 versus L2H-TURTLE's 0.917 and 0.803. In the supplementary comparison (Table 6), L2H-TURTLE also outperforms Lowe et al. (2024) (UMAP + Ward's agglomerative on CLIP embeddings) on CIFAR-100, achieving NMI 0.917 vs. 0.753 and DP 0.803 vs. 0.445, even though Lowe et al. is evaluated on the test set (an unfair advantage for that baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the recent line of work on deep specialized architectures for hierarchical clustering (DeepECT, TreeVAE, HypHC) by arguing that such purpose-built models are unnecessary. Instead of designing new deep architectures, L2H repurposes the logits of any pre-trained flat clustering model (e.g., TURTLE, TEMI) and applies a simple agglomerative merging procedure based on masked softmax reassignment to construct a hierarchy. Conceptually, it is closest to classical agglomerative clustering but operates on logit-space probabilities rather than pairwise distances in an embedding space, and it extends naturally to supervised classifiers. The method carves new ground by showing that the hierarchical structure is already implicitly encoded in the logits of a well-trained flat model, requiring no fine-tuning or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: L2H reduces hierarchical clustering on ImageNet-scale data to under a minute on CPU, compared to hours for TreeVAE, while achieving markedly better performance across all metrics. This makes hierarchical clustering broadly accessible to practitioners who already have a pre-trained flat model, including via API access to proprietary models. Key limitations include the assumption that logits serve as a reliable proxy for cluster relatedness (which may fail with poorly calibrated models) and the lack of automatic level selection in the inferred hierarchy. Open questions include extending the approach beyond vision (e.g., text, tabular data), investigating whether the logit-based relatedness measure can be formalized more rigorously, and developing strategies to automatically identify the most informative levels of the hierarchy without manual inspection.
