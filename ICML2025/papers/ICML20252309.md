## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting in class-incremental continual learning causes significant accuracy drops on previously learned tasks, and existing methods (regularization, replay, parameter isolation) still struggle to fully mitigate this. While prior work showed that early-layer representations change less during continual learning, this stability has not been exploited to improve performance or reduce inference cost. The paper asks whether lightweight classifiers attached to these stable intermediate layers can simultaneously reduce forgetting and enable early-exit inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252309/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100 or 224×224 ImageNet-100) and produces a single class label as output. Internally, the network contains N classifiers (typically 6 for ResNet32/ResNet18, up to 18 for VGG19, or 11 for ViT-base), each emitting a full probability distribution over all classes seen so far. During dynamic inference, the network evaluates classifiers sequentially and exits early as soon as any classifier's maximum class probability exceeds a user-set confidence threshold λ; if no classifier meets the threshold, the prediction with the highest confidence across all classifiers is returned, enabling a tunable accuracy-vs-computation trade-off without retraining.

## Method

The paper attaches lightweight auxiliary classifiers (ACs) to intermediate backbone layers—pooling + linear for CNNs, LayerNorm + linear on the [CLS] token for ViT—and trains them end-to-end alongside the main classifier, with per-AC loss weights proportional to each classifier's share of total FLOPs to prevent early-layer overfitting. The key insight, validated via CKA analysis, is that early-layer representations undergo significantly less change across tasks, so ACs on those layers retain discriminative power for old classes even when the final classifier forgets. At inference, two modes are supported: static inference (select the prediction with the highest confidence across all N classifiers) and dynamic inference (early-exit when confidence ≥ λ, falling back to static selection otherwise). The approach is method-agnostic: the same AC architecture and training recipe is plugged into any existing CL method (LwF, EWC, ER, BiC, SSIL, ANCL, LODE, DER++, GDumb) by replicating that method's loss across all classifiers, yielding an average ~10% relative accuracy gain and 10–60% inference cost reduction without additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Adding auxiliary classifiers (ACs) to 11 continual learning methods yields a consistent average relative improvement of ~10–13% across CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 (5- and 10-task splits). The largest absolute gains appear for exemplar-free methods: FT improves by +9.49 pp (CIFAR-100×5) and +11.67 pp (ImageNet-100×5), while EWC gains +10.02 pp and +11.73 pp respectively. Exemplar-based methods (ER, LODE, DER++) show moderate gains of +4–7 pp, and distillation-based methods (BiC, SSIL, ANCL) benefit less (+1–3 pp). Through dynamic inference, AC-enhanced networks match single-classifier accuracy at only 50–70% of the computation on CIFAR-100 and 40–60% on ImageNet-100, with performance saturating at 80–90% compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC-enhanced variants outperform all 9 baseline methods across every dataset and task-split configuration tested. The largest relative gains are observed for exemplar-free baselines: FT improves by +9.49 pp on CIFAR-100×5 and +11.67 pp on ImageNet-100×5, while EWC gains +10.02 pp and +11.73 pp respectively, suggesting that ACs are most beneficial when no replay or distillation mechanism is present. Exemplar-based baselines (ER, LODE, DER++) show moderate but consistent improvements of +4–7 pp, with LODE benefiting most (+6.31 to +6.80 pp). Distillation-based methods (BiC, SSIL, ANCL) see the smallest gains (+0.7 to +3.4 pp), which the authors attribute to distillation potentially hindering the diversity that ACs exploit. Notably, even the strongest baselines (BiC at 62.55% on ImageNet-100×5, SSIL at 60.54%) are surpassed by their AC-enhanced counterparts (65.22% and 63.89% respectively). The paper also demonstrates that AC-enhanced models reduce average forgetting (e.g., FT drops from 64.85 to 50.04 on CIFAR-100×5) and enable dynamic inference that matches baseline accuracy at 50–70% computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: continual learning (CL) methods that combat catastrophic forgetting (LwF, EWC, BiC, DER++, etc.) and early-exit classifier architectures from the network efficiency literature (Panda et al., 2016; Kaya et al., 2019). While prior CL work (Liu et al., 2020b; Yan et al., 2024) has explored multiple classifiers for online CL via ensembling or self-distillation, and early-exit work assumes monotonically improving accuracy through layers, this paper is the first to systematically analyze intermediate representation stability in CL (via CKA) and show that auxiliary classifiers at early layers can outperform the final classifier on old tasks—particularly in exemplar-free settings. The approach departs from standard early-exit inference by using a confidence-based selection rule rather than always falling back to the last classifier, since in CL the best classifier varies by task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a simple, plug-and-play enhancement (adding lightweight linear classifiers at intermediate layers) that consistently yields ~10% relative accuracy gains across 11 CL methods and 4 benchmark configurations, while simultaneously enabling 10–60% inference cost reduction through dynamic early exit. This dual performance-and-efficiency benefit is particularly valuable for resource-constrained deployment scenarios. Limitations include the focus on offline class-incremental image classification, a ~50% training time overhead for small networks, and the fact that gains are smaller for methods already incorporating distillation (ANCL, BiC, SSIL). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to online CL, other modalities, and whether the training overhead can be reduced through selective gradient propagation.
