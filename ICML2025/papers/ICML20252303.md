## Problem

Existing diffusion-based purification (DBP) methods apply a single constant noise level t* to all samples during the forward diffusion process, yet the paper demonstrates that the optimal t* varies across samples: too little noise fails to remove adversarial perturbations, while too much noise destroys semantic information. This sample-shared strategy leads to a suboptimal accuracy-robustness trade-off that cannot be resolved by simply tuning the global constant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 224×224 ImageNet-1K) that may be either clean or adversarially perturbed, and outputs a purified image passed to a pre-trained classifier (WideResNet, ResNet-50, or Swin-Transformer) for label prediction. Unusually, before the forward diffusion step, each sample is first scored by a pre-trained score network to compute its Expected Perturbation Score (EPS) norm, which determines the per-sample noise injection level. The reweighting process adds only ~0.5 s (CIFAR-10) or ~5 s (ImageNet-1K) of inference overhead per image.

## Method

SSNI replaces the fixed noise level t* with a sample-specific t(x) computed as f(‖EPS(x)‖, t*), where EPS(x) is the expected perturbation score aggregated over a range of noise levels (t^S=20) to reduce sensitivity to a single reference level. Two reweighting functions are provided: a linear mapping that normalizes the EPS norm against a clean-data reference, and a sigmoid-based non-linear function with a temperature parameter τ that smoothly saturates at the maximum noise level. A bias term b is added to allow the reweighted t* to exceed the original t*, ensuring sufficient noise for heavily perturbed samples. The framework is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play: it wraps any existing DBP method (DiffPure, GDMP, GNS) by only modifying the forward noise-injection step while leaving the reverse denoising process and classifier unchanged. Theoretically, the paper proves that sample-shared DBP is a special case of SSNI and that SSNI strictly expands the purification range Ω_SH ⊊ Ω_SI.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 against adaptive white-box PGD+EOT ℓ∞(ε=8/255), SSNI-N consistently improves both standard and robust accuracy across all three DBP baselines. The largest gains are on standard accuracy: +3.58% for DiffPure (89.71→93.29% on WRN-28-10), +1.63% for GDMP (92.45→94.08%), and +3.45% for GNS (90.10→93.55%). Robust accuracy also improves, most notably for GDMP (+4.23% on WRN-28-10, +2.48% on WRN-70-16). On ImageNet-1K (ℓ∞, ε=4/255), SSNI-N boosts DiffPure's robust accuracy from 30.34% to 33.66% and GNS's standard accuracy from 70.18% to 72.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSNI-N is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of three existing DBP methods: DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), GDMP (Wang et al., 2022), and GNS (Lee & Kim, 2023). Against DiffPure, SSNI-N improves standard accuracy by 3.58% (WRN-28-10) and 3.58% (WRN-70-16) while also gaining 0.65% and 0.32% in robust accuracy respectively. Against GDMP, the gains are more pronounced on robustness: +4.23% (WRN-28-10) and +2.48% (WRN-70-16), alongside standard accuracy improvements of +1.63% and +2.47%. Against GNS, SSNI-N delivers the largest standard accuracy boost (+3.45% and +4.43%) with modest robust accuracy gains (+0.40% and +0.06%). In all cases, SSNI-N wins on standard accuracy and either wins or ties on robust accuracy, demonstrating that sample-specific noise injection improves the accuracy-robustness trade-off without sacrificing either metric. The improvements are consistent on ImageNet-1K, where SSNI-N adds +2.02% standard and +3.32% robust accuracy to DiffPure under ℓ∞(ε=4/255).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based purification (DBP) line of work initiated by DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), GDMP (Wang et al., 2022), and GNS (Lee & Kim, 2023), all of which inject a fixed, sample-shared noise level t* during the forward diffusion process. The key departure is the observation that an optimal t* is sample-specific, motivated by the score-based generative modeling literature (Song & Ermon, 2019) and the Expected Perturbation Score (Zhang et al., 2023), which the authors repurpose as a lightweight proxy for estimating how much noise each sample requires. Rather than proposing a new purification pipeline, SSNI is framed as a general reweighting module that plugs into any existing DBP method, preserving their reverse-process designs while replacing the constant t* with a score-norm-adaptive one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single t* is suboptimal across samples challenges a universal design assumption in all current DBP methods, and the proposed fix is lightweight (~0.5s overhead on CIFAR-10) and architecture-agnostic, making it immediately adoptable. However, the gains are moderate (2–4% standard accuracy, 0–4% robust accuracy) and the reweighting functions are hand-designed rather than learned, leaving open whether data-driven or learned reweighting could yield larger improvements. The paper also notes that the maximum EPS noise level t^S remains shared across samples, and that the linear/non-linear reweighting forms are likely suboptimal—both are flagged as future work. For practitioners building DBP defenses, this is a practical, low-cost upgrade; for the broader field, it opens the question of whether other hyperparameters in generative-model-based defenses should also be sample-adaptive.
