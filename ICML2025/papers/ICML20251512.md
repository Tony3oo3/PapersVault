## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models focus exclusively on comprehension (text output) and lack the ability to generate medical images, while general unified models that handle both tasks suffer severe performance degradation in the medical domain due to (i) the limited scale and quality of available medical data, and (ii) fundamental conflicts between comprehension (which favors abstract semantics) and generation (which requires fine-grained visual detail) during joint autoregressive training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts medical images across 11 modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, pathology, OCT, ultrasound, fundus, etc.) paired with text instructions or prompts. For comprehension tasks, it produces free-form or multiple-choice text responses; for generation tasks, it autoregressively predicts a sequence of 8192 VQGAN-f8-8192 codebook indices (bounded by <|START_IMG|> and <|END_IMG|> tokens) that are decoded into a reconstructed or transformed medical image. The visual encoder (CLIP-L/14) extracts features at two hierarchical levels—shallow layers for generation and deep layers for comprehension—selected dynamically by task type before concatenation with text tokens.

## Method

HealthGPT unifies medical comprehension and generation within a single autoregressive LLM (Phi-3-mini, Phi-4, or Qwen2.5-32B) using three key components. First, Heterogeneous Low-Rank Adaptation (H-LoRA) stores task-specific knowledge in separate LoRA plugins (one for comprehension, one for generation), each containing k LoRA experts whose matrices are concatenated into a single merged A and B matrix; a lightweight router produces per-token expert weights that are expanded via replication and applied via element-wise multiplication, reducing per-layer overhead from O(5k+1) in MoELoRA to a constant O(6). Second, Hierarchical Visual Perception routes shallow ViT features (concrete-grained) to generation and deep ViT features (abstract-grained) to comprehension, preventing cross-task feature interference. Third, a Three-Stage Learning Strategy first aligns separate visual adapters and H-LoRA submodules for each task, then fine-tunes only the shared embedding layer and output head on mixed data to unify the plugins, and finally performs instruction fine-tuning on only the H-LoRA modules—avoiding the catastrophic forgetting and optimization conflicts of naive mixed training.

## Results

On medical visual comprehension (Table 1), HealthGPT-XL32 (32B) achieves the highest average score of 71.1 across VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, MMMU-Med, and OmniMedVQA, while the smaller HealthGPT-M3 (3.8B) reaches 61.3, surpassing the best medical-specific model (HuatuoGPT-Vision, 50.7) and the best general LVLM (Llama-3.2, 54.7). On generation tasks, HealthGPT-M3 achieves SSIM 79.38 on CT-to-MRI brain conversion (vs. 71.09 for Pix2Pix and 71.47 for DiffMa) and SSIM 78.19 / LPIPS 12.02 on 4× MRI super-resolution (vs. 73.27 / 22.93 for LIIF). The method demonstrates clear scalability, with average comprehension scores rising from 61.3 (M3) to 66.4 (L14) to 71.1 (XL32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against medical-specific LVLMs, HealthGPT-M3 (3.8B) outperforms HuatuoGPT-Vision (7B) by 10.6 points in average comprehension score (61.3 vs. 50.7), LLaVA-Med by 13.7 points, and Med-Flamingo by 19.9 points, despite having fewer parameters. Against general-purpose LVLMs, HealthGPT-M3 surpasses Llama-3.2 (11B) by 6.6 points and InternVL2 (8B) by 8.8 points, while the larger HealthGPT-XL32 extends the lead to 16.4 points over Llama-3.2. Among unified comprehension-and-generation models, HealthGPT shows the most dramatic advantage: it outperforms Emu3 (8B) by 14.1 points, Janus-Pro (7B) by 15.7 points, Show-o by 18.7 points, and Unified-IO 2 by 27.5 points in average comprehension, indicating that existing unified models suffer severe medical-domain degradation. On generation tasks, HealthGPT-M3 beats all dedicated image-to-image baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, BBDM, Vmamba, DiffMa) on all four CT↔MRI modality conversion subtasks (e.g., SSIM 79.38 vs. 71.47 for DiffMa on CT-to-MRI Brain) and outperforms all super-resolution baselines (SRGAN, DASR, Real-ESRGAN, LIIF, BSRGAN) on SSIM, PSNR, MSE, and LPIPS. In reconstruction, HealthGPT-M3 achieves SSIM 91.73 on CT-Brain, surpassing Unified-IO 2 (83.93) and SEED-X (20.18) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

HealthGPT extends the LLaVA-style ViT-adapter-LLM architecture and the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm into the medical domain, while drawing on the discrete-token autoregressive generation approach established by Unified-IO 2, Janus, and Emu3 for general-purpose unified models. Its core architectural departure is H-LoRA, which restructures the MoELoRA expert-routing mechanism into a single merged matrix with element-wise gating, reducing per-layer overhead from O(5k+1) to O(6) operations. The paper positions itself as the first to apply a fully unified comprehension-and-generation autoregressive framework specifically to medical imaging, filling a gap left by prior medical LVLMs (Med-Flamingo, LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision) that handle only text-output comprehension tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 3.8B-parameter model can match or exceed 7B+ unified models on medical comprehension while simultaneously performing generation tasks, HealthGPT offers a practical, resource-efficient path for deploying multimodal medical AI in settings where compute is constrained. The VL-Health dataset and the three-stage training recipe provide a reproducible foundation for future medical multimodal research. However, the approach remains limited to 2D images and a fixed set of modalities; whether the H-LoRA decoupling strategy generalizes to 3D volumetric data, video, or other specialized domains (e.g., genomics, pathology whole-slide images) remains an open question. The generation baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN) are somewhat dated, leaving the true competitive gap in medical image generation less clearly established.
