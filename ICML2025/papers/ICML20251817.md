## Problem

Exact unlearning for fine-tuned LLMs remains an open problem: existing methods like SISA require retraining costs on the order of the original training run, and no prior work has explored whether in-context learning (as an alternative to parameter fine-tuning) admits efficient exact unlearning. The paper identifies that the example-selection step in in-context learning is the bottleneck for unlearning and that it can be made dataset-size independent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251817/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA 7B), a fine-tuning dataset D of text input-output pairs, a target number of in-context examples k, and a quantization parameter ε. Training examples are encoded into fixed-dimension vectors via Sentence-BERT before clustering. At inference, the k selected examples are prepended to the user's text prompt, and the LLM generates a text response; at unlearning time, the output is an updated set of k in-context examples that exactly matches what the algorithm would have produced had the removed datapoint never been in D.

## Method

ERASE replaces the k-means++ clustering used by ACoT with quantized k-means (Ginart et al., 2019), which snaps intermediate centroids to a randomly shifted ε-lattice. This quantization makes the final cluster assignments stable to single-point deletions with high probability (1 − O(1/ε|D|)), so unlearning a point typically requires no recomputation, yielding an expected unlearning cost of O(m²d^{5/2}/ε) for m requests—independent of both model and dataset size. The paper further introduces a 'holistic unlearning cost' metric (Definition 4.1) that captures the trade-off between cheaper unlearning operations and higher per-inference costs (from prepending k examples), quantifying how many inferences per unlearning request a method can handle before becoming more expensive than the 1-SISA retraining baseline. Empirically, ERASE matches or exceeds ACoT accuracy on 3 of 4 tasks and is comparable to SISA variants on over half of 15 Big-Bench tasks, while 2-shot ERASE achieves roughly 2× better holistic unlearning cost than 4-SISA.

## Results

On 15 Big-Bench tasks, ERASE with 3 or 4 in-context examples matches or exceeds the normalized aggregate score of all SISA variants (1-, 2-, 4-SISA) on more than half of the tasks, while 2-shot ERASE remains competitive with the most unlearning-efficient SISA variants. Against ACoT on 4 tasks, ERASE outperforms it on 3 of 4 (e.g., Fantasy Reasoning: 24.24 vs. 18.02 normalized aggregate score). In terms of holistic unlearning cost (inferences per unlearning request before exceeding 1-SISA baseline), 2-shot ERASE achieves 2.5×10³ on WinoWhy and 0.4×10² on Timedial, roughly doubling the threshold of 4-SISA (1.4×10³ and 0.2×10² respectively), while 4-shot ERASE (1.2×10³) falls below 4-SISA due to higher inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACoT (Zhang et al., 2022), ERASE outperforms on 3 of 4 evaluated tasks (Fantasy Reasoning: 24.24 vs. 18.02; Disambiguation QA: 2.48 vs. −4.22; Intent Recognition: comparable) while achieving dataset-size-independent unlearning cost O(m²d^{5/2}/ε) versus ACoT's O(m|D|d). Against SISA (Bourtoule et al., 2021), ERASE with 3–4 in-context examples matches or exceeds the normalized aggregate score of 1-, 2-, and 4-SISA on more than half of 15 Big-Bench tasks, and 2-shot ERASE remains competitive with the most unlearning-efficient SISA variants. On holistic unlearning cost, 2-shot ERASE roughly doubles the inference-per-unlearning threshold of 4-SISA across all four cost-measured tasks (e.g., 2.5×10³ vs. 1.4×10³ on WinoWhy), while 4-shot ERASE—despite having the most efficient unlearning operation—actually falls below 4-SISA due to its higher inference cost from prepending more examples. The paper notes that 4-shot ERASE's unlearning FLOPS are ≈0 (model- and dataset-independent) compared to 14×10¹² for 4-SISA, but this advantage is offset at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: exact machine unlearning (Cao & Yang 2015; Ginart et al. 2019; Bourtoule et al. 2021) and in-context learning for LLMs (Brown et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2022). Prior exact unlearning methods for deep learning (notably SISA) assume SGD-based training and incur costs proportional to model size, while prior in-context learning work (ACoT) uses k-means++ for example selection without considering unlearning. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a pre-trained LLM's in-context learning capability as the 'training algorithm' for the fine-tuning stage, then substituting quantized k-means (Ginart et al., 2019) for k-means++ to obtain dataset- and model-size-independent unlearning operations. The paper also introduces a holistic unlearning cost metric that jointly accounts for unlearning operation cost and inference cost, a trade-off not previously formalized in the unlearning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that exact unlearning for LLM fine-tuning need not scale with model or dataset size, offering a practical path toward compliance with data deletion requirements at the task adaptation stage. However, the holistic cost analysis reveals a non-trivial trade-off: the inference overhead from prepending in-context examples can negate unlearning savings, meaning the approach is only advantageous when unlearning requests are sufficiently infrequent relative to inference queries. Key limitations include the dependence on in-context learning matching fine-tuning accuracy (which holds for only a subset of tasks), the context-window constraint limiting the amount of data that can be 'learned,' and the open question of when in-context learning is sufficient versus when parameter updates are necessary. The paper leaves open the broader problem of unlearning pre-training data and the prediction of when in-context learning will be competitive without requiring a fine-tuning baseline for comparison.
