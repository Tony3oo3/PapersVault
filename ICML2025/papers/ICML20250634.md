## Problem

LLM-based recommender systems can produce systematically different recommendations for users who differ only in protected attributes (e.g., gender, age), yet existing bias-mitigation techniques either require retraining model weights (infeasible for black-box API deployments) or lack a principled, statistically grounded threshold for deciding when a semantic disparity constitutes a fairness violation. The paper addresses this gap by asking: how can one calibrate fairness constraints with finite-sample guarantees and iteratively correct biases purely through prompt-level interventions, without any parameter updates?

## Input / Output

The system consumes a user context vector (e.g., watch-history embedding), a protected attribute label (gender or age), and a dynamically maintained fairness instruction prompt as input to a black-box LLM call. It produces a recommended item (or text), a binary fairness-violation flag, an updated system prompt containing concrete 'avoid' examples extracted from recurring bias patterns, and an adaptively decayed conformal threshold. The fairness evaluation itself operates on sentence-transformer embeddings (paraphrase-mpnet-base-v2, fine-tuned on recommendation data), comparing the new output's embedding against cross-group neighbors within a cosine-similarity radius τ_ρ=0.9.

## Method

FACTER operates in two coordinated phases. In the offline calibration phase, it constructs a pairwise cross-group similarity matrix over calibration samples, computes a non-conformity score S_i that combines predictive error (1 − cosine similarity to the reference item) with a fairness penalty λ·Δ_i (the maximum embedding distance to cross-group neighbors with similar contexts), and sets an initial threshold Q_α^(0) as the (1−α)-quantile of these scores, yielding a finite-sample coverage guarantee under exchangeability. In the online phase, each incoming query is scored in real time; if S_new exceeds the current threshold, the system (1) appends the violation to a FIFO buffer of size M=50, (2) scans the buffer for output features (genre, title, studio) appearing in ≥3 violations sharing the same protected attribute and injects corresponding 'Avoid: (attribute) → (feature-only)' examples into the system prompt, and (3) adaptively tightens the threshold via exponential decay Q_α^(t+1) = γQ_α^(t) + (1−γ)min(Q_α^(t), S_new). The key insight is that semantic variance in embedding space serves as a measurable proxy for demographic bias, and conformal prediction converts this into a statistically calibrated detection rule that drives a closed-loop prompt-repair cycle—requiring no LLM retraining.

## Results

On MovieLens-1M, FACTER (Iteration 3) reduces fairness violations to just 5 (a 95.5% reduction from the Zero-Shot baseline of 112) while achieving NDCG@10 of 0.445 and Recall@10 of 0.389. On the sparser Amazon Movies & TV dataset, FACTER cuts violations from 198 to 18 (90.9% reduction) with NDCG@10 of 0.339. The conformal threshold provides a theoretical Type I error bound of ≤0.201, while the empirical violation rate is only 0.018, confirming conservative and effective calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACTER dominates both baselines on all fairness metrics. Against Zero-Shot (Hou et al., 2024), FACTER reduces violations by 95.5% (5 vs. 112 on MovieLens; 18 vs. 198 on Amazon) and lowers CFR from 0.742 to 0.591, at a modest accuracy cost of 0.013 NDCG@10 and 0.013 Recall@10. Against UP5 (Hua et al., 2023), FACTER achieves 82% fewer violations (5 vs. 28) while simultaneously outperforming UP5 on both NDCG@10 (0.445 vs. 0.427) and Recall@10 (0.389 vs. 0.381), making it the only method that wins on both fairness and accuracy. On Amazon, the same pattern holds: FACTER beats UP5 on violations (18 vs. 63), CFR (0.634 vs. 0.721), and NDCG@10 (0.339 vs. 0.328). The iterative nature of FACTER means violations decrease monotonically across iterations (112 → 28 → 5), whereas both baselines remain static.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACTER sits at the intersection of conformal prediction (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) and prompt-based bias mitigation (Reynolds & McDonell, 2021; Yang et al., 2022), repurposing the former's finite-sample coverage guarantees to calibrate a semantic-variance threshold for detecting fairness violations in LLM outputs. It departs from parameter-level debiasing (adversarial training, reparameterization) by operating entirely in the black-box regime, and extends embedding-based post-hoc bias detection (Lucy & Bamman, 2021; Borkan et al., 2019) by adding a principled, data-driven threshold rather than relying on ad-hoc distance cutoffs. Compared to prior fairness-aware recommenders like UP5 (Hua et al., 2023) and zero-shot LLM rankers (Hou et al., 2024), FACTER introduces a closed-loop mechanism where detected violations trigger both threshold tightening and targeted prompt injection, creating an iterative correction cycle absent in static calibration approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: industrial LLM deployments are typically black-box APIs where retraining is infeasible, yet fairness monitoring still requires statistically grounded thresholds rather than subjective heuristics. The 95.5% violation reduction on MovieLens-1M is compelling, but the evaluation is limited to small sampled subsets (750 and 1,125 test interactions) and two datasets, leaving open whether the conformal guarantees and prompt-correction loop scale to production-scale recommendation workloads with millions of users. Open questions include the sensitivity of the heuristic 'three-occurrence' pattern-detection rule, whether the approach generalizes beyond gender/age to intersectional or continuous protected attributes, and whether accumulated prompt modifications eventually degrade recommendation quality over long deployment horizons.
