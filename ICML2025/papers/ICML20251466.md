## Problem

Existing multimodal emotion recognition (MER) methods are confined to predefined emotion taxonomies (e.g., Ekman's six basic emotions), which cannot capture the full spectrum of human emotional experiences—Plutchik estimated humans can express ~34,000 distinct emotions. No prior framework enables prediction of arbitrary emotion categories beyond a fixed label space, and no dataset, evaluation metric, or solution exists for this open-vocabulary setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a short video clip (1–4 seconds, single speaker, with audio and visual content) plus its text subtitle as input, and produces an unordered set of 1–9 open-vocabulary emotion words (most samples have 2–4) as output. Because the label space is unrestricted, evaluation requires set-based metrics (Precision_S, Recall_S, F_S) that first group semantically similar emotion words via GPT-based or Emotion Wheel-based clustering before computing set intersection, making the score invariant to label ordering and paraphrasing (e.g., 'joyful' vs. 'happy').

## Method

The paper introduces OV-MER as a new paradigm and provides three pillars: (1) a human-LLM collaborative dataset construction pipeline where GPT-4V pre-annotates visual clues (3 uniformly sampled frames), SALMONN pre-annotates acoustic clues, and GPT-3.5 merges all clues with text into a CLUE-Multi description, from which open-vocabulary emotion labels are extracted in both English and Chinese and manually verified over two rounds; (2) set-based evaluation metrics with two grouping strategies (GPT-based semantic grouping and Emotion Wheel-based hierarchical mapping with synonym and word-form expansion) to handle the open label space; (3) a zero-shot benchmark evaluating 15+ MLLMs via a CLUE-MLLM generation strategy (S2: MLLM extracts emotion-related descriptions from video/audio, then a separate LLM combines these with text to predict emotion labels), revealing that current MLLMs achieve only ~53 F_S at best versus 80 F_S for the human-verified CLUE-Multi upper bound.

## Results

On OV-MERD, the upper-bound CLUE-Multi (human-LLM collaboration) achieves F_S of 80.05 (English) and 85.16 (Chinese), with near-perfect Precision_S and Recall_S (~80% and ~80% in English). Among MLLM baselines, GPT-4V attains the best F_S of 55.51 (English) and 57.21 (Chinese), followed by Chat-UniVi (53.08/53.86) and mPLUG-Owl (52.73/50.95). The Random heuristic baseline scores only 17.42 (English) and 16.59 (Chinese), confirming that MLLMs partially address OV-MER but remain far from the upper bound (~25-point gap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V is the strongest MLLM baseline, achieving F_S of 55.51 (English) and 57.21 (Chinese), but it still trails the CLUE-Multi upper bound by ~25 and ~28 points respectively, highlighting a substantial gap between current MLLM capabilities and human-verified multimodal reasoning. Chat-UniVi (53.08/53.86) and mPLUG-Owl (52.73/50.95) follow, with LLaMA-VID (51.25/52.01) and Video-ChatGPT (50.52/54.73) forming the next tier. Audio-capable models (Qwen-Audio, SALMONN, SECap) generally underperform video-capable models, with Qwen-Audio scoring only 38.13 (English) versus GPT-4V's 55.51. Among the upper-bound CLUE variants, CLUE-Audio (65.35) outperforms CLUE-Video (60.55), which in turn outperforms CLUE-Text (46.00), indicating that audio cues are the most discriminative single modality for OV-MER. All MLLM baselines substantially exceed the Random baseline (17.42/16.59), confirming that MLLMs can partially address OV-MER, yet none approaches the upper bound, underscoring the difficulty of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the open-vocabulary learning paradigm—well established in vision tasks like object detection and segmentation—into multimodal emotion recognition, where all prior work (OH-MER, ML-MER, FG-MER) operates within a fixed, predefined emotion taxonomy. It builds on the MER2023 dataset as its data substrate and repurposes Plutchik's emotion wheel as a structured grouping mechanism for evaluation, while introducing a human-LLM collaborative annotation pipeline that departs from purely human or purely model-based labeling. The solution framework leverages MLLMs in a zero-shot generative setting rather than the discriminative classifiers that fixed-label-space MER has traditionally relied upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~25-point gap between the CLUE-Multi upper bound (80 F_S) and the best MLLM (GPT-4V at 55.5 F_S) reveals that current multimodal models are far from capturing the full nuance of open-vocabulary emotion, making this a meaningful open challenge for the MLLM community. The paper's set-based metrics and emotion-wheel grouping provide a reproducible, cost-effective evaluation protocol that avoids dependence on specific LLM API versions. Key limitations include the dataset's derivation from Chinese-language movies/TV (limiting cultural generalizability), the absence of a trained model (only zero-shot MLLM evaluation), and the long-tail distribution of 236 emotion categories that will challenge future supervised approaches.
