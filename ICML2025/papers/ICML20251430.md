## Problem

Existing masked graph autoencoders for self-supervised graph learning treat all masked edges uniformly during training, ignoring that different edges have vastly different reconstruction difficulties. This uniform treatment either overwhelms the randomly initialized GNN encoder with overly hard reconstructions early in training or keeps it on trivially easy tasks too long, yielding suboptimal node representations. The paper is the first to introduce a curriculum learning strategy into masked graph autoencoders, addressing how to schedule edge masking from easy to hard in a principled, self-paced manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A as input, but the encoder does not see the full graph at any single training step—instead, it receives a dynamically perturbed subgraph Ã = A − A_mask where the masked edge set evolves over training iterations according to the curriculum schedule. The output is a set of node embeddings (used downstream via a linear SVM for classification or a scoring function for link prediction) plus per-edge reconstruction probabilities from the cross-correlation decoder. The edge selection matrix S^(t) is itself a learned, continuously-relaxed variable updated alongside the GNN weights in a bi-level optimization loop.

## Method

Cur-MGAE integrates three components into a unified self-supervised framework. (1) A structure-aware masked autoencoder uses a multi-layer GNN encoder and a cross-correlation decoder that computes edge embeddings via element-wise multiplication of all layer-pair node representations (h_v^(k) ⊙ h_u^(j)), concatenated and passed through an MLP with sigmoid to predict edge existence. (2) A complexity-guided curriculum masking module quantifies edge difficulty using the reconstruction residual R = A − A_re (the difference between the original and model-predicted adjacency matrices), treating edges with smallest residuals as easiest. (3) A self-paced mask scheduler relaxes the binary edge selection to continuous values in [0,1], optimized via a Lagrangian-regularized loss L_SPCL = βΣ S_ij R_ij + f(S;λ,A) where λ increases over training to progressively incorporate more edges; a split ratio hyperparameter balances difficulty-guided selection (exploitation) with random selection (exploration) to prevent overfitting. The overall training alternates between updating GNN weights w (minimizing the reconstruction loss on the current masked subgraph) and updating the edge selection matrix S (minimizing the self-paced curriculum loss), with convergence guarantees showing the procedure avoids strict saddle points and converges to second-order stationary points.

## Results

On node classification across six datasets, Cur-MGAE achieves the best average rank (2.83), with standout gains of +1.67% over S2GAE on Pubmed (85.86% vs. 84.19%) and +0.98% on OGBN-arxiv (73.00% vs. 72.02%). On link prediction, Cur-MGAE attains the best average rank (2.67), improving by ~2.5% over S2GAE-SAGE on OGBL-ddi (68.50% vs. 66.00% Hits@20) and ~2% over S2GAE-GCN on OGBL-ppa (5.96% vs. 3.98% Hits@10). The method is competitive but slightly trails MaskGAE on small Planetoid link prediction (e.g., 95.22% vs. 96.66% AUC on Cora).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cur-MGAE outperforms all 15 baselines on Pubmed (85.86% vs. S2GAE's 84.19%) and OGBN-arxiv (73.00% vs. S2GAE's 72.02%) for node classification, and wins on OGBL-ddi (68.50% vs. S2GAE-SAGE's 66.00%) and OGBL-ppa (5.96% vs. S2GAE-GCN's 3.98%) for link prediction. On the smaller Planetoid node classification datasets, Cur-MGAE is within 0.1–0.5% of the best baselines (MaskGAE on Cora at 87.31%, GIC on Citeseer at 76.39%), while on Coauthor-CS and Coauthor-Physics it trails BGRL by ~0.7% and ~0.25% respectively. For link prediction on small graphs, MaskGAE leads on Cora (96.66% vs. 95.22% AUC) and Citeseer (98.00% vs. 95.20%), and S2GAE-GCN edges out Cur-MGAE on OGBL-collab (54.74% vs. 52.28% Hits@50). Contrastive methods (DGI, MVGRL, BGRL, GIC) are consistently outperformed by generative methods and Cur-MGAE across nearly all datasets, with BGRL being the strongest contrastive baseline (rank 4.00 on node classification). The paper's key advantage over the closest generative competitors (S2GAE, MaskGAE) is most pronounced on large-scale and structurally complex graphs, where the curriculum-based difficulty scheduling provides the largest marginal gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cur-MGAE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: masked graph autoencoders (particularly S2GAE, from which the cross-correlation decoder design is directly adopted) and self-paced/curriculum learning for GNNs (Zhang et al., 2023a, from which the convergence guarantees and the soft edge-selection relaxation are inherited). The key conceptual departure is applying curriculum learning to the *self-supervised* graph setting, where no labels exist to define sample difficulty; instead, the paper uses the model's own reconstruction residuals as a proxy for edge difficulty. This distinguishes it from prior curriculum learning work on graphs (e.g., GNN-CL, CurGraph), which relies on supervised labels and treats samples as independent, whereas here edges are structurally interdependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic treatment of curriculum scheduling in graph self-supervised learning, offering a principled (residual-based) difficulty measure and a self-paced scheduler with convergence guarantees for the resulting bi-level optimization. However, the practical gains are modest (1–2% over strong baselines like S2GAE), and the method introduces several hyperparameters (split ratio, mask ratio, λ_initial) that require careful tuning. An open question is whether the reconstruction-residual difficulty proxy generalizes well to heterogeneous or dynamic graphs, and whether the curriculum benefit persists when the encoder is already well-initialized (e.g., via pre-training).
