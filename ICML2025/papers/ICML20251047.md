## Problem

While linear mode connectivity (LMC) has been empirically established for pairs of independently trained networks under permutation symmetries, it remains unclear whether multiple models can be simultaneously transferred into a single approximately convex low-loss basin. The paper shows that naively applying two-model permutation search (WM, STE) to each model relative to a reference leads to monotonic degradation of merged-model accuracy as the number of models grows, and that existing multi-model methods (MergeMany, CCMM) also suffer performance loss with increasing model count.

## Input / Output

The method takes n independently trained neural networks of identical architecture (MLP, VGG-11, or ResNet-20 with widened channels) and their parameter vectors as input. It outputs n−1 permutation matrices (the nth model is fixed as identity without loss of generality by Theorem D.1) and a merged model formed as a uniform convex combination of the permuted parameter vectors. The merged model is then evaluated on the same classification task (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, or CIFAR-10) as the originals, and its loss sharpness is measured via the Hessian trace weighted by parameter magnitudes.

## Method

STE-MM extends the straight-through estimator to multiple models by introducing continuous dummy parameters initialized to the reference model. In each training epoch, the WM algorithm finds the best permutation aligning each model to its dummy parameter, a random Dirichlet weight vector is sampled, the loss of the weighted sum of permuted models is computed, and gradients are backpropagated through the permuted models to update the dummies; after convergence, final permutations are extracted via a last WM pass. The objective directly minimizes the expected loss of the merged model over the simplex, rather than minimizing pairwise L2 distances as in MergeMany or CCMM. To address the high cost of solving the linear assignment problem at every WM call, the paper proposes an accelerated LAP solver that reuses dual variables from the previous iteration to shift the cost matrix, reducing the effective difficulty of the dual problem and yielding 10–40× speedups in practice.

## Results

STE-MM is evaluated on MLP (512-unit, 3 hidden layers), VGG-11 (4× width), and ResNet-20 (16× width) trained on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10. The headline finding is that STE-MM is the only method where merged-model test accuracy *increases* with the number of models (up to 10), while MergeMany and CCMM show monotonic accuracy degradation. The loss gap between the merged model and the average original-model loss remains close to zero under STE-MM, whereas MergeMany and CCMM exhibit growing gaps. Additionally, the Hessian-trace sharpness (tr(H⊙θθᵀ)) of the merged model *decreases* with more models under STE-MM, while it increases under MergeMany and CCMM, supporting the claim that STE-MM transfers models into a flatter, approximately convex basin. The accelerated LAP solver reduces permutation-search time by ~10× on average and up to 40× for ResNet-20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-model WM and STE methods (Ainsworth et al., 2023), STE-MM clearly wins: when those pairwise methods are naively extended to n models, both test accuracy and loss degrade monotonically as n grows (Figure 1), whereas STE-MM maintains or improves accuracy. Against MergeMany (Ainsworth et al., 2023), which minimizes the sum of pairwise L² distances, STE-MM outperforms on both test accuracy (which rises vs. falls) and loss difference (which stays near zero vs. grows), and critically on sharpness (which decreases vs. increases with model count). Against CCMM (Crisostomi et al., 2024), which uses a Frank-Wolfe-based cycle-consistent objective, STE-MM again wins on all three metrics: accuracy trend, loss-barrier magnitude, and Hessian-trace sharpness. The geometric analysis (L² distance histograms and angle histograms) shows that STE-MM, MergeMany, and CCMM all produce approximately regular-simplex configurations, so the advantage of STE-MM is not in geometry but in keeping the loss surface flat across the simplex interior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the permutation-based model merging framework of Ainsworth et al. (2023) from the two-model setting to multiple models, adapting the straight-through estimator (originally from Bengio et al., 2013) to jointly optimize permutations for n models simultaneously. It departs from prior multi-model approaches (MergeMany, CCMM) that minimize pairwise L2 distances by instead directly minimizing the loss of the merged model, and demonstrates that this distinction is critical: pairwise LMC does not guarantee multi-model LMC. The LAP acceleration technique builds on the complementary slackness structure of the Hungarian/Jonker-Volgenant algorithms to reuse dual variables across iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that the 'single convex basin' conjecture requires careful multi-model treatment, and offers a practical method (STE-MM) that maintains test accuracy when merging up to 10 models. The Hessian-trace sharpness analysis—showing sharpness decreases as more models are merged—gives a geometrically meaningful signal for approximate convexity that is harder to verify directly. Key limitations include reliance on small architectures (ResNet-20, VGG-11, MLP) and simple datasets (MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10), and the requirement for substantially widened models; whether STE-MM scales to large-scale vision or language models remains open, as does the theoretical question of why suitable multi-model permutations exist in the first place.
