## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods face a fundamental trade-off between efficiency and controllability: reference-free methods like SimPO are computationally cheap but suffer from training instability due to the absence of reference constraints, while constraint-based methods like TDPO achieve fine-grained control through sequential KL divergence at the cost of loading a reference model and computing over the full vocabulary (265K tokens for Gemma-2). No prior method simultaneously achieves SimPO-level memory efficiency and TDPO-level generation diversity control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with preferred and less-preferred completions from UltraFeedback, along with pre-computed offline artifacts: a scalar length-normalized reference margin per preference pair and a sparse activation vector (top-k out of 16,000 SAE features) per sample. During training, only the policy model is loaded; the reference model's contributions are read as cached one-dimensional tensors via the dataloader, eliminating the need to maintain a second full model in GPU memory. The output is an aligned text response generated by the updated policy model.

## Method

FPO reformulates the DPO loss into a unified form comprising a length-normalized log-probability difference (LPD), an adaptive reference margin γ_ref-LN, and a constraint term δ_FPO. The key innovation is replacing TDPO's sequential KL divergence over the full vocabulary with an MSE computed over the top-k most active SAE features (typically ~50 out of 16,000) at a chosen intermediate layer, after average-pooling across token positions. All reference-model-dependent quantities (the margin and the reference sparse activations) are pre-computed and cached offline, so training requires only the policy model plus minimal I/O for cached tensors. A theoretical result (Appendix B) proves that under accurate SAE reconstruction and bounded operator norm conditions, minimizing the MSE of sparse activations upper-bounds the KL divergence between token probability distributions, justifying the substitution. The approach achieves ~17% memory reduction over TDPO-2 while matching or exceeding its alignment accuracy and diversity.

## Results

On Gemma-2-2B, FPO achieves up to a 5.08% absolute improvement in winning rate over the strongest baseline on AlpacaEval-2, and up to 0.5 score improvement on MT-Bench. On Gemma-2-9B, FPO shows consistent but smaller gains over all baselines. FPO also achieves the highest generation diversity (entropy) among all methods while maintaining the second-highest training accuracy on UltraFeedback, and reduces GPU memory consumption by approximately 17% compared to TDPO-2 while matching the memory footprint of reference-free SimPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPO outperforms DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) across all three benchmarks on both model sizes, with the largest gap on AlpacaEval-2 WR-L (e.g., ~56.8% vs. 51.7% on Gemma-2-2B), attributed to FPO's feature-level constraint providing stronger control than DPO's implicit reward difference alone. Against TDPO-1 and TDPO-2 (Zeng et al., 2024), FPO achieves comparable or superior winning rates while reducing GPU memory by ~17% and eliminating the need to load a reference model during training; FPO also matches TDPO-2's KL divergence margin control (Figure 3) at significantly lower computational cost. Compared to SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), FPO achieves higher winning rates (e.g., ~56.8% vs. 51.1% WR-L on Gemma-2-2B) and greater training stability, because FPO retains an offline reference-based adaptive margin (γ_ref-LN) that SimPO's fixed margin lacks, while maintaining SimPO's memory efficiency through offline caching. In the accuracy-vs-diversity trade-off (Table 2 right), FPO achieves the highest entropy (diversity) among all methods while ranking second in training accuracy, outperforming SimPO and DPO on both axes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPO unifies two previously separate lines of DPO improvement: SimPO's efficiency (length normalization, reference-free training) and TDPO's controllability (sequential KL divergence for diversity control). The key conceptual departure is repurposing Sparse Autoencoders—previously used exclusively for interpretability (Huben et al., 2024)—as a constraint mechanism in alignment, replacing the expensive token-level KL over a 265K vocabulary with a sparse feature-level MSE over ~50 active features out of 16K. The paper also provides a theoretical bridge (Appendix B) showing that minimizing MSE on SAE activations upper-bounds the KL divergence between token distributions, grounding the substitution in a formal argument rather than pure empiricism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPO offers a practical path to alignment that is as memory-efficient as reference-free methods (SimPO) while retaining the stability and diversity control of KL-constrained methods (TDPO), with a 17.6% memory reduction over TDPO-2. The accurate-control experiments (Table 4) demonstrating targeted suppression of specific capabilities (French output, JSON formatting, safety) by adjusting β on individual SAE features open a new axis of fine-grained model steering that prior alignment methods cannot achieve. Limitations include modest gains at 9B scale, dependence on pre-trained SAE quality, and the finding that multi-layer SAEs degrade performance—suggesting the approach may be most effective in the 2–3B regime where SAE decomposition is cleaner. Open questions include whether the MSE-as-KL-proxy holds under larger distributional shifts and whether the offline reference computation becomes a bottleneck for very large preference datasets.
