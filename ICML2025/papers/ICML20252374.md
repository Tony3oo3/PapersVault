## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot segmentation (CD-FSS) must bridge a large domain gap while fine-tuning on only a handful of target samples, and existing approaches rely on explicit domain-decoupling losses to separate domain-specific from domain-agnostic knowledge. This paper discovers that adapters, when placed in deep layers of a frozen backbone with residual connections, naturally decouple domain information purely through their structural position—without any auxiliary loss—addressing both the domain gap and the data-scarcity challenge simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model receives a support image with its binary mask and a query image, both resized to 400×400. The support mask is bilinearly interpolated and channel-expanded to mask the support feature maps at each pyramid level before computing 4D cosine-similarity correlation tensors between navigated query and masked support features. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask for the query image, produced by a 4D convolutional pyramid encoder followed by a 2D convolutional decoder.

## Method

The Domain Feature Navigator (DFN) is a 1×1 convolutional adapter inserted into the deeper layers of a frozen ResNet-50 (or ViT) backbone with residual connections, connected at low, middle, and high feature levels to ensure semantic consistency. During source-domain training, DFN implicitly absorbs domain-specific patterns while the encoder-decoder is steered toward domain-agnostic representations, verified via CKA similarity measurements showing decreased backbone domain similarity and increased encoder domain similarity. To prevent DFN from overfitting source samples, SAM-SVN performs SVD on DFN's weight matrix and applies Sharpness-Aware Minimization exclusively to the singular value matrix, flattening the loss landscape without suppressing domain-information absorption. During target-domain adaptation, the backbone and encoder-decoder are frozen while DFN is fine-tuned (50 iterations) to capture target-specific features, aligning the feature space for each domain. An Information Bottleneck analysis formalizes why the residual adapter structure causes gradient flow to separate into domain-invariant updates for the encoder-decoder and domain-specific updates for the adapter.

## Results

On the CD-FSS benchmark (PASCAL source → four target domains), DFN with ResNet-50 achieves 61.98% and 68.81% average MIoU in 1-shot and 5-shot settings, while DFN with ViT-base reaches 63.99% and 69.77%. This surpasses the previous state-of-the-art APSeg (ViT-base) by 2.69% (1-shot) and 4.68% (5-shot) average MIoU. The largest per-dataset gains are on Chest X-ray (85.21% 1-shot, 90.34% 5-shot with ResNet-50) and Deepglobe (45.66% 1-shot, 47.98% 5-shot with ResNet-50), where domain gaps are most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFN (ResNet-50) outperforms the strongest ResNet-50 baseline APM (NeurIPS-24) by 1.95% (1-shot) and 3.63% (5-shot) average MIoU, and beats ABCDFSS (CVPR-24) by 1.31% and 3.84% respectively. Against the strongest ViT-base baseline APSeg (CVPR-24), DFN (ViT-base) leads by 2.69% (1-shot) and 4.68% (5-shot). DFN also substantially surpasses the architectural baseline HSNet (ICCV-21) by 14.41% (1-shot) and 17.43% (5-shot), and the CD-FSS benchmark method PATNet (ECCV-22) by 5.92% and 6.82%. Notably, DFN achieves the best per-dataset scores on FSS-1000, Deepglobe, and Chest X-ray in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings with ResNet-50, while ISIC remains its weakest target (36.30% 1-shot), where ABCDFSS (45.70%) and APSeg (45.43%) still lead. In the appendix comparison under IFA's batch-size-96 protocol, DFN also outperforms IFA (CVPR-24) across all four datasets, with the largest margin on Chest X-ray (86.1 vs. 74.0 in 1-shot).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adapter paradigm (Houlsby et al., 2019) and the HSNet few-shot segmentation architecture (Min et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from prior CD-FSS methods that rely on loss-based domain decoupling (domain confusion, CCSA, DIFEX). The key conceptual shift is discovering that adapters, when inserted into deep backbone layers with residual connections, inherently decouple domain-specific from domain-agnostic information through their structure alone—requiring no explicit domain-decoupling loss. The SAM-SVN component adapts Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2020) to operate exclusively on the singular value matrix of the adapter, drawing on the observation that singular values control representation importance during transfer (Chen et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that structural design (position + residual connection) alone can induce domain-information decoupling offers a simpler, loss-free alternative to the regularization-heavy approaches dominating domain adaptation, and the insight could transfer to other adapter-based fine-tuning settings beyond segmentation. However, the Information Bottleneck analysis relies on strong assumptions (orthogonal projections, adapter approximating the negative domain-specific component) that are not rigorously validated, and the empirical evidence is concentrated on a single base architecture (HSNet) with ResNet-50 and ViT backbones. The authors acknowledge the method does not address many-shot scenarios, which have significant practical value, and the CKA-based decoupling verification, while convincing, measures correlation rather than causal contribution to downstream performance.
