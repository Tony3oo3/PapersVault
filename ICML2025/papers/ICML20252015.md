## Problem

High-performance deep learning watermarking models require millions of parameters, making them impractical for resource-constrained deployment, while existing lightweight models sacrifice robustness. The paper identifies the root cause: commonly used decoding losses (MSE, BCE) contain optimization directions (inflation and regularization) that are irrelevant to the actual decoding goal yet consume model capacity, disproportionately harming lightweight models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252015/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 128×128×3 RGB cover image and a 64-bit binary message (values in {-1,1}) and produces a watermarked RGB image of the same dimensions. The decoder takes a noised watermarked image (distorted by a combined noise layer of Gaussian blur, median blur, Gaussian noise, salt-and-pepper noise, JPEG compression, and dropout) and outputs a 64-dimensional real-valued vector whose sign determines the extracted bits. The entire model uses only 0.016M parameters and 0.22G FLOPs, roughly 2.2% of the parameter count of state-of-the-art frameworks like CIN or MBRS.

## Method

The paper decomposes the MSE decoding loss into three components—deflation (penalizing incorrectly decoded bits), inflation (pushing correct bits away from the decision boundary), and regularization (preventing output explosion)—and shows that only deflation directly reduces decoding error. To eliminate the parameter cost of irrelevant directions, two plug-and-play solutions are proposed: (1) a Detachable Projection Head (PH) that absorbs inflation/regularization during training via four residual projection blocks and is discarded at inference, and (2) a Decoding-Oriented Surrogate Loss (DO) that retains only the deflation term plus a bounded inflation term restricted to correctly decoded bits within a 'safe distance' ε of the boundary. The model is built within a new five-module framework (Image Preprocessing, Message Preprocessing, Feature Fusion, Noised Watermarked Image Preprocessing, Message Extraction) using exclusively transposed convolution and convolution layers with LeakyReLU, enabling independent per-module parameter reduction. Trained with combined noise across seven distortions, the DO variant achieves 99.36% average accuracy and 41.70 dB PSNR, matching or exceeding much larger SOTA models.

## Results

The proposed lightweight model with the decoding-oriented surrogate loss (DO) achieves 41.70 dB PSNR and 99.36% average decoding accuracy under combined noise, outperforming all SOTA baselines (FIN: 41.58 dB / 99.28%, MBRS: 40.72 dB / 99.25%, CIN: 40.31 dB / 97.10%, HiDDeN: 27.28 dB / 76.14%). The model uses only 0.016M total parameters (0.009M encoder + 0.007M decoder) and 0.22G FLOPs, representing just 2.2% of FIN's parameter count and 0.6% of CIN's. The detachable projection head (PH) variant achieves 41.67 dB PSNR and 99.28% average accuracy, matching FIN's performance while being 47× smaller in parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HiDDeN (Zhu et al., ECCV 2018), the proposed DO method achieves a massive 14.42 dB PSNR improvement (41.70 vs. 27.28) and 23.22 percentage-point accuracy gain (99.36% vs. 76.14%), while using only 3.6% of HiDDeN's parameters. Against MBRS (Jia et al., ACM MM 2021), DO wins on both PSNR (41.70 vs. 40.72 dB) and average accuracy (99.36% vs. 99.25%), with particularly large gains on JPEG compression (99.12% vs. 98.85%) and Gaussian noise (97.40% vs. 97.68% — a narrow loss on GN only). Against CIN (Ma et al., ACM MM 2022), DO dominates on both metrics (41.70 vs. 40.31 dB; 99.36% vs. 97.10%), with the largest gap on JPEG (99.12% vs. 86.01%). Against FIN (Fang et al., AAAI 2023), the closest competitor, DO edges out on PSNR (41.70 vs. 41.58 dB) and average accuracy (99.36% vs. 99.28%), while using only 2.2% of FIN's parameters (0.016M vs. 0.75M) and 6.2% of its FLOPs (0.22G vs. 3.56G). The PH variant matches FIN's average accuracy exactly (99.28%) but with 47× fewer parameters, confirming that the performance gap is attributable to the training methodology rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard END (Encoder-NoiseLayer-Decoder) watermarking lineage (HiDDeN → MBRS → CIN → FIN) by questioning a foundational assumption: that MSE or BCE are appropriate surrogate losses for the decoding task. Rather than scaling up architecture, the authors decompose these losses into deflation, inflation, and regularization components and show that only the deflation term directly reduces decoding error, while the other two consume parameters without improving accuracy. The detachable projection head and decoding-oriented surrogate loss are novel loss/architecture interventions that repurpose the existing END framework for extreme parameter efficiency, and the five-module decomposition (IP, MP, FF, NWIP, ME) provides a finer-grained design vocabulary than prior encoder/decoder treatments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.2% parameter footprint matching or exceeding SOTA robustness and invisibility is practically significant for deploying watermarking on edge devices, in diffusion-model pipelines, or for NeRF copyright protection where the backbone is already compute-heavy. The plug-and-play nature of DO and PH means the loss-decomposition insight can be grafted onto other lightweight architectures without redesign. However, the model's robustness degrades notably under geometric distortions (RandomPerspective, RandomAffine), and the evaluation is limited to 128×128 images with 64-bit messages, leaving open whether the efficiency gains and loss-design principles transfer to higher resolutions, longer payloads, or more complex distortion regimes.
