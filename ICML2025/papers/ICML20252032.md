## Problem

Image super-resolution faces a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based methods achieve high fidelity metrics but struggle to generate realistic textures, while diffusion-based methods produce rich details at the cost of high computational complexity and pixel-level fidelity degradation. Visual autoregressive modeling with next-scale prediction had not been explored for ISR, despite its natural coarse-to-fine generation paradigm aligning well with the task's structural requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252032/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-resolution RGB image (128×128) and produces a high-resolution RGB image (512×512), supporting arbitrary higher resolutions via tiled generation. The LR image is consumed by a pyramid-style image encoder that downsamples it 16× into a 32×32 feature map, which is then used as prefix tokens prepended to the autoregressive token sequence. The output is assembled from 10 discrete token scales (16×16 through 512×512) plus a continuous residual from the diffusion refiner, decoded by a VQVAE with 16× upsampling.

## Method

VARSR is a three-stage framework: (1) an image encoder maps the LR image into prefix tokens that serve as a preceding scale in the autoregressive sequence, providing global conditional guidance without the overhead of ControlNet; (2) a GPT-2-style transformer (24 layers, width 1536) performs next-scale prediction over 10 multi-scale token maps, using Scale-aligned Rotary Positional Encodings (SA-RoPE) that apply 2D RoPE with normalized coordinates to align spatial positions across scales and with the LR condition; (3) a lightweight 6-block MLP diffusion refiner (<40M params) predicts the continuous quantization residual conditioned on the final-scale hidden states, recovering high-frequency details lost in discrete tokenization. Image-based Classifier-Free Guidance is implemented via positive/negative quality embeddings injected through AdaLN modulate layers, guiding the sampling distribution toward higher-quality outputs without additional training. The model is trained in three stages—VQVAE training with scale dropout, C2I pretraining on a 4M-image large-scale dataset, and ISR finetuning—using a combined cross-entropy and diffusion loss.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val, VARSR achieves the best no-reference IQA scores across all three metrics: MANIQA 0.5340, CLIPIQA 0.7347, and MUSIQ 71.27, outperforming the closest competitor SeeSR (0.5046, 0.6959, 68.35) by a wide margin. On real-world benchmarks RealSR and DRealSR, VARSR similarly leads all IQA metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 71.26 and 68.15 respectively), while remaining competitive on reference-based fidelity metrics (PSNR 24.61 on RealSR, 28.16 on DRealSR). Inference time is 0.59s per image with 10 steps, roughly 10× faster than the next-best diffusion method DiffBIR (5.85s, 50 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VARSR achieves the best no-reference IQA scores (MANIQA, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ) across all three evaluation datasets, with the closest competitor being SeeSR (e.g., MUSIQ 71.27 vs. 68.35 on DIV2K-Val, a ~3-point gap). All GAN-based baselines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, DASR) are substantially outperformed on perceptual quality metrics but retain an advantage on reference-based fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), reflecting the well-known fidelity–realism tradeoff. Among diffusion-based methods, VARSR leads SeeSR, PASD, DiffBIR, StableSR, SinSR, and LDM on all IQA metrics while remaining within ~1 dB on PSNR and within ~0.02 on SSIM. On the C2I base-model comparison (Table 10), VARSR's large-scale pretraining improves MUSIQ from 60.92 (original VAR on ImageNet) to 74.32, validating the generative prior. In a user study on 100 real-world images, VARSR receives a 57.1% selection rate versus 21.05% for SeeSR and 17.3% for PASD, confirming the objective metric advantages align with human perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the next-scale prediction paradigm from VAR (Tian et al., 2024) to image super-resolution, a task previously dominated by GAN-based and diffusion-based methods. It borrows the diffusion-as-refiner concept from MAR and HART to address quantization loss, extends RoPE into a scale-aligned 2D variant (SA-RoPE) for spatial structure preservation, and introduces prefix tokens as a lightweight conditioning mechanism that replaces the heavier ControlNet approach used in diffusion-based ISR. The image-based classifier-free guidance, which uses quality-class embeddings rather than text prompts, is a novel adaptation of CFG to the AR setting for ISR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VARSR demonstrates that autoregressive next-scale prediction can match or exceed diffusion-based ISR methods on perceptual quality metrics while achieving a 10× inference speedup (0.59s vs. 5.85s+), making high-quality SR practically deployable. However, the fidelity gap on PSNR/SSIM relative to GAN methods remains, reflecting the well-known perception-distortion trade-off, and the 4M-image training set still limits semantic coverage compared to Stable Diffusion's billions of pairs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (e.g., 4K+), whether the fidelity gap can be closed without sacrificing realism, and whether knowledge distillation can further reduce the number of scales needed at inference.
