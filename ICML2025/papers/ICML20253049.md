## Problem

Proving tight hardness bounds (competitive ratio upper bounds) for online bipartite matching requires constructing hard input instances, a task that has traditionally relied on expert-designed families of graphs. A recent RL-based attempt (Zhang et al., ICML 2024) improved one such bound but suffered from an exponential action space, poor generalizability to larger instances, heavy dependence on expert intervention during training, and no leverage of known hard-instance structure. This paper asks whether a unified, end-to-end generative framework can systematically produce harder instances across multiple OBM variants without these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a distribution q over known hard bipartite graph instances (constructed by edge-flipping a base hard graph with probability γ, e.g., γ=0.25 for thick-z) and a competitive ratio calculator that evaluates ALG(𝓘)/OPT(𝓘) for a given instance. It outputs N×N binary adjacency matrices (real-valued during diffusion, rounded via threshold 0.5) representing novel hard instances. The DiT-based denoising network operates on these matrices as image-like inputs with patch size 4 and embedding dimension 32, and the final instances are validated by computing their theoretical CR against a specific online algorithm (Water-filling, Ranking, or Balance).

## Method

DiMa follows a pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm. In pre-training, a diffusion transformer (DiT) is trained as a standard DDPM to learn the distribution q of known hard instances. In fine-tuning, the reverse denoising process is reformulated as a T-step MDP where each denoising step is an action, and the policy is optimized to minimize expected competitive ratio. The key technical contribution is the Shortcut Policy Gradient (SPG) algorithm, which skips k steps in the denoising trajectory (e.g., k=T=100) and estimates the gradient at each skipped step using DDIM's implicit sampling formula rather than backpropagating through the full chain. This reduces GPU memory from >24 GB (traditional computation at 20×20) to 1.14 GB while achieving far better convergence than DDPO's per-step REINFORCE gradient. The reward is r(𝓘)=λ(1−CR(𝓘)) with λ=5, and the algorithm converges to known hardest distributions (upper-triangular for fractional OBM) or discovers novel harder instances for open problems (improving OMRA bound to 0.723 and OMSR bound to 0.594).

## Results

DiMa improves the state-of-the-art competitive ratio upper bounds for two open-ended OBM problems: from 0.727 to 0.723 for Online Matching with Random Arrivals (OMRA) and from 0.597 to 0.594 for Online Matching with Stochastic Rewards (OMSR). On the classic fractional OBM, DiMa successfully reproduces the known hardest upper-triangular instance corresponding to the 1−1/e ≈ 0.632 bound. In terms of efficiency, SPG (the proposed shortcut policy gradient) handles 80×80 instances using 22.59 GB memory in 45 s/epoch, whereas the traditional gradient computation exceeds 24 GB even for 20×20 instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DDPO (Black et al., 2023), the standard RL fine-tuning method for diffusion models. In direct comparison (Figure 4), SPG achieves a steadily increasing average reward during fine-tuning at both instance sizes N=20 and N=40, while DDPO suffers poor convergence and fails to produce any novel harder instances. The key advantage of SPG is its step-skipping mechanism (inspired by DDIM), which reduces the trajectory space and avoids the gradient explosion that plagues DDPO's per-step REINFORCE estimation. Beyond DDPO, DiMa also outperforms the prior RL-based approach of Zhang et al. (2024a) on OMSR, improving the upper bound from 0.597 (at N=7) to 0.594 (at N=10), and scales to substantially larger instances (e.g., N=48 for OMRA) where Zhang et al.'s method is limited to fewer than 10 offline vertices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM generative framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the DDIM step-skipping mechanism (Song et al., 2020), adapting them to the novel domain of combinatorial optimization theory. It departs from the RL-based approach of Zhang et al. (2024a), which used an MDP with exponential action spaces and required expert intervention during training, by instead modeling hard-instance generation as a denoising diffusion process with a DiT backbone. The fine-tuning stage follows the DDPO (Black et al., 2023) paradigm of formulating reverse diffusion as a T-step MDP, but introduces the Shortcut Policy Gradient (SPG) algorithm that skips k steps in the gradient computation, borrowing DDIM's implicit sampling to achieve a dramatic performance-efficiency trade-off over standard REINFORCE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiMa is the first unified, end-to-end framework that uses a generative model to discover hard instances improving theoretical competitive-ratio upper bounds, moving beyond the ad-hoc RL approach of Zhang et al. (2024a) that was limited to small graphs (<10 vertices) and required iterative expert adjustments. The practical impact is demonstrated by improved SOTA bounds for two open OBM variants (OMRA: 0.727→0.723; OMSR: 0.597→0.594) and the ability to scale to 80×80 instances within 24GB GPU memory. Key limitations include the continued need for hand-crafted theoretical proofs of the generated instances, the dependence on a well-chosen initial distribution q (controlled by hyperparameter γ), and the modest numerical gains. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other online optimization problems, approximation algorithms, or whether LLMs could complement the diffusion-based instance search.
