## Problem

Existing methods for conditioning diffusion processes—such as diffusion bridges or adding post-hoc controls to trained generative models—rely on gradient information from the terminal reward or likelihood. In many practical settings, including the canonical diffusion bridge where the reward is a Dirac delta, this gradient is singular or undefined, making standard score-matching and optimal-control approaches numerically unstable or inapplicable. This paper asks whether one can construct conditioning loss functions that remain well-defined and trainable even when the reward is entirely non-differentiable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference SDE (drift b_t, volatility σ_t), a fixed initial state x_0, and a conditioning target y that may be a full terminal state, a partial/noisy observation, or a discrete classification label—no differentiability of the observation operator G is required. The output is a neural-network-parameterised control drift u_t(x; y) that, when added to the reference SDE, produces trajectories distributed as the reference process conditioned on Y = y. Training is amortised: a single network handles all conditioning targets y simultaneously, and the loss depends on G only through sampled observations, never through its gradient.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalisation of Tweedie's formula to nonlinear SDEs, derived by lifting the classical integration-by-parts identity to Wiener space via the Malliavin derivative. This yields a simulable score process S_s (a stochastic integral involving the Jacobian J_{t|s} of the SDE flow and a free matrix-valued weighting α) whose conditional expectation equals the desired bridge drift, without ever differentiating the singular reward. The training loss is a simple L² regression between a neural network u_t(x; y) and Monte Carlo samples of S_s, computed efficiently via an adjoint SDE that propagates only matrix-vector products (via reverse-mode autodifferentiation) rather than the full Jacobian. The choice of α provides a unifying family: specific selections recover the Bismuth–Elworthy–Li formula, the reparametrisation trick of stochastic optimal control matching, and Gaussian-approximation bridge losses, while a variance-optimal α (Lemma 3.1) minimises Monte Carlo noise. The resulting forward-KL interpretation on path space explains the method's mode-covering behaviour and connects it to prior bridge-simulation work.

## Results

On stochastic shape process bridges (conditioning a circle of radius 1 to reach radius 1.5), BEL average achieves a mean pointwise distance of 0.085, outperforming the time-reversal method of Heng et al. (0.090) and the adjoint-paths method of Baker et al. (0.498) by 5.6% and 83% respectively. On the 10-dimensional double-well rare-event problem, BEL first achieves the best mean-variance metric (0.21) and distance metric (0.33), while BEL last and the reparametrisation trick perform worst (MV 0.59 and 0.55, Dist 1.1 and 0.52). For 10-dimensional Brownian motion bridges, BEL average attains the lowest distance (0.023) among all variants, with all methods achieving comparable MV (~0.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the time-reversal method of Heng et al. (2022), BEL average achieves a 5.6% lower distance on shape process bridges (0.085 vs. 0.090); the paper attributes this advantage to BEL average's use of gradient information from the drift and non-local score information, whereas the time-reversal approach differentiates with respect to the final time point and uses a fixed start point instead of amortisation. Against the adjoint-paths method of Baker et al. (2025), BEL average is dramatically superior (0.085 vs. 0.498, an 83% reduction), as the adjoint method relies on a Feynman-Kac representation requiring additional simulations. Against the reparametrisation trick of Domingo-Enrich et al. (2024b), the comparison is setting-dependent: on 1D Brownian motion the reparametrisation trick matches or slightly beats BEL variants (Dist 0.0022 vs. 0.0032), but on the 10D double-well problem it underperforms BEL first (Dist 0.52 vs. 0.33) and BEL average (Dist 0.30 vs. 0.30, tied on MV at 0.34 vs. 0.31). The paper notes that BEL last and the reparametrisation trick consistently exhibit the lowest performance on the double-well rare-event task, aligning with the authors' empirical experience.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously distinct approaches to diffusion bridge simulation and stochastic optimal control under a single Malliavin-calculus framework parameterized by a matrix-valued function α. It generalizes Tweedie's formula (Efron, 2011) from linear to nonlinear SDEs via the Bismuth-Elworthy-Li integration-by-parts identity on Wiener space, and shows that the reparametrisation trick (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024b) and Gaussian-approximation bridge methods (Heng et al., 2022; Baker et al., 2025) are special cases of its loss family. The key departure from prior work is that the resulting loss functions are gradient-free with respect to the observation operator G, enabling them to handle singular rewards (e.g., Dirac deltas for exact diffusion bridges) that break all existing gradient-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to condition diffusions on singular terminal observations without requiring differentiability of the reward opens the door to exact diffusion bridges, classification-style conditioning, and post-hoc control of already-trained diffusion models without retraining the base score network. The framework's reliance only on the reference SDE dynamics and the adjoint SDE (computable via reverse-mode autodifferentiation) makes it directly applicable to manifold-valued and infinite-dimensional settings, which the authors flag as future work. Open questions include the computational cost of the adjoint SDE in high dimensions, the optimal choice of α beyond the Brownian-motion analysis, and whether the modest empirical gains over time-reversal and adjoint-path baselines persist at larger scales or with more complex conditioning operators.
