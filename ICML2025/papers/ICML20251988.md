## Problem

Despite extensive theoretical work on RNNs, most analyses characterize properties of trained solutions while ignoring the learning process itself, and the influence of temporally structured data on learning dynamics has not been studied analytically. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first closed-form analytical treatment of how task dynamics (the temporal structure of input-output correlations) shape learning in linear RNNs, connecting recurrence to learning speed, solution stability, extrapolation, and feature learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251988/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a sequence of T input vectors x_t and a task specified by input-output correlation matrices Σ^{YX_t} whose singular values s_t vary across trajectory timesteps (decomposed as s_t = δf(λ,t) into a scaling term δ and temporal component f(λ,t)). It produces a decoupled energy function per singular-value dimension, analytical solutions for connectivity modes (input a, recurrent b, output c), the finite-width NTK, and predictions about solution stability and extrapolation to unseen trajectory lengths. The key structural constraint is that the analysis operates in the gradient-flow regime (small learning rate) with data-aligned, diagonalizable weights, reducing the network to independent 3-variable dynamical systems per singular value dimension.

## Method

Under assumptions of whitened inputs, constant singular vectors across timesteps, and data-aligned initialization, the authors diagonalize the LRNN to obtain decoupled connectivity modes (a_α, b_α, c_α) per singular-value dimension, then derive a closed-form energy function E = (1/2τ)Σᵢ(s_α,i − c_α b_α^{T−i} a_α)² whose gradient flow yields the learning dynamics. They decompose data singular values into scaling (δ) and temporal (f(λ,t)) components, showing that input-output modes learn the scaling while recurrent modes learn the dynamics, and that learning speed is ordered by both SV magnitude and temporal precedence (larger, later SVs learned first due to exponential downscaling by b<1). The energy function is further decomposed into a data-driven term and an effective regularization term (c²a²(1−b^{2T})/(1−b²)) that incentivizes small weights and mediates a phase transition between recurrent and feedforward computation, analyzed via Landau theory. They also derive the NTK for finite-width LRNNs without alignment assumptions, showing that recurrence (increasing T) drives greater kernel movement and thus feature learning.

## Results

The paper derives a closed-form energy function for LRNNs decoupled along singular-value dimensions, revealing that learning is ordered by both SV magnitude and temporal precedence (larger, later SVs are learned faster due to exponential downscaling by the recurrent mode b<1). For perfectly learnable task dynamics, three solution regimes are identified: constant (b=1, ac=δ), inverse-exponential (b=λ, ac=δ), and exponential (b=1/λ, ac=δλ^T), where early-importance dynamics (s_t > s_{t+1}) produce unstable solutions (|b|>1). The energy function contains an effective regularization term c²a²(1−b^{2T})/(1−b²) that incentivizes small weights and mediates a first-order phase transition (for T>3) between recurrent and feedforward computation. In the sensory integration proof-of-concept (relaxing all theoretical assumptions), the theory accurately predicts both the learned connectivity modes and the extrapolation behavior: sum-integration extrapolates perfectly while mean-integration accumulates error with trajectory-length mismatch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-vs-method benchmarking; the 'baselines' are prior analytical frameworks and conceptual comparisons. The paper extends the Saxe et al. (2014; 2018) linear-network learning dynamics framework from feedforward to recurrent architectures, showing that unlike feedforward networks (which learn largest SVs first regardless of time), LRNNs learn SVs ordered by both scale and temporal position. Compared to Schuessler et al. (2020b), who studied low-rank connectivity changes in LRNNs using constant input at infinite trajectory length, this work accounts for general task dynamics and finite T. Compared to Smekal et al. (2024), who analyzed learning in the frequency domain focusing on overparameterization effects, this work provides a closed-form energy function in the singular-value domain. The empirical validation (sensory integration task) confirms that theoretical predictions hold even when all simplifying assumptions (whitened data, aligned weights, diagonalizable state space) are removed, with the network SVs converging to the predicted values and extrapolation behavior matching theory exactly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytical learning-dynamics framework for deep linear networks (Saxe et al., 2014; 2018) from feedforward to recurrent architectures, and builds on Schuessler et al. (2020b) who studied low-rank connectivity changes in LRNNs but used a constant-input task in the infinite-length limit. The key departure is the introduction of a task-dynamics framework that decomposes data singular values into scaling and temporal components, enabling the first closed-form energy function for LRNNs decoupled along singular-value dimensions. The NTK derivation for finite-width LRNNs extends Jacot et al. (2018) and complements Liu et al. (2024) and Schuessler et al. (2024), who studied rich/lazy regimes in RNNs through connectivity structure and readout scale respectively, by isolating the effect of recurrence itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analytical treatment connecting the temporal structure of task data to learning dynamics in recurrent networks, bridging two previously separate literatures (RNN theory and learning dynamics) and providing mechanistic explanations for low-rank connectivity, stability failures, and extrapolation limits. The identification of an effective regularization term and phase transitions between recurrent and feedforward computation offers a principled account of when and why RNNs prune dimensions. Key limitations include the restriction to linear networks with aligned weights and constant singular vectors, the single-output setting, and the gradient-flow regime; extending these results to nonlinear RNNs, unaligned initializations, and autoregressive outputs remains open and is critical for connecting to biological and practical settings.
