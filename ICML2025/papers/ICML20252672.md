## Problem

State-of-the-art overfitted image codecs (e.g., C3, COOL-CHIC) still fall short of classical codecs like VTM in rate-distortion performance, while AE-based neural codecs achieve competitive RD at the cost of high decoding complexity and large parameter counts. The paper identifies that the fundamental bottleneck is the need to transmit full real-valued network weights (13+ bits per parameter) as the compressed representation, and asks whether a randomly initialized network's structure can encode image statistics, eliminating the need to transmit trained weights entirely.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a single RGB image (tested at 512×768 on Kodak and up to 1370×2048 on CLIC2020) and a shared random seed, and produces a bitstream of four components: autoregressive model parameters ψ̂, quantized latent modulations ẑ, ModNet parameters θ̂, and a binary mask τ. The decoder reinitializes the network from the seed, applies the mask to select a subnetwork, and reconstructs the image by feeding decoded latents through the ModNet into the masked network. The mask ratio (fraction of active weights, adjustable from 0.1 to 0.9) directly controls decoding complexity, enabling adaptive trade-offs between quality and computational cost on the decoder side.

## Method

LotteryCodec proposes the lottery codec hypothesis: a sufficiently over-parameterized, randomly initialized network contains an untrained subnetwork (identified by a binary mask) that can match the RD performance of a fully trained synthesis network at equal latent rate. The architecture comprises four components: (1) a SuperMask network with Fourier initialization, where a score matrix P is optimized via straight-through gradients to select the top-r_a% weights as the active subnetwork; (2) a ModNet that upsamples multi-resolution latent vectors z and generates hierarchical modulation vectors through 1×1 convolutions; (3) a rewind modulation mechanism that concatenates ModNet outputs in reverse order (deepest to shallowest) and appends them to each SuperMask layer's intermediate output, enriching the frozen random weights with learned sign and magnitude information; and (4) an autoregressive entropy model for compressing the latents. The key rate advantage is that the binary mask uses 1 bit per entry versus 13+ bits per real-valued weight in standard overfitted codecs, so despite its higher dimensionality, the mask contributes far less to the total bitrate, yielding superior RD performance while keeping decoding complexity low and adjustable.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, LotteryCodec achieves a BD-rate of −3.73% relative to VTM-19.1 (PSNR), outperforming C3 by −6.4% and C3-adapt by −3.73%. On the CLIC2020 dataset, the advantage grows to −9.79% over VTM-19.1, with −5.69% over C3 and −25.57% over COOL-CHIC v2. Under the MS-SSIM metric on Kodak, LotteryCodec achieves up to −43.39% BD-rate reduction over VTM-19.1. LotteryCodec is the first overfitted neural codec to surpass VTM in RD performance while maintaining low decoding complexity, requiring two orders of magnitude fewer MACs than MLIC+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LotteryCodec outperforms the classical VTM-19.1 codec by −3.73% BD-rate on Kodak and −9.79% on CLIC2020 (PSNR), making it the first overfitted neural codec to beat VTM. Among overfitted codecs, it surpasses C3 by −6.4% (Kodak) and −5.69% (CLIC2020), and dramatically outperforms COOL-CHIC v2 by −25.57% on CLIC2020. Against the state-of-the-art AE-based codec MLIC+, LotteryCodec trails by only +2.33% BD-rate (relative to VTM) on Kodak but requires roughly two orders of magnitude fewer decoding operations, offering a far more favorable RD-complexity trade-off. On the MS-SSIM metric, LotteryCodec achieves −43.39% over VTM-19.1 but remains +10.41% behind MLIC+, confirming that AE-based codecs still lead in pure RD performance at the cost of much higher decoding complexity. LotteryCodec also provides adaptive decoding complexity via adjustable mask ratios (10%–90%), allowing users to trade a small BD-rate penalty for substantially reduced computation—a flexibility none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

LotteryCodec sits at the intersection of two research threads: overfitted implicit neural representation codecs (COIN → COOL-CHIC → C3) and the strong lottery ticket hypothesis (Ramanujan et al., 2020; Pensia et al., 2020). It directly builds on C3's compression pipeline (autoregressive entropy model, multi-resolution latent modulation, Kumaraswamy soft-rounding) but replaces the trained synthesis network with a randomly initialized one, transmitting only a binary mask and lightweight modulation parameters instead of full network weights. The Fourier initialization draws on Shi et al. (2024) for richer INR expressiveness, while the SuperMask scoring and straight-through gradient update follow Ramanujan et al. (2020). The key conceptual departure is the 'lottery codec hypothesis'—that image statistics can be encoded into the *structure* (which weights are active) of a shared random network rather than into its learned parameter values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LotteryCodec is the first overfitted neural codec to surpass VTM in BD-rate while maintaining low and *adaptable* decoding complexity (adjustable via mask ratio), which is practically significant for resource-constrained and multi-user streaming scenarios where encoding is done once offline. The binary mask (≤1 bit per entry) is substantially cheaper to transmit than the real-valued synthesis network weights (≥13 bits per entry) in prior overfitted codecs, explaining much of the RD gain. However, encoding complexity remains high (tens of seconds per image), and the method still trails state-of-the-art AE-based codecs like MLIC+ in absolute RD performance. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to video (temporal mask sharing across groups of frames), whether meta-learning or parallel mask learning can reduce encoding cost, and whether the optimal over-parameterization level generalizes across image content types.
