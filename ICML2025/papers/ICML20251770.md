## Problem

Current Generalist Virtual Agent (GVA) training relies on coarse-grained, outcome-based rewards from labor-intensive human-annotated trajectories, providing no fine-grained process supervision to pinpoint intermediate-step failures and making inference-time scaling impractical. Additionally, no benchmark exists for evaluating step-wise, multi-dimensional reward models in the virtual agent domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multimodal input comprising a text task instruction, a platform observation screenshot (RGB), the agent's prior action trajectory, a candidate action pair, and the current step index. It produces five per-dimension scores (Helpfulness, Odds of Success, Efficiency, Task Relevance, Coherence) and a scalarized reward obtained by a prompt-aware gating network that dynamically re-weights the five dimensions per input. The downstream evaluation task is binary preference selection: choosing the better action from the candidate pair under a specified dimension or the integrated total.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, a five-dimensional process supervision framework computes Helpfulness, Odds of Success, and Efficiency automatically from MCTS rollouts (using trajectory length, success rate, and step-count reduction), while Task Relevance and Coherence are assessed by an MLLM (GPT-4o) as binary judgments. Second, a modified MCTS-P algorithm uses the five-dimensional composite score for node selection and backpropagation to automatically collect and annotate 110k step-wise data points across four platforms (Web, Android, Linux, Windows), eliminating the need for human annotation. Third, a Triple-M (Multi-step, Multi-objective, Multi-modal) training strategy operates in two stages: a linear regression head on a frozen MLLM backbone predicts the five-dimensional score vector via L2 loss, then a prompt-aware gating MLP trained with Bradley-Terry preference loss learns input-dependent weights that combine the five dimensions into a single scalarized reward. The resulting Similar model is deployed both as a DPO reward signal for agent training and as an MCTS evaluation function for inference-time scaling.

## Results

On the SRMEval benchmark, Similar-TM-Llama achieves an average accuracy of 61.2 across all evaluation dimensions, outperforming GPT-4o (51.4) by 19.1% and GPT-4-Turbo (46.6) by 31.3%. The Triple-M strategy yields a 13.5% improvement over the RL-only variant (53.9 → 61.2), with the largest per-dimension gains in Helpfulness (48.2 → 63.8, +32.3%) and Efficiency (47.1 → 59.2, +25.6%). In downstream agent tasks, Similar-TM-Llama boosts OS-Atlas's task success rate from 30.4% to 35.4% on Android World and from 14.3% to 17.8% on OSWorld during inference-time scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Similar-TM-Llama (Avg 61.2) decisively outperforms all MLLM-based reward model baselines on SRMEval: GPT-4o (51.4, +19.1%), GPT-4-Turbo (46.6, +31.3%), Qwen2-VL (42.9, +42.7%), and InternVL-2.5 (40.9, +49.6%). As an agent baseline, OS-Atlas without a reward model achieves 30.4% SR on Android World and 14.3% on OSWorld; with Similar-TM-Llama for inference-time scaling, these improve to 35.4% and 17.8% respectively (+16.4% and +24.5%). UGround as an agent baseline reaches 19.6% on WebArena without a reward model, rising to 36.7% with Similar-TM-Llama for DPO training (+87.2%). The Qwen2-VL backbone variant (Similar-TM-Qwen, Avg 57.3) also surpasses GPT-4o, confirming that the gains are not backbone-specific. Across all four compared baselines, Similar consistently wins on every evaluation dimension and both training and inference settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Process Reward Model (PRM) paradigm from math reasoning (Lightman et al., 2023; Uesato et al., 2022) to the virtual agent domain, where prior work relied on coarse-grained, outcome-based rewards from human-annotated trajectories. It adapts MCTS-based automated data collection (Luo et al., 2024; ReST-MCTS*, Zhang et al., 2024b) into MCTS-P, which simultaneously annotates five interpretable dimensions rather than a single scalar Q-value (cf. Zhai et al., 2024). The Triple-M training strategy combines regression-based multi-dimensional score prediction with a Bradley-Terry preference-optimized gating network, drawing on DPO-style preference learning (Rafailov et al., 2023) but applied to a multi-objective, multi-modal setting. The key departure from prior virtual-agent reward work is the shift from single-dimensional, outcome-oriented supervision to step-wise, multi-dimensional, cross-platform process supervision with fully automated annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SRM benchmark (110K annotated data points across Web, Android, Linux, and Windows) is the first dedicated evaluation suite for step-wise, multi-dimensional reward models in the virtual agent domain, filling a gap where prior agent benchmarks (WebArena, Android World, OSWorld) evaluated agents but not their reward models. The five-dimensional framework (Helpfulness, Odds of Success, Efficiency, Task Relevance, Coherence) provides interpretable, fine-grained signals that could guide more efficient agent training and inference-time scaling beyond what single-scalar rewards allow. Key open questions include: whether the MCTS-P annotation quality degrades in highly ambiguous or long-horizon tasks, whether the framework generalizes to domains beyond the four tested platforms, and whether the performance plateau observed at N>8 in MCTS reflects a fundamental agent-model limitation or a reward-model calibration issue. The reliance on GPT-4o for both data collection and MLLM-based dimension scoring (TR, C) introduces a potential circularity that limits the independence of the evaluation.
