## Problem

Predicting the electronic Hamiltonian of realistic, disordered materials (amorphous phases, defects, interfaces) requires unit cells with thousands of atoms and 10+ Å dimensions, making DFT computationally prohibitive. Existing equivariant GNN approaches for Hamiltonian prediction have only been demonstrated on small molecules or periodic crystals with ≤150 atoms per unit cell, and cannot handle the dense connectivity and memory demands of large disordered structures. The core challenge is that these graphs cannot be naively partitioned for GPU training without corrupting the local atomic environments the network needs to learn from.

## Input / Output

The network takes a graph where nodes represent atoms (initialized with atomic numbers) and edges represent interatomic interactions within a cutoff r_cut (initialized with scalar distances), producing the full electronic Hamiltonian matrix H in a localized contracted-Gaussian orbital basis. The output is structured as per-atom diagonal blocks H_ii and per-edge off-diagonal blocks H_ij, each expressed in coupled angular momentum eigenstates and post-processed back to the uncoupled basis for matrix assembly. The graph is densely connected (>100 edges per node for r_cut = 8 Å) because orbital interactions decay only over ~10 Å, and the resulting H can contain ~28 million nonzero entries for a 3,000-atom structure.

## Method

The architecture is a strictly local (1-hop) equivariant GNN combining eSCN SO(2) convolutions with multi-headed attention (EquiformerV2-style), where node embeddings predict diagonal Hamiltonian blocks and learnable edge embeddings predict off-diagonal blocks. The key technical contribution is an augmented partitioning scheme: the input graph is sliced along the longest periodic dimension, and virtual nodes/edges are added for all 1-hop neighbors outside each slice, preserving exact local connectivity while keeping each partition within single-GPU memory. Orbital interaction blocks are pre-transformed into the coupled angular momentum basis via Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, reducing the number of Wigner-D rotations needed during equivariant processing. Training uses MSE loss in the coupled space on padded tensors, while evaluation reports MAE in the uncoupled space after extracting un-padded data. The approach achieves 2.17–2.58 meV Hamiltonian prediction accuracy on 1,000–3,000-atom amorphous structures and reproduces eigenvalue spectra to ≤0.53% relative L1 error, with a 6.5× per-epoch speedup and 7.2× memory reduction over full-graph training.

## Results

The model achieves prediction errors of 2.17–2.58 meV (ε_tot) on unseen amorphous structures containing 1,000–3,000 atoms and up to 1,792,760 edges, with node errors (ε_n) of 0.77–2.16 mE_h and edge errors (ε_e) of 0.08–0.16 mE_h. The predicted Hamiltonian reproduces the full eigenvalue spectrum to within 0.530% relative L1 error (0.446% for occupied states only) on a 3,000-atom a-HfO₂ structure. Augmented partitioning with 8 slices yields a 6.5× per-epoch speedup and 7.2× memory reduction versus full-graph training without degrading accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only formal head-to-head baseline comparison is against QHNet (Yu et al., 2023b) on the MD17 small-molecule benchmark. This work outperforms QHNet on all three molecules: water (5.60 vs. 10.79 μE_h), malondialdehyde (16.60 vs. 21.52 μE_h), and uracil (13.80 vs. 20.12 μE_h), with improvements of roughly 48–48% in total Hamiltonian error. For the large disordered structures that are the paper's primary focus, no direct GNN baseline exists in the literature; the authors note their 2.17–2.58 meV accuracy on 1,000–3,000-atom amorphous cells is comparable to the 2.2 meV reported by Wang et al. (2024b) for much smaller (≤150 atom) crystalline unit cells, demonstrating that the method extends Hamiltonian prediction to a regime where DFT is computationally prohibitive (3.65 node-hours per structure vs. 0.04 for H₂O).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SO(2)-convolution framework of eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) and the equivariant attention of EquiformerV2 (Liao et al., 2023), combining them with the learnable edge-embedding approach for off-diagonal Hamiltonian blocks introduced by Gong et al. (2023) and Wang et al. (2024a). The key departure is the augmented partitioning strategy, which inserts virtual nodes and edges at partition boundaries to preserve exact 1-hop connectivity during training on arbitrarily large graphs—a problem that prior Hamiltonian-prediction GNNs (targeting molecules or small periodic unit cells) simply did not face. The paper positions itself explicitly against the open problem of predicting Hamiltonians for large, structurally disordered materials where DFT is computationally prohibitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating meV-level Hamiltonian accuracy on 3,000-atom amorphous structures and showing that the predicted matrices can drive quantum transport simulations of resistive-switching devices, the work opens a practical pathway for ML-accelerated electronic-structure analysis of realistic materials with defects, interfaces, and compositional disorder. The augmented partitioning technique is general and could be applied to other large-graph learning problems beyond Hamiltonians. Limitations include the strictly local (1-hop) receptive field, testing on only three material families, and the fact that device-transport predictions show qualitative but not fully quantitative agreement with DFT. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-body interactions, larger cutoffs, and heterogeneous device geometries beyond the single TiN/a-HfOx/Ti/TiN stack demonstrated.
