## Problem

Non-Autoregressive Transformers (NATs) for de novo peptide sequencing rely on CTC loss, whose complex reduction rules and vast alignment search space cause frequent training failures including loss explosion, extreme overfitting, and unstable convergence (up to 80% failure rate on some datasets). No prior method had addressed the optimization difficulty inherent in CTC-based NAT training for biological sequence generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an MS/MS spectrum as a sequence of (mass-to-charge, peak-intensity) pairs, along with the precursor mass and peptide charge as scalar constraints. The spectrum is encoded via sinusoidal positional encoding of each float value into a 400-dimensional embedding. The output is a full amino acid sequence, post-processed by a Precise Mass Control (PMC) unit that enforces the precursor mass constraint via a knapsack-style dynamic programming solver over the per-position token probability distributions.

## Method

RefineNovo introduces a CTC-based curriculum learning strategy where, at each training step, the model performs a forward pass to obtain per-position probabilities, computes the best CTC alignment path to the target, and uses that path as a partially-masked conditional input to the decoder. A difficulty annealing schedule adaptively sets the masking ratio as ρ = α(1 − acc), so early training exposes most ground-truth tokens (easy) and later training masks more (hard), progressively reducing the effective search space. At inference, a self-refining module iteratively feeds the previously argmax-decoded sequence back through the learned token embedding layer for N=3 passes, exploiting the curriculum embedding to correct alignment errors before final PMC decoding. The backbone is a 9-layer encoder-decoder Transformer with 8 attention heads, trained with CTC loss and AdamW over 30 epochs on MassIVE-KB.

## Results

On the 9-species-V2 benchmark, RefineNovo achieves an average amino acid precision of 0.907 and peptide recall of 0.790, surpassing the previous NAT state-of-the-art PrimeNovo (0.891 / 0.777) and the best AR model CasanovoV2 (0.862 / 0.714). On the 9-species-V1 benchmark, the model reaches 0.800 AA precision and 0.653 peptide recall, outperforming all 10 baselines in 8 of 9 species on both metrics. The curriculum learning strategy also reduces NAT training failure rates by over 90% (1/20 failures vs. 18/20 for PrimeNovo across 20 random data splits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefineNovo outperforms all 10 baselines on the 9-species-V2 benchmark in average AA precision (0.907 vs. 0.891 for PrimeNovo and 0.862 for CasanovoV2) and peptide recall (0.790 vs. 0.777 and 0.714). Against the closest competitor PrimeNovo (the prior NAT SOTA), RefineNovo gains +1.6 pp in AA precision and +1.3 pp in peptide recall on average, winning in all 9 species for precision and 8 of 9 for recall. Against the best AR model CasanovoV2, the gap is larger: +4.5 pp in AA precision and +7.6 pp in peptide recall. On the older 9-species-V1 benchmark, RefineNovo similarly leads all methods including AdaNovo (0.697 AA prec.) and PointNovo (0.687), with the largest margin over the DB method Peaks (0.663). On NovoBench (yeast), RefineNovo also surpasses InstaNovo (0.53), SearchNovo (0.55), and HelixNovo (0.52) in the 9-species yeast subset, though the paper notes a distribution mismatch on the 7-species subset due to different MS instrument precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PrimeNovo (Zhang et al., 2025), the first non-autoregressive Transformer for de novo peptide sequencing, by addressing its critical training instability (loss explosion, overfitting, unstable convergence) through two additions: a CTC-path-based curriculum learning strategy with adaptive difficulty annealing, and a self-refining iterative inference module. The curriculum learning concept is repurposed from general ML into a novel within-sequence, within-CTC-path granularity that had not been explored before, while the iterative refinement draws inspiration from AlphaFold2 and ESM-3 but operates on CTC alignment paths rather than directly on output tokens. The Precise Mass Control (PMC) knapsack decoder is inherited unchanged from PrimeNovo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% reduction in NAT training failures is practically significant: prior NAT peptide sequencing models were unreliable to train (failing in 18/20 random splits), making the architecture difficult to adopt. The within-sequence difficulty annealing is a generalizable technique applicable to any CTC-based NAT model beyond peptide sequencing. However, the performance gains over PrimeNovo are modest (~1-2% absolute), and the NAR model still slightly trails the best AR model (ContraNovo) on human peptide recall, leaving open whether NAT can fully close the AR gap. The approach's reliance on a fixed 3-iteration refinement and a single peek factor α limits its adaptability to varying sequence lengths and difficulty distributions.
