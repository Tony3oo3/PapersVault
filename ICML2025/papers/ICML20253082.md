## Problem

Existing discrete diffusion methods are fragmented across incompatible theoretical foundations (denoising score entropy, CTMC objectives, absorbing-state assumptions), and they predominantly focus on pre-training while lacking the post-training capabilities—reward fine-tuning, preference optimization, and knowledge distillation—that have proven essential for autoregressive models. This paper asks whether a single principled objective can unify both pre-training and post-training for discrete diffusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253082/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes a noisy token sequence x_t (an intermediate state along the diffusion path from a source distribution such as uniform or all-mask) and predicts the clean sequence x_1 via the denoising distribution p_{1|t}(x_1|x_t), parameterized over a k-Hamming neighborhood of single-token substitutions. At inference, the model generates text by simulating the learned CTMC velocity field from a source state (e.g., all-mask) to a clean sequence. In post-training, additional inputs include a scalar reward function R(x), pairwise preference data (query, preferred, non-preferred), or a pre-trained AR teacher model whose conditional distributions serve as the distillation target.

## Method

TCSM matches the concrete score of the target (clean) distribution p_1 rather than the forward noising kernel p_{t|1}, making it the discrete analog of continuous Target Score Matching. The concrete score at a point x is the vector of probability ratios [p(y)/p(x)] over neighbors y in a k-Hamming neighborhood, and the training objective minimizes a Bregman divergence between the true and model concrete scores. Two equivalent formulations are provided: a score-based objective operating on probability ratios and a distribution-based objective operating on conditional distributions p(x_1^i | x_1^{≠i}, x_t), linked by a softmax relationship. For pre-training, Monte Carlo estimators reduce the objective to a pseudo-likelihood plus an entropy term (score-based) or a standard cross-entropy (distribution-based), and a parametric model of p_1 (e.g., a pre-trained BERT or hollow transformer) can substitute for the intractable true target to accelerate convergence. For post-training, TCSM leverages density ratio estimation with Bregman divergences (LSIF, BCE, generalized KL) to fine-tune relative to a reference model, enabling reward-guided optimization, direct preference optimization, and AR-to-diffusion distillation—all within the same clean-data-space formulation.

## Results

On OPENWEBTEXT zero-shot perplexity (lower is better), the best TCSM variant (absorbing source + L_distrib with density-ratio post-training, Sec. 5.1) achieves ≤47.29 on LAMBADA, ≤96.71 on PTB, ≤31.56 on WikiText, and ≤65.82 on 1BW, surpassing MDLM (≤47.52, ≤95.26, ≤32.83, ≤67.01) on three of four benchmarks. On TEXT8 BPC, TCSM Absorb L_distrib (Sec. 5.1) reaches ≤1.25, matching EDLM (≤1.24) and outperforming MD4 (≤1.37) and SEDD Absorb (≤1.39). With uniform source, TCSM L_distrib achieves ≤1.45 BPC on TEXT8, improving over SEDD Uniform (≤1.47). The density-ratio post-training (Sec. 5.1) consistently adds 1–3 perplexity points of improvement over the pre-training-only TCSM across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (the strongest discrete diffusion baseline), TCSM with absorbing source and density-ratio post-training wins on LAMBADA (47.29 vs 47.52), WikiText (31.56 vs 32.83), and 1BW (65.82 vs 67.01), but trails on PTB (96.71 vs 95.26). Against MD4, TCSM (pre-training only, Sec. 4.2) wins on LAMBADA (48.37 vs 48.43) and WikiText (34.92 vs 35.90) but is slightly behind on PTB (101.85 vs 102.26) and 1BW (68.43 vs 68.10). With uniform source, TCSM L_distrib clearly outperforms SEDD on PTB (133.67 vs 140.12), WikiText (46.91 vs 49.60), and 1BW (98.52 vs 101.37). On TEXT8 BPC, TCSM Absorb L_distrib (Sec. 5.1) at ≤1.25 is nearly on par with EDLM (≤1.24) and substantially beats MD4 (≤1.37), SEDD Absorb (≤1.39), and D3PM Uniform (≤1.61). The density-ratio post-training step (Sec. 5.1) provides a consistent 1–3 perplexity point gain over pre-training-only TCSM across all four OPENWEBTEXT benchmarks, and on TEXT8 improves BPC from ≤1.37 to ≤1.25.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCSM extends Concrete Score Matching (Meng et al., 2022) from the forward noising kernel to the clean target distribution, making it the discrete analog of Target Score Matching (Bortoli et al., 2024) in continuous diffusion. It is built on the discrete flow matching framework (Campbell et al., 2024; Gat et al., 2024) and unifies several previously fragmented approaches—SEDD, MD4, MDLM, and DFM—as special cases under different choices of divergence, parameterization, and probability path. A key departure from prior discrete diffusion work is the shift from denoising score matching (operating through p_{t|1}) to target score matching (operating directly on p_1), which naturally enables integration with reward functions, preference data, and pre-trained models that only operate in clean data space. The density ratio estimation component draws on Bregman divergence-based methods (Sugiyama et al., 2012) to enable stable post-training without adversarial two-stage optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant contribution is bridging the post-training gap for discrete diffusion models: reward-guided fine-tuning, direct preference optimization, and AR-to-diffusion distillation were previously unavailable or ad hoc, and TCSM provides a principled, unified mechanism for all three. The parametric target estimation trick (using pre-trained AR/BERT/hollow-transformer models to estimate p_1) offers a practical path to faster convergence, which is critical for discrete diffusion to compete with autoregressive models in compute efficiency. However, empirical validation is limited to character-level and word-level language modeling on relatively small datasets (TEXT8, OPENWEBTEXT), and performance gains over strong baselines like MDLM and EDLM are modest. Open questions include whether the framework scales to large-scale LLM training, whether the post-training recipes transfer to more complex generation tasks (code, structured data), and whether the theoretical unification holds under non-factorized parameterizations at scale.
