## Problem

Single-view object-centric learning cannot resolve spatial ambiguities such as occlusions and view transformations, while existing multi-view methods (MULMON, OCLOC, NeRF-based approaches) require viewpoint annotations and lack formal identifiability guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first theoretical framework for identifiable object-centric representations from multi-view data without any viewpoint supervision, under partial or full occlusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251237/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V unannotated RGB images of the same scene from different viewpoints as input, with the critical constraint that the number of visible objects may differ across views (allowing partial or full occlusion). It produces three types of latent variables: view-invariant content vectors (c) as a GMM aggregated across all views, view-specific slot representations (s) as per-view GMMs in a shared implicit space, and inferred viewpoint vectors (v) as a GMM. At inference, sigmoid-normalized alpha masks enable visual estimation of occluded objects, resolving spatial ambiguities.

## Method

VISA (View-Invariant Slot Attention) extends probabilistic slot attention to the multi-view setting through four coupled components: (1) a per-image view encoder infers a global viewpoint vector from a GMM prior, which parameterizes an affine spatial transformer to project each view into a common implicit space; (2) within each projected view, an EM algorithm fits a spatial GMM over feature embeddings to obtain view-specific slot posteriors; (3) Hungarian matching aligns slots across views to a base representation, with a view-warmup strategy to stabilize early training; (4) a content aggregator computes a convex combination of aligned slots weighted by their mixing coefficients, naturally down-weighting objects absent due to occlusion. Training maximizes the ELBO with a shared decoder (additive CNN/MLP or non-additive transformer) across all views, and the content prior is set to the aggregate posterior (optimal prior) rather than an explicit KL term. The paper proves three theoretical results: identifiability of content up to permutation-affine equivalence (~s), invariance of content across viewpoint subsets satisfying viewpoint sufficiency, and approximate equivariance of view representations under homeomorphic image transformations.

## Results

On CLEVR-MV, VISA achieves the highest identifiability scores across all three metrics: SMCC 0.67, INV-SMCC 0.66, and MCC 0.60, outperforming the closest multi-view baseline OCLOC (0.63, 0.64, 0.48). On GSO, VISA leads with SMCC 0.60, INV-SMCC 0.61, and MCC 0.58, again surpassing OCLOC (0.58, 0.54, 0.46). On GQN, VISA achieves the best INV-SMCC (0.63) and MCC (0.52) while matching OCLOC's SMCC (0.59 vs 0.60). On the proposed MV-MOVIC dataset, VISA-MLP achieves SMCC 0.52 and INV-SMCC 0.61 in-domain, maintaining comparable performance out-of-domain (SMCC 0.51, INV-SMCC 0.58), demonstrating generalisability across viewpoint changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISA consistently outperforms OCLOC (Yuan et al., 2024), the closest multi-view baseline, across all three datasets and most metrics. The largest gap appears on the MCC (view equivariance) metric: VISA achieves 0.60 vs OCLOC's 0.48 on CLEVR-MV, 0.52 vs 0.42 on GQN, and 0.58 vs 0.46 on GSO, indicating substantially better recovery of viewpoint information. On SMCC (slot identifiability), VISA leads on CLEVR-MV (0.67 vs 0.63) and GSO (0.60 vs 0.58) but is marginally behind OCLOC on GQN (0.59 vs 0.60). Single-view baselines (AE, SA, PSA) cannot report INV-SMCC or MCC since they do not model view information, and they trail VISA by 0.18–0.43 on SMCC. MulMON (Li et al., 2020) requires viewpoint annotations and does not estimate view representations, so MCC is inapplicable; its SMCC (0.61 on CLEVR-MV) is still below VISA's 0.67. On the proposed MV-MOVIC dataset, VISA-MLP achieves SMCC 0.52 and INV-SMCC 0.61, substantially outperforming SA-MLP (0.36, –) and PSA-MLP (0.38, –), with the gap widening to ~0.14 on SMCC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Probabilistic Slot Attention (PSA, Kori et al. 2024) from single-view to multi-view settings, introducing View-Invariant Slot Attention (VISA) that aggregates view-specific GMM slot posteriors into view-invariant content representations. It departs from prior multi-view OCL methods (MULMON, DYMON, OCLOC) by eliminating the need for viewpoint annotations, instead learning a global view distribution and applying inverse spatial transformer networks to project all views into a common implicit space. The theoretical framework builds on the GMM-based identifiability results for nonlinear ICA (Kivva et al. 2022; Khemakhem et al. 2020) and single-view OCL identifiability (Brady et al. 2023; Lachapelle et al. 2023), extending them to the multi-object, multi-view regime with a viewpoint-sufficiency assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide formal identifiability guarantees for multi-object, multi-view object-centric representations under occlusions and view ambiguities without requiring viewpoint labels, filling a notable gap between single-view identifiability theory and practical multi-view OCL. The viewpoint-sufficiency assumption (all objects visible across the union of views) is strong and may not hold in real-world scenes, though the authors show limited degradation on their MV-MOVID dataset where cameras vary per scene. Open questions include whether the weak injectivity condition on mixing functions holds for more complex architectures, how the method scales to very large numbers of views, and whether the GMM-based identifiability framework can be relaxed to accommodate non-Gaussian latent structures.
