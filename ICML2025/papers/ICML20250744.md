## Problem

Standard convolutional layers for time series are highly sensitive to common deformations such as offset shifts, amplitude scaling, and linear trends, causing significant performance degradation under distribution shift. While invariance can be partially learned via contrastive learning, hard-coded architectural invariances for time series deformations remain largely unexplored, and existing approaches lack an exact mathematical formulation. This paper provides a rigorous group-action framework and constructs exact invariant convolutions for these deformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete univariate or multivariate time series of arbitrary length, operating on local windows of size W (typically 30–128 timestamps) within each signal. The invariant convolution projects each window onto the orthogonal complement of the deformation subspace (e.g., removing the constant or linear-trend component) before computing the kernel inner product, producing an invariant embedding per position. Downstream, these embeddings feed a linear classifier for class labels, a reconstruction decoder for anomaly detection, or a transfer-learning head for cross-domain classification. Computation leverages FFT to handle the large kernel sizes efficiently, yielding O(BNCL log L) complexity independent of kernel width.

## Method

The paper formalizes time series deformations (amplitude scaling, offset shift, linear trend) as a semidirect product group R+* ⋉ H acting on L², where H is a finite-dimensional subspace spanned by basis functions (e.g., constants for offset, {1, t} for linear trend). The invariant embedding is the orthogonal projector onto H⊥, optionally normalized for scaling invariance, guaranteeing both G-invariance and orbit-injectivity. The invariant convolution applies this projection to each sliding window of the input signal before taking the inner product with the kernel, decomposing into 2N+2 dot products that are shared across positions via FFT. The proposed INVCONVNET architecture concatenates standard, offset-invariant, and trend-invariant convolutions in a single inception-like layer with multiple kernel sizes, followed by global average pooling and a linear classifier, requiring no Z-normalization preprocessing and no learned invariance.

## Results

On the 26-dataset UEA benchmark, INVCONVNET achieves 71.81±0.80% average classification accuracy, outperforming all 12 baselines including ROCKET (71.29%), TSLANET (68.70%), and RESNET (67.37%). On the additional datasets, it reaches 96.63% on UCIHAR, 84.95% on Sleep-EDF, and 98.43% on Epilepsy. In the robustness study, the trend-invariant convolution variant degrades by only 6% under smooth random walk deformations, compared to 59% for standard convolutions and 34% for the contrastive TS-TCC baseline. In transfer learning on Fault-Diagnosis, INVCONVNET outperforms both TS-TCC and TS2VEC by at least 4% across all domain pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INVCONVNET (a single inception-like layer combining standard, offset-invariant, and trend-invariant convolutions) achieves the best average accuracy on UEA at 71.81%, narrowly beating ROCKET (71.29%) and clearly outperforming all deep learning baselines by 3–11 points. Among transformer-based methods, CROSSFORMER (66.37%) and PATCHTST (66.18%) are the strongest, yet both trail INVCONVNET by over 5 points; the MLP-based DLINEAR is the weakest at 61.51%. On UCIHAR, INVCONVNET (96.63%) edges out RESNET (96.04%) and INCEPTION (95.26%); on Sleep-EDF, RESNET (85.62%) slightly surpasses INVCONVNET (84.95%), likely due to its deeper architecture. In the robustness study (Table 1), the trend-invariant variant maintains near-constant accuracy under offset, linear trend, and random walk deformations (≤6% drop), while standard convolutions degrade by up to 59% and the contrastive TS-TCC by up to 34%. In transfer learning (Table 4), supervised INVCONVNET outperforms both self-supervised TS-TCC and TS2VEC by at least 4% on average across all 12 source→target domain pairs, with notably lower variance. For anomaly detection (Table 9), INVCONVNET achieves 84.44% average F1, ranking second to TSLANET (85.42%) but best on SWaT (92.82%), and consistently outperforms standard CNN variants (INCEPTION, RESNET, CNN) by 4–10 points in the ablation (Table 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the group-action invariance framework well established for images (translation, rotation via steerable CNNs) and graphs (permutation invariance) into the time series domain, where prior work has largely relied on either preprocessing (Z-normalization, baseline removal) or learned invariance via contrastive learning (TS-TCC, TS2VEC, TIMECLR). The key departure is providing an exact, closed-form construction of invariant convolutions via orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of a deformation subspace, rather than approximating invariance through data augmentation or auxiliary losses. The resulting INVCONVNET architecture is a single-layer inception-like pool combining standard, offset-invariant, and linear-trend-invariant convolutions, positioned as a lightweight alternative to deep CNNs (InceptionTime, ResNet) and transformer-based models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hard-coded invariance to affine deformations yields substantially better out-of-distribution robustness than both standard convolutions (48–59% accuracy drop) and contrastive-learned invariance (up to 34% drop), while a single-layer architecture matches or exceeds deep multi-layer baselines on UEA classification. Limitations include restriction to linear (first-order) trend approximation, a specific semidirect-product group structure, and the need for large kernel sizes (≥30) where FFT is required for tractability. Open questions the authors identify include extending to non-linear trends (splines, higher-degree polynomials), incorporating seasonal components, exploring alternative merging strategies (attention, reinforcement learning), and adapting the framework to unsupervised settings.
