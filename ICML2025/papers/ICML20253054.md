## Problem

Finding the optimal data mixture for LLM pretraining is critical yet computationally prohibitive: methods like DoReMi and RegMix require training proxy models and refitting performance functions, with costs growing non-linearly as the number of datasets increases. Moreover, these approaches lack scalability—any change in dataset composition (adding sources, filtering data) necessitates resampling mixtures, retraining, and refitting from scratch. The paper asks whether one can identify optimal mixtures in a training-free, scalable manner that adapts to changing datasets without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple training datasets (e.g., 17 components of The Pile), a validation dataset (e.g., Pile-CC), and the token distribution of training datasets as input. Each document is passed through a fine-tuned Qwen2-1.5B meta-domain classifier that outputs a 260-dimensional probability vector; the dataset-level domain vector is the mean over N sampled documents. The output is a set of domain vectors (one per dataset, each a 260-dim distribution over meta-domains) and the optimal mixture ratio vector r constrained to sum to 1 with per-dataset token-availability limits (#TotalTokens · r_i ≤ |D_i|).

## Method

DOMAIN2VEC constructs a vocabulary of 260 meta-domains (120 English + 120 Chinese + 20 Code) via k-means clustering on 5.2 TB of text embedded with bge-small models, then trains a 260-class Qwen2-1.5B classifier to assign documents to meta-domains. Any dataset is represented as a domain vector—the average classifier output over sampled documents—enabling all mixture-optimization operations to occur in this fixed 260-dimensional space. Two optimization strategies are provided: (1) DA² (Distribution Alignment Assumption), a training-free method that minimizes Huber loss between the mixed training domain vector V_train·r and the validation domain vector v_valid; and (2) DOMAIN2VEC+RegMix, which fits per-meta-domain LightGBM regressors mapping domain vectors to validation loss, then composes them via the validation set's domain vector weights. A pilot study confirms that mixture-ratio rankings are nearly invariant across model sizes (Spearman 0.9743 between 83M and 1.6B models), justifying the use of small or no proxy models. Because the meta-domain vocabulary is fixed, adding new training or validation datasets requires only computing their domain vectors—no refitting of performance functions is needed.

## Results

On 12 downstream benchmarks (averaged over 0–5 shot), DOMAIN2VEC+RegMix achieves 0.436 average performance and DOMAIN2VEC+DA² achieves 0.432, compared to 0.424 for the original Pile mixture (Human), 0.439 for DoReMi, and 0.441 for RegMix. Critically, both DOMAIN2VEC variants require only 9.66×10¹⁶ FLOPs (0.26% of DoReMi's 3.7×10¹⁹), while RegMix requires 3.5×10¹⁸ FLOPs (9.46%). On Pile-CC validation loss, DOMAIN2VEC+RegMix matches Human's loss using only 51.5% of training steps, and under equal compute reduces validation loss by 4.64% (DA²: 4.04%) and improves downstream performance by 2.83% (DA²: 1.89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DoReMi (3.7×10¹⁹ FLOPs), both DOMAIN2VEC variants achieve near-comparable average downstream performance (0.432/0.436 vs. 0.439) while using only 0.26% of the computational cost, representing a ~380× reduction in FLOPs. Against RegMix (3.5×10¹⁸ FLOPs), DOMAIN2VEC+RegMix scores 0.436 vs. RegMix's 0.441 (a 0.5% gap) but at roughly 1/36th the compute. Both DOMAIN2VEC methods clearly outperform the Human (original Pile) mixture by 1.89% (DA²) and 2.83% (RegMix) in average downstream accuracy. The Pile-CC Only baseline (0.439) serves as an upper-bound oracle that requires knowing the validation set in advance; DOMAIN2VEC+RegMix (0.436) comes within 0.3% of this ideal without such privileged information. On the validation-loss ranking task (Table 2), DOMAIN2VEC+DA² achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.6657 with actual rankings, substantially outperforming random (0.0497) and the kNN embedding baseline (0.3543), confirming the training-free approach's predictive power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-mixture optimization line initiated by DoReMi (proxy-model reweighting) and RegMix (regression over mixture ratios) by introducing a vectorized dataset representation (domain vectors over clustered meta-domains) that decouples the mixture-search problem from the specific set of training sources. The key conceptual departure is the Distribution Alignment Assumption (DA²), which replaces the need for any proxy-model training with a simple distance minimization between the mixed training distribution and the validation distribution in meta-domain space. The meta-domain construction via k-means clustering over 5.2 TB of text and the use of a fine-tuned Qwen2-1.5B classifier as a document-to-meta-domain mapper are practical instantiations that have no direct analogue in prior mixture-optimization work, though the 'dataset as a point in a latent space' idea echoes Dataset2Vec (Jomaa et al., 2021) in a different application context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.26% compute cost relative to DoReMi (9.66×10¹⁶ vs 3.7×10¹⁹ FLOPs) is a substantial practical efficiency gain for teams that need to re-optimize mixtures as their data sources evolve, since the meta-domain vocabulary and classifier remain fixed while new datasets can be vectorized on the fly. However, the method's ceiling is constrained by the quality of the 260 meta-domains and the 74.73% classifier accuracy, and the DA² assumption—while intuitive—lacks a formal guarantee linking distributional alignment to validation loss. Open questions include how the approach behaves when the validation set is not a clean subset of the training distribution, whether the meta-domain vocabulary needs to be re-derived for substantially different language or domain compositions, and whether the moderate Spearman correlation (0.6657) in the training-free setting is sufficient for high-stakes production decisions.
