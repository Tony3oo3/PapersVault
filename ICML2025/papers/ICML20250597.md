## Problem

Foundation models for wearable data have been applied almost exclusively to low-level biosignals (PPG, ECG, accelerometer), yet higher-level behavioral metrics (activity, gait, cardiovascular fitness) are better aligned with the physiologically relevant timescales needed for health detection tasks. No prior work had built a large-scale foundation model for irregularly sampled behavioral time series from wearables, despite these data being more informative for many detection problems and presenting unique modeling challenges (irregular sampling, high missingness, variable rates across 27 variables).

## Input / Output

The model consumes weekly windows of hourly-aggregated data across 27 HealthKit behavioral quantities (activity, cardiovascular, vitals, gait/mobility, body measurements, fitness), represented as a 168×54 dense matrix (168 hours × 27 variables + 27 missingness indicators) after z-scoring and global-mean imputation of missing values. Each week is encoded into a single embedding vector by averaging the bi-directional Mamba-2 outputs across the 168 hourly patches. Downstream, these embeddings feed into ridge-regularized linear probes that produce classification AUROCs (for 47 static disease/medication tasks and 4 time-varying detection tasks) or regression R²/MAE values (for sleep metrics and age prediction).

## Method

WBM uses a TST-style dense tokenizer that projects each hourly patch (a 54-dim vector of z-scored values plus missingness flags) through a single linear layer, followed by a 24-layer bi-directional Mamba-2 backbone (hidden dim 256, FFN dim 1024) whose forward and backward outputs are concatenated, projected back, and averaged across time to yield the final embedding. Pre-training employs a subject-level positive-sampling contrastive objective (symmetric InfoNCE with cosine similarity) regularized by KoLeo loss (λ=0.21), with data augmentation via random dropping of 23.3% of input tokens. The architecture was selected through a systematic 3×3 ablation over tokenizers (TST, mTAN, Tuple) and backbones (Self-Attention Transformer, Rotary Transformer, Mamba-2), where the simple TST+Mamba-2 combination consistently outperformed more sophisticated alternatives across 47 downstream tasks. The model was trained for 6 epochs on 15.14M participant-weeks (~2.5B hours) from 162K Apple Heart and Movement Study participants using AdamW with linear warmup and exponential decay, taking 16 hours on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

WBM achieves strong performance across 57 health detection tasks. On time-varying tasks, WBM+PPG yields the best results: pregnancy AUROC 0.921, infection AUROC 0.763, injury AUROC 0.688, and sleep duration R² 0.601. WBM alone dramatically outperforms the PPG model on sleep tasks (e.g., sleep duration R² 0.590 vs. 0.110) and outperforms the simple statistical baseline in 39 of 47 static health outcomes (median AUROC gain of 0.017). For age prediction, WBM+PPG achieves MAE 2.46, substantially better than WBM alone (3.67), PPG alone (2.89), or the baseline (7.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the PPG foundation model from Abbaspourazad et al. (2024a), a large-scale model trained on raw photoplethysmogram sensor data. WBM outperforms PPG on 18 of 47 static health/medication outcomes and dominates on sleep tasks (e.g., sleep duration R² 0.590 vs. 0.110; deep sleep R² 0.266 vs. 0.327 where PPG is slightly ahead). PPG outperforms WBM on tasks where physiology is sufficient, most notably time-varying diabetes (AUROC 0.829 vs. 0.765) and 29 of 47 static outcomes. The combination WBM+PPG achieves the best performance in 42 of 47 static outcomes and all time-varying tasks except diabetes, with notable gains over PPG alone for Afib (+0.034 AUROC), beta-blockers (+0.055), and calcium-channel blockers (+0.033). Against the simple statistical baseline (mean/SD of 27 variables + demographics), WBM wins 39 of 47 outcomes with a median AUROC improvement of 0.017, and significantly outperforms it on all 8 time-varying tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the wearable foundation model line of work (Abbaspourazad et al., 2024a; Narayanswamy et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024) from low-level biosignals (PPG, ECG, accelerometer) to higher-level behavioral metrics (activity, gait, cardiovascular fitness) derived from those sensors. It adapts the TST dense tokenization (Zerveas et al., 2021) and the subject-level contrastive objective with KoLeo regularization from Abbaspourazad et al. (2024a), but departs from the Transformer-centric default by finding that a bi-directional Mamba-2 backbone (following Wang et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2024) outperforms both standard and rotary-position Transformers. The only prior SSL work on behavioral wearable data (Merrill & Althoff, 2023) used just three variables on 5.2K individuals, making this the first large-scale treatment of the modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that behavioral and sensor data capture complementary health information, with the combined WBM+PPG model outperforming either alone on 42/47 static tasks and all time-varying tasks except diabetes. This has direct practical implications for wearable health monitoring: deploying both a behavioral and a sensor foundation model yields meaningfully better detection of conditions like pregnancy (AUROC >0.92), Afib, and beta-blocker use. Key limitations include Apple-device specificity, reliance on self-reported survey labels, and the contrastive objective's inability to reconstruct frequently observed variables. Open questions include whether findings generalize to non-Apple wearables, whether JEPA or other SSL objectives would better handle the noise in behavioral data, and whether the model can be extended to forecasting future health states.
