## Problem

Diffusion-based visuomotor policies require iterative denoising from Gaussian noise at every decision step, making real-time inference prohibitively slow for sequential robotic control. Existing acceleration techniques either demand task-specific retraining (distillation, Streaming Diffusion Policy) or suffer significant performance degradation when sampling steps are aggressively reduced (DDIM, DPMSolver), leaving no training-free, plug-and-play solution that preserves both speed and multimodal expressiveness.

## Input / Output

At each timestep t, Falcon consumes the latest T_o observations (state vectors or RGB images) and a latent buffer of historically generated partial denoised actions at various noise levels. It outputs a clean action sequence of shape T_p × D_a, but critically, the denoising process begins not from a fresh Gaussian sample but from a partially denoised action retrieved from the buffer at noise level k_s < K, requiring only K − k_s additional denoising steps instead of the full K. The latent buffer is managed as a priority queue with a fixed capacity |B|, evicting older and higher-noise entries first.

## Method

Falcon exploits the sequential dependency between consecutive actions in visuomotor tasks by reusing partially denoised actions from prior timesteps as the starting point for the current denoising process. It first designates the unexecuted portion of the previous action sequence as a reference action, then applies Tweedie's formula (one-step posterior estimation) in parallel to all entries in the latent buffer conditioned on the current observation, measuring each candidate's Euclidean distance to the reference. A threshold ε filters candidates, and a temperature-scaled softmax over noise levels selects the starting point, with an exploration rate δ occasionally sampling from N(0,I) to prevent error accumulation. The selected partial denoised action then undergoes only the remaining K − k_s denoising steps. The entire procedure is training-free and composes with existing ODE solvers (DDIM, DPMSolver) by simply replacing the sampling kernel in the denoising loop, adding only ~12 MB of memory overhead.

## Results

On the RoboMimic benchmark (5 tasks × 2 demo types), Falcon achieves a 2–7× speedup in NFE over DDPM (100 → ~13–47 NFE) while matching or slightly exceeding the original success rates (e.g., Lift ph: 1.00 → 1.00; Square ph: 0.91 → 0.95). When stacked on top of DDIM or DPMSolver (16-step baselines), Falcon further reduces NFE to ~1–10, yielding an additional 1.5–2.5× speedup. In real-world robotic tasks, Falcon+DDPM achieves 3.07× speedup (Dexterous Grasping, 100% success) and 2.86× speedup (Square Stick Insertion, 90% success). On the RDT-1B VLA foundation model, Falcon delivers 31× and 34× speedups over 100-step DDPM with no success-rate loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (100-step), Falcon matches success rates across all 10 RoboMimic task variants while reducing NFE by 2–7×, with the largest gains on smooth-motion tasks (Lift: 7.78×) and smaller but consistent gains on complex tasks (Transport: ~2.1×). Compared to SDP (fixed 2-step), Falcon achieves higher speedups (up to 7.78× vs. 2×) on simple tasks and comparable speedups on complex tasks, while also using less CPU memory in real-world deployment (3730 MB vs. 3808 MB). Stacked on DDIM (16-step), Falcon provides an additional 1.5–2.5× speedup (NFE drops from 16 to ~1–10) with no success-rate degradation. Stacked on DPMSolver (16-step), Falcon similarly reduces NFE to ~1–15; critically, in the reduced-step regime (Table 3), DPMSolver* at the same NFE as DPMSolver+Falcon suffers up to 35.6% success-rate drops (Square_mh), whereas Falcon maintains the original performance. Against 3D Diffusion Policy on MetaWorld (10-step DDIM), Falcon achieves 2–5× additional speedup (NFE 10 → 2–5) with negligible success-rate changes across 39 tasks. In the multimodality comparison on PushT, Falcon preserves diverse trajectory modes while Consistency Policy exhibits mode collapse toward a single direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

Falcon builds directly on the diffusion policy framework (Chi et al., 2023) and the DDPM denoising loop (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from existing acceleration strategies in a key way: rather than reformulating sampling as an ODE (DDIM, DPMSolver), distilling to one-step generation (Consistency Policy, OneDP), or using handcrafted noise schedules with large buffers (SDP), Falcon exploits the sequential dependency between consecutive actions in visuomotor tasks to reuse partially denoised actions from a latent buffer. The one-step estimation via Tweedie's formula (Efron, 2011) provides a principled, training-free criterion for selecting which historical partial denoised action to continue from, and the temperature-scaled softmax over noise levels adds an adaptive selection mechanism. This positions Falcon as an orthogonal, composable layer on top of existing solvers rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Falcon addresses a practical bottleneck—real-time inference for diffusion-based robot policies—without requiring retraining or sacrificing multimodal expressiveness, making it immediately deployable on existing trained checkpoints (demonstrated on RDT-1B with 31–34× speedup). The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is the need for task-specific hyperparameter tuning (ε, δ, k_min), and future work on adaptive parameter selection is needed for broader generalizability. An open question is how well the sequential-dependency assumption holds in highly non-stationary or reactive environments where consecutive actions may diverge sharply. The preservation of multimodality (Fig. 3, Fig. 12) versus mode collapse in distillation-based methods is a meaningful differentiator for tasks requiring diverse behavioral strategies.
