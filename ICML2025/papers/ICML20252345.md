## Problem

Existing end-to-end query-based 3D multi-object tracking methods remain in early stages and fail to robustly track objects in complex driving scenarios involving occlusions and small target sizes. The paper identifies that the vanilla query-based framework lacks systematic improvements across its three constituent stages—query initialization, query propagation, and query matching—leading to lost tracks and suboptimal convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surrounding camera images per frame (1600×900 cropped to 1600×640 at full resolution, or further downscaled to 800×320 at small resolution) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processed over 3 consecutive frames with 500 initial queries. During training, projected LiDAR points supervise the auxiliary depth head. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with consistent object IDs across frames, producing continuous tracking trajectories without any heuristic post-processing or data-association module.

## Method

S2-Track enhances the query-based tracking pipeline with three targeted modules. (1) 2D-Prompted Query Initialization (PQI) adds auxiliary 2D detection (YOLOX) and depth prediction heads on shared FPN features, back-projecting predicted 2D centers and depths into 3D locations to seed initial queries, while retaining random queries for missed objects. (2) Uncertainty-aware Probabilistic Decoder (UPD) replaces deterministic cross-attention with probabilistic attention: an MLP over query-key pairs outputs mean and variance of a Gaussian, and attention scores are sampled via the reparameterization trick, trained with a negative log-likelihood loss. (3) Hierarchical Query Denoising (HQD) perturbs ground-truth boxes, classifies noised queries into positive (IoU > 0.70), negative (IoU < 0.30), or ignore based on 3D IoU thresholds, and selectively applies box and classification losses to enhance training robustness. The full model uses a DETR3D decoder with V2-99 or ViT-L backbone, and the total loss is a weighted sum of tracking, PQI, UPD, and HQD terms (all weights set to 1.0).

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, S2-Track-F with ViT-L backbone achieves 66.3% AMOTA and 0.815 AMOTP, surpassing the previous best end-to-end tracker Sparse4D-v3 (57.4% AMOTA) by 8.9% AMOTA. On the nuScenes val set, the same configuration reaches 65.2% AMOTA with 72.2% RECALL and only 134 IDS (identity switches). With the lighter V2-99 backbone, S2-Track-F still achieves 60.8% AMOTA on the test set, outperforming all prior query-based trackers by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2-Track outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every tracking metric on both the nuScenes val and test sets. Against the previous best end-to-end tracker Sparse4D-v3 (57.4% AMOTA on test), S2-Track-F (ViT-L) gains 8.9% AMOTA while also improving AMOTP from 0.970 to 0.815 and reducing IDS from 669 to 844. Compared to PF-Track-F (43.4% AMOTA on test), the improvement is 22.9% AMOTA, with particularly large gains in challenging sub-scenarios: +6.3% AMOTA under low visibility (0–40%), +5.5% for small objects (<2 m), and +4.1% for distant objects (>30 m). Among query-based trackers, S2-Track surpasses DQTrack (52.3% AMOTA) by 14.0% and MUTR3D (27.0%) by 39.3% on the test set. The V2-99 variant (60.8% AMOTA) already exceeds Sparse4D-v3 by 3.4% AMOTA, confirming that the gains are not solely attributable to the larger ViT-L backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2-Track is positioned as a systematic, modular refinement of the end-to-end query-based 3D MOT framework established by PF-Track and Sparse4D, rather than a new architectural paradigm. It decomposes the existing pipeline into three constituent stages—query initialization, query propagation, and query matching—and introduces a targeted improvement to each: 2D-Prompted Query Initialization (borrowing from YOLOX-style 2D detection and LiDAR-supervised depth estimation), an Uncertainty-aware Probabilistic Decoder (adapting Gaussian-distribution attention from Bayesian deep learning to the tracking decoder), and Hierarchical Query Denoising (extending DN-DETR's denoising with IoU-based challenging-level stratification). The DETR3D decoder and the overall query-propagation structure are inherited directly from prior work, making the contribution one of principled component-level upgrades within an established framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.9% AMOTA gap over the previous best end-to-end tracker on nuScenes test is substantial for this field and the modular design means each component (PQI, UPD, HQD) can be adopted independently by other query-based trackers. However, the method remains camera-only and introduces modest additional latency (7.5 vs. 9.2 FPS for the small setting), and the authors explicitly leave multi-sensor (LiDAR-camera) fusion and further efficiency gains as future work. A key open question is whether the probabilistic attention mechanism generalizes beyond the nuScenes-scale dataset and whether the hierarchical denoising thresholds transfer to other tracking benchmarks without re-tuning.
