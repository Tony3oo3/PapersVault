## Problem

Generative models used as target proposers in Target-Assisted Planning (TAP) agents inevitably hallucinate infeasible targets—states that can never be reached—causing delusional planning behaviors that compromise performance and safety. Existing TAP methods either blindly trust all generated targets or rely on feasibility estimators that themselves produce delusional estimates, and no general, minimally-intrusive mechanism exists to robustly identify and reject infeasible targets across diverse planning architectures.

## Input / Output

The evaluator takes a source state s, a generated target embedding g⊙ (paired with an indicator function h that checks set membership), and the agent's policy π as input, and produces a histogram over supports [1, 2, …, T] representing the distribution of D_π(s, g⊙), the number of steps to reach the target. From this single histogram, τ-feasibility p(D_π(s,g⊙) ≤ τ) is extracted for any τ < T by summing bins, and the cumulative discount distribution γ_π(s, g⊙, τ) is obtained by transplanting the histogram onto a rescaled support [γ¹, γ², …, γ^τ, γ^τ, …]. In experiments the evaluator is a 3-layer ReLU MLP with T=16 output bins, and it operates as a passive add-on that learns purely by observing the agent's environment interactions and the generator's outputs without modifying either.

## Method

The evaluator learns the distribution of distance-to-target D_π(s, g⊙) via an off-policy compatible Bellman update on a parallel MDP where all transitions yield reward +1 and target-satisfying states become terminal with value 0, so infeasible targets are gradually pushed toward ∞. A C51-style distributional architecture outputs a histogram over pre-defined integer supports, enabling simultaneous extraction of τ-feasibility for all τ < T from one learned T-feasibility distribution. To prevent the evaluator itself from developing delusional feasibility estimates, two new hindsight relabeling strategies augment standard EPISODE relabeling: GENERATE performs just-in-time relabeling with targets proposed by the generator during planning (exposing the evaluator to hallucinated G.1 targets), and PERTASK relabels with observations from the same task sampled across the entire replay buffer (exposing the evaluator to previously experienced but currently infeasible G.2 targets). The default training mixture (E+P+G) combines 50% EPISODE, 25% PERTASK, and 25% GENERATE to balance sample efficiency for close-proximity feasible targets against coverage of infeasible and long-distance targets.

## Results

Across 8 experiment sets on SSM and RDS environments (20 seeds each), the proposed target evaluator (3-layer MLP, T=16 bins, (E+P+G) relabeling) consistently reduces feasibility estimation errors (E.0, E.1, E.2) and delusional plan frequencies compared to unassisted baselines. The generator hallucinates G.1 targets at ~3% and G.2 targets at ~5% of candidate selections. For Dyna on SSM, Dyna+ (with rejection at 5% feasibility threshold) achieves faster convergence to optimal Q-values and higher training performance, with a stable rejection rate as both generator and evaluator learn. OOD generalization (aggregated over 4×20 tasks at difficulties 0.25–0.55) improves for all assisted variants, with the gains most pronounced in G.2-dominated SSM for Skipper+ and in G.1-dominated RDS for LEAP+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baseline TAP agents against their evaluator-assisted counterparts. Skipper+ (with the proposed evaluator) outperforms Skipper on all feasibility error metrics (E.0, E.1, E.2), reduces the frequency of delusional plans involving both G.1 and G.2 targets, and achieves higher aggregated OOD performance on SSM (Fig. 4). LEAP+ outperforms LEAP on RDS by selecting significantly fewer G.1 subgoals in planned sequences and achieving better OOD evaluation performance (Fig. 5); on SSM, LEAP+ similarly reduces delusional plan ratios and improves OOD performance (Fig. 12). Dyna+ outperforms baseline Dyna on SSM by rejecting hallucinated 1-step transitions, resulting in faster convergence to optimal Q-values (lower L1 distance) and higher task performance on the 50 training tasks (Fig. 6). In all cases, the evaluator is a minimally-intrusive add-on that does not modify the agent's architecture or generator, and the improvements are attributed to the combination of the C51-style distributional architecture, off-policy learning rule, and the (E+P+G) hindsight relabeling mixture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hindsight Experience Replay (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) by introducing two new relabeling strategies—GENERATE and PERTASK—that specifically target hallucination-induced feasibility delusions, going beyond HER's original focus on sample efficiency for experienced targets. It leverages the C51 distributional architecture (Bellemare et al., 2017) in a novel way: rather than learning return distributions, it learns the distribution of distance-to-target, enabling unified τ-feasibility estimation across arbitrary horizons. The paper departs from prior feasibility-aware planning methods (Nasiriany et al., 2019; Lo et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024) by proposing a minimally-intrusive, off-policy-compatible evaluator that learns purely by observation without modifying the agent or its generator, and from prior hallucination-mitigation work (Jafferjee et al., 2020; Talvitie, 2017) by providing a general, agent-agnostic solution rather than method-specific fixes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative models become central to planning agents (MuZero, Dreamer, SimPLe, etc.), the risk of hallucinated targets causing delusional and potentially unsafe behaviors is a growing safety concern highlighted by Bengio et al. (2024). This paper provides the first systematic taxonomy of infeasible targets (G.0/G.1/G.2) and a practical, plug-and-play evaluator that can be attached to diverse TAP agents, making it a useful building block for safer model-based RL. Key limitations include evaluation primarily on simple MiniGrid-based environments, the DreamerV2 integration showing negligible gains on Atari100k (likely due to state representation instability), and the open question of how to handle action-based targets that lack a natural state-reachability indicator h. The approach's generalization to high-dimensional, partially observable settings with continuous actions remains to be validated at scale.
