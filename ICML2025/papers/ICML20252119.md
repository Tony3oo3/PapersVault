## Problem

Learning-rate schedules for large model training (cosine, wsd) have been chosen almost exclusively through empirical trial-and-error, with no fundamental theoretical explanation for why cooldown produces a sudden loss drop, why wsd matches cosine, or how to optimally tune and transfer learning rates across schedules. This paper asks whether a convergence bound from non-smooth stochastic convex optimization can reproduce and explain these empirical observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training horizon T, a schedule specification (cosine or wsd with a cooldown fraction), uniform gradient norm bounds G_t = G, and a distance-to-optimum D as inputs. It produces the optimal base learning-rate γ* (scaling as 1/√T), a time-resolved suboptimality bound Ω_t, and practical schedule-modification parameters: a decreasing factor ρ for extending training (e.g., ρ = 0.525 for 2× extension) and a multiplicative transfer factor ln(γ*(1)/γ*(c)) for converting the optimal learning rate from a partial-cooldown schedule to full linear-decay. Notably, the bound is derived for SGD on convex problems yet is used to predict behavior of AdamW on non-convex transformer pretraining.

## Method

The paper applies a last-iterate convergence bound for SGD on non-smooth convex problems (Defazio et al., 2023, Thm. 10) to derive explicit suboptimality bounds for the wsd and cosine schedules, showing that wsd's cooldown period eliminates the logarithmic H_{T-1} term present in the constant-schedule bound. The key insight is that the last-iterate bound (not the standard min-suboptimality bound) reproduces the shape of empirical loss curves, including the characteristic sudden drop during cooldown, which also appears in PEP lower bounds and in a 2D non-smooth convex toy problem. The theory is then exploited in two practical directions: (i) for continued training, the optimal schedule is constructed by either increasing the cooldown fraction or decreasing the schedule by a computed factor ρ to keep γ* optimal; (ii) for learning-rate transfer, the multiplicative dependence of γ* on G and D allows computing the optimal learning rate for a new schedule (e.g., linear-decay) from a known one (e.g., 20% cooldown) without additional sweeps. Experiments on 124M and 210M Llama-style models confirm that theoretically adapted schedules improve validation loss by ~0.01, equivalent to 6–8% more training tokens.

## Results

The theoretical suboptimality bound from non-smooth convex optimization closely matches empirical validation loss curves for 124M and 210M Llama-style models trained with AdamW on SlimPajama. The optimal base learning-rate scales as 1/√T and is approximately 2× larger for cosine than for wsd (ratio ≈ 0.92/0.47 ≈ 2), matching empirical observations. For continued training from T₁=50k to T₂=100k/200k, the theory-guided schedule adaptation (decreasing the learning rate by ρ=0.525 or ρ=0.375) yields a ~0.01 validation loss improvement over naive continuation, equivalent to 6–7.25% additional training. Learning-rate transfer from 20% cooldown to linear-decay (c=1) via the factor e^0.7 ≈ 2.01 achieves a final validation loss of 2.9535 vs. 2.9660 for the best 20%-cooldown run on the 124M model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cosine schedule (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017), the wsd schedule matches performance when cosine uses a base learning rate approximately 2× larger; this ratio is confirmed both theoretically (Fig. 3b) and empirically across SlimPajama (124M/210M), OpenWebText2 (60M/93M/166M), and ImageNet (ResNet50 with SGD). Against the wsd schedule as introduced by Hägele et al. (2024), this paper provides the missing theoretical explanation: the cooldown period removes the logarithmic term H_{T−1} from the convergence bound (reducing it from O(√(1+ln T)/√T) to O(1/√T)), and the characteristic sudden loss drop during cooldown is reproduced in both upper and lower (PEP) bounds as well as on a 2D non-smooth convex toy problem. Against the linear-decay schedule (Defazio et al., 2023), which attains the worst-case optimal bound DG/√T, wsd with 20% cooldown carries a constant roughly 2× larger (≈2.0001 vs. 1 for T=10⁵), but the paper shows that the optimal learning rate can be transferred from wsd to linear-decay via a multiplicative factor e^0.7≈2 without additional sweeps, yielding a 0.0125 loss improvement (2.9535 vs. 2.9660) on the 124M model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the last-iterate convergence bound for SGD from Defazio et al. (2023) to the wsd (warmup-stable-decay) schedule, which was proposed purely empirically by Zhai et al. (2022), Hu et al. (2024), and Hägele et al. (2024) without prior theoretical analysis. The key conceptual departure is showing that this bound from non-smooth stochastic convex optimization closely matches empirical loss curves from non-convex deep learning trained with AdamW, and that the practical benefit of cooldown is reflected in the absence of logarithmic terms in the bound. The paper also extends the analysis to mirror descent (Appendix F) and uses the PEP framework to verify that the cooldown drop appears in worst-case lower bounds as well.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why the wsd schedule and its cooldown phase work, and demonstrates that the theory can be turned into practical tools: extending schedules for continued training and transferring optimal learning rates across schedule types (e.g., from 20% cooldown to full linear-decay) with near-perfect accuracy. The main open question is why a convex, SGD-based bound matches non-convex, AdamW-trained deep learning so well — the authors acknowledge this gap and suggest it may relate to the near-convexity of overparameterized loss landscapes. Practical impact is currently limited to small models (124M–210M) with modest loss improvements (~0.01), and the theory does not yet account for optimizer-specific effects.
