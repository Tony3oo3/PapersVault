## Problem

Prior work on knowledge conflicts in language models assumed attention heads exclusively promote either parametric memory or contextual knowledge, and focused primarily on a single conflict type (substitution-based) and a single direction (enhancing contextual reliance). This paper reveals that highly influential attention heads simultaneously contribute to both memory and context—a phenomenon termed CP superposition—and that naively combining individually effective interventions can counteract each other, leaving a gap for a unified, training-free method that reliably steers models toward either knowledge source across diverse conflict types.

## Input / Output

JUICE takes a pre-trained transformer LM, a text prompt containing potentially conflicting context (clean, substitution-based, or coherent paragraph-level), and a minimal head identification set of only 4 samples spanning multiple conflict types. It produces steered token generation by modifying attention head activations at inference time, requiring no fine-tuning or weight updates. The unusual operational detail is the dual-run inference: the model processes the input twice, with the first run's identified head outputs saved and then injected as scaled residuals into the second run's corresponding layers.

## Method

JUICE operates in two stages. Stage 1 (head identification) scores each attention head by the expected change in target probability under individual scaling, computed across a 4-sample set covering multiple conflict types; only heads with non-negative scores across all conflict types are retained, and the top K (5–10) are selected. Stage 2 (dual-run inference) runs the model twice: the first run saves the raw outputs of identified heads, and the second run adds scaled versions of those saved outputs to the corresponding residual stream activations, using separate positive and negative scaling factors tuned on validation. The key insight is that CP superposition means a head's role (memory vs. context) depends on the residual stream it receives, so single-pass interventions like PH3's knockout can produce counteracting effects when applied jointly; the dual-run design uses the unmodified first-run activations as more reliable steering directions, avoiding this instability. A theoretical analysis with a two-layer transformer formalizes how gradient descent on parallel factual-recall and induction tasks naturally produces superposed attention heads, and proves that JUICE's dual-run intervention succeeds where single-pass methods fail when the inductive component dominates.

## Results

On the parametric memory task (Table 3), JUICE achieves average accuracy of 79.1/75.3/73.4% across clean/substitution/coherent conflicts on Gemma, 82.2/82.5/83.0% on Llama2, and 84.5/84.2/84.7% on Llama3, substantially outperforming all baselines especially on the hardest coherent-conflict setting (Type 3) where the original model scores near 0%. On the contextual reliance task (Table 4), JUICE achieves 66.2%, 66.6%, and 66.8% average accuracy on Gemma, Llama2, and Llama3 respectively, surpassing the best baseline (PH3l) by 6–15 points. Across 11 datasets and 6 model architectures, JUICE sets new state-of-the-art with only 4 samples for head identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PH3 (Jin et al., 2024b), JUICE wins decisively on all three models and all three conflict types. The gap is most dramatic on coherent conflicts: on Gemma, JUICE reaches 73.4% vs. PH3$_l$'s 42.4% and PH3$_s$'s 0.1%; on Llama3, JUICE achieves 84.7% vs. PH3$_l$'s 39.2%. PH3$_s$ (matched to JUICE's 4-sample identification budget) collapses to near-zero on coherent conflicts, while JUICE maintains >73% across all models. Against CAD (Shi et al., 2024b) in the contextual reliance setting, JUICE outperforms by 15.1 points on Gemma (66.2% vs. 51.1%), 25.2 points on Llama2 (66.6% vs. 41.4%), and 15.6 points on Llama3 (66.8% vs. 51.2%). The ablated single-run variant JUNE also consistently trails JUICE by ~10–20 points, confirming the dual-run design is critical for mitigating counteracting effects of multiple head interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and challenges the mechanistic framework of Jin et al. (2024b, PH3), which posited that attention heads are exclusively 'memory heads' or 'context heads' and that knocking out the wrong type resolves knowledge conflicts. The key conceptual departure is the discovery of CP superposition: the same attention head can promote parametric knowledge under one conflict type and contextual knowledge under another, invalidating the exclusive-role assumption. The broader knowledge-conflict literature (Yu et al. 2023; Xie et al. 2024; Longpre et al. 2021) is treated as motivating context, but this paper unifies the previously one-sided perspectives (parametric-only or context-only) into a single bidirectional steering framework and extends evaluation from a single substitution conflict type to coherent paragraph-level conflicts across six factual domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CP superposition finding reframes how practitioners should think about attention-head interventions: the 'locate-and-edit' paradigm that assumes stable, exclusive head roles is fundamentally limited, and any future intervention method must account for input-dependent head functionality. JUICE offers a practical, training-free, and hyperparameter-robust solution that works across six architectures and eleven datasets, making it immediately applicable to RAG and agent systems where users need to toggle between parametric and contextual reliance. Open questions include whether the superposition phenomenon persists in much larger models (70B+), whether it extends to the other conflict types identified by Xu et al. (2024) such as inter-context and intra-memory conflicts, and whether the dual-run design can be generalized to other mechanistic intervention settings beyond knowledge conflicts.
