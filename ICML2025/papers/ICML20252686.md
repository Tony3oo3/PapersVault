## Problem

Decoder-only LMMs incur heavy computational cost from long vision token sequences, and existing token reduction methods (pruning, merging) risk irreversible information loss on dense or structured images. This paper identifies an underexplored dimension—computation-level redundancy on vision tokens—showing that vision tokens from pretrained encoders do not require all self-attention and FFN operations in the LLM decoder, and that this redundancy can be eliminated without discarding any visual information.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-resolution images encoded via the AnyRes multi-grid strategy (up to 5 grids of 336×336, each yielding 24×24 CLIP-ViT-L features, totaling up to 2880 vision tokens) concatenated with text prompts, and produces a standard text response. All original vision tokens are retained throughout the forward pass—no tokens are pruned or merged—so the output preserves full fine-grained visual detail. Efficiency gains are measured on the prefilling stage with a fixed configuration of 2880 vision tokens and 50 text tokens on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

ProxyV introduces a small set of proxy vision tokens (the full 576-token grid downsampled by factor r=4 to 36 tokens) that substitute for the full vision tokens in the compute-intensive self-attention and FFN operations of the LLM decoder. In each decoder layer, proxy and text tokens act as queries while keys/values include proxy tokens, full vision tokens, and text tokens; only proxy and text tokens pass through FFNs. After attention, each proxy token guides its spatially corresponding r×r block of full vision tokens through a lightweight 2-layer guided-update MLP (hidden dim = 1/4 of LLM hidden dim, ~14.68M params per layer for Vicuna-7B). A non-spatial variant replaces the spatial downsampling with learnable query embeddings that attend over full vision tokens, enabling seamless combination with token reduction methods like VisionZip for up to 62% FLOPs reduction. Training follows a 2-stage pipeline: pretraining the projector and vision-specific modules on 1.2M ShareGPT4V captions, then finetuning the full model on 779K LLaVA-Next instruction samples.

## Results

On fine-grained OCR benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA, TextVQA, OCRBench), ProxyV-L12 on Vicuna-7B achieves a Score_fine of 55.16 (101.0% relative to the 54.64 baseline) while reducing prefilling FLOPs by 46% and time by 41%. ProxyV-L16 further improves to 55.94 (102.4%) with 36% FLOPs and 31% time reduction. On the document parsing task (CCpdf), ProxyV-L12 retains a BLEU of 0.7923 (vs. 0.7991 baseline), whereas token reduction methods drop to 0.72–0.73, and on RefCOCO grounding ProxyV-L12 scores 84.41 versus 79.11 (VisionZip) and 80.75 (PyramidDrop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxyV-L12 outperforms both token reduction baselines on every reported metric. Against VisionZip, ProxyV gains +0.58 on Score_fine (55.16 vs. 54.58), +5.30 on RefCOCO grounding (84.41 vs. 79.11), and +0.070 BLEU on document parsing (0.7923 vs. 0.7218), while also achieving greater FLOPs reduction (46% vs. 32%). Against PyramidDrop, the margins are +0.53 on Score_fine (55.16 vs. 54.63), +3.66 on RefCOCO (84.41 vs. 80.75), and +0.066 BLEU on document parsing (0.7923 vs. 0.7261), with comparable FLOPs savings (46% vs. 42%). The key differentiator is that token reduction methods suffer significant information loss on dense/structured visual content (document parsing, grounding), whereas ProxyV retains all vision tokens and thus avoids this degradation. The non-spatial ProxyV variant combined with VisionZip further pushes efficiency to 62% FLOPs and 65% time reduction while maintaining a Score_fine of 54.83, demonstrating that the two approaches are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant token-reduction line of work (VisionZip, PyramidDrop, FastV) by reframing the efficiency problem as one of computation-level rather than token-level redundancy on vision tokens. It builds on the LLaVA-Next decoder-only architecture and draws conceptual inspiration from cross-attention-based LMMs (Flamingo, Idefics) and EE-MLLM's observation that vision tokens can serve only as keys/values in attention, but avoids their additional parameters and training complexity. The proxy-token mechanism with a lightweight guided-update MLP is a new architectural insertion, and the finding that decoupled vision-specific modules improve performance echoes Libra's insight about separated vision modeling, though with far fewer parameters (9.44M per layer vs. Libra's substantial vision-specific blocks).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProxyV offers a practical, drop-in efficiency improvement (36–46% FLOPs, 31–41% prefill time) that preserves all visual tokens, making it particularly valuable for dense-document OCR, grounding, and multi-turn scenarios where token pruning risks irreversible information loss. The non-spatial variant's seamless combination with VisionZip (62% FLOPs reduction) suggests a composable efficiency stack. Open questions include whether the guided-update module can be further enriched with local attention or convolution, whether the layer-specific redundancy patterns differ systematically across LLM families, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image settings where vision token counts are an order of magnitude larger.
