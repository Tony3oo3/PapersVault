## Problem

Existing constrained reinforcement learning (CRL) algorithms frequently exhibit persistent constraint violations during training, undermining their reliability in safety-critical deployments. The paper identifies the underestimation of the cost value function—caused by the minimization objective disrupting the zero-mean noise assumption in function approximation—as a key driver of these violations, making high-cost states appear deceptively safe and enticing the agent into unsafe exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250706/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations and environment transitions (extrinsic costs, rewards) from a CMDP, with states embedded into a lower-dimensional latent space via a Gaussian random projection layer (Johnson-Lindenstrauss) before KNN-based similarity queries against the flashbulb memory. The output is a safe policy parameterized by a neural network that maximizes expected discounted return while keeping cumulative discounted cost below a threshold d, with the intrinsic cost signal computed per-step as a normalized pseudo-count over k-nearest unsafe states in memory.

## Method

MICE introduces a flashbulb memory module that stores previously explored unsafe states (those with positive extrinsic costs) from the prior policy iteration, mirroring human risk-aversion from vivid recall of dangerous experiences. The intrinsic cost is computed as the square root of the sum of kernel similarities between the current state's embedding and its k-nearest neighbors in memory, yielding a pseudo-count that quantifies proximity to known high-cost regions. This intrinsic cost is combined with the extrinsic cost in an extrinsic-intrinsic cost value function (Q_C^EI), augmented by an adaptive balancing factor β that is updated via a bias-correction rule to prevent excessive over- or under-estimation. The optimization objective replaces the standard cost constraint with the extrinsic-intrinsic constraint and adds a KL-divergence trust region to keep the updated policy aligned with the policy that generated the memory samples, solved via MICE-CPO (trust-region with first/second-order approximations) or MICE-PIDLag (PID-controlled Lagrangian multiplier). Theoretically, the paper proves convergence of Q_C^EI to the optimal value function and establishes a worst-case constraint violation bound tighter than CPO's, with the intrinsic cost term I providing an additional safety margin.

## Results

Across Safety Gym (4 navigation tasks) and Safety MuJoCo (4 physics tasks), MICE significantly reduces constraint violations (threshold = 25) while maintaining policy performance comparable to or better than baselines. In Safety MuJoCo, MICE achieves zero constraint violations throughout training with convergence speed comparable to or faster than baselines. In Safety Gym Goal1 tasks with multiple hazards, MICE actually enhances policy performance while maintaining constraint satisfaction, as the intrinsic cost provides predictive signals to avoid hazards. The estimation bias in MICE approaches zero over training, confirming the adaptive balancing factor β effectively corrects both underestimation and overestimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MICE outperforms PID Lagrangian on constraint satisfaction across all environments; notably in HalfCheetahVelocity, PIDLag exceeds the constraint threshold of 25, making its higher return meaningless as a policy comparison. Against CUP, MICE achieves lower constraint violations while matching or exceeding reward performance. Compared to Saute and Simmer (state augmentation methods designed for zero constraint violations), MICE matches their constraint satisfaction levels but surpasses them in policy performance, demonstrating that the memory-driven intrinsic cost is not merely conservative. Against CPO, MICE reduces constraint violations; in SafetyCarGoal1-v0, CPO exceeds the threshold so its higher return does not indicate a better policy. The ablation study confirms that replacing the memory-driven intrinsic cost with fixed constants (3, 5, 15) degrades policy performance, with constant 5 actually producing more constraint violations than constant 3, validating that the pseudo-count mechanism is essential rather than a simple penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

MICE extends the trust-region constrained policy optimization lineage (CPO, PID Lagrangian) by identifying cost value underestimation as a root cause of constraint violations and introducing a memory-driven intrinsic cost to correct it. The intrinsic cost mechanism borrows the pseudo-count formulation from NGU (Badia et al., 2020) but repurposes it from encouraging exploration of novel states to discouraging revisits of previously dangerous states, drawing an analogy to human flashbulb memory. The trust region constraint is repurposed not merely for policy stability but to ensure the stored unsafe states remain relevant to the updated policy, creating a tighter coupling between memory and optimization than in prior memory-augmented RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Persistent constraint violations during training remain a major barrier to deploying CRL in safety-critical domains like robotics and autonomous driving, and MICE offers a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to reduce them without sacrificing policy performance. The ablation showing that simply adding a larger fixed constant to the cost (e.g., 5 vs. 3) can actually increase violations is a useful caution against naive conservatism. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional continuous control beyond Safety MuJoCo, handling noisy or delayed cost signals in real-world settings, and whether the memory-based approach can be replaced by learned representations without losing the convergence guarantees.
