## Problem

Existing visual reprogramming (VR) methods for CLIP train a single visual prompt (VP) to align input images with all attribute descriptions of every downstream class simultaneously. This single VP has limited learning capacity, causing it to (1) fail to capture diverse descriptive aspects (e.g., shape, color, texture) and (2) develop an unreasonable bias toward less informative attributes that happen to have higher cosine similarity with images but low inter-class discriminability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250235/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes downstream RGB images (arbitrary spatial dimensions, padded to match CLIP's input size) and a set of text attribute descriptions per class (e.g., 'artichoke is round and spiky with green leaves'), which are pre-embedded via CLIP's text encoder. Descriptions are partitioned into v groups before training, and each group is assigned its own trainable noise pattern. The output is a per-class probability vector computed by summing PRM-weighted CLIP similarities across all description partitions, followed by a softmax over the label space. The PRM entries are continuous values in [0,1] estimated from training-set co-occurrence counts, providing an interpretable measure of each description's contribution to each class prediction.

## Method

DVP replaces the single VP with v decoupled VPs, each trained exclusively on a partition of the description set. Two partitioning strategies are offered: DVP-cse groups descriptions by explicit semantic causes (e.g., 'shape', 'color', 'texture') identified via an LLM query, while DVP-cls groups them by K-means clustering of their text embeddings. A Probabilistic Reweighting Matrix (PRM) with continuous [0,1] entries replaces the fixed max/avg aggregation, estimated via maximum likelihood from k-nearest-neighbor co-occurrence counts on the training set. Training alternates between updating the PRM (closed-form MLE) and updating each VP independently via cross-entropy loss over its assigned description partition. Theoretically, the paper proves via two lemmas and a corollary that DVP's optimally achievable empirical risk is no greater than standard VR's, since the decoupled VP set spans a superset of the single-VP function space and the PRM subsumes all fixed aggregation schemes.

## Results

On 16-shot downstream classification with ViT-B/16-based CLIP, DVP-cse achieves 80.1% average accuracy across 11 datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline AttrVR (78.5%) by 1.6% and the AR baseline (76.5%) by 3.6%. DVP-cls achieves 79.7% average accuracy, a 1.2% improvement over AttrVR. Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: DVP-cse improves over AttrVR by 4.2% on Stanford Cars, 3.7% on FGVCAircraft, and 2.5% on Flowers-102, while DVP-cls gains 3.0% on UCF-101 and 2.5% on Cars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVP-cse consistently outperforms all three baselines across 10 of 11 datasets, with the largest margins on fine-grained tasks where multiple visual aspects matter: +4.2% over AttrVR on Cars, +3.7% on Aircraft, and +2.5% on Flowers. DVP-cls, which groups descriptions via unsupervised K-means clustering rather than explicit LLM-generated causes, also beats AttrVR on 10/11 datasets but with a smaller average margin (1.2% vs. 1.6%), excelling particularly on UCF-101 (+3.0%) where action classes form natural subcategories. Both DVP variants fail to improve over AttrVR on Food-101, which the authors attribute to food classification relying primarily on smell and taste rather than visual cues. Against the simpler VP and AR baselines, DVP-cse gains 5.7% and 3.6% average accuracy respectively, confirming that the decoupling-and-reweighting framework provides substantial benefits beyond merely adding more parameters. The paper further validates that the gains are not due to increased parameter count: a single-VP variant of DVP (DVPlite) still improves over AttrVR by 1.3% average with identical parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual reprogramming (VR) line for CLIP, which includes VP (Bahng et al., 2022), AR (Tsai et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2023), BlackVIP (Oh et al., 2023), and most recently AttrVR (Cai et al., 2025), all of which train a single visual prompt to align images with all class descriptions simultaneously. The key departure is a decoupling-and-reweighting framework: instead of one VP handling all descriptions, multiple VPs are each optimized for a partition of descriptions (grouped by LLM-identified semantic causes or by K-means clustering), and their outputs are integrated via a learned Probabilistic Reweighting Matrix (PRM) estimated by MLE. The paper also positions itself within the broader model reprogramming paradigm (Vinod et al., 2020; Chen, 2024) and distinguishes VR from prompt learning (Jia et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022a,b) by emphasizing input-space modification without architectural coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PRM provides a probabilistic interpretability layer that reveals how individual visual prompts and description causes contribute to classification decisions, offering a new lens for understanding model reprogramming beyond black-box accuracy. The theoretical result (Corollary 4.6) formally establishes that DVP achieves a lower or equal empirical risk than standard VR, grounding the design choice in learning theory rather than empirical observation alone. Limitations include no improvement on Food (where visual cues are weak), the reliance on LLM-generated causes for DVP-cse, and the fact that the PRM is estimated via MLE rather than end-to-end optimization, which may limit its expressiveness. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle transfers to other modalities (audio, graph) or to multi-task settings, and whether the number of VPs and their partitioning can be learned jointly rather than set a priori.
