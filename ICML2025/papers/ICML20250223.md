## Problem

As sparse autoencoder (SAE) dictionary size increases, the sparsity objective incentivizes general features to be split into specialized sub-features or partially absorbed by them, leaving high-level concepts missing or riddled with holes. This means larger SAEs, despite achieving lower reconstruction loss, actually perform worse on downstream interpretability tasks like probing and concept erasure. The paper asks whether a single SAE can be trained to retain both abstract and fine-grained features without this degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250223/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense residual stream activations (e.g., from layer 12 of Gemma-2-2B with hidden dimension 2304, or from a 4-layer TinyStories model with hidden size 768) and produces a single sparse latent vector of dimension m (up to 65,536) along with multiple nested reconstructions, each computed using only a prefix of the latents. Sparsity is enforced via BatchTopK, which selects the B×K largest activations across an entire batch rather than per-example, allowing per-token active latent counts to vary while maintaining a fixed average L0. At inference, BatchTopK is replaced by a global threshold calibrated on training data.

## Method

Matryoshka SAEs train a single encoder-decoder pair with a maximum dictionary size m, but impose reconstruction losses at multiple nested prefix sizes M = {m1 < m2 < ... < mn = m} simultaneously. The total loss sums the L2 reconstruction error at each prefix scale plus a standard auxiliary sparsity loss, forcing the first m1 latents to reconstruct the input on their own, the first m2 to do so, and so on. This creates a natural hierarchy: early latents must capture broadly applicable general features to satisfy the smallest-prefix reconstruction, while later latents only participate in larger-prefix reconstructions and can specialize in specific sub-concepts without absorbing the general ones. Two variants are explored—random prefix (prefix sizes sampled per batch from a truncated Pareto distribution) and fixed prefix (a fixed set of sizes as a hyperparameter)—with fixed prefix performing slightly better on Gemma-2-2B. The approach is implemented with minimal architectural changes to a standard BatchTopK SAE, requiring only a modest (~50%) increase in training time for five nested scales.

## Results

At L0=40 on Gemma-2-2B (layer 12, 65536 latents), Matryoshka SAEs reduce feature absorption from 0.49 (BatchTopK) to 0.05 and feature splitting from 3 latents per first-letter feature to 1. Reconstruction variance explained is slightly lower (70% vs 72% for BatchTopK at L0=40), but downstream cross-entropy loss is comparable at larger L0s. On targeted probe perturbation (TPP) and spurious correlation removal (SCR), Matryoshka SAEs significantly outperform all six baseline architectures, and on sparse probing they achieve the best performance at lower sparsity levels while consistently outperforming BatchTopK across all sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Matryoshka SAEs are compared against six SAE architectures from SAE Bench: ReLU, TopK, BatchTopK, Gated, JumpReLU, and P-Annealing. On feature absorption, Matryoshka reduces the rate from 0.49 (BatchTopK) to 0.05 at L0=40, a roughly 10× improvement, and similarly outperforms all other baselines. On feature splitting, Matryoshka requires only 1 latent per first-letter feature versus 3 for BatchTopK, with comparable or better results against the remaining baselines. On reconstruction (variance explained), Matryoshka is slightly worse than BatchTopK (70% vs 72% at L0=40) and JumpReLU, but the gap narrows at higher L0s where downstream CE loss becomes comparable. On TPP and SCR, Matryoshka significantly outperforms all six baselines, indicating more disentangled and causally isolated concept representations. On sparse probing, Matryoshka achieves the best scores at lower sparsity levels and consistently beats BatchTopK at all sparsity levels. The key tradeoff is a small reconstruction penalty (~2% variance) and ~50% longer training time, but Matryoshka improves or maintains downstream performance as dictionary size scales from 4k to 65k, whereas the baseline architectures generally degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework for neural network interpretability (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023) by adapting the nested-prefix reconstruction principle from Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2024) to the SAE setting. While prior SAE work identified feature absorption, splitting, and composition as scaling pathologies (Chanin et al., 2024; Leask et al., 2025), no prior approach modified the training objective to explicitly enforce multi-scale reconstruction. The method also builds on the BatchTopK activation function (Bussmann et al., 2024) and is evaluated using the SAE Bench framework (Karvonen et al., 2024a), positioning it as a drop-in architectural variant within the existing SAE ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key implication is that feature absorption and splitting—previously considered fundamental consequences of the SAE sparsity objective—can be substantially mitigated by a simple modification to the training loss, opening the door to training arbitrarily large SAEs without losing interpretable high-level features. This reframes SAE scaling from a tradeoff (bigger dictionary → worse features) to a solvable engineering problem. Limitations include a modest reconstruction fidelity cost (~2% variance explained), ~50% increased training time, evaluation on a single LLM (Gemma-2-2B), and reliance on quantitative proxies rather than human interpretability judgments. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other SAE pathologies, whether finer-grained control over latent group sparsity helps, and whether the reconstruction tradeoff persists at larger model scales.
