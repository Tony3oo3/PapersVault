## Problem

Existing vision-language datasets for traffic scenes are predominantly vehicle-centric (driving perspective) and address isolated tasks such as image-level QA or referred multi-object tracking, leaving a significant gap for roadside surveillance video understanding. No prior benchmark unifies video question answering, spatio-temporal object grounding, and referred object captioning within a single evaluation framework, and no standardized language representation captures an object's identity, temporal position, and spatial location simultaneously in video.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a traffic video uniformly sampled into 101 frames at 384×384 resolution, encoded by SigLIP, alongside a natural-language question or grounding query. For the spatio-temporal grounding task, the expected output is a pair of tuples [(c, fn′, x′, y′), (c, fn″, x″, y″)] specifying the object's unique ID, normalized frame timestamp, and normalized bounding-box center at both its first and last visible frames. For referred object captioning, the query references an object via two such tuples at different timestamps, and the model must produce a free-form appearance description evaluated with BLEU, CIDEr, ROUGE, METEOR, and SPICE.

## Method

TraffiX-Qwen couples a SigLIP visual encoder with a Qwen-2 LLM (0.5B or 7B) through a 2-layer MLP projector, with the key architectural contribution being a frame-level multi-resolution token sampling module that assigns high-resolution (27×27) tokens to keyframes and low-resolution (14×14) tokens to the remaining 100 frames. Four sampling strategies are compared: uniform spatial pooling, first-frame keyframe retention, cosine-similarity-based token pruning (retaining 25% of tokens per non-keyframe), and question-adaptive temporal pooling where keyframes are selected from the query's temporal focus. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the projector is trained on open-ended captioning pairs with frozen encoder and LLM (initialized from LLaVA-OneVision), then the full LLM is fine-tuned on the 85k multi-choice QA pairs in instruction-following format while the encoder and projector remain frozen. The dataset itself is generated via a semi-automatic pipeline combining off-the-shelf detectors/trackers, pseudo-3D projection from camera calibration, VLM-based appearance captioning, and a hybrid template-plus-LLM QA generation process with human quality checks.

## Results

On the TUMTraf VideoQA Multi-Choice QA benchmark, the TraffiX-Qwen 7B baseline with MultiRes Temporal-Pooling achieves 81.95% overall top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming the best open-source model (LLaVA-OneVision 7B at 56.36%) by over 25 percentage points. The 0.5B variant reaches 79.83% overall, demonstrating that even a lightweight model benefits greatly from domain-specific fine-tuning. For Spatio-Temporal Grounding, the 7B baseline achieves a spatio-temporal error of 0.2382 (vs. 0.6896 for the best open-source model, VideoLLaMA2 7B-8F), and for Referred Object Captioning it scores 49.16 SPICE (vs. 0.2391 for Qwen2-VL 7B), highlighting the large gap between zero-shot and fine-tuned performance on this domain-specific benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The TraffiX-Qwen baseline dominates all three open-source VLMs across every task. On Multi-Choice QA, the 7B TraffiX-Qwen (81.95% overall) outperforms LLaVA-OneVision 7B (56.36%) by 25.6 points, Qwen2-VL 7B (48.82%) by 33.1 points, and VideoLLaMA2 7B-16F (48.05%) by 33.9 points. The advantage is most pronounced on hard multi-hop questions: for Positioning-Hard, TraffiX-Qwen 7B scores 77.24% versus 22.03% (LLaVA-OneVision) and 24.35% (Qwen2-VL). On Spatio-Temporal Grounding, the 7B baseline achieves a spatio-temporal error of 0.2382, roughly 3× lower than the best open-source model (VideoLLaMA2 7B-8F at 0.6896), while open-source models without fine-tuning produce near-random errors (~0.80–1.00). For Referred Object Captioning, TraffiX-Qwen 7B scores 49.16 SPICE and 37.60 BLEU-4, compared to Qwen2-VL 7B's 0.2391 CIDEr and 10.47 BLEU-4, indicating that zero-shot models largely fail to generate meaningful object descriptions in this domain. Notably, even the 0.5B TraffiX-Qwen (79.83% MQA, 0.2600 ST error, 47.17 SPICE) surpasses all open-source 7B models, underscoring the value of domain-specific instruction tuning over raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of vision-language benchmarks for traffic scenes (DriveLM, NuScenes-QA, DRAMA) by shifting from ego-centric driving to third-party roadside surveillance and unifying three previously separate tasks—video QA, referred object captioning, and spatio-temporal grounding—into a single evaluation framework. The 4-tuple object representation (c, fn, x, y) generalizes DriveLM's camera-based tuple by replacing camera ID with a normalized frame timestamp, enabling cross-frame temporal referencing. The TraffiX-Qwen baseline builds on the LLaVA-OneVision architecture (SigLIP + projector + Qwen-2) but introduces multi-resolution token sampling strategies (spatial pooling, token pruning, temporal pooling) to handle 101-frame video inputs efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark specifically targeting roadside traffic video understanding, filling a notable gap where prior datasets focused almost exclusively on in-vehicle driving perspectives. The finding that fine-tuned models can answer a substantial fraction of questions without any visual input reveals a serious hallucination problem in domain-specific VLMs that the field has underappreciated. The observation that multi-resolution strategies improve QA accuracy but degrade spatio-temporal grounding performance highlights an unresolved tension between fine-grained spatial detail and temporal consistency in video VLMs. Open questions include how to design representations that simultaneously support precise spatial localization and robust temporal tracking, and how to mitigate text-only pattern exploitation in specialized domains.
