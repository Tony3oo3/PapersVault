## Problem

Despite abundant empirical evidence that random-permutation coordinate descent (RPCD) outperforms random coordinate descent (RCD), no prior theoretical work had established a provable performance gap between the two algorithms. Previous analyses (e.g., Gürbüzbalaban et al., 2018) only compared upper bounds to upper bounds, which is insufficient to demonstrate that one algorithm is strictly faster than the other. This paper closes that gap by deriving a rigorous RCD lower bound that exceeds the RPCD upper bound for every instance in a class of quadratics with permutation-invariant Hessians.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a positive definite Hessian A with unit diagonals, parameterized by σ = λ_min(A) ∈ (0,1], and an initial point x_0 ≠ 0, and produces asymptotic contraction rate bounds for the expected squared iterate norm. The convergence measure is the per-epoch (RPCD) or per-iteration (RCD) limit lim (E[||x_K||²]/||x_0||²)^{1/K}, with the central result being that the RPCD upper bound max{(1−1/n)^n, (1−σ/n)^{2n}} is strictly smaller than the RCD lower bound for all σ ∈ (0,1). The function class A_σ consists of block-diagonal matrices diag{σI_k + (1−σ)1_k 1_k^T, I_{n−k}} for 2 ≤ k ≤ n, extended by sign-flip invariance.

## Method

The core technique defines a linear matrix operator M_A(X) = E[T_A^T X T_A] whose spectral radius governs the asymptotic contraction rate of the expected iterate norm. For RPCD on permutation-invariant Hessians A = σI + (1−σ)11^T, the operator is closed in the 2-dimensional subspace span{I, 11^T}, reducing the spectral analysis to a 2×2 matrix M_A whose entries are explicitly computed as polynomials in σ via the Lee–Wright lemma on permutation expectations. The RCD lower bound is established by showing E[x_T^T x_T | x_0] ≥ x_0^T (I − A/n)^{2T} x_0 and extracting the dominant eigenvalue, yielding a per-iteration rate of (1−σ/n)². The key comparison shows that per epoch (n iterations), RCD contracts at rate at least (1−1/n) while RPCD contracts at rate at most max{(1−1/n)^n, (1−σ/n)^{2n}}, with the former being strictly smaller. A stronger RCD lower bound of 1 − 1/n + (1−σ)²/n (Theorem 3.4) then establishes a clear, quantifiable gap between the two algorithms for all σ ∈ (0,1), with the proof relying on polynomial coefficient analysis and case splitting over σ ranges.

## Results

The paper establishes that for all problem instances with Hessian A ∈ A_σ (permutation-invariant structures), the RPCD contraction upper bound max{(1−1/n)^n, (1−σ/n)^(2n)} is strictly smaller than the RCD contraction lower bound (1−1/n+(1−σ)²/n)^n for every σ ∈ (0,1). This proves a provable performance gap: RPCD converges faster than RCD on every individual instance, not just in expectation over a function class. The RCD lower bound (Theorem 3.1) holds for all positive-definite quadratics, while the stronger RCD lower bound (Theorem 3.4) and the matching RPCD upper bound (Theorem 3.3) hold for the permutation-invariant subclass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between RCD (uniform sampling) and RPCD (random permutations). The paper proves that RPCD's upper bound (Theorem 3.3) is strictly below RCD's lower bound (Theorem 3.4) for all σ ∈ (0,1) and all n, establishing a provable gap for every problem instance in A_σ. Compared to prior RPCD analyses, the paper's bound in Theorem 3.3 covers the full range σ ∈ (0,1] and all n (using the (1−1/n)^n term for large σ), whereas Lee & Wright (2019) only cover σ ∈ (0, 0.4] with n ≥ 10, and Wright & Lee (2017) give a weaker rate O(K(1−7σ/5)^K) for a slightly larger function class. Gürbüzbalaban et al. (2018) only compare upper bounds of RCD vs RPCD (not a rigorous gap) and consider a different Hessian form with negative off-diagonals. The paper also provides empirical validation (Figure 3) showing RPCD outperforms RCD on quadratic, quadratic+LSE, and logistic regression objectives, supporting the conjecture that the gap extends to general positive-definite quadratics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the convergence analysis framework for coordinate descent established by Nesterov (2012) for RCD and Lee & Wright (2019) for RPCD on permutation-invariant Hessians, and draws a direct analogy to the well-studied benefit of random reshuffling over i.i.d. sampling in SGD. It departs from Gürbüzbalaban et al. (2018), who compared only upper bounds of RCD and RPCD without establishing a rigorous performance gap, by introducing a novel RCD lower bound (Theorem 3.1) that can be directly contrasted with the RPCD upper bound (Theorem 3.3). The matrix operator framework (M_A) and the spectral-radius-based convergence measure unify the analysis of both algorithms under a common lens, extending the diagonalizability argument from prior work to handle the permutation-averaged case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable, instance-by-instance performance gap between RPCD and RCD, resolving a long-standing open question that had only empirical support. The conjecture that permutation-invariant Hessians constitute the worst case for RPCD among all positive definite quadratics, if proven, would extend the gap to the full class of strongly convex quadratics and provide a clean theoretical justification for the widespread practical use of RPCD. Key open questions include proving Conjecture 4.1 for general quadratics, extending the analysis to non-quadratic (strongly) convex functions, and understanding whether the permutation benefit persists in block coordinate descent or non-convex settings.
