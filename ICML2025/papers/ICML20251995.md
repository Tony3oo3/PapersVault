## Problem

Despite the practical success of Deep Q-Networks, a rigorous theoretical understanding of their universal approximation capability and training convergence—especially in continuous-time settings—has been lacking. This paper addresses that gap by establishing a formal connection between DQNs, stochastic optimal control, and Forward-Backward SDEs, providing the first continuous-time approximation and convergence theorems for DQNs driven by general square-integrable martingales.

## Input / Output

The DQN takes a state vector s_t ∈ S ⊂ ℝⁿ (evolving via a controlled SDE driven by a d-dimensional continuous martingale) and an action a_t ∈ A ⊂ ℝᵐ as input, producing a scalar Q-value estimate Q^θ(t,s,a). The network is constrained to L=N layers with time step Δt=T/N, and approximation guarantees hold on compact sets K_R = [0,T]×{s:‖s‖≤R₁}×A where the state-action trajectory resides with probability at least 1−δ, with R₁ determined by large deviation bounds on the SDE.

## Method

The paper constructs a DQN architecture whose residual block update x^(l+1) = x^(l) + h_θl(x^(l),a)Δt mirrors Euler discretization of an ODE, then proves two main results. Theorem 3.1 (approximation) shows that for any ε>0 and δ∈(0,1), a sufficiently deep/wide ResNet-based DQN uniformly approximates the optimal Q-function Q* on the high-probability compact set K_R, combining the universal approximation theorem for residual networks (Li et al., 2022) with large deviation bounds for the state process. Theorem 3.8 (convergence) proves that a continuous-time Q-learning algorithm with Robbins-Monro learning rates converges almost surely to Q* in the L∞ norm, via stochastic approximation theory (Kushner & Yin, 2003) and a Lyapunov argument requiring representability, identifiability, and a negative-correlation condition on the Bellman error. The optimal value function V* is characterized as the unique viscosity solution to the HJB equation with generator incorporating the martingale's quadratic variation density C(t), while Q* is characterized by the Bellman optimality equation and linked to V* via a BSDE representation.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Theorem 3.1 establishes that DQNs with residual block architecture can uniformly approximate the optimal Q-function Q* to arbitrary accuracy ε on compact sets K_R with probability at least 1−δ, and Theorem 3.8 proves almost-sure convergence of the Q-learning training algorithm to Q* in the L∞ norm. The numerical experiments (Appendix B) on a 1D continuous control task (ds_t = a_t dt + σ dW_t, state in [−1,1], actions {−1,0,1}) confirm that the residual-block DQN trains stably, with the baseline (2 residual blocks, LR=5×10⁻⁴, γ=0.99) achieving consistent negative rewards and decreasing loss over 300 episodes. No quantitative benchmark metrics are reported; results are presented as learning curves (Figures 1–2) and qualitative policy visualizations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not compare its DQN method against other RL algorithms or architectures on standard benchmarks. The numerical experiments in Appendix B are ablation studies varying hyperparameters (learning rate, number of residual blocks, environment noise σ, target network update frequency) rather than comparisons against competing methods. The closest comparison is between the residual-block DQN (2 ResBlocks) and a standard MLP (0 ResBlocks) on the 1D control task, where the paper notes the MLP may show 'slightly slower convergence or lower final reward,' suggesting a modest benefit from the residual structure, though the simple 1D environment does not fully stress-test the architectural advantage. The theoretical framework itself builds on and extends prior work by Li et al. (2022) on ResNet approximation, Kushner & Yin (2003) on stochastic approximation convergence, and Fleming & Soner (2006) on viscosity solutions, but no head-to-head empirical comparison with these prior theoretical frameworks is conducted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal approximation theorem for residual networks (Li et al., 2022; Weinan et al., 2019) to the specific setting of Deep Q-Networks operating in continuous-time MDPs, using FBSDE theory (Ma & Yong, 1999; El Karoui et al., 1997) and viscosity solutions to the HJB equation (Fleming & Soner, 2006) as the connecting mathematical framework. It departs from prior DQN theory (Fan et al., 2020; Kim & Yang, 2020), which is largely discrete-time, by formulating the state dynamics as an SDE driven by a general square-integrable martingale and adapting stochastic approximation convergence results (Kushner & Yin, 2003; Borkar & Meyn, 2000) to this continuous-time setting. The large deviation bounds for the state-action process (Lemma 2.10) and the identification of relevant compact sets via these bounds are the key technical ingredients that allow the standard UAT to apply with high probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a rigorous theoretical foundation for DQNs in continuous-time settings relevant to physical systems and high-frequency data, but the convergence theorem (Theorem 3.8) relies on strong representability, identifiability, and gradient conditions (Assumptions 3.5–3.6) whose verification for deep networks remains an open challenge. The numerical experiments are limited to a trivial 1D control task, so practical validation of the continuous-time framework in higher dimensions is lacking. Open questions include deriving explicit approximation rates under stronger regularity of Q*, relaxing the ergodicity assumption, analyzing sample complexity, and extending to partially observable or multi-agent settings.
