## Problem

In diffusion and flow models, sample log-density correlates negatively with image detail, yet standard samplers do not optimize for or control this quantity. Prior Guidance (latent code scaling) empirically affects detail but lacks a theoretical explanation for when it works, and no existing method allows explicit targeting of a desired log-density value during sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained diffusion or flow model (e.g., EDM2 at 512×512, Stable Diffusion v2.1, or a VP-SDE on CIFAR-10 at 32×32) and takes as input a Gaussian latent code, an optional condition (class label or text prompt), and a target quantile q ∈ [0,1] specifying the desired log-density level. It outputs a generated image whose log-density is controlled to match the target quantile, with the guidance applied only over the time interval [T, t*] where log SNR = 1, since this is where log-density most strongly correlates with image detail.

## Method

The paper introduces three linked contributions. First, Score Alignment (SA) is a condition (Eq. 11) requiring the push-forward of the prior score to align with the data score, which provably guarantees that latent scaling monotonically changes log-density; SA can be verified without the score function via augmented sensitivity equations (Eq. 13) using only Jacobian-vector products. Second, Density Guidance (DGS) modifies the PF-ODE by rescaling the score with a spatially and temporally adaptive factor η_t(x) = 1 + (√(2D)·Φ⁻¹(q)) / ‖σ_t ∇log p_t(x)‖², derived by solving a constrained optimization that minimizes deviation from the original trajectory while enforcing a prescribed log-density evolution rate b_t; this requires no extra network evaluations since the score is already computed at each step. Third, Stochastic Density Guidance extends DGS to SDE sampling by replacing isotropic noise with a score-orthogonal projection P_t = I − (∇log p_t/‖∇log p_t‖)(∇log p_t/‖∇log p_t‖)ᵀ, ensuring log-density evolves deterministically while stochasticity is confined to directions orthogonal to the score, enabling controlled variation in structure or fine details without altering the target detail level.

## Results

The paper's key quantitative findings are: (1) Score Alignment holds for 97% of latent codes on CIFAR-10 and 100% on EDM2 (ImageNet 512), confirming that latent scaling monotonically controls log-density in practice. (2) Negative log-density −log p_t(x_t) correlates at 84% with PNG compression size (a proxy for image detail), substantially outperforming the FLIPD intrinsic-dimensionality measure at 29%. (3) Explicit quantile matching achieves >99% correlation between target and achieved log p_0 even with as few as 32 Euler steps, and the score-free SA verification (Eq. 13) tracks the ground-truth alignment with 98.8% correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLIPD (Kamkari et al., 2024), the paper shows that −log p_t(x_t) is a far superior proxy for image detail: it achieves 84% correlation with PNG compression size on EDM2 (ImageNet 512) versus only 29% for FLIPD, and the correlation peaks at an intermediate noise level (log-SNR ≈ 1) rather than at t ≈ 0. Against Prior Guidance / latent scaling (Song et al., 2021b), Density Guidance (Eq. 23) produces perceptually similar samples but adds the critical ability to target an explicit log-density value (via quantile q) rather than only monotonically increasing or decreasing detail; the paper also proves that Prior Guidance is only guaranteed to work under the Score Alignment condition, which it shows holds for 97–100% of cases in practice but can fail (counterexample in Appendix C.3). The stochastic extension (Eq. 25) further outperforms both by allowing controlled variation in high-level structure or fine details while maintaining the target log-density, something neither baseline supports.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CNF/PF-ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018; Song et al., 2021b) and the generalized density-evolution formulas from Karczewski et al. (2025), extending them from SDE models with linear drift to arbitrary flow models. It provides a theoretical explanation (Score Alignment) for the previously observed but unexplained latent-code-scaling heuristic from Song et al. (2021b), and departs from prior density-control methods (Sehwag et al., 2022; Karras et al., 2024a) by enabling exact log-density targeting at arbitrary quantiles rather than only extreme high- or low-density regions. The stochastic extension leverages the CNF-to-SDE casting from Eijkelboom et al. (2024) and introduces a score-orthogonal projection to decouple density control from stochastic variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Density Guidance offers a cost-free (no extra network evaluations) mechanism for fine-grained detail control in any score-based or flow-based generator, which is practically valuable for applications requiring specific texture levels. The Score Alignment condition provides a tractable diagnostic for whether simple latent scaling will work for a given model, and its near-universal empirical satisfaction (97–100%) explains a widely-used heuristic. Open questions include how well the implicit quantile matching (which relies on a CLT approximation for h(x)) holds for very low-dimensional or highly anisotropic distributions, and whether the method transfers cleanly to non-diffusion flow models (e.g., rectified flows) where the score may be less well-defined at early times.
