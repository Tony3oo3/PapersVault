## Problem

Existing multimodal diffusion models rely on external tokenizers or VAEs to project all modalities into a single unified latent space before generation, creating a hard dependency on high-accuracy encoder-decoder pairs that are difficult to train in data-scarce settings and preventing extension to arbitrary state spaces. The paper asks whether joint multimodal generation can be performed natively in each modality's own space without any format unifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-image generation, the model consumes text captions (120 tokens during training on SAM-LLaVA, truncated to 40 tokens at MS-COCO evaluation) and/or noisy images, and can produce a generated image, a generated caption, or a joint text-image pair from a single trained model by choosing different time variable configurations. For tabular synthesis, it takes mixed-type data with both numerical and categorical columns and outputs synthesized rows. The key structural detail is that each modality carries its own independent noise level (time variable), so conditioning can be specified at any noise level from fully clean (t=0) to pure noise (t=T), enabling a continuum between unconditional and fully conditional generation.

## Method

The core contribution is a decoupled noise schedule where each modality evolves under its own independent time variable, formalized within the denoising Markov model framework. The paper derives a generalized explicit score matching (GESM) objective over the product state space and proves (Theorem 1) that its unique minimizer is the true joint marginal. A critical result (Proposition 1) shows that, due to conditional independence of the per-modality forward processes given initial conditions, the multimodal score matching loss decomposes into a sum of unimodal conditional score matching losses, making training as simple as jointly optimizing standard per-modality diffusion objectives. The architecture uses an MMDiT backbone with per-modality time conditioning followed by unimodal DiT blocks for refined learning. Additionally, the paper introduces noisy guidance, which replaces the unconditional score in classifier-free guidance with a partially-noised conditional score (σ > s), improving FID by leveraging the model's learned conditional scores at intermediate noise levels.

## Results

On MS-COCO text-to-image generation (FID-30K), the proposed model achieves FID 16.16 with only 481M parameters and a single 83M VAE encoder, compared to 6.5B parameters for DALL-E 2 (FID 10.39) and 3B for Imagen (FID 7.27). Among multimodal generation models, it outperforms Chameleon (FID 26.7), JetFormer (FID 20.86), UniD3 (FID 25.11), and Versatile Diffusion (FID 11.10), though it trails Transfusion (FID 6.78) and MMDiT-improved (FID 6.79). For mixed-type tabular data synthesis, the model achieves best or second-best Trend and MLE scores across all six UCI datasets with only ~64K parameters—100 to 200 times smaller than competing methods (e.g., ~10.7M for TABSYN, ~25M for CoDi).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dedicated text-to-image models (DALL-E 2, Imagen, Stable Diffusion, PixArt-α, MMDiT-improved), the proposed method achieves a higher FID (16.16 vs. 6.79–12.63) but uses a substantially smaller backbone (481M vs. 600M–6.5B) and avoids large text encoders (123M–4.6B) and super-resolution modules. Among multimodal generation models, it clearly outperforms Chameleon (FID 26.7), JetFormer (FID 20.86), UniD3 (FID 25.11), and Versatile Diffusion (FID 11.10), while remaining competitive with Show-o (FID 9.24) and Transfusion (FID 6.78) at a fraction of the parameter count (481M vs. 1.3B–7B). For tabular data synthesis, the ~64K-parameter model matches or exceeds TABSYN (the strongest baseline) on Trend for Adult (98.75 vs. 98.46), Shoppers (98.24 vs. 97.93), and News (98.57 vs. 98.56), and on MLE AUC for Adult (.915 vs. .915), Default (.764 vs. .764), Shoppers (.924 vs. .920), Magic (.941 vs. .938), and RMSE for Beijing (.543 vs. .582), all with 100–200× fewer parameters. The key trade-off is that the method's FID on MS-COCO is higher than the best dedicated T2I models, but it uniquely supports joint text-image generation, image-to-text, and noisy guidance within a single model without requiring pretrained foundation models for representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising Markov model framework (Benton et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2025b) from unimodal to multimodal settings by introducing decoupled time variables per modality and deriving a unified generalized explicit score matching objective. While prior multimodal diffusion methods (UniDiffuser, OmniFlow, AVDiT) already employed decoupled noise schedules, they treated this as an engineering trick without theoretical justification; this paper provides the first formal proof that the multimodal training objective decomposes into a sum of unimodal losses (Proposition 1). Architecturally, it builds on MMDiT and DiT for cross-modal interaction, but departs from the dominant paradigm of projecting all modalities into a shared latent space via VAEs or VQ-VAEs, instead operating natively on the product of heterogeneous state spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the dependency on high-accuracy encoder-decoder pairs (VAEs, VQ-VAEs, tokenizers) that bottleneck data-limited applications and introduce generation artifacts, offering a principled path to multimodal generation on arbitrary state spaces. The parameter efficiency is striking for tabular synthesis (64K params matching 10M+ param baselines), though the text-image FID of 16.16 remains notably behind the best baselines (6.78–7.32), suggesting the framework's full potential hasn't been realized at scale. Open questions include whether pretrained unimodal diffusion models can be used as initialization (acknowledged limitation), how the approach scales to more than two modalities or higher-resolution images, and whether noisy guidance generalizes beyond the continuous-discrete setting.
