## Problem

Deep learning models degrade significantly under real-world image corruptions (noise, blur, weather effects), and existing remedies—image restoration and model fine-tuning—struggle with complex, unknown corruptions while often reducing accuracy on clean data. This paper asks whether modifying the object's own appearance (texture) can make it inherently recognizable to downstream classifiers regardless of imaging degradation, a data-centric perspective that prior work has not explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images of a 3D object (100 hemispherical-viewpoint renders per object in IM3D) and produces a bounded color perturbation δ (‖δ‖ < ε=5.0) with the same spatial shape as the 160³ voxel color grid. For universal robust textures, a single δ_U is optimized across 8 same-category objects with differing geometries and then applied zero-shot to unseen objects. The output is not a 2D image edit but a 3D volumetric color offset that, when added to the reconstructed voxel grid, yields robust renderings from any viewpoint.

## Method

The pipeline first reconstructs a 3D voxel representation (density + RGB color grids at 160³) via DVGO with ReLU activation, then freezes the density grid and optimizes only a color perturbation δ. During each training iteration, a random viewpoint is rendered, a randomly sampled corruption (15 types × 5 severity levels from ImageNet-C) is applied, and the degraded image is classified by a proxy model; gradients backpropagate through the rendering and corruption pipeline to update δ. For universal textures, a single δ_U is trained by randomly sampling object pairs from the same category each iteration, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen geometries. A key empirical finding is that smaller-parameter surrogate models (e.g., ResNet-18) produce more transferable robust textures than larger ones, inverting the usual adversarial-perturbation transferability intuition.

## Results

On the IM3D dataset, the proposed single-object robust texture achieves 92.7% clean accuracy and 85.4% random-corruption accuracy with VGG16 as proxy (mCE 0.175, R.mCE 0.188), and 92.1% / 84.3% with ResNet-18 (mCE 0.177, R.mCE 0.190). These results substantially outperform the best fine-tuning baseline VQSA (74.1% random accuracy, mCE 0.302 with VGG16) and the restoration baseline URIE (45.3% random accuracy, mCE 0.670). The universal robust texture, while weaker than the single-object version, still achieves 32.5% random accuracy (VGG16) and 31.8% (ResNet-18), surpassing the clean baseline (23.0% / 23.7%) and most competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The single-object robust texture decisively outperforms all four baselines on both proxy models. Against VQSA (the strongest fine-tuning baseline), the proposed method improves random-corruption accuracy from 74.1% to 85.4% (VGG16) and from 80.5% to 84.3% (ResNet-18), while also boosting clean accuracy from ~89% to ~92%. URIE (restoration) achieves only 45.3% random accuracy with VGG16, roughly 40 points below the proposed method, and its mCE of 0.670 is nearly 4× worse than the proposed 0.175. DCP (fine-tuning) and Unadv (augmentation) both degrade performance below the clean baseline in several settings, with DCP dropping to 9.7% random accuracy (VGG16) and Unadv to 31.8%. The universal robust texture, while weaker than the single-object version, still exceeds the clean baseline and outperforms DCP and Unadv, though it trails VQSA and URIE on some metrics, indicating room for improvement in cross-geometry generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for handling image corruption—input restoration (URIE, Pei et al.) and model fine-tuning (VQSA, DCP)—by instead modifying the 3D object's texture to be inherently robust against degradation. It builds on the voxel-grid radiance field framework of DVGO (Sun et al., 2022) with the ReLU activation from Karnewar et al. (2022) for fast 3D reconstruction, and extends the 'unadversarial examples' concept of Salman et al. (2021) from single-object adversarial robustness to general multi-type corruption robustness and cross-geometry universal textures. The universal robust texture formulation draws a direct analogy to universal adversarial perturbations (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2017), but inverts the objective: rather than finding a perturbation that fools all models, it finds one that helps all models recognize correctly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-centric framing has practical implications for manufacturing and product design (e.g., traffic signs, autonomous-vehicle components), where altering appearance is feasible while retraining deployed models is not. However, the universal robust texture—the more practically appealing contribution—underperforms single-object textures substantially (e.g., 0.32 vs 0.85 random-severity accuracy on VGG16), and the evaluation is confined to 40 IM3D classes with 10 objects each, leaving open whether the approach scales to the full ImageNet taxonomy or to truly unknown corruption types. The finding that weaker surrogate models yield more transferable textures is counterintuitive relative to adversarial perturbation transferability but is only validated within the ResNet family, so its generality remains untested.
