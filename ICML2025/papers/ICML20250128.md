## Problem

Comparing heterogeneous data distributions that live on incompatible metric spaces (e.g., 3D shape surfaces, single-cell feature spaces, Gaussian mixture models) requires a joint embedding into a common reference space, but existing methods like JMDS are restricted to Euclidean targets. This paper addresses the gap by formulating joint embedding as an unbalanced optimal transport problem with Gromov-Wasserstein marginal penalization, enabling alignment into arbitrary metric spaces including spheres, tori, and the space of 2D Gaussians equipped with the Wasserstein distance.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete metric measure spaces (X_i, d_Xi, ξ_i) — point sets with pairwise dissimilarity matrices and probability weights — along with a fixed finite reference space Z = {z¹,...,zᵐ} equipped with a metric d_Z. In the discrete setting, the supports of the embedded measures are fixed to the grid points of Z, and the method optimizes only over the weight vectors μ_i ∈ Δ_m and the transport plans, which is the key distinction from JMDS (which fixes uniform weights and optimizes point positions in ℝ^d). The output is a 4-plan α ∈ P(X₁×Z₁×Z₂×X₂) whose projections yield the per-space transport plans γ_i (giving barycentric projections of input points into Z) and the joint transport plan π between the two embeddings.

## Method

The core formulation EW_λ minimizes over a transport plan π on Z×Z the sum of the squared Wasserstein cost between its marginals and λ times the squared GW distances between each marginal and the corresponding input mm-space, thereby enforcing near-isometric embeddings while aligning the two spaces. This is equivalently rewritten as a quadratic, multi-marginal, unbalanced OT problem over 4-plans α ∈ P(X₁×Z₁×Z₂×X₂) with fixed marginals ξ_i on X_i. A bi-convex relaxation decouples the quadratic objective into alternating linear multi-marginal unbalanced OT subproblems, each solved by an entropic multi-marginal Sinkhorn scheme within a block-coordinate descent loop (Algorithm 1). The paper proves existence of minimizers, shows that EW_λ converges to the embedded Wasserstein distance as λ→∞ (recovering exact isometric embeddings when they exist), and to a fixed-support GW barycenter as λ→0. Unlike JMDS, which is restricted to Euclidean free-support optimization via SMACOF, the fixed-support formulation here admits arbitrary non-Euclidean reference spaces and is solved entirely through Sinkhorn iterations.

## Results

On the SNAREseq single-cell dataset, the proposed EW_λ method achieves a FOSCTTM of 0.165 and KNN-Accuracy of 0.943, outperforming JMDS (0.219 / 0.872), SCOT (0.402 / 0.447), and UnionCom (0.436 / 0.380). On scGEM, EW_λ achieves FOSCTTM 0.208 and KNN-Acc 0.610, again best or near-best among all methods. On the FAUST 3D shape dataset, EW_λ achieves lower GW distance (0.0048±0.004) and Wasserstein distance (0.027±0.016) between embeddings than JMDS (0.0057±0.005 / 0.046±0.023) at λ=10, confirming superior joint embedding quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against JMDS (the closest competitor, also derived from the same functional), EW_λ wins on FOSCTTM for both SNAREseq (0.165 vs 0.219) and scGEM (0.208 vs 0.230), and on KNN-Accuracy for SNAREseq (0.943 vs 0.872), but slightly loses on scGEM KNN-Accuracy (0.610 vs 0.616). On the FAUST 3D shape benchmark, EW_λ achieves lower GW and Wasserstein distances than JMDS at both λ=1 and λ=10, and visually avoids the artifact of splitting extremities that JMDS exhibits. Against SCOT, EW_λ dominates on all metrics for both datasets (e.g., FOSCTTM 0.165 vs 0.402 on SNAREseq) while being roughly 2.6× faster (28.9s vs 76.7s). Against UnionCom, the gap is even larger (FOSCTTM 0.165 vs 0.436 on SNAREseq), with EW_λ also running ~3× faster. Overall, EW_λ is the best or near-best method across all evaluated metrics and datasets, with the only marginal loss being a 0.006 KNN-Accuracy gap to JMDS on scGEM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the embedded Wasserstein distance (previously defined only for Euclidean target spaces by Salmona et al., 2024) to arbitrary compact metric spaces by formulating it as an unbalanced optimal transport problem with Gromov-Wasserstein marginal penalization. The method draws on Sturm's GW distance for the isometry-enforcing penalty, Mémoli's GW for the computable penalization term, and the unbalanced multi-marginal Sinkhorn solver of Beier et al. (2022) for numerical solution. A key conceptual contribution is showing that the recently proposed JMDS (Chen et al., 2023) is a special case of the same functional where weights are fixed and supports are optimized, whereas the present method fixes supports and optimizes weights—enabling non-Euclidean target spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled way to jointly embed heterogeneous datasets into non-Euclidean reference spaces (sphere, torus, space of Gaussians with Wasserstein metric), which is impossible for JMDS and most prior alignment methods restricted to Euclidean targets. Theoretical guarantees (existence of minimizers, Γ-convergence to EW as λ→∞ and to GW barycenters as λ→0) give the method a solid mathematical foundation. Key limitations include the requirement of a pre-specified discrete reference space (restricting practical dimensionality) and the bi-convex relaxation potentially yielding suboptimal solutions. Open directions include developing a free-support non-Euclidean solver and extending to multiple input spaces.
