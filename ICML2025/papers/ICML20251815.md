## Problem

The Hadwiger-Nelson problem and its variants require finding colorings of continuous geometric spaces that satisfy discrete distance-avoidance constraints, a mixed discrete-continuous challenge that has resisted systematic computational exploration for decades. While SAT solvers and discrete optimization have advanced lower bounds, discovering new valid colorings (upper bounds) has remained largely manual, with only a handful of constructions found in thirty years for key variants. This paper addresses the gap by formulating the coloring problem as a continuous, differentiable optimization task amenable to gradient-based neural network training.

## Input / Output

The network takes 2D or 3D spatial coordinates as input, optionally augmented with one or more distance parameters (for the off-diagonal variant) or triangle side lengths (for the triangle-avoidance variant), and outputs a probability vector in the c-dimensional simplex Δ_c representing a probabilistic coloring. The argmax of this vector yields a discrete color assignment, while the full distribution is used during training to compute a differentiable conflict loss. For the almost-coloring variant, the output simplex is extended to Δ_{c+1}, with the extra coordinate penalized via a Lagrangian term to control the fraction of the plane assigned to the 'waste' color.

## Method

The core reformulation replaces hard discrete color assignments with probabilistic colorings p: R² → Δ_c, so that the inner product p(x)ᵀp(y) gives the probability of a monochromatic conflict, yielding a smooth loss L_R(p) = ∫∫ p(x)ᵀp(y) dν(y) dμ(x) over a finite box. A small MLP (2–4 hidden layers, 32–256 neurons) with sine activations (SIREN-style) parameterizes p_θ, leveraging the universal approximation property and the spectral bias of neural networks toward low-frequency, structured patterns. The nested integrals in the loss are estimated via Monte Carlo sampling of n center points and m points on the unit circle (or sphere of radius d_k for the off-diagonal variant), and the network is trained with Adam using batched gradient descent over 16K–65K steps. For the almost-coloring variant, a Lagrangian relaxation trades off conflict rate against the area assigned to an extra color, and a fully automated pipeline (Algorithm 1) extracts periodicity, discretizes the fundamental parallelogram, and iteratively resolves residual conflicts to produce a rigorously verified formal coloring.

## Results

The framework discovers two novel six-colorings of the plane that extend the known continuum of realizable distances for type (1,1,1,1,1,d) colorings from [√2−1, 1/√5] ≈ [0.414, 0.447] to [0.354, 0.657], the first improvement in thirty years. For the almost-coloring variant, a formalized 5-coloring covers all but 3.74% of the plane (improving the prior best of 4.01%), and a 14-coloring covers all but 3.46% of ℝ³. The method also extends the parameter regions where 3–5 colors suffice to avoid monochromatic triangles, reducing the area still requiring seven colors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Parts (2020b), the paper achieves a clear win on the 5-color almost-coloring: the formalized construction removes only 3.74% of the plane versus the prior 4.01%, directly answering Parts' explicit call for new approaches to push below 4%. For 1–4 color almost-colorings, the method essentially matches Croft's (1967) constructions (e.g., 77.13% vs. 77.04% for 1 color), confirming the framework recovers known optimal patterns. The headline result is a major win over Hoffman & Soifer (1996) and Soifer (1994b): the continuum of six-colorings of type (1,1,1,1,1,d) is extended from [√2−1, 1/√5] ≈ [0.414, 0.447] to [0.354, 0.657], roughly tripling the known range. Against Aichholzer & Perz (2019) on triangle avoidance, the paper expands the regions where 3–5 colors suffice, significantly shrinking the parameter space still requiring seven colors. The method also provides negative evidence: it fails to find a conflict-free 5-coloring for the polychromatic number (best conflict rate ~5%), supporting the conjecture that χ_p(ℝ²) = 6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the implicit neural representation paradigm (Park et al., 2019; Sitzmann et al., 2020) and the probabilistic relaxation + differentiable loss framework of Karalias & Loukas (2020) from purely discrete graph problems to a mixed discrete-continuous geometric setting. It draws on physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al., 2019) for the idea of training an NN via unsupervised residual minimization against geometric constraints, but replaces PDE residuals with unit-distance conflict penalties. The paper positions itself within the AI-assisted mathematical discovery lineage (Davies et al., 2021; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) but distinguishes itself by using NNs as a discovery tool whose outputs require human interpretation and formalization, rather than as an end-to-end solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, reusable template for exploring mixed discrete-continuous geometric optimization problems that have resisted traditional computational approaches, and the automated formalization pipeline (Algorithm 1) bridges the gap between numerical NN outputs and rigorous mathematical constructions. The 30-year improvement to the off-diagonal six-coloring continuum and the 5-color almost-coloring improvement demonstrate concrete mathematical impact, while the negative results (no 6-coloring of the plane, no 5-color polychromatic solution) provide valuable numerical evidence for long-standing conjectures. Key open questions include whether the chromatic number of the plane is 7, whether the polychromatic number is 6, and whether the framework extends to other discrete-geometric problems such as graph limits or higher-dimensional variants.
