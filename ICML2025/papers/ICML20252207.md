## Problem

Existing variational inference methods for ultrametric phylogenetic trees either require MCMC subroutines to restrict the tree space over which inference is performed (Zhang and Matsen IV, 2024) or can only express a limited subset of tree topologies due to a sparse matrix representation (cubeVB, Bouckaert, 2024). No prior method performs differentiable variational inference over the entirety of ultrametric tree space without relying on MCMC to fix the support or initialize parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes aligned genetic sequences (DNA or RNA) with N taxa and M sites, along with a Kingman coalescent prior (effective population size N_e) and a Jukes-Cantor substitution model. It produces a variational posterior over ultrametric trees parameterized by 2·C(N,2) log-normal parameters (one mean and one standard deviation per pairwise coalescent time), from which trees can be sampled via single-linkage clustering. The estimated marginal log-likelihood is computed via importance sampling from the learned variational distribution.

## Method

VIPR defines a variational family by placing independent log-normal distributions on the entries of a pairwise distance matrix T, then mapping T to an ultrametric tree (τ, t) via single-linkage clustering. The key theoretical contribution is a closed-form density q_φ(τ, t) for the induced distribution over trees (Proposition 1), expressed as a product over bipartitions involving the density and survival function of each pairwise coalescent time; every taxa pair appears exactly once, yielding O(N²) evaluation cost. The density is differentiable with respect to the variational parameters, enabling gradient-based optimization of the ELBO using three estimators: leave-one-out REINFORCE, the reparameterization trick, and VIMCO. Unlike SBN-based methods whose parameter count grows super-linearly with N, VIPR requires only O(N²) parameters total, and unlike cubeVB it is supported on the full tree space without MCMC.

## Results

On 11 benchmark genomic datasets (DS1–DS11) and a 72-taxon SARS-CoV-2 dataset (COV), VIPR achieves marginal log-likelihood (MLL) gaps from the BEAST gold standard ranging from +0.94 nats (VIMCO on DS5) to −611 nats (LOOR on COV), while VBPI10 ranges from +0.27 to −18.79 nats. In terms of ELBO, VIPR methods outperform VBPI10 on exactly half of the 12 datasets (DS2, DS4, DS5, DS9, DS11, COV), with the largest ELBO advantage on DS11 (−6289.60 vs −6329.37 nats). On the COV dataset, VIPR underperforms VBPI substantially in MLL (gaps of −214 to −611 nats vs −39.1 nats), likely due to poor initialization in a low-ELBO region. Computationally, VIPR scales approximately O(N) in the number of taxa, outperforming VBPI at 512 taxa where VBPI's super-linear parameter growth becomes a bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is VBPI (Zhang and Matsen IV, 2024), a subsplit Bayesian network approach that requires MCMC to restrict tree support. VBPI10 achieves smaller MLL gaps on 8 of 12 datasets (e.g., −0.53 vs −2.29 on DS1; −0.88 vs −5.38 on DS10), but VIPR methods win on DS2, DS4, DS5, DS9, and DS11 where VBPI's MCMC-restricted support is less effective. On ELBO, the two methods are essentially tied: VIPR (across its three gradient estimators) beats VBPI10 on exactly 6 of 12 datasets, with the largest margin on DS11 (≈40 nats) and DS4 (≈29 nats). The most striking divergence is on the COV dataset, where VBPI10 achieves a −39.1 nat gap while VIPR's best variant (VIMCO) reaches only −214 nats, suggesting initialization and optimization challenges on high-dimensional, low-signal data. Computationally, VIPR uses O(N²) parameters (2·C(N,2) for log-normal means and stds) versus VBPI's super-linear SBN parameter count (319,533 vs 261,632 at N=512), and VIPR's per-iteration wall-clock time grows roughly linearly while VBPI's grows super-linearly, giving VIPR a speed advantage at large N.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIPR extends the single-linkage clustering framework introduced by Bouckaert (2024, cubeVB) for variational inference over ultrametric trees, but resolves two key limitations: it specifies a variational distribution over the entire tree space (rather than a restricted cube) and eliminates the need for MCMC subroutines to determine the support. The method also positions itself against the SBN-based variational Bayesian phylogenetic inference of Zhang and Matsen IV (2019, 2024), which requires MCMC to identify likely subsplits before optimization. The core theoretical contribution—a closed-form density (Proposition 1) for the tree distribution induced by single-linkage clustering on a random distance matrix—builds on the SLINK algorithm (Sibson, 1973) and coalescent theory (Kingman, 1982) to make the variational family differentiable and amenable to gradient-based optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIPR provides the first MCMC-free, fully differentiable variational family for ultrametric phylogenetic trees that covers the entire tree space, offering a practical alternative to both MCMC (BEAST) and SBN-based VI (VBPI) for time-calibrated phylogenies relevant to pathogen epidemiology. However, the method's reliance on independent log-normal distributions for pairwise coalescent times limits its expressiveness—posterior predictive checks reveal underestimation of tree-length variance, and the COVID-19 dataset shows substantial MLL gaps (200–600 nats) compared to VBPI, suggesting the variational family struggles with high-dimensional, low-signal data. Open questions include whether richer distributions (mixtures, normalizing flows), conditional parameters, and annealing schedules can close this gap, and whether the approach extends to relaxed-clock models and effective population size inference.
