## Problem

Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for continuous action spaces relies on hierarchical bandit exploration (HOOT), but existing methods either use logarithmic bonuses that lack convergence guarantees under non-stationary value estimates (HOOT) or provide polynomial-bonus convergence only for deterministic MDPs (POLY-HOOT). No algorithm with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees existed for the combined setting of continuous actions and stochastic transitions, a critical gap for real-world domains with transition and reward uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a continuous initial state s₀ ∈ ℝⁿ and access to a generative model that provides sampled transitions s_{d+1} ~ P(·|s_d, a_d) and rewards r ~ R(s_d, a_d, s_{d+1}) on demand. It outputs the estimated optimal action at the root node and a value estimate V̂_n(s₀). The method operates over a bounded planning horizon D with a playout policy π₀ applied only at leaf nodes, and requires rewards to be bounded in [0, R_max] with a reward transformation (max(0.01, (r+offset)×scaling)) to ensure strictly positive inputs for the power mean operator.

## Method

Stochastic-Power-HOOT replaces the standard arithmetic-mean value backup with a power mean operator V̂(s_d) = (Σ_a [T_{s_d,a}/T_{s_d}] · Q̂(s_d,a)^p)^(1/p), where the tunable exponent p interpolates between conservative (p=1) and optimistic (p→∞) aggregation, and replaces the logarithmic UCB bonus with a polynomial exploration term Ct^(b_{d+1}/β_{d+1}) · T_{h,i}^(−α_{d+1}/β_{d+1}) to handle non-stationary value estimates across the HOO partition tree. The algorithm iteratively performs selection (traversing the HOO tree by B-values), expansion (sampling an action within the selected partition), rollout (simulating under π₀ to depth D), and backpropagation (updating power-mean values and polynomial UCBs). The theoretical analysis proceeds inductively from leaves to root: Lemma 1 (from Dam et al. 2024b) shows Q-value concentration is preserved under stochastic transitions, and Theorem 2 proves the power mean aggregation preserves polynomial concentration rates, yielding an overall convergence rate of O(n^(−ζ)) for ζ ∈ (0,1/2) under parameter constraints in Table 1.

## Results

On classic stochastic control tasks, Stochastic-Power-HOOT (p=2) achieves optimal scores of 77.85 on CartPole-IG and Acrobot, 1397.40 on Pendulum, and 27.285 on MountainCar, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., MountainCar: 27.285 vs. −0.055 for POLY-HOOT). On high-dimensional MuJoCo environments, it achieves a 3.1× improvement over the UCT baseline on Humanoid (−44.12 vs. −136.98) and 2.5–2.6× on Hopper (13303–13348 vs. 5217). Notably, Voronoi MCTS, designed for deterministic settings, fails catastrophically on stochastic Hopper (411.70, 12.7× worse than UCT), validating the need for explicit stochasticity handling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stochastic-Power-HOOT outperforms all five baselines on the classic control suite. Against Discretized-UCT and HOOT, it achieves dramatically higher MountainCar scores (27.285 vs. −0.020 and −0.007) and CartPole-IG scores (77.85 vs. 37.95 and 38.51), indicating that logarithmic bonuses and naive discretization fail under stochasticity. Against POLY-HOOT (the closest deterministic competitor), it wins on Pendulum (1397.40 vs. 1205.89) and MountainCar (27.285 vs. −0.055) while tying on CartPole-IG; on MuJoCo, both achieve 3.1× over UCT on Humanoid, but Stochastic-Power-HOOT edges ahead on Hopper (13303 vs. 13231). Against Progressive Widening (PW), it wins on CartPole-IG (77.85 vs. 68.46), Pendulum (1397.40 vs. 1141.51), and MountainCar (27.285 vs. −0.020). Voronoi MCTS, designed for deterministic continuous spaces, performs reasonably on Humanoid (−48.69, 2.8× over UCT) but collapses on stochastic Hopper (411.70, 12.7× worse than UCT), confirming that deterministic regret bounds do not transfer to stochastic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (1) the MCTS+HOO lineage for continuous actions (HOOT → POLY-HOOT) and (2) power mean value backups developed for discrete MCTS (Dam et al., 2020; 2024a; 2024b). POLY-HOOT (Mao et al., 2020) established polynomial-rate convergence for deterministic continuous MDPs using a polynomial exploration bonus, while the power mean backup operator had only been analyzed for discrete settings. Stochastic-Power-HOOT unifies these by applying the power mean as the value backup operator within the HOO tree structure and extending the polynomial bonus analysis to handle stochastic transitions, closing the gap that neither thread addressed alone. The theoretical machinery (Assumptions 1–2, B-value propagation, concentration lemmas) is adapted directly from Bubeck et al. (2011) and Mao et al. (2020), with the key new ingredient being Lemma 1 from Dam et al. (2024b) for stochastic Q-value concentration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is a clean theoretical closure: continuous stochastic MDPs now have non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for MCTS, matching the O(n^{-ζ}) rate already known for the deterministic case. This is practically relevant for robotics and control where both continuous actions and stochastic dynamics are unavoidable, and the MuJoCo experiments (particularly the 12.7× degradation of Voronoi MCTS on Hopper) concretely demonstrate why deterministic-only methods fail. Limitations include the bounded-horizon assumption in the analysis, the need for a generative model, and the sensitivity of the power parameter p to environment characteristics (p=2 for Humanoid vs. p=8 for Hopper), which lacks a principled selection rule. Open questions include whether the rate is tight, how the method scales to very deep trees or high-dimensional action spaces beyond 17D, and whether the power mean can be replaced by learned aggregation without losing guarantees.
