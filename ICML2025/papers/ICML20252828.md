## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of consistency models used a variance-preserving (VPSDE) forward process with uniform stepsize, while empirical consistency models adopt a variance-exploding (VESDE) process with a decay (EDM) discretization scheme. This mismatch produced discretization complexity bounds (e.g., L_f/ε_W2^7) that are significantly worse than diffusion model results and fail to explain the competitive empirical performance of consistency models. The paper asks whether a setting closer to practice can yield bounds comparable to diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The consistency model takes a single sample from N(0, T²I_d) (pure Gaussian noise with variance scaled by the forward-process horizon T) and produces one generated sample from the target data distribution q_0 in a single forward pass of the learned consistency function f_θ. The key theoretical quantity is the discretization complexity K — the number of training intervals needed so that the W_2 distance between the generated distribution and q_0 is at most ε_W2. The analysis also covers a 2-step variant where the model first generates a sample, re-noises it at τ₂ = T/2, and maps it back, reducing the required K.

## Method

The paper analyzes consistency distillation under VESDE (σ_t² = t², giving a linear solution trajectory) with the EDM stepsize t_k = (δ + kh)^a. The core technical contribution is a time-dependent score perturbation lemma bounding E[‖∂_t ∇log q_t(X_t)‖] ≤ R³/t⁵ + R²√d/t⁴, which captures how the score's time-derivative decays as t grows. This replaces the uniform-in-t bound used by prior work and is essential for exploiting the non-uniform EDM stepsize: summing the per-step PFODE error with the time-dependent bound yields ∑h_k²/t_k² ≍ (T/δ)^{1/a}/K, producing K = O(L_f/ε_W2^{3+2/a}) for one-step generation (improving from L_f/ε_W2^9 with a uniform bound). For 2-step sampling, the re-noising step reduces the required T from R²/ε_W2 to R^{1.5}/√ε_W2, tightening the bound to K = O(L_f/ε_W2^{3+3/(2a)}). The analysis also covers the exponential-decay stepsize limit (a→∞), recovering K = O(L_f/ε_W2³), which is competitive with the best diffusion-model PFODE bounds.

## Results

The paper proves the state-of-the-art discretization complexity for one-step consistency models under VESDE + EDM stepsize: K = O(L_f / ε_W2^(3+2/a)), which with the standard a=7 gives O(L_f / ε_W2^(23/7)) ≈ O(L_f / ε_W2^3.29). Under the theoretically-friendly exponential decay stepsize, the bound improves to K = O(L_f / ε_W2^3), which is competitive with the SOTA diffusion model bound of O(1/ε_W2^4). The 2-step sampling algorithm further reduces the complexity to K = O(L_f / ε_W2^(3+3/(2a))), explaining its empirical success in reducing the required number of training discretization points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against three prior theoretical works on consistency models: Lyu et al. (2024) achieves O(L_f/ε_W2^7) under VPSDE + uniform stepsize, Li et al. (2024a) achieves O(L_f^3/ε_W1) under a VPSDE-specific noise schedule, and Dou et al. (2024) achieves O(L_f^2/ε_W1^10) under VPSDE + uniform stepsize. The proposed VESDE + EDM analysis improves the exponent from 7 to 3+2/a (≈3.29 for a=7), a significant reduction. Against diffusion model baselines, the exponential decay result O(L_f/ε_W2^3) is better than the SOTA diffusion PFODE bound O(1/ε_W2^4) whenever L_f ≤ O(1/ε_W2), which the paper argues holds for realistic score functions. The 2-step sampling result O(L_f/ε_W2^(3+3/(2a))) further tightens the bound, resolving a gap where Lyu et al. (2024) showed multi-step sampling reduces W_2 error but did not improve the K requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical analyses on consistency model discretization complexity (Lyu et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024a, Dou et al. 2024) by shifting the analysis from the VPSDE + uniform stepsize setting used in prior theory to the VESDE + EDM stepsize setting that matches empirical practice. The key technical departure is a time-dependent score perturbation lemma (Lemma 4.14) that replaces the uniform bound used in all prior works, which is essential for exploiting the non-uniform EDM stepsize. The paper also builds on diffusion model convergence theory (Chen et al. 2023c, Gao and Zhu 2025) to position consistency model results competitively against multi-step diffusion sampling guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap between the theoretical and empirical settings of consistency models, showing that the previously reported O(L_f/ε^7) complexity was an artifact of the wrong forward process and stepsize choice rather than a fundamental limitation. The result that consistency models can match or beat diffusion model discretization complexity (O(L_f/ε^3) vs. O(1/ε^4)) under realistic settings validates the empirical success of one-step generation and the EDM noise schedule. Open questions remain: the CT paradigm under the one-step PFODE training operation (matching practice), end-to-end analysis incorporating the learning process of both score and consistency functions, and the stability of continuous-time consistency models (Lu and Song 2024) which lack a well-defined discretization parameter.
