## Problem

Existing fully-dynamic consistent clustering algorithms provide only absolute recourse bounds (e.g., O(T) after T updates), which are tight but achieved on pathological sequences where data has no consistent structure. This paper initiates the study of competitive recourse guarantees for fully-dynamic k-center, facility location, and k-median, where the algorithm's total recourse is bounded relative to the optimal offline recourse of a β-approximate solution (OPT_REC^β), a more meaningful benchmark for realistic data streams.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a metric space (M,d) with aspect ratio Δ, a set of candidate center locations F, and a fully dynamic stream of client insertions and deletions over T time steps. At each step it must output a set of open centers S^t ⊆ F that is O(β)-approximate for the clustering objective, while minimizing total recourse (the sum of center additions and deletions over time). The algorithm uses resource augmentation: (1+ε)·k centers for k-center and O(k) centers for k-median, and the recourse guarantee is O(log|F|·log Δ)·OPT_REC^β rather than an absolute O(T) bound.

## Method

The algorithm first obtains a fractional solution via a reduction to the Positive Body Chasing framework (Bhattacharya et al., FOCS 2023), which maintains a point in a sequence of convex bodies defined by covering and packing constraints with O(log|F|/ε)·OPT_REC^β fractional ℓ₁-movement. The core contribution is an online rounding procedure that converts this fractional solution to an integral one while preserving both approximation and recourse: for k-center, the algorithm maintains a set of well-separated balls of varying radii around open centers, dropping a ball only when its fractional mass dips below a threshold or when a new uncovered client forces an addition, and a potential function with log(Δ) terms bounds the number of such drops per unit of fractional movement. For facility location, the rounding is a dynamic analog of the Shmoys–Tardos–Aardal greedy scheme, maintaining well-separated balls around active clients and selecting the cheapest facility within each, with Markov's inequality guaranteeing sufficient fractional mass in each ball. The k-median algorithm follows directly from the facility location rounding by setting all opening costs to zero. Logarithmic lower bounds are established via uniform binary HST metrics with adversarial root-to-leaf request sequences.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1.1, which provides O(β)-approximate dynamic clustering algorithms with recourse bounded by O(log|F| log Δ)·OPT_REC^β for facility location and k-median (using O(k) centers), and O(1/ε² log(|F|/ε) log Δ)·OPT_REC^β for k-center (using (1+ε)k centers). Theorem 1.2 establishes matching lower bounds of Ω(min{log|F|, log_α Δ})·OPT_REC^β, showing the logarithmic dependence is nearly tight. Experimentally, on UCI datasets (Glass, Wine Quality, Airfoil Self-Noise) with T=100 time steps, k=4, β=3/2, ε=1/4, the algorithms' objective values are close to the fractional optimum (significantly below the α·β·OPT^t worst-case bound), and recourse is ≪ T—often bounded by a constant—validating the competitive recourse framework over absolute recourse guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, and the experiments do not include direct head-to-head comparisons against prior dynamic clustering algorithms (e.g., Lacki et al. 2024 for k-center, or Bhattacharya et al. 2022a for facility location). Instead, the experimental evaluation benchmarks the proposed algorithms against two internal references: (1) the fractional optimum OPT^t at each time step, and (2) the theoretical worst-case bound α·β·OPT^t guaranteed by the theorems. The algorithms' objective values track closely with the fractional optimum for facility location and k-median, and remain well below the worst-case bound for k-center. On the recourse side, the algorithms' total recourse is substantially lower than T (the trivial absolute recourse bound) and in many k-center cases even lower than the offline fractional optimum recourse (enabled by resource augmentation). The paper notes that a direct empirical comparison against existing absolute-recourse algorithms is left as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Positive Body Chasing framework of Bhattacharya et al. (FOCS 2023), which provides the online fractional solution, and adapts the classic greedy disjoint-ball rounding of Shmoys et al. (STOC 1997) to the dynamic setting. It departs from prior fully-dynamic consistent clustering work (Lattanzi & Vassilvitskii 2017; Lacki et al. 2024; Fichtenberger et al. 2021), which all targeted absolute recourse bounds of the form c·T, by instead pursuing competitive recourse guarantees relative to an offline optimum. The paper's core technical contribution is the online rounding step: converting the fractional solution from Positive Body Chasing into an integral clustering solution while simultaneously preserving both the approximation ratio and the recourse bound, a task that is substantially harder than offline rounding due to stability requirements over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from absolute to competitive recourse is a meaningful conceptual advance: it acknowledges that some update sequences inherently require Ω(T) recourse, and instead measures an algorithm's quality relative to the best possible offline solution. The near-tight O(log|F| log Δ) upper bound with a matching Ω(log|F|) lower bound gives a clean characterization of the problem's complexity. Key open questions include closing the remaining logarithmic gap, determining whether exactly k centers (rather than (1+ε)k) suffices, and understanding the relationship between absolute and competitive recourse guarantees. The experiments on UCI datasets confirm that competitive recourse is the right lens in practice, with observed recourse far below T and often below the offline fractional optimum.
