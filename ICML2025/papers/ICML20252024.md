## Problem

Existing relaxation-based methods for Max-k-Cut (SDP, Lovász extension) either lack guarantees that the relaxed solution's objective value matches the original discrete problem, or are computationally intractable beyond a few hundred nodes. The paper addresses the need for a scalable framework that simultaneously maintains theoretical consistency between continuous relaxation solutions and discrete Max-k-Cut solutions while handling arbitrary signed edge weights and general k.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252024/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with arbitrary real-valued edge weights W_ij (positive, negative, or zero) and a partition count k, and produces a k-partition of the vertex set as a k×N one-hot assignment matrix. The GNN consumes the graph topology with edge weights as node-neighbor aggregation features, and the final output is obtained by sampling T=100 discrete assignments from the continuous simplex solution and selecting the best. Unlike prior methods limited to unweighted or k=2 cases, ROS handles weighted instances with any k.

## Method

ROS operates in three stages: (1) Relax — the discrete one-hot constraint is replaced by a probability simplex constraint, yielding a non-convex continuous problem min Tr(XWX^T) over Δ_k^N; (2) Optimize — a 2-layer GNN with GraphNorm is parametrized to directly output the relaxed solution, trained via a 'pre-train + fine-tune' strategy where the model is first trained on 1500 random regular graphs (N=100) and then fine-tuned on the target instance using Adam with early stopping; (3) Sample — each vertex's partition is drawn independently from a categorical distribution parameterized by the corresponding column of the continuous solution, and the best of T=100 samples is returned. The key theoretical contribution is proving that all points in the neighborhood of a global optimum share the same objective value (Theorem 3.2), and that the random sampling preserves the objective value in expectation (Theorem 3.3), establishing exact consistency between the continuous and discrete solutions.

## Results

On random regular graphs (Table 2), ROS achieves cut values of 128.20 (k=2) and 240.30 (k=3) at N=100, scaling to 12,856.85 (k=2) and 24,085.95 (k=3) at N=10,000, all solved in a few seconds. On weighted Gset instances with 20,000 nodes (Table 3), ROS achieves cut values of 72,057.24 (k=2) and 82,450.68 (k=3), outperforming MD (67,403.00 / 78,065.07) and PI-GNN (62,439.97 / N/A). On the real-world Bitcoin-OTC signed network (Table 4), ROS achieves 39,850 (k=2), 48,980 (k=3), and 53,778 (k=10), surpassing MD (39,076 / 47,595 / 53,563) and ANYCSP (10,431 / 14,265 / 19,372). ROS solves all instances with up to 20,000 nodes in seconds, requiring only ~1% of the computational time of PI-GNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROS is the only method that simultaneously supports arbitrary k, arbitrary edge weights (including negative), and scales to 20,000 nodes. On unweighted random regular graphs, ANYCSP and ECO-DQN achieve slightly higher cut values for k=2 (e.g., ANYCSP 13,692 vs ROS 12,857 at N=10,000), but ANYCSP cannot handle weighted instances and ECO-DQN cannot scale beyond N=1,000. On weighted Gset instances (σ~[0,10]), ROS clearly dominates: at N=20,000 (G81), ROS achieves 72,057 (k=2) vs MD's 67,403, PI-GNN's 62,440, and ANYCSP's −13.52 (which fails entirely on weighted graphs). On the Bitcoin-OTC signed network, ROS outperforms MD by ~2% (39,850 vs 39,076 for k=2) and outperforms ANYCSP by ~283% (39,850 vs 10,431). GW, BQP, and Genetic all time out (N/A) for N≥10,000, while PI-GNN requires retraining per instance and is ~100× slower than ROS. The pre-training stage (ROS vs ROS-vanilla) reduces computational time by ~40% on Gset with only a marginal decrease in cut value, demonstrating effective transfer learning across graph types and sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SDP-relaxation lineage (Goemans-Williamson, Frieze-Jerrum) by adopting a probability simplex relaxation that, while non-convex, guarantees exact objective-value consistency with the original Max-k-Cut problem—a property SDP and QUBO relaxations lack for general k and arbitrary edge weights. On the optimization side, it follows the learning-to-optimize (L2O) trend initiated by PI-GNN (Schuetz et al.) and ANYCSP (Tönshoff et al.) but introduces a pre-train + fine-tune GNN strategy that avoids training from scratch per instance, and replaces their problem-specific formulations (QUBO, CSP) with a unified continuous relaxation. The random sampling step with its expectation-preservation guarantee (Theorem 3.3) is conceptually related to Goemans-Williamson's randomized rounding but is grounded in a different geometric landscape analysis of the simplex relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: solving 20,000-node weighted Max-k-Cut instances in seconds (vs. minutes to hours for SDP or evolutionary baselines) makes large-scale graph partitioning tractable in real-world settings like network design and signed social networks. The theoretical value-consistency guarantee between the continuous relaxation and the sampled discrete solution is a clean contribution that distinguishes ROS from heuristic rounding approaches. Open questions include whether the non-convex relaxation can be optimized more reliably (the GNN may converge to local minima), whether alternative sampling strategies (e.g., importance sampling, deterministic rounding) could tighten the gap, and whether the framework extends naturally to other combinatorial problems with simplex-structured constraints.
