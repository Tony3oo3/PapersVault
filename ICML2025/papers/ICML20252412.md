## Problem

Despite the growing literature on machine unlearning, no general bounds existed on the computational cost of unlearning relative to full retraining, making it impossible to rigorously determine when unlearning is actually worthwhile. This paper establishes the first upper and lower bounds on the minimax unlearning complexity ratio (unlearning time over retraining time) for strongly convex objectives, revealing three distinct regimes where unlearning is trivial, efficient, or infeasible.

## Input / Output

The unlearning algorithm receives a pre-trained model at the full-dataset optimum θ*, samples only from the retain distribution D_r (the forget set is inaccessible), and is constrained by a differential-privacy budget (ε, δ) quantified via κ_{ε,δ} = ε⁻¹√(2 ln(1.25/δ)). The analysis is conducted in minimax form over the class of all iterative first-order algorithms and strongly convex L-Lipschitz loss functions, producing worst-case bounds on the number of stochastic gradient steps needed to reach a target excess risk e. The output is characterized by the unlearning complexity ratio T_e^U / T_e^S, which determines whether unlearning saves computation relative to retraining from scratch.

## Method

The paper introduces a minimax optimization framework for unlearning, defining the unlearning complexity ratio as the ratio of minimax unlearning time to minimax retraining time over the class of strongly convex Lipschitz losses. Three theorems delineate a phase diagram: (1) a trivial regime where adding Gaussian noise with standard deviation κ_{ε,δ}·(r_f/(1−r_f))·(L/μ) to θ* achieves unlearning with zero gradient steps; (2) an inefficient regime proven via a Le Cam two-point argument combined with the (ε,δ)-unlearning constraint, showing no first-order method can asymptotically beat retraining when e is below a threshold proportional to r_f²(1+κ²)e₀; and (3) an efficient regime where a 'noise and fine-tune' algorithm (perturbed SGD with decreasing step size 2/(μ(t+1))) achieves a complexity ratio of O(r_f²(1+dκ²)e₀/e), demonstrating that small forget fractions can be removed at quadratically small cost. The lower bound construction uses a family of non-smooth losses parameterized by a Rademacher function g to create two indistinguishable optimization problems whose retain-optima are separated, forcing any algorithm to incur near-full retraining cost.

## Results

The paper establishes the first upper and lower bounds on the unlearning complexity ratio T_e^U / T_e^S, identifying three distinct regimes. In the Trivial Regime (TR), unlearning requires zero gradient steps (ratio = 0) when the target excess risk e ≥ (r_f/(1−r_f))(r_f/(1−r_f) + √d·κ_{ε,δ})·e_0. In the Inefficient Regime (IR), no first-order unlearning method can asymptotically beat retraining (ratio = Ω(1)) when e < c·(r_f/(1−r_f))²(1+κ_{ε,δ}²)·e_0. In the Efficient Regime (ER), the 'noise and fine-tune' algorithm achieves a ratio of O((r_f/(1−r_f))²(1+d·κ_{ε,δ}²)·e_0/e), demonstrating that unlearning cost scales quadratically with the forget ratio r_f. Empirical validation on synthetic worst-case functions and the Digit dataset (r_f = 10⁻²) confirms all three regimes in the phase diagram.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between unlearning and full retraining from scratch, rather than between competing unlearning methods. The retraining baseline (SGD) achieves the optimal minimax rate Θ(e_0/e) for strongly convex Lipschitz functions. The 'noise and fine-tune' unlearning algorithm (adapted from Neel et al., 2021) outperforms retraining in the Efficient Regime, achieving a complexity ratio proportional to r_f²(1+dκ_{ε,δ}²)·e_0/e, which is strictly less than 1 for small r_f. In the Inefficient Regime, the paper proves an impossibility result: no first-order unlearning algorithm can asymptotically beat retraining, answering an open problem posed by Allouah et al. (2024). In the Trivial Regime, unlearning is strictly better than retraining (zero cost vs. Θ(e_0/e) steps). On the Digit dataset with r_f = 10⁻², the empirical phase diagram confirms that for moderate privacy budgets (κ_{ε,δ} ≈ 1) and realistic excess risk targets (e ~ 1/n), the unlearning complexity ratio is well below 1, validating the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the differential-privacy-based unlearning framework introduced by Ginart et al. (2019) and the perturbed gradient descent algorithm of Neel et al. (2021), but departs from prior work by shifting from algorithm-specific upper bounds (Chourasia & Shah 2023; Allouah et al. 2024) to a minimax complexity analysis over the entire class of first-order unlearning algorithms. It introduces the unlearning complexity ratio as a formal metric and provides the first lower bound on unlearning time, answering an open problem posed by Allouah et al. (2024). The slightly stronger (ε,δ)-Unlearning definition (Definition 2) replaces the reference-algorithm-dependent formulation, enabling cleaner impossibility arguments via Le Cam's two-point method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-regime phase diagram (trivial, inefficient, efficient) gives practitioners a principled way to assess whether unlearning is worth the engineering effort for a given privacy budget, forget ratio, and accuracy target. The negative result—that unlearning cannot asymptotically beat retraining for sufficiently small excess risk targets—challenges the implicit assumption that unlearning is always cheaper. Key limitations include the restriction to strongly convex Lipschitz objectives (not directly applicable to deep networks), the inaccessibility of the forget set, and the unmatched √d gap between upper and lower bounds, all of which are flagged as open directions for future work.
