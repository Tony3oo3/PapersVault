## Problem

Existing graph contrastive learning methods suffer from three critical issues: augmentation strategies (node dropping, edge perturbation) risk disturbing underlying graph semantics, GCN encoders fail to capture long-range dependencies, and node sampling techniques are rigid and computationally expensive due to pairwise distance calculations. No prior work has provided a theoretical grounding for positive/negative pair selection in GCL, leaving sampling strategies ad hoc.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251474/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the original node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} and binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} — no augmented views are generated. It produces node embeddings Z ∈ R^{N×D} (D ≪ F) via a single GCN forward pass. Unusually, the positive-pair weight matrix W^p is computed in closed form from the raw input features X (not learned embeddings) before any encoder training begins, and negative-pair weights are uniform over non-neighbor nodes, eliminating all pairwise distance computations during training.

## Method

ML²-GCL marries Locally Linear Embedding (LLE) with GCN-based contrastive learning. For each anchor node v_i, its k-hop neighbors form the positive pair set P_i, and reconstruction weights w_ij^p are obtained by solving a ridge-regularized quadratic program (min ‖x_i − Σ w_ij^p x_j‖² + λ‖w_i^p‖²) subject to non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints, yielding the closed-form solution w_i^p = (X_i X_i^T + λI)^{-1} X_i x_i followed by ReLU and normalization (Propositions 1–2). Negative pairs (all non-neighbor nodes) receive uniform weight 1/|N_i|. A standard GCN encoder maps the single original graph to embeddings Z, and a weighted InfoNCE loss (Eq. 7) pulls the anchor toward its weighted positive neighbors while pushing it away from weighted negatives, with a temperature τ controlling hard-negative sensitivity. The key architectural difference from prior GCL is the elimination of all augmentation and pairwise distance computation, making the per-step cost roughly half that of two-view methods like GRACE.

## Results

On node classification, ML²-GCL achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all 6 datasets, with headline results of 83.0% on Amazon-Computers (3.2% relative improvement over NCLA's 79.8%) and 73.3% on Wiki-CS (3.0% over NCLA's 70.3%). On link prediction, ML²-GCL reaches 96.9% AUC on Cora (3.6% relative improvement over AUGCL's 93.3%) and 98.7% AUC on Pubmed, outperforming all 11 baselines on every dataset under both AUC and AP metrics. The method uses only a single view (no augmentation) and a closed-form solution for positive pair weights, halving the per-step computational cost compared to two-view methods like GRACE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ML²-GCL outperforms all 13 node classification baselines on every dataset. The largest margins are on Amazon-Computers (83.0% vs. NCLA 79.8%, GTCA 79.2%) and Wiki-CS (73.3% vs. NCLA 70.3%, GTCA 69.7%), where the multi-positive-pair manifold learning strategy provides the most benefit. On smaller datasets like Cora and Citeseer, gains over NCLA are modest (83.0 vs. 82.2; 71.7 vs. 71.7, a tie), suggesting the advantage grows with class count and graph complexity. For link prediction, ML²-GCL dominates all 11 baselines on all 6 datasets, with the most striking gap on Cora (96.9 AUC vs. AUGCL 93.3, a 3.6% relative gain) and Pubmed (98.7 vs. ARGE 96.8). Augmentation-based methods (GRACE, GCA, MVGRL) consistently underperform augmentation-free approaches (SUGRL, AFGRL, NCLA), and ML²-GCL further improves upon the best augmentation-free baselines without requiring multiple views or pairwise distance computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ML²-GCL departs from the dominant multi-view augmentation paradigm in graph contrastive learning (GRACE, GCA, NCLA) by eliminating augmented views entirely and instead deriving positive-pair weights from a locally linear embedding (LLE)–inspired quadratic program with a closed-form solution. Conceptually, it inherits the local-to-global structure recovery principle from LLE (Roweis & Saul, 2000) and couples it with a standard GCN encoder, positioning itself as the first explicit marriage of manifold learning and GCL. Unlike prior node-sampling approaches that rely on k-means, GMMs, or random perturbation to estimate pair probabilities, ML²-GCL computes weights directly from the original node features, avoiding both semantic disturbance from augmentation and the randomness/complexity of probabilistic samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is the lightweight single-view pipeline: no augmentation, no pairwise distance computation, and a closed-form weight solution reduce both compute and memory (Table 7 shows the lowest GPU usage among top methods). However, the evaluation is confined to six small-to-medium citation and co-purchase graphs (≤20K nodes) with a fixed GCN encoder, leaving open whether the LLE-inspired weighting generalizes to heterogeneous, dynamic, or very large-scale graphs. The authors themselves flag deep integration with dynamic/temporal graph structures as future work, and the 'personalized graph embedding task' framing (Theorem 1) remains underexplored beyond a single Taylor-expansion argument.
