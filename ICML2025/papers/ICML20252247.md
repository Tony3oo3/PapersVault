## Problem

DocVQA models are opaque black boxes whose attention-based explanations are noisy, imprecise, and fail to justify why a particular answer was selected rather than merely highlighting where it appears. No self-explainable DocVQA framework existed that learns visual explanations which are both contextually sufficient (capturing all regions needed to answer) and representation-efficient (excluding irrelevant areas), grounded in formal explainability principles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-resolution RGB document image (aspect ratio preserved via Pix2Struct's screenshot-parsing pretraining) and a natural-language question rendered onto the image. It produces a free-form text answer and a visual relevance map that, after postprocessing (background-token filtering and connected-component bounding), yields the top-k bounding boxes (default k=3) enclosing the contextually critical regions. The mask is applied multiplicatively to the input image before the answer is predicted, so the explanation is causally linked to the prediction rather than being a post-hoc overlay.

## Method

DocVXQA extends a pretrained Pix2Struct encoder-decoder with a lightweight MLP mask head that generates a per-token relevance mask M from decoder attentions and positional encodings; the masked image X⊙M is then fed back through the same frozen-decoder network to predict the answer, making the explanation a direct input to the prediction. Training is governed by an information-bottleneck-inspired objective combining three losses: cross-entropy on the masked-input prediction (sufficiency), an L1-norm plus anisotropic total-variation continuity penalty on the mask (minimality/sparsity), and an MSE alignment term against a ColPali-derived prior mask (token interactions) that prevents the mask from overfitting to the answer string alone and instead captures broader contextual evidence. The ColPali prior is computed once per training sample via late-interaction matching between question tokens and document image patches, providing a soft spatial guide without requiring answer-location annotations. At inference, a two-stage postprocessing step removes low-variance background tokens and encloses connected relevance regions in bounding boxes, retaining only the top-k by confidence score.

## Results

On DocVQA (threshold 0.70), DocVXQA achieves 38% accuracy and 0.54 ANLS with a pixel ratio of 0.23, matching ColPali+Pix2Struct on accuracy while improving ANLS by 4 points at the same coverage. On PFL-DocVQA (threshold 0.70), DocVXQA reaches 43% accuracy and 0.66 ANLS with pixel ratio 0.22, substantially outperforming ColPali+Pix2Struct (28% ACC, 0.39 ANLS). All post-hoc baselines (Raw Attention, Attention Rollout, Grad-CAM) degrade severely under masking, dropping below 7% accuracy on DocVQA at moderate thresholds. Human evaluation confirms DocVXQA's superiority, scoring 4.49/5 on context-awareness and 3.56/5 on clarity versus 3.97 and 3.02 for the best baseline (ColPali+Pix2Struct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unmasked Pix2Struct upper bound (56% ACC, 0.68 ANLS on DocVQA), DocVXQA at threshold 0.70 retains 38% ACC and 0.54 ANLS while covering only 23% of the image, demonstrating that the learned mask preserves most predictive utility. Compared to ColPali+Pix2Struct (the strongest baseline), DocVXQA matches accuracy on DocVQA (38% vs 38%) but improves ANLS from 0.50 to 0.54 at the same pixel ratio; on PFL-DocVQA the gap widens dramatically, with DocVXQA achieving 43% ACC and 0.66 ANLS versus 28% and 0.39 for ColPali+Pix2Struct. All post-hoc methods—Raw Attention, Attention Rollout, and Grad-CAM—collapse under masking: at threshold 0.25, Raw Attention drops to 7% ACC on DocVQA, and Grad-CAM at 0.50 yields only 1% ACC, confirming that these explanations are too noisy or imprecise to support the model's reasoning when applied as input masks. In the human preference study, DocVXQA was preferred over ColPali+Pix2Struct in 64.7% of trials (95% CI [58.4%, 70.6%], p < 0.001), with all 12 participants favoring DocVXQA overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-explainable deep learning paradigm (Choi et al., 2024; Møller et al., 2024) to the DocVQA domain, where prior work has relied almost exclusively on post-hoc attention visualization or gradient-based saliency maps. It builds on Pix2Struct as the DocVQA backbone and incorporates ColPali as an external prior to regularize mask learning, departing from purely internal attribution methods. The information bottleneck formulation (Tishby & Zaslavsky, 2015) is repurposed here to jointly optimize sufficiency (cross-entropy on masked input) and minimality (L1 + total variation on the mask), a combination not previously applied to document understanding. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, demonstrated with both Pix2Struct and Donut backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DocVQA systems are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare, law) where black-box predictions erode user trust, yet no prior method provided built-in, context-aware visual justifications for document answers. The ablation revealing that minimality alone causes catastrophic answer-overfitting (accuracy drops from 0.51 to 0.19) and that an external retrieval prior is essential to recover contextual coverage is a practically useful finding for anyone designing explainable VLMs. Limitations include a modest performance gap versus the unmasked model, evaluation on only two datasets with a base-size backbone, and reliance on a specific ColPali prior whose availability and quality may constrain deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-page or reasoning-heavy DocVQA settings and whether the information bottleneck trade-off can be made adaptive per question complexity.
