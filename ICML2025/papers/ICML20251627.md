## Problem

Data-driven protein conformation generators (e.g., diffusion models trained on PDB or MD trajectories) produce structurally valid candidates but do not explicitly model thermodynamic properties, so they fail to match the Boltzmann distribution over conformational states. Directly optimizing energy-based objectives is intractable due to the partition function over all possible conformations, and existing amortized sampling methods do not scale to proteins with thousands of atoms. The paper asks how to efficiently incorporate fine-grained force-field feedback into a generative model so that it learns to balance conformational states according to their physical energy differences.

## Input / Output

The model takes an amino acid sequence and its multiple sequence alignment (MSA) as conditioning, encoded via the AlphaFold3 MSA Module and PairFormer into residue-level embeddings. During alignment training, K reference structures sharing the same sequence are sampled from the dataset and their potential energies are pre-computed off-policy using OpenMM with the CHARMM36 force field and GBn2 implicit solvation. The output is an ensemble of all-atom 3D coordinates (heavy atoms only, excluding hydrogens) for the full protein, with 250 conformations sampled per test target at inference time using a 20-step denoising schedule with random rigid transformations at each step.

## Method

The core contribution is Energy-based Alignment (EBA), a physics-informed objective that aligns a diffusion model's learned distribution with the target Boltzmann distribution by exploiting the Boltzmann factor (probability ratios determined by energy differences ΔE) within a stochastic mini-batch of K conformational states, thereby circumventing the intractable partition function. For diffusion models, the energy is defined as the sum of per-timestep KL divergences between the forward posterior and the learned reverse step; Jensen's inequality on the log-sum-exp then yields a tractable upper bound where the denoising error ‖ε − ε_θ(x_t, t, c)‖² replaces the KL term, weighted by Boltzmann factors computed from physical energies. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on ATLAS MD trajectories with an SE(3)-invariant loss (Kabsch-aligned MSE + Smooth LDDT), followed by EBA alignment using a much smaller learning rate (1×10⁻⁷) and gradient accumulation. The paper further shows that DPO is a special case of EBA (K=2, β→∞), unifying preference-based alignment under the same Boltzmann-weighted framework, and introduces a length-dependent temperature rescaling (β ∝ 1/L^0.5) to stabilize training across proteins of varying sizes.

## Results

On the ATLAS MD ensemble benchmark (N=250, 250 test targets), EBA achieves state-of-the-art performance across nearly all metrics: pairwise RMSD correlation r=0.62, global RMSF r=0.71, per-target RMSF r=0.90, root mean W₂-distance 2.43, MD PCA W₂-distance 1.19, and exposed residue Jaccard 0.70. EBA outperforms the strongest baseline AlphaFLOW-MD (Full) by 29% on pairwise RMSD correlation (0.62 vs 0.48) and by 40% on exposed residue Jaccard (0.70 vs 0.50), while running 78× faster (0.9 vs 70 GPU sec/sample). The EBA objective also improves over its DPO variant (EBA-DPO) on 7 of 13 metrics, confirming that explicit energy-difference weighting beyond binary preferences provides additional signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBA outperforms AlphaFLOW-MD (Full) on 11 of 13 metrics, with the largest gains in exposed residue Jaccard (0.70 vs 0.50, +40%) and exposed MI matrix Spearman ρ (0.36 vs 0.25, +44%), while matching it on PC-sim > 0.5 (both 44%) and being 78× faster (0.9 vs 70 GPU sec/sample). Against MDGen, EBA leads on all comparable metrics (e.g., pairwise RMSD 0.62 vs 0.48, MD PCA W₂ 1.19 vs 1.89, exposed residue 0.70 vs 0.29), though MDGen is 4.5× faster at inference (0.2 vs 0.9 sec). EBA also consistently improves over its own EBA-DPO variant (K=2, binary preference) on 7 metrics including pairwise RMSD (0.62 vs 0.59), global RMSF (0.71 vs 0.69), PC-sim (44 vs 38), and exposed residue (0.70 vs 0.68), demonstrating that the multi-state energy-weighted objective captures richer thermodynamic structure than pairwise win/lose signals. Compared to the pre-trained (unfine-tuned) AlphaFold3 diffusion module, EBA yields dramatic gains across the board (e.g., exposed MI ρ: 0.36 vs 0.01; weak contacts J: 0.65 vs 0.23), confirming that both supervised fine-tuning and physical alignment are necessary. MSA subsampling baselines (AlphaFold2 with 32–256 MSA rows) are substantially weaker on all metrics, with the best variant (256 rows) reaching only 0.15 pairwise RMSD and 0.33 exposed residue Jaccard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AlphaFold3 all-atom diffusion architecture (Abramson et al., 2024) as its generative backbone and extends the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) alignment framework (Rafailov et al., 2024; Wallace et al., 2024) from binary preference pairs to a multi-state, Boltzmann-weighted objective (EBA) that directly encodes physical energy differences. Conceptually, EBA unifies the RLHF alignment paradigm with statistical mechanics: the Boltzmann factor between conformational states replaces the learned reward model, and the intractable partition function is circumvented by restricting to stochastic mini-batches of K states. The paper also draws on prior protein conformation generation work (AlphaFlow, MDGen, ConfDiff, Boltzmann generators) as baselines and on SE(3)-invariant structural losses (Kabsch alignment, Smooth LDDT) to make the denoising objective geometry-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EBA provides a principled, scalable mechanism for injecting physical energy feedback into generative models without requiring intractable partition function computation, which is a key bottleneck for equilibrium sampling in molecular systems. The notably large improvements in exposed-residue Jaccard similarity (0.70 vs. 0.50 for AlphaFlow) and exposed MI matrix correlation (0.36 vs. 0.25) suggest that energy-weighted alignment captures collective long-range dynamics that purely data-driven training misses. However, the approach is limited to single-chain proteins, relies on classical force fields (CHARMM36) whose accuracy is well below quantum-level, and the base AlphaFold3 architecture was designed for folding rather than long-timescale dynamics. Open questions include whether EBA extends to multi-chain complexes, whether quantum or semi-empirical energy evaluations (DFT, GFN2-xTB) would further improve alignment, and whether the framework transfers to other generative model families beyond diffusion.
