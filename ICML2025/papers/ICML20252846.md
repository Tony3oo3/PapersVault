## Problem

Existing one-shot federated learning methods that leverage diffusion models require deploying foundation models (e.g., CLIP, BLIP) on client devices or training diffusion models locally, imposing heavy computational and communication burdens that limit practicality and prevent support for heterogeneous client architectures. This paper asks whether diffusion-model-based OSFL can be achieved using only standard locally trained client models, with no additional client-side infrastructure beyond what traditional FL already requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252846/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input locally trained client classifier models of arbitrary architecture (ResNet, MobileNet, VGG, ShuffleNet, etc.) uploaded in a single communication round, a pre-trained text-conditioned diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5/v2.1 or LDM) residing on the server, and category labels. It produces a synthetic labeled image dataset on the server (30 images per class per client by default) and an aggregated classification model (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50) trained on that synthetic data. Notably, the client models also contribute their batch-normalization layer statistics (per-layer means and variances) as additional guidance signals, and no download communication is required since no foundation model is deployed on clients.

## Method

FedLMG uses locally trained client models as classifier-guidance signals for a server-side diffusion model, combining cross-entropy loss (for category alignment) with a novel BN loss that matches the per-layer mean and variance statistics of the client model's batch-normalization layers (for contextual/style alignment). A key practical insight is that gradients are computed on the predicted clean image x̂₀ₜ (obtained by inverting the diffusion schedule) rather than the noisy xₜ, since client models are not trained on noised inputs. The theoretical contribution proves that the KL divergence between the synthetic data distribution and the client's local distribution is bounded by a constant plus the negative log-likelihood of the client model, which is minimized by cross-entropy training. Three aggregation strategies (fine-tuning, multi-teacher distillation, and specific-teacher distillation) are provided to train the final model on the synthetic dataset, with specific-teacher distillation performing best under feature distribution skew and multi-teacher distillation excelling under heterogeneous model structures.

## Results

Under feature distribution skew, FedLMG_SD achieves 58.86% average accuracy on OpenImage, surpassing the centralized-training ceiling of 56.03%, and 75.96% on Unique NICO++ and 58.16% on Common NICO++. FedLMG_MD achieves the best DomainNet average of 35.99%, approaching the 36.89% ceiling. Across all four dataset splits, FedLMG variants consistently outperform every baseline by 2–9 percentage points in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLMG outperforms all traditional FL baselines (FedAvg, FedDF, FedProx, FedDyn) by 4–9 points in average accuracy across every dataset, with the largest gap on Common NICO++ (+9.05 over FedAvg). Against the two diffusion-based OSFL baselines, FedLMG_SD beats FedDISC by 5.73 points on OpenImage and 4.52 on Common NICO++, and beats FGL by 5.48 on OpenImage and 3.37 on Common NICO++. Notably, FedLMG_SD exceeds the centralized-training ceiling on OpenImage (58.86 vs. 56.03), and on individual clients (e.g., client5: 59.90 vs. 51.38 ceiling), demonstrating that the diffusion model's prior knowledge can augment beyond what the client data alone provides. The 'Prompts Only' ablation (text-prompt generation without client models) lags FedLMG by 10–17 points, confirming that the locally trained client models are essential for distribution-matched generation. Under heterogeneous client models (Table 11), FedLMG_MD further widens its lead, reaching 81.07% on Unique NICO++ versus 76.21% for FGL, while FedAvg/FedProx/FedDyn are inapplicable due to parameter-averaging incompatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-guided diffusion (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) into the one-shot federated learning setting, but departs from prior DM-based FL methods (FedDISC, FGL, FedDEO) that require deploying foundation models like CLIP or BLIP on client devices. Instead, FedLMG repurposes standard locally-trained CNNs (ResNet, MobileNet, VGG) as the classifier guidance signal, augmented with a novel BN-layer statistics loss to capture contextual distribution features beyond category identity. The theoretical contribution—a KL divergence bound between the conditional DM distribution and client data distribution—provides formal grounding absent in prior empirical DM-FL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in client computation (3.64G vs. 334.73G FLOPS compared to FedDISC) and zero download cost make one-shot FL practically deployable on resource-constrained devices, addressing a key barrier to real-world adoption. The ability to handle heterogeneous client architectures (different model structures per client) broadens applicability beyond the homogeneous-model assumption of most FL methods. Open questions include scalability to very large numbers of clients with diverse domains, sensitivity to the quality of the pre-trained DM's coverage of client distributions, and whether the BN-loss guidance generalizes to non-CNN architectures or non-image modalities.
