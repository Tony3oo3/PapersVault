## Problem

Higher-order (hypergraph) interactions are prevalent in real-world systems, but annotating them is often infeasible, and existing hypergraph predictors are either restricted to transductive settings, produce only soft/weighted structures, or require heavy regularization to enforce sparsity. No general, end-to-end differentiable method exists that infers a discrete hypergraph structure unsupervised from point-wise observations while remaining compatible with arbitrary hypergraph decoders and applicable to both inductive and transductive tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250571/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes node feature vectors as input: for trajectory prediction, the (x,y) spatial coordinates over the first T observed timesteps (augmented with velocity for NBA); for 3D shape classification, GVCNN, MVCNN, or combined visual features. It produces a discrete binary incidence matrix (N×M) specifying exactly which k nodes belong to each of M hyperedges, which is then consumed by a standard hypergraph neural network to yield node-level predictions (future positions or class labels). The hypergraph is a hard, sparse object rather than a soft probability matrix, making it directly compatible with any existing hypergraph architecture.

## Method

SPHINX decomposes hypergraph inference into two differentiable stages: (1) a sequential slot-attention module that predicts hyperedge membership probabilities one hyperedge at a time, where at each step the node features are enriched with a binary history vector encoding which previously predicted hyperedges each node belongs to, thereby resolving the symmetry ambiguity that causes simultaneous slot prediction to collapse; and (2) a constrained k-subset sampler (SIMPLE or AIMLE) that discretizes each probability distribution into exactly k selected nodes per hyperedge while preserving gradient flow. The resulting discrete hypergraph is fed into a pluggable hypergraph decoder (e.g., AllDeepSets, HGNN, HCHA) for the final prediction. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end using only the downstream task loss, requiring no auxiliary sparsity or smoothness regularization, and the k-cardinality constraint stabilizes optimization compared to unconstrained Gumbel-Softmax sampling.

## Results

On the NBA SportVU inductive trajectory prediction task, SPHINX achieves minADE20/minFDE20 of 0.30/0.43 (1s), 0.59/0.94 (2s), 0.88/1.38 (3s), and 1.16/1.74 (4s), outperforming the best graph-based method (EvolveGraph: 0.35/0.48 at 1s) and the best hypergraph baseline (GroupNet: 0.34/0.48 at 1s). On transductive 3D shape classification, SPHINX reaches 94.67% on NTU (GV+MV features) and 98.92% on ModelNet40, surpassing the strongest dynamic baseline TDHNN* (92.70% and 98.69% respectively). On the synthetic Particle Simulation, SPHINX achieves 90% overlap with ground-truth hyperedges, compared to 67% for TDHNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHINX outperforms all 17 baselines across both inductive and transductive settings. On the NBA inductive task, SPHINX beats the strongest graph method (EvolveGraph) by 0.05 minADE20 at 1s and the closest hypergraph competitor (GroupNet) by 0.04 minADE20 at 1s, with the gap widening at longer horizons (e.g., 0.48 minFDE20 advantage over GroupNet at 4s). TDHNN, which uses top-k selection for sparsity, is substantially outperformed (0.68 vs 0.30 minADE20 at 1s), and the paper notes TDHNN's hyperedges collapse to include all nodes. On transductive NTU, SPHINX (94.67%) surpasses TDHNN* (92.70%) by ~2% and all static methods (best: HGNN+ at 84.18%) by over 10%. On ModelNet40, SPHINX (98.92%) edges out TDHNN* (98.69%) by a narrow margin. On the synthetic Particle Simulation, SPHINX achieves 90% hyperedge overlap with ground truth versus 67% for TDHNN, and its single-trajectory ADE/FDE (0.59/0.92 at 1s) beats both GroupNet (0.65/1.03) and TDHNN (0.68/1.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHINX sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural relational inference (NRI, fNRI, dNRI) which learns graph structure from point-wise observations, (2) slot attention (Locatello et al., 2020) repurposed here as a soft clustering mechanism for hyperedge discovery, and (3) differentiable k-subset sampling (SIMPLE, AIMLE, IMLE) adopted as the discretization step. The key departure from prior hypergraph predictors (EvolveHypergraph, TDHNN, GroupNet) is the combination of sequential slot attention with constrained k-subset sampling, which eliminates the need for Gumbel-Softmax, top-k selection, or auxiliary regularization losses while producing a truly discrete structure compatible with any hypergraph neural network decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: higher-order structure is rarely annotated in real data, yet many systems (neuroscience, chemistry, multi-agent dynamics) exhibit group interactions. SPHINX provides a general, plug-and-play module for inferring such structure end-to-end from downstream task signal alone, without architecture-specific modifications. Key limitations include the fixed maximum number of hyperedges M and fixed cardinality k (restricting to k-regular hyperedges), and the prediction of a single static hypergraph per trajectory rather than a time-evolving one. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger node sets, whether variable or learned cardinalities can be incorporated without domain knowledge, and whether the sequential prediction strategy generalizes beyond the small-scale experiments presented.
