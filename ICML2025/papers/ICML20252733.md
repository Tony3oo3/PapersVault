## Problem

Existing trust guarantees for AI/ML (memorization, data poisoning, copyright) rely on Input-Independent Indistinguishability (III), which enforces worst-case indistinguishability across all possible input pairs. This is unnecessarily conservative when only indistinguishability relative to a specific set of safe reference datasets is needed, leading to heavy utility losses that scale with output dimensionality. The paper asks whether a data-specific relaxation can provide provable trust guarantees with significantly sharper utility-trust tradeoffs.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a training dataset U, m reference subsets R_{[1:m]} (each excluding specific data from U), a processing function F (which may be a black-box optimizer), and per-reference security budgets γ_{[1:m]}. It outputs a multivariate Gaussian noise vector e ∈ ℝ^d whose covariance Σ(U) is anisotropic and data-dependent, computed by solving a convex optimization over only m Lagrangian multipliers. The final model is F(U) + e, and the noise scale is independent of the ambient dimensionality d, scaling only as O(√min{m,d}).

## Method

The core insight is that any f-divergence between two Gaussians sharing a covariance is a monotone function of their Mahalanobis distance (Lemma 3.1), so the DSI constraints reduce to bounding ‖z_i‖²_{Σ⁻¹} for each reference difference z_i = F(R_i) − F(U). The optimal noise is confined to the rank-m subspace spanned by {z_i}, and the covariance is parameterized as Σ(λ) = (Σᵢ λᵢ xᵢᵀxᵢ)^{1/2} where λ ∈ ℝ₊^m are Lagrange multipliers found via projected gradient descent (Theorem 3.3, Algorithm 1). For deep learning, Algorithm 2 applies this per-round with a black-box optimizer (e.g., local SGD without clipping), using multiplicative composition under α-divergence (Theorem 3.4) to allocate per-round budgets from a global (ε,δ) guarantee. This yields noise of Õ(√T/(√n·ε)) in l₂-norm, which is O(√(d/n)) smaller than DP-SGD's isotropic noise and independent of model dimensionality.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20 (0.3M params), DSI-Local-SGD achieves 83.9% test accuracy at ε=8, δ=10⁻⁵, outperforming the best DP-SGD baseline (76.0%) by 7.9 points. For the larger WideResNet-16 (2.6M params), DSI-Local-SGD reaches 87.1% at ε=8 versus 79.5% for the strongest DP-SGD baseline, a 7.6-point gap that widens with model scale. In memorization experiments on WikiText-5, DSI guarantees (ε=2, δ=10⁻⁵) reduce (v=10, c=4) exposure from 16.0 to 2.4 for GPT2-small while maintaining comparable perplexity (~22.8 vs 20.5). For backdoor defense on poisoned CIFAR-10, DSI significantly reduces Attack Success Rate compared to training without guarantees, with the gap increasing as the number of data sources m grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSI-Local-SGD consistently outperforms all four DP-SGD baselines on CIFAR-10, with the advantage growing for larger models and tighter privacy budgets. Against Xiao et al. (2023b) (standard DP-SGD with clipping), DSI gains 8.1 points at ε=2 and 7.9 points at ε=8 on ResNet-20, and 11.2 points at ε=2 on WideResNet-16. Against Yu et al. (2021) (gradient embedding perturbation), DSI wins at ε≥2 (e.g., 71.2% vs 70.1% at ε=4 on ResNet-20) but trails slightly at ε=1 (57.4% vs 59.7%). Against De et al. (2022) (DP-SGD at scale) and Bao et al. (2023) (DP-Mix), DSI achieves the largest margins on WideResNet-16: 11.2 points over De et al. at ε=2 and 7.3 points over Bao et al. at ε=8. The paper notes that the dimensionality-independent noise scaling of DSI (O(√T/√n·ε) vs O(√dT/n·ε) for DP-SGD) explains the widening gap as model parameters d increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the indistinguishability lineage from Shannon's perfect secrecy through Differential Privacy (DP) and DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) by introducing Data-Specific Indistinguishability (DSI) as a principled relaxation of Input-Independent Indistinguishability (III). The key conceptual departure is that input independence—essential for confidentiality protection in DP—is not necessary for trust applications like memorization mitigation, backdoor defense, or copyright control, where only indistinguishability relative to specific safe reference outputs is required. This enables data-dependent, anisotropic Gaussian noise whose optimal form is determined by a convex optimization over m Lagrange multipliers (where m is the number of references), fundamentally decoupling noise scale from output dimensionality d. The framework also subsumes and generalizes prior work on per-instance privacy (Wang, 2019; Thudi et al., 2024) and the k-NAF copyright framework (Vyas et al., 2023) under a single mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSI framework provides a unified, provable approach to multiple trust concerns (memorization, poisoning, copyright) that were previously addressed by ad-hoc, task-specific methods, and its dimensionality-independent noise (scaling as O(√min{m,d}) rather than O(√d)) makes high-dimensional trustworthy training practically feasible where DP-SGD's isotropic noise becomes prohibitive. However, the authors acknowledge that provable DSI bounds remain conservative relative to empirical performance, and a key open direction is incorporating amplification effects from subsampling and other inherent randomness sources. Broader open questions include generalizing the 'influence' notion to fairness and other trustworthiness concepts, and whether the framework can handle truly black-box (non-iterative) processing functions end-to-end, which the authors show is computationally intractable for deep learning due to sensitivity to random seeds.
