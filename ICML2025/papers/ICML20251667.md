## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers are limited to meshes of up to ~400k–700k points, far below the million-scale geometries required by industrial CFD simulations (e.g., car and aircraft design). When scaling Transolver to million-scale inputs, two bottlenecks emerge: slice weights degenerate toward uniformity (causing attention to collapse into average pooling), and feedforward layers over million-scale point representations exhaust single-GPU memory.

## Input / Output

The model consumes unstructured 3D meshes of up to 2.5 million points, where each point carries spatial coordinates, surface normal vectors, and task-specific input fields (e.g., geometry structure, velocity, porous-medium permeability). It outputs the full physical field on the mesh (pressure, velocity, stress, density) for both surface and surrounding volume regions, from which drag and lift coefficients are derived via surface integrals. Unlike prior work that subsamples to ~50k points, Transolver++ processes the complete mesh directly, with the parallelism framework distributing points across GPUs.

## Method

Transolver++ augments Transolver's Physics-Attention with two mechanisms to learn 'eidetic' (distinguishable) physical states: a local adaptive temperature (Ada-Temp) that learns a per-point scalar to modulate softmax sharpness, and a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization that makes the discrete point-to-state assignment differentiable while encouraging peaked, non-uniform slice weights. For computation, the paper designs a parallelism framework exploiting the structure of state aggregation: each GPU independently computes partial weighted sums (numerator and denominator) over its local points, then only two AllReduce operations of size O(MC) and O(M) synchronize the results, making communication volume invariant to input mesh size N. Additionally, removing a redundant projection (f) halves memory usage, pushing single-GPU capacity to 1.2 million points. The model is trained end-to-end with supervised relative-L2 loss on pre-collected CFD simulation data.

## Results

On six standard PDE benchmarks (Elasticity, Plasticity, Airfoil, Pipe, NS2D, Darcy), Transolver++ achieves the lowest relative L2 error across all tasks, yielding a 13% average relative improvement over the dataset-specific second-best baseline (e.g., 0.0052 vs. 0.0069 on Elasticity, 0.0719 vs. 0.0830 on NS2D). On million-scale industrial datasets, Transolver++ delivers over 20% average improvement: 11.0% and 12.6% on DrivAerNet++ Full volume and surface fields, 24.1% on DrivAerNet++ Surface, and 30.4% on AirCraft surface error. For design-oriented metrics, drag coefficient error drops by 41.0% (0.036 vs. 0.061) and lift coefficient error by 36.3% (0.014 vs. 0.037) relative to Transolver. Inference on a new simulation case takes under 1 second, compared to 5–6 hours for the traditional UnsCFD solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Transolver++ achieves state-of-the-art on all eight evaluated datasets, outperforming 22 baselines spanning neural operators, Transformers, and GNNs. On the six standard benchmarks, the closest competitors are LNO (best on Elasticity at 0.0069, Airfoil at 0.0053, Pipe at 0.0031, NS2D at 0.0830) and OFormer (best on Plasticity at 0.0017); Transolver++ beats each by 10–19% relative L2. On industrial million-scale datasets, Transolver (the prior SOTA) is the nearest competitor, but Transolver++ surpasses it by 11–30% on field errors and 36–41% on coefficient errors, with R² reaching 0.997–0.999. GNN-based methods (GraphSAGE, PointNet, Graph U-Net, MeshGraphNet, GNO) degrade severely on large meshes due to geometric instability, with surface errors 2–7× higher than Transolver++. Geo-FNO performs worst on industrial tasks (0.892 surface error on DrivAerNet++ Full) because mapping irregular million-point meshes to uniform latent grids becomes intractable at scale. GNOT and LNO, while competitive on standard benchmarks, still trail Transolver++ by 10–25% on industrial surface fields, confirming that the eidetic-state mechanism and parallelism framework are essential for million-scale physics learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Transolver++ directly extends Transolver (Wu et al., ICML 2024), which introduced Physics-Attention to group mesh points into a small set of learned physical states for PDE solving. The key departures are twofold: (1) an 'eidetic states' mechanism combining Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a pointwise adaptive temperature to prevent the slice weights from degenerating into uniform distributions at million-scale, and (2) a parallelism framework that exploits the physics-state structure to achieve O(MC) cross-GPU communication—completely invariant to input mesh size—contrasting sharply with O(N) or O(N²) overheads of tensor parallelism, Ring Attention, and DeepSpeed-Ulysses. The paper also introduces a new AirCraft dataset of ~300K-point 3D aircraft meshes, filling a gap in high-fidelity aerodynamic benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural PDE solver to handle million-scale meshes (2.5M points) with linear GPU scaling, opening a path toward practical surrogate models for industrial CFD (car and aircraft design) that previously required days of traditional simulation. The input-size-invariant communication cost of the parallelism framework is a genuinely useful systems contribution that could inform other structured-attention architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even larger geometries (10M+), whether the small training sets (140–200 cases) limit robustness, and whether the foundation-model vision (scaling data and parameters) holds at the scale needed for production use.
