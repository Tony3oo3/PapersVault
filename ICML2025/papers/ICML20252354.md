## Problem

Existing concept-based interpretability methods analyze a single model in isolation, and prior approaches to identifying universal features across models (e.g., Rosetta Neurons, Rosetta Concepts) operate post-hoc by extracting concepts independently and then matching them through compute-intensive filtering or optimization. This post-hoc paradigm is limited in scalability, lacks the efficiencies of gradient-based training, and precludes direct translation between models within a unified concept space, making tasks like coordinated activation maximization across multiple networks cumbersome.

## Input / Output

The method consumes final-layer activations (768-dimensional patch tokens) from three pretrained vision models—DinoV2, SigLIP, and ViT—on ImageNet, with class tokens removed and DinoV2 tokens interpolated to 16×16 patch size for consistency. Activations are standardized per-model using 1000 random training samples to mitigate scale differences. The output is a shared 6144-dimensional sparse code space (8× expansion factor) that reconstructs any model's activations via model-specific decoders, enabling concept heatmaps, cross-model R² reconstruction scores, and coordinated activation maximization images that visualize the same concept across all models simultaneously.

## Method

USAEs extend the TopK Sparse Autoencoder by replacing the single encoder-decoder pair with M model-specific encoders that all project into one shared overcomplete concept space Z, paired with M model-specific decoders. At each training iteration, a single model's encoder is randomly selected to encode a batch of its activations into Z, and Z is then decoded through every model's decoder; the universal loss aggregates Frobenius-norm reconstruction errors across all M models, and backpropagation updates only the selected encoder and its associated decoder. This design enforces concept alignment end-to-end during training rather than relying on post-hoc matching, while keeping memory and compute costs manageable by updating one encoder-decoder pair per step. The encoder uses a linear layer with batch normalization and ReLU, the decoder is a plain dictionary matrix, and the standard l2 reconstruction loss is replaced with an l1 loss for improved interpretability. A key application, Coordinated Activation Maximization, optimizes one input per model to simultaneously maximize the same concept dimension k across all aligned models, revealing how each model encodes the same universal concept.

## Results

USAEs achieve positive cross-model R² reconstruction scores (off-diagonal entries in Fig. 5), confirming that the shared concept space captures features transferable across DinoV2, SigLIP, and ViT. In the concept consistency analysis (Fig. 7), USAE-discovered concepts show 23–38% cosine-similarity overlap (>0.5) with independently trained TopK SAEs per model, far exceeding the random baseline (AUC 0.13, 0% overlap). The correlation between concept co-firing frequency and reconstruction energy reaches r=0.89 for high-frequency concepts (>1000 co-fires), indicating that the most important concepts are also the most universal. OOD generalization to DTD and CelebA (Table 1) shows consistent R² scores (e.g., DinoV2: 0.77→0.69→0.75) and stable co-firing trends, confirming the learned concept space is not overfit to ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between USAE-discovered concepts and those from independently trained TopK SAEs (Gao et al., 2024) on each model. USAE outperforms the random baseline (AUC 0.13, 0% overlap) by a wide margin across all three models, with ViT showing the strongest alignment (AUC 0.41, 38% of concepts matching) and SigLIP the weakest (AUC 0.30, 23%). This gap indicates that universal training preserves meaningful concept structure rather than learning arbitrary representations. However, the 23–38% overlap also means that 62–77% of USAE concepts are *new* shared representations not discovered by independent single-model SAE training, suggesting the joint objective surfaces cross-model structure that isolated training misses. For the top 1,000 co-firing (most universal) concepts, consistency increases further, with DinoV2 reaching an average cosine similarity of 0.65, ViT 0.52, and SigLIP 0.40, confirming that the most universal concepts are the best preserved under independent training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TopK Sparse Autoencoder (Gao et al., 2024) from a single-model to a multi-model setting by introducing a joint training objective that enforces a shared sparse concept dictionary across multiple architectures simultaneously. It departs from prior universal-feature mining approaches (Rosetta Neurons, Rosetta Concepts) that operate post-hoc—extracting concepts independently from each model and then matching them via compute-intensive filtering—by instead imposing concept alignment during end-to-end gradient-based training. The coordinated activation maximization application builds on the Maco optimization strategy (Fel et al., 2023a) but extends it to jointly maximize a shared concept dimension across all aligned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USAEs offer a practical, gradient-based alternative to post-hoc cross-model concept matching, enabling tasks like coordinated activation maximization that are cumbersome under existing pipelines. The finding that the most important concepts are also the most universal (r=0.89 correlation for high-frequency concepts) provides a useful heuristic for prioritizing interpretability efforts. However, the current scope is limited to three vision models at a single layer depth, and scaling to more models, deeper layers, or other modalities remains an open question. The observation that universal training discovers shared representations absent from independently trained SAEs suggests the joint objective itself shapes what concepts emerge, raising questions about whether these are genuinely 'universal' or artifacts of the shared reconstruction pressure.
