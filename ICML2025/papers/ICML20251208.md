## Problem

In offline RL, ReLU-based Q-networks exhibit linear extrapolation beyond the data range, causing Q-value overestimation for out-of-distribution actions that lie outside the convex hull of in-distribution samples. While layer normalization (LN) provides a bound on Q-function outputs, it does not actively reduce Q-values in the out-of-data region, leaving the extrapolation error unresolved without online interaction. The paper identifies the need for mechanisms that not only bound but also guide a gradual decrease of Q-values beyond the data range.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard offline (s, a, r, s') tuples from D4RL datasets and, during online fine-tuning, additionally ingests environment interactions with a configurable offline/online data mix ratio. A key design choice is the extension of the action space to include an infeasible region A_I = R^n \ A_F, from which actions are sampled uniformly at a distance of 100–1000× the feasible boundary for the penalty term. The reward is multiplied by a scaling factor c_reward (ranging from 5 to 10,000 depending on the domain) before entering the TD target, and the output is a policy that transitions seamlessly from offline training to online fine-tuning without architectural changes.

## Method

PARS builds on the TD3+BC actor-critic framework and introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Reward Scaling with Layer Normalization (RS-LN) multiplies rewards by c_reward while applying LN to the Q-network; this increases the output scale, which—combined with LN's confinement of inputs to the unit sphere—reduces the neural tangent kernel (NTK) similarity between in-distribution and OOD actions, so that positive gradient updates from the data region have weaker influence on OOD Q-values, naturally suppressing them. Second, Penalizing Infeasible Actions (PA) adds a squared-error loss that drives Q-values in a distant infeasible action region toward Q_min, enforcing a downward trend at the convex hull boundary while a guard interval prevents interference with in-distribution estimates. The total critic loss is the standard TD loss plus α·PA loss, and a theoretical analysis proves the resulting PARS Bellman operator is a contraction under the infinity norm, guaranteeing convergence. The reward scaling also increases network expressivity by reducing the dormant neuron ratio from ~90% to ~55%, enabling faster adaptation during online fine-tuning.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (28 datasets across AntMaze, Adroit, and MuJoCo), PARS achieves average normalized scores of 81.6 (AntMaze), 79.9 (Adroit), and 77.3 (MuJoCo) in offline training, outperforming the best prior baselines (MSG/ReBRAC at 60.6, ReBRAC at 75.0, and SAC-N at 76.3, respectively). In online fine-tuning (300K steps), PARS reaches 95.2, 113.2, and 111.6 on the three domains, surpassing the strongest baseline RLPD (78.4, 102.5, 96.2). Most notably, on the extremely challenging AntMaze Ultra datasets, PARS achieves 66.4 (ultra-play) and 51.4 (ultra-diverse) offline, compared to the best prior scores of 22.4 and 14.2, and is the only algorithm to successfully complete offline-to-online training on these tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In offline training, PARS achieves the best average score across all three domains: AntMaze (81.6 vs. MSG/ReBRAC at 60.6), Adroit (79.9 vs. ReBRAC at 75.0), and MuJoCo (77.3 vs. SAC-N at 76.3). The most dramatic gains appear on AntMaze Ultra, where PARS scores 66.4 and 51.4 versus the best prior baselines of 22.4 (ReBRAC) and 14.2 (IQL), representing roughly 3× and 3.6× improvements. In online fine-tuning (300K steps), PARS outperforms all 8 baselines on nearly every dataset, with domain averages of 95.2 (AntMaze), 113.2 (Adroit), and 111.6 (MuJoCo) versus the strongest competitor RLPD at 78.4, 102.5, and 96.2. PARS is the only algorithm to achieve strong performance on AntMaze ultra-play (86.5), ultra-diverse (86.4), and Adroit relocate-cloned (53.8) in the online setting, where all other baselines score below 41. PARS slightly underperforms SAC-N on a few individual MuJoCo random-policy tasks (e.g., hopper-r: 25.4 vs. 31.3) and ReBRAC on pen-expert (152.7 vs. 154.1), but these are isolated cases within an otherwise dominant comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARS builds directly on TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) and augments it with two targeted modifications: reward scaling combined with layer normalization (RS-LN) and a penalty on infeasible actions (PA). Conceptually, it departs from prior critic regularization methods (CQL, SVR, MSG) that penalize OOD actions via density estimation or auxiliary behavior models, instead adopting a Q-network-centric perspective that uses NTK analysis to show reward scaling reduces gradient similarity between in-distribution and OOD actions. The use of LN for bounding Q-values follows Ball et al. (2023) and Yue et al. (2024), but the paper is the first to connect reward scaling to reduced NTK similarity and to link dormant neuron ratios (Sokar et al., 2023) to OOD mitigation in offline RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARS demonstrates that a minimal actor-critic algorithm with a few lines of additional code can simultaneously achieve SOTA across all three D4RL domains (AntMaze, Adroit, MuJoCo) in both offline training and online fine-tuning, outperforming complex goal-conditioned methods (HIQL, GC-IQL) and specialized offline-to-online approaches without domain-specific architectures. The NTK-based mechanistic explanation for why reward scaling suppresses OOD overestimation provides a principled understanding that could guide future regularization design. Key limitations include the assumption of positive target Q-values and reliance on critic ensembles for MuJoCo/Adroit; open questions include the optimal LN placement, activation function design compatible with RS-LN, and whether the approach extends to negative-reward-dominant tasks.
