## Problem

Existing two-stage 3D generation pipelines rely on a fixed, small number of views synthesized by multi-view diffusion models, which are often inconsistent or low quality, directly degrading the final 3D reconstruction. Moreover, reconstruction models in these pipelines are typically designed for a fixed number of views at fixed angles, preventing them from exploiting additional high-quality views when available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250854/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a single RGB image or text prompt and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation consisting of 1 million Gaussians sampled from a 100×100×100 tri-plane grid. The number of input views fed to the reconstructor is not fixed but determined dynamically by the view selection stage (ranging from 1 to 32 views at varying azimuth and elevation angles), with each view at 512×512 resolution. The entire reconstruction runs in under 0.5 seconds on a single A100 GPU with real-time rendering.

## Method

Flex3D is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 generates ~20 candidate views using two fine-tuned Emu diffusion models (one for four elevation angles, one for 16 azimuth views), then curates them via a DINO+SVM back-view quality classifier and Efficient LoFTR feature matching to retain only consistent, high-quality views. Stage 2, FlexRM, extends Instant3D by appending a 768-dimensional camera token to each view's 1024 image tokens for stronger camera conditioning, and bridges the tri-plane NeRF representation to 3D Gaussian Splatting via an MLP that predicts position offsets (constrained by tanh with α=3/4), color, opacity, rotation, and scale. A key training innovation is imperfect input view simulation: FlexRM's own reconstructed Gaussians are perturbed with parameter-specific noise (position, color, opacity, scale) and re-rendered to create noisy training views, teaching the model to tolerate residual multi-view inconsistencies without additional supervision signals.

## Results

On the 3D generation task (404 DreamFusion prompts), Flex3D achieves the highest CLIP text similarity (0.277) and VideoCLIP text similarity (0.255), outperforming the closest competitor GRM (0.268 / 0.253). In a user study with 5 evaluators over 40 prompt pairs, Flex3D achieves a win rate of 92.5%–100% against all baselines. On the GSO reconstruction benchmark, FlexRM with 32 input views reaches PSNR 26.77, SSIM 0.907, LPIPS 0.066, and Chamfer Distance 1.169×10⁻², surpassing GRM (4 views: PSNR 25.03, LPIPS 0.102) and InstantMesh (4 views: PSNR 21.33, LPIPS 0.133).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flex3D outperforms all seven baselines on both CLIP and VideoCLIP text similarity in the 3D generation task. The closest competitor is GRM (0.268 CLIP, 0.253 VideoCLIP), which Flex3D edges out by 0.009 and 0.002 respectively; InstantMesh is second-closest on CLIP (0.272) but trails more on VideoCLIP (0.236). In the user study, Flex3D wins against every baseline with a minimum win rate of 92.5% (vs. GRM) and a maximum of 100% (vs. OpenLRM). On the GSO reconstruction benchmark, FlexRM with just 4 input views already surpasses GRM in PSNR (25.55 vs. 25.03) and LPIPS (0.074 vs. 0.102), and substantially outperforms InstantMesh (PSNR 21.33, LPIPS 0.133). Scaling to 32 views further improves FlexRM to PSNR 26.77 and LPIPS 0.066, a gap no fixed-view baseline can match. The direct 3D diffusion methods (LN3Diff, 3DTopia-XL) trail the feed-forward approaches by a wider margin on text alignment (CLIP 0.252–0.254 vs. 0.277), suggesting that the two-stage generation-then-reconstruction paradigm with view curation remains more effective for text-to-3D fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flex3D builds directly on the Instant3D tri-plane transformer architecture and extends it with 3D Gaussian Splatting decoding, stronger camera conditioning, and support for an arbitrary number of input views. It departs from the standard two-stage feed-forward 3D generation pipeline (multi-view diffusion → fixed-view reconstruction) by introducing a view curation stage that generates a large candidate pool and selects the best subset, rather than relying on a small fixed set of potentially inconsistent synthesized views. The imperfect-input simulation strategy is conceptually related to feedback-loop methods like Cycle3D and IM-3D but operates at the input level with negligible computational overhead, avoiding multi-step refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view curation + flexible reconstruction pipeline is a practical, modular recipe that can be dropped into existing two-stage 3D generation systems to improve quality without architectural overhaul, making it likely to be widely adopted. The paper's own limitations (3DGS mesh extraction difficulty, tri-plane resolution constraints for large scenes) and the acknowledged failure cases (thin geometries, floaters) point to open problems in robustness. The authors also sketch a natural extension to 4D generation, suggesting the pipeline's design principles generalize beyond static object generation.
