## Problem

A single language instruction can correspond to many diverse video trajectories, creating a fundamental mismatch between the abstract simplicity of language and the detailed specificity of video. Existing approaches either refine language into finer-grained skills (constrained by semantic expressiveness and predefined skill libraries) or generate frame-by-frame video (computationally expensive and prone to error accumulation), but neither adequately bridges this modality gap for robust embodied decision-making.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB image frames (encoded by a CNN trained from scratch) and a natural language instruction (encoded by a frozen DistilBERT), and produces a sequence of robot actions (discrete or continuous). During inference, the agent operates in an observe-abstract-reason-act-reobserve loop: historical frames are scored and fused into dynamic representations, future dynamics are predicted autoregressively, and the action transformer conditions on both the predicted dynamics and accumulated state-action history to emit the next action step by step.

## Method

DynaMind replaces frame-by-frame video generation with reasoning over compact dynamic representations extracted via a three-module pipeline. First, an adaptive FrameScorer (2-layer FC network with sigmoid) assigns per-frame importance scores trained with a combined semantic-consistency loss (cosine alignment to language embeddings) and a visual-saliency loss (margin against high-variance frames), then a sliding-window weighted fusion (window size C) compresses the video into a short sequence of dynamic tokens. Second, a 1-layer 4-head autoregressive transformer with causal self-attention and cross-attention to language predicts future dynamic tokens from historical ones, trained with MSE. Third, an action transformer of the same architecture takes interleaved historical frame features, historical actions, and a goal token (the predicted future dynamic) to output the action sequence, using a hybrid assignment strategy that stochastically mixes predicted dynamics with ground-truth features during early training for stability. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with direct action supervision, avoiding the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm used by prior video-generation-based methods.

## Results

On LOReL Sawyer, DynaMind achieves a 53.67% average success rate across six manipulation tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline SkillDiffuser (43%) by over 10 percentage points. On Franka Kitchen (long-horizon 4-subtask tasks), DynaMind reaches 39.81% success rate versus 32.94% for GR-1 and 28.69% for LISA. On BabyAI navigation with only 1k training trajectories, DynaMind leads on all three task suites (GoToSeq 72.7%, SynthSeq 50.7%, BossLevel 52.3%), and in real-world Franka arm experiments it achieves 94% average success over five tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaMind outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against vanilla imitation learning methods, it surpasses Vanilla BC (29% → 53.67%) and Decision Transformer (15% → 53.67%) on LOReL Sawyer by large margins. Among language-decomposed methods, DynaMind beats LISA (40% → 53.67% on LOReL; 28.69% → 39.81% on Franka Kitchen) and SkillDiffuser (43% → 53.67% on LOReL), with the gap widening on instruction-generalization tests (53.73% vs. 39.71% for SkillDiffuser). For multimodal alignment methods evaluated on Franka Kitchen, DynaMind exceeds GR-1 (32.94% → 39.81%) and MT-R3M (30.50% → 39.81%), confirming that abstracting video into dynamic representations is more effective than enforcing direct video–language alignment. In real-world Franka arm trials, DynaMind (94%) also surpasses LISA (82%) and DT (80%), with the largest gains on the most complex task (fold towel: 80% vs. 50% for LISA).

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaMind departs from two dominant paradigms in language-conditioned embodied control: (1) video-generation-based methods (RoboDreamer, SkillDiffuser) that produce frame-by-frame future images as intermediate goals, and (2) language-decomposition methods (LISA) that refine underspecified instructions into finer-grained semantic skills. Instead of enriching the language side or generating full video, DynaMind inverts the abstraction direction—compressing video into compact 'dynamic representations' via an adaptive FrameScorer and reasoning over these abstractions with an autoregressive transformer. The architectural backbone (transformer blocks, DistilBERT language encoder, CNN image encoder) is standard, but the sliding-window importance-weighted fusion and the dynamic-guided action policy constitute the novel pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that abstracting video into a few high-level dynamic tokens can outperform both full video generation and language decomposition, while using roughly 8× fewer parameters than SkillDiffuser (7.84M vs. 60.29M), suggesting a more efficient path to language-conditioned robot control. The mutual-information analysis showing that combining DynaMind with LISA degrades performance is a useful negative result, indicating that language decomposition can introduce information loss rather than complementing video-centric reasoning. Open questions include whether fixed reasoning intervals can be replaced by learned adaptive triggers, whether the approach scales to multi-view or 3D observations, and whether the dynamic representation abstraction generalizes beyond tabletop manipulation to more open-ended embodied settings.
