## Problem

Visual deep RL agents suffer from low sample efficiency, forcing most pipelines to rely on sim-to-real transfer rather than training directly on physical robots. Two specific bottlenecks are underexplored: (1) standard MLP backbones experience gradient conflicts when handling multi-stage or multi-task robotic scenarios, and (2) existing perturbation-based exploration methods sample candidates from fixed random distributions that carry no task-relevant information.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes stacked RGB frames (84x84x3) captured at the beginning, midpoint, and end of the previous action from multiple cameras (wrist, side, and slot-specific views depending on the task), yielding 6-9 images per observation. The policy outputs a continuous end-effector delta pose with task-specific dimensionality (2-4 DOF), where each action updates the current pose by a small normalized increment; the reduced DOF relative to a full 6-DOF arm is a deliberate safety constraint for exploration from scratch without demonstrations.

## Method

MENTOR makes two architectural and optimization changes to a DrQ-v2-style SAC pipeline. First, it replaces the MLP policy backbone with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer: a CNN encoder maps visual inputs to a latent vector, a router computes logits over N experts, and the top-k experts' FFN outputs are combined via softmax-gated weights before an action projector; a negative-entropy load-balancing loss (weight 0.002) is added to the actor loss to prevent expert collapse. This dynamic routing lets different task stages or subtasks activate specialized experts, empirically raising gradient cosine similarity from negative (MLP) to consistently positive (MoE). Second, it introduces a task-oriented perturbation mechanism: a fixed-size buffer S_top (size 10) stores the weights of the highest-reward agents seen so far, and every T_p steps the current agent's weights are interpolated toward a candidate sampled from a Gaussian whose mean and standard deviation are computed over S_top, with the interpolation factor alpha still modulated by the dormant-ratio as in DrM. This replaces DrM's random Gaussian perturbation with a distribution that progressively accumulates task-relevant weight structure, yielding perturbation candidates whose standalone episode reward steadily increases during training rather than remaining at zero.

## Results

On three real-world robotic manipulation tasks (Peg Insertion, Cable Routing, Tabletop Golf), MENTOR achieves an average success rate of 83%, compared to 32% for the strongest baseline (DrM) within the same training time. In simulation, MENTOR outperforms all baselines across 12 tasks: achieving ~17% and ~10% higher episode rewards on DMC Dog Stand and Dog Walk, reducing training frames by 70% on MW Hammer (Sparse), and reaching near-100% success on Adroit tasks with ~23% less training time on the Door task. Ablation shows removing MoE drops performance by ~30% and switching to random perturbation causes an additional ~30% decline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MENTOR is compared against four model-free visual RL baselines: DrM (Xu et al., 2023), DrQ-v2 (Yarats et al., 2021), ALIX (Cetin et al., 2022), and TACO (Zheng et al., 2023). MENTOR decisively outperforms all four across all 12 simulation tasks and 3 real-world tasks. Against the strongest baseline DrM, MENTOR achieves 83% vs 32% average success on real-world tasks, ~17% higher reward on DMC Dog Stand, and 70% fewer training frames on the extremely sparse-reward MW Hammer task. DrQ-v2, ALIX, and TACO all trail DrM in most settings, with ALIX and TACO failing to reach competitive performance on Adroit tasks even after 6M frames. The ablation confirms both contributions are necessary: removing MoE (reverting to MLP) drops real-world success from 83% to ~62%, and replacing task-oriented perturbation with DrM's random perturbation further degrades performance to DrM's level.

## Relation to Prior Work

MENTOR builds directly on the DrQ-v2 visual RL pipeline (Yarats et al., 2021) and the dormant-ratio-based perturbation mechanism from DrM (Xu et al., 2023) and Sokar et al. (2023), which periodically resets agent weights to escape local minima. The paper departs from this lineage in two ways: it replaces the standard MLP policy backbone with a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture (borrowed from the NLP/vision MoE literature) to mitigate gradient conflicts in multi-stage robotic tasks, and it modifies the perturbation candidate distribution from fixed Gaussian noise to a heuristically updated distribution formed by top-performing agents in training history. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of architectural design (MoE for policy networks) and optimization strategy (targeted perturbation), both aimed at improving sample efficiency rather than introducing a new learning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 83% vs. 32% success-rate gap on real-world robotic manipulation tasks (trained from scratch, no sim-to-real transfer) is practically significant for the robotics community, as it demonstrates that model-free visual RL can be viable on physical hardware within reasonable training budgets. However, the evaluation is limited to a single robot embodiment (Franka Panda) and three hand-crafted tasks, and the MoE implementation incurs a notable time-efficiency cost (37 FPS vs. 55–78 FPS for baselines in simulation). Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of tasks or diverse embodiments, whether more efficient MoE implementations (e.g., true sparse computation) can close the wall-clock gap, and whether the task-oriented perturbation mechanism generalizes beyond the specific dormant-ratio framework it is built upon.
