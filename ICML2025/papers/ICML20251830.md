## Problem

Existing adversarial RL methods for observation robustness (e.g., ATLA, PA-ATLA) require alternating training between the agent and adversary, which doubles environmental interactions and creates mutual dependencies incompatible with off-policy learning. No off-policy actor-critic method has been shown to handle adversarial observation perturbations within the standard MDP framework, leaving a gap in sample-efficient robust RL for continuous control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251830/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector s from the environment and an adversarially perturbed observation s̃ = s + δ where ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε (ε varies per task: 0.05–0.15), and outputs a continuous action a. The adversary is modeled as a conditional distribution ν(s̃|s) over the ε-bounded perturbation space, parameterized either as a variational model (VALT-SOFT) or approximated via numerical gradient search (VALT-EPS). Training data is collected from a replay buffer using a behavior policy that mixes clean and adversarial observations (50:50 ratio for high-dimensional tasks like Ant).

## Method

VALT eliminates explicit adversary RL by proving a symmetry theorem: the adversary's soft-optimal value function equals the negative of the agent's soft-worst value function (Theorem 4.6), so the adversary's optimal policy can be derived directly from the agent's Q-function without additional environment steps. The hard L∞-ball constraint is relaxed into a soft-constrained optimization via f-divergence regularization (Eq. 6), yielding two practical variants: VALT-SOFT uses the analytical KL-divergence solution (a Boltzmann-weighted distribution over perturbations, Eq. 9) trained via a variational model, while VALT-EPS approximates the α-divergence solution by concentrating probability mass on the worst-case perturbation found via PGD-style gradient steps (Eq. 12). Policy improvement then proceeds with a KL-constrained update (Proposition 4.11) that fixes the derived adversary and optimizes the agent's policy using only replay buffer data, making the entire pipeline compatible with off-policy SAC.

## Results

Across four MuJoCo benchmarks, VALT-EPS and VALT-SOFT achieve the best worst-case robustness scores in HalfCheetah (4647 and 3584, respectively, vs. 3504 for ATLA-PPO) and Ant (2757 for VALT-SOFT vs. 2408 for ATLA-PPO), while matching or exceeding SAC-PPO in Hopper (3212 vs. 3198). Critically, these results are obtained with 3–10× fewer environment steps than PPO-based baselines (1M–3M vs. 5M–10M steps), demonstrating that the virtual alternative training framework eliminates the sample cost of explicit adversary training. On Walker2d, Robust-SAC (5180) slightly outperforms VALT-SOFT (4981) in worst-case score, and VALT-EPS exhibits high variance (3442±2428), indicating task-dependent sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VALT-EPS and VALT-SOFT outperform all PPO-based baselines (PPO, Robust-PPO, ATLA-PPO, PA-ATLA-PPO, WocaR-PPO) in worst-case robustness on HalfCheetah and Ant, while using 3–10× fewer environment steps. Against the closest off-policy competitor SAC-PPO (which implements true alternating training with SAC as agent and PPO as adversary), VALT-EPS achieves a higher worst-case score on HalfCheetah (4647 vs. 1823) and Hopper (3212 vs. 3198) with 6× fewer steps. Against Robust-SAC (simple regularization), VALT-EPS dominates on HalfCheetah (4647 vs. 2026) and Ant (1487 vs. 68), but Robust-SAC edges out VALT-SOFT on Walker2d (5180 vs. 4981). The key advantage is sample efficiency: VALT methods require only 0.5M–3M steps versus 5M–10M for PPO-based methods and 6M–13M for SAC-PPO, while matching or exceeding their robustness. The authors attribute the gap over SAC-PPO to the reformulation as a Quantal Response Equilibrium (QRE) rather than a hard Nash equilibrium, which avoids the instability of finding sharp saddle points in alternating training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SA-MDP adversarial observation robustness framework (Zhang et al., 2020b) and the alternating training paradigm (ATLA, Zhang et al., 2021; PA-ATLA, Sun et al., 2022) by eliminating the need for a separate RL process for the adversary. The key conceptual departure is a symmetry theorem showing that the adversary's value function is the negative of the agent's value function under a fixed policy, allowing the optimal adversary to be derived directly from the agent's Q-function. This is combined with f-divergence soft constraints (building on Belousov & Peters, 2017) to make the adversary optimization tractable within an off-policy SAC framework, whereas prior methods were restricted to on-policy algorithms due to the mutual dependency between agent and adversary training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for robust RL practitioners: VALT achieves 3–10× sample efficiency over PPO-based methods while matching or exceeding their robustness, and it is the first method to bring learned-adversary robustness to off-policy continuous control. However, the paper acknowledges that the replay buffer distribution mismatch (data collected under a proxy policy vs. the current adversarial policy) remains an open challenge, and robustness is task-dependent (e.g., Hopper and Walker2d show near-perfect robustness even with vanilla SAC, while HalfCheetah and Ant are much harder). Future directions include integrating functional smoothness, extending to domain randomization, and adapting to offline/curriculum settings.
