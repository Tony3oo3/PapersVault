## Problem

Tree ensembles like XGBoost and Random Forests are widely used but their size creates practical barriers: verification of fairness or robustness properties scales exponentially with ensemble size, and smaller models are needed for battery-powered devices. Existing compression methods are limited in what they can prune—some only remove entire trees, others only refine leaf values, and ForestPrune cuts each tree at a single uniform depth—leaving a gap for a method that can prune arbitrary subtrees at any level while simultaneously updating remaining leaf values to preserve accuracy.

## Input / Output

LOP takes a previously learned tree ensemble (XGBoost or Random Forest), a training dataset, and a validation dataset, and produces a structurally compressed ensemble where some subtrees have been replaced by single leaf nodes and remaining leaf values have been rescaled and shifted. The compression is governed by a user-specified maximum accuracy loss Δ (e.g., 0.5% balanced accuracy drop) on the validation set and a number of rounds R of level-wise compression. The output forest has the same tree structure except pruned subtrees are collapsed into leaves, and all remaining leaf values are updated.

## Method

LOP processes the ensemble level-by-level from root (level 0) to the deepest leaves. At each level, it introduces a scaling parameter c_n and bias parameter b_n for every active node n, then solves a single global optimization problem that minimizes a loss function plus L1 regularization on the c parameters. The L1 penalty drives c_n toward zero: when c_n=0, the entire subtree rooted at n is pruned and replaced by a leaf with value b_n; when c_n≠0, all leaf values under n are updated to c_n·v_k + b_n. The regularization strength α is tuned via binary search in log-space to find the maximum compression that keeps validation accuracy loss within Δ. The level-wise procedure is repeated for R rounds, allowing subtrees that were not prunable in earlier rounds to become prunable as the forest shrinks.

## Results

On 14 binary classification datasets, LOP achieves an average compression ratio of 50.0× on XGBoost models and 1167.2× on RandomForest models, while maintaining a balanced accuracy drop of only 0.6 percentage points. The best individual compression ratios reach 356.8× (Compas, XGBoost) and 12086.8× (Compas, RandomForest). LOP also produces models that are 2.5–10× faster to verify for adversarial robustness (median 13.3s vs. 32–148s for competitors) and use substantially less memory (median 8.2 KB vs. 15–59 KB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOP systematically outperforms all four baselines on compression ratio across nearly every dataset. ForestPrune (FP) is the closest competitor, achieving 33.8× average compression on XGBoost vs. LOP's 50.0×, but LOP extends this lead to 1167× vs. 698× on RandomForest models. Global Refinement (GR) achieves 7.9× on XGBoost and 135× on RandomForest, consistently trailing LOP by a factor of 2–3 on XGBoost and ~9× on RandomForest. Individual Contribution (IC) and LRL1 are the weakest baselines, averaging only 5.1× and 1.5× on XGBoost respectively, as they can only remove entire trees (level-0 pruning) rather than subtrees at arbitrary depths. In terms of accuracy, all methods maintain a small loss (<0.5 pp on average), with LOP's 0.6 pp drop being comparable to FP (0.4 pp) and slightly higher than GR (0.1 pp) or LRL1 (0.0 pp), confirming that LOP's superior compression does not come at a meaningful accuracy cost. Notably, LOP also produces more robust models (average empirical robustness 0.183 vs. XGBoost's 0.136) and reduces robustness verification time by 2.5–10× compared to competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOP builds on the additive forest formulation T(x) = s(x)·v introduced by Ren et al. (2015) for Global Refinement, but departs fundamentally from the global, all-leaves-at-once optimization. Where GR optimizes every leaf value independently and LRL1 (Buschjager & Morik, 2023) only prunes entire trees at level 0, LOP introduces a level-wise decomposition: at each depth it solves a much smaller problem with per-node scaling (c_n) and bias (b_n) parameters, using L1 regularization on c_n to induce subtree removal. This contrasts with ForestPrune (Liu & Mazumder, 2023a), which enforces a single cut depth per tree, whereas LOP can prune heterogeneous subtrees at different depths within the same tree.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× compression advantage over all baselines while maintaining accuracy within 0.5% has direct practical consequences: verification of fairness/robustness properties scales exponentially in forest size, and smaller models reduce energy consumption on battery-powered devices. The counterintuitive finding that LOP-compressed ensembles are often *more* robust than their uncompressed originals (Table A7) suggests pruning can act as a form of regularization. Open questions include whether the level-wise sequential processing can be parallelized for very deep forests, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-binary trees or ensembles with heterogeneous tree structures.
