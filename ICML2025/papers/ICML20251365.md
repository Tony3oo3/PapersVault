## Problem

Multi-class logistic regression with one dominant class and multiple rare classes (e.g., >95% background) is computationally intractable via standard MLE because the Newton-Raphson Hessian scales as (p+1)K × (p+1)K, and the asymptotic theory for the MLE under rare-class conditions was previously unestablished. This paper fills that theoretical gap and derives the key structural insight that rare-class coefficients are asymptotically independent, enabling a decomposition into independent pairwise problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes p-dimensional feature vectors (512-dim VGG16 embeddings in the TikTok application, extracted from 100×40 sub-images of 720×1280 screenshots) and multi-class labels where class 0 is the major/background class and classes 1–K are rare car models. It produces per-class regression coefficient vectors θ_k* ∈ R^{p+1} for each rare class, from which prediction probabilities and class labels are derived. A key structural property is that the asymptotic covariance matrix is block-diagonal with K blocks of size (p+1)×(p+1), reflecting asymptotic independence across rare classes.

## Method

The core insight is that under rare-class conditions (α_N → −∞ and α_N + log N → ∞), the MLE's asymptotic covariance matrix is block-diagonal, so the (K+1)-class problem decomposes into K independent two-class logistic regressions (major class vs. each rare class). The Pairwise MLE (PMLE) exploits this by solving K separate two-class problems, each with only a (p+1)×(p+1) Hessian invertible via Newton-Raphson, and all K problems are fully parallelizable. To further reduce cost, the Subsample-based PMLE (SPMLE) randomly downsamples the major class with probability π_N and corrects the intercept to α_N^sub = α_N − log π_N, provably retaining the same √(Ne^{α_N})-consistency and asymptotic efficiency as the full MLE provided π_N/e^{α_N} → ∞. In the TikTok application, features are obtained via transfer learning from a pre-trained VGG16 network, and the SPMLE with π_N = 0.25 reduces CPU time from 145.85 s (GMLE) to 12.12 s while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Results

On the TikTok Screenshots (TTS) dataset (N=56,016 sub-images, p=512 features, 9 classes), the proposed PMLE achieves 0.835 overall accuracy and 0.999 AUC, matching the GMLE (0.836 ACC, 0.997 AUC) while reducing computation time from 145.85 s to 42.12 s. The SPMLE further reduces time to 12.12 s with only a slight accuracy drop (0.824 ACC) while maintaining 0.999 AUC. In simulation studies (p=50, K=20), PMLE and SPMLE achieve nearly identical RMSE (e.g., 0.039 vs 0.039 at N=5×10⁵) with SPMLE using ~3.7× fewer major-class samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three proposed estimators (GMLE, PMLE, SPMLE) outperform every baseline on overall accuracy. PMLE (0.835 ACC) beats Focal Loss [Lin et al., ICCV 2017] (0.794) by 4.1 points, Class-Balanced Loss [Cui et al., CVPR 2019] (0.789) by 4.6 points, Cost Sensitive Loss [Fernández et al., 2018] (0.747) by 8.8 points, and Random Downsampling [Fernández et al., 2018] (0.763) by 7.2 points. On AUC, PMLE and SPMLE (0.999) marginally exceed FL and CBL (0.998) and clearly surpass CSL (0.991) and RDS (0.996). The key practical advantage is computational: PMLE runs 3.5× faster than GMLE and SPMLE runs 12× faster, both via parallel Newton-Raphson on (p+1)×(p+1) Hessians rather than a single (p+1)K×(p+1)K system, with no learning-rate tuning required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-class rare-event logistic regression framework of Wang (2020) to the multi-class setting with one dominant class and multiple rare classes. The key theoretical departure is the discovery that the MLE's asymptotic covariance matrix has a block-diagonal structure under the rare-class conditions, implying asymptotic independence across rare-class parameters. This insight, absent from the standard multi-class logistic regression literature (Nelder & Wedderburn, 1972; McCullagh & Nelder, 2019), motivates decomposing the joint optimization into independent pairwise two-class problems. The subsampling strategy for the major class builds on the nonuniform negative sampling ideas of Wang et al. (2021), adapted here with a rigorous efficiency guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The block-diagonal asymptotic covariance result provides a principled justification for replacing a computationally prohibitive joint multi-class optimization (requiring inversion of a (p+1)K × (p+1)K Hessian) with K independent, parallelizable two-class problems at no asymptotic efficiency cost. The SPMLE further reduces computation by subsampling the majority class, with a clean condition (π_N/e^{α_N} → ∞) guaranteeing no efficiency loss. Open questions include extending to settings where rare classes have different orders of magnitude, and whether the pairwise decomposition idea transfers to more complex models (e.g., deep networks) beyond logistic regression. The real-data application, while illustrative, is small-scale (56K sub-images, 9 classes) and uses a fixed VGG16 feature extractor, limiting the demonstrated practical impact.
