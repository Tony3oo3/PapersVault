## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers—whether physics-informed (PINNs) or data-driven (neural operators)—are restricted to a narrow set of PDE instances with limited coefficient variations, failing to generalize to diverse equations. Prior attempts to improve generalization either incorporate only partial PDE information (e.g., coefficients alone) or rely purely on scaling data and parameters without structured physics conditioning, leaving a gap in how to systematically embed the complete mathematical structure of a PDE into a deep solver.

## Input / Output

The model consumes initial condition observations as a patchified token sequence on a spatial mesh (resolutions from 64×64 to 128×128 in 2D, 256 in 1D), alongside complete PDE components: equation symbols (encoded as 4096-dim embeddings via LLaMA-3 8B from LaTeX prompts), scalar/vector coefficients, boundary condition type labels, external force fields, binary geometry masks, and boundary value functions. The output is the full spatiotemporal solution field at the target resolution, predicted autoregressively one timestep at a time during evaluation. A binary masking channel indicates which PDE components are present for a given equation, enabling the model to handle heterogeneous benchmarks where different PDEs have different component sets.

## Method

Unisolver is a PDE-conditional Transformer that classifies all PDE components into domain-wise (equation symbols, coefficients, boundary types) and point-wise (force fields, geometry, boundary values) categories based on their spatial influence pattern, then embeds each group into separate deep condition vectors. Equation symbols are encoded by averaging the last-layer hidden states of LLaMA-3 8B prompted with LaTeX code (using symbolic placeholders rather than numeric values), while other components use MLPs or patchify embeddings. The key architectural mechanism is subspace decoupling: the hidden channel dimension is split in half, with one half modulated by domain-wise conditions and the other by point-wise conditions, minimizing interference between the two condition types. Within each Transformer block, conditions are injected via DiT-style scale-shift-select operations applied before and after both self-attention and feedforward modules, with domain-wise conditions repeated along the sequence dimension to provide uniform physical guidance. The model is trained with supervised relative L2 loss on jointly diverse PDE datasets, and the decoupled conditioning is shown via ablation to be critical for zero-shot generalization.

## Results

On the HeterNS benchmark, Unisolver (4.1M params) achieves relative L2 of 0.0098 (viscosity, in-distribution) and 0.1053 (force, zero-shot), outperforming all six baselines with average promotions of 27.2% (viscosity) and 66.7% (force) in zero-shot settings. On 1D time-dependent PDEs (3M samples), Unisolver (19M params) reduces in-distribution relative L2 from 0.0225 to 0.0108 (52.0% improvement over PDEformer) and achieves 22.5–31.0% zero-shot gains on Burgers and Advection equations. On 2D mixed PDEs (12 datasets, 74.1k samples), Unisolver (33M params) achieves an average relative L2 of 4.54×10⁻², beating DPOT (5.50×10⁻²) in 11 of 12 test sets with a 17.5% average promotion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unisolver outperforms all six HeterNS baselines (FNO, PINO, ViT, FactFormer, ICON, MPP) across both in-distribution and zero-shot settings, with the most dramatic gains in force zero-shot generalization (66.7% average promotion), where all baselines fail catastrophically (relative L2 > 0.31) while Unisolver achieves 0.1053. On 1D time-dependent PDEs, Unisolver (19M params) beats PDEformer (22M params) by 52.0% in-distribution and 22.5–31.0% in zero-shot Burgers/Advection tasks, despite having fewer parameters. On 2D mixed PDEs, Unisolver (33M) surpasses DPOT (30M) in 11 of 12 test sets with a 17.5% average relative L2 reduction, with particularly strong gains in coefficient generalization (FNO-NS: up to 53.4%), force generalization (PDEArena: 30.7%), and geometry generalization (CFDBench: 22.9%). The only exception is the small Diffusion-Reaction dataset where DPOT edges out Unisolver, likely due to the task's already-saturated error level (<5% relative L2). Notably, Unisolver consistently uses fewer or comparable parameters than baselines while achieving substantially better generalization, suggesting the PDE-conditional architecture is more parameter-efficient than simply scaling data or model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

Unisolver synthesizes two previously separate paradigms in neural PDE solving—data-driven neural operators (FNO, GNOT, FactFormer) and physics-informed conditioning (PINO, CAPE, PDEformer)—into a unified conditional Transformer framework. It departs from prior PDE-conditioning work (PDEformer's graph-based approach, DPOT's pure scaling) by introducing a mathematically-motivated taxonomy of 'complete PDE components' split into domain-wise and point-wise categories, and by leveraging an LLM (LLaMA-3) to semantically embed equation symbols rather than treating them as discrete tokens or graph nodes. The subspace decoupling mechanism for integrating conditions draws on DiT-style adaptive conditioning but applies it to the specific structure of PDE physics, where domain-level and point-level influences act on different parts of the solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generalization in neural PDE solving is driven not merely by data/parameter scale but by explicitly and completely encoding the governing physics as structured conditions—a finding that reframes how future 'foundation models for PDEs' should be designed. The 66.7% zero-shot improvement on force generalization and the clean scaling-law curves (60× data, 21× params) suggest a viable path toward a single model replacing task-specific numerical solvers. Open questions include whether the LLM embedding is truly necessary versus simpler symbolic encodings (the ablation shows only ~5% gain for one of six components), how the approach extends to 3D PDEs and irregular geometries, and whether the binary masking trick for missing components generalizes to more complex real-world scenarios with partially known physics.
