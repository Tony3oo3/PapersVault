## Problem

Applying language models to 3D molecule generation requires converting continuous 3D geometric structures into discrete token sequences, but no principled tokenization method existed that simultaneously preserves full geometric information and guarantees SE(3)-invariance (invariance to rotation and translation). This gap prevented LMs from directly modeling 3D molecular geometries, despite their strong sequence-generation capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251424/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For random generation, the model starts from a sampled initial atom token (almost always 'H') and autoregressively produces a sequence of atom-type and spherical-coordinate tokens until a stop token or max length is reached. For controllable generation, a conditioning token—obtained by projecting a target quantum property value through a trainable linear layer into the embedding space—is prepended to the sequence. The output vocabulary is finite (~1.8K tokens for QM9, ~16K for GEOM-DRUGS), consisting of atom type tokens (e.g., 'C', 'N', 'O', with chirality suffixes '@'/'@@') and discretized spherical coordinate values rounded to 2–3 decimal places.

## Method

Geo2Seq converts a 3D molecule into an SE(3)-invariant 1D discrete sequence via two steps: (1) canonical labeling using the Nauty algorithm reorders atoms so that isomorphic graphs yield identical orderings, reducing the graph to a 1D sequence without structural information loss; (2) an equivariant global coordinate frame is built from the first three non-collinear atoms in canonical order, and all remaining atoms are expressed in spherical coordinates (distance d, polar angle θ, azimuthal angle φ) relative to that frame, guaranteeing invariance under any SE(3) transformation. The final sequence concatenates [z_i, d_i, θ_i, φ_i] for each atom in canonical order. The paper proves (Theorem 3.5) that this mapping is bijective up to 3D isomorphism: identical molecules produce identical sequences, and distinct molecules produce distinct sequences. The resulting sequences are modeled with standard autoregressive next-token prediction using GPT or Mamba backbones, making the approach model-agnostic and compatible with any sequence architecture.

## Results

On QM9 random generation, Geo2Seq with Mamba achieves 93.2% molecule stability and 97.1% valid percentage, surpassing the best diffusion baseline GEOLDM (89.4% and 93.8%), though it trails on valid&unique (81.7% vs. 92.7%). On GEOM-DRUGS, Geo2Seq with Mamba reaches 82.5% atom stability and 96.1% valid, comparable to GEOLDM (84.4% and 99.3%). In controllable generation on QM9, Geo2Seq with Mamba achieves MAE of 0.46 (polarizability), 98 meV (HOMO-LUMO gap), and 0.164 D (dipole moment), outperforming EDM and GEOLDM by 5–7× across all six quantum properties and approaching the data lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo2Seq with Mamba outperforms all five baselines on QM9 molecule stability (93.2% vs. 89.4% for GEOLDM, 84.6% for EDM-Bridge, 82.0% for EDM) and valid percentage (97.1% vs. 93.8% for GEOLDM), but underperforms GEOLDM on valid&unique (81.7% vs. 92.7%), which the authors attribute to the discrete tokenization limiting diversity on small datasets. On GEOM-DRUGS, Geo2Seq is competitive but slightly behind GEOLDM on atom stability (82.5% vs. 84.4%) and valid (96.1% vs. 99.3%). The most dramatic wins appear in controllable generation: Geo2Seq with Mamba reduces MAE by 5–7× compared to EDM and GEOLDM across all six quantum properties (e.g., 98 meV vs. 587–655 meV for HOMO-LUMO gap; 0.164 D vs. 1.108–1.111 D for dipole moment), approaching the data lower bound. E-NFs and G-SchNet, the older autoregressive/flow baselines, are substantially outperformed on all QM9 metrics. Overall, Geo2Seq matches or exceeds diffusion-based methods on random generation and dominates on controllable generation, validating LMs as a viable backbone for 3D molecule generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of 3D molecule generation, which relies on equivariant architectures (EDM, GEOLDM, E-NFs) or autoregressive point-cloud models (G-SchNet) that require purpose-built geometric modules. Rather than modifying model architectures to handle 3D geometry, Geo2Seq operates purely at the data level: it combines canonical labeling (via Nauty) with SE(3)-invariant spherical coordinates to produce a bijective 1D token sequence, making any standard LM (GPT, Mamba) applicable without architectural changes. This contrasts with prior chemical language models that handle only 2D representations (SMILES, SELFIES) or that bolt geometric GNNs onto transformer backbones, and with the initial XYZ-file LM attempt by Flam-Shepherd & Aspuru-Guzik, which lacked formal invariance guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design means Geo2Seq can immediately leverage any future LM (including API-only models) for 3D molecule generation without retraining geometric modules, which is practically significant as LLM capabilities continue to scale. The dramatic controllable-generation margins on QM9 (e.g., 0.46 vs 2.76 MAE for polarizability) suggest that long-context sequence modeling captures conditional structure more effectively than equivariant diffusion, though this advantage is demonstrated only on small molecules (<9 heavy atoms). Key limitations include discretization-induced information loss, reduced diversity on small datasets, and untested performance on larger drug-like molecules with modern LLMs; open questions include whether finer tokenization or hybrid continuous-discrete schemes can close the gap on GEOM-DRUGS.
