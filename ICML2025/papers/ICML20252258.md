## Problem

Existing adversarial CMDP algorithms (Wei et al., Qiu et al.) only handle soft constraints, where positive and negative violations can cancel across episodes—an unreasonable relaxation in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Conversely, hard-constraint CMDP algorithms (Liu et al., Shi et al.) are restricted to stochastic losses and cannot handle general non-stationary environments. This paper is the first to study CMDPs with both adversarial losses and stochastic hard constraints under bandit feedback, addressing three progressively stronger violation guarantees: sublinear, zero (safety), and constant.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a loop-free episodic CMDP with finite state and action spaces, adversarial loss vectors in [0,1], i.i.d. stochastic cost matrices in [0,1], and constraint thresholds α ∈ [0,L]^m. Under bandit feedback, only losses and costs for the single trajectory visited per episode are observed, and the transition function P is unknown. For the safety (S-OPS) and constant-violation (CV-OPS) settings, a strictly feasible policy π^◇ and its expected costs β are additionally required (known in S-OPS, estimated in CV-OPS). The output is a per-state action distribution (policy) selected each episode, accompanied by high-probability guarantees on cumulative regret and constraint violation.

## Method

All three algorithms operate in the occupancy-measure space and build on OMD-style updates with optimistic loss estimators (dividing observed losses by upper occupancy bounds plus an exploration factor). SV-OPS projects the OMD update onto the set of occupancy measures satisfying lower confidence bounds on constraint costs (an 'optimistic' feasible set that always subsumes the true feasible set), yielding sublinear regret and sublinear positive violation. S-OPS extends SV-OPS by randomizing between the projected policy and a known strictly feasible policy, with the mixing probability λ_t chosen pessimistically via upper confidence bounds on costs and upper occupancy bounds, guaranteeing per-episode safety while preserving sufficient exploration for sublinear regret. CV-OPS first runs a constant-length primal-dual estimation phase—using an anytime adversarial MDP regret minimizer to find a near-strictly-feasible policy and an online linear optimizer to identify the most violated constraint—then hands the estimated policy and Slater's parameter to S-OPS, achieving constant cumulative violation with regret scaling as 1/min{ρ,ρ²}.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The three main guarantees are: (1) SV-OPS achieves sublinear regret O(L|X|√(|A|T ln(T|X||A|/δ))) and sublinear positive violation O(L|X|√(|A|T ln(T|X||A|m/δ))) under only feasibility (Assumption 2.2); (2) S-OPS achieves sublinear regret O(Ψ L³|X|√(|A|T ln(T|X||A|m/δ))) while guaranteeing zero violation at every episode (safety) with high probability, requiring a known strictly feasible policy; (3) CV-OPS achieves sublinear regret O(Θ L³|X|√(|A|T ln(T|X||A|m/δ)) + L/ρ⁴(Cᴬᴾ + L ln(1/δ) + Cᴬᴰ + L)⁴) with constant violation O(L/ρ⁴(Cᴬᴾ + L√ln(1/δ) + Cᴬᴰ + L)⁴), requiring only the existence of a strictly feasible policy. A lower bound (Theorem 6.6) shows that any algorithm with o(√T) violation must incur Ω(1/ρ √T) regret, confirming the Slater's parameter dependence is unavoidable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical comparisons against baseline algorithms. The positioning is relative to prior theoretical work: all existing adversarial CMDP results (Wei et al. 2018, Qiu et al. 2020, Stradi et al. 2024a) handle only soft constraints, where positive and negative violations can cancel across episodes, whereas this paper's hard-constraint guarantees (sublinear, zero, or constant positive violation) are strictly stronger. The few works on stochastic hard constraints (Liu et al. 2021, Shi et al. 2023, Müller et al. 2024, Stradi et al. 2025) are restricted to stochastic losses and cannot handle adversarial (non-stationary) environments. The paper's contribution is the first to combine adversarial losses with hard constraints under bandit feedback, and the lower bound (Theorem 6.6) establishes that the 1/ρ dependence in the regret is fundamental for any algorithm achieving o(√T) violation, not merely an artifact of the proposed algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the occupancy-measure-based online mirror descent framework for adversarial MDPs (Jin et al., 2020; Rosenberg & Mansour, 2019) by introducing constraint-aware projection and pessimistic randomization steps. It departs from prior adversarial CMDP work (Qiu et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2018), which only handles soft constraints allowing positive and negative violations to cancel, and from prior hard-constraint CMDP work (Liu et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2023; Müller et al., 2024), which is restricted to stochastic losses. The three algorithms (SV-OPS, S-OPS, CV-OPS) form a hierarchy: SV-OPS projects onto an optimistic constraint set, S-OPS adds a pessimistic randomization with a known safe policy, and CV-OPS uses a primal-dual estimation phase to discover the safe policy when it is unknown.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to jointly handle adversarial (non-stationary) losses and stochastic hard constraints in CMDPs, a combination that arises naturally in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and budget-constrained bidding where the environment is non-stationary and constraint violations are unacceptable. The lower bound showing that any algorithm achieving o(√T) violation must depend on the Slater's parameter in its regret bound is a clean negative result with broad applicability. Open questions include whether the additional Ψ and Θ factors in the regret bounds (compared to the unconstrained case) are tight, whether the loop-free MDP assumption can be relaxed without the H-fold state duplication, and whether the constant-violation estimation phase can be made more sample-efficient.
