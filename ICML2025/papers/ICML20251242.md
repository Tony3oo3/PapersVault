## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models face a fundamental trade-off: fully autoregressive models quantize images into discrete tokens, causing irreversible information loss on high-frequency details, while AR-diffusion mixed models (e.g., Transfusion, Show-o) feed noisy images into the shared transformer, making joint modeling of visual understanding (clean inputs) and generation (noisy inputs) difficult. Orthus addresses this by maintaining a fully AR formulation over continuous image features while decoupling the diffusion process from the transformer backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Orthus consumes interleaved sequences of discrete text tokens (from a standard tokenizer) and continuous patch-wise image features (from a pre-trained VAE encoder at 512×512 resolution), delimited by special tokens [BOI], [EOI], and [SEP]. During inference, it autoregressively alternates between next-token prediction (text) and next-patch prediction (image), switching modes when [BOI] is sampled and appending [EOI] after a fixed number of patches. Generated image patches are decoded to 512×512 RGB images via a fine-tuned VAE decoder using DDIM sampling (100 steps, CFG scale 5).

## Method

Orthus embeds both discrete text tokens and continuous image patch features into a shared representation space, processed by a single transformer with causal attention. Two modality-specific heads sit on top: a standard LM head predicts the next text token categorically, while a novel diffusion head (a 3-layer MLP with AdaLN) predicts the next image patch feature by denoising a noisy version of v_{i+1} conditioned on the transformer output state f_i. The key architectural insight is that diffusion operates only in the head, not in the backbone, so the transformer always sees clean features and the AR dependency structure remains straightforward. For efficient construction, Orthus starts from Chameleon-7B, replaces the hard VQ lookup with a softmax-weighted embedding (temperature τ=1) to make the vision embedding differentiable, adds the diffusion head, and fine-tunes only these 0.3B parameters on 10k images (~72 A100 GPU hours). Post-training uses the combined objective L = L_ar + 100·L_diff with all parameters (except the VAE) updated.

## Results

On visual understanding, Orthus (7B) achieves POPE 79.6, MME-P 1265.8, VQAv2 63.2, GQA 52.8, and MMMU 28.2, outperforming all unified understanding-and-generation baselines including Chameleon† (7B) and Show-o (1.3B). On visual generation, Orthus reaches GenEval 0.58 and HPS 28.2 at 512×512 resolution, surpassing specialized text-to-image models SDXL (GenEval 0.55) and DALL-E 2 (GenEval 0.52). For image editing on Instruct-Pix2Pix, Orthus achieves CLIP text-alignment 0.238 and diversity 0.049, matching or exceeding the editing-specific InstructPix2Pix model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-scale baseline Chameleon† (post-trained on the identical mixed dataset), Orthus wins on every understanding metric (POPE 79.6 vs 77.8, MME-P 1265.8 vs 1056.9, VQAv2 63.2 vs 57.8, GQA 52.8 vs 49.6, MMMU 28.2 vs 26.7) and every generation metric (GenEval 0.58 vs 0.43, HPS 28.2 vs 26.9), with the largest gap on MME-P (+208.9) and GenEval (+0.15). Orthus also dominates Show-o (1.3B) and LWM (7B) across all shared benchmarks, confirming the advantage of continuous image features over discrete VQ tokens or AR-diffusion mixing. On generation, Orthus slightly surpasses the specialized SDXL (GenEval 0.58 vs 0.55) and DALL-E 2 (0.58 vs 0.52), though it trails SD3 (0.64) which uses a much larger architecture. For image editing, Orthus matches InstructPix2Pix on text-instruction alignment (0.238 vs 0.233) and diversity (0.049 vs 0.045) while being marginally lower on image fidelity (0.87 vs 0.88). Compared to understanding-only models like LLaVA-v1.5, Orthus scores lower on MME-P (1265.8 vs 1510.7) and VQAv2 (63.2 vs 78.5), which is expected given the shared capacity cost of the generation head, but it remains competitive with the larger InstructBLIP-13B on POPE (79.6 vs 78.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

Orthus sits at the intersection of two lines of unified multimodal modeling: fully autoregressive models (Chameleon, LWM) that discretize images via vector quantization, and AR-diffusion hybrid models (Transfusion, Show-o) that embed denoising directly in the transformer backbone. It departs from the former by replacing the hard VQ lookup with a differentiable softmax-based embedding (a 'hard-to-soft' adaptation of Chameleon's input module) and from the latter by decoupling diffusion into a lightweight MLP head rather than feeding noisy features through the shared transformer. Conceptually, it generalizes the masked-AR (MAR) idea of using a diffusion head for continuous image features from image-only generation to cross-modality interleaved image-text generation, while retaining a purely causal AR formulation for the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 72-A100-GPU-hour construction recipe (fine-tune only 0.3B parameters on 10k images) makes it practical to convert any existing fully-AR unified model into a continuous-feature variant, lowering the barrier to adopting lossless visual representations. The clean separation of diffusion into a head avoids the ~15% captioning degradation that Transfusion reports when noise is injected into the shared transformer, offering a principled alternative for joint understanding and generation. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B parameters, how inference latency from iterative DDIM sampling through the diffusion head compares to single-pass discrete decoding at larger resolutions, and whether the architecture extends naturally to video or audio modalities.
