## Problem

While single-objective distribution matching is well-studied in machine learning, the multiobjective variant—finding a distribution that simultaneously aligns with multiple target distributions under potentially conflicting divergences—has received little theoretical attention. This paper asks: what is the exact geometric structure of the Pareto set and front for multiobjective distribution matching, and can it be characterized explicitly for important distribution families?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253026/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m image datasets (e.g., QuickDraw categories at 28×28 resolution, ~12K images total) and a user-specified preference vector λ ∈ Δ_m as input. At inference, the encoder produces m latent MVN parameters (μ_k, Σ_k), the Pareto-optimal MVN is computed analytically from λ, and the decoder outputs a single interpolated image. The preference vector continuously controls the trade-off position along the Pareto front, so any point in the simplex yields a valid output without retraining.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution derives the Pareto set for multiobjective distribution matching on dually flat manifolds using information geometry: under KL divergence the PS is the convex hull in expectation parameters, and under inverse KL it is the convex hull in natural parameters. For multivariate normal distributions this yields closed-form equations for the mean and covariance of every Pareto-optimal distribution (Eqs. 2–3), and an analogous result holds under the 2-Wasserstein distance (Eq. 4). The MOVAE architecture trains a single VAE with shared encoder and decoder on all m distributions; at inference, the encoder outputs m MVN parameters, the Pareto-optimal latent MVN is computed in closed form from the preference vector, and the decoder maps it to an image. This avoids training separate models per preference and replaces costly semi-definite programming with a simple m-dimensional simplex optimization (Algorithm 1). The paper further extends the theory to α-divergence, showing the PS takes the form of a generalized geometric mean of the target densities (Eq. 5).

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with a brief qualitative experimental demonstration. MOVAE is evaluated on the QuickDraw dataset (28×28 images, ~12K training images, ~157K parameters per encoder/decoder, Adam optimizer at lr=3e-5). Results are shown qualitatively as interpolated image grids at five uniform preference levels from [1,0] to [0,1], demonstrating smooth transitions between two source distributions (e.g., alarm/clock vs. circle) under inverse KL divergence (Figure 3), KL divergence (Figure 4), and Wasserstein distance (Figure 5). No quantitative metrics (FID, IS, etc.) are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare MOVAE against any baselines. There is no head-to-head comparison with standard VAEs, mixture-of-experts models, or other multiobjective generation methods. The experimental section serves as a qualitative demonstration that the theoretically derived Pareto-optimal MVN distributions (via Equations 2, 3, or 4) can be decoded into visually coherent interpolated images, rather than a benchmarking study. The authors note as a limitation that the paper 'mainly focuses on the theoretical results on multiobjective distribution matching and do not discuss much on the applications,' and future work will address broader application comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of gradient-based multiobjective optimization (MGDA, PMTL, EPO, Pareto Set Learning) and distribution matching (GANs, VAEs, domain adaptation), but departs from both by providing closed-form Pareto set characterizations via information geometry. It builds directly on Amari's dually flat manifold framework—exponential and mixture families, canonical divergence, dual coordinate systems—to show that the Pareto set for multiobjective distribution matching is simply the convex hull of target distributions in natural or expectation parameters. Unlike prior MOO methods that iteratively approximate Pareto solutions via gradient descent or aggregation functions, this work derives the exact Pareto set analytically for the entire exponential family, and unlike prior distribution matching work that handles a single target, it addresses the multi-target setting with potentially conflicting objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit Pareto set formulation (convex hull in natural/expectation parameters) and the reduction of the optimization to a simplex-constrained problem (Theorem 11) provide a principled, computationally efficient alternative to iterative multiobjective solvers for distribution matching tasks. The connection to model merging and the MOVAE application suggest broader utility in controllable generation and multi-source domain adaptation. However, the empirical validation is limited to 28×28 QuickDraw images with a small model (~157K parameters), and the paper itself acknowledges it focuses mainly on theory with limited application exploration. Open questions include extending beyond exponential families, handling distributions outside the manifold, and scaling to high-dimensional real-world generation tasks.
