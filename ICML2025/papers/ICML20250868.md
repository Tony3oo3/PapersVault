## Problem

Sampling from high-dimensional Boltzmann distributions defined by expensive energy functions (e.g., molecular DFT energies) remains intractable with existing diffusion-based samplers, which either require ground-truth data, rely on MCMC/SMC augmentation with many energy evaluations, or need full SDE simulation per gradient update. No prior on-policy method could perform significantly more gradient updates than energy evaluations, preventing scaling to complex amortized molecular conformer generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a molecular connectivity graph (atom types as node features, bond orders as edge features) and an unnormalized energy function (e.g., the eSEN neural network energy model) as input, requiring no atomic configuration data during training. It outputs molecular conformers as either full 3D Cartesian coordinates (conditioned on the graph) or torsion angles (conditioned on elements, bond lengths, and bond angles), enabling amortized sampling across arbitrary molecules. The energy function is evaluated only once per outer-loop sample, while the inner loop performs many gradient updates using a replay buffer of (X₁, ∇g) pairs.

## Method

Adjoint Sampling reframes sampling as a stochastic optimal control problem and solves it via a Reciprocal Adjoint Matching (RAM) objective that replaces expensive full-trajectory SDE simulation with sampling (X_t, X_1) pairs from the closed-form Gaussian posterior of the zero-drift base process. A replay buffer decouples energy evaluation from gradient updates: the outer loop simulates the controlled process once to collect (X₁, ∇g(X₁)) pairs, while the inner loop performs many cheap gradient steps on the RAM loss using these cached pairs. Theoretically, the alternating scheme is shown to implicitly perform a Reciprocal projection onto the Schrödinger bridge sharing the same terminal distribution, guaranteeing that the SOC objective monotonically decreases and the fixed point is the optimal control. The drift is parameterized by E(n)-equivariant GNNs (EGNN or Tensor Field Networks via e3nn) to enforce permutation, rotation, and translation symmetries, with a zero-center-of-mass projection for Cartesian sampling and periodic boundary conditions (wrapped Gaussians on the flat torus) for torsional sampling.

## Results

On the amortized conformer generation benchmark (Table 2), Cartesian Adjoint Sampling with pretraining and relaxation achieves 96.65% recall coverage and 0.60 AMR on SPICE, and 87.01% recall coverage and 0.76 AMR on GEOM-DRUGS, substantially outperforming RDKit ETKDG (81.61%/0.79 and 71.72%/0.93 respectively). On synthetic energy benchmarks (Table 1), Adjoint Sampling matches iDEM on geometric W2 distance (e.g., 4.50 vs 4.69 on LJ-55) while achieving dramatically lower energy W2 (58.04 vs 93.53 on LJ-55), using only 0.002 energy evaluations per gradient update versus 512 for iDEM. The method also outperforms PIS and DDS, which fail to scale beyond LJ-13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RDKit ETKDG (the domain-specific chemistry baseline), Adjoint Sampling wins decisively on recall coverage across both SPICE and GEOM-DRUGS with relaxation (96.65% vs 81.61% and 87.01% vs 71.72%), while also achieving lower AMR (0.60 vs 0.79 on SPICE). On GEOM-DRUGS precision, RDKit retains an advantage (75.37% vs 61.96% coverage), but the recall gap is the more critical metric for molecular design. Against iDEM (the prior state-of-the-art learned sampler), Adjoint Sampling achieves comparable geometric W2 on LJ-55 (4.50 vs 4.69) but far superior energy W2 (58.04 vs 93.53), indicating iDEM fails to concentrate samples in low-energy regions; moreover, iDEM requires ~256,000× more energy evaluations per gradient update (512 vs 0.002). PIS and DDS (SOC-based methods) degrade severely at scale, with PIS achieving W2 of 4.79 on LJ-55 but with 131.27 standard deviation, and LogVariance cannot scale beyond DW-4. The Torsional variant outperforms Cartesian without pretraining on precision, but Cartesian with pretraining and relaxation surpasses all other variants on recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper specializes and extends Adjoint Matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024), which itself reframes stochastic optimal control (SOC) problems as moving regression targets, into a highly efficient sampling algorithm. The key departure from prior SOC-based samplers (PIS, DDS, LogVariance) is the introduction of a Reciprocal projection combined with a replay buffer, which decouples the expensive operations (SDE simulation and energy evaluation) from the gradient updates, enabling orders of magnitude more parameter updates per energy call. Unlike PDDS and TSM, which use a similar regression formula but require intractable sampling from the target distribution, Adjoint Sampling operates on-policy with a moving target, and unlike off-policy methods (iDEM), it explicitly leverages the energy gradient without requiring Monte Carlo score estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× reduction in energy evaluations per gradient update (0.002 vs. 1 for PIS/DDS, 512 for iDEM) is the critical enabler that makes learned samplers practical for expensive energy models like neural network potentials approximating DFT, opening the door to amortized conformer generation across thousands of molecules — a task no prior learned sampler has attempted at scale. The new conformer generation benchmark (SPICE/GEOM-DRUGS with CREST references) provides a concrete, chemistry-relevant target for future sampling methods. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger molecules with more rotatable bonds, how it compares to physics-based methods (CREST, ORCA) on absolute energy accuracy, and whether the pretraining step via Bridge Matching can be eliminated entirely.
