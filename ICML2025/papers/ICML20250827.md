## Problem

Existing temporal knowledge graph completion (TKGC) models treat temporal information as a supplementary signal rather than modeling the distinct behavioral patterns facts exhibit over time. The paper identifies two underexplored temporal perspectives—diachronicity (facts evolve differently across temporal domains) and synchronicity (relations between entities interact within a specific temporal context)—and notes that no prior model jointly captures both in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250827/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a quadruple (s, r, o, τ) where the timestamp τ can be a point, a full interval [τb, τe], or a partial interval with a missing bound. For interval timestamps, the quadruple is split into two point-timestamp instances and scored as their average; for partial intervals, only the known bound is used. The output is a scalar ranking score per candidate entity computed via quaternion inner product, used to rank all entities for link prediction.

## Method

TeDS embeds all entities, relations, and timestamps as quaternions and designs two parallel temporal perception channels. The Synchronic Perception (SP) channel reorganizes the synchronic timestamp and relation quaternions into two composite quaternions, applies the non-commutative Hamilton product to entangle time and relation, and rotates the head entity by the resulting unit quaternion. The Diachronic Perception (DP) channel maps each discrete time point to a continuous angle via trigonometric functions to build a diachronic timestamp quaternion, then rotates the relation by this timestamp to encode temporal evolution. The final score is the inner product of the sum of both rotated head-entity representations with the tail entity. Training uses a cross-entropy loss augmented with a tensor 4-norm regularizer and a linear temporal regularizer that enforces smoothness between adjacent timestamps, optimized with Adagrad at dimension k=100.

## Results

TeDS achieves the best MRR across all six benchmarks: 90.7 on ICEWS14, 87.3 on ICEWS05-15, 45.8 on ICEWS18, 77.3 on YAGO11k, 95.4 on Wikidata12k, and 71.5 on GDELT. The most dramatic gains appear on YAGO11k (+50.2 MRR over the best baseline MDRQS) and Wikidata12k (+52.2 MRR over SANe), where long temporal spans and sparse event distributions challenge existing models. On the ICEWS series, TeDS edges out the strongest baseline TPComplEx by 0.9 MRR on ICEWS14 and 2.8 on ICEWS05-15, while pulling ahead by 11.6 MRR on the denser ICEWS18. TeDS also uses roughly 10× fewer parameters than TPComplEx (e.g., 20.6M vs. 201.2M on Wikidata12k) while training 35–43% faster per epoch on smaller datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeDS outperforms all 20 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competition is with TPComplEx on the ICEWS series: TeDS leads by 0.9 MRR on ICEWS14 (90.7 vs. 89.8) and 2.8 on ICEWS05-15 (87.3 vs. 84.5), but extends the gap to 11.6 MRR on ICEWS18 (45.8 vs. 34.2), where higher fact density within the same temporal window amplifies the benefit of the synchronic perception channel. On YAGO11k and Wikidata12k, where facts span centuries with long-tail temporal distributions, TeDS's diachronic perception channel yields massive margins: +50.2 MRR over MDRQS (77.3 vs. 27.1) and +52.2 over SANe (95.4 vs. 43.2). On GDELT, TeDS surpasses the strongest baseline GLARGCN by 14.4 MRR (71.5 vs. 57.1). Ablation results confirm both channels contribute: removing SP drops ICEWS14 MRR from 90.7 to 71.1, while removing DP drops it to 79.6, and the baseline quaternion model HTM (no temporal mixing) scores only 56.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeDS extends the quaternion embedding lineage initiated by QuatE (Zhang et al., 2019) to the temporal setting, but its core conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of temporal information into two orthogonal channels: a synchronic channel (capturing inter-relation interactions within a fixed time context) and a diachronic channel (capturing how a single relation evolves across time). The synchronic channel reorganizes components of the relation and timestamp quaternions before applying the Hamilton product, while the diachronic channel maps timestamps to angles via trigonometric functions to produce smooth, continuous temporal rotations. The loss formulation follows ComplEx's cross-entropy approach, and the linear temporal regularizer is borrowed from TeLM, but the dual-perception architecture and the specific quaternion reorganization strategy are novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual diachronic/synchronic framing offers a principled way to think about why temporal facts are hard: relations interact within a moment (synchronicity) and also drift across moments (diachronicity), and prior TKGC models conflate or ignore one of these. The 10× parameter reduction relative to TPComplEx on Wikidata12k (20.6M vs. 201.2M) makes the approach practically attractive for large-scale TKGs where memory is a bottleneck. Open questions remain around periodic fact patterns, complex temporal expressions (intervals, missing endpoints), and whether the quaternion-specific advantage over complex or dual-quaternion spaces is fundamental or merely a capacity artifact; the paper itself flags these as future directions.
