## Problem

Chain-of-Thought prompting degrades significantly on complex spatial reasoning tasks because it relies exclusively on textual representations that fail to capture intricate visual patterns and spatial layouts. Prior multimodal extensions (two-stage captioning, ReAct-style tool pipelines) remain dependent on separate visual modules or external tools, and text-based Visualization-of-Thought uses overly simplistic textual proxies for mental images. The paper asks whether MLLMs can genuinely 'imagine' in visual modality while reasoning, generating actual image visualizations as part of their reasoning traces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251436/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a grid-based environment image (abstract maze, symbolic mini-behavior layout, or comic-style frozen lake at 3×3 to 6×6 or 7×10 grid sizes) concatenated with a text action sequence and task instructions. During inference, it autoregressively generates interleaved verbal thought tokens and image tokens (discrete VQGAN codebook entries) at each reasoning step, producing a sequence of text-image pairs before concluding with a multiple-choice answer. The generated images are reconstructed from discrete image tokens via a frozen detokenizer, and the model is trained with input augmentation (repeated tokenization/detokenization 0–10 times) to improve robustness to reconstruction noise.

## Method

MVoT is implemented by fine-tuning Anole-7B (a Chameleon-based autoregressive MLLM with a unified Transformer processing both text and image tokens) via LoRA instruction tuning on interleaved text-image rationales. The key technical contribution is the token discrepancy loss L_D, which addresses the misalignment between separately trained text and image tokenizers: it computes a pairwise MSE similarity matrix in the visual embedding space and penalizes the predicted probability distribution for assigning mass to image tokens that are far from the ground-truth token's visual embedding neighborhood. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy loss over all tokens (text and image) with L_D applied only to image tokens, while both tokenizers remain frozen. This loss bridges the gap between the language modeling embedding space and the visual codebook embedding space, significantly improving visualization accuracy (V-Acc. from 0.64 to 0.93 on MAZE) and reducing redundant pattern generation during recursive multi-step reasoning.

## Results

On the three dynamic spatial reasoning tasks, MVoT (Anole-7B) achieves 92.95% accuracy on MAZE, 95.14% on MINIBEHAVIOR, and 85.60% on FROZENLAKE. MVoT surpasses the Direct baseline by 7.7% on FROZENLAKE and by 22.6% on MINIBEHAVIOR. On the most challenging FROZENLAKE task, MVoT outperforms CoT by 24.1 percentage points (85.60% vs. 61.48%), while CoT actually underperforms the Direct baseline (61.48% vs. 77.88%). When used as a plug-in for GPT-4o, MVoT-generated visual thoughts improve GPT-4o's accuracy by over 15% across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CoT baseline (Wei et al., 2022), MVoT achieves comparable accuracy on MAZE (92.95% vs. 97.92%) and MINIBEHAVIOR (95.14% vs. 98.12%) but dramatically outperforms it on FROZENLAKE (85.60% vs. 61.48%), where CoT actually falls below the Direct baseline (77.88%). The paper attributes CoT's FROZENLAKE failure to its heavy reliance on textual coordinate descriptions of environment entities (e.g., holes), with 90.8% of CoT errors stemming from inaccurate hole coordinates. Against GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024) zero-shot baselines, MVoT outperforms GPT-4o Direct on all three tasks (92.95% vs. 71.00% on MAZE, 95.14% vs. 45.76% on MINIBEHAVIOR, 85.60% vs. 49.76% on FROZENLAKE) and also surpasses GPT-4o CoT. Notably, when MVoT-generated visual thoughts are fed to GPT-4o as a plug-in in a ReAct-style pipeline, GPT-4o's accuracy improves by over 15% across all tasks, demonstrating MVoT's utility as a modular reasoning component for proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) line of work, which elicits spatial reasoning by introducing textual 'mental images' as reasoning steps, by replacing text proxies with actual generated images as visual thoughts. It departs from ReAct-style multimodal pipelines that rely on external tools (code interpreters, vision models) and two-stage captioning/scene-graph approaches by leveraging the native image-generation capability of autoregressive MLLMs (Chameleon/Anole-7B) to produce interleaved text-image reasoning traces end-to-end. The token discrepancy loss is a novel technical contribution that addresses the embedding misalignment between separately trained image and text tokenizers in autoregressive MLLMs, a problem noted by Tschannen et al. (2024) but not previously resolved with a training-time loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVoT establishes that native visual generation can serve as a reasoning modality complementary to verbal CoT, with the two approaches failing on different subsets of problems (upper-bound ensemble reaches ~100% on MAZE/MINIBEHAVIOR). The finding that CoT actually degrades below Direct on FROZENLAKE due to coordinate-description errors in complex environments challenges the assumption that more explicit textual reasoning always helps. Key limitations include the evaluation being confined to grid-based, symbolic environments with simple entity counts, the computational overhead of recursive image generation at inference, and visualization quality degradation on fine-grained patterns. Open questions include whether MVoT generalizes to real-world spatial scenes, whether the token discrepancy loss transfers to other autoregressive MLLM architectures, and whether guidance techniques from diffusion models can further improve visualization fidelity.
