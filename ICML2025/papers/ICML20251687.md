## Problem

Standard message-passing GNNs and their K-Hop/K-Path extensions suffer from redundant structures that cause repeated encoding of the same structural information across layers, inflating computational cost without adding expressive power. While K-Hop and K-Path GNNs improve expressiveness over 1-WL, their complexity grows proportionally with the number of layers, and no prior work had formally identified and removed the redundant aggregation patterns while provably preserving expressiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251687/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The frameworks consume a graph adjacency matrix and (optionally) node feature vectors, producing per-node representations that can be read out for node-level tasks (SSP, eccentricity, Laplacian features), graph-level tasks (connectivity, diameter, radius, substructure counting), or molecular property regression (QM9, ZINC). A key structural difference from standard MP-GNNs is that at layer k the pruned MP-GNN aggregates over 2^(k-1)-walk neighbors rather than only 1-hop neighbors, so the receptive field grows exponentially with depth; for K-Path/K-Hop variants, layer l aggregates from l-path (or l-hop) neighbors up to K-path (or K-hop) neighbors rather than always from 1-path/1-hop neighbors.

## Method

The paper identifies redundant aggregation in three GNN families and proposes pruned variants: (1) Pruned MP-GNN aggregates from 2^(k-1)-walk neighbors at layer k, making the information-gathering range grow exponentially so fewer layers are needed; (2) Pruned K-Path GNN at layer l≤K aggregates from l-path to K-path neighbors (skipping 1-path through (l-1)-path neighbors already encoded in prior layers), and for l>K aggregates only from K-path neighbors; (3) Pruned K-Hop GNN applies the analogous scheme with hop neighborhoods. Using the matrix language MATLANG, the authors prove that pruned MP-GNN retains 1-WL-level expressiveness (via a 'viewable sequence' condition satisfied by a_k=2^(k-1)), pruned K-Path is exactly as expressive as K-Path, and pruned K-Hop matches K-Hop on regular and strongly regular graphs. The pruned K-Path and K-Hop frameworks achieve Θ(n·L/K) time and space complexity versus Θ(n·L) for the originals, while distinguishing more non-isomorphic graphs than standard MP-GNN.

## Results

On the TU graph classification benchmarks, PR K-Path achieves the best accuracy on 4 of 5 datasets: MUTAG (92.6%), D&D (83.3%), PTC-MR (67.5%), and PROTEINS (79.1%), while PR K-Hop leads on IMDB-B (76.2%). On ZINC molecular property prediction, PR K-Hop achieves the lowest test MAE (0.077±0.009) with 476K parameters, compared to 575K for the original K-Hop. On QM9, PR K-Path achieves the best MAE on 9 of 12 molecular properties, including μ (0.268), ε_HOMO (0.00197), and U_0 (0.0502). The pruned frameworks consistently use ~17-20% fewer parameters than their unpruned counterparts while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PR K-Path outperforms the original K-Path on 4 of 5 TU datasets (MUTAG 92.6 vs 91.7, D&D 83.3 vs 82.1, PTC-MR 67.5 vs 65.9, PROTEINS 79.1 vs 78.6) but trails on IMDB-B (74.9 vs 76.8). PR K-Hop beats K-Hop on D&D (84.1 vs 81.9), PROTEINS (77.0 vs 75.0), and IMDB-B (76.2 vs 74.5), while being marginally lower on MUTAG (91.8 vs 92.1). Both pruned variants substantially outperform GIN (e.g., PR K-Path on PROTEINS: 79.1 vs 74.9) and GraphSAGE (e.g., PR K-Hop on D&D: 84.1 vs 78.1). On ZINC, PR K-Hop achieves the best MAE (0.077) with 17% fewer parameters than K-Hop (476K vs 575K), and PR K-Path uses 21% fewer parameters than K-Path (456K vs 582K) while matching its MAE (0.079 vs 0.082). The WL subtree kernel baseline is consistently outperformed by all GNN-based methods across all TU datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the K-Hop GNN (Nikolentzos et al., 2020) and K-Path GNN (Michel et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2020) frameworks, which extend standard message passing to higher-order neighborhoods to surpass 1-WL expressiveness. The key departure is the use of MATLANG (Brijder et al., 2019; Geerts, 2021) as a formal algebraic tool to identify redundant aggregation structures in all three GNN families and prove that removing them preserves expressiveness. The 'viewable sequence' characterization and the specific choice of a_k = 2^(k-1) for the pruned MP-GNN are novel theoretical contributions that connect the WL test's iterative refinement to a combinatorial covering condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that GNNs can achieve equivalent or higher expressiveness with fewer layers and parameters, reducing both training cost and the nonlinearity accumulation that degrades deep GNNs (Suzuki, 2020). A significant limitation is that the K-Hop equivalence proof only covers regular and strongly regular graphs, leaving the general case open — the authors explicitly note that generating K-hop matrices from K-path matrices loses information about shortest-path counts and longer-path counts. Future work could extend the K-Hop equivalence to arbitrary graphs, explore whether the pruned framework's exponential receptive field growth enables fewer layers on large-scale graphs, and investigate whether the redundancy identification generalizes to other GNN variants (e.g., attention-based or subgraph GNNs).
