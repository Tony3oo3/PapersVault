## Problem

Existing multi-view graph clustering (MVGC) methods that rely on GNNs struggle to simultaneously handle homophilic and heterophilic graphs, as they either explicitly decouple homophilic and heterophilic information or overemphasize one aspect, leading to inadequate interaction between the two during contrastive analysis. The paper asks how to encode both types of graph information within a unified representation framework without compromising either.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252053/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} for each of V views, and produces a consensus embedding H that is fed into k-means for final cluster assignments. Notably, the GCN used for feature recovery is parameter-free (no learnable weights), and the heterophilic benchmark datasets (Texas, Chameleon, Wisconsin) are single-view graphs that are duplicated to form a second view. The homophily weight ω is estimated iteratively via pseudo-labels from the previous iteration's k-means result, creating a self-supervised feedback loop.

## Method

NGCE introduces a node-guided contrastive encoding mechanism where an autoencoder first refines node features into embeddings Z, from which a cosine-similarity correlation matrix S is computed. A homophily-sensitive joint graph encoding Â = S + ωA is constructed, where ω is estimated from pseudo-labels and the adjacency structure. The key denoising step masks original node features with Bernoulli noise and forces a parameter-free GCN (using Â as its propagation matrix) to recover the original features, with a contrastive loss that adaptively selects positive/negative samples based on which features were masked. A cross-view contrast module then fuses per-view embeddings with similarity-based weights and applies an NT-Xent loss using k-nearest neighbors as positives and cluster centroids from the previous iteration as negatives, activating only after a warm-up epoch threshold.

## Results

On homophilous datasets, NGCE achieves the best NMI (80.5%), ARI (85.0%), ACC (94.7%), and F1 (94.8%) on ACM, while remaining competitive on DBLP (79.1% NMI, 93.3% ACC) and IMDB (54.6% ACC, 12.7% ARI). On heterophilous datasets, NGCE delivers substantial gains: 47.8% NMI and 77.6% ACC on Texas, 22.6% NMI and 42.2% ACC on Chameleon, and 46.8% NMI and 73.7% ACC on Wisconsin, outperforming all baselines on NMI, ARI, and ACC across all three. The most dramatic improvement is on Wisconsin, where NGCE's 73.7% ACC exceeds the runner-up VGMGC (56.6%) by 17.1 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGCE achieves the best NMI, ARI, and ACC on 5 out of 6 datasets, with its most dominant wins on heterophilous graphs: on Texas it surpasses the runner-up VGMGC by 12.4 NMI points (47.8 vs 35.4) and 22.4 ACC points (77.6 vs 55.2), and on Wisconsin it leads VGMGC by 5.2 NMI and 17.1 ACC points. On the homophilous ACM dataset, NGCE edges out BMGC (the strongest homophilous baseline) by 2.1 NMI and 0.6 ACC points. On DBLP, BMGC retains a slight advantage (80.1 vs 79.1 NMI, 94.0 vs 93.3 ACC), making it the one dataset where NGCE does not lead. On IMDB, NGCE wins on ARI (12.7 vs 12.5 DuaLGR) and ACC (54.6 vs 52.6 VGMGC) but trails CMGEC on F1 (43.4 vs 51.0). Overall, NGCE's unified node-guided encoding strategy proves particularly effective on heterophilic graphs where prior methods (VGAE, O2MAC, MvAGC, MCGC, BMGC) degrade severely, while remaining competitive on homophilic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising autoencoder (DAE) paradigm by replacing its learned encoder/decoder with parameter-free GCNs, repurposing the denoising objective as a node-guided graph encoding mechanism. It departs from prior multi-view graph contrastive learning methods (DSSL, GREET, MUSE) that explicitly decouple or separately filter homophilic and heterophilic information, instead proposing a unified framework where both are preserved through a single joint encoding guided by pseudo-label-estimated homophily weights. The cross-view contrast module draws on NT-Xent-style losses and cluster-centroid-based negative sampling from recent contrastive clustering work, while the overall MVGC pipeline follows the autoencoder + consensus embedding + k-means structure established by O2MAC and successors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in graph contrastive learning: methods that handle heterophilic graphs well often underperform on homophilic ones and vice versa. By showing that a single node-guided denoising framework can achieve competitive or SOTA results across both regimes without explicit separation, it offers a simpler and more general design principle for graph encoders. The stated limitation—that the method requires node features and cannot handle structure-only graphs—remains an open question, with the authors suggesting random-walk embeddings or topology-based feature encoders as potential remedies. Scalability to larger graphs (O(N²Z) per GCN layer) is also acknowledged as a practical constraint.
