## Problem

Existing IRL-based trajectory predictors rely on rasterized bird's-eye-view representations that lose geometric and topological detail, while dominant supervised (behavior cloning) predictors suffer from covariate shift under distribution changes (e.g., drivable-area modifications) and modality collapse from single-ground-truth supervision. No prior work integrates the MaxEnt IRL paradigm with vectorized, graph-based scene representations to combine IRL's generalization and interpretability advantages with the rich context encoding of modern graph encoders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes past trajectories of the target agent and surrounding agents over t_p timestamps, vectorized HD map lane segments with connectivity topology, and sampled drivable-area nodes, all normalized to a target-centric coordinate frame. The scene is encoded into a 100×100 grid (1 m resolution) that is downsampled to 25×25 for the MDP with planning horizon H=25. Output consists of K multimodal future trajectories (K=6 on Argoverse, K=10 on nuScenes) parameterized by 5th-order Bézier curves, each assigned a probability score that fuses the MCMC sampling distribution with a learned classification head.

## Method

GoIRL is a two-stage framework. In the policy-inference stage, an agent-centric double-layered graph encoder (LaneConv for lane segments, PointDA for drivable areas, FPN for agent kinematics, and graph-attention fusion) produces vectorized context features; a novel feature adaptor then assigns these features to a grid map aligned with drivable nodes, and CNNs downsample the grid to feed a MaxEnt IRL module that learns a reward distribution via approximate value iteration (Algorithm 1) and yields an optimal policy π*(a|s). In the trajectory-generation stage, L≫K plans are sampled from π* via MCMC, clustered by K-means into K proposals, and decoded into continuous trajectories by a GRU-with-attention decoder that recurrently emits Bézier control points conditioned on per-state context, reward, and location embeddings. A refinement module then retrieves fine-grained local features along the complete (past + predicted) trajectory to produce per-timestep offsets, and the final probability for each modality is a normalized product of the MCMC-induced proportion and a Hinge-loss-trained classification score.

## Results

On the Argoverse single-model benchmark, GoIRL achieves a brier-minFDE₆ of 1.7957, Brier score of 0.6232, minFDE₆ of 1.1725, minADE₆ of 0.8087, and MR₆ of 0.1204, outperforming all compared supervised baselines across every metric. On the Argoverse ensemble setting, GoIRL reaches brier-minFDE₆ = 1.6947 and Brier score = 0.5684, surpassing the strong Wayformer baseline (1.7408 / 0.5792). On the nuScenes long-term prediction benchmark, GoIRL achieves minADE₅ = 0.86, minADE₁₀ = 0.75, minFDE₁ = 6.53, MR₅ = 0.31, and MR₁₀ = 0.21, setting new state-of-the-art on all five metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Argoverse (single-model), GoIRL outperforms the closest supervised baseline HeteroGCN by 0.044 in brier-minFDE₆ (1.7957 vs 1.8399) and 0.029 in Brier score (0.6232 vs 0.6521), and beats DSP by 0.063 in brier-minFDE₆. In the ensemble setting, GoIRL surpasses Wayformer (the strongest supervised ensemble) by 0.046 in brier-minFDE₆ (1.6947 vs 1.7408) and 0.011 in Brier score, though Wayformer retains a slight edge in minADE₆ (0.7676 vs 0.7828). On nuScenes, GoIRL dominates all baselines by wide margins: it improves minADE₅ over Goal-LBP (1.02) by 0.16, and over the IRL-based P2T (1.45) by 0.59, while also achieving the lowest MR₁₀ (0.21 vs 0.27 for Goal-LBP and 0.46 for P2T). The qualitative comparison against DSP on drivable-area changes further demonstrates that GoIRL adapts to out-of-distribution scene modifications where the supervised baseline produces irrational predictions, highlighting the generalization advantage of the IRL paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoIRL bridges two previously separate lines of work in trajectory prediction: the MaxEnt IRL paradigm (Ziebart et al., 2008; Deo & Trivedi, 2020), which has been limited to rasterized BEV inputs, and the vectorized/graph-based scene encoding paradigm (LaneGCN, DSP, VectorNet), which dominates supervised predictors. The key architectural departure is the feature adaptor that projects lane-graph and drivable-area features into grid space, enabling the IRL MDP to operate on rich vectorized context for the first time. The hierarchical decoder with Bezier-curve parameterization and MCMC-augmented probability fusion extends the plans-to-trajectories pipeline from P2T with a coarse-to-fine refinement stage and a statistically grounded confidence mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most compelling contribution is the qualitative demonstration that IRL-based predictors can adapt to out-of-distribution drivable-area changes (e.g., temporary roadblocks) where supervised BC models produce irrational predictions, offering a principled pathway to address covariate shift in motion forecasting. However, the quantitative SOTA gains on standard benchmarks are modest (e.g., ~0.05 brier-minFDE over DSP on Argoverse), and the generalization advantage is shown only through hand-crafted scenario modifications rather than a systematic OOD evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the IRL advantage persists under truly novel, uncontrolled distribution shifts, how the framework scales to joint multi-agent prediction (acknowledged as future work), and whether the computational overhead of the inner-loop value iteration limits real-time deployment.
