## Problem

GaLore, a widely adopted memory-efficient LLM training method that projects gradients into periodically recomputed low-rank subspaces, lacks any convergence guarantee under standard stochastic optimization assumptions. This paper reveals that GaLore provably fails to converge to stationary solutions when gradient noise is anisotropic and dominates the true gradient near a local minimum, and asks under what conditions or modifications convergence can be restored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-parameter stochastic gradients (or deterministic/large-batch gradients) for each weight matrix X_ℓ ∈ R^{m_ℓ×n_ℓ}, along with a user-specified rank r_ℓ and subspace recomputation period τ. It produces updated model parameters with a provable O(1/√T) convergence rate for GoLore or O(1/T) for deterministic GaLore. A key structural detail is the momentum projection (MP) mechanism: when the subspace changes every τ steps, the retained momentum state is projected back to the full space and then into the new subspace to avoid discontinuity errors.

## Method

The paper first constructs an explicit counterexample (a quadratic problem with structured gradient noise) proving GaLore's SVD-based projection captures only noise when the true gradient vanishes, causing permanent non-convergence. It then establishes convergence under three additional conditions: noise-free gradients (O(1/T) rate), large-batch gradients with B=Θ(√T) (O(1/√T) rate), and isotropic noise. The main algorithmic contribution, GoLore, replaces GaLore's greedy SVD projection with a uniform random projection sampled from the Stiefel manifold St_{m,r} (via the Chikuse construction X=Z(Z^T Z)^{-1/2}), which is unbiased in expectation and prevents the subspace from being hijacked by anisotropic noise. In practice, a hybrid schedule is recommended: GaLore's SVD projection is used in early training when the true gradient dominates, then GoLore's random projection takes over in the final phase when noise dominates, yielding better empirical performance than either method alone.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-Base fine-tuning, rank 4), GoLore@20% achieves an average score of 86.03, surpassing GaLore (85.77) and FLORA (84.72), and approaching full-parameter training (86.15). On BoolQ (OPT-13B fine-tuning), GoLore@30% achieves 81.96 accuracy at 77.68 GB memory, outperforming GaLore (79.79) by over 2 points while using identical memory. In pre-training LLaMA-60M on C4, the hybrid GaLore→GoLore strategy shows a dramatic perplexity drop when switching from GaLore to GoLore in the later training phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoLore@20% consistently outperforms GaLore across 7 of 8 GLUE tasks (e.g., CoLA 61.66 vs 61.32, RTE 78.34 vs 77.62, MNLI 87.02 vs 86.92), achieving a higher average (86.03 vs 85.77) while matching full-parameter performance on SST2. Against FLORA, GoLore@20% wins on every GLUE task, with the largest gap on CoLA (61.66 vs 57.71, a 3.95-point improvement). On the larger OPT-13B BoolQ task, GoLore@30% achieves 81.96 accuracy versus GaLore's 79.79 at identical 77.68 GB memory, a 2.17-point gain that is particularly notable given the model scale. GoLore@20% remains within 0.12 points of full-parameter training on average GLUE score, demonstrating that the hybrid GaLore→GoLore strategy recovers nearly all the performance of unconstrained training while retaining the memory savings of low-rank subspace optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024), which projects full-parameter gradients into periodically recomputed low-rank subspaces via SVD to reduce optimizer-state memory for LLM training. It positions itself within the broader family of memory-efficient training methods (LoRA, ReLoRA, FLORA, SIFT) and the convergence-analysis literature for lossy gradient compression, noting that GaLore's subspace projection is neither unbiased nor contractive in the standard sense. The key conceptual departure is showing that GaLore's greedy SVD-based subspace selection is fundamentally biased in stochastic settings, and replacing it with a uniform random projection on the Stiefel manifold (GoLore) to restore convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first discrete-time convergence analysis for GaLore-like subspace optimization algorithms under standard non-convex smooth assumptions, filling a notable gap given GaLore's widespread empirical adoption. The non-convergence result, while theoretically clean, has limited practical severity since the failure mode only manifests near convergence when the true gradient is small relative to anisotropic noise. A key limitation is that the analysis does not extend to the Adam optimizer or recent variants like Fira and LDAdam, and the empirical gains of GoLore over GaLore are modest (e.g., 86.03 vs. 85.77 average on GLUE), suggesting the theoretical fix addresses a corner case that rarely dominates in practice.
