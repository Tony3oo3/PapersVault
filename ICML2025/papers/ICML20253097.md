## Problem

Existing federated reinforcement learning (FRL) methods for tabular episodic MDPs provide only worst-case guarantees, yielding √T-type regret bounds and communication costs that scale with MSA·log T. No prior work established gap-dependent (problem-dependent) bounds for online FRL, leaving open whether the benign structure of a positive suboptimality gap can yield logarithmic regret and whether the communication cost can be decomposed into exploration and exploitation components that remove the MSA dependence from the log T term.

## Input / Output

The analysis applies to a tabular episodic finite-horizon MDP with S states, A actions, H steps per episode, M agents each interacting with an independent copy of the MDP, and a total episode budget T per agent. The outputs are (i) a gap-dependent expected regret bound O(H⁶SA·log(MSAT)/Δ_min + C_f) that is logarithmic in T, and (ii) a high-probability upper bound on communication rounds and global switching cost that separates exploration cost (depending on 1/Δ_min and log T) from exploitation cost (depending on log(T/HSa) without MSA). The communication cost bound additionally requires the G-MDP assumption: unique stationary visiting probabilities under optimal policies and unique optimal actions on the support of those policies.

## Method

The paper develops a new theoretical framework for gap-dependent analysis of online FRL with delayed (round-based) policy updates, applied to the existing FedQ-Hoeffding algorithm. The framework simultaneously achieves two goals: controlling the cumulative error in Q-value estimates (Lemma 4.1) to bound regret, and estimating the number of visits to each state-action-step triple (Lemma 5.2) to bound communication rounds. The key technical tool is a concentration inequality for visiting numbers under varying policies, proved via an error recursion over episode steps that relates the policy-dependent state distribution P(s_h=s|π^k) to the optimal stationary visiting probability P*_{s,h} through the discrepancy in non-optimal action selections (bounded by Lemma 5.1). Additional lemmas establish agent-wise (Lemma 5.3) and state-wise (Lemma 5.4) simultaneous sufficient increases in visit counts, which remove the linear M and SA dependencies from the log T term in the communication bound. The resulting regret bound achieves an O~(1/M) error reduction rate per agent (versus O~(1/√M) for worst-case bounds), and the M=1 case recovers the single-agent gap-dependent regret of Yang et al. (2021) while also yielding a tighter global switching cost that removes SA from the log T term.

## Results

The paper establishes the first gap-dependent regret bound for online federated Q-Learning: O(H⁶SA·log(MSAT)/Δ_min + C_f), which is logarithmic in T (vs. the √T worst-case) and achieves an O~(1/M) per-agent regret speedup when M=1 reduces to the single-agent UCB-Hoeffding bound. The gap-dependent communication cost bound is O(MH³SA·log(MH²ι₀) + H³SA·log(H⁵SA/Δ_min²)) + H³S·log(MH⁹SAι₀/(Δ_min²C_st)) + H²·log(T/(HSA)), where the final log T term is independent of M, S, and A. When M=1, the global switching cost removes the SA factor from the log T term, improving upon all existing single-agent model-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five prior FRL baselines (Byzan-UCBVI, FedQ-Hoeffding, FedQ-Bernstein, FedQ-Advantage, Fed-UCBVI) provide only worst-case √T-type regret bounds and communication costs whose log T term scales with MSA or SA. The proposed gap-dependent analysis strictly improves upon all of them: the regret becomes logarithmic in T (vs. √T), and the communication cost's log T term drops the MSA dependence entirely. Compared to the best worst-case communication bound (Fed-UCBVI's O*(HSA log T)), the new bound's log T term is O(H² log T), removing both S and A. Against the single-agent UCB-Hoeffding (Jin et al., 2018), the M=1 case exactly recovers the known gap-dependent regret O(H⁶SA·log(SAT)/Δ_min), while the multi-agent version achieves an O~(1/M) per-episode regret speedup versus the O~(1/√M) rate of all worst-case FRL methods. Numerical experiments confirm the log T regret pattern and demonstrate that the communication cost's log T coefficient is empirically independent of M, S, and A.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gap-dependent analysis line from single-agent model-free RL (Yang et al., 2021 for UCB-Hoeffding; Xu et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2025b) to the multi-agent federated setting, specifically analyzing FedQ-Hoeffding (Zheng et al., 2024). All prior online FRL works (Zheng et al., 2024; 2025a; Labbi et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2022) provided only worst-case √T-type regret and communication bounds with MSA-dependent log T terms. The key technical departure is handling delayed policy updates inherent to federated communication, which breaks the instant-update assumptions used in single-agent gap-dependent analyses, requiring a new tool: concentration inequalities for visiting numbers under varying policies via error recursion over episode steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first gap-dependent analysis for online FRL, establishing that the O~(1/M) regret speedup (vs. O~(1/√M) worst-case) and MSA-free log T communication cost are achievable under benign MDP structures. The disentangling of exploration cost (first two terms) from exploitation cost (last two terms) in the communication bound provides a clearer theoretical understanding of where communication is actually spent. Open questions include whether FedQ-Bernstein can achieve tighter gap-dependent regret, whether the G-MDP assumption (unique optimal actions on the support) can be relaxed, and whether single-agent model-free RL can eliminate the SA dependence in the log T switching cost term. Limitations include the restriction to tabular MDPs and synthetic-only empirical validation.
