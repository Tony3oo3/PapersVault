## Problem

Multi-view camera-based HD map construction methods (e.g., MapTR, HIMap) depend on complete multi-view images and suffer catastrophic performance degradation when one or more camera views are missing due to occlusion or sensor failure. While robustness to sensor failures has been explored for 3D object detection (MetaBEV, UniBEV, M-BEV), incomplete multi-view camera-based HD map construction remains largely unaddressed, leaving a critical gap for safe autonomous driving in real-world conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB camera images (6 views at 1600×900 for nuScenes, 7 views for Argoverse2) and produces vectorized map elements—road boundaries, lane dividers, and pedestrian crossings—each represented as 20 points in a BEV grid of 0.75 m resolution over a [-15 m, 15 m] × [-30 m, 30 m] perception range. During training, a Random View Masking scheme randomly drops one or more views to simulate failures, and the model is fine-tuned for 8 epochs (nuScenes) or 2 epochs (Argoverse2) on top of a pre-trained MapTR or HIMap backbone, making it a plug-and-play add-on rather than a from-scratch architecture.

## Method

SafeMap introduces two complementary modules. The Gaussian-based Perspective View Reconstruction (G-PVR) module tiles all available views into a panoramic perspective view and generates reference points sampled from a Gaussian distribution (centered at the midpoint, variance σ²=3) along the horizontal axis and uniformly along the vertical axis, encoding the prior that adjacent views contribute more information than distant ones. These reference points drive deformable attention to aggregate features from all available views into a learnable query, which is then decoded via MAE-like transformer blocks to reconstruct the missing view's 2D features. The Distillation-based BEV Correction (D-BEVC) module further aligns the BEV features derived from incomplete observations to the complete panoramic BEV features via an MSE loss during training. The overall objective combines the standard map loss with reconstruction and correction losses (L = L_map + 0.05·L_Rec + 5·L_Cor), and the entire framework adds only 0.4–3.6 M parameters with negligible impact on inference speed and GPU memory.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, SafeMap achieves 52.5 mAP with all views present (vs. 50.3 for MapTR and 65.5 for HIMap), and maintains strong performance under incomplete observations, reaching 42.4 mAP when the front view is missing (vs. 31.3 for MapTR) and 54.3 mAP (vs. 38.0 for HIMap). On Argoverse2, SafeMap improves mAP from 58.7 to 59.7 in the complete setting and from 51.5 to 55.6 when the front view is lost. Under MapBench sensor corruptions, SafeMap reduces the mean Corruption Effect (mCE) from 100.0 to 90.6 (MapTR) and from 100.0 to 83.2 (HIMap), while increasing mean Relative Robustness (mRR) by 1.9% and 6.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeMap is compared against two baselines: MapTR (ICLR 2023) and HIMap (CVPR 2024), both retrained with their official configurations. Against MapTR on nuScenes, SafeMap improves mAP from 50.3 to 52.5 in the complete setting and achieves gains of 4.7–12.0 mAP points across all six single-view-missing scenarios (e.g., 42.4 vs. 31.3 when the front view is lost). Against the stronger HIMap baseline, SafeMap yields a modest 0.5 mAP gain in the complete setting (66.0 vs. 65.5) but dramatic improvements under incomplete observations, with mAP gains of 2.4–18.2 points (e.g., 54.3 vs. 38.0 for front-view loss, 51.3 vs. 33.1 for back-view loss). On Argoverse2, SafeMap consistently outperforms MapTR by 1.0–5.8 mAP across all seven missing-view scenarios. Under MapBench sensor corruptions, SafeMap reduces mCE by 9.4% (over MapTR) and 16.8% (over HIMap) while increasing mRR by 1.9% and 6.2% respectively, confirming robustness gains beyond simple view-masking. SafeMap adds only 0.4–3.6 M parameters and maintains nearly identical inference speed (21.4 vs. 21.5 FPS for MapTR; 9.2 vs. 9.7 FPS for HIMap), making it a plug-and-play addition to existing pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeMap builds directly on MapTR's encoder-decoder architecture for vectorized HD map construction, adding two reconstruction modules on top. It departs from prior robustness work (MetaBEV, UniBEV, M-BEV) that targeted 3D object detection by applying view-reconstruction ideas specifically to the HD map task, where static environmental semantics make missing-view recovery more demanding. The G-PVR module repurposes deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) with a novel Gaussian-distributed reference point strategy over a concatenated panoramic view, replacing M-BEV's local neighboring-view cropping with a global, view-importance-aware sampling scheme. The D-BEVC module introduces a distillation-style MSE loss between complete and incomplete BEV features, a technique borrowed from knowledge distillation but applied here as an auxiliary training signal rather than a teacher-student setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Camera failures are a realistic and safety-critical failure mode for autonomous driving, yet prior HD map construction methods degrade catastrophically when even one view is lost (e.g., MapTR drops from 50.3 to 31.3 mAP when the front view is missing). SafeMap's plug-and-play design with minimal parameter overhead (≤3.6 MB) makes it practically deployable on top of existing pipelines, which is valuable for industry adoption. However, performance still degrades substantially with 4–5 missing views (47.3 → 6.0 mAP), and the method does not address multi-sensor fusion robustness, leaving open how to handle simultaneous camera and LiDAR failures. Future work on multi-modal robust architectures and temporal consistency across frames would be natural extensions.
