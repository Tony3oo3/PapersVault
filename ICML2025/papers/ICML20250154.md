## Problem

Existing symmetry discovery methods (LieGAN, LieGG, L-conv) are restricted to global transformations that apply uniformly across the entire space, but arbitrary manifolds generally lack global symmetries. This means the reported symmetry group can be a misrepresentation of the true symmetry structure, and globally equivariant networks cannot be applied to general manifolds. The paper formalizes local symmetry as atlas equivariance and develops a pipeline to discover it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of input-output feature field pairs (scalar or vector fields on a shared manifold) and a known atlas of local charts. Each chart's feature field is pulled back to a flat Euclidean neighborhood (with zero-padding outside the chart domain), and the method operates on these localized patches rather than the full manifold. The output is a Lie algebra basis of m×m matrices (infinitesimal generators for continuous symmetries) plus a set of coset representative matrices (for discrete symmetries), together specifying the maximal Lie group of atlas equivariance.

## Method

AtlasD first trains a separate predictor network Φ_c for each chart in the atlas, where each Φ_c is a localized map between pulled-back input and output patches rather than a global function. It then discovers the Lie algebra basis by optimizing k trainable m×m matrices under an equivariance loss L(Φ_c(g·x), g·Φ_c(x)), using a novel standard basis regularization (element-wise absolute value before cosine similarity) that drives generators toward interpretable, non-overlapping standard form. For discrete symmetries, it optimizes K random matrices as coset representatives, normalizing each to have |det|=1, then filters duplicates by checking whether C_i·C_j^{-1} lies in the identity component via the discovered Lie algebra. The resulting group can be plugged into a gauge equivariant CNN as the gauge group, yielding downstream performance gains with fewer parameters.

## Results

On the top-quark tagging task, AtlasGNN (using the discovered O⁺(1,3) symmetry) achieves 0.939 accuracy and 0.9852 AUROC, nearly matching LorentzNet (0.941/0.9862) which uses the ground-truth Minkowski metric. On ClimateNet, the GL⁺(2) gauge equivariant model matches the SO(2) baseline in mean IoU (0.487 vs 0.490) while using 7× fewer parameters (111K vs 766K). On the MNIST-on-sphere task, the gauge equivariant CNN trained with the discovered SO(2) symmetry achieves 0.9381 test accuracy versus 0.6975 for a vanilla CNN, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen rotations (±180° test vs ±60° train).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LorentzNet (Gong et al., 2022), which uses the ground-truth Minkowski metric, AtlasGNN achieves nearly identical performance (0.939 vs 0.941 accuracy; 0.9852 vs 0.9862 AUROC), confirming that the discovered symmetry is a faithful substitute for the known one. Against EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021), AtlasGNN outperforms by 1.4% accuracy and 0.53% AUROC, showing the benefit of Lorentz-equivariant structure over E(3)-equivariant structure for this task. Against LieGAN (Yang et al., 2023), AtlasD achieves a higher cosine correlation with the true Minkowski tensor (0.9996 vs 0.9975) and uniquely discovers the parity coset (orientation-reversing symmetry) that LieGAN cannot recover; in the MNIST experiment, LieGAN's global rotation produces out-of-distribution inputs while AtlasD's local transformation preserves data structure. Against LieGG (Moskalev et al., 2022), AtlasD succeeds in the PDE experiment where a heat source breaks global symmetry: LieGG's discovered generators show no vanishing singular values (all singular values remain high), while AtlasD accurately recovers the O(2) local atlas equivariance group.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry discovery literature (LieGAN, LieGG, L-conv) from global transformations to local ones, formalizing the notion of atlas equivariance as a rigorous definition of local symmetry. It builds directly on gauge equivariant CNNs (Cohen et al., 2019) by proving a theorem connecting atlas equivariance to gauge equivariance, thereby providing an automatic method for determining the gauge group that previously required manual specification. The downstream application leverages LorentzNet (Gong et al., 2022) and icoCNN architectures, but the core contribution is the discovery pipeline itself, which departs from adversarial (LieGAN) and polarization-matrix (LieGG) approaches by training localized predictor networks and optimizing a Lie algebra basis with a novel standard basis regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap: arbitrary manifolds generally lack global symmetries, rendering globally equivariant networks inapplicable, yet existing discovery methods cannot recover the local symmetries that do exist. The demonstrated 7× parameter reduction in ClimateNet (GL+(2) flat-kernel CNN matching SO(2) baseline) suggests that discovered local symmetries can yield substantial efficiency gains in practical geoscience and vision tasks. Key limitations include the requirement for a priori atlas knowledge, the restriction to a subset of all diffeomorphisms, and the fact that the method does not jointly discover the atlas and the symmetry group. Open questions include extending to symmetries acting on both manifold and features, and developing atlas discovery in tandem with group discovery.
