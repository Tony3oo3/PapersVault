## Problem

Existing diffusion-based probabilistic time series forecasting models rely on the Additive Noise Model, which assumes a fixed, stationary Gaussian variance. This constant-variance assumption fails to capture the non-stationary nature of real-world time series, where uncertainty patterns shift contextually over time (e.g., increasing patient counts and deviations in influenza data). As a result, these models cannot accurately estimate how prediction intervals should widen or narrow across different regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×D} (input length N=168, D feature dimensions) and produces a full probabilistic forecast over M future steps by generating 100 samples from the learned conditional distribution. Two pre-trained components consume the input: a Non-stationary Transformer backbone estimates the conditional mean E[Y|X], and a 3-layer MLP with softplus activation estimates the context-dependent variance Var(Y|X) using sliding-window ground-truth labels (stride 1, window 96). The final output is a sample-based distribution from which prediction intervals and quantile-based metrics (CRPS, QICE) are computed.

## Method

NsDiff replaces the standard Additive Noise Model with a Location-Scale Noise Model (LSNM), Y = f_φ(X) + √g_ψ(X)·ε, so that the diffusion endpoint becomes N(f_φ(X), g_ψ(X)) rather than N(0,I) or N(f(X),I). The key architectural innovation is an uncertainty-aware noise schedule in the forward process, where the per-step variance σ_t = β_t²g_ψ(X) + α_t·β_t·σ_{Y_0} smoothly interpolates from the actual data variance at t=0 to the endpoint variance g_ψ(X) at t=T, integrating time-varying uncertainty directly into the diffusion trajectory. The training loss extends the standard DDPM noise-matching objective with explicit variance-matching terms derived from the KL divergence between the true posterior and the parameterized reverse distribution, so the denoising network ξ_θ jointly predicts both noise and variance at each step. At inference, the unknown data variance σ_{Y_0} is recovered by solving a quadratic equation derived from the reverse posterior, enabling the full reverse process to be executed without access to ground-truth labels.

## Results

On nine real-world datasets, NsDiff achieves the best CRPS and QICE on 8 out of 9 datasets (losing only on Solar CRPS, where uncertainty variation is minimal at 0.92). Compared to the second-best baseline TMDM, NsDiff reduces QICE by up to 66.3% (Traffic) and CRPS by up to 36.9% (ECL: 0.290 vs 0.461). On synthetic datasets with time-varying variance, NsDiff improves QICE by 78.3% (linear) and 88.3% (quadratic) over TMDM, demonstrating its strength in capturing non-stationary uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NsDiff outperforms all five baselines on both CRPS and QICE across nearly all nine real-world datasets. Against the closest competitor TMDM (which uses endpoint N(f(X), I)), NsDiff achieves the largest QICE reductions on high-uncertainty-variation datasets: 66.3% on Traffic (3.601 vs 10.676), 53.6% on ETTh2 (2.074 vs 4.471), and 47.9% on ETTh1 (1.470 vs 2.821). TimeGrad and DiffusionTS, which denoise from N(0, I), perform worst on QICE (often >6) because they cannot capture either the shifting mean or the varying variance. CSDI is competitive on CRPS for some ETT datasets but degrades sharply on QICE (e.g., 8.106 on ETTm2, 13.613 on Traffic). TimeDiff achieves low CRPS values but produces extremely wide, poorly calibrated intervals (QICE >13 on most datasets), indicating it captures point estimates well but fails at uncertainty quantification. The only exception where NsDiff does not achieve the best result is Solar CRPS (0.300 vs TimeGrad's 0.293), a dataset with minimal uncertainty variation (0.92), suggesting the LSNM advantage is most pronounced when non-stationarity is strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

NsDiff extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the TMDM endpoint design (Li et al., 2024a) by replacing the Additive Noise Model's constant-variance assumption with a Location-Scale Noise Model, where the diffusion endpoint becomes N(f(X), g(X)) rather than N(f(X), I). The key methodological departure is the uncertainty-aware noise schedule, which interpolates the forward-process variance from the observed data variance σ_Y0 at t=0 to the learned endpoint variance g(X) at t=T, rather than using a fixed β_t·I schedule. This positions NsDiff as the first diffusion-based probabilistic forecaster to explicitly model context-dependent uncertainty within the diffusion process itself, rather than only at the endpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing diffusion-based probabilistic forecasters assume stationary uncertainty, which fails on datasets with high test-to-train variance ratios (e.g., Traffic at 181.83×). The large QICE improvements (up to 66.3% on real data, 88.3% on synthetic) are well-supported by ablations showing that the noise schedule—not just the endpoint variance—is the critical component. Open questions include whether the pretrained variance estimator g(X) can be replaced by lighter architectures without performance loss, whether the quadratic σ_Y0 estimation at inference remains stable under extreme distribution shifts, and whether the uncertainty-aware schedule generalizes to other diffusion variants (score-based, flow-matching). The approach is model-agnostic for the mean estimator, which broadens its applicability.
