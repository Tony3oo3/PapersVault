## Problem

Despite over a decade of work on constrained reinforcement learning, no polynomial-time algorithm—exact or approximate—existed for several fundamental settings: chance constraints, deterministic policies under multiple expectation constraints, non-homogeneous (mixed-type) constraints, and continuous-state processes. This paper resolves these open questions by proving that a broad class of constrained MDPs admits polynomial-time bicriteria approximations, with matching lower bounds showing the guarantees are optimal under P≠NP.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a finite-horizon cost-accumulating MDP (states, actions, transition/reward/cost distributions), a cost criterion satisfying the shortly-recursive (SR) property (covering expectation, chance, almost-sure, and anytime variants), a budget vector B∈ℝ^m for m constraints, and a policy class (deterministic or stochastic). It outputs either a deterministic policy achieving a (0,ε)-additive bicriteria guarantee—value within ε of optimal and cost within ε of budget—or a certificate of infeasibility. The SR requirement means the cost must decompose recursively via an associative, nondecreasing, 1-Lipschitz binary operator f and a [0,1]-valued function g rooted at 0, which is the key structural assumption enabling dynamic programming.

## Method

The core technique transforms a global history-dependent constraint into per-step action constraints by augmenting the state with an artificial budget and the action with a vector of future budgets for each successor state, yielding a reduced MDP whose feasible actions automatically satisfy the constraint. However, the augmented action space is exponentially large, making each Bellman update as hard as knapsack. The paper overcomes this by decomposing the budget-choice subproblem into a sequential dynamic program over successor states (exploiting associativity of f), then applying dynamic rounding of partial costs to multiples of ℓ to bound the number of distinct partial-cost values. Setting ℓ = ε/(1+(S+1)H) yields a (0,ε)-bicriteria approximation in polynomial time O(H^{6m+1} S^{4m+2} A ‖c_max−c_min‖_∞^{3m}/ε^{3m}) for tabular CMDPs. For continuous-state MDPs satisfying Lipschitz continuity assumptions, a state-space discretization at resolution ℓ_d combined with the same budget rounding produces a (ε,ε)-bicriteria approximation, extending the result to expectation, almost-sure, and anytime constraints in infinite-state settings.

## Results

The paper proves a polynomial-time (0, ε)-additive bicriteria approximation for tabular SR-criterion CMDPs (Theorem 5.4), running in time O(H^{6m+1} S^{4m+2} A ‖c_max − c_min‖_∞^{3m} / ε^{3m}), where m is the number of constraints. For continuous-state CMDPs under Lipschitz continuity assumptions, it achieves a (ε, ε)-bicriteria guarantee (Theorem 5.12) with discretization parameter ℓ_d = ε/(2(λ_r+λ_c+λ_p)H·max(c_max,r_max)(s_max−s_min)). Matching lower bounds (Proposition 5.8) show that when m = Ω(n^{1/d}), computing an ε-feasible policy is NP-hard, confirming the approximation guarantees are optimal under P ≠ NP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper's contribution is measured against prior theoretical results: McMahan (2024) provides an FPTAS for deterministic policies but only for a single constraint and excludes chance and anytime-expectation constraints; Khonji et al. (2019) achieves an FPTAS for expectation and chance constraints but only in the constant-horizon setting; McMahan and Zhu (2024b) gives a polynomial-time (0,ε)-bicriteria for anytime and almost-sure constraints but is specialized to those types. The current work subsumes all three by handling arbitrary constant combinations of expectation, chance, almost-sure, and their anytime variants simultaneously, and extends to continuous-state spaces — resolving over a decade of open polynomial-time approximability questions for chance constraints, multi-constraint deterministic policies, and non-homogeneous constraint settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two prior lines of work on constrained MDPs: McMahan (2024), which gave an FPTAS for deterministic policies under a single constraint (expectation, almost-sure, anytime), and McMahan and Zhu (2024b), which provided a (0,ε)-bicriteria approximation for anytime and almost-sure constraints. The key conceptual departure is the SR-criterion framework, which abstracts the recursive structure shared by expectation, chance, and almost-sure constraints into a single (f,g)-form, enabling a unified reduction from history-level constraints to per-step action constraints via state augmentation with artificial budgets. This generalization is what allows the paper to handle chance constraints (previously only solvable in pseudo-polynomial time by Xu and Mannor, 2011) and non-homogeneous constraint combinations, neither of which fit within either prior framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves several decade-old open complexity questions in constrained RL, most notably establishing the first polynomial-time approximation for chance constraints and for non-homogeneous (mixed-type) constraints, which are central to safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical treatment planning. The (0,ε) bicriteria guarantee—zero value loss with only additive cost violation—is shown to be optimal via matching lower bounds, closing the approximability question for this broad class. Limitations include the requirement for polynomially-bounded costs, the focus on deterministic policies in the main text (stochastic variants are deferred), and the exponential dependence on the number of constraints m, which Proposition 5.8 shows is unavoidable. Open questions include whether the discretization approach for continuous-state CMDPs can be made sample-efficient in a learning (model-free) setting, and whether the SR framework can be extended to partially observable or multi-agent settings beyond the Markov game remark.
