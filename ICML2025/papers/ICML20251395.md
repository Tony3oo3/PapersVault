## Problem

Gradient projection methods in continual learning enforce stability by restricting updates to orthogonal subspaces, but this severely limits plasticity on new tasks. While model merging can recover plasticity, prior approaches rely on empirical assumptions (e.g., independent Gaussian posteriors per task) and manually tuned merging coefficients, leaving two open questions: can merging provably improve the stability-plasticity trade-off, and can the optimal merging coefficient be derived in closed form without grid search?

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential RGB image datasets (32×32 CIFAR-100, 84×84 or 64×64 MiniImageNet, 64×64 TinyImageNet) split into k tasks under a task-incremental protocol where each task has its own classifier head. At each task boundary, the algorithm takes two model checkpoints—the gradient-projected model θ_t^GP and the unconstrained model θ̂_t—along with their respective Fisher Information Matrices, and produces a single merged parameter vector θ_t* = (1−λ*)θ_t^GP + λ*θ̂_t. The final output is a class label per input image, with the merged model serving as the running model for subsequent tasks.

## Method

BECAME is a two-stage framework that first trains with gradient projection to obtain a stable model θ_t^GP, then continues training without constraints to obtain a plastic model θ̂_t. The core theoretical contribution reformulates continual learning via Bayes' rule, where the posterior over previous tasks becomes the prior for the new task, and applies a Laplace approximation to express the MAP objective as the new task loss plus a quadratic penalty weighted by the accumulated precision matrix. By substituting the linear interpolation θ(λ) = (1−λ)θ_t^GP + λθ̂_t and performing a second-order Taylor expansion around θ̂_t, the paper proves the objective is convex along the merging path and derives a closed-form optimal coefficient λ* = Δθ^T F_t(θ̂_t) Δθ / Δθ^T (F_t(θ̂_t) + Λ_{t−1}) Δθ, where F_t is the Fisher Information Matrix and Λ_{t−1} is the recursively accumulated precision from all prior tasks. In practice, the FIM is computed with a diagonal approximation for efficiency, and the precision matrix is updated recursively as Λ_t = F_t(θ_t*) + Λ_{t−1}, requiring only ~0.69 s per task with no inference overhead.

## Results

On 10-Split CIFAR-100, BECAME applied to GPM achieves 75.05% average accuracy (ACC) and +0.02% backward transfer (BWT), surpassing the GPM baseline (71.81% ACC, −0.11% BWT) by over 3%. On 20-Split MiniImageNet, SGP+Ours reaches 70.06% ACC with −0.26% BWT, the best among all GPM-based methods. In NSCL-based experiments, Adam-NSCL+Ours improves ACC by 6.2% on 20-Split CIFAR-100 (81.88% vs. 75.66%) and by 8.8% on 10-Split CIFAR-100 (81.66% vs. 72.91%), while maintaining BWT within −0.80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BECAME consistently outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the four gradient projection baselines (GPM, TRGP, SGP, GPCNS), the method adds 2.2–3.2% ACC on 10-Split CIFAR-100 and 2.4–3.1% on 20-Split MiniImageNet while keeping BWT within ±1.5%, e.g., SGP+Ours reaches 76.74% ACC vs. SGP's 74.97% on 10-S CIFAR. Against Adam-NSCL, the improvement is even larger: +6.2% ACC on 20-S CIFAR and +8.8% on 10-S CIFAR, also surpassing Connector (80.80% on 20-S CIFAR) by 1.1%. In the merging-strategy ablation (Table 3), BECAME's adaptive coefficient beats fixed-weight 1/t (74.71%), CoMA (74.07%), and CoFiMA (74.36%) on 10-S CIFAR, achieving 75.05% ACC with the best BWT (+0.02%). Regularization-based (EWC, SI), replay-based (LwF, GD-WILD, ER-Res), and other optimization-based (OWM, A-GEM, FS-DGPM) methods are all substantially outperformed, with gaps of 5–20% ACC depending on the dataset. The method also improves BWT relative to its own baseline in most settings (e.g., GPM+Ours: +0.02% vs. GPM's −0.11% on 10-S CIFAR), confirming that the adaptive merge recovers plasticity without sacrificing stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of gradient projection methods (GPM, Adam-NSCL) and model merging for continual learning, but departs from prior merging work (IMM, CoMA, CoFiMA, Connector) by replacing empirically chosen or grid-searched coefficients with a closed-form solution derived from Bayesian continual learning and the Laplace approximation. The theoretical contribution (Lemma 3.1) formalizes why linear interpolation between a stability-preserving model and a plasticity-focused model must yield a better cumulative loss than either endpoint, grounding what was previously an empirical observation in MAP estimation. The two-stage training paradigm (gradient projection followed by unconstrained training, then adaptive merge) is a novel composition that no prior method explicitly combines in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled, hyperparameter-free merging coefficient, BECAME removes a key practical bottleneck in model-merging-based CL and offers a drop-in improvement for any gradient projection baseline, which is significant given that GP methods are among the strongest stability-preserving approaches. The main limitation is the additional training stage and FIM computation, which increase compute cost, and the method's reliance on the assumption that both models reside in the same loss basin. Open questions include whether the closed-form coefficient generalizes to class-incremental or domain-incremental settings, whether the FIM can be further approximated for very large models, and whether the two-stage paradigm extends to non-gradient-projection baselines (e.g., replay or regularization methods).
