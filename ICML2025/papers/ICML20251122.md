## Problem

Existing GAN-based seismic waveform synthesis models produce waveforms that lack seismological realism, particularly in P/S phase arrival times and amplitude scaling, and they require extensive conditioning information (focal mechanism, VS30, etc.) that is expensive or unavailable to obtain. This paper identifies the problem as a mixed artifact of the generation architecture and over-reliance on unreliable conditioning, and asks whether a diffusion-based model trained on the intrinsic paired structure of seismic datasets can synthesize realistic ground motions from only minimal metadata.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 11-dimensional condition vector built from station and epicenter coordinates (encoded via polar-coordinate transformation), back-azimuth, normalized epicentral distance, hypocenter depth, and local magnitude, plus an optional source waveform from the same earthquake event (60 s, 100 Hz, 1–45 Hz bandpass, 3-component E-N-Z). It outputs a 3-component seismic waveform of the same format and a corresponding spectrogram (3×64×376 via STFT with hop 16, nfft 128, log-scale). When no source waveform is provided, generation starts from pure Gaussian noise, enabling synthesis for fictitious or unobserved earthquakes.

## Method

HEGGS introduces a pair-exploiting latent diffusion model: for each earthquake, a source waveform's spectrogram is encoded by an autoencoder, and a latent transform η (composed with a U-Net denoiser via cross-attention on the condition vector) maps the source latent to the target station's latent space, so the model learns earthquake-specific morphology from the pair rather than from scratch. Because no pretrained VAE exists for seismic spectrograms and separate VAE pretraining diverged, the entire pipeline—encoder, diffusion U-Net, decoder, and an Amplitude Correction Module (ACM) based on MP-SeNet's TSConformer blocks with Griffin-Lim phase spectrogram as auxiliary input—is trained end-to-end under a single reconstruction loss in sample space. The ACM, using Softplus-activated TSConformer blocks, corrects amplitude errors that the vanilla decoder cannot capture. Training runs on a single RTX A6000 for 500 epochs (~65 h) with AdamW at lr 1e-5, effective batch size 16, and T=1000 diffusion steps.

## Results

On SCEDC, HEGGS achieves P_MAE of 0.476 s and S_MAE of 0.548 s with source waveform input, and 0.503 s / 0.800 s without, substantially outperforming LDM (0.563 s / 0.781 s with W^src) and all GAN-based baselines (SeismoGen: 1.84 s / 3.33 s; ConSeisGen: 3.91 s / 6.81 s). HEGGS also leads on envelope correlation (0.819 with W^src on SCEDC, 0.877 on KMA, 0.830 on INSTANCE) and spectrogram MSE (0.151, 0.187, 0.138 respectively), while BBGAN diverged entirely when conditioned on a source waveform. The model is trained on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU in ~65 hours with an effective batch size of 16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEGGS decisively outperforms all three GAN-based baselines (SeismoGen, ConSeisGen, BBGAN) on every metric: phase arrival MAE is 2–10× lower, envelope correlation is 0.3–0.5 higher, and spectrogram MSE is 3–6× lower. BBGAN diverged entirely when given a source waveform as input, making it non-comparable in that setting. Against the strongest baseline, LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), HEGGS wins on P_MAE (0.476 vs 0.563 s on SCEDC; 0.299 vs 1.352 s on KMA; 0.519 vs 0.819 s on INSTANCE), S_MAE, envelope correlation, and spectrogram MSE across all three datasets. The one metric where LDM edges out HEGGS is PSNR on SCEDC with W^src (19.63 vs 24.66 dB — actually HEGGS wins here too; LDM's PSNR drops sharply when using W^src). Notably, HEGGS without W^src still beats LDM with W^src on phase arrivals and envelope correlation, demonstrating that the pair-exploiting training strategy transfers earthquake-specific information effectively even in the unconditional generation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEGGS adapts the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al., 2022) and the TANGO audio-generation architecture (Ghosal et al., 2023) to seismic waveform synthesis, replacing the GAN-based approaches (Seismogen, ConSeisGen, BBGAN) that have dominated this task. Its key conceptual departure is the 'pair-exploiting' training strategy: rather than conditioning on expensive geological metadata (focal mechanism, V_S30), it leverages the natural pairing of multiple station observations from the same earthquake to transfer source-specific information through a learned latent transform. The Amplitude Correction Module is borrowed from MP-SENet (Lu et al., 2023), a speech-enhancement model, repurposed to correct the amplitude fidelity that the autoencoder decoder fails to capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only station/epicenter coordinates, depth, and magnitude, HEGGS makes high-fidelity ground-motion synthesis accessible without costly site-specific geological surveys, which is practically important for seismic hazard assessment and early-warning system development. The pair-exploiting formulation is a clean way to exploit the multi-station structure inherent in seismic catalogs, and the magnitude-manipulation experiments suggest the model learns physically consistent scaling (corner frequency, seismic moment). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to a global model across heterogeneous regional networks, whether additional geological conditions (focal mechanism, V_S30) would improve extrapolation to large-magnitude events, and how well the generated waveforms perform in downstream tasks like early-warning triggering.
