## Problem

Federated learning generalization involves two distinct levels—generalizing from training data to unseen data of participating clients (out-of-sample gap) and from participating clients to unseen clients (participation gap)—yet most prior information-theoretic analyses address only the first. While the conditional mutual information (CMI) framework resolves the unboundedness problem of standard MI-based bounds in centralized learning, it has not been adapted to FL's two-level structure, leaving a gap in principled, bounded generalization guarantees that account for both client heterogeneity and data-level overfitting.

## Input / Output

The framework takes K clients (each with n local data points drawn i.i.d. from a client-specific distribution sampled from a meta-distribution), an FL algorithm comprising local training procedures and a server-side aggregation rule, and a bounded loss function. It produces CMI-based upper bounds on the expected generalization error decomposed into a participation-gap term (CMI between the global hypothesis and client-participation Bernoulli variables) and an out-of-sample-gap term (CMI between the hypothesis and data-membership Bernoulli variables), along with high-probability and excess-risk variants. A key practical advantage is that the evaluated CMI (e-CMI) bounds involve only one-dimensional random variables (scalar loss values) rather than the full high-dimensional hypothesis, making them tractable to estimate empirically.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'superclient' construction: a K×2 matrix of client distributions drawn from the meta-distribution, paired with per-client supersamples (n×2 matrices of data points) and two layers of Bernoulli random variables—V_i selecting which client distribution participates and U_j^{i,b} selecting which data point serves as training versus test. This symmetric structure yields an unbiased estimator for the participation gap via (-1)^{V_i} loss differences across the two client columns, and for the out-of-sample gap via (-1)^{U_j} loss differences across data columns. Applying the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation with Hoeffding's lemma to these estimators gives the first CMI bound for two-level FL generalization, where both CMI terms are inherently bounded by log 2. The paper then derives fast-rate e-CMI bounds via the single-loss technique (trading a square-root for a multiplicative constant on empirical risk, yielding O(1/K + 1/Kn) rates in the low-risk regime), extends to Bregman and strongly convex/smooth losses for faster K-dependence under model averaging, and shows that local and global differential privacy constraints directly bound the CMI terms. Empirical validation on MNIST and CIFAR-10 with FedAvg confirms the e-CMI bounds are non-vacuous and track the actual generalization gap.

## Results

The paper's empirical verification (Section 6) confirms that both the fast-rate e-CMI bound (Theorem 4.1) and the evaluated square-root CMI bound (Theorem 3.1) are numerically non-vacuous, remaining well below the loss upper bound of 1 on both MNIST and CIFAR-10. On MNIST, where FedAvg achieves a small training error, the fast-rate e-CMI bound is significantly tighter and tracks the expected generalization gap more closely. On CIFAR-10, where the empirical risk remains non-negligible due to the simpler CNN and limited communication rounds (300), the evaluated square-root CMI bound outperforms the fast-rate bound in most settings. In both cases, the CMI bounds are numerically very close to the measured expected generalization gap, validating the theoretical framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between theoretical bounds rather than competing FL algorithms. The paper's CMI-based bounds are strictly tighter than prior MI-based FL generalization bounds (Yagli et al. 2020; Barnes et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024), which can grow unbounded even when the true generalization error is small (Bassily et al. 2018). Specifically, Corollary 3.1 is always tighter than the IOMI bound in Zhang et al. (2024, Theorem 5.1) because CMI is no larger than the corresponding input-output MI (Haghifam et al. 2020). Among the paper's own bounds, the fast-rate e-CMI bound (Theorem 4.1) achieves an O(1/K + 1/Kn) rate in the low empirical risk regime, recovering the best-known convergence rate from Hu et al. (2023), while the square-root bound (Theorem 3.1) gives O(1/√K + 1/√Kn) without requiring small empirical risk. Under Bregman loss with model averaging (Theorem 5.1), both the participation gap and out-of-sample gap achieve fast-rate O(1/K) convergence with respect to the number of clients, improving upon Barnes et al. (2022) which only bounded the out-of-sample gap. Under smooth and strongly convex losses (Theorem 5.2), the out-of-sample gap decays at an even faster O(1/K³) rate, extending Gholami & Seferoglu (2024) into the information-theoretic framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional mutual information (CMI) generalization framework of Steinke & Zakynthinou (2020) from centralized learning to federated learning by introducing a 'superclient' construction that mirrors the supersample idea at the client level. It operates within the two-level generalization framework of Yuan et al. (2022), which decomposes FL generalization error into a participation gap and an out-of-sample gap, and leverages the single-loss technique of Wang & Mao (2023c) to derive fast-rate evaluated CMI bounds. The model-aggregation-specific results build on Barnes et al. (2022) for Bregman losses and Gholami & Seferoglu (2024) for strongly convex losses, unifying these under a single information-theoretic lens. Unlike prior MI-based FL bounds (Yagli et al. 2020; Barnes et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024) that can be unbounded, the CMI formulation guarantees bounded generalization measures by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled CMI-based generalization guarantees for two-level FL, filling a gap where prior information-theoretic FL bounds relied on unbounded MI terms. The empirical verification that e-CMI bounds are non-vacuous and closely track actual generalization gaps on MNIST and CIFAR-10 gives practitioners a practical diagnostic tool for understanding FL generalization behavior. Open questions remain: extending the framework to multi-round FedAvg with non-trivial aggregation, handling unbounded losses beyond sub-Gaussianity, and connecting the CMI terms to interpretable data-heterogeneity metrics. The privacy-implies-generalization result (Lemma 3.2) also opens a path toward certifying generalization via DP parameters in FL deployments.
