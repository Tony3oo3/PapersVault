## Problem

The optimal O(√d·h) W₂ error bound for Langevin Monte Carlo was previously established only under strong log-concavity of the potential. In non-convex settings, the best-known mixing times were O~(d·ε⁻¹) or worse, leaving open whether the √d dimension dependence could be preserved without strong convexity. This paper answers affirmatively by replacing strong log-concavity with a log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) on the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the gradient ∇U of a potential (which may be non-globally Lipschitz with superlinear polynomial growth), an initial point x₀ with E[|x₀|²] ≤ σ₁d, and a uniform step size h. It outputs samples from the target measure π ∝ e^{-U(x)}dx along with an explicit non-asymptotic W₂ error bound of the form C₁√d·h + C₂√d·e^{-λnh}. For the superlinear-growth case, a projection operator T^h with parameter ϑ ≥ 1 is applied at each step to tame the gradient, yielding a bound of order d^{(11γ+2)/4}·h.

## Method

The core contribution is a new non-convex theoretical framework for uniform-in-time convergence of SDE discretizations (Theorem 3.4). Rather than relying on strong convexity to control error propagation, the framework starts from a finite-time mean-square fundamental convergence theorem with explicit parameter dependence (Theorem 3.3), then combines it with uniform-in-time moment bounds of the numerical scheme and exponential ergodicity of the SDE in W₂-distance (guaranteed by one-sided Lipschitz + LSI). The resulting uniform-in-time rate is expressed in terms of the SDE contraction rate η and the local finite-time convergence order p₂, yielding W₂(νP̃ₙ, π) ≤ K̂₁h^{p₂-1/2} + K̂₂e^{-λnh}. For LMC (Euler-Maruyama with p₂ = 3/2), this gives the O(√d·h) bound; for the projected LMC handling superlinear gradient growth, the framework yields a d^{(11γ+2)/4}·h bound.

## Results

The paper proves a non-asymptotic error bound of O(√d·h) in W₂-distance for the classical LMC (Euler-Maruyama discretization of Langevin SDE), yielding a mixing time of Õ(√d·ε⁻¹) to achieve accuracy ε. This matches the best-known rate in the strongly log-concave setting (Li et al., 2022) but is obtained under only a log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) on the target distribution, without requiring strong convexity. For the projected LMC (pLMC) handling superlinearly growing gradients (growth exponent γ), the error bound is O(d^((11γ+2)/4)·h), giving a mixing time of Õ(d^((11γ+2)/4)·ε⁻¹). Numerical experiments on a Gaussian mixture (d=100) and a double-well potential confirm first-order convergence rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's LMC achieves the same Õ(√d·ε⁻¹) mixing time as Li et al. (2022) but without the strong convexity assumption, requiring only a log-Sobolev inequality instead. Compared to the best non-convex results, it improves upon Majka et al. (2020) which gives Õ(d·ε⁻⁴) and Mou et al. (2022) which gives Õ(d·ε⁻¹), both in dimension dependence (√d vs. d) and in the required smoothness assumptions (3rd-derivative linear growth vs. Hessian Lipschitz). The paper also extends to the superlinearly growing gradient case via a projected LMC (pLMC), for which no prior W₂-distance non-asymptotic bound with explicit dimension dependence was available. The key theoretical contribution is a new non-convex uniform-in-time convergence framework for SDE discretizations that combines finite-time mean-square convergence with exponential ergodicity, avoiding the strong convexity machinery used in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMC convergence analysis lineage from strongly log-concave settings (Dalalyan 2017, Li et al. 2022) to non-convex potentials satisfying only a log-Sobolev inequality, by establishing a new uniform-in-time convergence framework for SDE discretizations. The framework builds on Milstein's (1988) mean-square convergence paradigm and Tretyakov & Zhang's (2013) finite-time theorems, but crucially departs from Li et al. (2022) by starting from finite-time convergence plus uniform moment bounds and exponential ergodicity (via Wang 2020) rather than relying on strong convexity for contraction. The projected LMC (pLMC) extension for superlinearly growing gradients directly adopts the algorithm from Pang et al. (2025) while providing the first non-asymptotic W₂ error bound for it in this non-convex setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a notable gap by showing the optimal √d·h rate in W₂-distance does not fundamentally require strong convexity, only LSI plus dissipativity and one-sided Lipschitz conditions — assumptions satisfied by common non-convex targets like Gaussian mixtures and double-well potentials. The general SDE framework (Theorem 3.4) is reusable for other discretization schemes and metrics, positioning it as a tool for future convergence analyses. However, the LSI assumption, while broader than strong convexity, remains non-trivial to verify for arbitrary targets, and the empirical validation is limited to two simple potentials in d=100, leaving open whether the √d scaling holds in practice for more complex, high-dimensional distributions. The authors note the framework extends to higher-order LMC methods as ongoing work.
