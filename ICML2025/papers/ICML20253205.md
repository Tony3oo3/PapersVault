## Problem

Given the weights of two open-weight language models, it is unclear whether they were trained independently (from independent random initializations) or derived from one another, a question critical for IP protection and liability tracking. Prior work either lacked provably valid p-values or was vulnerable to simple adversarial transformations (e.g., permuting hidden units) that preserve model output while evading detection. This paper addresses both gaps by providing exact p-values under mild assumptions and a robust test that survives such transformations and architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight tensors of two models; in the constrained setting both must share identical architecture, while the unconstrained setting permits different hidden dimensions and even different architectures (e.g., GLU MLP vs. standard MLP). To compute activation-based statistics, the method performs a forward pass through each model on a set of input token sequences (sampled from WikiText-103 or random vocabulary tokens), storing intermediate MLP hidden-layer activations. The primary output is a scalar p-value (or p-value-like statistic in [0,1]) for whole-model independence; additionally, per-layer and per-component p-values can be extracted to localize which specific Transformer blocks, attention matrices, or hidden units are non-independent between the two models.

## Method

In the constrained setting, the paper introduces PERMTEST, which simulates exchangeable copies of one model by randomly permuting its hidden units (valid under permutation-equivariant training and permutation-invariant initialization), then compares a test statistic on each copy against the original pair to yield an exact p-value. The key test statistics (φ_U for up-projection weights, φ_H for hidden activations) use an optimal-assignment matching (solved via the linear assignment problem) to align rows of extracted matrices between the two models, followed by a Spearman rank correlation with the identity permutation; per-layer p-values are aggregated across all Transformer blocks via Fisher's method. In the unconstrained setting, the robust test φ_MATCH instead correlates the MATCH permutation from gate-projection activations with the MATCH permutation from up-projection activations (rather than with the identity), making it invariant to hidden-unit permutations and other output-preserving transformations. For architectures without GLU MLPs (e.g., GPT-2's standard MLP), the method learns proxy GLU MLP parameters that approximate the original submodel's input-output mapping and then applies the same φ_MATCH test to the proxies, enabling cross-architecture independence testing.

## Results

On 21 Llama-2-architecture models (210 pairs, 69 non-independent), the proposed tests φ_U(ℓ) and φ_H(ℓ) yield p-values of ε = 2.2×10⁻³⁰⁸ (numerical underflow) for all 69 dependent pairs, while p-values on independent pairs are uniformly distributed in [0,1]. The φ_ℓ2 baseline with PERMTEST (T=99) achieves a minimum p-value of only 0.01 for dependent pairs, and φ_JSD fails to separate dependent from independent pairs (e.g., CodeLlama-7b-hf has a *larger* JSD with Llama-2-7b-hf than independently-trained models). In the unconstrained setting, φ_MATCH is close to uniform over 141 independent pairs and at most ε for all non-independent pairs, remaining robust even after retraining individual MLP layers from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior-work baselines: Jensen-Shannon divergence (φ_JSD, Lin 2006) and ℓ₂ weight distance (φ_ℓ₂, Xu et al. 2024). φ_JSD fails to distinguish dependent from independent pairs: CodeLlama-7b-hf (a fine-tune of Llama-2-7b-hf) exhibits a *smaller* log-JSD (−10.62) than independently-trained llama-7b-hf (−11.10) and Amber (−10.69), making it unreliable as an independence indicator. φ_ℓ₂ with PERMTEST (T=99) does correctly flag all dependent pairs but is bottlenecked at a minimum p-value of 0.01 due to the finite number of permutations; the authors note that Xu et al. originally reported raw ℓ₂ distances as unreliable, but PERMTEST effectively standardizes them. The proposed φ_U(ℓ) and φ_H(ℓ) tests dominate both baselines, achieving p-values of ε ≈ 10⁻³⁰⁸ for every dependent pair while maintaining valid uniform p-values under the null, and they do so without the computational bottleneck of repeated PERMTEST evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model fingerprinting and provenance line of work (Xu et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024) by reframing model independence detection as a formal hypothesis testing problem. Where Zeng et al. (2024) computed raw cosine similarities of weight-matrix products without p-values, and Xu et al. (2024) proposed ℓ2 distance as a fingerprint signal, this paper introduces permutation-equivariance-based test statistics (MATCH + Spearman + Fisher aggregation) that yield provably exact p-values in a constrained setting and a robust cross-architecture test in an unconstrained setting. The core mechanism—solving a linear assignment problem to align hidden units and measuring rank correlation of the alignment—is repurposed from the optimal-assignment literature into a statistical testing framework for deep network weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open-weight models proliferate, the ability to verify provenance and enforce IP/licensing is increasingly critical for auditors and model developers; this paper provides the first framework with both theoretical guarantees (exact p-values under equivariance assumptions) and practical robustness (surviving layer retraining, architecture changes, and unit permutations). Key open questions include whether the unconstrained test can be given exact p-value guarantees, whether one can reconstruct full model family trees (distinguishing direct fine-tunes from shared-ancestor relationships), and how the methods fare against more sophisticated adversarial strategies beyond the simulated layer-retraining attack. The practical demonstration of identifying specific pruned layers (Llama 3.1-8B → Llama 3.2-3B) and shared components (Mistral-7B / StripedHyena-7B) suggests immediate utility for model auditing.
