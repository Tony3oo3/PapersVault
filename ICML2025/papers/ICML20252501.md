## Problem

Existing multiplex network and multimodal learning methods either train a single predictor across all layers (ignoring intrinsic heterogeneity of link patterns) or employ Mixture-of-Experts gating that activates only a subset of experts, inherently limiting the exploitation of cross-layer complementary information. The paper identifies that no prior framework enables all experts to cooperatively contribute to a final prediction while accounting for their varying proficiency across different node patterns.

## Input / Output

The method accepts V heterogeneous networks G = {G_1,...,G_V}, each with an adjacency matrix A_v ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and node feature matrix X^v ∈ R^{N×d_f}; for multimodal data lacking topology (e.g., 100-dim image and text features from VGG-16/BERT), KNN graphs are first constructed. The output is a per-node class label over c categories, produced by a softmax over the confidence-tensor-weighted aggregation of all k expert predictions. A key intermediate is the confidence tensor Θ ∈ R^{c×c×k}, which encodes each expert's credibility for predicting class r when the true class is s.

## Method

CoE encodes heterogeneous information into refined multiplex networks via a Graph Structure Learning step (SGC encoder + KNN sparsification + symmetrization/normalization), then trains a two-level expert hierarchy: low-level experts maximize I(G_i'; Y) on individual networks while high-level experts maximize I(G_i'; G_j') on networks fused through mutual-information lower bounds (contrastive score critic). Rather than gating-based competition, all experts contribute to the final prediction through a learnable confidence tensor Θ, which is optimized by a large-margin mechanism that maximizes the gap between the top-1 and top-2 softmax outputs (approximated via logsumexp for differentiability), combined with a cross-entropy term for correctness. The overall loss jointly optimizes expert training, network fusion, and the confidence tensor, with theoretical guarantees of partial convexity, Lipschitz continuity, O(1/T) gradient convergence, and a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound scaling as B_Θ·G_e·√k/(γ√n).

## Results

On the five multiplex network datasets, CoE achieves the highest node classification accuracy across all benchmarks: 94.21% on ACM, 92.27% on DBLP, 93.40% on Yelp, 78.37% on MAG, and 98.01% on Amazon, while also exhibiting the lowest standard deviation (e.g., ±0.14 on ACM vs. ±1.17 for IDGL). On the four multimodal datasets, CoE similarly leads with 81.11% on ESP, 70.24% on Flickr, 71.04% on IAPR, and 66.80% on NUS. The method's advantage is most pronounced on ACM (+1.4% over the runner-up InfoMGF) and least on Amazon (+0.12% over Mowst), indicating consistent but varying margins of improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoE outperforms all 22 baselines on every dataset. The closest competitors are the most recent multiplex and MoE methods: InfoMGF (NeurIPS 2024) trails by 1.4% on ACM, 0.8% on DBLP, 1.4% on Yelp, 1.05% on MAG, and 0.23% on Amazon; Mowst (ICLR 2024) is beaten by 8.5% on ACM, 2.6% on DBLP, 2.1% on Yelp, 1.0% on MAG, and 0.12% on Amazon; GMoE (NeurIPS 2024) lags by 3.9% on ACM and 0.23% on Amazon. Among classical GSL methods, SUBLIME and GRCN are the strongest single-network baselines but cannot handle multiplex structure natively, and HAN/LDS/ProGNN/GEN/STABLE/GSR all fail (OOM) on the large-scale MAG and Amazon datasets. On multimodal data (Table 2), CoE surpasses the best multimodal baseline QMF by 1.0% on ESP, 1.0% on Flickr, 1.96% on IAPR, and 1.38% on NUS, and outperforms multi-view methods like CPM-Nets by up to 3.7% (IAPR). The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that replacing the large-margin mechanism with Random Forest or Weighted Random Forest drops accuracy by 0.6–1.8% across datasets, validating the proposed collaboration strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multiplex network learning, graph structure learning, and mixture-of-experts frameworks. It builds directly on the margin-maximizing ensemble mechanism of Yuan et al. (2024) for the large-margin confidence tensor optimization, on the factorized contrastive mutual information lower bound of Liang et al. (2024) for network fusion, and on the variational information bottleneck result of Sun et al. (2022) that justifies using cross-entropy as a proxy for mutual information in expert training. The key conceptual departure is from existing Graph-MoE methods (GMoE, Mowst) that employ gating to select a subset of experts (competition), whereas CoE activates all experts simultaneously and coordinates them through a learned confidence tensor optimized via a large-margin objective (cooperation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from expert competition to cooperation in multiplex network learning is a meaningful reframing that could influence how multi-relational and multi-modal graph models are designed, particularly in settings where no single relational view dominates. The theoretical guarantees (partial convexity, Lipschitz continuity, convergence, and Rademacher-based generalization bounds) provide a level of rigor uncommon in applied GNN papers. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that performance gains over strong baselines like InfoMGF and Mowst are modest (1–3%), and the confidence tensor mechanism is specific to classification tasks, leaving open questions about applicability to link prediction, clustering, or very large-scale graphs beyond the ~114K-node MAG benchmark.
