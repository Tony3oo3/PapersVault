## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs target only a single structure unit (e.g., blocks, layers, or weight matrix rows/columns), creating an inherent trade-off between inference speed and model performance. Additionally, most methods apply a uniform prescribed pruning ratio across all modules, ignoring that different blocks and layers contribute unequally to model functionality. This paper addresses both limitations by simultaneously pruning multiple structure units with dynamically allocated ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA2/3, Vicuna), 128 calibration samples from WikiText2, and a global target pruning ratio (30%, 40%, or 50%). The output is a pruned model where each block receives a distinct pruning ratio determined by its transfer entropy score—ranging from 0% (preserved) to 100% (fully removed)—and within each surviving block, specific attention heads and MLP columns are eliminated. A per-channel bias compensation vector is added to each pruned linear layer to restore activation statistics without any fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework operates in two hierarchical stages. At the block level, transfer entropy (TE) is computed by masking each block and measuring the resulting entropy change in the output hidden state; blocks are partitioned into low/medium/high TE groups via mean±α·std thresholds, and a Depth-First Search solves a knapsack-like optimization to assign exact per-block ratios that sum to the target. Within each block, an importance score IS_j = H(|W_{:,j}| · ||X_j||₂) is computed for every column, K-means clustering groups columns by entropy, and the lowest-entropy cluster is pruned (with corresponding rows in paired matrices removed to preserve structural consistency). A weighted bias compensation term, computed as a σ-weighted average of calibration activations over pruned channels, is added to each affected linear layer to recover output statistics in a single forward pass.

## Results

On WikiText2 perplexity at 30% pruning, the proposed method achieves 8.71 on LLaMA2-7B (Dense: 5.47), outperforming all structured baselines including SliceGPT (8.63), FLAP (9.13), and SLEB (27.45). At 50% pruning, the method reaches 20.07 PPL on LLaMA2-7B and 8.92 on LLaMA3-70B, substantially ahead of SLEB (106.02 and 24.13) and BlockPruner (58.86 and 61.47). On zero-shot tasks (Table 2), the method achieves 56.65 average accuracy at 30% pruning on LLaMA2-7B, surpassing the best single-structure baseline (FLAP at 55.12) and semi-structured SparseGPT (53.99). Inference speed improves from 29.86 to 38.78 tokens/s on LLaMA2-7B at 50% pruning, a 30% speedup over the dense model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model and pruning ratio tested. Against row-and-column pruning methods (SliceGPT, FLAP, LLM-Pruner), the advantage is most dramatic at 50% pruning: on LLaMA2-7B, Ours achieves 20.07 PPL versus SliceGPT's 21.08, FLAP's 25.98, and LLM-Pruner's 286.07. Against block-level pruning (SLEB), the gap is even wider—SLEB degrades to 106.02 PPL on LLaMA2-7B at 50% while Ours stays at 20.07, and on LLaMA3-8B SLEB reaches 390.56 versus Ours's 21.97. Against layer-level pruning (BlockPruner), Ours wins by similar margins (e.g., 20.07 vs. 58.86 on LLaMA2-7B at 50%). Compared to semi-structured methods (SparseGPT, Wanda), Ours achieves lower PPL at 30% pruning on all models (e.g., 8.71 vs. 9.33/11.00 on LLaMA2-7B) and also delivers actual inference speedup (38.78 vs. ~30 tokens/s), which unstructured methods cannot provide. The only case where a baseline is competitive is SliceGPT on LLaMA2-7B at 30% (8.63 vs. 8.71), a negligible 0.08 PPL difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured pruning line for LLMs by unifying multiple pruning granularities (blocks, attention/MLP layers, and weight-matrix rows/columns) under a single information-theoretic framework, whereas prior methods (SLEB, BlockPruner, SliceGPT, FLAP, LLM-Pruner) each target only one structure unit with a fixed, prescribed ratio. The key conceptual departure is the use of transfer entropy to rank block importance and information entropy (via K-means clustering) to allocate fine-grained pruning load, replacing gradient-based, PPL-based, or weight-magnitude criteria. The hierarchical allocation with a Depth-First Search compensation step to satisfy a global sparsity budget is a new combinatorial formulation not present in prior single-structure pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in offering a training-free, one-shot method that simultaneously improves inference speed (by removing entire blocks/layers) and preserves perplexity (by carefully pruning rows/columns), addressing the speed-vs-accuracy dilemma that single-structure methods face. Limitations include reliance on a small calibration set (128 WikiText2 samples), evaluation limited to perplexity and zero-shot classification (no generation-quality or fine-tuned downstream benchmarks), and the DFS optimization whose complexity scales with the number of blocks. Open questions include whether the hierarchical strategy generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the transfer-entropy criterion remains informative at extreme sparsity (>60%), and how the method interacts with other compression techniques like quantization.
