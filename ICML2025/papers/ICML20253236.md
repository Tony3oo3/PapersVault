## Problem

Spatial transcriptomics data is super-high dimensional (20,000+ genes), extremely sparse (up to 90% zeros), and sampled at discrete, unstructured locations, making it incompatible with standard INRs that map low-dimensional inputs to low-dimensional outputs and tend to produce smooth, normally-distributed predictions. No prior method has addressed how to scale INRs to this regime while preserving the zero-inflated structure and numerical fidelity of non-zero gene expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SUICA takes 2D spatial coordinates of sampled spots and the associated gene expression matrix (varying from ~238 genes in MERFISH to ~36,000 in Visium) as input, and produces a continuous, queryable gene expression field over the tissue slice. The method operates in a reference-free, degradation-agnostic manner: it can predict gene expressions at unseen spatial locations (spatial imputation), recover randomly muted gene channels (gene imputation), or remove injected Gaussian noise (denoising), all without knowing the degradation type a priori.

## Method

SUICA decouples spatial mapping from gene expression reconstruction in a three-stage pipeline. First, a Graph Autoencoder with a GCN encoder (k=5 kNN graph) and MLP decoder is self-supervised to compress the sparse, high-dimensional expression into a 32-dim dense embedding that is structure-aware and disentangled. Second, an INR backbone (FFN for dense spatial distributions, SIREN for sparse ones) is trained to map 2D coordinates to these embeddings via MSE. Third, the INR is frozen and the pre-trained GAE decoder is fine-tuned to decode predicted embeddings back to raw expression, using a composite loss combining MSE on non-zero values, L1 loss, and a Dice Loss term that maps outputs through tanh into pseudo-probabilities and enforces sparsity via a quasi-classification objective. This two-stage INR-then-decoder training prevents error accumulation and avoids the instability of end-to-end optimization through the decoder.

## Results

On the Stereo-seq MOSTA dataset (spatial imputation, 80/20 train/test split), SUICA achieves MAE 5.66×10⁻², MSE 0.85×10⁻², cosine similarity 0.797, Pearson 0.792, Spearman 0.447, and ARI 0.343, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On Slide-seqV2 Mouse hippocampus, SUICA reaches MAE 0.265, MSE 0.125, and cosine similarity 0.752 (a 16.5% improvement over the next best method). For gene imputation (70% muted) and denoising on MOSTA, SUICA achieves the best MAE (4.30×10⁻² and 6.03×10⁻² respectively) and Spearman correlation (0.317 and 0.379) across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUICA outperforms both vanilla INR baselines (FFN and SIREN) across all metrics on every dataset, with the largest gaps in ARI (e.g., 0.343 vs. 0.143 for FFN on MOSTA) and cosine similarity. Against the SOTA reference-free method STAGE, SUICA wins on MAE, MSE, cosine similarity, Spearman, and ARI for MOSTA, and on MAE, MSE, and cosine for Slide-seqV2, though STAGE edges out SUICA by ~0.01 on Pearson and Spearman for Slide-seqV2. In the appendix, SUICA also beats the explicit hash-encoding INR NGP on all five metrics for MOSTA (e.g., MSE 0.85 vs. 1.14). On Visium datasets, SUICA surpasses the histology-aided methods TRIPLEX and UNIv2 on most metrics; notably, TRIPLEX collapses to near-zero Pearson/Spearman on Visium-Mouse Brain while SUICA maintains 0.800/0.660, and SUICA achieves the best ARI (0.393) on Visium-Mouse Brain. On MERFISH human heart, SUICA achieves the lowest MAE (5.54×10⁻¹) and highest cosine similarity (0.864), though the margin over SIREN is smaller, consistent with the paper's observation that SUICA's advantage diminishes when data no longer exhibits the super-high-dimensional sparse structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUICA sits at the intersection of two research threads: Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) such as SIREN and FFN, which have been applied to low-dimensional-to-low-dimensional signal reconstruction (e.g., R²→R³ for images in NeRF), and graph-based spatial transcriptomics methods (SpaGCN, STAGATE, GraphST) that model cell-cell spatial context. The key departure is applying INRs to the super-high dimensional (20,000+ channel) gene expression space of ST, which prior INR work has not addressed, by first compressing the raw space via a Graph Autoencoder and then fitting the INR in the compact embedding space. The quasi-classification Dice Loss for zero-inflated regression is also a novel adaptation, as conventional INR training assumes Gaussian-distributed errors incompatible with the extreme sparsity of ST data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SUICA provides a unified, reference-free, degradation-agnostic framework for ST reconstruction that works across heterogeneous platforms (Stereo-seq, Slide-seqV2, Visium, MERFISH) without requiring histological images or platform-specific tuning, which is practically valuable given the high cost of ST data. However, the case-by-case training paradigm (a new model per slice, analogous to NeRF) limits scalability, and the method's performance degrades on lower-density or lower-sequencing-depth data, as acknowledged in the ablations. Open questions include whether the framework can be made generalizable across slices (e.g., via meta-learning or foundation-model-style pretraining) and whether the INR component adds meaningful value beyond the GAE alone on less extreme datasets.
