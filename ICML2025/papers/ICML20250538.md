## Problem

Existing adversarial fine-tuning methods for VLMs (e.g., TeCoA, PMG, FARE) align only a single final adversarial prediction with its clean counterpart, overlooking the rich spectrum of intermediate adversarial samples encountered along the PGD trajectory that cross the decision boundary. While prior attack works have shown that simplices formed from consecutive intermediate adversaries contain highly disruptive and transferable adversarial candidates, explicitly sampling and aligning these regions is computationally prohibitive for fine-tuning (e.g., 91M images for a 1M-image dataset with κ=10 samples per simplex). This paper closes the gap by deriving a closed-form upper bound of the alignment loss that captures the entire simplex region without any explicit sampling.

## Input / Output

During fine-tuning, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet along with class-specific text prompts (e.g., 'This is a photo of a [CLASS_c]') encoded by a frozen CLIP text branch. PGD with m=10 steps (ε=2/255, α=1/255) generates intermediate adversarial samples whose perturbation vectors δ_{x,i} define simplex vertices; only the clean image x is passed through the vision encoder for Jacobian and Hessian-vector product computation. At inference, the robustified model takes a single RGB image and the same frozen text prompts, outputting a C-dimensional softmax probability distribution over class labels via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings.

## Method

AdvSimplex constructs m−1 triangular simplices per image from the clean vertex x and consecutive pairs of intermediate PGD adversaries (x+δ_{x,i}, x+δ_{x,i+1}), then upper-bounds the Euclidean alignment loss Ω(x) via a second-order Taylor expansion of g_θ(x+δ_x) around x. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) provides a closed-form expression Σ_x = E[pp^T] for the second-order statistic over all points in a Q-vertex simplex, equivalent to infinitely dense uniform sampling, requiring only four outer products of vertex vectors. The resulting loss combines standard cross-entropy with a weighted sum of ⟨Σ_{x,i}, J_g(x)⟩ and ⟨Σ_{x,i}, (H_g(x))_c⟩² terms across all simplices, where the Hessian term is evaluated efficiently via Hessian-vector products (3 per simplex) rather than full Hessian matrices. An adaptive re-weighting scheme ω_i(x) based on the prediction gap at each intermediate adversary emphasizes more disruptive simplices, and the overall objective is shown to upper-bound the robust risk R_rob(g) ≤ R_nat(g) + R_counter(g; Δ_X).

## Results

On the primary zero-shot robustness benchmark (PGD-20, ε=2/255, averaged over 15 datasets), AdvSimplex achieves 35.68% robust accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FARE (30.94%) by 4.74 points and TeCoA (27.33%) by 8.35 points. In clean accuracy, AdvSimplex reaches 60.23% on average, approaching Standard CLIP's 64.90% while all adversarial fine-tuning baselines suffer larger clean-accuracy drops. The method also leads under CW (34.93%) and Auto-Attack (34.06%) evaluations, and extends to ViT-L (59.52% PGD) and ResNet-50 (26.23% PGD) architectures, as well as medical imaging (AUC 0.579 on ChestX-ray14 under PGD) and vision-language understanding tasks (58.7% robust text retrieval on Flickr30k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvSimplex consistently outperforms all four adversarial fine-tuning baselines across every dataset and attack type. Against TeCoA (Mao et al., ICLR 2023), the gap is largest at 8.35 points in average robust accuracy (35.68% vs. 27.33%), with per-dataset margins reaching 15+ points on fine-grained sets like FGVC Aircraft (4.94% vs. 2.52%). Against PMG-FT (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), AdvSimplex leads by 7.24 points on average, and against FARE (Schlarmann et al., ICML 2024) by 4.74 points—the closest competitor, particularly on clean accuracy (56.68% vs. 60.23%). Compared to unmodified Standard CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), AdvSimplex trades only ~4.7 points of clean accuracy for a dramatic 31.8-point gain in robust accuracy (35.68% vs. 3.85%). In the appendix comparison with TGA-ZSR (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024), AdvSimplex wins on all seven attack metrics (image-level PGD/AA, text-level BERT-Attack/GBDA, and bi-level Co-Attack/SGA), e.g., 35.68% vs. 31.15% under PGD and 32.95% vs. 29.30% under Co-Attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial fine-tuning paradigm for zero-shot VLM robustness (TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE) by replacing point-wise prediction alignment with region-wise alignment over adversarial path simplices. The simplex concept is borrowed from the attack literature (Gao et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022a), where triangular regions between consecutive adversarial samples were used to diversify attacks, but here it is repurposed for defense. The key methodological departure is making this region-wise alignment computationally tractable via Taylor expansion and closed-form second-order statistics (Theorem 3.1), avoiding the prohibitive cost of explicit simplex sampling. The robust risk bounding framework follows TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019), extended to count-based boundary risk over simplex regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form simplex alignment provides a principled way to incorporate the full adversarial trajectory geometry into fine-tuning at a fraction of the cost of naive sampling (4.1h vs 13.6h per epoch), with the elegant property of being equivalent to infinite uniform sampling. However, the method still requires Hessian-vector products per class per sample, which limits scalability to very large models, and the Taylor approximation's validity for larger perturbation radii remains an open question. The consistent ~5% robust accuracy gain over FARE across 15 datasets and multiple architectures is practical but incremental; the broader open question is whether region-wise alignment principles can be extended to certified robustness guarantees or to larger perturbation regimes where second-order approximations break down.
