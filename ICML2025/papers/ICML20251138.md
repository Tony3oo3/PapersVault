## Problem

Conventional NAS for few-shot learning suffers from two fundamental limitations: it targets a single predefined task, so new FSL tasks require either expensive re-searching or sub-optimal architecture borrowing, and meta-learning-based adaptation NAS demands over 100 GPU days. The paper asks whether the best neural architectures for FSL can be selected without any training, eliminating the search cost entirely for new tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrained neural architectures sampled from a DAG-based search space (e.g., NAS-Bench-201 cells) along with a small batch of input data at random initialization. It constructs the Jacobian matrix of the network's output with respect to its parameters, extracts eigenvalues, and produces a scalar entropy-based expressivity score per architecture. The output is a ranked list of candidate architectures from which the top-scoring meta-architecture is selected and subsequently adapted to new FSL tasks via Reptile/RFS on a few labeled support samples.

## Method

IBFS combines a theoretical result with an information-bottleneck-driven zero-cost proxy. First, Theorem 4.1 proves that MAML's global convergence is guaranteed by considering only the first-order loss landscape, reducing the architecture design problem to measuring architecture expressivity at initialization without training. Second, grounded in the IB principle, the method scores each untrained architecture by computing the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalue distribution of its Jacobian matrix (output w.r.t. parameters on a batch of inputs); higher entropy indicates a more expressive architecture that will achieve higher accuracy after convergence. This entropy-based proxy exhibits smaller score variance than existing zero-cost proxies like NASWOT, yielding more stable rankings. The entire search requires no gradient updates or training, achieving search costs of ~3.82 seconds on NAS-Bench-201 and 0.1 hours on FSL benchmarks, while the selected architecture is then fine-tuned with Reptile for few-shot adaptation.

## Results

On mini-ImageNet 5-way, IBFS achieves 64.55% (1-shot) and 81.52% (5-shot) accuracy with only 0.1 hr search cost, outperforming MetaNTK-NAS (64.26%/80.35%) and MetaNAS (63.1%/79.5%). On tiered-ImageNet 5-way, IBFS reaches 72.56% (1-shot) and 86.73% (5-shot), surpassing MetaNTK-NAS (72.37%/86.43%) and MetaNAS-retrained (72.32%/86.48%). On NAS-Bench-201, IBFS achieves 94.37% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 46.33% on ImageNet-16-120 with only 3.82s search time, matching or exceeding IS-DARTS (94.36%/46.34%) while being orders of magnitude faster. On ImageNet-1k, IBFS (searched on CIFAR-10) attains 76.7% Top-1 accuracy with 0.0042 GPU-days, outperforming SWAP (75.0%) and NASI-ADA (75.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBFS outperforms all NAS-based FSL baselines: it beats MetaNTK-NAS by +0.29/+1.17 points (1-shot/5-shot) on mini-ImageNet and +0.19/+0.30 on tiered-ImageNet, while reducing search cost from 1.92 hr to 0.1 hr (19× faster). Compared to MetaNAS (168 hr) and AutoMeta (2688 hr), IBFS achieves substantially higher accuracy with 1680× and 26880× less search time, respectively. Against non-NAS FSL methods, IBFS slightly trails MetaDiff (64.99% vs 64.55% on mini-ImageNet 1-shot) but surpasses it on 5-shot (81.52% vs 81.21%) and on tiered-ImageNet (72.56% vs 72.33% 1-shot; 86.73% vs 86.31% 5-shot). On NAS-Bench-201, IBFS (94.37% CIFAR-10 test, 3.82s) matches IS-DARTS (94.36%, 7200s) and outperforms NASWOT (91.53%, 4.4s) by 2.84 points, with a Kendall's Tau of 0.752 vs NASWOT's 0.422. On ImageNet-1k, IBFS (76.7% Top-1, 0.0042 GPU-days) exceeds SWAP (75.0%), NASI-ADA (75.0%), and TENAS (75.5%) by 1.2–1.7 points while using the least search cost among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBFS sits at the intersection of three threads: zero-cost NAS (NASWOT, GradSign, ZiCo, AZ-NAS, SWAP), meta-learning for FSL (MAML, Reptile, AutoMeta, MetaNAS, MetaNTK-NAS), and Information Bottleneck theory (Tishby et al., 2000). The paper explicitly borrows the global convergence conclusion from MetaNTK-NAS (Wang et al., 2022b) but reframes it with a simpler proof, then departs from prior NAS-for-FSL work by eliminating the training requirement entirely—previous meta-learning NAS methods (AutoMeta: 2688 hr, MetaNAS: 168 hr) all require extensive search-stage training. The key conceptual departure is using IB-derived entropy of Jacobian eigenvalues as a task-agnostic, training-free proxy specifically calibrated for FSL expressivity, rather than the Hamming-distance-based scoring used by NASWOT for standard classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic search-cost reduction (0.1 hr vs. 168–2688 hr for prior NAS-for-FSL methods) makes architecture search for FSL practically accessible, and the IB-theoretic justification provides a principled reason why initialization-time entropy should predict FSL performance. However, the accuracy gains over the closest competitor MetaNTK-NAS are modest (e.g., 64.55 vs. 64.26 on 1-shot mini-ImageNet), and the method is explicitly restricted to CNN backbones, excluding transformer-based FSL. Open questions include whether the IB-entropy proxy generalizes to cross-domain or varying-way/varying-shot FSL settings, and whether the approach can be deployed on resource-constrained edge devices as the authors envision.
