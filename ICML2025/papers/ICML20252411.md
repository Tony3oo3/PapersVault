## Problem

Existing combinatorial hyperbolic tree embeddings suffer from two critical limitations: the hyperspherical point separation step (a Tammes-problem variant) is solved poorly by current methods like Hadamard codes or precomputed point sets, degrading embedding distortion, and the precision needed for low-distortion embeddings near the Poincaré ball boundary requires multiple-precision arithmetic that is incompatible with GPU-accelerated deep learning pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a (possibly weighted) tree T=(V,E), a scaling factor τ>0, and an embedding dimension n, and produces a mapping φ:V→Dⁿ placing each node in the Poincaré ball. The construction is a single combinatorial traversal (no iterative optimization of the embedding itself), and the output embeddings can be represented as floating point expansions—sums of t GPU-compatible float terms (e.g., float64)—so that precision scales linearly with t while remaining fully GPU-compatible. Pairwise hyperbolic distances are computed via FPE-aware cosh⁻¹ or tanh⁻¹ routines.

## Method

HS-DTE replaces the fixed hyperspherical point generation in Sarkar's Delaunay construction with an optimization step that directly maximizes the minimal pairwise angle (MAM objective) via projected gradient descent on the unit hypersphere, solving the Tammes problem for arbitrary (n,k) configurations without the power-of-2 dimension restriction of Hadamard codes; results are cached per node degree, bounding the number of optimizations to O(√N). HypFPE reformulates the precision-critical steps of the construction (scaling, hyperspherical inversion, distance computation) using ulp-nonoverlapping floating point expansions, where each number is a sum of t standard GPU float terms. New FPE-aware routines for cosh⁻¹ and tanh⁻¹ are derived with theoretical error bounds showing the effective Poincaré ball radius grows linearly in t, matching the precision of arbitrary-precision arithmetic while remaining implementable entirely with standard tensor operations in PyTorch.

## Results

On phylogenetic trees (10-dim, float64), HS-DTE achieves D_wc of 9.42 (Mosses), 2.03 (Weevils), 11.7 (Carnivora), and 23.5 (Lichen), outperforming all optimization-based methods which produce NaN or values >100. Adding HypFPE (417 bits) reduces D_wc to 1.06, 1.04, 2.03, and 3.30 respectively, with D_ave dropping to 0.04, 0.03, 0.03, and 0.05. On complete m-ary trees (ℓ=8, 10-dim), HS-DTE achieves D_wc of 1.07–1.12 and perfect MAP=1.00, compared to D_wc of 1.14–1.31 for the precomputed-points baseline and NaN for h-MDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HS-DTE decisively outperforms all three optimization-based baselines (Poincaré embeddings, HEC, distortion optimization) on D_wc across every phylogenetic tree; PE and HEC produce NaN D_wc on Weevils, Carnivora, and Lichen, while DO fails to converge on Lichen entirely. Against h-MDS, HS-DTE trades a slightly higher D_ave (e.g., 0.40 vs. 0.04 on Mosses) for a finite D_wc (9.42 vs. NaN), since h-MDS collapses leaf nodes into single points, rendering embeddings unusable for downstream tasks. Compared to the two combinatorial baselines from Sala et al. (2018) — Hadamard and precomputed hyperspherical points — HS-DTE achieves lower D_wc in every setting where both are applicable (e.g., 2.03 vs. 34.3 on Weevils, 11.7 vs. 18.8 on Carnivora at float64). Adding HypFPE to HS-DTE further reduces D_wc by 5–9× (e.g., 9.42→1.06 on Mosses, 23.5→3.30 on Lichen) and D_ave by 5–10×, while remaining fully GPU-compatible via standard float64 tensor operations. On m-ary trees, HS-DTE achieves D_wc of 1.07–1.12 with perfect MAP, versus 1.14–1.31 for the precomputed-points baseline and NaN for h-MDS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the combinatorial Delaunay tree embedding construction of Sarkar (2011) and its n-dimensional generalization by Sala et al. (2018), which relied on Hadamard codes or precomputed hyperspherical points for separating child nodes. The key departure is replacing these fixed-point constructions with a minimal angle maximization (MAM) optimization that directly targets the Tammes problem, and replacing arbitrary-precision arithmetic with floating point expansion (FPE) routines built on Popescu's (2017) certified arithmetic framework. The FPE approach for hyperbolic space was previously introduced by Yu & De Sa (2021), but this paper generalizes the routines to tensor operations in PyTorch and derives new error guarantees for hyperbolic distance computation under FPE representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hyperbolic tree embeddings are increasingly used as target spaces in deep learning pipelines (action recognition, knowledge graphs, visual hierarchies), but the constructive methods that guarantee low distortion have been incompatible with GPU-accelerated training due to their reliance on multi-precision arithmetic. This paper demonstrates that ~100 bits of precision are needed for quality embeddings on real phylogenetic trees, exceeding float64's 53-bit limit, and provides a GPU-compatible path to that precision. Open questions include whether the MAM optimization scales efficiently to very high dimensions or extremely large branching factors, and whether the FPE overhead becomes prohibitive in end-to-end training loops with many hyperbolic distance computations.
