## Problem

Existing obstruction removal methods are trained on specific obstruction categories (fences, raindrops, flares) and fail to generalize to unseen obstructions, while all-in-one restoration models remain constrained by their training distributions. No prior approach can handle arbitrary obstructions in a zero-shot manner, limiting practical deployment in dynamic real-world scenarios like autonomous driving where obstruction types are unpredictable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an obstructed RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'remove the semi-transparent rain streaks') as input, producing a clean background image at the same resolution. The text instruction can be randomly sampled from a GPT-4-generated database during training or freely typed by the user at inference. An initial mask is obtained via SAM2 (for opaque obstructions) or a U-Net (for semi-transparent ones), and the model is trained on 128×128 to 256×256 patches with progressive patch-size scheduling.

## Method

Instruct2See formulates obstruction removal as a unified soft-hard mask restoration problem: a mask detector estimates the obstruction region, and a tunable adapter (conv + Transformer blocks) dynamically refines the mask into a soft mask for semi-transparent obstructions, while opaque obstructions use the hard mask directly. The soft/hard switch is activated by computing cosine similarity between the CLIP text-encoder embedding of the instruction and reference embeddings for 'semi-transparent' vs. 'opaque', with the text encoder fine-tuned via contrastive learning to sharpen this distinction. Multi-modal prompts—concatenated CLIP text and visual embeddings of the original image—are injected into a Restormer-based encoder-decoder through cross-attention units at every Transformer block, providing both semantic context for reconstruction and task-specific guidance. The visual prompt compensates for semantic loss caused by masking out the obstruction, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen obstruction types without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On unseen (zero-shot) obstructions, Instruct2See achieves an average PSNR of 31.37 dB and SSIM of 0.9086, outperforming all baselines by a substantial margin. Per-obstruction, it reaches 34.85 dB PSNR on Snow (vs. 32.40 for Histoformer), 29.45 dB on Stroke (vs. 29.39 for PromptIR), and 29.82 dB on Rain Streak (vs. 29.68 for WGWSNet). The method also attains the best average LPIPS (0.1071) and CLIPS (0.9140), confirming superior perceptual quality and semantic fidelity in zero-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Instruct2See achieves the highest average PSNR (31.37 dB) across all three unseen obstructions, surpassing the strongest baseline WGWSNet (29.18 dB) by 2.19 dB and Histoformer (29.49 dB) by 1.88 dB. On Snow, Instruct2See leads by a wide margin (34.85 vs. 32.40 dB for Histoformer), while on Stroke it narrowly edges out PromptIR (29.45 vs. 29.39 dB) and on Rain Streak it beats WGWSNet (29.82 vs. 29.68 dB). The only metric where Instruct2See trails is CLIPS on Rain Streak (0.9296 vs. WGWSNet's 0.9422), though it still leads on average CLIPS. Against inpainting/editing baselines (Table 3), Instruct2See outperforms LaMa (31.37 vs. 29.83 avg PSNR), RePaint (28.25), and DiffEdit (19.92) by large margins, confirming that dedicated restoration with multi-modal prompting substantially outperforms generic inpainting for obstruction removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Restormer Transformer architecture for the restoration backbone and CLIP for multi-modal prompt generation, but departs fundamentally from both specific-obstruction methods (deraining, de-fencing, desnowing) and all-in-one restoration models (PromptIR, TransWeather, Histoformer) that remain constrained by their training distributions. The key conceptual departure is formulating obstruction removal as a unified soft-hard mask restoration problem where any obstruction—seen or unseen—is represented through multi-modal prompts (text instructions + visual semantics) rather than requiring task-specific training data. The tunable adapter for dynamic soft masking and the CLIP text encoder fine-tuning via contrastive learning to select masking strategy are novel architectural contributions not present in prior restoration or inpainting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization to unseen obstructions (rain streaks, snow, strokes, yarn, power cables) addresses a critical practical gap: real-world applications like autonomous driving and robotics encounter unpredictable obstructions that no fixed training set can cover. The paper's limitation to small/numerous obstacles (explicitly excluding large occlusions where inpainting may be preferable) and its relatively high computational cost (56.69M parameters, 146.23 GFLOPs) are open questions for broader deployment. An interesting open direction is whether the multi-modal prompting strategy can be extended to video or 3D reconstruction settings, and whether the contrastive fine-tuning of CLIP's text encoder generalizes to entirely new obstruction categories beyond the two (opaque vs. semi-transparent) it was trained to distinguish.
