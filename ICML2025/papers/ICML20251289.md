## Problem

Large Brainwave Foundation Models (LBMs) such as LaBraM and NeuroGPT are expected to bring the same transformative benefits to EEG-based BCI that foundation models delivered to NLP and vision, yet their practical advantage over compact deep learning baselines remains unverified. This paper asks whether current LBMs, after fine-tuning, genuinely outperform traditional architectures like EEGNet and EEGInception, and if so, at what parameter cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251289/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LaBraM consumes 200 Hz EEG bandpass-filtered to 0.5–45 Hz, segmented into 1-second channel-specific patches (256 patches per trial) augmented with temporal position and global electrode-position embeddings; NeuroGPT requires 250 Hz EEG filtered to 0.05–100 Hz with a fixed channel set, zero-filling or nearest-electrode-substituting any missing channels. Both models output a class label for the downstream task (motor imagery, ERP, working memory, sleep staging, or eyes open/closed) via an untrained classification head appended to the frozen or adapted backbone.

## Method

The paper systematically fine-tunes LaBraM and NeuroGPT (full model and encoder-only) on five BCI benchmarks and compares them against EEGNet and EEGInception trained from scratch under identical 10-fold subject-independent cross-validation. It then pioneers the application of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to LBMs, decomposing weight updates into low-rank matrices (ranks 1–16) applied to attention, fully-connected, and convolutional layers with a fixed scaling factor α=8. Ablation studies reveal that LoRA yields performance gains only when applied to combinations of two or three layer types simultaneously, and that convolutional (temporal-encoding) layers contribute more to downstream accuracy than attention layers alone. Finally, introducing dropout (p=0.5) into the LoRA adapters provides additional accuracy gains, particularly on memory and sleep tasks, while reducing trainable parameters by orders of magnitude relative to full fine-tuning.

## Results

Across five BCI benchmark tasks (Motor, ERP, Memory, Sleep, Eyes), the best-performing configuration—NeuroGPT encoder-only with full fine-tuning—achieves a mean classification accuracy of 74.5%, compared to 73.3% for EEG-Inception and 73.1% for EEGNet. LaBraM reaches 74.2% mean accuracy with 5.85M parameters, while NeuroGPT (full model) achieves 73.6% with 78.5M parameters. The improvement over the best traditional deep learning baseline is only 0.9–1.2% in mean accuracy, despite requiring 2,000–33,000× more trainable parameters. On the Memory task (absent from pre-training data), traditional baselines actually outperform both foundation models by ~1.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EEGNet (2,394 params), NeuroGPT (Encoder) wins on Motor (69.5% vs 65.7%), Sleep (64.7% vs 62.4%), and Eyes (84.3% vs 80.3%), but loses on ERP (90.8% vs 91.2%) and Memory (63.4% vs 66.0%). Against EEG-Inception (22,366 params), NeuroGPT (Encoder) leads on Motor (69.5% vs 59.0%) and Eyes (84.3% vs 82.3%), but trails on Memory (63.4% vs 66.9%) and Sleep (64.7% vs 68.8%). LaBraM (5.85M params) beats EEG-Inception on ERP (91.1% vs 89.6%), Sleep (70.4% vs 68.8%), and Eyes (84.0% vs 82.3%), but underperforms on Memory (64.3% vs 66.9%). The overall mean-accuracy gap between the best LBM and the best traditional model is only ~1.2%, while parameter counts differ by 30× (NeuroGPT Encoder) to 33,000× (NeuroGPT Full). Paired t-tests confirm that NeuroGPT (Encoder) significantly outperforms EEG-Inception on Motor (p=0.007), ERP (p=0.005), Memory (p=0.040), and Sleep (p=0.050), but not on Eyes (p=0.106). Notably, on the Memory task—which was not represented in either LBM's pre-training corpus—traditional baselines slightly outperform both foundation models, suggesting limited generalization to unseen paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical empirical evaluation of two existing Large Brainwave Foundation Models—LaBraM (VQ-GAN-inspired neural codebook with masked prediction) and NeuroGPT (EEGConformer encoder with GPT-style auto-regressive training)—against compact CNN baselines (EEGNet, EEGInception) on standard BCI classification benchmarks. It extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature by pioneering the application of LoRA to brainwave foundation models, drawing a direct analogy to its use in time-series foundation models (Gupta et al., 2024). Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper repurposes the LoRA low-rank decomposition framework from NLP to probe which components of LBM architectures are actually necessary for downstream EEG adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art LBMs achieve only 0.9–1.2% improvement over models with 100–2000× fewer parameters challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale pre-training will deliver transformative gains in EEG, and suggests current architectures and pre-training objectives may be misaligned with the structure of brainwave signals. The ablation revealing that LoRA benefits require simultaneous adaptation of multiple layer types (convolution + attention or convolution + FC) rather than attention alone questions whether standard NLP fine-tuning heuristics transfer to EEG. Open questions include whether domain-specific pre-training strategies (e.g., brain-inspired masking, multi-modality integration) could close the gap, and whether the marginal gains observed here would persist with substantially larger pre-training corpora or different downstream task distributions.
