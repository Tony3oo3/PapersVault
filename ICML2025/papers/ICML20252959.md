## Problem

Spectral-based neural operators (e.g., FNO) provide strong generalization and resolution invariance through fixed spectral priors but lack point-level adaptability for capturing fine-scale variations. Attention-based operators (e.g., Transolver) offer flexible point-wise processing but overfit with limited data and degrade sharply on unseen resolutions. No existing framework unifies both domain-level spectral structure and point-wise physical adaptivity in a single representation space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional state vectors defined at N discrete points on either structured rectangular grids (e.g., 85×85 for Darcy Flow) or unstructured triangle meshes (e.g., 2290–8232 nodes for industrial problems), and produces the corresponding solution field at the same spatial locations. A key design choice is that the spectral basis Φ is computed as Laplace-Beltrami operator eigenfunctions from the mesh geometry itself, allowing the same architecture to handle both regular and irregular domains without modification. The model is resolution-agnostic: trained at one resolution, it generalizes zero-shot to higher or lower resolutions and even inconsistent aspect ratios.

## Method

HPM introduces a Holistic Physics Transform T_HPT that maps features into a unified spectral-physical space via a coupling function H(x, Φ) = Softmax(MLP(x)) ⊙ Φ, where Φ are precomputed LBO eigenfunctions and the point-wise Softmax(MLP(x)) lets each spatial location adaptively weight different spectral components. The forward pass is T_HPT⁻¹ ∘ Project ∘ T_HPT(x), where Project is a LayerNorm + MLP applied in the holistic spectral space. Both fixed spectral operators (H = Φ, recovering FNO) and linear attention (H = ψ(MLP(x))) emerge as degenerate special cases, positioning HPM as a strict generalization. The full solver stacks multiple Holistic Physics Blocks (each with the mixer plus a feedforward layer) with multi-head parallelism, and is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on PDE solution pairs. A universal approximation theorem is proved by showing HPM is equivalent to a learnable integral neural operator with kernel κ(x,ξ,u(x),u(ξ)) = H(x,Φ)(x)·H(x,Φ)(ξ)ᵀ·R.

## Results

On structured mesh PDE problems, HPM achieves the lowest Relative L2 error across all four benchmarks: 4.59e-3 on Darcy Flow, 4.72e-3 on Airfoil, 7.34e-2 on Navier-Stokes, and 8.00e-4 on Plasticity. These results outperform the strongest attention-based baseline Transolver (5.70e-3, 5.30e-3, 9.00e-2, 1.23e-3) and the strongest spectral baseline SpecSolver (5.41e-3, 5.13e-3, 9.46e-2, 1.21e-3) by 10–35% relative improvement. On unstructured mesh problems (Table 2), HPM similarly achieves the best Relative L2 across all five benchmarks, e.g., 1.84e-4 on Heat Transfer vs. 1.11e-3 for SpecSolver and 2.70e-4 for NORM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPM consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across both structured and unstructured mesh problems. Against the strongest attention-based method Transolver, HPM reduces Relative L2 by 19.5% on Darcy Flow (4.59e-3 vs 5.70e-3), 11.1% on Airfoil (4.72e-3 vs 5.30e-3), 18.4% on Navier-Stokes (7.34e-2 vs 9.00e-2), and 34.9% on Plasticity (8.00e-4 vs 1.23e-3). Against the strongest spectral baseline SpecSolver, HPM achieves 14.8% improvement on Darcy Flow, 8.0% on Airfoil, 22.4% on Navier-Stokes, and 33.9% on Plasticity. On unstructured meshes, HPM beats the best prior method NORM by 32.6% on Heat Transfer (1.84e-4 vs 2.70e-4) and 22.5% on Blood Flow (2.89e-2 vs 3.73e-2). HPM also demonstrates superior generalization: with only 200 training samples on Navier-Stokes, HPM achieves 1.85e-1 vs 3.76e-1 for Transolver, and it maintains strong zero-shot resolution generalization (e.g., 5.34e-2 at 45×11 vs 9.73e-2 for Transolver) while Transolver degrades severely on unseen resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dominant neural operator paradigms: spectral-based methods (FNO, WMT, NORM) that enforce domain-level continuity through fixed basis functions, and attention-based methods (Transolver, GNOT, ONO) that provide point-wise flexibility but lack spectral priors. HPM unifies both as degraded special cases of a single 'holistic spectral space' where a learnable coupling function H(x, Φ) adaptively modulates spectral basis functions based on local physical states. It builds directly on Chen et al. (2023) for LBO eigenfunction computation on irregular domains and adopts the Transolver (Wu et al., 2024) architecture as its backbone, while the theoretical grounding follows the integral neural operator framework of Kovachki et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in resolving a long-standing trade-off: spectral methods generalize well to unseen resolutions and limited data but cannot capture fine-scale local variations, while attention methods excel with abundant data but degrade sharply under distribution shift. HPM's consistent advantage across nine diverse PDE problems, combined with its minimal computational overhead (17.4ms vs 30.9ms for Transolver), makes it a practical drop-in replacement for existing neural operator pipelines. However, the coupling function remains a simple point-wise Softmax-MLP design, and the paper acknowledges that more sophisticated mechanisms for specific physical scenarios are needed. Open questions include whether the learned modulation patterns generalize to 3D problems, whether the approach extends to inverse PDE problems, and whether the framework applies to non-PDE spatial tasks like image or signal processing.
