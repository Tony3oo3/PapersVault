## Problem

While the manifold hypothesis suggests that neural network latent spaces encode class-specific lower-dimensional manifolds, existing vanishing ideal algorithms from computational algebra are computationally infeasible for the high-dimensional latent representations produced by deep networks. This paper bridges that gap by adapting vanishing ideal computation to NN latent spaces, enabling algebraic characterization of class manifolds and their use as discriminative features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100, but the critical input to the polynomial layer is not the raw image—it is the latent activation vector at a chosen intermediate layer of a pretrained ResNet (after PCA reduction to 128 dimensions and tanh-rescaling to [−1,1]^128). The output is a K-class logit vector produced by a single linear layer applied to the polynomial-transformed features, yielding a standard class label prediction.

## Method

VI-Net truncates a pretrained ResNet at an intermediate layer and replaces all subsequent layers with a single polynomial layer plus a linear classifier. For each class, approximate vanishing ideal generators are computed on the latent activations using stochastic variants of ABM or OAVI (with PCA dimensionality reduction and tanh-rescaling for numerical stability), then pruned via a discriminative-power score that ranks generators by their minimal average non-vanishing on other classes. The resulting per-class polynomials p_i^k are evaluated as absolute values to form a feature map G(z) that is zero on the generating class and positive elsewhere, rendering the classes linearly separable. The polynomial coefficients and final linear weights are jointly fine-tuned via SGD. Theoretically, the paper encodes the polynomial layer using an InEx activation function and proves via spectral complexity analysis that VI-Nets can achieve tighter generalization bounds than the full baseline network.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), VI-Net18-Medium achieves 92.92% test accuracy with only 4.35M parameters (vs. 11.24M for the full ResNet-18 at 92.89%), while VI-Net18-Small reaches 92.66% with just 2.84M parameters (~75% reduction). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), VI-Net34-Large matches the full ResNet-34 (74.78% vs. 74.75%) with 19.32M vs. 20.35M parameters, and VI-Net34-Medium achieves 74.66% with 14.60M parameters (~28% reduction). Throughput improves substantially for smaller variants: VI-Net18-Tiny reaches ~100,798 img/s compared to 66,533 img/s for the full ResNet-18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VI-Net against the full pretrained ResNet baselines (He et al., 2015) on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. On CIFAR-10, VI-Net18-Medium (4.35M params) slightly exceeds the full ResNet-18 (11.24M params) in accuracy (92.92% vs. 92.89%) while using ~61% fewer parameters and achieving higher throughput (71,851 vs. 66,533 img/s). On CIFAR-100, VI-Net34-Large (19.32M params) marginally outperforms the full ResNet-34 (20.35M params) at 74.78% vs. 74.75%, and VI-Net34-Medium (14.60M params) achieves 74.66% with ~28% fewer parameters. The smaller VI-Net variants (Tiny, Small) trade some accuracy for dramatic parameter and throughput gains: VI-Net18-Small uses only 25% of the baseline parameters while retaining 92.66% accuracy (a 0.23% drop), and VI-Net18-Tiny uses just 17% of the parameters at 88.62% accuracy. Overall, VI-Net matches or slightly beats the full ResNet baselines on accuracy while consistently reducing parameter count and improving inference throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges computational algebra and deep learning by adapting vanishing ideal algorithms (ABM from Limbeck 2014, OAVI from Wirth & Pokutta 2022) to characterize the latent manifolds of neural networks, a connection previously explored only in low-dimensional settings (≤67 dimensions). It extends the linear probing paradigm (Alain & Bengio, 2016) by replacing the appended linear classifier with a polynomial layer whose generators are derived from per-class vanishing ideals, directly encoding the algebraic structure of class manifolds rather than approximating them indirectly via simplicial complexes or k-NN graphs. The manifold disentanglement perspective of Brahma et al. (2016) motivates the core assumption that intermediate layers organize data into lower-dimensional manifolds amenable to polynomial characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single polynomial layer constructed from vanishing ideal generators can replace up to ~50% of a ResNet's convolutional layers while maintaining competitive accuracy, offering a principled, algebraically-grounded approach to model compression. The spectral complexity analysis provides theoretical justification for why the sparser polynomial layer can yield tighter generalization bounds than the full network. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18/34, and the authors acknowledge that overlapping or high-intrinsic-dimensionality latent manifolds may defeat the polynomial approximation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer architectures, larger datasets (ImageNet), and whether the algebraic structure discovered in CNN latent spaces transfers to other model families.
