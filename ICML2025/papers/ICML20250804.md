## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning paradigms (CoT, ToT, LATS) are designed for mathematical or logical tasks with single definitive answers, making them ill-suited for complex planning that requires extended reasoning chains, diverse interdependent constraints, and coordination across multiple distinct sub-tasks. Tree-based methods like ToT support multi-path exploration but only at a single level of decomposition, lacking the structural mechanism to model the multi-level divide-and-conquer strategy that humans naturally employ when planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language planning query and an optional external knowledge base (e.g., lists of attractions, flights, accommodations) as input. The output is a comprehensive final plan, but the intermediate planning phase produces a hypertree-structured outline whose leaf nodes collectively represent solutions to distinct sub-tasks—unlike ToT which selects a single leaf. The two-phase design (planning → plan generation) means the outline is first built structurally without domain knowledge, then enriched with detailed content from the knowledge base during self-guided planning.

## Method

HTP replaces the standard tree structure with a hypertree, where each edge connects a parent node to a *set* of child nodes rather than a single child, providing a natural structural encoding of divide-and-conquer. A top-down construction algorithm iteratively builds the hypertree through four stages—selection (LLM-guided choice of which hyperchains and divisible leaf nodes to expand), expansion (sampling rules to generate child node sets), construction (iterative deepening), and decision (selecting the optimal hyperchain as the planning outline). The selected hyperchain serves as a task-specific planning outline that guides a self-guided planning phase, where the LLM iteratively expands leaf nodes using external knowledge to produce detailed sub-task solutions. This hierarchical thinking mechanism—multi-level divide-and-conquer across the hypertree—reduces effective reasoning chain length while maintaining planning integrity, yielding 3.6× improvement over o1-preview on TravelPlanner.

## Results

HTP achieves state-of-the-art success rates across all three planning benchmarks with every backbone model tested. With Gemini-1.5-Pro, HTP reaches 36.1% SR on TravelPlanner (4.06× over the best baseline EvoAgent at 8.9%), 67.2% SR on Blocksworld (1.78× over RAP at 37.7%), and 42.8% SR on Trip Planning (1.15× over LATS at 37.2%). With GPT-4o, HTP achieves 20.0%, 54.7%, and 36.9% SR respectively, again outperforming all baselines. Notably, HTP with Gemini-1.5-Pro surpasses o1-preview on TravelPlanner (36.1% vs. 10.0%, a 3.6× improvement) and Trip Planning (42.8% vs. 36.2%) while costing only 13% and 8.7% of o1-preview's API cost, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTP consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every benchmark and backbone model combination. On TravelPlanner, the largest gains are observed: HTP with Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves 36.1% SR versus 8.9% for EvoAgent (the strongest agent baseline) and 7.9% for RAP (the strongest planning-strategy baseline), a 4.06× improvement. On Blocksworld, HTP with GPT-4o reaches 54.7% SR, surpassing RAP (41.2%) and LATS (40.2%) by 13.5 and 14.5 points respectively. On Trip Planning, HTP with Gemini-1.5-Pro (42.8%) edges out LATS (37.2%) and EvoAgent (35.6%). Against closed-source SOTA models, HTP with Gemini-1.5-Pro beats o1-preview on TravelPlanner (36.1% vs. 10.0%) and Trip Planning (42.8% vs. 36.2%) at a fraction of the cost, but falls short on Blocksworld (67.2% vs. 97.8%), where o1-preview's extended reasoning provides a substantial advantage. The paper also shows HTP with GPT-4o matches o1-preview on Trip Planning (36.9% vs. 36.2%) while costing only 8.7% as much.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree-of-Thought (ToT) paradigm by replacing its tree structure with a hypertree, where each edge connects a parent node to a *set* of child nodes rather than a single child. The hypertree formalism is borrowed from Lample et al.'s neural theorem proving work, but repurposed here to model divide-and-conquer decomposition of planning subtasks rather than proof steps. The key conceptual departure from ToT and related tree-search methods (LATS, RAP) is that branching in those methods explores alternative paths for the *same* subtask (exploration vs. exploitation), whereas HTP's hypertree edges represent *decomposition* into distinct subtasks, enabling multi-level hierarchical thinking. The paper also positions itself against in-context learning approaches and multi-agent systems, arguing that its fully autonomous, rule-driven hypertree construction eliminates the need for hand-crafted examples or agent personas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6× improvement over o1-preview on TravelPlanner at roughly 27% of the cost, combined with the ablation showing that removing the hierarchical division module collapses performance from 36.1% to 7.2% (Gemini-1.5-Pro), provides strong evidence that structured multi-level decomposition is a critical missing ingredient in LLM planning. However, the method still requires domain-specific rule sets (e.g., TravelPlanner's transportation/accommodation/attraction/dining taxonomy), limiting its claim to full generality. Open questions include whether the hypertree structure can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-specified, how it interacts with self-reflection and backtracking for error correction, and whether the gains persist on tasks with truly open-ended subtask boundaries rather than pre-defined taxonomies.
