## Problem

In asynchronous MARL with macro-actions of varying durations, agents select new actions at different timesteps, so joint observations are incomplete at any given step. Existing CTDE methods pad missing observations with stale values, which introduces misleading temporal information and spurious correlations that degrade value estimation. The paper asks how to build a centralized critic that accurately represents per-agent histories without padding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251121/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's decentralized actor consumes its own macro-observation (a discrete state token) together with the absolute timestep at which that observation was obtained; the timestep is embedded via sinusoidal positional encoding and concatenated with the observation before encoding. The centralized critic does not receive a raw joint observation vector but rather the set of per-agent encoded history vectors (one GRU hidden state per agent), which are aggregated by an attention module to produce a single scalar value. Only agents that have just completed a macro-action feed a new observation into their encoder; agents still executing reuse their existing hidden state, so no padding or duplication occurs.

## Method

ACAC replaces the padded joint-history critic with two components: (1) an agent-centric encoder per agent that processes its macro-observation sequence through an MLP+GRU, with the observation's timestep sinusoidally encoded and concatenated to preserve duration information, and (2) an attention-based aggregation module that fuses the N per-agent hidden states into a centralized value estimate. Training uses a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective for the decentralized actors and a PopArt-normalized value loss for the critic. The key algorithmic contribution is a modified GAE that shifts lambda-discounting from the micro-timestep level to the macro-timestep level: the exponent of lambda counts the number of macro-action decision points elapsed rather than the number of micro-timesteps, so that longer macro-actions are not penalized by excessive discounting of their future rewards. This design eliminates the redundant and misleading signals introduced by padding while preserving the standard GAE bias-variance trade-off (lambda=0 gives single-step TD, lambda=1 gives empirical return minus baseline).

## Results

Results are reported as training curves (return vs. environment steps) across five random seeds rather than a single numerical table. ACAC converges faster and reaches higher, more stable final returns than all baselines on BoxPushing, Overcooked, and Overcooked-Rand. On the substantially harder Overcooked-Large (11×11 grid, 6 agents, 4 tools), ACAC achieves the optimal return in nearly all tasks while existing baselines often fail to learn effectively. Micro-action baselines (MAPPO_micro, ACAC_micro) generally fail to reach optimal performance across all environments, attributed to the sparse reward structure requiring long-term planning that macro-actions better capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACAC is compared against two padding-based macro-action baselines from Xiao et al. (2022): Mac-NIACC (single centralized critic) and Mac-IAICC (individual critic per agent), as well as the micro-action baseline MAPPO_micro (Yu et al., 2022). ACAC outperforms both Mac-NIACC and Mac-IAICC on every environment in terms of convergence speed, final return, and training stability; the paper attributes this to padding introducing redundant and misleading information that complicates value function learning. Against MAPPO_micro, ACAC also wins decisively, as micro-action approaches struggle with the sparse-reward, long-horizon nature of the tasks. In the ablation, ACAC-Vanilla (same architecture but without PPO clipping) still beats Mac-NIACC and Mac-IAICC but trains slower than full ACAC, confirming the benefit of the PPO-style objective. On Overcooked-Large, the performance gap widens further, with baselines failing to learn while ACAC reaches optimal returns, suggesting that longer macro-actions make padding-based architectures particularly unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MacDec-POMDP framework for asynchronous MARL established by Xiao et al. (2020a,b, 2022), which previously relied on padding missing macro-observations to apply synchronous CTDE methods. The key architectural departure is replacing padding with per-agent GRU encoders (augmented with sinusoidal timestep embeddings) and an attention-based aggregation module, eliminating redundant duplicate observations in the centralized critic. The training algorithm builds on MAPPO's PPO objective and PopArt normalization (Yu et al., 2022), with a novel modification to GAE that shifts λ-discounting from micro-timesteps to macro-timesteps to respect variable macro-action durations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The agent-centric encoding design offers a principled alternative to padding for any CTDE method operating in asynchronous or temporally-abstracted settings, and the macro-level GAE modification is a reusable insight for hierarchical RL more broadly. However, the approach is limited to pre-defined discrete macro-actions with fixed intra-option policies; extending it to continuous action spaces or learned macro-actions remains open, and the authors note the absence of suitable benchmarks for such settings. The practical impact is strongest for domains with naturally variable-duration actions (robotics, logistics, scheduling), where asynchrony is inherent rather than artificial.
