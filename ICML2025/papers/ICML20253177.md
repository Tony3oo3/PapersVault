## Problem

Flow Matching (FM) has become a highly effective training procedure for Continuous Normalizing Flows, yet its theoretical guarantees remain incomplete. Prior analyses either assumed the velocity field is already accurately estimated (bypassing the learning step) or imposed strong, hard-to-verify Lipschitz conditions on the velocity field itself. This paper provides the first end-to-end error analysis, closing the gap between learning the velocity field from finite samples and the resulting distribution recovery quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from the target distribution π₁ (assumed supported on [0,1]ᵈ) and m samples from a standard Gaussian prior N(0, I_d) paired with uniform time draws from [0, T]. It outputs generated samples from the learned distribution at an early stopping time T(n) < 1, with the Wasserstein-2 distance to π₁ bounded in probability. The stopping time and Euler discretization step size are chosen as explicit functions of n and d (e.g., T(n) = 1 − (log n)^(−1/6) for consistency, or T(n) = 1 − n^(−1/(3(d+5))) for the improved rate), reflecting a fundamental tradeoff between estimation accuracy and distribution recovery.

## Method

The analysis decomposes the total error into three components: (1) approximation, where the true velocity field v*(x,t) = E[X₁−X₀ | X_t = x] is shown to be approximable by a Lipschitz-constrained ReLU network under the L²(π_t) norm, with explicit width, depth, and sparsity scaling in the Lipschitz constant ζ and dimension d; (2) generalization, where Empirical Risk Minimization over the network class is controlled via covering-number arguments, yielding the first explicit sample complexity bound for FM of order Õ(ζ^{d/2}/(1−T)⁴ · n^{−2/(d+5)}); and (3) sampling, where the Wasserstein-2 distance between the generated and target distributions is bounded using a Gronwall-type argument on the ODE flow, incorporating both the velocity estimation error and Euler discretization error. A key technical contribution is deriving the Lipschitz continuity of v* from the target distribution's properties (bounded support alone, or bounded support plus a Lipschitz score condition) rather than assuming it, which enables the improved convergence rate Õ(n^{−1/(3(d+5))}) under the stronger assumption.

## Results

The paper establishes the first end-to-end convergence guarantee for Flow Matching-based CNFs. Under a bounded support assumption alone (Theorem 1.6), the generated distribution converges to the target in Wasserstein-2 distance: W₂(π̃_{T(n)}, π₁) → 0 in probability, with step size O(n^{-1/(d+5)}) and early stopping T(n) = 1 − (log n)^{-1/6}. Under an additional Lipschitz score condition (Theorem 1.7), the rate improves to W₂(π̃_{T(n)}, π₁) = Õ(n^{-1/(3(d+5))}) with probability at least 1 − 1/n, using step size O(n^{-4/(3(d+5))}) and T(n) = 1 − n^{-1/(3(d+5))}. The generalization bound (Theorem 4.4) gives the first explicit sample complexity for FM: the L²(π_t) velocity field estimation error is Õ(ζ^{d/2}/(1−T)⁴ · n^{-2/(d+5)}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three prior theoretical works (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden 2023; Chen et al. 2023c; Lu et al. 2022) provide only partial guarantees: they assume the estimated velocity field or score function is already Lipschitz continuous and bound the distributional error conditional on that assumption, but do not analyze the learning step (i.e., whether a neural network can actually achieve the required Lipschitz approximation from finite samples). This paper is the first to provide a full end-to-end analysis covering approximation, generalization, and sampling error simultaneously. Compared to Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden (2023), who give a W₂² bound in terms of the velocity field regression objective H(v̂) under an assumed Lipschitz constant K̂, this paper derives the Lipschitz constant from the target distribution's properties and provides an explicit sample complexity. Compared to Chen et al. (2023c), who bound KL divergence under a strong score regularity condition, this paper works directly with W₂ distance under a milder bounded-support assumption. Compared to Lu et al. (2022), who control Fisher divergence under bounded Hessian assumptions, this paper achieves a concrete W₂ rate of Õ(n^{-1/(3(d+5))}) under a standard log-concavity-type condition (−αI ⪯ ∇²V ⪯ αI). The key distinction is that all prior results are conditional on the velocity field being well-estimated, whereas this paper closes the loop by showing a Lipschitz ReLU network can achieve the required approximation with polynomial sample complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) and the CNF/ODE-based generative modeling paradigm (Chen et al., 2018), providing the first end-to-end theoretical analysis that unifies three previously disconnected components: neural network approximation of the velocity field, statistical generalization of the learned field, and distribution recovery via ODE sampling. It departs from prior theoretical analyses (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Chen et al., 2023c; Lu et al., 2022) in two key ways: (1) those works assumed the velocity field was already accurately estimated or assumed Lipschitz continuity as a given, whereas this paper derives the Lipschitz property of the velocity field from mild properties of the target distribution (bounded support, or bounded Hessian of the potential); and (2) it closes the learning gap by providing explicit sample complexity bounds via covering number arguments for the ERM procedure. The Lipschitz ReLU network construction follows Chen et al. (2023a), but the key distinction is that the Lipschitz constant is derived from the data distribution rather than assumed uniformly over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous justification that Flow Matching-based CNFs converge to the target distribution under only a bounded support assumption, and quantifies the convergence rate as Õ(n^{-1/(3(d+5))}) under an additional Lipschitz score condition. For the theory community, it establishes a complete pipeline (approximation → generalization → sampling) that was previously fragmented across separate analyses. However, the bounded support assumption is restrictive for many practical distributions (e.g., unbounded image data), and the polynomial dependence on dimension d limits the rate's informativeness in high-dimensional settings. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to unbounded or multi-modal targets, whether the rate can be improved with better interpolants or network architectures, and whether the theoretical guarantees translate to practical guidance for choosing early stopping time T and discretization step size in real FM implementations.
