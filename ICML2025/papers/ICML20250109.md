## Problem

The Ehrenfeucht-Haussler rank of a Boolean function is a classical measure from PAC learning and proof complexity, but its relationship to the computational power of modern Transformer architectures remained unexplored. This paper asks: what is the exact number of Chain of Thought (CoT) steps a single-layer Transformer with hard attention needs to compute a given function, and how does this relate to the function's rank? No prior work had provided an exact characterization of decoder expressiveness for a fixed number of iterations.

## Input / Output

The general setting considers functions f: Σ₁ × ... × Σₙ → O over arbitrary finite alphabets, with inputs encoded as d-dimensional vectors via a positional encoding plus an end-of-line token. Two concrete problems are studied: t-Comp takes a sequence of n integers from {1,...,n} (interpreted as a function φ) and outputs φ^(t)(1), while k-thOne takes a Boolean sequence of length n and outputs the position of the k-th 1 (or n+1 if fewer than k ones exist). The decoder produces a sequence of d-dimensional output vectors, one per CoT iteration, from which the final function value is extracted by an output map.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 4.1) proves that the rank rk(f) of any function equals the minimum number of CoT iterations dd^(1)(f) of a single-layer, single-head hard-attention decoder. The upper bound shows each CoT step can be simulated by one assignment query (a-query), while the lower bound constructs a decoder whose attention mechanism selects the correct a-query answer and whose MLP (via ReLU) computes the next decision-tree node as a one-hot vector. For multi-head decoders, the paper defines H-head rank rk^(H)(f) and proves the analogous equality rk^(H)(f) = dd^(H)(f). Tight lower bounds are then established: for t-Comp, a communication-complexity reduction from pointer chasing shows rk^(H)(t-Comp) = t for all H; for k-thOne, a combinatorial self-reducibility argument (since communication complexity is insufficient) proves rk^(H)(k-thOne) = k for all H and large n.

## Results

The paper establishes an exact equivalence between Ehrenfeucht-Haussler rank and decoder depth: rk(f) = dd^(1)(f) for single-head decoders (Theorem 4.1), and rk^(H)(f) = dd^(H)(f) for H-head decoders (Theorem 5.2). For the t-fold iterated composition function, the paper proves rk^(H)(t-Comp_n) = t for all H and all sufficiently large n (Corollary 5.5), showing that even unlimited attention heads cannot reduce the number of CoT steps below t. Similarly, for the k-thOne function, rk^(H)(k-thOne_n) = k for all H and all sufficiently large n (Theorem 5.6). A general upper bound rk(f) ≤ H · rk^(H)(f) (Proposition 5.3) shows multi-head attention can reduce CoT steps by at most a factor of H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The closest prior result is Peng et al. (2024), who showed that single-layer decoders with soft attention require Ω(t) iterations to compute t-Comp, but under assumptions on embedding dimension d, number of heads H, and bit precision p (specifically t = √(n/(dHp))). The present paper's result is stronger in that it gives the exact value t (not just a lower bound) and requires no assumptions on dimension or precision, working in the hard-attention setting. The paper also contrasts its communication-complexity-based lower bound technique (Lemma 5.4) with the combinatorial argument used for the single-head case (Proposition 2.3), noting that the combinatorial approach is insufficient for multi-head lower bounds on t-Comp but a more intricate combinatorial argument suffices for k-thOne (Theorem 5.6), where communication complexity tools are inapplicable due to the existence of 2-round O(k log n) protocols (Proposition 5.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of research: the Ehrenfeucht-Haussler rank from PAC learning and decision tree theory, and the expressive power analysis of Transformer decoders. Prior work (Pérez et al., Merrill & Sabharwal, Liu et al.) characterized what decoders can compute with unbounded or polynomially many iterations, but no prior result gave an exact characterization for a fixed number of iterations. The paper also draws on communication complexity (Kushilevitz & Nisan; Duris et al.) as a proof technique for multi-head lower bounds, specifically reducing from the pointer-chasing problem to show that additional attention heads cannot reduce the required number of CoT steps for iterated composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact equivalence between rank and CoT steps provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding why certain problems are inherently sequential for Transformers, even with parallel attention heads. This has implications for interpreting the limitations of chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs: some tasks fundamentally require a fixed number of sequential steps regardless of model width. Key open questions include extending the characterization to multi-layer architectures and soft attention, where the connection to rank is expected to break down. The paper also notes that concurrent work (Chen et al.) addresses multi-layer soft-attention lower bounds for a different formulation of iterated composition, suggesting the full picture remains incomplete.
