## Problem

In the Local Glivenko-Cantelli (LGC) setting, Cohen & Kontorovich (2023) characterized which infinite-dimensional product Bernoulli distributions are learnable by the Empirical Mean Estimator (EME) and at what rate. However, it remained open whether allowing arbitrary estimators could expand the learnable family beyond LGC or yield strictly better risk decay rates. This paper resolves both questions in the negative (modulo infinite-dimensional pathologies), establishing that the EME is minimax optimal and LGC is essentially the largest learnable class.

## Input / Output

The input consists of n i.i.d. draws X^(1),…,X^(n) from a product Bernoulli measure μ(p) on {0,1}^N, where p ∈ [0,1]^N is an infinite sequence of success probabilities. The estimator p̃ maps the n×N binary data matrix to [0,1]^N, and the risk is the expected uniform deviation E‖p̃ − p‖_∞. A family P ⊂ [0,1]^N is deemed learnable if this risk vanishes as n→∞ for every p ∈ P; the paper characterizes exactly which families admit such an estimator and at what minimax rate.

## Method

The proof of Theorem 2.1 (LGC maximality) reduces estimation to recovering a random sign vector via the Neyman-Pearson lemma, then applies the second Borel-Cantelli lemma to show that any p* outside LGC forces a constant lower bound on the error. Theorem 2.2 (minimax lower bound) constructs J+1 step-profile hypotheses with a single bump and applies the generalized Fano method (Yu 1997) with KL-divergence bounds on the product measures, yielding a lower bound of order √(s/n) ∨ (t/n)·Q(t,s) that matches the EME's rate. Theorem 2.3 (expansion via structure) introduces a Borel-Cantelli-based test Φ that detects whether the underlying sequence is constant (T(p)=∞) versus decaying (T(p)<∞), switching between a global average estimator and the standard EME accordingly, thereby making LGC ∪ const learnable.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. Theorem 2.1 shows that under decaying and strong-symmetry conditions, LGC (characterized by T(p) < ∞) is the largest learnable family—no estimator can learn a strictly larger class. Theorem 2.2 proves the EME is minimax optimal: for any estimator, the worst-case risk over P_{s,t} is lower bounded by c√(s/n) ∨ Q(t,s)·t/n, matching the EME's known √(S(p)/n) decay rate up to constants. Theorem 2.3 shows that relaxing the decay/symmetry restrictions allows non-trivial expansion: LGC ∪ {(c,c,…) : c ∈ [0,1]} is learnable via a test-and-estimate procedure.

**Table 1: Summary of main theoretical results.**

| Result | Statement | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Thm 2.1 (Maximality) | No estimator learns a family P ⊄ LGC | P decaying + strongly symmetric about 1/2 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the Empirical Mean Estimator (EME) from Cohen & Kontorovich (2023) and arbitrary estimators. The paper proves the EME is minimax optimal: no alternative estimator can achieve a better worst-case risk decay rate over non-pathological (decaying, strongly symmetric) distribution families (Theorem 2.2). In terms of learnability, the EME's natural domain LGC is shown to be maximal—no other estimator can expand the class of learnable measures under the same regularity conditions (Theorem 2.1). The only setting where the EME is 'beaten' is when the learner exploits infinite-dimensional structure (e.g., testing for constant sequences), where a hybrid estimator outperforms the pure EME (Theorem 2.3, Appendix A.2 simulations). The simulations in Appendix A.1 confirm the tightness of the minimax lower bound, with empirical deviations closely tracking the theoretical √(s/n) prediction across q ∈ {0.002, …, 0.2}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Local Glivenko-Cantelli (LGC) framework introduced by Cohen & Kontorovich (2023), which characterized the family of infinite-dimensional product distributions for which the Empirical Mean Estimator (EME) achieves uniform convergence. While prior work (Blanchard & Voráček 2024; Blanchard et al. 2024) tightened finite-sample bounds and extended analysis to correlated coordinates, this paper asks a fundamentally different question: whether allowing arbitrary estimators (beyond the EME) can expand the learnable family or improve decay rates. The answer, under mild regularity conditions (decay and strong symmetry), is a clean negative: LGC is essentially maximal and the EME is minimax-optimal, with the proof relying on a reduction to sign-recovery via the Neyman-Pearson lemma and Borel-Cantelli arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result that the EME is minimax-optimal for LGC provides a clean optimality certificate for the most natural estimator in this infinite-dimensional setting, closing a natural question left open by the original LGC paper. The positive result (Theorem 2.3) showing that infinite-dimensional 'pathologies' (exploiting the full bit-string of each sample) can non-trivially expand LGC highlights a sharp boundary between structured and unstructured learning in infinite dimensions. Open questions include whether the decay and symmetry conditions in Theorem 2.1 are truly necessary, and whether richer structured families beyond constant sequences can be adjoined to LGC while preserving learnability.
