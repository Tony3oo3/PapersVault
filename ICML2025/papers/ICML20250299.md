## Problem

DNA-Encoded Library (DEL) data is intrinsically noisy due to synthesis variability, PCR amplification bias, and the gap between on-DNA binding (measured in DEL screens) and off-DNA binding (relevant for drug candidates), yet large publicly available DEL datasets with orthogonal biophysical validation are scarce. This limits the development and fair comparison of machine learning methods for denoising DEL data and predicting true binding affinity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume DEL sequence counts (number of unique redundant DNA encodings per molecule, not raw read counts) as supervision signal, with molecular representations ranging from 2048-bit Morgan fingerprints (radius 2) to full molecular graphs and synthon-level decompositions (A, B, C positions). The output is a predicted enrichment score (Poisson enrichment or zero-inflated Poisson likelihood) used to rank molecules by binding affinity, evaluated via Spearman correlation against experimentally measured KD values from fluorescence polarization (on-DNA) and surface plasmon resonance (off-DNA) assays on 30–50 resynthesized compounds per target.

## Method

The paper benchmarks a suite of models trained on the top 1M compounds of the 81M-member KinDEL trisynthon library against two kinase targets (MAPK14, DDR1), including tree-based models (RF, XGBoost), kNN, a 5-layer DNN on Morgan fingerprints, graph neural networks (GIN, Chemprop DMPNN), and the probabilistic DEL-Compose model. DEL-Compose is the key methodological contribution: it parameterizes a zero-inflated Poisson distribution over observed sequence counts, with two encoding variants — full-molecule (M) using a 4-layer MLP on Morgan fingerprints, and synthon-based (S) that embeds each synthon independently, processes pairwise and triple combinations (AB, BC, AC, ABC) through 2-layer MLPs, and aggregates via 4-head attention pooling. Three data-splitting strategies (random, disynthon, and UMAP+HDBSCAN cluster) stress-test generalization, with the disynthon split removing entire synthon combinations from training to force out-of-distribution inference. The paper also releases docked 3D poses (top 3 per receptor, 6 receptor conformations) generated with Uni-Dock/Vina to enable structure-based modeling.

## Results

On DDR1 (random split), DEL-Compose(M) achieves the highest Spearman's ρ of 0.731 (on-DNA, n=30) and 0.509 (off-DNA, n=33), outperforming all other baselines including Chemprop (0.729/0.335) and DNN (0.703/0.335). On MAPK14 (random split), DEL-Compose(M) achieves 0.756 off-DNA Spearman's ρ (n=33), substantially exceeding the Poisson enrichment baseline (0.166) and all other ML models (best: DNN at 0.514). Across all three data splits (random, cluster, disynthon), DEL-Compose consistently ranks validation compounds off-DNA better than raw DEL enrichment metrics, demonstrating that structure-based probabilistic models can denoise DEL data for drug-relevant predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEL-Compose(M) outperforms all discriminative baselines (RF, XGBoost, kNN, DNN, GIN, Chemprop) on off-DNA Spearman's ρ for DDR1 across all three splits (e.g., 0.509 vs. best baseline 0.335 for Chemprop/DNN on random split), with a gap of ~0.17–0.20. On MAPK14, DEL-Compose(M) similarly dominates off-DNA (0.756 random, 0.730 cluster, 0.716 disynthon) versus the next-best DNN (0.514/0.592/0.429). The Poisson enrichment baseline, while strong on on-DNA (0.779 for DDR1), drops to 0.441 off-DNA, confirming that raw DEL counts are biased by the DNA tag and that structure-based models provide meaningful denoising. For on-DNA predictions, simpler models like RF and kNN are competitive with DEL-Compose on MAPK14 (0.694/0.649 vs. 0.448), but DEL-Compose still leads on DDR1 (0.731 vs. 0.578 for RF). On the challenging disynthon split (out-of-distribution), all models degrade substantially, but DEL-Compose(M) retains the highest off-DNA correlation for both targets, suggesting its probabilistic structure provides better generalization to unseen synthon combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground primarily as a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a methodological advance. It builds on the DEL-Compose probabilistic framework (Chen et al., 2024) and standard fingerprint/GNN/tree-based approaches already applied to DEL data, but departs from prior dataset releases (Iqbal et al. 2024, Leash Bio/BELKA, Gerry et al. 2019) by providing the first publicly available DEL dataset with 3D molecular docking poses, comprehensive biophysical validation on both on-DNA (FP) and off-DNA (SPR) modalities, and three distinct data-splitting strategies. The 81M-compound scale and dual-kinase focus (MAPK14, DDR1) position it as a more generalizable benchmark than prior targeted libraries of ~100K–7M compounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release addresses a critical bottleneck: the scarcity of large, publicly accessible DEL datasets with orthogonal validation data, which has limited the development and fair comparison of ML methods for DEL-based hit identification. Key open questions remain around out-of-distribution generalization (the extended test set is small), the lack of allosteric/orthosteric discrimination in the selection protocol, and whether the observed denoising benefit of structure-based models over raw enrichment metrics holds at larger scale or for non-kinase targets. The inclusion of docking poses opens a path toward integrating 3D structural information into DEL models, a direction the authors explicitly flag as underexplored.
