## Problem

In structure-based drug design, joint generation of continuous 3D atom positions and discrete 2D molecular topologies follows a 'twisted probability path' whose noise schedule has been designed heuristically, typically prioritizing 3D positions first. The paper identifies that this 3D-dominant path prevents the model from leveraging cleaner 2D topology to inform 3D conformation, leading to distorted molecular geometries and poor protein-ligand interactions. Crucially, the authors prove that unlike single-modality settings where the VLB is invariant to schedule shape, the multi-modality VLB becomes a path-dependent line integral, making the noise schedule a first-order design variable that was previously unexplored.

## Input / Output

For de novo design, the model takes a protein pocket (3D atom positions and amino acid types) and generates a complete ligand: continuous 3D atom coordinates, discrete atom types (C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl), and discrete bond types (single, double, triple, non-bond). For molecular docking, it additionally receives the 2D molecular topology (atom types and bond types) as fixed input and generates only the 3D conformation. The protein-ligand complex is represented as a heterogeneous graph with K-nearest-neighbor edges for intermolecular interactions and a fully connected ligand graph for intramolecular bonds, processed by an equivariant Graph Transformer with 9 layers and 128 hidden dimensions.

## Method

The core contribution is VLB-Optimal Scheduling (VOS), which reframes noise schedule design as a path optimization problem. The authors prove that the multi-modality VLB is a line integral over a 2D noise-level plane parameterized by time-rescaling functions t_c=f(t) and t_d=g(t), and that the design space of all valid joint schedules reduces to this plane. To make the search tractable, they introduce a generalized loss (a surface integral over the entire plane) that trains a single BFN-based equivariant Graph Transformer to denoise accurately at all noise-level combinations simultaneously. At inference, they estimate the loss landscape on a discretized grid, fit a smooth B-spline surface, and apply dynamic programming to find the minimum-cost path from (0,0) to (1,1), yielding the VLB-optimal schedule. The derived schedule reveals a two-stage generation: an initial shape-driven phase focusing on 3D positions, followed by a topology-driven docking phase where the conformation adjusts to fit the discrete molecular graph.

## Results

On the in-distribution CrossDock test set, MolPilot achieves a PoseBusters passing rate (PB-Valid) of 95.9%, a >10% improvement over the strongest baseline MolCRAFT (84.6%), with an average Vina Score of −6.88 kcal/mol and 98.5% internal energy passing rate. On the out-of-distribution PoseBusters held-out test (180 proteins with <30% sequence identity to training), MolPilot maintains 79.1% PB-Valid and 98.1% energy passing, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: MolCRAFT at 57.8% PB-Valid). For molecular docking on PoseBusters V2, MolPilot achieves 44.0% RMSD < 2 Å, competitive with dedicated local docking methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolPilot outperforms all seven SBDD baselines on both ID and OOD settings. Against the strongest BFN-based baseline MolCRAFT (Qu et al., 2024), MolPilot improves PB-Valid by 11.3 points on CrossDock (95.9% vs. 84.6%) and by 21.3 points on PoseBusters OOD (79.1% vs. 57.8%), while also achieving better Vina Score (−6.88 vs. −6.55 ID; −7.59 vs. −7.29 OOD) and energy passing (98.5% vs. 91.1% ID; 98.1% vs. 71.6% OOD). Diffusion-based baselines degrade most severely on OOD: TargetDrop falls to 32.3% PB-Valid and DecompDiff to 40.2%, with DecompDiff also showing the worst Vina Score (−3.14) on OOD. Autoregressive models (AR, Pocket2Mol) suffer from low generation success on OOD (Connected drops to 39.1% and 67.7% respectively) and smaller molecule sizes. FLAG (fragment-based) is the weakest overall at 16.0% PB-Valid on ID. For molecular docking on PoseBusters V2 (Fig. 5), MolPilot's 44.0% RMSD < 2 Å is competitive with dedicated local docking methods and approaches AlphaFold 3's docking performance, despite not being specifically optimized for docking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bayesian Flow Network (BFN) framework of MolCRAFT (Qu et al., 2024) and the variational diffusion theory of Kingma et al. (2021), which established that in single-modality generation the VLB is invariant to the shape of the noise schedule. The key departure is proving that this invariance breaks in multi-modality settings (continuous 3D positions + discrete 2D topologies), where the VLB becomes a path-dependent line integral over the joint noise schedule space. Prior SBDD works (TargetDiff, DecompDiff, MolCRAFT) adopted heuristic modality-specific schedules that implicitly prioritize 3D denoising; this paper replaces that heuristic with a principled VLB-optimal schedule found via dynamic programming on a 2D loss surface, and shows the approach generalizes to diffusion-based frameworks like TargetDiff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The path-dependence result provides the first theoretical justification for why multi-modality noise schedule design matters, moving the field beyond ad-hoc heuristics. The >10% PoseBusters passing rate improvement and the identification of CrossDock data leakage (via MMseqs2 analysis) are practically important for the SBDD community, as they expose inflated benchmark numbers and provide a more rigorous OOD evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the dynamic programming approach scales to more than two modalities, whether bilevel optimization can enable end-to-end schedule learning (which the authors show fails naively), and whether the two-stage 'sketch-then-dock' interpretation generalizes to other multi-modality generative tasks beyond molecular design.
