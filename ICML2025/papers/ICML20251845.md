## Problem

Existing synthetic text generation methods for LLM fine-tuning are either heuristic (relying on GPT-4 prompting, VAEs, or diffusion models) without theoretical performance guarantees, or produce unreadable continuous embeddings that cannot transfer across models. No prior approach simultaneously guarantees convergence to a neighborhood of the real-data solution, preserves the privacy of training examples, and produces human-readable text. This paper asks whether a small set of synthetic text can be generated that replicates the fine-tuning dynamics of real data with formal guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small subset of real prompt-response pairs (as few as 5 validation examples, or a larger fine-tuning set) and the parameters of a pretrained LLM (Phi-1.5) as input. It produces a set of synthetic text examples (e.g., 100 samples) where each example is a sequence of vocabulary tokens with low perplexity, constrained to match the (optionally noised) last-layer gradient of the real data at the pretrained parameters. The synthetic data is model-agnostic in the sense that it can be used to fine-tune different LLMs (Llama-3.2-1B, OPT-1.3B) beyond the one whose gradient was matched.

## Method

GRADMM formulates synthetic text generation as a discretely constrained non-convex optimization in embedding space: minimize the gradient distance between synthetic and real data subject to each token embedding belonging to the vocabulary and the sequence having low perplexity. It solves this via ADMM, alternating between a proximal step (optimizing continuous embeddings to match the target gradient using Adam for ~50 iterations) and a projection step (mapping each embedding to the nearest vocabulary token, restricted to top-k candidates conditioned on previously projected tokens to enforce readability). To handle the high dimensionality of LLM gradients, only the last-layer gradient is matched, reducing memory by 2.6× and time by 2.3×. Differential privacy is achieved by clipping per-sample gradients to an ℓ₂ norm and adding calibrated Gaussian noise to their average before matching. A post-hoc filtering step removes mislabeled examples, selects the lowest-loss samples per category, and balances average loss across categories, reducing the synthetic set from 200 to ~68 examples while improving accuracy.

## Results

In the data-scarce regime, GRADMM generates 100 synthetic samples from as few as 5 real examples, outperforming training on those 5 real examples by 15.7%, 29.7%, and 31.5% on SST-2, Tweet Emotions, and Rotten Tomatoes respectively. In the distillation setting (generating 100 synthetic samples from a larger training set), GRADMM achieves 89.7% accuracy on SST-2, 86.5% on Tweet Emotions, and 85.0% on Rotten Tomatoes, outperforming all baselines by up to 13.1%. The differentially private variant (ε=0.05) retains strong performance (88.6%, 83.9%, 83.2%) while providing (ε,δ)-DP guarantees. GRADMM's synthetic data also transfers effectively to Llama-3.2-1B and OPT-1.3B, outperforming zero-shot and few-shot baselines on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRADMM outperforms LLM-based zero-shot and few-shot generation (Li et al., 2023b) across all three datasets at 100 samples: on SST-2, GRADMM achieves 89.7% vs. 87.5% (zero-shot) and 77.4% (few-shot); on Rotten Tomatoes, 85.0% vs. 81.3% and 82.3%. The gap is largest for few-shot, where GRADMM leads by up to 12.6 points (SST-2). Against coreset selection baselines, GRADMM beats Herding (Welling, 2009) by 0.8% on SST-2 and 2.2% on Rotten Tomatoes, and K-center (Farahani & Hekmatfar, 2009) by 0.4% on SST-2 and 2.9% on Rotten Tomatoes. Critically, GRADMM is the only method that preserves the privacy of training data via differential privacy (ε=0.05), while all baselines require direct access to real examples. GRADMM's synthetic data also has higher perplexity (5.2 on SST-2) than LLM-generated text (2.3), indicating greater diversity, while remaining close to real data perplexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-matching formulation of dataset distillation (Zhao et al., 2020) from continuous pixel space to the discrete token space of LLMs, using ADMM (Glowinski & Marroco, 1975) to solve the resulting discretely-constrained non-convex optimization in embedding space. It departs from prior text distillation methods that produce unreadable continuous embeddings or require training a proxy generator model, and from LLM-based synthetic data generation (zero-shot/few-shot prompting) that lacks theoretical guarantees. The top-k decoding mechanism (Fan et al., 2018) is repurposed as a projection step within ADMM to enforce readability, and differential privacy is integrated by adding calibrated noise to the target gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide simultaneous convergence, performance, and differential-privacy guarantees for synthetic text generation targeting LLM fine-tuning, filling a gap where prior approaches were either heuristic or produced unreadable outputs. The demonstrated transferability (synthetic data generated via Phi's gradients successfully fine-tunes Llama-3.2-1B and OPT-1.3B) suggests the approach could reduce dependence on proprietary model access for data generation. However, experiments are limited to binary/multi-class sentiment classification with sub-2B models, leaving open whether the method scales to generation tasks, multi-turn dialogue, or larger LLMs where gradient computation is prohibitively expensive.
