## Problem

Data-driven Riemannian geometry offers interpretable representations, but existing approaches face a scalability trade-off: generative-model-based methods (e.g., Sorrenson et al., 2024) train efficiently yet produce numerically challenging manifold mappings, while pullback-geometry methods (Diepeveen, 2024; de Kruiff et al., 2024) give cheap closed-form mappings but suffer from hard-to-train metrics. This paper asks how to strike a balance where both the Riemannian structure learning and the evaluation of its manifold mappings (geodesics, distances, exp/log maps) are simultaneously scalable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data samples in R^d (ranging from 2D synthetic curves to 1024-dimensional 32×32 images) and learns a density p(x) ∝ exp(−ψ(φ(x))) with a parameterized diagonal covariance matrix A ∈ R^{d×d} and a diffeomorphism φ. From this learned density it produces: (i) closed-form geodesics between any point pair via γ_{x,y}(t) = φ⁻¹((1−t)φ(x) + tφ(y)), (ii) a Riemannian autoencoder whose latent dimension d_ε is determined by a variance threshold ε on A's diagonal entries, and (iii) a global chart of the data manifold by sweeping the decoder over a box in the top d_ε latent coordinates. The encoder is a linear projection in φ-space onto the highest-variance directions, making the entire pipeline differentiable and the latent space ℓ²-isometric to the data manifold when φ is approximately isometric.

## Method

The core contribution is a score-based pullback Riemannian metric (Ξ,Φ)_x^{∇ψ∘φ} = (D_x ∇ψ∘φ[Ξ], D_x ∇ψ∘φ[Φ])₂, which for the quadratic choice ψ(x) = ½xᵀA⁻¹x yields closed-form geodesics, distances, and exp/log maps that provably pass through the data support (Theorem 3.3). The key insight is that when φ is approximately ℓ²-isometric on the data support, this pullback metric closely approximates the score-based metric D_x ∇log p, bridging generative modeling and Riemannian geometry in a single framework. To learn the density, the paper adapts standard normalizing flow training (affine coupling layers) with two modifications: an anisotropic base distribution (learnable diagonal covariance A instead of fixed identity) and ℓ²-isometry regularization on the flow's Jacobian, plus a Hessian-vector product regularization term for non-affine flows in high dimensions. The RAE is constructed by thresholding A's diagonal entries at level ε to select d_ε dimensions, then projecting in φ-space, with a theoretical reconstruction error bound of O(ε·tr(A)) that depends only on φ's deviation from isometry (Theorem 4.1).

## Results

On the manifold mapping experiments, the proposed score-based pullback Riemannian geometry achieves the lowest geodesic error across all three datasets: 0.0315 (Single Banana), 0.0180 (Squeezed Single Banana), and 0.1691 (River), compared to 0.0406–0.0817 for the baselines. For the Riemannian autoencoder, the method correctly recovers the intrinsic dimension on all synthetic datasets (e.g., 5/5 for Hemisphere(5,20), >99% variance in 22/51/101 dimensions for Gaussian Blobs with d'=20/50/100). On MNIST, the RAE assigns 90% of variance to 176 latent dimensions at ε=0.1, slightly exceeding the state-of-the-art LID estimate of 152, with visually convincing reconstructions at 176 dimensions and near-perfect reconstructions at 271.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: standard Normalizing Flow (NF) with isotropic Gaussian base, Anisotropic NF (parameterized diagonal covariance but no isometry), and Isometric NF (isotropic base with ℓ²-isometry regularization but no anisotropy). The proposed method (anisotropy + isometry) achieves the lowest geodesic error on all three datasets, with particularly large margins on Squeezed Banana (0.0180 vs. 0.0524 for NF, a ~3× improvement) and River (0.1691 vs. 0.2369 for NF). On variation error, the method wins on Single Banana and Squeezed Banana but is slightly outperformed by Isometric NF on the River dataset (0.0763 vs. 0.0636), suggesting that isometry alone can improve stability in some regimes. The ablation-style comparison reveals that anisotropy without isometry provides no significant improvement over standard NF, while isometry without anisotropy causes underfitting and substantially worse geodesic errors (e.g., 0.1967 vs. 0.0180 on Squeezed Banana), confirming that both components are necessary for the best overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: pullback Riemannian geometry (Diepeveen, 2024; de Kruiff et al., 2024), which provides closed-form manifold mappings but suffers from training scalability issues, and generative-model-based Riemannian geometry (Sorrenson et al., 2024), which trains efficiently via normalizing flows but yields numerically challenging manifold mappings. The key conceptual move is to parameterize the pullback diffeomorphism φ as a normalizing flow with anisotropic Gaussian base and isometry regularization, thereby inheriting closed-form geodesics from the pullback structure while retaining the efficient training paradigm of normalizing flows. The Riemannian autoencoder construction generalizes PCA to the Riemannian setting, with error bounds analogous to the classical PCA reconstruction bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first framework that simultaneously achieves scalable training and scalable evaluation of data-driven Riemannian geometry, unlocking closed-form geodesics, interpretable latent spaces, and intrinsic dimension estimation in a single pipeline that scales to 1024-dimensional image data. The unimodal assumption is a significant limitation, though the encouraging MNIST results suggest practical robustness beyond the theoretical guarantee. Open questions include extending the framework to genuinely multimodal distributions, incorporating more expressive flow architectures (e.g., rational quadratic splines), and leveraging the learned geometry for downstream tasks such as Riemannian optimization on the data manifold or controllable generative modeling.
