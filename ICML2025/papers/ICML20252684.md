## Problem

Existing AUC fairness methods (e.g., MinimaxFairAUC, InterFairAUC) assume clean, accurately labeled protected groups, but in practice these labels are frequently noisy—due to survey response bias, privacy concerns, or unverifiable demographic annotations on AI-generated faces. Training with standard AUC fairness under noisy group labels leads to significant group-level AUC gaps at deployment, yet no prior approach provides a robust AUC fairness guarantee under noisy protected groups.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either tabular feature vectors (Adult, Bank, Default) or face images (FF++, DFDC, DFD, Celeb-DF) paired with binary task labels and potentially noisy protected group labels (e.g., gender, race). It outputs a real-valued scoring function φ_θ that ranks positive examples above negative examples while enforcing group-level AUC fairness. Unusually, the protected group labels are treated as unreliable inputs: for image data, a pre-trained CLIP model is used solely to estimate the noise ratio (γ) between clean and noisy group distributions, not to relabel or classify samples, preserving the theoretical robustness guarantees.

## Method

The paper formulates a unified AUC fairness metric g_{z,z'}(θ) = AUC_{z,z'}(θ) − AUC(θ) that simultaneously captures intra-group and inter-group AUC disparities, then embeds it in a distributionally robust optimization (DRO) framework that maximizes over all empirical distributions within a Total Variation (TV) ball of radius γ_{z,z'} around the noisy group distribution. Theorem 4.1 proves that satisfying the DRO constraint on noisy groups guarantees clean-group fairness within a slack of γ_{z,z'}. The TV bound is estimated empirically via CLIP cosine similarity between image features and group-specific text prompts, avoiding auxiliary training. Optimization proceeds via stochastic gradient descent-ascent (SGDA) with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to flatten the loss landscape, using a stratified sampler that ensures each mini-batch contains positive and negative samples from all group pairs, and projecting the adversarial distribution onto the L_{1,1}-norm constraint at each step.

## Results

On tabular datasets (Adult, Bank, Default) with noise levels up to 0.5, the proposed method consistently achieves the lowest AUC fairness violation (e.g., 0.0316 on Adult at 50% noise vs. 0.0766 for AUCMax) and highest Min/Max AUC ratio (e.g., 0.9554 vs. 0.9141 for AUCMax), while maintaining competitive overall AUC. On deepfake detection (FF++, DFDC, DFD, Celeb-DF), the method achieves the lowest fairness violation across all four datasets with both Xception and EfficientNet-B4 backbones — e.g., 0.0061 violation on FF++ (EfficientNet-B4) vs. 0.0144 for PG-FDD — while also attaining the highest AUC (0.9766) and Min/Max ratio (0.9907). The method's advantage grows with noise level, confirming robustness to noisy protected group labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On tabular data, the method outperforms all three baselines (AUCMax, InterFairAUC, MinimaxFairAUC) in fairness violation and Min/Max ratio across all noise levels (0.1–0.5) and all three datasets, while AUCMax retains a slightly higher overall AUC due to the absence of fairness constraints. For example, at 50% noise on Adult, the method achieves 0.0316 violation vs. 0.0766 (AUCMax), 0.0374 (InterFairAUC), and 0.0375 (MinimaxFairAUC). On deepfake detection, the method beats PG-FDD (the strongest baseline) on fairness violation across all four datasets — e.g., 0.0061 vs. 0.0144 on FF++ (EfficientNet-B4) and 0.0048 vs. 0.0184 on DFDC (Xception) — while also achieving higher AUC on EfficientNet-B4 (0.9766 vs. 0.9721 on FF++). PG-FDD achieves marginally higher AUC on Xception for FF++ (0.9708 vs. 0.9644), but the method's fairness advantage is consistent and substantial. DAW-FDD and DAG-FDD are clearly outperformed on both fairness and utility metrics across all image datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: AUC-based group fairness (Yang et al. 2023, Yao et al. 2023, Vogel et al. 2021) and robust fairness under noisy protected groups (Wang et al. 2020, Celis et al. 2021). It adapts the TV-distance-bounded DRO framework from Wang et al. (2020)—originally designed for classification fairness—to the pairwise ranking setting of AUC fairness, and adopts the group-level AUC metric and Rawlsian formulation from Yang et al. (2023). The key departure is that all prior AUC fairness methods assume clean group labels, while all prior noisy-group fairness methods target classification (not ranking) metrics; this paper is the first to combine both. A practical addition is the use of CLIP as a zero-shot noise-ratio estimator, drawing on recent work showing vision-language models as noisy label detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that is increasingly relevant as demographic annotations in real-world datasets (deepfake detection, survey-based socioeconomic data) are inferred rather than verified. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that clean-group fairness holds within a TV-distance slack is a clean and useful result for practitioners who cannot guarantee label quality. However, the CLIP-based noise estimation is only instantiated for image data, leaving tabular settings without a concrete noise-ratio estimator, and the paper acknowledges that simultaneously preserving both utility and fairness under noisy groups remains open. Extending the framework to other pairwise ranking metrics (partial AUC, average precision) is identified as future work.
