## Problem

Sharpness Aware Minimization (SAM) improves generalization on vision tasks but consistently degrades performance in language modeling, even with twice the compute budget. The paper identifies the root cause: in NLP, SAM's sharpness reduction is dominated by a spurious 'logit path' that simply makes the network over-confident in its predictions, rather than genuinely improving the geometry of the learned function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252214/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text sequences (C4 corpus, 512-token sequences, batch size 256) for language modeling and RGB images (ImageNet-1K, JFT, ImageNet-21K) for vision diagnostics. It consumes the model's Jacobian (derivative of outputs w.r.t. parameters) and the loss gradient w.r.t. outputs separately, producing a modified gradient that is fed into ADAMW. The perturbation is applied only to the Jacobian rather than the full loss, and is preconditioned by ADAMW's diagonal second-moment statistics at no additional computational cost beyond standard SAM.

## Method

The paper decomposes the SAM sharpness gradient into two components via the Gauss-Newton Hessian decomposition: a logit-path term (δ_logit, tied to the GGN matrix H_G) and a functional-path term (δ_func, tied to the functional Hessian H_F). FUNCTIONAL-SAM isolates the functional path by perturbing only the Jacobian ∇_θ F(θ) while holding the output-space loss gradient ∇_F L(θ) fixed, yielding the update g = -∇_θ F(θ + ρε*) · ∇_F L(θ). PRECONDITIONED SAM separately addresses a geometry mismatch by scaling the SAM perturbation with ADAMW's inverse second-moment matrix M^{-1}, which reduces the relative weight of the logit path. The combined PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM achieves the best results across model scales from 2M to 1.2B parameters in both fixed-length and Chinchilla-style training regimes, outperforming both ADAMW and standard SAM.

## Results

In Chinchilla-style training on C4, PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM achieves the lowest evaluation loss across all model scales (23.9M–1208M parameters), with gains of 5×10⁻³ to 10⁻² over ADAMW. At the 117.9M scale, PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM reaches 3.070 eval loss versus 3.079 for ADAMW and 3.094 for SAM. At the 1208M (billion-parameter) scale, SAM produces NaNs while PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM achieves 2.557, outperforming ADAMW (2.561). In fixed-length (10K step) training, similar improvements hold, with PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM achieving 3.218 at 117.9M versus 3.228 for ADAMW and 3.257 for SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (Foret et al., 2020), the paper demonstrates that standard SAM is not merely ineffective but actively harmful in language modeling: it consistently produces higher eval loss than ADAMW at every model scale while requiring 2× the compute per step. At 117.9M parameters, SAM scores 3.094 versus ADAMW's 3.079, and at 1208M it diverges to NaN. PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM outperforms SAM by 0.016–0.024 in eval loss across scales. Against ADAMW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM achieves consistent but modest improvements (0.001–0.009 in eval loss), which the authors contextualize as significant given that typical gains from new LLM optimization methods (e.g., CASPR, selective attention) are of similar magnitude. PRECOND SAM (the indirect approach alone) also beats ADAMW at most scales but is slightly outperformed by PRECOND FUNCTIONAL-SAM, and both proposed methods are more robust than SAM at the billion-parameter scale where SAM and PRECOND SAM suffer numerical instabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2020) by decomposing its update rule into two distinct pathways—logit-path and functional-path sharpness reduction—using the Gauss-Newton Hessian decomposition (Schraudolph, 2002; Singh et al., 2021). The preconditioning strategy draws on ADAMW's second-moment statistics (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) and is conceptually related to Fisher SAM (Kim et al., 2022) and concurrent preconditioned SAM work (Zhang et al., 2025), but the key departure is the explicit diagnosis that in NLP the logit path overwhelms the functional path, making standard SAM counterproductive. The paper also aligns with recent work emphasizing the underappreciated functional Hessian (Dauphin et al., 2024) over the positive semi-definite GGN term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first successful application of sharpness-aware minimization to large-scale language modeling, resolving a long-standing gap where SAM consistently underperformed ADAMW despite 2× compute. The logit-vs-functional decomposition offers a general diagnostic tool applicable to any domain, and the finding that SAM minimizes the 'wrong' sharpness in NLP (lowest Hessian eigenvalue yet worst validation loss) reframes how practitioners should think about curvature regularization. Limitations include the persistent 2× gradient cost, evaluation only on C4 with decoder-only transformers, and modest absolute gains (5×10⁻³ to 10⁻² in eval loss); open questions include whether benefits amplify on noisy corpora, in multi-pass training, or for downstream fine-tuning and compression.
