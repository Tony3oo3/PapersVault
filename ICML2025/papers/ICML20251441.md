## Problem

Existing concept erasing methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely exclusively on text prompts to identify and suppress unwanted concepts, but the inherent gap between text and image modalities means semantically benign words can still trigger inappropriate visual content, and finite word sets cannot fully capture complex, entangled concepts. This forces a trade-off: aggressive text-based erasure degrades benign generation quality (usability), while conservative erasure leaves residual unwanted content (efficacy), making it difficult to satisfy both goals simultaneously.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes both a text prompt describing the target concept (e.g., 'a photo of nudity') and a set of self-generated images (n=50–200, 512×512) produced by the clean Stable Diffusion model using that prompt. The image branch is active only during fine-tuning; at inference the model accepts only a standard text prompt and produces images in the usual 512×512 latent diffusion pipeline, so the deployed architecture is unchanged from the original U-Net. The output is a fine-tuned U-Net with the target concept suppressed, evaluated on both adversarial erasure metrics (pre-ASR, ASR, P4D, CCE) and usability metrics (FID, CLIP score on COCO-10k).

## Method

Co-Erasing introduces a decoupled cross-attention mechanism in the U-Net where text embeddings (77×768) and image embeddings (4×768, projected into the text embedding space via IP-Adapter's pre-trained CLIP image encoder and projection layer) are fed through separate key/value pathways and their outputs summed, allowing the image modality to provide concept-specific visual supervision that bypasses the text-image semantic gap. A text-guided image concept refinement module applies cross-attention with the image as query and the target-concept text as key/value, isolating concept-relevant visual features (e.g., the cross and Gothic windows in a church image) while suppressing unrelated content (e.g., trees), thereby protecting benign generation. Training follows the ESD negative-guidance objective (Eq. 7), where the combined condition c = [c_text, c_image] is subtracted from the unconditional noise prediction with a guidance scale η, and the U-Net is fine-tuned with Adam at lr=1e-5. Self-generated images are preferred over real-world images because t-SNE analysis shows they better match the model's internal concept distribution, yielding lower ASR and FID than real NSFW images.

## Results

On the primary nudity-erasing task, Co-Erasing achieves a pre-ASR of 0.85% and ASR of 16.96%, dramatically outperforming text-only baselines like ESD (pre-ASR 20.42%, ASR 76.05%) and FMN (ASR 97.89%) while preserving usability with FID 18.77 and CLIP score 0.302—nearly identical to the un-erased Stable Diffusion baseline (FID 14.77, CLIP 0.312). On style erasure (Van Gogh), Co-Erasing matches AdvUnlearn's ASR of 2.00% while achieving a lower CCE (8.00% vs. 28.00%). On object erasure (parachute, church, tench), Co-Erasing consistently achieves ASR ≤ 6% with FID in the 16–21 range, whereas weight-saliency methods like SH degrade FID to 55–68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-Erasing decisively outperforms all text-only erasing methods (ESD, FMN, SPM, UCE) on efficacy: ASR drops from 76–98% to 16.96%, and pre-ASR drops from 20–88% to 0.85%, while FID and CLIP remain essentially unchanged (18.77/0.302 vs. 18.18/0.302 for ESD). Against the strongest usability-preserving competitor AdvUnlearn, Co-Erasing achieves lower ASR (16.96% vs. 21.13%), lower CCE (19.33% vs. 39.44%), and better CLIP (0.302 vs. 0.273), indicating superior prompt alignment. SalUn achieves a marginally lower ASR (11.27%) but at a severe usability cost—FID explodes to 53.21 and CLIP drops to 0.267—making it impractical for general use. Weight-saliency methods SH and ED achieve low ASR (6–24%) but destroy image quality (FID 55–68), confirming the efficacy–usability trade-off that Co-Erasing resolves. When merged with the multi-concept framework MACE, Co-Erasing further reduces RAB attack success to 0.00% (vs. 0.04% for MACE alone) while maintaining FID 17.12 and CLIP 0.277, demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) negative-guidance framework for concept erasing in latent diffusion models by introducing a parallel image branch via decoupled cross-attention, drawing on IP-Adapter (Ye et al., 2023) for aligning image embeddings into the text embedding space. It departs from the two dominant erasing paradigms—full-model fine-tuning (ESD, FMN, UCE) that fails against adversarial prompts, and weight-saliency retraining (SH, SalUn, ED) that preserves efficacy but degrades usability—by arguing that the text-image modality gap is the root cause of both failure modes. The text-guided image concept refinement module, which uses cross-attention between image and text encoders to isolate concept-relevant visual features, is a new architectural component not present in prior erasing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward balanced concept erasing: the image branch is training-only (inference architecture is unchanged), and self-generated images require no external dataset, making the approach deployable. However, open questions remain: the method depends on having access to a clean model to generate reference images, which may not be available in all safety-critical settings; failure cases persist for objects with non-unique visual features; and the approach is tied to cross-attention-based U-Net architectures, leaving open whether it transfers to transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., DiT). The finding that self-generated images outperform real-world images for erasing suggests the model's internal representation of a concept is a more faithful erasing target than external data, a principle that could inform broader unlearning research.
