## Problem

State-of-the-art protein structure generative models (RFdiffusion, FrameFlow, FoldFlow2) are restricted to conditioning on static properties such as motifs, symmetries, or binding targets, and produce highly thermostable, rigid backbones. This limits their utility for designing functional proteins, where per-residue flexibility is essential for catalysis, molecular recognition, and conformational dynamics. No existing framework enables direct conditioning of backbone generation on a target flexibility profile.

## Input / Output

FliPS takes a target per-residue flexibility profile (local RMSF values in Ångströms, binned into 8 bins up to 3 Å) and a protein length N (60–512 residues) as input, and outputs SE(3)^N backbone frames that recapitulate the target profile. BackFlip takes a protein backbone structure (sequence of N rigid-body frames) as input and outputs a per-residue flexibility vector in [0, 5 Å], requiring no sequence or evolutionary information. The local RMSF metric used throughout aligns each residue against a window of S=12 neighboring residues rather than the whole protein, avoiding non-local artifacts of global RMSF.

## Method

The framework combines two models: BackFlip, an SE(3)-equivariant predictor with 4 Clifford Frame Attention blocks (0.68M params) trained on ATLAS MD simulations to predict per-residue local RMSF from backbone geometry alone, and FliPS, a conditional flow matching model extending GAFL with three modifications: (1) flexibility profiles embedded as 8-bin node features, (2) a differentiable auxiliary loss that penalizes deviation between BackFlip-predicted flexibility of the predicted target structure and the conditioning profile, and (3) random flexibility masking during training (10% fully masked, 90% partially masked with random windows) to retain unconditional generation capability. FliPS is pre-trained on 3,673 SCOPe proteins then fine-tuned on 22,977 PDB proteins (21 GPU-days on 8 A100s). A training-free BackFlip-guidance (BG) strategy is also proposed as an alternative, adding the gradient of BackFlip-predicted flexibility deviation to the unconditional flow vector field at inference, though it underperforms the conditional model and can compromise structural validity at high guidance scales.

## Results

FliPS generates protein backbones whose MD-verified flexibility profiles achieve Pearson correlations of 0.70 (existing profiles) and 0.78 (hand-drawn profiles) for domain-sized proteins (60–120 residues), approaching the ~0.8 upper bound set by inter-replica MD noise. For larger proteins (200–300 residues), FliPS reaches r = 0.79, while unconditional baselines drop to r ≈ 0.35–0.41. Without BackFlip screening, FliPS still substantially outperforms all baselines on average flexibility correlation (e.g., r = 0.56 vs. 0.08–0.13 for hand-drawn profiles), confirming that the conditioning itself—not just screening—drives the improvement. Generated backbones maintain novelty (TM-score ~0.55–0.62) comparable to RFdiffusion and FoldFlow2, and contain both α-helices and β-strands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FliPS outperforms both RFdiffusion and FoldFlow2 (each augmented with BackFlip screening) on flexibility correlation across all profile types: for existing profiles, FliPS achieves r = 0.70 vs. 0.58 (RFdiffusion) and 0.45 (FoldFlow2); for hand-drawn profiles, r = 0.78 vs. 0.56 and 0.50. The gap widens dramatically without screening (all-samples r = 0.52–0.56 for FliPS vs. 0.08–0.28 for baselines), showing that conditioning is the primary driver. On novelty, FliPS is on par with RFdiffusion and FoldFlow2 (TM-scores 0.55–0.62), while SCOPe screening trivially scores 1.0 since it selects from natural proteins. For the flexibility prediction task, BackFlip (r = 0.80, MAE = 0.17 Å) substantially outperforms pLDDT (r = 0.54) and B-factors (r = 0.16) as proxies for MD-derived local RMSF, and matches or exceeds FlexPert-3D (r = 0.74) on the ATLAS test set while significantly outperforming it on de novo proteins (r = 0.63 vs. 0.51). The only metric where FliPS is worse is scRMSD (1.52–2.01 Å vs. ~0.72 Å for baselines), which the authors attribute to the inherent correlation between flexibility and refolding uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SE(3)-equivariant flow matching framework for protein backbone generation (FrameFlow, GAFL) by introducing per-residue flexibility as a conditioning signal, building directly on the Clifford Frame Attention architecture and geodesic-based vector field parametrization from prior work. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of BackFlip, a structure-only flexibility predictor that requires no sequence or evolutionary information, enabling flexibility annotation at scale and differentiable flexibility losses during training. Unlike prior attempts to incorporate dynamics (normal-mode guidance in Komorowska et al., sequence-space flexibility conditioning in FlexPert-3D), this work trains a conditional flow model directly on MD-derived flexibility profiles, positioning flexibility as a first-class design variable alongside static properties like motifs and symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new axis of control in de novo protein design, moving beyond the static structures that current generative models produce toward proteins with prescribed dynamic behavior—a prerequisite for catalysis, allostery, and molecular recognition. A notable conceptual finding is the demonstrated correlation between scRMSD (the standard designability metric) and flexibility, which questions the validity of scRMSD as a universal quality measure when flexible backbones are the design target. Key limitations include the 300 ns MD timescale for ground-truth flexibility (likely missing slower collective motions) and the observation that FliPS-generated backbones have higher scRMSD than unconditional samples, suggesting a genuine trade-off between flexibility and designability that remains unresolved.
