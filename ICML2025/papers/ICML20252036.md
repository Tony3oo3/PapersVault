## Problem

Norm-based and leverage-score-based importance sampling is well-developed for linear models, but systematic tools for nonlinear models remain largely absent. Prior nonlinear results (Gajjar et al., 2023; 2024) were restricted to single-neuron predictors and produced guarantees with a large multiplicative constant C > 1000, undermining practical utility. This paper asks whether linear importance sampling can be generalized to broad nonlinear model classes while preserving tight (1±ε) approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of n training points, a nonlinear model f_i(θ) with parameters θ ∈ ℝ^p, and a loss function (primarily squared loss). It produces per-sample importance scores derived from a nonlinear dual matrix F*(θ) ∈ ℝ^{n×p}, whose i-th row is the adjoint operator of f_i. A key practical subtlety is that these scores are inherently parameter-dependent (evaluated at the unknown optimum θ*), so the paper derives parameter-independent surrogates—e.g., linear leverage or row-norm scores scaled by a β-factor—that still yield valid sampling guarantees. The final output is a sampled subset of size O(p log(p/δ)/ε²) with a (1±ε) loss approximation bound.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the nonlinear adjoint operator f*(θ) = ∫₀¹ ∂f(tθ)/∂θ dt, which yields an inner-product-like decomposition f(θ) = ⟨θ, f*(θ)⟩ for any absolutely continuous f. Stacking these adjoints across all data points forms a nonlinear dual matrix F*(θ), so the total loss becomes ‖F*(θ)θ̂‖²—structurally identical to linear least squares—and nonlinear leverage/norm scores are defined as the standard scores of F*(θ)'s rows. For models with positively homogeneous inner functions (single-index models, ReLU networks), Proposition 3.1 gives closed-form adjoints, making scores computable via standard QR or SVD factorizations. The paper then proves these nonlinear scores are within a β-factor of their linear counterparts (Examples 3.3–3.4), and combines this with an ε-net covering argument and Lipschitz continuity of the sampled loss to establish the main (1±ε) guarantee (Theorem 3.1) with sample complexity O((p log(p/δ) + p² log(p/ε))/(βε²)).

## Results

On three regression benchmarks (California Housing, Medical Insurance, Diamonds), nonlinear importance sampling (both leverage-score and row-norm variants) achieves lower log relative error than uniform and linear sampling at every sample fraction tested, with the gap widening as the sample budget shrinks. On classification tasks (SVHN, NOTMNIST, Quick Draw, FER-2013), nonlinear leverage scores identify the most diagnostically meaningful and hardest-to-classify images, whereas linear leverage scores select visually random and less informative samples. The theoretical guarantee achieves the desired form L(θ*_S) ≤ L(θ*) + O(ε) with constant C = 1, improving upon the C > 1,000 multiplicative constant in prior work by Gajjar et al. (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares nonlinear adjoint-based importance sampling (leverage scores and row norms) against three baselines: uniform sampling, linear leverage-score sampling, and linear row-norm sampling, all evaluated on the same regression and classification datasets. Across all three regression datasets (California Housing, Medical Insurance, Diamonds), both nonlinear variants (LS-A, RN-A) consistently achieve lower log relative error than their linear counterparts (LS-L, RN-L) and uniform sampling (UN) at every sample size, with the advantage most pronounced at small sample fractions (e.g., 5–20% of data). On the classification tasks, nonlinear leverage scores produce qualitatively more meaningful selections—highlighting mislabeled, noisy, or visually distinctive images—while linear leverage scores select more random and less insightful samples. Theoretically, the paper's guarantee (Theorem 3.1) achieves the additive-error form with C = 1, whereas the closest prior nonlinear result (Gajjar et al., 2024) requires a multiplicative constant C > 1,000, though the latter has linear (vs. quadratic) dependence on dimension d. The paper does not report a single unified numerical table; results are presented as log-error curves (Figures 1, 4) and qualitative image grids (Figure 2), making direct point-wise numerical comparison across methods less straightforward than in standard benchmark papers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-developed linear importance sampling literature (leverage scores, row-norm sampling from Drineas et al. 2006; Woodruff et al. 2014) to nonlinear models by introducing the adjoint operator of a nonlinear map, which provides an 'inner-product-like' representation of nonlinear functions. It builds directly on Gajjar et al. (2023, 2024), who first analyzed leverage-score-based sampling for single-index models but obtained guarantees with a large multiplicative constant C >> 1; here the authors achieve the cleaner C = 1 guarantee and extend the framework to ReLU networks. The key conceptual departure is a systematic, model-agnostic framework (the nonlinear dual matrix) rather than the ad hoc high-dimensional probability arguments used in prior nonlinear sampling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework providing approximation guarantees for importance sampling in general nonlinear models (including multi-neuron ReLU networks), filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the RandNLA literature. The practical implications are twofold: reduced training cost via efficient subsampling, and a novel post-hoc diagnostic tool where high nonlinear leverage scores visually identify hard-to-classify or mislabeled samples. Key limitations include the requirement of a bounded parameter set C, a constrained optimization subproblem, and quadratic (p²) dimension dependence versus the linear (d) dependence in Gajjar et al. (2024); extending guarantees beyond squared loss and achieving fully parameter-agnostic sampling remain open.
