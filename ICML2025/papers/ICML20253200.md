## Problem

Existing context-based offline meta-RL methods assume adaptation contexts are in-distribution or collected over multiple rounds, but practical deployment requires one-shot context collection for safety and efficiency. Under this constraint, context ambiguity (transitions shared across tasks) and out-of-distribution transitions (outside the offline dataset's support) produce unreliable task representations, yet this issue has been largely overlooked in COMRL research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253200/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single trajectory of (s, a, r, s') tuples collected online by a dedicated context collecting policy in an unseen task, alongside a multi-task offline dataset used during meta-training. It outputs an action conditioned on a weighted task representation z' (a 20-dim vector), where per-transition weights are derived from softmax-normalized negative uncertainty scores. The uncertainty estimation network h_ψ outputs a scalar σ per transition, and the context collecting policy π(a|s, z_0) operates with a zero-initialized task representation z_0, distinguishing it from the adapted policy π(a|s, z').

## Method

CERTAIN is a plug-in framework with three components. First, it augments any COMRL task representation objective (classifier, reconstruction, or contrastive) with a heteroscedastic uncertainty loss L = L_φ/σ² + log σ, which provably yields σ* = √(2L_φ), making the predicted uncertainty a direct proxy for context ambiguity or OOD-ness. Second, it replaces the standard mean aggregation of per-transition representations with a softmax-weighted sum where weights are inversely proportional to uncertainty, and it trains a context collecting policy π(a|s, z_0) that maximizes reward minus an α·σ penalty, actively steering exploration toward low-uncertainty (task-discriminative) transitions. Third, during one-shot adaptation, the collected trajectory is processed through the uncertainty network to compute per-transition weights, producing a weighted task representation z' that suppresses the influence of ambiguous or OOD transitions before conditioning the meta-policy.

## Results

On the Point-Robot environment, CERTAIN+CLASSIFIER achieves a 36.9% improvement in one-shot average return over the original CLASSIFIER baseline, the largest gain across all environments. Across five MuJoCo and toy environments (Point-Robot, Ant-Goal, Half-Cheetah-Vel, Walker-Rand-Params, Hopper-Rand-Params), CERTAIN improves one-shot adaptation by up to 36% and zero-shot adaptation by up to 34% relative to the corresponding baselines. In the few-shot setting (5 episodes), CERTAIN+CLASSIFIER reaches −9.69 average return by Episode 5 in Point-Robot versus −13.14 for the original CLASSIFIER, while CERTAIN+FOCAL reaches −12.35 versus −18.48 for FOCAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CERTAIN is integrated as a plug-in into three COMRL baselines: CLASSIFIER (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025, classifier-based), FOCAL (Li et al., 2020, contrastive-learning-based), and UNICORN (Li et al., 2024, reconstruction-based). In one-shot adaptation, CERTAIN+CLASSIFIER yields the largest relative gain (36.9% in Point-Robot), while CERTAIN+FOCAL and CERTAIN+UNICORN also consistently match or exceed their respective baselines across all five environments. Against IDAQ (Wang et al., ICML 2023), which explicitly addresses OOD in online adaptation, CERTAIN+FOCAL consistently outperforms it in one-shot return. In zero-shot adaptation, CERTAIN tends to outperform baselines in most environments, though FOCAL retains an edge in Point-Robot and Ant-Goal, likely because contrastive learning produces a more balanced initial latent representation z₀. The ablation (Table 1) confirms that both the uncertainty-aware collecting policy and the weighted-representation mechanism are necessary: removing either (Random, Naive-z₀, or Mean variants) degrades performance across all environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the context-based offline meta-RL (COMRL) line of work by shifting focus from task representation learning quality to the context collection process itself under one-shot constraints. It builds on the heteroscedastic uncertainty framework of Kendall & Gal (2017) to estimate per-transition context uncertainty, and integrates as a plug-in into existing COMRL paradigms (classifier-based, reconstruction-based, contrastive learning-based). Prior COMRL methods largely assumed in-distribution contexts or multi-round collection, while CORRO and IDAQ addressed OOD only partially; CERTAIN departs by jointly training an uncertainty-aware collecting policy and a weighted-representation mechanism that explicitly down-weights ambiguous or OOD transitions during one-shot adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot adaptation setting is the realistic deployment scenario for safety-critical or resource-constrained applications, yet prior COMRL methods were not designed for it; CERTAIN's plug-in architecture makes it broadly applicable to existing frameworks without architectural overhaul. Key limitations remain: the method cannot distinguish OOD from ambiguous contexts, does not address spurious correlations, and its zero-shot gains are attributed to the collecting policy gathering low-uncertainty (often high-return) trajectories rather than genuine task inference. Open questions include disentangling the two uncertainty sources, handling expert-level data that introduces spurious correlations, and validating the approach on more complex, high-dimensional environments beyond MuJoCo.
