## Problem

Prior latent action learning methods (LAPO, LAPA, etc.) have been validated exclusively on distractor-free data where observation changes are primarily explained by ground-truth actions. Real-world video contains action-correlated distractors (background motion, camera shake, color changes) that can mislead the inverse dynamics model into encoding noise rather than control-relevant changes. This paper empirically demonstrates that LAPO fails in such settings and that even architectural improvements alone are insufficient without supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251862/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3 stacked 64×64 RGB frames (rendered from proprioceptive states with or without distractors) as observations, and optionally a small set of ground-truth action labels (as few as 2.5% of the full 5M-transition dataset) for supervision during LAM training. It produces 8192-dimensional latent actions between consecutive observations, which are then used as pseudo-labels for behavioral cloning; a small two-layer MLP decoder (256 hidden dims) maps latent actions to the final continuous control actions for evaluation in the environment.

## Method

LAOM modifies LAPO in four ways: (1) multi-step IDM that predicts latent action z_t from o_t and o_{t+k} with k uniformly sampled from {1,…,10}, inspired by control-endogenous minimal state theory; (2) removal of latent action quantization (VQ-VAE/FSQ) and increase of latent dimensionality from 128 to 8192, since the information bottleneck incentivizes encoding distractor noise; (3) replacement of image-space reconstruction with a latent temporal consistency loss, so IDM and FDM operate as MLPs in compact latent space rather than ResNets in pixel space; and (4) addition of random shift/rotation/perspective augmentations for training stability. The core finding is that even with all four improvements (yielding 8× better linear-probe quality), downstream performance remains poor without supervision; reusing the same small set of ground-truth action labels (2–128 trajectories) to linearly predict actions from latent actions during LAM training improves downstream performance by 4.2× on average, outperforming all baselines and recovering ~44% of full-BC performance while using only 2.5% of action labels.

## Results

On the Distracting Control Suite (4 environments, 3 seeds), LAOM improves latent action quality by 8× over LAPO as measured by linear probing, and doubles downstream performance. The key result is that LAOM+supervision—reusing as little as 2.5% of the dataset's ground-truth action labels during LAM training—achieves an average normalized score of 0.44, recovering nearly half the performance of BC trained on the full action-labeled dataset while using only 2.5% of the labels. This represents a 4.2× improvement over unsupervised LAOM and outperforms all baselines (BC from scratch, IDM) across all environments and label budgets (2–128 trajectories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LAPO (Schmidt & Jiang, 2023), LAOM doubles downstream performance in the presence of distractors, and LAOM+supervision further multiplies this by ~4.2×, making the supervised version roughly 8× better than vanilla LAPO. Against the IDM baseline (Baker et al., 2022, two-stage relabeling pipeline), LAOM+supervision outperforms IDM across all label budgets in all four environments (Figure 8). Critically, LAOM+supervision also generalizes better to novel distractor backgrounds (Figure 9a), whereas IDM's generalization degrades because its small labeled dataset cannot cover all distractor/action combinations. Both baselines (BC from scratch on the same labels, and IDM) are outperformed by LAOM+supervision, confirming that the benefit comes from pre-training on the full unlabeled dataset with supervision guiding the latent action space, rather than from the label budget itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LAPO (Schmidt & Jiang, 2023), the dominant latent action learning framework, and incorporates multi-step inverse dynamics from Lamb et al. (2022) and latent temporal consistency loss from Schwarzer et al. (2020) into a modified architecture called LAOM. It departs from the broader latent action learning literature (LAPA, DynaMo, MOTO, Genie) by demonstrating that all these methods assume distractor-free data, where observation changes are explained solely by ground-truth actions, and showing that this assumption breaks down for realistic video. The key conceptual departure is challenging the standard two-stage pipeline (unsupervised LAM pre-training followed by action decoding) and showing that integrating even minimal supervision during LAM training is essential when distractors are present.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the conventional unsupervised LAM pipeline is fundamentally suboptimal in the presence of distractors challenges a core assumption underpinning recent large-scale latent action pre-training efforts (LAPA, MOTO, DynaMo) that aim to leverage web-scale video for embodied AI. Practically, the result that 2.5% supervision yields a 4.2× improvement suggests that hybrid semi-supervised approaches may be necessary for real-world deployment, though the pessimistic cross-embodied results raise open questions about whether this strategy transfers to truly diverse, web-scale data. A critical limitation is that the evaluation is confined to four DCS environments with synthetic distractors, and the paper acknowledges that real-world video noise patterns may differ substantially. The finding that LAM representations encode control-irrelevant information (Figure 11) also casts doubt on using latent action learning purely as a visual representation pre-training objective.
