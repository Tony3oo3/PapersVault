## Problem

Wasserstein Barycenter (WB), the standard geometric center in Wasserstein space, minimizes the sum of distances to input distributions and thus concentrates measure where the majority of inputs cluster, leaving minority or outlier distributions disproportionately far away. This paper proposes the Wasserstein Ball Center (WBC), which instead minimizes the maximum Wasserstein distance to any input distribution (a minimax objective), ensuring more equitable treatment of all distributions. The key computational challenge is that the resulting LP is substantially larger and structurally more complex than the WB LP, making standard solvers infeasible at realistic scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes N discrete probability measures with support sizes m_i and a fixed support set Ω of size m (which need not coincide with the input supports), along with precomputed p-norm distance matrices D^(t) ∈ R^{m×m_i}. It produces the WBC probability vector w ∈ R^m, N coupling (transport plan) matrices Π^(t) ∈ R^{m×m_i}, and the minimal Wasserstein radius γ. The LP formulation has mΣm_i + m + N + 1 variables and Nm + Σm_i + N + 1 constraints; after removing N redundant rows to ensure full row-rank, the constraint matrix has dimensions (Nm + Σm_i + 1) × (mΣm_i + m + N + 1).

## Method

The WBC problem is cast as a standard LP by introducing a slack variable γ for the minimax objective, then solved via Mehrotra's predictor-corrector interior point method. The core technical contribution is an efficient solver for the Newton normal equation (ĀRĀ^T)Δy = f, which dominates per-iteration cost. By exploiting the Kronecker-product and block-diagonal structure of the constraint matrix, the authors design a three-step sequence of congruence transformations that converts the seemingly dense normal-equation matrix into a block-diagonal form; the most challenging block (G₁+G₂)⁻¹ is handled via the Woodbury matrix identity, reducing its application to O(Nm²) flops. This yields O(m²Σm_i + Nm³ + N²m² + N³) time per IPM iteration versus O(N³m⁴) for vanilla matrix inversion, a speedup of O(min{N²m, Nm², m⁴}), and the algorithm outperforms Gurobi's concurrent simplex/barrier solvers in practice.

## Results

The proposed algorithm achieves a per-iteration complexity of O(Nm³ + N²m² + N³) versus O(N³m⁴) for vanilla IPM, yielding a 10⁷× speedup for N=1000, m=100. On the FairFace dataset (700 face images across 7 races), WBC reduces the maximum Wasserstein distance for the most disadvantaged group ('Middle Eastern') from 78.30 (WB) to 45.37, only slightly above the average WD to WB (38.70). The algorithm converges at a super-linear rate, consistent with the theoretical guarantee of the Mizuno-Todd-Ye predictor-corrector method, and outperforms Gurobi Optimizer v11.0.0 across all tested scales, with Gurobi failing entirely for m > 500 due to memory constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) Gurobi Optimizer v11.0.0 (which simultaneously runs dual simplex, parallel barrier, and primal simplex) and (2) the standard Wasserstein Barycenter (WB) as a fairness baseline. Against Gurobi, the proposed algorithm is consistently faster across all tested problem sizes (varying N from 3 to 2000 and m from 100 to 30000), with the performance gap widening as scale increases; Gurobi cannot handle m > 500 due to memory limits while the proposed method scales to m = 30000. Against WB on the FairFace dataset, WBC dramatically reduces the worst-case Wasserstein distance for underrepresented races (e.g., 'Middle Eastern' drops from 78.30 to 45.37), and across 2,700 synthetic experiments WBC consistently achieves lower variance in Wasserstein distances to input distributions, confirming its fairness advantage. On ShapeNet 3D point clouds, WBC visibly preserves outlier shape characteristics (e.g., chair legs and backrest among car shapes) that WB completely loses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the interior-point method framework for Wasserstein barycenter (WB) computation introduced by Ge et al. (2019), adapting Mehrotra's predictor-corrector IPM to a new 'minmax' objective. The key departure is replacing the min-sum WB formulation with a min-max Wasserstein Ball Center (WBC) problem, which the authors connect conceptually to socially fair clustering (Abbasi et al., 2021; Ghadiri et al., 2021) but instantiate in the optimal transport setting. The technical core exploits a structural observation—that the normal equation matrix admits a block-diagonal decomposition via the Woodbury identity—that has no direct analogue in the WB literature, where the constraint matrix lacks the same coupling structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WBC offers a principled fairness-oriented alternative to WB for applications where minority distributions (e.g., underrepresented racial groups in medical imaging or face datasets) are systematically disadvantaged by the min-sum objective. The O(Nm³ + N²m² + N³) per-iteration complexity makes the problem tractable at scales where vanilla IPM or commercial solvers like Gurobi fail, which is practically important for real-world datasets with hundreds of distributions. Open questions include the free-support WBC (sketched in Appendix I as a nonconvex QCQP), robustness to noisy or spurious outlier distributions (acknowledged in the Impact Statement), and whether the minmax formulation generalizes to multi-marginal or entropically regularized settings.
