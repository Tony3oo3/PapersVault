## Problem

Language is highly aliased, meaning any focal linguistic attribute (e.g., factual inaccuracies) is correlated with surrounding non-focal language (e.g., inflammatory tone), so standard causal effect estimates conflate the focal attribute's effect with that of its correlated context. No prior framework formally defines or estimates the *isolated* causal effect of a single language attribute while holding the rest of the text fixed, nor does it provide tools to assess how sensitive such estimates are to the inevitable information loss when approximating the full non-focal language with a finite-dimensional representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text corpus with a known binary focal attribute a(X), a lower-dimensional non-focal language approximation a_s^c(X) (e.g., LIWC/Empath lexicon vectors, BERT/RoBERTa/MPNet/MiniLM embeddings, SenteCon category vectors, or LLM-prompted discrete attributes), and observed outcomes Y. It produces a doubly robust isolated effect estimate τ̂_DR with asymptotic confidence intervals, plus three diagnostic metrics—fidelity σ̂², overlap ν̂², and robustness value RV—that quantify sensitivity to omitted variable bias, along with user-calibrated OVB bounds (τ̂_DR⁻, τ̂_DR⁺) parameterized by sensitivity constants C_Y and C_D. The target distribution P* is chosen as either the observed corpus distribution (IATE) or the treated-subgroup distribution (IATT), and estimation uses 5-fold cross-fitting with separate training and estimation folds.

## Method

The core estimator is a doubly robust construction that combines importance-weighted reweighting of observed outcomes with an outcome model, where the transporting importance weight γ reweights each observation by the ratio P*(a^c(X)) / [P(a^c(X)) · P(a(X)=a'|a^c(X))] to enforce a common non-focal language distribution P* under both treatment conditions. Unbiasedness holds if either the weights or the outcome model is correctly specified. The key technical contribution for practical use is linking the approximation of non-focal language to omitted variable bias: the paper defines a fidelity metric σ² (residual variance of the short outcome model) and an overlap metric ν² (second moment of the short importance weights), then proves the bound |τ_DR_s − τ*|² ≤ σ²ν²C_Y²C_D², yielding interpretable OVB bounds on the effect. A robustness value RV = |τ_DR_s/(σν)| summarizes overall tolerance to bias, and the paper demonstrates a fidelity-overlap tradeoff where higher-dimensional representations improve fidelity but risk near-overlap violations that inflate ν², with dimensionality reduction (e.g., SVD) shown to improve both metrics simultaneously.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic Amazon dataset, the doubly robust estimator recovers the true isolated effect across all 10 LIWC interventions, with estimates converging toward ground truth as the non-focal representation dimensionality increases from 2 to 9 categories (fidelity σ² decreases, overlap ν² increases, and robustness values rise). On the real-world SvT dataset, all isolated effect estimates have wide 95% CIs that include both the true clinical-trial effect and 0, but SenteCon-Empath and MiniLM produce point estimates closest to the ground truth. SenteCon-Empath and LLM prompting achieve the highest robustness values, while MPNet exhibits a near overlap violation (much larger ν² than BERT or RoBERTa at the same dimensionality). The OVB lower bound for SenteCon-Empath remains positive even after omitting relevant lexical categories or masking key text information, suggesting the estimate is robust to moderate omitted variable bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing causal estimation methods; instead, it compares different non-focal language representations (LIWC, Empath, BERT, RoBERTa, MPNet, MiniLM, SenteCon-LIWC, SenteCon-Empath, LLM prompting) as approximations of a^c(X) within the same doubly robust framework. SenteCon-Empath emerges as the best representation on SvT, combining a point estimate close to the clinical-trial ground truth with the highest robustness value alongside LLM prompting. High-dimensional embeddings (MPNet, BERT, RoBERTa) suffer from worse overlap than low-dimensional lexicons, but SVD dimensionality reduction (200-dim RoBERTa+SVD, MPNet+SVD) improves overlap, maintains fidelity, and increases robustness value, moving effect estimates from outside to inside the true effect's confidence interval. A naive estimator that does not isolate the focal attribute (i.e., estimates the natural effect) is included as a contrast and does not correctly recover the true isolated effect, confirming the necessity of the isolation procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal effect estimation framework for natural language introduced by Lin et al. (2023), which defined the 'natural causal effect' where non-focal language follows its natural distribution. Here, the authors formalize a new target of inference—the 'isolated causal effect'—that averages over all possible non-focal language configurations, and provide a doubly robust estimator drawing on importance weighting (Robins et al., 1994) and OVB bounds from Chernozhukov et al. (2024). The work is positioned alongside other methods for estimating text-based causal effects (Pryzant et al., 2021; Dhawan et al., 2024; Imai & Nakamura, 2024), which implicitly target isolated effects but lack the formal sensitivity analysis provided here. The key conceptual departure is the explicit treatment of non-focal language approximation quality as a source of omitted variable bias, with fidelity and overlap metrics that diagnose why an estimate may be unreliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a principled way to assess how well their choice of language representation (lexicon, embedding, LLM-prompted features) supports valid causal inference, which is critical as language-based causal claims become more common in NLP and social science. The fidelity-overlap tradeoff offers a concrete diagnostic: high-dimensional embeddings may achieve good outcome model fidelity but suffer near-overlap violations that inflate importance weights, and the robustness value quantifies the net effect. Open questions include handling external confounding beyond the text, characterizing bias from estimation error in the focal attribute function a(·), and learning representations that explicitly optimize the fidelity-overlap tradeoff. The real-world SvT results, while suggestive, come with wide confidence intervals, leaving the practical magnitude of OVB in realistic settings partially unresolved.
