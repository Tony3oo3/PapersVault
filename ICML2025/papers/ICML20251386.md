## Problem

Low-rank compression (replacing large weight matrices with products of smaller ones) is widely used in practice to reduce computational cost, yet no universal approximation theory result guarantees that low-rank networks can approximate smooth functions to arbitrary precision. This paper fills that gap by proving that low-rank Swish networks with a fixed depth can approximate any function in a Hölder ball, providing a principled theoretical justification for the empirical success of low-rank compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point x ∈ [0,1]^d as input and produces a scalar approximation of f(x) for any f in the Hölder ball C^{β,R}([0,1]^d), where β > 0 is the smoothness parameter and R is the radius. The network architecture interleaves Swish-activated nonlinear layers of width H with narrow bias-free linear hidden layers of width H_i, subject to the low-rank condition 2H_i < H. The constructive proof guarantees H_i ≤ H/3, meaning the linear bottleneck width is at most one-third of the nonlinear width, which directly translates to at least a one-third reduction in multiplication operations per hidden layer.

## Method

The proof proceeds in four constructive steps: (1) approximate f by a sum over grid points of products of κ-order Taylor polynomials and approximate bump functions (built from Swish) that localize each polynomial to its neighborhood; (2) construct all Taylor polynomials simultaneously in a low-rank Swish network P by building monomials via Swish-based squaring (ρ(x/λ)+ρ(−x/λ) ≈ x²) and multiplication (polarization identity), then combining them with grid-dependent coefficients; (3) construct all bump functions in a parallel low-rank Swish network G using iterative pairwise multiplication; (4) combine P and G via a final inner-product layer to yield the full network. The key architectural insight is that each nonlinear layer is followed by a narrow linear layer (width ≤ H/3), so the effective weight matrix between consecutive nonlinear layers is a product of two smaller matrices, reducing both memory and FLOPs while preserving universal approximation capability for Hölder functions.

## Results

Across 8 UCI datasets (4 classification, 4 regression), low-rank Swish networks with linear hidden layers at 1/3 the width of nonlinear layers match classical feedforward Swish networks in performance. On classification tasks, accuracy differences are within 0.6 percentage points (e.g., Iris: 94.7% vs 95.3%; Adult: 85.8% vs 85.8%), and dependent t-tests confirm no statistically significant degradation. On regression, RMSE values are nearly identical (e.g., Abalone: .077 vs .077; WineQuality: .123 vs .123), with the notable exception of BikeSharing where the low-rank network achieves a significantly lower RMSE of .070 vs .100 (t-statistic 3.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares classical feedforward Swish networks against low-rank Swish networks (with narrow linear hidden layers at 1/3 the width of nonlinear layers) under identical depth and nonlinear-layer width settings. On all 8 datasets, the low-rank networks do not significantly underperform the classical counterparts, as confirmed by dependent t-tests (all |t| < 2 except BikeSharing). On BankMarketing, the low-rank network actually achieves higher accuracy (71.1% vs 68.9%), and on BikeSharing it achieves a significantly lower RMSE (.070 vs .100, t=3.90). The remaining six datasets show essentially identical performance (differences < 0.1 in RMSE or < 0.6 in accuracy). These results empirically validate the theoretical guarantee from Theorem 4.1 that a 1/3 compression ratio for linear hidden layers preserves approximation capability without meaningful performance loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal approximation theory (UAT) literature from standard feedforward networks to low-rank architectures, where narrow bias-free linear layers are interleaved between nonlinear layers. It builds directly on the low-rank compression framework of Denil et al. (2013), Sainath et al. (2013), and Idelbayev & Carreira-Perpiñan (2020), and adopts the approximate bump function technique of Gühring & Raslan (2021) for the constructive proof. Compared to Ohn & Kim (2019), who established UAT for networks with general smooth activations including Swish, this paper achieves a constant depth (vs. O(log 1/ε)) and tighter parameter bounds, while specifically proving the result holds under the low-rank width constraint. The work fills a notable gap: prior UAT results for pruning, quantization, and distillation exist, but no prior theoretical guarantee justified low-rank compression from an approximation-theoretic standpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first principled theoretical justification for low-rank compression, showing that a 1/3 width ratio for linear hidden layers provably preserves approximation capability for Hölder functions — a concrete, actionable guideline for practitioners designing compressed networks. The constructive proof also yields explicit bounds on depth, width, parameter count, and parameter magnitudes, which are useful for understanding the cost-accuracy tradeoff. Limitations include the curse of dimensionality (network width scales as ε^{-d/β}), the restriction to tabular data in experiments, and the fact that the 1/3 ratio is proven sufficient but not shown to be tight. Open questions include whether similar guarantees extend to convolutional or transformer architectures, and whether the constant-depth result can be tightened further for specific function classes.
