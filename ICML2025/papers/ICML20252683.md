## Problem

Existing LDL methods face a fundamental contradiction: those leveraging auxiliary tasks require domain-specific expert knowledge (e.g., facial action units, pathology criteria), limiting generalizability, while those without auxiliary tasks rely solely on primary-task losses and lack extra supervised signal to guide learning. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a way to generate useful auxiliary supervision from the label space itself, without any external knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×P} and a label distribution matrix D ∈ (Δ^{L-1})^N during training, where each row is a probability simplex over L labels. At test time, a single feature vector x' is mapped to a predicted label distribution d' ∈ Δ^{L-1}. The subtask construction step additionally requires the full training D to compute the mask matrix and generate subtask distribution matrices, making the framework a two-stage pipeline (offline subtask generation + online prediction) in the shallow regime, or a single end-to-end network in the deep regime.

## Method

S-LDL partitions the label space into subtask label spaces via a learned binary mask matrix M ∈ {0,1}^{T×L}, optimized with L-BFGS to jointly maximize description-degree coverage (suppressing local ignorance) and minimize pairwise cosine similarity among masks (ensuring diversity). Each subtask distribution is obtained by slicing D along the selected labels and normalizing with N_SUM, which Theorem 4.4 proves is the unique normalization guaranteeing reconstructability of the primary distribution from subtask distributions. In the deep regime, a plug-and-play network with three components—φ(·) for subtask-guided representation learning, ψ(·) for subtask distribution prediction (weighted MAE loss), and ω(·) for the primary task (any existing LDL loss)—is trained end-to-end with total loss ℓ_PRI + μ·ℓ_SUB. The framework is task-agnostic: the same subtask construction applies to vanilla LDL, LDL4C, IncomLDL, and label enhancement by simply swapping the primary loss and adapting the subtask targets accordingly.

## Results

On the JAFFE dataset, S-LDL consistently improves all base methods: S-LRR reduces Clark distance from .3230 (LRR) to .2934 (9.2% relative improvement) and S-KLD reduces it from .3608 (KLD) to .3007 (16.7% improvement). On Yeast_diau, S-QFD² achieves the best Spearman's coefficient of .4203, outperforming QFD² (.4118) and LRR (.4154). For the LDL4C task on sBU_3DFE, S-LDLM achieves the lowest 0/1 loss (.4809) and error probability (.7524), surpassing LDLM (.5258, .7619) and HR (.5167, .7596). The shallow regime S-LDSVR also improves over LDSVR on JAFFE (Cheby: .0959→.0859) and Natural_Scene (Cheby: .4899→.4692).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-LDL is applied as a plug-and-play wrapper to five existing LDL methods (KLD, SCL, LRR, QFD², CJS) and consistently improves all of them across 12 datasets. The largest gains appear for KLD (Clark distance drops from .3608 to .3007 on JAFFE, a 16.7% relative reduction), confirming that loss-function-engineering methods that consider label correlation only one-sidedly benefit most from subtask-augmented supervision. LRR shows strong synergy with S-LDL, achieving rank 1 on both Clark and Cosine on JAFFE and rank 1 on Cheby on Yeast_diau. SCL shows the smallest improvement, which the authors attribute to its reliance on shallow-regime methods in the prediction phase. For the LDL4C derivative task, S-LDLM achieves the best 0/1 loss on sBU_3DFE (.4809 vs .5258 for LDLM and .5167 for HR), while on Flickr the S-LDL4C variant is unstable (std .138) due to dataset sparsity. Against specialized-structure baselines (CPNN, AA-kNN, LDLFs, DF-BFGS), S-LDL variants of KLD/LRR/QFD²/CJS generally match or exceed them on ranking metrics (Spearman, Kendall) while being more flexible in task adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-LDL repurposes the label-space partitioning idea from multi-label learning (HOMER, RAkEL) into the label distribution learning setting, where no prior work had attempted subtask construction. It departs from existing LDL-with-auxiliary-tasks methods (LDL-ALSG, Hayashi-criterion grading, emotion-wheel multi-tasking) by eliminating the need for domain-specific expert knowledge, and from loss-engineering-only LDL methods (SCL, LRR, QFD², CJS) by providing genuinely extra supervised signal rather than reweighting a single objective. The plug-and-play design also extends ensemble-based LDL (LDLFs, DF-LDL) by showing that subtask label spaces (incomplete label spaces) can be aggregated, whereas prior ensemble methods assumed full label spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a general, knowledge-free way to generate auxiliary supervision for LDL, which is attractive given the scarcity and noise of crowdsourced label distributions. Theorem 4.4 (sum-normalization is the unique reconstructable normalization) provides a clean theoretical justification that practitioners can rely on when designing subtask pipelines. However, the method is explicitly limited for continuous/unimodal label distributions (e.g., age estimation), and the empirical gains, while consistent across 12 datasets and multiple base methods, are modest (typically 1–5% on ranking metrics), leaving open whether the approach scales to very large label spaces or benefits from learned (rather than fixed) subtask masks.
