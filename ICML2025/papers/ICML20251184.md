## Problem

Existing Data-Free Image Synthesis (DFIS) methods generate images by optimizing in a vast pixel space without any prior knowledge of natural images, producing samples with severe artifacts that deviate from the target training distribution. While text-to-image diffusion models offer a strong natural image prior, naively substituting them for the training data fails because the imprecise prompts (e.g., 'A dog') cannot capture the specific domain and class-level details encoded in the pre-trained classifier.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN classifier (with BN layers) and its class labels as the only available information, plus a random noise latent z_T ~ N(0,I). It produces 512×512 synthetic images (resized to 224×224 for downstream evaluation) and a per-class CAT embedding vector of dimension 1×784. The prompt is deliberately minimal ('A/An {S_c} {class label}') to reflect the data-free constraint, and once the CAT embedding is found, new images for that class can be generated via standard diffusion sampling without further optimization.

## Method

DDIS uses Stable Diffusion 2.1 as a frozen image prior and introduces two alignment mechanisms. Domain Alignment Guidance (DAG) operates at every diffusion timestep by projecting the latent to pixel space, computing the BN statistics of the decoded image, and applying a gradient step that pulls those statistics toward the running mean/variance stored in the classifier's BN layers, thereby injecting domain knowledge into the sampling trajectory. Class Alignment Token (CAT) adds a single pseudo-word token to the text encoder vocabulary and optimizes only its 784-dim embedding via cross-entropy loss on the final decoded image, capturing class-specific visual details (e.g., specific dog breeds, resolving homonyms like 'tiger cat' vs. 'tiger') that the class label alone cannot convey. The two mechanisms are complementary: DAG handles domain alignment during sampling while CAT handles class alignment through embedding optimization, and the SD network remains entirely frozen throughout, preserving its natural image prior.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (Photo domain), DDIS achieves an Inception Score of 15.92 and FID of 30.31, substantially outperforming DeepInversion (IS 9.52, FID 187.63) and PlugInInversion (IS 3.51, FID 220.62). Precision and Recall metrics further confirm the advantage: DDIS reaches 0.8028/0.7731 versus 0.5038/0.0907 (DI) and 0.2969/0.0528 (PII). In data-free knowledge distillation on ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), DDIS attains 41.68% accuracy, approaching the 43.30% original-data baseline, while DI and PII collapse to 4.67% and 2.01% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDIS dominates both prior DFIS methods (DeepInversion and PlugInInversion) across every dataset and metric. On ImageNet-1k, DDIS reduces FID from 187.63 (DI) and 220.62 (PII) to 30.31, and boosts Recall from 0.0907 (DI) and 0.0528 (PII) to 0.7731, indicating far better coverage of the training distribution. Against vanilla Stable Diffusion 2.1 (used as a non-guided baseline), DDIS consistently outperforms in data-free knowledge distillation: on PACS-art (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), DDIS reaches 28.46% vs. SD's 21.93%, and on ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), DDIS achieves 41.68% vs. SD's 33.02%, approaching the 43.30% original-data upper bound. In data-free pruning experiments, DDIS similarly surpasses DI, PII, and SD at all pruning ratios (50%–90%) on both PACS and ImageNet-1k. The gap is most dramatic on non-photo domains (PACS Art/Cartoon, Style-Aligned Manga/Caricature), where DI and PII produce near-zero Recall (≤0.004) while DDIS maintains Recall above 0.31.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Data-Free Image Synthesis (DFIS) line of work initiated by DeepDream, DeepInversion, and PlugInInversion, which iteratively optimize pixel-space inputs with weak regularizers (total variation, BN statistics) to approximate a classifier's learned distribution. The key departure is repurposing a pre-trained Text-to-Image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1) as a strong natural-image prior to collapse the vast pixel-space search, combined with two novel guidance mechanisms: Domain Alignment Guidance (DAG), which steers diffusion sampling via BN-layer running statistics, and Class Alignment Token (CAT), a single optimized pseudo-word embedding that captures class-specific details beyond the label string. Conceptually, DAG is a diffusion-space analogue of the BN regularizer in DeepInversion, while CAT draws on the textual-inversion paradigm (Gal et al., 2022) but applies it in a data-free, class-alignment context rather than personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDIS is the first DFIS method to handle non-photo domains (art painting, cartoon, manga, caricature) and to resolve lexical ambiguity in class labels (e.g., 'tiger cat' vs. 'tiger'), broadening the practical scope of data-free applications like knowledge distillation and pruning. The ~267× reduction in optimization iterations over DeepInversion for 100K ImageNet images makes large-scale DFIS computationally feasible on a single GPU. However, the method is restricted to classifiers with BN layers, struggles with abstract domains like sketch, and the reliance on a specific T2I model (SD 2.1) raises questions about generalizability to newer architectures. Open questions include model-agnostic domain alignment, extending to non-CNN backbones, and whether the single-token CAT generalizes to fine-grained or open-vocabulary settings.
