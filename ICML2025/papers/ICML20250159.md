## Problem

Existing point cloud data augmentation methods either destroy local geometric structure (mix-based methods like PointMixup, PointCutMix, SageMix) or produce distorted samples with significant semantic deviation (self-augmentation methods like PointWOLF, PointAugment). None of these approaches guarantee topological consistency between the original and augmented point clouds, leading to augmented samples that diverge from the true data manifold and degrade model generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud P = {p₁, ..., pₙ} (1024 points for classification, 2048 for segmentation) normalized to unit sphere space and produces a deformed point cloud P′ with identical point count and labels. The output is guaranteed to be a homeomorphic image of the input, preserving topology and class/part labels. During training, both original and augmented point clouds are fed to the network; at inference, only the original point cloud is used to avoid disrupting geometric priors.

## Method

SinPoint applies a sine-based residual displacement field P′ = P + A·sin(ωP + φ) to deform point clouds, where the mapping is proven to be a homeomorphism when |Aω| ≤ 1, ensuring topological consistency. Two variants are designed: SinPoint-SSF (single sine function with φ=0) for classification tasks that benefit from global shape perturbation, and SinPoint-MSF (superposition of k sine functions with anchor points as phases) for segmentation tasks requiring diverse local detail. Amplitude A and angular velocity ω are sampled uniformly from U(-a,a) and U(-w,w) respectively to maximize diversity. A Markov Chain Augmentation Process further expands the distribution by randomly sequencing basic affine transformations (scaling, shifting, rotation, jittering) with a fixed transition probability, and the final augmented sample is mixed with the original during training.

## Results

On 3D shape classification, SinPoint-SSF achieves 93.4% OA with PointNet++ and 93.7% OA with DGCNN on ModelNet40, and 90.2% OA on both OBJ_ONLY and PB_T50_RS (ScanObjectNN) with PointNet++ and DGCNN respectively. The average OA improvement over the best prior method is 2.6% on synthetic datasets and 5.9% on real-world datasets, with a maximum gain of 7.3% (DGCNN on PB_T50_RS). On part segmentation (ShapeNetPart), SinPoint-MSF achieves 85.8 mIoU with PointNet++ (+1.0% over baseline) and 85.5 mIoU with DGCNN (+0.7%). SinPoint also improves robustness under Gaussian noise (σ=0.05: 61.5% vs. 48.4% baseline), 180° rotation (Z-axis: 44.1% vs. 32.4%), and 50% dropout (79.5% vs. 75.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SinPoint outperforms all ten compared augmentation methods across every dataset and backbone in the main classification table. The closest competitors are PointWOLF (Kim et al., ICCV 2021) and SageMix (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2022), which achieve 93.2% and 93.3% respectively with PointNet++ on ModelNet40, while SinPoint reaches 93.4%; the gap widens substantially on real-world ScanObjectNN where SinPoint gains 5.9–7.3% over the best prior method. Mix-based methods (PointMixup, PointCutMix, RSMix, WOLFMix, PCSalMix, PointPatchMix) generally trail self-augmentation methods on real-world data and are not reported on all four datasets. On part segmentation, SinPoint-MSF edges out SageMix (85.7 mIoU) and PointWOLF (85.2 mIoU) with PointNet++, achieving 85.8 mIoU. In terms of training efficiency, SinPoint requires only 8 seconds per epoch versus 84 s for PointAugment, 51 s for SageMix, and 15 s for PointWOLF, while simultaneously delivering the highest accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SinPoint departs from the dominant line of point cloud augmentation work (PointMixup, PointCutMix, SageMix, PointWOLF, PointAugment) by introducing a topological guarantee—homeomorphism via sine-based displacement—that prior methods lacked. While mix-based methods explicitly destroy local structure by splicing two point clouds, and self-augmentation methods like PointWOLF apply local rigid transforms that can cause folding, SinPoint frames augmentation as a continuous, invertible deformation that provably preserves topology. The Markov chain framework for sequentially composing basic affine transformations is borrowed from general augmentation pipelines but is applied here in conjunction with the homeomorphic sine deformation, a combination not seen in prior point cloud work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The homeomorphism guarantee offers a principled way to control augmentation intensity: by constraining Aω ≤ 1, practitioners can ensure the augmented samples remain topologically equivalent to the originals, avoiding the semantic drift that plagues PointWOLF and mix-based methods. However, the practical gains, while consistent across backbones and datasets, are modest (1–7% OA), and the method has only been validated on classification and part segmentation—scene-level tasks (S3DIS, SemanticKITTI show small gains) and object detection remain unexplored. An open question is whether the topological constraint is truly the source of the improvement or whether the smooth, bounded displacement field simply provides a well-behaved regularizer comparable to a carefully tuned PointWOLF.
