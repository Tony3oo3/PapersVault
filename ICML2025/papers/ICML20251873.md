## Problem

Applying LLMs to time series forecasting faces three intertwined challenges: cross-domain generalization (identical values carry domain-specific meanings), cross-modality alignment (LLMs lack native understanding of temporal data), and error accumulation in autoregressive prediction. Existing methods address these challenges in isolation, leaving no unified framework that simultaneously enables multi-domain pre-training, effective language-time-series alignment, and stable long-horizon autoregressive forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251873/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series of lookback length L=96 (divided into non-overlapping patches of size 24) along with Temporal Comprehension Prompts containing dataset description, channel description, and timestamp as structured text. During pre-training, input lengths vary over {96, 288, 480, 672} to build adaptive capacity. Output is produced autoregressively in chunks of 4 patches (96 values per step) to reach horizons of 96, 192, 336, or 720 steps, with the time series first compressed into a single embedding token before prediction.

## Method

LangTime combines three components: a channel-independent Temporal Encoder (4-layer transformer with group query attention, 512-dim latent space) that maps patches into the LLM's word embedding space; Temporal Comprehension Prompts (TCPs) that encode dataset/channel metadata as natural language instructions guiding the LLM (Qwen2-0.5B-Instruction) to compress the series into one token via a <|EMB|> placeholder and generate predictions via <|OUT|>; and TimePPO, a PPO-based fine-tuning algorithm with a multi-dimensional reward (reciprocals of MSE, MAE, and KL divergence plus an MSE policy-penalty term) and a repeat-based value function that estimates sequence-wide returns. Pre-training jointly optimizes reconstruction and prediction with Huber loss and random patch masking (rate 0.4), while TimePPO uses GAE with a sensitivity parameter ξ to mitigate error accumulation during autoregressive rollout.

## Results

On the seven standard long-term forecasting benchmarks (lookback 96, horizons 96/192/336/720), LangTime fine-tuned with TimePPO achieves the best average MSE on 6 of 7 datasets, with headline values of 0.397 (ETTm1), 0.284 (ETTm2), 0.435 (ETTh1), 0.375 (ETTh2), 0.201 (Electricity), 0.361 (Exchange), and 0.252 (Weather). The pre-trained cross-domain model (LangTimePT) attains the best MSE in 58 out of 70 entries among cross-domain-trained baselines, and the TimePPO fine-tuned variant surpasses individually-trained models in 45 out of 70 entries. In zero-shot transfer to unseen domains, LangTimePT outperforms UniTime and AutoTimes on both Traffic (avg MSE 0.573 vs 0.601 vs 0.639) and Illness (avg MSE 4.023 vs 4.359 vs 4.898).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary cross-domain LLM baselines, LangTime dominates: on ETTm1, LangTimeTimePPO achieves MSE 0.397 versus AutoTimes at 0.940 (a 58% reduction), while on ETTh1 it reaches 0.435 versus AutoTimes at 0.485. Compared to UniTime (the other cross-domain LLM baseline), LangTime wins on all seven datasets in the pre-trained setting, with the largest gap on Electricity (0.230 vs. higher UniTime values reported in Table 1). Among per-dataset baselines (S2IP-LLM, Time-LLM, GPT4TS, PatchTST, TimesNet), LangTimeTimePPO surpasses all in 45 of 70 MSE/MAE entries, establishing new state-of-the-art on most datasets. In zero-shot transfer to unseen domains (Traffic, Illness), LangTimePT outperforms both UniTime and AutoTimes by a wide margin (e.g., Traffic avg MSE 0.573 vs 0.601 vs 0.639; Illness avg MSE 4.023 vs 4.359 vs 4.898), confirming the effectiveness of TCPs for domain generalization. The TimePPO fine-tuning consistently improves over SFT by 0.5–3% MSE across datasets, with the largest gains on Electricity (0.201 vs 0.211) and Weather (0.252 vs 0.263), validating its role in mitigating autoregressive error accumulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LangTime sits at the intersection of two active research threads: LLM-based time series forecasting (UniTime, AutoTimes, S2IP-LLM, Time-LLM, GPT4TS) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (InstructGPT, PPO). It departs from prior LLM-for-time-series work by simultaneously addressing cross-domain generalization and cross-modality alignment through Temporal Comprehension Prompts, whereas existing methods tackle these challenges in isolation. The TimePPO component is a direct adaptation of PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) to the continuous action space of autoregressive time series prediction, introducing a multi-dimensional reward (MSE, MAE, KL divergence) and a repeat-based value function that has no direct analogue in the NLP RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply PPO-style reinforcement learning fine-tuning to autoregressive time series forecasting, offering a principled mechanism to mitigate error accumulation that supervised fine-tuning cannot address. The zero-shot transferability results (outperforming UniTime and AutoTimes on unseen Traffic and Illness datasets) suggest the TCP-based language guidance provides genuine domain-agnostic structure. However, the performance gains from TimePPO over standard SFT are modest (typically 1–3% MSE reduction), and the framework is validated only on seven relatively small datasets with a 0.5B-parameter backbone, leaving open whether the RL fine-tuning advantage persists at larger scale or on more diverse domains. Future work on broader time series tasks (anomaly detection, classification) and larger LLMs would strengthen the case.
