## Problem

Conventional reinforcement learning follows a bottom-up trial-and-error paradigm that struggles with sparse rewards, limited exploration budgets, and the vast decision spaces of real-world domains. While hierarchical RL, environment shaping, and LLM-assisted methods offer partial remedies, they typically rely on manually engineered sub-goals, single-plan generation, or purely data-driven structure discovery. This paper asks whether a top-down, human-inspired strategic reasoning process can systematically generate multiple candidate policy distributions that balance specificity (narrowing the search) and value (preserving near-optimality), thereby reducing sample complexity without sacrificing expressivity.

## Input / Output

The Strategist agent takes as input a natural-language description of the environment and goal (e.g., Crafter game mechanics and 'maximize meat collection'), which GPT-4o processes offline to produce a Strategy Tree—a hierarchical structure of natural-language nodes annotated with LLM-estimated feasibility and value scores. The RL agent then receives 64×64 pixel observations from Crafter and a shaped reward signal computed by a ResNet-18 network (distilled from LLM state-scoring on 5,000 sampled states) combined with the original sparse environment reward via a decaying mixing coefficient α. The final output is a trained policy (PPO, EDE, or DreamerV3) that exhibits strategy-aligned behaviors such as cow breeding or tool crafting.

## Method

The Strategist agent constructs a Strategy Tree via iterative LLM-based tree search: starting from the root goal, it repeatedly selects the leaf with the highest feasibility×value product and prompts GPT-4o to decompose it into either mutually exclusive Approach nodes (alternative strategies) or sequential Plan nodes (complementary sub-goals), continuing until nodes reach sufficient specificity. The paper formalizes this as the Strategy Problem—finding a distribution over policies δ_Π with bounded entropy H(δ_Π) ≤ H_max whose ε-support contains a near-optimal policy—and proves (Theorem 2.1) that sample complexity scales with H_max rather than the full state-action space. For training, the selected root-to-leaf strategy path is translated into a dense reward: GPT-4o scores each state 0–5 on proximity to the sub-goal, scores are distilled into a ResNet-18 student, and the resulting shaped reward is blended with the environment reward as r = (1−α)R + αR̃, with α annealed from 1.0 to 0 over training to progressively hand control back to the true objective. This plug-and-play reward shaping is demonstrated to accelerate convergence and elicit complex behaviors (e.g., breeding) that vanilla PPO, EDE, and DreamerV3 fail to discover within the same budget.

## Results

On Crafter-Medium, Strategy-guided PPO achieves hunting (first positive reward) in 8/8 runs within 0.59–0.89M steps, compared to vanilla PPO's 4/8 runs at 1.35M steps. Critically, the breeding strategy (a complex multi-step behavior) is achieved in 3/8 runs by Strategy Breed, while vanilla PPO never discovers it (0/8). On the original Crafter environment, Strategist+EDE with Fight, Resource, and Craft strategies significantly outperform vanilla EDE in cumulative achievement rewards, with the Craft-guided agent requiring substantially fewer steps to complete tool-crafting achievements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PPO, Strategist+PPO wins decisively: hunting is achieved in 8/8 runs (vs. 4/8) at roughly 35–55% fewer steps, and the breeding behavior—a complex multi-step strategy involving sapling collection and cow interaction—is discovered only by the strategy-guided agent (3/8 runs) and never by PPO (0/8). Against EDE, Strategist+EDE with the breeding strategy significantly outperforms vanilla EDE on Crafter-Medium in episode reward, with the key differentiator being consistent sapling-to-cow behavior that vanilla EDE fails to learn. Against DreamerV3 (50M parameters), Strategist+PPO still outperforms on Crafter-Medium, indicating that the strategic guidance provides gains orthogonal to model capacity. Against Eureka (an LLM-based reward design method), the paper reports comparisons in the appendix (SC.8), positioning Strategist as a more structured, top-down alternative that avoids Eureka's trial-and-error reward code generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by synthesizing ideas from hierarchical RL, LLM-assisted planning, and reward shaping into a unified top-down framework, rather than building directly on any single prior method. It departs from LLM-as-policy approaches (Yan et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024) that treat the LLM as a state-by-state action prior, and from LLM-based reward code generation (Eureka, Ma et al., 2024) that still relies on trial-and-error reward tuning. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of the Strategy Problem—searching over distributions of policies with an explicit specificity-value trade-off—and the use of an LLM to construct a Strategy Tree offline before any RL training begins, separating strategic reasoning from low-level policy learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, plug-and-play mechanism to reduce sample complexity in sparse-reward RL, with a clean theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) showing sample complexity scales with strategy entropy rather than the full state-action space. Empirically, it elicits qualitatively new behaviors (e.g., cow breeding) that neither vanilla PPO nor EDE discover, and it composes with multiple RL backbones. Key limitations include LLM miscalibration of feasibility/value estimates, potential reversion to simpler strategies as the shaping coefficient decays, and evaluation restricted to the Crafter environment family. Open questions include mid-training strategy pivoting, closing the feedback loop between agent progress and strategy updates, and scaling to truly high-stakes real-world domains.
