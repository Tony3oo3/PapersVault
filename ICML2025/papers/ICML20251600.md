## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods (RAFT, RRHF, DPO) that generate additional training data treat data generation and model training as separate, static processes, discarding lower-scoring samples and thus inefficiently utilizing generated data. The paper identifies that these two processes are highly interdependent and proposes a framework that dynamically couples them so that all generated responses contribute to training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251600/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method takes a text query x and a human-designed principle prompt p (covering ethical, informative, and helpful criteria) to generate two responses per query: an original response y ~ π(·|x) and a principle-guided response y^prompt ~ π(·|[p,x]). At inference time, only the query x is provided and the model produces a single aligned text response. The principle prompt is a training-only mechanism and is not present at test time.

## Method

PLE (Progressively Label Enhancement) dynamically adjusts the training loss based on the reward score gap between the principle-guided and original responses. When the gap s^prompt − s exceeds a threshold τ_t, a length-normalized ranking loss (borrowed from RRHF) pushes the model toward the better response and away from the worse one. When the gap is at or below τ_t, both responses are retained via a weighted SFT loss where weights are normalized to [−1,1] using a softmax-like function of the reward scores, ensuring even lower-scoring data contributes to training. The threshold decays geometrically (τ_t = τ_{t-1}·α) as the model improves, progressively tightening the criterion for what counts as a 'significant' improvement. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee bounding the probability that the trained model deviates from the optimal policy by more than ε/2, under a pure level-set assumption on the reward margin distribution.

## Results

On the HH dataset (the primary evaluation), PLE achieves the highest reward model scores across both LLaMA3-8B (RM-Gemma-2B: -2.3266, RM-Mistral-7B: 6.8386) and Qwen2.5-7B (RM-Gemma-2B: -2.2013, RM-Mistral-7B: 6.3633), while maintaining PPL comparable to SFT (8.4213 vs. 8.4231 on LLaMA3-8B). On IMDb, PLE attains the best RM score (1.3289) and BLEU (0.0493), outperforming the next-best PPO (RM 1.0232) and RAFT-4 (BLEU 0.0436). On TL;DR, PLE achieves the best RM score (0.3845) but is slightly below RAFT-4 on BLEU (0.8674 vs. 0.8725).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLE consistently outperforms all four baselines (SFT, DPO, PPO, RAFT-4) on reward model scores across all three datasets. On HH with LLaMA3-8B, PLE's RM-Mistral-7B score of 6.8386 exceeds the next-best DPO (6.2029) by 0.64 and RAFT-4 (5.8925) by 0.95; on Qwen2.5-7B the gap is even larger (6.3633 vs. RAFT-4's 3.8697). On PPL, PLE matches SFT (the best PPL baseline) on LLaMA3-8B (8.4213 vs. 8.4231) and is competitive on Qwen2.5-7B (7.6179 vs. PPO's 7.5382), while DPO and PPO suffer significant PPL degradation. On BLEU, PLE ties RAFT-4 on LLaMA3-8B (0.8771) and surpasses all baselines on Qwen2.5-7B (0.8688 vs. DPO's 0.8589). On IMDb, PLE leads on both RM (1.3289 vs. PPO's 1.0232) and BLEU (0.0493 vs. RAFT-4's 0.0436); on TL;DR, PLE wins on RM (0.3845 vs. PPO's 0.3121) but trails RAFT-4 slightly on BLEU (0.8674 vs. 0.8725).

## Relation to Prior Work

PLE composes three established ideas into a unified framework: the length-normalized ranking loss from RRHF (Yuan et al., 2023), the label-enhancement weighting scheme from Xu et al. (2021), and the principle-guided prompting mechanism from Sun et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure from prior alignment methods (RAFT, DPO, PPO, LIMA) is the explicit coupling of data generation and model training through a dynamically decaying threshold, rather than treating them as separate static stages. This allows lower-scoring generated responses to contribute to training via weighted SFT instead of being discarded, addressing what the authors identify as an underexplored inefficiency in existing reward-ranked fine-tuning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLE offers a practical path to LLM alignment without the stability and scalability issues of full RLHF, while making more efficient use of generated data than RAFT-style selection. The theoretical convergence guarantee (Theorem 5.3) is a notable addition, though it relies on a strong approximation-error assumption (Eq. 8) that is difficult to verify empirically. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted principle prompts generalize across domains, how the method scales to much larger models (experiments use GPT-2 for IMDb/TL;DR and 7–8B models for HH), and whether the dynamic threshold schedule can be adapted automatically rather than set with a fixed decay factor.
