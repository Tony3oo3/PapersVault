## Problem

Existing density ratio estimation methods (TRE, DRE-∞) suffer from two fundamental failures: the density-chasm problem, where multi-modal or divergent distributions produce unstable ratio estimates, and the support-chasm problem, where minimal overlap between supp(q0) and supp(q1) renders ratios ill-defined. Additionally, the absolute time score E[|∂t log qt|] diverges at the boundaries t→0 and t→1 for all prior deterministic and diffusion-bridge interpolants, causing numerical instability in estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. paired samples (x0, x1) drawn independently from q0 and q1, plus a query point x ∈ ℝ^d. It outputs the log density ratio log r(x) ≈ ∫₀¹ s_θ*^t(x,t) dt, computed by numerically integrating the learned time-score network via adjoint ODE solving. For density estimation, log q1(x) is recovered as log r(x) + log q0(x); for mutual information, the ratio is evaluated on correlated Gaussian pairs. The OTR variant additionally requires mini-batches of N paired samples to compute the Sinkhorn coupling π_{2γ²} before interpolation.

## Method

D³RE constructs a continuous-time interpolant X_t' = I(X0', X1', t) + √(t(1-t)γ² + (α_t²+β_t²)ε) · Z_t, where I is a deterministic interpolant (e.g., linear), X_i' = X_i + z_ε are Gaussian-dequantized boundary samples, and Z_t ~ N(0, E_d). The Brownian bridge noise term expands the support of intermediate distributions (Theorem 3.2), while Gaussian dequantization (GD) with small ε ensures the time score remains uniformly bounded over [0,1] (Corollary 3.8) and the dequantified ratio r' uniformly approximates r with O(ε) error (Proposition 3.5). The DSBI variant further applies entropically regularized optimal transport rearrangement (OTR) via the Sinkhorn algorithm to re-pair mini-batch samples, which provably reduces the variance of the time score (Theorem 3.7) and implicitly solves the Schrödinger bridge problem (Proposition 3.6). Training uses an integration-by-parts score-matching loss L3 that avoids intractable ∂t log q_t' terms, optimized with Adam; the final log-ratio is obtained by ODE-integrating the trained score model over t ∈ [0,1].

## Results

On MNIST density estimation (bits-per-dimension, lower is better), D³RE(DSBI) achieves the best BPD across all three noise types: 1.293 (Gaussian), 1.170 (Copula), and 1.066 (RQ-NSF), outperforming DRE-∞ (1.33, 1.21, 1.09) and TRE (1.39, 1.24, 1.09). On eight 2-D synthetic datasets, D³RE produces sharper and more accurate density estimates than DRE-∞, particularly on complex multi-modal distributions like rings, checkerboard, 2spirals, and pinwheel. For mutual information estimation at d=120, D³RE converges faster and with fewer fluctuations around the true MI value compared to DRE-∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³RE consistently outperforms all three baselines on MNIST density estimation. Against NCE (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2012), D³RE(DSBI) reduces BPD by 0.667 (Gaussian), 0.160 (Copula), and 0.024 (RQ-NSF). Against TRE (Rhodes et al., 2020), the improvements are 0.097, 0.070, and 0.024 BPD respectively. Against the closest baseline DRE-∞ (Choi et al., 2022), D³RE(DSBI) achieves 0.037, 0.040, and 0.024 BPD reductions. The intermediate variant DRE-∞+OTR (DRE-∞ with optimal transport rearrangement but without diffusion bridge or Gaussian dequantization) narrows the gap but still trails D³RE, confirming that the diffusion bridge and GD components contribute meaningfully beyond OTR alone. On 2-D synthetic density estimation, DRE-∞ struggles with complex multi-modal datasets (rings, checkerboard, 2spirals, pinwheel) where D³RE produces visibly sharper estimates, and on MI estimation at d=120, D³RE converges faster and more stably than DRE-∞.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the continuous-time density ratio estimation line (TRE → DRE-∞) by reframing prior deterministic interpolants (DI) as a special case and introducing three progressive enhancements: a Brownian bridge component (DBI) borrowed from denoising diffusion bridge models (Zhou et al., 2024a) and stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023), Gaussian dequantization (GD) to stabilize boundary time scores, and entropically regularized optimal transport rearrangement (OTR) via Sinkhorn to solve the Schrödinger bridge problem (De Bortoli et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure is showing that these three mechanisms each target a distinct failure mode—support-chasm, boundary divergence, and coupling inefficiency—and can be composed into a single principled framework (D³RE) with matching theoretical guarantees (support expansion, bounded time scores, variance reduction). Unlike DRE-∞, which only trains a single time-score network on deterministic paths, D³RE's DSBI variant implicitly solves the Schrödinger bridge problem, connecting the DRE literature to the broader Schrödinger bridge / entropic OT community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical treatment of both the density-chasm and support-chasm problems in DRE, with clean guarantees (uniform approximation via Prop. 3.5, bounded time scores via Cor. 3.8, variance reduction via Thm. 3.7) that were previously absent. However, empirical validation is limited to 2D synthetic datasets and MNIST, with modest BPD improvements over DRE-∞ (e.g., 1.33 → 1.293 on Gaussian noise), and no evaluation on high-dimensional real-world tasks beyond MI estimation. Open questions include whether the framework scales to high-dimensional data (e.g., ImageNet-scale), whether adaptive or learned solvers can reduce the remaining function-evaluation overhead, and whether more expressive interpolants beyond linear + Brownian bridge can handle highly multi-modal distributions. The NFE reduction from OTR is a practical efficiency gain that could make continuous-time DRE more competitive with discrete telescoping methods.
