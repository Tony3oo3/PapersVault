## Problem

Unsupervised motion transfer methods struggle to accurately estimate motion when the pose difference between source and driving images is large, because directly modeling complex large-displacement transformations is inherently difficult. Existing approaches such as FOMM, TPSMM, and CPABMM improve motion representation but still produce artifacts or inaccurate poses under large displacement conditions, and prior chain-of-pose methods require identity-matched intermediate frames that are unavailable in the unsupervised setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252511/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image and a driving video (sequence of RGB frames) at 256×256 resolution and produces an animated video where the source object follows the driving pose, along with a set of intermediate interpolated images generated at discrete time points {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}. The interpolated images are not directly used as final output but serve as a bridge: a selector picks the optimal interpolated frame to replace the source image for the downstream motion transfer step, so the actual motion estimation only needs to cover a smaller displacement from the interpolated image to the driving frame.

## Method

The core mechanism is a keypoint-based anytime interpolation that linearly interpolates keypoint positions between source and driving images at time t, then uses a TPS-based dense motion network to warp the source image into an interpolated image at that time. A selector scores all generated interpolated images by a normalized combination of ViT-based appearance distance to the source (preserving identity) and keypoint distance to the driving image (minimizing residual motion), and picks the one with the smallest sum. To train without paired data, a bidirectional training strategy runs two pipelines (S→D and D→S) sharing the same interpolation keypoints and network weights, constraining that both directions produce consistent interpolated images. A pre-trained ViT model provides structural consistency loss (self-similarity matrix matching) and appearance consistency loss ([CLS] token matching) to reduce the solution space of interpolated images and improve generation quality, while a VGG-19 perceptual loss ensures photorealism of the final output.

## Results

On the video reconstruction task across four datasets, the proposed method achieves the best motion-related metrics (AKD and MKR) in all settings. On Tai-Chi-HD, Ours achieves AKD=4.21 and MKR=0.014, outperforming TPSMM (4.57, 0.018) by 7.9% and 22.2% respectively. On TedTalks, Ours reaches AKD=3.15 and MKR=0.005, beating CPABMM (3.21, 0.008) by 1.9% and 37.5%. However, appearance metrics (L1, AED) are slightly worse than CPABMM on Tai-Chi-HD (L1: 0.047 vs 0.041; AED: 0.157 vs 0.117) and TedTalks (L1: 0.028 vs 0.022; AED: 0.136 vs 0.085), which the authors attribute to appearance degradation introduced by the interpolation step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven unsupervised motion transfer baselines (X2Face, FOMM, MRAA, DAM, MTIA, TPSMM, CPABMM) on four datasets. The proposed method consistently achieves the best AKD and MKR (motion accuracy) across all datasets where it is evaluated, with the largest gains over TPSMM on Tai-Chi-HD (7.9% AKD, 22.2% MKR improvement) and over CPABMM on TedTalks (1.9% AKD, 37.5% MKR improvement). However, on appearance metrics (L1, AED), CPABMM outperforms the proposed method on Tai-Chi-HD (L1: 0.041 vs 0.047; AED: 0.117 vs 0.157) and TedTalks (L1: 0.022 vs 0.028; AED: 0.085 vs 0.136), which the authors acknowledge is due to appearance degradation from the interpolation step. On Fashion, the method ties with DAM on L1 (0.011) and MKR (0.004) while winning on AKD (0.800 vs 1.041). The multi-view extension (Ours-V2) further improves AKD by 12.8% on Tai-Chi-HD and 26.1% on UvA-Nemo over the single-view version, while also improving appearance metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FOMM→TPSMM→CPABMM lineage of unsupervised keypoint-based motion transfer by inserting an intermediate interpolation stage between source and driving images. It reuses the TPS transformation and dense motion network architecture from TPSMM but departs from the direct source-to-driving motion estimation paradigm by decomposing large displacement into multiple small displacements via linearly interpolated keypoints. The bidirectional training strategy with ViT-based structural and appearance consistency losses is a novel training mechanism not present in prior motion transfer work, drawing on self-supervised ViT representations (DINO) as external semantic priors rather than relying solely on GAN-style adversarial or perceptual losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in unsupervised motion transfer—degraded pose accuracy under large source-driving displacement—without requiring paired data or object-specific priors. However, the decomposition strategy introduces a trade-off: motion metrics (AKD, MKR) improve meaningfully while appearance fidelity (L1, AED) slightly degrades due to interpolation-induced blurring and color shifts, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the linear keypoint interpolation assumption holds for highly non-rigid motions, whether the ViT-based constraints generalize beyond the 256×256 resolution tested, and whether the multi-view extension (Ours-V2) can close the appearance gap without further architectural changes.
