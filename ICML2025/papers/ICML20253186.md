## Problem

Forecasting-based time series anomaly detection methods overfit to local disturbances (peaks and drops), causing them to fail at detecting true anomalies when trained on noisy real-world data. The paper identifies that effective TSAD should model 'normal' behavior through smooth local patterns rather than fitting every fluctuation, and that the B-spline univariate functions in vanilla KAN are inherently localized and thus susceptible to the same overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a univariate time series sliding window of T points (default T=96), which is first-order differenced to suppress trend-induced constant-term fluctuations before being decomposed into 2N+1 univariate function channels (raw signal, N Fourier terms, N periodic-enhanced terms). It outputs a predicted next value x_{i+1} via a single linear layer, from which an anomaly score is derived by comparing prediction to observation; the entire model contains only 274 trainable parameters.

## Method

KAN-AD reformulates time series modeling as approximating the series with smooth univariate functions, replacing KAN's B-splines with truncated Fourier series (N terms) that provide local smoothness and natural periodicity. A periodic-enhanced mechanism adds index-based sine-cosine waves cos(2πni/T), sin(2πni/T) to capture fine-scale periods below 1/N. The reducing phase uses a stacked 1D CNN (kernel size 3, with BN and GELU) to learn the coefficients of these fixed univariate functions, followed by a residual connection and a 1-width down-convolution to produce the normal-pattern approximation. A single-layer MLP then projects this approximation to predict the next time point. First-order differencing as a constant-term-elimination preprocessing step isolates trend effects, allowing the model to focus on estimating Fourier coefficients rather than tracking a fluctuating mean.

## Results

On four univariate TSAD benchmarks, KAN-AD achieves an average Event F1 of 0.7798, surpassing the next-best method (LSTMAD, 0.6808) by 15%. The largest gain is on TODS (0.8940 vs. 0.6995 for FCVAE, a 27% improvement), where training data contains substantial anomalies. On the multivariate benchmark (five datasets), KAN-AD achieves the highest average Best F1 of 0.9076 with only 4,491 trainable parameters, compared to 8.07M for UniTS. KAN-AD requires just 274 parameters (vs. 1,360 for vanilla KAN) and runs 50% faster in inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAN-AD outperforms all 11 univariate baselines on three of four datasets: KPI (0.7989 vs. 0.7816 KAN), TODS (0.8940 vs. 0.6995 FCVAE, a 27% margin), and UCR (0.5335 vs. 0.4120 KAN, a 29% margin). On WSD, LSTMAD edges out KAN-AD slightly (0.9028 vs. 0.8927), though KAN-AD still ranks second. Against the closest competitor KAN (the direct architectural predecessor), KAN-AD improves average F1e by 14% while using 80% fewer parameters (274 vs. 1,360). On the multivariate benchmark, KAN-AD achieves the best average Best F1 (0.9076), beating UniTS (0.8921) by 1.5% and TimesNet (0.8526) by 5.5%, with 1,800× fewer parameters than UniTS. Notably, KAN-AD's advantage is most pronounced on TODS and UCR, where training data contains anomalies or strong periodicity—precisely the scenarios where B-spline-based KAN and deep forecasting models (TimesNet, OFA) degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAN-AD builds directly on the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN, Liu et al., 2025), replacing its B-spline univariate functions with truncated Fourier series and a periodic-enhanced variant to better suit time series structure. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant forecasting-based TSAD paradigm (LSTMAD, TimesNet, OFA) and reconstruction-based approaches (FCVAE, TranAD) by reframing anomaly detection as coefficient estimation over a fixed library of smooth univariate functions, grounded in the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem. The 1D CNN for coefficient learning and the constant-term-elimination preprocessing are practical design choices that distinguish it from both vanilla KAN and prior frequency-domain methods like FITS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme parameter efficiency (274 trainable parameters for univariate, 4,491 for multivariate) combined with SOTA-level detection accuracy makes KAN-AD particularly attractive for resource-constrained, large-scale deployment where per-series model training is required. The demonstrated robustness to anomalies in training data (27% Event F1 gain on TODS) addresses a practical gap: production systems must train on data that inevitably contains anomalies, yet most baselines degrade significantly under this condition. Open questions include whether the channel-independent multivariate strategy captures cross-channel dependencies, whether the fixed Fourier basis generalizes to highly non-stationary series, and the authors' own suggestion of exploring more efficient normal-pattern representations with additional data.
