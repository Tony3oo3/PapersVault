## Problem

Existing similarity learning methods compute point-to-point distances between finite training instances, but these scattered points cannot adequately cover the infinite sample space, leading to decision boundaries that lack the flatness needed for generalizable classification. The paper asks whether extending each data point to a volume-bearing data ball can naturally enrich sample-space coverage and improve the robustness of the learned similarity metric without requiring additional augmented data.

## Input / Output

The method accepts raw inputs (RGB images at standard resolutions, text sentences via BERT, or molecular/social-network graphs) and produces a 512-dimensional (or 128-dimensional) feature embedding per instance along with a positive scalar volume predicted by a lightweight MLP measure-head. The final output is a pairwise volume-aware distance score between two data balls, which replaces the standard Euclidean distance inside any contrastive or metric-learning loss. The volume is unbounded above (V: R^d → R⁺) and is regularized to grow, effectively expanding each point into a ball whose radius encodes local neighborhood coverage.

## Method

The core contribution is a Volume-Aware Distance (VAD) defined as D(x, x̂) = ‖φ(x) − φ(x̂)‖₂ / e^(V(x)+V(x̂)), where φ is a standard encoder (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, or BERT) and V = H∘φ is a two-layer MLP measure-head that predicts each instance's volume. A Volume Expansion Regularizer (VER) = E[Σ e^(−V(x_bj))] is added to the empirical contrastive/metric loss to encourage larger volumes, making data balls overlap and cover more of the sample space. Theoretically, the paper proves that VAD tightens the generalization error bound (the bound shrinks as the regularization parameter λ grows), that VAD can realize any partial ordering of pairwise distances for a fixed embedding (distance flexibility), and that a finite set of volume-bearing balls can cover any prescribed fraction of the feature space. The VAD is a semi-metric (non-negative, symmetric) that intentionally relaxes the triangle inequality, providing flexibility for real-world similarity relations that do not obey metric axioms.

## Results

On supervised metric learning, VADSL-PA (ProxyAnchor + VAD) achieves 94.13% R@1 on CAR-196, 78.69% R@1 on CUB-200, 86.23% R@1 on SOP, and 93.82% R@1 on In-Shop, outperforming the strongest baseline DASL by 1.82%, 4.73%, 2.91%, and 1.00% respectively. On unsupervised contrastive learning with ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-1K (800 epochs), VADSL (cluster-used) reaches 80.5% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing MTE (78.3%), DINO (76.2%), and iBOT (75.2%). On the STS text benchmark, VADSL achieves 81.38% average accuracy, exceeding the best baseline SCL (80.13%) by 1.25%. On face verification, VADSL (Arc.+VAD) attains 95.21% on AgeDB30 and 96.24% on CFP-FP, beating MFC by 0.23% and 1.03% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VADSL-PA (ProxyAnchor + VAD) achieves the best R@1 across all four supervised retrieval benchmarks, with the largest margin on CUB-200 (+4.73% over MetricFormer) and SOP (+2.91% over DASL), while VADSL-NP (N-pair + VAD) also surpasses all baselines on every dataset. In face recognition (Table 3), VADSL (Arc.+VAD) outperforms MFC by 0.23% on AgeDB30 and 1.03% on CFP-FP, and beats Arcface+SEC by 0.87% on MegaFace-10^6. For unsupervised contrastive learning, VADSL (cluster-used) with ViT-B/16 surpasses DINO, iBOT, and MTE on ImageNet-1K Top-1 (80.5% vs. 76.2%, 75.2%, 78.3%), and with ResNet-50 it outperforms GCA, INTL, HCL, PCL, BYOL, and SimCLR on ImageNet-100. On the STS text benchmark, VADSL (81.38% avg) beats SCL (80.13%), ADNCE (79.38%), and SimCSE (76.84%), with the largest gain on STS15 (+3.50% over SCL). On graph embedding (Fig. 6), VADSL consistently improves InfoGraph across all eight datasets and achieves higher mean accuracy with lower variance than GraphCL, JOAO, and CI-GCL in most cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard projected Euclidean distance used in metric learning (N-pair loss, ProxyAnchor) and contrastive learning (SimCLR, SwAV) by introducing a volume-aware distance (VAD) that treats each data point as a data ball with a predicted volume. Conceptually, it departs from the point-to-point similarity paradigm that dominates both supervised metric learning and self-supervised contrastive learning, instead measuring field-to-field geometric proximity. The measure-head network and volume expansion regularizer are new architectural components, but the overall training framework (encoder + loss + SGD) follows the established structure of prior similarity learning methods. The paper also positions itself against regularization and augmentation strategies that aim to improve generalization, arguing that volume-awareness achieves similar coverage benefits without requiring additional augmented data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The volume-aware formulation provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to address the fundamental coverage limitation of finite training sets in similarity learning, with a tighter generalization error bound and a proof that finite data balls can achieve arbitrary sample-space coverage. The plug-and-play nature (negligible computational overhead, compatible with existing encoders and losses) makes it broadly applicable across supervised and unsupervised settings. Open questions include whether the volume concept can be extended beyond the exponential scaling used here, how it interacts with more complex geometric structures (e.g., hyperbolic spaces), and whether the relaxed triangle inequality could cause issues in downstream tasks that assume metric properties. The authors note plans to apply VAD in broader ML paradigms beyond similarity learning.
