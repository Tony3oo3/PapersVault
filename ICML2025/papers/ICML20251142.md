## Problem

LLMs finetuned on toxic prompts with older teacher models (e.g., GPT-3.5) achieve high safety but develop severe overrefusal, rejecting benign prompts that merely appear toxic. Two gaps remain unexplored: how overgenerating completions with more advanced teacher models (e.g., GPT-4o) shifts the safety–usefulness trade-off during instruction finetuning, and whether a post-training method can reduce overrefusal in already-safe models without eroding their safety.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes three prompt sources—20,000 general-purpose instructions (Alpaca), 20,000 toxic instructions (Anthropic Red-teaming, transformed via GPT-4o), and seemingly toxic prompts (OR-Bench 80k)—and produces 8 completions per prompt from GPT-4o (or Llama-3-70B) at temperature 0.5. For POROver, the input is a set of preference triples (prompt, losing_completion, winning_completion) where losing completions are either the target model's overrefusal or the least-safe GPT-4o response, and winning completions are the best compliant or safest GPT-4o response. The output is a DPO-aligned student model (Llama-3.1-8B, Phi-3-7B, Falcon-3-7B, Llama-3.2-3B/11B) that generates text completions with reduced overrefusal and preserved safety.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, overgeneration for instruction finetuning: 8 completions per prompt are generated with GPT-4o at high temperature, then one is selected via rejection sampling using criteria such as ArmoRM helpfulness/safety heads, Llama Guard 2 soft scores, or DeBERTa reward-model scores; for toxic prompts, safety-specific criteria (ArmoRM safety head or Llama Guard 2) are applied, and scaling from 2,000 to 20,000 Added Safety Data (ASD) pairs is required to match the safety level achieved with GPT-3.5 data. Second, POROver applies DPO (β=0.025) to the finetuned checkpoint using preference data from two complementary sources: (a) for seemingly toxic prompts where the target model overrefuses, the refusal is the losing completion and the top ArmoRM-helpfulness-scored GPT-4o compliant response is the winner; (b) for genuinely toxic prompts where GPT-4o produced highly contrastive completions (Llama Guard 2 scores spanning a containment threshold τ), the safest and least-safe completions form the winning/losing pair. This dual-source preference signal teaches the model to comply with benign prompts while retaining refusal behavior for truly harmful ones.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, overgenerating general-purpose completions with GPT-4o (ArmoRM helpfulness criterion) raises the OR-Bench F1 score from 74.4% (GPT-3 baseline) to 91.8%, driven by a large safety gain (Not-Unsafe Rate: 59.9% → 91.6%) with only a modest usefulness drop. For toxic prompts, overgeneration with GPT-4o + ArmoRM safety at 20,000 ASD lifts the Not-Overrefusal Rate from 11.1% (GPT-3.5, 2,000 ASD) to 57.6% while matching safety levels. Applying POROver (DPO) on top further increases the Not-Overrefusal Rate to 82.1% with the Not-Unsafe Rate remaining at 97.9% (vs. 98.6% before). AlpacaEval Win Rate stays stable (~39%) across all safety-data configurations, confirming no capability degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original GPT-3-generated Alpaca data. Replacing it with GPT-4o overgenerated completions (ArmoRM helpfulness) improves the OR-Bench F1 score by 17.4 points (74.4% → 91.8%) and XSTest F1 by 5.4 points (90.75% → 96.13%), with the safety gain (Not-Unsafe Rate +31.8 pp on OR-Bench) far outweighing the small usefulness loss (−6.3 pp). Among rejection sampling criteria, ArmoRM helpfulness achieves the best F1 on both benchmarks, outperforming random selection by 1.02 pp and DeBERTa by 0.84 pp on OR-Bench F1. For toxic prompts, GPT-4o + ArmoRM safety at 20,000 ASD matches the safety of GPT-3.5 at 2,000 ASD but yields a 46.5 pp higher Not-Overrefusal Rate (57.6% vs. 11.1%). POROver (DPO) then pushes the Not-Overrefusal Rate from 57.6% to 82.1% (+24.5 pp) while the Not-Unsafe Rate drops only 0.7 pp (98.6% → 97.9%), demonstrating that preference optimization can recover usefulness without meaningful safety cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety-tuning line of work initiated by Bianchi et al. (2023), who showed that adding toxic instruction data improves safety but increases overrefusal, by demonstrating that the *quality* of the teacher model generating those completions fundamentally shifts the safety-usefulness tradeoff. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the preference optimization backbone but departs from prior alignment work by specifically targeting the underexplored scenario of already-safe models that overrefuse, for which no dedicated post-training method previously existed. The overgeneration-plus-rejection-sampling pipeline (using ArmoRM or Llama Guard 2 as selection criteria) is a practical extension of knowledge distillation that the authors show has non-trivial effects on safety beyond mere capability transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simply upgrading the teacher model from GPT-3.5 to GPT-4o for overgeneration yields a large safety gain (F1: 74.4%→91.8%) with minimal capability loss, and that GPT-4o toxic completions reduce overrefusal by 5× (11.1%→57.6%) while preserving safety, provides a practical, low-cost recipe for practitioners already in the instruction-finetuning pipeline. POROver's further improvement (57.6%→82.1% Not-Overrefusal) with negligible safety degradation offers a concrete post-training step for the common deployment scenario of safe-but-overcautious models. Open questions include whether the 10× data requirement for GPT-4o toxic completions (20k vs 2k ASD) reflects a fundamental property of more nuanced safety signals or is an artifact of the specific benchmarks, whether automated or adaptive tuning of the containment threshold τ can replace the current grid search, and how these findings scale as student models approach teacher model size.
