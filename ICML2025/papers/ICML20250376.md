## Problem

Existing online knowledge distillation methods apply symmetric supervision to both teacher and student, ignoring the spatial structure of their intermediate features. Through CAM-based analysis, the paper reveals that student-teacher feature similarity concentrates on foreground objects while teachers attend to foreground more strongly than students, yet no prior OKD method exploits this spatial asymmetry to create role-specific learning strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250376/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels for classification, pixel-level masks for semantic segmentation (Cityscapes), or generated images from text prompts in diffusion distillation. Critically, ADM operates on the penultimate spatial feature maps (before global average pooling) of both teacher and student networks, computing a per-pixel cosine similarity map that modulates the classification loss without adding trainable parameters.

## Method

ADM decomposes into two asymmetric losses applied to the spatial feature maps of teacher and student. For the student, a Consensus Loss (L_co) multiplies features by a monotone-increasing attention (1+S)/(1+S̄) where S is the per-pixel cosine similarity to the teacher, amplifying supervision in high-agreement foreground regions. For the teacher, a Divergence Loss (L_di) applies a monotone-decreasing attention (1−S)/(1−S̄), intensifying learning in low-similarity regions where the teacher retains superior discriminative capacity. A gradient-detached MSE feature loss (L_feat) provides additional student-to-teacher feature alignment. The total loss is L_dml + γ·L_feat + α·L_co + β·L_di, creating a dynamic where students progressively close the gap on 'easy' foreground patterns while teachers continuously explore harder regions, preventing premature convergence to student-level representations.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-34→ResNet-18, DML+ADM achieves 71.59% top-1 accuracy, outperforming DML (71.12%), KDCL (71.54%), and SwitOKD (71.00%). On heterogeneous pairs (0.5MobileNetV2→ResNet-18), ADM yields a larger gain of +0.89% over DML (65.10% vs 64.21%). For diffusion step distillation on CIFAR-10, PD+ADM reduces FID from 15.93 to 13.94 at 1-step sampling. On Cityscapes semantic segmentation, DML+ADM reaches 74.55% mIoU with DeepLabV3, surpassing all OKD baselines by 0.4–0.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADM consistently outperforms DML across all tested settings: +0.47% on ImageNet (ResNet-34→18), +0.89% on heterogeneous pairs (0.5MobileNetV2→ResNet-18), and +0.36% on CIFAR-100 (VGG-13→ResNet32×4). Against KDCL, ADM wins in most configurations (e.g., 71.59% vs 71.54% on ImageNet ResNet-34→18; 65.10% vs 64.32% on 0.5MobileNetV2→ResNet-18), though KDCL edges ahead by 0.02% in the identical ResNet-18→ResNet-18 setting. SwitOKD is outperformed in nearly all cases, with the largest margin of +0.62% on ImageNet (ResNet-50→ResNet-101: 77.10% vs 76.78%). In offline KD, ADM adds +1.33% over KD and +0.42% over DKD on CIFAR-100, and +0.13% over DisWOT and +0.12% over NORM on ImageNet. For diffusion step distillation, PD+ADM reduces FID by 1.99 (1-step) and 0.49 (2-step) over vanilla PD. When combined with SHAKE, ADM still provides a +0.10% gain (71.75% vs 71.65%), confirming its plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online knowledge distillation (OKD) framework initiated by Deep Mutual Learning (DML), where teacher and student train simultaneously from scratch, by introducing a spatially-aware feature modulation loss that operates on intermediate representations rather than output logits. It departs from prior OKD methods (KDCL, SwitOKD, SHAKE) that rely exclusively on logit matching by leveraging teacher-student feature similarity to create an asymmetric training signal: the student receives consensus-weighted supervision on high-similarity (foreground) regions, while the teacher receives divergence-weighted supervision on low-similarity regions. Unlike object-detection distillation methods (FGD, GID) that use ground-truth bounding boxes to explicitly separate foreground from background, ADM implicitly identifies these regions through spatial feature similarity, making it architecture-agnostic. The feature distillation component adapts the MSE-based approach from FitNets to the online setting with a gradient-detach mechanism to prevent teacher degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADM provides a simple, parameter-free plug-and-play module that consistently improves existing OKD baselines across classification, semantic segmentation, and diffusion distillation, addressing the practical concern of teacher performance degradation in online settings. The CAM-driven analysis offering a mechanistic explanation for why spatial feature alignment matters (foreground-centric consensus) adds interpretability value beyond the performance gains. However, the improvements are modest (typically 0.2–0.5% on ImageNet), and the paper acknowledges that choosing an effective teacher model for distillation remains an open question. The extension to diffusion distillation, while demonstrating breadth, yields only small FID/IS gains, leaving open whether the spatial-similarity principle transfers meaningfully to generative tasks or is merely a convenient loss regularizer.
