## Problem

Existing Open-Ended Detection (OED) models like GenerateU require large-scale datasets (5M+ images), substantial GPU resources (16 A100s), and many training epochs (149) while suffering from three structural issues: an unbridged semantic gap between vision queries and language embeddings, contradictory supervision signals from same-category objects in a mini-batch, and noisy early-stage alignment that disrupts heavy-weight LLM head weights. The paper asks whether a more efficient OED framework can simultaneously accelerate convergence, reduce data and compute requirements, and improve detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces bounding boxes paired with free-form object names generated by a FlanT5-base LLM. During inference, the Vision-Language Aligner and VLM are omitted, making the system vocabulary-free—no text queries or category lists are needed. A threshold-based query selection mechanism (sigmoid > 0.05) adaptively determines the number of decoder queries from encoder tokens, allowing detection of a variable number of objects rather than a fixed count.

## Method

Open-Det comprises four collaborative components: (1) an Object Detector with a decoupled decoder where the first four layers use one-to-many cross-attention matching and the last two use one-to-one self-attention matching, accelerating box convergence without extra branches; (2) a Prompts Distiller whose VLD-M module uses deformable cross-attention to enrich region queries with background context, then projects them into VL-prompts that replace raw vision queries as LLM input, with knowledge distilled from a frozen CLIP VLM via cosine similarity loss; (3) an Object Name Generator that freezes the 24.7M-parameter T5 head and substitutes a 0.526M-parameter LoRa head during early training, combined with Gaussian-noise Text Denoising on text embeddings for robustness; and (4) a Vision-Language Aligner with bidirectional V-to-L and L-to-V cosine alignment (BVLA-M) to address the channel-dimension mismatch between 256-d queries and 768-d text embeddings. Two novel losses further improve training: a Masked Alignment Loss that binarizes text-embedding similarity to suppress contradictory gradients from same-category objects, and a Joint Loss that couples the binary classification score with IoU and alignment scores to stabilize dynamic matching supervision.

## Results

On zero-shot LVIS MiniVal, Open-Det (Swin-Tiny, trained on VG only, 31 epochs) achieves 21.0 AP_r, 24.8 AP_c, 30.1 AP_f, and 27.0 AP, outperforming GenerateU (17.4 AP_r, 25.4 AP) by +3.6% AP_r and +1.6% AP while using only 20.8% of the training epochs (31 vs. 149) and 4 V100 GPUs vs. 16 A100 GPUs. With the Swin-Large backbone, Open-Det reaches 31.2 AP_r and 33.1 AP, surpassing GenerateU-Swin-Large (22.3 AP_r, 27.9 AP) by +8.9% and +5.2% respectively, using only 1.5% of the training data (0.077M vs. 5.077M). On COCO2017 and Objects365, Open-Det (Swin-Large) achieves 35.8 and 13.8 AP, exceeding GenerateU by +2.2 and +3.3 AP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary OED baseline GenerateU, Open-Det wins on all metrics: with identical VG training data and Swin-Tiny backbone, it improves AP_r by +3.6% (21.0 vs. 17.4) and AP by +1.6% (27.0 vs. 25.4) in only 31 epochs vs. 149. Even when GenerateU is augmented with 5M grounding images from GRIT5M, Open-Det still leads by +1.0% AP_r (21.0 vs. 20.0) using just 1.5% of the data. With Swin-Large, the gap widens dramatically: Open-Det achieves 31.2 AP_r vs. GenerateU's 22.3 (+8.9%) and 33.1 AP vs. 27.9 (+5.2%). Against OVD baselines, Open-Det outperforms GLIP(A) by +6.8% AP_r and +8.5% AP using only 11.7% of its training data, and edges out GLIP(C) (trained on 5.456M images) by +1.0% AP while using 1.4% of the data. Compared to Grounding DINO (5.460M data), Open-Det gains +2.9% AP_r but trails slightly on AP (27.0 vs. 27.4). Against MDETR, Open-Det achieves comparable AP_r (21.0 vs. 20.9) but a clear +2.8% AP advantage, while remaining vocabulary-free at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GenerateU (Lin et al., 2024), which first formulated Open-Ended Detection (OED) as a generative task combining a DETR-style detector with an LLM for vocabulary-free object name generation. Open-Det departs from GenerateU's architecture in several key ways: it replaces the direct vision-query-to-LLM pipeline with VL-prompts distilled from a frozen VLM (CLIP) via a Vision-to-Language Distillation Module, introduces bidirectional (V-to-L and L-to-V) alignment instead of unidirectional mapping, and incorporates one-to-many/one-to-one decoupled matching in the detector inspired by DINO and DAc-DETR. The training-side innovations—LoRA head for the LLM, text denoising, Masked Alignment Loss, and Joint Loss—draw on parameter-efficient fine-tuning, contrastive learning corrections, and focal-loss-style reweighting, respectively, but are specifically tailored to the OED setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practical contribution is the dramatic reduction in training resources: Open-Det achieves higher AP_r than GenerateU using only 1.5% of the training data, 20.8% of the epochs, and 4 V100 GPUs versus 16 A100s, making OED feasible for research groups without large-scale compute. The identification and correction of contradictory supervision (same-category text embeddings acting as both positive and negative in the same batch) is a useful diagnostic for any contrastive alignment training in detection. However, absolute performance remains modest (27–33 AP on LVIS MiniVal), the framework is still constrained by cross-modal semantic gaps between region features and image-level text embeddings, and the field of OED is only in its second paper—open questions include whether stronger backbones/VLMs/LLMs close the remaining gap, whether a segmentation decoder can further improve region-text alignment, and whether the efficiency gains hold when scaling to larger, more diverse training corpora.
