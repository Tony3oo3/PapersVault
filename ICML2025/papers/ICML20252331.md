## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based signal recovery methods either target linear measurement models or require explicit knowledge of the nonlinear link function, making them inapplicable to semi-parametric single index models (SIMs) where the link function is unknown and potentially discontinuous (e.g., sign for 1-bit measurements). This paper bridges that gap by showing how pre-trained diffusion priors can recover signals from SIM observations without knowing the link function, while also reducing the number of neural function evaluations by an order of magnitude compared to prior diffusion-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian measurement matrix A ∈ R^{m×n}, a nonlinear observation vector y ∈ R^m generated as y = f(Ax*) (with f unknown and possibly discontinuous), and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model, and outputs a reconstructed image x̂ ∈ R^n. The signal is constrained to the unit sphere (‖x*‖₂ = 1) for identifiability. Experiments use 1-bit (y = sign(Ax*+e)) and cubic (y = (Ax*)³+e) measurements on FFHQ 256×256, ImageNet 256×256, and CIFAR-10 32×32, with m ranging from n/16 to n/8 for the larger datasets and 500–1000 for CIFAR-10.

## Method

The key insight is that (1/m)A^T y is a noisy version of μx* with noise level O(√(log n)/√m) per coordinate (Lemma 2), so it should be treated as a sample from an intermediate diffusion time t* rather than from the data distribution at t=ε. The SIM-DMIS approach computes t* by matching σ_{t*}/α_{t*} = C_s/√m, then performs a partial inversion from t* to T using the second-order DM2M solver, followed by a full sampling pass from T to ε using DDIM. This avoids the full inversion from ε that prior generative-prior methods (e.g., Liu & Liu 2022) require, reducing total NFEs to 150 versus 1000+ for DPS/DAPS or 11,555 for QCS-SGM. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3) bounds the reconstruction error in terms of the numerical discretization order of the inversion and sampling solvers, under Lipschitz continuity of the score network.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 with 1-bit measurements (m=n/8=24576), SIM-DMIS achieves PSNR 19.87±2.77, SSIM 0.60±0.09, and LPIPS 0.37±0.05 using only 150 NFEs, substantially outperforming all baselines. On ImageNet 256×256 with 1-bit measurements (m=n/16=12288), SIM-DMIS reaches PSNR 18.06±3.16, SSIM 0.45±0.13, and LPIPS 0.46±0.08, again with just 150 NFEs. For cubic measurements on FFHQ, SIM-DMIS achieves PSNR 17.56±2.56 and LPIPS 0.40±0.07, the best across all metrics. The method also runs in 5.66 seconds per image versus 142–160 seconds for DPS/DAPS baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIM-DMIS decisively outperforms QCS-SGM (Meng & Kabashima, 2022) on all three metrics (PSNR 19.87 vs. 12.91, SSIM 0.60 vs. 0.51, LPIPS 0.37 vs. 0.50) while requiring only 150 NFEs versus 11,555 NFEs — a ~77× reduction in neural function evaluations. Against DPS (Chung et al., 2023), SIM-DMIS wins by a wide margin: PSNR 19.87 vs. 11.14 (DPS-N) and 8.57 (DPS-L), with DPS-N/DPS-L using 1000 NFEs. Against DAPS (Zhang et al., 2024), SIM-DMIS achieves higher PSNR (19.87 vs. 16.59 for DAPS-N) and lower LPIPS (0.37 vs. 0.48), though DAPS-N has a smaller PSNR variance (±0.54 vs. ±2.77). On CIFAR-10 cubic measurements, SIM-DMIS outperforms OneShot (Liu & Liu, 2022) by approximately 15–25% in PSNR and 12–20% in SSIM across m=500 and m=1000 settings. Notably, SIM-DMIS does not require knowledge of the link function f, whereas DPS-N and DAPS-N explicitly use it, yet SIM-DMIS still achieves superior reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-iterative recovery framework of Liu & Liu (2022), which recovers signals from SIMs via G∘G†(A^T y/m) using GAN/VAE generators, by replacing the generator with a diffusion model and, crucially, replacing full inversion from time ε with partial inversion from an intermediate time t*. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based signal recovery methods (DPS, DAPS, DDRM, MCG) is that those methods either assume linear measurements or require explicit knowledge of the link function f, whereas this paper handles unknown and potentially discontinuous link functions (e.g., sign) without any gradient-based data-consistency step. The closest prior work, QCS-SGM (Meng & Kabashima, 2022), also uses score-based models for quantized measurements but requires over 11,000 NFEs and is restricted to quantized (not general nonlinear) observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in neural function evaluations (150 vs. 1000+ NFEs) while achieving superior reconstruction quality makes diffusion-prior-based SIM recovery practically viable for real-time or resource-constrained applications. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3) provides a principled justification for the partial-inversion strategy, grounding the heuristic choice of t* in the concentration of A^T y/m around μx*. Open questions include whether the approach extends to measurement models where μ=0 (e.g., phase retrieval with f(x)=x²), whether the tuning parameters C_s and C_s' can be determined automatically, and whether the framework generalizes to conditional or latent diffusion models for higher-resolution reconstruction.
