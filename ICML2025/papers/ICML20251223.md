## Problem

Spectral dimension reduction methods like Diffusion Maps are empirically robust to noise, yet existing consistency results require the noise amplitude or dimensionality to decay with sample size n. This paper asks: under realistic fixed noise (constant amplitude r, fixed ambient dimension D), which part of the manifold's Laplacian spectrum is recoverable, and what is the precise threshold governing this recovery?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251223/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n high-dimensional samples from a tubular neighborhood T_r(M) of a d-dimensional manifold M ⊂ R^D, where noise of amplitude r is added in the (D−d)-dimensional normal space. It produces the full spectral decomposition of the Neumann Laplacian on T_r(M) — eigenvalues λ_i and eigenfunctions φ_i — from which a low-dimensional embedding is obtained by truncating to the first m eigenfunctions. The critical output structure is a sharp threshold at O(r^{-2}): eigenpairs below this value faithfully encode manifold geometry, while those above are dominated by noise and irrecoverable.

## Method

The core technique is a decomposition of the Laplacian on the tubular neighborhood using the Sasaki metric, which cleanly separates the operator into a horizontal (manifold) component Δ_{H,p} and a vertical (noise) component (1/r²)Δ_{V,q} that commute and share eigenfunctions. This yields an exact spectral threshold: eigenvalues below λ_1^V/r² correspond to constant-fiber extensions of the clean manifold's eigenfunctions. To transfer this result from the Sasaki metric to the actual induced Euclidean metric, the paper applies a perturbation argument via quasi-unitary equivalence, showing the two metrics differ by O(r) and hence the induced Laplacian's low spectrum approximates the Sasaki one with O(r) error. A multi-scale Weyl law is then derived using packing numbers to characterize spectral growth above and below the threshold. The theory is validated on synthetic manifolds and molecular dynamics data, and the same threshold behavior is observed for VAEs and LTSA, which do not rely on Laplacian estimation.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp threshold phenomenon: eigenpairs of the noisy manifold Laplacian Δ_{T_r(M)} with eigenvalues below λ_1^V/r² are recovered with error O(r) (linear in noise amplitude), while eigenpairs above this threshold are irrecoverable. Empirically, the error between low-spectrum eigenspace projectors increases linearly with r and shows a mild decrease as the noise dimension d_n grows. On real MDS data (Toluene, Malonaldehyde, Ethanol with D=50), low-frequency eigenvalues remain nearly constant as r increases until they approach the noise threshold λ_1^V/r², after which they become volatile and eigenvectors lose correlation with the manifold coordinate space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Diffusion Maps (DM) against two non-Laplacian-based manifold learning algorithms: VAE and LTSA. For VAEs trained on S² with d_n=16, the first three latent coordinates (φ_1, φ_2, φ_3) are stable with respect to both r and d_n, indispensable for reconstruction, and cannot be predicted from noise coordinates via polynomial regression; the fourth coordinate (φ_4) is uncorrelated with the manifold, unnecessary for reconstruction, and easily predicted from noise — mirroring the DM threshold behavior. For LTSA on the S-Shape, low-frequency embeddings remain stable as r increases while high-frequency embeddings (e.g., φ_10) become corrupted once the noise threshold is crossed, and increasing d_n from 16 to 32 mitigates the noise effect on low-frequency coordinates. Both baselines exhibit the same qualitative threshold phenomenon as DM, confirming that the robustness of low-frequency spectral components to noise is a general property of manifold learning rather than an artifact of Laplacian-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of Diffusion Maps and Laplacian eigenmaps by introducing the Sasaki metric decomposition of the tubular neighborhood Laplacian into horizontal (manifold) and vertical (noise) components—a geometric tool not previously used in the manifold learning literature. It departs from prior consistency results (García Trillos et al. 2020; Landa & Cheng 2023) that required noise amplitude or dimensionality to decay with sample size, instead proving spectral recovery under fixed noise amplitude and fixed ambient dimension. The perturbation argument builds on quasi-unitary equivalence techniques from Post (2012) and convergence results for Dirichlet semigroups on tubular neighborhoods from Nobis & Wittich (2019), while the horizontal-vertical Laplacian decomposition follows the framework of Bergery & Bourguignon (1982).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sharp O(r^{-2}) threshold provides a clean theoretical explanation for why truncating to low-frequency eigenvectors in Diffusion Maps is the correct practical strategy: beyond the threshold, the noise spectrum dominates and recovery is impossible. This resolves a gap in the theoretical understanding of spectral dimension reduction under realistic (non-vanishing) noise, and the observation that VAEs and LTSA exhibit the same threshold behavior suggests the phenomenon is fundamental to manifold learning rather than specific to Laplacian-based methods. Open questions include establishing a matching lower bound, extending results to non-tubular or spatially varying noise, and understanding the implications for supervised learning procedures that rely on infinite Laplacian bases.
