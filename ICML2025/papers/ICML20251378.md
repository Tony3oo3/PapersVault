## Problem

Generative models exhibit 'critical windows'—narrow intervals of the sampling process where key output features are abruptly decided—but existing theory for these windows is restricted to diffusion models and relies on strong distributional assumptions (Gaussian mixtures, log-concavity) or diffusion-specific tools like Girsanov's theorem. No simple, general framework explains critical windows across both diffusion and autoregressive models with minimal assumptions on the data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251378/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a stochastic localization sampler (diffusion or autoregressive), a mixture data distribution p = Σ w_θ p^θ over a discrete label set Θ, and a fixed forward degradation process. The forward-reverse experiment consumes a sample from a sub-mixture p^{S_init}, degrades it for time T̂, then resamples from the full distribution's posterior; the output is a TV distance bound certifying when the resampled distribution is close to a target sub-mixture p^{S_targ}, parameterized by two threshold times T_st^S and T_end^S defined via TV distances between sub-mixtures along the forward process. The theory also produces an ε-mixture tree encoding the hierarchical sequence of critical windows.

## Method

The core contribution is Theorem 3.1, which shows that for T̂ ∈ [T_end^S, T_st^S], the TV distance between the forward-reverse experiment output and the target sub-mixture is bounded by ε·(1 + max(1,W)/√2), where W is the weight ratio between the target and complement sub-mixtures. The proof exploits only the Markov property of the observation process: a data processing inequality bounds the 'within-target' error, while a coupling argument combined with Bayes' rule and a TV-Hellinger ratio inequality bounds the 'leakage from outside the target' error. This yields dimension-independent bounds, strictly improving over Li & Chen (2024) which required log-concavity and Girsanov's theorem and carried polynomial dimension dependence. The critical window is then formally defined as [T_after^C, T_before^C], the interval where the sampler speciates from a larger sub-population to a smaller one, and the framework is instantiated for Gaussian mixtures under diffusion, token-level jailbreaks under autoregression, and in-context learning, with empirical validation on LLAMA-3.1-8B, Phi-3-7B, and Qwen-2.5-7B across seven reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) provides dimension-independent TV bounds on critical windows for all stochastic localization samplers, improving over Li & Chen (2024) which had polynomial-in-dimension error terms. Empirically, critical windows are identified in 42.2%, 16.6%, and 30.2% of generations from LLAMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, and Phi-3-7B-Instruct on the MATH benchmark. Generations containing critical windows show up to 73% lower accuracy (on ARC Easy for LLAMA-3.1-8B-Instruct), and incorrect responses contain 11%–33% more critical windows than correct ones across all seven benchmarks tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no direct method-vs-method baseline comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is to Li & Chen (2024), who derived rigorous critical window bounds for diffusion models using Girsanov's theorem under strong log-concavity and score regularity assumptions with polynomial-in-dimension error terms; the present work removes both the distributional assumptions and the dimension dependence, yielding a constant-factor bound that applies to all stochastic localization samplers (diffusion and autoregressive). Empirically, the paper validates its theoretical predictions by showing that critical windows coincide with reasoning failures: across all seven benchmarks (MATH, AQUA-RAT, ARC Challenge, ARC Easy, LogiQA, MMLU, TruthfulQA), generations with critical windows are significantly less accurate, with the largest gap on ARC Easy (73% lower accuracy for LLAMA-3.1-8B-Instruct). The paper also connects its framework to the all-or-nothing phase transition in statistical inference and provides a theoretical model for jailbreak critical windows (Example 4.3), explaining why finetuning on corrections is effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the critical window theory from Li & Chen (2024), which was restricted to Gaussian diffusion models and required log-concavity and score regularity assumptions via Girsanov's theorem, to all stochastic localization samplers including autoregressive language models. It builds on the stochastic localization framework of Montanari (2023b) and Chen & Eldan (2022), but repurposes it from a sampling algorithm design tool into an analytical lens for understanding feature emergence. The key departure is that the proof relies only on the data processing inequality and total variation distance, eliminating both the statistical physics machinery of Sclocchi et al. (2024, 2025) and the dimension-dependent polynomial bounds of prior rigorous work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a unified, assumption-light explanation for why sudden behavior shifts (jailbreaks, math reasoning failures, image class emergence) occur in both LLMs and diffusion models, with practical implications for safety alignment (finetuning on corrections at critical windows) and interpretability. The empirical finding that generations with critical windows show up to 73% lower accuracy across seven benchmarks gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic signal. Open questions include why critical windows are sharp in practice (the theory characterizes location but not width tightly), how the framework connects to statistical physics predictions for richer data models like random hierarchies, and whether the hierarchy depth difference between autoregressive and diffusion models has measurable consequences for what features each paradigm can learn.
