## Problem

The central model of differential privacy achieves high utility but requires trusting a single server, while the local model avoids trust at the cost of large noise that degrades accuracy. The paper asks: what is the least expressive class of functions that can be securely distributed across multiple servers to recover central-model-level utility? It proposes the linear transformation model (LTM), where clients can only apply a public linear transformation (a sparse Johnson-Lindenstrauss sketch) to their collective inputs, as a practical middle ground.

## Input / Output

Each client holds one row of a data matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} (plus a corresponding entry of target vector b for regression) and a public OSNAP sketch matrix S ∈ {−1,0,1}^{m×n} with s non-zeros per column. Clients locally add noise (Gaussian for (ε,δ)-DP or Laplace/Gamma-difference for pure ε-DP) to their rows, then the servers jointly compute the matrix product S·(A+G) via linear secret sharing. The published output is the noisy sketched matrix, from which an analysis function A extracts a rank-k orthogonal projection X (low-rank approximation), a regression parameter vector x̂ (ridge regression), or a frequency moment estimate F_k.

## Method

The LTM mechanism has three stages: (1) each client i computes R(x_i) = x_i^s + g_i, copying its row s times and adding i.i.d. noise from an infinitely divisible distribution D; (2) the resulting n×ds matrix is secret-shared across k servers using linear secret sharing, and the servers jointly compute T_S(A) = (1/√s) Σ S_i A_i via MPC (simple matrix multiplications on shares); (3) the published result is fed to an analysis function. The key privacy insight is that because the noise distribution is infinitely divisible, the sum of noise contributions from the (n−s−t′)/(2m) honest clients expected to touch any entry of a column in SA follows the same distribution as a single DP noise sample, so the mechanism satisfies (ε,δ)-DP even with up to t′ corrupt clients and t corrupt semi-honest servers. For utility, the paper leverages OSNAP subspace-embedding guarantees (Cohen, 2016 parameterization) to show the additive error for low-rank approximation is Õ(kd^{3/2}·α_S^{−3}·ε^{−1}·log δ^{−1}) and for ridge regression is poly(ε^{−1}, d, log 1/δ), both independent of n.

## Results

On four real-world datasets (ε=0.05), the LTM Gaussian mechanism achieves excess risk ψ within 10–20% of the central MOD-SULQ baseline: 2.1×10⁻⁶ vs 1.8×10⁻⁶ on Power (n=2M), 4.7×10⁻⁸ vs 4.5×10⁻⁸ on Elevation (n=435K), and 2.4×10⁻⁶ vs 7.9×10⁻⁶ on Ethylene (n=4.2M, where LTM actually outperforms central). The LTM Laplace mechanism (pure ε-DP) is 3× better than the local model on Power (4.0×10⁻³ vs 1.2×10⁻²) and 8× better on Elevation (1.5×10⁻² vs 1.2×10⁻¹). Theoretically, the LTM achieves Õ(kd^{3/2}/α_S³ · ε⁻¹) additive error for low-rank approximation, interpolating between the local model's O(k²nd) and the central model's Θ̃(k√d·ε⁻¹).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the central MOD-SULQ baseline (Chaudhuri et al., 2012), the LTM Gaussian mechanism matches or nearly matches central utility: within 17% on Power, within 4% on Elevation, and actually 3× better on Ethylene (2.4×10⁻⁶ vs 7.9×10⁻⁶), while tying on Songs. Against the local model (Arora et al., 2018 for low-rank; Bassily et al., 2020 for frequency), the LTM Gaussian mechanism is 2–3 orders of magnitude better on excess risk (e.g., 2.1×10⁻⁶ vs 1.2×10⁻² on Power), and the LTM Laplace mechanism is 3–8× better. Theoretically, the LTM's Õ(kd^{3/2}/α_S³ · ε⁻¹) bound for low-rank approximation is strictly better than the local model's O(k²nd) additive error but slightly worse than the central model's optimal Θ̃(k√d·ε⁻¹) (Dwork et al., 2014). For frequency estimation, the LTM achieves Õ(ε⁻¹), matching the shuffle model bound (Ghazi et al., 2021) and the central model, while the local model requires Õ(√n). The LTM thus closes most of the utility gap between local and central DP without requiring a single trusted server.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the Linear Transformation Model (LTM) as a principled intermediate between the central and local models of differential privacy, building on the multi-central DP framework of Steinke (2020) and the OSNAP sparse subspace embeddings of Nelson & Nguyen (2013) with parameterizations from Cohen (2016). It departs from the shuffle model by replacing the trusted permutation with a trusted linear matrix multiplication, which is strictly less expressive but far cheaper to distribute via linear secret sharing. The paper also positions secure aggregation (used in federated learning) as a special case of the LTM where the linear transform is simply a sum, unifying these previously separate lines of work under one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LTM provides a practical path to near-central-model utility without a single point of failure: the noise added per client is independent of the number of participants, and the MPC overhead is limited to parallelizable matrix multiplications (under 2 seconds per server for 1M clients). A key open question is whether the LTM can match central-model bounds for ridge regression, where the current additive error depends on d³/λ and is not competitive. The paper also highlights that existing shuffle-model DP definitions are not robust to client collusion, and the formalized multi-corruption definition could strengthen privacy guarantees across intermediate trust models more broadly.
