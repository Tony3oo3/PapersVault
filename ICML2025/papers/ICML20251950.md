## Problem

Conformal prediction guarantees valid coverage only under exchangeability between labeled and test data, but this assumption is broken when individual entries of the test feature are contaminated by cellwise outliers (e.g., measurement errors or natural variation in a few biomarkers). Existing approaches like weighted conformal prediction require estimating an unknown likelihood ratio, which is infeasible without distributional assumptions on the contamination. This paper is the first to address predictive inference with cellwise outliers in the test feature in a distribution-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially corrupted test feature (where a subset of coordinates is replaced by arbitrarily distributed outlier values), a labeled calibration set, a pre-fitted regression model, and user-specified detection and imputation procedures (e.g., DDC for detection, Mean/kNN/MICE for imputation). It outputs a single prediction interval for the scalar test label. A key structural detail is that the detection procedure is applied not only to the test feature but also to each calibration feature, and the resulting masks are jointly used to construct exchangeable processed features before computing conformal residuals.

## Method

The framework follows a detect-then-impute pipeline: a cellwise score function with thresholds identifies outlier coordinates in the test feature, and an imputation function fills those coordinates using the remaining clean entries. The critical insight for preserving conformal validity is that the same detection procedure must be applied to calibration features, and the calibration features must be masked by the union of their own detected outliers and the test feature's detected outliers, producing exchangeable copies of the processed test feature. Two algorithms are developed: PDI-CP uses split conformal prediction on these proxy features and achieves 1−α coverage minus a gap term depending on false discoveries and imputation error; JDI-CP uses a Jackknife+ construction with pairwise joint masking (each calibration point is masked by its own detection output plus the test detection output, and vice versa), achieving a distribution-free 1−2α finite-sample coverage guarantee under a sure-detection assumption. The approach is modular and wraps around any detection and imputation procedure, with coverage analysis extending beyond the isolated-detection assumption to methods like DDC where scores depend on other coordinates.

## Results

Under Setting A with ε=0.1 contamination, PDI-CP achieves 90.1–90.9% empirical coverage (target 90%) and JDI-CP achieves 89.5–90.4% across detection thresholds p∈{0.99, 0.9, 0.7, 0.5}, with interval lengths nearly identical to the oracle Baseline. On real datasets (Airfoil, Wind direction, Riboflavin), both PDI-CP and JDI-CP maintain coverage at or above the 90% target, while SCP and WCP fall below target or produce infinite-width intervals. The JDI-CP method provides a finite-sample 1−2α = 80% coverage guarantee in the worst case, and empirically exceeds this bound in all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PDI-CP and JDI-CP against two standard conformal prediction baselines: SCP (split conformal prediction applied directly to the contaminated test feature) and WCP (weighted conformal prediction with likelihood-ratio reweighting). Both SCP and WCP fail to achieve the target 90% coverage in all simulation settings and real-data experiments; at high contamination (ε=0.2), WCP even outputs prediction intervals of infinite width. In contrast, PDI-CP and JDI-CP consistently meet or exceed the 90% coverage target while producing intervals with lengths nearly identical to the oracle Baseline (which knows the true outlier locations O*). The key advantage is that PDI-CP and JDI-CP require no knowledge of which cells are contaminated, yet match the oracle's efficiency, whereas SCP and WCP degrade gracefully in coverage but fail to provide valid guarantees under cellwise contamination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: distribution-free conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005; Barber et al., 2021), cellwise outlier detection and imputation (Raymaekers & Rousseeuw, 2021; 2024b), and conformal prediction under non-exchangeability (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Zaffran et al., 2023). It departs from weighted conformal prediction, which requires consistent likelihood ratio estimation that is infeasible under arbitrary cellwise contamination, and from missing-data CP (Zaffran et al., 2023), which assumes exchangeable missing patterns that do not hold when outliers are detected adaptively. The key conceptual departure is the 'adaptive masking' strategy: applying the detection procedure to calibration features to restore exchangeability with the processed test feature, a technique analogous to post-selection inference but applied to the DI pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and solve the problem of distribution-free prediction intervals when test features contain cellwise outliers, a practically important setting (sensor errors, measurement anomalies, adversarial contamination). The negative result (Theorem 3.5) establishing that sure detection is fundamentally necessary—without it, no valid PI can exist in a distribution-free sense—sharpens the problem and guides practitioners toward aggressive detection thresholds. Open questions include closing the 1-2α gap of JDI-CP to 1-α without excessive conservatism, extending to high-dimensional settings where sure detection becomes harder, and adapting the framework to classification or multi-output tasks.
