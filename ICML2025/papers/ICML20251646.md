## Problem

Existing federated learning algorithms address either communication compression or data heterogeneity in isolation, rather than tackling both simultaneously. The paper identifies that heterogeneous client update magnitudes cause certain clients' contributions to be dominated during global averaging, degrading generalization, while full-precision transmission of these updates incurs prohibitive communication costs. FedSMU aims to resolve both issues in a single mechanism that also remains robust under partial client participation.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the full-precision global model from the server and processes its local non-iid data (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or character sequences for Shakespeare) through K steps of local SGD. The client then transmits only a 1-bit sign vector (one bit per parameter dimension) representing the symbol of its momentum-weighted update, while the server broadcasts the updated full-precision global model back. This asymmetric communication pattern—1-bit uplink, full-precision downlink—reduces uplink cost by a factor equal to the bit-width of the original parameters (e.g., 32× for float32).

## Method

FedSMU decomposes the Lion optimizer into a client-side component (local SGD followed by a sign operation on a momentum-weighted update, plus a local sliding-average momentum update) and a server-side component (aggregation of 1-bit symbolic updates with weight decay). The key insight is that the sign operation normalizes the magnitude of every client's update to ±1 per dimension, enforcing what the authors call Magnitude Uniformity, which prevents large-magnitude updates from dominating the global average under data heterogeneity. The dual momentum tracking (β₁ for the sign computation, β₂ for the local momentum state) and server-side weight decay are borrowed from Lion and are critical: ablation experiments show that removing the sliding average (β₁=0) drops accuracy from 52.35% to 28.03% on CIFAR-100, and removing symbolization (full-precision updates) drops it to 42.67%. The paper proves O(1/√T) convergence for general non-convex objectives under standard smoothness, bounded-variance, and bounded-gradient assumptions, matching the rate of FedAvg and FedPAQ.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with LeNet (the most heterogeneous setting, Dirichlet-0.25, 10% participation), FedSMU achieves 51.87% top-1 test accuracy, outperforming FedAvg (41.44%) by over 10 percentage points and SCAFFOLD (41.28%) by ~10.6 points. On CIFAR-10, FedSMU reaches 80.12–82.66% depending on the setting, roughly matching or slightly trailing SCAFFOLD (81.6–84.12%) while beating all compression-based baselines. On Shakespeare (non-iid, high participation), FedSMU attains 47.81% accuracy, edging out FedAvg (47.58%) and FedLion (47.11%) but trailing SCAFFOLD (51.28%). With larger models (ResNet-18, ViT-S), FedSMU achieves 49.76% on CIFAR-100 (ViT-S) and 33.53% on Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-18), again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSMU decisively outperforms FedAvg on CIFAR-100 by 7.8–10.9 percentage points across all heterogeneity and participation settings, while on CIFAR-10 the gap narrows to roughly 0–1.2 points (FedSMU is slightly behind in 2 of 4 settings). Against SCAFFOLD, FedSMU wins on CIFAR-100 by 2.4–10.6 points but trails by 1.0–3.5 points on CIFAR-10 and by 3.5 points on Shakespeare, indicating SCAFFOLD's control-variate approach still has an edge on less heterogeneous or text tasks. All three compression-based baselines (FedEF-HS, FedEF-TopK, FedEF-Sign) are consistently outperformed by FedSMU by 2–15 points across every dataset and setting. FedLion, which applies Lion locally and transmits full-precision momentum, is beaten by FedSMU on CIFAR-100 (by 4–7 points) and is roughly matched on CIFAR-10, while FedSMU also uses far less communication (1-bit vs. full-precision momentum upload). SCALLION, which combines SCAFFOLD with compressed uploads, is outperformed by FedSMU on CIFAR-100 (by 6–8 points) but slightly ahead on Shakespeare (47.86 vs. 47.81).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSMU builds directly on the Lion optimizer (Chen et al., 2024) from centralized learning, splitting its momentum-tracking and weight-decay components across clients and server so that only 1-bit sign updates are transmitted. It departs from prior compression-based FL methods (signSGD, FedPAQ, FedEF) that reduce communication but do not address data heterogeneity, and from optimization-based methods (SCAFFOLD, FedGen) that mitigate heterogeneity at the cost of additional communication. The closest prior work, FedLion (Tang & Chang, 2024), applies Lion locally without exploiting its full structure and still requires full-precision momentum transmission. The paper introduces the Magnitude Uniformity (MU) index, inspired by Jain's fairness index, as a new diagnostic linking update-magnitude heterogeneity to generalization degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedSMU offers a practical path to deploying FL in bandwidth-constrained, highly heterogeneous settings by simultaneously cutting uplink communication to 1 bit per parameter and improving generalization, with demonstrated stability under very low participation rates (3%). Key limitations include the inability to compress the downlink (server-to-client) broadcast, the fixed 1-bit precision that may not suit all scenarios, and the observation that sign-based noise can slow convergence in terms of communication rounds. Open questions include adaptive bit-width quantization, integration with adaptive optimizers like AdamW for large language models, and theoretical generalization guarantees for compressed FL beyond convergence rates.
