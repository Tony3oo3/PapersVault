## Problem

Real-world multimodal data exhibit complex many-to-many structural relationships that go far beyond one-to-one image-caption pairs, yet most VLMs are limited to such simple mappings. The prior graph-based approach MMGL treats the graph as a standalone modality and concatenates all neighbors equally, failing to distinguish multi-hop proximity and creating a semantic gap between small-scale GCN embeddings and large-scale pretrained vision-language representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251863/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multimodal attributed graph where each node carries optional text and image attributes, and edges encode text-to-text, image-to-image, or text-to-image relationships. For a target node, the model extracts τ-hop textual and visual subgraphs (capped at 11 nodes), encodes images via a frozen CLIP encoder into 4 visual tokens per image, and inserts learnable MM tokens immediately after each node's text tokens to preserve one-to-one correspondence. Outputs are either a generated text sequence (e.g., a section's first sentence) or a zero-shot class label selected by fuzzy string matching between the LLM response and class descriptions.

## Method

Graph4MM integrates two key modules. First, Hop-Diffused Attention injects multi-hop graph structure into self-attention by applying a causal mask that restricts attention to graph neighbors, then propagating information across hops via a diffusion matrix A = Σ θᵢAⁱ with exponential decay θᵢ = α(1−α)ⁱ, which generalizes Personalized PageRank and provably retains higher Dirichlet energy (less over-smoothing) than stacking GAT layers. Second, MM-QFormer performs cross-modal fusion using learnable query tokens that first attend to text via shared self-attention and then extract relevant visual features via cross-attention, producing a fixed number of multimodal tokens per node that are projected into the LLM's token space. The framework uses frozen pretrained encoders (CLIP for vision, OPT-125M or LLaMA-1B for language) with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Prefix-Tuning or LoRA), and a theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.1) formalizes why treating graph topology as an independent modality is suboptimal due to the mutual information gap between small-scale graph encoders and large-scale pretrained models.

## Results

On the generative task (WikiWeb2M section summarization), Graph4MM with Hop-Diffused MM-QFormer achieves BLEU-4 of 0.0800 and ROUGE-L of 0.4076 with OPT-125M, and BLEU-4 of 0.1177 and ROUGE-L of 0.4713 with LLaMA-1B, outperforming the strongest MMGL baseline (Subgraph's Text&Image) by ~2.8% and ~1.7% in BLEU-4 respectively. On the discriminative task (Ele-Fashion zero-shot classification), Hop-Diffused MM-QFormer reaches 100% accuracy, recall, and precision with both backbones, compared to 99.85% accuracy and 83.25% recall for the best MMGL baseline (OPT-125M) and 98.07% accuracy with 84.55% recall (LLaMA-1B). The paper reports a 6.93% average improvement over the strongest baselines across all textual and visual metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph4MM consistently outperforms all three baseline families. Against MMGL (Yoon et al., 2023), the strongest multimodal graph baseline, Graph4MM with Hop-Diffused MM-QFormer improves BLEU-4 from 0.0778 to 0.0800 (OPT-125M) and from 0.1157 to 0.1177 (LLaMA-1B), while dramatically boosting discriminative recall from 83.25% to 100% (OPT-125M) and from 84.55% to 100% (LLaMA-1B). Notably, MMGL's GNN-augmented variant (Subgraph's T&I+GNN), which treats graph structure as a standalone modality, actually degrades performance substantially (e.g., BLEU-4 drops to 0.0633 and R-L to 0.5771 on OPT-125M), validating the paper's argument that graph topology should guide fusion rather than be injected as a separate embedding. Against VLMs, BLIP-2-OPT-2.7B and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct perform the worst across all settings, with BLEU-4 of 0.0000 and discriminative accuracy below 32%, because their pretraining focuses on one-to-one image-caption alignment rather than many-to-many structured multimodal reasoning. The paper also shows that even the lightweight Hop-Aware variant (learnable hop embeddings) matches or slightly exceeds full Hop-Diffused Attention on some metrics (e.g., BLEU-4 of 0.0801 vs. 0.0800 on OPT-125M), offering a lower-complexity alternative at O(|V|·d) versus O(|V|·d²).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MMGL (Yoon et al., 2023), which first modeled multimodal data as attributed graphs for generative tasks, by fundamentally changing how graph structure is incorporated: rather than treating the graph as a standalone modality (via GCN embeddings concatenated to text/vision features), Graph4MM uses graph topology to guide attention through causal masking and a Personalized PageRank-inspired diffusion mechanism. The MM-QFormer component adapts BLIP-2's QFormer architecture (learnable query tokens with cross-attention) to handle multi-node multi-mapping fusion with shared self-attention conditioned on text, while the Hop-Diffused Attention draws directly from the multihop attention GNN formulation of Wang et al. (2020). The paper also departs from the broader VLM literature (BLIP-2, Qwen2-VL) that assumes one-to-one image-text alignment, explicitly targeting many-to-many structural relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that graph structure should guide how multimodal information is selected and fused (via attention masking and hop-weighted diffusion) rather than being injected as an additional input modality — a finding supported by both a mutual information argument and consistent empirical degradation when GCN embeddings are added. Limitations include evaluation only on small backbones (OPT-125M, LLaMA-1B) and two datasets, with the discriminative task reaching near-saturation (100% accuracy) that obscures meaningful comparison. Open questions include whether the Hop-Diffused Attention paradigm scales to larger foundation models (7B+), whether it generalizes to denser or heterogeneous graphs, and whether the lightweight Hop-Aware variant (which sometimes matches or exceeds Hop-Diffused) is sufficient in practice, potentially simplifying deployment.
