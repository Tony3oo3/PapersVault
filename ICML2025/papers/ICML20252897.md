## Problem

Deep learning methods for electronic-structure Hamiltonian prediction face a fundamental conflict: directly applying non-linear activations to SO(3)-equivariant features (degree l≥1) destroys equivariance, while avoiding non-linearities severely limits expressiveness and accuracy. Existing remedies—gated mechanisms, spherical channels, local coordinates, and hybrid regression—either lack strict theoretical equivariance guarantees, fail under non-rigid perturbations, or do not enforce physical dimensional correspondence between invariant and equivariant quantities.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D atomic structures (atomic positions and element types) from crystalline or molecular systems as input and produces the full electronic-structure Hamiltonian matrix, partitioned into atomic pairwise blocks H^{l_p⊗l_q} of varying angular-momentum degrees. A key design choice is that the SO(3)-invariant trace quantity T = tr(H·H†) is computed directly from the Hamiltonian target labels, serving as a free supervisory signal without requiring additional DFT calculations. Downstream, the predicted Hamiltonian yields occupied orbital energies and electronic wavefunctions for property prediction.

## Method

TraceGrad exploits the fact that SO(3)-invariance is preserved under arbitrary non-linear operations, unlike equivariance. It first trains SO(3)-invariant features z through a non-linear network s_nonlin(·) supervised by the trace quantity T = tr(H·H†), which is provably SO(3)-invariant (Theorem 4.1). A gradient-based mechanism v = ∂z/∂f then transfers the non-linear expressiveness of z into SO(3)-equivariant features v while provably preserving equivariance (Theorem 4.2), and naturally maintains the physical dimensional correspondence dim H = dim T / dim H that gated mechanisms (v = z·f) lack. The framework is stacked across K encoding modules on top of existing equivariant backbones (DeepH-E3 or QHNet), with a multi-task loss combining Hamiltonian regression and trace regression, regularized by a gradient-discarded scaling coefficient to balance the two loss terms.

## Results

On the six DeepH benchmark databases (crystalline systems), TraceGrad reduces MAE_all^H of the DeepH-E3 baseline by up to 34% (e.g., 0.251→0.175 meV on MG, 0.406→0.285 meV on MM) and MAE_cha_b^H by up to 48% on the most challenging Hamiltonian blocks. On the two QH9 molecular databases, TraceGrad reduces MAE_all^H of QHNet by up to 40% (1.962→1.191 meV on QS, 4.733→2.819 meV on QD), reduces MAE^ε (occupied orbital energy error) by up to 76% (264.483→63.375 meV on QD), and improves wavefunction cosine similarity Sim(ψ) by up to 17% (0.792→0.927 on QD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art SO(3)-equivariant baselines: DeepH-E3 (Gong et al., 2023) for crystalline systems and QHNet (Yu et al., 2023b) for molecular systems. Against DeepH-E3, TraceGrad achieves lower MAE on all six DeepH databases across every metric (MAE_all^H, MAE_cha_s^H, MAE_cha_b^H), with the largest relative improvements on MG (30% reduction in MAE_all^H) and MM (30% reduction), and particularly strong gains on twisted subsets (e.g., BG^t: 0.264→0.198, a 25% reduction) and spin-orbit-coupling-heavy systems (BB^t: 2.399→1.284 meV in MAE_cha_b^H, a 47% reduction). Against QHNet, TraceGrad wins on all five metrics for both QS and QD, with the most dramatic improvement on MAE^ε for QD (264.483→63.375 meV, a 76% reduction) and a substantial Sim(ψ) gain on QD (0.792→0.927), indicating much better prediction of downstream electronic wavefunctions. The method consistently outperforms both baselines without any loss of strict SO(3)-equivariance, and the gains are largest on the most challenging generalization scenarios (twisted structures absent from training, new thermal-motion trajectories, and out-of-distribution atom counts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DeepH-E3 (Gong et al., 2023) and QHNet (Yu et al., 2023b) equivariant graph neural network architectures, extending them with a new non-linear expressiveness mechanism. It departs from the gated activation mechanism (Weiler et al., 2018) that these baselines rely on, proposing instead a gradient-based operator that transfers non-linearity from SO(3)-invariant features to SO(3)-equivariant features while provably preserving equivariance. Conceptually, it also positions itself against spherical channel methods (Zitnick et al., 2022), which sacrifice strict continuous equivariance for discrete rotational equivariance, and against local coordinate strategies (Li et al., 2022), which fail under non-rigid perturbations. The key novelty is the dual use of the trace quantity tr(H·H†) as both a theoretical invariant derived from the target and a supervisory signal, combined with the gradient operation as a dimensionally-consistent bridge between invariant and equivariant representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in equivariant deep learning—strict SO(3)-equivariance versus non-linear expressiveness—through a clean theoretical framework (two theorems) and a practical mechanism that is broadly applicable to any equivariant regression task. The 76% reduction in occupied orbital energy MAE on QH9-dynamic and consistent 30–40% Hamiltonian MAE reductions across eight diverse databases (crystalline and molecular, with/without spin-orbit coupling, twisted/non-twisted) demonstrate strong practical impact for electronic-structure calculations. Open questions include whether the gradient-based mechanism generalizes to other equivariant tasks beyond Hamiltonian prediction (the paper shows preliminary energy/force results in Appendix M), whether the trace supervision signal can be replaced by other invariant quantities for different physical targets, and how the approach scales to much larger systems where the number of basic blocks grows quadratically.
