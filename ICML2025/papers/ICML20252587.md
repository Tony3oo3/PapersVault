## Problem

Existing visual localization methods are limited to single-camera images and scenes with minimal appearance variation, failing in real-world cross-domain settings where query images come from diverse cameras (pinhole, fisheye, 360) and scenes exhibit significant photometric changes (day/night) and dynamic objects. Cross-domain datasets further suffer from long-tail distributions—abundant primary-domain (e.g., daytime) data but extremely sparse secondary-domain (e.g., nighttime) data—making it difficult for 3D reconstruction and localization models to learn accurate scene representations across all domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single query image from any camera domain (pinhole at 85° FOV, fisheye at 120°/150°/195°, or 360°) resized to 256×256, along with a pre-trained cross-domain 3DGS scene model, and outputs a 6-DoF camera pose (translation in meters and rotation in degrees). During training, the 3DGS is built from 360 image-pose pairs (e.g., 581 daytime + 10 nighttime frames for Atrium), and cross-camera training images are generated online by mapping rendered 360 images through per-camera projection functions, avoiding the storage cost of pre-computing all camera variants.

## Method

The pipeline has three core components. First, a cross-domain 3DGS built on Scaffold-GS introduces learnable photometric embeddings (encoding image photometric histograms) combined with anchor-based Gaussian features via two separate MLPs—one predicting static color and one predicting dynamic confidence plus dynamic photometric compensation—enabling photometric variation modeling and dynamic object suppression without pre-trained detectors; a two-phase training strategy (primary-domain-only, then secondary-domain fine-tuning with frozen geometry) prevents hallucination noise from corrupting scene geometry. Second, a fine-tuned InstructPix2Pix editing model transforms primary-domain images into secondary-domain images using temporal prompts, with a custom fine-tuning strategy that pairs rendered primary images with sparse secondary ground truth and incorporates scene-prior data to suppress hallucinations. Third, an anchor-based generation method renders images at poses sampled within a spherical radius around each photometric embedding anchor (ensuring correct photometric association), and a positional attention mechanism converts per-camera image coordinates into a normalized global 360-coordinate space and concatenates them with the image channels, resolving the data ambiguity caused by misaligned appearance features across different camera distortions and fields of view.

## Results

The method reports median translation error (m) and orientation error (°) across four scenes (Atrium, Concourse, Hall, Piatrium) in both daytime (primary) and nighttime (secondary) domains, evaluated on five camera types (Pinhole, Fisheye1/2/3, 360). On the primary domain, the method achieves scene-average translation errors of 1.2–2.8 m and orientation errors of 6.4–14.5°, outperforming the prior best MS-T-VC2 by 51% on average. On the secondary (nighttime) domain, it achieves 1.8–4.5 m translation and 7.5–20.4° orientation, a 67% average improvement over MS-T-VC2. Overall, the method delivers a 59% average improvement across all domains and an 85% improvement over the unaugmented MS-T baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines on the 360Loc benchmark: PoseNet (PN), MS-T, and their 360Loc-augmented variants PN-VC2 and MS-T-VC2. The method decisively outperforms all baselines on every scene and camera domain in both daytime and nighttime settings. Against the strongest prior method MS-T-VC2, the improvement is 51% on average in the daytime domain (e.g., 2.6 m vs. 6.5 m translation in Atrium_Day) and 67% in the nighttime domain (e.g., 2.2 m vs. 9.5 m in Atrium_Night), where the long-tail data scarcity makes the gap especially pronounced. Compared to the unaugmented MS-T, the improvement reaches 85%, underscoring how data-hungry cross-domain localization is. PN-VC2, which also uses data augmentation but with limited diversity from 360Loc's mapping, is consistently outperformed, confirming that the proposed cross-domain 3DGS generation and secondary-domain augmentation provide substantially richer training data than prior augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Scaffold-GS's anchor-based 3D Gaussian representation and NeRF-W's uncertainty-weighted dynamic object suppression, extending them into a cross-domain 3DGS that explicitly encodes photometric histograms rather than relying on pre-trained detectors (as in WildGaussians or SpotLessSplats). For data augmentation, it repurposes InstructPix2Pix's text-guided diffusion editing with a custom fine-tuning strategy to address the long-tail distribution problem that prior cross-domain localization work (notably 360Loc) left unaddressed. The positional attention mechanism is a novel addition to the MS-T transformer-based pose regression backbone, addressing cross-camera feature misalignment that no prior APR method has tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain visual localization is critical for real-world deployment in autonomous driving, AR, and robotics, where camera types and lighting conditions vary substantially. The 59% average improvement over prior methods demonstrates that data diversity (via 3DGS rendering + diffusion editing) is a more effective lever than architectural changes alone for cross-domain generalization. However, the method is limited to the 360Loc benchmark's four indoor scenes and does not handle geometric changes over time, leaving open questions about scalability to outdoor scenes, additional appearance domains (weather, seasons), and whether the two-phase training strategy generalizes when the secondary domain is even sparser.
