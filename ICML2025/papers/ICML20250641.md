## Problem

Heavy ball momentum is a core component of practical min-max algorithms like Adam, yet its role in min-max games remains largely unexplored compared to minimization. In particular, it is unclear how the momentum parameter and the choice between simultaneous versus alternating updates affect local convergence and global trajectory behavior in games where player interactions dominate. This paper addresses these gaps by providing a unified continuous-time analysis that reveals fundamental differences from the minimization setting.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a smooth payoff function f(x,y) with bounded derivatives up to fourth order, a momentum parameter β ∈ (-1,1), and a step size h > 0 as inputs. It produces first-order ODE models (not the standard second-order ODEs used in minimization) with O(h³) local approximation error, eigenvalue-based local convergence rate bounds, and a characterization of how trajectories interact with gradient-norm geometry. The modified loss function F(x,y) that drives the ODEs includes explicit gradient-norm correction terms involving ‖∇ₓf‖² − ‖∇ᵧf‖², which is structurally different from the minimization analogue.

## Method

The paper derives first-order continuous-time ODEs for both simultaneous and alternating heavy ball momentum by constructing a modified loss function F(x,y) that incorporates gradient-norm corrections, achieving O(h³) local error (improving on prior O(h²) models). Local convergence is analyzed by computing the Jacobian of these ODEs at equilibrium and decomposing it into potential (S) and Hamiltonian (A) parts, then studying eigenvalue behavior under Assumption 4.4 (A ≫ S, i.e., strong player interaction). The key finding is that smaller β (more negative momentum) expands the range of step sizes for convergence, while positive β is optimal for small h — the opposite of minimization. For alternating updates, matrix perturbation theory shows an exponential acceleration term −h|σ|²/(1−β)² in the dominant eigenvalue's real part. Globally, the ODE structure reveals that smaller momentum and alternating updates both steer trajectories toward regions of lower gradient norm (shallower slopes), explaining the observed stability benefits of negative momentum in GAN training.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical. For simultaneous updates (Theorem 4.6), Continuous Sim-HB achieves local convergence when h < min_λ 2(1−β)²/(1+β) · |Re(λ)|/(Im(λ)²−Re(λ)²), with smaller β expanding the admissible step-size range (Corollary 4.7). For alternating updates (Theorem 4.9), the dominant eigenvalue satisfies Re(λ'_A) = Re(λ_S) − h/(1−β)² · |σ|² + O(h²+hα), showing exponential acceleration over simultaneous updates when h and α are small. In GAN experiments on CIFAR-10 (WGAN, ResNet-32), smaller momentum (more negative β) yields lower cumulative average slopes and lower FID, and alternating updates produce lower average slopes than simultaneous updates at fixed β.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline comparison is against the O(h²) local error continuous-time model of Bailey et al. (2020) for the β=0 (GDA) case. The paper's O(h³) model correctly predicts that simultaneous GDA diverges in bilinear games, whereas the O(h²) model incorrectly predicts cyclic behavior (Figure 1). Beyond this model-accuracy comparison, the paper's central empirical comparisons are internal: (i) Sim-HB vs. Alt-HB, where alternating updates consistently produce lower average slopes and faster local convergence; (ii) different momentum values β, where smaller (more negative) β guides trajectories toward shallower gradient regions and improves GAN FID. The paper does not benchmark against other min-max algorithms (e.g., Extra-gradient, optimistic methods) in terms of convergence rate, explicitly noting that proving HB outperforms Extra-gradient is impossible (Azizian et al., 2020); instead, it characterizes how momentum parameters shape dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time ODE framework for momentum analysis (Ghosh et al., 2023) from minimization to min-max games, adapting the modified-loss-function-to-first-order-ODE technique to handle two interacting players. It builds on the Jacobian decomposition J = S + A from Letcher et al. (2019) and the generic local convergence conditions of Wang & Chizat (2024), but deliberately departs from the standard second-order ODE approach (Su et al., 2016) to decouple step size from momentum, enabling independent analysis of each parameter. The paper also supersedes the O(h²) continuous-time model of Bailey et al. (2020), which incorrectly predicts cyclic behavior for simultaneous GDA in bilinear games, by providing an O(h³) model that correctly captures divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental reversal in the role of momentum: in min-max games, smaller (even negative) momentum enhances stability and guides trajectories toward flatter regions, opposite to the minimization setting where larger momentum is beneficial. This provides a theoretical explanation for the empirically observed benefits of negative momentum in GAN training and connects implicit gradient regularization to training quality (FID). Key limitations include the absence of stochastic gradient analysis and the restriction of convergence results to local neighborhoods; a general theoretical framework for implicit regularization in min-max games remains an open question.
