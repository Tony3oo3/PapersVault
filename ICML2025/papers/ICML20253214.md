## Problem

3D voxel-space representations are essential for volumetric medical image translation and downstream tasks like tumor segmentation, but existing 3D diffusion models are infeasible due to extreme computational and data demands (e.g., ~500 GB GPU memory for batch size 1). Prior methods that combine perpendicular 2D diffusion models (TPDM, TOSM, MADM) improve accuracy but rely on simple weighted averaging of scores, producing outputs without true 3D representation and limited volumetric realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D MRI volume (192×192×152) as condition—either a 4× downsampled version for super-resolution, a different modality (e.g., T1ce) for modality translation, or both simultaneously—and produces a full-resolution 3D volume in the target modality. For video super-resolution, videos are treated as 3D volumes along (x, y, t) axes with a sliding window strategy. The 2D models consume individual slices from transverse and sagittal planes, while the 3D fusion network operates on the full volume, receiving both the 2D score estimates and hierarchical feature maps as auxiliary inputs.

## Method

Score-Fusion replaces the fixed weighted averaging in TPDM with a learned 3D U-Net that fuses score estimates from two perpendicular 2D diffusion models in score-function space. The 3D network outputs a spatially varying weight vector w and a residual R, combined as (0.5+w)·ε₂D(a) + (0.5−w)·ε₂D(b) + λR, enabling dynamic, context-dependent selection between 2D estimates plus 3D-specific content. Critically, a zero-initialized convolution (ControlNet-style) ensures the model starts exactly at the TPDM baseline, reducing 3D training to fine-tuning; additionally, MLP-aligned 2D feature maps are injected into each 3D encoder layer so the 3D network learns an ensemble of pre-trained 2D representations rather than from scratch. Patch-wise pre-training on 3D crops further accelerates convergence, and self-consistency projection enforces data fidelity at each denoising step for inverse problems. The framework extends naturally to multi-modality fusion (conditioning 2D models on different inputs) and to video super-resolution by adding time-space plane 2D models alongside a spatial-temporal latent diffusion backbone.

## Results

On the BraTS 2021 super-resolution task, Score-Fusion-MADM achieves the best PSNR of 33.31 dB, SSIM of 0.945, MMD of 13.46, and FID of 6.57×10⁻⁴, substantially outperforming all baselines. The most dramatic gains are in volumetric realism: MMD drops from 30.92 (MADM) to 13.46 and FID from 35.64 to 6.57, reflecting the benefit of learned 3D representation over simple score averaging. On the HCP dataset, Score-Fusion-TPDM improves PSNR from 28.17 to 29.62 dB over TPDM. For video super-resolution, Score-Fusion-MGLD achieves a DOVER score of 0.755 versus 0.748 for MGLD-VSR, confirming the value of time-space plane representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Score-Fusion consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every task and metric. Against the closest competitors—TPDM, TOSM, and MADM, which also combine perpendicular 2D diffusion models—Score-Fusion gains ~1.0 dB PSNR in super-resolution (33.24–33.31 vs. 32.23–33.02) and dramatically reduces MMD/FID (e.g., MADM FID drops from 35.64 to 6.57), confirming that learned 3D fusion in score space captures volumetric realism that simple weighted averaging cannot. Against 2D baselines (Pix2pix, U-net, Palette, I2SB), Score-Fusion leads by 3–6 dB PSNR and orders of magnitude in MMD/FID, reflecting the advantage of 3D volumetric modeling. Against 3D baselines (Palette-3D/Med-DDPM, Pix2pix-3D, U-net-3D), Score-Fusion achieves higher PSNR and far better realism metrics, while Palette-3D fails entirely on modality translation. For video super-resolution, Score-Fusion-MGLD improves DOVER from 0.748 (MGLD-VSR) to 0.755, a modest but consistent gain attributed to the added time-space plane representations. In downstream tumor segmentation, Score-Fusion-TPDM achieves 93.71% recovery rate on Tumor Core (vs. 46.27% for TPDM), demonstrating that the improved 3D fidelity translates to better performance with 3D segmentation models like SwinUNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

Score-Fusion extends the line of work on combining perpendicular 2D diffusion models for 3D medical imaging, initiated by TPDM (Lee et al., 2023) and further developed by TOSM and MADM. While these prior methods approximate the 3D score function via a fixed weighted average of 2D score estimates, Score-Fusion replaces this averaging with a learned 3D U-Net that operates in score function space, thereby introducing genuine 3D voxel-space representation. The 3D architecture is borrowed from Med-DDPM (Dorjsembe et al., 2024), and the video super-resolution extension builds on MGLD-VSR (Yang et al., 2024). A key design departure is the initialization strategy: the 3D network starts as an exact average of the 2D models (matching TPDM), reducing 3D training to a fine-tuning process rather than training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D voxel-space representation is not merely a theoretical nicety but materially improves both volumetric realism (MMD/FID gaps of 2–3× over baselines) and downstream clinical tasks (tumor segmentation recovery rates jumping from ~40% to ~94% in super-resolution). The plug-and-play nature of the approach—requiring only 16 GPU days of 3D fine-tuning on top of existing 2D models—makes it a practical path to 3D-aware medical image translation without the 120+ GPU-day cost of full 3D diffusion training. Limitations include the inability to perform joint end-to-end training of 2D and 3D components, and the reliance on patch-wise pre-training that may hinder tasks requiring long-range spatial coherence (e.g., compressed sensing MRI, large-area inpainting). Open questions include whether the score-fusion paradigm generalizes to latent-space 3D models and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution volumes or more than two 2D conditioning directions.
