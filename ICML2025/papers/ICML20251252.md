## Problem

Social network interventions to reduce polarization or disagreement under the Friedkin-Johnsen opinion dynamics model give rise to a bilevel optimization problem with non-convex bilinear constraints (the FJ equilibrium), which existing approaches handle only with problem-specific heuristics or handcrafted algorithms. No unified, scalable framework existed that could accommodate arbitrary differentiable objectives and general convex intervention constraints while solving the resulting large-scale non-convex program efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a directed or undirected weighted graph (adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{n×n}), a vector of internal (innate) opinions s ∈ R^n, a budget parameter b, and a convex constraint set W ⊆ B encoding intervention limits (e.g., fixed edges, lower bounds on weights, budget caps). It produces optimized network weights w*, the corresponding equilibrium opinions y*(w*), and the minimized objective value. Fixed or constrained weights are dropped from the decision vector a priori, reducing the effective dimensionality from n² to m = n(n−1) or fewer; the method scales to m up to 4.8 million variables on a single GPU.

## Method

BeeRS formulates the intervention as a bilevel program: the outer level minimizes a differentiable objective φ(w, y*(w)) over weights w subject to convex constraints W, while the inner level enforces the FJ equilibrium A(w)y = s. The key technical contribution is computing the hypergradient ∇_w φ(w, y*(w)) via implicit differentiation: rather than explicitly inverting the n×n Jacobian A(w) to obtain the full sensitivity Jy*(w) ∈ R^{n×m}, the algorithm solves a single n×n linear system (Eq. 9) for the vector-Jacobian product Jy*(w)ᵀ ∇₂φ, reducing per-iteration cost from m linear solves to one. The outer loop uses projected gradient descent with momentum, where the projection onto W is handled by a convex solver (OSQP/Clarabel/SCS). The entire pipeline—equilibrium solve via GMRES, hypergradient computation, and projected update—is implemented in JAX with just-in-time compilation and sparse matrix representations, enabling GPU-accelerated convergence in roughly 20 iterations on datasets with millions of edges.

## Results

BeeRS achieves a 39.5% and 40.0% reduction in polarization on the full DBLP (3.08M nodes) and LiveJournal (4.85M nodes) datasets, respectively, solving problems with up to 4.8 million modifiable weights in 195.4 s and 766.8 s on a single GPU. On the Reddit dataset (553 nodes), BeeRS reduces polarization by 40.2% and disagreement by 21.5%, while the NAD baseline *increases* polarization by 128.7% and disagreement by 41.4%. Against the IPOPT solver, BeeRS is 1–3 orders of magnitude faster and attains zero relative suboptimality, whereas IPOPT exhibits relative suboptimality of 5.6e-3 to 1.15e-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IPOPT (a state-of-the-art nonlinear programming solver), BeeRS wins on both runtime and solution quality: it is 1–3 orders of magnitude faster for larger problem sizes and achieves zero relative suboptimality, while IPOPT shows relative suboptimality ranging from 5.6e-3 (DBLP, n=2000) to 1.15e-1 (LiveJournal, n=2000). Against NAD (Network Administrator Dynamics from Chitra & Musco, 2020), BeeRS wins decisively on both polarization (−40.2% vs. +128.7%) and disagreement (−21.5% vs. +41.4%) on the Reddit dataset, but at roughly 4× the runtime (141.6 s vs. 35.9 s). Against the regularized variant NAD* (λ=0.2), BeeRS again dominates on both metrics (−40.2% vs. −0.7% polarization; −21.5% vs. +0.3% disagreement) while being ~5× slower (141.6 s vs. 27.3 s). The paper attributes NAD's poor performance to its myopic optimization of weights while holding opinions fixed, which inadvertently strengthens same-bias connections and weakens cross-cutting ones, whereas BeeRS's backpropagation through the FJ equilibrium explicitly accounts for how weight changes shift opinions toward moderation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of opinion dynamics (Friedkin-Johnsen model) and bilevel/hypergradient optimization. It builds directly on the FJ model for opinion equilibrium computation and on implicit differentiation techniques for hypergradients (Maclaurin et al., 2015; Grontas et al., 2024), applying them to the network intervention problem. The key departure from prior intervention work (Chitra & Musco, 2020; Cinus et al., 2023) is the unified bilevel formulation that handles arbitrary differentiable objectives and convex constraints simultaneously, rather than problem-specific heuristics. Most critically, it departs from NAD's myopic weight updates (which fix opinions during optimization) by properly backpropagating through the FJ equilibrium, capturing the feedback between weight changes and opinion shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that NAD—a published method—actually increases polarization by 128.7% while BeeRS decreases it by 40.2% on the same dataset is a significant finding that challenges the assumed safety of naive disagreement-minimizing interventions in recommender systems. The 3-orders-of-magnitude speedup over IPOPT at superior solution quality makes large-scale intervention design (millions of edges) practically feasible on a single GPU. Key limitations include the convexity assumption on the constraint set (precluding binary edge-presence decisions) and the restriction to the FJ model; future work on connecting weight modifications to actionable platform interventions (e.g., boost/penalty factors) and on distributed solving would broaden practical applicability.
