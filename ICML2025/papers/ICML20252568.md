## Problem

In Semi-supervised Federated Learning, the re-labeling step determines pseudo-label quality, yet existing methods rely on either the global model (limited by the distribution gap between global and local data) or the local model (suffering from knowledge solidification where errors cannot be self-corrected). No prior work provides a theoretical explanation for why neither single-model approach is optimal, nor a principled mechanism to combine them adaptively per sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN) along with the current global model from the server and its own locally trained model. The server additionally holds a small labeled set (500 samples for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 2500 for CIFAR-100) used to compute class priors and the K-dimensional closeness vector Q. The method outputs per-sample pseudo labels as K-dimensional probability vectors (weighted blends of global and local model softmax outputs), which are thresholded to form high-confidence training sets for local model updates.

## Method

BSemiFL replaces the single-model re-labeling step with a Bayesian-weighted ensemble of the global and local models. For each local sample, it computes a 'closeness' score to the global training distribution and to the local training distribution via Variational Bayesian Inference (minimizing a negated ELBO), yielding per-class conditional probabilities that are aggregated with class priors into scalar closeness values p̂(x) and p̂_m(x). These are normalized into weights α and 1−α, and the pseudo label is the weighted sum α·f_global(x) + (1−α)·f_local(x). Theoretically, the paper proves this ensemble's cross-entropy loss is bounded above by max(L_global, L_local) and, under a Bayes-consistency condition, is strictly smaller than the loss of naive 50/50 averaging. The approach requires only an additional K-dimensional vector Q to be transmitted from server to client, preserving privacy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 2 shards (most non-IID), BSemiFL achieves 78.31% test accuracy, outperforming the best baseline Orchestra (68.57%) by 9.8 percentage points. On SVHN with 2 shards, BSemiFL reaches 94.52% vs. Orchestra's 86.57% (+8.0%). On CIFAR-100 with 8 shards, BSemiFL achieves 49.75% vs. SemiFL's 47.87%. The method consistently leads across all three datasets under Shards-based Non-IID partitioning, with the largest gains under the most heterogeneous settings (2 shards).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSemiFL outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of settings. Against SemiFL (the closest SSFL competitor), BSemiFL gains +11.97% on CIFAR-10 S=2 (78.31 vs. 66.34) and +6.53% on CIFAR-100 S=8 (49.75 vs. 47.87), though SemiFL slightly edges out on CIFAR-10 under Dirichlet α=10 (88.19 vs. 87.27) where the global model is well-trained. Against Orchestra (the strongest UFL baseline), BSemiFL leads by +9.8% on CIFAR-10 S=2 and +8.0% on SVHN S=2, but Orchestra narrowly wins on CIFAR-100 S=2 (39.93 vs. 39.09) due to its clustering advantage on highly concentrated local data. The remaining baselines—FedMatch, FedU, FedEMA, (FL)², FedFAME, and pFedKnow—are all outperformed by 5–30+ percentage points across all datasets, with the largest gaps on CIFAR-100 where label scarcity is most severe. The paper notes that BSemiFL and Orchestra target different steps of SSFL (re-labeling vs. local training) and could be combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SemiFL (Diao et al., 2022) framework for semi-supervised federated learning by replacing its global-model-only re-labeling step with a Bayesian-based weighted ensemble of both the global and local models. The closeness estimation draws directly on domain adaptation theory (Ben-David et al., 2010), using variational Bayesian inference to estimate how close each local sample is to the global or local training distribution. The paper positions its contribution as orthogonal to other SSFL improvements targeting local training (FedFAME, (FL)²) or aggregation (pFedKnow), and distinct from unsupervised FL methods (FedU, Orchestra) that focus on the local training step rather than re-labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical analysis providing clean error bounds for global-only, local-only, and ensemble re-labeling gives practitioners a principled understanding of why neither model alone is optimal, which was previously only observed empirically. The Bayesian closeness-based weighting is a lightweight, per-sample adaptive mechanism that could potentially transfer to other federated or semi-supervised settings beyond SSFL. However, the method is validated only on small-scale image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) with a 'labels at server' assumption, and its interaction with other SSFL improvements (e.g., contrastive local training) remains unexplored. Open questions include scalability to larger datasets and more complex architectures, and whether the Bayesian closeness estimation remains reliable under extreme distribution shift.
