## Problem

Existing mixed-precision quantization (MPQ) methods require the same large-scale dataset for both bitwidth policy search and model deployment, incurring prohibitive search costs (e.g., ~72 GPU hours for ResNet50 on ImageNet). Substituting a small proxy dataset for search causes severe accuracy degradation (up to 10% Top-1 loss) due to distribution shift, and prior transferable-policy methods rely on intricate feature-map computations without theoretical grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (ResNet-18/50, MobileNet-V2, or VGG16) and a small proxy dataset (CIFAR-10, Flowers, or Food-101, roughly 0.5% the size of the target) to search for a per-layer bitwidth policy over candidate sets B^θ and B^a (e.g., {2,3,4,6} for ResNet). The output is a transferable MPQ policy that is directly applied to quantize and fine-tune the model on the target large dataset (ImageNet or VOC), requiring only weight adjustment rather than a full re-search. The first and last layers are fixed at 8 bits, and the policy is constrained by a BOPs budget Ω₀.

## Method

ASGA reformulates SAM's min-max perturbation into a single-step objective that implicitly aligns the gradients of the empirical loss L(θ) and the perturbed loss Lp(θ) by optimizing L(θ − μ∇L(θ)), which via Taylor expansion maximizes their inner product and resolves gradient conflicts with the surrogate gap h(θ). An adaptive perturbation radius ρ = min(ρ_max, φ/ln(h(θ)+1)) dynamically increases as the landscape flattens, accelerating sharpness optimization without a fixed-ρ mismatch. This ASGA objective is integrated into the differentiable MPQ (DMPQ) supernet as a generalization regularization alongside the accuracy and complexity losses. The paper provides a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound showing that minimizing h(θ) tightens the upper bound on quantization generalization error, plus a convergence guarantee under non-convex SGD with decaying learning rate.

## Results

On ImageNet, ASGA consistently improves Top-1 accuracy over all baselines while reducing convergence epochs: for ResNet18 (3MP/3MP), EdMIPS+ASGA reaches 67.9% Top-1 (vs. 66.4% baseline) in 75 epochs (vs. 110); for ResNet50 (4MP/4MP), HAWQ+ASGA achieves 74.9% Top-1 in 92 epochs (vs. 98); for MobileNet-V2 (4MP/4MP), GMPQ+ASGA reaches 69.1% Top-1 in 91 epochs (vs. 100). On VOC, HAQ+ASGA with ResNet18-Faster R-CNN improves mAP from 69.0 to 69.3 while reducing search epochs from 82 to 76. The method achieves up to 150% search speedup over SOTA MPQs by using only CIFAR10 (0.5% of ImageNet training data) as the proxy dataset for policy search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASGA is applied as a plug-in module on top of nine existing MPQ methods and improves or matches all of them. The largest accuracy gain is on ResNet18 with EdMIPS, where ASGA boosts Top-1 from 66.4% to 67.9% (+1.5%) while cutting convergence epochs from 110 to 75 (−32%). On ResNet50, HAQ+ASGA improves Top-1 from 72.6% to 73.5% (+0.9%) with 24 fewer epochs, and HAWQ+ASGA reaches 74.9% (vs. 74.6%) in 92 epochs. For MobileNet-V2, GMPQ+ASGA achieves 69.1% Top-1 (vs. 68.8% GMPQ, 68.9% HMQ) with 9 fewer epochs than GMPQ. On VOC object detection, HAQ+ASGA with ResNet18-Faster R-CNN improves mAP from 69.0 to 69.3 (+0.3) with 6 fewer search epochs, while EdMIPS+ASGA on VGG16-SSD matches HAQ's 69.4 mAP with 21 fewer epochs. The one notable exception is EWGS on ResNet18, where ASGA yields only a negligible +0.01% Top-1 gain (53.85→53.86), though it still reduces convergence epochs from 123 to 98.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from Foret et al. (2020) into the mixed-precision quantization (MPQ) policy search setting, building on the differentiable MPQ (DMPQ) framework of EdMIPS (Cai & Vasconcelos, 2020) and the gradient alignment mechanism from Wang et al. (2023). It departs from prior transferable-MPQ methods (GMPQ, SEAM) that rely on intricate feature-map computations (attribution rank, classification margins) by instead exploiting the loss landscape sharpness as a dataset-independent generalization signal. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an adaptive perturbation radius and implicit gradient alignment to resolve conflicts between the accuracy loss and the perturbed loss, which standard SAM does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: searching MPQ policies on a 0.5%-size proxy dataset (CIFAR10) with up to 150% speedup while maintaining target-dataset accuracy removes the need for expensive large-scale quantization fine-tuning. The PAC-Bayesian generalization bound and convergence analysis provide theoretical grounding that prior feature-map-based transferable MPQ methods lack. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CNN backbones (ResNet, MobileNet, VGG) on image classification and detection, with no evidence for transformer architectures or non-vision tasks. An open question is whether the proxy dataset choice (CIFAR10 outperforms Flowers/Food) can be systematically predicted, and whether the approach extends to more aggressive quantization regimes (e.g., 2-bit).
