## Problem

3D vision-language learning is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale 3D datasets, while 2D datasets are vast and well-curated. No high-performing pre-trained 3D encoder exists comparable to CLIP for 2D, creating a significant performance gap that slows adoption of 3D models in embodied systems. The paper asks whether leveraging large-scale 2D data and pre-trained 2D models can bridge this gap without requiring proportional scaling of 3D annotations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a language query alongside either a sequence of N posed RGB-D images (each with an associated 3D pointmap of shape N×H×W×3, obtained by unprojecting depth via pinhole-camera equations) or a single monocular RGB image lifted to a 3D pointmap via a neural 2D-to-3D lifting model (Moge). The 3D pointmap need not be metric—relative coordinates suffice. Outputs include per-object 3D segmentation masks (decoded as per-point predictions), a text span over the input utterance identifying the referenced object, and optionally a generated text answer for question-answering tasks. During training, 2D images are lifted to 3D with 50% probability; at test time, 2D images remain in their original 2D space to avoid noise from predicted pointmaps.

## Method

UniVLG encodes each RGB image independently with a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone, then fuses information across views using 3D k-NN attention where the 3D pointmap serves as relative positional embeddings—this single mechanism distinguishes 2D from 3D processing without separate encoders. The core novelty is a language-conditioned mask decoder that concatenates learnable object queries with JinaCLIP text tokens, then alternates masked cross-attention with visual tokens and self-attention, while critically also updating the visual tokens via cross-attention to the refined queries and text (a step absent in prior open-vocabulary decoders like ODIN). Masks are decoded as a dot-product between refined queries and updated visual features, and a T5 decoder generates text answers conditioned on the object queries. A novel box loss computes an enclosing 3D bounding box for each predicted mask and supervises it with L1 and GIoU losses to suppress outlier points and merged instances. By lifting 2D images to 3D pointmaps during training, all parameters are shared between 2D and 3D pathways, and the mask-based output space unifies per-pixel and per-point predictions across modalities.

## Results

On 3D referential grounding with sensor point clouds (the more realistic, embodied-aligned setting), UniVLG achieves 73.0% Acc@25 on SR3D, 58.3% on NR3D, and 63.5% on ScanRefer in the detection (Det) setup, outperforming the prior best single-stage method BUTD-DETR (43.3%, 32.2%, 42.2%) by 29.7, 26.1, and 21.3 points respectively. Even the 3D-only variant (without 2D co-training) reaches 71.6%, 54.7%, and 60.7%, surpassing all prior methods that use mesh point clouds. On 3D question answering, UniVLG achieves 25.7% EM on ScanQA and 50.2% on SQA3D, outperforming 3D-VisTA (21.6%, 46.9%) and PQ3D (21.0%, 47.0%). On 2D RefCOCO, co-training with 3D data does not degrade 2D performance (69.2% vs. 69.4% for the 2D-only variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVLG substantially outperforms all prior methods on 3D referential grounding in the sensor point cloud setting. Against the strongest prior single-stage method BUTD-DETR (43.3% SR3D @25 Det), UniVLG gains +29.7 points; against the strongest two-stage method PQ3D (62.0% on mesh PC), UniVLG gains +11.0 points even while operating on the harder sensor PC input. The 3D-only variant already surpasses PQ3D's mesh-PC results, demonstrating that the architectural improvements (mask decoder, 3D k-NN attention, shared 2D-3D parameters) are the primary driver rather than 2D co-training. On 3D QA, UniVLG beats 3D-VisTA by +4.1 EM on ScanQA and +3.3 on SQA3D, and PQ3D by +4.7 and +3.2 respectively. On 2D RefCOCO, UniVLG (2D-3D) is competitive with LAVT (69.2% vs. 72.7%) and matches X-Decoder(L) on RefCOCOg (64.1% vs. 64.6%), confirming that 3D co-training does not sacrifice 2D capability. The out-of-domain L3DD results show consistent gains over the 3D-only variant (e.g., 51.9% vs. 49.7% on HM3D), indicating that 2D data improves generalization to unseen scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVLG extends ODIN (Jain et al., 2024), which unified 2D and 3D segmentation via positional encodings, to referential grounding and question answering, while sharing all parameters across modalities through 2D-to-3D lifting rather than ODIN's partial sharing. The mask decoder is adapted from Mask2Former (Cheng et al., 2022) with two key modifications: language-conditioned masked cross-attention and bidirectional updating of visual features during query refinement. The paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (3D-VisTA, PQ3D) that relies on ground-truth object proposals, and from single-stage box-decoding methods (BUTD-DETR), by proposing a single-stage mask-decoding approach evaluated on raw sensor point clouds without mesh reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D data and pre-trained 2D features can substantially close the 3D vision-language gap without requiring massive 3D annotations, offering a practical path for embodied systems that already have depth sensors but lack large-scale 3D labels. The finding that mask decoding dramatically outperforms box decoding at tight IoU thresholds (33.2 vs 1.1 at Acc@75) challenges the established box-based evaluation paradigm and suggests the field should reconsider its output representations. Open questions include whether 2D-to-3D generalization can be further improved (the model remains well below the full-3D-supervision upper bound on AI2-THOR), how the approach extends to dynamic 3D environments, and whether scaling to larger 2D datasets yields additional 3D gains. The shift to sensor point cloud evaluation is important for embodied AI but may limit direct comparability with prior mesh-based results.
