## Problem

Decentralized federated learning (DFL) suffers significant performance degradation under statistical heterogeneity, where clients hold data from different distributions. While NTK-based optimization has been shown to improve convergence in centralized federated learning, no prior work has adapted NTK-based weight evolution to the decentralized setting. This paper asks how to design a DFL approach that effectively addresses heterogeneity without a central server.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252359/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of 300 clients holds local grayscale images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST; variable-size digit images for FEMNIST) with 10-class labels, partitioned via Dirichlet distributions (label-skew) or writer identity (feature-skew). Rather than transmitting gradients or weight vectors, clients exchange Jacobian matrices, true labels, and function evaluations with their graph neighbors (κ=5 regular graph, 300 nodes). The output is a global 2-layer MLP (100 hidden neurons) obtained by averaging client models after training, producing 10-class predictions on a global holdout test set.

## Method

NTK-DFL operates in communication rounds over a decentralized graph. Each round: (1) clients perform per-round parameter averaging with neighbors, (2) each client computes Jacobians of its local data with respect to its own and each neighbor's averaged weights, (3) clients exchange these Jacobians, labels, and function evaluations, and (4) each client stacks all received Jacobians into a local tensor, constructs a local NTK via pairwise Frobenius inner products, and evolves weights using the closed-form NTK gradient-flow solution w_i^(k,t) = Σ(J_i)ᵀ R_i + w̄_i^(k), selecting the timestep t with lowest residual loss. The key insight is a synergy between NTK evolution and final model averaging: NTK updates generate a moderate inter-client model deviation that, when averaged, yields a global model with substantially better generalization than any individual client model. Theoretically, the convergence bound scales as O(1/√(KT)), where the local iteration count T can be 1–2 orders of magnitude larger than in SGD-based DFL methods, yielding 4.6× fewer communication rounds to reach target accuracy under high heterogeneity.

## Results

On Fashion-MNIST with high heterogeneity (α=0.1), NTK-DFL establishes a 3–4% test-accuracy lead over the best baseline within just 5 communication rounds and maintains this advantage throughout training. NTK-DFL reaches 85% test accuracy in 4.6× fewer communication rounds than DFedAvg, the next-best decentralized baseline. Across sparsity levels (κ=2 to κ=8), NTK-DFL consistently outperforms all baselines by 2–3%, and its aggregated model accuracy remains stable (within ~1%) as heterogeneity varies from α=0.1 to α=0.5, while baseline methods degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTK-DFL outperforms all five decentralized baselines (D-PSGD, DFedAvg, DFedAvgM, DFedSAM, DisPFL) on Fashion-MNIST, FEMNIST, and MNIST across all heterogeneity levels tested. The most striking gap is against DFedAvg at α=0.1, where NTK-DFL converges in 4.6× fewer rounds; DFedAvgM, DFedSAM, and DisPFL all trail DFedAvg in this regime. Against the centralized NTK-FL baseline (73/85/180 rounds for IID/α=0.5/α=0.1), NTK-DFL achieves comparable or better convergence in fewer rounds while operating fully decentralized, demonstrating that the NTK evolution mechanism transfers effectively without a central aggregator. Under sparsity variation (κ=2–8), NTK-DFL maintains a stable 2–3% accuracy advantage over all baselines, whereas DFedSAM and DisPFL show greater sensitivity to network density. The per-round averaging ablation (Figure 7) confirms that removing the neighbor-averaging step causes a large distributional shift toward lower accuracies, validating that the synergy between NTK evolution and averaging is essential to the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: NTK-based weight evolution in federated learning (Yue et al., 2022), which replaced SGD with kernelized updates in a centralized server architecture, and decentralized federated averaging (Sun et al., 2021), which eliminated the central server in favor of peer-to-peer gossip over a graph. NTK-DFL transplants the NTK evolution mechanism into the decentralized setting, where clients exchange Jacobians rather than gradients or weights, and introduces a per-round averaging step that constructs a local NTK from both own and neighbor Jacobians. The paper also identifies a synergy between NTK-based updates and final model averaging, showing that the inter-client model deviation generated by NTK evolution (rather than SGD) is beneficial for the aggregated model's generalization—a connection not explored in prior DFL or NTK-FL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.6× reduction in communication rounds under high heterogeneity is practically significant for latency-sensitive or bandwidth-constrained deployments, and the theoretical convergence bound (Corollary 4.6) formalizes why larger local iteration counts T are viable with NTK evolution but not with SGD-based DFL. However, all experiments use a two-layer MLP on MNIST-scale datasets (≤70k images), leaving open whether the approach scales to CNNs, transformers, or real-world cross-silo settings where Jacobian memory and communication costs become prohibitive. The reconstruction attack analysis in Appendix F, while brief, raises legitimate privacy questions about transmitting Jacobians, and the paper acknowledges that compression (sparsification, random projection) is needed to make the method deployable—yet the privacy guarantees under these compressions remain uncharacterized.
