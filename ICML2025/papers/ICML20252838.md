## Problem

Existing N:M sparse training methods (SR-STE, LBC, MaxQ) rely on dense backward propagation to update all weights for identifying the optimal N:M mask, incurring substantial training FLOPs and memory overhead. This dense-gradient requirement becomes a critical bottleneck as model sizes grow, limiting the practicality of N:M sparsity for resource-constrained settings. The paper asks whether one can maintain consistent sparsity in both forward and backward passes while still discovering high-quality N:M masks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet-1K) and outputs class labels, but its primary learned artifact is the N:M binary mask B ∈ {0,1}^{K×M} that constrains at most N non-zero weights per block of M consecutive weights along the input-channel dimension. The N:M pattern (2:4, 1:4, or 1:16) is fixed per experiment, and the mask is evolved block-by-block rather than globally, with the constraint that exactly N positions remain active in each block throughout training.

## Method

BAME maintains sparse forward and backward propagation throughout training, computing dense gradients only every ΔT iterations for mask adaption. Its Loss-Aware Mask Adaption (LMA) component uses first-order Taylor expansion to estimate the loss impact of pruning each active weight (Eq. 4) and the loss benefit of reviving each pruned weight (Eq. 5), then performs a unified ranking within each N:M block to prune the least-impactful weights and revive the most-beneficial ones, guaranteeing net loss decrease per adaption cycle. Its Oscillation-Aware Block Freezing (OBF) component tracks per-block mask fluctuation via EMA of Sparse Architecture Divergence scores and freezes the top-β proportion of most-oscillating blocks (and additionally blocks with high weight magnitudes via an α threshold) from further mask changes, stabilizing the loss landscape. The full pipeline follows a three-phase schedule: gradual pruning to reach target sparsity, BAME-driven mask evolution, and final static sparse fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet, BAME achieves 72.0% top-1 accuracy for 1:16 sparse ResNet-50, surpassing SR-STE (71.5%) by 0.5% while using only 0.29× training FLOPs (vs. 0.67× for SR-STE), a 2.37× training FLOPs reduction. For 1:4 sparse ResNet-50, BAME reaches 76.1% top-1 accuracy, outperforming SR-STE (75.3%) and Bi-Mask (75.6%) with 0.39× training FLOPs. On CIFAR-10, BAME achieves 94.99% top-1 for 2:4 sparse ResNet-32 and 93.32% for 1:16 sparse MobileNet-V2, both state-of-the-art among N:M sparse training methods. BAME also extends to transformers, reaching 79.7% top-1 for 2:4 sparse DeiT-small on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAME outperforms SR-STE across all N:M patterns on ImageNet: +0.4% on 2:4 (77.4 vs 77.0), +0.8% on 1:4 (76.1 vs 75.3), and +0.5% on 1:16 (72.0 vs 71.5), while simultaneously reducing training FLOPs by roughly 2× (e.g., 0.29× vs 0.69× for 1:16). Against Bi-Mask, BAME wins on 1:4 (+0.5%) and 1:16 (+0.5%) with lower training FLOPs, and ties on 2:4 (77.4%) while using fewer FLOPs (0.59× vs 0.66×). BAME slightly trails MaxQ by 0.2% top-1 on 2:4 ResNet-50 (77.4 vs 77.6) but achieves a 1.54× reduction in training FLOPs (0.59× vs 0.91×). Compared to ASP, BAME improves 2:4 ResNet-50 accuracy by 0.6% (77.4 vs 76.8) while cutting training FLOPs from 1.24× to 0.59×. On CIFAR-10, BAME surpasses RigL (a representative unstructured sparse training method) by 1.14% top-1 on 1:16 ResNet-32 (93.15 vs 92.01), demonstrating the advantage of loss-aware, block-constrained mask adaptation over global magnitude/gradient-based pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAME sits at the intersection of two research threads: N:M sparse training (ASP, SR-STE, LBC, Bi-Mask, MaxQ), which has relied on dense backward propagation via straight-through estimators to identify optimal masks, and unstructured sparse training (RigL, Top-KAST, Sparse Momentum), which performs iterative pruning-and-regrowing but without the block-level N:M constraint. The paper adapts the loss-aware Taylor-expansion criterion from Molchanov et al. and the three-step gradual-pruning pipeline from Top-KAST into the N:M block structure, while departing from all prior N:M methods by maintaining sparse backward propagation throughout training. It also integrates with Bi-Mask (Zhang et al., 2023b) to enforce N:M sparsity in the backward pass, addressing a hardware compatibility gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: BAME achieves 2.37× training FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding dense-backward baselines, making N:M sparse training accessible on resource-constrained hardware. The conceptual contribution—that sparse backward propagation is sufficient for identifying high-quality N:M masks when combined with loss-aware block-level pruning/regrowing—challenges the field's default assumption that dense gradients are necessary. Open questions include the sensitivity to hyperparameters (α, β, ΔT), the hardware implementation challenge of maintaining N:M sparsity through transpose operations in backpropagation, and whether the approach scales to much larger models or non-vision tasks.
