## Problem

While binary linear classification under Random Classification Noise (RCN) admits polynomial-time algorithms, the computational complexity of multiclass linear classification (MLC) with RCN was completely open even for three labels. This paper establishes the first super-polynomial Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds for MLC with RCN, showing that the problem becomes computationally intractable for k ≥ 3, even with constant noise separation σ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250523/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives i.i.d. labeled examples (x, y) where x is drawn from an unknown distribution on R^d and y is generated by a ground-truth multiclass linear classifier corrupted by a known row-stochastic noise matrix H with non-negative separation σ = min_{i≠j} H_ii − H_ij. The goal is to output a hypothesis with 0-1 error at most opt + ε (or C·opt for approximate learning), where opt is the optimal achievable error. All hardness results hold even when H is given as input and σ is a positive constant, and the results extend to ruling out even beating the trivial uniform-guess hypothesis of error 1 − 1/k.

## Method

The proof reduces MLC learning to a correlation testing problem: distinguishing whether labels are generated by a structured multiclass classifier or independently from a fixed distribution h_k. The key construction uses hidden direction distributions where the 1D marginal along a hidden direction v is a moment-matching distribution G_{δ,ξ} (Gaussian restricted to equally-spaced intervals), which closely approximates N(0,1) up to high-order moments. A critical insight is that when the kth row of H is a convex combination of the other rows and the probability mass of the corresponding regions is assigned proportionally, the marginal label distribution exactly matches h_k, making the structured and unstructured cases indistinguishable to SQ algorithms. The SQ dimension argument then converts small pairwise chi-squared correlations between distributions parameterized by nearly-orthogonal directions into super-polynomial query lower bounds. The Veronese mapping embeds the polynomial classification instance into a higher-dimensional linear problem, with the polynomial degree m carefully chosen to balance moment-matching quality against the dimension blowup d = N^{O(m)}.

## Results

The paper establishes the first super-polynomial SQ lower bounds for Multiclass Linear Classification (MLC) with Random Classification Noise (RCN). For k=3 labels with constant separation (H_ii − H_ij ≥ 0.1), learning to error opt + ε requires d^Ω(log^0.98(d)/ε^1.98) queries or a query of tolerance d^−Ω(log^0.98(d)/ε^1.98) (Theorem 6.1). For larger k and smaller separation Ω(1/C), even achieving a C-approximation to the optimal loss is SQ-hard (Corollary 6.3). Most strikingly, for any k ≥ 3 with separation Ω(1/d), it is SQ-hard to achieve error 1 − 1/k − o(1) even when opt = O(1/k), meaning no efficient SQ algorithm can beat a uniform random guess (Corollary 6.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central contrast is with the binary (k=2) case, where polynomial-time SQ algorithms are known (Blum et al. 1998; Dunagan & Vempala 2004; Diakonikolas et al. 2021, 2023b; Kontonis et al. 2024) and the problem is solvable even when the separation min_{i≠j} H_ii − H_ij = 0. The SQ-hardness results show that this tractability breaks down sharply at k=3. Prior algorithmic approaches for multiclass RCN (Patrini et al. 2017; Van Rooyen & Williamson 2018; Lipton et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2021) rely on inverting the noise matrix H and have complexity scaling inversely with the minimum singular value of H, which can be arbitrarily small even for k=3 with separation σ=0.1. The paper's hardness results demonstrate that no SQ algorithm can overcome this barrier in general, even when H is known and the separation is a positive constant. The results also subsume and generalize prior SQ-hardness results for binary halfspace learning under Massart noise (Diakonikolas & Kane 2022; Nasser & Tiegel 2022) to the multiclass setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SQ-hardness framework of Diakonikolas et al. (2017) and the moment-matching distribution constructions of Diakonikolas & Kane (2022) and Nasser & Tiegel (2022) from binary to multiclass classification. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'hard-to-distinguish condition' (Definition 4.1) that exploits the convex-combination structure of noise matrix rows, enabling a reduction from a correlation testing problem to multiclass learning. The Veronese mapping is used to lift polynomial classification hardness to linear classification, a standard technique repurposed here in a novel way to bridge the gap between the constructed hard distributions and the target linear model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first super-polynomial SQ lower bound for multiclass linear classification with RCN, establishing a sharp phase transition: binary RCN learning is polynomial-time solvable (Blum et al., 1998; Dunagan & Vempala, 2004), but the problem becomes SQ-hard at k=3 even with constant noise separation. The result that even beating a uniform random guess is SQ-hard for large k with small noise (Corollary 6.4) is striking and suggests that existing algorithmic approaches (which invert the noise matrix) are fundamentally limited. Open questions include whether non-SQ algorithms (e.g., leveraging specific structure of the marginal distribution) might circumvent these bounds, and whether the hardness extends to structured noise models or to the deep learning setting where the last layer is a softmax.
