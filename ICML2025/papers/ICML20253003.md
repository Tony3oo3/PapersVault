## Problem

Pose-guided human video generation suffers from three intertwined issues: inaccurate pose estimation introduces noisy guidance that causes hand distortion and temporal flickering, existing methods sacrifice image detail for temporal smoothness, and generating long videos with seamless segment transitions remains challenging. Prior approaches either apply hard confidence thresholds that discard useful pose information or use simple averaging at segment boundaries that produces visible artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (resized to 576×1024) and a pose sequence extracted frame-by-frame with DWPose, where each pose is rendered as a stick-figure image with keypoint and limb brightness modulated by per-keypoint confidence scores. The output is a human motion video of arbitrary length, generated as 16-frame latent segments that are progressively fused in overlapping regions (C frames) before being decoded by a temporal VAE into pixel-space frames.

## Method

MimicMotion extends Stable Video Diffusion with a lightweight PoseNet (stacked Conv2d+SiLU layers) that encodes pose sequences and injects them additively into the first U-Net convolution layer, while the reference image enters via CLIP cross-attention and VAE latent channel concatenation. The key novelty is confidence-aware pose guidance: instead of binary thresholding, each keypoint and limb's color is multiplied by its confidence score, so unreliable poses contribute less signal during both training and inference. A hand region enhancement mechanism amplifies the diffusion loss by 10× in bounding boxes around hands whose keypoints all exceed a confidence threshold, biasing the model toward accurate hand geometry. For long videos, a position-aware progressive latent fusion strategy assigns linearly varying blend weights to overlapping frames (closer to a segment's center → higher weight), replacing the uniform averaging used by MultiDiffusion/Lumiere and eliminating abrupt transitions at segment boundaries.

## Results

On the TikTok test set, MimicMotion achieves the best scores across all four metrics: FID-VID 9.3, FVD 594, SSIM 0.795, and PSNR 20.1. These represent substantial improvements over the strongest baseline (Moore) of 3.1 in FID-VID (25% lower) and 134 in FVD (18% lower), and over MagicPose by 0.019 in SSIM and 1.3 dB in PSNR. A user study with 36 participants confirms these gains, with near-unanimous preference for MimicMotion over MagicPose and Moore, and 75.5% preference over MuseV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimicMotion outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against MagicPose (Chang et al., 2023), the closest competitor on SSIM (0.776) and PSNR (18.8), MimicMotion leads by 0.019 SSIM and 1.3 dB PSNR, while also achieving a much lower FVD (594 vs. 916), indicating far better temporal coherence. Against Moore-AnimateAnyone, the best baseline on FID-VID (12.4) and FVD (728), MimicMotion improves FID-VID by 3.1 points and FVD by 134 points. Against MagicAnymate (Xu et al., 2024), the weakest baseline overall, the gap is largest: 6.9 in FID-VID and 254 in FVD. Against MuseV (Xia et al., 2024), which focuses on long-video generation, MimicMotion still leads on all four metrics (FID-VID 9.3 vs. 14.6, FVD 594 vs. 754, SSIM 0.795 vs. 0.766, PSNR 20.1 vs. 17.6). Qualitatively, the paper highlights that MagicPose shows abrupt temporal transitions, Moore exhibits unstable clothing textures, and MuseV struggles with consistent text rendering, whereas MimicMotion maintains stable frame-to-frame transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MimicMotion builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its pre-trained image-to-video backbone, extending it with a lightweight PoseNet for pose conditioning and CLIP features for identity preservation. For long video generation, it refines the latent fusion strategy introduced by MultiDiffusion and adapted to the temporal domain by Lumiere, replacing simple uniform averaging with a position-aware progressive weighting scheme. The key conceptual departure from prior pose-guided methods (AnimateAnyone, MagicAnimate, MagicPose) is the confidence-aware modulation of pose signals—encoding keypoint reliability into the guidance map's brightness rather than applying a hard threshold—and the targeted loss amplification for high-confidence hand regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confidence-aware pose guidance and hand region enhancement address two persistent, practically visible failure modes (hand distortion and temporal flickering) that have limited the real-world usability of pose-guided video generation. The progressive latent fusion provides a training-free, drop-in improvement for segment-boundary smoothness that could be adopted by other long-video diffusion pipelines. Limitations include training on only ~4,400 dancing videos and evaluation primarily on a single TikTok test split, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse motion categories, multi-person scenes, or substantially longer sequences. The cross-domain results (cartoons, animals) are promising but underexplored.
