## Problem

Conventional federated learning degrades under data heterogeneity and class imbalance, while existing clustered FL methods either require a-priori knowledge of the number of clusters, impose high communication overhead by forcing clients to evaluate all cluster models (IFCA), or are hypersensitive to hyperparameter tuning (CFL). No prior approach efficiently groups clients using only lightweight loss signals while automatically determining the optimal cluster count and maintaining low communication cost.

## Input / Output

Each participating client transmits a vector of S scalar empirical loss values (one per local SGD iteration, typically S=8) alongside its updated model parameters to the cluster server; the loss vector is negligible in size relative to the model. The server produces cluster assignments (a partition of K clients into N_cl groups) and one personalized model per cluster. Clustering is triggered adaptively when the MSE of the interaction matrix drops below a threshold ε, and the number of clusters is selected automatically via Davies-Bouldin score rather than being fixed a priori.

## Method

FedGWC transforms each client's empirical loss trajectory into a Gaussian reward (Eq. 1) that measures proximity to the round's mean loss, then maintains a running average of these rewards as Gaussian weights γ_k via a Robbins-Monro update. A K×K interaction matrix P is incrementally updated from these weights to encode pairwise distributional similarity, from which Unbiased Perception Vectors (UPVs) are extracted and mapped through an RBF kernel to form a symmetric affinity matrix W. Spectral clustering is applied to W, and the optimal number of clusters is selected by minimizing the Davies-Bouldin score (rejecting splits if the minimum DB exceeds 1 to prevent over-splitting). The procedure is recursive: each resulting cluster independently continues training and can be further split, yielding a hierarchical partition. Theoretical results prove that the Gaussian weights converge almost surely to the true expected reward and that their variance is strictly smaller than the raw reward variance, while the interaction matrix entries are bounded and converge to 1.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (Dirichlet α=0.5, 100 clients), FedGWC achieves 53.4±0.4% balanced accuracy with 4 automatically detected clusters, outperforming IFCA (47.5±3.5%), CFL (41.6±1.3%), and FedAvg (41.6±1.3%). On large-scale real-world datasets, FedGWC reaches 57.4±0.3% on Google Landmarks (823 clients, 5 clusters) and 47.8±0.2% on iNaturalist (2,714 clients, 4 clusters), surpassing all standard FL baselines by 7–21 percentage points. FedGWC also achieves the best clustering quality (WAS 0.1, WADB 2.4) on CIFAR-100, indicating well-separated and cohesive clusters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGWC clearly outperforms IFCA (Ghosh et al., 2020) on CIFAR-100 (53.4% vs 47.5% accuracy) and both large-scale datasets (57.4% vs 49.4% on Landmarks; 47.8% vs 45.8% on iNaturalist), while IFCA edges out FedGWC by 0.6% on FEMNIST. Against FeSEM (Long et al., 2023), FedGWC matches accuracy on CIFAR-100 (53.4% vs 53.4%) but achieves dramatically better clustering quality (WADB 2.4 vs 38.4), and wins on all large-scale benchmarks. CFL (Sattler et al., 2020) fails to detect any clusters on CIFAR-100 (reducing to FedAvg-level 41.6%), while FedGWC identifies 4 clusters and gains 11.8 points. Among standard FL methods, FedGWC surpasses FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) by 11.8 points on CIFAR-100 and 16.9 points on Landmarks; it also beats FedAvgM (Asad et al., 2020) and FedProx (Li et al., 2020) on all datasets except FEMNIST, where FedAvgM's 83.3% exceeds FedGWC's 76.1%. On large-scale datasets, FairAvg (Michieli & Ozay, 2021) is outperformed by 18.4 points on Landmarks and 2.7 points on iNaturalist. The paper further demonstrates (Appendix J) that FedGWC is orthogonal to personalization methods pFedMe (T Dinh et al., 2020) and Per-FedAvg (Fallah et al., 2020), consistently boosting their performance when applied on top of FedGWC's detected clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGWC extends the clustered federated learning line initiated by CFL (Sattler et al., 2020) and IFCA (Ghosh et al., 2020), but departs from them in a key conceptual way: rather than using model updates or gradient norms to infer client similarity, it transforms individual empirical loss processes into Gaussian rewards that estimate distributional alignment. This loss-based approach is inspired by the biased client selection idea of Cho et al. (2022), but is repurposed here to build an interaction matrix and drive spectral clustering with automatic cluster-number selection via Davies-Bouldin scoring. The paper also introduces the Wasserstein Adjusted Score, a new evaluation metric derived from the Kantorovich-Rubinstein metric, which fills a gap in how clustering quality is assessed under class imbalance in FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGWC offers a practical, low-overhead clustering mechanism for cross-device FL that automatically determines the number of clusters without a-priori knowledge, addressing a key limitation of IFCA and FeSEM. The Wasserstein Adjusted Score provides the field with a theoretically grounded, class-imbalance-aware metric for comparing clustering algorithms, which was previously missing. Open questions include whether loss-based clustering remains robust under Byzantine or adversarial clients (the paper hints at this as future work), how the method scales to federations with tens of thousands of clients, and whether the spectral clustering step can be further accelerated for very large K. The orthogonality to any FL aggregation method makes it a composable building block, but its practical adoption will depend on whether the modest accuracy gains over strong baselines justify the additional server-side complexity in production systems.
