## Problem

Existing optimality-preserving reward shaping methods (PBIM, GRM, PIES) are provably unable to handle complex, exploration-heavy environments with long-duration episodes: PBRS-based methods require action-independent cumulative intrinsic returns and episodic structure, while PIES must eventually eliminate all shaping rewards. In Montezuma's Revenge, all three methods fail to outperform baseline RND, demonstrating that preserving the optimal policy set is insufficient if the agent cannot actually learn. The paper asks whether a broader class of optimality-preserving shaping functions exists that can accommodate action-dependent intrinsic returns without the restrictive assumptions of prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the agent's critic network estimates of four quantities—extrinsic value V_E, intrinsic value V_I, extrinsic action-value Q_E, and intrinsic action-value Q_I—alongside the raw intrinsic shaping reward F (e.g., RND novelty signal) at each timestep. It outputs a single adjusted scalar shaping reward F' = F + F_2 that is added to the extrinsic environment reward before the policy update. The critic networks must handle extrinsic and intrinsic rewards in separate heads, a standard practice in IM-based RL. The method is plug-and-play: no assumptions are made about the form of F, and it operates on any MDP without requiring episodic structure or future-agnosticity of the shaping function.

## Method

ADOPS directly enforces the two necessary conditions for optimality preservation (Equations 13–14) at each timestep by computing a correction term F_2 from critic estimates: if the current action is extrinsically suboptimal (Q_E < V_E), F_2 is capped at zero or negative to prevent the intrinsic reward from making it appear optimal; if the action is extrinsically optimal (Q_E ≥ V_E), F_2 is floored at zero or positive to ensure its combined intrinsic-extrinsic return matches that of other optimal actions. This mechanism allows cumulative intrinsic returns to depend on the agent's actions—something PBRS-based methods provably cannot accommodate—while still guaranteeing argmax_a Q_IE = argmax_a Q_E for all stable policies. The proof relies on a mild 'stability' assumption that the training algorithm converges only to locally optimal policies. A practical variant, ADOPES, multiplies F_2 by a coefficient that scales linearly from 0 to 1 over training, giving the agent full access to the base IM early on while gradually tightening the optimality constraint, unlike PIES which scales the IM itself to zero.

## Results

In Montezuma's Revenge with RND intrinsic motivation, ADOPES achieves the highest average cumulative extrinsic reward among all tested methods, outperforming the RND baseline to a statistically significant degree (p=0.038). PBIM (both normalized and non-normalized) fails entirely, never obtaining nonzero extrinsic rewards due to exponential reward explosion at episode end. GRM (D=1) and PIES both underperform the RND baseline, with GRM's deficit being statistically significant (p=0.009 normalized, p=0.031 non-normalized) and PIES's nearly so (p=0.059). ADOPS and ADOPES w/ F/2 also significantly outperform RND, with ADOPES being the best-performing method overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RND (Burda et al., 2019), the baseline intrinsic motivation method, ADOPES achieves a statistically significant improvement in average cumulative extrinsic reward (p=0.038), making it the first optimality-preserving method to outperform unconstrained IM in this environment. Against PBIM (Forbes et al., 2024a), ADOPS-based methods win decisively: PBIM saturates action probabilities immediately and never obtains any extrinsic reward, while all ADOPS variants achieve substantial extrinsic returns. Against GRM (Forbes et al., 2024b), ADOPS methods also win clearly; both normalized and non-normalized GRM (D=1) fail to match even the RND baseline, with statistically significant gaps (p=0.009 and p=0.031 respectively). Against PIES (Behboudian et al., 2022), ADOPES wins by a wide margin (p=4.4e-5); PIES initially performs well but degrades rapidly once its zeta coefficient begins scaling the intrinsic reward toward zero, and never recovers. The paper also notes that ADOPES with a halved initial IM coefficient (F/2) learns more quickly than standard ADOPES, suggesting that the early success of PIES was partly due to incidentally finding a better IM scaling factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of optimality-preserving reward shaping initiated by Ng et al. (1999) and developed through PBIM (Forbes et al., 2024a), GRM (Forbes et al., 2024b), and PIES (Behboudian et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is proving that the class of optimality-preserving shaping functions is strictly larger than what potential-based methods can express: ADOPS permits cumulative intrinsic returns to be action-dependent, whereas PBRS-based methods require them to be action-independent. It also removes the episodic-environment and future-agnosticity assumptions required by PBIM and GRM, and unlike PIES (which scales shaping to zero), ADOPS allows the agent to receive shaping rewards indefinitely while still preserving the optimal policy set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all three prior optimality-preserving methods (PBIM, GRM, PIES) fail to outperform plain RND in Montezuma's Revenge is practically important: it shows that theoretical optimality guarantees do not translate to effective learning in complex, long-episode, sparse-reward environments. The exponential reward explosion in PBIM/GRM for long episodes is a concrete failure mode that practitioners should be aware of. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single environment, a single IM method (RND), and a single RL algorithm (PPO), leaving open whether ADOPS generalizes to other sparse-reward domains, other intrinsic reward formulations, or different policy optimization algorithms. The stability assumption (Assumption 5.1) is reasonable but not trivially verifiable in practice, and its interaction with function approximation errors is only briefly addressed.
