## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for adapting image foundation models to video (e.g., AIM, DiST, EVL) process spatial and temporal information in separate branches, preventing patches at different spatial-temporal positions from interacting directly. Applying full attention over the entire spatial-temporal sequence would capture these interactions but incurs quadratic complexity that is prohibitive for multi-frame video. The paper asks how to achieve full spatial-temporal modeling within a pre-trained IFM using only a small number of trainable parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252836/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip sampled into T frames, each encoded by CLIP's patch embedding into a spatial-temporal token sequence of shape HWT×C (e.g., 16 frames at 224×224 resolution yields 16×14×14 tokens for ViT-B/16). The output is a single action class label obtained by averaging the final-layer token features and passing through a classification head. During training, a CLIP distillation loss is added alongside the classification loss to preserve the pre-trained feature space.

## Method

MoMa inserts a two-stage Divide-and-Modulate block into every CLIP Transformer layer while keeping all CLIP weights frozen. In the Divide stage, each frame is split into non-overlapping w×w windows (w=8) and the pre-trained CLIP attention is applied independently within each window, reducing attention complexity from O((HWT)²) to O(w²·HWT). In the Modulate stage, a lightweight Mamba SSM layer (with bidirectional scanning over spatial and temporal dimensions, following VideoMamba) processes the full spatial-temporal sequence and outputs two sequences y₁ and y₂ via a doubled output projection. The proposed SeqMod operation then combines them with the Divide output x as y₁⊙x + y₂ + x, extending the scalar scale/bias of adaptive normalization (FiLM/AdaN) to sequence-level tensors so that spatial-temporal information is injected as a fine-grained, position-wise modulation rather than a global scalar. Only the SSM layers are trainable, and the modulation design ensures the pre-trained CLIP features are adjusted rather than overwritten, preserving zero-shot transfer ability.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, MoMa with CLIP ViT-L/14 achieves 87.8% top-1 accuracy (98.0% top-5) using only 39M tunable parameters and 4152 GFLOPs, outperforming DiST (87.6%, 4534 GFLOPs) and AIM (87.3%, 5604 GFLOPs) while using ~25.6% fewer FLOPs than AIM. On Something-Something V2, where temporal modeling is critical, MoMa ViT-L/14 reaches 73.8% top-1, surpassing DiST (73.1%) and AIM (70.6%). On long-video benchmarks, MoMa (f64) attains 96.9% on Breakfast and 90.0% on COIN, exceeding VideoMamba (95.8%/89.5%). In zero-shot transfer, MoMa ViT-L/14 scores 59.1% on HMDB51 and 76.2% on UCF101, beating DiST (57.5%/74.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among PEFT baselines, MoMa ViT-L/14 outperforms DiST by 0.2% top-1 on K400 (87.8 vs 87.6) and by 0.7% on SSv2 (73.8 vs 73.1), while using ~8.5% fewer FLOPs; against AIM, the gains are larger (87.8 vs 87.3 on K400, 73.8 vs 70.6 on SSv2) with ~25.6% fewer FLOPs. Against full-parameter methods, MoMa ViT-L/14 surpasses X-CLIP (87.8 vs 87.1 on K400) and MViTv2-L (87.8 vs 86.1) while training only 39M parameters versus 420M and 218M respectively. On long-video benchmarks, MoMa (f64) beats VideoMamba by 1.1% on Breakfast (96.9 vs 95.8) and 0.5% on COIN (90.0 vs 89.5), and substantially outperforms non-end-to-end methods like Distant Supervision (96.9 vs 89.9 on Breakfast) and ViS4mer (96.9 vs 88.2). In zero-shot transfer, MoMa ViT-L/14 exceeds DiST by 1.6% on HMDB51 (59.1 vs 57.5) and 1.3% on UCF101 (76.2 vs 74.9), and surpasses X-CLIP by 14.5% and 4.2% on those datasets respectively. MoMa also outperforms all CNN-based baselines (MViT, MViTv2, VideoSwin, MTV) and earlier Transformer baselines (ViViT, TimeSformer, UniFormer) on both K400 and SSv2, with the largest margins on SSv2 where temporal modeling is most critical (e.g., 73.8 vs 62.4 for TimeSformer-L).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of image foundation models for video (AIM, DiST, EVL, ST-Adapter) and Mamba-based state space models for sequence modeling. Prior PEFT methods handle spatial and temporal information in separate branches, limiting cross-modal interaction, while prior Mamba-Transformer hybrids (Jamba, MambaVision, PoinTramba) are designed for from-scratch training and focus on layer-ratio architecture search. MoMa departs from both by using Mamba as a lightweight adapter *inside* a frozen pre-trained CLIP, and by introducing SeqMod—a sequence-level extension of the FiLM/AdaN modulation principle—that injects spatiotemporal information via learnable scale and bias tensors rather than directly modifying or concatenating features. The bidirectional scanning strategy is borrowed from VideoMamba, and the window-based Divide stage draws on the local-attention idea from Swin Transformers but applies it to reduce attention cost within a frozen CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoMa offers a practical recipe for adapting any Transformer-based IFM to video with minimal trainable parameters (~11M for ViT-B/16) and substantially lower FLOPs than competing PEFT methods, making it attractive for resource-constrained video understanding deployments. The SeqMod operation is a general-purpose mechanism for integrating auxiliary modules into pre-trained backbones without disrupting their learned feature distributions, which could transfer to other modalities or tasks beyond video. Limitations include evaluation only on CLIP ViT backbones, a fixed 2D window size, and modest absolute performance gains over the strongest baselines (DiST ViT-L/14 achieves 87.6 vs. MoMa's 87.8 on K400). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer IFMs (e.g., convolutional or diffusion-based), whether the window size can be learned adaptively, and how the method scales to very long or high-resolution video sequences beyond the 32-frame regime tested.
