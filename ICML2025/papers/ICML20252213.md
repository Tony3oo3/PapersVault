## Problem

Existing Byzantine-resilient federated learning algorithms either assume homogeneous data or require strong per-iteration gradient-difference bounds, making them inapplicable to vertically federated low-rank matrix learning where node data is inherently heterogeneous. This paper fills the gap by providing the first provably secure, communication-efficient algorithms with non-asymptotic guarantees for three partly-decoupled LR matrix problems (LRMC, LRCS, LRPR) under vertical federation, including the first non-asymptotic guarantee for the Krum estimator in this setting.

## Input / Output

In the vertical federation setting, L nodes each hold a disjoint column sub-matrix of the target n×q rank-r matrix X*, with at most 40% of nodes being Byzantine. For LRMC, each node observes a random subset of entries (probability p) in its column block via sparse 0-1 diagonal matrices A_k; for LRCS/LRPR, each node has dense Gaussian (or phaseless Gaussian) measurements of its columns. The algorithm outputs the shared left factor U (n×r, orthonormal columns) and local right factors B_ℓ (r×|S_ℓ|) at each node, jointly reconstructing X* = U*B*.

## Method

The core algorithm, Byz-AltGDmin, alternates between local least-squares minimization of B_k at each node and a global GD step on U using robust aggregation (Krum or GM) of the nr-dimensional node gradients. A key technical contribution is a filtering step that discards gradients whose application would violate row-incoherence of U—essential for LRMC convergence but unnecessary for LRCS/LRPR where only right-singular-vector incoherence is required. For initialization, a novel Subspace-Krum aggregates projection matrices UU^T rather than raw U matrices, since the latter can differ in sign and scale across nodes. The theoretical analysis requires only a bounded heterogeneity assumption G_B on the column sub-matrices of X* (rather than per-iteration gradient-difference bounds), yielding exponential convergence to a floor of 14Cκ̃²G_B in subspace distance with per-node sample complexity O(r²q̃ log q̃ log(1/ε)).

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2.2) shows that Krum-AltGDmin achieves exponential convergence of the subspace distance SD_F(U*, U_T) ≤ max(ε, 14C₁κ̃²G_B) with sample complexity nq̃p ≥ Cκ̃¹⁰μ²q̃r²log(q̃)log(1/ε) per node, under ≤40% Byzantine nodes. Empirically, both Krum-AltGDmin and GM-AltGDmin converge under a Reverse Gradient Attack with L_byz=8 out of L=20 nodes on synthetic LRMC (n=1000, q=500, r=3) and LRCS (n=1000, m=50, q=1000, r=3) problems, while CWMedian-AltGDmin fails to converge in these synthetic settings due to its prohibitively high sample complexity. On the real-world MovieLens 1M dataset (n=6040, q=3940, r=3, p≈0.042), all three methods converge, with Byz-AltGDmin-LRMC successfully recovering the matrix even with 40% Byzantine nodes. Increasing the heterogeneity parameter C (from 1 to 6) causes the subspace distance to saturate at a higher level, consistent with the theoretical bound involving G_B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three robust aggregation strategies applied to the AltGDmin framework: Krum, Geometric Median (GM), and Coordinate-wise Median (CWMed). Krum-AltGDmin and GM-AltGDmin share identical sample complexity (r²q̃ log q̃ log(1/ε)) and communication cost (nr log(1/ε)), but Krum is an exact algorithm while GM can only be approximated (e.g., via Weiszfeld's algorithm), making Krum faster in practice. CWMedian-AltGDmin has the lowest center compute cost (nr²L log(L) log(1/ε)) but requires a sample complexity of order rq̃√(n log q̃ log nr log(1/ε)), which is unreasonably high for the synthetic experiments (n=1000, q̃=25), causing it to fail to converge. On the larger MovieLens 1M dataset where nq̃p is sufficiently large, CWMedian does converge, confirming that its failure in synthetic settings is purely a sample-complexity issue. The paper also shows that increasing data heterogeneity (parameter C in Assumption 3) degrades the final subspace distance for all methods, with the saturation level matching the theoretical 14C₁κ̃²G_B bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AltGDmin framework (Abbasi & Vaswani, 2024) for federated low-rank matrix completion by adding Byzantine resilience through Krum and Geometric Median aggregation, while also extending the approach to LR column-wise sensing and LR phase retrieval. The closest prior work (Singh & Vaswani, 2024b) handles only the horizontally federated (homogeneous) LRCS setting, which avoids the incoherence-maintenance challenge that arises in the vertically federated (heterogeneous) setting studied here. A key technical departure is the introduction of a novel filtering step that removes non-incoherent gradients before Krum aggregation, which is necessary because the robust aggregate itself may not preserve the row-incoherence property required by the LRMC analysis. The Subspace-Krum initialization technique borrows from Singh & Vaswani (2024c), and the Krum guarantee is derived by modifying the asymptotic argument in Blanchard et al. (2017) into a non-asymptotic probabilistic form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides what may be the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee for the Krum estimator in a non-convex federated setting, which is notable given Krum's widespread practical use despite the absence of such guarantees. The bounded-heterogeneity assumption (G_B) is a practical limitation: convergence is only guaranteed up to a floor proportional to the heterogeneity level, and the required sample complexity (order n r² log q) is substantial. Open questions include whether the filtering step can be replaced by a lighter mechanism, whether the results extend to more than 40% Byzantine nodes, and whether the framework applies to other partly-decoupled problems beyond the three studied here. The practical impact is relevant to secure federated recommender systems, dynamic MRI, and Fourier ptychography, but the theoretical scope remains narrow.
