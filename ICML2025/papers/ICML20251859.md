## Problem

Decomposing a composite graphic design (e.g., a poster) into its constituent ordered layers with full metadata remains largely unsolved. Existing approaches—image segmentation, single-layer inpainting, and SVG vectorization—either fail to recover occluded regions, cannot handle multiple simultaneous layers, or lose complex visual and textual detail. No prior method jointly predicts layer structure, spatial metadata, and pixel-accurate RGB-A content for all layers in a single pass.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single composite poster image (processed at 336×336 for the visual encoders) and optionally a text prompt or point coordinates for selective layer extraction. It outputs an ordered sequence of RGB-A layers (each 192×192 or 128×128 depending on semantic density) accompanied by per-layer JSON metadata including bounding-box coordinates (x, y, w, h), layer order index, text content, font attributes, color values, and VQ-GAN codebook indices for image layers. Re-rendering the layers in the predicted order should reconstruct the original composite image.

## Method

DeaM is a unified multimodal model combining a conjoined visual encoder (CLIP + DINOv2 features concatenated along the channel dimension to capture both high-level semantics and low-level decorative patterns), an InternLM2-7B language model backbone, and a condition-aware RGB-A decoder. Image layers are tokenized by a modified RGB-A VQ-GAN (4-channel, with weighted reconstruction loss favoring RGB over alpha) at f=16 downsampling, producing 144 or 64 discrete tokens per layer that are injected as special vocabulary tokens into the LLM. The LLM autoregressively generates a JSON structure per layer containing spatial metadata and VQ codebook indices, with learnable newline tokens appended after each row of indices to regularize the sequence length. The condition-aware decoder then reconstructs pixel-accurate RGB-A images by conditioning on the corresponding crop region of the input image, explicitly learning an occlusion mask to focus generation on obscured areas while suppressing irrelevant foreground information in those regions. Training proceeds in three decoupled phases: VQ-GAN pre-training, instruction tuning (including GPT-4-generated open-ended data), and decoder fine-tuning with a frozen VQ encoder.

## Results

On the Crello test set (~2,000 images), DeaM achieves a FID of 70.629 for composite image reconstruction, substantially outperforming the OCR+matting+inpainting baseline (FID 99.603). For layer position prediction (mean IoU via Hungarian matching), DeaM scores 0.7128 versus 0.7069 for the baseline. The ablation study reveals that the condition-aware RGB-A decoder contributes the largest single improvement (FID drops from 95.488 to 70.629), while the conjoined visual encoder (CLIP + DINOv2) and prediction-regularity strategy each provide incremental gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is against a custom baseline that chains OCR, image matting, and inpainting (via the Volcano Engine API) to produce at most three layers (background, primary imagery, text). DeaM reduces FID by roughly 29 points (99.603 → 70.629) while also decomposing into more than three layers, and marginally improves position accuracy (0.7128 vs. 0.7069). The second comparison is qualitative, against LIVE (Ma et al., 2022), a layer-wise SVG vectorization method shown in Figure 7; DeaM handles complex natural-image posters more faithfully, whereas LIVE performs well on simple logo-style graphics but degrades on detailed photographic content. No head-to-head quantitative benchmark against LIVE or other unified multimodal models is provided, which limits the strength of the comparative evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of layered image decomposition work (Monnier et al., 2021; Sbai et al., 2018; Du et al., 2023) by reframing it as a unified understanding-and-generation task within a large multimodal model, rather than a purely generative or segmentation problem. It repurposes VQ-GAN (Esser et al., 2021) as an RGB-A tokenizer whose codebook indices become autoregressive tokens for an LLM backbone (InternLM2-7B), and pairs this with a condition-aware decoder that leverages the original composite image to recover occluded regions. The conjoined visual encoder (CLIP + DINOv2) and the instruction-tuning data pipeline (text-guided, point-guided, GPT-4-generated) draw on standard LMM design patterns from LLaVA and Shikra, but are applied here to the structurally demanding problem of multi-layer decomposition with metadata.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practically relevant task (decomposing composite graphic designs into editable RGB-A layers with metadata) and a large-scale dataset (CreatiLD, 224K images) that could catalyze research in design-aware image editing and content reuse. However, the evaluation is limited to a single test set (Crello, ~2K images) and a weak baseline (OCR + inpainting + matting), with no quantitative comparison against other UMMs or modern segmentation/inpainting pipelines; the absolute FID of ~70 and near-identical localization scores (0.707 vs. 0.713) suggest substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex or non-poster designs, how to improve text-layer fidelity (font prediction is acknowledged as weak), and whether the VQ-GAN tokenization bottleneck can be overcome with more powerful image tokenizers.
