## Problem

Prior work on private model personalization (Jain et al., 2021) required centralized processing, assumed standard Gaussian features, and suffered from a privacy error term scaling as d³k². This paper asks whether one can achieve private shared-representation personalization in a naturally federated setting under broader sub-Gaussian distributions and noisy labels, while improving the privacy-utility trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252227/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of n users holds m data points (x, y) with x ∈ R^d drawn from a sub-Gaussian distribution with identity covariance and y generated as x^T U* v_i* + ζ (regression) or y ∈ {-1,1} (classification). The algorithm outputs a shared embedding U^priv ∈ R^{d×k} (k ≪ d) broadcast to all users under the billboard model, plus user-specific vectors v_i^priv ∈ R^k computed locally on a held-out data split. For classification, a Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection M ∈ R^{k'×d} is applied first, so the effective embedding learned is M^T Ũ ∈ R^{d×k} with k' = O(r²Γ² log(nm/δ)/ρ²), independent of d.

## Method

The core algorithm (Private FedRep) alternates between two steps per iteration: (1) each user locally solves argmin_v L̂(U_t, v; B_{i,t}) on a disjoint batch to obtain v_{i,t}, then computes a gradient ∇_{i,t} w.r.t. U on a second disjoint batch; (2) the server aggregates clipped gradients, adds Gaussian noise calibrated via z-CDP composition, and re-orthonormalizes via QR decomposition. The use of disjoint batches yields tighter Frobenius-norm bounds on gradients, reducing required noise. A private initialization step applies a noisy top-k SVD to a second-moment estimator to guarantee dist(U_0, U*) < 1. For binary classification, an information-theoretic construction applies the JL transform to reduce dimensionality, then runs the exponential mechanism over a Frobenius-norm γ-cover of the projected embedding space, yielding a margin-based population risk bound independent of d.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 10) establishes an excess population risk bound of Õ((R²+Γ²)Γ⁴Λ²d²k/(n²ε²σ²_min,*λ²) + (R²+Γ²)Γ⁴Λ²d/(nmσ⁴_min,*λ²)) + R²k/m for the private federated regression algorithm, improving the privacy error term over Jain et al. (2021) by a factor of Õ(dk) in natural parameter regimes. For binary classification (Theorem 17), the paper achieves a margin-based population loss bound of Õ(r²Γ²k/(nερ²) + √(r²Γ²/(mρ²))) that is independent of the data dimension d. In the synthetic experiment (n=20,000, d=50, k=2, m=10, R=0.01), Algorithm 1 achieves lower population MSE than Priv-AltMin across all tested privacy parameters ε∈[1,8].

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Priv-AltMin (Jain et al., 2021), the proposed Private FedRep algorithm improves the privacy error term by a factor of Õ(dk) under the same parameter regime (condition number Õ(1)), reducing the bound from Õ(d³k²/(n²ε²σ⁴_min,*)) to Õ(d²k/(n²ε²σ⁴_min,*)). The synthetic experiment in Figure 1 confirms this advantage empirically: Algorithm 1 achieves consistently lower population MSE than Priv-AltMin across all privacy levels ε∈[1,8] with n=20,000, d=50, k=2, m=10. Additionally, the paper relaxes the data assumption from standard Gaussian (required by Jain et al.) to sub-Gaussian distributions, and operates in a federated setting rather than requiring centralized processing. Compared to non-private FedRep (Collins et al., 2021), the proposed method adds user-level DP guarantees and extends the analysis to handle noisy labels, which the original FedRep analysis does not cover. The classification result (Theorem 17) has no direct prior comparison in the private personalization literature, as it is described as the first margin-based population loss bound independent of data dimension d in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FedRep algorithm (Collins et al., 2021) from non-private federated personalization to the user-level DP setting, and improves upon the first private personalization algorithm (Jain et al., 2021) in three key ways: it operates in a federated rather than centralized architecture, relaxes Gaussian feature assumptions to sub-Gaussian distributions, and handles noisy labels. For initialization, it adapts the subspace recovery estimator of Duchi et al. (2022) with Gaussian noise and top-k SVD. For the classification setting, it repurposes the Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform and exponential mechanism—techniques well-established in private learning (Bassily et al., 2022; Lê Nguyen et al., 2020)—to obtain dimension-independent margin-based risk bounds, a combination not previously applied to shared-representation personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that private model personalization need not be centralized, which is practically significant for federated learning deployments where a single server processing all data is infeasible. The O(dk) improvement in the privacy error term and the dimension-independent classification bound are meaningful theoretical advances, but the identity-covariance assumption and restriction to quadratic loss limit immediate applicability to real-world heterogeneous data. Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending to arbitrary covariance matrices (via adaptive preconditioning) and generalizing beyond least-squares to logistic or hinge losses, both of which are important for broader adoption.
