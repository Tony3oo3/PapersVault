## Problem

Existing de novo peptide design methods either require stable 3D target structures (precluding disordered/dynamic targets) or are restricted to the 20 canonical amino acids (excluding non-natural amino acids and cyclic modifications). Furthermore, discrete generative models lack effective multi-objective guidance mechanisms that can simultaneously optimize conflicting therapeutic properties without gradient estimation or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252343/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more target protein sequences (not 3D structures) and a user-selected set of K property objectives (e.g., binding affinity, membrane permeability, solubility, hemolysis, non-fouling) as conditioning signals. It outputs a Pareto-optimal set of peptide SMILES strings—enabling representation of non-natural amino acids and cyclic backbone modifications—each annotated with K-dimensional property scores from trained classifiers/regressors. The output is a non-dominated set rather than a single sequence, preserving the trade-off landscape across objectives.

## Method

PepTune builds on the Masked Discrete Language Model (MDLM) framework with a RoFormer backbone, introducing two training-time innovations: (1) a bond-dependent masking schedule where peptide-bond tokens follow a log-polynomial schedule (slower masking) while side-chain tokens follow log-linear, with a formally derived NELBO and reverse posterior that increases structural validity; and (2) a differentiable invalid-peptide loss that penalizes argmax-decoded sequences failing a SMILES-to-peptide filter. At inference, Monte Carlo Tree Guidance (MCTG) reformulates MCTS for discrete diffusion: it maintains a growing Pareto-optimal set, selects children via non-dominated selection score vectors balancing cumulative classifier rewards and exploration, expands nodes using Gumbel-noise batched sampling from the reverse posterior for diversity, rolls out to clean sequences scored by property classifiers, and backpropagates per-objective reward vectors (fraction of Pareto set beaten) to guide subsequent iterations—all without gradient estimation or model retraining.

## Results

PepTune achieves 100% validity and 100% uniqueness on generated peptide SMILES with a diversity score of 0.677 and SNN of 0.486, while the unconditional PepMDLM baseline reaches only 45% validity. For property prediction, PepTune's classifiers outperform PeptideBERT on F1 for hemolysis (0.846 vs 0.483), solubility (0.660 vs 0.597), and non-fouling (0.768 vs 0.699). In docking validation, PepTune-generated GLP-1R binders score −7.4 and −7.0 kcal/mol, surpassing semaglutide (−5.7) and liraglutide (−5.1), and dual-target TfR/GLAST binders achieve scores as low as −10.5 and −9.2 kcal/mol respectively. Validity reaches 100% after only 20 MCTS iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PeptideBERT (Guntuboina et al., 2023), PepTune's property classifiers win decisively on F1 for hemolysis (0.846 vs 0.483, a 75% relative improvement) and solubility (0.660 vs 0.597), and also on non-fouling F1 (0.768 vs 0.699), though PeptideBERT retains an edge on non-fouling accuracy (0.873 vs 0.766). Against HELM-GPT (Xu et al., 2024), the unconditional PepMDLM generator achieves higher uniqueness and diversity with a lower SNN, indicating broader coverage of the valid peptide SMILES subspace. In the GLP-1R docking comparison, PepTune-generated short peptides (−7.4, −7.0 kcal/mol) outperform the clinically approved agonists semaglutide (−5.7) and liraglutide (−5.1) by 1.3–2.3 kcal/mol while binding to overlapping motifs. For dual-target TfR/GLAST binding, all seven selected PepTune binders achieve docking scores ≤ −7.5 kcal/mol on both targets simultaneously, with the top binder reaching −10.5 kcal/mol (TfR) and −9.2 kcal/mol (GLAST), demonstrating that multi-objective MCTG guidance avoids significant property trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PepTune builds on the Masked Diffusion Language Model (MDLM) framework (Sahoo et al., 2024) and extends state-dependent masking (Shi et al., 2024) with a bond-dependent log-polynomial schedule derived specifically for peptide SMILES structure, along with a novel invalid-loss that propagates discrete validity penalties to continuous logits. For guidance, it reformulates the MCTS framework (Coulom, 2007) into Monte Carlo Tree Guidance (MCTG), an inference-time multi-objective algorithm that operates entirely in the discrete token space without gradient estimation or model retraining. This departs from prior discrete diffusion guidance methods (classifier-based, classifier-free, denoising posterior prediction) that struggle with conflicting objectives and data sparsity, and from structure-based binder design (RFdiffusion, BindCraft) that requires 3D target structures, as well as from sequence-based peptide generators (PepMLM, HELM-GPT) restricted to the 20 canonical amino acids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PepTune opens a path toward de novo design of chemically modified and cyclic peptides for disordered or conformationally diverse targets without requiring 3D structures, while simultaneously optimizing multiple therapeutic properties (affinity, permeability, solubility, hemolysis, non-fouling) through a modular, inference-time Pareto search. The MCTG framework is general and could transfer to other discrete sequence generation tasks (DNA, protein, code) where multi-objective guidance is needed. Key open questions include: all validation is computational (Vina docking, classifier scores) with no wet-lab confirmation of binding or function; the MCTS search cost scales with the number of objectives and iterations; and it remains unclear whether the bond-dependent masking and invalid loss generalize to other structured molecular representations beyond peptide SMILES.
