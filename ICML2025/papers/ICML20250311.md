## Problem

Standard post-training quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ) suffer severe accuracy degradation when applied to Mixture-of-Experts LLMs because they ignore two inherent imbalances: inter-expert imbalance, where domain-specific calibration sets produce long-tailed sample distributions across experts, and intra-expert imbalance, where the gating mechanism assigns varying affinity scores to tokens that are uniformly treated during Hessian computation and error minimization. This leads to overfitting on calibration data and significant loss of generalization and reasoning capability after quantization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-MoE-16B, Qwen-MoE-14B, Mixtral-8x7B) and a target bit-width (4-bit or 3-bit) as input, and produces per-channel symmetric uniform quantized weights. Notably, the calibration data is entirely self-generated by the model (data-free), requiring no external datasets. The framework is plug-and-play: it wraps around existing PTQ backends (GPTQ, AWQ, or QuaRot+GPTQ) without modifying their core quantization algorithms, only altering the calibration set construction and the Hessian/loss computation.

## Method

MoEQuant introduces two complementary techniques. First, Expert-Balanced Self-Sampling (EBSS) generates calibration sequences by self-sampling from the model's vocabulary, using a joint objective that minimizes perplexity (via cumulative log-probability) while maximizing expert balance (via standard deviation of expert usage frequencies). A probability-guided path pruning strategy retains only w top-scoring branches at each step, reducing search complexity from O(m^n) to O(wn). Second, Affinity-Guided Quantization (AGQ) incorporates gating-network affinity scores into the quantization process: for AWQ-style methods, the quantization loss is reweighted by per-token affinity (L = Σ c_i ‖Wx_i − Ŵx_i‖²); for GPTQ-style methods, the Hessian is computed as H = (X·√c)(X·√c)ᵀ, so tokens with higher routing probabilities exert proportionally greater influence on sensitivity scores and weight updates. Together, these address the two imbalances and are orthogonal to the underlying PTQ algorithm.

## Results

On 4-bit weight quantization, MoEQuant++ (built on QuaRot+GPTQ) achieves the best average accuracy across all three MoE models: 49.59 on Qwen-MoE-14B, 40.01 on DeepSeek-MoE-16B, and 55.58 on Mixtral-8x7B, outperforming the strongest baseline (QuaRot+GPTQ) by 0.59, 1.00, and 2.16 points respectively. The most dramatic gains appear on reasoning tasks: on DeepSeek-MoE-16B, MoEQuant++ recovers HumanEval to 25.00 (vs. 21.34 for GPTQ) and GSM8K to 19.18 (vs. 11.60 for GPTQ). On Mixtral-8x7B, MoEQuant++ achieves 61.79 on GSM8K and 32.15 on HumanEval, approaching the full-precision scores of 65.88 and 32.93. The method also delivers 3.2–3.7× memory savings and 1.17–2.08× inference speedup over FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoEQuant++ (on QuaRot+GPTQ) consistently outperforms all baselines in average accuracy across all three models. Against GPTQ, the gains are most pronounced on Mixtral-8x7B (+6.74 avg) and DeepSeek-MoE-16B (+2.63 avg), driven primarily by large recoveries on GSM8K (+9.58 and +7.58 points) and HumanEval (+7.76 and +3.66 points). Against AWQ, MoEQuant+ (the AWQ-based variant) wins by 1.75, 0.45, and 2.55 points on Qwen, DeepSeek, and Mixtral respectively, with the largest single-task gap on Mixtral GSM8K (50.66 vs. 38.67). Against QuaRot+GPTQ, MoEQuant++ provides a steady 0.59–2.16 point improvement, with the clearest advantage on Mixtral GSM8K (61.79 vs. 57.92) and HumanEval (32.15 vs. 27.60). Against OmniQuant, MoEQuant++ leads by 1.28, 0.29, and 1.02 points on the three models, though OmniQuant remains competitive on perplexity. Notably, GPTQ achieves the lowest WikiText2 PPL (due to calibration overfitting) but generalizes poorly on C4 and downstream tasks, whereas MoEQuant achieves both lower C4 PPL and higher task accuracy simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) lineage—GPTQ's Hessian-based error compensation, AWQ's activation-aware smoothing, and QuaRot's Hadamard rotation—by identifying that these methods, designed for dense LLMs, fail on MoE architectures due to two structural imbalances: uneven sample distribution across experts and varying token-expert affinities from gating. Rather than proposing a new quantization algorithm, MoEQuant introduces two orthogonal, plug-and-play modifications: Expert-Balanced Self-Sampling (EBSS) for calibration data generation and Affinity-Guided Quantization (AGQ) for incorporating gating weights into the quantization loss and Hessian. This positions the work as a bridge between existing PTQ techniques and MoE-specific requirements, composable with any underlying quantizer (GPTQ, AWQ, QuaRot+GPTQ).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE models (DeepSeek-V3, Mixtral, Qwen-MoE) become the dominant architecture for efficient scaling, their deployment is bottlenecked by the need to keep all experts in memory; MoEQuant's 3.2× memory reduction and 1.2× speedup make these models viable on consumer GPUs. The paper's finding that standard PTQ methods catastrophically degrade reasoning capabilities (HumanEval, GSM8K) on MoE models—while appearing fine on perplexity—highlights a critical gap in the quantization literature. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large MoE models with hundreds of experts (e.g., DeepSeek-V3's 256 experts per layer), whether it extends to activation quantization, and whether the self-sampling calibration strategy remains effective as model scale and expert count grow.
