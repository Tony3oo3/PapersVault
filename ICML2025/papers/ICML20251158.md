## Problem

PPO-based RL finetuning of LLMs relies on value networks to estimate intermediate state values for credit assignment, but the paper shows these critics produce biased estimates that barely outperform random baselines in ranking candidate reasoning steps. This raises the question of whether improved credit assignment—rather than discarding it as in GRPO or RLOO—can meaningfully enhance RL training of LLMs on reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem prompt and generates a step-by-step reasoning response, with a binary reward (1/0) applied only at the terminal token based on final-answer correctness. Credit assignment operates at the reasoning-step granularity rather than the token level: tokens within a reasoning step are grouped and share a single advantage value, trading granularity for computational efficiency. Intermediate states are defined as the concatenation of the prompt and all previously generated tokens, and the MC rollouts are generated by re-feeding this partial context to the current policy.

## Method

VinePPO replaces PPO's learned value network with unbiased Monte Carlo value estimation, exploiting the fact that language environments allow resetting to any intermediate state by simply re-feeding the partial context. For each state s_t in a training trajectory, the method samples K auxiliary rollouts from that state under the current policy and averages their returns to obtain V̂_MC(s_t), which is then plugged into the standard advantage formula. The auxiliary rollouts are used exclusively for value estimation and do not contribute to policy gradient updates. The rest of the PPO framework—clipped policy updates, KL penalty against the reference SFT model, and all hyperparameters—remains unchanged, isolating the effect of refined credit assignment. Increasing K reduces estimator variance and consistently improves both accuracy and wall-clock efficiency, as each iteration becomes more effective despite being slower.

## Results

VinePPO consistently outperforms standard PPO, GRPO, RLOO, RestEM, and DPO+ on Pass@1 accuracy across both MATH and GSM8K datasets with two model sizes (DeepSeekMath 7B and RhoMath 1.1B). Despite slower per-iteration runtime (up to 2× for 7B, 5× for 1.1B), VinePPO reaches PPO's peak accuracy in ~3.0× less wall-clock time (RhoMath 1.1B) and ~1.51× less time (DeepSeekMath 7B), requiring 9× and 2.8× fewer gradient steps respectively. VinePPO also achieves the steepest generalization slope (highest test accuracy for a given training accuracy) and a better KL-divergence trade-off, while PPO's value network achieves only 65% value prediction accuracy and performs near chance in ranking candidate next steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PPO (the main baseline), VinePPO wins on every metric: higher peak Pass@1 accuracy, fewer gradient steps to reach that peak, less total wall-clock time, lower KL divergence at matched accuracy, and steeper generalization slope. Against GRPO and RLOO (which discard credit assignment entirely and use group-average baselines), VinePPO achieves higher test accuracy on both MATH and GSM8K, with the gap widening on the harder MATH dataset. Against RestEM (iterative rejection finetuning), VinePPO outperforms throughout training, while RestEM overfits near the end of training. Against DPO+ (DPO-Positive), VinePPO also achieves higher final accuracy. The paper notes that the performance gap between VinePPO and PPO is most pronounced on MATH (harder) versus GSM8K (easier), and that VinePPO is more robust to higher sampling temperatures (T=1.0) where PPO diverges.

## Relation to Prior Work

VinePPO builds directly on PPO's actor-critic framework for LLM finetuning (Ouyang et al., 2022) but replaces the learned value network with unbiased Monte Carlo value estimates, reviving the 'Vine' variant of TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015) that was previously limited to environments supporting intermediate state resets. The key enabling observation is that language generation, formulated as an MDP, uniquely allows such resets by simply refeeding partial context to the policy. The paper positions itself against credit-assignment-free baselines (GRPO, RLOO, DPO, RestEM) that discard fine-grained credit assignment entirely, and is explicitly distinct from MCTS-based approaches (AlphaGo/AlphaZero lineage) that use tree search at inference time rather than MC samples for training-time value estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete diagnosis for why simplified methods (GRPO, RLOO) that abandon credit assignment perform comparably to PPO: the value network is fundamentally broken, not merely undertrained, performing near chance at ranking candidate reasoning steps despite high explained variance. This reframes the design space for RL-based LLM post-training—rather than discarding credit assignment, one can fix it at modest additional compute cost. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-binary rewards, non-math reasoning tasks, and whether the per-iteration MC overhead becomes prohibitive at larger model scales or longer reasoning chains. The finding that better credit assignment steepens the generalization slope (more test accuracy per training sample) is particularly relevant given the scarcity of high-quality verifiable reasoning data.
