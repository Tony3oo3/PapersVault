## Problem

The Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC) is the standard evaluation metric for selective classifiers, yet it has never been formally defined at the population level or equipped with finite-sample statistical guarantees. Prior work (Franc et al., 2023) introduced the SELE score as a lower-bound proxy to make AURC amenable to optimization, but this approximation was never justified by a rigorous analysis of the empirical AURC's bias, variance, or convergence behavior. The paper asks: can AURC itself be characterized as a well-defined population quantity with principled, efficient finite-sample estimators that are directly usable as a training loss?

## Input / Output

Given a finite i.i.d. sample of n (x, y) pairs, a classifier f producing k-dimensional logits, a confidence scoring function g mapping to [0,1] (e.g., MSP, Negative Entropy, MaxLogit), and a loss function (0/1 or cross-entropy), the method outputs a scalar AURC estimate computed in O(n log n) time via a single sort-and-cumsum pass. It also produces per-sample weight estimates α̂_i that depend only on each sample's rank r_i in the CSF-sorted ordering, and these weights are differentiable with respect to the model parameters, enabling the AURC estimate to serve directly as a training loss during fine-tuning with standard gradient descent.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form population AURC as E[α(x)ℓ(f(x),y)] where α(x) = −ln(1−G(x)) and G is the CDF of the CSF, showing AURC is a reweighted risk function whose weights integrate to one. Two plug-in estimators follow: α̂_i = H_n − H_{n−r_i} (harmonic-number based, derived via Monte Carlo integration over Beta-distributed order statistics) and α̂'_i = −ln(1−r_i/(n+1)) (log-based, obtained by applying Jensen's inequality to the former). The authors prove both weight estimators are consistent, derive explicit bias formulas and tight MSE bounds of order β_i/(n(1−β_i)+1), and establish a convergence rate of O(√ln(n)/n) for the full plug-in AURC estimator. They further show the SELE score's claimed upper-bound property (2×SELE ≤ AURC) does not hold in general, and demonstrate that the plug-in estimators can be used as differentiable losses to fine-tune pre-trained CNNs and transformers, achieving lower population AURC than both CE and SELE training.

## Results

Fine-tuning with the proposed plug-in estimators (α̂ and α̂') achieves population AURC_p values within 0.01–0.05×10⁻² of the SELE score across all six model architectures on CIFAR-10/100, while CE loss yields substantially higher AURC_p (e.g., ResNet18 on CIFAR-10: 4.967 for CE vs. 4.473 for α̂). As finite-sample estimators, both plug-in estimators converge to the population AURC_p with decreasing MAE and MSE as batch size grows from 8 to 1024, whereas SELE consistently underestimates and 2×SELE overestimates the population value. The theoretical convergence rate of O(√ln(n)/n) is empirically validated across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, and Amazon Reviews with multiple model architectures and confidence score functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the SELE score from Franc et al. (2023), which serves as a lower bound for empirical AURC. As an estimator, the proposed plug-in estimators (α̂ and α̂') consistently outperform SELE: SELE exhibits the largest bias among all weight estimators (Fig. 1), systematically underestimating the population AURC_p, while 2×SELE overestimates it. The plug-in estimators achieve lower MSE and MAE than SELE across all tested batch sizes (8–1024), model architectures (VGG, ResNet, WideResNet, ViT, Swin, BERT, RoBERTa), and confidence score functions (MSP, Negative Entropy, MaxLogit, Softmax Margin, MaxLogit-ℓ₂, Negative Gini). As a training objective, fine-tuning with α̂ or α̂' yields AURC_p values essentially matching SELE (within 0.01–0.05×10⁻²) while being theoretically grounded as consistent estimators, whereas CE loss produces noticeably higher AURC_p (e.g., 4.967 vs. 4.473 for ResNet18 on CIFAR-10). The paper also demonstrates that the claimed 2×SELE upper bound on empirical AURC (Franc et al., Theorem 8) does not hold, providing a counterexample where empirical AURC exceeds 2×SELE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AURC evaluation metric introduced by Geifman et al. (2019) for selective classifiers and the SELE score proposed by Franc et al. (2023) as a tractable lower bound for empirical AURC. It departs from prior work by formalizing the population-level AURC as a reweighted risk function with weights α(x) = -ln(1-G(x)), where G is the CDF of the confidence score, and by deriving plug-in estimators via Monte Carlo integration with explicit bias, MSE, and convergence-rate guarantees. A key conceptual departure is the demonstration that the SELE lower-bound approximation is unnecessary for optimization, since direct AURC estimation is equally amenable to gradient-based training at O(n log n) cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous statistical characterization of AURC estimators, filling a gap where the metric was used empirically without theoretical grounding. The counterexample showing that 2×SELE does not always upper-bound empirical AURC (contradicting Franc et al.'s Theorem 8) is a notable correction to the literature. Open questions include how these estimators behave under distribution shift, class imbalance, and in Bayesian model settings, all of which the authors flag as future directions. For practitioners, the practical takeaway is that AURC can now be used directly as a differentiable training objective with known convergence properties, removing the need for the SELE surrogate.
