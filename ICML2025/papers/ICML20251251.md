## Problem

Most time series forecasting models rely on point-wise MSE loss, which treats each time step independently and ignores structural dependencies such as correlation, variance, and mean. The paper demonstrates that predictions with nearly identical MSE can differ dramatically in directional alignment, fluctuation dynamics, and overall level, and that global structural metrics fail to capture local variations in non-stationary series. No existing loss function measures these structural properties at a localized, patch-level granularity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on multivariate time series with C channels: a lookback window of length L is fed to a backbone model to produce a forecast of length T. During training, the PS loss is computed between the predicted and ground-truth forecast sequences by first segmenting both into overlapping patches (length P, stride P/2) whose size is adaptively determined from the dominant frequency of the ground truth via FFT. The final training objective is L_MSE + λ·L_PS, so the backbone architecture and its input/output interface remain unchanged; only the loss computation is modified.

## Method

The PS loss comprises three components. (1) Fourier-based Adaptive Patching (FAP) identifies the dominant frequency of the ground truth via FFT, sets the patch length to P = min(⌊p/2⌋, δ) where p = ⌊T/f⌋, and segments both series into N overlapping patches with stride ⌊P/2⌋. (2) The patch-wise structural loss averages three per-patch terms: a correlation loss (1 − Pearson correlation), a variance loss (KL divergence between softmax-normalized deviations from the patch mean), and a mean loss (MAE of patch means). (3) Gradient-based Dynamic Weighting (GDW) sets each sub-loss weight inversely proportional to its L2 gradient norm with respect to the output layer, and further scales the mean-loss weight by two factors c and v that increase as correlation and variance alignment improve, so the mean term gains influence only after the other two are well-matched. The total loss is L_MSE + λ·L_PS, making PS loss a drop-in replacement that can be combined with any existing backbone.

## Results

PS loss achieves consistent improvements across five SOTA backbones (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimeMixer, DLinear, TimesNet) on seven datasets, outperforming MSE in 134 out of 140 evaluation cases. The largest gains appear on DLinear, with average MSE reductions of 5.22% and MAE reductions of 4.39% across all datasets and horizons. On iTransformer (the strongest backbone), PS loss reduces average MSE from 0.457 to 0.440 on ETTh1 and from 0.292 to 0.281 on ETTm2. Zero-shot experiments confirm generalization benefits, with PS loss outperforming MSE in 34 of 36 cross-dataset transfer scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS loss outperforms both TILDE-Q and FreDF in the majority of settings across ETTh1, ETTm1, and Weather. Against TILDE-Q, PS loss wins in 11 of 12 MSE comparisons and 12 of 12 MAE comparisons; TILDE-Q's only MSE advantage is on ETTh1-720 (0.521 vs. 0.480 for PS, where TILDE-Q actually degrades relative to plain MSE). Against FreDF, PS loss wins in 10 of 12 MSE comparisons and 11 of 12 MAE comparisons; FreDF's sole MSE edge is on ETTh1-336 (0.471 vs. 0.474), a margin of 0.003. The largest PS-loss advantages over FreDF appear on ETTm1-720 (0.472 vs. 0.486, a 2.9% MSE reduction) and Weather-96 (0.167 vs. 0.170). Overall, PS loss provides the most consistent and largest average improvements, particularly on longer horizons where structural alignment matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant practice of using point-wise MSE in time series forecasting by introducing structural similarity metrics (correlation, variance, mean) computed at the patch level. It conceptually extends the patching paradigm from model architectures (PatchTST, TimesNet) into the loss function itself, and builds on shape-focused losses (Soft-DTW, TILDE-Q) and frequency-domain losses (FreDF) by localizing the comparison to adaptively-sized patches determined via FFT. The gradient-based dynamic weighting strategy is borrowed from multi-task learning (GradNorm, uncertainty weighting), repurposed here to balance three structural loss terms. Unlike prior shape-focused losses that operate globally or require expensive warping, PS loss achieves localized structural alignment with modest computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PS loss offers a model-agnostic, plug-and-play improvement that consistently enhances forecasting across Transformer, MLP, CNN, and LLM-based architectures, suggesting that loss function design is an underexploited lever in time series forecasting. The observation that PS loss yields higher training error but lower test error points to a regularization effect from structural constraints, which could inform broader discussions on generalization in sequence prediction. Open questions include whether the FFT-based patching remains effective for highly non-stationary series with shifting periodicity, whether the approach scales to very long forecasting horizons, and whether the modest but consistent gains (typically 2–6% MSE reduction) compound when combined with other architectural innovations. The computational overhead (~30–35% per epoch) may limit adoption in resource-constrained settings.
