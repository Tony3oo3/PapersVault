## Problem

Standard gradient descent-ascent methods for minimax games require players to exchange strategies at every iteration, which is infeasible in resource-constrained distributed settings (e.g., drones with limited battery, corporate competitors with infrequent information sharing). Existing local-update approaches for federated minimax additionally require bounded cross-player gradient noise variance, an assumption that fails in realistic asymmetric-noise environments. This paper introduces the setting of intermittent strategy communication (ISC-games) and asks whether communication costs can be provably reduced by exploiting low player interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253246/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each player accesses a stochastic gradient oracle that provides low-variance estimates of their own gradient but potentially arbitrarily high-variance estimates of the opponent's gradient (only diagonal variance σ_uu², σ_vv² need be bounded). The method takes initial strategies (u₀, v₀), a step size γ, and a number of local steps K per round, and outputs an approximate saddle point after R communication rounds. The key output guarantee is a communication complexity bound of O(1/(1−4κ_c) · log(1/ε)) rounds in the weakly coupled regime, which is independent of the players' individual condition numbers κ_u and κ_v.

## Method

Decoupled SGDA lets each player perform K local gradient steps using only their own reliable gradient component and the opponent's strategy frozen at the start of the round (the 'reference point' x̄), rather than requiring the opponent's current parameters. The core technical contribution is introducing the coupling parameter L_c (and κ_c = L_c/μ) that quantifies the interaction level between players, separating it from the overall smoothness L. In the weakly coupled regime (κ_c ≤ 1/4), a novel proof technique exploiting this separation yields a per-round contraction of 4κ_c, giving communication complexity O(1/(1−4κ_c) log(1/ε)) that is independent of player conditioning—eliminating the κ_u + κ_v dependency that plagues GDA, EG/OGDA, and FOAM. For fully decoupled games (κ_c = 0), the method converges with zero communication. A heuristic extension, Ghost-SGDA, further accelerates convergence by predicting opponent strategies via a 'ghost sequence' of local updates.

## Results

For weakly coupled SCSC games (κ_c ≤ 1/4), Decoupled SGDA achieves communication complexity O(1/(1−4κ_c) · log(1/ε)), which is independent of player condition numbers κ_u and κ_v. In the fully decoupled limit (κ_c = 0), the method requires zero communication rounds, while GDA still needs O((κ_u+κ_v) log(1/ε)) rounds. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 and SVHN GAN training, Decoupled SGDA with K=5 local steps achieves lower FID scores than standard GDA (K=1) at the same number of communication rounds, and in noisy federated minimax settings it maintains performance while Local SGDA degrades significantly under imbalanced off-diagonal noise variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GDA (Lee et al., 2024), Decoupled SGDA always achieves better communication complexity in the weakly coupled regime since 1/(1−4κ_c) < κ_u+κ_v+κ_uv², and in the fully decoupled limit it requires zero rounds versus GDA's O((κ_u+κ_v) log(1/ε)). Against the optimal first-order method FOAM (Kovalev & Gasnikov, 2022), Decoupled SGDA can outperform it in communication rounds when κ_c ≤ ½√(1−1/√(κ_uκ_v)), a condition that recovers the weakly coupled regime as max{κ_u,κ_v}→∞. Compared to PEARL-SGD (Yoon et al., 2025), Decoupled SGDA achieves zero communication in the fully decoupled setting while PEARL-SGD still requires O(κ log(1/ε)) rounds. In noisy federated minimax experiments (Figure 4), Decoupled SGDA is robust to arbitrarily large off-diagonal noise variance (σ_uv, σ_vu) while Local SGDA / Federated Minimax (Deng & Mahdavi, 2021) degrades sharply, since Decoupled SGDA only requires bounded diagonal variance. In GAN training on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, Decoupled SGDA with K=5 achieves lower FID scores than GDA, Optimistic GDA (Popov, 1980), Alternating GDA, and Extragradient (Korpelevich, 1976) at the same number of communication rounds, with the gap widening as the interaction term decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient descent-ascent (GDA) framework for minimax optimization to a new setting—intermittent strategy communication (ISC-games)—where players update locally using outdated opponent strategies. It departs fundamentally from federated minimax methods (Deng & Mahdavi 2021, Sharma et al. 2022) by requiring only bounded noise on each player's own gradient rather than on cross-player gradients, and from concurrent work by Yoon et al. (2025, PEARL-SGD) by introducing the coupling parameter L_c that enables communication acceleration independent of player conditioning. The analytical framework builds on standard SCSC game assumptions but introduces a non-standard smoothness decomposition (L_u, L_v, L_uv, L_vu) that separates self-smoothness from interaction smoothness, a distinction absent from prior GDA/EG/OGDA analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'weakly coupled' regime (κ_c ≤ 1/4) where communication complexity decouples from player conditioning is practically significant for distributed and federated optimization, where communication is the bottleneck. The robustness to unbalanced noise (arbitrarily large σ_uv, σ_vu) addresses a real limitation of existing federated minimax methods. Open questions include extending theoretical guarantees to non-convex games, adapting K per player, developing privacy-preserving variants (since players never share gradients directly), and scaling the Ghost-SGDA heuristic beyond the current empirical validation on toy quadratic games and small GAN training runs.
