## Problem

Existing diffusion-based talking face generators tightly couple facial attributes (expression, head pose, lip movement) to audio cues, making it impossible to independently control emotion or pose without modifying the driving audio. GAN/NeRF-based methods achieve good lip-sync but fail to produce expressive, lifelike facial dynamics. The paper addresses this by decoupling facial attributes in a 3D implicit space and introducing explicit control over emotion and head pose.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static reference image (cropped to 256×256 via InsightFace landmarks, rendered at 512×512), a 16 kHz speech audio clip, and optionally a driving image for pose conditioning and an emotion label. Audio features are extracted with Wav2Vec2 and aligned to motion via a self-attention module. The output is a 25 fps talking face video where lip movements follow the audio, head pose follows the driving image (or is freely generated), and facial expression can be steered by the emotion condition.

## Method

Playmate is a two-stage framework built on LivePortrait's 3D implicit space (canonical keypoints, expression deformations, rotation, translation, scale). In stage 1, a motion-decoupled module combines VASA-1's pairwise pose/expression transfer loss with adaptive normalization (global statistics for expression, per-identity statistics for head pose) to train a diffusion transformer (Conformer + Pose-MLP + Exp-MLP) that generates disentangled motion sequences directly from audio. In stage 2, the diffusion transformer is frozen and a two-block DiT-based emotion-control module is trained to inject emotion conditions into the latent features before the Exp-MLP, enabling fine-grained emotion steering. Inference uses multi-condition classifier-free guidance with separate scales for audio (w_a) and emotion (w_e), and Gaussian noise augmentation on previous-frame motion representations mitigates error accumulation during incremental generation.

## Results

On HDTF, Playmate achieves the best FID (19.14 vs. 27.71 for MEMO, the second-best), FVD (231.05 vs. 288.39 for Hallo2), CSIM (0.848), and LPIPS (0.099), representing ~30% and ~20% improvements in FID and FVD over the next-best method respectively. On the collected dataset, Playmate again leads in FID (34.72), FVD (227.87), CSIM (0.797), and LPIPS (0.128). For lip synchronization, Playmate is second-best to Sonic on both Sync-C (8.58 vs. 9.14 on HDTF) and Sync-D (6.99 vs. 6.55 on HDTF), while still outperforming all other diffusion-based methods. In the emotion control evaluation (Table 2), Playmate achieves the highest Emo-A (0.550) and best FID (68.23) among all emotion-controllable talking head methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Playmate decisively outperforms all five main baselines (Hallo, Hallo2, MEMO, Sonic, JoyVASA) on video quality metrics (FID, FVD) and identity preservation (CSIM, LPIPS) across both HDTF and the collected dataset, with FID improvements of 8–15 points over the nearest competitor. The sole exception is lip synchronization: Sonic achieves the best Sync-C (9.14 on HDTF, 7.97 on collected) and Sync-D (6.55, 7.12), edging out Playmate (8.58/7.13 and 6.99/8.01), though the authors note Sonic is a purely audio-driven method with no independent pose or expression control. In the emotion control comparison (Table 2), Playmate surpasses EAMM, DreamTalk, EDTalk, and EAT on all four metrics, achieving Emo-A of 0.550 versus 0.460 (EDTalk) and 0.450 (EAT) for the next-best methods, while also delivering substantially better FID (68.23 vs. 95.09 for EAT). In the user study (Table 3), Playmate leads on Video Definition (3.857) and Visual Appeal (3.643), while Sonic edges ahead on Lip Sync (3.821 vs. 3.750) and Naturalness (3.750 vs. 3.464). JoyVASA, despite also using a 3D-implicit space, lags significantly behind Playmate on all metrics, suggesting that the motion-decoupled module and diffusion transformer training are critical to the quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Playmate builds directly on the 3D implicit face representation from LivePortrait and face-vid2vid, adopting their appearance/motion decomposition (canonical keypoints, expression deformations, rotation, translation, scale) as an intermediate latent space rather than generating pixels end-to-end. It departs from prior diffusion-based talking face methods (EMO, Hallo, MEMO, Sonic) that couple facial attributes to audio in a shared latent space, by introducing a motion-decoupled module with adaptive normalization and a pairwise transfer loss (borrowed from VASA-1) to explicitly separate head pose from expression. The emotion-control stage repurposes DiT blocks as a plug-in on top of a frozen audio-conditioned diffusion transformer, a two-stage freezing strategy not previously applied to talking face generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation in current diffusion-based talking face systems: the inability to independently adjust emotion or head pose without re-generating from audio. By grounding generation in a decoupled 3D implicit space, Playmate offers a template for multi-condition control (audio + pose image + emotion label) that could be extended to other controllable video generation tasks. However, the method remains face-region only, suffers from artifacts under extreme head rotations due to the implicit (non-explicit-mesh) representation, and the lip-sync metrics, while competitive, do not surpass purely audio-driven baselines like Sonic—suggesting a trade-off between controllability and raw synchronization fidelity that remains open.
