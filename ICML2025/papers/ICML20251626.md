## Problem

Existing multi-view classification methods assume all views are equally reliable and aligned with ground truth, but in practice views can produce conflicting predictions (e.g., one view indicates 'safe' while another indicates 'unsafe'). Standard belief fusion (BCF) in the Evidential MVC framework can then yield incorrect predictions with deceptively low uncertainty, because it cannot down-weight unreliable views on a per-instance basis. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a mechanism that assesses and discounts the reliability of each view's prediction individually before fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted multi-view feature vectors (or encoder outputs from ResNet-50/BERT for end-to-end training) for each instance, where each view has its own dimensionality (e.g., 20/59/40 for Scene15's GIST/PHOG/LBP views). Each view-specific evidential network outputs a Dirichlet parameter vector yielding belief masses and uncertainty; a separate referral network takes both the raw feature and the functional belief as concatenated input to produce a binomial trust opinion. The final output is a fused opinion (belief masses, uncertainty, and derived class label) after trust-discounted BCF combination across all views.

## Method

The core contribution is an instance-wise Probability-Sensitive Trust Discounting (TD) module built on Subjective Logic. For each view, a referral evidential network (parameterized separately from the functional network) produces a binomial opinion whose predictive probability serves as the Degree of Trust; the functional opinion's belief masses are then scaled by this trust score while uncertainty is inflated accordingly (Eq. 5), and the discounted opinions are fused via BCF. Training follows a four-stage algorithm: (1) warm-up the referral network using a Beta-distribution ACE loss targeting whether the functional prediction matches ground truth (with label smoothing η=0.9), (2) update the functional network on both per-view and fused-opinion losses, (3) adjust the referral network using the fused-opinion loss, and (4) fine-tune the functional network again. Two theoretical propositions are proved: TD maximizes the ground-truth belief mass in the fused opinion (given at least one correct view), and the fused uncertainty under TD is strictly greater than without discounting, properly reflecting inter-view conflict.

## Results

On six benchmark multi-view classification datasets, the proposed ETF method (with pseudo-view) achieves the best average Top-1 accuracy of 90.22%, outperforming the strongest baseline ETMC (88.74%) by 1.48% on average. The method also achieves the highest average Fleiss' Kappa of 0.66, indicating superior inter-view prediction consistency compared to all baselines (next best: CCML at 0.58). On the large-scale Food101 dataset (end-to-end training with ResNet-50 + BERT), ETF reaches 93.09% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing ETMC (92.49%) and CCML (92.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETF consistently outperforms all six baselines on average Top-1 accuracy. Against the closest pseudo-view baseline ETMC, ETF gains +2.94% on PIE (94.63 vs. 91.69) and +3.60% on Scene15 (82.01 vs. 78.41), with smaller margins on other datasets. Against the conflict-resolution pioneer ECML, ETF leads by a substantial +9.66% on Scene15 (82.01 vs. 64.34) and +3.23% on PIE, highlighting superior conflict handling. Even without pseudo-views, TF outperforms ETMC on three of six datasets (Caltech101: 95.26 vs. 94.41; PIE: 93.31 vs. 91.69; HMDB: 74.35 vs. 74.01), suggesting the trust-discounting mechanism itself provides meaningful gains. On Fleiss' Kappa, ETF achieves the best score on all six datasets (avg. 0.66), with a particularly dramatic improvement on HMDB where ETMC shows negative agreement (−0.15) but ETF recovers to 0.65. On the Food101 end-to-end experiment, ETF leads all methods by 0.60% over CCML and 0.60% over ETMC in Top-1 accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evidential Multi-view Classification framework (TMC/ETMC) by integrating a probability-sensitive Trust Discounting mechanism from Subjective Logic, operating at the instance level rather than the global level used in the original SL formulation. It departs from the conflict-resolution approach of ECML (Xu et al., 2024a), which uses a Degree of Conflict loss to harmonize views, by instead training a separate referral network that assesses per-view reliability and discounts functional opinions before Belief Constraint Fusion. The stage-wise training algorithm is a key architectural departure, as the authors show that naively incorporating the trust module into ETMC's training loop degrades performance, necessitating a warm-up phase and alternating updates between functional and referral networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: when views genuinely disagree (e.g., one sensor sees what another cannot), standard evidential fusion can produce confidently wrong predictions. The theoretical guarantees (Propositions 3.5–3.6) that trust discounting maximizes ground-truth belief mass and increases fused uncertainty under conflict are valuable for building trustworthy multi-view systems in safety-critical domains. Key limitations include the time cost of stage-wise training, the reliance on late fusion (no early cross-view interaction), and the observation that the AUROC for uncertainty can decrease on small datasets even as accuracy improves, suggesting the trust mechanism's calibration benefits are data-size-dependent. Open questions include whether the stage-wise protocol can be replaced by a single end-to-end objective and whether the approach extends to early-fusion or partially-overlapping view settings.
