## Problem

Pretrained language models (PLMs) initialized into Decision Transformers show strong performance in short-term offline RL environments but underperform compared to randomly initialized DTs in long-term environments. The specific mechanism by which NLP knowledge transfers to RL—and why it helps in some settings but hurts in others—remains unexplained. This paper identifies and characterizes the exact attention-head structure responsible for this asymmetry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of (returns-to-go, state, action) tuples from a trajectory, where each element is a numerical vector (e.g., 17-dim state for MuJoCo, 2-dim position for PointMaze). The GPT-2-small pretrained weights initialize the transformer layers, and the MoA gating network receives the input embedding vector to produce a softmax weight over all 12 attention heads per layer. The output is a single next-action vector predicted via squared-error regression against the ground-truth action in the offline dataset.

## Method

The paper defines Markov heads as attention heads whose W_q(W_k)^T product satisfies a Markov matrix condition (diagonal elements dominate off-diagonal by a factor r > ln(d−1)+1), causing extreme attention on the last input token. Theoretical proofs show this property is invariant under any random embedding initialization (Theorem 4.3) and cannot be eliminated by finite-time fine-tuning (Theorem 4.5), explaining why PLM-initialized DTs are locked into short-term planning bias. To overcome this, GPT2-DTMA introduces a Mixture of Attention (MoA) mechanism: a trainable linear gating network followed by softmax assigns input-dependent weights to each attention head's output, effectively treating heads as experts. This allows the model to suppress Markov head influence in long-term environments (e.g., PointMaze) while retaining it in short-term ones (e.g., MuJoCo Locomotion), adding only ~3% parameters over the base DT.

## Results

On short-term MuJoCo Locomotion tasks, GPT2-DTMA achieves 77.4 normalized score on Hopper-medium and 79.9 on Walker2d-medium, matching or exceeding GPT2-DT (77.9 and 77.1 respectively). On long-term PointMaze tasks, GPT2-DTMA reduces episode length to 203.0 on PointMaze-large, narrowing the gap with DT (195.3) compared to GPT2-DT's 257.3. The MoA mechanism adds only ~3% parameters over base DT while adaptively modulating Markov head influence across environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DT, GPT2-DTMA wins on most short-term tasks (e.g., Hopper-m: 77.4 vs 67.4, Walker2d-m: 79.9 vs 74.3) but loses on PointMaze-large (203.0 vs 195.3 episode length), confirming that Markov heads cannot be fully eliminated. Against CQL, GPT2-DTMA dominates on MuJoCo (Hopper-m: 77.4 vs 57.7) but CQL is stronger on PointMaze-large (162.0 vs 203.0). Against Decision ConvFormer (DC), GPT2-DTMA is slightly behind on Hopper-m (77.4 vs 79.7) but substantially ahead on PointMaze-medium (146.3 vs 236.3) and PointMaze-large (203.0 vs 288.0). Against GPT2-DT (LaMo), GPT2-DTMA is comparable on short-term (77.4 vs 77.9 on Hopper-m) but dramatically better on long-term PointMaze-large (203.0 vs 257.3), demonstrating that MoA effectively suppresses the detrimental Markov head bias in long-horizon settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on transferring pretrained language models (PLMs) to offline reinforcement learning, building directly on Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) as the base architecture and LaMo (Shi et al., 2024) as the PLM-initialized baseline. It departs from prior work by providing a mechanistic, head-level explanation for why PLMs help in short-term RL: the identification of 'Markov heads' whose weight matrices exhibit extreme diagonal dominance, causing disproportionate attention to the most recent token. The proposed GPT2-DTMA method adapts the Mixture of Attention (MoA) mechanism (Zhang et al., 2022) to this RL setting, treating each head as an expert whose contribution is gated adaptively per environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, theoretically grounded explanation for the asymmetric benefit of PLM initialization in offline RL (helpful for short-term, harmful for long-term tasks), which prior work had observed empirically but not explained at the head level. The proof that Markov heads are invariant under fine-tuning is a strong constraint on what practitioners can expect from standard fine-tuning pipelines. However, the practical improvement from GPT2-DTMA is modest—it narrows but does not close the long-term performance gap—and the analysis is limited to GPT2-small on D4RL benchmarks. Open questions include whether alternative pretraining objectives could produce head structures better suited to long-horizon planning, and whether the Markov head phenomenon generalizes to larger or differently-architected PLMs.
