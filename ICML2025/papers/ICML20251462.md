## Problem

Molecular generative models struggle to produce high-quality molecules because molecules are supported on a low-dimensional manifold embedded in a high-dimensional 3D space (N×(3+d) dimensions), making direct distribution learning error-prone. This is especially acute in conditional generation, where models must satisfy specific property constraints, yet current approaches still produce molecules with property errors far above the data lower bound. Additionally, prior representation-conditioned generation work (RCG) lacked theoretical guarantees and did not handle the Euclidean and permutation symmetries inherent to molecular data.

## Input / Output

The method takes the molecule size N as a mandatory condition for the representation generator, and optionally a target property value c (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap, polarizability) for conditional generation. It produces a 3D molecular conformation as a point cloud of atomic coordinates (N×3) and node features (N×d, e.g., atomic numbers, charges), from which bond types are inferred post-hoc via lookup tables or OpenBabel. The pre-trained geometric encoder (Frad or Unimol) maps each molecule to a fixed-dimension representation vector r ∈ R^{d_r}, which serves as the intermediate conditioning signal between the two generation stages.

## Method

GeoRCG decomposes molecule generation into two parallel-trained stages: a lightweight MLP-based diffusion model generates a geometric representation r (conditioned on atom count N), and an equivariant molecule generator (EDM or SemlaFlow with EGNN backbone) generates the 3D conformation conditioned on r via a gated feedforward layer inserted between network blocks. The key insight is that the representation space is free of O(3)/SO(3) and S(N) symmetries present in the extrinsic molecular distribution, making it substantially simpler to model. For conditional generation, only the representation generator is retrained on (molecule, property) pairs while the molecule generator remains frozen, enabling efficient multi-property control. The paper provides a total variation distance bound (Theorem 3.2) showing that representation-conditioned diffusion achieves no higher error than standard diffusion, with the error decomposed into forward-process convergence, discretization, conditional score estimation, and representation generation error terms. Additional techniques include representation perturbation during training to prevent overfitting, classifier-free guidance for quality-diversity trade-off, and low-temperature sampling for the representation generator.

## Results

On QM9 unconditional generation, GeoRCG (EDM) achieves 92.32% molecule stability and 96.52% validity, substantially outperforming the base EDM (82% Mol Sta, 91.9% Valid) and all compared baselines. On GEOM-DRUG, GeoRCG (Semla) improves SemlaFlow's SOTA results across all metrics, reducing energy from 95.72 to 88.6 kcal/mol and strain from 56.42 to 47.64 kcal/mol. In conditional generation on QM9, GeoRCG (EDM) achieves an average 50% improvement in property MSE over the best existing models (e.g., 0.86 vs 1.97 for polarizability α, 325.2 vs 577 for HOMO-LUMO gap Δε). With only 100 diffusion steps, GeoRCG (EDM) reaches 91.85% Mol Sta and 96.49% Valid on QM9, nearly matching its 1000-step performance while other methods degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoRCG (EDM) outperforms all 3D-only baselines on QM9 unconditional generation, with the largest gains over EDM itself (+10.3% Mol Sta, +4.6% Valid) and over GeoBFN (the strongest baseline at +1.45% Mol Sta, +1.21% Valid). Against flow-based methods EquiFM and GOAT, GeoRCG leads by 4.0% and 8.2% in Mol Sta respectively. On GEOM-DRUG, GeoRCG (Semla) consistently improves SemlaFlow on every metric (Mol Sta 97.6 vs 97.4, Valid 95.3 vs 94.4, Energy 88.6 vs 95.72, Strain 47.64 vs 56.42), and substantially outperforms EQGAT-diff (Energy 88.6 vs 148.8) and MiDi (Valid 95.3 vs 77.8). In conditional generation, GeoRCG (EDM) nearly halves the property MSE of the best prior method across all six QM9 properties (e.g., α: 0.86 vs GCDM's 1.97; Δε: 325.2 vs GeoBFN's 577), while LDM-3DG performs far worse (α: 12.29). The only comparison where GeoRCG does not achieve the top score is QM9 Valid&Unique (92.45% vs GCDM's 93.3%), reflecting a slight diversity trade-off from representation conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoRCG extends the two-stage representation-conditioned generation (RCG) framework from Li et al. (2023), which was designed for fixed-size images, to 3D molecular data where O(3)/SO(3) equivariance and variable atom counts introduce fundamentally different symmetry challenges. Unlike GraphRCG (Wang et al., 2024), which applies RCG to 2D graphs but requires complex bootstrapped training with noise alignment and sequential encoder training, GeoRCG leverages frozen pre-trained geometric encoders (Frad, Unimol) and enables fully parallel training of the representation and molecule generators. The paper also departs from prior latent molecular generation approaches (GeoLDM, LDM-3DG) by modeling the decoder as a full generative model rather than a deterministic autoencoder, and provides the first rigorous theoretical guarantees for the RCG framework, which was previously purely empirical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% average improvement in conditional molecule generation (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap, polarizability) is practically significant for drug design workflows where property-targeted generation is the primary use case, and the 10x reduction in diffusion steps while preserving quality addresses a key computational bottleneck for sequential generative methods. The framework's generality—consistently improving both EDM and the SOTA SemlaFlow—suggests it can be a drop-in enhancement for future equivariant generators. Key limitations include dependence on encoder pre-training quality (less meaningful representations on GEOM-DRUG with weaker encoders) and the 2x compute overhead from classifier-free guidance, leaving open whether more effective pre-training objectives or representation regularization can further close the gap to data-level performance.
