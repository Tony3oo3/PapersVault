## Problem

Accurately segmenting tightly touching cells in biomedical images remains challenging: detection-based methods miss elongated or overlapping cells, contour-based methods are sensitive to threshold choices, and distance-mapping methods require complex multi-branch architectures with heavy post-processing. The paper addresses the open problem of balancing segmentation accuracy with computational efficiency across diverse cell morphologies and imaging modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 patches from fluorescence, H&E-stained, bright-field, or phase-contrast microscopy images and produces a five-channel output (one background channel plus four color-encoding channels). The four foreground channels do not encode semantic categories but rather spatial adjacency relationships: adjacent cells are guaranteed different color labels, so the four-class semantic mask can be decoded into per-instance segmentations without watershed or NMS post-processing.

## Method

The core idea reformulates cell instance segmentation as a four-class semantic segmentation problem by applying the four-color theorem: a greedy algorithm assigns each cell a color from {1,2,3,4} such that adjacent cells receive distinct colors, treating cells as 'countries' and background as 'ocean.' To handle the non-uniqueness of valid four-color encodings (substitution, exchange, rule modification), the authors design an asymptotic training strategy that first learns binary foreground/background separation and then predicts the four-color encoding within the foreground, combined with an encoding transformation module (two convolutional layers) that maps predictions to a minimum-color canonical form. An orthogonality constraint (cosine similarity loss on sampled features of adjacent cell pairs) enforces spatial heterogeneity in the feature space. The paper proves that the greedy coloring is globally optimal for cell graphs due to their low maximum degree (≤4) and chain/rectangular structure, making cell coloring inherently simpler than map coloring.

## Results

FCIS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets. On DSB2018, it reaches DICE 0.939, AJI 0.828, and PQ 0.770; on BBBC006v1, DICE 0.954, AJI 0.921, and PQ 0.935; on PanNuke, DICE 0.816, AJI 0.653, and PQ 0.610; and on YeaZ, DICE 0.922, AJI 0.819, and PQ 0.456. The method uses 39.75M parameters and 58.03G FLOPs, substantially lower than distance-mapping methods like HoverNet (49.70M, 192.70G) and CellViT (96.81M, 124.25G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCIS outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is CellPose (Stringer, 2025), which FCIS surpasses by +1.6 DICE and +0.6 PQ on DSB2018 (0.939 vs 0.923; 0.770 vs 0.764) and by +0.5 DICE and +2.1 PQ on BBBC006v1 (0.954 vs 0.949; 0.935 vs 0.914). Among distance-mapping methods, HoverNet (Graham et al., 2019) is the strongest at 0.898 DICE on DSB2018, trailing FCIS by 4.1 points, while CPP-Net (Chen et al., 2023) reaches 0.914 DICE, still 2.5 points behind. Detection-based DoNet (Jiang et al., 2023) lags significantly at 0.823 DICE, and contour-based DCAN (Chen et al., 2016) is the weakest at 0.795 DICE on DSB2018. On the challenging multi-organ PanNuke dataset, FCIS leads by +2.9 PQ over CellPose (0.610 vs 0.591) and +11.5 PQ over HoverNet (0.610 vs 0.495), confirming strong generalization. Notably, FCIS achieves these gains with fewer parameters (39.75M) and lower FLOPs (58.03G) than HoverNet (49.70M, 192.70G) and CellViT (96.81M, 124.25G).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from all three established paradigms for cell instance segmentation (detection-based, contour-prediction, and distance-mapping) by repurposing the four-color theorem to reformulate the problem as a four-class semantic segmentation task. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it uses a standard U-Net backbone but introduces a novel encoding scheme, an asymptotic training strategy (binary foreground/background first, then four-color classification), and an encoding transformation module to handle the non-uniqueness of color assignments. The theoretical contribution—proving greedy coloring is globally optimal under the structural constraints typical of cell graphs (low max degree, chain/rectangular arrangements)—provides a principled justification that distinguishes this work from heuristic approaches in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the complex multi-branch decoding and post-processing pipelines required by distance-mapping and detection-based methods, yielding a simpler model (39.75M params, 58.03G FLOPs) that nonetheless achieves SOTA across four diverse imaging modalities (fluorescence, H&E, bright-field, phase-contrast). Open questions include whether the four-color constraint remains sufficient for extremely dense 3D tissue volumes or highly irregular cell morphologies where local degree might exceed 4, and whether the encoding transformation generalizes to other constrained labeling problems beyond cell segmentation. The authors note future directions toward adaptive encoding strategies and extension to downstream tasks like nucleus classification.
