## Problem

Standard LLM fine-tuning (RLHF, SFT) optimizes single-turn response quality, causing models to passively follow user requests rather than actively clarifying intent or planning ahead in multiturn conversations. This misalignment between per-turn rewards and long-term task success leads to inefficient, frustrating interactions where users must iteratively correct the model. The paper asks how to train LLMs to optimize for the long-term collaborative outcome of an entire conversation rather than immediate response quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252676/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives the full multiturn conversation history and the current user message, producing a text response that may include clarifying questions, targeted content, or task-specific solutions. During training, each candidate response is additionally fed into a collaborative simulation where a user simulator (GPT-4o-mini) generates forward conversation turns up to a window of w=2 additional turns, and the resulting trajectory is scored by a conversation-level reward combining task-specific metrics (BLEU, Pass Rate, accuracy) with a token-count efficiency penalty and LLM-judged interactivity.

## Method

COLLABLLM introduces Multiturn-aware Rewards (MR), which estimate the expected long-term contribution of a response by forward-sampling future conversations with an LLM-based user simulator and scoring the resulting trajectories with a conversation-level reward R*(t|g) = R_ext + R_int. The user simulator is prompted to role-play realistic users with implicit goals, vague initial requests, and natural mistakes, generating diverse forward trajectories that capture how a response shapes subsequent turns. Training proceeds via SFT on top-ranked synthetic conversations, followed by offline DPO (pairwise preference data from MR rankings) and online DPO/PPO where the policy model itself participates in the collaborative simulation to compute MRs dynamically. The framework is task-agnostic: the same MR mechanism and training pipeline apply across document editing, code generation, and math, with only the extrinsic reward function changing per task.

## Results

On the three multiturn benchmarks, COLLABLLM (Online DPO) achieves 18.5% higher task-specific performance (BLEU 36.8, PR 13.0, ACC 16.5), 13.3% fewer tokens per conversation, and 46.3% higher interactivity (ITR) on average compared to the best baseline (Proactive Base). In the real-world user study with 201 participants, COLLABLLM increases user satisfaction by 17.6% and reduces time spent by 10.4% over the Base model. On zero-shot generalization to Abg-CoQA, COLLABLLM achieves 62.58% macro accuracy (vs. 55.52% for GPT-4o and 53.33% for Base), with 52.84% action-level accuracy on ambiguous questions versus 15.44% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares COLLABLLM against two baselines: the pretrained Llama-3.1-8B (Base) and a prompt-engineered variant (Proactive Base) that encourages follow-up questions. COLLABLLM (Online DPO) outperforms Base on every metric across all three tasks—for example, BLEU rises from 32.2 to 36.8 on MediumDocEdit-Chat, Pass Rate from 9.3 to 13.0 on BigCodeBench-Chat, and accuracy from 11.0 to 16.5 on MATH-Chat—while also reducing token counts by 19.7%, 17.6%, and 30.3% respectively. Against Proactive Base, the gains are larger on interactivity (ITR up 48.3%, 54.3%, and 36.4%) and task performance (BLEU +5.14%, PR +18.2%, ACC +32.0%), confirming that fine-tuning with Multiturn-aware Rewards substantially exceeds what static prompting can achieve. In the user study, COLLABLLM scores 8.50/10 on document quality versus Base's lower rating, with 91.4% of participants rating it 'good' or above, and reduces interaction time by 10.4% over Base and 15.6% over Proactive Base. On the zero-shot Abg-CoQA generalization test, COLLABLLM achieves the highest macro accuracy (62.58%) and dramatically outperforms both GPT-4o (15.44%) and Base (16.26%) on ambiguous-question action accuracy, though it trails GPT-4o on non-ambiguous accuracy (72.32% vs. 95.60%). Overall, COLLABLLM wins on all primary metrics against both baselines, with the largest relative gains in interactivity and task accuracy on MATH-Chat.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF/DPO training paradigm from single-turn response optimization to multiturn collaboration by introducing Multiturn-aware Rewards (MR) computed via forward simulation with a user simulator. It departs from prior multiturn RLHF approaches (MTPO, Archer) that assign post-hoc trajectory-level rewards by instead estimating the causal (interventional) effect of individual responses on future conversation trajectories, drawing explicitly on Pearl's causal inference framework. The user simulator component builds on recent work using LLMs to role-play users (Hong et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023), but repurposes it for reward estimation rather than dialogue generation. Unlike clarification-specific methods (ClarifyGPT, STaR-GATE), the framework is task-agnostic and supports diverse collaborative strategies beyond asking questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented user frustration—LLMs passively follow ambiguous requests rather than actively guiding users toward their goals—and provides a general, scalable training framework that demonstrably improves both task performance and user experience (17.6% satisfaction gain, 10.4% time reduction in a 201-participant study). Key open questions include the quality gap between simulated and real users (the authors note open-source models perform poorly as simulators), whether the approach scales to larger foundation models beyond Llama-3.1-8B, and how to incorporate real-time knowledge and personalization. The framework's applicability to safety-critical domains (e.g., medical decision-making where models should seek expert confirmation) suggests broader impact beyond conversational AI.
