## Problem

Concave Utility Reinforcement Learning (CURL) generalizes standard RL by optimizing a convex (rather than linear) function over the state-action distribution induced by a policy, but the non-linearity invalidates Bellman equations and dynamic programming. In the online setting with adversarial losses and unknown transition kernels, no prior method achieved sub-linear regret without strong structural assumptions on the dynamics (e.g., known deterministic transitions plus noise). This paper is the first to provide near-optimal regret for online CURL with fully unknown dynamics, and the first to address the bandit-feedback variant where only the scalar objective value is observed.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an episodic MDP specification (finite state space X, action space A, horizon N), an initial state-action distribution μ_0, and per-episode feedback on the convex objective F^t. Under full-information feedback the entire function F^t is revealed; under bandit RL only losses at visited state-action pairs are seen; under bandit CURL only the single scalar F^t(μ^{π^t,p}) is returned. The output is a sequence of policies π^t computed via a closed-form backward recursion, along with a high-probability regret guarantee of Õ(√T) for full-information and bandit-RL settings, and Õ(T^{3/4}) for bandit CURL.

## Method

The core algorithm (Bonus O-MD-CURL) runs Online Mirror Descent over the set of state-action distributions induced by the current estimated transition kernel, using a custom Bregman divergence Γ that admits a closed-form policy solution via a backward Q-function recursion. The key technical novelty is a carefully designed exploration bonus vector b^t with entries proportional to L(N−n)·C_δ/√max{1, N_n^t(x,a)}, subtracted from the objective's sub-gradient in each OMD update; this bonus simultaneously drives exploration of unvisited state-action pairs and algebraically cancels the problematic model-estimation regret term that plagued prior CURL methods. For bandit CURL, two gradient-estimation strategies are developed: an entropic-regularization approach using Flaxman-style spherical perturbations in a lower-dimensional parameterization of the occupancy-measure polytope (requiring a lower-bounded transition kernel), and a self-concordant log-barrier approach using ellipsoid-based surrogates (requiring a known MDP), both achieving T^{3/4} regret.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.2, which establishes a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(LN^3|\mathcal{X}|^{3/2}\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|T})$ for online CURL with full-information feedback and unknown transition kernels, matching the state-of-the-art rate in more restricted settings. For bandit RL (Theorem 4.1), the method achieves the optimal $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ rate. For the more challenging bandit CURL setting, two algorithms are proposed achieving $T^{3/4}$ regret (Theorems 4.3 and 4.5). Empirically, on the Four-Room Grid World (N=40), Bonus O-MD-CURL reaches multi-objective targets significantly faster than Greedy MD-CURL and successfully solves the constrained MDP task where the baseline fails entirely after 1000 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is Greedy MD-CURL (Moreno et al., 2024), which achieves sub-linear regret only under the restrictive assumption that dynamics follow $x_{n+1}=g_n(x_n,a_n,\varepsilon_n)$ with known deterministic $g_n$ and independent noise. On the multi-objective Four-Room task, Bonus O-MD-CURL concentrates the state distribution on all three targets much faster than Greedy MD-CURL after 50 iterations, and shows lower log-loss and regret after 1000 iterations. On the constrained MDP task, Greedy MD-CURL fails to reach the target state even after 1000 iterations, while Bonus O-MD-CURL successfully reaches it while avoiding constraint states, demonstrating the critical role of the additive exploration bonus. Theoretically, the proposed method is the first to simultaneously handle CURL, adversarial losses, unknown transition kernels without model assumptions, and bandit feedback (Table 1), whereas Jin et al. (2020) and Luo et al. (2021) handle only linear RL objectives, and Moreno et al. (2024) requires strong dynamics assumptions and does not support bandit feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the occupancy-measure-based CURL framework of Moreno et al. (2024), which achieved sub-linear regret only under restrictive deterministic-dynamics-plus-noise assumptions, by removing all model assumptions and introducing exploration bonuses into the OMD sub-gradient to handle the exploration-exploitation trade-off. For the bandit CURL setting, the paper is the first to address this problem, adapting spherical gradient estimation (Flaxman et al., 2005) and self-concordant log-barrier regularization (Saha & Tewari, 2011) from the bandit convex optimization literature to the MDP occupancy-measure setting. The transition kernel concentration bounds from Jaksch et al. (2008) underpin the model estimation analysis, while the Bregman divergence Γ and its closed-form policy solution are inherited directly from Moreno et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm achieving near-optimal Õ(√T) regret for online CURL with adversarial losses and completely unknown dynamics, and the first treatment of bandit CURL at all. The key open question is whether the T^{3/4} regret for bandit CURL can be improved to the optimal √T rate, and whether the self-concordant regularization approach can be extended to the unknown-MDP setting where the constraint set changes over time. The exploration bonus technique also offers a new, computationally efficient alternative to model-optimistic methods (UCRL-style) for occupancy-measure-based RL, potentially applicable beyond CURL. Limitations include the requirement of a lower-bounded transition kernel (Assumption 4.2) for the unknown-MDP bandit algorithm, and the narrow scope to tabular finite-horizon MDPs.
