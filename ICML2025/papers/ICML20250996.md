## Problem

Fine-tuning Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on downstream tasks causes catastrophic forgetting of the generalization knowledge acquired during pre-training, creating a trade-off between task specialization and broad capability retention. Existing anti-forgetting methods either require architecture modifications, introduce hyperparameters, or alter the loss function, limiting their compatibility and transferability across different MLLM backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250996/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard MLLM inputs: an RGB image and a text instruction, producing a language response (image caption or VQA answer). During training, the visual encoder is frozen while only the connector module and the last L=2 LLM layers are updated. The method works at the parameter level during the backward pass, requiring no changes to the input format, output structure, or inference pipeline compared to standard MLLM fine-tuning.

## Method

SPIDER introduces two components: Importance Discrepancy Measurement (IDM) and Importance Selection Mask (ISM). IDM quantifies parameter importance for generalization using the frozen pre-trained weight magnitude (normalized and sigmoid-rescaled to (0,1)) and for specialization using momentum-accumulated fine-tuning gradients (coefficient 0.85, similarly normalized). ISM then constructs a soft update mask where parameters with higher specialization importance than generalization importance receive a proportional weight G/(G+I), rescaled by the mean of selected elements and capped at 1. Parameters below the threshold are reset to their pre-trained values via element-wise blending w = w⊙M + w*⊙(1−M). This requires no additional hyperparameters, no architecture changes, and no loss modification, operating purely as a parameter-level gating mechanism during the standard training loop.

## Results

On the image captioning task (Flickr30k, VILA-1.5-3B), SPIDER achieves a harmonic mean (H) of 66.61, surpassing Full FT (55.16) by +11.45 and the strongest baseline Half FT (59.52) by +7.09, while also improving target performance (A^T) from 66.68 to 83.49. On COCO-Caption (VILA), SPIDER reaches H=76.86 vs. Full FT's 65.48 (+11.38). For visual question answering (IconQA, VILA), SPIDER attains H=66.71 vs. Full FT's 56.13 (+10.58), and on ScienceQA it achieves H=66.60 vs. 61.82 (+4.78). Across all four downstream datasets and both VILA and LLaVA architectures, SPIDER consistently achieves the best H and O scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIDER outperforms all six baselines on both H and O metrics across every evaluated dataset. Against Full FT, SPIDER gains +11.45 H on Flickr30k and +11.38 on COCO-Caption (VILA), while also substantially improving target accuracy (83.49 vs. 66.68 on Flickr30k). Regularization-based methods (L2-Reg, Grafting) perform worst, with H scores of 48.33 and 49.58 on Flickr30k, indicating that loss-level constraints are insufficient to preserve generalization. Among partial-update methods, Half FT (random 50% selection) is the strongest baseline at H=59.52, but SPIDER still leads by +7.09, demonstrating that importance-driven selection is superior to random masking. DARE (H=53.66) and Tailor (H=54.56) both underperform Full FT on generalization (A^S), confirming that post-hoc rescaling of delta parameters introduces instability. On the VQA task (IconQA), SPIDER achieves H=66.71 vs. Half FT's 63.89 and Full FT's 56.13, with the gap narrowing on ScienceQA (H=66.60 vs. 64.86) where the task distribution is closer to pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIDER sits at the intersection of parameter importance estimation from the pruning literature (magnitude-based selection à la Han et al. and Frankle & Carbin; gradient-based selection à la EWC and synaptic intelligence) and the partial-updating paradigm for MLLM fine-tuning (DARE, Tailor, GPS, SPU). It departs from prior partial-update methods by computing a *soft*, importance-discrepancy-driven mask during training rather than applying a binary or post-hoc rescaling, and by jointly referencing frozen pre-trained weights and accumulated fine-tuning gradients to separate generalization-critical from specialization-critical parameters. Unlike regularization-based approaches (L2-Reg, Grafting), it requires no loss modification or hyperparameter tuning, and unlike additive methods (adapters, LoRA), it introduces no new architecture components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, architecture-agnostic, hyperparameter-free recipe that practitioners can drop into any MLLM fine-tuning pipeline, and the ~10-point H-metric gains over Full FT across multiple datasets and two model families make it practically attractive. However, the evaluation is limited to two small-scale models (VILA-1.5-3B, LLaVA-1.5-7B) and four downstream datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger MLLMs or to more diverse task distributions. The fixed momentum coefficient (0.85) and the assumption that weight magnitude reliably proxies generalization importance are not stress-tested, and the method's behavior when the downstream task closely overlaps the pre-training distribution (where minimal updates suffice) is acknowledged but not deeply characterized.
