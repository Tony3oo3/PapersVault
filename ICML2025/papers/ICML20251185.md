## Problem

Zero-Shot TTS models can replicate any speaker's voice from a 3-second audio prompt, yet no method exists to selectively remove a specific speaker's identity from a pre-trained model's parameters while preserving its synthesis quality for all other speakers. Existing machine unlearning techniques from computer vision and NLP do not address the unique challenge that ZS-TTS generalizes to unseen voices in-context, making approximate unlearning insufficient, and that the unlearned model must avoid settling into any fixed alternative voice style that could be reverse-engineered back to the forgotten identity.

## Input / Output

The unlearned model receives a 3-second audio prompt (80-dim log Mel spectrogram at 16 kHz, 1024-point STFT, 10 ms hop) and a phonemized text transcription, and produces a synthesized speech waveform via HiFi-GAN vocoding. For remain speakers, the output should closely match the prompt speaker's voice; for forget speakers, the output must be in a random, non-reproducible voice style that bears no traceable resemblance to the prompt. The unlearning fine-tuning operates on 75-second audio segments with classifier-free guidance (α=0.7) at inference using 32 NFEs.

## Method

Teacher-Guided Unlearning (TGU) exploits the fact that VoiceBox, when conditioned solely on text (no audio prompt), generates speech in a random voice style that varies with the Gaussian noise initialization. For each forget-speaker input pair (x^f, y), the pre-trained teacher θ generates a text-only target x̄ = θ(y), and the student θ⁻ is trained via a Conditional Flow Matching loss to produce x̄ when given (x^f, y) as context, effectively decoupling the forget speaker's voice from the generation. A weighted combination (λ=0.2) of this forget loss and the standard CFM loss on remain-speaker data preserves synthesis quality for retained identities. A simpler Sample-Guided Unlearning (SGU) variant concatenates forget and remain speech pairs with masking, but suffers from limited context and style mismatch. The authors also introduce the spk-ZRF metric, which measures the Jensen-Shannon divergence between speaker-verification embeddings of the unlearned model's output and a text-only random-voice baseline, quantifying how random (and thus untraceable) the generated voice is for forget prompts.

## Results

On the LibriSpeech test-clean evaluation set with 10 forget speakers from LibriHeavy, TGU achieves WER-R of 2.5, SIM-R of 0.631, WER-F of 2.4, SIM-F of 0.169, and spk-ZRF-F of 0.871, closely matching the original VoiceBox model's content accuracy (WER-R 2.1) while reducing forget-speaker similarity from 0.708 to 0.169 (near the 0.09 mean for different-speaker pairs). TGU also achieves the highest spk-ZRF-F (0.871) among all methods, indicating the greatest randomness in generated speaker identities for forget prompts. SGU achieves comparable WER (2.5–2.6) but suffers a 21% drop in SIM-R (0.523 vs. 0.649 original), whereas TGU retains only a 2.8% decrease.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGU decisively outperforms the Negative Gradient (NG, Thudi et al. 2022) baseline on every metric: WER-R drops from 6.1 to 2.5, SIM-R rises from 0.437 to 0.631, and SIM-F falls from 0.402 to 0.169, while spk-ZRF-F improves from 0.842 to 0.871. Against the Selective KL divergence method (Li et al. 2024; Chen & Yang 2023), TGU avoids the catastrophic WER-F of 47.2 (the model generates inaudible noise) and maintains SIM-R of 0.631 versus KL's 0.408, while achieving a marginally lower SIM-F (0.169 vs. 0.179) and substantially higher spk-ZRF-F (0.871 vs. 0.810). Compared to Fine Tuning (Warnecke et al. 2022), TGU reduces SIM-F from 0.675 to 0.169 and increases spk-ZRF-F from 0.853 to 0.871, at the cost of a small SIM-R decrease (0.631 vs. 0.658) and WER-R increase (2.5 vs. 2.2). Exact Unlearning (retraining from scratch on D^R) fails to reduce SIM-F (0.687) or spk-ZRF-F (0.846), confirming that simply excluding forget speakers from training is insufficient for zero-shot voice cloning models. Overall, TGU is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-original content accuracy, near-different-speaker similarity on the forget set, and the highest randomness score, validating its effectiveness as a speaker identity unlearning framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VoiceBox's Conditional Flow Matching framework for zero-shot TTS and adapts the broader machine unlearning literature (exact unlearning, gradient ascent, KL-divergence-based methods) to the speech domain. It departs from prior unlearning work in CV and NLP, which focuses on removing specific concepts or sequences, by introducing 'guided unlearning' that actively trains the model to produce random voice styles for forget speakers rather than merely suppressing their influence. The spk-ZRF metric is adapted from the Zero Retrain Forgetting metric (Chundawat et al., 2023) but restructured to isolate speaker-identity randomness from overall speech quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and address speaker identity unlearning in ZS-TTS, a task with direct implications for GDPR/RTBF compliance in voice AI services. The finding that simply excluding forget speakers from training (exact unlearning or fine-tuning) fails to protect voice privacy—because zero-shot generalization preserves the model's ability to clone any voice—highlights a fundamental gap between approximate unlearning assumptions and ZS-TTS capabilities. Open questions include whether the guided unlearning approach generalizes beyond flow-matching architectures, how to handle speakers with acoustically similar voices without degrading remain-speaker performance, and how to provide verifiable, auditable certificates of unlearning for regulatory purposes.
