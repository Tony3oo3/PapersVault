## Problem

Conventional prompt engineering assumes that well-crafted instructions and coherent demonstrations are necessary for effective in-context learning, yet prior work has shown that incoherent 'secret language' prompts can outperform natural instructions. However, discovering such prompts has relied on ad-hoc token-level searches or coincidence, and existing attribution methods and prompt compression algorithms fail to produce reliable token importance scores that guide pruning. The paper asks whether a systematic, self-discovering search can autonomously find effective pruning strategies that transform standard ICL prompts into high-performing, seemingly incoherent variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard few-shot ICL prompt (K input-label pairs concatenated in context) and a held-out evaluation dataset, and produces a pruned prompt that is a fixed-order subsequence of the original tokens (no reordering or insertion). The subsequence length is not predefined and adjusts continuously during optimization. The search objective is a non-differentiable task metric (e.g., classification accuracy, Joint Score for style transfer, or Attack Success Rate for jailbreaking), and the final output is the highest-scoring pruned prompt selected via a calibration-then-selection phase on the full held-out set, with test performance reported separately to prevent data leakage.

## Method

PROMPTQUINE frames prompt pruning as a guided search over fixed-order token subsequences and solves it with a Genetic Algorithm where binary token masks serve as genotypes and the resulting pruned ICL prompts as phenotypes. Mutation is implemented as bit-flip 1-to-0 operations (removing tokens), with the number of flipped bits drawn uniformly from {1,2,3,4} to balance exploration and exploitation. The population is initialized with duplicates of the original ICL prompt, and the algorithm employs regularized evolution—where only new offspring compete for population slots—combined with tournament selection at reduced pressure to mitigate premature convergence in the multimodal, deceptive fitness landscape. Two GA variants (Generational and Steady-state) are explored, with SSGA used by default for 1-shot ICL. A final calibration-then-selection phase re-ranks candidates using the full held-out accuracy to correct for overfitting to the imperfect in-search fitness proxy, yielding the final pruned prompt.

## Results

On classification tasks with Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, PROMPTQUINE (1-shot ICL) achieves 77.5% average accuracy across six datasets (SST-2, Subj, AG's News, Yelp-5, SNLI, Yahoo), surpassing the best prior method Promptbreeder (75.8%) by 1.7 points and the original 1-shot ICL baseline (69.6%) by 7.9 points, while compressing the prompt by 52.9%. Scaling to 4-shot ICL, PROMPTQUINE reaches 81.3% average accuracy, outperforming 4-shot ICL (72.0%) by 9.3 points. On text style transfer (Yelp), PROMPTQUINE achieves 61.0 Joint Score (greedy) on Llama3-8B-It, beating Promptbreeder (59.1) and ICL (54.4). On jailbreaking (AdvBench), PROMPTQUINE nearly doubles attack success rate from ~50% (ICL) to ~99% on both Vicuna-7b and Mistral-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROMPTQUINE (1-shot) outperforms all six baselines on average: it beats Promptbreeder (75.8%) by 1.7 points, EvoPrompt (73.6%) by 3.9 points, 4-shot ICL (72.0%) by 5.5 points, RLPrompt (67.5%) by 10.0 points, LLMLingua (68.1%) by 9.4 points, and LLMLingua2 (55.7%) by 21.8 points. Notably, PROMPTQUINE achieves these gains while compressing the prompt by 52.9%, whereas LLMLingua2 achieves higher compression (71.2%) but substantially lower accuracy. On style transfer (Table 2), PROMPTQUINE surpasses Promptbreeder (59.1 vs. 61.0 on Llama3-8B-It) and ICL (54.4) in Joint Score, and also outperforms RLPrompt which degrades severely on Llama3-8B-It (4.1). On jailbreaking (Table 3), PROMPTQUINE achieves ~99% ASR-EM versus ~50% for 2-shot ICL on both Vicuna-7b and Mistral-7B, representing a near-doubling of attack success. The paper also notes that SAHCPruning (a steepest-ascent variant) does not consistently outperform the simpler TAPruning, highlighting the deceptive, multimodal nature of the search landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes prompt compression as a guided subsequence search problem, departing from both the prompt compression literature (LLMLingua, LLMLingua2) that targets inference speed and the prompt optimization literature (RLPrompt, EvoPrompt, Promptbreeder) that searches in natural language space or uses LLMs as prompt engineers. Methodologically, it builds on genetic algorithms and regularized evolution (Real et al., 2019) for population management, with a Threshold Accepting hill-climbing baseline (Dueck & Scheuer, 1990), while explicitly showing that attribution methods and existing compression algorithms fail to produce reliable token importance scores for guiding pruning. Conceptually, it connects to the 'secret language' / unnatural prompt literature (Shin et al., 2020; Deng et al., 2022) but provides a systematic, self-discovering search framework rather than relying on coincidental discovery or limited token-level search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pruning ICL demonstrations into incoherent 'gibberish' can match or surpass SOTA prompt optimization challenges the field's working assumption that semantically coherent, well-crafted prompts are necessary, and raises mechanistic questions about what LLMs actually attend to in context. The safety implications are significant: the same pruning that improves task performance nearly doubles jailbreak attack success rates on aligned models, exposing gaps in current alignment that is primarily optimized for natural language inputs. Key limitations include sensitivity to ICL template choices (up to 14.3% accuracy fluctuation on SNLI) and the restriction to fixed-order subsequence search, which limits the search space. Open questions include why pruning works mechanistically, whether richer mutation operators (token insertion, replacement) could overcome template sensitivity, and whether the alignment vulnerabilities discovered here generalize to newer models.
