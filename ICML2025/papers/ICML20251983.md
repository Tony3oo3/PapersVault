## Problem

Self-alignment methods for complex reasoning tasks struggle because LLMs cannot reliably judge the correctness of their own solutions, and external reward models degrade on out-of-distribution problems. This paper addresses the gap of training LLMs on complex reasoning without access to gold answers or a trustworthy reward model, by repurposing the inference-time self-consistency signal as a training-time preference signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled reasoning problems (text queries) and optionally a small set of gold-labeled examples for the semi-supervised variant. For each query, the current model generates k responses via temperature sampling (k=8 for math, k=16 for ZebraLogic), from which final answers are extracted and vote-counted. The output is updated model weights; at inference time the model produces a chain-of-thought rationale followed by a final answer. New training problems are self-generated via few-shot prompting and filtered by a vote threshold τ to ensure answerability.

## Method

ScPO iteratively trains a model by constructing preference pairs from self-consistency: for each query, k responses are sampled, the most-voted final answer's response becomes the chosen (y⁺) and the least-voted becomes the rejected (y⁻), provided the chosen's vote exceeds a threshold τ. The training loss is a weighted DPO term plus a weighted NLL term, where the instance-level weight w(x) = (V(y⁺) − V(y⁻))/k scales the loss by the vote margin, encoding model confidence in the preference labeling. Training proceeds for T=2 iterations, with each iteration generating new problems via few-shot prompting and building preference pairs from the current model's samples. A semi-supervised variant incorporates gold labels by setting w=1 for labeled instances and using self-consistency for unlabeled ones, reducing to standard IRPO when all data is labeled. The key distinction from prior self-training is that correctness is inferred from answer consistency across samples rather than from a model-as-judge or external reward model, and the weighted loss explicitly down-weights low-confidence preference pairs.

## Results

On GSM8K, two iterations of unsupervised ScPO improve Llama-3 Base 8B greedy accuracy from 41.17% (seed) to 63.91% (+22.74%), closely matching the supervised IRPO_Gold baseline (64.29%). On MATH, ScPO Unsup M2 reaches 19.72% greedy accuracy (+5.26% over seed), within 0.6% of IRPO_Gold (20.32%). On ZebraLogic, ScPO-trained Llama-3 Instruct 8B achieves 18.1% overall puzzle accuracy, outperforming Llama-3 70B (17.2%), Gemma-2 27B (16.3%), and Claude-3 Haiku (14.3%). Semi-supervised ScPO further improves GSM8K greedy accuracy to 66.64%, surpassing IRPO_Gold by 2.35%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScPO Unsup decisively outperforms the zero-shot seed model by 22.74% (GSM8K greedy) and 5.26% (MATH greedy) after two iterations. Against IRPO_RM (which uses ArmoRM-Llama3-8B as an external reward model), ScPO Unsup M1 leads by 12.36% on GSM8K greedy and 1.64% on MATH greedy, with the gap attributed to ArmoRM's out-of-distribution failures on ZebraLogic (12.3% more correct preference orderings for self-consistency). ScPO Unsup also beats LMSI (NLL-based self-improvement) by 7.20% on GSM8K and 2.76% on MATH, highlighting the benefit of the weighted preference loss over plain supervised fine-tuning. Unsupervised ScPO M2 is within 0.38% of IRPO_Gold on GSM8K greedy and within 0.60% on MATH greedy, matching the supervised upper bound without any gold labels. Semi-supervised ScPO M2 surpasses IRPO_Gold by 2.35% on GSM8K greedy and 1.24% on MATH greedy (M1), demonstrating that model-generated problems add value even when gold labels are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScPO repurposes self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), originally an inference-time decoding strategy, as a training-time signal for constructing preference pairs without gold labels. The iterative training loop follows the Self-Rewarding LM paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024) but replaces the unreliable self-evaluation step (shown to fail on complex reasoning by Huang et al., 2024) with a consistency-based vote function. The loss function adapts the DPO+NLL structure from IRPO (Pang et al., 2024) by introducing an instance-level weight proportional to the vote margin between chosen and rejected responses, which the authors argue reflects model confidence in the preference labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScPO demonstrates that a simple inference-time heuristic (majority voting over sampled answers) can serve as a viable unsupervised training signal for complex reasoning, closing the gap with gold-label-based preference optimization to within ~1% on GSM8K and MATH. The semi-supervised variant shows that even when gold labels are available, augmenting them with model-generated problems and consistency-weighted pairs yields further gains, suggesting a practical recipe for scaling reasoning training beyond annotated data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks without easily parseable final answers (e.g., summarization), whether more than two iterations help, and whether the consistency-as-confidence assumption holds across model families and task distributions beyond math and logic puzzles.
