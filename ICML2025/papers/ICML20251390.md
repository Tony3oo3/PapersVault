## Problem

MuZero-class methods achieve superhuman performance but assume unperturbed states, making them vulnerable to small state perturbations from malicious attacks or noisy sensors. No prior work addresses robustness for MuZero-class methods because existing robust DRL strategies rely on gradient-based policy search, while MuZero uses gradient-free MCTS for planning. This paper is the first to make a MuZero-class method robust to both worst-case and random-case state perturbations without requiring prior knowledge of environment dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method receives a state observation vector (which may be perturbed within an l∞-norm ball of radius ε) and produces an action sampled from a search policy generated by sampled MCTS in an abstract hidden space. Unusually, training operates in two parallel environments simultaneously—one under worst-case perturbation (via ATLA-PPO) and one under random-case perturbation (uniform noise)—feeding separate replay buffers. The representation network must map both the original and perturbed observations to similar initial hidden states so that MCTS planning yields consistent policies regardless of perturbation.

## Method

RobustZero replaces MuZero's standard representation network with a self-supervised contrastive network (BYOL-style with a projector and asymmetric predictor) that pulls the hidden states of perturbed and unperturbed observations together, ensuring MCTS planning produces consistent policies. The training loss combines a worst-case term, a random-case term, and an L2 decay term, where the random-case weight w1 is set adaptively via a sigmoid of the worst-case contrastive loss (prioritizing worst-case defense first, then gradually incorporating random-case), and the decay weight w2 is set adaptively from gradient magnitudes to guide the model toward flat minima. Data is collected in parallel from two environments (worst-case and random-case perturbations) into separate buffers, and the adaptive weights are smoothed with exponential moving averages for stable training.

## Results

Across 12 environments (classical control, energy, transportation, and MuJoCo), RobustZero achieves the highest worst-case and random-case rewards among all methods while maintaining natural rewards comparable to S-MuZero. For example, on Racetrack, RobustZero obtains a worst-case reward of 477.00 vs. 352.20 for S-MuZero and 429.36 for PROTECTED, and a random-case reward of 494.00 vs. 370.40 and 459.10 respectively. On IEEE 8500-node, RobustZero's worst-case reward of −1374 substantially outperforms S-MuZero (−1863) and PROTECTED (−1376). The paper reports worst-case reward improvements of 0.15%–184.37% and random-case reward improvements of 0.4%–101.43% over the strongest baseline, depending on the environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustZero outperforms all three primary baselines on worst-case and random-case rewards across all 12 environments. Against S-MuZero (the closest MuZero-class method without any defense mechanism), RobustZero achieves comparable natural rewards (e.g., −83.48 vs. −82.58 on Pendulum; 520.60 vs. 567.80 on Racetrack) but dramatically superior robustness: worst-case reward improves from −250.02 to −87.92 on Pendulum (184% gain) and from 352.20 to 477.00 on Racetrack (35% gain). Against PROTECTED (the state-of-the-art model-free method handling both perturbation types), RobustZero wins on worst-case and random-case rewards in every environment, e.g., −87.92 vs. −91.96 on Pendulum worst-case and 477.00 vs. 429.36 on Racetrack worst-case. Against ATLA-PPO (trained only under worst-case perturbations), RobustZero dominates on all three metrics, with the gap being largest on random-case rewards where ATLA-PPO was never trained. The S-MuZero-worst and S-MuZero-random ablation baselines confirm that training under a single perturbation type without a defense mechanism leads to over-specialization and poor generalization to the other perturbation type.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobustZero extends S-MuZero (Hubert et al., 2021), which itself generalizes MuZero to complex action spaces via sampled MCTS, by adding a self-supervised contrastive representation network and an adaptive loss-weighting mechanism. It departs from the dominant line of robust RL work (ATLA-PPO, PROTECTED, BA-DDPG), which targets model-free methods with gradient-based policy search, by addressing the fundamentally different challenge of defending MCTS-based policy improvement against state perturbations. The contrastive representation learning component is repurposed from EfficientZero (Ye et al., 2021), where it served sample efficiency, here instead enforcing consistency between perturbed and unperturbed hidden states. The adaptive weight-decay mechanism for w2 is adapted from Ghiasi et al. (2023) to guide convergence toward flat minima in the joint worst-case/random-case training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that MuZero-class methods can be made robust to state perturbations, opening the door to deploying model-based RL agents in real-world settings where sensor noise and adversarial attacks are unavoidable. The simultaneous handling of worst-case and random-case perturbations via the adaptive w1 schedule addresses a practical gap: prior robust RL methods optimized for worst-case often degraded under the random noise prevalent in real sensors. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small-scale environments (up to 32-dimensional hybrid action spaces), single-agent settings, and black-box attacks only; the authors note multi-agent and IoT extensions as future work. Whether the contrastive representation approach generalizes to higher-dimensional or partially observable settings remains an open question.
