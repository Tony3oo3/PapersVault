## Problem

Quantum algorithms for reinforcement learning have previously been limited to infinite-horizon or time-invariant MDPs, or suffered from exponential dependence on the state space. This paper addresses the open question of whether quantum speedups can be rigorously established for time-dependent, finite-horizon MDPs in both the exact dynamics setting (full knowledge of transition probabilities) and the generative model setting (sample access), with provable improvements over classical lower bounds.

## Input / Output

In the exact dynamics setting, the algorithm receives the MDP via a quantum oracle O_QM that maps |s⟩|a⟩|h⟩|s'⟩|0⟩|0⟩ to the binary-encoded reward r_h(s,a) and transition probability P_{h|s,a}(s'), with real numbers stored in fixed-point representation of precision 2^{-p}. In the generative model setting, the input is a quantum generative model G that produces superposition states ∑_{s'}√P_{h|s,a}(s')|s'⟩|j_{s'}⟩ encoding transition probabilities in amplitudes rather than classical samples. Outputs include the optimal policy π* and V-value function V_0* (exact setting), or ε-optimal policies, V-value functions, and Q-value functions (generative setting), all with high probability 1−δ.

## Method

The paper introduces four quantum value iteration algorithms. QVI-1 replaces the classical max-over-actions step in Bellman recursion with quantum maximum searching (QMS), reducing per-state cost from O(A) to O(√A) and achieving S²√(AH) query complexity. QVI-2 further introduces QMEBO, a novel subroutine that converts a binary oracle encoding a probability distribution into an amplitude oracle via controlled rotations, enabling O(√S) mean estimation of P_{h|s,a}^T V instead of O(S), yielding S^{1.5}√(AH³)/ε complexity. In the generative model setting, QVI-3 applies quantum mean estimation (QME1) to estimate expected next-state values with O(√H) quantum samples per state-action pair, combined with QMS, achieving S√A·H³/ε sample complexity. QVI-4 additionally incorporates variance reduction (K=O(log(H/ε)) epochs with halving error) and a total variance technique adapted to the quantum setting using QME2 with estimated variance bounds, reaching SAH^{2.5}/ε. A monotonicity technique (subtracting one-sided error) ensures value functions remain bounded, and quantum lower bounds prove asymptotic optimality up to log factors for constant H.

## Results

In the exact dynamics setting, QVI-1 achieves a quantum query complexity of Õ(S²√(AH)) for computing the optimal policy and V-value function, compared to the classical value iteration bound of O(S²AH), yielding a quadratic speedup in the action space size A. QVI-2 further reduces the state-space dependence to S^1.5, achieving Õ(S^1.5√(AH³)/ε) for ε-optimal solutions. In the generative model setting, QVI-3 achieves Õ(S√A·H³/ε) quantum samples and QVI-4 achieves Õ(SAH^2.5/ε), both improving over the SOTA classical bound of Õ(SAH⁴/ε²) in H and ε, with QVI-3 additionally achieving a quadratic speedup in A. Quantum lower bounds of Ω̃(S√A·H^1.5/ε) confirm that QVI-3 and QVI-4 are asymptotically optimal up to log factors for constant H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical value iteration algorithm of Puterman (2014) with O(S²AH) query complexity, QVI-1 achieves a quadratic speedup in A (reducing A to √A) while maintaining the same S² dependence, and QVI-2 further reduces the state-space dependence from S² to S^1.5 for near-optimal solutions. Against the SOTA classical generative model algorithm of Li et al. (2020) with Õ(SAH⁴/ε²) sample complexity, QVI-3 achieves a speedup in all three parameters A (quadratic), H (from H⁴ to H³), and ε (from ε⁻² to ε⁻¹), while QVI-4 achieves speedups in H (from H⁴ to H^2.5) and ε (from ε⁻² to ε⁻¹) but not in A. The classical lower bound of Ω(SAH³/ε²) from Sidford et al. (2018) is matched by the quantum lower bound of Ω̃(S√A·H^1.5/ε) for the policy/V-value goal, confirming that QVI-3's S√A·H³/ε upper bound is within a factor of H of optimal. For the Q-value goal, the quantum lower bound Ω̃(SAH^1.5/ε) shows QVI-4's SAH^2.5/ε is within a factor of H of optimal. Overall, the quantum algorithms strictly dominate all classical baselines in their respective dependences on S, A, H, and ε, with the gap between upper and lower bounds being at most polynomial in H.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of quantum RL algorithms from infinite-horizon MDPs (Cherrat et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2021, Cornelissen 2018) to the more general finite-horizon and time-dependent setting, where value functions depend on the time step. It builds on classical variance-reduced value iteration (Sidford et al. 2018, 2023) by replacing classical sampling and maximization subroutines with quantum mean estimation (Montanaro 2015) and quantum maximum searching (Durr & Hoyer 1999). A key technical departure is the introduction of QMEBO, a novel quantum subroutine that performs mean estimation using binary oracles (which encode probabilities in quantum states rather than amplitudes), bridging a gap that prevented direct application of standard amplitude estimation to the exact-dynamics setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous treatment of quantum algorithms for finite-horizon, time-dependent MDPs, closing a gap where prior quantum RL work was restricted to infinite-horizon or time-invariant settings. The proven quantum lower bounds matching upper bounds up to logarithmic factors (for constant H) establish that the quantum speedups in A, S, H, and ε are essentially tight, giving a complete complexity picture. However, all results are purely theoretical with no experimental validation, and practical deployment requires fault-tolerant quantum hardware with QRAM access, which remains years away. Open questions include whether the H-dependent gaps between upper and lower bounds can be closed, and whether the qubit overhead (particularly the O(S) queries for constructing oracles in QVI-1) can be further reduced.
