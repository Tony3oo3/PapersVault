## Problem

Transformer world model (TWM) based MBRL agents still underperform on complex open-world benchmarks like Craftax-classic, where no prior agent had surpassed human-level reward. The paper identifies three specific weaknesses in existing TWM-based MBRL pipelines: policies trained exclusively on imagined trajectories are brittle, VQ-VAE tokenizers produce non-stationary codebooks that hinder TWM learning, and fully autoregressive teacher forcing causes compounding drift within each timestep.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a 63×63×3 RGB observation (a local 9×9 grid of 7×7 pixel patches showing the map, agent health, and inventory) and outputs one of 17 discrete actions. The TWM additionally predicts the next timestep's 81 observation tokens (drawn from a codebook of up to 4096 entries), a binary reward, and a termination flag. The image is factorized into 81 non-overlapping patches that are independently encoded/decoded, and the TWM uses block causal attention to predict all 81 next-timestep tokens in a single parallel forward pass rather than sequentially.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary improvements to TWM-based MBRL, each verified via ablation. First, 'Dyna with warmup' trains the PPO policy on a mix of real environment trajectories and TWM-generated rollouts (reviving the original Dyna idea), but only begins imagination training after 200k environment steps to ensure the world model is sufficiently accurate. Second, a patch nearest-neighbor tokenizer (NNT) replaces the VQ-VAE: each 7×7 patch is matched to the nearest codebook entry, and if the distance exceeds a threshold (τ=0.75), the patch is added as a new code, yielding a static-but-growing codebook that keeps the TWM's target distribution stationary. Third, block teacher forcing (BTF) modifies both the supervision signal and the attention mask so that all L tokens of the next timestep are predicted jointly from the previous timestep's tokens (removing intra-timestep autoregressive conditioning), using a block causal attention pattern that enables parallel sampling and reduces autoregressive drift. The MFRL baseline itself is improved by adding a GRU memory module (256-dim hidden state) to the Impala ResNet CNN encoder, with the RNN output concatenated to the image embedding before the actor-critic heads.

## Results

On Craftax-classic after 1M environment steps, the best MBRL agent (M5: M4+BTF, slow) achieves a reward of 69.66±1.20% and a score of 31.77±1.43%, surpassing the human expert reward of 65.0±10.5% for the first time. The faster variant (M5 fast, with 500 TWM updates) reaches 67.42±0.55% reward in ~759 min vs. ~2749 min for the slow variant. The best MFRL agent (55.6M params) achieves 55.49±1.33% reward in just 15 min on a single A100, already outperforming DreamerV3 (53.2% reward, 201M params). On Craftax (full), the MBRL agent reaches 7.20% reward vs. 6.59% for the previous SOTA, and on MinAtar the MBRL agent achieves an average normalized reward of 2.41× over the MFRL baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The best MBRL agent (69.66% reward) outperforms DreamerV3 (53.2% reward) by 16.5 percentage points, IRIS (25.0%) by 44.7 points, Δ-IRIS (35.0%) by 34.7 points, and the previous best MFRL from Moon et al. (46.91%) by 22.8 points. It also exceeds the human expert reward of 65.0% for the first time, though it trails humans on score (31.77% vs. 50.5%). The MFRL agent alone (55.49% reward) already beats DreamerV3, IRIS, Δ-IRIS, and the previous best MFRL, while training in 15 min vs. DreamerV3's ~2100 min. On Craftax (full), the MBRL agent (7.20% reward) surpasses the previous SOTA M3 (Cohen et al., 2025) at 6.59%. On MinAtar, the MBRL agent significantly outperforms the MFRL baseline on all four games (e.g., Asterix: 44.81 vs. 7.47; Space Invaders: 186.16 vs. 131.9), confirming the generality of the three proposed improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer world model (TWM) line of MBRL work initiated by IRIS and extended by Δ-IRIS and DART, but makes three deliberate departures from the standard recipe. First, it revives the Dyna principle of mixing real and imagined trajectories for policy training—a technique largely abandoned in favor of pure imagination training in DreamerV3 and IRIS. Second, it replaces the learned VQ-VAE tokenizer with a static nearest-neighbor codebook over image patches, exploiting the grid structure of Craftax-classic. Third, it introduces block teacher forcing, which predicts all tokens within a timestep jointly rather than autoregressively, drawing a parallel to Retentive Environment Models but remaining architecture-agnostic. The MFRL baseline itself extends the Impala ResNet + PPO setup from Moon et al. (2024) by adding a GRU memory module and increasing model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first agent to surpass human-level reward on Craftax-classic (69.66% vs. 65.0%), and its three components—Dyna with warmup, patch NNT, and BTF—are individually simple and potentially transferable to other TWM-based MBRL systems. However, the score gap to humans remains large (31.77% vs. 50.5%), and the NNT's reliance on a known patch grid limits immediate applicability to non-grid environments. Open questions include whether the static-codebook idea generalizes to continuous or high-dimensional observation spaces, whether off-policy mixing of real and imaginary data can further improve sample efficiency, and whether pretrained visual encoders (SAM, DINOv2) can replace hand-crafted patch tokenization to reduce sensitivity to patch size.
