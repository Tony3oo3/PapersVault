## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM systems must maintain and optimize all Gaussians covering the entire scene in limited GPU memory throughout training to preserve geometry and color consistency across all previous views. This global optimization requirement caps the number of usable Gaussians and prevents scaling to extremely large scenes, degrading both rendering quality and tracking accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential RGB images and depth maps (from RGBD sensors) and produces per-frame camera poses, a scene representation of view-tied spherical Gaussians organized into sections of N consecutive frames, rendered RGB and depth images, and 3D meshes extracted via marching cubes from fused TSDF. A key structural detail is that only Gaussians in the current section and a selected overlapping section are held in GPU memory at any time, while the full scene representation spans tens of millions of Gaussians (e.g., ~98M on Replica) far exceeding what prior methods can store simultaneously.

## Method

The core representation is a view-tied 3D Gaussian: a simplified sphere (color, radius, opacity — 5 parameters vs. 14 in standard 3DGS) whose 3D position is determined solely by back-projecting a depth pixel through the camera pose, eliminating learned locations, rotations, and multi-dimensional variances and saving 64.3% storage per Gaussian. This memory efficiency allows initializing one Gaussian per valid depth pixel, yielding far denser local representations. Tracking and mapping operate section-by-section: for head frames, an overlapping section is selected via visibility-based candidate ranking and pre-tracking to anchor spatial consistency and prevent pose error cumulation; for regular frames, only the current section's Gaussians are rendered and optimized. Mapping initializes Gaussians at head frames and incrementally complements uncovered pixels at regular frames, optimizing only the current section's Gaussians against the latest view and a random in-section view. Bundle adjustment (joint pose and Gaussian optimization) is applied exclusively at head frames to stabilize the trajectory without the cost of per-frame global optimization.

## Results

On Replica, VTGaussian-SLAM achieves 43.34 dB PSNR / 0.996 SSIM / 0.012 LPIPS for rendering and 0.28 cm ATE RMSE for tracking, outperforming all non-prior-based 3DGS baselines. On TUM-RGBD, it reaches 30.20 dB PSNR (vs. 25.05 for Gaussian-SLAM) and 2.6 cm ATE RMSE. On ScanNet, it achieves 31.10 dB PSNR and 0.961 SSIM, and on ScanNet++ it attains the best tracking accuracy of 1.55 cm ATE RMSE, surpassing LoopSplat (2.05 cm) and Gaussian-SLAM (2.68 cm). The method uses ~97.8M total Gaussians across a scene while keeping only ~2.7M in GPU memory at any time, enabling scalability to large scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On rendering quality, VTGaussian-SLAM consistently outperforms all baselines across all three main datasets: it beats the strongest non-prior 3DGS method Gaussian-SLAM by 1.26 dB PSNR on Replica (43.34 vs. 42.08), 5.15 dB on TUM-RGBD (30.20 vs. 25.05), and 3.40 dB on ScanNet (31.10 vs. 27.70), with LPIPS reduced from 0.248 to 0.108 on ScanNet. On Replica tracking (ATE RMSE), the method achieves 0.28 cm, beating Gaussian-SLAM (0.31 cm), SplaTAM (0.36 cm), and GS-SLAM (0.50 cm), though it is slightly behind LoopSplat (0.26 cm) and CG-SLAM (0.27 cm), both of which rely on pre-trained data-driven priors for loop closure. On TUM-RGBD tracking, it achieves 2.6 cm, outperforming Gaussian-SLAM (2.9 cm) and SplaTAM (3.3 cm) but trailing CG-SLAM (2.0 cm) and LoopSplat (2.3 cm) which use priors. On ScanNet tracking, the method (11.3 cm) underperforms compared to Loopy-SLAM (7.7 cm) and LoopSplat (7.7 cm), both using priors, and also compared to CG-SLAM (8.1 cm). On ScanNet++, it achieves the best tracking accuracy of 1.55 cm, beating LoopSplat (2.05 cm) and Gaussian-SLAM (2.68 cm) without any priors. For reconstruction on Replica, it achieves 0.53 cm D-L1 and 90.0% F1, comparable to LoopSplat (0.51 cm, 90.4%) and Gaussian-SLAM (0.68 cm, 88.9%), while Point-SLAM (0.44 cm, 89.8%) benefits from ground-truth depth input as an unfair advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) but departs from it in two key ways: it ties each Gaussian to a depth pixel (eliminating learnable positions) and simplifies ellipsoids to spheres (removing rotations and multi-dimensional variances), reducing per-Gaussian storage by 64.3%. It also introduces a section-based organization where only Gaussians from N consecutive frames are jointly optimized, contrasting with prior 3DGS-SLAM methods (SplaTAM, Gaussian-SLAM, GS-SLAM, LoopSplat) that maintain and optimize all scene Gaussians globally throughout training. The tracking strategy of rendering from a selected overlapping section for head frames (rather than the current section) to prevent pose error cumulation is a novel design not present in prior 3DGS-SLAM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view-tied Gaussian representation enables ~17× more Gaussians (97.8M vs 5.8M for SplaTAM) within the same GPU memory budget, which is the key enabler for scaling 3DGS-based SLAM to much larger scenes including city-level KITTI sequences. The section-based approach removes the need for global map consistency maintenance and loop closure, simplifying the system but also limiting its ability to correct accumulated drift over very long trajectories. Open questions include whether the section-based strategy can be combined with lightweight loop closure for truly large-scale deployment, and whether the approach extends to monocular or stereo settings where depth is not directly available.
