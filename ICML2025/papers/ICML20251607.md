## Problem

Existing TabPFN variants improve performance by addressing either bias (e.g., fine-tuning, KNN-based context selection) or variance (e.g., ensembling, bagging), but rarely both simultaneously, and often at the cost of inference efficiency or scalability. The paper identifies this gap through a bias-variance decomposition analysis and shows that no prior method reduces both error components while preserving PFN-style batching and handling high-dimensional or large-scale datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251607/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BETA accepts a labeled tabular training set of arbitrary feature dimensionality d (unlike TabPFN's fixed 100-feature limit) and unlabeled test samples, both of which are first mapped through a lightweight encoder into a fixed 100-dimensional latent space before being concatenated into a single PFN-style prompt. The output is a probability distribution over class labels obtained by averaging predictions across K encoder paths, each conditioned on its own bootstrapped subset of the training set. For multiclass tasks exceeding 10 classes, the method switches to an ECOC scheme with 32 binary encoder paths, each producing a single-bit code that is decoded into the final class.

## Method

BETA introduces a two-stage adaptation of frozen TabPFN. In the fine-tuning stage, a lightweight two-layer MLP encoder (with ReLU and periodic activation) is prepended to TabPFN and trained end-to-end to align downstream feature distributions with the pre-trained prior, reducing bias; Batch Ensemble replaces the encoder's linear layers with shared-weight, member-specific scaling parameters, yielding K diverse encoder paths (default K=16) at minimal parameter overhead, which reduces variance through representation diversity. In the inference stage, bootstrapped sampling generates K random subsets of the training set as support contexts, and the final prediction averages the posterior distributions from all K encoder–bootstrap pairs, further reducing variance without additional model evaluations. The entire pipeline preserves PFN-style batching (training and test tokens in one forward pass), enabling efficient scaling to large datasets and high-dimensional features, while the ECOC extension handles multiclass tasks beyond TabPFN's 10-class limit.

## Results

On 186 TALENT classification datasets (fewer than 10 classes), BETA achieves the best overall performance via Wilcoxon-Holm significance testing, outperforming all compared methods without hyperparameter tuning. On 20 high-dimensional datasets (up to 22,283 features), BETA attains the lowest average rank, surpassing TabM, MLP, and all tree-based methods. On 12 multiclass datasets with more than 10 classes, BETA achieves the highest mean accuracy among all compared methods. BETA reduces bias by 4.40% (Adult) and 1.65% (Bank) while reducing variance by 16.23% (Adult) and 36.57% (Bank) relative to TabPFN, with an inference time of only 0.65s and a 0.60 MB checkpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BETA consistently outperforms the original TabPFN across all 186 TALENT datasets, with relative improvement increasing as dataset size grows (Figure 5), and dramatically on high-dimensional data where TabPFN cannot operate at all (e.g., Dataset 17: BETA 81.37% vs. TabPFN 75.99%). Against tree-based methods (XGBoost, CatBoost, Random Forest, LightGBM), BETA wins on the majority of datasets but trails on a few where boosted trees excel (e.g., Dataset 25: CatBoost 90.82% vs. BETA 89.90%). BETA outperforms LocalPFN on most datasets but is slightly outperformed on the very largest ones; however, BETA's inference time (0.65s) is ~19× faster than LocalPFN (12.51s) with a 0.60 MB checkpoint vs. 98.65 MB. Against TabM-16 (the closest ensemble-based competitor), BETA wins on several datasets (e.g., Dataset 18: 87.94% vs. 86.75%; Dataset 11: 98.80% vs. 98.62%) while being competitive on others. On the 12 multiclass datasets with >10 classes, BETA achieves the highest mean accuracy, outperforming XGBoost, Random Forest, MLP, ModernNCA, and TabM, where tree-based methods show notable degradation. In the Appendix D.3 comparison, BETA (average rank 4.63) is slightly behind TabPFN-v2 (4.07) and TabICL (4.40) in their full configurations, but substantially outperforms their standardized subsampled variants (TabPFN-v2-S: 5.83, TabICL-S: 5.84) and all classical baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023) as a frozen backbone, integrating Batch Ensemble (Wen et al., 2020) into a lightweight input encoder and ECOC (Dietterich & Bakiri, 1995) for multiclass handling. Its key conceptual departure is the use of Nagler's (2023) bias-variance decomposition to systematically categorize existing TabPFN variants, revealing that prior work (TuneTables, LocalPFN, MixturePFN, TabForestPFN) addresses either bias or variance but rarely both. BETA positions itself as the first method to jointly reduce both components while preserving PFN-style batching, in contrast to context-selection methods that disrupt inference efficiency or full fine-tuning approaches that incur high computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias-variance lens provides a useful diagnostic framework for understanding why various TabPFN variants succeed or fail, and the practical result is a method that extends TabPFN's applicability to high-dimensional features, larger datasets, and >10-class problems without sacrificing inference speed. However, the approach remains limited to classification (regression is explicitly left open), and the concurrent release of TabPFN-v2 partially addresses the same scalability limitations, reducing the urgency of BETA's contributions. An open question is whether the encoder-based alignment strategy generalizes to other tabular foundation models beyond TabPFN, and whether the bootstrapped sampling benefit persists as context sizes grow with future model generations.
