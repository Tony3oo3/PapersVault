## Problem

Existing defenses against harmful fine-tuning attacks on safety-aligned LLMs—whether applied at the alignment stage (e.g., Vaccine, RepNoise) or the fine-tuning stage (e.g., Lisa, LDIFS)—fail when the fine-tuning uses a large learning rate or many epochs, which are often necessary for good downstream task accuracy. The paper identifies this as the 'hyper-parameter sensitivity issue' and asks whether a defense can be agnostic to fine-tuning hyper-parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Antidote operates entirely post-fine-tuning: it takes the already-corrupted fine-tuned model weights, a re-alignment dataset of harmful prompt–harmful answer pairs (e.g., 2000 samples from BeaverTails with is_safe=False), and a mask ratio α (default 0.2, 0.05 for GSM8K). It produces a single pruned model ready for deployment, with no additional training loop. The Wanda score is computed as the mean over the re-alignment dataset of |w_j|·‖A_j(x,w)‖₂ for each weight coordinate, and only the top-α fraction of coordinates are zeroed out in one shot.

## Method

Antidote applies a one-shot pruning step after the user fine-tuning stage is fully completed, making it agnostic to the learning rate, number of epochs, or any other hyper-parameter used during fine-tuning. It computes Wanda importance scores (borrowed from the model-sparsification literature) over a harmful re-alignment dataset to identify the weight coordinates most responsible for generating harmful content, then extracts a binary mask via ArgTopK_α that selects the top-α fraction of coordinates. The harmful parameters are removed by a single Hadamard product: w̃ = (1−m) ⊙ w. The key insight is that regardless of how harmful parameters were formed during fine-tuning, removing them recovers the model's safety alignment because the alignment knowledge is distributed across the remaining weights. Empirically, this reduces harmful score by up to 17.8% while incurring at most ~1.83% accuracy loss on downstream tasks, with negligible system overhead (1.02× total clock time, same peak GPU memory).

## Results

On the default setting (SST2, p=0.2, lr=1e-4, 20 epochs), Antidote achieves an average harmful score of 60.88, reducing it by 11.56% compared to SFT (73.52) while incurring only a 1.45% finetune accuracy loss (93.05 vs. 95.00). Across four fine-tuning datasets (SST2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), Antidote reduces the average harmful score by 11.75% with a 3.08% accuracy loss. On different backbone models, Antidote achieves 11.6%, 20.0%, and 22.5% harmful score reduction on Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, and Gemma-7B respectively, with at most 1.72% accuracy degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Antidote outperforms all four baselines on average harmful score: it achieves 60.88 vs. SFT (73.52), Vaccine (73.68), RepNoise (72.32), LDIFS (66.46), and Lisa (63.80), representing reductions of 11.56%, 12.8%, 11.44%, 5.58%, and 2.92% respectively. Against the two fine-tuning-stage defenses (Lisa and LDIFS), Antidote achieves the best harmful score while maintaining comparable finetune accuracy (93.05% vs. Lisa's 93.12% and LDIFS's 93.53%). The critical differentiator is hyper-parameter robustness: when learning rate is increased to 1e-3 or epochs to 40, Lisa and LDIFS degrade significantly (e.g., Lisa's FA drops to 24.7% at lr=1e-3 on GSM8K), whereas Antidote remains stable because it operates post-fine-tuning and is agnostic to training hyper-parameters. The alignment-stage defenses (Vaccine, RepNoise) show minimal improvement over SFT in most settings, with Vaccine actually performing worse than SFT on average harmful score (73.68 vs. 73.52). Antidote's one-shot pruning incurs only 1.02x total pipeline time and the same peak GPU memory as SFT, compared to 1.54–1.62x time and 1.71–2.12x memory for the other defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant lines of defense against harmful fine-tuning—alignment-stage hardening (Vaccine, RepNoise) and fine-tuning-stage regularization (Lisa, LDIFS)—by introducing a post-fine-tuning stage solution that is agnostic to training hyper-parameters. The core mechanism repurposes the Wanda importance score from the model sparsification literature (Sun et al., 2023) in a novel safety-alignment context: rather than compressing the model, the top-k Wanda-scored weights (computed on a harmful dataset) are identified as 'harmful parameters' and zeroed out in a single pruning step. The paper also positions itself against a growing set of concurrent post-fine-tuning realignment methods (RESTA, LAT, Safe LoRA, SOMF, NLSR, SafetyLock, Panacea), distinguishing itself by being the first to systematically characterize the hyper-parameter sensitivity weakness shared by prior defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyper-parameter sensitivity finding is practically significant: service providers cannot always constrain users' fine-tuning hyper-parameters, and the paper shows that large learning rates or many epochs—often necessary for downstream task accuracy—break all existing defenses. Antidote's one-shot pruning is computationally cheap (1.02× total pipeline time, same GPU memory) and requires no modification to the alignment or fine-tuning stages, making it easy to deploy as a post-hoc safety check. Limitations include the need for a curated harmful dataset to compute Wanda scores, a non-trivial accuracy trade-off (up to ~3% on some tasks), and the fact that the mask ratio α must be manually tuned per task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more sophisticated attacks (e.g., covert malicious fine-tuning), whether it works with full fine-tuning rather than LoRA, and whether the pruning can be made adaptive or learned rather than one-shot.
