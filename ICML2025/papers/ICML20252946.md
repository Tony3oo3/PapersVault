## Problem

Variance reduction techniques (SVRG, STORM, SPIDER) have proven effective in accelerating stochastic optimization for convex and nonconvex problems, yet they have failed to find widespread success in training deep neural networks or large language models. The paper asks whether variance reduction can be meaningfully integrated into adaptive gradient methods to improve large-model training, noting that the typical obstacles (data augmentation, batch normalization, dropout) are largely absent in modern LLM pretraining.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, MARS consumes the current model parameters x_t, the previous parameters x_{t-1}, and a fresh data batch ξ_t to compute two stochastic gradients (at x_t and x_{t-1} with the same ξ_t) for the variance-reduction correction term. In the practical MARS-approx variant, the second gradient is computed with ξ_{t-1} instead to avoid a double forward-backward pass. The output is an updated parameter vector x_{t+1} produced by a preconditioned mirror-descent step, with the preconditioner H_t varying across the three instantiations (diagonal EMA for AdamW, sign-based for Lion, SVD/eigenspace for Shampoo).

## Method

MARS unifies preconditioned gradient methods with variance reduction through a scaled stochastic recursive momentum (STORM) estimator c_t = ∇f(x_t,ξ_t) + γ_t·β₁/(1−β₁)·(∇f(x_t,ξ_t) − ∇f(x_{t−1},ξ_t)), where the scaling parameter γ_t ∈ [0,1] controls the strength of the gradient correction. The estimator undergoes clipping-by-norm before feeding into an exponential moving average for the first-order momentum, and the second-order momentum v_t is redefined as the EMA of c_t² (rather than the raw gradient squared) to maintain correct per-coordinate scaling. Three instantiations—MARS-AdamW, MARS-Lion, and MARS-Shampoo—plug in different preconditioners (diagonal EMA, sign, and SVD-based eigenspace, respectively), and the paper shows that Lion and Muon emerge as special cases of MARS-Lion and MARS-Shampoo under specific γ_t choices. Theoretically, MARS achieves an O(T^{−1/3}) convergence rate under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, improving on the O(T^{−1/4}) rate of AdamW, and empirically reduces the tokens needed to reach a target validation loss on GPT-2 large from 50B (AdamW) to 28B.

## Results

On GPT-2 large (770M) pre-trained on OpenWebText, MARS-AdamW achieves the best average 5-shot benchmark score of 50.15%, outperforming AdamW (48.64%), Lion (48.61%), and Muon (47.31%). In terms of validation loss, MARS-AdamW reaches 2.511 versus 2.568 for AdamW, and requires only 28 billion tokens to reach a validation loss of 2.58 where AdamW needs 50 billion tokens. MARS-Lion achieves an average of 49.80% and a best validation loss of 2.534, also surpassing all baselines. The performance gap widens with model scale, and MARS maintains lower validation loss than all baselines at equivalent wall-clock training times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS-AdamW outperforms AdamW by 1.51 points on average (50.15% vs. 48.64%), with the largest gains on BoolQ (+6.45) and HellaSwag (+3.11), while slightly trailing on ARC-C (26.28 vs. 28.67). Against Lion, MARS-AdamW leads by 1.54 points on average, driven primarily by BoolQ (+10.36) and HellaSwag (+2.25), though Lion retains an edge on WinoGrande (54.46 vs. 52.49). MARS-AdamW dominates Muon by 2.84 points on average, with Muon trailing on nearly every benchmark. MARS-Lion similarly beats all three baselines on average (49.80% vs. 48.64%, 48.61%, and 47.31%), with its strongest advantages over Muon on SciQ (+6.40) and PIQA (+2.45). In wall-clock time comparisons, MARS-AdamW and MARS-Lion incur only a slight per-iteration overhead over AdamW but achieve consistently lower validation loss at equivalent training durations, while Muon is substantially slower per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS unifies preconditioned adaptive gradient methods (AdamW, Lion, Shampoo) with variance reduction via a scaled STORM momentum, filling a gap left by prior work: Defazio & Bottou (2019) argued variance reduction fails in deep learning due to data augmentation, batch normalization, and dropout, but LLM pretraining largely omits these, making the finite-sum structure amenable to variance reduction. The closest prior work, SuperAdam (Huang et al., 2021), also integrates STORM into Adam but suffers from a mismatch between its first-order and second-order momentum definitions and lacks the scaling parameter and gradient clipping that MARS introduces. The paper further reveals that Lion and Muon are special cases of MARS-Lion and MARS-Shampoo respectively, providing a unifying lens on recent optimizer designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44% token-efficiency gain on GPT-2 large (28B vs 50B tokens to reach validation loss 2.58) and the theoretical improvement from O(T^{-1/4}) to O(T^{-1/3}) convergence rate make MARS a potentially significant cost-reduction tool for LLM pretraining. However, all experiments are limited to GPT-2 (≤770M parameters) on OpenWebText, leaving open whether the gains persist at 7B+ scale where optimization dynamics differ substantially. The MARS-approx variant, which avoids the double-gradient computation of exact STORM, performs nearly identically in practice, making the method practical without extra forward passes. A key open question is whether the variance-reduction benefit scales with model size or whether the high-variance regime that motivates it changes character at larger scales.
