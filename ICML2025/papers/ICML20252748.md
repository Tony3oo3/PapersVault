## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses treat all misclassifications as equally harmful, an assumption that fails in safety-critical domains where, e.g., misclassifying a malignant tumor as benign is far more dangerous than the reverse. Prior cost-sensitive robustness methods either rely on heuristics without formal guarantees or use convex relaxations that do not scale to deep networks and large datasets. This paper is the first to provide a scalable, provably robust certification and training framework for cost-sensitive adversarial defense built on randomized smoothing.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 160×160 Imagenette, 224×224 ImageNet, 299×299 HAM10k) and an m×m non-negative cost matrix C specifying the harm of each seed-to-target misclassification pair. At inference, Gaussian noise (σ=0.5 by default) is injected into the input before classification. The output is a predicted class label together with a cost-sensitive certified radius—either groupwise (protecting against all cost-incurring target classes simultaneously) or pairwise (protecting against a single target class)—computed via Monte Carlo sampling with (1−α) confidence bounds, guaranteeing no adversarial perturbation within that ℓ₂ radius can induce a cost-incurring misclassification.

## Method

The paper introduces two cost-sensitive certified radii (groupwise and pairwise) that are provably larger than the standard randomized-smoothing radius whenever the set of cost-sensitive target classes Ω_y is a proper subset of all non-ground-truth classes. The certification algorithm replaces the standard runner-up upper bound p_B = 1 − p_A with a tighter union-bound-based upper confidence bound computed separately over each class in Ω_y, yielding tighter certificates without sacrificing statistical validity. For training, the proposed Margin-CS method approximates the certified radius using a soft-smoothed classifier G_θ(x) = E_δ[F_θ(x+δ)] and applies a hinge-based margin loss with class-specific thresholds γ₁ (normal examples) and γ₂ (sensitive examples), weighted by the cost matrix entries C_yj. This fine-grained thresholding allows the optimizer to prioritize expanding the certified radius for the most consequential misclassification subpopulations while preserving overall accuracy, outperforming both standard robust training baselines and simple reweighting adaptations by ~20% in certified cost-sensitive robustness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=0.5, σ=0.5), Margin-CS achieves 54.8% certified cost-sensitive robustness (Rob_cs) in the S-Seed setting, outperforming the best standard baseline SmoothAdv (27.1%) by ~20 percentage points while maintaining 67.5% certified overall accuracy. In the S-Pair setting, Margin-CS reaches 92.4% Rob_cs with only 0.05 robust cost, compared to 80.4% and 0.31 for the best adaptive baseline SmoothAdv-CS. On the real-world HAM10k medical dataset, Margin-CS achieves 34.4% Rob_cs and 41.3% recall for malignant detection, substantially exceeding the best baseline MACER (21.1% Rob_cs, 25.0% recall) while matching overall accuracy (83.2% vs 82.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Margin-CS consistently outperforms all four standard randomized smoothing baselines (Gaussian, SmoothMix, SmoothAdv, MACER) across every cost-sensitive scenario on both CIFAR-10 and Imagenette, with the largest gains in the S-Pair setting (92.4% vs 57.1% Rob_cs on CIFAR-10). Among the reweighting-adapted baselines (Gaussian-CS, SmoothMix-CS, SmoothAdv-CS), Margin-CS still leads in Rob_cs and Rob_cost while also achieving the highest certified overall accuracy, indicating that targeted margin optimization is more effective than simple sample reweighting. Against the only prior cost-sensitive certification method (Zhang & Evans, ICLR 2019), Margin-CS achieves comparable or higher Rob_cs (93.5% vs 92.4% in S-Pair) while maintaining 80.9% accuracy versus 61.2%, and reduces robust cost by 14–45% in unequal-cost M-Pair settings. On the HAM10k medical dataset, Margin-CS improves Rob_cs by 63% over the best baseline (34.4% vs 21.1% for MACER) and nearly doubles recall for malignant detection (41.3% vs 25.0%), a critical metric for clinical safety. The paper notes that Zhang & Evans' convex relaxation approach is infeasible for large datasets like Imagenette and HAM10k, limiting its practical applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing certification framework (Cohen et al., 2019) to the cost-sensitive setting, where a cost matrix encodes unequal harm from different misclassification types. It departs from the only prior certified cost-sensitive robustness work (Zhang & Evans, 2019), which relies on convex relaxation and is infeasible for deep networks or large datasets, by showing that randomized smoothing can be adapted to produce tighter certificates when only a subset of target classes is costly. On the training side, it builds on certified-radius optimization methods (MACER, Zhai et al. 2020) but introduces a cost-weighted margin loss that targets specific data subpopulations defined by the cost matrix, going beyond the reweighting adaptations used in prior cost-sensitive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cost-sensitive framing is more realistic for safety-critical deployments (e.g., missing a malignant tumor vs. false-alarming on a benign one), and this paper provides the first scalable certification and training pipeline that respects such asymmetries. A key limitation is the worst-case quadratic scaling of the certification algorithm with the number of sensitive target classes, though in practice cost matrices are typically sparse. Open questions include whether the margin-based training strategy transfers to other certification paradigms (e.g., interval bound propagation) and whether the approach handles multi-label or hierarchical cost structures beyond the pairwise matrix formulation.
