## Problem

Existing parameter-space Lipschitz bounds for neural networks suffer from two critical limitations: they are not invariant under the neuron-wise rescaling symmetry of ReLU networks (making them arbitrarily pessimistic under benign weight rescalings), and they only apply to plain layered fully-connected MLPs, excluding modern architectures with skip connections, pooling, convolutions, and batch normalization. This paper addresses both gaps by proving a Lipschitz bound expressed through the ℓ¹-path-metric that is rescaling-invariant by construction and valid for any ReLU DAG architecture.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the full parameter vector θ of any ReLU DAG network (ResNets, VGGs, U-Nets, etc.) together with the DAG architecture specification. The Lipschitz bound and the Path-Mag pruning scores are computed via forward and reverse-mode autograd passes over the network, requiring no data or loss function. The pruning output is a binary mask s ∈ {0,1}^G identifying which weights to zero out, applied via Hadamard product θ' = s ⊙ θ; the bound itself is a scalar upper bound on ‖R_θ(x) − R_{θ'}(x)‖₁ for any input x, computable in two forward passes under practical conditions (pruning or zero-rounding quantization).

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for any ReLU DAG network and parameters θ, θ' with matching entrywise signs, ‖R_θ(x) − R_{θ'}(x)‖₁ ≤ max(‖x‖∞, 1) · ‖Φ(θ) − Φ(θ')‖₁, where Φ is the path-lifting (a vector indexed by all input-to-output paths, each coordinate being the product of weights along that path). The proof constructs a log-space trajectory between θ and θ' whose path-lifting coordinates are monotone, causing the ℓ¹-norm to telescope across finitely many activation-change breakpoints. For pruning, the paper defines Path-Mag(θ, i) = ‖Φ(θ) − Φ(θ_{-i})‖₁, which under the coordinatewise dominance condition |Φ(θ)| ≥ |Φ(θ_{-i})| (satisfied by pruning and zero-rounding quantization) reduces to ‖Φ(θ)‖₁ − ‖Φ(θ_{-i})‖₁, computable in two forward passes or via a single reverse-mode autograd pass as θ ⊙ ∇_θ ‖Φ(θ)‖₁. The pruning rule is reverse hard thresholding on these scores, yielding a rescaling-invariant criterion that matches magnitude-pruning accuracy on ResNet-18/ImageNet while being immune to arbitrary neuron-wise rescalings.

## Results

On a ResNet-18 trained on ImageNet-1k (original top-1: 67.7%), path-magnitude pruning achieves 68.6%, 67.9%, and 66.0% top-1 accuracy at 40%, 60%, and 80% sparsity respectively, matching classical magnitude pruning (68.8%, 68.2%, 66.5%) within 0.5 percentage points. Crucially, path-magnitude is completely invariant to random neuron-wise rescaling, while magnitude pruning after rescaling collapses to 63.1%, 57.5%, and 15.8% at the same sparsity levels. Scoring all weights via path-magnitude takes 32 ms on ResNet-18, comparable to a single forward pass (3.6–142 ms) and substantially faster than OBD (51–155 ms).

| Path-magnitude | 68.6 | 67.9 | 66.0 |
| Magnitude | 68.8 | 68.2 | 66.5 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2016), path-magnitude ties on the un-rescaled network (within 0.5 pp at all sparsity levels) but gains a decisive advantage under rescaling: at 80% sparsity, magnitude-after-rescaling drops to 15.8% (a 50.7 pp collapse) while path-magnitude remains at 66.0%, demonstrating that the rescaling-invariance is not merely cosmetic. Against OBD / loss-sensitivity pruning (LeCun et al., 1989), path-magnitude is faster (32 ms vs. 51–155 ms on ResNet-18) and is rescaling-invariant by construction, whereas OBD is only invariant in exact arithmetic; the practical Hutchinson-based Hessian approximation used to compute OBD scores breaks invariance, as the paper explicitly demonstrates. The paper does not report OBD accuracy numbers in the main table, so the comparison is limited to runtime and invariance properties rather than end-to-end pruning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the path-lifting framework introduced by Stock & Gribonval (2023) and developed into a practical toolkit by Gonon et al. (2024a), which provides a rescaling-invariant coordinate system for ReLU networks. It departs from prior parameter-space Lipschitz bounds (Gonon et al. 2023; Neyshabur et al. 2018; Berner et al. 2020) that were restricted to plain layered MLPs and used standard ℓ^p norms on weights, by proving the first such bound expressed through the ℓ¹-path-metric that holds for arbitrary DAG-ReLU architectures (ResNets, U-Nets, CNNs with pooling and skip connections). The pruning application extends the classical magnitude-pruning and loss-sensitivity (OBD) criteria by introducing a path-magnitude score that inherits rescaling invariance from the path-lifting while retaining a provable error bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rescaling-invariance property is not merely cosmetic: the paper demonstrates that magnitude pruning applied after a benign neuron-wise rescaling can catastrophically fail (68.8% → 15.8% top-1 at 80% sparsity on ResNet-18), while the path-magnitude criterion is immune. The impossibility result showing that no finite Lipschitz constant exists once two weights change sign is a clean theoretical contribution that clarifies the boundary of what path-lifting-based bounds can achieve. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single architecture (ResNet-18), a single dataset (ImageNet), and one-shot pruning; the authors explicitly leave iterative/greedy extensions, comparison with CHITA, and applications to quantization and generalization as future work. The broader impact lies in providing a theoretically grounded, symmetry-respecting metric that could underpin future compression and generalization analyses for modern architectures.
