## Problem

Existing world models for visual control either require online data collection (making them task-specific and needing retraining per task) or, in the offline setting, depend on auxiliary information such as expert demonstrations, dense reward functions, or pretrained inverse models that compromise generality. The central question is whether a world model can be trained purely on offline trajectories and then used for zero-shot, task-agnostic planning at test time without any additional task-specific signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253185/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the system receives a current 224×224 RGB observation and a goal 224×224 RGB observation, both encoded by a frozen DINOv2 model into 14×14 spatial patch feature maps of dimension 384. Planning is performed entirely in this latent patch-feature space via MPC with CEM, producing a sequence of K-dimensional actions to execute. An optional transposed-convolution decoder can reconstruct predicted frames for visualization but is not involved in planning or training of the dynamics model.

## Method

DINO-WM replaces the learned observation encoder with frozen DINOv2 patch features, providing a spatially structured, object-centric representation that is task- and environment-independent. The transition model is a decoder-only ViT (6 layers, 16 heads, ~19M parameters) that takes a history of H past latent frames and corresponding actions, and autoregressively predicts the next frame's patch features using frame-level causal attention (each patch attends to all patches from prior frames, unlike IRIS's token-level attention). Actions are mapped through an MLP and concatenated to each patch vector for conditioning. Training uses teacher forcing with a simple MSE loss between predicted and ground-truth DINOv2 patch features, entirely in latent space with no pixel reconstruction objective. At test time, planning is formulated as minimizing the MSE between the predicted final latent state and the goal's latent state, optimized via CEM within an MPC loop that re-plans at each step.

## Results

On the six main control environments, DINO-WM achieves 0.98 SR on Maze, 0.96 SR on Wall, 0.92 SR on Reach, 0.90 SR on PushT, 0.41 Chamfer Distance on Rope, and 0.26 CD on Granular. The most dramatic gains appear in manipulation and deformable-object tasks: DINO-WM improves PushT success rate from 0.30–0.32 (IRIS/DreamerV3) to 0.90, and reduces Rope CD from 1.11–2.49 to 0.41. On the harder generalization suites (WallRandom, PushObj, GranularRandom), DINO-WM again leads with 0.82 SR, 0.34 SR, and 0.63 CD respectively. In terms of prediction quality, DINO-WM achieves LPIPS of 0.007 on PushT and 0.035 on Granular, a 56% improvement over prior state-of-the-art on the hardest tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINO-WM outperforms all four baselines on the majority of tasks, with the largest margins in manipulation and deformable-object settings. Against IRIS, DINO-WM wins on all six environments, most notably on PushT (0.90 vs. 0.32 SR) and Rope (0.41 vs. 1.11 CD). Against DreamerV3, DINO-WM wins on Reach (0.92 vs. 0.64), PushT (0.90 vs. 0.30), Rope (0.41 vs. 2.49), and Granular (0.26 vs. 1.05), while DreamerV3 holds a marginal edge on the simpler Maze (1.00 vs. 0.98) and Wall (1.00 vs. 0.96) tasks. TD-MPC2 fails entirely (0.00 SR on all navigation/manipulation tasks) because its latent representations depend on reward signals that are unavailable in the offline, task-agnostic setting. Against AVDC (a diffusion-based generative video model), DINO-WM produces substantially more physically plausible predictions, achieving LPIPS of 0.007 vs. 0.046 on PushT and 0.035 vs. 0.106 on Granular, while AVDC's open-loop rollouts diverge from ground truth over long horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

DINO-WM departs from the dominant online RL world model paradigm (DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) by training entirely on offline trajectories without reward signals, discount factors, or termination conditions, and from pixel-space world models (IRIS, AVDC) by operating exclusively in a frozen DINOv2 patch-feature latent space. The key architectural choice is replacing learned encoders with pretrained DINOv2 patch embeddings and a small causal-attention ViT transition model, decoupling the optional pixel decoder from the dynamics training objective. This positions the work at the intersection of pretrained visual representation learning and model-based control, showing that the spatial structure in DINOv2 features is sufficient for accurate latent dynamics prediction without task-specific supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a clean recipe—frozen pretrained features + small ViT + offline training + MPC/CEM at test time—that achieves zero-shot goal reaching across six environment families without expert demonstrations, reward functions, or inverse models, suggesting pretrained visual representations can serve as a universal substrate for world modeling. Key limitations include the reliance on offline data with sufficient state-action coverage, the need for ground-truth actions (ruling out internet video), and evaluation restricted to relatively simple 2D/tabletop environments rather than real-world robotics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous control, how to integrate exploration for data collection, and whether hierarchical planning (high-level goals + low-level policies) can extend the method to more fine-grained manipulation tasks.
