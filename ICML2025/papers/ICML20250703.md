## Problem

Existing safe linear bandit (SLB) methods guarantee low regret without constraint violations, but all require a priori knowledge of a safe initial action whose safety margin directly affects regret bounds. No prior work addresses how to discover such a point when the constraint matrix is unknown, leaving a critical gap in the applicability of these methods and hiding their true safety costs. This paper formalizes the Feasible Action Search (FAS) problem—finding a near-optimal safe point or detecting infeasibility for unknown linear constraints—and provides the first efficient solution.

## Input / Output

The learner operates over a known convex action space A ⊂ {a: ‖a‖ ≤ 1} and receives noisy constraint measurements S_t = Φ_* a_t + noise at each round, where Φ_* ∈ R^{m×d} is unknown. Given approximation parameter ε and confidence δ, the algorithm adaptively stops at time τ and either declares infeasibility (when M_* < 0) or outputs an ergodic-average action a_out = (1/τ)Σa_s with a certificate M_out satisfying M(a_out) ≥ M_out ≥ (1−ε)M_*, where M_* is the unknown optimal safety margin. The stopping time and safety cost S_τ = Σ(−min_i Φ_*^i a_s)_+ both adapt to the magnitude of M_* without requiring it as input.

## Method

FAST constructs a Thompson Sampling perturbation Φ̃_t = Φ̂_t + ω_t(δ) H_t V_t^{1/2} where H_t = 1_m ζ^⊤ applies an identical scaled spherical perturbation to every row of the constraint estimate, then plays a maximin point of the induced matrix game. The key technical novelty is a convexity-free analysis: since the multiobjective value K(Φ) = max_a min_λ λ^⊤Φa is nonconvex in Φ, the standard gradient-based TS analysis of Abeille & Lazaric (2017) fails, so the paper instead proves a tracking inequality via a local optimism event at the saddle point a_*, showing that the coupled noise design ensures P(optimism | history) ≥ 0.15 without convexity. A computable boundary process B_t(μ,δ) built from the elliptical potential lemma and a law of iterated logarithms yields upper/lower confidence bounds U_t, L_t on M_*, driving a reliable stopping rule that halts when U_t < 0 (infeasible) or L_t > (1−ε)U_t > 0 (feasible with certificate). This yields τ = Õ(d³/(ε²M_*²)) rounds and safety cost S_τ = Õ(d³/M_*), and when combined with LC-LUCB produces the first SLB method achieving Õ(√(d³T/M_*²)) regret at O(1) safety cost without any a priori safe action.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 9) shows that FAST stops in Õ(d³/ε²M*²) rounds in the feasible case (or Õ(d³/|M*|²) in the infeasible case) while incurring a safety cost of Õ(d³/M* + d^{5/2}√log(m/δ)/(εM*)). Instantiating prior SLB methods (LC-LUCB) with FAST's output (Corollary 10) yields the first SLB algorithm without a priori safe-action knowledge that achieves Õ(√(d³T/M*²)) regret and O(1) total safety cost. In the simulation comparison (d=6, m=2, M*=0.4), FAST's stopping time is approximately 27× smaller than EOGT's, and each round is approximately 100× faster computationally.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is EOGT (Gangrade et al., 2024b), a computationally intractable feasibility test that requires solving an NP-hard program per round (or Ω((2d)^m) matrix games under ℓ₁-relaxation). In the paper's simulation (d=6, m=2, M*=0.4), FAST achieves a stopping time ~27× smaller than EOGT while each round is ~100× faster, making FAST both statistically and computationally superior even for small m. Theoretically, FAST recovers the same Õ(d²/(εM*)²) stopping behaviour as unrelaxed EOGT up to an extra factor of d, which is inherent to all known efficient linear bandit methods. For the downstream SLB problem, the two-phase method (FAST + LC-LUCB) improves upon prior hard-enforcement methods by a factor of M*/M(a_safe) in regret, which can be arbitrarily large when the a priori safe action has small margin, while incurring only O(1) total safety cost independent of T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Thompson Sampling analysis of Abeille & Lazaric (2017) from single-objective linear bandits (where the value function is convex) to a multiobjective maximin setting where the value function K(Φ) is nonconvex, replacing the gradient-based convexity argument with a saddle-point-based local optimism analysis. It also builds directly on the EOGT feasibility test of Gangrade et al. (2024b), replacing its intractable nonconvex optimization (NP-hard even with convex confidence sets) with an efficient LP-time-per-round procedure. The coupled noise design—applying an identical spherical perturbation to all constraint rows rather than independent per-row perturbations—is a key departure from natural extensions of single-objective TS, motivated by the observation that independent perturbations yield only 2^{-Ω(m)} optimism rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAST eliminates the a priori safe-action assumption that all prior hard-enforcement SLB methods require, yielding the first SLB algorithm with Õ(√(d³T/M*²)) regret and O(1) safety cost without knowing a safe point in advance. The convexity-free TS analysis via local optimism events is a reusable technique that could apply to other bandit problems with nonconvex value functions (e.g., constrained or multi-objective settings). Open questions include proving reliability under the smaller practical noise (N(0, I_d/√d)) that simulations suggest works well, and whether the coupled noise design generalizes to settings where constraints have heterogeneous uncertainty. The d-factor gap between FAST's stopping time and the information-theoretic lower bound for m=1 remains unresolved.
