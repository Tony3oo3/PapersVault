## Problem

DreamerV3's compressed vector latent space loses crucial visual details in complex environments like Crafter, degrading agent perception and performance. Recent accurate world models (Δ-IRIS, DIAMOND) fix reconstruction quality but force the agent to learn from pixel reconstructions, forfeiting the benefits of latent-space representations such as long-term memory and efficient batch processing. The paper asks whether a world model can be simultaneously accurate in its spatial predictions and efficient in both decoding and agent training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250218/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64×3 RGB frames and a discrete action (17 actions in Crafter) at each timestep. The encoder produces a spatial latent of shape 4×4×32×32 (H×W×G groups of D/G categories), which is fundamentally different from DreamerV3's single 32-dimensional vector. The agent (actor-critic) operates on the combined state [z_t, h_t] in latent space rather than on reconstructed pixels, and the world model outputs predicted latent tokens via MaskGIT, a symlog-discrete reward, a Bernoulli continuation flag, and a 64×64×3 reconstructed image for the reconstruction loss.

## Method

EMERALD replaces DreamerV3's vector latent with a spatial latent (4×4 grid of 32 categorical groups) and a Transformer State-Space Model with masked self-attention and relative positional encodings for temporal hidden states. The key architectural contribution is a spatial MaskGIT predictor that uses a cosine mask schedule to predict latent tokens in parallel with S=3 scheduled refinement steps, replacing the autoregressive sequential decoding of IRIS/Δ-IRIS. This yields both higher prediction accuracy (spatial weight sharing provides position bias) and faster decoding (parallel token prediction). The actor-critic is trained entirely in latent space via 15-step imagination, allowing the agent to exploit the world model's internal temporal representations (long-term memory) without auxiliary pixel-space encoders. Truncated backpropagation through time with cached attention keys/values preserves long-range context during both training and imagination.

## Results

On the Crafter benchmark (10M environment steps), EMERALD achieves a 58.1% achievement score and 16.8±0.6 mean return, becoming the first method to surpass human expert performance (50.5% score, 14.3±2.3 return). EMERALD uses 30M parameters and collects 27 FPS on an RTX 3090, compared to 37M/38 FPS for DreamerV3(M), 200M/23 FPS for DreamerV3(XL), and 25M/12 FPS for Δ-IRIS. On Atari 100k, EMERALD achieves comparable aggregated performance to Δ-IRIS and DIAMOND while requiring only 17 hours to reach 400k steps on Breakout versus 27 hours (Δ-IRIS) and 75 hours (DIAMOND).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMERALD decisively outperforms all prior model-based methods on Crafter: it exceeds DreamerV3(M) by 23.2 percentage points (58.1% vs 34.9%), DreamerV3(XL) by 18.5 points, and Δ-IRIS by 15.6 points in achievement score, while also achieving the highest mean return (16.8 vs 16.1 for Δ-IRIS). Notably, EMERALD surpasses human expert performance (50.5%) by 7.6 points, a first on this benchmark, and unlocks all 22 Crafter achievements at least once. On Atari 100k, EMERALD achieves comparable aggregated scores to Δ-IRIS and DIAMOND but with substantially higher training efficiency (17h vs 27h and 75h on Breakout), and outperforms DreamerV3, STORM, IRIS, TWM, and SimPLe on several individual games (e.g., Gopher: 14702 vs 8240 for STORM; Kangaroo: 8780 vs 5382 for DIAMOND). EMERALD uses fewer parameters than DreamerV3(M) and DreamerV3(XL) while matching or exceeding their performance, and achieves 27 FPS compared to only 12 FPS for Δ-IRIS, making it both more accurate and more efficient than the closest competing method.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMERALD sits at the intersection of two lines of model-based RL research: Dreamer-style latent-space world models (which keep the agent in latent space for efficiency and access to inner representations) and accurate world models like Δ-IRIS and DIAMOND (which improve reconstruction quality but force the agent to learn from reconstructed pixels). The paper's key architectural contribution is transplanting MaskGIT's scheduled parallel decoding—originally designed for vector-quantized image generation—into the world model's latent token prediction, replacing the autoregressive sequential decoding used by IRIS and Δ-IRIS. It also adopts the Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM) from TransDreamer for temporal memory and the action mixer from STORM, while retaining DreamerV3's actor-critic learning, symlog predictions, and EMA return normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMERALD becomes the first method to surpass human expert performance on the Crafter benchmark within 10M steps and unlocks all 22 achievements, demonstrating that spatial latent states combined with MaskGIT decoding can resolve the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff that has limited prior approaches. The ablation reveals that the spatial latent representation is the critical component (a Transformer with vector latents actually underperforms DreamerV3's RSSM), suggesting that preserving spatial structure in the latent space matters more than the specific temporal architecture. However, the advantage is most pronounced on memory-demanding, visually complex environments; on Atari 100k, EMERALD achieves comparable but not superior performance to Δ-IRIS and DIAMOND, leaving open whether the approach scales to even more complex settings like full Minecraft or continuous control domains.
