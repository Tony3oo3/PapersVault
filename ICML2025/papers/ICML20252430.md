## Problem

Positional encoding (PE) and persistent homology (PH) are two prominent strategies for enhancing the expressivity of message-passing GNNs beyond the 1-WL limit, yet no rigorous characterization of their relative merits existed. The paper establishes that neither paradigm subsumes the other—each has concrete graph pairs it cannot distinguish that the other can—and asks whether they can be harmonized to yield provable expressivity gains over either alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252430/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix, node feature vectors, and a chosen base positional encoding (e.g., Laplacian eigenmaps, random-walk diffusion, or distance-to-anchor scores) as initial node embeddings. At every message-passing layer it produces two additional per-node topological embeddings derived from 0-dimensional and 1-dimensional persistence diagrams computed over the *current* (layer-dependent) positional encodings, rather than over static node features. These topological embeddings are concatenated with the structural and positional embeddings at each layer and pooled globally at readout for graph-level predictions.

## Method

PiPE (Persistence-informed Positional Encoding) interleaves positional-encoding message passing with persistent-homology computation at every layer: starting from a base PE p_v^0, it (i) applies a learnable filtration function f_ℓ to the current positional embeddings to build a vertex-color filtration, (ii) computes 0-dim and 1-dim persistence diagrams D^0_ℓ and D^1_ℓ, (iii) vectorizes them via MLPs into node-level topological embeddings r_v^{ℓ,0} and r_v^{ℓ,1} (with edge-level aggregation for 1-dim), and (iv) updates the positional embeddings p_v^{ℓ+1} using both the topological embeddings and an order-invariant aggregation over neighbors. The backbone GNN then operates on the concatenated representation h_v^ℓ = [x_v^ℓ ∥ p_v^ℓ ∥ r_v^{ℓ,0} ∥ r_v^{ℓ,1}]. The key theoretical insight is that because PH is computed on the *evolving* positional encodings rather than fixed node colors, PiPE is provably strictly more expressive than both LSPE (by masking topological features it recovers LSPE) and PH+LPE (by leveraging GNN message passing to exploit degree and local-structure differences invisible to static filtrations), while Proposition 4.4 links its expressivity to the k-FWL hierarchy via color-separating sets.

## Results

On ZINC (molecule property prediction), PiPE with SPE+RePHINE achieves the best test MAE of 0.0588, reducing error by 67% over the no-PE/no-PH baseline (0.1772). On Alchemy, the same configuration reaches 0.103 MAE versus 0.112 for the baseline. On the DrugOOD out-of-distribution benchmark, PiPE achieves the best OOD-Test AUC-ROC of 73.87 (Assay domain, SignNet+RePHINE) and 70.92 (Scaffold domain, SPE+RePHINE), outperforming all PE-only baselines. On the BREC expressivity benchmark, PiPE reaches 72% accuracy on Basic graph pairs versus only 3% for standalone PH and 10% for PH+LPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiPE consistently outperforms every PE-only baseline when augmented with RePHINE persistence: SPE+RePHINE (0.0588) beats SPE alone (0.0693), SignNet+RePHINE (0.0632) beats SignNet alone (0.0853), and RW+RePHINE (0.070) beats RW alone (0.090) on ZINC MAE. Against the TOGL topological GNN baseline, SPE+RePHINE PiPE (0.0588) edges out SPE+TOGL (0.062) on ZINC, while RW+TOGL (0.080) and PEG+TOGL (0.143) are substantially worse. On OGBG-MOLHIV, RW+RePHINE PiPE achieves 0.798 AUC-ROC versus 0.762 for RW alone and 0.776 for SPE alone. On DrugOOD OOD-Test, PiPE variants achieve the top scores in both Assay (73.87, SignNet+RePHINE) and Scaffold (70.92, SPE+RePHINE) domains, exceeding the best PE-only baselines by 1.3–3.4 points. On the BREC expressivity benchmark, PiPE dramatically outperforms both standalone PH (3–7% accuracy) and PH+LPE (3–15%), reaching 40–72% across graph categories, confirming the theoretical expressivity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on enhancing message-passing GNNs: positional encodings (Laplacian PE, Random Walk PE, Distance PE, and their learnable variants like LSPE, SignNet, PEG, SPE) and persistent homology-based topological features (TOGL, RePHINE, VC filtrations). The key conceptual departure is establishing that these two paradigms are incomparable in expressivity—neither subsumes the other—through explicit counterexample constructions, and then proposing PiPE as a message-passing framework that provably subsumes both. The expressivity analysis builds on the k-WL hierarchy and the color-separating set framework of Immonen et al. (2023), extending their results to show that k-FWL-stable colorings yield trivially separable 0-dim persistence diagrams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The incomparability theorem provides a clean theoretical foundation for why combining PE and PH is strictly beneficial, moving the field beyond ad-hoc combinations. PiPE's consistent empirical gains across molecule property prediction, graph classification, OOD generalization, and synthetic tree tasks suggest the unified framework captures complementary structural information that neither paradigm alone can access. Key limitations include computational overhead from PH computation, restriction to 1-dimensional simplicial complexes, and potential loss of permutation invariance when combining message passing with persistence-augmented representations. Open questions include whether higher-dimensional topological features (2-simplices, voids) yield further gains, and whether the PH bottleneck can be alleviated through approximate or learned persistence computations at scale.
