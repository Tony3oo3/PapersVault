## Problem

Fake image detectors trained on real images inadvertently learn spurious correlations with real-distribution features (e.g., WEBP compression artifacts, fine details), which undermines robustness to post-processing and generalization to newer generators within the same family. The paper argues that an image should be classified as fake if and only if it contains artifacts from the generative model, making reliance on real-image features unnecessary and potentially misleading.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252305/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (no fixed crop/resize at inference, enabled by ResNet-50's spatially-adaptive average pooling) and outputs a binary fake/real score via a sigmoid on the final linear layer. The architecture assumes a ReLU activation between the feature extractor and the final linear classifier, which is essential for the sign-based decomposition of features into real-associated (negative weights) and fake-associated (non-negative weights) components.

## Method

Stay-Positive is a two-stage algorithm: first, a standard ResNet-50 detector is trained with binary cross-entropy loss; second, only the final linear layer is re-trained (backbone frozen) under a non-negativity constraint that clamps all weights to zero after each SGD step (w ← max(w, 0)). The key insight is that with ReLU before the final layer, negative weights correspond to features whose presence decreases the fake score (i.e., real-image features), so forcing all weights non-negative eliminates the real score entirely and forces the detector to classify based solely on generative artifacts. This last-layer-only retraining (rather than full-network training with the constraint) avoids the network learning to use the *absence* of real features as a proxy for fakeness, which would reintroduce the same vulnerability.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (Table 2, post-processed fake images across 9 generators), Corvi⊕ achieves 97.17% average AP, outperforming the original Corvi (92.80%) by 4.37 points and all SOTA baselines including DRCT (87.02%), AEROBLADE (85.03%), and ClipDet (78.16%). The most dramatic improvement is on FLUX-generated images, where Corvi⊕ reaches 94.23% AP versus 74.57% for Corvi (+19.66) and 60.34% for AEROBLADE. On clean (unpost-processed) images (Table 1), Corvi⊕ achieves 99.88% average AP versus 94.23% for Corvi, with FLUX jumping from 57.25% to 99.33% AP. For partially inpainted images at 50% inpainting, Corvi⊕ achieves 97.48% AP while Corvi drops to 6.13%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Corvi⊕ (Ours) outperforms the original Corvi by 4.37 points in average AP (97.17 vs. 92.80), with the largest gains on FLUX (+19.66) and PixArt (+4.14), while matching or slightly exceeding Corvi on generators where it already performs well (SD, MJ, LCM). Rajan⊕ (Ours) improves over Rajan by 0.23 points in average AP (96.96 vs. 96.73), with notable gains on FLUX (+4.05) and aMUSEd (+1.88). Against the strongest SOTA baseline DRCT (ICML 2024), Corvi⊕ leads by 10.15 points in average AP (97.17 vs. 87.02), with particularly large margins on FLUX (+17.63) and PixArt (+20.20). The CLIP-based methods (UFD-ProGAN, UFD-LDM, ClipDet) are substantially outperformed, with Corvi⊕ exceeding them by 19–32 points in average AP. AEROBLADE, the strongest zero-shot method, trails Corvi⊕ by 12.14 points in average AP, with the gap widening to 33.89 points on FLUX. On the GenImage benchmark (Appendix A.4), Corvi⊕ and Rajan⊕ also outperform all baselines, with Rajan⊕ improving VQDM detection by 4.15% AP over Rajan.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on training-based fake image detectors for latent diffusion models, specifically Corvi et al. (2023) and Rajan et al. (2025), which use ResNet-50 trained on real images (COCO, LSUN) versus LDM-generated fakes. Conceptually, it departs from the standard binary-classification framing by arguing that features associated with the real distribution are not merely unhelpful but actively harmful, and proposes a non-negative weight constraint on the final layer as a minimal architectural intervention. This connects to the broader spurious-correlation literature (Kirichenko et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2021) but differs by constraining the network's parameter space rather than curating training data in a second stage, and by providing a clear mechanistic interpretation (negative weights = real features) grounded in the ReLU + sigmoid architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Stay-Positive algorithm is a remarkably simple post-hoc fix (freeze the backbone, retrain the last layer with a max(w,0) projection) that yields dramatic improvements on newer generators within the same family (42 AP points on FLUX) and substantially better robustness to post-processing artifacts like WEBP compression and downsizing. A practical bonus is improved detection of partially inpainted real images, where conventional detectors fail because the real portion triggers their real-feature associations. Key limitations remain: the method only addresses real-distribution spurious correlations (fake-distribution spurious features, e.g., upsampled images, persist), the backbone can still implicitly encode real-feature absence as a fake signal, and extending the constraint to full-network training has not yet been achieved. The open question of whether a principled full-network formulation exists that avoids these residual issues is the most important direction for follow-up work.
