## Problem

State-of-the-art utility-preserving private attributes protection methods rely on adversarial training, where the obfuscation module is trained against a jointly-trained adversarial classifier. The paper demonstrates both theoretically and empirically that these methods are severely vulnerable to a simple 'Probing Attack'—where an attacker collects (x, x') pairs from the deployed obfuscation model and trains a fresh classifier—causing private attribute NAG to jump from near-zero to 50–65%, rendering the protection practically useless in real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

PASS takes a single input sample x (an audio clip, a 128-length accelerometer/gyroscope magnitude vector, or a 160×160 facial image) and a fixed substitution dataset D_substitute of 4096 real samples drawn from the training set. It produces a substituted sample x' drawn from D_substitute, guaranteeing the output remains in the same data space as the input (satisfying the SUIFT invariance requirement). The substitution is stochastic: at inference, x' is sampled from the learned categorical distribution P_θ(X'=x'|X=x) over all 4096 substitute samples.

## Method

PASS abandons adversarial training entirely and instead learns a stochastic substitution probability P_θ(X'|X) parameterized by a neural network f(x) that embeds the input and learnable embeddings g(x') for each substitute sample, combined via cosine similarity with a softmax (τ=0.01). The training loss L̂ is rigorously derived from the information-theoretic objective min I(X';S_i) − λ·I(X';U_j) − μ·I(X';X) and decomposes into three terms: (1) maximizing H(X'|S_i) to make each substitute sample equally likely under all private-attribute classes, (2) a cross-entropy term encouraging the substitute's useful attributes to match the original's, and (3) minimizing H(X'|X) to preserve general features. Theorem 4.1 proves that E[L̂]+C ≥ L for a computable constant C, so minimizing L̂ provably minimizes an upper bound of the true information-theoretic objective. Because no adversarial classifier is involved, PASS is inherently robust to the Probing Attack: the substitution distribution is fixed at deployment and cannot be 'out-trained' by a stronger classifier.

## Results

On AudioMNIST (suppressing 'gender', preserving 'digit'), PASS achieves 0.0% NAG on the private attribute and 96.5% NAG on the useful attribute, yielding an mNAG of 55.0%, compared to the best baseline ADV at 71.4% NAG on gender and mNAG of only 1.0%. On Motion Sense (suppressing 'gender' and 'ID', preserving 'activity'), PASS achieves 0.0% NAG on both private attributes and 98.1% NAG on activity (mNAG = 98.1%), while the best baseline BDQ achieves 56.0% and 30.8% NAG on the private attributes (mNAG = 47.1%). On CelebA (suppressing 'Male', preserving 'Smiling' and 'Young'), PASS achieves 4.9% NAG on the private attribute and mNAG of 72.9%, versus the best baseline MSDA at 91.6% NAG on Male and mNAG of 2.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASS decisively outperforms all six adversarial-training baselines (ADV, GAP, MSDA, BDQ, PPDAR, MaSS) across all three datasets under the Probing Attack evaluation protocol. On AudioMNIST, PASS achieves 0.0% NAG on 'gender' while the best adversarial baseline (GAP) still leaks 13.3% NAG, and the worst (MaSS) leaks 88.9%; PASS's mNAG of 55.0% exceeds the best baseline (ADV, 1.0%) by over 54 points. On Motion Sense, PASS achieves 0.0% NAG on both 'gender' and 'ID' with 98.1% NAG on 'activity' (mNAG 98.1%), whereas all baselines leak 30–66% NAG on private attributes and achieve mNAG of only 28.6–47.1%. On CelebA, PASS reduces 'Male' NAG to 4.9% versus 83–99.9% for all baselines, with mNAG of 72.9% compared to −15.9 to +2.3% for baselines. Against the four DP-based baselines (Laplace Mechanism, DPPix, Snow, DP-Image) on CelebA, PASS also shows superior performance, as these methods target membership inference rather than specific attribute suppression. SPAct (Dave et al., 2022) is additionally outperformed by PASS in supplementary experiments (Appendix F.3). The key differentiator is that PASS's stochastic substitution is inherently robust to the Probing Attack, whereas all adversarial-training methods suffer a 40–65 percentage-point NAG increase on private attributes when evaluated with an attacker's classifier instead of the protector's.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the adversarial training paradigm that underpins all prior utility-preserving private attributes protection methods (ALR, GAP, MSDA, BDQ, PPDAR, MaSS), instead proposing stochastic data substitution parameterized by contrastive-learning-style embeddings. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from multiple fields: the substitution mechanism borrows from randomized response in local differential privacy and k-anonymity/l-diversity, while the loss function is derived from the information bottleneck objective shared with prior works. The Probing Attack evaluation protocol, though simple, is a novel contribution that reframes how the field should assess robustness, drawing an explicit parallel to the adversarial robustness literature's findings about the fragility of jointly-trained defenders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a trivially simple Probing Attack (collecting input-output pairs and training a fresh classifier) universally breaks all adversarial-training-based methods is a significant conceptual finding that should reshape how the field evaluates and designs privacy obfuscation. PASS's practical strength—achieving 0 NAG on private attributes while preserving 96-98% utility across three modalities—makes it a compelling deployment option. However, the method's reliance on a fixed substitution dataset (4096 samples) constrains the output space to real training samples, raising open questions about scalability to very large or streaming datasets, applicability to continuous-valued attributes, and whether the substitution approach can generalize to settings where the training data is not fully available at inference time.
