## Problem

Most graph-based machine learning methods for protein modeling rely exclusively on static structural information, ignoring the inherently dynamic nature of proteins. Although molecular dynamics trajectories are increasingly available, computational methods for incorporating this dynamic information into graph representations for property prediction remain largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes protein-ligand complex 3D structures (PDB) and 10 ns molecular dynamics trajectories (Amber20) as input, with trajectories aligned to initial frames via rigid-body superposition to isolate intrinsic conformational dynamics. Nodes are non-hydrogen atoms (atomic-level, 4.5 Å distance threshold) or Cα atoms (residue-level, 10 Å threshold), with features comprising one-hot type encodings and atom charges. The model produces three types of outputs: per-atom adaptability scores (node-level regression), per-residue binding site labels (binary classification for residues within 10 Å of the ligand), and per-complex binding affinity values (graph-level regression).

## Method

The core contribution is a heterogeneous graph representation with two complementary edge types: distance-based edges capturing spatial proximity from static structure, and correlation-based edges computed as the mean cosine similarity of displacement vectors across aligned MD trajectory frames (threshold |Cij| > 0.6 atomic / 0.3 residue). These correlation edges create direct shortcuts between dynamically coupled but spatially distant regions, effectively performing graph rewiring that reduces diameter and mitigates over-squashing. The heterogeneous graph is processed by relational GNNs (RGCN, RGAT) that learn relation-specific transformation matrices for each edge type, enabling the network to capture distinct structural and dynamic interaction patterns. The approach is validated across five architectures (RGCN, RGAT, R-EGNN, R-GPS, R-SS-GNN) on three tasks, with the Combined Graph consistently outperforming either edge type alone, confirming the complementary value of static and dynamic information.

## Results

On binding affinity prediction (PDBbind 2020 core set), the Combined Graph with R-SS-GNN achieves the best overall performance with MAE 1.087 and Pearson R 0.787, outperforming the Distance Graph (MAE 1.238, Pearson R 0.764) and Correlation Graph (MAE 1.228, Pearson R 0.723). For atomic adaptability prediction, the Combined Graph with RGCN reaches Pearson R 0.733 and MAE 0.198, a 39.3% improvement over the Distance Graph baseline (Pearson R 0.526). For binding site detection, R-EGNN with the Combined Graph achieves the highest F1 score of 0.414, compared to 0.362 (Distance) and 0.301 (Correlation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's baselines are the Distance Graph (static structure only) and Correlation Graph (MD trajectory only) variants, compared against the proposed Combined Graph across five architectures. The Combined Graph consistently outperforms both single-source baselines on all three tasks and all architectures. On binding affinity prediction, R-SS-GNN with the Combined Graph achieves MAE 1.087 vs. 1.238 (Distance) and 1.228 (Correlation), a 12.2% and 11.4% MAE reduction respectively. On atomic adaptability, the Correlation Graph alone already surpasses the Distance Graph (e.g., RGCN Pearson R: 0.643 vs. 0.526), and the Combined Graph further improves to 0.733. On binding site detection, the Combined Graph with R-EGNN reaches F1 0.414 vs. 0.362 (Distance) and 0.301 (Correlation). A notable exception is R-EGNN on atomic adaptability, where the Correlation Graph alone (Pearson R 0.740) slightly outperforms the Combined Graph (0.653), which the authors attribute to limitations in their preliminary equivariant fusion implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the use of relational graph neural networks (RGCN, RGAT) from their prior applications in small-molecule generation and protein representation learning to the novel setting of processing heterogeneous protein graphs enriched with molecular dynamics information. It departs from Chiang et al. (2022), who incorporated dynamic information via normal mode analysis, by instead deriving correlation edges directly from MD trajectories, and from prior dynamical network analysis work that used correlation matrices for single-protein investigation rather than as input to large-scale predictive models. The graph-rewiring interpretation connecting correlation edges to over-squashing mitigation (Topping et al., 2021) provides a theoretical grounding that prior protein-dynamics ML work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the rapidly growing corpus of MD trajectory datasets (e.g., MISATO, ATLAS) can be systematically exploited for protein property prediction through a simple yet effective graph construction, opening a practical pipeline for drug design and structural biology. The consistent Combined Graph advantage across five architectures and three tasks strengthens the case that static and dynamic signals are genuinely complementary rather than redundant. Open questions include whether the R-EGNN anomaly (where fusion fails) reflects a fundamental limitation of equivariant architectures for heterogeneous edges, and whether the approach generalizes to longer trajectories or different correlation measures such as mutual information.
