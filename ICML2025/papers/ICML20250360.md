## Problem

Long reasoning models (e.g., o1, QwQ, DeepSeek-R1) allocate excessive test-time compute to simple problems by generating multiple redundant solutions that contribute minimally to accuracy or reasoning diversity. This paper presents the first systematic study of this 'overthinking' phenomenon, introducing both outcome and process efficiency metrics to quantify the waste, and proposing a self-training-based mitigation that reduces token usage by up to 44.5% without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math word problem (or science question) as input and produces a reasoning trace containing one or more explicit solutions followed by a final answer. During training, the model samples 10 reasoning traces per question at temperature 1.0, and a separate LLM (Llama-3.3-70B) segments each trace into individual 'solutions'—substrings that contain an explicit answer. The preference pairs are constructed by pairing a simplified trace (positive) with the longest sampled trace (negative), so the model learns to compress its own multi-solution outputs into concise ones.

## Method

The approach is a self-training pipeline with three stages: (1) generate long multi-solution reasoning traces on PRM12K using the base model (QwQ-32B-Preview), (2) simplify each trace by pruning redundant solutions using one of three strategies—First-Correct Solutions (FCS), FCS+Reflection (retain first correct solution plus one additional reflection round), or Greedily Diverse Solutions (GDS, keep only solutions introducing new reasoning strategies as judged by GPT-4o clustering), and (3) apply preference optimization (SimPO selected over DPO and RPO) on the (short, long) pairs to teach the model to favor concise reasoning. The key empirical insight driving the design is that over 80% of correct answers appear in the first solution round (which constitutes <60% of total tokens), and the distinctness ratio of reasoning strategies drops sharply after the second solution, making later rounds largely redundant. The FCS+Reflection variant is chosen as the default because pure FCS degrades performance on harder benchmarks (MATH500, GPQA, AIME) where additional reflection is genuinely beneficial.

## Results

On MATH500, the proposed SimPO + FCS+Reflection method reduces token usage by 44.5% (2398.5 → 1330.3) while maintaining accuracy (91.2% → 91.4%) and improving outcome efficiency from 51.4% to 79.1%. On GSM8K, tokens drop by 44.9% (756.1 → 416.5) with accuracy actually improving from 94.7% to 95.9%. On the hardest benchmark AIME24, tokens are halved (9480.6 → 5154.4) with a modest accuracy drop (46.7% → 43.3%), while GPQA accuracy slightly improves (59.1% → 59.6%) with a 35.4% token reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the unmodified QwQ-32B-Preview baseline. The proposed SimPO + FCS+Reflection method wins on all efficiency metrics across every benchmark: outcome efficiency improves by 28.8 pp on ASDIV, 74.2 pp on GSM8K, 27.7 pp on MATH500, 19.3 pp on GPQA, and 1.8 pp on AIME24. Accuracy is maintained or improved on four of five benchmarks (ASDIV +0.1, GSM8K +1.2, MATH500 +0.2, GPQA +0.5), with only AIME24 showing a 3.4 pp drop. In Table 1, the paper also contextualizes these results against DeepSeek-R1 and conventional LLMs (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-Math-72B), showing that while long reasoning models like QwQ and R1 achieve higher accuracy than conventional models, their outcome efficiency (53–67%) is far below the 100% of single-solution conventional models, confirming the overthinking gap the method addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-training paradigm (STaR, Zelikman et al. 2022; Ho et al. 2023) and preference optimization (SimPO, Meng et al. 2024) to address a problem it frames as distinct from prior efficient-thinking work. While earlier efforts on token-budget-aware reasoning, early stopping, and difficulty-adaptive allocation (Han et al. 2024; Manvi et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024) targeted conventional LLMs, this paper identifies 'overthinking'—the generation of redundant multi-solution traces—as a phenomenon specific to long reasoning models (o1, R1, QwQ). The key departure is that rather than externally constraining compute or prompting for brevity, the paper trains the model via self-generated (short, long) preference pairs to internalize concise reasoning, and introduces outcome and process efficiency metrics absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 80% of correct answers appear in the first solution round, with subsequent solutions contributing minimally to accuracy or diversity, challenges the implicit assumption that extended multi-solution reasoning is uniformly beneficial. The 44.5% token reduction on MATH500 while preserving accuracy has direct cost implications for deploying o1-class models at scale. However, the mitigation is validated only on QwQ-32B-Preview, and the efficiency metrics rely on GPT-4o for solution clustering, introducing a potential circularity. Open questions include whether adaptive strategies can dynamically allocate thinking depth per problem, whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical domains, and whether the self-training signal degrades over multiple iterations.
