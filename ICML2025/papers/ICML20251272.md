## Problem

Asymmetric actor-critic algorithms, which leverage additional environment state information at training time to learn policies for partially observable environments, are known to be theoretically optimal but lack a precise justification for their empirical speed advantage over symmetric learning. No finite-time analysis had previously quantified what error terms are eliminated by conditioning the critic on the environment state, leaving the convergence benefit of asymmetric learning unexplained.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes partial observations and actions to maintain an internal agent state (e.g., a recurrent hidden state or sliding window), and additionally receives the full environment state during training for the asymmetric critic. The critic is a linear function approximator over features φ(s,z,a) for the asymmetric case or χ(z,a) for the symmetric case, with weights constrained to an ℓ₂-ball of radius B. The output is a log-linear policy over actions given the agent state, along with a finite-time suboptimality bound that decomposes into temporal-difference, approximation, distribution-shift, and (symmetric-only) aliasing terms.

## Method

The paper adapts the finite-time convergence analysis of Cayci et al. (2024) for symmetric natural actor-critic in POMDPs to the asymmetric setting, using m-step temporal difference learning with linear function approximators for the critic and natural policy gradient (via stochastic gradient descent on a least-squares loss) for the actor. The key technical result is that the asymmetric critic, conditioned on the environment state s in addition to the agent state z, eliminates the aliasing error term ε_alias that appears in the symmetric bound — this term measures the total-variation distance between the true belief (posterior over states given full history) and the approximate belief (posterior over states given only the agent state). The proof proceeds by establishing a Lyapunov drift bound for the m-step TD update, showing that the (s,z) pair is Markovian so the asymmetric Bellman operator's fixed point equals the true Q-function, whereas the symmetric Bellman operator's fixed point deviates from the true Q-function by exactly the aliasing bias. The resulting actor-critic suboptimality bound (Theorem 5) shows the inference error term vanishes in the asymmetric setting, providing a concrete theoretical explanation for why asymmetric learning is less sensitive to aliasing in the agent-state process.

## Results

The paper's main result is a finite-time suboptimality bound (Theorem 5) for both asymmetric and symmetric natural actor-critic algorithms. The key finding is that the asymmetric critic eliminates the aliasing error term ε_alias (Eq. 39) present in the symmetric critic bound, and the inference error term ε_inf vanishes entirely in the asymmetric setting (ε_inf,asym = 0, Eq. 44). The remaining error terms (temporal difference, function approximation, distribution shift, actor, and gradient) are structurally identical between the two settings, meaning the asymmetric algorithm's advantage is precisely the removal of aliasing-related bias. The bounds hold under a concentrability coefficient assumption and use linear function approximators with m-step temporal difference learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed asymmetric natural actor-critic algorithm and the symmetric natural actor-critic algorithm of Cayci et al. (2024), which serves as the direct baseline. The asymmetric algorithm strictly dominates in the theoretical bound: it eliminates both the aliasing term ε_alias in the critic error (Theorem 3 vs. Theorem 4) and the inference term ε_inf in the suboptimality bound (Theorem 5). The trade-off is a potentially larger residual gradient approximation error ε_grad,asym, since the asymmetric advantage A^{π_t}(s,z,a) is a higher-dimensional target than the symmetric advantage A^{π_t}(z,a) for the same policy gradient features ∇_θ log π_t(a|z). The paper notes this residual may be higher even in the tabular case, but the elimination of aliasing bias is the net theoretical advantage. No empirical benchmarks are provided; the contribution is entirely in the finite-time analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-time convergence analysis of natural actor-critic for POMDPs (Cayci et al., 2024) to the asymmetric setting, where the critic conditions on the true environment state available only at training time. The asymmetric actor-critic algorithm itself builds on the unbiased formulation of Baisero & Amato (2022), which corrected earlier biased asymmetric critics, and operates within the agent-state policy framework of Dong et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure from Cayci et al. is that the asymmetric Bellman operator is contractive with the true Q-function as its fixed point (since (S_t, Z_t) is Markovian), whereas the symmetric Bellman operator's fixed point differs from the true Q-function by an aliasing bias. This structural difference is what the paper exploits to show the asymmetric bound is strictly tighter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous finite-time justification for why asymmetric actor-critic algorithms converge faster in practice: the aliasing error term (measured by the TV distance between the true belief and the approximate belief given the agent state) vanishes entirely in the asymmetric setting. The analysis is limited to linear function approximators, a fixed agent-state process, and expectation bounds (not high-probability), and the concentrability coefficient assumption is standard but restrictive. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonlinear approximators (e.g., recurrent networks), learnable agent-state processes, and understanding whether the residual gradient approximation error ε_grad,asym can exceed ε_grad,sym in practice, which would partially offset the aliasing elimination benefit.
