## Problem

Ben-David et al. (2023) showed that realizably learnable distribution classes remain robustly learnable under oblivious additive adversaries, but left open whether this positive result extends to adaptive additive adversaries—adversaries that can inspect and modify individual samples with full knowledge of the learner's algorithm. This paper answers that question in the negative, proving that additive corruptions are strictly more powerful in the adaptive model than in the oblivious model.

## Input / Output

The theoretical construction takes a superlinear function g: N→N to define a distribution class C_g of three-component mixtures (a point mass at the origin, a uniform over a large finite set, and a rare indicator point) over a discrete domain X = N×N. The subtractive adversary V_sub,η operates on i.i.d. multisets of size m by preferentially removing indicator points up to its budget η·m, and the constructed additive adversaries output augmented multisets whose size is bounded by (1+kη)·m. The output is a proof that no learner can achieve error below α·η + ε for any α ≥ 1, along with explicit universal adversary constructions.

## Method

The proof proceeds in three stages. First, a general indistinguishability criterion (Theorem 5.2) shows that if an adversary V makes samples from a fixed distribution p and a meta-distribution Q over C nearly indistinguishable (TV < ζ) while p and every q in supp(Q) remain far apart (TV > γ), then C is not α-robustly learnable. Second, they verify this criterion for V_sub,η on C_g by bounding the TV distance between manipulated samples via the birthday paradox (for repeated odds elements) and Markov's inequality (for indicator overflow), choosing parameters j, η, γ′ independent of m. Third—and most distinctively—they show that a successful subtractive adversary implies the existence of additive adversaries by inverting the subtraction: the subtractive adversary carves out a common core S_core, and the additive adversary reconstructs the 'opposite piece' from the other distribution via conditional reverse mappings V_sub^{-1}. For a universal additive adversary, the construction randomizes over k+1 reverse-mapping applications (picking u uniformly from {0,…,k}), so the output contains a shifted count of p-derived versus Q-derived samples, yielding a single adversary with budget kη that defeats every learner.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.1) establishes an infinite separation: for every superlinear function g, the class C_g is realizably learnable with sample complexity O(log(1/δ)·g(1/ε)), yet is NOT adaptively additive α-robustly learnable for any α ≥ 1. This means no learner can achieve error bounded by α·η + ε against an adaptive additive adversary with budget η, regardless of sample size. The paper further constructs explicit universal adversaries (both additive and subtractive) that defeat every learner for C_g, and shows that the subtractive adversary V_{sub,η} successfully (γ,ζ)-confuses C_g-generated samples with TV-distance ≤ 1/8 between manipulated sample distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The key comparison is between adversary models: Ben-David et al. (2023) showed that realizably learnable classes remain robustly learnable under oblivious additive adversaries (Theorem 1.7), but this paper proves that the same guarantee does NOT extend to adaptive additive adversaries (Theorem 4.1), establishing a strict separation. In the oblivious setting, subtractive adversaries are strictly more powerful than additive ones; in the adaptive setting, the paper shows (Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.2) that additive and subtractive adversaries are closely related — a universal subtractive adversary implies the existence of universal additive adversaries. The separation from Ben-David et al.'s oblivious additive result is an infinite gap in sample complexity, in contrast to the polynomial gap shown by Canonne et al. (2023) for robust Gaussian mean testing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly resolves an open problem posed by Ben-David et al. (2023), who showed that realizably learnable distribution classes are robustly learnable under oblivious additive adversaries but not under oblivious subtractive ones, and explicitly asked whether their positive oblivious-additive result extends to the adaptive setting. The non-learnability technique builds on a lower-bound framework from Ben-David & Lechner (2025) connecting sample indistinguishability to learning hardness. The paper also positions itself against Blanc et al. (2022, 2024), who showed oblivious-to-adaptive reductions for many statistical tasks, explaining that the reduction's sample overhead depends on support size and thus fails for the unbounded-support distributions studied here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes a clean, infinite-gap separation between adaptive and oblivious additive robustness for distribution learning, resolving a question that was explicitly left open. A notable structural insight is that, unlike in the oblivious setting where subtractive adversaries are strictly more powerful than additive ones, the adaptive setting collapses this hierarchy: a successful subtractive adversary can be 'inverted' to construct a successful additive adversary. Limitations include the reliance on a somewhat contrived class with unbounded support, and the open question of whether polynomial (rather than infinite) separations can be shown for more natural, bounded-support distribution classes. The universal adversary construction also suggests that no single learning algorithm can defend against all adaptive additive strategies for such classes.
