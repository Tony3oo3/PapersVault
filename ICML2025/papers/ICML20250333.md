## Problem

Evaluating K target policies in a finite-horizon MDP to ε-accuracy simultaneously is sample-inefficient when done via K independent single-policy evaluations, since the cost scales linearly in K and ignores structural overlap among policies. Prior work (Dann et al., 2023) addressed this online via trajectory synthesis but introduced an unfavorable 1/d^max(s) term that can reintroduce K-dependence. This paper tackles the problem from the offline perspective, seeking a single sampling distribution with good coverage over all target policies and a sample complexity that depends only on the overlap of their visitation distributions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives K known target policies, an accuracy parameter ε, a confidence parameter δ, and operates in a tabular episodic MDP with |S| states, |A| actions, and horizon H. It outputs simultaneous value estimates V̂_1^{π^k} for all K policies such that |V̂_1^{π^k} − V_1^{π^k}| ≤ ε with probability at least 1−δ. A notable feature is that the instance-dependent sample complexity bound, which involves the true visitation distributions and the optimal sampling distribution, can be made fully concrete by substituting the coarse estimators computed in Phase I, yielding a known (rather than unknown) sample complexity.

## Method

CAESAR operates in two phases. In Phase I, it coarsely estimates the visitation distributions of all K target policies at a lower-order sample cost of Õ(1/ε) (rather than Õ(1/ε²)) using a multiplicative-accuracy guarantee, then solves a convex optimization over the convex hull of these coarse estimators to obtain an approximately optimal sampling distribution that minimizes the maximum visitation ratio. In Phase II, it draws trajectories from this distribution and applies IDES, a step-wise extension of DualDICE to finite-horizon MDPs, which estimates the importance-weighting ratios at each step h by minimizing a quadratic loss via projected stochastic gradient descent, with the previous step's ratio estimator feeding into the current step's loss. The key technical distinctions from DualDICE are: (i) the step-wise objective replaces the single Bellman-transformed loss, (ii) coarse distribution estimators eliminate the need for on-policy samples in the second loss term, and (iii) the analysis yields high-probability (via Median-of-Means) rather than in-expectation guarantees with a tight constant tied to the visitation ratios. The overall sample complexity is Õ(H⁴/ε² · Σ_h max_k Σ_{s,a} (d_h^{π^k}(s,a))²/μ_h*(s,a)), which is independent of K when policies share visitation structure.

## Results

CAESAR achieves a non-asymptotic, instance-dependent sample complexity of Õ(H⁴/ε² · Σ_h max_{k∈[K]} Σ_{s,a} (d_h^{π^k}(s,a))²/μ_h*(s,a)) for evaluating K policies to ε-accuracy with probability 1−δ (Theorem 4.9). For deterministic policies, this simplifies to an instance-independent bound of Õ(poly(H)·S²A/ε²) (Corollary 4.10), matching the reward-free exploration complexity of Jin et al. (2020). The coarse estimation phase requires only Õ(1/ε) trajectories per policy, making it a lower-order cost relative to the main Õ(1/ε²) sampling phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Dann et al. (2023), the only prior work on multiple-policy evaluation with instance-dependent guarantees. In a constructed two-layer MDP example (Section 5.1), CAESAR achieves Õ(1/ε²) sample complexity independent of K, while Dann et al.'s trajectory-stitching approach yields Õ(K/ε²) due to an unfavorable 1/d^max(s) term. Beyond sample complexity, CAESAR operates in an offline/off-policy framework (constructing an optimal sampling distribution first, then evaluating via importance weighting), whereas Dann et al. use an online on-policy approach requiring generative model estimation. Compared to single-policy offline evaluation methods (Yin and Wang 2020; Xie et al. 2019), CAESAR extends the framework to K policies simultaneously with a sampling distribution optimized for the worst-case policy, and provides non-asymptotic (high-probability) guarantees where prior offline methods offer only asymptotic or in-expectation results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline policy evaluation framework to the multiple-policy setting, departing from Dann et al. (2023) who tackled the same problem online via trajectory synthesis. The core estimation subroutine (IDES) is a non-trivial extension of DualDICE (Nachum et al., 2019) from infinite-horizon to finite-horizon MDPs, replacing Bellman-based variable transformations with a step-wise quadratic loss optimized via SGD. The coarse estimation subroutine (MARCH) builds on the EULER algorithm (Zanette and Brunskill, 2019) and the policy cover construction of Jin et al. (2020), introducing a novel β-distance metric to handle the exponential number of deterministic policies with polynomial sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first offline algorithm for multiple-policy evaluation with instance-dependent sample complexity that avoids the unfavorable K-dependency present in prior online approaches. The coarse estimation technique—showing that multiplicative-accuracy distribution estimates suffice for designing near-optimal sampling distributions—is a potentially reusable tool beyond this specific problem. Key limitations include the H⁴ sample complexity (conjectured improvable to H²), the restriction to tabular MDPs, and the absence of a matching lower bound for the multiple-policy evaluation problem. The β-distance notion and the connection to reward-free exploration (matching Jin et al.'s sample complexity for deterministic policies) suggest broader applicability in exploration and policy identification.
