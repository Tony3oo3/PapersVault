## Problem

Existing few-shot point cloud semantic segmentation methods rely on pre-trained DGCNN backbones that suffer from domain shifts and limited geometric structure modeling, while the small number of support samples produces biased prototypes that fail to bridge the categorical information gap between query and support sets. The paper addresses these issues by proposing a pre-training-free architecture that better captures local 3D geometry and explicitly refines prototypes to reduce inter-set structural differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic N-way K-shot setting (N∈{2,3}, K∈{1,5}) where each episode provides K labeled support point clouds (2048 points per 1m×1m block with 3D coordinates and additional features) and H unlabeled query point clouds. The model outputs per-point semantic labels assigning each query point to one of N target classes or a background class, with segmentation computed via similarity between query point features and refined prototypes. Training uses cross-entropy loss over 100 test episodes per configuration, with data augmentation including random rotation, translation, and point jittering.

## Method

DyPolySeg is a pre-training-free encoder-decoder that stacks DyPolyConv and Mamba Block layers to form a 'local-global' feature extractor. DyPolyConv unifies a low-order convolution (LoConv) that captures flat local structure via position encoding with a dynamic high-order convolution (DyHoConv) that adaptively models complex geometric details using learned spatial priors, attention coefficients, and power exponents inspired by Taylor series expansion; the transformation function generalizes both affine and radial basis functions with a learnable sign parameter. A lightweight Prototype Completion Module (PCM), stackable in multiple layers, refines class prototypes through a Self-Enhancement Module (auto-correlation-based intra-set feature distribution learning) and an Interactive Enhancement Module (cross-set correlation-based domain gap reduction), with residual connections preserving information flow. The final segmentation is obtained by matching query point features against the PCM-refined prototypes, requiring no pre-training on seen categories.

## Results

On S3DIS, DyPolySeg achieves 72.92% mIoU in the 2-way 1-shot setting and 75.66% in 2-way 5-shot, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Seg-PN by 6.51% and 6.10% respectively. In the more challenging 3-way settings, DyPolySeg reaches 66.24% (1-shot) and 67.92% (5-shot) mIoU, surpassing Seg-PN by 6.47% and 5.82%. On ScanNet, the method achieves 71.89% mIoU in 2-way 1-shot (+8.15% over Seg-PN) and 69.45% in 3-way 1-shot (+5.87%). On ScanObjectNN classification, DyPolySeg attains 90.8% accuracy with only 2.64M parameters, exceeding PointMamba (89.3%, 16.9M params) and PCM (88.1%, 34.2M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyPolySeg outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest prior method Seg-PN (Zhu et al., 2024, CVPR), DyPolySeg gains +6.51% mIoU on S3DIS 2-way 1-shot (72.92% vs 66.41%) and +8.15% on ScanNet 2-way 1-shot (71.89% vs 63.74%), with consistent 4–8% margins across all N-way K-shot configurations. Against pre-training-based methods like PAP3D (He et al., 2023), the advantage is even larger: +10.16% on S3DIS 2-way 1-shot (72.92% vs 62.76%), demonstrating that the pre-training-free design does not sacrifice performance. On ScanObjectNN classification, DyPolySeg (90.8%) surpasses PointMamba (89.3%) by 1.5% while using only 2.64M parameters versus PointMamba's 16.9M, and outperforms PCM (88.1%) by 2.7% with roughly 13× fewer parameters. The method also dominates earlier few-shot baselines (MPTI, AttMPTI, BFG, 2CBR) by 14–22% mIoU on S3DIS, confirming that the combination of DyPolyConv, Mamba Block, and PCM provides a substantial architectural advantage over prior prototype-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyPolySeg extends the Taylor series-inspired local structure fitting line initiated by TaylorSeg (Wang et al., 2025b) by replacing its static TaylorConv with a unified Dynamic Polynomial Convolution that combines low-order position-encoding convolution with learnable high-order terms whose exponents and attention weights are generated from explicit geometric features. It departs from the dominant DGCNN-pretraining paradigm (AttMPTI, PAP3D) by being entirely pre-training-free, and introduces Mamba-based state-space modeling for global context—a component absent from all prior few-shot point cloud segmentation methods. The Prototype Completion Module (PCM) with its self-enhancement and interactive-enhancement sub-modules represents a new mechanism for bridging the support-query feature gap, going beyond the simpler prototype alignment strategies in BFG, 2CBR, and Seg-PN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-training-free design removes a significant practical barrier (domain shift and extended training time) that has limited deployment of few-shot point cloud segmentation in real-world settings. The ablation revealing that PCM contributes roughly 17 percentage points of mIoU—far more than the entire DyPolyConv+Mamba encoder stack—suggests that prototype quality, not feature extraction capacity, is the dominant bottleneck in few-shot point cloud segmentation, a finding that could redirect future research effort. Open questions include whether the polynomial fitting approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes beyond the 2048-point indoor blocks used here, and whether the Mamba component's contribution (only ~2% over DyPolyConv alone) justifies its computational cost at scale.
