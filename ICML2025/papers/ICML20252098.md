## Problem

Pretrained latent diffusion models (LDMs) have not been explored for perceptual consistency in No-Reference Image Quality Assessment (NR-IQA). Existing diffusion-based IQA methods are ad hoc, often converting NR-IQA into full-reference problems or requiring fine-tuning on specific IQA datasets, which limits generalization. This paper asks whether a frozen, unconditionally pretrained LDM can be guided by perceptual features to extract quality-relevant representations without any fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single distorted RGB image (no reference image required) and produces a scalar perceptual quality score. The input image is encoded into the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5, and during 10 DDIM sampling steps (t ∈ [0, 100]), intermediate feature maps are collected from all upsampling layers of the denoising U-Net. These multi-scale, multi-timestep feature maps (diffusion hyperfeatures) are aggregated and passed through a small two-hidden-layer regression network to yield the final quality score. The diffusion backbone remains entirely frozen; only the lightweight regression head is trained on MOS labels.

## Method

LGDM introduces Perceptual Manifold Guidance (PMG), a two-step conditional guidance applied at each DDIM step within the latent space of a frozen LDM. The first step (data consistency) minimizes an L2 loss between the decoded Tweedie estimate and the input measurement, anchoring the sample to the content-bias region on the manifold. The second step (perceptual consistency) minimizes an L2 loss between perceptual quality features ψ_p of the decoded estimate and those of the input, pushing the sample toward a perceptually consistent sub-region. Because all updates occur in the low-dimensional latent space, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that gradient updates remain on the tangent space of the data manifold, guaranteeing on-manifold sampling. The key architectural choice is extracting diffusion hyperfeatures—intermediate feature maps from multiple U-Net layers across multiple timesteps—rather than relying on a single final-layer representation, capturing both local texture artifacts and larger-scale structural inconsistencies. The approach is plug-and-play: it works with any pretrained LDM and any perceptual metric ψ_p, with SD v1.5 itself used as the perceptual feature extractor yielding the best results.

## Results

LGDM-ψ_SDv1.5 achieves state-of-the-art NR-IQA performance across all ten evaluated datasets. On authentic distortion benchmarks, it reaches PLCC/SRCC of 0.940/0.908 on LIVEC, 0.972/0.967 on KonIQ-10k, 0.812/0.705 on FLIVE, and 0.948/0.947 on SPAQ. On synthetic datasets, it attains 0.988/0.986 (LIVE), 0.981/0.964 (CSIQ), 0.921/0.883 (TID2013), and 0.961/0.958 (KADID-10k). On AIGC datasets, it scores 0.903/0.891 (AGIQA-1K) and 0.929/0.863 (AGIQA-3K), outperforming the previous best GenZIQA by 4.2 and 3.7 PLCC points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGDM-ψ_SDv1.5 outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset. On the hardest authentic benchmark FLIVE, it leads RE-IQA (0.733/0.645) by 7.9/6.0 PLCC/SRCC points and GenZIQA (0.718/0.613) by 9.4/9.2 points, demonstrating substantial gains on real-world social-media distortions. On KonIQ-10k, it surpasses the previous best DP-IQA (0.951/0.942) by 2.1/2.5 points and LoDA (0.944/0.932) by 2.8/3.5 points. On SPAQ, it edges out Q-Align (0.933/0.930) by 1.5/1.7 points. On AIGC datasets, LGDM beats GenZIQA by 4.2 PLCC on AGIQA-1K and 3.7 on AGIQA-3K, indicating a stronger prior for AI-generated content. Notably, even the ablated variant without perceptual guidance (LGDM-ψ_φ) already outperforms most baselines, confirming that the frozen diffusion backbone alone provides strong quality-relevant representations, while PMG adds a further 5–10 point boost on authentic datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., 2023) and the on-manifold sampling theory from Manifold Preserving Guided Diffusion (He et al., 2024) into the NR-IQA domain. Unlike prior diffusion-based IQA methods (DP-IQA, GenZIQA) that fine-tune or distill the diffusion backbone, LGDM keeps the pretrained LDM entirely frozen and introduces Perceptual Manifold Guidance (PMG) — a two-step gradient update combining data consistency and perceptual feature matching — to steer sampling toward perceptually consistent regions. The multi-scale, multi-timestep feature extraction (diffusion hyperfeatures) is borrowed from Luo et al. (2024) but repurposed here as the quality-relevant representation fed to a lightweight regression head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot use of a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone for NR-IQA is practically appealing: no per-dataset fine-tuning is needed, and the method generalizes across synthetic, authentic, and AIGC distortions without retraining the diffusion model. A notable limitation is inference speed (~10s for 10 DDIM steps vs. ~0.1s for transformer-based methods), which limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video IQA, whether the SD v1.5 > v2.0 finding reflects a fundamental architectural property or a training-data artifact, and whether the regression head can be replaced by a more interpretable or parameter-free quality estimator.
