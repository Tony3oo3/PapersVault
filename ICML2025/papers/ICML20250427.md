## Problem

Prior work on collaborative mean estimation with strategic agents assumed homogeneous agents with identical collection costs, making data validation straightforward. This paper tackles the heterogeneous setting where agents face different costs per distribution, creating a fundamental tension: naively minimizing social penalty assigns most work to the cheapest agents, violating individual rationality, while ensuring IR requires a more complex division of labor that must still be enforceable against free-riding and data fabrication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250427/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a collection scheme n ∈ ℕ^{m×d} (specifying how many samples each agent should draw from each of d distributions), a cost matrix c ∈ (0,∞]^{m×d}, and the actual data submissions Y_i from each agent (which may be fabricated or altered). It returns to each agent an estimate I_i ∈ ℝ^d of the unknown mean vector μ. The output for each agent is a weighted combination of their own submitted data and corrupted data drawn from other agents' submissions, with corruption coefficients calibrated so that deviating from the recommended collection amount increases the agent's penalty.

## Method

The mechanism M_CBL (Corrupt Based on Leverage) operates in two phases. First, a Compute-n-Approx subroutine modifies the IR-optimal collection scheme so that a sufficient number of agents collect from each distribution, enabling cross-validation of any single agent's submission. Second, for each agent-distribution pair, the mechanism returns a weighted average of the agent's own data and corrupted data (Gaussian noise added to other agents' samples), where the corruption coefficient α_{i,k} is the smallest value > √n*_{i,k} satisfying a specific equation G_{i,k}(α)=0 that makes the agent's penalty minimized exactly at the recommended collection amount. Under a favorable condition (each agent benefits from sharing per-distribution data), the mechanism achieves an 8-approximation to the IR-optimal social penalty; in the worst case it achieves O(√m). The paper also shows this √m price of stability is unavoidable, and extends the mechanism to accommodate axiomatic bargaining solutions (Nash, egalitarian, Kalai-Smordinsky) for fairer work allocation.

## Results

The proposed mechanism M_CBL achieves a √m-approximation to the minimum IR-constrained social penalty in the worst case (Theorem 1), and an 8-approximation under the favorable condition (8) where each agent benefits from sharing data for each distribution. This worst-case bound is tight: Theorem 4 establishes that no mechanism can achieve better than Ω(√m) price of stability in the worst case. Additionally, two hardness results (Theorems 2 and 3) show that no mechanism can simultaneously guarantee dominant-strategy incentive compatibility or IR at every strategy profile while maintaining efficiency — any such mechanism forces every agent to incur penalty at least p_i^IND / 2 regardless of others' strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the IR-constrained social penalty minimization baseline (n^OPT, solved via convex optimization in Eq. 7), which is the best achievable penalty among all IR mechanisms but is not incentive-compatible. The proposed mechanism matches this baseline within a factor of √m in the worst case and within a factor of 8 under favorable conditions (e.g., homogeneous or similar costs). The paper also contrasts with the unconstrained social penalty minimization, which assigns all data collection to the cheapest agent and violates IR for that agent (e.g., agent 1's penalty becomes (√m + 1/√m)σ√c_{1,1} > 2σ√c_{1,1} = p_1^IND). For fairness, the mechanism extends to Nash bargaining and egalitarian bargaining solutions, achieving the same 8-approximation guarantee when condition (8) holds, at the cost of higher social penalty than n^OPT. The hardness results (Theorems 2–4) establish that the √m worst-case gap is fundamental and that stronger IC/IR guarantees are impossible for any efficient mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chen et al. (2023), which studied collaborative univariate normal mean estimation with strategic agents under homogeneous costs, to the heterogeneous setting where agents face different collection costs across multiple distributions. The corruption-based verification technique from Chen et al. (2023) and Cai et al. (2015) is adapted, but the heterogeneous cost structure fundamentally complicates verification since agents collect varying amounts from different distributions, requiring the Compute-n-Approx subroutine to ensure sufficient cross-validation data. The paper also introduces a novel integration of axiomatic bargaining solutions (Nash, egalitarian, Kalai-Smordinsky) with noncooperative mechanism design for enforcement, a combination the authors note has not been explored in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete theoretical characterization of what is achievable in heterogeneous collaborative estimation: the O(√m) approximation is tight (matching Ω(√m) lower bound), and the impossibility results for dominant-strategy equilibrium and universal IR rule out stronger guarantees that practitioners might hope for. The tension between efficiency (minimizing social penalty) and fairness (equalizing agent penalties) is made concrete, showing that the IR-constrained optimum can leave the cheapest agent no better off than working alone. Limitations include the purely theoretical treatment with no empirical validation, the restriction to Gaussian distributions, and the complexity of the mechanism, which may limit practical deployment. Open questions include whether the √m gap can be closed under additional structural assumptions and how the framework extends to non-Gaussian or high-dimensional settings.
