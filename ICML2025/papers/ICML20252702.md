## Problem

Existing LLM compression techniques (pruning, quantization, entropy coding) are applied after training is complete, leaving the training process itself unexploited for proactive size-performance trade-offs. Additionally, prior work assumes Gaussian parameter distributions and relies on Huffman coding, both of which are suboptimal for the actual post-training parameter statistics of LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised training data (text corpora for classification or generation tasks), a pre-specified model architecture, and a user-chosen Lagrange multiplier λ that controls the size-accuracy trade-off. It outputs a compressed model whose parameters are quantized at step 2⁻⁸, frequency-ranked via value mapping, and entropy-coded into a bitstream using exp-Golomb (k=0) codes along with a compact code table. The same EG code table is reusable across different model sizes and architectures, unlike per-model Huffman tables.

## Method

BackSlash integrates rate-distortion optimization directly into the training loop by augmenting the standard task loss with a Discretized Generalized Gaussian Rate (DGGR) term: J = L(X,Y,f,θ) + λ·(1/N_p)Σ(|θᵢ|+ε)^ν. The key insight is that trained LLM parameters follow a generalized Gaussian distribution with shape parameter ν < 2 (e.g., 1.36 for BERT, 0.85 for DeepSeek), not the Gaussian assumed by prior work; ν is dynamically re-estimated each batch via the moment-ratio ρ(ν) = E[θ²]/E²[|θ|]. A soft gradient clipping constant ε prevents the gradient singularity that arises when ν < 1 and θ→0. For actual deployment, parameters are entropy-coded with exp-Golomb (k=0) codes after frequency-based value mapping, achieving coding efficiency within a few percentage points of the entropy limit while remaining robust to distribution shift and reusable across models.

## Results

BackSlash achieves 74–89% parameter compression (via EG coding) across BERT (110M), GPT-2 (774M), Llama-3 (1B), and Gemma-2 (2B), compared to only 27–60% for unconstrained training followed by the same EG coding. Accuracy is largely preserved: BERT drops from 93.63% to 92.59%, while GPT-2 and Llama-3 actually improve (88.73% vs 85.92% and 86.93% vs 86.09% respectively). Under pruning, BackSlash-trained BERT maintains accuracy at 80% pruning rate, whereas normally trained BERT loses predictive ability at 60%. The optimal λ=2000 setting yields only 2.52% accuracy loss while reducing model size to 13% of the original.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is unconstrained (normal) training followed by post-hoc entropy coding (EG or Huffman). BackSlash consistently outperforms this baseline by a large margin: EG compression jumps from 27% (BERT) to 74%, from 29% (GPT-2) to 78%, from 45% (Llama-3) to 83%, and from 60% (Gemma-2) to 89%. In terms of accuracy, BackSlash matches or exceeds normal training on GPT-2 (+2.81%) and Llama-3 (+0.84%), while incurring only a 1.04% drop on BERT and 1.09% on Gemma-2. EG coding after BackSlash is within 1–2% of Huffman coding efficiency (e.g., 2.64 vs 2.42 bits for BERT), yet EG codes are universal across models and require no per-model code table. In the ablation, DGGR outperforms fixed-shape L_p regularizers: L_2 achieves slightly better compression (88% vs 86%) but drops accuracy by 2.89%, while L_0.5 preserves accuracy better but doubles the code length (2.90 vs 1.37 bits).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of post-training compression (pruning, quantization, Huffman coding) by integrating rate-distortion optimization directly into the training loss function, building on the RDO frameworks of Gao et al. (2018) and Isik et al. (2021) which applied RDO only after training. It replaces the standard Gaussian assumption for LLM parameter distributions with a generalized Gaussian (shape < 2) model, drawing on the EG coding theory of Wen & Villasenor (1999) for quantized GG sources and the shape-parameter estimation procedure of Sharifi & Leon-Garcia (1995). The DGGR rate term is shown to generalize L1 and L2 regularization as special cases, positioning BackSlash as a principled unification of sparsity-inducing regularizers under an information-theoretic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60–90% parameter reduction with minimal accuracy loss, combined with robustness to 80% post-hoc pruning, offers a practical path to edge deployment that is orthogonal to (and potentially composable with) quantization. However, the evidence is confined to models of 110M–2B parameters and relatively simple classification/generation tasks, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 70B+ models where compression is most needed. The ad hoc selection of the Lagrange multiplier λ (described as 'trials and errors') and the lack of a principled criterion for its optimal value are significant practical limitations. Future work on hardware-aware training that exploits the induced sparsity during inference could substantially increase the practical impact.
