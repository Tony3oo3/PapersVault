## Problem

In repeated uniform price multi-unit auctions, value-maximizing buyers with per-round return-on-investment (RoI) constraints must bid without knowing competing bids, yet prior work either assumed quasilinear utility or required knowledge of competitors' bids to ensure feasibility. This paper introduces 'safe' bidding strategies that guarantee RoI feasibility for all possible competing bids and develops the first efficient online learning algorithm for them, along with a quantitative analysis of how much value is lost relative to richer (non-safe) strategy classes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the bidder's fixed valuation curve v ∈ R^M (with diminishing marginal returns), the bidding format parameter m (number of bid-quantity pairs), and per-round feedback: either the full competing bid vector (full information) or only the realized value and payment (bandit). It outputs an m-uniform safe bidding strategy per round, specified by m bid-quantity pairs (b_j, q_j) where each bid b_j equals the average of the first Q_j valuations (w_{Q_j}), making the strategy fully determined by the cumulative quantity thresholds Q_1 < Q_2 < ... < Q_m ≤ M. The strategy space is finite but exponential in m (O(M^m) strategies), which the algorithm navigates without enumerating.

## Method

The key structural insight is that safe undominated strategies form a finite nested class S_m* where each bid b_j = w_{Q_j} (the average cumulative valuation up to quantity Q_j), depending only on the bidder's own valuation curve and not on competing bids. For the offline problem, the paper constructs a polynomial-sized layered DAG whose s-d paths bijectively correspond to strategies in S_m*, reducing the search to a maximum-weight path problem solvable in poly(m,M) time. For the online setting, Algorithm 1 implements the Hedge algorithm via the weight-pushing method of Takimoto & Warmuth (2003), maintaining edge-level probabilities on the DAG rather than path-level weights, achieving Õ(M√(mT)) regret under full information and Õ(M²√(m³T)) under bandit feedback. The paper further computes the 'richness ratio' α quantifying the worst-case value gap between safe strategies and richer clairvoyant classes (e.g., α = 1/2 for non-safe m-uniform strategies, α = m/m' for safe strategies with more bid-quantity pairs), establishing that safe strategies achieve α-approximate sublinear regret against these stronger benchmarks.

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves sublinear regret of Õ(M√(mT)) under full-information feedback and Õ(M²√(m³T)) under bandit feedback, matching the Ω(M√T) lower bound up to logarithmic factors. The richness ratio α quantifies the worst-case value loss from restricting the learner to safe strategies: α = 1/2 against non-safe m-uniform strategies (independent of m), α = m/m′ against safe strategies with m′ ≥ m pairs, and α = m/(2m′) against non-safe strategies with m′ ≥ m pairs. Empirically, on EU ETS emission permit auction data (2022–2023), the upper bound on Λ_{F_m^{H−}, U_m*} drops to ≈1.05 for m ≥ 4 (vs. the theoretical worst-case bound of 2), and the ratio Λ_{U_m*, U_1*} is only ≈1.25 even at m = 10 (vs. the theoretical bound of 10), indicating safe strategies are near-optimal in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct algorithmic baselines; the 'comparisons' are between the learner's restricted safe-strategy class and progressively richer clairvoyant benchmark classes. Against the strongest benchmark (non-safe, feasible strategies with the same m), safe strategies lose at most a factor of 2 in the worst case (α = 1/2), a bound that is independent of m and proven tight. Against safe strategies with more bid-quantity pairs (m′ > m), the loss scales linearly as m/m′, and against non-safe strategies with m′ pairs, it scales as m/(2m′). Empirically on EU ETS data, all these worst-case bounds are substantially exceeded: the factor-of-2 loss for non-safe m-uniform strategies is never observed (empirical ratio ≈1.05 for m≥4), and the linear m/m′ degradation for safe strategies is far milder in practice (ratio ≈1.25 at m=10 vs. theoretical bound of 10). The paper also contrasts its approach with prior multi-unit auction learning works (Brânzei et al. 2023; Potfer et al. 2024; Galgana & Golrezaei 2024), noting that the safe-strategy structure eliminates the need for bid-space discretization, yielding time complexity independent of T and improving the bandit regret dependence from T^{2/3} to √T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning to bid in repeated multi-unit auctions (Brânzei et al., 2023; Galgana & Golrezaei, 2024; Potfer et al., 2024) by replacing the standard quasilinear utility model with value-maximizing buyers subject to per-round RoI constraints. The core technical machinery builds on the weight-pushing method of Takimoto & Warmuth (2003) for efficient exponential weight updates over DAG paths, and the Hedge/EXP3 framework of Freund & Schapire (1997), but applies them to a novel 'safe strategy' class whose nested structure (bids equal to average cumulative valuations) eliminates the need for bid-space discretization required by prior works. The richness ratio concept, quantifying the value gap between safe and richer strategy classes, is a new analytical tool not present in the prior online-learning-for-auctions literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, computationally efficient framework for bidders in real-world multi-unit auctions (EU ETS, Treasury) who must balance value maximization with financial risk, showing that the cost of 'safety' (guaranteeing RoI feasibility regardless of competitors) is bounded by a factor of 2 and is near-optimal in practice (~1.05 on EU ETS data). The tightness of the theoretical bounds and the clean independence from m for the non-safe comparison are elegant, but the practical impact is domain-specific. Open questions include extending to discriminatory-price auctions, time-varying valuations, joint RoI-and-budget constraints, and combinatorial auctions with non-identical items, all of which would broaden applicability.
