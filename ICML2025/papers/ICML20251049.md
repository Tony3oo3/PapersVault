## Problem

The regret analysis of GP bandit algorithms (MVR, PE) relies on an upper bound for the maximum posterior variance whose dependence on noise variance parameters is suboptimal in the low-noise regime, preventing tight guarantees in three important settings: noiseless observations, varying RKHS norm bounds, and non-stationary (heteroscedastic) noise variance. In particular, the noiseless cumulative regret for PE had not been shown to match the conjectured lower bound, the RKHS norm dependence in regret bounds was not proven optimal, and the kernelized extension of the linear bandit with heteroscedastic noise had no existing GP bandit analysis at all.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a compact input domain X ⊂ R^d with a known positive definite kernel (SE or Matérn) and an RKHS norm bound B on the reward function. A key design choice is that the GP noise parameter λ² is set adaptively as Θ(B⁻²) to achieve optimal B-dependence, and in the non-stationary setting the learner directly accesses the true per-step variance proxies ρ_t². The output is a high-probability regret guarantee (cumulative R_T or simple r_T) along with a recommended maximizer x̂_T, with the regret characterized by the cumulative variance proxy V_T = Σρ_t² in the non-stationary case.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new uniform upper bound on the maximum posterior variance under MVR (Lemma 3.1) that improves the noise-parameter dependence by introducing a strictly positive 'tilde' noise parameter λ̃_T ≥ λ̄_T and exploiting the inequality min{1,a} ≤ 2ln(1+a) within a standard MIG-based information gain argument. The proof leverages the elliptical potential count lemma (from Flynn & Reeb, 2025) to bound the number of 'large-variance' steps, yielding a bound of O(T⁻¹√(λ̃_T² · T · γ_T)) that remains valid as the noise variance approaches zero. This bound is then applied in three ways: (i) in the noiseless setting, it gives PE an O(ln T) cumulative regret for SE kernels matching the conjectured lower bound, and MVR an exponentially converging simple regret; (ii) by setting λ² = Θ(B⁻²), it yields regret bounds whose polynomial dependence on B matches the Scarlett et al. (2017) lower bound; (iii) extended to non-stationary variance via a heteroscedastic elliptical potential count lemma (Lemma C.1), it gives VA-PE and VA-MVR algorithms whose regret depends on V_T = Σρ_t² and matches the derived lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

In the noiseless setting, PE achieves O(ln T) cumulative regret for the SE kernel, matching the conjectured lower bound from Vakili (2022), and Õ(T^((d−ν)/d)) for the Matérn kernel with ν < d. For simple regret, MVR achieves O(exp(−½T^(1/(d+1)) ln^(−α)T)) for SE and Õ(T^(−ν/d)) for Matérn, the latter matching the known lower bound. In the RKHS-norm-dependent setting, MVR achieves simple regret Õ(B^(d/(2ν+d)) T^(−ν/(2ν+d))) for Matérn, matching the polynomial dependence on B in the Scarlett et al. (2017) lower bound. For the non-stationary variance setting, VA-PE and VA-MVR achieve regret bounds depending on the cumulative variance proxy V_T = Σρ_t², matching the corresponding lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For noiseless cumulative regret with the SE kernel, PE (this work) achieves O(ln T), strictly improving over GP-UCB's O(√(T ln^d T)) and matching Kernel-AMM-UCB's O(ln^(d+1) T) up to the log power while being deterministic. For Matérn with ν < d, PE matches the conjectured lower bound Õ(T^((d−ν)/d)), tying with REDS but without requiring a level-set assumption, and improving over GP-UCB's Õ(T^((ν+d)/(2ν+d))). For RKHS-norm-dependent regret (Tables 3–5), the proposed MVR and PE analyses achieve optimal polynomial dependence on B (e.g., B^(d/(2ν+d)) for Matérn), whereas existing PE (Li & Scarlett 2022) and MVR (Vakili 2021) carry a max{B,1} factor that is suboptimal when B grows with T. In the non-stationary variance setting (Tables 6–8), VA-PE and VA-MVR achieve regret depending on the cumulative variance V_T (e.g., Õ(V_T^(ν/(2ν+d)) T^(d/(2ν+d))) for Matérn), matching the lower bound, while naive GP-UCB and existing PE depend on the maximum variance ρ̄_T² and are strictly worse when V_T grows sublinearly. The direct extension of the heteroscedastic linear bandit algorithm (VA-GP-UCB) is shown to be suboptimal compared to VA-PE/VA-MVR due to the √γ_t(Σ_t) factor in the adaptive confidence bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP bandit analysis lineage established by Srinivas et al. (2010), Vakili et al. (2021a) for MVR, and Li & Scarlett (2022) for PE, by deriving a tighter uniform upper bound on the maximum posterior variance (Lemma 3.1) that improves the dependence on noise variance parameters in the decreasing regime. The proof technique builds on the elliptical potential count lemma from Flynn & Reeb (2025) and standard information-gain arguments, but achieves a cleaner noise-parameter scaling that prior analyses could not capture. The non-stationary variance setting is explicitly framed as the kernelized extension of heteroscedastic linear bandits (Zhou et al., 2021), a problem the authors note has no prior GP-bandit treatment, and they show that the natural UCB-based extension (VA-GP-UCB) is strictly suboptimal compared to their MVR/PE-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes several optimality gaps in GP bandit theory: matching the conjectured noiseless lower bound for PE cumulative regret, achieving optimal RKHS-norm dependence in regret bounds, and providing the first near-optimal guarantees for GP bandits with time-varying noise variance. The non-stationary variance result is practically relevant since heteroscedastic noise is common in real-world sequential optimization (e.g., experimental design with varying measurement precision), yet had no theoretical treatment in the kernelized setting. Open questions include whether the MIG upper bound can be tightened to the conjectured O(ln^{d/2}(T/λ²)) rate (which would improve the SE-kernel simple regret exponent), whether the PE analysis can leverage the full Corollary 3.2 bound to relax the B-growth conditions, and whether the unknown-variance-proxy setting can be handled.
