## Problem

Existing deep generative models for structure-based drug design primarily optimize binding affinity while neglecting other critical molecular properties such as synthetic accessibility, strain energy, and conformational validity. Purely likelihood-based training struggles to expand the Pareto front across multiple objectives, and single-reward fine-tuning (e.g., DPO on Vina score alone) often degrades the other properties. The paper asks how to jointly steer a generative model toward multiple, heterogeneous quality metrics without sacrificing any single one.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein target represented as a set of 3D atomic coordinates and one-hot atom-type vectors (elements H, C, N, O, S, Se plus 20 amino-acid types and backbone flags), along with a k-NN graph over the binding-site region, and outputs a 3D ligand molecule as a set of continuous atomic coordinates and discrete one-hot atom-type vectors (C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl). During fine-tuning, preference pairs are constructed offline by generating 10 candidate molecules per protein from the pretrained model and ranking them using external tools (AutoDock Vina for docking scores, RDKit for strain energy, SA, and QED), so the training loop itself does not call these tools at each step.

## Method

The paper derives a Direct Preference Optimization loss specifically for Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN-DPO), decomposing it into an atom-coordinate term (weighted squared-error difference between fine-tuned and reference predictions) and an atom-type term (difference of log-likelihoods under categorical sender/receiver distributions), both modulated by the BFN noise schedule α_t. For multi-reward optimization, each reward (Vina Dock, strain energy, QED) is normalized via a softmax over a mini-batch after a direction-consistent transformation, then combined with a variance penalty that discourages molecules excelling on one metric while failing on others. Training further adopts E²PO, which down-weights the DPO loss when the winning molecule's multi-reward advantage is already large, preventing mode collapse. The backbone is MolCRAFT's BFN with an SE(3)-equivariant PosNet3D receiver network (9 layers, 128-dim hidden, 16-head attention), fine-tuned with Adam (lr=0.005, batch 32) for two epochs on a single GPU.

## Results

On the CrossDocked test set (100 target proteins, 100 molecules each), the proposed multi-reward BFN-DPO method achieves the best Vina Score (−7.18 avg), Vina Min (−7.89 avg), SE median (5.56), SA (0.74), and QED (0.55), while tying with AliDiff for best Vina Dock median (−8.64). The method attains four best and four second-best results across eight headline metrics, expanding the Pareto front in binding affinity vs. synthetic accessibility and strain energy. Generated molecule size (22.8 atoms avg) remains comparable to MolCRAFT (22.7), ruling out affinity hacking via larger molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest binding-affinity baselines, AliDiff (single-reward DPO on IPDiff) and IPDiff, the proposed method achieves comparable Vina Dock scores (−8.62 vs. −8.65 avg) while dramatically reducing strain energy (SE median 5.56 vs. 27.60/27.05) and improving SA (0.74 vs. 0.56/0.61), confirming that prior affinity-focused fine-tuning sacrifices conformational validity. Compared to the BFN backbone MolCRAFT, the multi-reward fine-tuning improves Vina Score from −6.59 to −7.18, SE median from 7.92 to 5.56, and QED from 0.50 to 0.55, demonstrating that preference optimization on top of BFNs yields consistent gains across all dimensions. Autoregressive baselines (AR, Pocket2Mol) generate smaller molecules (~17.7 atoms) that excel in SE and SA but lag significantly in binding affinity (Vina Score −5.75/−5.14 vs. −7.18). Diffusion-based baselines (TargetDiff, DecompDiff, DecompOpt) underperform on both binding and conformation stability, with DecompOpt producing the largest molecules (29.4 atoms) and worst SE (21.37). FLAG performs poorly across nearly all metrics, including a positive Vina Score average (16.48), indicating frequent invalid or non-binding structures. Overall, the method is the only one to simultaneously achieve top-tier binding affinity, low strain energy, and high synthetic accessibility, expanding the Pareto front identified in Figure 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from the LLM and diffusion-model literature to Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs), specifically the MolCRAFT architecture, which performs denoising in parameter space rather than data space. The multi-reward extension draws on Kim et al. (2024)'s confidence-aware reward normalization for text-to-image models and AliDiff's (Gu et al., 2024) single-reward DPO for diffusion-based SBDD, but generalizes both to a BFN setting with softmax-based cross-reward normalization and a variance penalty. The SE(3)-equivariant receiver network (PosNet3D) and the BFN sender/receiver/Bayesian-update machinery are inherited directly from MolCRAFT and Graves et al. (2023), so the core generative backbone is unchanged; the novelty lies entirely in the preference-optimization objective and its multi-reward composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that prior SOTA SBDD models (AliDiff, IPDiff) achieve strong Vina scores but generate a large fraction of physically invalid molecules, as confirmed by PoseBuster checks; this paper shows that multi-reward DPO can push the Pareto frontier outward on binding affinity, strain energy, and synthetic accessibility simultaneously rather than trading one for another. A notable limitation is the reliance on offline reward computation via external tools (AutoDock Vina, RDKit), which constrains the method to an offline DPO loop and makes iterative online refinement impractical. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to online reward settings, whether the Clash metric (which resists improvement even under its own single-reward optimization) can be addressed, and whether the BFN-DPO derivation extends to other parameter-space generative frameworks.
