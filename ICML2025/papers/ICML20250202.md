## Problem

Design uncertainty quantification in causal inference has been limited to the completely randomized design paired with the difference-in-means estimator, where the worst-case copula (isotone copula) is known in closed form. In practice, small geographical experiments with heterogeneous effects rely on non-standard designs (matched pairs, covariate-balancing) and estimators (doubly robust, regression-adjusted) for which no analytical variance bounds exist, leaving practitioners without valid uncertainty quantification for design uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the observed potential outcomes (one per unit), binary treatment assignment indicators, the full covariance matrix of the assignment mechanism, and optionally pre-period covariates. The outcome space is discretized to the support of observed outcomes, and a slackness parameter ε controls the trade-off between feasibility and tightness of the marginal-matching constraints. The output is the variance-maximizing joint distribution (worst-case copula) over potential outcomes, from which confidence intervals are constructed via a causal bootstrap that imputes counterfactuals and resamples by rerandomizing units to treatment.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating the variance-maximizing copula problem as an integer linear program, eliminating the need for closed-form solutions. The estimator's design variance is expressed as a quadratic form X^T Q X over binary indicator variables X (encoding which outcome value each unit's potential outcome takes), where Q is derived from the assignment covariance matrix; the quadratic objective is linearized by introducing product variables with standard linear constraints. Constraints enforce that each unit receives exactly one outcome per treatment arm, fix observed outcomes, and impose (relaxed) equality between observed and missing marginal distributions. The formulation generalizes to linear-in-treatment estimators by modifying Q, to quadratic-in-treatment estimators (e.g., regression with covariates) via fourth-order assignment covariances, and to arbitrary known probabilistic assignments by reweighting the marginal constraints with inverse-probability weights. Asymptotic validity is established for unconfounded, conditionally unconfounded, and individualistic assignments with bounded confoundedness, with explicit failure-probability bounds that decay exponentially in N.

## Results

On simulated geographical experiments using IMF GDP data (top 50 countries), the optimal causal bootstrap reduces median 95% CI widths by at least 10% compared to the standard sampling bootstrap across all settings, with an exceptional 50% reduction for the difference-in-means estimator under multiplicative effects (e.g., CI width 154 vs. 185 for 10% effect with 2018 GDP covariates under complete randomization). For the matched-pairs design, the isotone copula bootstrap catastrophically fails with 0% coverage (CIs never include zero in A/A tests), while the optimal causal bootstrap achieves 100% coverage. The method maintains a stable ≥11% CI width improvement over the sampling bootstrap across all effect sizes, with no loss in power or coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard sampling bootstrap (Efron, 1979), the optimal causal bootstrap consistently achieves narrower CIs (≥10% reduction) with equal or better coverage and power across all settings; for example, with 2018 GDP covariates and a 10% multiplicative effect under complete randomization, CI width drops from 185 to 154 (17% reduction) while maintaining 100% power. Against the conservative analytical variance bounds (Imbens & Rubin, 2015), the optimal causal bootstrap is competitive or superior: the conservative estimator achieves the narrowest CIs for small effects under matched pairs (84 vs. 97 at 2.5%) but becomes wider for large effects (310 vs. 203 at 10%), and has much lower power under complete randomization (0.24 vs. 0.39 at 2.5% with covariates). Against the isotone copula causal bootstrap (Aronow et al., 2014; Imbens & Menzel, 2021), the optimal causal bootstrap matches performance exactly under complete randomization (as expected, since the isotone copula is the known variance-maximizing solution there) but is the only method that works under matched pairs: the isotone copula produces 0% coverage in A/A tests and unrealistically narrow CIs (width 44) because it imputes each unit's counterfactual with its paired unit's observed outcome, yielding zero within-pair variance. The paper notes that analytical variance bounds are not typically available for complex designs, whereas the integer programming approach extends to any design with a known treatment covariance matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal bootstrap framework of Aronow et al. (2014) and Imbens & Menzel (2021), which identified the isotone (assortative) copula as the variance-maximizing joint distribution for the difference-in-means estimator under complete randomization. The key departure is replacing the closed-form copula solution with a numerically solved integer program, which removes the need for analytical tractability and enables generalization to arbitrary linear- and quadratic-in-treatment estimators (Horvitz-Thompson, doubly-robust, regression-adjusted) and to non-standard assignment mechanisms (matched pairs, conditionally unconfounded, bounded confoundedness). The approach also connects to the broader design-uncertainty literature (Neyman 1923; Freedman 2008; Lin 2013; Abadie et al. 2020) and to optimal-transport-based bounds (Ji et al. 2023), but avoids the restriction to quadratic forms that limits the convex-optimization approach of Harshaw et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method fills a practical gap for small-sample geographical experiments (hundreds of units, heterogeneous effects, non-standard designs) where design uncertainty dominates but no existing closed-form variance bound applies. The empirical results show 10–50% CI width reductions over the sampling bootstrap with maintained or improved coverage, and the isotone copula bootstrap's complete failure (0% coverage) under matched pairs highlights the fragility of methods not designed for the target setting. Key limitations are computational (solving times scale poorly beyond a few hundred units) and the discrete-outcome assumption, while open questions include extending validity to general confounded assignments and unknown propensity scores.
