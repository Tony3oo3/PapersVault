## Problem

Existing diffusion models for crystalline materials face a fundamental mismatch when operating on fractional coordinates: the score network must be periodic-translation-invariant to respect the hypertorus geometry, but the denoising score-matching target is not invariant, leading to suboptimal training. Prior work either performs Riemannian diffusion directly on the hypertorus (requiring wrapped-normal approximations) or avoids the issue by working in real space, neither of which cleanly resolves the architecture–supervision signal conflict at low noise levels.

## Input / Output

For Crystal Structure Prediction, the model takes a fixed atomic composition a ∈ ℤ^K and generates fractional coordinates f ∈ [0,1)^{3×K} (on a hypertorus) and lattice parameters l as a 6-dimensional rotation-invariant vector (three edge lengths and three interaxial angles). For De-novo Generation, the model additionally samples the number of atoms K from the empirical training distribution and generates the composition a via one of three discrete/continuous diffusion schemes (one-hot, analog-bits, or absorbing-state). The multi-modal output is structured as a fully connected geometric graph combining discrete species labels with continuous geometric features.

## Method

KLDM generalizes the Trivialized Diffusion Model (TDM) to geometric graphs by coupling fractional coordinates (elements of the Lie group SO(2)^{3×K} ≅ hypertorus) with auxiliary Euclidean velocity variables on the Lie algebra. The forward process is a coupled ODE–SDE: coordinates evolve deterministically via d f_t = f_t v_t dt (guaranteeing the trajectory stays on the manifold), while velocities follow an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE in flat space, so all stochastic noise is injected into the Euclidean variables. The transition kernel factors into a Wrapped Normal over coordinate displacements and a Gaussian over velocities, and the denoising score-matching target is computed with respect to the velocity variables (Euclidean) rather than the coordinates (manifold), eliminating the need for Riemannian gradient approximations. Three key design choices accelerate convergence and improve performance: (1) zero initial velocities (v₀ = 0), which enables a simplified score parameterization where one term of the target is computed in closed form; (2) a GNN score network with periodic-translation invariance via pairwise coordinate differences featurized with periodic functions; and (3) a zero-net-translation constraint on the velocity field (Σ_k v_k = 0) that prevents global drift and mitigates the architecture–target mismatch at low noise levels.

## Results

On the CSP task, KLDM-x0(PC) achieves the best Match Rate on both MP-20 (65.83% @1, 80.18% @20) and MPTS-52 (23.93% @1, 37.10% @20), outperforming all baselines. On PEROV-5, KLDM is competitive with FLOWMM (53.14% vs 53.15% MR @1) and achieves the best RMSE (0.0647 @1). On the DNG task (MP-20), KLDM-x0(D) achieves 59.21% stability and 0.155 eV/atom above hull, surpassing DIFFCSP (41.25%, 0.189) but trailing MATTERGEN-MP (47.05%, 0.201) on stability while beating it on energy above hull.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CSP, KLDM-x0(PC) clearly outperforms all four baselines on the two larger datasets: on MP-20 it achieves 65.83% MR vs. FLOWMM's 61.39%, EQUICSP's 57.59%, and DIFFCSP's 51.49%; on MPTS-52 it reaches 23.93% MR vs. FLOWMM's 17.54%, EQUICSP's 14.85%, and DIFFCSP's 12.19%. On the simpler PEROV-5 dataset, KLDM ties FLOWMM on MR@1 (53.14% vs. 53.15%) but achieves the lowest RMSE (0.0647 vs. 0.0992). On DNG, KLDM-x0(D) beats DIFFCSP on all three primary metrics (RMSD 0.283 vs. 0.413, above-hull 0.155 vs. 0.189, stability 59.21% vs. 41.25%) but trails MATTERGEN-MP on RMSD (0.283 vs. 0.147) and S.U.N. (18.52% vs. 25.76%), while surpassing it on energy above hull (0.155 vs. 0.201) and stability (59.21% vs. 47.05%). The authors attribute the remaining gap to MATTERGEN's more expressive real-space denoiser, its use of a PC sampler for lattice parameters, and training on a re-optimized (filtered) version of MP-20.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLDM generalizes the Trivialized Diffusion Model (TDM) of Zhu et al. (2024) from single Lie group elements to geometric graphs of crystalline materials, coupling fractional coordinates on the hypertorus with auxiliary Euclidean velocity variables in the Lie algebra. This departs from the dominant approach in crystal diffusion (DIFFCSP, EQUICSP) that performs Riemannian diffusion directly on the torus via wrapped-normal transition kernels, instead offsetting the noise process to flat space so that the score is computed with respect to Euclidean velocity variables. The score-based SDE framework of Song et al. (2021) and the graph neural network architecture of Jiao et al. (2023) remain the backbone, while the zero-net-translation velocity constraint addresses the architecture–supervision mismatch identified by Lin et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KLDM offers a principled, simulation-free alternative to Riemannian diffusion on the hypertorus that is competitive with current SOTA on both CSP and DNG, and the kinetic-Langevin-on-Lie-algebra formulation could transfer to other periodic or group-structured data. However, validation still relies on ML interatomic potentials (MatterSim) rather than DFT, and the translation-invariance mismatch is mitigated but not fully eliminated at high noise levels. Open directions include incorporating space-group or Wyckoff-position constraints, exploring alternative velocity noise schedules, and scaling to larger systems such as MOFs.
