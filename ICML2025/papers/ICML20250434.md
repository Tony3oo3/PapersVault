## Problem

Class imbalance in multi-class classification with long-tailed distributions remains a major challenge, yet existing remedies—data resampling, cost-sensitive weighting, and logistic loss modifications—lack solid theoretical grounding. The paper proves that cost-sensitive methods are not Bayes-consistent with respect to the standard zero-one loss, and shows that the closest prior theory (Cao et al., 2019) targets the balanced loss rather than misclassification and prescribes margin values that directly contradict the correct theoretical solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) paired with class labels during training and outputs a predicted class label. A distinguishing detail is that the loss operates on per-class confidence margins ρ_k applied multiplicatively to pairwise logit differences h(x,j)−h(x,k), rather than additively shifting individual logits; the ρ_k values are tuned via cross-validation around theoretically derived optima proportional to m_k^{1/3}.

## Method

The paper introduces a class-imbalanced margin loss that assigns distinct confidence margins ρ_+ and ρ_− (binary) or ρ_k (multi-class) to each class, and proves its strong H-consistency with respect to the zero-one loss for any complete hypothesis set. Generalization bounds are derived in terms of a new class-sensitive Rademacher complexity that weights Rademacher variables by 1/ρ_k per class. The resulting IMMAX algorithm minimizes a regularized objective where a non-increasing convex surrogate Ψ (hinge, logistic, or exponential) is applied to logit differences scaled by (ρ_++ρ_−)/ρ_k, yielding a strict generalization of SVM and logistic regression. In the separable case, the optimal margins scale as ρ_k ∝ m_k^{1/3}, prescribing a larger margin for the majority class—opposite to the recommendation of Cao et al. (2019)—and the multi-class extension expresses the complexity term via Rényi divergence of order 3 to guide efficient hyperparameter search.

## Results

On long-tailed imbalanced CIFAR-10 (ratio 200), IMMAX with ResNet-34 achieves 96.11% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline LDAM (95.45%) by 0.66 points. On CIFAR-100 (ratio 200), IMMAX reaches 80.47% vs. LDAM's 79.18%, and on Tiny ImageNet (ratio 200) it achieves 65.20% vs. LDAM's 63.70%. The paper reports that IMMAX consistently outperforms all baselines across all three datasets and all imbalance ratios (200, 100, 10), with at least a 0.6% absolute accuracy improvement over the runner-up in every scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMMAX outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and imbalance ratio tested. The closest competitor is LDAM (Cao et al., 2019), which IMMAX beats by 0.66 points on CIFAR-10 (96.11 vs. 95.45), 1.29 points on CIFAR-100 (80.47 vs. 79.18), and 1.50 points on Tiny ImageNet (65.20 vs. 63.70) at ratio 200. Cost-sensitive methods (RW, CB, FOCAL) and logistic loss modifications (BS, EQUAL, LA) all trail IMMAX by 1–4 points on CIFAR-100 at ratio 200, with RW performing worst (67.52% vs. IMMAX's 80.47%). On the step-imbalanced setting (Table 2), IMMAX again leads all baselines, e.g., 95.42% vs. LDAM's 94.85% on CIFAR-10 at ratio 200. In binary classification with linear models (Table 3), IMMAX with hinge loss beats both standard SVM (HINGE) and LDAM by roughly 0.5–1.0 points across three one-vs-rest CIFAR-10 tasks. The paper notes that the advantage of IMMAX grows with increasing imbalance ratio, suggesting the theoretical margin-based approach scales better under extreme class disparity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ρ-margin loss framework from Mohri et al. (2018) and the H-consistency bounds methodology of Awasthi et al. (2022) to the imbalanced classification setting, introducing class-specific confidence margins (ρ+, ρ−) in place of a single margin. It departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of cost-sensitive learning and logistic loss modifications (LDAM, Balanced Softmax, Logit Adjustment) by targeting the standard zero-one misclassification loss rather than the balanced loss, and by applying margin parameters multiplicatively to logit differences rather than additively to individual logits. The paper explicitly positions against Cao et al. (2019), showing that LDAM's theoretical margin recommendations are the opposite of what is optimal for zero-one loss, and proves that cost-sensitive methods lack Bayes-consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that cost-sensitive methods are not Bayes-consistent with respect to zero-one loss is a significant negative result that challenges a widely-deployed practical approach, and the demonstration that LDAM's separable-case theory prescribes margins in the wrong direction for standard misclassification corrects a commonly-cited NeurIPS 2019 analysis. The IMMAX framework provides the first H-consistency guarantees for imbalanced classification targeting the standard zero-one loss, offering a principled alternative to heuristic-driven reweighting. Open questions include whether the theoretical margin prescriptions (involving cube-root scaling with class frequencies) translate to consistent gains in non-separable, high-dimensional regimes, and whether the framework extends naturally to other evaluation metrics like F1 or AUC where the zero-one loss is not the target.
