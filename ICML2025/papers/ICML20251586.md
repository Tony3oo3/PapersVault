## Problem

Parametric-based Generalized Category Discovery methods (e.g., SimGCD) rely exclusively on self-supervised pseudo-labels for clustering unlabeled data, leading to unreliable base class discrimination that falls below even a supervised-only reference. The paper quantifies this gap via a new oracle base accuracy (OB) metric and identifies noisy soft labels from self-supervision as the root cause of degraded base performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (resized and randomly cropped) from seven GCD benchmarks, using a pre-trained DINO or DINOv2 ViT backbone. Each image is processed through two augmented views, and the model outputs a probability distribution over K total classes (base + novel) via a cosine-similarity prototype classifier. Training is transductive: all labeled and unlabeled samples participate jointly, with the total class count K assumed as a prior. At inference, only the main CLS branch is retained, making latency negligible compared to SimGCD.

## Method

RLCD introduces a Reciprocal Learning Framework that inserts a shared auxiliary token (AUX) before the final ViT block, feeding a base-only classifier while the CLS token feeds an all-class classifier. During training, the main branch filters pseudo-base samples to the auxiliary branch, which in turn distills reliable base-class distributions back via a KL-divergence loss weighted by the auxiliary branch's maximum probability, creating a virtuous cycle that strengthens base discrimination. To counteract the resulting bias toward base classes, a Class-wise Distribution Regularization (CDR) loss enforces consistency of class-wise expected probability matrices between two views, effectively boosting prediction confidence toward one-hot distributions and improving novel class recall. The method also removes SimGCD's InfoNCE loss (which conflicts with SupCon) and applies CDR to both branches, with the auxiliary branch discarded at inference.

## Results

On the four fine-grained GCD datasets (CUB200, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Herbarium-19) with DINO ViT-B/16, RLCD achieves an average cluster accuracy of 60.5%, outperforming the strongest parametric baseline SPTNet (56.9%) by 3.6% and SimGCD (53.1%) by 7.4%. On the three generic datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, ImageNet-100), RLCD reaches 89.2% average accuracy with DINO and 94.1% with DINOv2, surpassing LegoGCD (88.4%) and CiPR (92.5%) respectively. The method also achieves the best oracle base accuracy across all seven datasets, with particularly large gains on fine-grained benchmarks (e.g., 86.9% OB on CUB200 vs. 80.5% for SimGCD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLCD achieves the best overall accuracy on all seven GCD datasets, with the largest margins on fine-grained benchmarks: it outperforms SPTNet by 4.2 points on CUB200 (70.0 vs. 65.8), 5.9 on Stanford Cars (64.9 vs. 59.0), and 1.3 on FGVC-Aircraft (60.6 vs. 59.3). Against the dominant parametric baseline SimGCD, RLCD gains 9.7 points on CUB200 and 11.1 on Stanford Cars, driven primarily by superior base-class discrimination (79.1% vs. 65.6% base accuracy on CUB200). On generic datasets, RLCD edges out LegoGCD by 0.8% average (89.2 vs. 88.4 on DINO) and CiPR by 1.6% on DINOv2 (94.1 vs. 92.5), though it trails CiPR by 0.3 points on CIFAR10 with DINO (97.4 vs. 97.7). Clustering-based methods (k-means, GCD, GPC) are consistently 10–20 points behind parametric approaches, confirming the paper's motivation for the parametric framework. The auxiliary branch's pseudo-label accuracy (Table 4) exceeds both SimGCD and the main CLS branch across all datasets, validating the reciprocal learning design as the source of the base-class gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parametric GCD framework introduced by SimGCD (Wen et al., 2023), which replaced clustering-based inference with a prototype classifier trained via self-consistency and entropy regularization. The key departure is the identification that self-supervised pseudo-labels in parametric GCD actually degrade base-class discrimination below a supervised-only reference, and the proposal of a reciprocal learning framework (auxiliary token + base-only classifier + cross-branch KL distillation) to correct this. The Class-wise Distribution Regularization (CDR) loss is adapted from Zhang et al. (2024, ICML) to counteract the base-class bias introduced by the distillation, making the two components complementary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle base accuracy (OB) metric provides a useful diagnostic that reveals a previously underappreciated weakness in parametric GCD methods: they can be worse than supervised-only training on known classes. The practical impact is strong—RLCD achieves SOTA across seven datasets with negligible inference overhead (auxiliary branch discarded at test time), making it a drop-in improvement for practitioners using the SimGCD framework. Open questions remain around domain shift, continual discovery, and performance under very limited labeled data, all of which the authors flag as future directions.
