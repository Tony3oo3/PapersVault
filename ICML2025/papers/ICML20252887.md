## Problem

Standard imitation learning assumes the expert and imitator share the same action space, but in practice the imitator often operates under action constraints (e.g., lower torque limits, power budgets) that make expert actions infeasible. This capability gap causes 'occupancy measure distortion'—projection layers prevent the imitator from matching the expert's state-action distribution, leading to compounding trajectory misalignment. No prior method addresses this action-constrained imitation learning (ACIL) setting where the demonstrative expert has a strictly larger feasible action set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unconstrained expert state-action trajectories, a state-dependent feasible action set C(s) (box, power-limit, or L2 constraints), and a learned ensemble of probabilistic dynamics models. It produces two outputs: (1) surrogate action-constrained demonstrations—state-action trajectories that trace similar state sequences to the expert but use only feasible actions, potentially longer in duration—and (2) a trained policy via any off-the-shelf IL algorithm (BC, GAIL, CFIL, etc.) applied to the surrogate data. The surrogate generation is fully offline, requiring no environment interaction beyond dynamics model training.

## Method

DTWIL decomposes ACIL into two decoupled stages: (1) generating surrogate demonstrations via MPC-based trajectory alignment, and (2) training a policy with any standard IL method on the surrogate data. In Stage 1, for each expert trajectory, MPC sequentially plans H-step rollouts using a CEM optimizer with rejection sampling to enforce constraints, selecting the rollout that minimizes the DTW distance between the planned state sequence and the corresponding expert segment. A progression parameter t_pg tracks how far the surrogate has advanced through the expert trajectory, updated asynchronously based on the DTW warping path, allowing the constrained agent to take more steps than the expert. Expert Regularized Control (ERC) stabilizes planning by blending the first few sampled actions with projected expert actions, which is critical in balance-sensitive tasks like Hopper. The DTW criterion is essential over pointwise ℓ₂ distance because it naturally handles the temporal stretching that occurs when constrained actions require more steps to cover the same state-space path.

## Results

DTWIL achieves the best performance across all 8 constrained tasks (Maze2d M+B/M+O, Hopper H+B/H+M, HalfCheetah HC+B/HC+O, Table-Wiping W+B/W+L2) under a 50K-step budget. On Hopper, DTWIL reaches 2844.68±57.77 (H+B) and 2873.88±240.46 (H+M), substantially outperforming plain BC (2204.83 and 1233.96 respectively). On HalfCheetah, DTWIL attains 2669.41±4.56 (HC+B) and 2637.34±26.82 (HC+O), while online baselines like GAIL and OPOLO remain near zero return. On Maze2d, DTWIL achieves 0.77±0.04 (M+B) and 0.87±0.04 (M+O) success rate, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTWIL consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across every task. Against the closest offline competitor CFIL-sa, DTWIL achieves 2844.68 vs. 2666±55 on Hopper H+B and 2873.88 vs. 76.8±6.5 on H+M, a dramatic gap under tight power constraints. Online methods (GAIL, GAIfO, OPOLO, DIFO) suffer severe sample inefficiency under the 50K-step budget, with returns near zero or negative on Hopper and HalfCheetah (e.g., OPOLO: −163±47 on H+B; DIFO: 6±31 on H+B). BCO, the strongest LfO baseline, reaches only 0.22 on Maze2d M+B and 10.8 on HalfCheetah HC+B, far below DTWIL's 0.77 and 2669.41. Plain BC (without surrogate data) drops from 2844.68 to 2204.83 on H+B and from 2873.88 to 1233.96 on H+M, confirming that the DTWIL surrogate generation is the critical component. The ablation in Table 3 further shows DTW alignment outperforms ℓ₂ alignment by ~1700 return on Hopper H+B (2844 vs. 1054), validating the choice of warping metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of action-constrained reinforcement learning (ACRL) and imitation learning (IL), but deliberately departs from both. From ACRL, it borrows the problem structure of constrained action spaces but rejects the standard projection-layer solution, arguing it causes 'occupancy measure distortion' that is fundamentally incompatible with IL's distribution-matching objective. From IL, it draws on the GAIL/OPOLO/CFIL lineage for downstream policy learning but introduces a novel preprocessing stage: generating surrogate demonstrations via MPC guided by DTW distance (itself a classical time-series alignment tool from speech recognition, repurposed here for trajectory matching). The CEM optimizer and probabilistic dynamics model ensemble are inherited directly from PETS, making the method a principled composition of model-based planning and sequence alignment rather than a new learning architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode (occupancy measure distortion) that explains why naively combining ACRL projection with IL is insufficient, and provides a clean two-stage decomposition that decouples constraint satisfaction from policy learning. This modularity means DTWIL can plug into any downstream IL algorithm (BC, CFIL, etc.), which is validated experimentally. Key limitations include the reliance on a learned dynamics model (which may be inaccurate in high-dimensional or stochastic environments), the computational cost of MPC at every timestep, and evaluation limited to relatively low-dimensional MuJoCo/Robosuite tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., dexterous hands), whether it works without a reliable dynamics model, and how it performs in real-world deployment where constraint boundaries may be uncertain.
