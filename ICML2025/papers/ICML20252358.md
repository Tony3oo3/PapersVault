## Problem

Permutation-based privacy-preserving LLM inference schemes (PermLLM, STIP, Centaur) claim security by arguing the permutation space is too large to reverse, but no prior work demonstrated that LLM hidden states are actually decodable in the open-weights setting. This paper shows that a simple vocab-matching attack nearly perfectly recovers user prompts from intermediate hidden states, even under sequence, hidden-dimension, and factorized-2D permutations, effectively invalidating the security guarantees of these schemes.

## Input / Output

The attack takes layer-l hidden states h ∈ R^{N×d} (optionally permuted or noised) and known model weights, and outputs the original N-token input sequence. Matching uses L1-distance with a tuned ε-threshold to handle floating-point non-determinism, and a proposal model orders vocabulary iteration to reduce average search from V/2 to ~100 tokens per step. Cascade takes a user prompt, shards its tokens into disjoint index sets {R_i} across α CompNodes and {S_j} across β AttnNodes, and produces standard LLM inference output with no approximation of non-linearities.

## Method

The vocab-matching attack exploits the autoregressive (unidirectional) property of decoder-only transformers: at step n, it performs a batched forward pass over all V vocabulary tokens appended to the already-decoded prefix, and selects the token whose candidate hidden state's last row matches the target within L1-distance ε, reducing an exponential V^N search to O(VN). For permuted settings, the attack is adapted by matching against any remaining row (sequence-dim), using sorted L1-distance (hidden-dim), or combining both (factorized-2D). The Cascade defense shards tokens along the sequence dimension so that each CompNode holds only a (c,δ)-clustered subset of positions, creating gaps of size δ−c+1 between consecutive tokens it holds; this forces any generalized vocab-matching attack to search over V^{δ−c+1} sequences per gap, which is infeasible for realistic vocabulary sizes. Cascade splits each layer into a pre-pass (CompNodes compute Q,K,V projections), an attention-pass (AttnNodes compute cross-shard attention using partial softmax statistics), and a post-pass (CompNodes reassemble attention outputs), incurring negligible computational overhead and ~160× less communication than prior SMPC schemes.

## Results

The vocab-matching attack achieves 100% perfect decoding of hidden states into original input tokens across all tested layers (1–26) for both Gemma-2-2B-IT and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and retains 97–100% accuracy even under sequence-dimension, hidden-dimension, and factorized-2D permutations. For the proposed Cascade defense, a single 128-token forward pass on Bert-Large takes 1.57 s with α=8 CompNodes, compared to 141.2 s for MPCFormer and 73.7 s for Puma. Communication overhead is 0.203 GB for Cascade (α=8) versus 32.6 GB for MPCFormer and 27.2 GB for Puma on the same task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cascade is compared against two recent SMPC-based LLM inference schemes: MPCFormer (Li et al., 2023) and Puma (Dong et al., 2023). On Bert-Large with a 128-token prompt, Cascade (α=8) completes a forward pass in 1.58 s, making it approximately 90× faster than MPCFormer (141.2 s) and 45× faster than Puma (73.7 s). In terms of communication, Cascade transmits 0.203 GB versus 32.6 GB for MPCFormer (≈160× less) and 27.2 GB for Puma (≈140× less). Even in its slowest multi-node configuration, Cascade remains within an order of magnitude of vanilla (unprotected) inference (0.27 s), whereas the SMPC baselines are 270× and 273× slower than vanilla, respectively. The paper also demonstrates that the vocab-matching attack defeats the permutation-based privacy mechanisms used by schemes like PermLLM, STIP, and Centaur, which are not directly benchmarked in the runtime table but are shown to be insecure in the open-weights setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of privacy-preserving LLM inference (SMPC schemes like MPCFormer and Puma) and permutation-based schemes (PermLLM, STIP, Centaur) that exploit transformer permutation-equivariance to avoid expensive cryptographic operations. It departs from prior hidden-state reversal work (Wan et al. 2024, Morris et al. 2023b), which trained learned transformers to reverse hiddens with ~60–75% F1, by showing that a simple vocab-matching attack exploiting the autoregressive property and known model weights achieves near-perfect decoding. The paper also extends the reversal problem from single-vector embedding inversion (Song & Raghunathan 2020, Morris et al. 2023a) to full intermediate LLM state sequences, and introduces Cascade as a token-sharding defense that occupies a new middle ground between full SMPC and permutation-based obfuscation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that permutation-based schemes are fundamentally insecure in the open-weights setting invalidates the security justification of several recent, widely-cited protocols and forces the field to reconsider what constitutes adequate obfuscation for LLM inference. Cascade offers a practical 90–160× efficiency improvement over existing SMPC baselines, but it provides only statistical (not cryptographic) security, and the paper itself notes that the combination of permutation plus low-level Gaussian noise is necessary but not proven sufficient. Open questions include whether the vocab-matching threshold ρ can be pushed higher by alternative sharding strategies, whether the attack generalizes to encoder-only or bidirectional architectures, and whether the open-weights assumption (increasingly common) can be relaxed without losing the attack's effectiveness.
