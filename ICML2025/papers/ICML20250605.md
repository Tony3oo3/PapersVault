## Problem

Existing protective perturbation methods against voice cloning have never been systematically evaluated under realistic threat models where attackers apply adversarial purification before cloning. This paper reveals that such protections are vulnerable to purification, and that existing purification methods introduce systematic distortions in VC model embedding spaces—reducing inter-class separability and deviating purified samples from their clean counterparts—which degrades cloning fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protected audio waveform (16 kHz, with bounded adversarial perturbation δ) and its text transcription as input, producing a purified audio waveform as output. The Purification stage operates directly on the raw waveform, while the Refinement stage operates in the complex spectrogram domain (STFT with window 510, hop 128, square-root Hann window, square-root magnitude warping). The phoneme conditioning signal Λ is a time-aligned sequence of average magnitude spectrograms retrieved from a precomputed phoneme dictionary via forced alignment.

## Method

PhonePuRe is a two-stage cascade: (1) a fine-tuned unconditional DiffWave model performs forward-then-reverse diffusion (T_pur=3 steps) on the waveform to preliminarily remove adversarial noise; (2) a phoneme-guided score-based diffusion model (NCSN++ architecture, OUSDE formulation) refines the purified complex spectrogram toward the clean distribution using 30 predictor-corrector steps with annealed Langevin dynamics. The key insight enabling the Refinement stage is that after Purification, clean and protected samples exhibit similar distributions in VC embedding space, so the model is trained only on (purified-clean → clean) pairs and the learned mapping generalizes to purified-protected inputs. Phoneme representations—computed as average linear magnitude spectrograms per phoneme via Montreal Forced Aligner—are concatenated with the purified spectrogram as conditioning, exploiting the observation that protective perturbations target speaker identity rather than linguistic content.

## Results

On the overall evaluation (25 speakers × 5 sentences × 6 VC models), PhonePuRe achieves an average xSVA of 71.1% and dSVA of 81.8%, substantially outperforming the best baseline AudioPure (59.7% xSVA, 64.7% dSVA) and WavePurifier (41.9% xSVA, 39.4% dSVA). Against the strongest protection method (AntiFake), PhonePuRe reaches 66.0% xSVA and 76.2% dSVA, surpassing AudioPure by 25.9 and 31.1 percentage points respectively. Objective MOS scores confirm that purified speech retains naturalness (3.36 ± 0.58), close to clean speech (3.42 ± 0.59), while protected speech degrades to 3.16 ± 0.65.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhonePuRe outperforms all five baselines across every protection method and VC model. The strongest baseline, AudioPure (diffusion-based, ICLR 2023), achieves 59.7% xSVA / 64.7% dSVA overall but is surpassed by PhonePuRe by 11.4 and 17.1 percentage points respectively; the gap is largest against AntiFake (31.1 pp on dSVA). WavePurifier (hierarchical diffusion, MobiCom 2024) reaches 41.9% xSVA / 39.4% dSVA, trailing PhonePuRe by roughly 29 pp on both metrics. The transformation-based methods (WaveGuard, SpeakerGuard) and DualPure perform poorly overall (≤25.6% xSVA), as simple filtering or low-resolution mel-spectrogram reconstruction fails to preserve the fine-grained spectral details that VC models require. Notably, even under white-box adaptive protection (BPDA and adjoint-gradient strategies), PhonePuRe maintains dSVA above 0.8, indicating that current perturbation-generation techniques struggle to counter the proposed purification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based adversarial purification line (DiffWave for waveform-level purification, NCSN++/OUSDE score-based diffusion for the refinement stage, and Welker et al.'s complex-STFT formulation) but repurposes them for a specific security task: bypassing protective perturbations designed to defeat voice cloning. The key conceptual departure from prior audio purification work (AudioPure, WavePurifier, DualPure) is the observation that methods optimized for classification preserve only coarse features and introduce systematic distortions in VC model embedding space, motivating the phoneme-guided refinement stage that aligns purified samples back toward the clean distribution. The paper also extends the threat model for VC defense evaluation by explicitly incorporating an attacker-side purification step, which prior protective perturbation papers (AntiFake, AttackVC, VoiceGuard) did not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art protective perturbations can be substantially neutralized by purification methods (raising SVA from <20% to 60–80%) challenges the assumption that adversarial perturbation-based VC defenses provide meaningful security, with direct implications for organizations relying on such protections. Open questions include whether more robust perturbation strategies (e.g., multi-level feature perturbations, distortion-layer integration) can resist purification, whether adaptive timesteps or VC-model-aware purification would further close the gap, and how to balance the computational cost of the two-stage diffusion pipeline against the offline nature of real attacks. The paper's limitation of not leveraging VC model information during purification also leaves room for more targeted attack methods.
