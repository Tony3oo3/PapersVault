## Problem

In cooperative MARL under partial observability without communication channels, existing agent-modelling approaches infer belief representations that are (a) not optimized with respect to the agent's own value function, (b) contaminated by redundant joint-state features that harm policy performance, and (c) disconnected from exploration, making them ineffective in sparse-reward settings where agents must discover high-value states through coordinated joint actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251235/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes its own local observation o^i (structured vectors encoding positions, velocities, levels, or grid-cell contents depending on the benchmark) and its action-observation history h^i to produce a discrete action a^i and a continuous belief embedding z^i. The variational encoder is deliberately conditioned only on the current observation o^i rather than the full history h^i, so that novelty in the intrinsic reward reflects genuinely new observations rather than new trajectories. During training (CTDE), the encoder-decoder also receives other agents' observations o^{-i} as reconstruction targets, but at execution time only z^i is available to the policy network.

## Method

SMPE² builds on the MAA2C actor-critic backbone and adds two concurrent mechanisms. First, a per-agent variational encoder-decoder infers a belief embedding z^i from o^i and reconstructs other agents' observations, but a set of learnable AM filters w^i (sigmoid MLPs, one per other agent) element-wise gate the reconstruction targets in a self-supervised manner, explicitly discarding non-informative joint-state features; a second critic V_k trained on the filtered state ŝ = o^i ⊕ (w^i·o^{-i}) ensures the filters are shaped by policy optimization rather than pure reconstruction. Second, an adversarial count-based intrinsic reward is computed via SimHash over z^i (reward = 1/√n(SH(z^i))), which incentivizes each agent to discover novel observations that simultaneously serve as unseen, adversarial reconstruction targets for the other agents' encoders, thereby improving everyone's state-discriminating ability. The overall loss combines the standard actor-critic policy gradient, the ELBO reconstruction loss with filtered targets, a KL divergence on z^i, a norm regularization preventing w^i from collapsing to zero, and the second critic's TD loss.

## Results

SMPE² is evaluated on three cooperative MARL benchmarks (MPE, LBF, RWARE) using average episodic reward as the primary metric over 10M (MPE/LBF) or 40M (RWARE) training steps, averaged over six seeds. On MPE Spread (3–8 agents), SMPE (without intrinsic rewards) consistently outperforms MAA2C, COMA, MAPPO, and ATM, with the gap widening as agent count increases. On LBF, full SMPE² fully solves the previously open-challenge tasks 2s-9x9-3p-2f and 4s-11x11-3p-2f, while all other baselines fail to achieve positive episodic reward on the 11×11 tasks. On RWARE (tiny-2ag-hard, tiny-4ag-hard, small-4ag-hard), SMPE² achieves superior performance across all three tasks. In terms of efficiency, SMPE² runs approximately 25× faster than MASER, 30× faster than EMC, 17× faster than EOI, and 2× faster than ATM on LBF 2s-12x12-2p-2f.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMPE² outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark it is evaluated on. Against the standard CTDE baselines (MAA2C, COMA, MAPPO, ATM), SMPE² achieves higher episodic reward on MPE Spread (all agent counts 3–8), LBF (all six tasks), and RWARE (all three tasks), with the advantage most pronounced in sparse-reward settings where the adversarial exploration component is active. Against the intrinsic-motivation baselines (EOI, EMC, MASER) on LBF and RWARE, SMPE² is the only method that fully solves the hardest sparse-reward tasks (2s-9x9-3p-2f, 4s-11x11-3p-2f); EMC and MASER fail to improve upon QMIX-level performance because they generate misleading intrinsic rewards from low-value data or irrelevant sub-goals, while EOI's diversity-based exploration is unnecessary for homogeneous agents. In the modelling-method comparison (Figure 6), SMPE² significantly outperforms both SIDE and MLIAM on MPE and LBF, attributed to SIDE not using its inferred embeddings at execution time and MLIAM suffering from an exponentially large action-reconstruction target space. SMPE² is also substantially faster than all exploration-based baselines (EOI, EMC, MASER, ATM) in wall-clock training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the agent modelling (AM) / opponent modelling line of work in MARL (LIAM, SIDE, and related approaches) that uses encoder-decoder architectures to infer beliefs about unobserved state from local observations. It departs from standard AM in three key ways: (1) it formally connects state modelling to policy optimization via a variational objective (Proposition 2.1), rather than treating belief inference as a disconnected auxiliary task; (2) it introduces learnable AM filters (w^i) that explicitly discard non-informative joint state features during reconstruction, addressing a known performance-degradation issue; and (3) it repurposes the inferred belief embeddings for a novel adversarial count-based exploration scheme where discovering novel observations simultaneously serves as adversarial reconstruction targets for other agents. The method is implemented on top of the MAA2C CTDE backbone and leverages SimHash-based intrinsic rewards (à la Tang et al., 2017) applied to the latent belief space rather than raw observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical challenge in cooperative MARL: how agents can form useful state beliefs and explore effectively under partial observability without communication channels, which is critical for real-world deployments where communication is costly or unavailable. The finding that SMPE² fully solves LBF tasks previously identified as open challenges (2s-9x9-3p-2f, 4s-11x11-3p-2f) is notable, and the adversarial exploration framing—where an agent's curiosity implicitly improves others' state inference—offers a conceptually clean mechanism for coupling exploration with cooperation. Open questions remain around scalability to larger agent populations, robustness to stochastic/noisy observations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively low-dimensional grid and particle environments tested here. The authors also acknowledge broader MARL limitations (limited theoretical guarantees, poor generalization, explainability) that this work does not address.
