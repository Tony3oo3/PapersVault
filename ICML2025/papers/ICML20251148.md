## Problem

Sparse autoencoder (SAE) development has been guided primarily by the unsupervised sparsity-fidelity tradeoff, yet gains on this proxy metric do not reliably translate to better practical performance on interpretability, feature disentanglement, or downstream tasks like unlearning. No existing benchmark captures the full range of properties practitioners care about, making it difficult to determine whether architectural improvements genuinely advance SAE quality or merely shift the sparsity-fidelity curve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251148/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAEBench takes pre-trained SAE weights (across 7 architectures at widths of 4k–1M latents and L0 sparsities from 20 to 1000) along with precomputed residual stream activations from Gemma-2-2B (layer 12) or Pythia-160M (layer 8), and produces eight metric scores organized into four capability categories: Concept Detection (Sparse Probing, Feature Absorption), Interpretability (LLM-judged auto-interp), Reconstruction (Loss Recovered), and Feature Disentanglement (RAVEL, Unlearning, SCR, TPP). All eight evaluations run via a single script in approximately 65 minutes per SAE after a one-time 107-minute setup phase, and results are browsable interactively at neuronpedia.org/sae-bench.

## Method

SAEBench organizes evaluation around four fundamental SAE capabilities measured by eight metrics, combining established approaches (sparsity-fidelity, LLM-judged interpretability, sparse probing, RAVEL, unlearning) with two novel zero-ablation metrics—Spurious Correlation Removal (SCR) and Targeted Probe Perturbation (TPP)—that test whether concepts are fully captured by small, isolated latent sets. The benchmark trains and evaluates over 200 SAEs across seven architectures (ReLU, Matryoshka BatchTopK, TopK, BatchTopK, Gated, JumpReLU, P-Annealing) using the dictionary_learning library, sweeping widths and sparsities under identical training protocols. A key finding is that Matryoshka SAEs, despite slightly worse sparsity-fidelity scores, substantially outperform other architectures on feature disentanglement metrics, and this advantage grows with dictionary size—revealing that hierarchical architectures avoid the feature-splitting degradation that causes inverse scaling in non-hierarchical designs. The framework is designed for extensibility, speed, automation, and reproducibility, enabling systematic comparison of new SAE methods against a standardized suite of open-source baselines.

## Results

Across 200+ SAEs (7 architectures, widths 4k–65k, L0 20–1000) on Gemma-2-2B layer 12, Matryoshka BatchTopK achieves the best scores on 5 of 8 SAEBench metrics (Absorption, RAVEL, Sparse Probing, SCR, TPP) in the typical L0 range of 40–200, while performing worse than TopK and BatchTopK on the sparsity-fidelity frontier. Hierarchical (Matryoshka) architectures outperform non-hierarchical ones by 30–40% on SCR and feature absorption. All SAEs significantly outperform a PCA baseline (sparse probing accuracy 0.65 on Gemma-2-2B layer 12), and moderate L0 values of 50–150 provide the best compromise across metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Matryoshka BatchTopK dominates on feature disentanglement and concept detection (5/8 metrics) but trails TopK and BatchTopK on the sparsity-fidelity frontier, revealing that reconstruction quality does not predict downstream interpretability performance. BatchTopK and TopK lead on Loss Recovered in the low-L0 regime (<100), with BatchTopK > TopK Jump > Gated > Matryoshka > P-Anneal > ReLU, but these gaps shrink substantially at L0 100–500. The original ReLU SAE is outperformed on 5 of 8 metrics and is worst on Loss Recovered, yet it uniquely achieves the best 1-sparse probing scores at L0 > 200 and shows comparable unlearning performance. Gated SAE outperforms P-Anneal on Loss Recovered while P-Anneal outperforms Gated on absorption, illustrating metric-specific trade-offs. Critically, Matryoshka is the only architecture that improves on feature disentanglement metrics (SCR, Absorption) as dictionary width scales from 4k to 65k, whereas all other architectures exhibit inverse scaling on these metrics due to feature splitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the fragmented landscape of SAE evaluation methods—automated interpretability (Paulo et al., 2024), sparse probing (Gurnee et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024), RAVEL (Huang et al., 2024), feature absorption (Chanin et al., 2024), and unlearning (Farrell et al., 2024)—into a single standardized benchmark, while introducing two novel feature disentanglement metrics (SCR and TPP) adapted from the SHIFT methodology (Marks et al., 2024c). It departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating SAEs primarily through the sparsity-fidelity tradeoff, arguing that this proxy metric is necessary but insufficient for capturing practical interpretability quality. The benchmark is applied across seven SAE architectures (ReLU, TopK, BatchTopK, Gated, JumpReLU, P-Annealing, Matryoshka) that represent the major recent architectural innovations in the dictionary learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that gains on the sparsity-fidelity frontier do not reliably translate to better downstream interpretability performance challenges a core assumption driving SAE development, and the observation that hierarchical (Matryoshka) architectures show positive scaling on disentanglement while non-hierarchical architectures exhibit inverse scaling provides concrete architectural guidance. Key limitations include the dependence of supervised metrics on limited ground-truth concepts, the inability to combine metrics into a single score, and evaluation restricted to Gemma-2-2B and Pythia-160M, leaving open how findings generalize to larger or architecturally different models. The benchmark's practical value is high: it provides a reproducible, automated framework (under 2 hours per SAE) that the community can adopt for future SAE comparisons, and the open-sourcing of 200+ SAEs enables systematic study of scaling trends.
