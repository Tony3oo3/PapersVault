## Problem

Curiosity-driven exploration methods in RL are highly sensitive to the intrinsic reward coefficient β: too small a value causes the agent to get trapped in suboptimal policies, while too large a value leads to over-exploration and optimization instability. The proper range of β depends heavily on environment-specific reward scales, necessitating exhaustive per-environment hyperparameter sweeps that severely limit the practical applicability of this exploration paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard state-vector observations from the environment (e.g., 6-dimensional position/velocity/goal coordinates in PointMaze, or joint positions/velocities in MuJoCo) and produces a continuous or discrete action at each step. Internally, it maintains two separate policies—an exploration policy updated with the curiosity bonus and an exploitation policy updated with task reward only—alongside a learned intrinsic reward model (e.g., Disagreement ensemble or RND). The repositioning phase length is sampled from a bounded geometric distribution truncated to [1, H], where H is the episode horizon.

## Method

Hyper introduces a repositioning-and-exploration mechanism that decouples exploration from exploitation within each episode. In the repositioning phase, the agent follows the exploitation policy for a randomly sampled length (drawn from a bounded geometric distribution with truncation probability p that decays over training), moving it to states where task knowledge is already strong. In the subsequent exploration phase, the agent follows the exploration policy augmented with a curiosity bonus to discover novel transitions from that promising starting point. The exploitation policy is updated on the shared replay buffer without the intrinsic reward, while the exploration policy is updated with it, preventing the intrinsic reward from corrupting task learning. The bounded geometric distribution (rather than a standard geometric) avoids probability mass accumulating at the horizon, ensuring the agent retains sufficient exploration time even as p decays. Under a linear MDP assumption, the provably efficient variant (Linear-UCB-Hyper) achieves Õ(d³H⁴/ε²) sample complexity for an ε-optimal exploitation policy.

## Results

The paper reports results primarily as learning curves (success rate for navigation, cumulative reward for locomotion) rather than single-point numbers. In the hardest navigation task (LargeMaze-Hard, horizon 300, optimal path ≈250 steps), Hyper is the only method that consistently solves the task, while Curiosity finds the goal but never learns to exploit it, and Decouple fails entirely. In the β-sensitivity analysis (Figure 6), Hyper maintains near-peak performance across β ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100}, whereas Curiosity peaks at small β and drops drastically for β > 10. In MiniGrid, Hyper outperforms LESSON in UnlockPickup and LavaCrossingS9N1 and is comparable in Empty16x16 and Fetch8x8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3 (random-noise exploration), Hyper clearly wins: TD3 receives zero reward in all sparse-reward MuJoCo locomotion trials and finds the goal location only once across all navigation trials, while Hyper solves both task families. Against Curiosity (TD3 + Disagreement intrinsic reward), Hyper wins on the exploitation side of navigation tasks—Curiosity locates the goal but never learns to consistently revisit it, especially in LargeMaze-Hard—and on β-robustness, where Curiosity's performance collapses for β > 10 while Hyper remains stable. Against Decouple (Schäfer et al., 2021), Hyper wins on the hardest navigation task (LargeMaze-Hard) and on high-dimensional locomotion, where Decouple suffers from distribution shift between the exploration and exploitation policies. Against LESSON (Kim et al., 2023, options-based), Hyper outperforms in 2 of 4 MiniGrid tasks (UnlockPickup, LavaCrossingS9N1) and ties in the other two (Empty16x16, Fetch8x8), validating that the repositioning-and-exploration mechanism is competitive with learned option switching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curiosity-driven exploration paradigm (UCB-Q, Disagreement, RND) by introducing a repositioning-and-exploration mechanism that decouples the exploitation policy from the exploration policy, building directly on the decoupling idea of Schäfer et al. (2021) and Whitney et al. (2021). It departs from prior decoupling work by adding a repositioning phase guided by the exploitation policy and a bounded geometric distribution to regularize exploration visitation, which mitigates the distribution-shift problem that caused prior decoupled methods to fail in high-dimensional settings. The theoretical analysis follows the linear MDP framework of Jin et al. (2020) and Yang & Wang (2020), while the empirical evaluation positions Hyper against options-based methods (LESSON) and Go-Explore, showing it achieves comparable robustness without requiring environment reset or domain knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperparameter sensitivity of curiosity-driven exploration to β is a well-known practical bottleneck that limits the accessibility of these methods; Hyper's demonstrated robustness across a wide range of β values (Figure 6) makes curiosity-driven exploration significantly more plug-and-play. The theoretical guarantee of polynomial sample complexity under linear function approximation provides a worst-case safety net, though the practical gains over standard UCB come from the repositioning mechanism rather than the bound itself. Open questions include whether the repositioning mechanism scales to very high-dimensional continuous control (beyond MuJoCo locomotion), whether the truncation probability schedule can be made fully automatic, and whether the approach extends to model-based or offline RL settings where the two-phase structure may interact differently with data collection.
