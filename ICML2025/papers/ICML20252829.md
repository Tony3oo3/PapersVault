## Problem

Existing light-related image enhancement methods (exposure correction, image retouching, low-light enhancement, tone mapping) are designed individually for specific tasks, making them inconsistent and inapplicable across multiple lighting scenarios. The paper identifies two shared properties across these tasks that prior unified frameworks overlooked: different color channels exhibit distinct light properties, and these channel differences manifest differently in the spatial versus frequency domains. This motivates a multi-task architecture that explicitly models per-channel light characteristics rather than treating RGB as a monolithic signal.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single low-quality RGB image and produces a high-quality RGB image with perceptually optimal lighting. Light adaptation is performed at 1/8 resolution (three downsampling levels) to reduce computational cost, then a learnable differential pyramid with iterative detail enhancement progressively restores full resolution while refining high-frequency textures. The same architecture handles all four tasks—exposure correction, image retouching, low-light enhancement, and tone mapping—by simply swapping training data, with no task-specific architectural changes.

## Method

LALNet decomposes light adaptation into a dual-branch design: a channel-separation branch using Dual Domain Channel Modulation (DDCM) that processes each RGB channel independently via 2D FFT, per-channel convolution on real and imaginary parts, IFFT, and channel attention to capture color-specific luminance distributions in both spatial and frequency domains; and a channel-mixing branch using wavelet-based Mixed Channel Modulation (MCM) combined with a Light State Space Module (LSSM) built on 2D selective scanning (SS2D) for efficient long-range dependency capture. The key mechanism is Light Guided Attention (LGA), which uses the color-separated features as query vectors to attend over color-mixed key-value features, forcing the network to focus on inter-channel luminance differences while maintaining visual consistency. A lightweight variant (LALNet-Tiny, 230K params, 1.75 GFLOPs) achieves competitive performance, and the full model (2.45M params, 6.70 GFLOPs) outperforms task-specific SOTA by 0.86–3.86 dB PSNR across four benchmarks.

## Results

LALNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four light-related tasks: 23.80 dB PSNR / 0.7372 SSIM on SCIE (exposure correction), 30.24 dB PSNR / 0.944 SSIM on HDR+ (image retouching), 25.26 dB PSNR / 0.855 SSIM on LOLv1 (low-light enhancement), and 32.46 dB PSNR / 0.969 SSIM on HDRI Haven (tone mapping). The largest margins are 3.86 dB PSNR over RetinexMamba on HDRI Haven and 2.15 dB over MambaIR on HDR+. The lightweight LALNet-Tiny variant (230K params, 1.75 GFLOPs) also outperforms all prior SOTA methods, while the full LALNet (2.45M params, 6.70 GFLOPs) uses only 16% of the FLOPs of RetinexMamba on LOLv1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LALNet outperforms all 37 compared baselines across all four tasks. On SCIE (exposure correction), LALNet surpasses CoTF (CVPR'24) by 2.29 dB PSNR and RetinexMamba by 1.22 dB, while also achieving the best NIQE (9.34 vs. 10.19) and MUSIQ (61.49 vs. 55.67). On HDR+ (retouching), LALNet leads MambaIR by 2.15 dB PSNR and 0.001 SSIM, and outperforms RetinexMamba by 3.43 dB PSNR; notably, LALNet-Tiny (230K params) already beats MambaIR (4.31M params) by 1.21 dB PSNR. On LOLv1 (low-light), LALNet improves over RetinexMamba by 1.23 dB PSNR and 0.028 SSIM while using only 16% of its FLOPs (6.70 vs. 42.82 GFLOPs). On HDRI Haven (tone mapping), LALNet achieves a 3.86 dB PSNR advantage over RetinexMamba and 5.16 dB over Restormer, with the best LPIPS (0.019) and ΔE (3.58). On the additional MSEC dataset, LALNet leads CoTF by 0.46 dB PSNR and achieves the best LPIPS (0.0801 vs. 0.1232). On third-party HDR Survey and UVTM video benchmarks, LALNet achieves the highest TMQI scores (0.9296 and 0.9584 respectively), confirming generalization beyond the training distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant task-specific paradigm in light-related image enhancement (Retinex-based methods for low-light, LUT-based methods for retouching, frequency-domain methods for exposure correction) by proposing a unified multi-task framework grounded in a shared observation: different RGB channels exhibit distinct light properties that manifest differently in spatial versus frequency domains. Architecturally, it builds on the Learnable Differential Pyramid (Yang et al., 2024) for progressive detail recovery and incorporates 2D Selective Scanning (SS2D) from MambaIR (Guo et al., 2024) within a novel Light State Space Module, while introducing a dual-branch design (channel-separated via DDCM + channel-mixed via MCM) coupled with Light Guided Attention as the key mechanism for cross-channel light adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework achieves strong SOTA across four diverse light-related tasks with a single architecture, and the LALNet-Tiny variant (230K params, 1.75 GFLOPs) demonstrates that channel-aware guidance can be highly parameter-efficient, which is practically relevant for mobile and real-time deployment. The channel-separation insight—that negative interference between channels in standard convolutions degrades light adaptation—offers a reusable architectural principle for other color-sensitive tasks. Open questions include whether the channel-aware guidance generalizes beyond light-related tasks (e.g., color restoration, white balance) and whether the multi-task framework can scale to even more diverse enhancement objectives without task-specific tuning.
