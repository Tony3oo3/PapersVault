## Problem

Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA) must simultaneously handle domain shift and the presence of unknown categories in the target domain, yet causal-inspired theoretical frameworks for OSDA remain largely unexplored. While causal models have been applied to closed-set domain alignment, no comprehensive causal-based risk theory or identifiability analysis has been developed for the open-set setting, leaving a gap in principled, theoretically grounded OSDA methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images (with K known classes) and unlabeled target-domain RGB images (containing K known classes plus unknown classes), processed through a ResNet-50 or CLIP backbone. The output is a per-sample prediction: either one of the K known class labels or an unknown-class flag. A key design choice is that unknown classes are not treated as a single monolithic category but are represented by U virtual prototypes (U=K by default), enabling finer-grained open-set boundary learning.

## Method

COSDA builds a structural causal model for OSDA and derives a counterfactual-based Susceptibility Risk (SR) that measures how much the label prediction changes when causal features are intervened, formalized as SR_t = O_t(c) − O_t(c′). A PAC-Bayesian upper bound (Theorem 2) decomposes SR into a target-domain disagreement term, a source-domain joint-error term scaled by β-divergence, and an open-set risk term, yielding a computable training objective. To satisfy the Exogeneity causal assumption, a Contrastive-inspired Feature Alignment (CFA) loss maximizes mutual information between target samples and source-domain class prototypes while pushing them away from virtual unknown prototypes. A Virtual Multi-unknown-categories Prototype (VMP) pseudo-labeling strategy generates K known-class prototypes from top-confidence target samples and U unknown-class prototypes via K-means on the remaining samples, assigning pseudo-labels by nearest-prototype similarity. The final loss combines target and source susceptibility risks, KL regularization, the CFA exogeneity loss, and a stability loss enforcing sufficiently large interventions on causal features.

## Results

On Office-31, COSDA achieves 92.6% average HOS, 93.9% average UNK, and 91.4% average OS*, outperforming all baselines across all six sub-tasks. On Image-CLEF, COSDA reaches 84.3% average HOS and 88.3% average UNK, winning 11 of 12 sub-tasks on HOS. With a CLIP backbone on the larger DomainNet and VisDA benchmarks, COSDA-CLIP achieves 73.9% and 78.4% HOS respectively, surpassing the previous best (CROW) by 14.9% and 9.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSDA consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across all five benchmarks. Against the strongest source-supported baseline ANNA (CVPR 2023), COSDA gains 2.9% HOS on Image-CLEF, 4.0% HOS on Office-31, and 71.6% vs. ~69.6% average HOS on Office-Home. Against the source-free SOTA GLC++ (2024), COSDA leads by 2.2% HOS on Office-31. On the large-scale CLIP-based benchmarks, COSDA-CLIP surpasses CROW (ICML 2024) by 14.9% HOS on DomainNet (73.9% vs. 59.0%) and 9.2% on VisDA (78.4% vs. 69.2%), and outperforms UNIOT (2023) by 22.7% on DomainNet. Older adversarial baselines (OSBP, ROS) and score-based methods (STA, UAN) are surpassed by 6–13% HOS on Office-31, while pseudo-label methods (OSLPP, DADO) trail by 5–11% HOS. The ablation confirms that removing the CFA module drops HOS by 1.5%, removing target risk L_t eliminates unknown-class recognition entirely (UNK→0%), and VMP outperforms global clustering pseudo-labeling by 2.7% HOS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's (2009) counterfactual susceptibility concept into the Open-Set Domain Adaptation setting, building directly on the invariant learning framework of Yang et al. (2024, NeurIPS) which introduced the stability loss and PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds for causal representation learning. It departs from prior OSDA methods (OSBP, ANNA, GLC) that rely on adversarial training or single-prototype clustering by instead grounding the entire risk minimization in a structural causal model with Exogeneity and Monotonicity assumptions. The VMP pseudo-labeling strategy is a simplified variant of GLC's multi-prototype approach, reducing per-epoch clustering from C operations to one, while the CFA module replaces adversarial domain alignment with a contrastive objective proven equivalent to the Exogeneity condition via mutual information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete causal-theoretic risk framework for OSDA, offering computable upper bounds that decompose target susceptibility risk into source empirical risk, domain disagreement, and open-set space risk — a decomposition that clarifies which components drive failure in practice. The ablation showing that removing the target-domain risk term (L_t) collapses unknown-class recognition to 0% underscores that open-set risk cannot be addressed by source-domain supervision alone. Open questions include whether the Exogeneity and Monotonicity assumptions hold under severe domain shifts (e.g., synthetic-to-real), whether the framework extends to open-set domain generalization or multi-source OSDA, and whether the VMP strategy's fixed U=K setting is optimal when the number of unknown classes is highly imbalanced.
