## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning traces in LLMs are computationally expensive because much of the text serves linguistic coherence rather than core reasoning, yet existing compression approaches (continuous latent-space reasoning, iterative CoT elimination) rely on multi-stage curriculum training and still underperform models trained with full textual traces. The paper asks whether discrete latent tokens can compress the early reasoning steps while matching or exceeding full-CoT accuracy, without complex multi-stage procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252267/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a standard text sample structured as prompt ⊕ CoT reasoning trace ⊕ solution. During training, the first m CoT tokens are replaced by discrete latent codes (drawn from a 1024-entry codebook at a 16:1 compression rate) delimited by <boLatent>/<eoLatent> special tokens, while the remaining CoT tokens stay as raw text. At inference, the LLM directly generates the mixed latent-text trace and solution without any VQ-VAE involvement, producing on average 17% fewer tokens than the full-CoT baseline.

## Method

The approach has two stages: (1) a VQ-VAE (50M params, 2-layer causal Transformer encoder/decoder) is trained to compress each chunk of L=16 CoT tokens into a single discrete latent code via nearest-neighbor quantization in a learned codebook; (2) the LLM is fine-tuned on modified samples where the leftmost m CoT tokens are replaced by their latent abstractions. The key design choice is a single-stage randomized replacement strategy: for each training sample, m is sampled uniformly from {0, 16, 32, …, m_max} where m_max ∈ {0, 72, 128, 160, 192, 224, 256}, exposing the model to varying degrees of latent abstraction in one pass. This avoids the multi-stage curriculum of prior work and enables fast adaptation to the extended vocabulary of unseen latent tokens. Partial (not full) left-to-right replacement is critical: the remaining text tokens act as semantic anchors that help the model interpret the opaque latent codes, and ablations show this AR strategy substantially outperforms all-replace, curriculum-replace, and Poisson-sampled replacement.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (fine-tuned on MetaMathQA), the latent approach achieves 17.8% average accuracy on Llama-3.2-1B, 28.1% on Llama-3.2-3B, and 37.9% on Llama-3.1-8B, consistently outperforming the CoT baseline by +3.7, +2.9, and +4.5 points respectively. Headline gains include +4.2% on MATH (1B and 3B), +4.1% on GSM8K (3B), and +13.3% on Fresh-Gaokao-Math-2023 (8B). On synthetic planning/logic tasks, the method reaches 62.8% on Keys-Finding Maze (vs. 43% CoT), 100% on ProntoQA, and 96.2% on ProsQA (vs. 77.5% CoT). The approach also reduces average reasoning trace length by ~17% compared to CoT while improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Sol-Only (no reasoning trace), CoT (full text reasoning), iCoT (Deng et al., 2024, curriculum-based CoT elimination), and Pause Token (Goyal et al., 2023, learnable pause tokens). The latent approach decisively outperforms iCoT across all model sizes and nearly all benchmarks — e.g., on Llama-3.1-8B the average gap is 37.9% vs. 18.3% (+19.6 points), and on GSM8K it is 84.1% vs. 29.6%. Against Pause Token, the latent method also wins broadly (e.g., 37.9% vs. 25.9% average on 8B), though Pause Token occasionally matches or exceeds it on individual benchmarks like MATH for the 8B model (39.6% vs. 37.2%). Compared to the CoT baseline, the latent approach achieves consistent gains of +3 to +5 points on average across model sizes, with the largest single-benchmark improvement being +13.3% on Fresh-Gaokao-Math-2023 (8B). Notably, the latent method also reduces generated token count by ~17% on average relative to CoT, whereas iCoT reduces tokens by ~43% but at a severe accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of chain-of-thought reasoning and discrete representation learning. It builds on the VQ-VAE framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and latent-space planning (Jiang et al., 2022, 2023), but departs from prior latent-reasoning methods like COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) and iCoT (Deng et al., 2024) in two key ways: it uses discrete (rather than continuous) latent tokens, and it replaces the multi-stage curriculum training with a single-stage randomized partial-replacement strategy. The partial left-to-right replacement, where only the initial CoT tokens are compressed while later tokens remain as text, is a novel design choice not present in prior work that either compresses the entire trace or eliminates it iteratively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a ~17% reduction in reasoning trace length while simultaneously improving accuracy across multiple model sizes and benchmarks, achieved with a simple single-stage training procedure that avoids the complex curriculum scheduling of prior methods. The VQ-VAE decoder provides a built-in interpretability mechanism for the opaque latent tokens, partially addressing safety concerns. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (tested only up to 8B), whether the optimal compression rate (r=16) is task-dependent, and whether the gains persist when the VQ-VAE is trained on more diverse or larger corpora. The method also introduces a non-trivial data-preparation dependency (training a separate VQ-VAE per domain) that adds engineering overhead.
