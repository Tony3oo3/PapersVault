## Problem

Existing clustering methods are either federated but lack differential privacy guarantees, or differentially private but incompatible with federated learning settings. The core difficulty is that DP clustering requires a high-quality initialization to avoid excessive privacy budget consumption across many refinement rounds, yet obtaining such initialization in a federated, private manner is non-trivial. This paper closes the gap by presenting the first algorithm that is simultaneously fully federated and differentially private for k-means clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes m client datasets P^j ∈ R^{d×n_j} (distributed across clients), a small server-side dataset Q that may be out-of-distribution relative to client data, the number of clusters k, and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs k cluster centers ν_1,...,ν_k in the original d-dimensional space along with the induced point-to-cluster assignment. Data points are clipped to norm Δ (set to the server dataset radius) to enforce bounded sensitivity, and the algorithm supports both data-point-level and client-level DP with corresponding sensitivity adjustments.

## Method

FedDP-KMeans operates in two stages. The key contribution, FedDP-Init, is a three-step private initialization: (1) clients locally compute outer products P^j(P^j)^T and the server forms a projection matrix Π onto the top-k eigenvectors of the noisy aggregate (Gaussian mechanism); (2) server data Q is projected onto this k-dimensional subspace and each server point q is assigned an importance weight w_q reflecting how many client points have q as nearest neighbor (Laplace mechanism); (3) the server clusters the weighted projected server data via k-means, then refines centers by one Lloyd's step on clients using Gaussian and Laplace mechanisms for sums and counts. The second stage, FedDP-Lloyds, iteratively refines centers over T rounds using the same aggregation-and-noise pattern. The critical insight is that the server data serves as a free proxy for initialization (no privacy budget consumed), while the spectral projection reduces dimensionality to k, lowering the noise required for privacy and improving the signal-to-noise ratio by preserving inter-cluster variance while suppressing intra-cluster variance. The initialization is typically so close to optimal that 0–2 Lloyd's steps suffice, and the paper proves exponential convergence to ground-truth centers under Gaussian mixture separation assumptions.

## Results

FedDP-KMeans achieves optimal (non-private) k-means cost on synthetic Gaussian mixture data at a total privacy budget of only ε_total ≈ 0.4, while all DP baselines plateau well above the optimal cost even as the budget grows. On the US Census dataset (51 state-level clients, k=10), FedDP-KMeans recovers the optimal non-private clustering at moderate privacy budgets and outperforms all baselines except in the very low-budget regime. A key practical finding is that the FedDP-Init initialization is so effective that at most 2 Lloyd's refinement steps are ever needed for Pareto-optimal performance, and often zero steps suffice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDP-KMeans clearly outperforms k-FED (Dennis et al., 2021) across nearly all privacy budgets on both data-point-level and client-level privacy settings; k-FED performs 'quite poorly overall' in the client-level Stack Overflow experiments, often losing even to the private baselines, and while it matches optimal performance on the well-separated synthetic data, it provides no differential privacy guarantees. Against the SpherePacking initialization (Su et al., 2017), FedDP-KMeans wins decisively: SpherePacking 'performs poorly' on both US Census and Stack Overflow, emphasizing the importance of leveraging related server data rather than a data-independent seeding strategy. The server-data baselines (ServerKMeans++ and ServerLloyds) are competitive only in the very low privacy budget regime where client-based initialization becomes too noisy; ServerLloyds edges out ServerKMeans++ slightly. Overall, FedDP-KMeans dominates the Pareto front in all four experimental settings (synthetic and real data × data-point and client-level privacy), with the gap over baselines widening as the number of clients increases due to improved amplification by subsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the spectral-projection clustering framework of Kumar & Kannan (2010) and Awasthi & Sheffet (2012) with the private SVD mechanism of Dwork et al. (2014) and the DP Gaussian-mixture analysis of Kamath et al. (2019), adapting all of these to a federated setting with secure aggregation. The key departure from prior work is the FedDP-Init step, which repurposes small, out-of-distribution server-side data as a proxy for initialization—a trick absent from both the centralized DP clustering literature (Su et al., Ren et al., Zhang et al.) and the federated clustering literature (k-FED, Diaa et al.). Prior methods either provided DP without FL compatibility, or FL without DP guarantees; this paper is the first to deliver both simultaneously for k-means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a well-chosen initialization from server data can reduce the number of private Lloyd's rounds to zero or one, the paper sidesteps the central DP-utility trade-off that plagues iterative private clustering: fewer rounds means less noise accumulation. The theoretical guarantee of exponential convergence in O(log n) rounds under standard Gaussian-mixture separation conditions is a clean result that gives practitioners confidence in the method's sample efficiency. Open questions include the difficulty of hyperparameter selection under privacy constraints (the authors acknowledge this as a shortcoming), the requirement that server data contain at least one sample per true cluster, and whether the server-data initialization could be replaced by a purely client-side private mechanism.
