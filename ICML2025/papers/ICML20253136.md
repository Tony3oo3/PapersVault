## Problem

Prior comparisons between deep learning and classical ML for quantum system learning (QSL) are often unfair, as DL protocols frequently rely on expensive or unlimited quantum measurements to generate labels while ML models use a limited number. This paper asks whether DL is truly necessary in QSL when quantum resources are restricted, by systematically benchmarking both model families under equivalent total measurement budgets (n×M) across three Hamiltonian families scaled to 127 qubits.

## Input / Output

The input consists of Hamiltonian parameters (e.g., coupling strengths J_ij for Heisenberg/TFIM, or Ω, R_b, a, Δ for Rydberg models) and classical shadow measurement outcomes (M snapshots of N-bit strings from Pauli-based classical shadow protocol, stored as O(NM) bits). Outputs are correlation function entries C_ij (an N×N matrix), 2nd-order Rényi entanglement entropy S_2 for each neighboring qubit pair, or a discrete phase label (Z_2-order, Z_3-order, or disordered). A critical constraint is that the total quantum resource cost n×M is held constant across all models, and for SSL models the budget is split as n_pre×M_pre + n_sft×M_sft = n×M.

## Method

The paper constructs a unified benchmarking framework where all models—Lasso/Ridge regression with random Fourier features, kernel methods (Dirichlet, RBF, NTK), tree ensembles (RF, GBT, LGBM, XGB), MLPs, CNNs, and SSL transformers (LLM4QPE, Shadow Generator)—are trained under identical quantum measurement budgets. ML models use problem-informed feature maps φ(x) derived from Hamiltonian geometry, while DL models implicitly learn representations via neural networks, with SSL variants performing a two-stage process: pretraining a transformer decoder to predict individual qubit measurement outcomes (minimizing KL-divergence), then fine-tuning a task-specific head on classically preprocessed labels. A key diagnostic is a randomization test that replaces real measurement outcomes with uniformly sampled integers, revealing that measurement features are largely redundant for ground-state property estimation but significantly informative for phase classification. The central empirical finding is that simpler ML models (Lasso, Ridge, RF, XGB) consistently match or exceed DL performance under fair resource constraints, challenging the assumed necessity of deep architectures in QSL.

## Results

On the 127-qubit Heisenberg correlation prediction task (M=64, n_sft=100), Lasso achieves the best RMSE of 0.02083, outperforming the best DL model LLM4QPE-T (0.02634) by ~21%. On the 31-qubit Rydberg phase classification task (M=256, n_sft=100), GBT achieves 94.93% accuracy, surpassing LLM4QPE-T (90.57%) by over 4 percentage points. A randomization test reveals that replacing real measurement outcomes with random values causes negligible degradation for GSPE (LLM4QPE-T: 85.36% → 66.93% only for QPC), confirming measurement features are largely redundant for property estimation but critical for phase classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Classical Shadow (CS) [Huang et al., 2020] serves as the no-learning baseline; the Shadow Generator (SG) [Wang et al., 2022] performs at or below CS level (RMSE 0.20575 vs 0.21447 at N=127), indicating that autoregressive generation without supervised labels provides no meaningful advantage. LLM4QPE-T [Tang et al., 2024], the strongest DL model tested, is consistently outperformed by Lasso [Lewis et al., 2024] and Ridge [Wanner et al., 2024] across all system sizes (e.g., 0.02634 vs 0.02083 RMSE at N=127, n_sft=100). Among kernel methods from [Huang et al., 2022], RBFK and NTK are competitive with LLM4QPE-T but still trail Lasso/Ridge, while DK degrades with more training data. For QPC, tree ensembles (GBT, XGB, RF) achieve 94–95% accuracy, beating LLM4QPE-T (90.57%) and even surpassing MLP/CNN (~94.5%), while LLM4QPE-T is notably more sensitive to n_sft than in GSPE tasks. Overall, the paper's central finding is that under unified quantum resource budgets (n×M fixed), simple ML models with problem-informed feature maps match or exceed DL models, challenging the assumed necessity of deep architectures in QSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the provable-efficiency ML framework established by Huang et al. (2022) and its extensions (Lewis et al., 2024; Wanner et al., 2024), as well as the DL-based QSL models LLM4QPE (Tang et al., 2024a) and Shadow Generator (Wang et al., 2022b). Its key departure is methodological: prior DL papers typically used substantially more quantum measurements than ML papers, making cross-paradigm comparisons inequitable. By enforcing a unified quantum resource budget (n×M) across all models and benchmarking them on the same Hamiltonian families up to 127 qubits, this work reframes the comparison from 'DL vs. ML' to 'DL vs. ML under equal quantum cost,' a question the field had not systematically addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple ML models (Lasso, Ridge, RF, XGB) consistently match or exceed DL models under fair resource constraints challenges the field's implicit assumption that deeper architectures are necessary for QSL, and provides concrete guidance for practitioners operating under realistic quantum hardware budgets. The randomization test revealing that measurement outcomes are largely redundant as input features for GSPE but essential for QPC offers a useful design principle for future model architectures. Open questions remain: whether DL gains an advantage on more complex Hamiltonians (e.g., 2D lattices, molecular systems), whether the redundancy of measurement features persists beyond the tested families, and whether novel DL protocols can be designed specifically for the QPC regime where measurement information is genuinely informative.
