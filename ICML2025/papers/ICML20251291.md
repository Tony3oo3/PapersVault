## Problem

Model-free algorithms for average reward MDPs have a substantial gap between their sample complexity and the known lower bound, and most prior methods require a priori knowledge of mixing times or the span seminorm of the bias vector to set iteration counts. This paper closes the gap by achieving near-optimal sample complexity (matching the lower bound up to a factor ||h*||_sp) while requiring no prior knowledge of any MDP-specific quantity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a generative model providing i.i.d. samples from P(·|s,a) for any state-action pair, an initial Q-matrix Q⁰ (which may be zero), and scalar parameters ε and δ. It outputs an ε-optimal stationary deterministic policy π, the final Q-estimate Q^N, and the empirical Bellman residual T^N. Unusually, the algorithm self-terminates via a doubling trick with a stopping rule on the empirical residual ||Q^{n_i} − T^{n_i}||_sp ≤ 14ε, guaranteeing finite termination with probability 1 without the user specifying a number of iterations.

## Method

The core algorithm SAVIA combines Halpern's anchored value iteration Q^k = (1−β_k)Q⁰ + β_k T^{k−1} with β_k = k/(k+2) (yielding O(1/k) Bellman residual decay in span seminorm) with a recursive variance-reduced sampling scheme that approximates T(Q^k) by estimating only the increment P(d^k) where d^k = h^k − h^{k−1}, exploiting the fact that ||d^k||_sp is much smaller than ||h^k||_sp. A key theoretical ingredient is an Azuma-Hoeffding-type concentration inequality for the recursive sampling martingale, ensuring ||T^k − T(Q^k)||_∞ ≤ ε simultaneously for all k with high probability. The outer wrapper SAVIA+ runs SAVIA for n_i = 2^i iterations in successive loops, restarting from Q⁰ each time to ensure independence, and stops when the empirical residual falls below 14ε. This eliminates all prior knowledge requirements while preserving the O(|S||A| ||h*||_sp²/ε²) sample complexity both in high probability and in expectation.

## Results

The main result (Corollary 3.5) establishes that SAVIA+ computes an ε-optimal policy for weakly communicating average reward MDPs with sample and time complexity $\widetilde{O}(|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}||h^*|_{\mathrm{sp}}^2/\varepsilon^2)$, without requiring any prior knowledge of $|h^*|_{\mathrm{sp}}$ or other MDP parameters. This matches the known lower bound $\Omega(|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}||h^*|_{\mathrm{sp}}/\varepsilon^2)$ up to a factor of $|h^*|_{\mathrm{sp}}$. The algorithm also achieves the same order in expected sample complexity (Corollary 3.7) and guarantees termination with probability 1 via an empirical Bellman residual stopping rule. For discounted MDPs, SAVID+ attains $\widetilde{O}(|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}||Q^*|_\infty^2/((1-\gamma)^2\varepsilon^2))$, which is the best known complexity for obtaining an ε-Bellman residual error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVIA+ achieves the same $\widetilde{O}(|S||A||h^*|_{sp}^2/\varepsilon^2)$ complexity as Zhang & Xie (2023, model-free) and Zurek & Chen (2025, model-based), but is the only model-free algorithm requiring no prior knowledge whatsoever (× vs ○ or △), thanks to its empirical Bellman residual stopping rule. Compared to the closest model-free competitor without prior knowledge, Bravo & Contreras (2024), SAVIA+ improves the dependence from $|h^*|_{sp}^7/\varepsilon^7$ to $|h^*|_{sp}^2/\varepsilon^2$, a dramatic reduction in both the span seminorm and accuracy exponents. Against Jin et al. (2024a), which achieves $t_{\text{mix}}^8/\varepsilon^8$ for mixing MDPs, SAVIA+ applies to the broader weakly communicating class with a far tighter bound. Among model-based methods, Zurek & Chen (2024) achieves the better $|h^*|_{sp}/\varepsilon^2$ rate but requires prior knowledge and a generative model for building a surrogate; SAVIA+ trades one power of $|h^*|_{sp}$ for being fully model-free and self-stopping. The only other algorithm requiring no prior knowledge is Tuynman et al. (2024), which achieves $D/\varepsilon^2$ but is model-based and restricted to communicating MDPs (a narrower class than weakly communicating). Overall, SAVIA+ is the first model-free algorithm to simultaneously achieve near-optimal $|h^*|_{sp}^2/\varepsilon^2$ complexity, apply to weakly communicating MDPs, and require zero prior knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: Halpern's anchored iteration for nonexpansive fixed-point problems (Halpern, 1967; Sabach & Shtern, 2017) and SARAH-type recursive variance-reduced sampling (Jin et al., 2024b). While each component has been applied to reinforcement learning in isolation—anchored iteration for value iteration (Lee & Ryu, 2023; Bravo & Contreras, 2024) and recursive sampling for discounted MDPs (Jin et al., 2024b)—their joint application to average reward MDPs in the generative setting is new. The paper departs from prior model-free methods for weakly communicating MDPs (Zhang & Xie, 2023; Bravo & Contreras, 2024) by eliminating the need for prior knowledge of the span seminorm through a doubling trick and an empirical Bellman residual stopping rule, achieving the same O~(|S||A|||h*||_sp²/ε²) complexity as the best model-based methods (Zurek & Chen, 2024, 2025) but in a model-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap in the model-free literature for average reward MDPs: it is the first model-free algorithm for weakly communicating MDPs that requires no prior knowledge of the bias vector's span seminorm while matching the best known complexity up to a factor ||h*||_sp. The expected-complexity results (Corollary 3.7) are, to the authors' knowledge, the first such estimates for MDPs with a generative model, broadening the analysis beyond standard PAC bounds. Open questions include closing the remaining ||h*||_sp gap to match the lower bound exactly, extending the anchoring framework to general multichain MDPs, and adapting the approach to episodic and online learning settings where the generative model assumption does not hold.
