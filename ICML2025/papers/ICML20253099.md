## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods for continuous-time event sequences focus on designing powerful test statistics but provide no rigorous guarantees on the false positive rate, undermining their reliability in safety-critical deployments. Moreover, widely used statistics (KS, 3S, Q+, Q−) suffer from severe non-identifiability issues, producing similar values for markedly distinct sequences and failing to detect certain anomaly patterns such as reduced event counts or relatively uniform spacings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-length marked event sequences X = {(t_i, m_i)} on [0,T] along with a hold-out calibration set of normal sequences and a neural TPP (LogNormMix) trained on a separate training split. It outputs a Bonferroni-corrected two-sided conformal p-value p_cor(X_test) and a binary OOD/ID decision at a user-specified significance level α. A key design constraint is that p-values are computed against the calibration set only (not the full dataset) to preserve exchangeability, since the non-conformity scores already depend on the training set through the learned CIFs.

## Method

CADES applies the time-rescaling theorem to transform a marked event sequence into a single standard Poisson process (SPP) realization Z on [0,V] via integrated CIFs and concatenation across marks. Two complementary non-conformity scores are then computed on Z: s_arr measures KL divergence (via Gaussian KDE) between normalized arrival times τ_i/V and Uniform([0,1]), capturing event-count anomalies, while s_int measures KL divergence between inter-event times w_i and Exponential(1), capturing uniform-spacing anomalies. Because both small and large score values can indicate OOD (e.g., self-correcting processes produce more uniform arrivals than SPP), the method uses two-sided conformal p-values for each score. The final decision combines the two p-values via Bonferroni correction with a tunable factor (1+ε), yielding a valid p-value that controls marginal FPR and, under a calibration-set-size condition, calibration-conditional FPR with probability at least 1−δ.

## Results

On the real-world LOGS and STEAD datasets (8 scenarios total), CADES achieves the best average rank of 1.50 in AUROC, outperforming all baselines. Notable margins include 99.30% AUROC on STEAD-Helmet,CA vs. 70.71% for MultiAD-Q+ and 92.88% on LOGS-Packet duplication(1%) vs. 78.15% for MultiAD-Q-. In the FPR control experiment at α=0.05 on STEAD, CADES achieves 95.46% TPR on Anchorage,AK and 98.38% on Helmet,CA, substantially exceeding the 3S statistic (74.30%, 69.20%) and MultiAD-Q+ (67.14%, 6.50%). CADES also consistently achieves the best or near-best AUROC across all nine GOF test scenarios for the standard Poisson process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADES outperforms all five baseline families on average across the 8 real-world scenarios (average rank 1.50 vs. 3.00 for the closest competitor MultiAD-Q-). The KS arrival and KS inter-event statistics (Barnard 1953; Cox & Lewis 1966) are the weakest baselines, with average ranks of 7.50 and 5.63 respectively, largely due to their insensitivity to event-count changes and non-identifiability issues. The Chi-squared statistic (Cox 1955) performs comparably poorly (rank 7.00), collapsing to 21.26% AUROC on LOGS-Packet duplication(1%). The 3S statistic (Shchur et al. 2021) is competitive (rank 3.63) but is outperformed by CADES on 6 of 8 scenarios, with the largest gap on STEAD-Helmet (99.30% vs. 93.82%). MultiAD-Q+ and MultiAD-Q- (Zhang et al. 2023) are the strongest baselines (ranks 3.50 and 3.00), yet CADES still wins on 5 of 8 scenarios; the most dramatic case is STEAD-Helmet where MultiAD-Q+ drops to 70.71% while CADES maintains 99.30%. In the FPR-controlled setting (α=0.05), CADES also achieves substantially higher TPR than 3S and MultiAD-Q+ on STEAD, confirming that its advantage is not merely in ranking but in detection power at a fixed false-positive budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: conformal anomaly detection (Vovk et al., 2005; Laxhammar & Falkman, 2015), which has been applied primarily to image and trajectory data, and anomaly detection in continuous-time event sequences via temporal point processes (Shchur et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023), which focused on designing powerful test statistics without FPR guarantees. The key conceptual departure is the use of the time-rescaling theorem (Brown et al., 2002) to transform marked event sequences into standard Poisson process realizations, enabling the design of two KL-divergence-based non-conformity scores that address the non-identifiability limitations of prior statistics (KS, 3S, Q+/Q-). The Bonferroni correction with a (1+ε) factor, borrowed from Magesh et al. (2023), is repurposed here to combine the two complementary scores while providing calibration-conditional FPR guarantees stronger than the marginal guarantees typical in conformal prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide finite-sample FPR guarantees for anomaly detection in event sequences, which is critical for safety-critical deployments in healthcare, finance, and information security where false alarms carry real costs. The calibration-conditional FPR guarantee (Theorem 3.4) is a meaningful strengthening over the marginal FPR control that prior conformal methods offer, though it requires a sufficiently large calibration set and a conservative ε parameter. Open questions include how the method behaves under distribution shift (the exchangeability assumption may be violated in streaming settings), whether the neural TPP model quality is a bottleneck (a poorly trained model degrades both scores simultaneously), and whether the approach extends naturally to online or sequential detection without re-calibration.
