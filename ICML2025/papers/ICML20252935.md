## Problem

Prior game-theoretic analyses of strategic content creators in recommendation platforms assume every creator acts in self-interest, but in practice creators increasingly form groups (e.g., MCNs) that coordinate to maximize collective utility. This paper asks how such group strategies reshape the equilibrium content distribution and whether they harm user welfare, a question that was entirely unexplored in the C³ game literature.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a C³ game instance specified by a user distribution over d-dimensional embeddings, per-creator action sets in R^d, a normalized relevance function σ, a top-K softmax matching with temperature β, a reward mechanism (exposure or engagement), and a partition of creators into L groups. The analysis produces equilibrium strategy profiles (vanilla, Type-I Stackelberg, Type-II group PNE), closed-form user welfare expressions, and Price of Anarchy bounds. A key structural detail is the bilevel optimization in the Type-I equilibrium: the group maximizes its utility anticipating that individual creators will best-respond, which makes the equilibrium characterization fundamentally different from standard Nash analysis.

## Method

The paper introduces a game-theoretic framework for group behavior in the C³ game, starting with a Bandit C³ game where users and creators are represented as orthogonal unit basis vectors. It defines two group equilibrium concepts—Type I (Stackelberg, where the group moves first and individuals best-respond) and Type II (treating the group as a single player in a PNE)—and proves that when group size is small relative to the minimum topic occupancy, both coincide with the vanilla equilibrium (Theorem 4.6). In the TvN game, it shows that a full group causes O(n) welfare loss under exposure rewards and, counterintuitively, that increasing K or β does not always improve welfare when users have diminishing attention spans (Theorem 4.9). For the general case, it proves PoA is unbounded under exposure rewards but bounded by K+1 under engagement rewards (Theorem 5.4), with a matching lower bound (Theorem 5.5) and a tighter PoA ≤ 2 in the binary relevance/attention case (Proposition 5.6). The PoA proof hinges on a submodularity property of the top-K welfare function (Lemma B.1) combined with a careful analysis of softmax-weighted group utilities. Simulations use the LBR local better-response algorithm for individual creators and multi-step gradient descent for the group, validating the theoretical predictions across group sizes, K, β, and attention score profiles.

## Results

The paper establishes that under exposure-based rewards, the Price of Anarchy (PoA) can be arbitrarily large (Theorem 5.3), while under user engagement rewards, PoA ≤ K+1 in the general case (Theorem 5.4) and PoA ≤ 2 in the binary relevance/attention case (Proposition 5.6). In the TvN game, user welfare loss under full-group exposure rewards reaches O(n), dropping from an optimal (n+1)/2 to just 1. Simulations with n=30 creators confirm that user welfare under exposure rewards drops sharply (e.g., from 4.85 to 3.26 for α=1.1) as group size increases from 10 to 30, while engagement rewards maintain welfare near 4.8–4.9 across all group sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method baselines; the central comparison is between two reward mechanisms (exposure vs. engagement) and between individual vs. group creator behavior. Under exposure rewards, the PoA is unbounded (→∞), meaning group strategies can drive user welfare arbitrarily close to zero relative to the optimum. Under engagement rewards, the PoA is bounded by K+1 (upper) and 1+(K−1)e^(−1/β) (lower), and tightens to PoA ≤ 2 in the binary case. In simulations, engagement rewards consistently outperform exposure rewards: at full group size (n_c=30), exposure welfare drops by 30–33% (e.g., 4.85→3.26 for α=1.1) while engagement welfare remains within 1% of its best value. The paper also contrasts the individual-creator equilibrium (vanilla PNE) with group equilibria (Type I/II), showing that small groups (n_c ≤ min_j q_j^v) preserve the vanilla equilibrium, but large groups fundamentally alter content distribution and can reduce welfare by O(n) in the TvN game.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the C³ (Content Creation Competition) game framework of Yao et al. (2023; 2024a) by introducing group-level strategic behavior, where creators within a group (e.g., an MCN) jointly optimize collective utility rather than acting as self-interested individuals. Prior work on strategic creators in recommendation systems (Hron et al., 2022; Jagadeesan et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023) assumed individual PNE, and the TvN game structure from Yao et al. (2024a) is directly reused here as the testbed for group dynamics. The paper also leverages the LBR algorithm and user importance reweighting intervention from Yao et al. (2024c) for both simulation and mitigation, positioning group behavior as a natural next step in the creator-strategy literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that user welfare does not monotonically improve with larger K or β under group behavior and diminishing attention directly contradicts established platform-design heuristics, providing concrete guidance for tuning recommendation parameters in the presence of coordinated creator groups. The arbitrarily bad PoA under exposure rewards (Theorem 5.3) is a strong negative result that motivates the shift to engagement-based rewards, while the PoA ≤ K+1 (and ≤ 2 in the binary case) bound gives platforms a quantifiable welfare guarantee. Open questions include how results extend to dynamic group formation/dissolution, multi-round interactions, and whether the engagement reward bounds remain tight under more realistic (non-bandit) relevance functions.
