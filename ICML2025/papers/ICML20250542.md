## Problem

Multiwinner voting research has been almost entirely confined to static, one-shot elections over a fixed candidate set, overlooking the inherently dynamic nature of real-world applications where candidates join or leave over time. This paper asks whether proportional committees can be maintained across a sequence of candidate-set updates while making only a small number of changes (low recourse) at each step, and characterizes which proportionality axioms admit such dynamic algorithms and which do not.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of instances where the candidate set evolves over time (incremental, decremental, or fully-dynamic), along with voter preferences expressed as approval ballots, linear orders, or distances in a pseudometric space, and a fixed target committee size k. At each time-step t, the algorithm must output a committee of exactly k candidates from the current candidate set that satisfies the relevant proportionality axiom, using only information available up to time t. The key constraint is on recourse: the committee at time t+1 must differ from the committee at time t by at most a bounded number of candidate swaps (e.g., amortized 2 changes per step, or a single swap for robustness).

## Method

The paper develops setting-specific mechanisms. For ordinal preferences, a counting argument (reserving 1/k-fractions of voters to each candidate in a k+1 set) proves that a single swap always restores PSC after a candidate addition, yielding a robust incremental algorithm; conversely, combinatorial constructions show Ω(log log k) changes are unavoidable after a single deletion. For proportional clustering, a budget-based pre-clustering phase groups voters into clusters of controlled diameter, and candidates are assigned to clusters via a greedy distance-minimizing procedure with an envy-cycle elimination step, achieving 4.24-proportional fairness and 5-q-core fully dynamically. For approval preferences, the core mechanism is maintaining a maximally affordable subcommittee (a budget system where each voter holds k/n budget and candidates cost 1), which guarantees PJR+ by construction; a token-based accounting scheme ensures amortized 2 changes per step in the fully-dynamic setting, while a modified GJCR with local stability yields an incremental EJR+ algorithm robust to single additions.

## Results

The paper establishes both positive algorithmic results and impossibility lower bounds for maintaining proportionality under dynamic candidate sets. For proportional clustering, a fully-dynamic algorithm achieves a (2+√5) ≈ 4.24-approximation to proportional fairness and a 5-approximation to the q-core, matching the best known offline bound. For approval preferences, robust fully-dynamic algorithms satisfy PJR+ with amortized 2 changes per step, and O(log k)-EJR+ with the same amortized cost; in the incremental setting, 2-EJR+ is achievable with amortized 2 changes. On the negative side, no robust decremental PSC algorithm exists (Ω(log log k) lower bound), and no incremental or decremental rank-JR algorithm can make o(√k) amortized changes per round.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a purely theoretical paper, this work does not run empirical comparisons against baseline algorithms. Instead, the key comparison is against the best known static (offline) guarantees: the 5-q-core approximation achieved by the fully-dynamic clustering algorithm matches the current best offline bound from Kellerhals and Peters (2024), meaning the dynamic constraint introduces no additional approximation loss. The paper also contrasts with Chen et al. (2024), who studied committee reconfiguration and showed only computational intractability/fixed-parameter tractability results for specific scoring rules; in contrast, this paper provides constructive algorithms that guarantee proportionality at every step. For ordinal preferences, the paper shows that while STV and EAR satisfy PSC statically, they can select committees that are not robust to a single deletion, motivating the need for the new dynamic algorithms. The impossibility results (no robust decremental PSC or PJR+ algorithms, Ω(√k) lower bound for rank-JR) establish fundamental limits that no static rule can overcome in the dynamic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static multiwinner voting literature to dynamic candidate sets, building directly on the maximally affordable committee framework of Brill and Peters (2024) for the approval setting and the spatial expanding approvals rule of Aziz et al. (2024) for proportional clustering. It departs from Chen et al. (2024) on multi-winner reconfiguration by requiring full proportionality at every time-step rather than merely bounding score differences between intermediate committees. The dynamic low-recourse formulation follows the recent line of work in dynamic matching (Matuschke et al., 2019; Bernstein et al., 2019) and dynamic clustering (Bhattacharya et al., 2024; Lacki et al., 2024), but applies it to the proportionality axioms of social choice theory (PSC, PJR+, EJR+, proportional fairness, q-core).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clear taxonomy of which proportionality axioms survive dynamic candidate changes and which fundamentally do not, providing both constructive algorithms (amortized 2 changes for PJR+, O(log k) for EJR+) and tight lower bounds (Ω(log log k) for PSC decremental, Ω(√k) for rank-JR). The most impactful open question is whether a robust incremental EJR+ algorithm exists, since EJR+ is the strongest polynomially achievable proportionality axiom for approval voting and its dynamic behavior is poorly understood. The paper also highlights that extending to dynamic voter sets (changing the quota n/k over time) remains an open and non-trivial direction, and notes that the understanding of EJR+ as an axiom is still quite narrow even in the static setting.
