## Problem

Standard Transformer attention routes sensory information (features of individual objects) but lacks an explicit mechanism for routing relational information (comparisons and relationships between objects). This entanglement of sensory and relational information in a single attention operation makes relational reasoning inefficient, as evidenced by poor data efficiency on synthetic relational benchmarks and limited systematic generalization in real-world tasks.

## Input / Output

The architecture is modality-agnostic and supports encoder-only, encoder-decoder, and decoder-only variants. For visual tasks, RGB images are split into patches (e.g., 4×4 for CIFAR, 12×12 for Relational Games) and flattened into token sequences; for language tasks, inputs are character- or token-level sequences. Outputs range from binary relational labels (Relational Games), class labels (CIFAR), generated text answers (mathematical problem solving, max 30 characters), to next-token distributions (autoregressive language modeling on 10B tokens of FineWeb-Edu).

## Method

The Dual Attention Transformer (DAT) replaces standard multi-head self-attention with a dual attention module containing two parallel types of heads: sensory attention (standard self-attention routing object features) and relational attention, which computes a d_r-dimensional relation vector r(x, y_i) = (⟨φ_{q,ℓ}^{rel}(x), φ_{k,ℓ}^{rel}(y_i)⟩)_{ℓ∈[d_r]} representing fine-grained comparisons between source and target under learned feature subspaces, then routes these relations weighted by attention scores. Each retrieved relation is tagged with a symbolic identifier (positional, position-relative, or learned equivalence-class symbol) that encodes sender identity without fully encoding object features, maintaining a relation-centric representation. The two head types are concatenated to produce a unified contextual representation, and a universal approximation theorem establishes that relational attention can approximate any function composed of query-dependent object selection followed by arbitrary relation computation. The architecture preserves O(n²) sequence-length complexity, supports causal masking, RoPE, and symmetry constraints (W_q^{rel} = W_k^{rel}), and is evaluated across relational games, mathematical reasoning, image classification, and language modeling at scales up to 1.3B parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ViDAT achieves 89.7% top-1 accuracy with 6.0M parameters, outperforming ViT (86.4%, 7.1M params) by 3.3 points while using fewer parameters. On CIFAR-100, ViDAT reaches 70.5% vs. ViT's 68.8%. For language modeling on 10B tokens of FineWeb-Edu, the 343M-parameter DAT achieves 16.09 validation perplexity versus 16.94 for the 353M-parameter Transformer, and this gap persists at the 750M and 1.3B scales. On the Relational Games benchmark, DAT is significantly more sample-efficient, with the effect most dramatic on the 'match pattern' task requiring second-order relational reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Transformer (Vaswani et al.), DAT consistently wins across all four task domains: relational reasoning (more sample-efficient learning curves), mathematical problem-solving (higher character-level accuracy at every model scale), image recognition (ViDAT beats ViT by 3.3% on CIFAR-10 and 1.7% on CIFAR-100 with fewer parameters), and language modeling (lower perplexity at every scale, e.g., 16.09 vs. 16.94 at ~350M params). Against ViT specifically (Dosovitskiy et al.), ViDAT achieves higher accuracy on both CIFAR datasets while using ~15% fewer parameters due to Grouped Query Attention. On the Relational Games benchmark, DAT generally outperforms PrediNet (Shanahan et al.) and Abstractor (Altabaa et al.), but performs marginally worse than CoRelNet (Kerg et al.), which incorporates strong task-specific inductive biases and was partially designed with this benchmark in mind. Overall, DAT's relational attention mechanism provides consistent gains over all sensory-only attention baselines, with the largest relative improvements on tasks requiring explicit relational structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer architecture by introducing a second attention mechanism—relational attention—that explicitly routes pairwise comparison vectors between objects, complementing the standard self-attention that routes sensory features. It builds directly on the symbol assignment mechanisms (positional, position-relative, symbolic attention) from Altabaa et al. (ICLR 2024) and positions itself as a general-purpose alternative to task-specific relational architectures like PrediNet, CoRelNet, and Abstractor, which impose subtractive inductive biases that limit applicability. The theoretical contribution (a universal approximation theorem for relational attention) extends prior approximation results for standard attention to the relational setting, showing that relational attention can approximate any 'select-then-relate' computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in Transformer architectures—the absence of an explicit computational mechanism for relational information—and demonstrates that adding one yields consistent data and parameter efficiency gains across synthetic relational reasoning, mathematical problem-solving, image classification, and language modeling at up to 1.3B parameters. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at much larger scales, whether the added architectural complexity is justified relative to simply scaling up standard Transformers, and what the mechanistic interpretability of relational attention reveals about how these models actually use the relational channel. The lack of hardware-aware optimizations (e.g., Flash-Attention equivalents) and the many configuration hyperparameters (head composition, symmetry, symbol type) limit immediate practical adoption.
