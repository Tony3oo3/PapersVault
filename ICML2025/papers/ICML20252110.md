## Problem

Conventional monotonic MLPs enforce monotonicity by constraining weights to be non-negative and using bounded activations (sigmoid, tanh), which introduces vanishing gradient problems and restricts the network to representing only bounded functions. The standard ReLU activation, despite being the workhorse of modern deep learning, cannot be used in non-negative weight-constrained MLPs for universal approximation because the composition of convex monotonic functions remains convex. This paper asks whether universal approximation for monotonic functions can be achieved with saturating (unbounded) activations like ReLU, and if so, how to do so without the optimization pathologies of weight reparameterization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard numerical feature vectors from tabular datasets (ranging from 7 to 280 features) where a subset of dimensions has a known monotonic relationship with the target. Monotonicity is enforced by architecture on the specified input dimensions, while remaining dimensions are unconstrained. The output is either a class probability (for classification tasks like COMPAS, Heart Disease, Loan Defaulter) or a continuous value (for regression tasks like AutoMPG, Blog Feedback), with the monotonicity guarantee holding for all points in the input space rather than only in-distribution.

## Method

The paper proves that an MLP with 4 layers, non-negative weights, and activations that alternate saturation sides (e.g., ReLU followed by its point reflection ReLU') is a universal approximator for monotonic functions, matching the best-known constant bound for threshold-activated networks. A key equivalence shows that non-positive weight constraints with a single saturating activation (like ReLU) are equivalent to non-negative weights with alternating activations, making the non-positive formulation surprisingly more expressive than the non-negative one. To avoid the optimization difficulties of weight reparameterization (|W| or W²), the authors propose a post-activation switch: the weight matrix is split into positive and negative parts W⁺ and W⁻, and the layer computes W⁺σ(x) + W⁻σ(−x) + b, so the activation saturation side is determined automatically by the sign of each weight. This eliminates constrained parameters, removes the need to manually alternate activations, and allows standard initializers (e.g., default PyTorch) without the vanishing gradient dynamics that plague bounded-activation constrained MLPs.

## Results

The proposed activation-switch monotonic MLP achieves the best or tied-best performance across all five benchmark datasets. On Heart Disease classification, it reaches 94%±1% test accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Expressive MNN at 90%±2%). On AutoMPG regression, it achieves 7.34±0.46 MSE, the lowest among all methods. On COMPAS, Blog Feedback, and Loan Defaulter, it matches or marginally exceeds the strongest baselines (e.g., 69.5% vs 69.3% accuracy on COMPAS; 0.149 vs 0.150 RMSE on Blog Feedback).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset where they are compared. Against the closest competitors—Constrained MNN (Runje & Shankaranarayana, 2023), Scalable MNN (Kim & Lee, 2024), and Expressive MNN (Nolte et al., 2023)—the gains are modest on COMPAS (69.5% vs 69.2–69.3%), Blog Feedback (0.149 vs 0.150–0.154 RMSE), and Loan Defaulter (65.4% vs 65.0–65.4%), but substantial on Heart Disease (94% vs 88–90%) and AutoMPG (7.34 vs 7.44–8.37 MSE). The older architectures (Min-Max Net, DLN, Non-Neg-DNN) are consistently weaker, with Min-Max Net lagging by up to 19 percentage points on Heart Disease (75% vs 94%). XGBoost and Certified Networks, which do not enforce monotonicity by construction, are competitive on COMPAS and Blog Feedback but are not evaluated on the regression and Heart Disease tasks. Notably, the proposed method requires no hyperparameter tuning beyond what was already established for Constrained MNN, and uses default PyTorch initialization without the careful bias tuning needed by weight-constrained alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained-MLP framework for monotonic networks initiated by Daniels & Velikova (2010) and the constant-depth universal approximation result of Mikulincer & Reichman (2022), which was previously limited to threshold activations. It departs from Runje & Shankaranarayana (2023), who required multiple activation types (including a bounded sigmoid) to achieve universal approximation, by showing that a single saturating activation (e.g., ReLU) suffices when saturation sides alternate across layers. The key conceptual departure is the equivalence between non-positive weight constraints and point-reflected activations, which reveals that the standard non-negative constraint is actually the *less* expressive choice for convex activations like ReLU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result removes the long-standing requirement for bounded activations (sigmoid, tanh, ReLU6) in monotonic networks, enabling the use of modern ReLU-family activations without the associated vanishing-gradient and initialization pathologies. The proposed activation-switch parametrization further eliminates weight reparameterization entirely, making training as straightforward as a standard MLP while preserving monotonicity by construction. Open questions remain: whether non-saturating activations (e.g., LeakyReLU) can be incorporated, and whether batch normalization can address residual initialization sensitivity. The practical impact is most relevant to domains requiring certified monotonicity (fairness, survival analysis, predictive maintenance), where the method matches or slightly exceeds state-of-the-art baselines.
