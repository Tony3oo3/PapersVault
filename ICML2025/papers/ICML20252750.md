## Problem

Vanilla multimodal fusion methods treat all inter-modal interactions uniformly, failing to distinguish between unique, redundant, and synergistic information that modalities contribute. While the Partial Information Decomposition (PID) framework provides a theoretical taxonomy of these interaction types, it has not been integrated into an end-to-end, interpretable deep learning pipeline. Prior attempts to model modality interactions are either restricted to pairwise settings, require separate per-interaction estimates, or lack sufficient interpretability at both sample and dataset levels.

## Input / Output

The method accepts n modalities (e.g., MRI + genetics + clinical + biospecimen for ADNI; vision + audio + text for MOSI) encoded by modality-specific encoders into latent embeddings. During training, each modality embedding is additionally replaced by a random vector in separate forward passes to generate weak supervision signals for the interaction experts. Beyond the standard prediction (class label or regression scalar), the model outputs per-expert soft importance weights from an MLP reweighting module, enabling sample-level local interpretation and dataset-level global interpretation via weight statistics across the test set.

## Method

I²MoE deploys a mixture of n+2 interaction experts (one uniqueness expert per modality, one synergy expert, one redundancy expert), each with separate fusion and prediction parameters, trained end-to-end with a dual-objective loss. The task loss is computed on the reweighted ensemble prediction from complete inputs, while the interaction loss uses random-vector masking of individual modalities to enforce specialization: uniqueness experts are trained via triplet margin loss to match the full-input prediction when their modality is present but diverge when it is masked; synergy and redundancy experts use cosine similarity to enforce divergence from or agreement with perturbed outputs, respectively. A sample-specific MLP reweighting model assigns soft importance scores to each expert's prediction, producing the final output as a weighted sum. The framework is backbone-agnostic and can be plugged into any existing fusion architecture (MulT, SwitchGate, MoE++, InterpretCC) without modifying the underlying fusion mechanism.

## Results

On ADNI (4-modality Alzheimer's classification), I²MoE-MulT achieves 65.08% accuracy and 81.09% AUROC, outperforming the strongest baseline SwitchGate (62.28% / 79.70%) by 2.8% and 1.4% respectively. On IMDB (23-genre multi-label classification), I²MoE-MulT reaches 61.00% Micro F1 and 52.38% Macro F1, surpassing MulT (59.68% / 51.41%) and MoE++ (58.15% / 50.49%). On MOSI sentiment analysis, I²MoE-MulT achieves 71.91% accuracy versus 68.80% for MulT, and on ENRICO UI classification it reaches 48.22% versus 47.37% for MulT. On MIMIC-IV, I²MoE-MulT (69.78% acc, 68.81% AUROC) is slightly below MoE++ (72.51% acc) but matches its AUROC, with the accuracy gap attributed to dataset class imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I²MoE-MulT consistently outperforms the MulT transformer backbone (Tsai et al., 2019) on four of five datasets: +5.5% accuracy on ADNI, +1.3% Micro F1 on IMDB, +3.1% accuracy on MOSI, and +0.9% on ENRICO, while slightly trailing on MIMIC accuracy (−2.6%). Against the sparse MoE baselines, I²MoE-MulT beats SwitchGate (Fedus et al., 2022) by 2.8% accuracy on ADNI and 5.1% Macro F1 on IMDB, and beats MoE++ (Jin et al., 2024) by 7.0% accuracy on ADNI and 2.9% Macro F1 on IMDB. The simple early/late fusion baselines (Baltrušaitis et al., 2019) and LRMF (Liu et al., 2018) are substantially outperformed across all datasets, with gaps exceeding 10% on ADNI accuracy. InterpretCC (Swamy et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on MIMIC (72.34% vs 69.78% accuracy) but I²MoE-MulT surpasses it on ADNI (+10.6% accuracy), IMDB (+3.0% Micro F1), MOSI (+4.1% accuracy), and ENRICO (+0.6% accuracy). Overall, I²MoE-MulT achieves the best or near-best result on 7 of 8 reported metrics, with the sole exception being MIMIC accuracy where MoE++ leads by 2.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MMoE (Yu et al., 2024), which first proposed a mixture of multimodal interaction experts for sentiment analysis, by making the framework fully end-to-end with weakly-supervised interaction losses and adding a sample-adaptive reweighting model for interpretability. Theoretically, it grounds its four interaction types (uniqueness ×2, synergy, redundancy) in the Partial Information Decomposition (PID) framework of Liang et al. (2023), which had previously lacked integration into trainable deep learning pipelines. Methodologically, it departs from prior MoE-based fusion approaches (Switch Transformer, MoE++, InterpretCC) by assigning each expert a distinct interaction objective enforced through perturbation-based contrastive losses rather than relying solely on routing-based specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backbone-agnostic design and consistent gains across five diverse datasets (medical, sentiment, UI design) make I2MoE a practical plug-in for existing multimodal pipelines, particularly in high-stakes domains like healthcare where interpretability of modality contributions is critical. However, the disagreement analysis reveals a concerning pattern: on IMDB, experts disagree on 99.99% of samples yet the model is correct only 15.85% of those cases, suggesting the interaction experts may not be as functionally specialized as intended for multi-label tasks. Open questions include whether the perturbation-based interaction losses scale gracefully to more than four modalities, whether the moderate computational overhead (roughly 2–12× training time) is acceptable in resource-constrained settings, and whether the framework's benefits persist when modality encoders are frozen or when data is severely imbalanced.
