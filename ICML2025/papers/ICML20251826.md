## Problem

Federated Learning requires each client to store and train an entire model, which is infeasible for large-scale models, while Split Learning alleviates this memory constraint but introduces high communication latency due to sequential server-side processing. Existing federated split learning methods that use local auxiliary models to enable parallel client training lack any server feedback, leading to degraded accuracy. The paper addresses how to combine FL's data parallelism with SL's model splitting while preserving accuracy through periodic server-side gradient estimation.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method takes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) and produces class labels; for text generation, it takes tabular structured data (E2E dataset) and produces natural language sentences. The model is split at a cut-layer: the client-side portion (e.g., 685K params for ResNet-18, 66.2M for GPT2-medium) processes raw input and outputs cut-layer features, while the server-side portion (10.5M and 365.7M params respectively) processes those features. Each client additionally hosts a smaller auxiliary model (e.g., 2.1M params for ResNet-18) that locally estimates the server's gradient response to the cut-layer features, eliminating the need to wait for the server during local training.

## Method

FSL-SAGE splits the model into client-side and server-side portions at a cut-layer, with each client hosting an auxiliary model that approximates the server-side model's gradient output (the cut-layer backward pass). Clients train in parallel by backpropagating through their auxiliary model to obtain gradient estimates for the client-side parameters, removing the sequential server bottleneck. Every l rounds, the S-server aligns each client's auxiliary model to match the current server-side model using a stored alignment dataset of (cut-layer features, labels) pairs, ensuring the estimates remain accurate. The F-server performs standard federated averaging of client-side models each round, while the S-server updates the server-side model Q times per round using periodically transmitted cut-layer features. The paper proves an O(1/√T) convergence rate under a PAC-learnability assumption on the auxiliary models, matching FedAvg, and introduces a 'lazy' variant that freezes auxiliary models after T′ rounds to further reduce communication at a characterized accuracy cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (i.i.d.), FSL-SAGE achieves 85.71% test accuracy with ResNet-18, outperforming all baselines; under non-i.i.d. (Dirichlet) data it reaches 82.75%, while FedAvg drops to 42.72%. On CIFAR-100, FSL-SAGE attains 56.06% (i.i.d.) and 51.90% (non-i.i.d.), again the best among all methods. In terms of communication efficiency, at a fixed 81% accuracy on CIFAR-10, FSL-SAGE requires roughly 2.2× less communication than CSE-FSL and 10× less than SplitFed-SS. For LoRA fine-tuning of GPT2-medium on the E2E dataset, FSL-SAGE converges faster and more accurately than CSE-FSL, approaching the speed of SplitFed-SS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSL-SAGE outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and data-distribution setting in Table 1. Against FedAvg, the gap is most dramatic under non-i.i.d. conditions: FSL-SAGE reaches 82.75% on CIFAR-10 versus FedAvg's 42.72%, a 40-point improvement, because FedAvg cannot handle the model size on commodity clients. Against SplitFedv1 (multi-server), FSL-SAGE gains 1.5 points on CIFAR-10 i.i.d. (85.71 vs. 84.21) and 5.3 points on CIFAR-100 i.i.d. (56.06 vs. 50.78), while also requiring far less server-side compute since it maintains a single server model. Against SplitFedv2 (single-server), FSL-SAGE wins by 4.3 points on CIFAR-10 i.i.d. and 16.4 points on CIFAR-100 i.i.d., and is dramatically better under non-i.i.d. data (82.75 vs. 46.79 on CIFAR-10). Against the closest competitor CSE-FSL, FSL-SAGE improves accuracy by 1.97 points on CIFAR-10 i.i.d. and 5.12 points on CIFAR-100 i.i.d., while simultaneously reducing communication cost by ~2.2× at matched accuracy, thanks to its periodic auxiliary-model alignment strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSL-SAGE sits at the intersection of Federated Learning (FedAvg) and Split Learning, specifically extending the Federated Split Learning (FSL) line initiated by SplitFed (Thapa et al., 2022) and CSE-FSL (Mu & Shen, 2023). The key departure from prior FSL methods is the periodic alignment of client-side auxiliary models with the server-side model: while CSE-FSL and Han et al. (2021) use auxiliary models to enable parallel client training, they never synchronize these auxiliaries with the server, leading to accumulating estimation errors. FSL-SAGE introduces this alignment step and, critically, provides the first convergence analysis that explicitly accounts for the auxiliary model's gradient estimation error across four coupled time-scales (local steps, server steps, federated averaging, and alignment).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for federated training of large models (e.g., LLMs) where client devices cannot store the full model, offering a 10× communication reduction over sequential SplitFed while matching FedAvg's O(1/√T) convergence rate. The explicit characterization of how auxiliary model capacity affects final accuracy (via the ε⋆ term) gives practitioners a principled way to choose auxiliary architecture size. A notable limitation is the absence of linear speedup with the number of clients, inherited from the sequential server-side processing inherent to all single-server FSL approaches; resolving this without resorting to non-scalable multi-server designs (SplitFedv1) remains an open challenge. The lazy variant's explicit accuracy-communication trade-off is practically useful but the optimal T′ selection in real deployments is not fully characterized.
