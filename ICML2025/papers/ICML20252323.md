## Problem

No actor-critic algorithm with general function approximation was known to achieve the minimax-optimal 1/ε² sample complexity or √T regret when strategic exploration is necessary (i.e., without reachability or coverage assumptions). Prior work either required state-space reachability, achieved only 1/ε³ or worse sample complexity, or was restricted to linear MDPs, leaving the general function approximation setting unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252323/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on finite-horizon episodic MDPs (state space S, action space A, horizon H, transition kernels, reward functions) with a function class F for Q-function approximation over T episodes. The output is an ε-optimal policy or a sublinear regret guarantee, with sample complexity governed by the Bellman eluder dimension d and the sequential extrapolation coefficient (SEC) of the function class, yielding O(dH⁵ log|A|/ε² + dH⁴ log|F|/ε²) trajectories. The method requires no reachability or coverage assumptions on the state-action space.

## Method

The core contribution is NORA (Algorithm 2), which combines three key ingredients: (1) optimistic exploration via confidence sets, (2) off-policy critic estimation that targets the optimal Q-function Q* rather than the current policy's Q^π(t), and (3) rare-switching critic updates accompanied by policy resets to the uniform distribution. The critical insight is that targeting Q* (rather than the moving target Q^π(t)) ensures sufficient optimism at every step and makes rare-switching updates tractable, since the Bellman operator is fixed across iterations. Policy resets after each of the O(dH log T) critic updates control the tracking error between the current policy and the greedy policy, while an increased learning rate (by a factor of √(dH log T)) mitigates the regret cost of these resets. This yields √T regret and 1/ε² sample complexity under general function approximation, resolving the open problem posed by Zhong and Zhang (2023) and Liu et al. (2023b).

## Results

NORA (Algorithm 2) achieves a sample complexity of dH⁵log|A|/ε² + dH⁴log|F|/ε² trajectories and matching √T regret for actor-critic methods with general function approximation, resolving a long-standing open problem. DOUHUA (Algorithm 1) achieves H⁴log|A|/ε² + dH⁴log|F|/ε² in the 'good case' (when the function class is closed under truncated sums). In experiments on a linear MDP tetris task, Algorithm 1 outperforms rare-switching LSVI-UCB, and Algorithm 2 also achieves √T regret. On antmaze-medium-diverse-v2, the hybrid variant Algorithm 2H outperforms Cal-QL, which in turn slightly outperforms Algorithm 1H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NORA (Algorithm 2) achieves the best known sample complexity bound for actor-critic methods with general function approximation, improving over Liu et al. (2023b) which requires 1/ε³ samples (or 1/ε⁴ when the policy class grows exponentially) and over Zhou et al. (2023) which requires 1/ε⁶ samples. Compared to Sherman et al. (2024) and Cassel and Rosenberg (2024), which achieve 1/ε² only for linear MDPs, NORA extends this to general function approximation at the cost of an additional dH log T factor. In the linear MDP tetris experiment, DOUHUA (Algorithm 1) outperforms rare-switching LSVI-UCB (Jin et al., 2019), while NORA (Algorithm 2) achieves √T regret but performs slightly worse than LSVI-UCB. On antmaze-medium-diverse-v2, the hybrid variant Algorithm 2H outperforms Cal-QL (Nakamoto et al., 2023), which in turn slightly outperforms Algorithm 1H, suggesting that the proposed design principles (targeting Q*, rare-switching, policy resets) are competitive with state-of-the-art hybrid RL even without pessimism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GOLF algorithm (Jin et al., 2021) and the rare-switching framework of Xiong et al. (2023), extending them from the 'good case' (where the policy class does not grow exponentially) to the general function approximation setting where it does. It departs from prior optimistic policy optimization work (Efroni et al., 2020; Cai et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023b) by identifying that the critic must target Q* rather than Q^π(t) to maintain sufficient optimism under rare-switching updates, and introduces policy resets to control the resulting tracking error. The linear MDP results of Sherman et al. (2024) and Cassel and Rosenberg (2024) achieved the same 1/ε² rate but required bespoke methods specific to linear structure; this paper generalizes to arbitrary function classes via the Bellman eluder dimension and sequential extrapolation coefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a explicitly stated open problem in the RL literature: whether actor-critic methods with general function approximation can achieve minimax-optimal 1/ε² sample complexity when strategic exploration is necessary (no reachability or coverage assumptions). The hybrid RL extension further shows that offline data enables both sample efficiency gains (with optimism) and computational efficiency gains (without optimism, via non-optimistic variants), addressing a gap in Zhou et al. (2023). Limitations include the computational inefficiency of Algorithm 2 (optimization over confidence sets each episode) and the dependence on the Bellman eluder dimension, which can scale as Ω(√T) in rare cases. The connection to DDPG/TD3 design principles suggests the theoretical insights could inform practical algorithm development, though the paper acknowledges that further numerical experiments are needed.
