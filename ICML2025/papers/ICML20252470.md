## Problem

Existing representation intervention methods for LLM hallucination mitigation (ITI, NL-ITI, TruthX) rely on an unverified assumption that a single universal truthful correction vector can steer all queries from hallucinated to truthful states. The paper demonstrates empirically via PCA visualization that individual queries have distinct optimal correction directions that frequently contradict the overall trend, making a one-size-fits-all vector insufficient for diverse inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252470/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, TruthFlow takes a text query and extracts the last-token hidden state at a chosen intermediate transformer layer (e.g., layer 12 for Llama-3-8B) as input to a lightweight 1D U-Net flow model (~0.1B parameters, <500 MB memory). The flow model outputs a query-specific correction vector, which is projected onto a truthfulness-related subspace and added (scaled by a multiplier α) to all token hidden states at that layer before the LLM continues generation. The final output is the LLM's standard text generation (open-ended or multiple-choice), now steered toward truthfulness without modifying any model weights.

## Method

TruthFlow trains a rectified flow matching model to learn the distributional mapping from query representations to their corresponding truthful correction vectors. For each training query, the source is the last-token hidden state h_q^l at layer l, and the target is d_q^l = h̄_c^l − h̄_i^l, the difference between average hidden states of the correct and incorrect answers appended to the query. The 1D U-Net (depth 4, feature scale 0.5, sinusoidal time embedding) is trained via the standard flow matching objective over linear interpolation trajectories, requiring only ~408 pairs and a few seconds of training. At inference, the ODE dz_t = v_φ(t, z_t)dt is solved with the Midpoint method (RK2) in 16 steps starting from z_0 = h_q^l to produce the correction vector. A key refinement projects this vector onto the top-k singular vectors of the training correction matrix (via SVD) to purify noisy, query-specific information and retain only the principal truthfulness-related directions.

## Results

On TruthfulQA open-ended generation, TruthFlow achieves an average ~7% improvement in GPT-4 True score over the base model across six LLMs, with the largest gain on Llama3 (52.32 → 64.79, +12.5 points) and Gemma2 (64.30 → 76.53, +12.2 points). The combined True*Info metric improves by ~13 points on Llama3 (47.97 → 61.15) and ~15 points on Gemma2 (58.33 → 73.35). On multiple-choice tasks, TruthFlow yields ~5% average gains in both MC1 and MC2 (e.g., Llama3 MC1: 32.76 → 41.08; MC2: 50.75 → 59.77). BLEURT scores also improve by ~5% on average, confirming gains are not limited to GPT-4-judged truthfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TruthFlow outperforms all five inference-time baselines (DoLa, AD, ITI, NL-ITI, TruthX) on the True and True*Info metrics across all six LLMs. The most dramatic wins are over ITI and NL-ITI on open-ended generation: on Llama3, TruthFlow achieves 64.79 True vs. 54.52 for both ITI and NL-ITI (+10.3 points), and 61.15 True*Info vs. 49.32/49.46 (+~12 points). Against DoLa, TruthFlow consistently leads by 5–12 points on True score (e.g., Llama3: 64.79 vs. 55.50; Gemma2: 76.53 vs. 66.26). On the only model where TruthX is available (Llama2-7B), TruthFlow wins on True (59.41 vs. 56.23) and True*Info (54.91 vs. 49.49) but trails slightly on BLEURT (57.95 vs. 58.44). Compared to the post-training baseline GRATH (Appendix C.1), TruthFlow achieves a nearly identical True*Info score (54.91 vs. 54.95 on Llama2-7B) without any iterative DPO training, highlighting its efficiency as a purely inference-time method.

## Relation to Prior Work

TruthFlow extends the representation intervention line of work (ITI, NL-ITI, TruthX, LITO) that edits LLM hidden states at inference time to elicit truthful outputs. The key conceptual departure is rejecting the unverified assumption of a universal correction vector: prior methods apply a single fixed vector to all queries, while TruthFlow uses a flow matching model (adapted from generative modeling) to produce query-specific correction vectors learned from the distribution of per-query truthful directions. The SVD-based truthfulness subspace projection draws on intrinsic dimensionality arguments and prior work on identifying low-dimensional behavioral subspaces in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that no single universal correction vector suffices across diverse queries challenges a foundational assumption in the representation intervention literature and motivates a shift toward query-adaptive methods. Practically, TruthFlow is lightweight (a ~0.1B parameter 1D U-Net trained in seconds) and requires no LLM retraining, making it deployable alongside existing models. Open questions include whether the flow matching formulation is truly necessary versus simpler query-conditioned predictors, whether the approach generalizes to steering other behaviors (safety, style) beyond truthfulness, and how performance scales when training data is limited or noisy.
