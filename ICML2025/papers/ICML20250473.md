## Problem

Dynamic correlation clustering with node insertions requires maintaining a good clustering as new nodes (and their edges) arrive in a database, using only sublinear database operations per update. The prior algorithm by Cohen-Addad et al. (ICML 2024) achieved a constant approximation with polylogarithmic update time, but its approximation constant was extremely large and never explicitly estimated. This paper closes that gap by providing a concrete (20+ε)-approximation while preserving the O_ε(log^O(1)(n)) amortized update time.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a graph stored in a database where nodes arrive in a fixed, non-adaptive order; each new node's incident edges are inserted immediately upon arrival. Access to the graph is restricted to three database operations: retrieving a node's degree, selecting a random neighbor, and checking whether two nodes are connected. The output is an incrementally updated clustering (partition of nodes into clusters) that minimizes disagreements with the edge set. The algorithm also supports soft deletions of random vertices, where deleted nodes remain in the database until explicitly purged during periodic recomputation.

## Method

SPARSE-PIVOT builds on the 5-approximation PIVOT variant of Behnezhad et al. (2023) but makes it efficient under the database model by avoiding full neighborhood scans. Each node u is assigned a random rank π(u)∈[0,1]; if π(u)≤L/d(u) (a small-rank node), the algorithm performs a full EXPLORE scan of u's neighborhood, while for large-rank nodes it samples O(log n) random neighbors and selects the lowest-ranked pivot among them. A critical additional step partitions each pivot's assigned node set B_v into a dense cluster C_v (nodes with in-cluster degree above a threshold t) and singletons, with the threshold chosen from O(log n) geometric candidates via cost estimation. This BREAK-CLUSTER/UPDATE-CLUSTER mechanism is what elevates the approximation from 5 to 20+ε, since it handles incorrectly assigned 'bad' nodes that would otherwise inflate cluster costs. The amortized update time is O_ε(log^O(1)(n)) database operations, achieved by charging EXPLORE costs to node budgets and recomputing the full clustering only after O(εN) updates to handle deletions.

## Results

On the drift dataset (13,910 nodes, 129-dimensional), SPARSE-PIVOT achieves the lowest normalized correlation clustering objective at 4 out of 5 density levels, with values ranging from 0.29 (density 42.25) to 0.60 (density 235.36). The theoretical approximation factor is 20+ε, a substantial improvement over the unquantified (very large) constant of Cohen-Addad et al. On the four SNAP real-world graphs (musae-facebook, emailEnron, ca-AstroPh, cit-HepTh), SPARSE-PIVOT also consistently outperforms both baselines in clustering objective, as shown in Figure 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARSE-PIVOT outperforms DYNAMIC-AGREEMENT (Cohen-Addad et al., ICML 2024) at every density level on the drift dataset, with margins ranging from 0.04 (at density 235.36: 0.60 vs. 0.69) to 0.06 (at density 42.25: 0.29 vs. 0.35). Against REFERENCE CLUSTERING (the dynamic implementation of the 5-approximation PIVOT variant from Behnezhad et al., SODA 2023), SPARSE-PIVOT wins at 4 of 5 densities (e.g., 0.41 vs. 0.50 at density 69.74) but is slightly worse at the densest setting (0.60 vs. 0.59 at density 235.36). On the four SNAP real-world graphs, SPARSE-PIVOT also achieves a lower clustering objective than both baselines across all steps. The paper also notes that SPARSE-PIVOT is faster in practice than DYNAMIC-AGREEMENT, though the primary focus of the experiments is solution quality rather than runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 5-approximation PIVOT variant of Behnezhad, Charikar, Ma, and Tan (SODA 2023) for semi-streaming correlation clustering, adapting it to the dynamic node-insertion database model introduced by Cohen-Addad, Lattanzi, Maggiori, and Parotsidis (ICML 2024). The key departure from Cohen-Addad et al. is the use of randomized sampling to avoid scanning full neighborhoods (reducing update time from potentially linear to polylogarithmic) combined with a BREAK-CLUSTER/UPDATE-CLUSTER mechanism that partitions each pivot's assigned nodes into a dense cluster and singletons. This yields a concrete 20+ε approximation factor, replacing the prior work's unestimated (and implicitly very large) constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20+ε approximation with polylogarithmic database operations per insertion makes dynamic correlation clustering practically viable for large-scale streaming applications (e.g., continuously clustering new items in an e-commerce catalog), where the prior algorithm's unbounded constant made its guarantee vacuous in practice. The soft-deletion model, while weaker than full deletion support, covers a natural and common operational pattern. Open questions include whether the approximation can be tightened toward the best static bound (1.437), whether the sampling-based pivot recovery can be made deterministic, and whether the approach extends to edge insertions/deletions in the same database model.
