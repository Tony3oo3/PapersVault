## Problem

Deep learning priors for imaging inverse problems have been dominated by Gaussian denoisers, with limited exploration of general restoration models (e.g., deblurring, MRI reconstruction networks) as priors. Existing restoration-prior methods like DRP rely on a single fixed degradation operator, failing to exploit the diversity of structured artifacts, and have not addressed the practical challenge of training priors when fully-sampled ground-truth data is unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250965/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ShaRP takes noisy incomplete measurements y = Ax + e (e.g., k-space subsampled MRI data or blurred-and-downsampled RGB images) along with the known measurement operator A, and iteratively produces a reconstructed image x. The pre-trained restoration prior R(s, H) is conditioned on a degradation operator H sampled stochastically from a distribution p(H) at each iteration, meaning the prior's training degradations need not match the target measurement operator A. For MRI, inputs are complex-valued 320×320 multi-coil k-space data; for SISR, inputs are 2× downsampled images with Gaussian blur.

## Method

ShaRP trains a U-Net restoration network on a family of degradation operators H_α = (1−α)I + αM (convex combinations of identity and the target degradation M), creating an ensemble of MMSE restoration operators R(s, H_α) = E[x|s, H_α]. At each iteration, the algorithm stochastically samples H from p(H), forms a noisy degraded observation s = Hx + n, applies the restoration network, and uses the residual H^T H(x − R(s,H)) as a stochastic gradient estimate for a novel regularizer h(x) = τ E[−log p(s|H)]. The paper proves this regularizer equals the expected negative log-likelihood of degraded observations, establishing ShaRP as a stochastic gradient method minimizing a composite data-fidelity-plus-regularizer objective. A second theoretical result shows that the MMSE restoration operator can be learned equivalently via a weighted self-supervised loss from pairs of undersampled measurements, eliminating the need for fully-sampled ground truth.

## Results

On CS-MRI with 4× uniform undersampling, ShaRP achieves 37.59 dB PSNR and 0.963 SSIM at σ=0.005, outperforming the best denoiser-based method (PnP-FISTA, 35.88 dB) by 1.71 dB and the best diffusion-based method (DDS, 35.21 dB) by 2.38 dB. At higher noise (σ=0.015), ShaRP maintains 34.92 dB PSNR versus 34.51 dB for DDS and 30.32 dB for PnP-FISTA. On 2× SISR with Gaussian blur (σ=1.25, noiseless), ShaRP reaches 30.09 dB PSNR and 0.891 SSIM, surpassing DRP (29.28 dB) and DiffPIR (28.92 dB), though DiffPIR achieves a lower LPIPS (0.152 vs. 0.179) indicating slightly better perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShaRP consistently outperforms all denoiser-based baselines (PnP-FISTA, PnP-ADMM) across all noise levels and undersampling rates, with the gap widening at higher noise (e.g., 4.6 dB over PnP-FISTA at σ=0.015). Against diffusion-model-based methods, ShaRP surpasses DPS by 2.3–4.6 dB PSNR and DDS by 0.4–2.4 dB, with the advantage growing as noise increases. Compared to the single-prior restoration method DRP, ShaRP's ensemble of restoration priors yields 1.3–2.4 dB PSNR gains, confirming the benefit of stochastic multi-prior regularization. On SISR, ShaRP achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all blur kernels and noise levels, beating DRP by ~0.8 dB and DiffPIR by ~1.2 dB, though DiffPIR retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.152 vs. 0.179) in perceptual quality. In the self-supervised setting (no fully-sampled ground truth), ShaRP-self outperforms SPICER by 2.0 dB and GRAPPA by 5.8 dB at 4× uniform, demonstrating that restoration priors trained without ground truth can match or exceed methods requiring fully-sampled data. The only method that surpasses ShaRP is E2E-VarNet (38.10 vs. 37.59 dB), but it requires task-specific retraining, whereas ShaRP uses a fixed pre-trained prior without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShaRP unifies and extends three distinct threads: (1) Regularization by Denoising (RED) and Stochastic Denoising Regularization (SNORE), which use Gaussian denoisers as implicit regularizers in iterative optimization; (2) Deep Restoration Priors (DRP), which replace the single denoiser with a general restoration operator but still use only one fixed prior; and (3) controllable-process training (InDI, I²SB), which trains a single network to act as an ensemble of MMSE operators across a family of degradations. ShaRP's key conceptual departure is combining the stochastic ensemble mechanism of SNORE with the general restoration operators of DRP, yielding a closed-form regularizer whose gradient is a stochastic approximation over multiple degradation-specific MMSE operators. Additionally, the paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that MMSE restoration operators can be learned equivalently from self-supervised (undersampled) measurements, a result not available for Gaussian denoisers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is twofold: first, ShaRP demonstrates that a simple optimization-based framework with restoration priors can consistently outperform both denoiser-based PnP methods and diffusion-model-based samplers (DPS, DDS, DiffPIR) on CS-MRI and SISR without task-specific retraining, suggesting that the field may be over-investing in diffusion-based inverse problem solvers when well-chosen restoration priors suffice for fidelity metrics. Second, the self-supervised training result is practically important for MRI, where fully-sampled reference data is often unavailable, yet ShaRP with self-supervised priors outperforms methods relying on fully-supervised denoisers. Open questions include whether sampling-based extensions can close the perceptual-quality gap (the paper acknowledges the perception-distortion tradeoff limits its optimization-based approach), and whether the framework extends to non-linear or non-Gaussian measurement models beyond the linear settings tested here.
