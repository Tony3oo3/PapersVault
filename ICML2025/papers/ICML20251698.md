## Problem

In multivariate long-term time series forecasting, different predictor variables influence the target over distinct time lags (the multi-delay issue), yet existing Mamba- and Transformer-based models process all variables or time points uniformly. This uniform treatment prevents the model from capturing complex variable relationships and obtaining non-trivial temporal representations, limiting forecasting accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251698/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×L} (N variables, lookback length L) and produces a forecast Y ∈ R^{N×H} for horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Before processing, the input is normalized via RevIN and each univariate series is divided into overlapping patches, yielding an embedding E₀ ∈ R^{N×P×D} that explicitly preserves both the variable and temporal dimensions rather than flattening them, which is essential for the subsequent hyper-state construction.

## Method

TimePro is a Mamba-based encoder with a core HyperMamba module that replaces the standard selective scan with a hardware-aware Hyper-scan. The Hyper-scan first scans along the variable dimension to obtain a plain hidden state, then applies a time-tune strategy: a convolution generates learnable offsets that, combined with reference points and differentiable linear interpolation, adaptively select M salient time points per variable to reshape the plain state into a variable- and time-aware hyper-state. A scanning inception design splits the channel in half and scans in opposite variable directions to capture global dependencies, and the final output is gated by a SiLU-activated projection. The architecture achieves O(NL) complexity by performing the state scan in GPU SRAM and keeping the number of SSM states at 1 per dimension, while ablations confirm that removing the depthwise convolution and post-scan linear projection (redundant given the TimeFFN) further improves both accuracy and efficiency.

## Results

TimePro achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across eight multivariate long-term forecasting benchmarks (ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, ECL, Exchange, Weather, Solar-Energy) with forecast horizons of 96–720 and lookback window L=96. It scores 12 firsts and 2 seconds out of 16 MSE/MAE metrics, with headline results including MSE 0.169 / MAE 0.262 on ECL, MSE 0.352 / MAE 0.399 on Exchange, and MSE 0.281 / MAE 0.326 on ETTm2. The model maintains linear O(NL) complexity while using only 67% of S-Mamba's parameters and 78% of its GFLOPs, with inference speeds 2.7× and 14.4× faster than PatchTST and TimesNet respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimePro outperforms S-Mamba (the closest Mamba-based competitor) on 6 of 8 datasets in both MSE and MAE, with the largest gap on ETTm2 (MSE 0.281 vs 0.288) and ETTh1 (MSE 0.438 vs 0.455), while tying on Weather. Against SOFTS, TimePro wins on ECL, Exchange, ETTm1, ETTm2, and ETTh1, but loses on Solar-Energy (MSE 0.232 vs 0.229) and ETTh2 (MSE 0.377 vs 0.373). TimePro dominates iTransformer on all eight datasets, with the most substantial margin on ETTm2 (MSE 0.281 vs 0.288) and ETTh1 (MSE 0.438 vs 0.454). Against Transformer-based methods like PatchTST and TimesNet, TimePro achieves consistent and large improvements (e.g., ECL MSE 0.169 vs 0.189 for PatchTST and 0.192 for TimesNet). Compared to the lightweight DLinear baseline, TimePro reduces MSE by 19–33% across datasets while maintaining linear complexity, confirming that the hyper-state mechanism captures non-trivial temporal and variable interactions that simple linear models cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimePro extends the Mamba (selective state space model) architecture for multivariate time series forecasting by replacing the standard selective scan with a hardware-aware Hyper-scan that preserves fine-grained temporal structure within each variable's state. The adaptive time-point sampling mechanism draws conceptually from deformable convolutions (Xiong et al., 2024), repurposing learnable offsets to select salient temporal positions rather than spatial locations. It departs from prior Mamba-based forecasters (Bi-Mamba+, S-Mamba, TimeMachine, TSMamba) that process all variables or time points uniformly, and from Transformer-based methods (iTransformer, PatchTST) that suffer quadratic complexity, by introducing a time-tune strategy that adaptively modulates the variable-dimension hidden state with selected temporal features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TimePro offers a practical efficiency-accuracy tradeoff for multivariate long-term forecasting: linear O(NL) complexity with minimal parameters and memory, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment on high-dimensional data (e.g., 321-channel ECL). The multi-delay framing, while standard in time series literature, is addressed here through a concrete architectural mechanism (adaptive time-point sampling) rather than explicit lag modeling, which could generalize to other sequential tasks with heterogeneous temporal influences. Open questions include whether the time-tune strategy scales to very long horizons or non-stationary regimes beyond the eight standard benchmarks, and whether the authors' stated goal of a time-series foundation model can be realized with this architecture.
