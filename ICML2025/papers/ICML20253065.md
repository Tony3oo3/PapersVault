## Problem

Prior analyses of adaptive stepsize gradient descent for logistic regression on separable data require the descent lemma, forcing the base stepsize to be small and the risk to decrease monotonically, which caps the convergence rate at exp(-Θ(t)). This paper asks whether allowing GD to violate the descent lemma and enter the edge of stability regime, while still using risk-adaptive stepsizes, can yield fundamentally faster convergence, and whether a margin-dependent burn-in phase is unavoidable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linearly separable dataset {(x_i, y_i)} with margin γ (‖x_i‖₂ ≤ 1, y_i x_i^⊤ w* ≥ γ), a classification loss (exponential, logistic, polynomial, or probit), and a base stepsize η > 0. At each iteration the stepsize adapts as η_t = η·(-ℓ⁻¹)'∘ℒ(w_t) ≈ η/ℒ(w_t), scaling inversely with current risk to compensate for the flattening curvature as the loss decays. The output is a risk upper bound of exp(-γ²ηt/8) on the averaged iterate (or best iterate for two-layer networks) after a margin-dependent burn-in of t₀ = Θ(1/γ²) steps, valid for arbitrarily large η.

## Method

The core reformulation views adaptive stepsize GD as constant-stepsize GD on a modified loss φ(w) = -ℓ⁻¹(ℒ(w)), which is convex for the considered losses. The proof employs a split optimization technique: the comparator u = u₁ + u₂ is decomposed so that u₂ = (C_ℓη/2γ)w* cancels the quadratic gradient term (2⟨∇φ(w), u₂⟩ + η‖∇φ(w)‖² ≤ 0, exploiting the margin and loss self-boundedness), while u₁ = (γηt/2)w* extracts the linear margin term. Telescoping the resulting per-step inequality and applying convexity of φ yields the exp(-Θ(ηt)) rate. Two lower bounds complement the upper bound: one showing stable-regime adaptive GD is limited to exp(-Θ(t)), and another (via a dimensionality argument) showing any first-order method requires Ω(γ^{-2/3}) burn-in steps. The framework extends to general losses satisfying a self-boundedness condition and to two-layer networks with leaky ReLU activations.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.2) shows that GD with adaptive stepsizes η_t = η/ℒ(w_t) achieves a risk upper bound of exp(-γ²ηt/8) for the averaged iterate once t ≥ t₀ = Θ(1/γ²), where the base stepsize η can be chosen arbitrarily large. This contrasts with prior adaptive-stepsize analyses (Ji & Telgarsky, 2021) that require η ≤ 1/β (small stepsize, stable regime) and yield only exp(-Θ(γ²ηt)) with bounded η. A matching lower bound (Theorem 3.1) shows that if GD stays in the stable regime (monotonic risk decrease), the best achievable rate is exp(-Θ(t)), confirming that entering the edge-of-stability regime is necessary for the arbitrarily fast rate. A second lower bound (Theorem 3.2) establishes that any first-order method requires Ω(γ^{-2/3}) burn-in steps to reduce the risk below ℓ(0)/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (large + adaptive stepsizes) strictly dominates all prior approaches for logistic regression on separable data. Compared to adaptive-stepsize GD in the stable regime (Ji & Telgarsky, 2021; Nacson et al., 2019), which achieves exp(-Θ(γ²t)) with a fixed small η, the new method achieves exp(-Θ(γηt)) with η → ∞, yielding arbitrarily fast convergence. Compared to constant-stepsize GD at the edge of stability (Wu et al., 2024), which achieves only O(1/t²) risk decay, the adaptive-stepsize method achieves exponential decay. The lower bound in Theorem 3.1 confirms that the exp(-Θ(t)) rate of stable-regime adaptive GD is tight, so the improvement fundamentally requires both large and adaptive stepsizes simultaneously. For two-layer networks with leaky ReLU (Theorem 4.1), the same exp(-Θ(α²γ²ηt)) rate holds for the best iterate along the trajectory, improving upon the O(1/t²) rate of Cai et al. (2024) for constant-stepsize GD in the EoS regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive-stepsize GD analysis of Nacson et al. (2019) and Ji & Telgarsky (2021), which required the descent lemma (monotonic risk decrease) and thus restricted the base stepsize η to be small, yielding only exp(-Θ(t)) rates. It also builds on the split-optimization technique and EoS analysis of Wu et al. (2024), who showed that large *constant* stepsizes enter the edge of stability but achieve only O(1/t²) rates. The key departure is combining large *and* adaptive stepsizes: the stepsize scales inversely with the current risk while the base η is allowed to be arbitrarily large, so GD is permitted to violate the descent lemma and oscillate. This yields an exp(-Θ(ηt)) rate for the averaged iterate, with η unbounded, a regime no prior adaptive-stepsize analysis could access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result reframes the edge of stability from a nuisance to a resource: deliberately violating the descent lemma with adaptive stepsizes removes the fundamental rate ceiling that prior theory imposed. The matching lower bounds (Theorem 3.1 showing EoS entry is necessary for the fast rate, and Theorem 3.2 showing a margin-dependent burn-in is unavoidable for any first-order method) give a near-complete picture of the optimization landscape. Open questions include closing the gap between the Θ(1/γ²) upper-bound burn-in and the Ω(γ^{-2/3}) lower bound, extending the analysis to non-separable or noisy data, and understanding whether the 'arbitrarily fast' rate translates into practical speedups in deep learning where the margin structure is less clean.
