## Problem

Existing LLM evaluations either target application-specific agent tasks or examine isolated cognitive abilities in isolation, leaving the holistic process of epistemic agency—how a model constructs, adapts, and monitors beliefs during sustained agent-environment interaction—unmeasured. Additionally, reliance on static text-heavy datasets introduces benchmark leakage risks that undermine long-term validity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task presents sequential, one-at-a-time text stimuli (e.g., a weather state plus sensor reading, a card description, a single letter, or a reward signal) with the LLM responding after each step before receiving the next stimulus and feedback. The parameterized design hides specific values (transition probabilities p, reversal trial indices, loss probabilities) from the model, so even a model that memorizes the task format cannot predict the evaluation parameters. Outputs include a 0–100 score per task (MAE-based for probabilistic tasks, accuracy for discrete tasks, embedding-similarity for the Oddball test), plus qualitative behavioral pattern classifications (e.g., 'shape sink' in WCST, 'win-stay-lose-switch' in PRLT).

## Method

Reflection-Bench adapts seven validated cognitive-psychology paradigms into interactive, parameterized text tasks, each isolating one dimension of epistemic agency: Weather Prediction Task (prediction), Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (decision-making), Oddball paradigm (perception), N-back (memory), Double-Choice Iowa Gambling Task (counterfactual thinking), Probabilistic Reversal Learning Task (belief updating), and a novel Meta-Bandit Task (meta-reflection). The key design principle is parameterization: task structure is fixed but numerical parameters (e.g., p=0.9 vs. 0.8, reversal every n=2 vs. n=4 trials) are configurable, preventing memorization-based shortcuts and enabling difficulty scaling for future models. Evaluation spans 16 LLMs (large reasoning models, mainstream LLMs, and Qwen-2.5 family) under three prompting strategies (direct generation, free output, zero-shot CoT), with chance-level baselines established via 1 million random simulations per task. A contamination check compares Centaur (fine-tuned on human cognitive test data) against its base model Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, confirming no performance gain from prior exposure to similar paradigms.

## Results

Across 16 LLMs evaluated with three prompting strategies (CoT, free output, direct generation) on entry-level difficulty, a clear three-tier hierarchy emerges: seven top models (Claude-3.5-Sonnet 68.78, o1-preview 66.57, DeepSeek-Reasoner 64.56, Gemini-2.0-flash-exp 63.29, GPT-4o 62.56, Grok-2 61.63, DeepSeek-V3 61.25) score above 60; eight intermediate models score between 50–60; and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (44.53) falls below 50. No model-strategy combination exceeds chance-level thresholds on all five applicable tasks, and all 16 models fail the Meta-Bandit Task (meta-reflection), unable to recognize the 2-step reversal pattern across 20 blocks. On hard-parameter settings, expected score decreases confirm the benchmark's long-term relevance and substantial headroom for future models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's key baseline comparison is between Centaur (a model specifically fine-tuned with human cognitive test data, Binz et al. 2024) and its base model Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, evaluated on both Easy and Hard settings with direct generation. Centaur scores 44.20 (Hard) and 46.34 (Easy, free output) overall, while Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct scores 51.35 (Easy, free output) and 55.17 (Easy, CoT) — Centaur actually underperforms its base model by 5–15 points. This result provides empirical evidence that Reflection-Bench's parameterized design effectively minimizes data leakage: even a model explicitly trained on human cognitive test data gains no advantage, confirming that the specific parameter configurations used in evaluation cannot be memorized. Among the 16 main models, Claude-3.5-Sonnet with CoT achieves the highest overall score (68.78), outperforming o1-preview (66.57) and DeepSeek-Reasoner (64.56) by 2–4 points, while the smallest model Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (44.53) trails the top tier by over 24 points, demonstrating the benchmark's discriminative power across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing line of work applying cognitive psychology paradigms to LLM evaluation (Binz & Schulz 2023; Kennedy & Nowak 2024) by unifying seven previously isolated cognitive tests into a single process-oriented framework grounded in the active inference / free-energy principle literature (Friston 2010; Parr et al. 2022). It departs from prior agent benchmarks (AgentBench, PlanBench, etc.) that evaluate application-specific or single-ability performance by targeting a base-model-level capacity—epistemic agency—as a holistic, cyclical process of prediction, decision, perception, memory, counterfactual reasoning, belief updating, and meta-reflection. The parameterized task design is a methodological departure from text-heavy static benchmarks, explicitly motivated by data-contamination concerns (Zhou et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no model—regardless of scale or reasoning capability—successfully recognized a simple 2-step reversal pattern across 20 blocks in the Meta Bandit Task reveals a fundamental gap in current LLMs' ability to abstract global temporal structure from sequential experience, with direct implications for building reliable autonomous agents. The parameterized design and demonstrated long-term relevance (score drops on harder settings) position Reflection-Bench as a sustainable evaluation tool, while the Centaur comparison empirically validates contamination resistance. Open questions include how epistemic agency manifests in multi-LLM collaborative systems, multi-modal or embodied settings, and more naturalistic open-ended contexts beyond structured cognitive tests.
