## Problem

Grokking—delayed generalization accompanied by non-monotonic test loss—has been observed in complex transformer models on algorithmic tasks, but its fundamental mechanism remained unclear. This paper asks whether grokking can arise in a minimal, analytically tractable setting, and if so, what structural property of the problem drives it. The authors show that grokking emerges naturally in binary logistic regression when the system is near a critical point where the overfitting and generalizing solutions exchange stability.

## Input / Output

The model takes N samples drawn from a d-dimensional isotropic Gaussian (equivalently, two Gaussians separated along one axis with noise σ_B in all perpendicular directions) and assigns all points the same binary label, reducing the problem to a d-dimensional logistic regression with a bias term. The output is the trained weight vector S ∈ ℝ^d and scalar bias b, from which generalization accuracy and loss are computed in closed form via the error function. The key control parameter is λ = d/N, which determines whether the training set is linearly separable from the origin (λ > 1/2) or not (λ < 1/2) in the large N, d limit.

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient descent dynamics on logistic regression using the implicit bias framework of Soudry et al. (2018), showing that late-time dynamics are governed by the hard-margin SVM solution in an extended (d+1)-dimensional space. A key technical tool is the introduction of conformal time τ = ∫e^{b(t)}dt, which decouples the dynamics of S from b and reveals that S(t) follows the same trajectory as bias-free exponential-loss optimization, but at a rate controlled by b. The authors prove that perfect generalization occurs if and only if the data is not linearly separable from the origin (Prop. 3.2), and that this separability threshold sits at λ = 1/2 by Wendel's theorem (Prop. 3.3). Near the critical point, ||S_∞|| diverges (Prop. 3.4), causing the dynamics to linger near the overfitting solution for arbitrarily long times before transitioning to the generalizing solution—this is the mechanism of grokking. A fully solvable two-point 1D model is constructed to explicitly capture the critical slowing down and reproduce the logarithmic divergence of grokking time.

## Results

The paper establishes that grokking in logistic regression occurs at the critical point λ_c = d/N = 1/2, where the training set transitions from being almost surely inseparable (λ < 1/2, yielding perfect generalization with A_gen → 1) to almost surely separable (λ > 1/2, yielding overfitting with A_gen = ½[1 + erf(1/(σM√2))]). The norm of the limiting weight vector |S_∞| diverges logarithmically as λ → 1/2⁻, and the grokking time (delay between training and generalization accuracy reaching threshold) diverges accordingly. In the tractable 1D simplified model, the grokking time ratio satisfies √(log(t_gen*/t_tr*)) = (1/√2)·log(1−2λ), verified numerically. Both λ → 1/2⁻ and σ → ∞ are required simultaneously for grokking to manifest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method baselines. Instead, it builds on and extends four key prior theoretical results: (1) Soudry et al. (2018) on the implicit bias of GD on separable data, whose Theorem 3 is directly rephrased as Theorem 1 here; (2) Nacson et al. (2019) and Ji & Telgarsky (2019) on late-time dynamics for separable and inseparable data, from which the exponential-loss approximation and Prop. 3.4 (divergence of |S_∞|) follow; and (3) Wendel's theorem (1962), which provides the sharp λ = 1/2 threshold for separability. The paper's key contribution over these works is identifying the *mechanism* of grokking as critical slowing down near the separability threshold, and showing that the simplified 1D model quantitatively reproduces the full model's grokking time scaling. The authors also compare qualitatively with Power et al. (2022) and Levi et al. (2023), noting that the critical-point structure is shared across these seemingly different grokking phenomena.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the implicit bias literature for logistic regression (Soudry et al. 2018; Nacson et al. 2019; Ji & Telgarsky 2019), which characterizes late-time GD dynamics in terms of the hard-margin SVM solution, and on Wendel's theorem (1962) for the geometric separability threshold at λ = d/N = 1/2. It positions itself within the growing body of solvable grokking models (Levi et al. 2023; Rubin et al. 2023, 2024; Gromov 2023) but deliberately strips away all architectural and task-specific complexity, showing that grokking in logistic regression on Gaussian data requires only proximity to the linear-separability critical point. The paper explicitly departs from prior grokking explanations that invoke regularization, circuit formation, or representation learning, arguing none of these are necessary in this minimal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the cleanest available mechanistic explanation for grokking as 'critical slowing down' near an interpolation threshold, drawing a direct analogy to phase transitions in statistical physics and suggesting the possibility of universality classes across different grokking phenomena. However, the analysis is confined to linear logistic regression in the gradient-flow limit with isotropic Gaussian data, and the authors themselves note that extending the separability-based argument to nonlinear models, correlated data, or finite sample spaces remains open. The conjecture that grokking in transformers on modular arithmetic shares the same critical-point mechanism is plausible but unproven, and the practical implications for training large models are indirect.
