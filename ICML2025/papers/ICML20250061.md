## Problem

Existing non-parametric multi-class calibration methods (one-vs-rest isotonic regression, flattened isotonic regression) are trained without accounting for probability normalization, meaning the fitted calibration map is optimized on unnormalized scores and only normalized post-hoc. This disconnect between the optimization objective and the actual normalized outputs limits their effectiveness, causing them to underperform parametric methods like Temperature Scaling despite isotonic regression's strong theoretical properties in the binary case.

## Input / Output

The methods consume a calibration set of (uncalibrated probability vector, one-hot label) pairs from any trained classifier, where each probability vector lies in the (k−1)-simplex. NA-FIR applies a single shared monotonic function g: [0,1]→R⁺ to each class probability and normalizes the resulting k values to form the output probability vector. SCIR instead operates on cumulative top-r sorted probabilities, producing k−1 cumulative calibrated values per input point from which individual class probabilities are recovered by differencing, with a small ε added to prevent zero probabilities.

## Method

The paper proposes two normalization-aware isotonic regression methods. NA-FIR modifies the Flattened Isotonic Regression objective by placing the normalization denominator Σⱼ g(pⱼ) directly inside the NLL loss, making the problem non-convex; it is solved via MCMC (simulated annealing) over a PAVA-initialized block structure, with O(m) per-iteration cost via incremental matrix updates. SCIR reformulates calibration as a 2D cumulative isotonic regression: each prediction point generates k−1 (cumulative_top_r_score, rank) sub-problems, and monotonicity is enforced jointly in both the cumulative probability and rank dimensions; a dynamic programming algorithm (adapted from Spouge et al.) solves this in O(m²k⁴) worst-case time. Both methods assume Permutation Invariance and relax the Category Independence assumption, with NA-FIR additionally guaranteeing Order Preservation of the calibrated output relative to the input ranking.

## Results

NA-FIR achieves the best average NLL rank on 4 of 7 datasets, winning 62–100% of model comparisons on 20 Newsgroups (rank 1.4, 70%), CIFAR-10 (rank 1.2, 75%), CIFAR-100 (rank 1.5, 62%), and ImageNet-1k (rank 1.0, 100%). SCIR achieves the best average conf-ECE rank on 4 of 7 datasets, winning 25–100% of comparisons on CIFAR-10 (rank 1.0, 100%), CIFAR-100 (rank 1.2, 85%), 20 Newsgroups (rank 2.6, 60%), and ImageNet-1k (rank 2.0, 25%). Both methods consistently outperform FIR, IR OvR, and uncalibrated models across all 7 datasets and 5 evaluation metrics (NLL, conf-ECE, Brier score, cw-ECE, TECE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FIR (Patel et al., 2020), both NA-FIR and SCIR achieve substantially lower NLL and conf-ECE across nearly all datasets; for example, on CIFAR-10 NLL, NA-FIR ranks 1.2 vs. FIR's 4.0, and on CIFAR-100 conf-ECE, SCIR ranks 1.2 vs. FIR's 4.7. Against IR OvR (Zadrozny & Elkan, 2002), the proposed methods dominate on NLL (e.g., NA-FIR rank 1.0 vs. IR OvR rank 8.0 on ImageNet) and conf-ECE (e.g., SCIR rank 1.0 vs. IR OvR rank 5.8 on CIFAR-10), confirming that the one-vs-rest decomposition is suboptimal. Against Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017), NA-FIR wins on NLL for 5 of 7 datasets (notably ImageNet at 100% vs. TS's 0%), while SCIR wins on conf-ECE for 5 of 7 datasets; TS retains an edge on Food-101 NLL (rank 1.6 vs. 2.5) and ImageNet cw-ECE (rank 2.0 vs. 3.6). Against Vector Scaling and Matrix Scaling, the proposed methods are competitive or superior on most datasets, with MS being the only baseline that outperforms both on Yelp Review NLL (rank 1.5, 77% wins) due to the dataset's abundant per-class samples. Overall, the normalization-aware formulation provides consistent gains over all non-parametric baselines and matches or exceeds the strongest parametric method (TS) on the majority of dataset–metric combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the isotonic regression (IR) lineage for post-hoc calibration, building directly on Flattened Isotonic Regression (FIR) from Patel et al. (2020) and the one-vs-rest IR decomposition from Zadrozny & Elkan (2002). The key conceptual departure is making the isotonic optimization normalization-aware: NA-FIR incorporates the probability simplex constraint directly into the NLL objective (making it non-convex and requiring MCMC), while SCIR reformulates the problem as a 2D cumulative isotonic regression over sorted class ranks. Both methods relax the Category Independence assumption that underlies prior non-parametric multi-class extensions, while retaining Permutation Invariance and (for NA-FIR) Order Preservation, positioning them as non-parametric analogues to Temperature Scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a principled non-parametric alternative to Temperature Scaling in settings where parametric assumptions (uniform logit scaling) may be limiting, achieving SOTA NLL and conf-ECE across 7 datasets and 75 models. However, practical adoption is constrained by computational cost: SCIR scales as O(m²k⁴) (≈120 min on ImageNet-1k) and NA-FIR requires MCMC optimization (~15 min on large datasets), versus seconds for TS. An open question the authors highlight is the trade-off between calibration and refinement: SCIR's consistently lower NLL (described as 'unwanted') suggests near-zero probabilities from cumulative overlaps, and the relationship between user-specific metrics like conf-ECE and full calibration remains underexplored.
