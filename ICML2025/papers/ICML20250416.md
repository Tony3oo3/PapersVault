## Problem

The respective roles of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) in enhancing generalization versus memorization for foundation model post-training remain unclear. This paper investigates whether these paradigms primarily memorize training data or acquire transferable principles, examining both textual rule-based generalization and visual out-of-distribution generalization in arithmetic reasoning and spatial navigation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In GeneralPoints, the model receives four playing cards (as text descriptions or rendered images) and must output a JSON-formatted arithmetic equation using each card's value exactly once to reach a target of 24. In V-IRL, the model receives a 360° street-view image plus natural-language navigation instructions and outputs a JSON action (turn_direction, forward, or stop). Both tasks use a sequential revision formulation where the prompt at step t accumulates all prior model outputs and verifier feedback, enabling multi-turn self-correction within a single episode; rule and visual OOD variants (e.g., face cards as 11/12/13 vs. 10, absolute vs. relative orientation, black vs. red suits, NYC vs. worldwide routes) are specified purely through the system prompt text.

## Method

The paper uses Llama-3.2-Vision-11B as the backbone and employs a multi-turn RL framework built on the sequential revision formulation, where PPO updates the policy using an outcome-based reward from a rule-based verifier that checks equation correctness or action validity at each step. The training pipeline first applies SFT to stabilize the model's output format, then scales RL and SFT compute separately from a shared initialized checkpoint to isolate their effects on generalization. Generalization is evaluated by training on one rule/visual variant and testing on unseen variants, with the key finding that RL consistently generalizes to both rule and visual OOD settings while SFT degrades sharply. Additionally, RL with outcome-based rewards improves the VLM's underlying visual recognition accuracy as a byproduct, and scaling the number of verification iterations further enhances OOD generalization.

## Results

On rule-based OOD generalization, RL improves performance across all four task variants (GP-L: 11.5%→15.0%, V-IRL-L: 80.8%→91.8%, GP-VL: 11.2%→14.2%, V-IRL-VL: 35.7%→45.0%), while SFT degrades on all (GP-L: 11.5%→3.4%, V-IRL-L: 80.8%→1.3%, GP-VL: 11.2%→5.6%, V-IRL-VL: 35.7%→2.5%). On visual OOD generalization, RL achieves +17.6% on GP-VL (23.6%→41.2%) and +61.1% on V-IRL-VL (16.7%→77.8%), whereas SFT drops by −9.9% and −5.6% respectively. As a byproduct, the multi-turn RL approach sets a new SOTA on the V-IRL VLN mini benchmark at 77.8% success rate, improving over the previous best of 44.0% by +33.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison in this paper is between SFT and RL as post-training methods, both initialized from the same SFT checkpoint on Llama-3.2-Vision-11B. RL consistently outperforms SFT on all OOD generalization metrics: on rule-based OOD, RL gains +3.0% to +11.0% over the shared initialization while SFT loses 5.6% to 79.5%; on visual OOD, RL gains +17.6% to +61.1% while SFT loses 5.6% to 9.9%. Against the external baseline of the previous V-IRL SOTA (Yang et al., 2024a, which uses a two-stage VLM-LLM pipeline with GPT-4 and tailored prompt engineering), this paper's end-to-end RL approach on an open-source model (Llama-3.2-Vision-11B) achieves 77.8% vs. 44.0% on the VLN mini benchmark, a +33.8% absolute improvement. The paper also shows that SFT is still necessary as a prerequisite for RL: without SFT initialization, all end-to-end RL runs fail due to the base model's poor instruction-following capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RL4VLM framework (Zhai et al., 2024) for multi-turn RL with vision-language models and the sequential revision formulation (Snell et al., 2024) for test-time compute scaling, adapting both into a unified post-training pipeline. It departs from prior work that has studied SFT or RL in isolation, or only in unimodal (LLM) or single-modality (VLM) settings, by systematically comparing their generalization versus memorization behavior across both textual rule variants and visual distribution shifts in a controlled, multi-task evaluation. The introduction of GeneralPoints as a purpose-built arithmetic reasoning environment with configurable rule and visual variants extends the Points24 setup specifically to isolate generalization from memorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clear separation between SFT's memorization and RL's generalization provides actionable guidance for practitioners structuring post-training pipelines: SFT should be used minimally (for format stabilization) rather than scaled aggressively, since additional SFT compute actively degrades OOD performance. The finding that RL improves underlying visual recognition as a byproduct of outcome-based reward training opens a new avenue for enhancing VLM perception without dedicated perception data. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond Llama-3.2-Vision-11B and the two studied tasks, the precise conditions under which SFT initialization helps versus hurts RL (the paper shows RL cannot recover from severely overfitted checkpoints), and whether the SFT-memorization phenomenon persists at larger model scales or with different SFT data distributions.
