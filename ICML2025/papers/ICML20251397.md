## Problem

Standard LoRA initializes one of its low-rank matrices (A or B) to zero to ensure fine-tuning starts from the pretrained model, yet no theoretical justification exists for this practice. The paper asks whether zero initialization is actually optimal and whether fine-tuning must strictly begin from the pretrained model, or whether the noise introduced by non-zero initialization can be absorbed during training without performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251397/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained model (T5-Base or Llama 3-8B), a downstream fine-tuning dataset, and LoRA hyperparameters (rank r=16, scaling factor α=16). The key design choice is the initialization variance, parameterized as (βσ_k)² where σ_k²=1/n is the Kaiming variance and β∈{1,2,4,8,16} controls the initialization size; for non-zero initialization, both A and B share this variance. The output is the fine-tuned LoRA weights (A, B) and downstream task accuracy, with the option to either subtract the initial AB product from W (Init[AB]) or leave W unmodified (Init[AB+]).

## Method

The paper derives scaling conditions for LoRA fine-tuning dynamics using an infinite-width γ-operator framework, showing that stability and efficiency require γ[η_A]+γ[η_B]=-1 with initialization constraints γ[A_0]≤γ[η_A] and γ[B_0]≤γ[η_B]. The central insight is that non-zero initialization (Init[AB], where both A and B are Θ(n^{-1/2})) makes the update terms δ₁ and δ₂ depend linearly rather than quadratically on the learning rate, granting robustness to suboptimal (particularly smaller) learning rates that are common during fine-tuning with learning rate decay. The paper further proves that the random noise AB introduces into the pretrained weights does not degrade fine-tuning accuracy when the initialization variance is within a broad range encompassing Kaiming initialization, eliminating the need to subtract AB from W. Experiments on GLUE (T5-Base) and commonsense/arithmetic reasoning (Llama 3-8B) confirm up to 10% accuracy gains at small learning rates and roughly 2× faster convergence compared to standard zero initialization.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (T5-Base), non-zero initialization (Init[AB]) achieves up to 10% accuracy improvement over standard zero initialization (Init[A]) at smaller learning rates, and still yields a 1% gain even at near-optimal learning rates. On QNLI with learning rate 3e-7, Init[AB] accelerates convergence by nearly a factor of two. On Llama 3-8B commonsense and arithmetic reasoning benchmarks, Init[AB] consistently outperforms Init[A] with notably larger margins than on GLUE, reflecting the higher sensitivity of these tasks to learning rate robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is standard LoRA with zero initialization (Init[A], where A uses Kaiming initialization and B is set to zero), as proposed by Hu et al. (2022). Init[AB] (both A and B initialized to non-zero values with equal variance) wins across all evaluated settings: on GLUE (T5-Base), it achieves up to 10% higher accuracy at small learning rates and ~2× faster convergence on QNLI; on Llama 3-8B commonsense/arithmetic reasoning, the improvement is even larger. The advantage is most pronounced at smaller learning rates (e.g., 1e-7 to 3e-7), which are common in practice due to learning rate decay schedules. At near-optimal learning rates, Init[AB] still provides a consistent ~1% accuracy gain. The paper also shows that the non-zero initialization noise does not degrade performance when the initialization variance is within a broad range (including Kaiming's default), meaning fine-tuning need not strictly start from the pretrained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width scaling analysis of LoRA fine-tuning dynamics established by Hayou et al. (2024a,b), which characterized stability and efficiency conditions for zero-initialized LoRA (Init[A] vs. Init[B]). The key departure is showing that non-zero initialization (Init[AB]) satisfies all three desiderata—stability, efficiency, and robustness to suboptimal learning rates—whereas zero initialization satisfies only the first two. The work also provides the first theoretical justification for a family of empirically-motivated non-zero initialization methods (LoftQ, PISSA, LoRA-GA) that had previously lacked a principled explanation for their effectiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LoRA need not start exactly from the pretrained model challenges a widely-held design assumption and offers a zero-cost practical improvement, particularly at smaller learning rates that become prevalent during learning-rate decay schedules. The broad range of suitable initialization variances (encompassing Kaiming) makes the recommendation easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the infinite-width, single-data-point analysis transfers faithfully to multi-layer, multi-sample settings at practical scales, and how non-zero initialization interacts with more complex PEFT variants (e.g., multi-LoRA serving, quantized fine-tuning) where the 'starting point' constraint is more rigid.
