## Problem

Optimizing diffusion models for downstream tasks—such as tuning the entropy regularization strength during reward fine-tuning or learning the noise schedule during training—naturally gives rise to bilevel optimization problems. However, conventional bilevel methods are inapplicable because the lower-level variable is a distribution in an infinite-dimensional probability space, gradients over distributions are inaccessible, and generating a single sample requires hundreds of neural network evaluations. This paper is the first to formalize and solve bilevel hyperparameter optimization specifically for diffusion models.

## Input / Output

For the fine-tuning application, the method takes a text prompt, a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1.5 score network, and two differentiable reward functions (e.g., CLIP score as upper-level, ResNet-18 or HPSv2 as lower-level), and produces generated images along with the optimized entropy strength λ. For the noise scheduling application, it takes MNIST training images and a U-Net architecture, and outputs a learned 4-parameter noise schedule (q_s, q_e, q_τ, q_ε) plus the trained score network. The fine-tuning procedure is entirely inference-only—no gradients flow through the pre-trained model—while the noise scheduling procedure trains the U-Net from scratch with 10⁶+ parameters.

## Method

The framework reformulates the generative bilevel problem as a single-level penalty problem and designs diffusion-specific first-order gradient estimators for two scenarios. For fine-tuning with a pre-trained model, the lower-level is solved via guided backward sampling (Algorithm 5) with reward-specific guidance terms, and the upper-level gradient ∇L*_γ(λ) is computed in closed form via KL divergence identities (Proposition 1), estimated by Monte Carlo sampling from the pre-trained distribution—eliminating the need for expensive guided sampling at each λ update. For noise scheduling without a pre-trained model, the lower-level optimizes the score network via standard score matching, the penalty problem uses zeroth-order (ZO) gradient estimation to avoid backpropagating through 50–100 backward sampling steps, and the upper-level scheduler is optimized by ZO perturbation on just 4 scalar parameters of a cosine or sigmoid schedule. A descent theorem (Theorem 1) guarantees convergence under standard bilevel assumptions, with the penalty constant γ = O(ε^{-0.5}) controlling approximation error.

## Results

For reward fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion V1.5, the bilevel method achieves FID 102.82 and CLIP score 39.54, outperforming the best baseline (weighted sum: FID 116.52, CLIP 36.50) by 11.76% in FID and 8.32% in CLIP, while matching its runtime (~3.35h vs. 4.31h). For noise scheduling on MNIST with cosine parameterization, the bilevel method achieves FID 65.41 and IS 1.78 in 3.88h, compared to 67.30/1.76 in 31.53h for grid search and 105.27/1.43 in 1.59h for default DDIM, making it ~6× faster than HPO baselines while matching or exceeding their quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The bilevel method consistently outperforms Bayesian optimization (Snoek et al., 2012) across both applications: in fine-tuning, it achieves FID 102.82 vs. 140.35 and CLIP 39.54 vs. 33.29, while also being ~4× faster (3.35h vs. 12.7h); in noise scheduling, it reaches FID 65.41 vs. 67.16 (cosine) in 3.88h vs. 26.85h. Against default DDIM (Song et al., 2021a), the bilevel noise scheduling method dramatically improves FID from 105.27 to 65.41 (cosine) and IS from 1.43 to 1.78, at a modest 2.4× runtime increase (3.88h vs. 1.59h). The bilevel approach also beats the weighted-sum baseline (which naively combines CLIP and reward with a grid-searched weight) on both FID and CLIP in fine-tuning, and outperforms greedy grid search in noise scheduling while being ~8× faster. Notably, the bilevel method's runtime is comparable to grid/random search because its upper-level gradient uses a closed-form Monte Carlo estimator over pre-trained samples, avoiding expensive guided backward sampling at each iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: first-order bilevel optimization (building on the penalty reformulation of Kwon et al., 2024 and equilibrium backpropagation) and diffusion model fine-tuning via guided sampling (Guo et al., 2024; Uehara et al., 2024). It departs from prior bilevel work by operating in infinite-dimensional probability space where the lower-level variable is a distribution rather than a finite-dimensional parameter vector, and from prior diffusion fine-tuning work by avoiding backpropagation through the pre-trained model entirely. The closed-form KL divergence gradient (Proposition 1) is derived from Tang (2024), and the zeroth-order estimation for noise scheduling draws on classical gradient-free optimization (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017), but the combination into a unified generative bilevel framework is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of bilevel hyperparameter optimization for diffusion models, providing both theoretical convergence guarantees and a practical inference-only algorithm that avoids the memory and compute costs of backpropagating through pre-trained diffusion models. The approach could be broadly applicable to any diffusion model hyperparameter that has a nested optimization structure (e.g., guidance scales, classifier-free guidance weights, timestep selection). However, the empirical validation is limited: noise scheduling is tested only on MNIST with a small U-Net, and fine-tuning uses the older Stable Diffusion V1.5 with relatively simple prompts. Open questions include whether the approach scales to modern large-scale diffusion models (SDXL, Flux), whether the bilevel formulation generalizes beyond the two specific applications studied, and how it compares to more recent RL-based or direct-optimization fine-tuning methods at scale.
