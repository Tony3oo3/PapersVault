## Problem

Diffusion models for PROTAC linker design are typically pre-trained on small molecule datasets (e.g., ZINC), but PROTACs occupy a distinctly different region of chemical space with substantially higher molecular weights and different physicochemical profiles. Direct fine-tuning on the small PROTAC-DB dataset (~3K samples) leads to overfitting and poor generalization, leaving a critical gap in how to efficiently adapt pre-trained generative models to the PROTAC domain without full retraining.

## Input / Output

The model takes two molecular fragments, each represented as a 3D graph with one-hot atom type vectors, one-hot bond type vectors, and 3D Cartesian coordinates, and generates a PROTAC linker as a 3D graph with the same representation (atom coordinates, atom types, bond types). The linker atom count is pre-specified, and the edge set of the linker includes both intra-linker bonds and the bonds connecting to the two fragments. The EGNN denoiser processes the full PROTAC graph (both fragments plus the noisy linker) jointly, with SE(3)-equivariant coordinate updates restricted to linker atoms via a binary mask.

## Method

DAD-PROTAC pre-trains a joint diffusion model on ZINC using Gaussian diffusion for continuous atom coordinates and multinomial diffusion for discrete atom/bond types, with an E(n)-Equivariant GNN (EGNN) as the denoiser. The key fine-tuning insight (Theorem 2.1) decomposes the optimal PROTAC-domain score estimator into the frozen pre-trained score plus a lightweight score correction term involving the log-density ratio between PROTAC and small molecule distributions in chemical space. Rather than retraining the full model, a binary classifier W is first trained to estimate the density ratio at each diffusion timestep, and a separate small network ψ is then trained to approximate the score correction via a regression loss against the classifier-derived density ratio. This two-stage procedure (density ratio estimation → score correction learning) avoids full fine-tuning, reduces computational cost by 1.35×–2.8×, and provides theoretical grounding for the domain adaptation.

## Results

On the PROTAC-DB test set (327 PROTAC samples), DAD-PROTAC achieves the highest validity (94.8%), recovery rate (45.7%), QED (0.74), lowest SA score (1.63), lowest Emin (92.4), and lowest RMSD (1.43) among all methods. The closest competitor, LinkerNet-Fine-tuning, reaches 82.9% validity and 32.8% recovery. DAD-PROTAC also requires the least fine-tuning time, with competing methods costing 1.35× to 2.8× more computational resources while achieving lower validity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAD-PROTAC outperforms the strongest baseline, LinkerNet-Fine-tuning, on validity (94.8% vs. 82.9%), recovery rate (45.7% vs. 32.8%), QED (0.74 vs. 0.69), SA (1.63 vs. 1.97), Emin (92.4 vs. 110.7), and RMSD (1.43 vs. 1.52). However, DiffLinker-Fine-tuning substantially outperforms DAD-PROTAC on uniqueness (98.8% vs. 69.3%) and novelty (99.3% vs. 71.5%), though it generates many chemically invalid structures (64.0% validity vs. 94.8%). 3DLinker-Fine-tuning is the weakest fine-tuned baseline across nearly all metrics, with only 58.6% validity and 24.3% recovery. All fine-tuned variants consistently outperform their from-scratch counterparts, confirming the benefit of pre-training on ZINC. In terms of efficiency, DAD-PROTAC's fine-tuning phase requires 1.35× to 2.8× less time than direct fine-tuning of the baselines while maintaining the highest validity score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the density-ratio-based score decomposition from transfer learning for diffusion models (Ouyang et al., NeurIPS 2024; Kim et al., ICLR 2024) to the specific problem of PROTAC linker design, where a distribution mismatch exists between the small-molecule pre-training domain (ZINC) and the target PROTAC domain (PROTAC-DB). The generative backbone follows the E(3)-equivariant diffusion framework of Hoogeboom et al. (ICML 2022) with concrete score matching (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2022) for discrete atom/bond types. Unlike prior PROTAC linker methods (3DLinker, DiffLinker, LinkerNet) that either train from scratch or perform naive full fine-tuning, DAD-PROTAC introduces a theoretically grounded lightweight correction term that avoids retraining the entire EGNN denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density-ratio decomposition provides a principled, computationally efficient alternative to full fine-tuning for domain-shifted molecular generation, with the ablation confirming that the density ratio term is critical (removing it drops validity from 94.8% to 81.9%). However, the approach assumes a fixed number of linker atoms and does not incorporate protein context or fragment rotation, limiting direct applicability to real-world PROTAC design pipelines. Open questions include whether this density-ratio fine-tuning strategy generalizes to other molecular domain shifts (e.g., peptide-to-small-molecule, or different E3 ligase families) and whether it can be extended to variable-length linker generation.
