## Problem

In decentralized learning systems, removing a specific client's influence is fundamentally harder than in federated settings: indirect communication paths make impact tracing difficult, dynamic topologies render retraining-based unlearning infeasible (since departed clients are unreachable), and no unified theoretical metric exists to quantify whether local unlearning operations collectively achieve the desired global forgetting effect. Existing decentralized unlearning methods (HDUS, BlockFUL) provide practical solutions but lack rigorous theoretical guarantees on unlearning effectiveness and subsequent convergence.

## Input / Output

Each client consumes its own and its neighbors' historical stochastic gradients (stored during training rounds 0 to t₁−1), the time-varying doubly stochastic communication matrices, and a single unlearning request identifying the target client. The output is a per-client modified parameter vector θ_i^u = θ̄_i^{t₁} + z_i, where z_i ~ N(0, (n−1)σ²I_d) is Gaussian noise calibrated via the Gaussian mechanism. The guarantee is (ε,β)-machine unlearning from a global perspective: the average of all remaining clients' unlearned models is statistically indistinguishable from the average of perturbed-retrained models, with no additional communication required at unlearning time.

## Method

PDUDT eliminates a target client's influence by having each remaining client locally compute a sequence of gradient residual approximations δ_i^t = ηΣⱼ W̃_ij^t ∇F_j(θ_j^t, ξ_j^t) − ηΣⱼ W_ij^t ∇F_j(θ_j^t, ξ_j^t), which approximate the difference between retraining and original training updates using only stored historical gradients. These approximations are weighted by p_i^t (the normalized squared norm of the neighbor-aggregated gradient in round t, measuring each round's contribution) and subtracted from the current model: θ̄_i^{t₁} = θ_i^{t₁} − Σ_t p_i^t δ_i^t. A Gaussian perturbation is then added to mask the residual gap between the unlearned and perturbed-retrained models, yielding (ε,β)-indistinguishability via the Gaussian mechanism. The key insight is that the weighted gradient residual subtraction, combined with noise, provably replicates the statistical behavior of full retraining without any retraining or extra communication. The paper further proves that subsequent decentralized learning from the unlearned models converges at rate O(1/T), matching state-of-the-art decentralized SGD rates, and introduces an early-stopping variant (PDUDT-ES) that stores only the first t_{1,i} ≤ t₁ rounds of gradients to reduce memory from O(t₁ N_max d) to O(t_{1,i} N_max d).

## Results

PDUDT reduces unlearning time by over 99% compared to full retraining (e.g., 3.0 s vs. 1322.2 s on MNIST, 9.6 s vs. 1696.2 s on CIFAR-10) while achieving membership-inference-attack (MIA) precision at the random-guessing level (~50%) across all four datasets. On MNIST, PDUDT attains 49.9% ± 0.6 MIA precision, matching Retrain (49.4% ± 1.3) and outperforming FATS-Unl (51.3% ± 1.2) and HDUS (50.2% ± 1.1). The early-stopping variant PDUDT(ES) further cuts unlearning time to 2.3 s on MNIST with only a marginal increase in MIA precision (50.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Retrain (the gold-standard exact unlearning), PDUDT matches or slightly beats MIA precision on every dataset (e.g., 49.9% vs. 49.4% on MNIST, 49.0% vs. 49.2% on Fashion-MNIST) while being roughly 440× faster (3.0 s vs. 1322.2 s on MNIST). Compared to FATS-Unl, PDUDT achieves lower MIA precision on MNIST (49.9% vs. 51.3%) and CIFAR-10 (50.1% vs. 53.1%) and is ~190× faster. Against FedRecovery, PDUDT is essentially tied on MIA precision (49.9% vs. 50.1% on MNIST) and marginally faster (3.0 s vs. 3.5 s). Versus HDUS, PDUDT is slightly better on MIA (49.9% vs. 50.2% on MNIST) and ~6× faster (3.0 s vs. 19.3 s). Overall, PDUDT is the only method that simultaneously achieves serverless operation, dynamic-topology support, a formal (ε,β)-machine-unlearning guarantee, O(1/T) post-unlearning convergence, and near-zero communication overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated unlearning line (FedEraser, FedRecovery) to a fully decentralized, serverless setting with dynamic topologies, departing from prior work that either requires a central server for coordination or relies on expensive retraining (FATS-Unl, FedRemover). Conceptually, it builds on the gradient-residual subtraction idea from FedRecovery but generalizes it to handle indirect neighbor-to-neighbor influence chains in a decentralized graph, using a weighted approximation of the retraining gap rather than exact backtracking. The theoretical machinery draws on the Gaussian mechanism from differential privacy to convert the bounded model gap into a formal (ε,β)-machine unlearning guarantee, and on spectral-gap convergence analysis from decentralized SGD (Lian et al., 2017) to characterize post-unlearning learning dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a rigorous (ε,β)-machine unlearning guarantee for a decentralized system with arbitrary dynamic topologies, filling a notable gap where prior decentralized unlearning methods (HDUS, BlockFUL) offered only empirical or heuristic solutions. The 99% unlearning-time reduction versus retraining is practically significant for settings where clients join and leave freely (IoT, mobile networks). Open questions include: whether the doubly-stochastic symmetric-matrix assumption holds in real peer-to-peer networks, how the space overhead O(t₁·N_max·d) scales for long-running systems with many neighbors, and whether the Gaussian noise calibration remains practical (i.e., does not degrade model utility) at larger privacy budgets ε. The early-stopping variant partially addresses memory but introduces a small accuracy trade-off.
