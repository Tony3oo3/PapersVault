## Problem

Density-based distances (Fermat distances) offer a principled Riemannian metric for metric learning where geodesics follow high-density data regions, but existing graph-based estimators using nearest-neighbor density approximations converge impractically slowly in practice despite theoretical guarantees. Moreover, graph-based methods degrade sharply in high dimensions as paths become jagged, and no prior work had quantitatively compared these methods against ground truth geodesics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of data samples from an unknown distribution and two query points in the same space, and produces a continuous geodesic path between them along with a scalar Fermat distance value. The geodesic is computed in the original data space under a conformal metric g(x) = ⟨u,v⟩/p(x)^{2β}, where β is an inverse temperature parameter (set to 1/D for dimension scaling). All distance calculations are performed in log space to handle the large dynamic range of density values.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage pipeline: first, a normalizing flow (spline coupling blocks via FrEIA) is trained on the data to provide accurate density estimates for weighting k-nearest-neighbor graph edges via numerical integration along each edge (Algorithm 2); second, a relaxation scheme (Algorithm 1) solves a reparameterized geodesic ODE with constant Euclidean speed, guided by a score function learned via sliced score matching, to smooth the jagged graph-based path into an accurate geodesic. The key insight is that density estimation—not the graph structure—is the convergence bottleneck, and that directly learning the score via score matching yields smoother gradients than differentiating a normalizing flow, enabling stable relaxation even in high dimensions. A dimension-scaled metric (β=1/D) is also introduced to prevent geodesics from making extreme detours toward the origin as ambient dimension grows.

## Results

The paper evaluates methods using the Log Path Ratio (LPR) metric, where 0 indicates the true geodesic. On 2D datasets with 200,000 samples, the power-weighted graph method (Bijral et al.) shows LPR values that decrease extremely slowly with sample size, while the proposed density-weighted graph (using normalizing flows) converges at nearly the same rate as using ground truth densities. In the dimension scaling experiment (up to 25D, n=200,000), all graph-based methods show rapidly degrading LPR as dimension increases, whereas score-based relaxation maintains low LPR across all dimensions. On MNIST (10D autoencoder latent space), intra-class log distances are consistently lower than inter-class distances for all 10 digits, with the largest separation for digit 1 (2.04 ± 0.58) and smallest for digit 8 (0.46 ± 0.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the power-weighted shortest path (PWSPD) method of Bijral et al. (2011), which constructs k-nearest neighbor graphs with edge weights proportional to ‖x₁−x₂‖^(βd+1). The paper demonstrates that this method converges at a 'far inferior rate' compared to both the proposed density-weighted graph and ground-truth density-weighted graph across all five 2D test distributions (Figure 6). The proposed normalizing-flow density-weighted graph matches the convergence rate of the ground-truth density method, effectively closing the gap between theory and practice. In the high-dimensional scaling experiment (Figure 7), all graph-based methods (power-weighted, model-density-weighted, ground-truth-density-weighted) show rapidly degrading LPR as dimension increases from 2 to 25, while the proposed score-based relaxation method maintains consistently low LPR, effectively avoiding the curse of dimensionality. The paper also shows that differentiating a normalizing flow to obtain scores produces noisier gradients than dedicated sliced score matching, making the latter preferable for the relaxation step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fermat distance framework established by Bousquet et al. (2003), Bijral et al. (2011), and Groisman et al. (2022), which defines conformal Riemannian metrics inversely proportional to data density. It departs from prior work in two key ways: first, it is the first to compute ground truth geodesics via a numerically stable relaxation method, revealing that the power-weighted shortest path method's theoretical convergence guarantees (Hwang et al., 2016) translate to impractically slow empirical convergence; second, it replaces nearest-neighbor density estimates with normalizing flows for graph-based methods and with sliced score matching (Song et al., 2020) for a relaxation-based approach that overcomes the curse of dimensionality. The dimension-scaled metric (β=1/D) is a novel practical contribution not present in prior Fermat distance formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a significant gap between the theoretical elegance of density-based distances and their practical usability, particularly in high-dimensional spaces where graph-based methods fail due to the curse of dimensionality. The identification of density estimation (not graph structure) as the convergence bottleneck, and the demonstration that score matching outperforms differentiating normalizing flows for geodesic relaxation, provides actionable guidance for practitioners. However, the methods are validated only on toy 2D distributions and a 10-dimensional MNIST latent space; extending to complex real-world high-dimensional data, unifying the flow and score model components, and providing theoretical explanations for the observed convergence behavior remain open challenges.
