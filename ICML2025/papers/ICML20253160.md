## Problem

Standard RLHF formulates alignment as a contextual bandit where the entire response is a single action receiving a sentence-level reward, yet PPO is designed for multi-step MDPs requiring per-token reward signals. This mismatch forces open-source PPO implementations to assign the learned reward only to the final token, leaving all intermediate tokens with zero learned reward and making training sparse, unstable, and sample-inefficient. The paper asks whether reformulating RLHF as a token-wise MDP and supplying dense per-token rewards can resolve this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline preference dataset of (prompt, preferred response, rejected response) triples (UltraFeedback), a reference SFT model (Llama-3-8B), and a DPO-trained policy that serves as a proxy for the optimal policy. It outputs an aligned language model policy that generates text responses. A small 1B sentence-level reward model (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) is additionally trained to provide a scalar reward at the final token, preventing response-length degeneration without dominating the token-wise DPO signal.

## Method

RTO reformulates RLHF as a KL-regularized MDP where each token is a step with its own reward, proving that token-wise rewards reduce sample complexity from A^H (sentence-level) to A^{min{ξ+1,H}} (token-level). The practical algorithm has two stages: first, DPO is trained on the preference data to obtain π_dpo; second, PPO is run with a dense per-token reward r_rto = β₁·log(π_dpo(y_h|x,y_{1:h-1})/π_ref(y_h|x,y_{1:h-1})) − β₂·log(π(y_h|x,y_{1:h-1})/π_ref(y_h|x,y_{1:h-1})), plus a small sentence-level reward β₃·r_MLE at the final token. The key insight is that under the MDP formulation, DPO's implicit reward β·log(π*/π_ref) naturally decomposes into a sum of per-token terms, so the DPO-trained policy directly provides the dense token-wise reward signal that PPO needs—no separate token-level reward model is required. Ablations confirm the DPO reward acts primarily as reward shaping (its total sum equals r_MLE) rather than altering the overall reward magnitude.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2, RTO achieves a length-controlled win rate (LC) of 27.00% and a standard win rate (WR) of 22.45%, outperforming PPO (19.47% LC, 12.89% WR) by 7.5 and 9.6 points respectively. On Arena-Hard, RTO scores 20.3% style-controlled (SC) and 21.4% WR, exceeding PPO (16.2% SC, 15.6% WR) by 4.1 and 5.8 points. RTO also surpasses the strongest direct preference learning baseline SimPO (25.46% LC, 14.5% SC) by 1.5 and 5.8 points on the two benchmarks. Additionally, RTO matches PPO-level performance using only 1/8 of the training data and continues to improve with more data, whereas PPO saturates early.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTO wins against all six baselines across all four benchmark metrics. Against standard PPO, RTO gains 7.53 points on AlpacaEval 2 LC and 4.1 points on Arena-Hard SC, demonstrating the benefit of dense token-wise rewards over sparse sentence-level rewards. Against the strongest direct preference learning baseline SimPO, RTO leads by 1.54 points on AE(LC) and a substantial 5.8 points on AH(SC), indicating that combining DPO-derived token rewards with PPO training is more effective than modifying the DPO objective alone. R-DPO and TDPO, which attempt to fix length bias or reformulate DPO at the token level, trail RTO by 8.7 and 6.9 points on AE(LC) respectively. The SFT baseline (the shared initialization) is outperformed by all alignment methods, confirming that the gains are attributable to the alignment procedure rather than the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes RLHF from the standard sentence-level bandit formulation to a token-wise MDP, building on the KL-regularized MDP framework from offline RL theory (Jin et al., 2021; Zhan et al., 2023a) and the closed-form optimal policy structure of DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is showing that DPO's implicit reward, originally derived under the bandit setting, naturally decomposes into a token-wise reward function under the MDP formulation, enabling it to serve as a dense reward signal for PPO. This distinguishes RTO from prior dense-reward approaches (Chan et al., 2024, which redistributes sentence rewards via attention) and from direct preference learning methods (DPO, SimPO, TDPO) that skip RL entirely, as well as from theoretical RLHF work (Xiong et al., 2023; Azar et al., 2023) that remains confined to the bandit setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sample complexity separation between token-wise and sentence-wise rewards (Proposition 3.2) provides a principled theoretical justification for why dense token-level signals should outperform sparse sentence-level ones in LLM alignment, and the practical RTO pipeline (DPO for reward extraction + PPO for optimization) is straightforward to implement on top of existing RLHF infrastructure. The ablation revealing that DPO's primary contribution is as a reward-shaping mechanism rather than a reward-model replacement is a useful practical insight for practitioners tuning RLHF pipelines. Open questions include whether the token-wise reward extraction generalizes beyond DPO (e.g., to other preference learning objectives), how the method scales to larger models and more diverse alignment tasks, and whether the theoretical guarantees extend to stochastic transition settings common in agentic or tool-use scenarios.
