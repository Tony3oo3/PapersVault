## Problem

Existing perceptual image compression methods employ diverse and inconsistent optimization objectives (KL divergence, adversarial loss, LPIPS, DISTS) without a unified theoretical explanation for why distribution-distance measures appear in the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff. This paper addresses that gap by grounding perceptual compression in synonymity-based semantic information theory, where perceptually similar images form a 'synset' sharing the same semantic meaning, and derives a principled variational inference framework that unifies and explains these empirical choices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251479/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images and produces a compressed bitstream encoding only the synonymous (semantic) portion of the latent representation, while the detailed portion is discarded and resampled at decode time. A single progressive model with 512 latent channels partitioned into 16 levels of 32 channels each supports 16 distinct coding rates, and at each rate the decoder can generate multiple reconstructed images (a reconstructed synset) that share perceptual similarity with the original rather than reproducing it pixel-for-pixel.

## Method

The core contribution is Synonymous Variational Inference (SVI), which replaces the standard KL divergence in VAE-style variational inference with a partial semantic KL divergence that operates across two levels: a syntactic-level parametric density q(ỹ|x) and a semantic-level posterior over an ideal synset. This yields a new compression scheme, Synonymous Image Compression (SIC), where the encoder transmits only the synonymous representation ŷ_s (equivalent to a quantized latent synset) and the decoder generates multiple perceptually similar reconstructions by sampling diverse detailed representations ŷ_ε,j. The architecture uses Swin Transformers for analysis and synthesis transforms with a joint autoregressive and hierarchical prior for entropy modeling, and a progressive training strategy alternates across 16 synonymous levels using a loss combining rate, MSE distortion, and LPIPS (as a practical proxy for the theoretical KL-divergence perception term). The paper proves that minimizing the partial semantic KL divergence is equivalent to optimizing a synonymous rate-distortion-perception tradeoff that subsumes the existing RDP formulation as the special case of a single-sample reconstructed synset.

## Results

The paper presents rate-distortion-perception curves (Figures 4–6) rather than single-point metric tables. A single progressive SIC model (C=512, L=16 levels, 32 channels per level) achieves full-rate perceptual quality adaptability across 16 coding rates on CLIC2020, DIV2K, and Kodak. On DISTS, the method surpasses No-GAN MS-ILLM over a large bitrate range while PSNR continuously approaches and even exceeds the No-GAN baselines, with LPIPS remaining very similar. After GAN fine-tuning (2×10⁵ steps), DISTS and FID improve notably at higher bitrates, with DISTS gradually approaching MS-ILLM (with GAN), though PSNR degrades, confirming the distortion-perception tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BPG and VTM (traditional compression benchmarks for distortion), the paper's PSNR quality is competitive, approaching or exceeding the No-GAN learned baselines across the rate range. Against HiFiC (Mentzer et al., 2020), the proposed SIC method shows a modest DISTS advantage over the No-GAN variant but the gap to HiFiC's GAN-guided performance remains 'still obvious, especially on FID,' attributed to using LPIPS as a proxy for the KL divergence term rather than a full adversarial objective. Against MS-ILLM (Muckley et al., 2023), the base SIC model (M=1) surpasses No-GAN MS-ILLM on DISTS in a large coding rate range; with M=5 sampling, slight additional advantages appear at low and medium rates. After GAN fine-tuning, DISTS gradually approaches MS-ILLM (with GAN) at higher bitrates, but FID improvement remains limited compared to the DISTS gains, suggesting the detailed sampling mechanism is insufficient for full distributional fidelity. Overall, the method trades some FID and PSNR for competitive DISTS while offering the unique capability of a single progressive codec covering 16 rate points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-frames perceptual image compression through the synonymity-based semantic information theory of Niu & Zhang (2024), introducing Synonymous Variational Inference (SVI) that replaces the standard KL divergence in VAE-style variational inference with a partial semantic KL divergence over latent synsets. It explicitly generalizes the rate-distortion-perception (RDP) tradeoff of Blau & Michaeli (2019) by showing the single-reconstruction RDP is a special case of a 'synonymous RDP' where the decoder generates multiple perceptually similar reconstructions from one encoding. Architecturally, the progressive SIC codec extends the LIC framework of Ballé et al. (2017) by encoding only the synonymous representation while sampling detailed representations, and uses a Swin Transformer backbone with an autoregressive/hierarchical prior for rate estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why distribution-based perceptual measures (KL divergence, LPIPS as proxy) empirically appear in PIC optimization objectives, grounding them in semantic information theory rather than treating them as ad hoc choices. This unification offers a principled design guideline for future PIC methods, suggesting that discriminative (GAN-based) losses should replace the E-KLD term within the synset framework. However, the empirical validation is modest: the progressive SIC codec achieves comparable but not clearly superior RDP performance to HiFiC and MS-ILLM, with a notable gap on FID, and the equal-channel-slicing partitioning strategy is acknowledged as suboptimal. Open questions include better synonymous-level partitioning, integrating adversarial losses within the synset framework, and improving distributional fidelity of the sampled reconstructions.
