## Problem

While SGD is known to avoid saddle points and converge to local minimizers in non-convex landscapes, no prior work provides tight, quantifiable estimates for the time it takes to reach a *global* minimum. Existing guarantees are at best local (criticality bounds, second-order optimality), and the global convergence time depends on non-local landscape features—spurious minima, basin geometry, noise statistics—that had not been characterized with matching upper and lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a C²-smooth coercive loss function with a finite critical set, a stochastic first-order oracle with sub-Gaussian noise (unbiased, positive covariance, smooth growth), a constant step size η, and an initialization point x. It produces matching exponential bounds on E_x[τ] of the form exp((E(Q|x)±ε)/η), where the energy E(Q|x) is computed from a weighted directed transition graph on the critical components: edge weights are quasi-potentials (least-action costs) derived from the oracle's Lagrangian, and E(Q|x) is a min-max over transition forests and prunings that encodes the hardest chain of obstacles between x and arg min f.

## Method

The paper adapts Freidlin-Wentzell large deviations theory to discrete-time SGD via Kifer's subsampling: the accelerated process x_n^η = x_{n⌊1/η⌋} satisfies a trajectory-wise LDP with action functional S_T[γ] = ∫ L_G(γ(t), γ̇(t)) dt, where L_G is the convex conjugate of the oracle's cumulant-generating function. From this LDP, they define quasi-potentials B_ij between critical components as infimum actions over connecting curves, construct a weighted transition graph, and then compute the 'pruned energy' E(Q|x) by minimizing over transition forests toward the global minimum and subtracting the minimum cost of prunings that sever the path from x. The key insight is that the global convergence time is dominated not by the direct path to arg min f but by the most costly indirect route through spurious minima—captured by the max over all starting nodes p of [E(Q|p) − B(x,p)]_+—so that even a single deep spurious minimum can exponentially inflate the convergence time relative to the local escape time from the initialization's basin.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) provides matching upper and lower bounds on the global convergence time: exp((E(Q|x)−ε)/η) ≤ E_x[τ] ≤ exp((E(Q|x)+ε)/η), where E(Q|x) is a 'pruned energy' defined via the transition graph of critical points. In the three-hump camel validation (Fig. 2), the predicted exponent E(p₁|p₃) = 2(f(p₂)−f(p₅))/σ² matches the empirical log-scale slope with R² = 0.97 (theory) vs. R² = 0.99 (data fit), confirming the exponential-in-1/η scaling. The key structural finding is that the convergence time is dominated by the *most* costly obstacle (the deepest spurious minimum the process can fall into), not the shortest/cheapest path to the global minimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmarking; the 'competing approaches' are prior analytical frameworks rather than algorithms. The paper explicitly contrasts with (i) exit-time analyses [7, 25, 30, 33, 61, 86], which characterize the time to escape a *single* local minimum but cannot capture the global convergence time because SGD may be re-trapped in other spurious minima an arbitrary number of times; (ii) diffusion-approximation / DMFT approaches [44, 45, 59, 60, 77], which study the continuous-time SDE limit but whose approximation guarantees fail on the exponentially long time scales relevant here; and (iii) overparameterized-network convergence results [1, 19, 65, 68, 92], which require specific width/depth/initialization structure and do not apply to general non-convex landscapes. The paper's contribution is orthogonal to all three: it provides the first sharp (matching) exponential bounds on the *global* hitting time for general non-convex f under only smoothness and sub-Gaussian noise assumptions, with the exponent explicitly encoding the landscape's spurious-minimum geometry via the transition graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the large-deviations analysis of Azizian et al. (ICML 2024), which characterized the *stationary distribution* of SGD, to the fundamentally different question of *how long* SGD takes to reach the global minimum. The theoretical machinery is adapted from the continuous-time Freidlin-Wentzell theory of randomly perturbed dynamical systems and Kifer's discrete-time subsampling framework, which the authors bridge to the constant-step-size SGD setting. A key conceptual departure from prior exit-time literature (which characterizes escape from individual minima) is that the global convergence time is governed not by the shortest or least-costly transition path to the global minimum, but by the most costly chain of transitions the process may be forced through via spurious traps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first matching upper and lower bounds on the global convergence time of SGD in general non-convex landscapes, with the characteristic exponent E(Q|x) giving a concrete, computable measure of landscape hardness tied to the depth of spurious minima. For neural network training, this implies that subexponential convergence requires the absence (or logarithmic shallowness) of spurious minima, connecting geometric landscape properties directly to practical convergence times. Limitations include the constant step-size requirement, sub-Gaussian noise assumption, and the 'attracting strength' condition needed for the lower bound; open directions include extending the framework to adaptive methods (Adam), oscillatory schedules, and the interpolation regime where noise vanishes at the minimum.
