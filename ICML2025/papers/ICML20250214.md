## Problem

Preference-based RL in multi-agent settings has been largely limited to two-phase frameworks that first train a reward model from preference data and then optimize a policy separately, leading to misalignment between phases and requiring large preference datasets to cover the joint state-action space. No end-to-end method existed for cooperative MARL that directly recovers policies from offline pairwise trajectory preferences without explicit reward modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250214/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of pairwise trajectory preferences (σ₁ ≻ σ₂), where each trajectory is a sequence of joint (observation, action) tuples, and agents only access their own local observations oᵢ rather than the global state. Preferences are generated either rule-based (comparing trajectory quality from poor/medium/expert datasets) or via GPT-4o annotations of trajectory state summaries. The output is a set of decomposable local policies πᵢ(aᵢ|oᵢ) whose product equals the globally optimal joint policy, extracted via weighted behavior cloning rather than direct value-based policy formulas.

## Method

O-MAPL exploits the one-to-one mapping between reward functions and soft Q-functions in MaxEnt RL to reformulate preference learning entirely in Q-space, bypassing explicit reward recovery. A linear mixing network aggregates local Q and V functions into global Q_tot and V_tot, and the training alternates between maximizing a Bradley-Terry likelihood over preference pairs (with a χ² regularizer) and minimizing an Extreme-V loss that enforces the log-sum-exp consistency between V_tot and Q_tot. Local policies are then extracted via a weighted behavior cloning objective that uses the global Q−V gap as importance weights, guaranteeing global-local consistency (GLC) even under nonlinear mixing. The paper proves convexity of the preference loss under linear mixing (Prop. 4.1), non-convexity under two-layer networks (Prop. 4.2), and GLC of the WBC solution (Thm. 4.3), providing theoretical grounding for the stable single-phase training procedure.

## Results

On SMACv1 (rule-based preferences), O-MAPL achieves the highest win rates on 3 of 4 tasks: 74.4% on 2c_vs_64zg (vs. 71.1% for IPL-VDN), 19.3% on 5m_vs_6m, and 4.5% on 6h_vs_8z, while corridor is a near-tie with IPL-VDN (93.2% vs. 93.9%). On MaMujoco, O-MAPL achieves the best returns across all three tasks: 1114.4 on Hopper-v2 (vs. 890.0 for SL-MARL), 1406.4 on Ant-v2, and 4382.0 on HalfCheetah-v2. With LLM-generated preference data, O-MAPL further improves, reaching 79.5% on 2c_vs_64zg and 64.5% on protoss_20_vs_20 in SMACv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O-MAPL consistently outperforms all four baselines across both SMAC and MaMujoco benchmarks. Against IIPL (independent IPL, Hejna & Sadigh 2024), O-MAPL achieves substantially higher win rates on SMAC (e.g., 74.4% vs. 60.4% on 2c_vs_64zg) and returns on MaMujoco (e.g., 1114.4 vs. 782.0 on Hopper-v2), demonstrating the benefit of coordinated multi-agent value factorization over independent per-agent learning. Against SL-MARL (two-phase reward learning + OMIGA, Wang et al. 2022), O-MAPL shows the largest margins on harder tasks: 93.2% vs. 49.0% on corridor and 4.5% vs. 1.6% on 6h_vs_8z, highlighting the instability of the two-phase approach. Against IPL-VDN (IPL with fixed unit-weight VDN aggregation), O-MAPL wins on most tasks but is slightly behind on corridor (93.2% vs. 93.9%), suggesting the learned mixing network provides marginal gains over fixed linear sum. Against BC, O-MAPL improves by 10–25 percentage points on SMAC and 200–300 return units on MaMujoco, confirming that preference-based learning captures richer signal than simple imitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent Inverse Preference Learning (IPL) framework of Hejna & Sadigh (2024) to cooperative multi-agent settings, leveraging the MaxEnt RL reward-to-soft-Q-function mapping (Garg et al., 2021; Haarnoja et al., 2018) to bypass explicit reward modeling. Unlike prior multi-agent PbRL work (Kang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a) that relies on a two-phase pipeline of supervised reward learning followed by MARL policy optimization, O-MAPL unifies both into a single end-to-end objective in Q-space. The key technical departure is a carefully designed linear mixing network for value factorization that preserves convexity of the preference objective and enables a weighted behavior cloning (WBC) policy extraction with provable global-local consistency, building on Extreme Q-Learning (Garg et al., 2023) for efficient V-function updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the two-phase misalignment problem and providing convexity guarantees under linear mixing, O-MAPL offers a more stable and theoretically grounded path to multi-agent preference learning than existing approaches. The finding that LLM-annotated preference data (GPT-4o) consistently outperforms rule-based labels at modest cost (~$42 per task) opens a practical avenue for scalable preference data generation, though this is currently limited to environments with interpretable state features. Key open questions include extending the framework to mixed cooperative-competitive settings, improving sample efficiency when human feedback is the bottleneck, and whether the convexity-preserving linear mixing constraint limits expressiveness in more complex agent interactions.
