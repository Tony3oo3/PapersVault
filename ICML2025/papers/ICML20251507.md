## Problem

While LLMs perform well on general reasoning benchmarks, their ability to handle complex deductive and non-monotonic logical reasoning remains poorly characterized because existing evaluations conflate domain knowledge with pure logic and offer no fine-grained control over problem difficulty. This paper asks whether scaling model size, increasing the number of sampled candidates, or extending chain-of-thought reasoning tokens can overcome the performance collapse that occurs as logical puzzle complexity grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each puzzle specifies N houses (2–6), M attributes (2–6), and K natural-language clues (mean 10.4), presented in a one-shot in-context format with a single worked example. The model must return a JSON object with a free-text "reasoning" field and a "solution" field mapping every house to its attribute values; a puzzle counts as solved only if all cells are correct. Complexity is quantified along two complementary axes: the combinatorial search-space size (N!)^M (spanning ~10² to ~10¹⁵) and the mean number of Z3 SMT-solver conflicts, which proxies the depth of backtracking required.

## Method

ZebraLogic generates puzzles by sampling a random solution grid, enumerating all valid clue relationships across seven clue types (FOUNDAT, NOTAT, SAMEHOUSE, DIRECTLEFT/RIGHT, SIDEBYSIDE, LEFT/RIGHTOF, ONE/TWOBETWEEN), then iteratively removing clues—weighted toward simpler types—while a SAT solver confirms the remaining set still yields a unique solution, producing a minimal clue set. The resulting 1,000-puzzle benchmark spans 25 grid sizes (N×M, N,M ∈ {2,…,6}) grouped into four search-space tiers. The empirical study probes three scaling dimensions: model size (Llama-3.1 3B→405B, GPT-4o, o1, DeepSeek-R1, Claude), repeated sampling (Best-of-N with oracle/majority-voting/reward-model selection up to N=128), and test-time compute (hidden CoT token count and multi-turn self-verification prompting). The central finding is a "curse of complexity": accuracy drops sharply beyond ~10⁷ search space or ~20 Z3 conflicts regardless of model scale, while o1's roughly 10× more hidden reasoning tokens (scaling at ~400 tokens per conflict up to ~30 conflicts) partially but not fully mitigates the decline.

## Results

On ZebraLogic (1,000 logic grid puzzles across 25 grid sizes), o1 achieves the highest overall puzzle-level accuracy at 81.0%, followed by DeepSeek-R1 at 78.7% and o1-preview at 71.4%. The best non-reasoning LLM, Sonnet-3.5-1022, reaches only 36.2%, while the largest open-weight model Llama-3.1-405B achieves 32.6%. Performance collapses sharply with complexity: even o1-preview drops to 17.0% on X-Large puzzles (search space ≥10¹⁰), and most non-reasoning models fall below 5% on Large puzzles (search space ≥10⁶).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 10+ LLMs on ZebraLogic, with the central comparison being reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek-R1) versus non-reasoning models (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1, Sonnet). o1 (81.0%) outperforms DeepSeek-R1 (78.7%) overall, though R1 edges out o1 on Small and Medium puzzles while o1 leads on Large and X-Large. Against the strongest non-reasoning baseline, Sonnet-3.5-1022 (36.2%), o1 gains +44.8 points; against Llama-3.1-405B (32.6%), the gap is +48.4 points. Scaling model size within the Llama family (3B→8B→70B→405B) yields clear gains on Small/Medium puzzles but all sizes collapse to near-zero on X-Large, confirming that parameter scaling alone cannot break the curse of complexity. On the test-time compute axis, Best-of-N with oracle selection lifts GPT-4o from 31.7% to 69.1% (approaching o1-mini's 59.7%), but practical selection via majority voting only reaches 38.0% and reward-model selection (33.9%) underperforms even majority voting, indicating current reward models are poorly suited for logical reasoning verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on evaluating LLM logical reasoning (LogiQA, Richardson & Sabharwal 2022 on natural language satisfiability, Dziri et al. 2023 on grid-puzzle compositionality) by introducing a CSP-based framework with two quantifiable, independently controllable complexity metrics (search space size and Z3 conflict count). Unlike prior grid-puzzle evaluations that used fixed or loosely controlled difficulty, ZebraLogic enables systematic scaling studies across model size, sampling, and test-time compute dimensions. The paper also connects to the broader LLM scaling literature (Wei et al. 2022 on CoT, o1/R1 on test-time compute) by applying those scaling axes specifically to deductive reasoning, and it leverages the NP-completeness of the underlying problem (via reduction from Latin square completion) to ground its complexity analysis in computational theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'curse of complexity' finding—that accuracy collapses beyond ~10^7 search space or ~20 Z3 conflicts regardless of model size, and that even pass@128 cannot recover—provides a concrete, quantified boundary for current LLM reasoning that the field lacked. The observation that o1's hidden CoT tokens scale with complexity but plateau (suggesting an optimal token-to-conflict ratio that is not always reached) opens a specific research direction for RL-trained reasoning models. Key open questions include whether sufficiently long backtracking chains (beyond o1's current ~5K tokens) can break the plateau, whether the NP-completeness lower bound implies a fundamental architectural limit for autoregressive decoding, and whether neuro-symbolic hybrids (e.g., CLOVER-style) can complement LLM reasoning where pure scaling fails.
