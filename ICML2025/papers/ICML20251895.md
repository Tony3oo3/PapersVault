## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods have been widely adapted to single-cell genomics, but no systematic benchmark exists that compares generic SSL architectures (originally designed for images or text) against specialized single-cell frameworks across multiple downstream tasks, datasets, and hyperparameter configurations. This gap limits the community's ability to determine which methods, augmentations, and design choices are actually effective for single-cell data integration, cell typing, and multi-omics prediction.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes preprocessed cell-by-gene count matrices (scRNA-seq) or paired multi-modal matrices (RNA + protein via CITE-seq, or GEX + ADT), with 4,000 highly variable genes selected for single-modal and 2,000 for multi-modal datasets after SCANPY normalize-total (10,000) and log-transformation. Outputs are low-dimensional cell embeddings (default 64-dim) evaluated on three tasks: batch correction via scIB metrics (biological conservation, batch correction, and a weighted total), cell type annotation via KNN probing (MacroF1 and accuracy on held-out batches), and missing modality prediction via Pearson correlation between original and kNN-inferred protein values.

## Method

scSSL-Bench systematically evaluates nineteen SSL methods—seven generic (SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, NNCLR, BYOL, VICReg, BarlowTwins), four specialized contrastive (Concerto, CLEAR, CLAIRE, scCLIP), five generative/foundation models (scVI, totalVI, scGPT, Geneformer, scBERT), two multi-omics VAEs (scButterfly, scTEL), and two baselines (SCDC, PCA)—across nine datasets and three downstream tasks, all trained with five random seeds. The benchmark conducts ablations over augmentation strategies (random masking, Gaussian noise, InnerSwap, CrossOver, MNN, BBKNN), representation dimensionality (8–1024), projector scaling, and temperature, and tests design decisions borrowed from computer vision such as domain-specific batch normalization and retaining the projection head at inference. For multi-omics data, three integration strategies (element-wise addition, concatenation, and CLIP-style contrastive learning) are compared. Key findings include that specialized methods (scVI, CLAIRE, finetuned scGPT) dominate uni-modal batch correction while generic methods (VICReg, SimCLR) excel at multi-modal integration and cell typing, random masking outperforms all biology-specific augmentations, and concatenation is the most effective multi-modal combination strategy.

## Results

For uni-modal scRNA-seq batch correction, scVI achieves the highest total scores (PBMC: 0.899, Pancreas: 0.688, Immune Cell Atlas: 0.754), followed by CLAIRE (PBMC: 0.774, Pancreas: 0.732) and finetuned scGPT (ICA: 0.781). For multi-modal CITE-seq batch correction, generic methods dominate: SimCLR achieves the best BMMC total score (0.767) and PBMC-M total (0.700), with VICReg close behind (BMMC: 0.761, PBMC-M: 0.651). For cell type annotation on the Immune Cell Atlas hold-out, VICReg leads with MacroF1 0.820 and Accuracy 0.866, while finetuned Geneformer achieves the highest accuracy (0.878). For missing modality prediction, SimCLR and VICReg achieve the highest Pearson correlations (PBMC-M: 0.866 and 0.865; BMMC: 0.757 and 0.759), substantially outperforming specialized methods like scCLIP (0.614/0.175) and scTEL (0.022/0.047).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generic SSL methods, SimCLR and VICReg are the strongest overall: SimCLR leads multi-modal batch correction (BMMC Total 0.767 vs. scButterfly 0.587, totalVI 0.562) and missing modality prediction (PBMC-M Pearson 0.866), while VICReg dominates cell type annotation (ICA MacroF1 0.820 vs. SimCLR 0.788, MoCo 0.794) and also tops BMMC missing modality prediction (0.759). MoCo overcorrects batch effects across all datasets (e.g., PBMC Batch 0.594 but Bio only 0.336), and BYOL collapses on uni-modal data (PBMC Bio 0.134). Among specialized contrastive methods, CLAIRE achieves the best PBMC batch correction (Total 0.774) but overcorrects (Batch 0.866, Bio 0.714), while Concerto consistently underperforms even the PCA baseline (e.g., Pancreas Total 0.208 vs. PCA 0.516). For generative methods, scVI/totalVI is the clear winner on uni-modal batch correction (PBMC Total 0.899, Pancreas 0.688) but fails on multi-modal data (BMMC Total 0.562, nearly half of SimCLR's 0.767); finetuned scGPT is competitive on ICA (Total 0.781) but weak on smaller datasets (Pancreas Total 0.662). scCLIP and scTEL perform poorly across all tasks (scCLIP BMMC Total 0.460; scTEL BMMC Pearson 0.047), and Geneformer (finetuned) shows near-zero bio conservation on all datasets. The PCA baseline, while unable to correct batch effects, retains adequate bio conservation (e.g., PBMC Bio 0.558), and SCDC is outperformed by most SSL methods on total score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first comprehensive benchmark systematically comparing generic SSL methods (originally designed for images/text) against specialized single-cell SSL frameworks across multiple downstream tasks and datasets. It extends the limited comparison in Richter et al. (2024a), which evaluated only two SSL methods and masked autoencoders without including specialized single-cell models or exploring hyperparameters and augmentations. The benchmark repurposes well-established contrastive and non-contrastive architectures (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, VICReg, etc.) for single-cell genomics and places them alongside domain-specific methods (Concerto, CLEAR, CLAIRE, scCLIP) and generative approaches (scVI, totalVI, scGPT, Geneformer, scBERT) under a unified evaluation protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the single-cell genomics community with a standardized platform and concrete recommendations for applying SSL, filling a critical knowledge gap in how to adapt and optimize these methods. A key open question it surfaces is the lack of a specialized multi-modal single-cell integration framework that can match generic architectures, suggesting current domain-specific approaches are insufficient for multi-omics. The finding that simple random masking outperforms sophisticated biology-specific augmentations (MNN, BBKNN) challenges the assumption that domain knowledge in augmentation design is necessary, and the underperformance of Concerto relative to even PCA baselines raises questions about the design choices in existing specialized frameworks.
