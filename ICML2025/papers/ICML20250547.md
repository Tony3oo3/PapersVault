## Problem

Zero-shot vision-language models like CLIP are vulnerable to spurious correlations, relying on background or co-occurring features rather than invariant task-relevant features for predictions. Existing mitigation strategies either require fine-tuning with expert-annotated group labels or depend on LLMs to generate debiasing prompts, both of which introduce additional costs and fail to exploit the rich semantic information already present in the vision encoder's patch token representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a set of class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a {CLASS}'), and an auxiliary text prompt (either a superclass phrase like 'bird in photo' for ERICT, or an average of top-K class prompt embeddings for ERICT-C) as input. It produces a binary token-level mask over the ViT patch tokens and a final class label. The mask is applied unilaterally to the CLS token's attention weights in the last two transformer layers of the vision encoder, so all patch tokens still participate in full attention computation but the CLS token's perception of spurious-region tokens is suppressed.

## Method

ERICT operates entirely at inference time in two steps. First, it computes cosine similarity scores between an auxiliary text embedding and all patch token representations in the ViT, then applies a SparseMax-like thresholding procedure (with a temperature parameter τ) to generate a per-image binary mask that retains tokens with lower similarity scores—counterintuitively, tokens whose semantics align with the prompt score lower. Second, the mask is multiplied into the CLS token's query-key attention in the last two vision encoder layers, redirecting the global representation toward invariant regions without removing any tokens. The ERICT-C variant replaces the hand-specified superclass prompt with an average of the top-K class prompt embeddings, making the method applicable to large-scale datasets like ImageNet without requiring a clear superclass. A theoretical analysis provides an error probability bound showing that increasing the mask ratio over spurious components monotonically reduces the worst-group error.

## Results

On CelebA (ViT-L/14), ERICT-C achieves a worst-group accuracy of 85.44% with a gap of only 1.09, outperforming all baselines across every backbone. On Waterbirds (ViT-B/32), ERICT reaches 71.44% worst-group accuracy, surpassing the previous best (PerceptionCLIP at 66.07%) by over 5 points. On the challenging Urbancars dataset (ViT-L/14), ERICT improves worst-group accuracy from 12.40% (ZSCLIP) to 49.60% while also raising average accuracy from 54.00% to 63.30%. The method consistently narrows the worst-group-to-average gap to under 3 points on CelebA and under 15 points on Waterbirds and Urbancars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERICT and ERICT-C outperform all three baselines on worst-group accuracy across all three datasets and all three backbones. Against ZSCLIP (Radford et al., 2021), the gains are most dramatic on Urbancars, where ERICT lifts worst-group accuracy from 12.40% to 49.60% on ViT-L/14, and on Waterbirds ViT-B/32 where WG jumps from 39.33% to 71.44%. Against PerceptionCLIP (An et al., 2023), ERICT-C achieves a 4.6-point WG improvement on CelebA ViT-L/14 (85.44% vs. 80.84%) and a 16.25-point WG improvement on Waterbirds ViT-B/16 (57.32% vs. 41.07% for PerceptionCLIP+). Against ROBOSHOT (Adila et al., 2024), ERICT-C surpasses it on CelebA ViT-L/14 (85.44% vs. 82.6% WG) and on Waterbirds ViT-B/32 (57.2% vs. 54.4% WG for ERICT-C, 71.44% for ERICT). Notably, ERICT-C consistently achieves the smallest worst-group-to-average gap (as low as 1.09 on CelebA ViT-L/14), indicating the most balanced group performance among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERICT departs from the two dominant paradigms for mitigating spurious correlation in VLMs: fine-tuning with group labels (FairerCLIP, CPT) and text-modality-only inference-time debiasing (PerceptionCLIP, ROBOSHOT, Chuang et al.). Instead, it operates directly in the vision encoder's attention mechanism during inference, using a SparseMax-inspired thresholding procedure to generate a token-level mask that blocks the CLS token from attending to spurious regions. The method is conceptually adjacent to token pruning but differs in that all tokens still participate in full attention computation; only the CLS token's attention is unilaterally reduced. The theoretical analysis builds on the linearized CLIP loss characterization from Xue et al. (2024) to derive a formal error bound under masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERICT offers a practical, training-free, and LLM-free debiasing procedure that can be plugged into any CLIP-based pipeline at inference time, making it broadly applicable without the annotation or compute costs of prior methods. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.2) that a binary mask provably tightens the error bound as the masked spurious proportion increases provides rare formal grounding for an otherwise heuristic attention-masking approach. Key limitations remain: the auxiliary prompt is a hand-designed superclass or top-K aggregation that the authors acknowledge is suboptimal, and the method's effectiveness depends on the quality of this auxiliary signal. Open questions include how to automatically discover better auxiliary representations and whether the mask strategy generalizes to multi-label or open-vocabulary settings.
