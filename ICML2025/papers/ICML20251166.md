## Problem

Scaling self-supervised representation learning for cell microscopy is constrained by two practical bottlenecks: experimental datasets are dominated by morphologically uninformative (unperturbed-like) cells, and the optimal layer for extracting biological features from large ViTs has not been systematically identified. This paper addresses both by curating a phenotype-diverse training set and demonstrating that intermediate ViT blocks, not the final layer, yield the strongest biological representations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256×6 microscopy image crops (a six-channel Cell Painting implementation) and produces a single feature embedding per crop from a selected intermediate transformer block rather than the final layer. For whole-genome evaluation, 36 center crops per well are inferred across 2.2 million RxRx3 wells (~80M forward passes), then post-processed with Typical Variation Normalization and chromosome-arm bias correction before spherical-mean aggregation to the gene level. The trimmed MAE-G/8 uses block 38 of 48, while MAE-L/8 on Phenoprints-16M uses block 20 of 24.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a pre-trained MAE-L/8 and a weakly supervised model are used to compute a non-parametric perturbation consistency test (mean pairwise cosine similarity across replicates, tested against a permutation null) for every perturbation; only perturbations with p < 0.01 in either model are retained, reducing 93M images to the 16M-image Phenoprints-16M dataset with substantially higher relative phenotype diversity. Second, a 1.9B-parameter ViT-G/8 MAE (75% mask ratio, L2 + Fourier-space loss, Lion optimizer) is trained for 500 epochs on Phenoprints-16M using 256 H100 GPUs (~48,000 GPU-hours, >8B crops). Third, logistic-regression linear probes are fit to the output of every transformer block on two small tasks (RxRx1 1139-class siRNA gene prediction and a novel 40-class Anax functional gene-group prediction); the block with highest balanced accuracy is selected as the representation layer, and this choice correlates at ρ=0.97 with whole-genome StringDB relationship recall, enabling cheap model trimming that simultaneously improves biological performance and reduces inference cost.

## Results

On the whole-genome RxRx3 benchmark (Table 1), the trimmed MAE-G/8 (1.9B params, block 38) achieves 45.4% biological relationship recall (0.05–0.95 cosine threshold), a KS replicate-consistency statistic of 0.63, and a Cramér-von Mises statistic of 18.2, all the best among all models tested. On the RxRx3-core compound-gene activity benchmark (Table 3), the trimmed MAE-G/8 reaches 0.309 mean average precision (z-score 5.38 over random), a 42% relative improvement over the prior SOTA MAE-L/8 (0.290, z-score 3.77). On the external JUMP-CP dataset (Table 2), the trimmed MAE-G/8 obtains the highest StringDB recall (0.235) and CORUM recall (0.264) among all models, outperforming the CellProfiler classical-feature baseline on every database.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior SOTA MAE-L/8 RPI-93M (Kraus et al., 2024), the trimmed MAE-G/8 achieves a statistically significant 1.0-point gain in relationship recall (45.4% vs 44.4%, z-score 5.21), a 21% improvement in the KS replicate-consistency statistic (0.52→0.63), and a 48% improvement in the CM statistic (12.3→18.2). All microscopy-trained MAEs substantially outperform the natural-image baselines: the best DINOv2 model (ViT-L/14 trimmed, 38.8% recall) and the ImageNet MAE (ViT-L/16 trimmed, 37.8% recall) trail the smallest microscopy MAE (CA-MAE-S/16, 39.6%) by 1–2 recall points and by roughly 0.10–0.12 in KS. On the external JUMP-CP dataset, the trimmed MAE-G/8 outperforms the CellProfiler classical-feature baseline on all four relationship databases (e.g., StringDB 0.235 vs 0.191; CORUM 0.264 vs 0.219), confirming generalization beyond the pretraining distribution. On the RxRx3-core compound-gene activity benchmark, the trimmed MAE-G/8 (0.309 AP, z=5.38) beats CellProfiler (0.274, z=2.55) by 13% relative and the prior MAE-L/8 (0.290, z=3.77) by 42% relative, while the preliminary DINOv2 ViT-L/16 trained on RxRx3 underperforms even CellProfiler (0.258 vs 0.274).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the microscopy-specific MAE line initiated by Kraus et al. (2023, 2024) by scaling from ViT-L/8 to a 1.9B-parameter ViT-G/8 and replacing the 93M-image RPI-93M training set with a 5× smaller but more phenotypically diverse curated dataset (Phenoprints-16M). It builds on Celik et al. (2024)'s perturbation consistency framework for data filtering and their biological relationship recall benchmark for evaluation, and draws on the broader observation from Alkin et al. (2024) and Evci et al. (2022) that intermediate ViT blocks can outperform the final layer on linear probing tasks. The key departure is demonstrating that these intermediate-layer benefits, previously noted for ImageNet k-NN, translate into substantially better whole-genome biological relationship recall and replicate consistency in the microscopy domain, and that a cheap linear-probing proxy can reliably identify the optimal block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reusable three-step recipe (curate diverse data → scale MAE training → probe intermediate blocks) that could be applied to other experimental-data domains lacking ground-truth labels, and the intermediate-layer finding is broadly applicable to any ViT-based representation pipeline where the final layer is used by default. The 1.9B-parameter MAE-G/8 is the largest foundation model for cell microscopy to date, and the validation that scaling FLOPs continues to improve biological benchmarks into the billion-parameter regime strengthens the case for continued investment in this direction. Open questions include why DINOv2-style contrastive pretraining fails on microscopy data (a notable negative result), whether iterative curation with progressively stronger models yields further gains, and whether the intermediate-layer optimum shifts with model scale or training duration.
