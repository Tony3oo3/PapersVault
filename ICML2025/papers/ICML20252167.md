## Problem

Vision-language pretrained (VLP) models like CLIP can effortlessly interpret and exploit publicly shared images, making it difficult for users to protect their visual data from unauthorized indexing or reuse. Existing backdoor-attack-based compression defenses are irreversible—requiring separate encoding passes for clean and poisoned outputs—and cannot support dual-mode decoding where a single bitstream yields both a perceptually faithful image and a semantically misleading one. The unexplored challenge is how to efficiently encode both visual perception and machine-recognition information within one compression pipeline and selectively activate each under user-specified conditions.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a standard RGB image and produces a single compressed bitstream via an AE-Hyperprior backbone trained on 70,000 CC3M image-text pairs. At decode time, a user-supplied mode indicator (encrypted or full, e.g., a keyword) is fed to the trigger generator; the default path yields the encrypted version with near-invisible adversarial patterns, while providing the correct condition unlocks the full version with complete semantic fidelity. Both versions share the same bitstream and bitrate, so no additional transmission overhead is incurred for the privacy layer.

## Method

PSIC integrates three key components into a standard LIC pipeline. First, a Conditional Latent Trigger Generation (CLTG) module is deployed at each decoder block: a 2-layer MLP maps the mode indicator to a conditional bias feature, which is fused with the latent image feature via a learned element-wise weighting map, steering reconstruction toward either adversarial or faithful semantics. Second, an Uncertainty-Aware Encryption-Oriented (UAEO) loss replaces the naive cosine-similarity minimization by applying Dempster-Shafer Theory to the CLIP model's batch-level evidence, identifying the most uncertain (i.e., most likely wrong) text prompt for each image and maximizing similarity to that prompt, thereby producing more robust and transferable misleading patterns. Third, an adaptive two-stage multi-objective training strategy alternates between rate-perception and rate-encryption objectives in stage one (jointly optimizing encoder, entropy model, and decoder) and then freezes the encoder/entropy model in stage two to refine the divergent decoder representations, avoiding the need for hand-tuned loss balancing.

## Results

The PSIC encrypted version achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 80.8% (text-to-image retrieval), 72.3% (image-to-text retrieval), 67.0% (image classification on ImageNet-1k), and 51.5% (facial attribute analysis on CelebA), significantly misleading the target CLIP ViT-B/32 model. The full version maintains comparable machine-analytics performance to the AE-Hyperprior backbone and shows a 1.0 dB average PSNR improvement over BAvAFT's clean mode across four bpp points. On the unseen BLIP-2 image captioning task (Flickr8k), PSIC outperforms BAvAFT in BLEU@4, CIDEr, and SPICE, demonstrating generalization. The UAEO optimization function contributes an additional 6.3% and 6.2% ASR gain for t2i and i2t retrieval respectively over a naive cosine-similarity-minimization objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AE-Hyperprior backbone (Ballé et al., ICLR 2018), PSIC's full version preserves the same rate-distortion (PSNR) performance, confirming that the privacy mechanism does not degrade standard compression quality. Against BAvAFT (Yu et al., CVPR 2023), the cutting-edge LIC-oriented backdoor attack, PSIC achieves a 12.9% average ASR improvement across all four downstream tasks, indicating substantially stronger misleading of the target CLIP model. PSIC also surpasses BAvAFT's clean mode by 1.0 dB in average PSNR, and its full version consistently outperforms BAvAFT's clean version on image classification, facial attribute analysis, and image captioning. The advantage is attributed to injecting triggers at the latent representation stage (via CLTG) rather than at the input preprocessing stage, allowing the encoder to jointly optimize for both attacking and normal reconstruction. On the unseen BLIP-2 captioning task, PSIC further demonstrates superior generalization over BAvAFT in BLEU@4, CIDEr, and SPICE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of backdoor attacks on learned image compression (notably BAvAFT, which injects frequency-domain triggers at the input stage) by moving trigger injection to the latent representation stage via a Conditional Latent Trigger Generation module, enabling a single bitstream to be decoded into two semantically opposite versions. It departs from prior backdoor-on-compression work, which is inherently single-mode and irreversible, by introducing a conditional dual-decoding architecture where the same compressed data yields either a VLP-misleading encrypted version or a full-fidelity version depending on a user-supplied condition. The Uncertainty-Aware Encryption-Oriented loss, which uses Dempster-Shafer Theory to identify low-confidence image-text pairs in the training set and force the model to fit those uncertain (wrong) labels, is a novel optimization strategy not present in prior adversarial compression or VLP-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLP models like CLIP and BLIP-2 become the default interface for image search, captioning, and retrieval, the ability to protect images at the compression stage—without requiring users to alter their content or deploy separate protection tools—addresses a practical and timely privacy gap. The plug-and-play integration into existing LIC backbones (demonstrated on AE-Hyperprior) makes the approach broadly applicable, though generalization to other backbones, target VLP architectures, and real-world transmission pipelines (further transcoding, network artifacts) remains untested. Open questions include whether the adversarial patterns survive additional lossy compression cycles, how the 'customizable condition' (keyword) is securely transmitted to prevent an adversary from simply requesting the full version, and whether the approach scales to video or high-resolution imagery. The paper itself acknowledges the dual-use risk: the same mechanism could be weaponized to poison training data for downstream model developers.
