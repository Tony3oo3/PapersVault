## Problem

Finding optimal sparse decision trees (jointly maximizing accuracy and minimizing the number of splits) is NP-complete, and existing exact methods rely on either Depth-First Search (which is uninformed, inefficient at high depths, and requires a maximum-depth hyperparameter) or Best-First Search that does not fully exploit the problem's AND/OR structure. No prior method combines an informed best-first strategy with the full AND/OR graph formulation to achieve both optimality and tight complexity guarantees.

## Input / Output

BRANCHES takes a tabular dataset with categorical (not necessarily binary) features, class labels, and a penalty parameter λ ∈ (0,1), and outputs the optimal sparse decision tree maximizing H(T) − λ·S(T). Unlike all competing methods, it natively supports multi-way splits on ordinal-encoded features, avoiding the need for a preliminary binary encoding that inflates the search space. It also exhibits anytime behavior: upon reaching a time limit, it returns the best tree found so far via a greedy policy unrolling, whereas DFS-based competitors like STreeD return nothing.

## Method

BRANCHES formulates the sparsity problem as an AND/OR graph search where states are branches (conjunctions of feature-value clauses), actions are either a terminal action (stop splitting) or a split action (choose an unused feature), and transitions fan out to all children of the split. It solves this with an AO*-type Selection–Expansion–Backpropagation loop guided by a custom heuristic called the Purification Bound, which initializes each branch's value as max{H(l), −λ + P[l(X)=1]} and is recursively backpropagated through the graph. A memoization scheme keyed on the unique ordered-splits representation of each branch avoids redundant computation, and a priority-queue-based selection policy (choosing the UNSOLVED child with lowest greedy value) drives focused search. The paper proves both optimality (Theorem 4.3) and a complexity bound on the number of branch evaluations (Theorem 4.4) that is orders of magnitude tighter than the analogous bound for OSDT, and empirically demonstrates superior runtime, iteration count, and anytime quality across 11 UCI datasets.

## Results

On 11 UCI datasets (with binary and ordinal encodings), BRANCHES consistently finds optimal sparse decision trees in substantially fewer iterations than GOSDT (e.g., 146 vs. 83,928 on monk1; 60,611 vs. 392,759 on monk2) and faster wall-clock time despite being implemented in Python. On ordinal-encoded datasets, BRANCHES is the only method applicable and achieves optimal solutions in under 13 seconds across all datasets (e.g., 0.15s/6 iterations on mushroom-o), whereas binary-encoded counterparts often reach the 5-minute timeout. BRANCHES also exhibits superior anytime behavior: when both BRANCHES and GOSDT hit timeout, BRANCHES proposes a better solution (e.g., objective 0.838 vs. 0.757 on tic-tac-toe; 0.812 vs. 0.810 on nursery). The theoretical complexity bound of BRANCHES is orders of magnitude smaller than OSDT's (e.g., 10⁵ vs. 10²⁷¹ for q=10, λ=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GOSDT (the closest BFS competitor), BRANCHES wins on nearly all datasets in both wall-clock time and iteration count (e.g., 14.2s vs. 91.9s on monk2; 44.6s vs. 87.3s on zoo; 24.9s vs. timeout on nursery-f), with GOSDT only winning on car-eval-f (21.1s vs. 26.6s) and balance-f (162s vs. 190s). Against STreeD (the best DFS method), BRANCHES wins on most datasets at max depth 20 (e.g., 4.05s vs. 7.87s on monk3; 44.6s vs. 184s on zoo), but STreeD's depth-2 solver gives it a clear edge on mushroom (10.8s vs. timeout for BRANCHES). Against MurTree, BRANCHES matches or exceeds its objective on all comparable datasets while being faster. Against OSDT (theoretical comparison only), BRANCHES' complexity bound is orders of magnitude tighter (10⁵ vs. 10²⁷¹ for q=10, λ=0.05). BRANCHES uniquely supports ordinal/multi-way splits, enabling solutions in seconds that are intractable under binary encoding (e.g., mushroom-o: 0.15s vs. timeout for all binary methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical AND/OR graph search framework (Montanari et al., 1975; Martelli & Montanari, 1978) by formulating the optimal sparse decision tree problem as an AND/OR graph search and solving it with a novel AO*-type algorithm called BRANCHES, augmented with a custom heuristic called the Purification Bound. It departs from the dominant DFS-based approaches (DL8, MurTree, STreeD) and the earlier BFS methods (OSDT, GOSDT) by exploiting the full AND/OR structure of the problem for more efficient best-first search, and by supporting non-binary (multi-way) splits that all prior methods lack. The complexity analysis extends the only prior theoretical bound (Hu et al., 2019, Theorem E.2) with a significantly tighter guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BRANCHES provides the first AO*-based algorithm for optimal sparse DTs with both optimality and complexity guarantees, and its support for non-binary features eliminates the need for preliminary one-hot encoding that inflates the search space by orders of magnitude (Theorem E.1). The practical impact is substantial: BRANCHES outperforms C++-implemented competitors (GOSDT, STreeD) in runtime despite being in Python, and its anytime behavior yields better partial solutions than GOSDT at timeout. Open questions include whether a C++ implementation with multi-threading would widen the gap further, whether the depth-2 solver from MurTree can be integrated, and whether the Purification Bound can be tightened using auxiliary classifiers. The paper does not address scalability to very large datasets (n > 100k) or high-dimensional feature spaces, which remain key practical barriers for optimal sparse DTs.
