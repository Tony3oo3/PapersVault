## Problem

Existing EHR representation learning methods largely overlook patient cohorts, and those that do incorporate cohort information rely on coarse-grained divisions based on explicit features (e.g., age, gender) that fail to capture temporal patient similarity. No prior method simultaneously achieves fine-grained cohort segmentation and jointly exploits both local intra-cohort and global inter-cohort information to enhance downstream healthcare analytics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251611/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NeuralCohort operates as a plug-in that consumes initial patient representations R₀ produced by a backbone model (Med2Vec, MiME, or ClinicalBERT) along with the raw temporal EHR sequences (diagnosis, medication, and lab codes per visit). It outputs augmented patient representations R_final for binary prediction tasks (30-day readmission, long LOS >7 days) and soft cohort assignments via Jensen-Shannon divergence with student's t-distribution. The pseudo-similarity signal is derived from diagnosis codes with a time-decay-weighted Jaccard metric, and the hierarchical ICD-9 ontology tree structure is explicitly leveraged to differentiate semantically similar diagnosis codes.

## Method

The architecture comprises two serial modules. The Pre-context Cohort Synthesis Module first builds visit-level representations via a hierarchical engine that combines ontology tree path embeddings (graph-based) with GloVe semantic similarity weighting for diagnosis codes, then aggregates to patient-level representations using a reverse-time GRU attention mechanism augmented with cosine similarity to the anchor visit. Patient similarity is learned through a PseudoSim Training task that uses time-decay-weighted Jaccard similarity on diagnosis codes as pseudo labels, optimized via Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) with top-K positive and random negative samples; cohorts are then derived by minimizing Jensen-Shannon divergence with t-distribution soft assignments. The Biscale Cohort Learning Module encodes local intra-cohort structure via a GCN on fully-connected cohort graphs (edge weights from cosine similarity of patient representations) and global inter-cohort structure via an encoder-decoder that reconstructs cohort centroids with a combined reconstruction and contrastive loss. A cross-domain attention mechanism fuses the backbone, intra-cohort, and inter-cohort representations into the final augmented output, trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of PseudoSim, cohort derivation, cohort contrastive, and downstream task losses.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, NeuralCohort achieves the best performance across all three backbones (ClinicalBERT, Med2Vec, MiME) on both readmission and long LOS prediction tasks. The headline improvement is up to 8.1% in AUROC (ClinicalBERT long LOS: 0.671 vs. 0.590 baseline), 8.0% in AUPRC, and 16.3% in accuracy. On MIMIC-IV and Diabetes130, consistent gains are observed, e.g., MiME + NeuralCohort reaches 0.923 AUROC on long LOS (MIMIC-III) and 0.689 AUROC on readmission (Diabetes130), outperforming the strongest baseline (IDC) by 1.3 and 1.4 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralCohort consistently outperforms all five baselines (DEC, DEKM, GRASP, DGLoS, IDC) across all three datasets and both tasks. IDC is the strongest competitor, achieving the best baseline AUROC in most settings (e.g., 0.657 for ClinicalBERT readmission, 0.910 for MiME long LOS on MIMIC-III), yet NeuralCohort still surpasses it by 2.4 and 1.3 percentage points respectively. DEKM is the second-strongest baseline, particularly on readmission (0.659 AUROC with ClinicalBERT), but NeuralCohort leads by 2.2 points. GRASP and DGLoS frequently underperform even the bare backbone on AUROC (e.g., DGLoS drops ClinicalBERT readmission AUROC from 0.651 to 0.533), attributed to their coarse-grained or single-scale cohort modeling. The paper attributes these failures to the baselines' inability to simultaneously achieve fine-grained cohort division and exploit both intra- and inter-cohort information, whereas NeuralCohort's dual-module design addresses both desiderata.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EHR representation learning (Med2Vec, MiME, ClinicalBERT) by introducing a cohort-aware plug-in that addresses two gaps the authors identify in prior work: (1) existing cohort methods like GRASP and DGLoS operate at a coarse granularity and model only inter-cohort or only intra-cohort relationships, and (2) patient similarity approaches (Mahalanobis distance, deep similarity networks) do not drive cohort generation. NeuralCohort departs from these by using pseudo patient similarity (weighted Jaccard with time decay over diagnosis codes) to construct fine-grained cohorts via Jensen-Shannon divergence, then jointly encoding intra-cohort (GCN) and inter-cohort (encoder-decoder) information through a cross-domain attention fusion. The plug-in design explicitly positions it as orthogonal to backbone choice, unlike prior methods that are tightly coupled to specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in architecture makes NeuralCohort broadly applicable to any EHR backbone, and the consistent 2–8% AUROC gains across three datasets and three backbones suggest the cohort signal is genuinely informative rather than an artifact of a particular model. The negative result that traditional demographic/disease-based cohorts (gender, age, diabetes, hypertension) perform at or below bare backbones is a useful validation that coarse-grained, hand-specified cohorts are insufficient for representation learning. Open questions include whether the pseudo-similarity signal (relying solely on diagnosis codes) transfers to settings with richer multimodal data, how the method scales to the ~300K patients in MIMIC-IV without the O(N²) pairwise similarity computation, and whether the learned cohorts remain clinically interpretable at larger cohort counts.
