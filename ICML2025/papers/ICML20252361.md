## Problem

Standard conformal prediction relies on scalar non-conformity scores, which cannot capture the geometric structure of multivariate prediction errors (e.g., in multi-output regression or multiclass classification). Existing multivariate extensions impose predefined convex shapes—ellipsoids or hyperrectangles—that may misalign with the intrinsic data geometry, or they lack finite-sample distribution-free coverage guarantees. This paper asks whether one can construct prediction regions with flexible, potentially non-convex shapes that adapt to the calibration error distribution while retaining the distribution-free validity of classical CP.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained black-box model, a calibration set of n exchangeable (X, Y) pairs, and a user-specified multivariate score function s: X × Y → R^d_+ (e.g., component-wise residuals for regression or absolute probability differences for classification). The calibration set is split into D1 (for computing the MK rank map) and D2 (for calibrating the quantile threshold). The output is a prediction region in the output space—arbitrarily shaped for regression (a translation of the MK quantile region by the model prediction) or a subset of class labels for classification—accompanied by a finite-sample coverage guarantee of at least α.

## Method

OT-CP replaces the scalar quantile step of classical CP with a Monge-Kantorovich (MK) quantile region constructed via optimal transport. Specifically, multivariate calibration scores are transported onto reference rank vectors (i/n · θ_i, where θ_i are i.i.d. uniform on the unit sphere) by solving an assignment problem; the resulting MK rank map assigns each score a vector whose norm encodes a distribution-free multivariate rank. The quantile region is the sublevel set of this rank norm, with the threshold calibrated from a held-out subset D2 to ensure exchangeability and thus finite-sample coverage. For adaptivity, OT-CP+ replaces the global rank map with conditional MK rank maps computed over k-nearest neighbors in D1, yielding asymptotic conditional coverage under regularity assumptions. For classification, the reference vectors are drawn from the positive simplex to induce a left-to-right ordering appropriate for absolute-error scores in R^d_+.

## Results

In multi-output regression on simulated data, OT-CP produces prediction regions with smaller volume than both ellipsoidal (ELL) and hyperrectangle (REC) baselines while maintaining the target α=0.9 coverage guarantee. In the adaptive setting (OT-CP+), worst-set conditional coverage remains consistently close to α=0.9 across all six real datasets (atp1d, rf1, scm20d, jura, wq, enb), whereas the adaptive ellipsoidal baseline fails to reach the target and shows greater variability. In multiclass classification on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, OT-CP achieves label-wise conditional coverage on par with the APS score while matching the average set size and informativeness (singleton rate) of the simpler IP and MS scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ellipsoidal baseline (Johnstone & Cox, 2021; ELL), OT-CP wins on efficiency in multi-output regression, producing smaller and more precisely shaped prediction regions that adapt to anisotropic residual distributions, while both methods satisfy the marginal coverage guarantee. Against the hyperrectangle baseline (Neeven & Smirnov, 2018; REC), OT-CP similarly achieves smaller volumes by capturing inter-component correlations that independent per-dimension quantiles miss. In the adaptive regression setting, OT-CP+ outperforms the adaptive ellipsoidal method of Messoudi et al. (2022) on worst-set conditional coverage: OT-CP+ stays near α=0.9 across all datasets and sample sizes, while the ellipsoidal approach undercovers and exhibits higher variability, particularly on smaller datasets. In classification, OT-CP beats the IP and MS scores (Johansson et al., 2017) on worst-slab conditional coverage—matching the APS score of Romano et al. (2020b)—while retaining the smaller average set sizes and higher singleton rates characteristic of IP and MS. The trade-off is that OT-CP+ is more computationally intensive than the ellipsoidal baseline (solving multiple OT problems per test point), and for very small datasets (~350 samples) it tends to slightly overcover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: Monge-Kantorovich (MK) quantiles from optimal transport theory (Chernozhukov et al., 2017; Hallin et al., 2021) and the Conformal Prediction (CP) framework (Vovk et al., 2005). Prior CP methods for multivariate outputs either aggregate errors into a scalar score (losing geometric structure) or impose predefined shapes such as ellipsoids (Johnstone & Cox, 2021; Messoudi et al., 2022) or hyperrectangles (Neeven & Smirnov, 2018), which can misalign with the true data geometry. The key departure is using MK quantiles directly as the multivariate quantile mechanism within CP, yielding shape-free prediction regions with distribution-free finite-sample coverage. The splitting strategy for the coverage proof follows Kuchibhotla (2020), and the adaptive variant OT-CP+ builds on conditional MK quantile regression (del Barrio et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OT-CP provides a principled, model-agnostic recipe for constructing prediction regions that adapt to the actual geometry of multivariate prediction errors without distributional assumptions, filling a gap left by shape-constrained alternatives. The asymptotic conditional coverage guarantee for OT-CP+ (Theorem 3.2) is a meaningful step toward the long-sought goal of conditional validity in CP. However, the O(n³) computational cost of solving the OT problem limits scalability, and the authors acknowledge that OT-CP can be more conservative than ellipsoidal methods in some regression settings. Open questions include whether better reference distributions or entropic OT approximations (Klein et al., 2025) can close the efficiency gap, and whether more sophisticated multivariate scores (beyond simple residuals or one-hot differences) can further improve informativeness for large-K classification.
