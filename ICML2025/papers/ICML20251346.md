## Problem

Differentially private federated learning (DPFL) suffers from accumulating semantic disruption as Gaussian noise corrupts model parameters over communication rounds, degrading utility. Prior spectral-space approaches (CENTAUR, Jain et al.) apply SVD to each client's noisy matrix independently, ignoring the semantic complementarity that exists across clients whose data distributions are correlated. This paper asks whether inter-client collaboration in the spectral domain can flexibly recover disrupted semantic information while preserving strict privacy guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251346/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server receives K noisy local model parameter vectors (each of dimension d×h, after per-client gradient clipping and Gaussian noise addition) and stacks them into a third-order tensor of shape d×h×K. Every I communication rounds, the server outputs K updated per-client parameter vectors (the unfolded slices of the low-rank-approximated tensor) that are distributed back to clients for the next local training phase. The truncation threshold is not fixed but follows a geometric series λ/ϑ^(t/I), so the degree of smoothing adapts both to the privacy setting (via initial λ) and to the accumulated noise level (via round t).

## Method

FedCEO performs tensor low-rank proximal optimization at the server: every I rounds it folds the K noisy parameter vectors into a 3D tensor and solves min_W {τ‖W−W_N‖_F² + ‖W‖_*}, where ‖·‖_* is the tensor nuclear norm (TNN). Theorem 3.1 proves this objective is exactly equivalent to truncated tensor SVD (T-tSVD) with threshold 1/(2τ), which in the Fourier domain along the client dimension amounts to soft-thresholding singular values of each frequency slice. Because the first (lowest-frequency) slice dominates in singular value magnitude (Remark B.2), increasing the threshold progressively zeros out higher-frequency components, smoothing the global semantic space and letting clients with degraded local representations borrow complementary information from others. The adaptive geometric threshold (ϑ>1) means smoothing intensifies as noise accumulates over rounds, while a smaller initial λ for stronger privacy settings yields a lower-rank, smoother space from the start. This yields a utility-privacy trade-off bound of O(√d/N), improving over the O(d/N) of CENTAUR by a factor of √d.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (LeNet-5), FedCEO with adaptive threshold (ϑ>1) achieves 54.16% testing accuracy at σ_g=1.0, 50.00% at σ_g=1.5, and 45.35% at σ_g=2.0, consistently outperforming all baselines. On EMNIST (MLP-2-Layers), FedCEO reaches 78.05%, 72.44%, and 64.20% under the same privacy settings. The improvement is most pronounced under strong privacy (σ_g=2.0) on CIFAR-10, where FedCEO surpasses CENTAUR by 8.65 percentage points (45.35% vs. 36.70%). The paper also demonstrates that FedCEO's training runtime (3.908h on CIFAR-10) is comparable to UDP-FedAvg (3.876h), while PPSGD and CENTAUR exceed 24h.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCEO (ϑ>1) achieves the highest testing accuracy across all six dataset-privacy combinations in Table 1, winning against all three baselines in every setting. Against CENTAUR (Shen et al., 2023), the closest competitor, FedCEO gains 0.79–2.08 points on EMNIST and 4.02–8.65 points on CIFAR-10, with the largest margin at σ_g=2.0 on CIFAR-10 (45.35% vs. 36.70%). Against PPSGD (Bietti et al., 2022), FedCEO leads by 1.04–10.74 points, again most dramatically under strong privacy on CIFAR-10. UDP-FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2018) serves as the weakest baseline, trailing FedCEO by 1.46–18.47 points depending on dataset and noise level. In the privacy attack experiment (Figure 3), FedCEO and UDP-FedAvg both resist DLG gradient inversion with low PSNR, while plain FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) leaks sensitive images, confirming that the tensor low-rank processing does not compromise DP guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCEO extends the SVD-based spectral processing approach introduced by CENTAUR (Shen et al., 2023) and Jain et al. (2021), which apply truncated SVD independently to each client's noisy parameter matrix at the server. The key conceptual departure is stacking all K clients' noisy parameters into a third-order tensor and applying truncated tensor SVD (T-tSVD, Kilmer & Martin, 2011) with a tensor nuclear norm regularizer (Lu et al., 2016), thereby exploiting inter-client semantic complementarity rather than treating each client in isolation. The DP mechanism itself follows the standard user-level Gaussian noise + clipping pipeline from DP-FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2018), and the adaptive geometric-series truncation threshold is a novel scheduling mechanism layered on top of the T-tSVD operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(√d) improvement in the utility-privacy trade-off bound over the prior O(d) (CENTAUR) and O(d^1.5) (Jain et al.) is a clean theoretical contribution that gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer tensor-level over matrix-level spectral processing in DPFL. Practically, FedCEO matches UDP-FedAvg runtime (unlike PPSGD/CENTAUR which exceed 24h), making it deployable without additional server cost. However, experiments are limited to small models (MLP, LeNet-5, AlexNet) and small-scale FL (100 clients, 10 sampled), and the critical assumption that the first Fourier frequency component dominates (Remark B.2) has not been stress-tested on highly heterogeneous or large-scale settings. The authors themselves flag extension to heterogeneous FL as future work, and the adaptive hyperparameter (λ, ϑ, I) still requires per-dataset tuning.
