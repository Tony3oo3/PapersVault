## Problem

Offline RLHF methods such as DPO, IPO, and SLiC have been motivated by reparameterization arguments that rely on unformalized coverage assumptions and do not capture the full range of algorithmic variants. The literature lacks a unified theoretical framework explaining how key design choices—loss function, base policy for normalizing log-likelihoods, and data coverage—determine the quality of the learned policy, despite well-documented failure modes like probability mass collapse and preference hacking.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an offline preference dataset of tuples (x, y, y', ω) where x is an input prompt, y and y' are two responses sampled from a data distribution D_y(·|x), and ω ∈ {-1,1} is a binary preference label. It produces a policy π that minimizes a preference loss over a policy class Π, parameterized by three design axes: a loss function ℓ, an arbitrary (not necessarily normalized) base policy μ used to form log-likelihood ratios, and a policy class Π that absorbs constraints like cross-entropy regularization or early-stopping bounds. The framework is general enough to subsume DPO, IPO, SLiC-HF, GPO, CPO, R-DPO, and ODPO as special cases via appropriate choices of these three components.

## Method

The paper reframes offline RLHF as pure loss minimization on a preference dataset and derives a KL-divergence bound (Theorem 3.6) between the learned policy and a benchmark policy π* defined under realizability and proper-loss assumptions. The bound scales as √(ε/c_μ) + √(Cε/c_μ) + (e^ℜ/2)·(Cε/c_μ), where c_μ is the curvature constant of the loss around the optimum, C is a coverage constant relating the data distribution to the benchmark policy, and ℜ bounds the log-likelihood ratios. The key theoretical insight is that squared loss (IPO) satisfies the curvature condition with a constant c_μ = 1 across the full input range, while logistic loss (DPO) has a curvature constant that degrades exponentially as ℜ grows, explaining DPO's empirically observed collapse. Empirically, on the TL;DR summarization task with a 770M T5 model, squared loss variants maintain stable preference scores throughout training while logistic loss variants peak at ~2K steps and then collapse, and using μ = π_ref consistently outperforms the uniform base policy μ = 1 under squared loss.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization task with a 770M T5 model, the squared loss (IPO) variants remain stable throughout training while the logistic loss (DPO) variants suffer a catastrophic collapse in preference scores after approximately 2K steps. The empirical curvature constant c_μ is 2.0 for squared loss versus only 0.00066–0.00068 for logistic loss, and the effective range parameter R is ~4.7–4.9 for squared loss versus ~73 for logistic loss, confirming that the KL bound in Theorem 3.6 is dramatically tighter for squared loss. Using the reference policy μ=π_ref is consistently better than the uniform base policy μ=1 when combined with squared loss, while for logistic loss both variants collapse similarly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DPO (logistic loss, μ=π_ref; Rafailov et al., 2024b) and IPO (squared loss, μ=π_ref; Azar et al., 2024) along with their reference-free (μ=1) counterparts on TL;DR summarization. IPO clearly wins over DPO: the squared loss variants maintain stable preference scores throughout training, while DPO peaks at ~2K steps and then collapses, with log-likelihoods of both preferred and dispreferred responses crashing toward zero. The theoretical explanation is that DPO's logistic loss has a curvature constant c_μ that degrades exponentially with R (here R≈73, giving c_μ≈0.00066), whereas squared loss has a constant c_μ=2.0 regardless of R. For the base policy choice, μ=π_ref is consistently preferred over μ=1 under squared loss, validating the theory that normalizing by the reference policy likelihood yields a more desirable benchmark policy π*. Under logistic loss, the base policy distinction is less meaningful because both variants collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the reparameterization-trick argument that motivates DPO and its variants, instead reframing offline RLHF as a pure loss-minimization problem over a preference dataset. It builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and IPO (Azar et al., 2024), which identified curvature issues with the logistic loss, but provides the first unified theoretical treatment encompassing DPO, IPO, SLiC, CPO, KTO, SimPO, and other variants under a single framework parameterized by loss function, base policy, and policy class constraints. The analysis draws on proper loss theory from binary classification and coverage conditions from the offline RL literature (Xie et al., 2021; Jiang & Xie, 2024), while acknowledging Swamy et al. (2024) who showed offline methods cannot attain the optimal online RLHF policy in general.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a principled explanation for why squared loss (IPO) outperforms logistic loss (DPO): the curvature constant for logistic loss degrades exponentially as log-likelihood ratios grow, which is exactly the regime DPO enters during training. The coverage condition formalizes a fundamental limitation of offline methods that is not captured by the reparameterization argument, suggesting that data collection strategy before offline RLHF is as important as the choice of loss. Open questions include extending the framework to handle SimPO's length normalization, multi-response (S-DPO) and group-robust variants, and whether the theoretical predictions hold at larger model scales and on more diverse tasks beyond the single 770M T5 / TL;DR summarization experiment.
