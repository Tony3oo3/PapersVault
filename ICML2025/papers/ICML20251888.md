## Problem

In continuous action space RL, actor-critic methods like TD3 and SAC update critics using the Bellman operator (SARSA-based), estimating values only for the current policy and relying solely on policy updates for improvement, which limits sample efficiency. While the Bellman optimality operator (Q-learning-based) accelerates learning by directly targeting optimal values, it introduces overestimation bias when combined with function approximators. The paper addresses how to harness the speed of optimal value estimation early in training while eliminating the bias in later stages.

## Input / Output

The method takes state vectors from continuous control environments (10 DM Control locomotion tasks and 10 Meta-World manipulation tasks) and produces continuous actions within standard actor-critic frameworks. The sole modification is to the critic's loss function: the standard L2 (MSE) loss is replaced with an expectile loss parameterized by τ, which is linearly annealed from τ_init (0.7–0.9 depending on task) down to 0.5 over the full training horizon. No auxiliary V-function, additional action sampling, or architectural changes are required beyond what the base algorithm already uses.

## Method

The core contribution is Annealed Q-learning (AQ-L), which replaces the squared-error critic loss in TD3 or SAC with the expectile loss L₂^τ(u) = |τ − 1(u<0)|u², where τ controls the degree of optimality: τ→1 approximates the Bellman optimality operator (max over actions) while τ=0.5 recovers the standard Bellman operator (expectation under the current policy). During training, τ is linearly annealed from τ_init (close to 1) to 0.5 over T total steps via τ(t) = τ_init − (τ_init − 0.5)·t/T, smoothly transitioning from Q-learning-based to SARSA-based target computation. The rationale is that early overestimation bias promotes exploration by inflating Q-values of under-explored actions, while the gradual reduction to τ=0.5 eliminates bias once the policy has converged. This requires only a single Q-function (no auxiliary V-function) and amounts to a one-line code change to the critic update, making it a drop-in modification to existing actor-critic algorithms.

## Results

On 10 DM Control locomotion tasks at 3M steps, AQ-SAC achieves a mean score of 746.1 (IQM 832.4) and AQ-TD3 achieves 740.3 (IQM 820.0), substantially outperforming SAC (657.9 / 765.0), TD3 (492.7 / 516.0), and XQL (564.4 / 628.8). At 1M steps, AQ-SAC already reaches 624.8 mean (IQM 651.5) versus SAC's 493.7 (IQM 516.1), demonstrating a ~26% improvement in sample efficiency. On 10 Meta-World manipulation tasks, AQ-SAC also outperforms SAC in most tasks with higher asymptotic success rates and faster convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AQ-TD3 (740.3 mean) outperforms TD3 (492.7) by ~248 points, and AQ-SAC (746.1) outperforms SAC (657.9) by ~88 points on DM Control at 3M steps, with the gap widening on harder tasks like hopper-hop and humanoid-run where baselines plateau at low scores. Against XQL (Garg et al., 2023), which uses Gumbel regression to estimate soft-optimal values, AQ-SAC leads by ~182 points in mean score, attributed to the expectile loss being more stable than XQL's exponential-based loss. Compared to max-backup (Kumar et al., 2020), which samples multiple actions to approximate the max operator, AQ-SAC achieves higher scores on challenging tasks while avoiding the increased computational cost of multi-action sampling. On Meta-World manipulation tasks, AQ-SAC improves over SAC in most tasks, while AQ-TD3 only marginally improves over TD3, suggesting that entropy-based exploration (as in SAC) is more critical for manipulation than for locomotion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the expectile loss from IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022), originally designed for offline RL, into the online actor-critic setting of TD3 and SAC, and introduces a linear annealing schedule on the expectile parameter τ to transition from Bellman optimality operator updates to Bellman operator updates. It departs from XQL (Garg et al., 2023) and MXQL (Omura et al., 2024), which model soft-optimal values via Gumbel regression or Maclaurin expansion, by using the simpler and smoother expectile loss. It also differs from Ji et al. (2024), which maintains two separate Q-functions and combines their targets via a fixed weighted sum, by using a single Q-function whose update rule itself transitions over training. The conceptual motivation—that early overestimation bias aids exploration while late-stage bias must be suppressed—builds on observations from Lan et al. (2020) and Chen et al. (2021) but applies them through a principled annealing mechanism rather than ensemble-based bias control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is high: a two-line code change (replace L2 loss with expectile loss, add a linear τ schedule) yields large, consistent gains over TD3 and SAC across 20 diverse tasks, and the annealing makes the method robust to the initial τ hyperparameter. The finding that the V-function from IQL is unnecessary in online settings (contrary to IQL's own recommendation for stochastic environments) simplifies implementation. Open questions include whether non-linear or adaptive annealing schedules (e.g., bias-triggered) could further improve performance, whether combining the approach with ensemble methods for bias control would compound gains, and whether the benefits extend to higher-dimensional or vision-based continuous control tasks beyond the state-vector benchmarks tested here.
