## Problem

Refusal Training (RT) struggles to generalize against out-of-distribution jailbreaking attacks, yet it is unclear whether OOD attacks truly exceed the model's inherent safety capability or whether RT simply fails to elicit latent safety knowledge. The paper shows via Best-of-N sampling that RT models do possess sufficient latent safety knowledge, and identifies the root cause as the under-specified 'I'm sorry' refusal supervision, which encourages reliance on superficial shortcuts and produces non-generalizable representation mappings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252546/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, each illegal or helpful instruction is augmented by prepending a set of explicit safety guidelines C, forming an augmented query x_i^c = (C, x_i), which is fed to a teacher LLM to synthesize a structured reasoning trace T^c followed by a final response y. At inference time, the guidelines are removed and the reasoning trace T^c is hidden from the user; only the final response y (a refusal or helpful answer) is returned. The reasoning trace internally uses structured tags (<step>, <reflection>, <reward>, <safety degree>) to organize multi-step safety reasoning with self-evaluation scores in [0.0, 1.0].

## Method

Safety Reasoning with Guidelines (SRG) comprises three stages: (1) supervision synthesis, where a teacher LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) generates step-by-step reasoning responses guided by predefined safety guidelines covering reasoning patterns, context understanding, and ethical considerations, augmented with Self-Reflection (periodic <reflection> tags and safety-degree scoring) and Self-Refinement (explicitly conditioning the final response on the minimum safety score); (2) guideline-aligned rejection sampling, where the same guidelines serve as both generation directives and evaluative criteria, with separate LLM judges verifying reasoning adherence and appropriate refusal behavior; and (3) context distillation, where the model is trained via SFT to produce the full (T^c, y) sequence from the original instruction x alone (without C), internalizing the structured reasoning process so that at test time it adaptively invokes relevant safety knowledge without external guidelines.

## Results

On the large-scale dataset with greedy decoding, SRG achieves average ASR reductions of 46.3% (8B) and 54.8% (70B) over Refusal Training across four OOD attacks, with near-0% ASR on Jailbreak Chat and Self Cipher. With Best-of-N sampling (N=64), SRG achieves approximately 0% ASR on all evaluated attacks for both 8B and 70B models. On the LLaMA-3-8B-instruct comparison (Table 5), SRG reduces ASR from 21% to 6% on Past Tense and from 32% to 26% on Persuasive Attack relative to the baseline, though LAT and RR still achieve lower ASR (2% and 4% respectively) on those two attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Refusal Training (RT), SRG dominates on all OOD attacks: on the 8B model, greedy ASR drops from 45.5% to 4.0% (Jailbreak Chat), 58.0% to 0.0% (Self Cipher), 50.0% to 17.0% (Past Tense), and 92.0% to 44.0% (Persuasive Attack); the 70B model shows similar or larger gains. Against LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2024) and RR (Zou et al., 2024) on LLaMA-3-8B-instruct, SRG ties or wins on Jailbreak Chat (1% vs 0%) and Self Cipher (0% vs 0%), but loses on Past Tense (6% vs 2% for both) and Persuasive Attack (26% vs 2% LAT, 4% RR). The authors note SRG is complementary to LAT/RR: combining SRG with RR (Table 11) yields 3% ASR on Past Tense and 8% on Persuasive Attack, outperforming either method alone. On over-refusal (XSTest), SRG scores 92%, slightly below the 95% baseline and RR's 99%, but above LAT (80%) and GPT-4o (88%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Refusal Training paradigm (which pairs illegal instructions with simple 'I'm sorry' responses) by reframing safety alignment as a structured reasoning problem. It builds on Context Distillation (Askell et al., 2021) to internalize guideline-conditioned reasoning, and incorporates Self-Reflection/Self-Refinement (Madaan et al., 2023; Qu et al., 2024) to ensure the model actually utilizes its elicited safety knowledge. Conceptually, it positions itself against representation-intervention methods (LAT, Circuit Breakers/RR) by arguing that the root cause of RT's OOD failure is under-specified supervision leading to shortcut learning, rather than a fundamental representational deficiency. It also distinguishes itself from Deliberative Alignment (Guan et al., 2024) by using general, category-agnostic reasoning guidelines rather than category-specific response directives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BoN diagnostic (showing 60% ASR reduction as N increases) provides a compelling reframing: OOD jailbreaks don't exceed the model's latent safety knowledge, but RT's simplistic supervision prevents consistent elicitation. This shifts the research question from 'how do we teach models new safety knowledge?' to 'how do we make models reliably access knowledge they already have?' The method is complementary to representation-based approaches (SRG+RR outperforms either alone), suggesting a path toward combining reasoning-based and representation-based safety training. Open questions include whether RL-based safety reasoning can further close the remaining gap on harder attacks (PastTense, Persuasive Attack), and whether the guideline framework scales to novel attack categories without retraining.
