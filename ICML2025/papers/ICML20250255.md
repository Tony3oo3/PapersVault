## Problem

Symbolic cognitive models in neuroscience and psychology are typically developed through a hypothesis-first process relying on human creativity, yet the space of possible models is vast and the best model for a given dataset is far from guaranteed to have been considered. Data-driven model discovery has been largely restricted to differentiable model spaces such as neural networks, which fit data well but resist the kind of mechanistic interpretation that symbolic models afford. This paper asks whether LLM-powered program synthesis can automatically discover interpretable symbolic cognitive models that outperform the best human-designed baselines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes behavioral datasets from dynamic multi-armed bandit tasks (sequences of discrete choices and scalar rewards across sessions and subjects), a seed program whose informativeness ranges from generic function stubs (LowInfo) to the full baseline model (FullModel), and an LLM prompt describing the scoring procedure. It produces JAX-compatible Python programs that map (choice, reward, hidden_state, params) to (choice_logits, updated_hidden_state), constrained to at most 10 free parameters with unbounded hidden-state dimensionality. Programs are scored by per-subject normalized likelihood using even/odd session cross-validation, and final evaluation is performed on held-out test subjects never seen during evolution.

## Method

CogFunSearch adapts FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) into a bilevel optimization: the outer loop uses an LLM to stochastically mutate pairs of programs from a score-ranked database, while the inner loop fits each candidate's parameters per-subject via AdaBelief gradient descent (up to 10,000 steps, 10 random restarts) and evaluates via two-fold session cross-validation. Each LLM call receives two parent programs plus documentation of the evaluation protocol and a directive to produce an improved program; the new program is scored and added to the database, with parent selection weighted by score. Four seed-program configurations (LowInfo, Structured1, Structured2, FullModel) control how much domain knowledge is injected, and each configuration is run with three independent seeds for seven days, generating 100K–1M programs per dataset. The key architectural insight is that the symbolic program space (Python/JAX code) is explicitly interpretable—variable names, comments, and update rules are human-readable—unlike the weight matrices of the RNN baselines it is compared against.

## Results

The best CogFunSearch programs outperform all human-discovered cognitive model baselines across three species (all p < 0.002, Wilcoxon signed-rank test). On held-out test subjects, normalized likelihood improves by 5.17±0.15 percentage points (Human, 55.77→60.93), 0.54±0.06 (Rat, 66.63→67.22), and 0.48±0.05 (Fruit Fly, 53.99→54.62). The discovered programs approach but do not fully close the gap to GRU recurrent neural networks (62.04, 67.41, 54.71 respectively). In 35 of 36 independent CogFunSearch runs, the highest-scoring program outperformed its species-specific baseline on held-out data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogFunSearch outperforms all three human-discovered cognitive model baselines: PFQ (Eckstein et al., 2024) on the Human dataset (60.93 vs. 55.77, +5.17 pp), RHG (Miller et al., 2021) on the Rat dataset (67.22 vs. 66.63, +0.54 pp), and DFQ (Ito & Doya, 2009) on the Fruit Fly dataset (54.62 vs. 53.99, +0.48 pp), with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.002). Against the GRU recurrent neural network (Cho et al., 2014), CogFunSearch is narrowly outperformed on all three datasets (by 1.11 pp on Human, 0.19 pp on Rat, and 0.09 pp on Fruit Fly), indicating the discovered symbolic programs capture nearly all the predictive structure in the data. The improvement over baselines is most pronounced in the Human dataset, where 95.6% of held-out subjects are better fit by CogFunSearch, compared to 100% for Rat and 83.8% for Fruit Fly. The discovered programs also generate synthetic data that closely matches trial-history regression patterns in the real datasets, and exhibit favorable likelihood landscapes for gradient-based parameter fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024), an LLM-powered evolutionary program synthesis tool, to the domain of cognitive modeling by introducing a bilevel optimization: the outer loop evolves Python programs via LLM mutations, while the inner loop fits per-subject parameters via gradient descent. It departs from prior data-driven cognitive modeling efforts (Miller et al. 2021, Eckstein et al. 2024) that searched over small, hand-specified model spaces, and from symbolic regression approaches (Brunton et al. 2016, LaFollette et al. 2023) that discovered equations but not full sequential decision-making programs. The paper also distinguishes itself from LLM-based statistical model discovery (Li et al. 2024, Shojaee et al. 2025) by optimizing over executable programs with hidden state dynamics rather than closed-form equations, and from interpretable deep learning approaches (Miller et al. 2023) by searching over an explicitly symbolic space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that LLM-guided program synthesis can propose novel, interpretable hypotheses about reward-learning mechanisms across species, outperforming the best human-designed cognitive models in all three datasets. However, a quantitative gap with flexible RNNs remains, and the discovered programs still exhibit readability issues (misleading variable names, unused state variables, tautological lines). Key open questions include whether the dense population of similarly-scoring programs represents distinct computational mechanisms or notational variants, how to improve search efficiency (currently requiring hundreds of thousands of LLM calls), and whether the approach generalizes to more complex cognitive tasks beyond multi-armed bandits.
