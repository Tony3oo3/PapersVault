## Problem

Optimizing over labeled datasets represented as mixtures of class-conditional distributions requires gradient flows on the space of probability measures over probability measures (random measures), but no principled differential structure or gradient flow framework existed for this infinite-dimensional space. Prior works either restricted to finite-dimensional manifolds or imposed a Gaussian parametric assumption on each class-conditional distribution, limiting expressiveness. This paper fills the gap by constructing a full Riemannian and differential structure on the Wasserstein-over-Wasserstein (WoW) space and deriving tractable gradient flows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source dataset and a target dataset, each with C classes and n samples per class, represented as discrete mixtures P = (1/C)Σ δ_{μ^c} where each μ^c is an empirical distribution over image pixels (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FMNIST/KMNIST/USPS, 32×32×3 RGB for CIFAR-10/SVHN). The output is a flowed dataset of the same mixture structure where each class-conditional distribution has been transported toward its target counterpart. For dataset distillation, the output is a compact set of p synthetic samples per class; for k-shot transfer learning, the flowed n samples per class are concatenated with the k true samples to form an augmented training set.

## Method

The paper endows P₂(P₂(ℝᵈ)) with the WoW distance (OT with squared W₂ as ground cost) and constructs a differential structure: a tangent space built from cylinder functions, sub/super-differentials, and a unique WoW gradient. It proves existence of the WoW gradient flow via the JKO scheme for λ-convex functionals along generalized geodesics, then proposes a forward Euler discretization where each class distribution μ^c is updated by a Wasserstein gradient step in the inner space, yielding two-level interactions (intra-class and inter-class). The objective is an MMD between the source and target mixtures using Sliced-Wasserstein-based kernels between distributions (Gaussian SW kernel or Riesz SW kernel K(μ,ν) = −SW₂(μ,ν)). For discrete measures, the WoW gradient is computed via autodifferentiation of the MMD over all particle coordinates, rescaled by nC, making the scheme fully tractable and implemented in JAX.

## Results

On the k-shot transfer learning task (flowing MNIST toward FMNIST, KMNIST, or USPS), MMDSW achieves 40.5% accuracy at k=1 (vs. 30.5% for OTDD and 36.4% for Hua et al.), 65.4% at k=10, and 74.7% at k=100 on MNIST→FMNIST. For dataset distillation on MNIST with p=10 synthetic images per class, MMDSW reaches 93.2% test accuracy in the ambient space, outperforming Distribution Matching (88.2%). MMDSW is also dramatically faster: ~14 seconds per flow versus 294–2294 seconds for OTDD and ~132–164 seconds for Hua et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OTDD (Alvarez-Melis & Fusi, 2021), MMDSW wins in 9 of 12 k-shot settings, with the largest margins at low k (e.g., +10.0 pts at k=1 on M→F, +10.6 pts at k=10 on M→K). OTDD edges out MMDSW only at k=1 and k=5 on M→USPS by ~1–2 pts. Against Hua et al. (2023), MMDSW wins in 7 of 12 settings; Hua et al. has a slight advantage at k=5 and k=10 on M→FMNIST and at k=5 on M→KMNIST (by 1.2–1.6 pts). In dataset distillation (Table 1), MMDSW consistently outperforms Distribution Matching (Zhao & Bilen, 2021) in the ambient space by 4–5 pts at p=1 and 1–3 pts at p=10–50, and remains competitive when a neural embedding is added. Across all experiments, MMDSW is 20–160× faster than OTDD and ~10× faster than Hua et al. (14 s vs. 294–2294 s and 132–164 s respectively), owing to the use of Sliced-Wasserstein projections instead of full 2D OT with Gaussian approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein gradient flow framework (Ambrosio et al., 2008; Jordan et al., 1998) from the space P₂(ℝᵈ) to the space of random measures P₂(P₂(ℝᵈ)) endowed with the Wasserstein over Wasserstein (WoW) distance (Nguyen, 2016). The closest prior works—Alvarez-Melis & Fusi (2021) and Hua et al. (2023)—also flow labeled datasets but circumvent the infinite-dimensional challenge by restricting conditional class distributions to Gaussians, reducing the problem to finite-dimensional parameter optimization. Here, the authors instead build a full differential structure (tangent space, sub/super-differentials, gradient) on the WoW space using cylinder functions and the exp⁻¹ construction, enabling gradient flows over general (non-Gaussian) class-conditional distributions. The objective functional is a Maximum Mean Discrepancy (Gretton et al., 2012) with Sliced-Wasserstein-based kernels (Rabin et al., 2012; Kolouri et al., 2016), combining the MMD gradient flow literature (Arbel et al., 2019; Hertrich et al., 2024b) with the hierarchical OT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous differential structure on the space of probability measures over probability measures with the WoW distance, opening the door to principled optimization (beyond Gaussian approximations) over datasets viewed as random measures. The practical speed advantage over OTDD and Hua et al. (2023) is substantial (~20× faster for 1-shot transfer), making the approach more accessible. However, experiments are limited to small-scale image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) with at most 200 samples per class, and the theoretical treatment assumes compact manifolds; the paper explicitly leaves open the non-compact case, a full continuity equation for WoW flows, and convergence rate guarantees. Broader impact will depend on whether the framework scales to larger, higher-dimensional datasets and whether the two-level (intra/inter-class) interaction structure provides consistent advantages over simpler per-class optimization.
