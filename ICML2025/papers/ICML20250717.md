## Problem

Existing autoregressive video diffusion models (VDMs) re-compute all conditional (overlapping) frames at every autoregressive step, leading to quadratic computational cost as the conditional context extends for long-term generation. This redundancy makes efficient long-video or live-stream generation impractical, since the model must redundantly process frames that have already been generated and denoised in prior steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250717/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by T5) and a first-frame image (encoded by a Stable Diffusion VAE into a 256×256 latent) as input, and autoregressively generates l-frame video chunks (l=16 for T2V, l=8 for video prediction) at 256×256 resolution. The clean prefix of conditional frames can extend up to P_max frames (49 for T2V, 25 for video prediction), providing long-term context without increasing per-step computation. Each autoregressive step produces a denoised chunk that is then appended to the KV-cache queue for subsequent steps.

## Method

Ca2-VDM introduces two key mechanisms on a spatial-temporal Transformer backbone (initialized from Open-Sora v1.0). First, causal generation replaces bidirectional temporal attention with a lower-triangular causal mask so each frame attends only to its prefix, and adds prefix-enhanced spatial attention that concatenates a short sub-prefix (P'=3 frames) into the spatial key/value for stronger conditional guidance. This unidirectional dependency ensures the KV-cache of clean prefix frames can be precomputed once and reused. Second, cache sharing assigns a fixed timestep embedding t=0 to conditional frames (vs. t for noisy frames), making the cached keys/values independent of the denoising timestep so a single cache serves all 100 denoising steps. The temporal KV-cache is stored in a bounded queue of length P_max with Cyclic-TPEs (cyclically shifted temporal positional embeddings) to maintain train/inference alignment when the generated video exceeds the training length, while the spatial KV-cache stores only one chunk and is overwritten each step.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video generation, Ca2-VDM achieves FVD 181 on MSR-VTT (tied with SEINE) and FVD 277.7 on UCF101, competitive with SOTA models like PixelDance (242.8 on UCF101). After finetuning on UCF101, Ca2-VDM achieves FVD 184.5, outperforming all baselines including VideoFusion (220) and VDT (225.7). For autoregressive efficiency, Ca2-VDM generates 80 frames in 52.1s, compared to 77.5s (OS-Fix), 130.1s (OS-Ext), and 150s (StreamT2V), while maintaining extendable long-term context. In temporal consistency (FVD between AR step 1 and step 6), Ca2-VDM scores 304.7, lower than GenLV (310.3), StreamT2V (512.4), and OS-Fix (315.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zero-shot MSR-VTT, Ca2-VDM (FVD 181) ties SEINE (181) and substantially outperforms PixelDance (381), AnimateAnything (443), ModelScope (550), and VideoComposer (580). On zero-shot UCF101, Ca2-VDM (277.7) trails PixelDance (242.8) by ~35 FVD but beats PYoCo (355.2), Make-A-Video (367.2), and Video-LDM (550.6). After finetuning on UCF101, Ca2-VDM (184.5) achieves the best FVD, outperforming VideoFusion (220), VDT (225.7), Latte (333.6), PVDM (343.6), and MCVD (1143). For temporal consistency across autoregressive steps, Ca2-VDM shows lower FVD drift than GenLV, StreamT2V, and the fixed-condition OS-Fix baseline, confirming the benefit of extendable long-term context. In generation speed, Ca2-VDM (52.1s for 80 frames) is 1.5× faster than OS-Fix (77.5s), 2.5× faster than OS-Ext (130.1s), and 2.9× faster than StreamT2V (150s), while being the only method combining extendable condition with sub-linear time growth. In GPU memory, Ca2-VDM uses 0.86 GB for KV-cache (vs. 17.70 GB for Live2diff at T=50), demonstrating the T× savings from cache sharing across denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ca2-VDM builds on the spatial-temporal Transformer architecture of Open-Sora and Latte, and adopts the T5 text encoder from PixArt-α, but fundamentally departs from the bidirectional temporal attention used in all prior autoregressive VDMs (StreamT2V, GenLV, VDT, SEINE). The key conceptual leap is adapting LLM-style KV-caching to the multi-step denoising process of diffusion models: causal (unidirectional) temporal attention makes the conditional frames' features independent of the noisy target, enabling precomputation, while a distinct timestep embedding (t=0) for clean prefix frames allows the same cache to be shared across all denoising steps. This addresses two bottlenecks—redundant recomputation and per-timestep cache storage—that are specific to diffusion-based autoregressive generation and absent in the LLM setting from which the KV-cache idea is borrowed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic computational cost of existing autoregressive VDMs (where all conditional frames are reprocessed at every step) is a major barrier to long-form and real-time video generation; Ca2-VDM reduces this to linear by caching and reusing conditional features, achieving ~2.5× speedup with comparable FVD. The cache-sharing mechanism (T× memory reduction) is particularly important for practical deployment, as it decouples memory cost from the number of denoising steps. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge that finetuning bidirectionally-pretrained weights with causal attention may be suboptimal (causal pretraining from scratch is unexplored), training convergence is slower with extendable conditions and Cyclic-TPEs, and long-term quality degradation in extended generation is only partially mitigated by the KV-cache queue.
