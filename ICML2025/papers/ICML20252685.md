## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors trained on large synthetic datasets suffer notable performance degradation on unseen generative models, and collecting sufficient training data from closed-source models like DALL-E or Midjourney is often expensive or infeasible. The paper reconceptualizes detection as a few-shot classification task, arguing that even a handful of labeled samples from an unseen generator can be leveraged to adapt the detector without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252685/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and a small support set of K labeled samples (typically 10) from the test generator class. It outputs a binary real/fake decision by comparing the query image's embedding against two prototypes: one for the synthetic class (computed from the support set) and one for the real class. A zero-shot variant replaces the support set with precomputed metadata vectors derived from 1024 training samples per class, requiring no test-class samples at all.

## Method

FSD builds on Prototypical Networks with a ResNet-50 backbone (pretrained on ImageNet) that maps images to 1024-dimensional metric-space vectors. The key design choice is treating images from each generative model as a distinct class rather than aggregating all fakes into one, which forces the network to learn intra-generator commonality. Training uses episodic N-way K-shot sampling (3 classes, 5 support + 5 query per class) with cross-entropy loss over Squared Euclidean distances to class prototypes. At test time, the prototype for the unseen generator is computed as the mean embedding of its K support samples, and the query is classified by nearest prototype. This multi-class structure, validated via t-SNE visualizations, produces tighter intra-class clusters for unseen generators compared to a standard binary classifier, enabling more reliable real/fake separation.

## Results

On the GenImage dataset, FSD (10-shot) achieves 84.1% average accuracy across 6 unseen test classes, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art LARE² (72.5%) by +11.6%. The zero-shot variant of FSD also outperforms all baselines, reaching 77.1% average accuracy without any test-class samples. FSD attains the best accuracy on 5 out of 6 test subsets, with the only exception being VQDM (76.2% vs. LARE²'s 76.9%), where the quantization-based generation process makes it harder for the metric-space approach to generalize.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSD (10-shot) decisively outperforms LARE² (Luo et al., 2024), the previous state-of-the-art diffusion-reconstruction-based detector, by +11.6% average accuracy (84.1% vs. 72.5%) on the GenImage benchmark, winning on 5 of 6 test classes. The only class where FSD trails LARE² is VQDM (76.2% vs. 76.9%), which the authors attribute to VQDM's image quantization mechanism being fundamentally different from other diffusion models. Against the frequency-domain baseline F3Net (Qian et al., 2020), FSD achieves a much larger margin of +28.1% average accuracy (84.1% vs. 56.0%), reflecting the limited generalization of artifact-based detectors to modern diffusion-generated images. Notably, even FSD's zero-shot variant (77.1% avg) surpasses LARE²'s full training performance, suggesting that the metric-space representation captures cross-class commonalities that reconstruction-error methods miss. The paper also compares against four additional baselines (Spec, CNNSpot, GramNet, DIRE), all of which fall in the 55–60% average accuracy range, further highlighting the gap between older model-agnostic detectors and the proposed few-shot approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Prototypical Networks (Snell et al., 2017) to AI-generated image detection, a domain previously dominated by binary classifiers (CNNSpot, Spec, F3Net, GramNet) and diffusion-reconstruction methods (DIRE, LARE²). The key conceptual departure is reframing detection as a multi-class few-shot problem where each generative model defines a separate class, rather than aggregating all synthetic images into a single 'fake' category. This reframe allows the model to exploit intra-generator commonality in unseen domains via a small number of labeled support samples, something prior universal-indicator approaches explicitly ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: new generative models appear continuously, and collecting large labeled datasets from closed-source services (Midjourney, DALL-E) is expensive or infeasible, making few-shot adaptation a realistic deployment requirement. The +11.6% average accuracy gain over LARE² with only 10 samples, and the zero-shot result (77.1%) already exceeding the prior SOTA (72.5%), suggest the multi-class-by-generator framing captures structure that binary detectors miss. However, the method's significant failure on VQDM (a vector-quantized model architecturally distant from diffusion/GAN training data) reveals that the approach still cannot generalize across fundamentally different generative paradigms, leaving open how to handle truly novel model families without any representative training data.
