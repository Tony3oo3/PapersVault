## Problem

Deep learning-based automatic modulation classification (AMC) suffers from a systematic prediction ambiguity phenomenon where models output near-identical probabilities for confusable modulation types (e.g., 8PSK vs. QPSK), affecting roughly 25% of training samples across diverse datasets and architectures. Existing AMC research focuses on model architecture or input representation, leaving no regularization mechanism that explicitly targets and suppresses this prediction-space uncertainty with gradient behavior that adapts to the severity of ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard radio signal IQ samples (length 128 for RADIOML 2016.10a/10b, length 1024 for RADIOML 2018.01A) and outputs a modulation class label, but its distinctive interface is that FR operates entirely in the prediction space: it takes the top-k softmax probabilities from the final layer of any existing AMC model as its input signal. This makes FR a model-agnostic plug-in regularization rather than a new architecture, requiring no modification to the backbone network's input pipeline or output head.

## Method

FR is a prediction-space regularization with an adaptive gradient mechanism designed to penalize ambiguous softmax distributions while naturally reducing its own gradient as ambiguity diminishes. The theoretical analysis (Section 3.1) shows that when a model's discriminative ability between similar classes is weak, the expected cross-entropy loss is minimized at uniform predictions (u=0.5), explaining why models 'lazily' smooth their outputs. Ambiguity is quantified via a normalized L2-norm variance of the top-k predicted probabilities (T in Eq. 11), and the final FR loss (Eq. 10) combines this per-sample ambiguity with a batch-level entropy term (σ) inside a log-normal distribution form, so that the gradient magnitude is large when ambiguity is severe and shrinks as predictions sharpen. The log transformation ensures the regularization gradient follows an approximately 1/x decay profile, preventing destabilizing updates once the model has already resolved most ambiguity. FR is validated as a drop-in addition to five heterogeneous backbones (ResNet, DAELSTM, MCLDNN, Three-Stream, FEAT) across three RADIOML datasets, consistently improving accuracy by 1–5% and robustness under 20–60% additive white noise.

## Results

On RADIOML 2016.10a, FR-AMC achieves 81.68% ACC and 84.90% H-ACC, outperforming the best baseline (DAELSTM at 78.55%/80.84%) by ~3.1%/4.1%. On RADIOML 2016.10b, it reaches 92.62% ACC and 93.58% H-ACC versus DAELSTM's 91.63%/93.02%. On the more challenging RADIOML 2018.01A (24 classes, 1024-length samples), FR-AMC achieves 86.75% ACC and 96.62% H-ACC, surpassing the best baseline ThreeStream (82.08%/93.74%) by 4.67%/2.88%. Under 60% noise on Noise2016b, MCLDNN+FR improves ACC from 59.02% to 74.31% (+15.3%), demonstrating substantial robustness gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FR-AMC outperforms all five baselines (DAELSTM, FEAT, MCLDNN, ThreeStream, ResNet) across all three datasets on every metric (F1, ACC, H-ACC). The largest gains appear on Data2018: ThreeStream+FR jumps from 82.08% to 86.75% ACC (+4.67%) and 93.74% to 96.62% H-ACC (+2.88%), while MCLDNN+FR improves from 80.28% to 81.48% ACC. On Data2016a, MCLDNN benefits most from FR (69.66%→73.79% ACC, +4.13%), reflecting its high prediction-ambiguity proportion (35.2%). On Data2016b, where baselines already achieve ~91–93% ACC, improvements are modest (0.2–1.5%), consistent with the paper's observation that FR's corrective effect diminishes when the classification plane is already near-optimal. Under noisy conditions (60% noise factor), FR provides the most dramatic robustness gains: MCLDNN+FR recovers 15.3% ACC on Noise2016b (59.02%→74.31%) and ThreeStream+FR recovers 6.97% on Noise2018 (12.93%→19.90%), far exceeding the 1–3% gains seen in cleaner settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by shifting the regularization focus from model architecture and input representation (the dominant paradigm in AMC: CNN-LSTM dual-streams, complex-valued networks, attention mechanisms) to the prediction space itself. It draws on the broader regularization literature—label smoothing, gradient diversity, margin prototype constraints—but departs from these by targeting a specifically identified phenomenon (prediction ambiguity) and coupling the penalty with an adaptive gradient mechanism whose magnitude scales with the degree of ambiguity. The theoretical analysis, showing that under weak inter-class discriminability the model's 'lazy' smoothing of the softmax distribution is in fact the expected-loss optimum, provides a principled justification that prior AMC regularization work has not offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that ~25% of training samples across diverse models and datasets exhibit prediction ambiguity gives the phenomenon empirical credibility and suggests a broadly applicable diagnostic for fine-grained classification tasks beyond AMC. The model-agnostic nature of FR (plug-in regularization term) makes it practically attractive for practitioners who do not want to redesign architectures. However, the theoretical analysis relies on strong simplifying assumptions (binary classification, iid predictions, constant u), and the adaptive gradient form appears somewhat heuristic rather than derived from first principles. Open questions include whether the specific log-normal batch-level formulation is optimal, whether the approach transfers to other fine-grained classification domains (e.g., fine-grained image recognition), and whether the modest 1–4% gains persist at higher SNR regimes where ambiguity is less prevalent.
