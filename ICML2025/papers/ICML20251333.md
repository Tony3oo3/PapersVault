## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion methods for real-world image super-resolution suffer from two critical issues: fine-tuning a pre-trained text-to-image model on SR data causes a generative distribution shift that degrades image quality, and distilling a large student model (e.g., 12B-parameter FLUX) from an equally large teacher is computationally infeasible due to memory constraints. This paper addresses both by proposing a distillation framework that preserves the teacher's learned data distribution while making training tractable for very large models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (degraded via the RealESRGAN pipeline) and produces a high-resolution RGB image in exactly one sampling step, operating in the latent space of FLUX.1-dev's VAE. Training requires no real image data—only 2,400 noise-image pairs of size 1024×1024 precomputed offline by the FLUX model. The generator is parameterized as a LoRA adapter on the frozen FLUX transformer, so inference requires only one forward pass through the 12B-parameter model at a single time step T_L.

## Method

The core contribution is Flow Trajectory Distillation (FTD), which exploits the linearity of the rectified flow trajectory to derive the LR-to-HR velocity field u_t^SR as the difference between the noise-to-image velocity u_t and the noise-to-LR velocity u_t^L, thereby keeping the T2I endpoint distribution fixed and avoiding distribution shift. To make training feasible for a 12B-parameter model, the authors precompute noise-image pairs offline from FLUX and exclude the teacher from the training loop entirely, parameterizing u_t^SR directly with a single model that represents u_t^L over t ∈ [T_L, 1] and u_t^SR at t = T_L. Two anti-artifact losses are introduced: TV-LPIPS, which applies LPIPS to total-variation-filtered images to suppress high-frequency periodic artifacts while preserving edges, and Attention Diversification Loss (ADL), which penalizes cosine similarity between each token and the mean token across all transformer layers to break repetitive 16-pixel periodic patterns. The full training objective combines the FTD flow-matching loss, an MSE + TV-LPIPS reconstruction loss, and the ADL regularization, optimized via LoRA fine-tuning for 30k iterations.

## Results

On the RealSR testset (×4), FluxSR achieves the best no-reference scores among all one-step diffusion methods: MUSIQ 68.95, MANIQA 0.5335, TOPIQ 0.6699, and Q-Align 4.3781, surpassing the next-best one-step method TSD-SR (MUSIQ 68.31, MANIQA 0.4899, TOPIQ 0.6568, Q-Align 4.0926). On RealSet65, FluxSR leads all one-step baselines with MUSIQ 70.75, MANIQA 0.5495, TOPIQ 0.6670, and Q-Align 4.2134. On DIV2K-val, FluxSR again tops one-step methods on all NR metrics (MUSIQ 68.72, MANIQA 0.4615, TOPIQ 0.6426, Q-Align 4.6128). In full-reference metrics, FluxSR is competitive but trails SinSR and OSEDiff on PSNR/SSIM, consistent with the known fidelity–perceptual-quality trade-off in diffusion-based SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among one-step diffusion SR methods, FluxSR achieves the best no-reference quality (MUSIQ, MANIQA, TOPIQ, Q-Align) on all three test datasets (RealSR, DIV2K-val, RealSet65), with the largest margin over TSD-SR on Q-Align (+0.28 on RealSR, +0.46 on DIV2K-val) and MANIQA (+0.044 on RealSR). Against multi-step baselines, FluxSR outperforms StableSR (200 steps) on every NR metric across all datasets, and matches or exceeds DiffBIR (50 steps) on Q-Align while trailing slightly on TOPIQ (0.6699 vs. 0.6618 on RealSR; 0.6670 vs. 0.7015 on RealSet65). On full-reference metrics, FluxSR's PSNR (24.83) and SSIM (0.7175) on RealSR are lower than SinSR (25.83 / 0.7183) and OSEDiff (24.57 / 0.7202), reflecting the fidelity–perceptual trade-off; the authors note that PSNR/SSIM have limited reference value for diffusion-based SR. On RealSet65 (no ground truth), FluxSR's MUSIQ of 70.75 still trails DiffBIR's 71.23 and SeeSR's 69.79, but leads all one-step methods by a clear margin over TSD-SR (69.34) and OSEDiff (69.04). Overall, FluxSR's advantage is most pronounced in perceptual realism metrics, confirming that the FTD + TV-LPIPS + ADL combination effectively preserves the FLUX teacher's generative quality in a single step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step diffusion super-resolution line (OSEDiff, SinSR, TSD-SR, ADDSR) by replacing direct fine-tuning of a pre-trained T2I model with Flow Trajectory Distillation (FTD), which derives the LR-to-HR velocity field as the difference between the noise-to-HR and noise-to-LR flows under rectified flow's linearity. The theoretical grounding draws on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and the rectified flow trajectory (Liu et al., 2022), while the idea of training a separate flow model on LR data to approximate the LR distribution is borrowed from DMD (Yin et al., 2024). A key practical departure is the offline precomputation of noise-image pairs from the teacher, eliminating the need for a second 12B-parameter model during training — a constraint that made prior one-step distillation (e.g., OSEDiff) infeasible on FLUX-scale models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FluxSR is the first one-step diffusion SR method built on a 12B-parameter flow matching model, demonstrating that FTD plus offline flow precomputation makes such scale tractable. The FTD formulation offers a principled way to preserve the teacher's generative distribution by construction, addressing the distribution-shift problem that plagues fine-tuning-based one-step methods. However, the authors acknowledge that 16-pixel periodic artifacts persist and that the model remains computationally expensive, leaving open whether the approach can be compressed (e.g., via pruning) without sacrificing the realism gains that come from the large base model. The moderate but consistent gains on no-reference metrics over other one-step methods suggest the large-model prior is genuinely valuable for perceptual quality, even when fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM) do not improve.
