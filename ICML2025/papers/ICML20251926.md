## Problem

Ensemble distillation methods struggle to transfer the full diversity of ensemble teacher predictions to a single student model. Existing approaches either collapse to the mean of ensemble predictions (losing diversity entirely) or approximate only point estimates via Schrödinger bridges, failing to model the induced distribution over categorical probabilities. This paper asks whether flow matching can effectively learn the full predictive distribution of an ensemble teacher, and under what conditions (i.e., teacher diversity levels) such distribution-level distillation actually improves student performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input (32×32 RGB image or a multiple-choice text question) and a pretrained teacher network's penultimate-layer feature vector as conditioning. The lightweight student (a denoising MLP with 0.33M–1.6M parameters) operates in K-dimensional logit space and, via ODE integration with a Heun solver, produces multiple sampled logits (e.g., 256 ensemble predictions) from a single Gaussian noise draw. These logits are then softmaxed to yield class probability vectors; the entire ensemble is generated with as few as 5–7 network function evaluations, and the teacher backbone is executed only once per input regardless of the number of ensemble samples.

## Method

EDFM formulates ensemble distribution distillation as a conditional flow matching problem in logit space: a denoising MLP learns a vector field u_φ(t, z_t^x, x̄) that transports Gaussian noise N(0, σ²I) to the empirical distribution of ensemble teacher logits p₁(z₁^x|x), following a rectified-flow (linear interpolation) probability path. Training minimizes a conditional flow matching loss with v-prediction, an exponential time distribution concentrated near t=1, and EDM-style preconditioning that normalizes input/output scales. At inference, the student integrates the learned ODE with a Heun solver over 2–3 exponentially scheduled steps, producing diverse logits in parallel via batched MLP inference. The key design choices—operating pre-softmax in logit space rather than on the probability simplex, using a simple rectified flow rather than more complex formulations, and conditioning on frozen teacher features—yield superior fidelity to the teacher's predictive distribution while keeping the student orders of magnitude smaller than the teacher backbone.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EDFM achieves 93.1% ACC, 0.216 NLL, and 0.009 ECE, approaching the Multi-SWAG teacher (94.6% ACC, 0.167 NLL, 0.007 ECE) while using a single forward pass plus a lightweight MLP. On CIFAR-100, EDFM reaches 76.1% ACC, 0.932 NLL, and 0.056 ECE, closing the gap to the teacher (77.7% ACC, 0.835 NLL, 0.018 ECE). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-2-7B, EDFM improves over the IVON baseline on all three tasks (e.g., ARC-E: 89.2% ACC vs. 88.8%, 0.390 NLL vs. 0.645 NLL) using only 1.6M additional parameters and 3 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, EDFM achieves the best NLL (0.216) and ECE (0.009) among all distillation methods, outperforming KD (0.229 NLL, 0.020 ECE), EnDD (0.228, 0.023), DBN (0.218, 0.028), and FED (0.238, 0.009), though EnDD and DBN edge it out on ACC by 0.5–0.6%. On CIFAR-100, EDFM dominates all baselines on NLL (0.932 vs. 0.965–1.031) and ECE (0.056 vs. 0.073–0.098), while matching EnDD on ACC (76.1%). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-2-7B, EDFM substantially outperforms KD on all three tasks: ARC-C (71.5% vs. 66.2% ACC, 0.142 vs. 0.218 ECE), ARC-E (89.2% vs. 85.0% ACC, 0.390 vs. 0.570 NLL), and OBQA (81.8% vs. 77.6% ACC, 0.537 vs. 0.720 NLL), approaching the Multi-IVON teacher's calibration quality with only 1.6M extra parameters. The paper also shows EDFM achieves the best fidelity metrics (TVD, KLD, JSD, W₂) to the teacher's predictive distribution across all diversity settings, and scales ensemble predictions with near-linear NLL improvement at only marginal runtime cost compared to the full Multi-SWAG ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ensemble distribution distillation literature (EnDD, FED, DBN) by replacing parametric distribution assumptions (e.g., Dirichlet) or point-estimate Schrödinger bridges with a flow matching framework that learns a vector field from Gaussian noise to the full distribution of teacher logits. It builds directly on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023b) for the probability path, the denoising MLP architecture (Li et al., 2024) for the student network, and EDM preconditioning (Karras et al., 2022) for stable training. The key conceptual departure from DBN (Kim et al., 2024) is modeling the induced distribution q(π_x|x) over categorical probabilities rather than approximating only the ensemble's mean prediction, and the key departure from Dirichlet-based methods is removing the parametric constraint on the induced distribution entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a 0.33M-parameter MLP student produces ensemble-quality predictions in ~0.05ms per sample versus ~1.4ms for a single ResNet forward pass, enabling scalable parallel sampling that matches Multi-SWAG teacher NLL with marginal overhead. The paper's central empirical finding—that distillation methods only succeed 'where the diversity is' and that merely generating high-variance predictions is insufficient without genuine fidelity to the teacher's distribution—provides a useful diagnostic framework for practitioners. Open questions include whether more sophisticated flow matching formulations (better time distributions, simplex-constrained flows) can close the remaining gap to the teacher, and whether the approach extends to continuous-output tasks beyond classification.
