## Problem

Existing pipeline parallelism tools for large ML models require users to manually specify cutpoints, rewrite models in special libraries, or annotate partitions, making them inaccessible to typical ML practitioners. Moreover, these tools cannot handle non-sequential models (e.g., Stable Diffusion with skip connections) or mix pipelined modules with frozen pretrained components within a single training loop. Pfeife addresses this by providing the first fully automatic, transparent pipeline parallelism tool integrated directly into PyTorch's compilation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The input is a standard PyTorch model compiled via torch.compile (which triggers TorchDynamo to capture a static TorchFX data-flow graph) along with training mini-batches. The user wraps their forward-to-backward training loop in a single closure passed to a PipelineRunner; Pfeife intercepts execution at the compiled module boundary, slices inputs into micro-batches, and dispatches them across worker processes on multiple GPUs. The output is a pipelined execution schedule (a fused graph plus operation dependency graph) that runs transparently in place of the original model, with the user only observing the final loss. The system supports mixing the pipelined module with arbitrary frozen or non-pipelined modules (e.g., a pretrained autoencoder) within the same closure.

## Method

Pfeife operates as a torch.compile backend: after TorchDynamo captures the data-flow graph, Pfeife profiles each operation's compute time and memory, then co-optimizes graph slicing (split points) and scheduling parameters (total batch count B, loop count N_l, loop batch count B_l, and per-device prefetch counts B_f) using beam search guided by a critical-path cost model. The cost model interprets the operation dependency graph as a DAG and computes estimated latency via longest-path (Dijkstra with negated weights), making it agnostic to the specific pipeline schedule. A key finding is that uneven slicing combined with prefetching forward passes before the first backward pass is often optimal, contradicting the common assumption of balanced partitioning; the beam search explicitly explores this by moving split points asymmetrically to fill pipeline bubbles. The resulting schedule is expressed in a general specification language that subsumes GPipe, 1F1B, looped-1F1B, BFS, and TeraPipe schedules, and the runtime executes it across worker processes with support for partial pipeline parallelism combined with data parallelism.

## Results

On ViT-g/14, Pfeife achieves up to 22% higher throughput than DeepSpeed (151.4 vs. 124.5 item/s at 4 devices, 4 micro-batches) and up to 22% over Colossal-AI (151.4 vs. 122.6 item/s). On Llama2-7B, where DeepSpeed's ZeRO-DP runs out of memory, Pfeife achieves 7.46–8.81 item/s, outperforming Colossal-AI by up to 10%. On the non-sequential Stable Diffusion XL model, Pfeife matches ZeRO2 throughput while being 3–4× faster than ZeRO3 and uniquely supporting FP32 training without OOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSpeed (ZeRO 2 & 3), Pfeife wins on all ViT configurations except the largest (8 devices, 16 micro-batches, where DeepSpeed's maturity gives it a 5.5% edge). On Llama2-7B, DeepSpeed's ZeRO-DP simply runs out of memory, making it infeasible, while Pfeife trains successfully at 7.46–8.81 item/s. Against Colossal-AI, Pfeife consistently outperforms on ViT (by 2–22%, with the largest gaps at smaller device counts where pipeline bubbles dominate) and on Llama2-7B (by 1.4–10%). The paper attributes Pfeife's advantage to two factors: prefetching forward passes before the first backward pass (filling pipeline bubbles) and looped schedules (reducing the initial bubble), neither of which is available in the baselines. On the non-sequential SDXL model, Pfeife is comparable to ZeRO2 but 3–4× faster than ZeRO3, and is the only method that avoids OOM in FP32.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pfeife builds on PyTorch 2's TorchDynamo/TorchFX graph capture mechanism (Ansel et al., 2024) and adopts DAPPLE's (Fan et al., 2021) profiling-before-scheduling strategy, while parameterizing schedules using the looped 1F1B framework from Megatron-LM (Narayanan et al., 2021). It departs from prior pipeline tools (PiPPy, Varuna, Colossal-AI) by requiring no manual cutpoint specification or model rewriting, and from AutoPipe by co-optimizing graph slicing and scheduling simultaneously rather than fixing one before optimizing the other. A key conceptual departure is the discovery that uneven slicing combined with prefetching forward passes often outperforms balanced partitioning, directly contradicting AutoPipe's assumption that balanced partitioning is optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pfeife is the first tool to provide fully transparent, automatic pipeline parallelism within PyTorch's native compilation pipeline, removing the distributed-systems expertise barrier for ML practitioners and enabling training of models (e.g., Llama2-7B, Stable Diffusion XL) that exceed single-GPU memory. The finding that unbalanced schedules with prefetching are frequently optimal challenges a widely held heuristic and could inform future scheduler design beyond Pfeife. Limitations include dependence on torch.compile (11/49 TorchBench models fail to compile), single-node evaluation (8 GPUs max), and the linearization approximation that may degrade for models with extensive parallel branches. Open questions include multi-node scaling, interaction with tensor parallelism, and whether the beam-search heuristic generalizes to graphs with thousands of parallel branches.
