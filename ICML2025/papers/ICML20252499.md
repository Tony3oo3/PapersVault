## Problem

Pre-trained SO(3) equivariant GNNs (e.g., MACE) are the backbone of modern interatomic potentials, but standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA and adapters destroy the rotational equivariance that guarantees physically consistent predictions. No PEFT strategy had been designed to preserve equivariance during fine-tuning of these models, leaving a gap between the data efficiency benefits of PEFT and the symmetry constraints required by atomistic simulations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a molecular or crystal graph where each node (atom) carries features encoded as spherical harmonic coefficients h_{i,klm} indexed by rotation order l, magnetic quantum number m, and channel k, with edges defined by interatomic distances. It outputs a scalar total energy (invariant under rotation) and per-atomic force vectors (equivariant under rotation). Fine-tuning is applied to pre-trained MACE-OFF (organic, pre-trained on SPICE) or MACE-MP (inorganic, pre-trained on MPTrj) backbones, and the ELoRA parameters can be merged into the original weights at inference with zero additional latency.

## Method

ELoRA introduces a path-dependent low-rank decomposition for weight updates in SO(3) equivariant message passing networks. The key insight is that the three fundamental operations in EMPNNs—point convolution, self-interaction, and residual connection—can all be expressed as a unified fully connected tensor product, where learnable weights W_{k3 k2 k1, l3 l2 l1} couple input channels across rotation-order paths (l1, l2, l3). Rather than applying LoRA's single global low-rank factorization to the entire weight matrix, ELoRA decomposes the update independently along each valid path: ΔW_{l3 l2 l1} = B_{l3 l2 l1} · A_{l3 l2 l1}, where A projects the equivariant message on that path into a low-dimensional space of rank R and B maps it back to output channels. This path-wise structure respects the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient constraints that define which (l1, l2, l3) combinations are non-zero, thereby provably preserving SO(3) equivariance. The authors further derive PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds showing that the reduced stable rank of ΔW under ELoRA yields tighter generalization guarantees than full-parameter fine-tuning, explaining the observed data-efficiency advantage.

## Results

On the rMD17 organic benchmark (50 training configurations per molecule), ELoRA fine-tuning of MACE-OFF achieves a 25.5% average improvement in energy MAE and 23.7% in force MAE over full-parameter fine-tuning, setting new state-of-the-art across all 10 molecules. On 10 inorganic datasets (metals, alloys, water/ice, oxides, solid-state electrolytes), ELoRA fine-tuning of MACE-MP yields average improvements of 12.3% in energy RMSE and 14.4% in force RMSE over full-parameter fine-tuning. On the 3BPA extrapolation task, the accuracy gap between ELoRA and full-parameter fine-tuning grows from 0.3 meV (300 K) to 2.7 meV (1200 K) for energy, demonstrating superior out-of-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELoRA fine-tuning outperforms all from-scratch baselines (ACE, NequIP, PACE, MACE, Allegro, BOTNet, DPA2) on every organic and inorganic dataset tested. On rMD17, ELoRA achieves the best energy and force MAE for all 10 molecules, e.g., 7.3 meV energy / 17.6 meV/Å force on Aspirin vs. 15.7/37.4 for PACE (the best from-scratch method) and 10.3/23.6 for full-parameter fine-tuning. On the 10 inorganic datasets, ELoRA achieves the best force RMSE on 9 out of 10 (e.g., 4.4 meV/Å on Cu vs. 5.4 for full-parameter FT and 8.9 for DPA2), while for energy it slightly trails DPA2 on a few datasets due to architectural differences. The pre-trained MACE-MP without fine-tuning performs significantly worse than from-scratch models on inorganic tasks (e.g., 381.1 meV/Å force on Cu vs. 8.9 for DPA2), confirming that fine-tuning is essential. ELoRA also outperforms Adapter fine-tuning (which destroys equivariance) by large margins, e.g., 4.4 vs. 22.9 meV/Å force on Cu, and Readout-only fine-tuning (5.6 vs. 4.4 meV/Å on Cu), validating the path-dependent decomposition design.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELoRA extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) from transformer-based LLMs to SO(3) equivariant GNNs by replacing LoRA's global low-rank decomposition with a path-dependent one that respects the Clebsch-Gordan structure of tensor products. The paper's key conceptual move is proving that the three core operations in equivariant message passing (point convolution, self-interaction, residual connection) all reduce to a unified fully-connected tensor product formalism, which then admits a single equivariant PEFT strategy. This departs from prior PEFT-for-GNN work (AdapterGNN, GraphPrompt) that was designed for non-equivariant architectures and would destroy rotational symmetry when applied to models like MACE or NequIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELoRA is the first PEFT method that provably preserves SO(3) equivariance during fine-tuning, removing a fundamental barrier to adapting pre-trained equivariant interatomic potentials to new chemical systems without breaking physical symmetries. The PAC-Bayesian analysis connecting low-rank updates to tighter generalization bounds provides a theoretical explanation for why ELoRA outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning in the small-data regime, though the gap narrows as data grows. Open questions include whether other decomposition strategies (beyond path-dependent) could further improve efficiency, whether few-shot or one-shot fine-tuning is achievable, and whether rigorous error generalization bounds can be established beyond the current stable-rank arguments.
