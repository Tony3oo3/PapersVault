## Problem

As Graph Neural Networks grow deeper, node representations converge to indistinguishable states (oversmoothing), severely limiting expressiveness. Existing mitigations—skip connections, normalization, attention mechanisms—address symptoms through architectural patches without explaining the underlying convergence dynamics. This paper identifies oversmoothing as an emergent property of GNNs converging to low-dimensional attractor states and asks how to dynamically alter the attractor landscape to preserve node diversity in deep architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and per-node feature vectors, processing them through a 128-layer GAT with dynamic attention pruning at each layer. At every layer, Gaussian noise is injected into node features before computing a pairwise covariance matrix that drives the pruning decision. The output is a set of node embeddings and class labels, with the key distinction that attention weights are progressively pruned (not binary-masked) and recalibrated after each pruning step, yielding a sparser but more expressive deep network.

## Method

DYNAMO-GAT reconceptualizes the GAT message-passing as a dynamical system and shows via contraction mapping and spectral analysis that node features converge to a low-dimensional attractor whose dimension is bounded by the spectral gap of the fixed-point attention matrix. To counteract this, it injects independent Gaussian noise into node features at each layer, computes the resulting covariance matrix, and applies an Anti-Hebbian pruning criterion that targets connections between highly correlated nodes—thereby widening the spectral gap. Pruning is gradual (weights are multiplicatively reduced by a probability derived from the covariance term C_ii + C_jj ∓ 2C_ij, a dynamic threshold, and a layer-wise increasing rate), followed by re-normalization of surviving attention weights to maintain the stochastic row-sum constraint. Theoretical guarantees show the pruned Jacobian's spectral radius decreases, the spectral gap increases monotonically, and the covariance rank is preserved up to a bounded loss, ensuring feature diversity is maintained across all 128 layers.

## Results

On the three real-world node classification benchmarks (128-layer deep setting), DYNAMO-GAT achieves 83.21% on Cora, 82.01% on Citeseer, and 62.56% on Cornell, matching or exceeding the strongest baseline G2GAT (83.27%, 82.06%, 61.55%) while using roughly half the GFLOPS (e.g., 0.605 vs 1.209 on Cora). The Accuracy/GFLOPS ratio is the standout metric: DYNAMO-GAT scores 137.53 on Cora and 1226.67 on Cornell, compared to 68.88 and 700.34 for G2GAT and 35.11 and 306.52 for GAT. On large-scale OGB graphs, DYNAMO-GAT reaches 75.3% on ogbn-products (vs 73.9% for G2GAT) and 72.1% on ogbn-arxiv (vs 72.5% for G2GAT), again with substantially lower compute (14.5 vs 22.1 GFLOPS on products).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCN (2 layers), DYNAMO-GAT (128 layers) wins by 1.71 pp on Cora, 6.31 pp on Citeseer, and 8.36 pp on Cornell, while also matching GCN's GFLOPS on Cora (0.605 vs 0.598) and beating it on the Accuracy/GFLOPS ratio across all three datasets. Against GAT, the accuracy gap is larger (up to 6.26 pp on Cornell) and the computational advantage is dramatic: DYNAMO-GAT uses ~4× fewer GFLOPS on Cora and ~4× fewer on Citeseer despite being 32–64× deeper. The closest comparison is G2GAT (also 128 layers): DYNAMO-GAT trails by a negligible 0.06 pp on Cora and 0.05 pp on Citeseer but clearly wins on Cornell (+1.01 pp) and on ogbn-products (+1.4 pp). Crucially, DYNAMO-GAT halves the GFLOPS of G2GAT on every dataset (e.g., 0.605 vs 1.209 on Cora; 14.5 vs 22.1 on ogbn-products), yielding roughly 2× higher Accuracy/GFLOPS ratios, making it the most compute-efficient deep GNN in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYNAMO-GAT extends the GAT architecture (Veličković et al., 2018) by introducing a noise-driven, covariance-guided pruning mechanism inspired by Anti-Hebbian learning principles from neuroscience. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of oversmoothing mitigation work (skip connections, normalization, residual architectures) by reframing the problem through dynamical systems theory—characterizing oversmoothing as convergence to a low-dimensional attractor governed by the spectral gap of the attention matrix. The pruning strategy draws on prior noise-based pruning ideas (Moore & Chaudhuri, 2020) and graph sparsification (Spielman & Srivastava, 2011), but targets feature-dynamics rather than structural fidelity. The closest empirical competitor is G2GAT (Rusch et al., 2023a), which also targets deep GNNs but through gradient gating rather than attractor-aware pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamical systems formalization (fixed-point existence, spectral gap bounds, attractor dimension characterization) provides a theoretical lens for oversmoothing that prior work has largely addressed only empirically, potentially guiding design of future deep GNN architectures beyond GATs. However, the empirical gains over G2GAT are modest (within ~1% accuracy on most datasets), and the primary advantage is computational efficiency (lower GFLOPS at 128 layers) rather than a large accuracy breakthrough. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to graph-level tasks, extremely sparse or heterophilic graphs beyond OGB, and whether the stochastic noise injection introduces training instability at scale. The authors acknowledge limitations on very sparse graphs and note future work in physics/chemistry domains.
