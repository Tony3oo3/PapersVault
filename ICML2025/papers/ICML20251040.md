## Problem

Serving multiple fine-tuned MoE LLMs on a single GPU is impractical with conventional virtualization: NVIDIA MIG splits PCIe bandwidth across instances, nearly doubling expert-loading latency, while time-sharing requires up to two minutes to swap entire models. No existing approach can simultaneously serve multiple MoE variants at throughput comparable to single-model inference without severe quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251040/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a text prompt (20 tokens in experiments) paired with a target model identifier and produces up to 25 generated text tokens. A key operational detail is that the target model identifier triggers a runtime check: if it differs from the currently loaded model, the non-expert parameters (~3 GB) are swapped via PCIe before inference begins, adding ~1.2 s to TTFT only on model switches. All model weights reside in CPU DRAM, and the GPU holds a consolidated subset of experts plus one model's non-expert layers at any time.

## Method

The approach combines two mechanisms. First, similarity-based expert consolidation computes L2 distances between experts at identical (layer, expert) positions across all served models, ranks locations by ascending distance, and assigns the most similar locations to models in round-robin order, loading them onto GPU as a shared expert pool. Second, runtime partial reconfiguration swaps only the non-expert parameters (~3 GB, ~1 s over PCIe) when a request targets a different model, preserving the consolidated expert layout. During inference, if the gate selects an expert already resident on GPU (a hit), it is used regardless of which model it originated from; on a miss, the correct expert is fetched from CPU DRAM, guaranteeing that at least the non-expert layers and any missing experts belong to the requested model. This design keeps throughput near single-model levels while incurring only a ~0.5 s average TTFT penalty.

## Results

On a single NVIDIA A100 (80 GB) serving two Mixtral-8x7B variants, the proposed system achieves a mean turnaround time of 8.78 s and TTFT of 1.41 s, compared to 49.67 s and 5.86 s for NVIDIA MIG — an 85 % reduction in turnaround time. Throughput matches single-model serving (ridge point at λ = 0.060 req/s) while MIG plateaus earlier (λ = 0.040). On output quality, the consolidated model scores 8.16/10 on MT-Bench (vs. 8.13 for Instruct, 7.99 for weight-averaging), 71.7 % on MMLU, and 82.2 % on HellaSwag. For scalability with four Switch Transformer variants, ROUGE-1 on SAMSum stays at 0.46 versus 0.256 for the averaging baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NVIDIA MIG, the proposed system wins decisively on all QoS metrics: turnaround time drops from 49.67 s to 8.78 s (≈85 % reduction), TTFT from 5.86 s to 1.41 s, and the throughput ridge point shifts from λ = 0.040 to λ = 0.060 req/s, matching single-model serving capacity. The per-layer expert-miss overhead is also roughly halved (27.8 ms vs. 54.1 ms per expert) because MIG splits PCIe bandwidth across two instances. Against the weight-averaging baseline (Izmailov et al., 2018), the proposed method wins on MT-Bench (8.16 vs. 7.99), WikiText perplexity (3.85 vs. 3.89), C4 perplexity (7.33 vs. 7.38), and HellaSwag (82.2 % vs. 82.0 %), but trails slightly on MMLU (71.7 % vs. 72.2 %) and TruthfulQA (70.6 % vs. 73.0 %). On the scalability experiment with four Switch Transformer variants, the gap widens sharply: the averaging baseline's ROUGE-1 degrades from 0.49 to 0.256 as models are added, while the proposed consolidation holds at 0.46, demonstrating substantially greater resilience to the number of merged variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-model MoE offloading paradigm of Eliseev & Mazur (2023) to the multi-model setting, where multiple fine-tuned variants of the same MoE architecture must share a single GPU. It draws on two distinct research threads: (1) model merging at the parameter level (Izmailov et al., 2018; Matena & Raffel, 2022), which it adapts to expert-level granularity using L2-distance-based similarity and round-robin assignment, and (2) partial runtime reconfiguration from reconfigurable computing (El-Araby et al., 2009), repurposed here to swap non-expert layers on the fly. Unlike prior expert-pruning or expert-caching work that targets a single model's memory footprint, this paper explicitly addresses the multi-tenant QoS problem where conventional space-sharing (NVIDIA MIG) and time-sharing both fail due to PCIe bandwidth splitting and full-model swap latency, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment of multiple fine-tuned MoE LLMs on a single GPU is an increasingly common constraint for organizations that cannot afford multi-GPU clusters, and this paper shows that a simple expert-consolidation-plus-partial-reconfiguration strategy can approach single-model throughput while preserving output quality. However, the evaluation is limited to single-batch requests, two model families, and one GPU, and does not integrate with modern inference optimizations (Flash Attention, continuous batching, speculative decoding) that are now standard in production serving stacks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to heterogeneous MoE architectures with different expert counts, whether the round-robin expert assignment remains optimal under skewed request distributions, and how quality degrades as the number of served variants grows beyond four.
