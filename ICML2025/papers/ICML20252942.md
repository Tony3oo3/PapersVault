## Problem

Previous work on distributed nonparametric estimation under communication constraints only covered either the dense-samples regime (m < n^γ with γ < 2r) or the extreme sparse case of a single sample per terminal (n=1), leaving all intermediate regimes unresolved. This paper fully characterizes the minimax optimal rates for all parameter regimes as samples per terminal vary from sparse to dense, revealing phase transitions in the dependence on the communication budget l.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252942/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of m terminals observes n i.i.d. samples X_ij = (T_ij, Y_ij) drawn from a distribution p_f parameterized by an unknown function f in the Sobolev ball H^r([0,1],L), and communicates an l-bit message in a sequentially interactive protocol. The decoder produces a reconstruction f̂ in H^r([0,1],L), and the paper characterizes the minimax rate R(m,n,l,r) up to logarithmic factors via an effective sample size N_ess that captures four distinct regimes determined by the relative magnitudes of m, n, and l. The framework subsumes density estimation, Gaussian, binary, Poisson, and heteroskedastic regression as special cases.

## Method

The upper bound is achieved by a two-layer estimation protocol: the outer layer applies a wavelet approximation at resolution H, truncates and quantizes sample-wise wavelet coefficient estimators into a finite-alphabet random variable W, thereby converting the nonparametric problem into a parametric distribution estimation problem; the inner layer then invokes the ASR protocol from Yuan et al. (2024) to estimate the distribution of W, with the resolution parameter H tuned so that approximation error and distribution estimation error are balanced. The lower bound uses a finite sieve of Sobolev functions indexed by Rademacher signs and a generalized tensorization of the strong data processing inequality, where the key technical step is bounding the strong data processing constant by interpreting the terminal-wise likelihood ratio through the classic balls-and-bins model (n balls thrown into k bins). This yields an exponential-in-l bound for the sparsest regime (m ≥ n^{2r+1}) and polynomial-in-l bounds for all other regimes, establishing the phase transitions in the optimal rate.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.1) establishes that the minimax optimal rate satisfies $R(m,n,l,r) \asymp (N_{ess})^{-2r/(2r+1)}$ up to logarithmic factors, where $N_{ess}$ is an effective sample size that depends on the regime of $(m,n,l)$. For the sparsest regime ($m \geq n^{2r+1}$, small $l$), the rate decays exponentially in $l$ as $(2^l mn)^{-r/(r+1)}$, while for all other regimes the dependence on $l$ is polynomial. The paper identifies clear phase transitions: the minimum communication budget to match centralized performance is $l = n$ for $m > n^{2r}$ and $l = (mn)^{1/(2r+1)}$ for $m \leq n^{2r}$. The results hold uniformly across density estimation, Gaussian, binary, Poisson, and heteroskedastic regression models.

| Centralized ($l=\infty$) | $mn$ | No dependence |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are with prior theoretical characterizations. Zaman & Szabo (2022) and Szabo & van Zanten (2020) established optimal rates only for the dense-samples regime $m \leq n^\gamma$ ($\gamma < 2r$) with the additional restriction $N_{ess} > n$; the present paper recovers their results as a special case (Remark 2.2) while removing the $N_{ess} > n$ restriction and extending to all $m, n$ regimes. Barnes et al. (2020) and Acharya et al. (2024) solved the density estimation problem only for the extreme sparse case $n=1$; the present paper subsumes this as the first case in Eq. (3) (Remark 2.3) and extends to arbitrary $n > 1$. The Gaussian sequence model studied by Zhu & Lafferty (2018) and Cai & Wei (2022) always yields polynomial dependence on $l$, whereas the random-design problem here exhibits exponential dependence in the sparsest regime (Remark 2.4), reflecting fundamentally different sample generation mechanisms. The paper also draws structural parallels to the parametric distribution estimation problem of Acharya et al. (2021) and Yuan et al. (2024), noting similar phase-transition phenomena in the optimal rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified distributed nonparametric estimation framework of Zaman & Szabo (2022), which was restricted to dense-sample regimes (m < n^γ, γ < 2r), and the sparse n=1 density estimation results of Barnes et al. (2020) and Acharya et al. (2024), by covering all regimes from sparse to dense samples per terminal. Methodologically, it builds on the parametric distribution estimation protocol of Yuan et al. (2024) as the inner layer of a novel two-layer architecture, where the outer layer converts the nonparametric problem into a distribution estimation problem via wavelet-based quantization. The lower bound technique departs from prior work by proving a generalized tensorization of the strong data processing inequality, with the key step of bounding the strong data processing constant through the classic balls-and-bins model—a connection not exploited in earlier distributed estimation analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper fully resolves the open problem of characterizing minimax optimal rates for distributed nonparametric estimation across all sample-per-terminal regimes, revealing a qualitative phase transition: the optimal rate depends exponentially on the communication budget l in the sparsest regime but polynomially in all others. The unified framework immediately yields optimal rates for density estimation, Gaussian, binary, Poisson, and heteroskedastic regression as corollaries, providing a single reference for the field. Open questions include extending to Besov spaces and L^q norms, incorporating differential privacy constraints, developing adaptive protocols for unknown regularity r, and analyzing fixed-design or heterogeneous-data settings where the optimal rates may differ.
