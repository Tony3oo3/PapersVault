## Problem

Time series foundation models (TSFMs) have achieved strong zero-shot performance across forecasting, imputation, and classification, yet their internal representations remain largely unexplored. Specifically, it is unknown how knowledge is organized across layers and model sizes, which human-interpretable concepts (e.g., trend, periodicity) are encoded and where, and whether these concepts can be manipulated at inference time to produce concept-informed predictions without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252663/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on pretrained, open-source TSFM weights (MOMENT-Large, Chronos-Large, MOIRAI-Large) and a synthetic dataset of univariate time series generated from controlled constant (y=mt+b) and sinusoidal (y=a·sin(2πt/f)+mt+b) patterns with varying slope, intercept, amplitude, and period. Outputs include pairwise CKA similarity heatmaps across all layer pairs, per-layer/per-token LDR maps localizing concept separability, pruned model checkpoints with reduced layer counts, and steered time series predictions where a constant input is transformed to exhibit trend, periodicity, or both. The steering matrix is a per-layer N×D matrix (N tokens × D hidden dim) derived from median activation differences between concept classes, applied as an additive offset h_i ← h_i + λS_i at inference.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary, training-free techniques for probing and intervening in TSFM latent representations. First, it computes pairwise CKA similarity across all transformer layers to reveal block-like redundancy structures, then applies a block-wise pruning strategy that retains only the first and last layer of each identified redundant block while skipping intermediate layers, reducing model size by up to 52% with minimal accuracy loss. Second, it trains linear probes optimized via the Fisher Criterion on residual-stream activations at every layer and token position to test whether concepts (constant vs. sinusoidal, increasing vs. decreasing trend) are linearly represented, then computes the Linear Discriminant Ratio (LDR) to localize which layers and tokens most discriminatively encode each concept. Third, it constructs a steering matrix by taking the difference between median activation matrices of two concept classes at each layer, stacks these into a per-layer matrix S, and adds λS_i to the hidden representation at every layer during inference, enabling controlled introduction of trend or periodicity into predictions without any fine-tuning; the scalar λ (typically 0.1–2.0) controls intervention strength, and multi-token steering across all positions is required for effective concept transfer.

## Results

Block-wise pruning of MOMENT-Large (all three redundant blocks removed) reduces estimated model size from 1301.76 MB to 615.76 MB (52.7% reduction) and inference time from 20.88 ms to 19.82 ms, while fine-tuned forecasting MSE remains on par with the unpruned model (e.g., ETTh1: 0.388 vs. 0.385; ETTm2: 0.173 vs. 0.171). For Chronos-Large, pruning all four blocks reduces model size from 2704.48 MB to 2080.48 MB (23.1%) with inference time dropping from 59.95 ms to 56.82 ms. Concept steering successfully transforms constant time series into periodic or trending signals, and on ECG5000, all 30 normal-heartbeat samples were steered to the abnormal class (100% class swap). Zero-shot imputation shows mixed results: pruning Block 3 of MOMENT-Large improves MAE on 5 of 9 datasets (e.g., ETTm2: 0.178 vs. 0.185; Exchange: 0.111 vs. 0.125) but degrades on others (e.g., Electricity: 0.428 vs. 0.372).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability and efficiency study rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no external competing baselines. The primary comparison is pruned vs. unpruned versions of the same TSFM. On fine-tuned long-horizon forecasting (Table 1), the fully pruned MOMENT-Large matches or slightly exceeds the vanilla model on 4 of 6 datasets (e.g., ETTm2 MSE 0.173 vs. 0.171; Weather MSE 0.152 vs. 0.153) while showing negligible degradation on the others (ETTh1 MSE 0.388 vs. 0.385). On zero-shot imputation, single-block pruning (Block 3) outperforms vanilla on 5 of 9 datasets in MAE, but aggressive all-block pruning degrades performance substantially (e.g., Electricity MAE 0.727 vs. 0.372). For zero-shot forecasting with Chronos-Large, pruning individual blocks yields mixed MASE results (some datasets improve, e.g., m4_yearly 3.359 vs. 3.559; others degrade, e.g., ercot 1.281 vs. 0.627). On UCR classification, the pruned model drops from 79.4% to 78.1% mean accuracy, and anomaly detection shows more severe degradation (Adjusted Best F1 mean difference of 0.2117, with the original model better on 28 of 41 datasets). Overall, the paper demonstrates that moderate pruning preserves task performance while delivering substantial efficiency gains, but aggressive pruning trades accuracy for speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper translates three established lines of work from vision and language models to time series foundation models: (1) CKA-based representation similarity analysis and layer pruning (Kornblith et al., 2019; Nguyen et al., 2021; Raghu et al., 2021), (2) linear probing and concept localization in latent spaces (Marks & Tegmark, 2024; Dalvi et al., 2019), and (3) representation engineering / activation steering (Zou et al., 2023). The key methodological departure is extending pruning from individual layers to entire blocks of self-similar layers, and demonstrating that simple median-difference steering vectors suffice for concept manipulation in TSFMs without any fine-tuning. The paper positions itself as the first comprehensive representational analysis of TSFMs, bridging the interpretability gap that has existed since these models were introduced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The block-wise pruning strategy offers a practical path to deploying large TSFMs (up to 52% inference speedup, >50% model size reduction) with minimal accuracy loss, which is directly relevant to practitioners seeking efficient time series inference. Concept steering provides a lightweight, training-free mechanism to inject domain knowledge (e.g., trends, periodicity) into predictions, with demonstrated utility on real ECG data. However, the concept experiments rely heavily on synthetic data with simple parametric patterns, and the steering results are limited to two model families (MOMENT, Chronos) with modest sample sizes for validation. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to state-space architectures, more complex real-world concepts, and tasks beyond forecasting and imputation.
