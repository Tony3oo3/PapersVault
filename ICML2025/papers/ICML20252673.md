## Problem

While LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning on pretrained ViTs effectively maintains model plasticity in class-incremental learning, the incremental integration of new tasks induces systematic shifts in both the mean and covariance of feature distributions for previously learned classes—a phenomenon the authors term semantic drift. Existing stability mechanisms (replay, distillation, regularization) impose significant storage or computational overhead, and prior work has not explicitly modeled or corrected the second-order (covariance) component of this drift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from CIL benchmarks (ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, ImageNet-A) and produces a single class label via an angular-penalty classifier over all classes seen so far. At inference the model is fully task-agnostic: all task-specific LoRA modules are aggregated into the backbone weights, so no task ID prediction is required. After each training session, the classifier head is retrained on features sampled from calibrated per-class Gaussians (mean and covariance stored from the old network), rather than on raw training data.

## Method

The framework builds on a frozen ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone with task-specific LoRA modules (rank 32) inserted into key/value attention projections. Three calibration components address semantic drift: (1) Mean Shift Compensation estimates the per-class mean shift for all previously learned classes by computing a Gaussian-kernel-weighted average of embedding differences between the old and current networks, then updates the stored class means; (2) Covariance Calibration introduces a Mahalanobis-distance loss that minimizes the absolute difference in pairwise Mahalanobis distances (computed with the old network's class covariance) between embeddings from the old and current networks, implicitly aligning intra-class covariance structure; (3) Feature-level Self-Distillation penalizes patch tokens that have low angular similarity to the class token by pulling them toward the corresponding patch tokens from the previous task, improving feature reuse. The total training objective is L_cls + L_cov + λ·L_distill, followed by post-hoc classifier retraining on Gaussian-sampled features using the calibrated statistics.

## Results

On the 10-task setting across four benchmarks, the proposed method (MACIL) achieves A_Last of 81.88% on ImageNet-R, 64.14% on ImageNet-A, 90.52% on CUB-200, and 91.94% on CIFAR-100, with corresponding A_Avg values of 85.95%, 71.45%, 93.93%, and 94.43%. The largest gains appear on domain-shifted datasets: ImageNet-R (+2.33% A_Last over the second-best SSIAT) and ImageNet-A (+1.49% over SSIAT). On CIFAR-100, the method is competitive but slightly trails the first-session adaptation baseline RanPAC (91.94% vs. 92.09% A_Last), which the authors attribute to the high overlap between ImageNet pretraining and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines on ImageNet-R (81.88% A_Last vs. 79.55% for SSIAT, the closest competitor, a +2.33% margin) and ImageNet-A (64.14% vs. 62.65% for SSIAT, +1.49%), confirming that semantic drift calibration is most impactful under large domain shifts. On CUB-200, it leads by a narrow margin (90.52% vs. 89.97% for MOS and 89.94% for RanPAC). On CIFAR-100, the method is competitive but slightly trails RanPAC (91.94% vs. 92.09% A_Last; 94.43% vs. 94.75% A_Avg), which the authors attribute to the high overlap between ImageNet-21K pretraining and CIFAR-100, making first-session adaptation particularly effective there. Among PEFT-based methods specifically, the proposed approach surpasses InfLoRA+CA by 3.10% on ImageNet-R and SSIAT by 0.53% on CIFAR-100, while prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, VQ-Prompt, OS-Prompt, CPrompt) and adapter-based methods (EASE, MOS) are consistently 2–11 points behind on the harder datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT-based class-incremental learning line (L2P, DualPrompt, SSIAT, EASE, InfLoRA) by identifying and explicitly calibrating the two-moment feature distribution shift—termed 'semantic drift'—that accumulates as LoRA modules are sequentially added. The mean shift compensation module builds directly on the prototype-shift estimation of Yu et al. (2020), but the paper's key departure is introducing a covariance calibration loss via Mahalanobis distance, which prior CIL methods have not addressed. The combination of LoRA as the PEFT backbone with explicit first- and second-order moment constraints, plus patch-token self-distillation, positions the work as a distributional-alignment framework rather than a new prompt or adapter architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean statistical diagnosis of why PEFT-based CIL degrades on old classes (mean + covariance drift) and offers a lightweight, replay-free remedy that is task-agnostic at inference. Its limitation is explicitly acknowledged: only first- and second-order moments are calibrated, leaving higher-order distributional shifts (skewness, kurtosis) unaddressed, which could matter for heavily imbalanced or domain-shifted datasets like ImageNet-A. An open question is whether the Mahalanobis-based covariance constraint generalizes beyond ViT backbones or to non-Gaussian feature distributions, and whether the approach remains effective as the number of tasks grows well beyond 20.
