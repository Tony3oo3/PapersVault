## Problem

Flow-matching-based generative models lack principled mechanisms for controlled generation (satisfying explicit property constraints) and for respecting domain-specific symmetries such as rotations, translations, and atom permutations. While Variational Flow Matching (VFM) reframes flow matching as posterior inference over trajectories, its potential for enabling both constraint-driven generation and equivariant modeling remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method starts from Gaussian noise x_0 and integrates an ODE over t ∈ [0,1] to produce a molecular structure. For controlled generation, a target property y (e.g., polarizability, HOMO/LUMO energy, dipole moment) is additionally provided. The output is a joint molecular structure combining discrete features (atom types, bond orders, formal charges as categorical variables) and continuous features (3D atomic coordinates as Gaussian variables), with the variational distribution using a fully factorized mean-field form over all D features. Sampling requires only ~100 function evaluations, substantially fewer than diffusion-based baselines (500–1000 NFEs).

## Method

The paper derives a controlled velocity field u_t(x|y) = E_{p_t(x_1|x,y)}[u_t(x|x_1)] that generates the conditional probability path p_t(x|y), and shows it can be approximated two ways: (1) end-to-end training of a variational posterior q_t^θ(x_1|x,y) minimizing a KL objective, or (2) post-hoc Bayesian inference on a pretrained unconditional model via fixed-point iteration x_1^(k+1) = μ_t(x) + Σ_t ∇_{x_1} log p(y|x_1^(k)), requiring no retraining. For equivariance, the paper proves that if the prior is G-invariant, the conditional velocity field is bi-equivariant, and the variational posterior is equivariant in its expectation, then the marginal path is G-invariant; the optimal-transport velocity field naturally satisfies bi-equivariance for linear groups like SO(3) and S_n. The variational distribution uses a mean-field factorization (Gaussian for coordinates, categorical for atom/bond types), reducing the high-dimensional posterior to D univariate distributions and enabling a unified treatment of mixed discrete-continuous molecular features within a single flow-matching objective.

## Results

On conditional molecular generation (QM9), G-VFM with combined end-to-end training and Bayesian inference achieves 1.98 MAE on polarizability, outperforming EDM (2.76), EQUIFM (2.41), and GEOLDM (2.37), while the Bayesian-inference-only variant (VI-VFM) reaches 2.25 MAE without any retraining. On joint molecular generation, G-VFM achieves 99.6% atom stability and 99.5% molecular stability on QM9 with only 100 NFE, matching SemlaFlow's 99.9%/99.6% at the same computational budget. On discrete generation, VFM attains 99.84% validity and 99.92% uniqueness on QM9, and 98.80% validity on ZINC250k, outperforming Discrete FM and Dirichlet FM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), G-VFM (Both) wins on all six conditional properties, e.g. 1.98 vs 2.76 MAE on polarizability, and also outperforms it on joint generation (99.6% vs 98.7% atom stability on QM9) and continuous generation (−120.7 vs −110.7 NLL). Against EFM (Klein et al., 2023), G-VFM improves NLL from −115.7 to −120.7 on QM9 continuous generation while matching atom/molecule stability. Against Discrete FM (Gat et al., 2024) and Dirichlet FM (Stark et al., 2024), VFM achieves higher validity (99.84% vs 98.01% and 99.10%) and lower FCD (0.471 vs 0.991 and 0.888) on QM9 discrete generation. Against MiDi (Vignac et al., 2023) and EQGAT-diff (Le et al., 2023) on GEOM-Drugs joint generation, G-VFM matches or slightly exceeds validity (95.3% vs 77.8% and 94.6%) with the same 100 NFE budget. The only method that outperforms G-VFM on conditional generation is D-Flow (1.39 MAE on polarizability), but D-Flow requires O(k×N) NFEs versus G-VFM's O(N), making it substantially more expensive at inference. GDSS (Jo et al., 2022) and DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) are clearly outperformed on discrete generation, with VFM achieving 99.84% validity versus 95.72% (GDSS) and 99.00% (DiGress) on QM9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Flow Matching (VFM) by Eijkelboom et al. (2024), which itself reframes Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as a variational inference problem over posterior probability paths. The key conceptual departure is showing that controlled generation in VFM naturally decomposes into a Bayesian inference problem: the pretrained unconditional model provides the prior p_t(x₁|x) while a task-specific classifier p(y|x₁) supplies the likelihood, enabling post-hoc control via fixed-point iteration without retraining. This contrasts with standard classifier guidance in diffusion (e.g., Chung et al., 2022), which requires time-dependent classifiers p_t(y|x_t), whereas VFM's structure permits direct use of off-the-shelf classifiers trained on data pairs. The equivariant formulation further builds on group-equivariant architectures (Satorras et al., 2021; Bekkers et al., 2024) by establishing precise conditions under which the variational posterior's equivariance propagates to invariance of the full generative path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The post-hoc Bayesian inference approach is practically significant: pretrained unconditional molecular generators can be repurposed for new property-targeted tasks by simply swapping in a different classifier, avoiding costly retraining. The equivariant VFM formulation provides a principled guarantee that generated molecules respect rotational, translational, and permutation symmetries, which the authors show is critical (performance drops below 80% stability without it). However, the fixed-point iteration is a simplified approximation rather than full MCMC, its convergence properties for non-concave likelihoods are unexplored, and the conditional generation results are demonstrated only on QM9. Open questions include whether the inference-based control scales to complex multi-property constraints, periodic materials, or protein design, and whether richer variational families (beyond mean-field) would close the remaining gap to dedicated conditional models like D-Flow.
