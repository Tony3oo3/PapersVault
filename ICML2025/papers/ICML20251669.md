## Problem

Existing causal attribution frameworks (Pearl, Dawid, Lu et al., Li et al.) are limited to binary outcomes, yet many practical settings—hypertension, developmental toxicity, income—involve continuous outcomes where binarization causes information loss or biased conclusions. This paper extends the posterior causal attribution framework to continuous outcomes, establishing identifiability and estimation for posterior total, direct, indirect, and intervention causal effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251669/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary cause vector X of dimension p (arranged in topological order), a continuous outcome Y with known compact support [y_x^l, y_x^u], and an observed event E (e.g., Y > 140) as conditioning evidence. It produces individual-level counterfactual mappings φ_{x→x'}(y) as the key intermediate object, from which all posterior causal estimands (postICE, postTCE, postNDE, postNIE) and ITEs are derived. The estimation is fully nonparametric—no parametric form for the outcome regression is required beyond the rank-preservation structure.

## Method

The core identification strategy exploits the perfect positive rank assumption: the outcome is a continuous, strictly increasing function of an unobserved latent variable ε, so individuals preserve their relative rank across treatment conditions. This yields the counterfactual mapping φ_{x→x'}(y) = F_{x'}^{-1}(F_x(y)), which matches quantiles of the observed conditional distributions to recover individual-level counterfactual outcomes. Estimation proceeds in two steps: first, a convex objective function ρ_{x→x'}(t;y) (motivated by quantile regression) is minimized for each observation to estimate the counterfactual mapping; second, inverse probability weighting and the monotonicity-based conditional probabilities from Lemma 3.5 are used to compute the posterior causal estimands. Under a known DAG, the mapping simplifies to depend only on the parent nodes of Y, greatly reducing dimensionality. The monotonicity assumption across causes (not on the outcome) is what enables identification of postNDE and postNIE beyond what the rank assumption alone provides.

## Results

For the hypertension example with evidence X=(1,1,1,1,1), Y>140, the proposed continuous postTCE identifies heart disease (X4) as the dominant risk factor with a value of 17.023, followed by exercise (X1) at 10.628 and diet (X2) at 4.561, while heartburn (X3) and chest pain (X5) have zero effects. The decomposition shows X4 acts purely through a direct pathway (postNDE=17.023, postNIE=0), whereas X1 has both direct (3.823) and indirect (6.805) components. The largest posterior intervention effect is −18.19, corresponding to the joint intervention of restoring exercise and removing heart disease in a hypertensive patient. Simulation studies confirm negligible bias and small standard errors (e.g., SE≈0.26 for n=10000) that decrease with sample size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is Lu et al. (2023), which provides posterior causal estimands for binary outcomes. When the continuous blood pressure outcome is binarized at 140, the proposed continuous postTCE (row 5 of Table 2) preserves the exact sign and ordering of the binary postTCE* (row 2): X4 > X1 > X2 > X3 = X5 = 0. The continuous framework adds quantitative information unavailable in the binary setting—specifically, the magnitude of effects in blood pressure units and the decomposition into direct and indirect pathways (postNDE and postNIE). Notably, the binary PN* assigns non-zero probability of necessity to X3 (0.133) and X5 (0.563), which are not causal parents of BP in the DAG; the continuous postTCE correctly assigns them zero, demonstrating that the perfect positive rank assumption avoids the spurious attributions that can arise from binarization. The paper also notes a discrepancy for evidence X=(1,1,1,0,1): Lu et al.'s binary postTCE* gives X1=0.200 while the continuous postTCE gives X1=2.000, attributed to the additional monotonicity-on-outcome assumption required in the binary case that is not needed under the continuous rank-preservation framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the posterior causal attribution framework of Lu et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2023), which was developed exclusively for binary outcomes, to the continuous-outcome setting. The identification strategy borrows the counterfactual mapping and rank-preservation ideas from the econometric literature on individual treatment effects (Heckman et al., 1997; Vuong & Xu, 2017; Feng et al., 2020), repurposing them for retrospective causal attribution. A key conceptual departure is replacing the outcome-level monotonicity assumption required in the binary case with a 'perfect positive rank' assumption on the outcome's structural equation, which is less restrictive for continuous variables. The estimation procedure adapts quantile regression to construct the counterfactual mapping, then uses inverse probability weighting to recover the posterior estimands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Many practically important outcomes (blood pressure, body weight, income) are continuous, and the authors argue that binarizing them for attribution analysis introduces information loss and potential bias. The framework provides the first formal identification and estimation results for posterior total, direct, and indirect causal effects with continuous outcomes under multiple correlated causes. Open questions include extending to continuous causes, multiple continuous outcomes, path-specific effects, and sensitivity analysis when the monotonicity assumption among causes is violated. The real-data application to NTP developmental toxicity data demonstrates feasibility but is limited to a single dataset with a small number of causes.
