## Problem

Existing Koopman-based dynamical similarity metrics suffer from two critical limitations: they operate primarily in the temporal domain and are insensitive to cross-scale coupling in multi-scale nonlinear dynamics, and they are prone to spectral pollution where spurious eigenvalues from finite-dimensional approximation distort similarity measurements. This limits their reliability when comparing complex physical, neural, or language-model systems that exhibit intricate interactions across multiple timescales.

## Input / Output

KoopSTD takes two time series of potentially different feature dimensions (N_d1, N_d2) and time lengths (T1, T2) as input, along with STFT hyperparameters (window length l, hop size s) and a mode count r. It outputs a single scalar dissimilarity score computed as the Wasserstein distance between the top-r Koopman eigenvalues of each system after spectral residual filtering. The method is designed to be invariant under isotropic scaling, rotation, and permutation of the representation space, making it robust to coordinate-system changes common in neural data.

## Method

KoopSTD first applies STFT to decompose each time series into a time-frequency representation, then performs SVD to project into eigen-time-frequency coordinates defined by the right singular vectors. A finite-dimensional Koopman operator is approximated via DMD on temporal snapshots of these singular vectors, explicitly capturing dynamics across decoupled timescales. Each candidate eigenpair is then scored by a Galerkin-based spectral residual (Eq. 7), and only the r eigenpairs with the smallest residuals are retained, filtering out spurious modes that would otherwise pollute the spectrum. The final dissimilarity is the Wasserstein distance between the two filtered eigenvalue sets. The paper proves invariance under a broad class of invertible linear transformations, ensuring the metric is robust to common representation-space changes.

## Results

On the Lorenz system (five dynamical classes), KoopSTD achieves a Silhouette coefficient of 0.94, substantially outperforming HAVOK-based DSA (0.47), Cross-Correlation (−0.27), Procrustes (−0.04), and CKA (−0.05). On the perceptual decision-making attractors (three classes with near-identical geometric trajectories), KoopSTD reaches 0.99 versus HAVOK's 0.90. On the 3-bit Flip-Flop RNNs (where all systems share the same dynamics and values near 0 are preferred), KoopSTD yields −0.04, correctly identifying minimal dynamical disparity, while Procrustes (0.98), CKA (0.20), and HAVOK (0.10) all incorrectly detect spurious differences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KoopSTD dominates all four baselines across all three experimental settings. Against the strongest dynamics-based baseline, HAVOK-based DSA (Ostrow et al., 2024), KoopSTD improves the Silhouette coefficient from 0.47 to 0.94 on Lorenz systems and from 0.90 to 0.99 on PDM attractors, while also correctly reporting near-zero dissimilarity (−0.04 vs. 0.10) on the Flip-Flop RNNs where all networks share the same computational dynamics. The representation-based baselines CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) and Procrustes analysis (Williams et al., 2021) fail to separate distinct dynamical classes on Lorenz and PDM systems (coefficients near 0 or negative), and Procrustes in particular produces a large false positive (0.98) on the Flip-Flop RNNs by overemphasizing geometric differences. Cross-Correlation (Rabiner, 1978) performs poorly across all settings, with negative Silhouette coefficients on both Lorenz (−0.27) and PDM (−0.30), indicating it cannot reliably distinguish dynamical classes. The ablation study further confirms that both the time-frequency transformation and the spectral residual control are necessary: removing either component degrades separation between chaotic and periodic dynamics or between line-like and ring-like trajectories, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

KoopSTD builds directly on Dynamic Mode Decomposition (Williams et al., 2015) as its core Koopman operator approximation engine and on the Galerkin-based spectral residual control from ResDMD (Colbrook & Townsend, 2024) to filter spurious eigenvalues. It departs from prior Koopman-based similarity metrics (Fujii et al., 2017; Ishikawa et al., 2018; Ostrow et al., 2024) by inserting a short-time Fourier transform before the Koopman analysis, explicitly decoupling multi-scale temporal dynamics that these earlier methods treat only in the raw time domain. The paper also provides a formal invariance proof under isotropic scaling, rotation, and permutation of the representation space, a property not established for the prior operator-theoretic metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KoopSTD offers a more reliable and transformation-robust metric for comparing nonlinear dynamical systems, with demonstrated utility spanning physical systems (Lorenz), neural circuits (perceptual decision-making attractors, 3-bit flip-flop RNNs), brain imaging (auditory cortex fMRI), and LLMs (GPT-2, LLaMA). The finding that larger LLMs exhibit more internally consistent dynamics across tasks provides a new dynamical-systems lens on scaling laws, while the auditory cortex results link functional hierarchy to myelination patterns. Open questions include whether the STFT-based timescale decoupling is optimal compared to wavelet or other time-frequency representations, how the method scales to very high-dimensional systems, and whether the spectral residual threshold can be made adaptive rather than relying on a fixed number of preserved modes r.
