## Problem

Text-to-image models can generate high-quality images but cannot reliably produce backgrounds with smooth, low-saliency regions suitable for text overlay. Prior work focused on arranging text layouts over static images or reducing attention in target regions, which degrades semantic fidelity. The paper identifies that directly suppressing attention in text areas causes semantic loss and instead asks whether conflicting objects can be relocated before background optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251475/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a planned blank region R (a rectangular area, e.g., 160×64 or 64×160 pixels within a 512×512 canvas) as input, and produces a text-friendly image where the prompt semantics are preserved while region R contains a smooth, low-saliency background. The approach is training-free and plug-and-play, operating as a guidance loss during the denoising steps of any pre-trained latent diffusion model (SD 1.5, SD 2.0, SDXL) without modifying model weights. It requires less than 15 GB of VRAM and runs in approximately 50 seconds for 50 denoising steps on an A40 GPU.

## Method

TextCenGen operates during the denoising process by simultaneously generating an original image and a result image, using the original's cross-attention maps to guide the result. A layer-wise conflict detector identifies tokens whose attention maps overlap with region R above a threshold, and a force-directed graph treats each conflicting object's attention centroid as a vertex subject to repulsive force (pushing away from R), margin force (preventing boundary overstepping), and warping force (affine rescaling to keep objects within canvas). After relocating objects, a Spatial Excluding Cross-Attention Constraint zeros out attention within R for all conflicting tokens, ensuring a smooth background. The key design choice is the two-stage strategy—relocate first, then suppress attention—which avoids the semantic degradation caused by directly reducing attention in text regions.

## Results

On the P2P Template dataset, TextCenGen (SD1.5) achieves a Saliency IOU of 22.86 (vs. 29.89 for vanilla SD1.5), TV Loss of 8.81 (vs. 14.11), and VTCM of 4.40 (vs. 2.95), representing 23.5%, 37.6%, and 49.2% improvements respectively. Across all three evaluation datasets (P2P Template, DiffusionDB, Syn Prompt), TextCenGen consistently achieves the lowest Saliency IOU and TV Loss while attaining the highest VTCM among all compared methods. The method maintains 98% of the original CLIP Score (CLIP Score Loss of only 0.30–0.31), confirming minimal semantic degradation while creating text-friendly whitespace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextCenGen outperforms all five baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against vanilla SD1.5, the improvement is most pronounced on P2P Template (49% VTCM gain) and DiffusionDB (38% VTCM gain), while the Syn Prompt dataset shows a smaller margin (28% VTCM gain), likely due to more complex natural-scene prompts. Compared to Desigen (the closest competitor, which uses attention reduction), TextCenGen achieves 23–32% lower Saliency IOU and 27–38% lower TV Loss, with VTCM gains of 28–49%, demonstrating that force-directed object relocation before attention exclusion produces substantially smoother and more semantically faithful results. AnyText and Dall-E3 perform poorly on all metrics (Saliency IOU >50, VTCM <2), as they lack explicit spatial control mechanisms for creating blank regions. Against the retrieval-based Text2Poster method (Table 6), TextCenGen achieves higher CLIP Scores (27.96 vs. 20.80 on P2P Template) and higher VTCM (4.40 vs. 2.48), confirming that generating a text-friendly background from scratch outperforms selecting from pre-existing images. The MLLM-as-Judge ELO ranking (Table 7) further confirms TextCenGen's superiority in design appeal, ranking first on DiffusionDB (702) and P2P Template (753) while Dall-E3 leads only on Syn Prompt (79 vs. 123 for TextCenGen).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-attention manipulation lineage established by Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) and Diffusion Self-Guidance (Epstein et al., 2023), which demonstrated that objects in latent diffusion models can be located and edited via their attention maps. The key conceptual departure from Desigen (Weng et al., 2024), which directly reduces attention within layout boxes, is the two-stage strategy: first relocating conflicting objects via force-directed graph dynamics, then applying spatial excluding constraints to smooth the vacated region. This avoids the semantic degradation that naive attention suppression causes, and the force-directed formulation (repulsive, margin, and warping forces) borrows from physics-based layout algorithms but applies them to attention map geometry rather than 2D bounding boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in T2I generation for graphic design, wallpaper creation, and e-commerce, where backgrounds must naturally accommodate text without post-hoc editing. The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately deployable across Stable Diffusion versions without retraining, and the 27,000-image benchmark with the proposed VTCM metric provides a reusable evaluation framework. However, the convexity assumption on objects limits applicability to non-convex or multi-part shapes (e.g., a person's limbs), and the diminishing returns on SDXL suggest the approach may not transfer cleanly to DiT-based architectures. Open questions include whether the force-directed formulation can be generalized to handle hierarchical object relationships and whether the method scales to video generation.
