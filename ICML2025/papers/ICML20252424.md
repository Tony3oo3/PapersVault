## Problem

Existing biomedical benchmarks lack end-to-end infrastructure for training, evaluating, and running inference on multimodal single-cell AI models in therapeutics. No prior platform unifies heterogeneous, continuously updated biological data sources with standardized therapeutic-task benchmarks and model inference endpoints, and no work systematically evaluates state-of-the-art graph representation learning methods on the single-cell drug-target nomination task using context-aware metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252424/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary single-cell drug-target nomination task, the model takes a target protein node and a cell-type-specific PPI subgraph as input and outputs a binary probability that the protein is a therapeutic target in that cell type. The platform additionally consumes single-cell gene expression matrices (tokenized via Geneformer), perturbation type and cell line identifiers, and protein-peptide sequence pairs. Evaluation uses context-specific metrics—AUROC and AP@R computed only over the top-K performing cell types—rather than aggregate scores, with K ranging from 1 to 50 cell types across ~156 cell types in the RA and IBD benchmarks.

## Method

PyTDC is an API-first, model-view-controller platform that unifies 10+ heterogeneous biomedical data sources (single-cell atlases, cell-type-specific PPI networks, scPerturb perturbation data, clinical trial outcomes, 3D structures, knowledge graphs) under a single retrieval interface with data lineage and versioning. A model server provides standardized inference endpoints and weight retrieval across distributed repositories (Hugging Face Hub, CELLxGENE Census, TDC storage) for foundation models including Geneformer, ESM, and PINNACLE, reducing a typical hundreds-of-lines workflow to under 30 lines. The key evaluation contribution is a context-specific metrics framework that computes weighted AUROC and AP@R restricted to the top-K cell types with highest per-cell-type performance, inspired by slice-based learning. The empirical case study benchmarks PINNACLE (GAT on cell-type-specific PPI), GATv2, Node2Vec, and a label-propagation baseline on RA and IBD drug-target nomination, finding that PINNACLE achieves the best context-specific scores (e.g., AP@5 Top-20 CT of 0.917 for RA) but cannot generalize to unseen cell types or incorporate additional modalities, while SoTA general-purpose graph methods (GATv2, Node2Vec) and domain-specific baselines perform poorly.

## Results

On the single-cell drug-target nomination task for rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), PINNACLE (Li et al., 2024) achieves the strongest context-specific retrieval performance, reaching AP@5 Top-20 CT of 0.917 (RA) and 0.884 (IBD), substantially outperforming GATv2 (0.334/0.348), Node2Vec (0.585/0.617), and Label Propagation (0.105/0.268). On classification, PINNACLE attains AUROC Top-1 CT of 0.765 (RA) and 0.935 (IBD), while GATv2 performs at chance level (AUROC = 0.5) across all cell-type subsets for both diseases. Notably, Node2Vec slightly edges out PINNACLE on RA AUROC Top-1 CT (0.766 vs. 0.765), and Label Propagation falls below random (AUROC = 0.451) on RA, highlighting the difficulty of the task for non-context-aware methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINNACLE (Li et al., 2024), a context-aware geometric deep learning method trained on cell-type-specific PPI networks, decisively outperforms all three baselines on retrieval metrics (AP@5 and AP@20) for both RA and IBD, achieving 0.917/0.884 AP@5 Top-20 CT versus 0.334/0.348 for GATv2, 0.585/0.617 for Node2Vec, and 0.105/0.268 for Label Propagation. GATv2 (Brody et al., 2022), the SoTA graph attention network, performs at chance level (AUROC = 0.5) on all cell-type subsets for both diseases, indicating that one-hot encoding cell-type labels onto a context-free PPI graph is insufficient for this task. Node2Vec (Grover & Leskovec, 2016) extracts non-random informative signals, outperforming Label Propagation on retrieval, but interestingly slightly surpasses PINNACLE on RA AUROC Top-1 CT (0.766 vs. 0.765), suggesting PINNACLE's classification advantage is more pronounced on IBD (0.935 vs. 0.883). Label Propagation (Aridhi et al., 2017), the domain-specific graph theory baseline, performs worst overall, falling below random on RA (AUROC = 0.451) due to fragmentation of disease pathways in PPI networks, though it retains some signal on IBD (AUROC Top-1 = 0.683). The authors conclude that even the best method (PINNACLE) cannot generalize to unseen cell types or incorporate additional modalities, leaving the task open for multimodal foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PyTDC builds directly on the Therapeutic Data Commons (TDC) ecosystem (Huang et al., 2021; 2022) by extending it with an API-first microservice architecture for multimodal single-cell data retrieval and a unified model server for biomedical foundation model inference. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: single-cell foundation models (Geneformer, scGPT, scBERT) and therapeutic task benchmarking, introducing the 'slice' abstraction from Chen et al. (2020a) as a formal context-specific metric framework. The paper departs from prior drug-target identification benchmarks (which use aggregate, context-free metrics) by formulating the task as cell-type-conditional prediction y = f(t, c), and from prior single-cell benchmarks (OpenProblems, CELLxGENE) by targeting therapeutic rather than canonical biology tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key open question the paper surfaces is whether any model can simultaneously achieve strong context-specific performance AND generalize to unseen cell types while incorporating additional modalities (protein structure, sequence, perturbation data) — PINNACLE achieves the former but fails the latter. The finding that SoTA graph representation learning (GATv2) is essentially random (AUROC = 0.5) and that domain-specific label propagation falls below random in RA underscores that the task is genuinely hard and that existing graph-based approaches do not capture the relevant biological signal. PyTDC's practical value lies in providing the first unified infrastructure for developing and evaluating multimodal, context-aware therapeutic models, lowering the barrier from hundreds of lines of code to under 30 for common workflows. Limitations include evaluation on only two therapeutic areas with small sample sizes (~150 positives), and the context-specific metrics, while novel, have not yet been validated against expert biological ground truth.
