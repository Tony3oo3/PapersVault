## Problem

Existing neural operators for high-dimensional PDEs (d ≥ 5) cannot enforce inequality constraints such as no-arbitrage bounds in multi-asset Black-Scholes or temperature limits in heat diffusion. Soft-penalty approaches suffer from unstable training and constraint violations, while hard-encoding methods impose architecture restrictions and do not scale beyond low dimensions. HEAP addresses this by natively embedding linear inequality constraints into the operator learning loop.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial condition function g(x) (fitted to obtain initial ansatz parameters θ₀), PDE coefficients and spatial domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d, and linear inequality constraint specifications H[u] ≥ 0 defined on a subdomain Ω_H. The output is the solution function u(x, T) at the terminal time, represented as the ansatz network evaluated at the evolved parameters θ_T. The L²(Ω) norm and constraint evaluation are approximated via Monte Carlo sampling over random point sets, and the constraint is linearized via a first-order Taylor expansion in θ with a small time step Δt, making the per-step problem a finite-dimensional QP.

## Method

HEAP reformulates the evolution operator learning as a quadratic programming problem in the ansatz parameter space, where the QP objective is the PDE residual and the QP constraints encode the physical inequality constraints. The A-PDHG (adaptive primal-dual hybrid gradient) algorithm is then unrolled as a differentiable QP solver inside the neural operator, but truncated to K=3 iterations and extended into a learnable latent space via linear expansion weights on intermediate primal and dual variables. A hypernetwork N_w perceives the current parameter state θ_t and predicts the initial primal/dual points, step sizes (τ, σ, γ, β), and all expansion weights, while projection operators are replaced by differentiable functions (Clamp, ReLU) for end-to-end training. The continuous-time evolution is integrated via Neural ODE with the adjoint method for backpropagation, and the training loss jointly penalizes PDE residual, physical constraint violation, and numerical box-constraint violation with tunable weights.

## Results

On constrained PDEs (the paper's primary setting), HEAP dramatically reduces physical constraint violation compared to CSO: on Black-Scholes T2 at d=20, HEAP achieves 0.0165 L2RE_con versus CSO's 0.6936 (a ~42× reduction), and on BS T1 at d=10, HEAP reaches 0.1932 vs. CSO's 0.9386. On unconstrained equations, HEAP also consistently achieves lower PDE residual L2RE_pde than CSO across all four PDE families (e.g., 0.0270 vs. 0.0329 on BS at d=5). HEAP costs ~1.6× GPU memory and ~2× training/inference time over CSO, with near-linear scaling in dimension up to d=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CSO (Gaby & Ye, 2024), the recent SOTA neural operator for high-dimensional PDEs that parametrises the parameter trajectory with a black-box network and handles constraints only via soft penalty terms. HEAP wins on every constrained PDE family tested: on Black-Scholes T2 (the most complex constraint), HEAP reduces constraint violation by 2.3× at d=5, 2.0× at d=10, 9.1× at d=15, and 42× at d=20 relative to CSO. On Reaction-Diffusion-C, HEAP achieves 3.9× lower constraint violation at d=20 (0.1794 vs. 0.6936) and 2.9× at d=10 (0.2585 vs. 0.7593). On unconstrained equations, HEAP also achieves lower PDE residual across all four PDE types and dimensions (e.g., 0.0270 vs. 0.0329 on BS at d=5; 0.0021 vs. 0.0028 on RD at d=20). The trade-off is computational: HEAP uses ~1.6× GPU memory and ~2× wall-clock time, and at d=100 both methods hit GPU memory limits, though HEAP's overhead remains modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-evolution neural operator framework of Gaby & Ye (2024) and Gaby et al. (2024), which learn high-dimensional PDE solution operators by evolving ansatz parameters via a learned hyper-operator. The key departure is reformulating the per-step evolution as a quadratic programming problem so that linear inequality constraints are natively encoded, then unrolling the A-PDHG solver (Lu & Yang, 2023) into a truncated, learnable neural architecture. The continuous-time integration follows the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018), and the adjoint method is used for backpropagation through the ODE. Unlike prior constrained-PDE approaches that rely on soft penalties or hard architectural encodings limited to low dimensions, HEAP embeds constraint satisfaction directly in the operator's forward pass via the QP structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural operator to handle hard linear inequality constraints in high-dimensional PDEs (d up to 20), addressing a practical gap in computational finance (no-arbitrage bounds) and physics (entropy conditions, temperature bounds). However, the empirical gains over the sole baseline (CSO) on constrained problems are modest and inconsistent—constraint violation is sometimes worse for HEAP (e.g., Heat-C at d=15,20), and accumulated residuals remain high (0.4–0.9). Open questions include extending to nonlinear constraints, validating against more baselines, and whether the QP reformulation's theoretical guarantees translate to practical robustness beyond the tested PDE families. The linear-constraint restriction and single-baseline comparison limit the strength of the empirical claims.
