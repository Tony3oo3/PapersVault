## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D generative models produce discrete representations (meshes, point clouds, NeRFs) that are fundamentally incompatible with real-world manufacturing constraints such as tight tolerances (±0.005 in), watertight 2-manifold geometry, and parametric editability. The paper demonstrates that existing CAD reconstruction models are severely overfit to simplified training datasets (e.g., sketch-extrude-only sequences) and fail to generalize to complex, human-designed objects with fillets, chamfers, and revolved surfaces, producing non-manifold outputs over 80% of the time on realistic data.

## Input / Output

For NeRF evaluation, the pipeline trains InstantNGP on noiseless, watertight 2-manifold meshes sampled from the F360 dataset and extracts output meshes via improved marching cubes at 256×256×256 resolution. For CAD reconstruction, 10,000-point clouds with surface normals are densely sampled from STL approximations of BREP/STEP files. The introduced dataset relabels each point by its nearest BREP face (a classless geometric segment) rather than by primitive class, and evaluation uses manufacturing-specific metrics: percentage of non-manifold outputs, count of non-manifold edges, and chamfer distance scaled against absolute tolerance thresholds rather than relative error.

## Method

This is an empirical position paper whose core contribution is a revised evaluation framework and dataset rather than a new architecture. The authors relabel the ~35,000-part F360 Segmentation Dataset using BREP face assignments as ground-truth geometric segments (eliminating hard primitive-class labels) and add a 200-model unrestricted Thang3D set. They then systematically evaluate SOTA models—InstantNGP (NeRF→marching cubes), Point2CAD, and Point2Cyl—against manufacturing validity criteria (2-manifold, watertight, tolerance compliance). For the decomposition baseline, they apply ParSeNet with an advanced curvature-aware sampling strategy that oversamples edges with dihedral angle θ > 0.3 rad within a threshold t = 0.035·m_d, ensuring ≥25% of points come from high-curvature regions. The key finding is that replacing learned primitive-class segmentation with ground-truth BREP-face decomposition reduces non-manifold reconstruction from 81% to 47% without any retraining, identifying the restrictive featurization step as the primary bottleneck in pipeline-style CAD generation.

## Results

On the F360 Segmentation Dataset (~35K human-designed parts), Point2CAD produces manifold CAD outputs for only 18.9% of shapes, and Point2Cyl for only 12.3%, compared to 92.1% and 81.2% respectively on the simpler ABC dataset. On the fully unrestricted Thang3D dataset (200 real parts), Point2CAD drops to 6.9% manifold and Point2Cyl to 0%. Even under idealized NeRF conditions (noiseless, watertight input, unlimited camera views), InstantNGP produces non-manifold meshes for ~50% of F360 shapes, with average chamfer distances of 0.029–0.056 that exceed manufacturing tolerances by an order of magnitude for typical part sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two SOTA primitive-to-CAD models (Point2CAD and Point2Cyl) and one NeRF-based mesh generator (InstantNGP) against manufacturing-relevant datasets. Point2CAD achieves 92.1% manifold outputs on the ABC dataset (restricted to sketch-extrude sequences) but collapses to 18.9% on the F360 Segmentation Dataset and 6.9% on Thang3D, indicating severe overfitting to simple construction sequences. Point2Cyl shows a similar pattern, dropping from 81.2% on ABC to 12.3% on F360 and 0% on Thang3D. InstantNGP, evaluated under deliberately idealized conditions (noiseless input, unlimited sampling, exact camera poses), still yields non-manifold meshes for 50% of F360 shapes, with objects containing fillets (55% NM), chamfers (60% NM), and revolutions (69% NM) performing worst. The paper's own ParSeNet-based geometric decomposition baseline achieves only 0.23 mIoU (0.62 without normals) on F360, establishing a low but more realistic starting point than prior class-based segmentation. Overall, no existing model produces a manufacturable output on more than 20% of realistic parts, and the gap widens dramatically with geometric complexity (fillets, chamfers, >7 extrusions).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two major lines of 3D generation work: (1) discrete-primitive generation (NeRF, mesh, point cloud, voxel) exemplified by InstantNGP, DreamFusion, and MeshGPT, and (2) primitive-to-CAD reconstruction (DeepCAD, Point2CAD, Point2Cyl, CAD-SIGNet, CAD-MLLM). It departs from both by introducing manufacturing-readiness (2-manifold watertightness, tolerance compliance, parametric BREP/STEP output) as the evaluation criterion rather than rendering fidelity or reconstruction accuracy on simplified datasets. The paper builds on ParSeNet's parametric surface fitting as a decomposition baseline but reframes the segmentation task from class-labeled primitive identification to classless BREP-face-level geometric decomposition, arguing that the restrictive featurization step inherited from RANSAC-based primitive fitting is the fundamental bottleneck in all pipeline-style CAD reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant gap between academic 3D generation benchmarks (ABC, MFCAD++) and the geometric complexity of real manufactured parts, showing that SOTA models overfit to simplified sketch-extrude sequences and fail catastrophically (>80% non-manifold) on unrestricted human-designed CAD. The revised F360 segmentation dataset with BREP-face labels and the unrestricted Thang3D dataset provide much-needed benchmarks that reflect actual manufacturing scenarios. Key open questions remain: how to achieve truly classless geometric decomposition at BREP granularity, how to train generative models to output parametric boundary representations directly, and how to incorporate manufacturing constraints (toolpath accessibility, tolerance, mechanical validity) into the generation objective. The paper's empirical evidence is limited to two models (Point2CAD, Point2Cyl) and one NeRF implementation (InstantNGP), so the generality of the failure modes across the broader model landscape remains to be confirmed.
