## Problem

Prior principal-agent bandit work assumes the agent either has full knowledge of true reward means (oracle-agent) or selects the true-maximizer when not exploring. This paper tackles the more realistic setting where the agent is self-interested and learning: she selects the arm maximizing her *empirical* mean plus the incentive, and may explore arbitrarily with a decreasing probability. The key questions are what regret bounds are achievable under this general agent model, and whether the Õ(T^{11/12}) bound of Dogan et al. (2023a) can be improved.

## Input / Output

At each round the principal observes only the agent's played arm and their own [0,1]-bounded reward; the agent's reward and empirical means are hidden. The principal outputs a non-negative incentive vector π(t) ∈ R^K_{≥0} (typically one-hot) to steer the agent's arm choice. The agent's decision rule is argmax_a {μ̂_a(t) + π_a(t)} with possible exploration at rate p_t ≤ c_0√(t^{-1}log(2t)), and the principal knows this strategy but not the empirical means themselves.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel phased-elimination framework that, unlike classical elimination (Even-Dar et al., 2006), deliberately plays bad arms in each phase to stabilize their estimators, preventing the min_i N_i(t) term from causing linear regret. A key subroutine is an asymmetric binary search (Algorithm 3) for near-optimal incentive estimation that tracks the last successful incentive and re-offers it upon failure, reducing the estimation error from O(log T / min N_i) to O(1 / min N_i). For the exploratory agent, the framework is made robust via probability amplification (repeating searches O(log T) times to guarantee at least one exploration-free call), iterative incentive testing with a threshold on non-target plays, and median-based elimination to filter out exploration-induced noise. For linear rewards, the algorithm replaces per-arm search with a conservative Multiscale Steiner Potential (MSP) cut over a confidence set for s*, using G-optimal design to avoid linear dependence on K, and enlarging the cut threshold to account for the agent's evolving OLS estimate ŝ_t.

## Results

For i.i.d. rewards with a greedy (non-exploratory) learning agent, Algorithm 1 achieves O(√KT log(KT)) expected regret, matching the Ω(√KT) lower bound up to a logarithmic factor. For the exploratory learning agent, Algorithm 5 achieves Õ(K^{1/3} T^{2/3}) regret, improving upon Dogan et al.'s Õ(T^{11/12}). When reduced to the exploratory oracle-agent setting of Dogan et al. (2023a), Algorithm 6 achieves Õ(√KT) regret, a significant improvement over their Õ(T^{11/12}). For linear rewards, Algorithm 7 achieves the first sublinear bound of Õ(d^{4/3} T^{2/3}), compared to C-IPA's O(d√T log T) which requires an oracle-agent assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Scheid et al. (2024b) IPA, Algorithm 1 matches the O(√KT log T) worst-case regret bound in the oracle-agent setting (when T ≥ K), while extending to the more general learning-agent setting where the agent uses empirical means. Against Dogan et al. (2023a), the paper achieves two improvements: Algorithm 5 reduces the regret from Õ(T^{11/12}) to Õ(T^{2/3}) under the more general self-interested learning agent model, and Algorithm 6 (under the reduced oracle-agent exploration setting) further improves to Õ(√KT), matching the lower bound up to log factors. Against C-IPA (Scheid et al., 2024b) for linear rewards, Algorithm 7 trades the O(d√T log T) bound for Õ(d^{4/3} T^{2/3}), which is worse in T-dependence but is the first result that handles a learning agent (as opposed to requiring an oracle-agent with known true means). The paper also notes that Scheid et al. (2024a)'s universal no-regret preservation assumption excludes the simple self-interested learning agents studied here, making direct comparison inapplicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the principal-agent bandit game framework of Dogan et al. (2023a) and Scheid et al. (2024b) by replacing the oracle-agent assumption (agent knows true reward means) with a self-interested learning agent that acts on empirical estimates, and by allowing arbitrary exploration with decreasing probability. Methodologically, it builds on the phased-elimination framework of Even-Dar et al. (2006) but introduces a key departure: bad arms are replayed each phase to stabilize estimators for incentive search, rather than being permanently eliminated. For the linear reward setting, it adapts the Multiscale Steiner Potential (Liu et al., 2021) with conservative space-cutting to handle the agent's estimation uncertainty, and uses G-optimal design to avoid linear dependence on the number of arms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper significantly tightens the regret bound for exploratory learning agents from Õ(T^{11/12}) (Dogan et al.) to Õ(T^{2/3}), and recovers the optimal Õ(√T) rate when the agent model reduces to the exploratory oracle-agent case. The novel elimination framework—particularly the insight that replaying bad arms is necessary to control incentive-search error—provides a reusable template for other settings where the decision-maker's estimates are unknown. Open questions include whether the T^{2/3} bound for the general learning agent can be improved to √T, whether the extra d-factor in the linear setting (giving d^{4/3}T^{2/3} vs. the standard d^{1/3}T^{2/3}) can be removed, and how to handle more general exploration rates p_t = Õ(t^{-α}).
