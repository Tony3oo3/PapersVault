## Problem

Existing AIGC forgery detection methods treat the diffusion generation process as a black-box, relying on reconstruction errors as a single scalar metric. This makes them highly sensitive to the pre-trained reconstruction model's training domain (e.g., a model trained on cats misclassifies real plant images as fake) and prevents them from capturing the deeper temporal dynamics of how noise is distributed and fitted across semantic regions during sampling. No prior method systematically models the spatio-temporal distribution fitting deviations that arise during the stepwise denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) as input. The image is run through a T-step DDIM sampling process (T=20 by default), and the predicted noise ε_θ^(t)(x_t) at each timestep is extracted as a (C,H,W) tensor. The output is a binary forgery label (real/fake) produced by an XGBoost classifier operating on a concatenated feature vector of dimension 3K (distance, match rate, and correlation per superpixel block), where K=10 superpixel blocks by default.

## Method

STD-FD tracks the predicted noise ε_θ^(t)(x_t) at every timestep of the DDIM deterministic sampling process, converting each noise tensor into a normalized image. Spatially, SLIC superpixel segmentation partitions each noise image into K semantically coherent blocks based on color and position; temporally, SSIM between adjacent timesteps within each block yields a (T−1)-length variation sequence per block. A discriminative factor (DFactor) is then learned per block by minimizing a KL-divergence loss over GDTW-weighted distances between candidate subsequences and the full time series, with local and global timing factors regularized via Lp norms. At test time, candidate DFactors are extracted from the unknown image's noise sequences and compared against the learned DFactors using Euclidean distance, trend match rate, and Pearson/NCC correlation, producing a 3K-dimensional feature vector fed to a simple XGBoost classifier. This design decouples the detection signal from any single reconstruction error value, instead encoding how the noise distribution evolves differently across semantic regions over the full sampling trajectory.

## Results

On the DeepFaceGen benchmark (12 forgery generators), STD-FD achieves an average AUC of 94.90%, outperforming all 11 baselines, with the largest margins over Xception (85.42%) and EfficientNet (86.25%). On the GenImage benchmark (8 generators, trained on SDv1.4), STD-FD reaches 92.01% average ACC, surpassing the closest competitor DRCT (89.49%) by 2.52 points and AIDE (86.88%) by 5.13 points. In the cross-domain generalization setting (train on one generator, test on all eight), STD-FD maintains the highest average cross-subset ACC (86.9% on BigGAN) while DIRE drops to 50.1% and DRCT to 84.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STD-FD outperforms all 18 baselines on both benchmarks. On DeepFaceGen, the closest competitors are UnivFD (91.30% avg AUC), RECCE (91.70%), and DIRE (90.69%), with STD-FD leading by 3.2–9.5 points; the largest gaps appear on DALL-E 1 (+4.56 over DRCT) and Midjourney (+4.58 over DNADet). On GenImage, DRCT (89.49%) and AIDE (86.88%) are the strongest baselines, but STD-FD still leads by 2.52 and 5.13 points respectively, with the most dramatic advantage on cross-generator subsets like BigGAN (81.79% vs. DIRE's 49.99%) and ADM (81.64% vs. CNNSpot's 53.48%). Reconstruction-error-based methods (DIRE, DRCT) suffer catastrophic drops when the pre-trained diffusion model domain mismatches the detection target (e.g., DIRE drops to 55.04% AUC on bedroom-pretrained vs. STD-FD's 92.77%), while STD-FD remains stable above 92% AUC across all mismatch scenarios. Frequency-domain methods (F3Net, FreqNet) and architecture-based detectors (Xception, EfficientNet, Conv-B, Swin-T) consistently underperform, with average AUC/ACC below 91% on both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) but departs fundamentally from the dominant reconstruction-error paradigm used by DIRE, DRCT, LaRE2, and AEROBLADE, which treat the diffusion model as a black-box tool and rely on final reconstruction discrepancies. Instead, STD-FD decomposes the sampling process step-by-step, tracking the predicted noise ε_θ^(t)(x_t) at every timestep and combining it with SLIC superpixel segmentation to capture spatio-temporal distribution fitting deviations. The method also borrows time-series shapelet mining (via GDTW and KL-divergence-based optimization) from the time-series classification literature, repurposing it to identify discriminative temporal patterns (DFactors) in the noise evolution across semantic regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that STD-FD is largely immune to the domain-mismatch failure mode that plagues reconstruction-error methods: when a cat-pretrained diffusion model is applied to bedroom or horse images, DIRE's AUC collapses to 55–73%, while STD-FD stays above 92%. This addresses a real deployment concern where the reconstruction model's training domain may not match the target content. However, the method remains dependent on a pre-trained diffusion model for the reconstruction step, is computationally multi-stage (superpixel segmentation, per-timestep SSIM, GDTW alignment, KL optimization), and the authors acknowledge the need for an end-to-end framework. Open questions include whether the DFactor concept generalizes to video forgery detection and whether the superpixel-based spatial decomposition can be replaced by learned segmentation for higher-resolution or more semantically complex scenes.
