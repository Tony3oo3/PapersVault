## Problem

End-to-end trained routers in multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models do not always produce optimal routing weights for individual test samples, especially on challenging or out-of-distribution tasks. The paper quantifies this gap by showing that oracle routing weights improve accuracy by ≥10% on most evaluated benchmarks, yet no prior work addresses test-time routing optimization without retraining any model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251387/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text question, a pre-curated reference set of up to 5,000 correctly predicted samples (each with its ground-truth routing weight vector), and the initial routing weights produced by the frozen base model's router. A pre-trained text embedding model (NV-Embed-V2) maps each sample's question into a shared embedding space where kNN (k=5) neighbors are retrieved. The output is a modified routing weight vector that re-weights the frozen experts, which is then fed back into the unchanged MoE pipeline to produce the final answer.

## Method

R2-T2 proposes three training-free strategies that adjust only the routing weight vector at inference time, leveraging the assumption that routing patterns transfer across similar tasks. The best-performing strategy, Neighborhood Gradient Descent (NGD), constructs a surrogate loss as a kernel-weighted average of the losses of k nearest correctly-predicted reference samples, then performs 10 steps of gradient descent on the routing weights using a cosine-annealed learning rate. Kernel Regression instead computes a Gaussian-kernel-weighted average of neighbors' routing weights and refines it via binary search along the line between the initial and estimated weights. Mode Finding (Meanshift) iteratively shifts the routing vector toward the highest-density region in routing-weight space using a weighted local mean. All three strategies operate in a task-embedding space defined by a frozen text encoder, requiring no gradient flow through the base model and no parameter updates.

## Results

On MoAI-7B, R2-T2 (kNN, NGD) achieves 85.2% on MMBench, 1785.5 on MME-P, 88.3% on SQA-IMG, 85.0% on AI2D, 73.5% on TextVQA, 77.0% on GQA, 77.9% on CVBench-2D, and 69.2% on CVBench-3D, improving over the base model by +5.9% to +9.9% across benchmarks. On MoVA-7B, the same method yields 81.2% MMBench, 1645.3 MME-P, 79.1% SQA-IMG, 81.8% AI2D, 83.2% TextVQA, 71.5% GQA, 68.3% CVBench-2D, and 68.9% CVBench-3D. The method captures 70–80% of the gap to the oracle upper bound (which uses ground-truth labels) while remaining fully training-free, and adds only ~6–7× FLOPs over the base model's single inference pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2-T2 applied to MoAI-7B (85.2 MMBench, 1785.5 MME-P, 88.3 SQA-IMG, 85.0 AI2D, 77.0 GQA, 77.9 CVBench-2D) outperforms all 7B and 8B baselines by wide margins and surpasses 13B models (Cambrian1-13B: 75.7 MMBench, 79.3 SQA-IMG; LLaVA-NeXT-13B: 70.0 MMBench, 73.5 SQA-IMG) on nearly every benchmark. Against 34B competitors, R2-T2(MoAI-7B) beats LLaVA-NeXT-34B on 7 of 8 benchmarks (e.g., MMBench 85.2 vs 79.3, GQA 77.0 vs 67.1) and Mini-Gemini-HD-34B on 6 of 8 (MMBench 85.2 vs 80.6, SQA-IMG 88.3 vs 77.7), while losing on CVBench-3D (69.2 vs 79.2) and TextVQA (73.5 vs 74.1). Against the strongest 34B baseline Cambrian1-34B, R2-T2(MoAI-7B) wins on 6 of 8 benchmarks (MMBench 85.2 vs 81.4, MME-P 1785.5 vs 1689.3, SQA-IMG 88.3 vs 85.6, AI2D 85.0 vs 79.7, GQA 77.0 vs 65.8, CVBench-2D 77.9 vs 74.0) but trails on TextVQA (73.5 vs 76.7) and CVBench-3D (69.2 vs 79.7). R2-T2(MoVA-7B) similarly outperforms all 7B/8B models and is competitive with 13B models, leading on MMBench (81.2 vs 75.7 Cambrian1-13B) and AI2D (81.8 vs 73.6). The paper also shows R2-T2 substantially outperforms ensemble baselines (Multiple Sampling +0.4%, Noisy Routing +0.9%) and RAG/ICL approaches (RAG 5-shot: 64.1%, ICL 5-shot: 62.9%) on the same MoAI-7B base, confirming that the routing-weight optimization mechanism is the primary source of gains rather than additional computation or retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multimodal MoE architectures of MoAI (Lee et al., 2025) and MoVA (Zong et al., 2024), which replace single vision encoders with mixtures of specialized experts to mitigate modality imbalance. It departs from the standard paradigm of end-to-end trained routers by introducing test-time optimization of routing weights, drawing conceptual inspiration from test-time adaptation (Wang et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2020) but restricting updates to the low-dimensional routing vector rather than model parameters. The three proposed strategies—Neighborhood Gradient Descent, Kernel Regression, and Mode Finding (Meanshift)—repurpose classical kernel methods and kNN-based neighborhood search in a novel application to expert re-weighting, a problem the authors explicitly frame as unaddressed in prior MoE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated oracle gap of ≥10% across benchmarks reveals substantial untapped potential in existing multimodal MoE routers, and R2-T2's training-free approach (capturing 70–80% of oracle gains) offers a practical path to closing this gap without retraining. However, the 3.3× inference latency increase and the requirement for a curated reference set of correctly predicted samples limit immediate deployment in latency-sensitive or zero-shot settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to deeper MoE stacks with hundreds of experts, whether reference set quality can be automated without task-specific curation, and whether the method transfers to non-visual MoE settings (e.g., audio, video) where the embedding-space neighborhood structure may differ.
