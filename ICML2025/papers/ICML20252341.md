## Problem

Transformer-based spiking language models for NLP tasks still depend on softmax and layer normalization, operations that require exponentials, divisions, and square roots incompatible with neuromorphic hardware. Prior SNN language models (SpikeLM, SpikingBERT) retained these operations, while hardware-compatible SNNs like SpikFormer avoided them only by borrowing convolutional/batch-normalization designs suited to vision. No transformer-based spiking language model existed that was both fully neuromorphic-hardware-compatible and competitive on NLP benchmarks.

## Input / Output

Sorbet consumes standard text sentences or sentence pairs (as in GLUE tasks) and outputs class labels for classification tasks (SST-2, QQP, MNLI, QNLI, RTE, MRPC) or a continuous similarity score for STS-B. All activations are encoded into binary spike trains over 16 timesteps using an average-integrate-and-fire (ASG) neuron, and the model operates with 1-bit weights and 4-bit activations, making it a 13.4M-parameter binary model suitable for on-chip neuromorphic inference.

## Method

Sorbet replaces the two hardware-incompatible operations in BERT with bit-shifting equivalents: PTsoftmax approximates softmax by rounding attention scores to integers, computing powers of two, and dividing via a right bit-shift by the ceiling-log2 of the denominator; BSPN replaces layer normalization by computing a per-group L1 norm, approximating it to the next power of two for a shift-based division, and then applying PowerNorm's relaxed zero-mean batch normalization with a quantized scaling factor. The architecture retains BERT's transformer structure but encodes all activations through spike neurons, converts matrix multiplications into spike-driven accumulations, and uses binary-weight linear layers. Training follows a multi-step distillation pipeline: first quantize BERT to 1-bit/4-bit via elastic binarization (modified to use bit-shifting), then distill with PTsoftmax replacing softmax, then distill with BSPN replacing LN, and finally convert the resulting ANN to an SNN via ASG spike generation. Theoretical analysis proves BSPN preserves bounded gradients and a 1-Lipschitz property, and PTsoftmax's approximation error is bounded within a constant factor of base-2 softmax.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, Sorbet (13.4M parameters, 1-bit weights, 4-bit activations) achieves 86.5 on QQP, 77.3 on MNLI-m, 90.4 on SST-2, 86.1 on QNLI, 60.3 on RTE, 79.9 on MRPC, and 78.1 on STS-B. It achieves state-of-the-art performance among SNN-based models on four datasets (SST-2, QNLI, MNLI-m, MRPC) while maintaining the same 13.4M parameter size as BiT. In terms of energy, Sorbet achieves 27.16× energy savings compared to BERT and 3.16× compared to SpikeLM, with PTsoftmax alone providing ~27.62× better energy efficiency than conventional softmax and BSPN providing 12.4× better efficiency than LN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiT (same 13.4M size, 1-bit weights), Sorbet wins on all seven GLUE tasks, with the largest gains on STS-B (+7.0, 78.1 vs 71.1) and QQP (+3.6, 86.5 vs 82.9), while also improving SST-2 by +2.7 and QNLI by +0.4. Compared to 1-bit SpikeLM, Sorbet wins on MNLI-m (+2.4), SST-2 (+3.8), QNLI (+1.6), and MRPC (+1.0), but trails on QQP (−0.7), RTE (−5.4), and STS-B (−5.8). Against 1-bit SpikingBERT, Sorbet leads on every reported task, with the largest margin on QNLI (+5.6, 86.1 vs 80.5) and MRPC (+4.1, 79.9 vs 75.8). The key differentiator is hardware compatibility: unlike SpikeLM and SpikingBERT, which still rely on softmax and layer normalization, Sorbet eliminates both operations entirely via PTsoftmax and BSPN, making it the first transformer-based spiking language model fully deployable on neuromorphic hardware without division, square root, or exponential units.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sorbet extends the line of transformer-based SNN work (SpikFormer, SpikeLM, SpikingBERT) by addressing the two operations those models either retained or sidestepped: softmax and layer normalization. It builds directly on PowerNorm (Shen et al., 2020) by replacing its RMSLN component with a bit-shifting L1-norm approximation (BSPN), and adapts BiT's elastic binarization (Liu et al., 2022) to use shifts instead of division. The spike neuron model is taken from the ASG framework (Yan et al., 2025), and the overall architecture is derived from BERT via ANN-to-SNN conversion with multi-step knowledge distillation. The key departure from prior SNN-NLP work is the explicit design constraint of neuromorphic hardware compatibility, which prior models like SpikeLM and SpikingBERT violated by retaining softmax and LN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first transformer-based spiking language model whose operations are all implementable on existing neuromorphic chips (Loihi, TrueNorth, NeuroGrid), closing a gap that has limited practical deployment of SNNs for NLP. The 27.16× energy savings over BERT and 3.16× over SpikeLM are meaningful for edge inference, and the theoretical guarantees (bounded gradients, 1-Lipschitz property) provide confidence in training stability. However, the paper validates only via Lava simulation and Verilog synthesis rather than physical hardware, and the performance gap versus full-precision BERT (e.g., 78.1 vs. 89.4 on STS-B) remains substantial. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or generative tasks, how approximation errors compound in deeper architectures, and whether real neuromorphic hardware effects (noise, timing) degrade the claimed energy savings.
