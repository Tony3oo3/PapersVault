## Problem

Existing token-level reward models (e.g., DPO-RM) derive rewards from the log probability ratio between a generative policy and a reference model, conflating language generation with reward assignment. This conflation causes two failures: tokens with high generation probability are not necessarily high-reward (e.g., confident but wrong answers), and the reference model introduces outlier rewards on non-critical tokens like line breaks. The paper asks whether token-level credit assignment can be learned from preference data without entangling it with the generative objective.

## Input / Output

The discriminative policy φ takes a state s_t (the prompt plus all previously generated tokens) and a specific action a_t (a single token) as joint input, and outputs a scalar logit Z(s_t, a_t) that serves as the token-level reward. Unlike a generative model that conditions on s_t and outputs a distribution over the entire vocabulary, φ evaluates one (state, action) pair at a time. During RL training, these logits are standardized to zero mean and unit variance across the rollout batch before being used as rewards in PPO or REINFORCE.

## Method

Q-RM defines a discriminative policy φ(s_t, a_t) = softmax(Z(s_t, a_t)) over the action space and shows that under the MaxEnt RL framework, the optimal discriminative policy satisfies β log φ*(s_t, a_t) = Q*(s_t, a_t) − V*(s_t), directly linking its logits to Q-values. The training objective is derived from the Bradley-Terry preference model: after substituting the Bellman equation and showing that the partition-function term V(τ) is bounded by trajectory entropy (which is near zero for SFT-initialized LLMs), the objective reduces to a BT loss on the mean logits of chosen versus rejected trajectories, with a single global offset γ. Proposition 3.4 proves that the deviation of Z* from its expectation equals the deviation of Q* from its expectation, meaning Z* can substitute for Q* in advantage computation, eliminating the need for GAE in PPO. The method is trained on standard preference pairs (no fine-grained step annotations) and integrates into PPO or REINFORCE by using standardized Z* values as per-token rewards.

## Results

On GSM8K and MATH, PPO+Q-RM achieves 72.23% Pass@1 and 92.49% Pass@16 on GSM8K, and 32.95% Pass@1 and 64.19% Pass@16 on MATH, yielding an average Pass@1 of 52.59%. REINFORCE+Q-RM reaches 72.10% Pass@1 on GSM8K and 34.45% Pass@1 on MATH (avg 53.28%), with the highest Pass@16 of 93.48% on GSM8K. Q-RM improves average Pass@1 by 5.85/4.70 points over ORM and by 4.56/5.73 points over DPO-RM under PPO/REINFORCE, respectively. Training efficiency is substantially improved, with Q-RM converging 12× faster than ORM on GSM8K and 11× faster than step-level PRM on MATH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-RM consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary GSM8K/MATH benchmarks. Against offline RL methods, PPO+Q-RM (52.59 avg Pass@1) beats DPO (48.98), SimPO (48.32), ORPO (48.43), RLOO (50.25), GRPO (49.32), and RFT (46.39) by 3.6–6.2 points. Among token-level reward model baselines, Q-RM surpasses DPO-RM by 4.56 (PPO) / 5.73 (REINFORCE) points, Implicit-PRM by 5.59 / 6.58 points, ABC-RM by 5.87 / 7.47 points, and PGG-RM by 4.06 / 6.20 points in average Pass@1. Against sequence-level ORM, Q-RM gains 5.85 (PPO) / 4.70 (REINFORCE) points. On QA-Feedback, PPO+Q-RM achieves 0.5956 average reward vs. 0.5631 for PPO+DPO-RM and 0.5608 for PPO+ORM. On AlpacaEval 2.0, REINFORCE+Q-RM leads with 29.34% win rate and 27.07% LC win rate, outperforming DPO (25.08%/24.98%), GRPO (25.13%/25.81%), and PPO+DPO-RM (25.64%/25.76%). Notably, Q-RM achieves lower pairwise accuracy on RewardBench (92.91% Chat) than ORM (96.63%) and DPO-RM (94.69%), yet produces superior policy performance, confirming that RM accuracy does not directly correlate with RL optimization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the maximum entropy RL framework (Ziebart, 2010) and the Bradley-Terry preference model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) to derive a token-level reward model, but departs fundamentally from prior token-level PRMs (DPO-RM, Implicit-PRM, ABC-RM, PGG-RM) that embed reward signals within a generative model's probability ratios. The key conceptual shift is replacing the generative policy π(a_t|s_t) with a discriminative policy φ(s_t, a_t) that evaluates a specific (state, action) pair, thereby decoupling reward modeling from language generation. This resolves the conflict identified in DPO-RM where high generation probability does not imply high reward, and eliminates the reference model dependency that introduces outlier rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-RM provides a theoretically grounded, annotation-free path to token-level credit assignment that integrates seamlessly into standard PPO/REINFORCE pipelines, with the practical benefit of ~12× faster convergence than ORM. The finding that higher RM accuracy does not translate to better policy performance challenges the implicit assumption that a 'better' reward model (in the accuracy sense) is always preferable for RL training. Open questions include whether the discriminative policy approach generalizes to multimodal settings, how it performs at inference time (e.g., as a best-of-N verifier), and whether the approximation of V(s_t) via MSE on Z*(s_t, a_t) can be tightened without the full partition function computation.
