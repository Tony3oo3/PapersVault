## Problem

Generating reliable chain-of-thought rationales for LLMs remains a core challenge: existing approaches either depend on expensive human-annotated reasoning traces or use rejection sampling to filter model-generated rationales, both of which fail to systematically optimize the quality of the thinking process itself. No prior work provides a unified probabilistic framework that formally connects latent reasoning generation, evaluation signals, and post-training objectives with provable convergence guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes (question, ground-truth answer) pairs from datasets like GSM8K or MATH, where the answer serves solely as a verification signal for reward computation rather than as a generation target. The base LLM (e.g., Gemma-1.1-7B-it, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B) is fine-tuned via LoRA (r=32, alpha=128) on 4×H100 GPUs. The output is a fine-tuned LLM that produces higher-quality thinking rationales at inference time, evaluated on pass@1 accuracy across math and code benchmarks.

## Method

BRiTE formalizes LLM reasoning as a probabilistic graphical model with latent thinking process Z and evaluation signal O, then optimizes the joint probability P(z∈Z, y∈Y, o∈O | x, θ) via an EM-style two-stage loop. In the ψ-update (E-step), an RL policy (PPO or GRPO) is trained to approximate the intractable posterior P(z|x, y*, O*) using a novel reward-shaping mechanism (Proposition 3.7) that assigns per-token rewards equal to log P(a_j | a_{1:j-1}, x) under the current model, converting Bayesian inference into a solvable MDP. In the θ-update (M-step), the base LLM is fine-tuned via SFT on the RL-generated rationales or via latent DPO on preference pairs constructed from Q-sampled outputs. The framework provably converges at rate 1/T and unifies SFT, PPO, DPO, and rejection-sampling EM (STaR, ReST^EM) as special cases under different choices of the latent/observation spaces.

## Results

On GSM8K and MATH across four base models (Mistral-7B, Gemma-1.1-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3-8B), BRiTE achieves 52.2/11.2, 59.2/23.7, 89.7/50.5, and 81.0/30.0 respectively, consistently outperforming rejection sampling (RS) by 1–10 points and matching or exceeding SFT with human-annotated rationales. With a larger 40K training set on Qwen2.5-7B, BRiTE achieves 79.1 on MATH500 and 57.7 on AMC23, surpassing RS by over 25 and 26 points respectively. On code generation (deepseek-coder-6.7B), BRiTE reaches 81.7% on HumanEval Basic and 15.5% on BigCodeBench Hard, outperforming both RS and SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRiTE consistently outperforms rejection sampling (RS/STaR-type methods) across all four base models, with gains of 1–10 points on GSM8K/MATH (e.g., 89.7 vs. 87.6 on Gemma-2-9B GSM8K; 59.2 vs. 58.4 on Gemma-1.1-7B GSM8K). Against SFT with human-annotated rationales, BRiTE matches or exceeds performance in most settings (e.g., 52.2 vs. 52.8 on Mistral GSM8K; 59.2 vs. 57.5 on Gemma-1.1 GSM8K; 89.7 vs. 80.1 on Gemma-2 GSM8K), demonstrating that RL-generated thinking processes can rival or surpass human-annotated ones. In the RLHF setting, BRiTE-DPO outperforms iterative DPO (Xiong et al., 2024a; Pang et al., 2024) across multiple benchmarks (Figure 2), attributed to higher-quality preference data from RL-bootstrapped rationales. With the larger Qwen2.5-7B setup (40K mixed dataset), the gap over RS widens dramatically: BRiTE achieves 79.1 vs. 54.3 on MATH500 (+24.8) and 57.7 vs. 31.6 on AMC23 (+26.1). On code generation, BRiTE leads RS by 2.4 points on HumanEval Basic (81.7 vs. 79.3) and by 4.0 points on BigCodeBench Hard (15.5 vs. 11.5), while also surpassing SFT on the Hard split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EM algorithm and its rejection sampling variant (Neal & Hinton, 1998) as applied to LLM fine-tuning, unifying STaR, ReST^EM, PPO-based RLHF, and DPO under a single probabilistic graphical model with a latent thinking process Z and evaluation signal O. The key departure from prior EM-type methods is replacing the E-step's rejection sampling with an RL-based policy (PPO/GRPO) trained via a novel reward shaping mechanism (Proposition 3.7) that converts the intractable posterior over rationales into a solvable MDP. The paper also extends the latent variable model perspective of Hu et al. (2023) and Hoffman et al. (2024) by providing the first convergence guarantee (1/T rate) for the generalized rejection sampling EM framework and by explicitly incorporating the latent reasoning process into DPO (latent DPO, Eq. 3.6).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides the first theoretical convergence analysis for generalized ReST^EM and connects it to mirror descent, giving practitioners a principled understanding of why iterative self-training works. The finding that RL-bootstrapped rationales can match or exceed human-annotated thinking processes (without any human-annotated CoT data) suggests a cost-effective path to scaling reasoning training, though the concavity assumption in the convergence proof and the observed training plateau in iteration 2 (Table 3) remain open limitations. A key open question is whether the RL-based E-step advantage over rejection sampling persists at much larger scales (100B+ parameters) where the base model's own sampling quality may already be high enough to make the RL overhead unnecessary.
