## Problem

Existing contextual MNL bandit algorithms suffer from regret bounds that explicitly depend on the norm-boundedness parameter B and the maximum assortment size K, and no variance-dependent regret bound has been established. The key gap is the absence of a B, K-free online confidence bound for MNL parameter estimation that maintains constant per-round computational cost, which is the bottleneck preventing tighter regret guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251314/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the agent receives d-dimensional feature vectors x_ti ∈ R^d (with ‖x_ti‖₂ ≤ 1) and rewards r_ti ∈ [0,1] for all N items, subject to an assortment size constraint K. The agent outputs an assortment S_t ⊆ [N] with |S_t| ≤ K and observes a single user choice c_t ∈ S_t ∪ {0} drawn from the MNL distribution. The parameter estimate w_t is updated online via restricted-space OMD within an ellipsoidal search space W_t^w(δ), and the confidence set C_t(δ) is an H_t-norm ball of radius β_t(δ) = O(√(d log t) + B√d) around w_t.

## Method

The core contribution is a sharper online confidence bound of O(√(d log t) + B√d) achieved through two key technical innovations: (1) establishing ℓ∞-norm self-concordant properties of the MNL loss (Proposition B.3, constant 3√2) that enable a B, K-independent step size η ≈ α, and (2) applying Ville's inequality to bound the estimation error without the smoothing technique that previously introduced an O(log K · log t) factor. The algorithm OFU-MNL++ employs a two-phase adaptive warm-up: during warm-up rounds, a single item maximizing the (H_t^w)^{-1}-norm is offered to build the restricted search space W_t^w(δ); during planning rounds, the parameter is updated only within this space (keeping α constant) and the assortment is selected via optimistic revenue maximization. A novel regret decomposition bounding regret by covariances between rewards and feature norms yields the variance-dependent bound O((d log T + Bd√(log T))√(Σσ_t²)). An MLE-based variant OFU-M²NL further achieves fully poly(B)-free regret of O(d log(BT)√(Σσ_t²)) at the cost of O(t) per-round computation.

## Results

OFU-MNL++ achieves a variance-dependent regret of O((d log T + Bd√log T)√Σσ_t²) with O(1) per-round computation, which simplifies to Õ(d√Σσ_t²) for sufficiently large T — the first B-free, K-free, variance-dependent optimal regret in contextual MNL bandits. The underlying online confidence bound improves from O(B√d log t log K) (Lee & Oh, 2024) to O(√d log t + B√d), a factor of √log t · log K tighter in the leading term. The MLE-based variant OFU-M²NL achieves a fully poly(B)-free, K-free regret of O(d log(BT)√Σσ_t²) at the cost of O(t) per-round computation. In experiments over T=3000 rounds, both proposed algorithms significantly outperform UCB-MNL, TS-MNL, and OFU-MNL+ in cumulative regret, with OFU-MNL++ exhibiting the lowest asymptotic regret slope.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior MNL bandit result, OFU-MNL+ (Lee & Oh, 2024), OFU-MNL++ improves the regret by a factor of O(B^{3/2} log K √log T) and eliminates the K-dependence entirely, while also replacing the worst-case √T with the variance-dependent √Σσ_t² (which is strictly smaller since σ_t² ≤ 1). Compared to UCB-MNL (Oh & Iyengar, 2021), which suffers an exponential e^B factor through 1/κ = O(K²e^{3B}), OFU-MNL++ removes both the B and K dependencies in the leading term. Against OFU-MNL (Perivier & Goyal, 2022), the new method eliminates the K factor and the 1/κ dependence while maintaining constant per-round computation (vs. intractable for OFU-MNL). In the logistic bandit special case (K=1), OFU-MNL++ matches the poly(B)-free regret of OFUGLB (Lee et al., 2024b) up to logarithmic factors but with O(1) per-round cost instead of O(t). The MLE-based OFU-M²NL achieves the tightest poly(B)-free bound O(d log(BT)√Σσ_t²) but at O(t) per-round cost, trading off computational efficiency for a slightly tighter non-leading term compared to OFU-MNL++. Empirically, both proposed algorithms outperform all baselines (UCB-MNL, TS-MNL, OFU-MNL+) in cumulative regret over 3000 rounds, with OFU-MNL++ showing the steepest regret reduction as B increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lee & Oh (2024) and Zhang & Sugiyama (2024), which established online mirror descent-based confidence bounds for MNL bandits with constant per-round computation. The key departures are: (1) establishing self-concordant properties of the MNL loss with respect to the ℓ∞-norm (rather than the traditional ℓ2-norm from Tran-Dinh et al., 2015), enabling a B,K-independent step size; (2) replacing Bernstein-type inequalities with Ville's inequality to avoid a smoothing step that previously introduced O(log K (log t)²) factors; and (3) introducing an adaptive warm-up mechanism that restricts parameter updates to a shrinking ellipsoid, keeping the update condition parameter α constant. The MLE-based variant (OFU-M²NL) is inspired by Lee et al. (2024b)'s poly(B)-free confidence sequences for GLM bandits, extended here to the MNL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first variance-dependent, poly(B)-free, and K-free regret bound in contextual MNL bandits, resolving a gap where all prior bounds scaled with both the parameter norm B and assortment size K. The constant per-round computational cost of OFU-MNL++ (O(Kd³ + poly(N))) makes it practically deployable, unlike MLE-based alternatives whose cost grows linearly with t. The ℓ∞-norm self-concordant properties of the MNL loss are of independent interest for online learning theory. Open questions include whether the residual B-dependence in the non-leading term (Bd√log T) can be fully eliminated, and whether the adaptive warm-up framework extends to other choice models (e.g., nested logit, mixed logit) or to the RL setting with MNL transitions.
