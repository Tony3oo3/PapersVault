## Problem

Prior work on online best scoring rule identification (BSRI) in principal-agent information acquisition only provided loose instance-independent sample complexity bounds of Õ(C_O³K⁶ε⁻³), with no instance-dependent results and no fixed-budget analysis. This paper closes both gaps by deriving near-optimal instance-dependent and instance-independent bounds that match the complexity of standard best-arm identification in multi-armed bandits.

## Input / Output

The principal receives K scoring rules from an action-informed oracle (each guaranteeing the agent selects a specific arm), the utility function u, and either a target accuracy (ε,δ) or a fixed budget T. Each round, the principal announces a scoring rule S: Ω×Δ(Ω)→R⁺, observes the agent's chosen arm k_t and reported belief σ̂_t, and only learns the true state ω_t at round's end. The output is an estimated scoring rule Ŝ* satisfying h(S*)−h(Ŝ*)≤ε with probability ≥1−δ, along with a stopping time τ (fixed confidence) or error probability δ̃ (fixed budget).

## Method

The core algorithm (OIAFC/OIAFB) maintains empirical estimates of belief distributions q_k and pairwise cost differences C(k,k′), then solves a UCB-LP linear program per arm to obtain an upper-confidence estimate ĥ_k^t of each arm's principal profit. The key innovation is a conservative scoring rule S_t = α_k^t S̃_k + (1−α_k^t) Ŝ_k,t with trade-off parameter α_k^t = min(√(M/L_k^t), 1), which blends the oracle rule (guaranteeing the desired arm) with the LP-estimated rule to balance normal exploration against forced exploration. When the agent deviates from the estimated best arm, a binary search along the segment between S_t and S̃_k refines the response-region boundary in logarithmic time. A breaking rule with threshold β_t = (ε − 2α_{k*}^t(B_S+B_u))/(1−α_{k*}^t) halts exploration once the confidence radius is sufficiently small, yielding an instance-dependent sample complexity of Õ(ε⁻²B_S²M(Σ_{k≠k*}Δ_k⁻² + ε⁻²)) and an instance-independent bound of Õ(ε⁻²B_S²MKε⁻²).

## Results

OIAFC achieves an instance-dependent sample complexity of Õ(ε⁻² B_S² M H_Δ) in the fixed-confidence setting, where H_Δ = 4(B_S+B_u)²(ε⁻² + Σ_{k≠k*} Δ_k⁻²), and an instance-independent bound of Õ(ε⁻² B_S² M H_ε) with H_ε = 4(B_S+B_u)² K/ε². The OIAFB algorithm matches the instance-independent bound, requiring a budget T = Õ(ε⁻² B_S² M H_ε). These results improve upon Chen et al. (2023), whose fixed-confidence bound was Õ(8(B_S+B_u)³ C_O³ K⁶ ε⁻³ ε⁻⁶), by reducing the dependence on C_O and K and improving the ε exponent from ε⁻³ to ε⁻².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Chen et al. (2023), the only prior work providing a fixed-confidence BSRI result. Chen et al. achieve Õ(C_O³ K⁶ ε⁻³) samples, while OIAFC achieves Õ(MK ε⁻²) instance-independently (with M ≤ K·C_O), yielding a polynomial improvement in both C_O and K dependence and a better ε exponent. Additionally, OIAFC provides the first instance-dependent bound Õ(M Σ Δ_k⁻² + M ε⁻²), which has no counterpart in prior work. In a simplified setting where each arm produces only two possible beliefs, the bound reduces to Õ(ε⁻² B_S² H_Δ), matching the known instance-dependent rate for standard fixed-confidence best-arm identification in multi-armed bandits (Even-Dar et al., 2006; Gabillon et al., 2012). The paper also notes that Cacciamani et al. (2023)'s explore-then-commit approach could achieve Õ(K/ε²) instance-independently but cannot yield instance-dependent guarantees due to the need to pre-set exploration length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Chen et al. (2023, ICML), which introduced the online information acquisition framework with the action-informed oracle assumption and provided the first (instance-independent) fixed-confidence BSRI result. The key departure is methodological: by introducing adaptive trade-off parameters α_k^t and a confidence-based breaking rule β_t, the authors replace Chen et al.'s suboptimal termination criterion (borrowed from Jin et al., 2018) with one that yields instance-dependent sample complexity for the first time in this setting. The analysis also draws on the UCB-LP structure from Chen et al. (2023) and the concentration inequalities for empirical distributions over finite supports (Mardia et al., 2018), while positioning the problem as a natural analogue of fixed-confidence best-arm identification in multi-armed bandits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first instance-dependent sample complexity bounds for online principal-agent information acquisition, showing that the additional complexity over standard MAB best-arm identification is governed by M (the belief distribution support size, bounded by K × C_O) and the oracle gap ε. This clarifies the fundamental cost of the strategic layer: the principal must simultaneously identify the best arm and estimate the response region V_{k*} at ε-accuracy. Open questions include extending to multi-agent settings (as in Cacciamani et al., 2023), removing the action-informed oracle assumption, and handling continuous or infinite state spaces. The purely theoretical nature (no experiments) and the reliance on the oracle assumption limit immediate practical impact.
