## Problem

SVD-based low-rank pruning of LLMs suffers from two fundamental issues: the decomposed weight matrices contain redundant parameters due to orthogonality constraints among singular vectors, and existing reconstruction methods propagate accumulated errors across layers by relying solely on degraded data flow. As a result, low-rank pruning typically doubles perplexity relative to semi-structured (N:M) pruning at the same density, despite offering superior hardware flexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253135/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA2 7B/13B/70B, LLaMA3-8B) and a small set of calibration text samples (128–512 sequences of 2048 tokens from WikiText2), processed sequentially to remain within GPU memory. It outputs a PIFA layer per linear module, consisting of pivot-row indices I, a pivot-row matrix W_p ∈ R^{r×n}, and a coefficient matrix C ∈ R^{(m−r)×r}, which together form a drop-in replacement for the original dense or low-rank linear layer. The entire pipeline is retraining-free and operates in a single forward pass over calibration data.

## Method

MPIFA combines two components. First, Pivoting Factorization (PIFA) is a lossless meta low-rank representation: given any rank-r matrix W' = UV^T, it identifies r linearly independent rows via QR decomposition with pivoting and expresses all remaining rows as linear combinations of these pivot rows, eliminating r²−r redundant parameters and reducing inference FLOPs from 2br(m+n) to 2br(m+n−r) with zero additional loss. Second, the Online Error-Accumulation-Minimization Reconstruction (M) refines U and V^T via a closed-form least-squares solution that (a) accumulates XX^T incrementally across calibration samples to avoid GPU memory overflow, (b) uses a blended target Y_t = λWX_o + (1−λ)WX_u (λ=0.25) combining dense and low-rank data flows to correct errors propagated from earlier layers, and (c) reconstructs both U and V^T (not just U as in SVD-LLM). The SVD-LLM truncation-aware whitening step provides the initial low-rank factorization before M and PIFA are applied.

## Results

On WikiText2, MPIFA achieves the lowest perplexity among all low-rank pruning methods across LLaMA2 (7B/13B/70B) and LLaMA3-8B at all tested densities (40%–90%). At 50% density, MPIFA reduces perplexity to 21.25 (LLaMA2-7B), 16.72 (LLaMA2-13B), 12.01 (LLaMA2-70B), and 47.02 (LLaMA3-8B), reducing the average perplexity gap versus the best baseline (SVD-LLM) by 66.4%, 53.8%, 40.7%, and 72.7% respectively. In efficiency, PIFA layers achieve 24.2% additional memory savings and 24.6% faster inference over standard SVD-based low-rank layers at r/d=0.5, and at d=32768 with 55% density, PIFA delivers a 2.10× speedup on A6000 while 2:4 semi-sparse (CUTLASS) is actually slower than the dense layer (0.79×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary low-rank baselines, MPIFA dominates: at 50% density on LLaMA2-7B, MPIFA achieves PPL 21.25 versus SVD-LLM's 54.20 and ASVD's 24178; on LLaMA3-8B the gap widens to 47.02 vs. 163.5 (SVD-LLM) and 13504 (ASVD). Against semi-structured 2:4 pruning at matched memory reduction (55% density), MPIFA_NS achieves PPL 9.68 (LLaMA2-7B) and 7.93 (LLaMA2-13B), outperforming RIA 2:4 (10.85 / 8.03), Wanda 2:4 (11.40 / 8.33), and Magnitude 2:4 (37.77 / 8.89), reducing the perplexity gap by 21.7% and 3.2% respectively. Against structured pruning (LLM-Pruner), MPIFA reduces the average perplexity gap by 87.2% on LLaMA2-7B (e.g., 12.77 vs. 40.49 at 60% density). Against ESPACE (NeurIPS 2024) at 50% density on LLaMA2-7B, MPIFA achieves PPL 12.77 versus ESPACE(GO-MSE)'s 41.75, and even when PIFA/M are applied as plug-ins to ESPACE, they consistently improve perplexity (e.g., ESPACE(GO-MSE)+MPIFA drops to 13.55). In hardware efficiency, PIFA surpasses all semi-sparse implementations: at d=32768 on A6000, PIFA achieves 2.10× speedup while 2:4 (CUTLASS) is 0.79× (slower than dense) and 2:4 (cuSPARSELt) fails entirely; end-to-end on LLaMA2-7B, MPIFA_NS delivers 472.6 tok/s vs. 306.6 (cuSPARSELt) and 327.5 (CUTLASS) on A6000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2024) for the initial low-rank pruning step, but introduces two conceptual departures: (1) PIFA, a lossless 'meta' factorization that exploits the orthogonality-induced redundancy (r²−r parameters) inherent in any SVD-based decomposition to further compress without quality loss, and (2) an online reconstruction method (M) that corrects error accumulation by blending dense and low-rank data flows, addressing a limitation shared by both low-rank (SVD-LLM) and semi-structured (SparseGPT, Wanda) pruning pipelines. Unlike prior low-rank methods that treat U and V^T as independent storage, PIFA reorganizes the singular matrix into pivot rows plus a coefficient matrix, yielding a rectangular parameter layout more amenable to GPU computation than the trapezoidal structure of LU/QR decompositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIFA provides a drop-in, lossless compression layer applicable to any existing low-rank pruning method, making it broadly useful for practitioners who already use SVD-based compression. The practical finding that 2:4 semi-sparse kernels (cuSPARSELt, CUTLASS) are slower than dense or fail entirely at d=32768 challenges the common assumption that hardware-supported sparsity always accelerates inference, and positions low-rank methods as the more scalable option for large-dimension LLMs. Limitations include the need for calibration data, the fact that PIFA does not reduce rank itself (it must be paired with a rank-reduction step), and evaluation restricted to LLaMA-family models. Future work on integrating PIFA into pre-training (noted as differentiable) and extending to non-LLM architectures would strengthen the contribution.
