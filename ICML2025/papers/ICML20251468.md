## Problem

Neural networks can solve constrained optimization problems in real time, but prediction errors push solutions outside the feasible region, and existing feasibility-recovery methods are either computationally expensive (orthogonal projection via iterative solvers) or restricted to specific constraint geometries (linear, convex, or ball-homeomorphic). The paper tackles the open problem of guaranteeing NN solution feasibility over general compact sets with non-empty interiors while maintaining bounded optimality loss and low per-iteration cost.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an input parameter θ ∈ Θ, an infeasible NN prediction x̃_θ ∈ ℝⁿ, and an interior point x°_θ ∈ int(C_θ) predicted by a separate IPNN. It outputs a feasible solution x̂_θ on the constraint boundary ∂C_θ by bisection along the line segment connecting x̃_θ and x°_θ. The IPNN itself is a standard feedforward network mapping θ → x°_θ, trained offline; at inference, only a feasibility-check oracle (evaluating g(x,θ) ≤ 0) is needed per bisection step, making the post-processing cost O(KG) where K is the number of bisection iterations (typically 5–10) and G is the constraint-evaluation cost.

## Method

Bisection Projection (BP) has two components. First, an Interior Point Neural Network (IPNN) is trained with a loss combining a perturbation-based constraint-violation penalty (analogous to randomized smoothing, sampling u from a unit ball and penalizing ‖g(x°_θ + γu, θ)⁺‖) with a −λ·log(γ) term that maximizes the inscribed-ball radius γ, effectively approximating the Chebyshev center and minimizing the eccentricity of the predicted IP. Second, given an infeasible prediction and the predicted IP, a bisection algorithm (Alg. 1) iteratively halves the line segment between them, using only feasibility checks, to converge to a boundary point in O(KG) time. The key theoretical insight is that the eccentricity of the IP—defined as the gap between its maximum and minimum distances to the boundary—directly bounds the worst-case projection distance (Prop. 4.1), and the full optimality gap decomposes into prediction error, eccentricity, and an exponentially decaying bisection term (Theorem 1). The framework unifies gauge projection and homeomorphic projection as special cases (Prop. 5.2) while extending to general input-dependent compact sets under a Hausdorff-continuity assumption.

## Results

Bisection Projection (B-Proj) achieves 100% feasibility across all six benchmark problems (QP, QCQP, SOCP, SDP, AC-OPF, JCC-IM) while maintaining solution optimality gaps within 0.05–0.24 percentage points of the raw NN predictor. Post-processing cost is 0.016–1.13 s, representing up to four orders of magnitude speedup over iterative-solver-based methods (400–480 s for QCQP warm-start/projection). On QP (n=400), B-Proj yields 2.31% solution optimality vs. 2.26% for the uncorrected NN, while H-Proj degrades to 16.49% and D-Proj retains only 80.9% feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DC3 (D-Proj, Donti et al. ICLR 2020), Bisection Projection wins decisively on feasibility: B-Proj achieves 100% across all six problems while D-Proj ranges from 58.0% (SDP) to 96.5% (AC-OPF), and B-Proj is 2–15× faster in post-processing (e.g., 0.016 s vs. 11.1 s on QP). Against Homeomorphic Projection (H-Proj, Liang et al. ICML 2023), B-Proj also achieves 100% feasibility but with substantially smaller optimality degradation: on QP, B-Proj preserves 2.31% solution optimality versus H-Proj's 16.49%, and on SDP the gap is 2.26% vs. 31.51%. B-Proj is also 20–60× faster in post-processing than H-Proj (e.g., 0.016 s vs. 0.763 s on QP; 0.016 s vs. 0.958 s on SDP), attributed to H-Proj's reliance on computationally expensive invertible neural networks during inference. On AC-OPF, B-Proj matches the raw NN's 0.15% solution optimality exactly, while H-Proj degrades to 0.63%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several existing projection-based feasibility methods: gauge projection (Mhammedi 2022; Tordesillas 2023) uses a fixed interior point to scale infeasible solutions onto convex boundaries, while homeomorphic projection (Liang et al. 2023, 2024) employs invertible neural networks to map between a unit ball and ball-homeomorphic constraint sets. The authors show (Prop. 5.2) that both are special cases of their proposed bisection projection, then generalize to arbitrary compact sets with non-empty interiors by introducing a dedicated IPNN that predicts low-eccentricity interior points in real time. The key conceptual departure is replacing the complex INN architecture of homeomorphic projection with a simple bisection loop plus a separately trained IPNN, while providing the first eccentricity-based optimality bound for the bisection operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For real-time safety-critical applications (power grid dispatch, inventory management), the combination of guaranteed 100% feasibility, up to 4× speedup over iterative projection solvers, and bounded optimality loss makes this a practically deployable post-processing step for any NN-based optimizer. The main limitations are the restriction to continuous (non-integer) constraints, the reliance on Lipschitz-based worst-case guarantees that are difficult to verify in practice, and the fact that the IPNN training convergence for finite-size networks with non-convex penalties remains unproven. Open directions include extending to mixed-integer constraints and jointly optimizing IP selection with the bisection trajectory.
