## Problem

Treatment effect estimation under a limited labeling budget is an active learning problem that existing methods handle suboptimally: QHTE ignores distribution alignment between treatment groups, Causal-BALD depends on complex uncertainty quantification, and MACAL requires pairwise queries that limit generalizability. No prior work provides a unified, model-independent theoretical bound that captures both within-group diversity and cross-group distributional discrepancy as jointly reducible quantities under the AL paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool set D of (covariate, treatment-indicator) pairs without outcome labels, a labeling budget B, and a pre-set covering radius δ (uniform across all four radii for efficiency, or per-radius when group discrepancy is large). It outputs a selected subset S for oracle labeling, which is then used to train a two-headed estimator (DUE-DNN for tabular covariates, DUE-CNN for 28×28 images) producing individual treatment effect estimates τ̂(x) = f(x, t=1) − f(x, t=0). The acquisition is iterative: at each step, one sample (TOY) or a batch of 50 (IBM, CMNIST) is selected from the remaining unlabeled pool, with the estimator retrained from the last best checkpoint rather than from scratch.

## Method

The paper derives a probabilistic upper bound on the subset generalization gap Δ (Theorem 3.4) that decomposes into four covering radii—factual δ_(t,t) and counterfactual δ_(t,1−t) for each treatment group—plus an IPM term capturing distributional discrepancy, all under Lipschitz continuity assumptions. To minimize this bound, two greedy algorithms are proposed: (1) a top-down radius reduction method (Algorithm 1) that iteratively selects the point reducing the currently largest radius, achieving a 2-OPT approximation under identical group distributions; and (2) FCCM (Algorithm 2), a bottom-up coverage maximization that constructs a weighted directed graph over all pool samples where factual edges carry weight 1 and counterfactual edges carry weight α, then greedily selects the vertex with the highest scaled out-degree, yielding a (1−1/e) approximation to the full-coverage constraint. FCCM's key distinction from prior covering-based AL (e.g., ProbCover) is its integration of cross-group counterfactual edges, enabling it to prioritize acquisition in the overlapping high-density region critical for treatment effect estimation.

## Results

FCCM achieves the lowest √ε_PEHE (risk) across all three datasets (TOY, IBM, CMNIST) at every budget consumption level, serving as the empirical risk lower bound. On CMNIST, the ablation shows FCCM reduces √ε_PEHE from 2.2073 (FCCM⁻, no counterfactual covering) to 1.6790 at full budget, a 24% gain; on TOY the gain is 16% (0.9024 → 0.7565). On IBM, where treated and control distributions heavily overlap, the counterfactual covering provides negligible gain (+0%), with FCCM reaching √ε_PEHE ≈ 0.329. The method consistently outperforms both general AL baselines (BADGE, BAIT, LCMD) and designated DTEE baselines (QHTE, Causal-BALD variants, MACAL) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCCM outperforms all six baselines on all three datasets in the main risk evaluation (Figure 5). Against the designated DTEE methods: MACAL (Wen et al., 2025) requires pairwise queries and cannot consistently reduce the overall risk bound; QHTE (Qin et al., 2021) omits distribution alignment during acquisition; Causal-BALD / μρ BALD (Jesson et al., 2021b) biases toward the overlapping region but does not prioritize high-density queries, leading to higher residual risk. Against general AL methods: BAIT (Ash et al., 2021) spreads queries across the entire treated group without considering counterfactual overlap, underperforming on TOY and IBM but surprisingly competitive on CMNIST; BADGE (Ash et al., 2019) and LCMD (Holzmüller et al., 2023) do not account for the treatment-group structure at all. The paper notes that current DTEE baselines cannot consistently outperform general AL methods across datasets, whereas FCCM dominates both groups uniformly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the core-set-based generalization bound for treatment effect estimation (Shalit et al., 2017; Qin et al., 2021) by introducing the counterfactual covering radius as a new reducible quantity alongside the factual covering radius, thereby unifying distributional alignment into the risk bound. It departs from MACAL (Wen et al., 2025) by replacing pairwise queries with single-point acquisition, and from Causal-BALD (Jesson et al., 2021b) by being model-independent rather than relying on deep kernel uncertainty. The FCCM algorithm draws on the max-coverage formulation (Yehuda et al., 2022) but generalizes it from single-class to binary-treatment-group settings with cross-group counterfactual edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterfactual covering radius provides a principled, model-independent criterion for data acquisition in treatment effect estimation that explicitly accounts for distributional overlap between treatment groups — a quantity previously only implicitly handled by methods like Causal-BALD through learned uncertainty. A key limitation is that FCCM struggles when treatment groups have non-overlapping support in the raw feature space (e.g., biased treatment assignment), and the suggested remedy of operating in a learned latent space would sacrifice the model-independence advantage. Open questions include whether the covering-radius framework extends naturally to multi-valued treatments, continuous treatment doses, or settings with interference between units.
