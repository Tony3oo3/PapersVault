## Problem

Zero-shot RL—solving any new task in an environment without additional interactions—remains elusive because existing methods either produce policy-dependent representations, assume specific task structures (e.g., goal-conditioned rewards), or cannot span the full space of possible behaviors. No principled, policy-independent basis exists that provably represents all valid visitation distributions in an MDP, limiting generalization to unseen reward functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes a reward-free offline dataset of (s, a, s') tuples collected via exploratory policies (RND, DQN+RND, or random walks). At test time, given only a reward function r (or a goal state for goal-conditioned tasks), the method solves a small linear program over d-dimensional weights w (d=128 in experiments) to produce the optimal successor measure M*, the optimal Q function Q* = M*r, and the corresponding optimal policy π*. The pre-learned basis Φ and bias b are fixed and depend solely on the MDP's transition dynamics, not on any specific policy or reward.

## Method

The core theoretical insight is that all valid state-action visitation distributions and successor measures in an MDP form an affine set (solutions to the Bellman Flow linear equations), so any such quantity can be written as Φw + b where Φ is a policy-independent basis spanning the null space of the Bellman Flow operator and b is a fixed bias. For training, PSM uses a discrete codebook of deterministic policies (parameterized by random integer seeds z) to reduce the two-player bi-level optimization into a single-player game (Eq 9), jointly learning Φ, b, and w(z) via a Bellman evaluation backup on successor measure densities—avoiding the unstable Bellman optimality backups used by Forward-Backward. At inference, obtaining the optimal policy reduces to solving the constrained linear program max_w (Φw+b)ᵀr s.t. Φw+b ≥ 0, which is a small-scale optimization over the d-dimensional weight vector. The paper further proves (Theorem 4.4) that for equal dimensionality, the successor-measure-based representation strictly covers a larger class of value functions than value-function-based bases (e.g., Proto Value Functions), and shows that successor features can be recovered as a special decomposition of the PSM basis.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped, Pointmass) with d=128 representation, PSM achieves average zero-shot returns of 689.07 (Walker), 626.01 (Cheetah), 618.74 (Quadruped), and 514.09 (Pointmass), outperforming all baselines on 3 of 4 environments. On the discrete gridworld and four-room tasks, PSM achieves a policy error rate of 2.05% (gridworld) and 11.54% (discrete maze), comparable to goal-biased FB (2.13% and 12.9%) but far below un-biased FB (14.53% and 28.80%) and Laplacian (19.28% and 38.47%). On Fetch-Reach, PSM reaches near-optimal success rate quickly while FB shows training instability and Laplacian remains far from optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSM outperforms Laplacian features [Wu et al., 2018] by a wide margin on Walker (689 vs 137), Cheetah (626 vs 317), and Pointmass (514 vs 501), with only a narrow gap on Quadruped (619 vs 601), confirming that eigenvectors of a random-policy Laplacian cannot span all value functions. Against Forward-Backward [Touati et al., 2023], PSM wins on Walker (689 vs 595), Cheetah (626 vs 586), and Pointmass (514 vs 427), and also on Quadruped (619 vs 569); the paper attributes FB's relative weakness to instability from Bellman optimality backups causing overestimation. PSM also dominates HILP [Park et al., 2024] across all four environments (e.g., 689 vs 348 on Walker, 626 vs 194 on Cheetah), as HILP restricts tasks to goal-reaching and uses a narrower offline RL objective. The only environment where PSM does not achieve the top average is Quadruped, where FDM (635.65) narrowly edges PSM (618.74), though PSM still beats SVD (618.36) and FB (568.64) there. On the discrete gridworld/maze tasks, PSM matches goal-biased FB in policy error (2.05% vs 2.13% on gridworld) while substantially outperforming un-biased FB (14.53%) and Laplacian (19.28%).

## Relation to Prior Work

PSM builds directly on the Forward-Backward (FB) framework for successor measure decomposition (Touati & Ollivier, 2021a; Touati et al., 2023), reusing its density-modeling loss objective but departing fundamentally in how the representation is structured: rather than tying a latent variable to the reward function (as FB does), PSM learns a policy-independent basis for the affine space of all valid successor measures, grounded in the linear programming view of RL (Manne, 1960). It also connects to and subsumes successor features (Barreto et al., 2017) and Proto Value Functions (Mahadevan, 2005), proving via Theorem 4.4 that the successor-measure basis spans a strictly larger space of value functions than any same-dimensional value-function basis. The discrete codebook of policies for sampling during pretraining is a novel practical mechanism not present in prior zero-shot RL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSM provides the first mathematically principled guarantee that a fixed, policy-independent representation can span ALL possible behaviors in an MDP, reducing zero-shot policy inference to a constrained linear program over compact coordinates. This reframing—treating the Bellman Flow solution set as an affine space rather than learning task-specific representations—offers a cleaner theoretical foundation for the zero-shot RL program. Key open questions remain: the optimal representation dimensionality is unclear (the paper notes it is sensitive to environment smoothness), the impact of dataset coverage on zero-shot performance is unexplored, and scaling to very large continuous state spaces with non-smooth dynamics is untested. The stability advantage over FB (avoiding Bellman optimality backups) is practically important for deployment.
