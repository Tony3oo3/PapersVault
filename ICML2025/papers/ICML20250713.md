## Problem

Existing continual learning research focuses almost exclusively on mitigating catastrophic forgetting in terms of accuracy, while ignoring calibration and uncertainty quantification. Conformal prediction offers distribution-free coverage guarantees but requires exchangeable data, an assumption fundamentally violated in continual learning where tasks are learned sequentially and previous-task data are limited or inaccessible. This paper asks how to adapt conformal prediction to the non-exchangeable, sequential setting of continual learning while retaining a rigorous coverage guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained continual learning model f_T (after sequentially learning T tasks), a calibration set of N_cal replayed samples per task (total T·N_cal observations), and a test input X_ut drawn from a union task encompassing all seen tasks. It outputs a regression prediction interval [lower, upper] with asymptotic conditional coverage 1−α, where the interval endpoints are obtained by inverting a sigmoid-based nonconformity score through learned conditional quantiles. The interval width is optimized over a free parameter β ∈ [0, α] to minimize length while preserving coverage.

## Method

CPCL addresses the non-exchangeability of sequential task data by reconstructing a new training set for Quantile Regression Forests (QRF) where each input is the full score sequence X_i^R = [S_i^1, …, S_i^T] across all T tasks, encoding the sequential dependencies among nonconformity scores. A sigmoid-based score s(X,Y) = σ(Y − f_T(X)) is used because its (0,1) range is required for the QRF tree-splitting proof and its invertibility allows rewriting the score-space interval back into the output space. The QRF is trained on this reconstructed dataset to produce a conditional quantile estimator Q̂^T(x; α), and the final prediction interval is [f_T(X) − ln(1/ŝ_β − 1), f_T(X) − ln(1/ŝ_{1−α+β} − 1)] with β chosen to minimize width. The paper proves that under Lipschitz continuity and strict monotonicity of the true conditional CDF, the estimated quantile converges to the true quantile as N_cal → ∞, yielding an asymptotic conditional coverage guarantee. A notable byproduct is that prediction interval length grows with the number of learned tasks, directly reflecting catastrophic forgetting in the underlying model.

## Results

On simulated data (2 regression tasks, 5000 training samples each), CPCL achieves coverage exceeding the target 1−α across all tested significance levels (α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3}) and all continual learning methods, with coverage above 0.95 when α=0.05 using EWC. On Tiny ImageNet (5 sequential tasks, 500 samples per task, ResNet-18), CPCL maintains coverage above the desired 1−α threshold for most CL methods at α ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}. The average prediction interval length increases with the number of learned tasks, reaching approximately 9 after task 5 versus below 8 after task 2 (EWC, α=0.1), reflecting progressive catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPCL is not compared against competing uncertainty quantification methods; instead, it is applied as a post-hoc calibration layer on top of five continual learning backbones: Synaptic Intelligence (SI), EWC, MAS, DGR, and Fine-tuning. Across all five CL methods, CPCL achieves valid coverage (≥ 1−α) on both simulated and real data, confirming its method-agnostic nature. On Tiny ImageNet, the coverage swarms for most CL methods sit above the target line, though Fine-tuning (which intentionally allows catastrophic forgetting) shows the most variability. The average prediction interval length serves as a forgetting diagnostic: it grows monotonically with task count for every CL method, with EWC and MAS showing the slowest growth and Fine-tuning the fastest, consistent with their known forgetting profiles. This demonstrates that CPCL's interval width is a meaningful proxy for the degree of catastrophic forgetting induced by each CL strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the sequential conformal prediction framework for time series (Xu & Xie, 2023a,b) to the continual learning setting, where the key difference is that past-task data is limited or inaccessible rather than fully available. It leverages Quantile Regression Forests (Meinshausen, 2006) as the conditional quantile estimator, following the same tree-based construction used in the time-series CP literature. The method departs from standard split conformal prediction by replacing the empirical quantile with a QRF-based conditional quantile to handle the sequential dependencies among scores induced by non-exchangeable task data. Conceptually, the paper also introduces a new link between prediction interval width and catastrophic forgetting, connecting the uncertainty quantification literature to the CL forgetting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally address calibration and uncertainty quantification in continual learning, filling a gap the authors identify as overlooked by the CL community. The asymptotic conditional coverage guarantee provides theoretical grounding, and the observation that interval length tracks forgetting offers a practical diagnostic for evaluating CL methods. However, the scope is limited: experiments cover only regression with at most 5 tasks, use a simple replay buffer for calibration, and the theoretical guarantee is asymptotic in N_cal. Open questions include extending to classification, scaling to many more tasks, incorporating richer replay strategies, and validating the forgetting-interval connection in safety-critical deployments.
