## Problem

Existing federated causal structure learning methods assume all clients observe identical variable sets and aggregate local updates based solely on overall graph quality, ignoring the varying importance of individual relationships. In practice, clients (e.g., hospitals with different specialties) observe partially overlapping variable sets, and non-overlapping variable pairs introduce spurious dependencies that corrupt the learned causal structure. This paper addresses how to design a federated aggregation strategy that identifies both 'correct' (definite) and 'good' (stable) causal relationships across distributed datasets with non-identical variable sets.

## Input / Output

Each client c_k holds a local dataset D_k over its own observed variable set O_k (with |O_k| = d_k < d total integrated variables), and the method takes the significance level α, interval parameters θ₁ and θ₂, and FCI parameters as hyperparameters. The output consists of two graphs: FedG, a global Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG) over the integrated variable set O = ∪O_k with directed, bi-directed, and undirected edges, and FeddG, a subgraph of FedG retaining only the theoretically verified definite causal and non-causal relationships. The relative latent variables L_k = O \ O_k (variables observed by other clients but not by c_k) are treated as unobserved confounders/mediators, which is the key structural difference from identical-set federated CSL.

## Method

FedCDnv operates in two client-server communication rounds using FCI for local causal discovery, then applies a Two-level Priority Selection Strategy (TPSS) for aggregation. The first theoretical component (Theorem 3.1, Lemmas 3.2–3.3) detects spurious dependencies: Theorem 3.1 identifies definitively non-causal relationships among non-overlapping variable pairs via adjacency-set intersection conditions, while Lemmas 3.2–3.3 flag overlapping-pair relationships as non-definite when they are adjacent to unresolved non-overlapping pairs or when their ideal separating sets contain unobservable variable pairs. The second component defines 'stable' relationships using a p-value uncertainty interval [α−θ₁, α+θ₂]: a causal (non-causal) edge is stable if its maximum (minimum) CI-test p-value falls outside this interval, encoding both correctness and statistical confidence. TPSS then resolves inconsistent adjacencies across clients in two levels: Level 1 checks whether the inconsistency is explained by unobserved confounders (Condition 3.6) or incomplete separating sets (Condition 3.7) due to non-identical variable sets, and Level 2 computes a weighted vote val_ij = Σ w_τ^{c_k'} × w_ij^{c_k'} where w_τ is a sample-size weight and w_ij is an adaptive p-value-scaled importance weight that distinguishes stable from non-stable relationships, with the sign encoding causal vs. non-causal status.

## Results

On the Sachs real-world dataset with non-identical variable sets, FedCDnv achieves the best F1-edge of 0.697 and F1-orie of 0.286, outperforming the next-best method (FCI-base) by ~10% in F1-edge. On synthetic networks (10–100 nodes), FedCDnv consistently attains the highest F1-edge and F1-orie across nearly all scales, with orientation F1 scores approximately 10% higher than competing methods. On six benchmark Bayesian networks (Child, Alarm, Insurance, Barley, Child3, Alarm3), FedCDnv surpasses baselines by 8–12% in F1-orie in four of six networks. For definite relationship extraction, FedCDnv reduces the false discovery rate (FDR) for definite non-causal relationships by up to 90% compared to all returned non-causal relationships.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDUIOV (Cao et al., 2024), FedCDnv wins decisively on F1-edge (0.697 vs. 0.444) and F1-orie (0.286 vs. 0.121) on Sachs; CDUIOV's high orientation precision (0.884) is offset by extremely low recall (0.086), reflecting its dependence on correctly identifying intervention targets. Against CD-MiNi (Huang et al., 2020), FedCDnv outperforms on F1-edge in 4 of 6 small networks (5–10 nodes) and on F1-orie across all six, with orientation F1 nearly 30% higher. Against the identical-variable-set federated baselines—Notears-ADMM (Ng & Zhang, 2022), FedCSL (Guo et al., 2024), and FedPC (Huang et al., 2023)—FedCDnv achieves the best F1-edge on Sachs (0.68 vs. 0.43, 0.38, 0.49 respectively) and on most synthetic networks, though Notears-ADMM edges out FedCDnv on pre-edge by ~5% on Sachs. The voting-based variant FedCDnv-vote consistently trails the full TPSS strategy, confirming that the two-level priority selection (handling spurious dependencies + weighting by relationship stability) provides meaningful gains over simple majority voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: federated causal structure learning (FedPC, FedCSL, Notears-ADMM, FedDAG, PERI), which assumes identical variable sets across clients, and distributed causal discovery over non-identical variable sets (CD-MiNi, CDUIOV, ION, IOD, COmbINE), which allows raw data sharing. FedCDnv carves new ground by combining the privacy-preserving federated paradigm with the non-identical variable set setting, while also incorporating latent variables via the MAG/PAG formalism. Its key methodological departure is the two-level priority selection strategy (TPSS), which aggregates relationships based on both correctness (via spurious-dependency detection theories) and goodness (via stable-relationship definitions using p-value intervals), rather than relying solely on sample-size weighting or voting as prior federated methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world federated settings (e.g., hospitals with different specialties) naturally produce non-identical variable sets, yet prior federated CSL methods ignore this heterogeneity. The theoretical tools for detecting spurious dependencies induced by non-overlapping variable pairs (Theorem 3.1, Lemmas 3.2–3.3) provide a principled way to filter unreliable edges, and the FDR experiments confirm that extracted definite relationships are more reliable than the full graph. However, the authors acknowledge that the definite-relationship theories are 'not yet comprehensive,' the privacy protection is limited to p-value scaling (no formal guarantees), and the comparison with identical-variable-set federated methods is explicitly 'rough' due to differing assumptions about latent variables. Open questions include developing stronger local learners for latent-variable settings, extending the theoretical coverage, and providing formal privacy guarantees.
