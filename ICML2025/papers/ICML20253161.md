## Problem

Existing MVML methods rely on matrix-level constraints that only capture pairwise (first- or second-order) correlations between views, failing to exploit higher-order structure across the full view space. Additionally, they treat cross-view feature correlation extraction and multi-label semantic characterization as independent components, ignoring the interaction between feature representation and label semantics, which leads to suboptimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V heterogeneous view feature matrices X^v ∈ R^{n×d_v} (with potentially different dimensions d_v per view) and a binary label matrix Y ∈ {0,1}^{n×q}. A diagonal filtering matrix S selects labeled samples, enabling semi-supervised training where only a subset of instances has ground-truth labels. The output is a predicted multi-label matrix Ŷ = (1/V)Σ Z_u^v W^v for unlabeled instances, where Z_u^v are low-dimensional representations obtained via per-view matrix factorization.

## Method

TMvML merges per-view mapping matrices W^v ∈ R^{k×q} into a 3-mode tensor and rotates its dimensions from k×q×V to q×V×k, so that each frontal slice is a q×V surface enabling simultaneous comparison of label-specific rows and view-specific columns. A novel Laplace Tensor Rank (LTR) is introduced as a tighter nonconvex surrogate for tensor rank, using the approximation f_LTR(x) = 1 − exp(−e^δ x/δ) which preserves large singular values toward 1 while strongly penalizing near-zero values (noise). The full objective combines a Frobenius reconstruction loss over labeled samples, an ℓ_{2,1} robustness term on reconstruction errors, and the LTR regularization on the rotated tensor, all solved via ADMM with five alternating subproblems (Z^v, E^v, W^v, A^v, and G). The G-subproblem is solved in the Fourier domain via t-SVD with a DC-programming proximal operator, yielding a closed-form iterative update for each singular value.

## Results

TMvML achieves the best Average Precision on all six datasets, with headline values of 0.811 (Emotions), 0.771 (Yeast), 0.440 (Corel5k), 0.608 (Plant), 0.306 (Espgame), and 0.631 (Human). The most dramatic gains appear in Coverage, where TMvML reduces COV to 0.150–0.470 while the best baselines range from 0.30 (IMvMLC on Emotions) to 210.9 (LrMMC on Espgame). Across 180 comparisons (6 datasets × 6 baselines × 5 metrics), TMvML wins in 94.44% of Ranking Loss cases, 91.67% of Hamming Loss cases, and 100% of AP, OE, and COV cases. Friedman and Bonferroni-Dunn tests confirm statistical significance at the 0.05 level for all five metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMvML outperforms all six baselines on every dataset for Average Precision, One Error, and Coverage. The closest competitor on AP is FIMAN (Wu et al., 2020), which reaches 0.806 on Emotions and 0.430 on Corel5k, but TMvML edges ahead with 0.811 and 0.440 respectively; on Yeast the gap widens to 0.771 vs. 0.740. ICM2L (Tan et al., 2021) is the strongest baseline on Plant (AP 0.587) and Human (AP 0.603), yet TMvML still leads by 2.1 and 2.8 AP points. The Coverage metric reveals the largest absolute gaps: on Espgame, TMvML achieves COV 0.409 versus 92.21 (IMvMLC) and 210.9 (LrMMC); on Corel5k, TMvML's 0.266 dwarfs the best baseline FIMAN at 53.94. SIMM (Wu et al., 2019) and ML-BVAE (Fu et al., 2024) are consistently outperformed across all metrics and datasets, with ML-BVAE showing particularly weak AP (0.572 on Emotions, 0.286 on Corel5k). LrMMC (Liu et al., 2015) is the weakest baseline overall, trailing TMvML by 4.8 AP points on Emotions and 13.6 on Corel5k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant matrix-based MVML paradigm (e.g., ICM2L, LVSL, GNAM) that handles cross-view consistency and multi-label semantics through separate, independent components, by unifying both into a single 3-mode tensor classifier whose frontal slices encode the joint label×view structure. The tensor low-rank constraint draws on the t-SVD/TNN lineage (Xie et al., 2018) and the more recent Enhanced Tensor Rank (Ji & Feng, 2025), but introduces a new Laplace-based rank surrogate (LTR) inspired by the Laplace function from nonconvex low-rank matrix minimization (Lu et al., 2015). The rotation of the coefficient tensor to reposition the label×view surface as the frontal slice is a novel structural choice not present in prior tensor-based multi-view methods, which typically operate on feature×view tensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual unification of cross-view and multi-label structure in a single tensor offers a cleaner theoretical framework for MVML and the LTR construction may be reusable in other tensor low-rank problems beyond classification. However, the evaluation is limited to six relatively small datasets (largest: 20,770 samples, 268 labels) with no comparison to deep-learning-based MVML methods, and the dramatic Coverage improvements (e.g., 0.266 vs. 96.72 on Corel5k) coexist with only modest Average Precision gains, leaving open whether the tensor formulation scales to large-scale or incomplete multi-view settings. A key open question is whether the ADMM-based optimization and t-SVD computations remain tractable as the number of views or labels grows substantially.
