## Problem

The price of differential privacy in adversarial bandits was poorly understood: existing algorithms (Agarwal & Singh, Tossou & Dimitrakakis) achieve regret O(√(KT log(KT))/ε) under local differential privacy, which becomes vacuous (linear regret) when ε < 1/√T, and it was unknown whether sublinear regret is achievable under central differential privacy at such high privacy levels. Additionally, no differentially private algorithms existed for the bandits-with-expert-advice problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of adversarial loss functions ℓ_t: [K]→[0,1] (revealed only for the selected arm) and, in the expert-advice variant, per-round expert predictions μ_t^j ∈ Π([K]) for j∈[N]. It outputs a sequence of arm selections I_t ∈ [K] that is ε-differentially private with respect to the loss sequence, along with worst-case expected regret guarantees. The privacy parameter ε and batch size τ are tunable hyperparameters that control the privacy-utility tradeoff, with τ = ⌈1/ε⌉ as the default choice.

## Method

The core contribution is a generic conversion (Theorem 3.1) that transforms any non-private bandit algorithm B into an ε-differentially private one by batching: the algorithm plays the same arm for τ consecutive rounds, averages the observed losses over the batch, adds independent Laplace noise of scale 1/(τε) to the batched loss, and feeds this noisy value to B. This reduces the required noise magnitude by a factor of τ compared to per-round noise, yielding regret O(√(KT)/√ε) when instantiated with HTINF (an FTRL variant for heavy-tailed losses), EXP3, or FTPL. For bandits with expert advice, the conversion composes with the standard bandit-to-expert-advice reduction (treating each expert as a meta-action), and a separate batched EXP4 algorithm (Theorem 4.5) uses per-expert Laplace noise on batched importance-weighted loss estimates to handle large N. The key technical requirement is that the underlying non-private algorithm must handle negative and unbounded losses, which is satisfied by algorithms designed for heavy-tailed settings.

## Results

For adversarial bandits, the paper achieves an ε-differentially private regret bound of O(√(KT)/√ε), improving upon the previous best of O(√(KT log(KT))/ε) for all ε ≤ 1. This yields sublinear regret even when ε ≤ 1/√T, establishing the first separation between central and local differential privacy for this problem. For bandits with expert advice, three new (ε,δ)-differentially private algorithms are given with regret O(√(NT)/√ε), O(√(KT log(N)) log(KT)/ε), and Õ(N^{1/6}K^{1/2}T^{2/3} log(NT)/ε^{1/3} + N^{1/2} log(NT)/ε), covering different regimes of K, N, and ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior works on private adversarial bandits: Agarwal & Singh (2017) and Tossou & Dimitrakakis (2017), both of which achieve O(√(KT log(K))/ε) regret but only under the stronger local differential privacy guarantee. The proposed method strictly dominates both baselines in every regime of ε ≤ 1, improving the dependence on ε from 1/ε to 1/√ε and removing the log(KT) factor. Crucially, the new bound remains sublinear (o(T)) for all ε ∈ ω(1/T), whereas the local DP baselines become vacuous (linear regret) once ε < 1/√T. For bandits with expert advice, no prior private algorithms existed, so the paper provides the first results, with three complementary guarantees covering low-dimension/high-privacy, high-dimension/low-privacy, and high-dimension/high-privacy regimes respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adversarial bandit literature (EXP3, EXP4, FTRL, FTPL) and prior private bandit work by Agarwal & Singh (2017) and Tossou & Dimitrakakis (2017), which added Laplace noise to individual losses and achieved 1/ε regret dependence. The key departure is a generic conversion that adds Laplace noise to *batched* losses rather than per-round losses, reducing the noise magnitude by a factor of the batch size and yielding 1/√ε dependence. This conversion requires the underlying non-private algorithm to handle negative, unbounded losses, creating a novel bridge to the heavy-tailed bandit literature (HTINF, Huang et al. 2022). For bandits with expert advice, no prior private algorithms existed, making this the first such result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first separation between central and local differential privacy for adversarial bandits: sublinear regret is achievable under central DP for ε < 1/√T, while local DP provably requires linear regret in that regime. This has practical implications for settings like medical trials and online advertising where high privacy budgets are needed. Open questions include whether an additive separation between ε and T is possible (the paper shows EXP3-type algorithms cannot achieve one), extending results to adaptive adversaries, and obtaining non-vacuous bounds for bandits with expert advice when N ≥ T and ε ≤ 1/√T.
