## Problem

Standard Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality reduction can handle weighted Euclidean distances only when weights are known and fixed at preprocessing time, since one simply scales input vectors before projecting. When weights are unknown at reduction time or change dynamically (e.g., query-dependent feature relevance in nearest-neighbor search or recommendation systems), no linear dimensionality reduction method exists that can recover weighted distances from the compressed vectors alone. This paper provides the first such technique.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vector x ∈ R^d and maps it to a reduced vector g(x) ∈ C^k via a linear map that is completely oblivious to any weight vector. Once a weight vector w ∈ R^d is revealed at query time, the estimator ρ(g(x), w) = Re(Σᵢ h(g(x),w)ᵢ) is computed solely from the reduced vector and w, yielding an unbiased estimate of ‖x‖²_w. Because g is linear, pairwise weighted distances are estimated as ρ(g(x)−g(y), w) without ever accessing the original vectors, enabling applications like nearest-neighbor search and clustering under dynamic weights.

## Method

The core construction uses a linear map g(x) = Ax/√k where A is a k×d complex random matrix with i.i.d. entries drawn uniformly from {+1, −1, +i, −i}. The estimator ρ(g(x),w) is built from element-wise products of the reduced vector with a weight-dependent term, and its expectation equals ‖x‖²_w. The analysis decomposes the estimator into sums of Rademacher chaoses (products of Rademacher random variables) and proves novel concentration inequalities for sums of independent Rademacher chaoses by generalizing Bonami's hypercontractive inequality to arbitrary index sets and combining it with sub-Weibull concentration bounds. For near-uniform vectors, a sparse block matrix with L independent sub-projections reduces the output dimension from Θ(Δ²) to Θ(Δ), where Δ captures the ratio ‖x‖²₂‖w‖⁴₄/‖x‖²_w.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) shows that a linear complex-valued projection into k = Ω(max{Δ² ln(8/δ)/ε², Δ ln(8/δ)²/ε}) dimensions yields an unbiased estimator of the weighted norm with additive error ε‖w‖₄²‖x‖₂²/Δ, and a multiplicative ε-approximation when ‖x‖₂²‖w‖₄²/‖x‖_w² ≤ Δ. For near-uniform vectors (Theorem 4.6), the sparse block construction reduces the required dimension from Θ(Δ²) to Θ(Δ), i.e., k·L = Θ(max{(Δ/ε) ln(8/δ)^{1/2}, (Δ/ε)^{2/3} ln(8/δ)^{4/3}}). In the proof-of-concept experiments (d = 2×10⁴, N = 10³ random matrices), the sparse block estimator (Theorem 4.6) with L = 100 and k·L ∈ {10², 10³, 10⁴} shows a significant variance reduction compared to the single-matrix linear estimator, nearly closing the gap with the nonlinear Kaban (2015) baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its linear complex-valued estimator against the nonlinear JL-based estimator of Kaban (2015), which applies standard random projections to the element-wise squared vectors x⊙x and w⊙w. The Kaban method achieves a tighter error bound (depending on ‖x‖₄² rather than ‖x‖₂²) but is fundamentally nonlinear, preventing it from estimating pairwise weighted distances. The experiments (Figures 1–5) confirm this trade-off: the nonlinear estimator has lower variance for near-uniform vectors, while the linear method (Theorem 1.1) shows wider spread in the worst-case near-uniform setting. The sparse block extension (Theorem 4.6) with L = 100 nearly closes this variance gap, as shown in Figures 3–4, while retaining linearity. For sparse vectors (Figure 5), the linear method's concentration depends on the sparsity level m rather than the ambient dimension d, demonstrating practical applicability. Overall, the paper's method sacrifices a slightly weaker norm dependence (‖x‖₂ vs ‖x‖₄) in exchange for linearity, which is essential for pairwise distance estimation in nearest-neighbor search and clustering applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality reduction framework to the setting where Euclidean distance weights are unknown at projection time and may change dynamically. It departs from the standard approach of pre-scaling vectors by known weights (which trivially reduces to classical JL) and from the nonlinear JL-on-squared-vectors trick of Kaban (2015), by introducing a complex-valued linear projection with entries in {+1,−1,+i,−i} that preserves linearity while enabling unbiased recovery of weighted norms. The analytical backbone generalizes Bonami's hypercontractive inequality to arbitrary Rademacher chaoses and combines it with sub-Weibull concentration (Kuchibhotla & Chakrabortty, 2022) to derive the first tail bounds for sums of independent Rademacher chaoses. Recent work on weighted nearest-neighbor search (Lei et al., 2019; Hu & Li, 2021) addresses the same dynamic-weight setting but via LSH with dimensionality expansion, not via dimensionality reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dimensionality reduction technique that is oblivious to weights and can recover weighted Euclidean norms and pairwise distances after weights are revealed, opening the door to applications in dynamic recommender systems, attention-weighted retrieval, and weighted least squares under changing feature relevance. The concentration inequalities for sums of independent Rademacher chaoses (Theorem 1.2) are stated to be of independent interest and could find use beyond this specific application. Key limitations include the boundedness condition ‖x‖₂²‖w‖₄²/‖x‖w² ≤ Δ required for multiplicative guarantees, which can be restrictive for sparse or highly non-uniform weights, and the fact that the near-uniform improvement (reducing dimension from Θ(Δ²) to Θ(Δ)) requires prior knowledge of the data distribution. Open questions include a fast complex JL transform, generalization to arbitrary Lp norms, and practical deployment in downstream ML pipelines.
