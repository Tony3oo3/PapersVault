## Problem

Existing diffusion inversion methods (DDIM, NTI, PNPInv, fixed-point iteration) produce noise maps that faithfully reconstruct the source image but fail to encode the target edit into the latent representation, resulting in poor edit fidelity and artifacts. The core gap is that inverted noise maps are optimized solely for source reconstruction, leaving them poorly aligned with the desired target prompt and limiting the flexibility of downstream attention-based editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251135/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt, and a target text prompt, and produces a sequence of editable noise maps (latent vectors z_T through z_1) in the Stable Diffusion latent space. These noise maps are then consumed by existing attention-based editing pipelines (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, Plug-and-Play) to generate the final edited image or video. The method operates at 50 DDIM inversion steps with CFG scale 7.5, and requires a separate inversion pass for each target prompt (unlike methods that reuse a single inversion across multiple edits).

## Method

ENM Inversion augments standard DDIM inversion with an iterative 'editable noise refinement' at each timestep. The key empirical observation is that noise maps yielding high-quality edits exhibit minimal L2 difference between the denoised outputs under the source prompt and the target prompt (Equation 5). At each inversion step, z_t is refined by minimizing a combined loss L = L_prev + λ·L_edit, where L_prev penalizes deviation from the source reconstruction and L_edit minimizes the gap between f(z_t, t, C_src) and f(z_t, t, C_tgt), effectively imprinting the target image onto the noise. A threshold τ terminates refinement early when the loss drops below a predefined value, balancing quality and speed. The resulting noise maps are then used with standard attention-transfer editing (cross-attention or self-attention injection) to produce the final edit, and the same procedure extends to per-frame video inversion for temporally consistent video editing.

## Results

On PIE-Bench with Prompt-to-Prompt, ENM Inversion achieves the best structure distance (10.13×10³), background preservation (PSNR 28.19, LPIPS 45.26×10³, SSIM 86.29×10²), and CLIP similarity (25.30 whole, 22.12 edited) among all tested inversion methods. With MasaCtrl and Plug-and-Play, it similarly leads on structure distance (22.89×10³ and 18.44×10³) and CLIP similarity. For video editing on DAVIS, ENM Inversion + Video-P2P achieves the highest CLIP score (26.57) and SSIM (75.43) while maintaining competitive temporal consistency (TEMP 0.9454). Inference time is 38.87 s per image, substantially faster than NTI (148.48 s) and StyleD (382.98 s) but slower than plain DDIM (18.22 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENM Inversion outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary P2P protocol: it reduces structure distance from 11.65 (PNPInv, the closest competitor) to 10.13×10³, improves PSNR from 27.22 to 28.19, and raises CLIP whole-image similarity from 25.02 to 25.30. Against DDIM Inversion, the gains are dramatic—structure distance drops from 69.43 to 10.13×10³ and LPIPS from 208.80 to 45.26×10³—confirming that the editable noise refinement corrects the large approximation errors of vanilla DDIM. Compared to fixed-point iteration methods (AIDI, FPI, ReNoise), ENM Inversion simultaneously improves both preservation (e.g., PSNR 28.19 vs. 26.96 for AIDI) and edit fidelity (CLIP edited 22.12 vs. 22.01 for AIDI), whereas those methods sacrifice CLIP similarity for reconstruction accuracy. In video editing, ENM Inversion + Video-P2P beats NTI + Video-P2P on CLIP (26.57 vs. 26.14), LPIPS (98.24 vs. 104.64), and SSIM (75.43 vs. 74.74), while matching its temporal consistency (TEMP 0.9454 vs. 0.9451). The main trade-off is inference time: at 38.87 s per image, ENM Inversion is roughly 2× slower than DDIM (18.22 s) and PNPInv (28.17 s), though still 4× faster than NTI and 10× faster than StyleD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDIM inversion as its base procedure but departs from the dominant line of inversion work (NTI, NPI, PNPInv, AIDI, FPI, ReNoise) that optimizes exclusively for source-image reconstruction fidelity. The key conceptual shift is reframing inversion as a joint problem: the noise maps should simultaneously encode the source image (for preservation) and the target image (for editability), formalized through a new edit-alignment loss (L_edit) that minimizes the gap between source-prompt and target-prompt denoised outputs at each timestep. The method is designed as a drop-in replacement for the inversion stage in existing attention-based editing pipelines (P2P, MasaCtrl, PnP, Video-P2P), inheriting their editing mechanisms while improving the quality of the inverted noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized trade-off in diffusion-based editing: methods that achieve high reconstruction fidelity (e.g., PNPInv, fixed-point iteration) tend to produce poor edits, while more 'editable' inversions sacrifice structural preservation. By encoding the target prompt into the noise maps during inversion, ENM Inversion narrows this gap, though the improvements over the strongest baseline (PNPInv) are moderate rather than dramatic. A notable limitation is that the method requires a separate inversion pass for each target prompt, unlike DDIM or PNPInv which can reuse a single inversion across multiple edits, making it less efficient for multi-edit workflows. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-attention-based editing paradigms, whether the per-timestep refinement can be accelerated, and how it behaves with next-generation diffusion architectures beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5.
