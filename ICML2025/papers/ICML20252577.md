## Problem

While prior work observed that transformer Hessians exhibit blockwise heterogeneity, no clear ordering principle across all major block types (embedding, QK, VO, FFN, normalization) had been established, and this structural property had not been exploited to accelerate LLM pre-training. The paper asks whether a consistent sharpness disparity exists across block types throughout training, and if so, whether it can be leveraged to design more efficient optimization strategies without compromising stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252577/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard transformer architecture (GPT-2 or LLaMA, 0.12B–2B parameters), a text corpus (OpenWebText, MiniPile, or C4), and a base optimizer (AdamW, Adam-mini, or Lion) as input, and produces a trained LLM with per-block-type learning rates. The four adjusting ratios (r(Emb)=10, r(QK)=8, r(FFN)=6, r(VO)=4) are tuned once on a single small-scale experiment and transfer directly across all model sizes, datasets, and optimizers without re-tuning. Sharpness is estimated efficiently via a diagonal Fisher approximation (Eq. 3) requiring only one mini-batch gradient computation rather than per-sample gradients.

## Method

The paper discovers and formalizes the Sharpness Disparity Principle: S(Emb) ≪ S(QK) < S(FFN) < S(VO) ≪ S(Norm), which emerges within the first ~2% of training steps and persists throughout. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 4.1–4.3) derives gradient-norm bounds showing that blockwise average sharpness scales as O(Ψ²/(D^k ‖W‖²_F)), where the denominator reflects parameter count and norm, explaining why blocks with fewer parameters (Norm) and smaller norms exhibit higher sharpness. The Blockwise LR strategy keeps the learning rate of the sharpest block (Norm) at the base value to preserve training stability, while amplifying LRs of flatter blocks by fixed ratios proportional to their relative flatness. This is seamlessly integrated into AdamW, Adam-mini, and Lion, achieving nearly 2× speedup with lower terminal loss across all tested configurations, and when combined with Adam-mini yields simultaneous 2× speedup and 2× memory saving.

## Results

AdamW with Blockwise LR achieves a nearly 2× speedup (1.92× on LLaMA 1.1B on OpenWebText) and lower terminal loss compared to vanilla AdamW, consistently across GPT-2 and LLaMA models (0.12B–2B) on OpenWebText, MiniPile, and C4. On downstream 0-shot evaluation (LLaMA 1.1B, 50K steps, OpenWebText), Blockwise LR outperforms AdamW on all seven tasks, e.g., HellaSwag 38.00 vs. 36.13, WinoGrande 59.83 vs. 55.17, and OBQA 22.60 vs. 19.40. When combined with Adam-mini, Blockwise LR yields a combined 2× speedup and 2× memory saving over vanilla AdamW; the same 2× speedup is observed with Lion and with the WSD scheduler.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla AdamW, Blockwise LR consistently wins: it reaches the same validation loss in roughly half the training steps (≈2× speedup) and achieves lower terminal loss at fixed step counts across all model sizes and datasets tested. Against Adam-mini (a memory-efficient Adam variant), Blockwise LR adds a further 2× speedup on top of Adam-mini's existing 2× memory reduction, yielding a combined 2× speedup + 2× memory saving over vanilla AdamW. Against Lion (a sign-based optimizer that eliminates second-order moments), Blockwise LR also delivers a 2× speedup, and the sharpness disparity principle (Eq. 1) persists under Lion training, confirming the method's optimizer-agnostic nature. The paper does not report any setting where Blockwise LR underperforms the corresponding baseline; the only negative result is an ablation showing that increasing the Norm block LR (the sharpest direction) degrades performance, reinforcing the rationale for keeping Norm at the base LR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on blockwise Hessian heterogeneity in transformers (Zhang et al., 2024b; Ormaniec et al., 2024) by establishing a comprehensive sharpness disparity principle across all five block types (Emb, QK, VO, FFN, Norm) rather than focusing on individual blocks or a single layer. It departs from Wang et al. (2024), which adjusted learning rates at the individual parameter level based on sharpness estimates, by operating at the coarser block-type level—requiring only four fixed hyperparameters rather than per-parameter Hessian estimation. The strategy is grounded in the edge-of-stability (EoS) literature (Wen et al., 2024; Song et al., 2024) on fast-slow dynamics, specifically targeting acceleration along low-sharpness 'river' directions while preserving stability along high-sharpness directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a four-hyperparameter modification to any Adam-family optimizer yields nearly 2× pre-training speedup with no additional memory overhead, and it composes cleanly with memory-efficient variants like Adam-mini (achieving simultaneous 2× speedup and 2× memory saving). The robustness of the adjusting ratios across architectures (GPT-2, LLaMA), scales (0.12B–2B), datasets, and optimizers (AdamW, Adam-mini, Lion) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include why Blockwise LR tends to outperform vanilla AdamW only from mid-to-late training (resembling WSD scheduler behavior), whether the principle extends to non-LLM architectures like Mamba, and whether blockwise weight decay or gradient clipping could yield further gains. The theoretical bounds are loose (big-O) and do not fully explain the QK < VO ordering, leaving room for tighter analysis.
