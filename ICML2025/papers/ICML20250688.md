## Problem

Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) with low held-out test errors do not consistently translate to accurate physical property predictions such as phonon calculations, thermal conductivity, and materials stability. The paper identifies that this gap arises when models fail to conserve energy during molecular dynamics simulations, and shows that passing an energy-conservation test restores a strong correlation between test-set error and downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250688/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes atomic positions, atomic numbers, and (for periodic systems) lattice parameters, and produces a total potential energy by summing per-atom energies predicted from the final layer's L=0 spherical-harmonic features. Forces and stress are obtained by backpropagating through the energy prediction rather than from a separate force head, ensuring the forces are exact gradients of the learned potential energy surface. For periodic systems, stress is the gradient with respect to lattice parameters.

## Method

eSEN is a message-passing equivariant network that alternates edgewise SO(2) convolution blocks (inherited from eSCN, with source/target embeddings concatenated before two SO(2) convolutions) and nodewise equivariant linear layers with SiLU-based gated non-linearity (from Equiformer). Three key design choices ensure a smoothly varying, energy-conserving PES: (1) forces are computed as the negative gradient of energy via backpropagation rather than predicted directly; (2) non-linearities are applied directly in the spherical-harmonic representation without projecting onto a discrete spatial grid, avoiding Nyquist-frequency sampling errors; and (3) a distance cutoff with polynomial envelope functions is used instead of a fixed neighbor limit, preventing discontinuities in the PES. Training employs a two-stage strategy: direct-force pre-training (with DeNS denoising) followed by conservative fine-tuning, which reduces wallclock training time by 40% while achieving lower validation loss than training a conservative model from scratch.

## Results

On the Matbench-Discovery compliant benchmark, eSEN-30M-MP achieves the highest F1 score of 0.831 and the lowest κ_SRME of 0.340, simultaneously excelling in both materials stability and thermal conductivity prediction—something no prior model achieves. On the non-compliant benchmark, eSEN-30M-OAM reaches F1=0.925 and κ_SRME=0.170. On the MDR Phonon benchmark, eSEN-30M-MP achieves SOTA compliant results with MAE(ω_max)=21 K, MAE(S)=13 J/K/mol, MAE(F)=5 kJ/mol, and MAE(C_V)=4 J/K/mol, while eSEN-30M-OAM achieves the best non-compliant results (15/10/4/3). On SPICE-MACE-OFF, eSEN-6.5M outperforms both MACE-4.7M and the 45M-parameter direct-force model EscAIP on all test splits for energy and force MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eSEN-30M-MP outperforms all 10 compliant baselines on every Matbench-Discovery metric, with the most dramatic gains in κ_SRME (0.340 vs. 0.525–1.725 for all competitors), confirming that energy-conserving design is critical for thermal conductivity prediction. The closest competitor, eqV2 S DeNS (direct-force), trails on F1 (0.815 vs. 0.831) but is far behind on κ_SRME (1.676 vs. 0.340), illustrating the cost of non-conservative forces. On the MDR Phonon benchmark, eSEN-30M-MP beats the best compliant baselines GRACE-2L(r6) and SevenNet-l3i5 on all four phonon metrics (e.g., MAE(ω_max): 21 vs. 40/26 K; MAE(S): 13 vs. 25/28 J/K/mol), and substantially outperforms MACE (61 K) and CHGNet (89 K). Among non-compliant models, eSEN-30M-OAM leads on F1 (0.925 vs. 0.917 for eqV2-M-OAM) and κ_SRME (0.170 vs. 0.210 for ORB v3), while matching SevenNet-MF-ompa on MAE(ω_max) (15 K) but leading on entropy (10 vs. 8 J/K/mol). On SPICE-MACE-OFF, eSEN-6.5M (6.5M params) surpasses both MACE-4.7M and the 9× larger EscAIP-45M (direct-force) on all seven test splits for both energy and force MAE, e.g., 0.15/2.50 meV/atom and meV/Å on Water vs. 0.72/10.31 for EscAIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

eSEN builds directly on the SO(2) convolution layer from eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) and the SiLU-based equivariant gated non-linearity and equivariant layer normalization from Equiformer (Liao & Smidt, 2022), but departs from the broader field trend toward direct-force prediction (Orb, EquiformerV2) by arguing that conservative force prediction via backpropagation is essential for physical property prediction. The paper's key conceptual departure is from the standard MLIP evaluation paradigm (test-set MAE on static DFT configurations) toward a dynamic evaluation criterion: whether the model conserves energy in NVE MD simulations, grounded in the Verlet integrator energy-drift bounds of Hairer et al. (2003). The ablation design choices (neighbor limits, envelope functions, representation discretization, radial basis count) are drawn from existing architectures but are here systematically isolated for their effect on energy conservation rather than test accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical MD energy conservation test offers a cheap, domain-agnostic diagnostic that correlates with downstream physical property prediction accuracy, potentially replacing expensive property-specific benchmarks during model development. The simultaneous SOTA on both F1 (materials stability) and κ_SRME (thermal conductivity) on Matbench-Discovery—where prior models could only excel at one metric—demonstrates that the conservative/smooth design philosophy is not a trade-off against accuracy. Open questions include whether the energy conservation test generalizes to all property prediction tasks (e.g., electronic structure, reaction barriers), whether the direct-force pre-training + conservative fine-tuning strategy can be further optimized, and whether the findings extend to larger-scale or multi-scale simulation settings.
