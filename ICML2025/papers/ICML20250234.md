## Problem

In sparse PCA under the single-spiked covariance model, information theory shows Ω(k log p) samples suffice to recover a k-sparse p-dimensional spike, yet all known polynomial-time algorithms require at least Ω(k²) samples. This paper bridges that gap by showing that, under the mild condition λ = Ω(||v||_∞^{-1}), a polynomial-time algorithm can achieve the information-theoretic sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes n noisy observations x_i = √λ g_i v + ξ_i in R^p (with g_i ~ N(0,1), ξ_i ~ N(0,I)) and the sparsity level k, and outputs an estimated k-sparse unit vector v̂. The key model assumption is that the signal strength λ scales as Ω(||v||_∞^{-1}), which is naturally satisfied by power-law-decay (compressible) signals. The estimation error is measured up to sign ambiguity via dist(v, v̂) = min{||v−v̂||₂, ||v+v̂||₂}.

## Method

The first algorithm replaces diagonal thresholding's support estimation (top-k diagonal entries of the empirical covariance) with a column-based thresholding step: it identifies j₀ as the index of the largest diagonal entry, then selects the top-k entries in the j₀-th column of Σ̂ in absolute value as the estimated support. This yields a larger statistical gap (scaling as λ|v_l|·min|v_j| rather than λ·min v_j²), reducing sample complexity from Ω(k² log p) to Ω(k log p). The second algorithm is a two-stage nonconvex procedure: the thresholding algorithm provides an initial estimate v⁰, which is then refined by truncated power iteration (retaining only top-k' entries at each step). The total error decomposes into an optimization error decaying linearly in iterations and a statistical error at the minimax optimal rate √(k log p / n).

## Results

The proposed thresholding algorithm achieves the information-theoretic sample complexity of Ω(k log p) for constant-error spike recovery (vs. Ω(k²) for all prior polynomial-time methods), under the condition λ = Ω(||v||∞⁻¹). On the standard 'Three-Peak' benchmark (p=2000, n=1000, λ=8), the two-stage algorithm attains an estimation error of 0.1907 and F-score of 0.8325, outperforming Diagonal Thresholding (error 0.6283, F-score 0.4804), Soft Covariance Thresholding (error 0.2000, F-score 0.8038), and Hard Covariance Thresholding (error 0.3395, F-score 0.7778). The two-stage algorithm achieves the minimax optimal statistical error rate of √(k log p / n) with linear convergence of the optimization error, and its total computational cost is O(np + nk²), matching diagonal thresholding and far below the O(np² + p³) of covariance thresholding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diagonal Thresholding (Johnstone & Lu, 2009), the proposed method achieves a 3.3× reduction in estimation error (0.1907 vs. 0.6283) and a 73% improvement in F-score (0.8325 vs. 0.4804) on the Three-Peak benchmark, while also reducing sample complexity from Ω(k² log p) to Ω(k log p). Against Soft Covariance Thresholding (Deshpande & Montanari, 2016), the proposed method achieves a slightly lower estimation error (0.1907 vs. 0.2000) and a higher F-score (0.8325 vs. 0.8038), while being dramatically faster in computation (O(np + nk²) vs. O(np² + p³)); at p=10000, Soft-CT's runtime grows dramatically while the proposed method scales near-linearly. Against Hard Covariance Thresholding (Krauthgamer et al., 2015), the proposed method achieves a 44% lower estimation error (0.1907 vs. 0.3395) and a higher F-score (0.8325 vs. 0.7778) with the same computational advantage. Across all dimensions tested (p=1000 to 10000) and signal strengths (λ=5, 10), the proposed algorithm consistently achieves the smallest estimation error among all four methods, with the gap over DT being the most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diagonal thresholding (Johnstone & Lu, 2009) by replacing its diagonal-entry-based support estimation with a column-entry-based approach, drawing inspiration from spectral initialization in sparse phase retrieval. The two-stage refinement stage directly incorporates the truncated power method (Yuan & Zhang, 2013), which previously lacked a provable initialization at the required sample complexity. The key conceptual departure from all prior polynomial-time methods (diagonal thresholding, covariance thresholding, SDP, SoS) is exploiting the signal-strength condition λ = Ω(||v||∞⁻¹) to amplify the statistical gap between in-support and out-of-support entries, thereby reducing sample complexity from Ω(k²) to the information-theoretic Ω(k log p).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a long-standing gap between information-theoretic and computational sample complexity for sparse PCA, showing that a mild structural assumption on signal strength (naturally satisfied by compressible/power-law-decaying spikes) is sufficient to achieve optimal sample complexity in polynomial time. The algorithm maintains O(np + nk²) computational cost—identical to the simpler diagonal thresholding—while achieving the minimax optimal statistical error rate. Open questions include whether the signal-strength condition is truly necessary, whether support recovery consistency can be established at the same sample complexity, and whether the approach extends to multi-spike or non-Gaussian settings.
