## Problem

Tensor Parallelism (TP) for LLM inference requires a blocking AllReduce after every attention and MLP sub-layer, which can consume 38–50% of end-to-end latency even on NVLink-connected GPUs. Prior overlap strategies (fused kernels, domain-specific compilers) are low-level, hardware-specific, and must be rewritten for each new accelerator generation. The paper asks whether a minimal architectural change to the model itself can decouple communication from computation, making overlap achievable at the framework level without custom device code.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard text prompts and produces generated text completions, with no change to the external I/O contract of the model. The unusual aspect is purely internal: each sub-module (attention, MLP) receives a 'stale' residual input from two layers back rather than the immediately preceding layer, while the residual stream itself still accumulates the full AllReduce output of every layer. This means the computation graph is rewired but the model still accepts a token sequence and emits a token sequence in the standard autoregressive fashion.

## Method

Ladder Residual reroutes the residual stream so that module h_{i+1} takes x_{i-1} (the residual after layer i−1) as its input instead of x_i, while the residual stream still updates normally as x_i = AllReduce(x_i*) + x_{i-1}. This single rewiring decouples the computation of h_{i+1} from the AllReduce of x_i*, allowing the AllReduce to be issued as an AsyncAllReduce and overlapped with the next layer's attention or MLP computation. The justification is that activation changes slowly in Transformers (each update's norm is small relative to the residual), so feeding a one-layer-stale input causes only minimal representation shift. In practice, AsyncAllReduce handles are passed between layers and waited on at the start of the next sub-module; the approach is validated by training 1.2B and 3.5B Ladder Transformers from scratch on 100B FineWeb-edu tokens (matching standard Transformer accuracy) and by adapting Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with only 3B tokens of SFT on the upper 16 layers, yielding 21–29% end-to-end inference speedup at TP world size 8.

## Results

For a 70B Transformer with TP world size 8, Ladder Residual achieves 29% end-to-end wall-clock speedup (1.29×) with P2P enabled and 59% speedup (1.59×) with P2P disabled. Training a 1.2B Ladder Transformer from scratch on 100B tokens yields 58.92 average benchmark accuracy and 18.42 Wikitext PPL, closely matching the standard Transformer (59.98 / 18.54). Adapting Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to Ladder Residual on the upper 16 layers with only 3B tokens of SFT recovers performance to 56.24 average across 9 benchmarks (vs. 56.11 for the original), while delivering 21% inference speedup at TP=8, batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Parallel Attention/MLP architecture (PaLM/GPT-J style), Ladder Residual wins on inference throughput: on the 70B model at TP=8, Ladder achieves 23.71% decode latency improvement vs. 18.04% for Parallel (Table 2), and 30.79% token/sec improvement vs. 21.75%. On training-from-scratch accuracy (Table 3), Ladder-1.2B (58.92 avg) slightly beats Parallel-1.2B (58.75) but both trail Standard-1.2B (59.98); at 3.5B scale, Ladder (62.91) and Parallel (63.03) are nearly tied, both ~1.2 points below Standard (64.11). Against Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Table 4), the 16-layer Ladder adaptation with 3B-token SFT matches the original at 56.24 vs. 56.11 average, with the largest per-benchmark gaps on MMLU (−2.2 pts) and GSM8K (−4.7 pts), while actually exceeding the original on IFEval (+6.5 pts) and AlpacaEval (+3.3 pts). The zero-shot (no retraining) variant drops to 41.65 average, confirming that light adaptation is necessary. The 20-layer variant (53.86) shows diminishing returns from adapting more layers without additional retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on Tensor Parallelism communication overlap (kernel fusion in Chang et al. 2024, DSL-based codegen in Jangda et al. 2022, decomposition in Wang et al. 2022) by arguing that the model architecture itself can be redesigned to make communication non-blocking, rather than relying on low-level system optimizations that require rewriting for each hardware generation. The key insight builds on Liu et al. (2023b)'s observation that residual stream activations change slowly, motivating the use of a 'stale' input one step behind. The parallel attention/MLP design from PaLM and GPT-J is used as a baseline but addresses the problem differently by halving the number of AllReduce operations rather than overlapping them with computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs grow beyond single-node capacity, cross-node Tensor Parallelism becomes increasingly necessary, and communication latency is the dominant bottleneck; Ladder Residual offers a hardware-agnostic, framework-level fix (applicable in PyTorch/JAX without custom kernels) that achieves 29–60% inference speedup depending on interconnect quality. The practical finding that only 3B tokens of SFT are needed to adapt Llama-3.1-8B (versus 50B for Mamba conversion) suggests the representation shift is mild and the technique could be a drop-in for existing checkpoints. Open questions include whether more layers can be adapted with smarter training (distillation, iterative fine-tuning), whether the small 3.5B-scale performance gap closes with more training, and how the method interacts with emerging parallelism strategies beyond TP.
