## Problem

Autoregressive image models (PixelCNN, iGPT, DALL-E) rely on deep Transformer architectures to capture high-order token dependencies, raising the question of whether a first-order process can retain comparable expressive power for image reconstruction. More fundamentally, the paper asks whether natural images share a common set of invariant dynamic laws in latent space, and if so, what form those laws take.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image and compresses it via a Mobile-Former encoder into a single C-dimensional vector q (or M vectors for multi-path), which is placed at the center of a W×H×C latent feature map. The feature map is generated at resolutions from 8×8 up to full 256×256, then decoded by a few upsampling and 3×3 convolutional layers into the reconstructed image. Notably, no explicit positional encoding is used; the entire spatial structure emerges from local propagation rules applied to the central initial condition. For self-supervised pre-training, the output is a frozen encoder producing task-agnostic representations.

## Method

FINOLA (First-Order Norm+Linear Autoregression) recursively propagates a central embedding across a feature map using z(x+1,y) = z(x,y) + A·ẑ(x,y) and z(x,y+1) = z(x,y) + B·ẑ(x,y), where ẑ is the channel-wise layer-normalized feature and A, B ∈ ℝ^{C×C} are shared learnable matrices invariant across all positions and all images. Multi-path FINOLA relaxes the single-path constraint by summing M independently propagated feature maps, each initialized from a different encoded vector but sharing the same A and B. The key empirical finding is that A·B⁻¹ is consistently diagonalizable, so in the eigenbasis ζ = V⁻¹z the dynamics reduce to decoupled one-way wave equations ∂ζₖ/∂x = λₖ ∂ζₖ/∂y, meaning each image is a unique solution fully determined by its initial condition at the center. The full encoder–FINOLA–decoder pipeline is trained end-to-end with L2 pixel loss, and a masked variant (Masked FINOLA) applies the same first-order propagation to predict occluded regions for self-supervised pre-training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (256×256), multi-path FINOLA with C=2048 channels and M=16 paths achieves a reconstruction PSNR of 29.1 (30.0 with scattered initial positions), outperforming DCT (27.5) and DWT (28.7) at comparable latent sizes. In image compression, single-path FINOLA (C=3072) achieves 24.9 PSNR at 0.19 bits/pixel on ImageNet, surpassing JPEG's 24.5 PSNR at 0.50 bits/pixel. For self-supervised pre-training, Masked FINOLA with MF-W2880 reaches 83.9% Top-1 on ImageNet fine-tuning, matching SimMIM (83.8%) and MAE (83.6%), while yielding +5.0 to +6.4 AP gains over supervised pre-training on COCO object detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINOLA consistently outperforms DCT at every matched latent size, achieving 29.1 vs 27.5 PSNR at ~32K latent and 27.1 vs 23.5 at ~8K latent. Against DWT/DTCWT, FINOLA wins at smaller latent budgets (e.g., 27.1 vs 24.3 at ~8K–15K) but trails DTCWT at the largest setting (29.1 vs 30.8) where DTCWT uses over 6× more latent coefficients. Compared to convolutional auto-encoders, FINOLA achieves higher PSNR (27.1 vs 26.0 at 8192 latent) while using only 16.6M decoder parameters versus 61.9M for Conv-AE. In end-to-end compression, FINOLA beats JPEG on both rate and quality (0.19 bpp / 24.9 PSNR vs 0.50 bpp / 24.5 PSNR on ImageNet). For self-supervised pre-training, Masked FINOLA matches or slightly exceeds MAE and SimMIM on ImageNet Top-1 (83.9% vs 83.6%/83.8%) with lower FLOPs, and substantially outperforms supervised pre-training on COCO detection (+5.0 to +6.4 AP). Against generative-model autoencoders (VQGAN, Stable Diffusion), FINOLA achieves higher PSNR (25.8 vs 24.1 for SD at 4096 latent) but lower FID (27.82 vs 0.87), a gap the authors attribute to using only L2 loss rather than perceptual/GAN losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINOLA departs from the dominant autoregressive image modeling paradigm (PixelCNN, DALL-E, iGPT) that relies on deep Transformer architectures to capture high-order token dependencies, instead reducing the process to a first-order norm-plus-linear recurrence with two C×C matrices. Conceptually, it also diverges from classical image transforms (DCT, DWT) that project images onto fixed wavelet/cosine bases; FINOLA instead encodes images as initial conditions to a shared system of learnable one-way wave equations. The self-supervised variant (Masked FINOLA) draws on the MAE/SimMIM masked-prediction framework but replaces random masking with regular block masking and attention-based prediction with the same first-order autoregression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that natural images may share a common set of one-way wave equations in latent space, with each image uniquely determined by its initial condition—offers a new geometric/dynamical lens on visual structure that could guide future representation learning and compression research. However, the authors explicitly acknowledge that the wave-equation interpretation is purely empirical (post-hoc diagonalization of learned matrices) and lacks rigorous theoretical grounding, leaving open whether the diagonalizability of AB⁻¹ is guaranteed or merely typical. Practical impact is tempered by the fact that the interpretation does not guide training, and the reconstruction gains over strong baselines (DCT, DWT, Conv-AE) are meaningful but not transformative; the self-supervised results are competitive but not state-of-the-art.
