## Problem

Offline alignment of language models with human preference feedback faces two practical threats—privacy leakage of annotator labels and adversarial label corruption—that prior theoretical work has addressed only separately, and only under linear function approximations. This paper asks whether optimal sample-complexity rates can be achieved under general (non-linear) function approximations when both differential privacy and Huber label corruption are present simultaneously, and whether the order in which privacy and corruption are applied matters.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a preference dataset of n tuples (prompt, two responses, binary label) where labels may be locally privatized via randomized response (LDP) and/or corrupted under an α-Huber model, along with a reference policy, a policy class Π, and a privacy parameter ε. It outputs an aligned policy π̂ from Π. Notably, the same algorithm is adaptive: it does not require prior knowledge of whether corruption occurred before or after local privacy (CTL vs. LTC), and the central-DP variant protects prompts, responses, and labels jointly via the exponential mechanism.

## Method

Square χPO replaces the log-loss in χPO with a Brier score (squared error over the sigmoid-transformed implicit reward difference), a one-line modification whose boundedness (≤1 per sample) is the key enabler for both privacy and robustness. Under local DP, the private label is rescaled by c(ε)=(e^ε+1)/(e^ε−1) to yield an unbiased probability estimate, and the resulting least-squares objective is minimized directly; under central DP, the exponential mechanism samples a policy proportional to exp(−εL/8) where L is the bounded square loss. For the general (non-transitive) preference model, an iterative version first estimates the preference function via the same private/robust least-squares step, then runs self-play policy optimization with a mixed χ²+KL regularizer. All guarantees rest on a new unified lemma giving generalization error bounds for least-squares regression under CTL, LTC, and cDP, which reveals that corruption-after-privacy (LTC) incurs a multiplicative √c(ε) penalty while corruption-before-privacy (CTL) and central DP yield only additive costs.

## Results

This is a primarily theoretical paper with no numerical benchmark tables. The headline results are suboptimality gap bounds for Square χPO under three privacy/corruption settings: under CTL, SG ≲ κ(π*)·(c(ε)√(log(|Π|/ζ)/n) + √α); under LTC, SG ≲ κ(π*)·(c(ε)√(log(|Π|/ζ)/n) + √(α·c(ε))); and under central DP (cDP), SG ≲ κ(π*)·((1+1/√ε)√(log(|Π|/ζ)/n) + √α), where κ(π*) = e^{2R_max}·(V_max/R_max)·√(C^{π*}) is the single-policy concentrability term. The key quantitative achievement is the optimal O(1/√n) rate under general function approximations with single-policy concentrability, matching the non-private χPO rate while adding privacy and robustness. The paper also establishes the first pure DP (δ=0) guarantees for general function approximations in offline alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chowdhury et al. (2024a) on robust alignment, Square χPO improves the rate from O(1/n^{1/4}) to the optimal O(1/n^{1/2}) even in the linear case, and replaces all-policy concentrability with the weaker single-policy concentrability. For general function approximations, Chowdhury et al. (2024a) suffers a non-vanishing suboptimality gap as n→∞, while Square χPO maintains the O(1/n^{1/2}) rate. Against Chowdhury et al. (2024b) and Korkmaz & Brown-Cohen (2024) on central DP, both prior works are restricted to linear reward models with approximate DP (δ>0) and achieve a privacy cost of O((d·log(1/δ))^{1/4}/√(nε)); Square χPO handles general function approximations under stronger pure DP (δ=0). Against the concurrent work Zhou et al. (2025), Square χPO extends from linear to general function approximations, adds central DP coverage, and uses a slightly weaker (Huber) corruption model, yielding a different interplay term √(c(ε)·α) vs. c(ε)·√α. The paper also notes that under LTC (corruption after privacy), the bound degrades by an extra √(c(ε)) factor compared to CTL, revealing that the order of privacy and corruption matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends χPO (Huang et al., 2024), which introduced χ²-regularization to achieve single-policy concentrability in offline direct alignment, by replacing its log-loss with a Brier score (square loss over probability). The reparametrization and self-play framework for general preference models are inherited from χPO and REBEL (Gao et al., 2024), while the privacy mechanisms (randomized response for LDP, exponential mechanism for central DP) and Huber corruption model are drawn from the private/robust statistics literature. The key conceptual departure is that the boundedness of the square loss (vs. unbounded log-loss) simultaneously enables bounded sensitivity for DP and controlled impact of label corruption, unifying the analysis across all settings via a new generalization bound for least-square regression under privacy and corruption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for private and robust offline direct alignment under general function approximations, filling a gap where prior work was restricted to linear models or RL-based (indirect) approaches. The separation between corruption-then-privacy and privacy-then-corruption (multiplicative vs. additive interaction) is a clean theoretical finding consistent with bandit and mean-estimation literature, but its practical implications for system design remain to be validated. A key limitation is that the central DP algorithm relies on the exponential mechanism and is information-theoretic rather than computationally efficient, and empirical validation is limited to proof-of-concept experiments. Open questions include developing a computationally efficient central DP variant, whether the corruption dependence can be tightened to α², and whether the Brier score loss offers practical advantages over log-loss in real alignment pipelines.
