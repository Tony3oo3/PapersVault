## Problem

The straight-through estimator (STE) is the dominant heuristic for quantization-aware training but lacks a principled optimization-theoretic justification, and prior regularization-based interpretations relied on nonconvex regularizers with only averaged-iterate convergence guarantees. This paper asks whether a convex regularizer can both induce discrete quantized weights and admit stronger last-iterate convergence for the resulting optimization algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251103/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet) and outputs class labels, but the distinguishing feature is that all model weights are constrained to a small discrete set of quantization values (1-bit, ternary, or 2–4 bits) during training. Unlike fixed-scale quantization, the target quantization values {q_k} are estimated online at every iteration via least-squares binary quantization (LSBQ), so the quantization grid adapts to the evolving weight distribution rather than being set a priori.

## Method

PARQ introduces a convex, piecewise-affine regularizer (PAR) whose nonsmooth kinks at the quantization values trap model parameters at discrete points, and solves the resulting regularized objective with an aggregate proximal stochastic gradient method (AProx) that accumulates all past gradients in a latent variable u^t before applying a single proximal map scaled by the cumulative step size γ_t = Ση_s. This aggregation prevents the quantization dead-zones from shrinking to zero (as in standard Prox-SGD), so the proximal map asymptotically converges to hard quantization, giving a principled derivation of STE as the limiting case. The practical PARQ algorithm estimates quantization values online via LSBQ and anneals the slope of the proximal map's slanted segments from 1 to ∞ over training, transitioning smoothly from soft to hard quantization. The paper proves last-iterate convergence (not just averaged-iterate) for convex losses, matching the O(1/√t) rate, and demonstrates competitive or superior accuracy to STE/BinaryConnect and BinaryRelax across ResNet and DeiT models at 1-bit through 4-bit precision.

## Results

On ImageNet, PARQ achieves the strongest 1-bit quantization results across all DeiT sizes: 55.43% on DeiT-Ti (vs. 51.62% STE, 52.62% BinaryRelax), 73.40% on DeiT-S (vs. 70.07%, 70.69%), and 79.35% on DeiT-B (vs. 78.79%, 79.02%). On CIFAR-10, PARQ reaches 90.48% for 1-bit ResNet-20 (vs. 89.56% STE) and 91.47% for 1-bit ResNet-56. On ImageNet ResNet-50, PARQ attains 66.71% at 1-bit and 74.83% at 4-bit, matching or exceeding both baselines across all bit-widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARQ is compared against STE/BinaryConnect (hard quantization with straight-through gradients) and BinaryRelax (nonconvex L1-based relaxation) across ResNet and DeiT models at 1-bit, ternary, 2-, 3-, and 4-bit. PARQ consistently outperforms both baselines at 1-bit and ternary, with the largest margins on smaller models: +3.81 points over STE and +2.81 over BinaryRelax on 1-bit DeiT-Ti, and +3.33 over STE on 1-bit DeiT-S. At 2-bit, PARQ leads by 1.4–1.8 points on DeiT-Ti and S. At 3- and 4-bit, the gap narrows to 0.1–1.1 points, with PARQ still achieving the best accuracy on DeiT-B (81.49% at 3-bit, 81.60% at 4-bit). On CIFAR-10 ResNet-20, PARQ wins at 1-bit (+0.92 over STE) and ternary (+0.51 over STE), while on ResNet-56 the advantage is most visible at ternary (92.97% vs. 92.42% STE). Overall, PARQ's advantage is most pronounced in the most challenging low-bit regimes and for smaller models, where the gradual soft-to-hard quantization transition stabilizes training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the regularization-based interpretation of BinaryConnect/STE developed by Dockhorn et al. (2021, ProxConnect) and the regularized dual averaging (RDA) framework of Xiao (2010). While ProxConnect worked with arbitrary monotone proximal maps without specifying a concrete regularizer, PARQ introduces a specific convex, piecewise-affine regularizer (PAR) and derives AProx as a direct extension of RDA, yielding an equivalent algorithm through a different (and more intuitive) balancing-of-forward-and-backward-steps derivation. The practical implementation further incorporates LSBQ (Pouransari et al., 2020) for online estimation of quantization values, avoiding the need to pre-specify them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The last-iterate convergence guarantee is a meaningful theoretical advance over ProxConnect's average-iterate results, since averaged iterates are generally not quantized and thus useless for QAT in practice. The conceptual insight that a convex regularizer (rather than the intuitively expected nonconvex one) can effectively induce discrete quantization structure challenges the prevailing assumption in the quantization-regularization literature. Open questions include extending convergence analysis to nonconvex losses and adaptive optimizers (Adam/AdamW), understanding PAR's generalization properties in a statistical learning framework, and whether the approach scales to language models or other modalities.
