## Problem

Information-theoretic generalization bounds suffer from slow O(1/√n) convergence rates, severe overestimation for deep networks, and failure to vanish for certain stochastic convex optimization problems. While fast-rate and binary KL-based approaches improve convergence, they remain vacuous for higher population risks. This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a binarized Jensen-Shannon divergence as a comparator between empirical and population risks, achieving exactly tight bounds without requiring stability or compressibility assumptions on the learning algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method operates within the supersample framework, taking 2n i.i.d. samples partitioned into training and test sets via uniform binary variables, along with a randomized learning algorithm and a bounded loss function in [0,1]. A key unusual aspect is a perturbation-and-rounding binarization step that converts continuous evaluated losses to binary variables (L̄ = ⌊L + D⌋ with D ~ Unif([0,1])), making the mutual information term I(L̄; U) directly estimable via simple binning rather than kernel density estimation. The output is an exact characterization of the population risk as L_μ = d_JS^{-1}(L_n, (1/n)Σ I(L̄_i^0; U_i)), which yields a unique solution under the mild assumption L_n ≤ L_μ.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a foundational lemma (Lemma 3.1) showing that the binary JS divergence between conditional expectations of a function given a Bernoulli(1/2) variable is bounded by the mutual information, with equality when the function is binary and invertible. This lemma is instantiated in two ways: a hypothesis-based bound (SICIMI) that conditions on a single supersample element rather than a pair, tightening all prior conditional MI bounds, and a prediction-based bound using single-loss mutual information I(L_i^0; U_i). The critical innovation for exact tightness is a binarization technique that adds uniform noise and rounds the evaluated loss to a Bernoulli variable, eliminating the extraneous information in continuous losses that causes existing bounds to fail on the Haghifam et al. (2023) counterexample. The result extends to unbounded losses via a truncation-and-summing strategy, to arbitrary f-divergences, and to Wasserstein distance, all while maintaining exact tightness for general randomized learning algorithms.

## Results

The paper's headline result is that the proposed Binary JS bound achieves *exact tightness*: it precisely recovers the true generalization error across all tested scenarios (linear classifier on synthetic Gaussian data, 4-layer CNN on MNIST 4-vs-9, and pretrained ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10). The bound provides non-vacuous upper-bound estimates for any population risk L_μ ∈ [L_n, 1], whereas competing bounds (Binary KL, Fast-Rate) become vacuous for L_μ ≳ 0.8. For 0-1 loss, the Binary JS bound closely tracks the Fast-Rate bound (both leverage I(L_i^0; U_i)), but for continuous (truncated cross-entropy) loss the gap widens substantially in favor of the Binary JS bound. The exactly tight characterization also resolves the failure mode identified by Haghifam et al. (2023) for stochastic convex optimization, where prior loss-based mutual information bounds do not vanish as n → ∞.

| Approach | Reference | Measure | Bound |

| Individual MI (IMI) | Bu et al., 2020 | I(W; Z_i) | (1/n) Σ √(I(W;Z_i)/2) |
| Conditional MI (CMI) | Steinke & Zakynthinou, 2020 | I(W; U \| Z̃) | √(2I(W;U\|Z̃)/n) |
| Conditional Individual MI (CIMI) | Haghifam et al., 2020 | I(W; U_i \| Z̃) | (1/n) Σ √(2I(W;U_i\|Z̃)) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the **Fast-Rate bound** (Wang & Mao, ICML 2023), the Binary JS bound is nearly identical for 0-1 loss (both depend on I(L_i^0; U_i)), but becomes significantly tighter for continuous loss functions where the Fast-Rate bound's linear combination of L_n and the information term is less informative. Against the **Binary KL bound** (Hellström & Durisi, NeurIPS 2022), the Binary JS bound is uniformly tighter: it yields non-vacuous estimates across the full range L_μ ∈ [L_n, 1], while the Binary KL bound becomes vacuous for L_μ ≳ 0.8 (as illustrated in Figure 1 with L_n = 0.2). Against the **f-information oracle bounds** (CMI, CSHI, CJSI from Wang & Mao, NeurIPS 2024), the Binary JS bound achieves exact tightness in all settings; CMI and CSHI can produce competitive estimates under SGLD by exploiting loss scale information, but they remain upper bounds rather than exact characterizations. The paper's SICIMI hypothesis-based bound (Theorem 3.2) is strictly tighter than all prior hypothesis-based bounds in Table 1 (MI → IMI → CMI → CIMI → ICIMI), as formally established by Proposition 3.4, and is shown to be exactly tight for specific learning tasks (Example 3.5) where further removal of conditioning variables is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supersample framework (Steinke & Zakynthinou, 2020) by replacing the binary KL divergence comparator with a symmetric binary Jensen-Shannon divergence, building directly on the binary KL bounds of Hellström & Durisi (2022b) and the single-loss MI framework of Wang & Mao (2023a). It also sharpens the hypothesis-based SICIMI bound by reducing the conditioning from two supersample elements to one, departing from the ICIMI approach of Zhou et al. (2022a). The key conceptual departure is a binarization technique (uniform perturbation plus rounding) applied to evaluated loss variables, which the authors show achieves exact tightness for general bounded loss functions without the stability or compressibility assumptions required by prior exact-characterization results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete failure mode identified by Haghifam et al. (2023), where evaluated-loss-based information measures (e-CMI, ld-CMI) do not vanish for certain stochastic convex optimization problems, by showing that a simple binarization step eliminates the extraneous information that prevents vanishing. The exact tightness result for general bounded loss functions under only permutation invariance is a clean theoretical contribution that broadens the applicability of information-theoretic bounds. However, the results are limited to expected generalization (not high-probability), and the unbounded-loss extension (Corollary 3.12) requires a stronger per-level assumption (δ_j L_n ≤ δ_j L_μ) that may not hold in practice. A natural open question is whether exactly tight high-probability bounds can be derived using the same binarization machinery.
