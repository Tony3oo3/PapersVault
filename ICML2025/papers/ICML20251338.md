## Problem

The 1-identification problem asks whether any arm in a multi-armed bandit has mean reward at least a known threshold μ₀, and prior work (Degenne & Koolen, 2019) established only asymptotically tight sample complexity bounds while explicitly noting that non-asymptotic analysis remains open. Existing non-asymptotic algorithms (HDoC, APGAI) are either suboptimal in the positive case, vacuous when an arm's mean equals μ₀, or fail to be (Δ,δ)-PAC. This paper closes the gap by providing near-optimal non-asymptotic upper and lower bounds on expected sample complexity for both positive and negative instances.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives K arms with unknown 1-sub-Gaussian reward distributions, a known scalar threshold μ₀, a confidence parameter δ ∈ (0,1), and tunable scheduling parameters (C > 1, sequences δ_k, α_k, β_k). It operates sequentially with a data-dependent stopping time τ and outputs either a single qualified arm index â ∈ [K] (certifying μ_â ≥ μ₀ with probability ≥ 1−δ) or None (certifying no arm meets the threshold). Unlike fixed-budget formulations, the number of pulls is adaptive, and the sample complexity is measured as E[τ], which must be finite for all instances in S^pos ∪ S^neg.

## Method

SEE (Sequential-Exploration-Exploitation) runs in phases k = 1, 2, …, each consisting of an exploration stage and a conditional exploitation stage. Exploration uses a modified LUCB_G oracle with a tighter confidence radius U(t,δ) and an inflated LCB (multiplied by C > 1) to guarantee that any identified arm satisfies μ_â > ωμ₁ + (1−ω)μ₀; a temporary container Q preserves sample-index continuity for concentration inequalities when the last sample of a candidate arm is deferred. Exploitation pulls the candidate arm with the true tolerance δ until its LCB exceeds μ₀ or a phase budget T_k^et is exhausted. The phase schedule (δ_k = 3^{-k}, β_k = 2^k, α_k = 5^k) ensures that the algorithm terminates with probability 1 and that the expected total pulls E[τ] = O(H log(1/δ) + H₁^pos log(K/Δ_{0,1}²)) in the positive case and O(H₁^neg(log(1/δ) + log H₁^neg)) in the negative case, matching lower bounds up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Results

The proposed SEE algorithm achieves a non-asymptotic upper bound of E[τ] ≤ O(H·log(1/δ)) + O(H₁^pos·log(K/Δ₀,₁)) for positive instances and E[τ] ≤ O(H₁^neg·(log(1/δ) + log H₁^neg)) for negative instances, where H = 2/Δ₀,₁². The matching lower bounds are E[τ] ≥ Ω(H·log(1/δ)) for positive and E[τ] ≥ Ω(H₁^neg·log(1/δ)) for negative instances, yielding a gap of at most a polynomial logarithmic factor. Numerically, SEE outperforms all benchmarks on negative instances and matches or exceeds adapted-TaS/adapted-MS on positive instances with K ≥ 200, while losing to them on small-K positive instances (K ≤ 50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEE is the first algorithm to achieve near-optimal non-asymptotic bounds in both positive and negative cases simultaneously. S-TaS (Degenne & Koolen, 2019) matches the asymptotic lower bound but provides no finite-time guarantee; HDoC and lilHDoC (Kano et al., 2017; Tsai et al., 2024) suffer from a vacuous K/ε² term and an H₁·log log(1/δ) factor that grows linearly in K, making them sub-optimal in both cases. APGAI (Jourdan & Reda, 2023) is nearly optimal only for negative instances and remains sub-optimal for positive ones. Numerically, SEE dominates all theoretical baselines (HDoC, lilHDoC, LUCB_G) across most instance groups, and outperforms the heuristic adapted-TaS and adapted-MS when K ≥ 200 in multi-qualified-arm settings, though it trails them on small-K (K ≤ 50) positive instances and the 'Unique Qualified' group. The paper also proves that HDoC, lilHDoC, and APGAI are not (Δ,δ)-PAC (i.e., E[τ] = ∞ on certain instances), a property SEE satisfies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Degenne & Koolen (2019), who established asymptotic optimality for the 1-identification problem via S-TaS but explicitly noted that non-asymptotic analysis remained open. It also draws on the Good Arm Identification literature (Kano et al., 2017; Jourdan & Reda, 2023), repurposing LUCB_G as an exploration oracle within a novel sequential exploration-exploitation framework. The key departure from prior work is achieving non-asymptotic near-optimality in both the positive (qualified arm exists) and negative (no qualified arm) cases simultaneously, whereas S-TaS was only asymptotically optimal, HDoC suffered from vacuous bounds when μ_a = μ₀, and APGAI was sub-optimal on positive instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a gap explicitly flagged by Degenne & Koolen (2019) and provides the first non-asymptotic sample complexity bounds that are near-optimal (within a polynomial log factor) for both positive and negative instances of 1-identification. The practical relevance is clear in settings like A/B testing against a benchmark, pharmaceutical trials, or campaign evaluation where one needs to certify the existence or absence of a superior option. An open question the authors identify is whether the δ-independent part of the upper bound (involving log(1/Δ_{0,1})) is truly necessary when multiple arms exceed the threshold, and whether tighter bounds can be established in that regime.
