## Problem

Existing hyper-relational knowledge graph (HKG) representation learning methods fail to effectively exploit the graph structure of HKGs: transformer-based approaches process each fact in isolation, while GNN-based methods use only one-hop neighborhoods or ignore relation types and entity positions within facts. This paper demonstrates that thoroughly leveraging an HKG's structure—how entities and relations co-occur, connect, and are positioned within facts—is both necessary and sufficient for state-of-the-art link prediction, and that a purely structure-driven method can also generalize inductively to entirely new entities and relations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251321/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MAYPL consumes only the structural connectivity of an HKG (which entities and relations appear in which facts, and at what positions: head, tail, qualifier entity; primary or qualifier relation) without any external text features, node attributes, or pre-trained embeddings. It outputs learned entity and relation representation vectors, and for link prediction, it scores all candidate entities by dot-product similarity against the query entity's representation to predict the missing entity in an incomplete hyper-relational fact. A key property is that at inference time, the same learned aggregation functions operate on a completely different HKG with unseen entities and relations, since no entity- or relation-specific parameters are stored.

## Method

MAYPL consists of two stages. First, a structure-driven initializer starts from a single shared vector for all entities (and another for all relations) and differentiates them over multiple layers via message passing over co-occurring entities and incident relations, using position-dependent learnable projection matrices to encode where each entity or relation sits within a fact. Second, an attentive neural message passing module computes fact-level messages by decomposing each fact into relation-entity pairs, applying position-aware Hadamard-product compositions, and averaging; these fact messages are then attentively aggregated (with Bahdanau-style attention) to update entity and relation representations, again conditioned on positions. The entire pipeline—from initialization through message passing to link prediction—uses only structural information, and because the learned parameters encode *how* to aggregate messages rather than *what* specific entities or relations mean, they transfer directly to a new HKG at inference time, enabling fully inductive link prediction with both new entities and new relations.

## Results

On transductive HKG link prediction, MAYPL achieves MRR 0.381 on WD50K (primary triplets) and MRR 0.519 on WikiPeople⁻, outperforming the best baselines HAHE (0.368, 0.509) and HyNT (0.357, N/A). On the WikiPeople NRR benchmark, MAYPL reaches MRR 0.488, surpassing HJE (0.450) and HyCubE (0.448). In inductive HKG settings, MAYPL dominates with MRR 0.486 on WD20K(100)v1 (vs. 0.107 for QBLP) and MRR 0.550 on MFB-IND (vs. 0.368 for HCNet), demonstrating that purely structure-based learning generalizes to entirely new entities and relations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAYPL outperforms all 40+ baselines across all 10 benchmark datasets. On transductive HKG tasks, it beats the strongest transformer-based methods (HAHE, HyNT, HyperFormer) by 1–3 MRR points on WD50K and WikiPeople⁻, and surpasses NRR/KHG methods (HJE, HyCubE, HCNet) by 3.8 MRR points on WikiPeople. On inductive KG completion (NL-50, WK-50, FB-50), MAYPL leads HyRel and InKGE-EIPR by 2.2 and 0.1 MRR points respectively, with a particularly large gap on WK-50 (0.109 vs. 0.076). The most dramatic wins appear in inductive HKG settings: on WD20K(100)v1, MAYPL achieves MRR 0.486 versus 0.107 for QBLP (the only other HKG inductive method), a 4.5× improvement; on MFB-IND it reaches 0.550 versus 0.368 for HCNet. No baseline matches MAYPL on any dataset-metric combination, and the gap widens substantially in inductive settings where structure-driven generalization is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAYPL departs from the dominant transformer-based HKG methods (StarE, HyNT, HAHE, HyperFormer) that process each hyper-relational fact in isolation or use only one-hop neighborhood information, arguing that these approaches fail to exploit the interconnected structure across facts. It also contrasts with NRR/KHG-based methods (NaLP, RAM, G-MPNN, HCNet) that lose information when converting HKGs into alternative representations. The method builds on InGram's dynamic split strategy for training but introduces a fundamentally different paradigm: rather than learning fixed entity and relation embeddings, MAYPL learns position-aware message aggregation functions that can be applied to any HKG structure, enabling inductive inference on entirely unseen entities and relations without external features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that purely structural information (connectivity, co-occurrence, and positional roles) is sufficient for state-of-the-art HKG reasoning—challenges the field's reliance on learned embeddings or external textual features. The inductive capability (handling both new entities and new relations simultaneously) is unique among HKG methods and opens the door to applying a single trained model to entirely new knowledge bases. However, the computational cost (20+ hours training, ~44 GB GPU memory on WikiPeople⁻) limits practical scalability, and the authors acknowledge that attention computation could be optimized. Open questions include whether the structure-only principle generalizes to other knowledge graph forms (bi-level KGs, numeric KGs) and whether theoretical guarantees can be established.
