## Problem

Existing digital twins operate in fixed modelling environments with rigid state-action dimensions and require architectural redesign and full re-training whenever new variables, data, or knowledge are introduced. This makes them unable to remain relevant in dynamic real-world settings where the system description, available data, and domain knowledge evolve over time. The paper asks whether LLMs can serve as digital twins that adapt to such changes purely through in-context learning at inference time, without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target system's rolling state-action history (converted to natural language via a mapping g), a dataset of related trajectories, a knowledge base of free-form variable and system descriptions, and an external action policy. It produces F future state-action pairs by iteratively querying an LLM, which generates the next state as natural language that is inverse-mapped back to structured values. A key constraint is that the LLM context window is finite, so the method must selectively retrieve and adapt a small set of context samples (typically c=5) rather than supplying the full dataset, and it re-evaluates which samples are relevant mid-generation whenever a new action appears or a resampling buffer is exceeded.

## Method

CALM-DT is a context-adaptive simulation loop with three stages: information retrieval, prompt formulation, and generation. For retrieval, it uses a fine-tuned bi-encoder (ModernBERT) trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss, where positive and negative training pairs are determined by LLM simulation performance (CRPS or MSE) when each candidate is used as an in-context example. LLM-generated natural-language trend summaries (e.g., 'increasing, stable, decreasing') are appended to trajectory representations to help encoders capture temporal patterns. The encoders are fine-tuned once in the initial environment and remain valid across subsequent environment changes because their inputs are dimension-agnostic natural language. During generation, the LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) estimates the transition distribution over the next state, and the context is re-selected only when the action changes or a resampling buffer is exceeded, enabling efficient simulation across large state-action spaces without overloading the context window.

## Results

On the CF dataset (3-year simulation), CALM-DT achieves MSE 55.3±0.81 and MAE 4.63±0.05, outperforming all 8 baselines with the best average rank of 1.25 across both datasets and metrics. On NSCLC (30-day simulation), CALM-DT achieves MSE 79.4±8.57 and MAE 4.28±0.16, ranking first overall. In the Ivacaftor adaptation experiments, CALM-DT improves from MSE 59.7 (action only) to 50.4 (action+knowledge+data) without any re-training, and further to 47.8 with three years of growing post-Ivacaftor data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALM-DT outperforms all five primary DT baselines on the CF dataset, with the largest margin over RNN (MSE 55.3 vs. 340) and SINDy (55.3 vs. 73.0). On NSCLC, CALM-DT achieves the best MSE (79.4) but HDTwin edges it out on MAE (3.42 vs. 4.28), making HDTwin the closest competitor overall. SINDy catastrophically fails on NSCLC (MSE 1.90×10⁴), likely due to its inability to capture the complex pharmacokinetic dynamics with a polynomial library. DyNODE and Transformer show high variance on NSCLC (MSE CIs of ±63.6 and ±52.7 respectively), while CALM-DT maintains tighter confidence intervals. The K-NN baseline (rank 3) is the strongest non-LLM competitor, but CALM-DT still leads by ~10 MSE points on CF and ~18 on NSCLC. Notably, even the training-free variant of CALM-DT (no encoder fine-tuning, no summaries) achieves MSE 76.3 on CF, surpassing DyNODE, Transformer, RNN, and SINDy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts bi-encoder retrieval methods from NLP prompt selection (Cheng et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a) to the domain of time-series trajectory retrieval, extending them with LLM-generated trend summaries and contrastive fine-tuning guided by LLM simulation feedback. It departs from existing digital twin approaches (DyNODE, SINDy, HDTwin, Transformer-based DTs) that require fixed architectures and re-training upon environment changes, by reframing digital twinning as an in-context learning problem over natural language. The work also extends LLM-based time-series forecasting (Gruver et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024) by incorporating action variables and policy simulation, which prior forecasters do not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALM-DT is the first DT method that can seamlessly incorporate new state/action variables, new data, and new knowledge without architectural redesign or parameter updates, addressing a critical gap for real-world deployment in rapidly evolving domains like medicine. The finding that even a fully training-free variant (pre-trained encoders + LLM summaries) surpasses most existing DTs suggests LLM in-context learning is a more powerful simulation primitive than previously appreciated. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets where full-context baselines become infeasible, integration of causal structure to prevent hallucinated dynamics, and whether the approach generalises beyond the two medical and two ecological domains tested.
