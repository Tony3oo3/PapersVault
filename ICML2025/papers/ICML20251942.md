## Problem

Autoregressive neural emulators of chaotic spatiotemporal systems (e.g., turbulent flows) inevitably drift out of distribution over long rollouts due to accumulated errors, causing trajectories to diverge exponentially. Existing stabilization strategies either modify the training objective (which requires expensive estimation of the model's own invariant measure) or add conditional refinement steps, but none provide a modular, inference-time-only correction that decouples the emulator from the stabilizer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251942/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained black-box emulator (U-Net or Dilated ResNet predicting residual timesteps on 64×64 vorticity fields or 2×64×64 potential vorticity fields) and a separately trained diffusion model (thermalizer) as inputs, combining them only at inference. At each timestep, the thermalizer's classifier head outputs a predicted noise level s_pred; if s_pred exceeds a threshold s_init, the state is noised to that level and denoised down to s_stop, producing a corrected state vector. The final output is a stabilized trajectory extending to 10^5 autoregressive steps, with the number of corrective denoising steps per trajectory typically in the low hundreds.

## Method

The thermalizer is a DDPM-style diffusion model trained on the invariant measure μ of the dynamical system, with a key architectural modification: the noise level is predicted by an added classifier head rather than passed as a conditioning input, enabling adaptive, on-the-fly denoising. At inference, each emulator step is followed by a forward pass of the classifier to estimate the current state's noise level; if it exceeds s_init, the state is noised to s_pred and then denoised over s_pred − s_stop reverse-diffusion steps back toward the equilibrium distribution. The theoretical justification rests on the data-processing inequality: the reverse diffusion from a correctly estimated noise level contracts the KL divergence between the perturbed state distribution and the invariant measure, effectively implementing a non-equilibrium transport map that is robust to non-Gaussian perturbations. Both models are trained independently with standard supervised losses (MSE residual prediction for the emulator; denoising MSE plus cross-entropy for the thermalizer), making the framework fully modular with minimal inference overhead since the full denoising network is only invoked when correction is needed.

## Results

The thermalizer extends stable autoregressive rollouts by approximately two orders of magnitude: un-thermalized Unet emulators diverge by ~4,000–5,000 steps on both Kolmogorov flow and QG turbulence, while thermalized trajectories remain stable for 10,000 steps and are demonstrated stable out to 1×10⁵ steps. For the QG Unet configuration, only ~140 thermalization steps are needed on average per 10,000-step trajectory, and the per-timestep noise-classification forward pass costs just 60% of a full Unet emulator step. Autocorrelation of thermalized fields is statistically indistinguishable from the numerical model, confirming minimal temporal disruption. Kinetic energy spectra of thermalized flows match the numerical model closely, with a minor offset observed in QG at 10⁵ steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other stabilization methods (e.g., PDE-Refiner, DySLIM, multi-timestep training) in a head-to-head quantitative comparison. Instead, the primary comparison is between the un-thermalized emulator (Unet 48M params and DRN 0.5M params) and the same emulator augmented with the thermalizer, evaluated against the numerical model as ground truth. On Kolmogorov flow, the Unet emulator diverges by ~5,000 steps while the thermalized version remains stable for 10,000+ steps; the DRN is somewhat more stable natively but still degrades, whereas the thermalized DRN also stays stable. On QG turbulence, all un-thermalized emulators go unstable by ~2,000–4,000 steps, while all thermalized variants remain stable for the full 10,000-step horizon. The thermalizer's advantage is thus a consistent and large extension of the stable rollout horizon across both architectures and both flow configurations, achieved with minimal computational overhead and without retraining the emulator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) in a fundamentally different role: rather than generating samples from scratch, a diffusion model of the stationary measure is used as an on-the-fly correction mechanism during autoregressive emulator rollouts. The emulator itself follows the standard multi-timestep backpropagation training paradigm (Brandstetter et al., 2022), while the theoretical justification draws on the score-estimation framework of Song & Ermon (2019) and the data-processing inequality to show that reverse-diffusion steps contract the KL divergence toward equilibrium. The key departure from prior stabilization work (Lippe et al., 2023; Schiff et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2023) is modularity: the emulator is treated as a frozen black box, and the diffusion model is trained entirely separately on the equilibrium distribution, with the two combined only at inference time via an adaptive noise-level classifier head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The thermalizer extends stable prediction horizons by two orders of magnitude (to 10⁵ steps) with minimal computational overhead (~140 corrections per 10,000-step trajectory), and the modular design means existing pretrained emulators can be stabilized without retraining. Key limitations include the isotropic Gaussian perturbation assumption, sensitivity to the s_init/s_stop hyperparameters requiring per-configuration grid search, and residual kinetic-energy spectrum offsets at very long rollouts in the QG system. Open questions include whether flow-matching or other non-Gaussian base measures would eliminate the need for careful hyperparameter tuning, whether the approach extends to time-inhomogeneous (e.g., seasonally forced) systems, and whether similar stabilization principles could apply to other long-autoregressive domains such as LLM generation.
