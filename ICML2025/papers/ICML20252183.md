## Problem

Existing drag-based image editing methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion) require over a minute per edit due to test-time latent optimization or gradient-based guidance, and they frequently fail to accurately relocate semantic content or preserve source identity. No prior approach achieves both sub-second latency and high accuracy on general images, making interactive drag editing impractical for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252183/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source image, a set of (handle, target) point pairs (1–20 pairs), and a binary mask delineating the editable region. Handle and target points are encoded as integer-valued point maps at image resolution before being processed by the Point Embedding Network. The output is a single edited image where the masked region is regenerated to move content from handle to target positions while the unmasked region remains pixel-identical. Inference uses DDIM with 25 steps by default, or 8 steps when combined with LCM-LoRA/PeRFlow acceleration.

## Method

LightningDrag redefines drag-based editing as a conditional generation task, eliminating test-time optimization entirely. The architecture combines three components: (1) a Stable Diffusion Inpainting U-Net backbone that enforces unmasked regions stay unchanged, (2) a reference-only Appearance Encoder that extracts identity features from clean source latents and injects them into self-attention layers for fine-grained appearance preservation, and (3) a 12-layer Point Embedding Network that encodes handle/target point maps into multi-resolution embeddings, which are added to queries and keys in a point-following attention mechanism to explicitly link source handle regions to target positions. Training uses 220k paired video frames where CoTracker2 provides point correspondences and optical flow determines handle point sampling, with only the Appearance Encoder and Point Embedding Network trained (backbone and IP-Adapter frozen). At inference, a noised-source-latent prior stabilizes generation, and a point-following classifier-free guidance (PF-CFG) with an inverse-square decay schedule balances point accuracy against image quality, achieving ~1s latency with LCM-LoRA.

## Results

On DragBench (205 samples), LightningDrag achieves the lowest Mean Distance (MD) of 18.62, substantially outperforming the next-best DiffEditor (28.58) and DragDiffusion (33.16) in point-following accuracy. Image Fidelity (IF, 1−LPIPS) is 0.885, competitive with all baselines except SDEDrag (0.921), which the authors attribute to SDEDrag's tendency to produce identity-mapping failures. In terms of speed, LightningDrag completes each edit in 2.06 s (or 0.92 s with LCM-LoRA) on an A100 GPU, compared to 9.5–98 s for prior methods, making it roughly 25–100× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightningDrag decisively wins on point-following accuracy (MD) against all five baselines, achieving 18.62 versus the next-best DiffEditor at 28.58 (a 35% reduction) and DragDiffusion at 33.16 (a 44% reduction). On Image Fidelity, LightningDrag (0.885) trails SDEDrag (0.921) by 0.036, but the authors show SDEDrag's high IF is inflated by frequent identity-mapping failures where the output is nearly identical to the input. Against DragGAN, Readout Guidance, and DragDiffusion, LightningDrag also leads on IF. In speed, LightningDrag (2.06 s) is ~27× faster than DragDiffusion (55 s), ~6× faster than DiffEditor (9.5 s), ~48× faster than Readout Guidance (98 s), and ~30× faster than SDEDrag (62 s); with LCM-LoRA it drops below 1 s. Overall, LightningDrag is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best MD, competitive IF, and sub-second-to-few-second latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightningDrag sits at the end of a lineage that began with DragGAN's GAN-latent optimization for point-based editing and continued through diffusion-based methods (DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion, SDEDrag, Readout Guidance) that all relied on test-time latent optimization or gradient-based guidance, incurring 10–100 second latencies. The key conceptual departure is redefining drag-based editing as a conditional generation problem—source image plus point instructions as conditions—thereby eliminating all test-time optimization and reducing latency to that of a single diffusion forward pass. Architecturally, it repurposes the reference-only architecture for identity preservation, IP-Adapter for image conditioning, and introduces a Point Embedding Network with a modified self-attention mechanism (Eq. 4) to explicitly couple handle and target points. The training signal comes from large-scale paired video frames with CoTracker2-derived point correspondences, a data strategy distinct from prior zero-shot or small-scale supervised approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–100× speedup (from ~55–98 s to <1 s) transforms drag-based editing from a research curiosity into a practically deployable interactive tool, and the compatibility with off-the-shelf accelerators (LCM-LoRA, PeRFlow) further lowers the barrier. The out-of-domain generalization to non-rigid deformations (stretching hair, twisting rainbows) despite training only on natural video motions is a promising but under-quantified property that opens questions about how far video-derived motion priors can be extrapolated. Limitations include reliance on the somewhat dated SD 1.5 backbone, an internal 220k-sample video dataset that is not publicly released, and the fact that the out-of-domain generalization evidence is primarily qualitative rather than benchmarked. Future work could explore scaling to newer diffusion backbones, public training data, and systematic evaluation of deformation generalization.
