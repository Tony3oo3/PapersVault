## Problem

Existing techniques for imposing symmetry equivariance and physical conservation laws on neural PDE surrogates are incompatible with the staggered grids (e.g., Arakawa C-grids) that are standard in computational fluid dynamics, because they assume all field variables are collocated at the same grid points. Additionally, while both constraint types have shown individual promise, their combined effect on surrogate accuracy and generalization has never been systematically evaluated, leaving open whether they are redundant or synergistic given their deep connection through Noether's theorem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes PDE variable fields defined on staggered Arakawa C-grids where scalar quantities (surface elevation ζ, pressure) live at cell centers, velocity components (u, v) live at cell interface midpoints, and vector potentials live at vertices, with grid dimensions of 100×100 (SWE) or 48×48 (INS). For the shallow water task, a binary boundary mask is additionally provided as a cell-centered scalar input. The network outputs next-step fields on the same staggered layout: surface elevation ζ for SWEs (with velocities then computed analytically via the solver's formula) and velocity components [u, v] for incompressible Navier-Stokes.

## Method

The core contribution is a pair of novel equivariant input and output layers that extend group-equivariant convolutions to staggered grids. The input layer uses separate convolutional filter banks with even-sized kernels along the axis orthogonal to each set of interfaces (and odd-sized along other axes), collectively transformed by each group element to produce a regular representation at cell centers; the output layer maps regular representations back to staggered positions via constrained linear combinations of neighboring cell-center values, with weight constraints derived to guarantee equivariance. Conservation laws are enforced as hard constraints in the output: mass via global mean subtraction of the update, momentum via per-component mean subtraction, and divergence-free velocity via a learned vertex-centered vector potential whose curl defines the interface velocities. The internal backbone is a modern U-net using standard escnn steerable convolutions on regular representations, and the full system is evaluated across p1/p4/p4m symmetry groups and all combinations of conservation laws on two PDE systems (closed-boundary shallow water and decaying incompressible turbulence), demonstrating that symmetries and physical constraints are individually beneficial and synergistically combine to outperform data augmentation and pushforward training baselines.

## Results

On the closed Shallow Water Equations, the doubly-constrained p4m/M surrogate is the only method that remains stable over 50-hour rollouts, achieving NRMSE-ζ of 0.14±0.02 at 25h while all other methods (p1/∅, p4/∅, FNO, drnet) diverge to NaN. On decaying incompressible turbulence, p4m/M+ρu achieves NRMSE-u of 1.20±0.05 at 60s versus 3.32±0.12 for the unconstrained p1/∅ baseline, and further improves to 1.06±0.02 with the pushforward trick. On real-world ocean current prediction, p4m/M+ρu achieves nRMSE of 0.88±0.02 at 12h and 1.10±0.03 at 120h, outperforming both the unconstrained p1/∅ (1.52/1.66) and the prior equivariant U-net of Wang et al. (0.91/1.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO, Li et al. 2021), the proposed method wins decisively on SWEs: FNO/∅ diverges to NaN by 25h with NRMSE-ζ of 0.58 at 1h, while p4m/M achieves 0.032 at 1h and remains stable at 0.14 at 25h. Against the Dilated ResNet (drnet, Stachenfeld et al. 2021), the constrained p4m/M+ρu Modern U-Net outperforms drnet on INS at 60s (1.20 vs. 1.30 NRMSE-u), and drnet/∅ diverges catastrophically on SWEs (2.3e4 NRMSE at 60s) while p4m/M stays at 0.14. Against the prior equivariant U-net of Wang et al. (2021), which enforces rotation symmetry but not staggered-grid compatibility or conservation laws, the proposed p4m/M+ρu achieves lower nRMSE on ocean currents (0.88 vs. 0.91 at 12h; 1.10 vs. 1.14 at 120h) and higher correlation (0.45 vs. 0.31 at 12h). The paper also shows that data augmentation and the pushforward trick, while helpful for unconstrained networks, cannot match the accuracy of the doubly-constrained p4m/M+ρu with standard training, though combining pushforward with p4m/M+ρu yields the best overall result (1.06 NRMSE-u at 60s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modern U-net architecture (Gupta & Brandstetter, 2023) with group-equivariant layers from the escnn framework (Cesa et al., 2022) and the vector-potential reparameterization for divergence-free fields (Wandel et al., 2021). The key departure is the introduction of novel input and output layers that make equivariant convolutions compatible with Arakawa C staggered grids, where existing equivariant layers fail because spatial resampling and local field transformation cannot be disentangled. While prior work has applied symmetry equivariance or conservation constraints to PDE surrogates individually, this paper is the first to systematically evaluate their combination and to demonstrate they are synergistic rather than redundant, despite the deep Noetherian connection between conservation laws and PDE symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The staggered-grid input/output layers remove a practical barrier that prevented equivariant methods from being applied to the most common fluid dynamics discretizations, making symmetry constraints broadly applicable to atmospheric, ocean, and industrial CFD surrogates. The finding that doubly-constrained networks outperform data augmentation and pushforward training—while themselves benefiting from pushforward—gives practitioners a clear, well-evidenced recipe for long-rollout accuracy. Open questions remain about how constraints limit error accumulation mechanistically, whether benefits persist for very large networks/datasets where constraints might be learned from data, and extension to 3D, continuous symmetry groups, and other conservation laws (e.g., energy).
