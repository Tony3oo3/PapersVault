## Problem

While empirical results show that allocating more inference-time compute via search, RL, and distillation dramatically improves LLM reasoning, there is no formal framework explaining *why* these methods work or what their fundamental limits are. This paper addresses that gap by modeling chain-of-thought generation as a metastable Markov process, providing the first provable guarantees for the benefits of inference-time search, policy-gradient fine-tuning, and distillation, as well as learning-theoretic lower bounds showing that global search is necessary.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a graph whose states are logical assertions (sentences or expressions, not individual tokens) and whose edges are reasoning steps, with the input being a pair (X_in, X_out) drawn from a distribution D over S×S. The pretrained base model is a transition kernel p^ε over this graph, learned via next-token prediction on bigram samples. Outputs include a valid CoT path, the set of identified sparse (hard) edges, a refined kernel with boosted sparse-edge probabilities after RL, a K×K meta-chain for the distilled model, and for the logical task, a group-action-computed value r(X_{0:T}) that depends on the entire path rather than just the endpoint.

## Method

The core technical contribution is modeling CoT as a perturbed Markov chain where easy reasoning steps form dense, rapidly-mixing clusters and hard steps form sparse inter-cluster edges with probability O(ε), creating metastable dynamics with a clear timescale separation. A tree search protocol (Algorithm 2) identifies sparse edges by running N parallel random walks from a random state: first exploring the local cluster for T_0 steps to estimate its extent, then detecting the first escape transition as a sparse edge. These edges are then used either as an external process reward model (PRM mode) or to fine-tune the base model via PPO-Clip (RL mode), both provably reducing the expected hitting time from Õ(KM/ε) to Õ(KM/ε_max) while preserving the pretrained model's capabilities (TV change o(1/M)). For distillation, the metastable dynamics are compressed into a meta-chain on K cluster representatives (Algorithm 4), trained via cross-entropy on recorded inter-cluster transitions, yielding a K²-sized model with hitting time O(K) independent of ε. The paper further introduces SDA (SQ dimension with access), a new statistical-query complexity measure that accounts for additional information the learner can access, to prove that without global search (only local neighborhood access), the logical reasoning task is intractable with polynomial compute.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result (Theorem 3.2) shows that the expected hitting time for CoT generation without search is Õ(KM/ε), where K is the number of clusters, M is the cluster size, and ε controls the sparsity of hard reasoning steps. Search + PRM or RL (PPO-Clip) improves this to Õ(KM/ε_max), yielding a speedup factor of ε_max/ε, while the total variation change to the pretrained model is only o(1/M). Distillation to a meta-chain (Theorem 4.3) further reduces the hitting time to O(K), independent of ε. A negative result (Theorem 5.3) establishes that without global search, the logical reasoning task has SDA ≥ e^{Ω(K)}, making it intractable for any polynomial-time learner with only local information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparisons' are between different reasoning protocols under the same metastable Markov framework. Without any search, the base model requires Õ(KM/ε) steps to find a valid path. Adding a process reward model (PRM) from sparse-edge search reduces this to Õ(KM/ε_max) with probability 1-Õ(1/K), and the search itself costs Õ(KM/ε) total time with only O(M+K) memory. RL via PPO-Clip achieves the same hitting-time improvement with additional cost Õ(K log(ε_max/ε)), which is negligible relative to pretraining time Õ(KM²ε⁻²). Distillation to a K×K meta-chain model achieves the best hitting time of O(K) and trains in Õ(M²ε⁻²) steps on a model of size K² rather than (KM)². Finally, the paper proves that restricting the learner to local neighborhood information (no global search) makes the logical task exponentially hard (SDA ≥ e^{Ω(K)}), establishing that global search is provably necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of metastable Markov chain theory (Bovier et al., 2002; Betz & Le Roux, 2016; Wicks & Greenwald, 2005) and the emerging literature on CoT reasoning as graph-based path-finding (Xu et al., 2019; Sanford et al., 2024a; Abbe et al., 2024). It departs from prior CoT expressivity analyses (Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023; Kim & Suzuki, 2024) by introducing a quantitative dynamical framework—hitting times, escape probabilities, and stochastic complementation—rather than focusing on representational capacity. The distillation scheme builds directly on the meta-chain construction of Wicks & Greenwald (2005), while the hardness result generalizes the statistical query dimension (Kearns, 1998; Feldman, 2017) into a new measure (SDA) that accounts for restricted information access, connecting to the 'globality barrier' conjectured by Abbe et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical account of *why* inference-time search, RL fine-tuning, and distillation each improve CoT reasoning, with clean quantitative bounds (hitting time Θ(KM/ε), improvement factor ε_max/ε from RL, O(K) hitting time for the distilled meta-chain). The lower bound showing that local search is fundamentally insufficient for logical reasoning (Theorem 5.3) formalizes a widely observed but previously unproven limitation. Key limitations include the purely theoretical nature (no empirical validation), the reliance on a simple softmax predictor, and the assumption of a fixed dense-sparse graph structure that may not capture all aspects of real LLM reasoning. Open questions include extending the framework to outcome-based rewards, iterative fine-tuning, and scaling laws for inference-time compute.
