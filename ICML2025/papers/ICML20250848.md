## Problem

While deep learning's susceptibility to label noise is well-known empirically, the theoretical understanding of how label noise shapes the nonlinear feature-learning dynamics of neural networks remains incomplete. Prior theoretical analyses either restrict to the lazy training regime or only characterize early-stage dynamics, failing to capture the full training trajectory where networks first learn generalizable features and then memorize noise. This paper asks: how does label noise affect the feature learning process of neural networks?

## Input / Output

In the theoretical setup, each input is a 2d-dimensional vector x = [x^(1), x^(2)] where one patch is the label-dependent signal yμ and the other is label-independent Gaussian noise ξ ~ N(0, σ²_ξ I_d); the observed label ỹ may be flipped from the true label y with class-dependent probabilities τ₊ and τ₋. The network outputs a binary prediction via a two-layer ReLU CNN. For real-world validation, the input is standard CIFAR-10 images (32×32 RGB) processed by a VGG network, with labels flipped at 20% probability, and the test distribution incorporates spurious features by sampling training-set noise vectors plus fresh Gaussian noise.

## Method

The paper rigorously analyzes the gradient descent training dynamics of a two-layer ReLU CNN (with fixed second-layer weights ±1/m) under logistic loss using a signal-noise decomposition that tracks weight evolution into signal coefficients γ and noise coefficients ρ. Under the condition n·SNR² = Θ(1) and constant label-flipping rates, the analysis reveals a two-stage picture: in Stage I, the model fits all clean samples while classifying noisy samples to their ground-truth labels (signal learning dominates), and in Stage II, as the training loss converges, noise coefficients on noisy samples surpass signal coefficients, causing the model to memorize noise and incur a non-vanishing test error lower-bounded by the flipping probability. The theory further provides provable justification for early stopping (stopping at the end of Stage I yields exponentially small test error) and for the small-loss sample selection criterion (a hard threshold at log(2) perfectly separates clean from noisy samples at the Stage I boundary).

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a theoretical two-stage characterization of feature learning under label noise for a two-layer ReLU CNN trained with gradient descent and logistic loss. In Stage I (ending at T₁ = Θ(η⁻¹nmσ_ξ⁻²d⁻¹)), the model fits all clean samples while ignoring noisy ones, with signal coefficients dominating noise coefficients; in Stage II (at loss convergence), the model overfits noisy samples and the test error is lower-bounded by 0.5·min{τ₊, τ₋}, a constant that does not vanish. Proposition 4.3 shows that early stopping at T₁ yields test error ≤ exp(−d·n⁻¹/C′) (arbitrarily small for d = Ω(n²)), and that clean/noisy samples are perfectly separable by the loss threshold log(2). Real-world validation on CIFAR-10 (first two classes, VGG, 20% label flip) confirms the two-stage accuracy pattern: noisy-sample accuracy initially drops to 0 then rises to 1, while test accuracy remains consistently lower than the noise-free baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper and does not propose a new training method to benchmark against competing label-noise-robust techniques (e.g., Co-teaching, SIGUA, loss correction). Instead, it provides the first feature-learning-theoretic justification for two widely used heuristics: early stopping and small-loss sample selection. The key comparison is between training with and without label noise under identical conditions: with noise, the model inevitably memorizes spurious features in Stage II, yielding a constant test error lower-bounded by 0.5·min{τ₊, τ₋}; without noise (Theorem 4.4), the test error decays exponentially as exp(d/n − n‖μ‖₄²/(C_D·σ_ξ⁴·d)), confirming that label noise is the sole source of generalization degradation. The paper also contrasts its analysis with prior feature-learning work (Kou et al., 2023; Cao et al., 2022), showing that the constant-order SNR condition (n·SNR² = Θ(1)) is essential to capture the two-stage dynamics, whereas prior analyses with n·SNR² = o(1) cannot distinguish clean from noisy sample behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning theory framework established by Cao et al. (2022) and Kou et al. (2023) for two-layer ReLU CNNs under a signal-noise data distribution, introducing label noise as a new perturbation. It departs from prior label noise analyses (Li et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023a) that were confined to the lazy training regime, and from prior feature learning works that assumed clean labels, by being the first to rigorously characterize the full nonlinear training dynamics under label noise. The key technical departure is the condition n·SNR² = Θ(1), which breaks the automatic balance of updates and time-invariant coefficient ratios that underpin prior analyses, necessitating a novel two-stage proof structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding for two widely deployed label-noise mitigation techniques—early stopping and small-loss sample selection—within the feature learning (rich) regime, explaining *why* they work rather than merely observing that they do. The two-stage picture (signal learning dominates, then noise memorization takes over) formalizes the empirically observed 'easy-to-hard' learning order and identifies a precise boundary (T₁) where generalization is optimal. Key limitations include restriction to random (class-conditional) label noise, binary classification, and two-layer CNNs; extending to data-dependent noise, multi-class settings, and deeper architectures with residual connections or normalization remains open and is critical for broader applicability.
