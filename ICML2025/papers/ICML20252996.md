## Problem

Unconstrained online convex optimization with adversarially corrupted gradients has not been studied before. Existing robust OCO methods assume a bounded domain with known diameter, while existing unconstrained OCO methods assume clean gradients. The unconstrained setting is fundamentally harder because iterates can grow exponentially, making even tiny corruptions catastrophic—unlike the bounded case where the diameter D naturally limits damage.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of potentially corrupted gradient vectors $\tilde{g}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (not the true gradients $g_t$), a time horizon $T$, and a corruption parameter $k$ that simultaneously upper-bounds both the number of rounds with large corruptions ($\|g_t - \tilde{g}_t\| \geq G$) and the cumulative normalized deviation. The Lipschitz constant $G \geq \max_t \|g_t\|$ may or may not be known a priori. The algorithm outputs predictions $w_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and guarantees comparator-adaptive regret $\|u\|G(\sqrt{T}+k)$ (known $G$) or an additional $(\|u\|^2+G^2)k$ penalty (unknown $G$), with constant regret at the origin $u=0$ regardless of $k$.

## Method

The core protocol (Algorithm 1) decomposes regret into four terms—composite regret $R_T^\mathcal{A}(u)$, ERROR, CORRECTION, and BIAS—and designs a regularizer to cancel ERROR while keeping BIAS small. Gradient clipping with threshold $h_t$ limits per-round corruption, and a Huber-type regularizer $f_t$ (from Zhang & Cutkosky, 2022) with $c=kG$ and $p=\ln T$ provides the CORRECTION that offsets the accumulated ERROR. The base learner is a centered mirror descent algorithm (Jacobsen & Cutkosky, 2022) that handles the composite loss $\langle\tilde{g}_t^c, w\rangle + r_t(w)$ without inflating the regret by a factor of $k$. For the unknown-$G$ case, a FILTER mechanism adaptively doubles the clipping threshold only after observing $k+1$ clipped gradients (ensuring $h_t \leq O(G)$), and a TRACKER mechanism partitions rounds into epochs where $\|w_t\|$ is bounded, enabling sparse quadratic regularization terms $\alpha_t\|w\|^2$ and $\beta_t\|w\|^2$ that cancel truncation and corruption errors respectively without incurring excessive bias.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret bound of Õ[εG + ‖u‖G(√T + k)] for unconstrained OCO with corrupted gradients when the Lipschitz constant G is known (Theorem 5.1), with a matching lower bound of Ω[‖u*‖(√T + k)] (Theorem 5.2). When G is unknown, the algorithm incurs an additional additive penalty of (k+1)(‖u‖² + [G]_{τ_G}²) (Corollary 6.2). A key property is that for k ≤ √T, the corruption penalty is sub-asymptotic to the standard uncorrupted regret Õ(‖u‖G√T), meaning up to √T corrupted rounds are tolerated 'for free.' Additionally, the regret at the origin u=0 remains O(ε) regardless of k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The primary comparison is to prior theoretical results: van Erven et al. (2021) achieve O(DG(√T + k)) for the bounded-domain setting, while this paper extends the guarantee to the unconstrained domain (W = ℝ^d) with comparator-adaptive dependence on ‖u‖ rather than a fixed diameter D. The paper demonstrates that the unconstrained setting is fundamentally harder—existing unconstrained algorithms (e.g., KT-bettor) suffer exponential regret blowup even with k = O(√T) corruptions (illustrated in Figure 1). The upper bound of Theorem 5.1 is shown to be tight via the lower bound of Theorem 5.2 (up to logarithmic factors, confirmed by Theorem E.5). For the unknown-G case, the extra (‖u‖² + G²)k penalty matches the optimal uncorrupted unknown-G rate from Cutkosky & Mhammedi (2024), though it remains open whether the multiplicative k dependence is optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: unconstrained (parameter-free) online convex optimization and robust OCO under adversarial corruptions. It extends the bounded-domain corruption-robust results of van Erven et al. (2021) to the unconstrained domain, where the authors show the problem is fundamentally harder because even tiny corruptions can cause exponential iterate growth. The technical toolkit draws on Huber-type regularization from Zhang & Cutkosky (2022), centered mirror descent from Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2022) (which they show handles composite losses 'for free'), and epigraph-based regularization from Cutkosky & Mhammedi (2024) for the unknown-G case. It departs from prior corruption-robust OCO work (Zhang & Cutkosky 2022, Jun & Orabona 2019) by assuming fully adversarial rather than stochastic corruptions, and from prior unconstrained OCO by never observing the true gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unconstrained setting is the natural regime for most practical ML problems (logistic regression, neural network training), and corrupted gradients arise from outliers, numerical errors, or adversarial interference, making this a realistic and previously unaddressed combination. The paper provides tight upper and lower bounds in the known-G case and a near-optimal bound in the unknown-G case, with the clean property that k ≤ √T corruptions are essentially 'free.' A key open question is whether the multiplicative k-dependence in the unknown-G penalty (‖u‖² + G²)k is optimal, and whether the FILTER/TRACKER mechanisms can be simplified. The online-to-batch corollary and distributionally robust optimization application suggest broader utility beyond pure OCO theory.
