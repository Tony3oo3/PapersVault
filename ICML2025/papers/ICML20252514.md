## Problem

Diffusion policies achieve state-of-the-art imitation learning performance in robotics but require tens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps at inference, yielding only ~1.5 Hz action prediction. This latency is prohibitive for dynamic environments where the robot must rapidly adapt to perturbations or human interference. The paper addresses this gap by distilling a pre-trained multi-step diffusion policy into a single-step action generator that preserves (or slightly exceeds) the original success rate while achieving an order-of-magnitude speedup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252514/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a set of past RGB observation images (120×160 resolution from front-view and wrist-mounted cameras) encoded by a ResNet-18 backbone, plus a random Gaussian noise vector z (for the stochastic variant OneDP-S; omitted in the deterministic OneDP-D). The output is a single action chunk of 16 consecutive robot actions produced in one forward pass, replacing the 100-step DDPM or 35-NFE EDM sampling of the original diffusion policy. In real-world deployment, this reduces wall-clock action generation from 660 ms (DDPM) or 66 ms (DDIM-10) to 7 ms, enabling 62 Hz control frequency.

## Method

OneDP distills a pre-trained diffusion policy π_φ into a one-step generator G_θ by minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the generator distribution and the policy distribution, but evaluated over diffused action samples rather than clean actions (inspired by Diffusion-GAN). The KL gradient is expressed as a score-difference loss: the difference between the score of the generator distribution and the score of the teacher policy, weighted by a reweighting function w(k)=σ_k². For the stochastic variant (OneDP-S), an auxiliary generator score network π_ψ is trained via denoising score matching on generator outputs to estimate the generator's score; for the deterministic variant (OneDP-D), the generator score is analytically available, eliminating the need for the auxiliary network. Both G_θ and π_ψ are warm-started from the pre-trained policy weights, and only the generator (lr=1e-6) and score network (lr=2e-5) are updated while π_φ remains frozen. This requires only 20 epochs of distillation (2% of the 1000-epoch pre-training) to converge, versus 450 epochs for the Consistency Policy baseline.

## Results

On the Robomimic benchmark (EDM setting), OneDP-S achieves 83.0% average success rate with a single NFE, matching the pre-trained Diffusion Policy (82.9% at 35 NFE) while being 35× faster in inference. In real-world Franka experiments, OneDP-S reaches 98% average success rate versus 83% for DP(DDIM), and completes the dynamic pnp-milk-move task at 100% success (vs. 80% for DP). Inference speed improves from 660 ms (DDPM 100 steps) to 7 ms (OneDP 1 step), boosting policy frequency from 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz. Distillation converges in just 20 epochs (2% of the 1000-epoch pre-training), compared to 450 epochs for Consistency Policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion Policy (DP), OneDP-S matches or slightly exceeds the multi-step teacher: 83.0% vs. 82.9% average success rate on Robomimic (EDM) with 1 NFE vs. 35 NFE, and 98% vs. 83% average success rate in real-world Franka experiments. The advantage is most pronounced in dynamic settings—OneDP achieves 100% on pnp-milk-move (human interference) where DP drops to 80%, and completes tasks ~25% faster on average (25.8 s vs. 36.4 s). Against Consistency Policy (CP), the primary distillation baseline, OneDP-S substantially outperforms CP at equal NFE: 83.0% vs. 67.2% (1 NFE) and 83.0% vs. 71.2% (3 NFE) average success rate on Robomimic, while converging 20× faster (20 epochs vs. 450 epochs). The stochastic variant OneDP-S consistently edges out the deterministic OneDP-D by 1–2% in simulation and 3% in real-world tasks, confirming the benefit of stochastic policies in complex environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the reverse-KL score-distillation framework from text-to-3D generation (SDS/DreamFusion, VSD) to robotic visuomotor control, distilling a pre-trained Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) into a single-step action generator. It departs from the closest prior work, Consistency Policy (Prasad et al., 2024), which uses Consistency Trajectory Models and requires multiple sampling steps, by instead optimizing KL divergence over the diffused action distribution (inspired by Diffusion-GAN's technique of optimizing divergence on noised samples). The method also inherits the action-chunking and CNN-U-Net architecture from Diffusion Policy, and the warm-start initialization strategy from recent image distillation methods (Luo et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42× inference speedup (1.5 Hz → 62 Hz) with only 2–10% additional training cost makes diffusion-based policies viable for dynamic, real-time robotic control where iterative denoising is prohibitive. The finding that a one-step student can match or slightly exceed its multi-step teacher is practically significant, suggesting that iterative denoising may introduce cumulative errors rather than being inherently necessary for quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to long-horizon tasks, whether KL is the optimal divergence (the authors note a discriminator term could help), and whether the 20 Hz real-world constraint masks further gains. The technique is likely to be broadly adopted as a standard post-training step for any diffusion-based policy.
