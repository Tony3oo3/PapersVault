## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dataset distillation methods suffer from three interrelated issues: the VAE latent space has low normality (a complex multi-component Gaussian mixture) making accurate distribution matching difficult, random noise injection during generation causes distribution deviation from the intended latents, and individual sampling of representative latents ignores the overall distribution of the distilled subset. These limitations prevent current methods from producing truly representative compact datasets that generalize across model architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full labeled image dataset (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet-1K) along with a pre-trained DiT diffusion model and VAE encoder-decoder, and produces a distilled dataset of n images per class (IPC = 1–100). A notable property is that the distilled dataset is architecture-agnostic: one generation pass suffices for training any student model (ResNet, MobileNet, VGG, DeiT, etc.), and the entire distilled dataset can be stored as just the Gaussian statistical parameters (mean and variance per dimension) plus DiT weights (~0.016 GB), rather than as actual images.

## Method

D³HR is a three-stage framework: (1) Domain Mapping applies deterministic DDIM inversion to bijectively map each VAE latent into a high-normality Gaussian noise space, preserving structural information that stochastic DDPM noise would destroy; (2) Distribution Matching fits a single per-class Gaussian (mean and variance per dimension) to the inverted latents, justified by a lemma showing that with sufficiently many inversion steps the noise-space distribution converges to Gaussian; (3) Group Sampling draws m random subsets of n i.i.d. latents each from the fitted Gaussian via the Ziggurat algorithm, then selects the subset whose empirical mean, standard deviation, and skewness most closely match the target Gaussian using a weighted metric. The selected latents are decoded back to images via DDIM sampling and the VAE decoder. This design eliminates the need for a teacher model during distillation and ensures the entire subset (not just individual points) is statistically representative of the full dataset.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, D³HR achieves 44.3% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10, surpassing the strongest baseline RDED (42.0%) by 2.3 points and D⁴M (27.9%) by 16.4 points. At IPC=50, D³HR reaches 59.4% vs. RDED's 56.5% and DWA's 55.2%. On CIFAR-10 at IPC=50, D³HR attains 70.8% (ResNet-18) and 63.9% (ResNet-101), improving over RDED by 8.7 and 12.3 points respectively. Across all four datasets and both architectures, D³HR achieves the highest accuracy in 9 out of 10 ResNet-18 settings and all 10 ResNet-101 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³HR outperforms all four main baselines (SRe²L, DWA, D⁴M, RDED) on ImageNet-1K across all IPCs for both ResNet-18 and ResNet-101, with the largest margin over D⁴M (e.g., 44.3% vs. 27.9% at IPC=10 on ResNet-18). The closest competitor is RDED, which D³HR beats by 2.3 points on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 and by 8.7 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (ResNet-18); however, RDED slightly edges out D³HR on Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50 with ResNet-18 (58.2% vs. 56.9%), though D³HR recovers a 18.2-point lead on the same setting with ResNet-101 (59.4% vs. 41.2%). Against D3S (Loo et al.), which uses 5 teacher models, D³HR with a single soft label achieves 44.8% vs. 39.1% on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 (ResNet-18) and ties at IPC=50 (60.2% each). On ImageWoof under hard labels, D³HR surpasses Minimax (Gu et al.) at every IPC, e.g., 39.6% vs. 37.6% at IPC=10 and 66.8% vs. 65.7% at IPC=100 with ResNet-18. In cross-architecture generalization (Table 2), D³HR with a one-time generation cost outperforms both SRe²L and RDED across ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, and VGG-11 student models on ImageNet-1K IPC=10, while SRe²L is entirely inapplicable to VGG-11 due to its lack of BN layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based dataset distillation line initiated by D⁴M (Su et al., 2024) and Minimax (Gu et al., 2024), which use VAE latent spaces and diffusion models to generate distilled images without teacher-model dependency. It departs from these methods by identifying three specific failure modes—low-normality distribution matching in VAE space, distribution shift from stochastic DDPM noise, and independent per-sample sampling—and addressing them through DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) as a bijective domain mapping to a high-normality Gaussian noise space, followed by a group-sampling scheme with statistical evaluation metrics. The latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022) provides the VAE encoder-decoder backbone, while the Ziggurat algorithm supplies efficient Gaussian sampling for the group selection stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-time generation cost enabling cross-architecture generalization (ResNet, MobileNet, VGG, EfficientNet, DeiT) without re-distillation is a practical advantage over teacher-dependent methods like SRe²L and RDED, which must regenerate datasets per architecture. The storage efficiency of retaining only per-class mean/variance parameters (≈0.016 GB for ImageNet-1K) rather than full image sets is notable for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the Gaussian approximation in the noise space holds for highly multimodal or long-tail classes, whether the approach extends to video or other modalities, and whether the DiT fine-tuning cost for non-ImageNet datasets limits practical adoption. The trade-off between inversion steps (normality gain vs. structural information loss) suggests a fundamental tension that may constrain further improvements.
