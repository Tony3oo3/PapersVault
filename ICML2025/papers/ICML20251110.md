## Problem

Multi-head attention activates all heads for every token, yet prior work shows many heads are redundant and can be pruned without accuracy loss. No existing method provides a dynamic, per-token routing mechanism that adaptively selects which heads to activate, nor a way to convert already-pretrained multi-head attention models into such a sparsely-activated form without retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MoH is a drop-in replacement for the attention layer, so the input/output interface is unchanged across all three evaluated frameworks: ViT takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs 1,000-class logits; DiT takes Gaussian noise plus a class label and outputs 256×256 images; LLMs take text token sequences and produce next-token distributions. The only internal change is that each token in the sequence is routed to a subset of Top-K heads (plus always-on shared heads) rather than all h heads, with per-head scalar weights modulating the summation.

## Method

The key insight is that multi-head attention can be rewritten in summation form by decomposing the output projection W_O row-wise, turning the concatenation of head outputs into a sum of per-head contributions. MoH replaces this uniform sum with a weighted sum where a router assigns each token a Top-K subset of heads and a scalar gate g_i, so the output is Σ g_i H^i W_O^i. A subset of heads is designated as shared (always active) to capture context-independent knowledge, while the remaining routed heads are selected per token. A two-stage routing scheme first determines which routed heads fire via Top-K on W_r x_t, then balances the relative contribution of shared vs. routed heads through a second softmax over W_h x_t. A load-balance loss regularizes the router to avoid collapse onto a few heads. For continue-tuning pre-trained models like LLaMA3-8B, the authors use a parameter-free router based on the ℓ₂ norm of each head's query and a straight-through estimator to back-propagate through the quantized (binary) routing decision, enabling conversion with only ~400B tokens of additional training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, MoH-ViT-B achieves 84.9% Top-1 accuracy using only 75% of attention heads, surpassing the TransNeXt-B baseline (84.8% at 100% heads); even at 50% heads it retains 84.7%. For class-conditional image generation at 256×256, MoH-DiT-XL/27K achieves FID 8.56 with 90% heads versus DiT-XL's 9.62 at 100% heads (and FID 2.94 vs 3.22 with CFG=1.25). For LLMs, MoH-LLaMA3-8B (continue-tuned from LLaMA3-8B with only 400B tokens) reaches 64.0% average accuracy across 14 benchmarks using 75% of heads, outperforming the original LLaMA3-8B by 2.4%. Training from scratch, MoH-LLM-S achieves 45.4% average on 6 benchmarks with just 50% heads versus 43.9% for the full-head baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest ViT baseline TransNeXt-B (84.8%, 100% heads), MoH-ViT-B wins by 0.1% while activating only 75% of heads, and matches it at 50% heads (84.7%). In image generation, MoH-DiT-XL/27K (FID 8.56, 90% heads) outperforms DiT-XL/27K (FID 9.62, 100% heads) by 1.06 FID points, and with CFG=1.25 the gap widens to 0.28 FID (2.94 vs 3.22). For LLMs trained from scratch, MoH-LLM-S at 50% heads (45.4% avg) beats LLM-S at 100% heads (43.9% avg) by 1.5 points, and MoH-LLM-B at 75% heads (47.8%) edges out LLM-B (47.4%). The headline LLaMA3-8B continue-tuning result shows MoH-LLaMA3-8B (75% heads) surpassing LLaMA3-8B (100% heads) by 2.4% average across 14 benchmarks, with particularly large gains on CEVAL (+9.2), CMMLU (+13.7), GSM8K (+7.4), and TruthfulQA (+8.6), though it slightly trails on MMLU (−2.2 in one evaluation setting). Overall, MoH consistently matches or exceeds full-head baselines across all three model families (ViT, DiT, LLM) while activating only 50–90% of attention heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the summation-form interpretation of multi-head attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the Mixture-of-Experts routing paradigm (Jacobs et al., 1991), proposing to treat attention heads as experts selected per-token via Top-K routing. It departs from prior head-selection work (MoA, SwitchHead, Peng et al.) in three key ways: it introduces shared heads with a two-stage routing mechanism that dynamically balances shared vs. routed contributions, it avoids increasing total parameters (unlike MoE which adds expert capacity), and it demonstrates that pre-trained multi-head models (e.g., LLaMA3-8B) can be continue-tuned into MoH using a parameter-free router and straight-through estimator. The paper explicitly contrasts with MoA (Zhang et al., 2022), noting that MoA's shared key/value design necessitates training from scratch, whereas MoH's architecture preserves compatibility with existing pre-trained weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the continue-tuning pathway: converting an existing 8B-parameter pre-trained model to MoH requires only ~400B tokens (~3% of original training) and yields a 2.4% average accuracy gain while activating only 75% of heads, making this a low-cost upgrade for deployed models. However, limitations remain: activation rates of 50–90% are still relatively high, DiT generation quality degrades at 75% activation (suggesting dense prediction tasks have less head redundancy), and Chinese/math capabilities show partial degradation during continue-tuning. Open questions include whether heterogeneous head sizes, sub-50% activation, and multimodal token routing can push the efficiency frontier further, and whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters.
