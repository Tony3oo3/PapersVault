## Problem

While LoRA's efficiency and structural properties (expressivity, convergence, submatrix asymmetry) have been extensively studied, its intrinsic security vulnerabilities under training-time attacks remain unexplored. The paper asks whether LoRA's low-rank structure makes fine-tuned models more or less robust than full fine-tuning against data poisoning and backdoor attacks, a question with direct implications for the millions of devices deploying LoRA-adapted LLMs.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on text sequences (max length 512) fed into BERT-large for six GLUE binary classification tasks, producing class labels as the model output. The security analysis itself produces robustness metrics: a simplified NTK-based metric M′ measuring gradient-update similarity between clean and poisoned samples, information bits (IB) quantifying the Fisher information determinant, and Rényi entropy H_α capturing the curvature of the fine-tuning manifold. These metrics are evaluated under varying poisoning rates (label flipping for UPA, trigger injection for BPA) and LoRA hyperparameters (rank 4–512, initialization variance scale 0.001–2.0).

## Method

The paper constructs an analytical framework that (1) reformulates training-time robustness (TTR) via the neural tangent kernel to eliminate training-step dependency, (2) derives closed-form NTK functions for both full fine-tuning and LoRA under the infinite-width Gaussian process limit, showing LoRA's NTK equals FF's NTK plus a residual term Δ_r governed by A^T A − I, and (3) bridges NTK to Fisher information via information geometry to prove that LoRA's kernel matrix is negative semi-definite (under r ≤ n_{l−1} and σ² ≤ 1/n_{l−1}), yielding strictly fewer information bits and lower Rényi entropy than FF. This smoother information geometry explains the double-edged security profile: a smaller search space for backdoor triggers (better BPA resistance) but an oversimplified decision surface that amplifies the effect of label-flipped samples (worse UPA resistance). The framework further identifies rank r and initialization variance σ² as the two critical security knobs, with experiments on BERT-large across six GLUE tasks confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Results

Under untargeted poisoning attacks (UPA), LoRA-based fine-tuning exhibits more severe accuracy degradation than full fine-tuning (FF), with the gap widening as the poisoning rate increases, particularly on QNLI and QQP. Under backdoor poisoning attacks (BPA), LoRA significantly outperforms FF, achieving up to 30% improvement in backdoor resistance on datasets such as SST-2 and QQP, while both methods maintain comparable performance on untriggered test data. Theoretical analysis shows LoRA's information bits (IB) and Rényi entropy (H_α) are always smaller than FF's when r ≤ n_{l-1} and σ² ≤ 1/n_{l-1}, explaining its smoother information geometry. Experiments on BERT-large across six GLUE tasks (SST-2, COLA, QNLI, QQP, RTE, MRPC) and generative LLMs (Stanford Alpaca) corroborate these findings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between LoRA and full fine-tuning (FF) under two attack scenarios. Under untargeted poisoning (random label flipping), LoRA consistently underperforms FF, with the accuracy gap growing as the poisoning rate increases; the degradation is most pronounced on QNLI and QQP, while SST-2 shows a smaller gap. Under backdoor attacks (trigger injection with label modification), LoRA substantially outperforms FF, achieving up to 30% higher accuracy on triggered test samples on SST-2 and QQP, indicating stronger backdoor resistance. On COLA, the two methods show comparable backdoor resistance. Both LoRA and FF maintain consistent performance on untriggered (clean) test data in backdoor experiments, confirming that backdoor injection does not degrade general model performance. The paper further shows that increasing LoRA's rank improves UPA resistance but weakens BPA resistance, revealing a fundamental trade-off, while smaller initialization variance of the A matrix enhances backdoor robustness with minimal impact on poisoning resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA formulation (Hu et al., 2021) and the neural tangent kernel framework (Jacot et al., 2021) to derive a new analytical lens for security analysis. Prior LoRA studies have examined expressive capacity, convergence smoothness, and submatrix asymmetry, while security-related work has either used LoRA as an attack vehicle or highlighted its benefits in differential privacy and federated learning. This paper departs from both lines by introducing a formal 'training-time robustness' (TTR) metric and connecting LoRA's low-rank structure to information geometry (Fisher information, Rényi entropy) to explain differential vulnerability across attack types — a theoretical bridge not previously drawn.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LoRA becomes the de facto standard for fine-tuning LLMs deployed at scale, understanding its intrinsic security trade-offs is practically critical. The actionable finding that rank and initialization variance create a tension between backdoor resistance and poisoning vulnerability gives practitioners concrete tuning guidance. Limitations include reliance on the NTK regime (which may not hold for very deep or narrow networks), initial analysis under a single-layer-difference assumption, and experiments primarily on BERT-large for GLUE tasks; whether the conclusions transfer to generative LLMs at scale remains partially open (though Appendix E.3 provides some evidence).
