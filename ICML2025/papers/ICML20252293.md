## Problem

MoE-LoRA combines multiple low-rank adapters to overcome LoRA's limited representational capacity, and Riemannian preconditioners correct LoRA's gradient sub-optimality on the low-rank manifold. However, when both are combined, the gate values that weight each expert's contribution rescale the manifold curvature of each expert, introducing a squared gate-value term (g_i²) in the gradient update that systematically underestimates the full-matrix gradient. No prior work addresses this gate-induced manifold distortion in MoE-LoRA training.

## Input / Output

The method is applied as a drop-in training strategy for MoE-LoRA modules inserted into the FFN layers of frozen foundation models (Llama-3.2-3B/1B, GLM-4-9B, LLaVA-v1.5-7B). For text tasks (QA, GLUE), the model receives text prompts and produces text answers or classification labels; for vision-language tasks (Visual7W, VMCBench), it receives an RGB image paired with a multiple-choice question and outputs the selected answer. The MoE configuration uses 20 experts of rank 4 with top-10 routing by default, and the gate module is trained with a much smaller learning rate (3e-8) and gradient norm capped at 1.0 for stability.

## Method

The paper identifies that in MoE-LoRA, the forward equation X = W + Σ g_i B_i A_i causes the chain rule to inject g_i into each expert's gradient, so that applying standard Riemannian preconditioners yields a g_i²-weighted sum of gradient projections (Eq. 10), which underestimates the true full-rank gradient. The theoretical fix divides each expert's Riemannian preconditioner by its gate value g_i (Eq. 11), restoring a linear g_i weighting that matches the single-LoRA projection scale (Eq. 12). Since per-token gate values are unavailable at backprop time, the engineering solution modifies the forward pass to X = Ŵ + Σ √ĝ_i B_i A_i + (g_i − √ĝ_i) B̂_i Â_i (Eq. 13), splitting each expert into an optimizable √g_i-scaled component and a detached residual, so that standard Riemannian preconditioners naturally produce the correct linear g_i weighting during backpropagation without any per-token bookkeeping.

## Results

On four QA benchmarks with Llama-3.2-3B, gate-rescaled Riemannian SGD (gRSGD) achieves an average accuracy of 63.5%, outperforming conventional Riemannian SGD (RSGD) at 58.5% (+8.5%); the corresponding AdamW improvement is smaller (76.7% vs. 75.6%, +1.5%). On the nine-task GLUE benchmark, gRSGD improves the average from 65.16 to 72.90 (+11.9%) under Llama-3.2-3B, and from 77.27 to 80.61 (+4.3%) under GLM-4-9B. On vision-language tasks (Visual7W/VMCBench) with LLaVA-v1.5-7B, gRSGD reaches 72%/70% versus RSGD's 68%/59%, showing the largest relative gains under SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Riemannian Preconditioned LoRA optimizer (Zhang & Pilanci, ICML 2024), the paper's gate-rescaled variant consistently wins: gRSGD outperforms RSGD by 8.5% average on QA and 11.9% on GLUE (Llama-3.2-3B), while gRAdamW improves over RAdamW by 1.5% and 3.0% respectively, confirming the gains are most pronounced under SGD. Against the pure MoE-LoRA baseline (Liu et al., 2023), the paper's MoELoRA-gRSGD achieves 63.22% average QA accuracy versus MoELoRA-SGD's 52.47%, a +10.75% improvement. Against MoLA (Gao et al., 2024), which allocates varying expert counts per layer, the integrated MoLA-gRSGD reaches 63.92% average versus MoLA-SGD's 53.98% (+9.94%), demonstrating that the gate-rescaling technique is composable with architectural MoE-LoRA variants. The paper also shows that its engineering modification does not help when applied to a plain SGD optimizer without Riemannian preconditioning (ablation in Fig. 3), confirming the method's tight coupling with the Riemannian framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) Mixture-of-Experts LoRA (MoE-LoRA), which addresses LoRA's limited representational capacity by routing inputs to multiple low-rank adapters (MoELoRA, MoLA, MixLoRA, etc.), and (2) Riemannian preconditioned LoRA (Zhang & Pilanci, ICML 2024), which corrects the gradient sub-optimality inherent to low-rank factorization by projecting full-matrix gradients onto the row/column spaces of A and B. The key departure is identifying that in MoE-LoRA, the per-expert gate value g_i acts as a manifold scaler that distorts each expert's curvature, causing the standard Riemannian preconditioner to under-estimate the assembled gradient (producing g_i² terms instead of g_i). The paper's gate-based rescaling (Eq. 11) and its engineering approximation (Eq. 13, a two-line code change) are the novel contributions, extending the single-adapter preconditioner theory to the multi-expert setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gate-induced manifold distortion is a previously unaddressed source of training instability in MoE-LoRA, and the proposed fix is both theoretically grounded and practically lightweight (two lines of forward-pass code). However, the gains are notably asymmetric: ~8.5% improvement over Riemannian SGD versus only ~1.5% over Riemannian AdamW, suggesting AdamW's adaptive per-parameter scaling already partially compensates for the gate distortion. The ablation confirming that the rescaling does not help a plain (non-Riemannian) SGD optimizer underscores that the method is tightly coupled to the preconditioning framework, limiting its standalone applicability. Open questions include whether the gate-distortion analysis generalizes to more complex routing (e.g., learned or input-dependent gates beyond softmax top-k), to non-LoRA expert architectures, and to settings where gate values vary significantly across tokens within a batch (which the engineering approximation handles only approximately).
