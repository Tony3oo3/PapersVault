## Problem

Existing multi-task diffusion models either require full retraining (universal modeling) or add specialized components per task, both of which inflate parameter count and computational cost, making them unsuitable for on-device deployment. The paper addresses the gap of enabling a single pretrained text-to-image diffusion model to handle multiple image-to-image tasks (editing, super-resolution, inpainting) while maintaining iso-FLOP and iso-parameter efficiency relative to the original single-task model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250281/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2I, the model takes a text prompt and generates a 512×512 image. For I2I tasks, the encoded input image is concatenated with noisy latents along the channel dimension, and a task-specific input convolution layer processes this combined tensor; the text prompt provides the editing instruction (or an empty string for SR). The router weights are pre-computed from a learnable task embedding, so at inference the task identifier alone determines which experts are activated, adding negligible overhead. All tasks share the same frozen backbone and produce a single decoded RGB image as output.

## Method

MTU is motivated by the empirical finding that FFN layers in diffusion models undergo the largest parameter deviation during task-specific fine-tuning, while attention layers remain relatively stable. Each FFN block is split into N smaller experts with hidden dimension d_FFN/N (preserving total parameter count), initialized by sharding the original dense weights. A two-layer MLP router, conditioned on a learnable task embedding, produces softmax weights that dynamically combine expert outputs per task. Task-specific layer norms are inserted before each expert to handle distributional shifts across tasks, and task-specific input convolution layers accommodate the extra conditioning channels. Only the experts, routers, and task-specific layers are trained (158M params for SDv1.5, 1.5B for SDXL) under a standard diffusion noise-prediction loss summed over all tasks, while the rest of the UNet remains frozen.

## Results

On SDv1.5, MTU achieves FID 7.2 on T2I, I-T Directional Similarity 17.2 on Image Editing, LPIPS 24.8 on Super-Resolution, and I-I Directional Similarity 44.0 on Inpainting, while maintaining 0.68 TFLOPs (vs. 0.67 for the single-task baseline). On SDXL, MTU achieves FID 3.9, IE 20.1, SR 26.5, and IP 44.2 at 1.54 TFLOPs (vs. 1.53 for the pre-trained model). In both cases, MTU matches or exceeds single-task fine-tuned performance across all four tasks while keeping parameter count and FLOPs nearly identical to the original pre-trained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTU outperforms all single-task baselines on most tasks: on SDv1.5, it beats the InstructPix2Pix editing model by 1.8 points (17.2 vs. 15.4 I-T Dir Sim) and the SR baseline by 13.2 points (24.8 vs. 38.0 LPIPS), though it trails the Inst-inpaint model by 2.5 points on Inpainting (44.0 vs. 46.5). On SDXL, MTU surpasses every single-task baseline, including IE (20.1 vs. 17.3), SR (26.5 vs. 26.9), and IP (44.2 vs. 43.2). Against multi-task baselines, MTU-SDv1.5 achieves a lower FID than UniDiffuser (7.2 vs. 7.4) and Versatile Diffusion (7.2 vs. 10.1) while using fewer FLOPs (0.68 vs. 0.83/0.87 TFLOPs) and fewer parameters (869M vs. 952M/1.1B). In the PEFT comparison (Table 3), MTU outperforms LoRA by 8.9 points on IE and 18.5 points on SR for SDv1.5, and beats IA3 by 11.7 points on IE and 24.5 points on SR for SDXL, demonstrating that task-specific adapters cannot match the multi-task capability of the upcycled expert architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the sparse upcycling paradigm from LLMs (He et al., 2024; Komatsuzaki et al., 2023) into the diffusion model setting, specifically adapting the fine-grained MoE concept (Krajewski et al., 2024) where a dense FFN is split into smaller experts governed by a granularity ratio. Unlike prior multi-task diffusion approaches such as UniDiffuser (universal joint-distribution modeling) and Versatile Diffusion (task-specific specialized components), which inflate parameter counts and FLOPs, MTU constrains the upcycled model to iso-FLOP with the original by shrinking each expert proportionally. The key empirical departure is the observation that FFN layers in UNet-based diffusion models undergo the largest weight deviation during task-specific fine-tuning, motivating the targeted replacement of FFNs rather than attention blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MTU provides the first multi-task diffusion recipe that preserves on-device computational budgets (iso-FLOP, near-iso-parameter), making it directly relevant for edge deployment of generative AI. The empirical finding that FFNs are the primary locus of task adaptation in diffusion UNets is a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing where to apply PEFT or architectural modifications. However, the approach is validated only on UNet-based Stable Diffusion variants (not DiT architectures), covers just four tasks, and exhibits non-trivial task interference (e.g., IP degrades when combined with T2I and SR), leaving open questions about scalability to many more tasks and to transformer-based denoisers. The paper also notes that gradient-conflict mitigation and dynamic loss balancing from the multi-task learning literature have not yet been explored in this diffusion setting.
