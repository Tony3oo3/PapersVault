## Problem

The common explanation for why TRPO and PPO outperform vanilla policy gradient (VPG) is that trust region constraints prevent excessive policy updates, yet empirical evidence shows these constraints are routinely violated in practice and smaller policy steps do not always yield better performance. This paper asks: if trust region enforcement is not the key factor, what actually drives the performance gap between VPG and PPO?

## Input / Output

The method operates on continuous-state, continuous-action MDPs (MuJoCo/Gymnasium benchmarks: Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid). The policy network takes a state observation vector and outputs a continuous action (Gaussian mean and log-std). The value network takes the same state observation and outputs a scalar value estimate. The critical design choice is the number of value network gradient steps per iteration (ranging from 1 to 100), while the policy network receives exactly one gradient step per collected batch of 2048 transitions.

## Method

The paper's core insight is that in chaotic continuous-control environments, the policy objective J(θ) is only α-Hölder continuous (α < 1) in the policy parameters due to positive Lyapunov exponents, meaning even small policy updates cause large, fractal-scale changes in true returns. In contrast, the value network output is Lipschitz continuous in its parameters, so the value regression landscape is smooth. Theorem 5.2 formalizes this asymmetry: the value network requires at least K₁β₁^α/(K₂β₂) gradient steps per policy step to track the rapidly shifting true returns. Empirically, simply increasing the number of value update steps per iteration in standard VPG (from 1 to 50–100) while keeping a single policy update step matches or exceeds PPO's performance across all five MuJoCo benchmarks, revealing that TRPO and PPO's apparent advantage stems from their implicit execution of more value steps (via separate optimization loops or mini-batching with multiple epochs) rather than from trust region enforcement.

## Results

On the MuJoCo continuous control benchmarks, VPG with 50+ value steps per iteration achieves cumulative rewards comparable to or better than PPO across all five environments (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid). Specifically, with GAE λ=0.95, VPG reaches 2601.57±232.14 on Hopper, 3457.79±646.70 on Walker, and 4928.88±807.04 on HalfCheetah, outperforming PPO's 1965.29±478.14, 2527.74±507.40, and 4488.60±2699.54 respectively. VPG also outperforms PPO in the higher-dimensional Ant and Humanoid tasks, and is more robust to learning rate and GAE factor choices. VPG stabilizes at 50 value steps, at which point the value estimation error oscillates around zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VPG (with 1, 10, 50, and 100 value steps per iteration) against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with default settings across five MuJoCo environments. VPG with 50+ value steps matches or exceeds PPO on Hopper (+32% cumulative reward), Walker (+37%), and HalfCheetah (+10%), and clearly outperforms PPO on the higher-dimensional Ant and Humanoid tasks where PPO's default hyperparameters lead to overly aggressive policy updates. VPG also demonstrates superior robustness: its performance remains stable across learning rates (0.0003–0.001) and GAE factors, whereas PPO's cumulative reward drops by over 40% in Walker and HalfCheetah when λ changes from 0.95 to 1. Additionally, VPG is more sample-efficient in terms of policy steps, and PPO's performance degrades after 2M environment steps in Walker and Ant while VPG continues to improve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the vanilla policy gradient (VPG/A2C) formulation from Sutton et al. (1999) and Mnih et al. (2016), but fundamentally repositions the explanation for why TRPO and PPO outperform VPG. Rather than attributing the performance gap to trust region enforcement (KL constraints, ratio clipping), the paper argues that the implicit increase in value network update steps—arising from TRPO's separate optimization loops and PPO's multi-epoch mini-batching—is the critical factor. It draws on the fractal landscape analysis of Wang et al. (2023) and the mollification perspective of Wang et al. (2024) to provide a theoretical framework (via Lyapunov exponents and Hölder continuity) explaining why value networks require more aggressive optimization than policy networks in chaotic continuous-control environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If value estimation accuracy is the dominant factor, then the elaborate trust region machinery in PPO/TRPO may be largely unnecessary for performance, opening the door to simpler, more robust on-policy algorithms with fewer hyperparameters to tune. This has direct practical implications for RL practitioners who struggle with PPO's well-documented brittleness and sensitivity to implementation details. However, the analysis is confined to continuous control (MuJoCo/Gymnasium benchmarks), and the authors explicitly refrain from claiming generalizability to other domains such as LLM training or discrete action spaces. Open questions include whether the Lyapunov-based theoretical framework holds for non-chaotic or partially observable environments, and whether the value-step prescription scales to higher-dimensional or longer-horizon tasks.
