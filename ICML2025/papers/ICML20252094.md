## Problem

Existing semantic-space jailbreaking methods (e.g., PAIR, TAP-T) rely on binary 'jailbroken or not' feedback from the target LLM, which provides no granular signal for intermediate search steps and performs poorly against adversarially trained models. This paper asks whether the test-time compute scaling techniques developed for reasoning (process reward models, tree search) can be repurposed to guide adversarial prompt search with a continuous loss signal, enabling more effective jailbreaking even against models that trade inference-time compute for robustness.

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to the target LLM's log-probability vectors to compute a cross-entropy loss over a desired affirmative string (e.g., 'Sure, here is the step-by-step instructions...'), making it gradient-free (forward passes only). For black-box targets, it substitutes an aggregated loss from multiple surrogate white-box models and performs a multi-shot transfer by querying the target with all collected prompts. The attacker, Feedback, and Refiner LLMs must be unmoderated; the attacker's temperature is set to 1.0 to encourage exploration, and the target's temperature is set to 0.

## Method

The paper formulates jailbreaking as a reasoning problem over a string S, where the attacker LLM maps S to a prompt P, and the goal is to minimize the target's cross-entropy loss on a desired affirmative string. Three LLM modules implement the reasoning loop: the Attacker generates n prompts from S, the Feedback LLM produces a textual analysis explaining why lower-loss prompts outperform higher-loss ones (analogous to a process reward model), and the Refiner LLM incorporates this feedback into an updated reasoning string (analogous to a textual gradient step). A tree search with a priority buffer of size B (inspired by Go-with-the-Winners) selects the highest-scoring node for expansion, prunes lower-scoring candidates, and backtracks when children fail to improve. The verifier score is the negative minimum loss over n sampled prompts, and the entire procedure is gradient-free, using only bandit information from the loss. For black-box transfer, losses from r surrogate models are averaged, and all collected prompts are queried against the target (multi-shot), substantially improving success rates over one-shot transfer.

## Results

On white-box targets, Adversarial Reasoning achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) on 4 of 5 models: 60% on Llama-2-7B, 88% on Llama-3-8B, 44% on Llama-3-8B-RR, 70% on Mistral-7B-v2-RR, and 100% on R2D2. On adversarially trained models (Llama-3-8B-RR, Mistral-7B-v2-RR), token-space methods (GCG, Adaptive Attack) drop to 0–6% while Adversarial Reasoning maintains 44–70%. In the multi-shot transfer setting, the method reaches 56% ASR on OpenAI o1-preview (vs. 16% for PAIR), 94% on GPT-4o, and 100% on DeepSeek-R1. With a weaker attacker (Vicuna-13B), it still achieves 64% ASR on Llama-3-8B—more than 3× PAIR (20%) and TAP-T (18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (token-space, gradient-based), Adversarial Reasoning wins on all five targets, with the largest margins on adversarially trained models (44% vs. 2% on Llama-3-8B-RR; 70% vs. 6% on Mistral-7B-v2-RR). Adaptive Attack (random search over crafted templates) is the only method that beats Adversarial Reasoning, reaching 100% on Llama-3-8B, but it collapses to 0% on both adversarially trained targets. Rainbow Teaming is consistently outperformed by 20–40 percentage points across all models. AutoDAN-Turbo is outperformed on every target except R2D2 (84% vs. 100%). PAIR is beaten on all five white-box targets (by 12–38 pp) and in the transfer setting (e.g., 56% vs. 16% on o1-preview). TAP-T is the closest competitor, tying at 100% on R2D2 but trailing by 12–30 pp on the other four models; with a weak attacker (Vicuna), the gap widens to 46 pp (64% vs. 18%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of jailbreaking research: token-space methods (GCG, GCQ) that use a continuous cross-entropy loss to guide optimization, and semantic-space methods (PAIR, TAP-T) that use LLM chain-of-thought reasoning to generate coherent adversarial prompts. The key departure is treating the token-space loss function as a process reward model (analogous to PRMs in the reasoning literature) to verify and prune a tree of reasoning strings, rather than relying on the sparse binary judge signal that prior semantic-space methods use. The Refiner component adapts the textual-gradient idea from TextGrad, and the buffer-based search with backtracking draws on Go-with-the-Winners and Greedy Coordinate Query, but the combination of loss-guided CoT construction with tree search in prompt space is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central implication is that the 'deliberative alignment' paradigm—where models trade inference-time compute for safety—does not provide a fundamental robustness advantage if attackers can also scale their test-time compute with structured reasoning. This challenges a core assumption in current safety research and suggests that future alignment work must account for reasoning-path-level attacks, not just individual prompt defenses. Open questions include whether defenders can develop analogous reasoning-based verification to detect adversarial reasoning paths, how the approach generalizes to multimodal or agentic settings, and whether the multi-shot transfer with surrogate losses remains effective as black-box models tighten API restrictions. The 100% ASR on DeepSeek-R1 and 56% on o1-preview (vs. 16% previously reported) underscore the urgency of these questions.
