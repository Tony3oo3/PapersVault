## Problem

Joint estimation of sparse regression coefficients and output covariance structure in multiple-output regression is computationally prohibitive at high dimensionality: standard ARD-based methods incur O(d³) cost per iteration due to matrix inversion, and existing penalized or MCMC-based approaches either lack principled uncertainty quantification or scale poorly. This paper addresses the gap by extending ARD to a network setting that simultaneously performs feature selection and sparse precision matrix estimation, while reducing per-iteration complexity through algorithmic innovations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature matrix X ∈ ℝ^{d×N} and an output matrix Y ∈ ℝ^{m×N} as input, where d can be ultra-high-dimensional (up to 30,000 in experiments) and m is the number of correlated outputs. It produces a sparse regression coefficient matrix W ∈ ℝ^{m×d} (with most columns zeroed out by the ARD mechanism), a sparse precision matrix Ω = V⁻¹ estimated via graphical lasso, and per-feature ARD hyperparameters α that determine feature inclusion. The key structural constraint is that the column covariance K of W is restricted to be diagonal, enabling per-feature sparsity control, while the row covariance V captures inter-output dependencies.

## Method

NARD places a matrix normal prior on W with a diagonal precision matrix K = diag(α₁,…,α_d) (the ARD mechanism) and an L₁-penalized graphical lasso on the output precision matrix, then alternates between Bayesian linear regression updates for W and graphical lasso updates for V via block coordinate descent. To reduce the O(d³) inversion cost, three variants are proposed: (1) Sequential NARD greedily adds/removes features using a closed-form α update derived from a rank-one decomposition of the marginal likelihood, reducing cost to O(m³+p³); (2) Surrogate NARD constructs a tight lower bound on the log marginal likelihood via a Lipschitz-based quadratic approximation that replaces the full S_xx inversion with a diagonal matrix (ρI + K), reducing cost to O(m³+d²); (3) Hybrid NARD combines sequential feature selection with surrogate-based updates, achieving O(m³+p²) per iteration where p ≪ d is the final active feature count.

## Results

On synthetic data (d=5000, m=1500, N=1500, sparsity p=0.1), NARD achieves TPR=0.9483 and FPR=0.0062 in 49 seconds, matching the TPR of Bayesian MCMC methods (HS-GHS: 0.9463, JRNS: 0.9485) while running 60×+ faster. The Hybrid NARD variant further reduces total time to 23 seconds with TPR=0.9471. On the A-shares stock dataset (d=60640, m=3032), Surrogate NARD completes in 5.5 hours versus 17 hours for MRCE, and Bayesian baselines (HS-GHS, JRNS) fail to finish within 4 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NARD clearly outperforms the frequency-based baselines MRCE and CAPME on both TPR (0.9483 vs. 0.9083 and 0.8972) and FPR (0.0062 vs. 0.0072 and 0.0124), with comparable or better runtime. Against the Bayesian MCMC baselines HS-GHS and JRNS, NARD achieves nearly identical TPR (0.9483 vs. 0.9463/0.9485) but with a modest FPR penalty (0.0062 vs. 0.0033/0.0037); the decisive advantage is computational: NARD completes in 49 seconds while both MCMC methods exceed 3000 seconds (>60× speedup). The Hybrid NARD variant further narrows the FPR gap while achieving the fastest runtime (23 s). On the large-scale A-shares dataset (d=60640), Surrogate NARD finishes in 5.5 hours versus 17 hours for MRCE, and HS-GHS/JRNS do not complete within 4 days, underscoring the scalability gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ARD/SBL framework (MacKay, 1992; Tipping, 2001) from single-output to multi-output regression by placing a matrix-normal ARD prior on the coefficient matrix W while simultaneously applying a graphical-lasso L1 penalty on the output precision matrix Ω. It builds directly on the joint mean-covariance estimation literature (MRCE, Rothman et al. 2010; CAPME, Cai et al. 2013) and the Bayesian MCMC approaches (HS-GHS, Li et al. 2021; JRNS, Samanta et al. 2022), but departs from them by using a deterministic BCD/EM-style optimization rather than sampling. The algorithmic contributions—sequential feature selection inspired by Tipping & Faul (2003) and a surrogate-function lower bound on the marginal likelihood—adapt known single-output SBL tricks to the multi-output setting where the precision matrix couples all outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for ultra-high-dimensional genomics and eQTL analysis, where d can exceed 20,000 features and MCMC-based baselines become intractable (the paper reports >3000s vs. 23s for Hybrid NARD). The O(m³ + p²) complexity of Hybrid NARD makes joint regression-plus-covariance estimation feasible on datasets that were previously out of reach for Bayesian methods. Limitations include the linear model assumption (kernel extension is only briefly demonstrated), noted numerical instability in Surrogate NARD on ill-conditioned real data, and the reliance on graphical lasso for the precision matrix step, which constrains the class of sparsity patterns. Open questions include sensitivity to the λ_glasso tuning, behavior when m is also very large, and whether the surrogate approximation degrades in quality as d grows relative to N.
