## Problem

LLMs lack an intrinsic mechanism to detect and correct errors in their own reasoning trajectories, and existing solutions either depend on expensive external verifiers or complex, unstable reinforcement learning that does not explicitly model intermediate-step verification. The paper asks whether a single LLM can be trained to both verify its own reasoning and refine it, without external feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251315/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text problem (math word problem or coding task) and produces a structured output: an initial reasoning trajectory followed by a verification token ([eos] to terminate or [refine] to continue), and if [refine] is emitted, a corrected reasoning path with a revised final answer. At inference, N candidate completions are sampled and aggregated via confidence-weighted majority voting, where each candidate's weight is the model's predicted probability of [eos] given that candidate, rather than a simple count.

## Method

ReVISE introduces a special [refine] token and trains the model via a two-stage curriculum, each stage using a combined SFT + DPO preference loss. In Stage 1, preference pairs are constructed from the model's own sampled outputs: correct answers pair [eos] as preferred over [refine], while incorrect answers invert this, teaching the model to self-verify. In Stage 2, the model is further trained to correct errors by constructing preference pairs where the positive sample is [refine] concatenated with the ground-truth reasoning (following an incorrect initial attempt) and the negative sample is [eos] after the same incorrect attempt, teaching when and how to refine. At test time, the model's [eos] probability serves as a verification confidence score that calibrates majority voting, enabling natural test-time scaling without external verifiers or RL.

## Results

On GSM8K with Llama-3.1-8B, ReVISE achieves 61.6% (Maj@1) and 69.2% (Maj@5), outperforming the strongest baseline RFT (58.9%/65.3%) by 2.7 and 3.9 points respectively. On MATH-500 with the same model, ReVISE reaches 33.6% (Maj@1) and 37.6% (Maj@5), surpassing RFT (30.8%/35.6%) by 2.8 and 2.0 points. On the coding benchmark MBPP (Llama-3.2-1B), ReVISE attains 33.1% Pass@1, exceeding SFT (30.0%) and STaR+ (30.7%). The confidence-aware sampling provides an additional ~4% gain over simple majority voting at Maj@5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReVISE consistently outperforms all baselines across both model sizes and both math benchmarks. Against the strongest baseline RFT, ReVISE gains +1.9% (Maj@1) and +4.2% (Maj@5) on GSM8K-1B, and +2.8%/+2.0% on MATH-500-8B. Compared to STaR+, the advantage is larger at Maj@5 (e.g., +2.9% on GSM8K-1B, +4.8% on GSM8K-8B), suggesting ReVISE's self-verification mechanism scales better with more samples. In the appendix, ReVISE also beats the DPO-only baseline by +5.5% on GSM8K-1B (28.1% vs 22.6%) and +2.6% on MATH-500-1B (13.4% vs 10.8%), and marginally outperforms the RL-based SCoRe on MATH-500 (23.2% vs 23.0% with Gemma-2-2B) while using 30× fewer generations. For verification quality, ReVISE's intrinsic verifier achieves 76.0% AUROC on GSM8K, surpassing the separately trained V-STaR verifier (69.5%). On instruction-tuned Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, all fine-tuning baselines (SFT, RFT) underperform zero-shot CoT, whereas ReVISE still improves over it (52.3% vs 48.6% on GSM8K), demonstrating robustness to catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReVISE builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its optimization backbone, but departs from prior self-correction work in two key ways. Rather than relying on external verifiers (Luo et al., 2024) or costly online RL (SCoRe, Kumar et al., 2024), it introduces a two-stage curriculum that first teaches the model to verify its own outputs via a special [refine] token, then to correct them, using preference pairs constructed from the model's own correct and incorrect generations. This contrasts with STaR/V-STaR, which iteratively fine-tune on self-generated solutions without explicit verification, and with Self-Refine, which performs verification only at inference time without training the model to do so.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× training efficiency gain over SCoRe while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes self-correction substantially more practical for resource-constrained settings, and the confidence-aware sampling provides a simple, model-internal mechanism for test-time scaling that avoids external verifiers. However, the verification AUROC of ~76% remains moderate, and the paper only evaluates on 1B–8B models for math and coding, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse reasoning domains. The observed slight degradation in verification accuracy during Stage 2 (catastrophic forgetting) and the limited exploration of iterative refinement (only 3 untrained rounds) are acknowledged gaps that future work should address.
