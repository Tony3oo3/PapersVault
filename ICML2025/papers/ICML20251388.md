## Problem

Existing SAM-based federated learning methods suffer from two critical bottlenecks: they require two backward passes per local iteration (doubling computation on resource-constrained edge devices), and they only minimize local sharpness rather than directly optimizing the flatness of the global model, which can deviate from the global objective under data heterogeneity and slow convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251388/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within a standard FL pipeline where clients receive the global model parameters w^t and an EMA model e^t (a weighted average of all past global updates) at each round. Local training uses the client's private dataset (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100/CINIC-10, or text sequences for AG News) to compute both the standard ERM loss and a KL-divergence-based trajectory loss between the EMA model's and local model's outputs. The final output is a class label, and the server aggregates local models via an ADMM-corrected averaging rule rather than plain FedAvg.

## Method

FedGMT introduces a global model trajectory loss that measures sharpness of the global objective by computing the KL divergence between the outputs of an EMA model e^t (which encodes all historical pseudo-gradients with exponential decay) and the current local model, requiring only one extra forward pass and no extra backward pass (1.33× vs. 2× computation of FedSAM). To prevent data heterogeneity from biasing this global sharpness measure, the method formulates an ADMM-augmented Lagrangian that penalizes the constraint w_m^t = w^t, yielding a dual variable that corrects local update directions and a modified server aggregation rule w^{t+1} = (1/N)Σw_{m,K}^t − βu^{t+1}. Theoretically, FedGMT achieves O(1/T) convergence under non-convex smooth losses with N× linear speedup in the last two terms. A communication-efficient variant, FedGMT-v2, approximates e^t using the previous active round's EMA, halving per-round communication at a slight accuracy cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (CNN, Path(2)), FedGMT achieves 78.04% test accuracy, outperforming FedSpeed (76.74%), FedSMOO (76.59%), and FedAvg (65.28%). On CINIC-10 (ViT, Path(2)), FedGMT reaches 52.67%, a substantial margin over FedSMOO (44.15%) and FedSpeed (42.98%). In terms of efficiency, FedGMT requires only 392 communication rounds and 40.38 minutes to reach 41% accuracy on CINIC-10, compared to 688 rounds / 149.30 min for FedSpeed and 692 / 167.69 for FedSMOO. On AG News (FastText, Path(2)), FedGMT attains 89.39% accuracy in 329 rounds / 33.72 min, versus 85.19% / 487 rounds / 73.86 min for FedSpeed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGMT outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against the strongest SAM-based competitors, FedSpeed and FedSMOO, FedGMT gains 1.3–1.5% on CIFAR-10 Path(2), 2.4–2.7% on CIFAR-100 p=10%, and 8.5–9.5% on CINIC-10 Path(2), while also reducing computation time by roughly 3.5× on CINIC-10 and 2× on AG News. FedLESAM-D, the most recent SAM variant, is competitive on AG News (85.37% vs. 89.39%) but lags significantly on vision tasks (40.96% vs. 52.67% on CINIC-10). FedSAM and FedAvg perform poorly under high heterogeneity (Path(2) and Dir(0.01)), with FedSAM barely exceeding FedAvg, confirming the paper's motivation that local-only sharpness minimization is insufficient. FedDyn and FedNTD, which use regularization/distillation rather than SAM, are consistently outperformed by FedGMT by 3–13% depending on the setting. The communication-efficient variant FedGMT-V2 retains nearly all of FedGMT's accuracy (within 0.1–0.7%) while halving the per-round communication cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-based FL line (FedSAM, FedSpeed, FedSMOO, FedLESAM) by replacing the local or single-step global perturbation with an EMA-weighted global model trajectory that aggregates pseudo-gradients from all previous rounds. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the sharpness measure uses KL divergence between the EMA model and local model outputs, requiring only one extra forward pass (1.33× cost vs. 2× for FedSAM), and (2) ADMM enforces local-global consistency to prevent the trajectory loss from being corrupted by client drift. The theoretical derivation connecting the trajectory loss to SAM's sharpness measure via Taylor expansion (Eq. 5→8) provides a principled bridge between the global objective and the local optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing SAM's computational overhead from 2× to 1.33× per local update while simultaneously addressing the misalignment between local and global sharpness — a combination that matters for resource-constrained edge devices in real FL deployments. The ablation revealing that the trajectory loss alone is ineffective under severe heterogeneity (Dir(0.01): 61.28% vs. 74.67% with ADMM) underscores that consistency enforcement is not optional but essential. Open questions include the sensitivity of the EMA coefficient α under high heterogeneity (the authors acknowledge needing an adaptive mechanism), whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks or larger-scale deployments, and whether the 1.5× communication cost of FedGMT (vs. 1× for FedGMT-v2) is acceptable in bandwidth-limited settings.
