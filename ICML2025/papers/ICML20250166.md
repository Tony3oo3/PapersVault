## Problem

Reference priors provide a principled 'objective' Bayesian prior that minimally influences posterior inference, but constructing them has always required access to the model's likelihood function. For complex simulation models with intractable likelihoods, no likelihood-free method existed for learning reference priors, leaving a gap in the simulation-based inference literature for objective Bayesian analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250166/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods take a simulator with implicit (intractable) likelihood, a parameter space Θ ⊆ ℝ^d, and a number n of iid output replications as input; the simulator may be differentiable or non-differentiable. The output is a learned reference prior parameterized as a normalizing flow (with sigmoid layers to enforce bounded parameter supports), and as a byproduct, either an amortized posterior density estimator (GED) or a density-ratio critic network (VLB) that enables simulation-based inference at no additional training cost.

## Method

The paper proposes two families of likelihood-free methods that approximate the reference prior by maximizing an estimate of the mutual information I_π(x_{1:n}, θ) between parameters and simulator outputs. The Generative Entropy Difference (GED) method estimates MI as a difference of entropies, using a normalizing flow for the prior and a conditional flow for the posterior, trained with a two-time-scale scheme where the posterior estimator updates faster. The Variational Lower Bound (VLB) methods use InfoNCE or SMILE objectives with a learned critic network, jointly optimizing the critic and the variational prior via stochastic gradient ascent. GED extends to non-differentiable simulators via score-function (REINFORCE-style) gradients, while VLB methods exploit pathwise gradients through differentiable simulators or surrogate gradients. A key practical advantage is that the networks trained during RP learning directly serve as SBI tools: GED's posterior flow samples posteriors in a single forward pass, while VLB's critic yields log-posterior estimates for MCMC.

## Results

On four tractable models with known reference priors, the proposed GED method achieves the best Wasserstein distance on the Gaussian Scale model (1.66 vs. 6.95 for the Numerical baseline), while InfoNCE and SMILE perform comparably to or better than the baseline on the Exponential, Triangular, and AR(1) models. On C2ST (lower is better), GED achieves the best scores on Gaussian (0.49) and Triangular (0.51), while InfoNCE leads on Exponential (0.58) and AR(1) (0.50). On the complex SLCP-D model where the true RP is unknown, the VLB methods (InfoNCE: 0.99, SMILE: 0.98) dramatically outperform both the Uniform prior (0.55) and GED (0.69) on the C2ST mutual-information proxy, confirming that learned RPs capture substantially more parameter-output dependence than default priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Numerical baseline from Berger et al. (2009), all three proposed methods (InfoNCE, SMILE, GED) outperform it on both Wasserstein and C2ST for the Exponential, Triangular, and AR(1) models; GED achieves the largest margin on the Gaussian Scale model (Wasserstein 1.66 vs. 6.95, a ~4x improvement). On the Gaussian model, InfoNCE marginally underperforms the Numerical baseline on Wasserstein (7.08 vs. 6.95), likely due to difficulty capturing the 1/θ asymptote with a proper density. On the complex SLCP-D model, the paper compares learned RPs against a Uniform prior using a C2ST mutual-information proxy: InfoNCE (0.99) and SMILE (0.98) substantially outperform Uniform (0.55) and GED (0.69), indicating VLB methods better capture the high-dimensional parameter-output dependence. For the SIR agent-based model, the paper demonstrates that SBI performed using the critic/posterior networks trained during RP learning produces posterior predictive samples comparable to separately trained NPE (Greenberg et al., 2019) and NRE (Miller et al., 2022) baselines, confirming that no additional training cost is incurred for downstream inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference prior literature (Bernardo 1979; Berger et al. 2009; Nalisnick & Smyth 2017) to the likelihood-free simulation-based inference setting, where all prior methods assumed access to a tractable likelihood function. It repurposes mutual information estimators from the representation learning literature (InfoNCE, SMILE, MINE) and the generative entropy difference framework from Pichler et al. (2022) as optimization objectives for learning variational reference priors via normalizing flows. The key conceptual departure is replacing the likelihood-based mutual information maximization of Nalisnick & Smyth (2017) with simulation-only MI estimation, enabling application to arbitrary black-box or differentiable simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that objective Bayesian inference (via reference priors) is feasible for simulation models without likelihood access, opening a path for 'default' priors in fields like epidemiology, economics, and robotics where simulators are the primary modeling tool. The practical bonus that SBI (posterior sampling) comes at no additional training cost once the RP is learned makes the approach immediately usable. Key limitations include the restriction to models without nuisance parameters, the difficulty of capturing improper/asymptotic RPs with proper generative models, and observed sensitivity to random seeds on challenging models like g-and-k. Future work on scaling to higher-dimensional parameter spaces, handling nuisance parameters, and extending to other classes of objective priors (e.g., intrinsic priors) would strengthen the contribution.
