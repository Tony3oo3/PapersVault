## Problem

Existing glycan machine learning methods model glycans as graphs of monosaccharide nodes, ignoring the atomic-level structures within each sugar unit that are critical determinants of properties like binding affinity. Directly applying small-molecule encoders to all-atom glycan graphs (hundreds of atoms vs. tens for small molecules) performs worse than monosaccharide-only models, leaving open how to effectively exploit atomic-level information in glycan representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252783/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an all-atom glycan structure as a heterogeneous graph containing heavy-atom nodes, monosaccharide nodes, and three edge types: covalent bonds (typed as single/double/triple/aromatic), atom-to-monosaccharide membership links, and glycosidic bonds (typed by linkage, e.g., alpha-1,6). The encoder produces a glycan-level representation via concatenation of mean and max pooling over monosaccharide nodes only (atom nodes are excluded to avoid redundancy from shared atomic substructures), which is then passed to a 2-layer MLP head for the specific downstream task (classification, regression, or interaction scoring).

## Method

GlycanAA performs hierarchical message passing in three sequential steps per block—atom-atom, atom-monosaccharide, then monosaccharide-monosaccharide—using relational graph convolution (RGConv) with per-relation weight matrices to capture typed covalent and glycosidic bonds from local to global scale. For self-supervised pre-training, PreGlycanAA introduces a multi-scale mask prediction objective: a fraction of atoms (ρ_a=0.45) and a fraction of monosaccharides (ρ_m=0.15) are masked, with the additional constraint that masking a monosaccharide also masks all its constituent atoms to prevent trivial recovery from characteristic atoms. The model is trained to recover masked node types via cross-entropy losses on separate MLP predictors, learning dependencies across both atomic and monosaccharide scales simultaneously on 40,781 curated glycans from GlyTouCan.

## Results

On the GlycanML benchmark (11 tasks), PreGlycanAA achieves the best weighted mean rank among all 16+ baselines, while GlycanAA ranks second. GlycanAA outperforms the best baseline on 10 out of 11 tasks, and PreGlycanAA further improves over GlycanAA on all 11 tasks. The evaluation uses Macro-F1 for taxonomy and glycosylation type prediction, AUPRC for immunogenicity, and Spearman's ρ for protein-glycan interaction prediction. In terms of computational cost, GlycanAA is ~22% slower and uses ~19% more memory than monosaccharide-level RGCN, a moderate overhead for the performance gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlycanAA outperforms all four monosaccharide-level sequence encoders (LSTM, ResNet, Transformer, Shallow CNN) and all nine monosaccharide-level graph encoders (GCN, GAT, MPNN, CompGCN, GIN, RGCN, GearNet, GearNet-Edge, ProNet) on 10 out of 11 tasks, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating atomic-level information. Critically, GlycanAA also surpasses the four state-of-the-art all-atom small-molecule encoders (Graphormer, GraphGPS, Uni-Mol+, VabsNet), confirming that architectures designed for molecules with tens of atoms are ineffective for glycans with hundreds of atoms. The All-Atom RGCN baseline (applying RGCN directly to the full atom graph without hierarchical structure) underperforms even the monosaccharide-level RGCN, highlighting that naive all-atom modeling without hierarchical message passing is counterproductive. PreRGCN (RGCN pre-trained with a similar mask prediction scheme) is outperformed by GlycanAA, showing that the hierarchical graph structure is essential for the pre-training to be effective. PreGlycanAA further outperforms GlycanAA on all 11 tasks, and also clearly beats the alternative pre-training strategies (GlycanAA-Attribute and GlycanAA-Context from Hu et al., 2019), validating the multi-scale mask prediction design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of glycan graph modeling (Burkholz et al., 2021; Lundstrøm et al., 2022; Carpenter et al., 2022) from monosaccharide-level to all-atom-level representation, using relational graph convolution (Schlichtkrull et al., 2018) as the core operator within a hierarchical message passing scheme inspired by hierarchical encoders for small molecules (Yu & Gao, 2022; Han et al., 2023) and proteins (Hermosilla et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023a). It departs from prior attempts to apply small molecule encoders (Graphormer, GraphGPS, Uni-Mol+) to glycans, which the paper shows to be ineffective due to the scale gap between tens-of-atoms molecules and hundreds-of-atoms glycans. The multi-scale mask prediction pre-training objective adapts the self-supervised paradigm established for molecules (Hu et al., 2019) and proteins (Elnaggar et al., 2021; Rives et al., 2021) to the unique two-level (atom + monosaccharide) structure of glycans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first all-atom glycan encoder that demonstrably outperforms both monosaccharide-level GNNs and repurposed small molecule encoders, filling a recognized gap where atomic-level structural information (critical for binding affinity and other properties) was previously inaccessible to glycan ML models. The moderate computational overhead (~22% slower, ~19% more memory) for consistent gains across 11 tasks makes the approach practically viable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger glycan databases, how it performs on 3D conformational information beyond 2D connectivity, and its utility in real-world applications like vaccine design and cancer biomarker discovery that the authors identify as future directions.
