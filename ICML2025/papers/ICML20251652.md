## Problem

When multiple binary labels (e.g., from different annotators or signal sources) are available for bipartite ranking, it is unclear whether loss aggregation (weighted sum of per-label AUCs) or label aggregation (AUC on a combined label) is preferable. Prior work lacked a theoretical characterization of the Bayes-optimal solutions for both approaches, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on which to use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251652/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instance features x ∈ ℝ^d together with K binary labels Y^(1),…,Y^(K) per instance, where labels are used only to construct the training objective (e.g., click and relevance in MSLR, mortgage and loan in Banking). The output is a single scalar score f(x) that synthesizes all K labels into one ranking, evaluated by per-label AUCs and their minimum. The key design choice is the aggregation mechanism: loss aggregation weights individual AUC objectives, while label aggregation first combines labels (e.g., via summation or product) and then optimizes a multipartite AUC with specified misranking costs.

## Method

The paper characterizes the Bayes-optimal scorers for both aggregation strategies. For loss aggregation, the optimal scorer is a weighted sum of class-probability functions η^(k)(x) with weights a_k/(π^(k)(1−π^(k))), where π^(k) is the marginal label prior. This prior-dependent weighting causes a 'label dictatorship' phenomenon: under deterministic labels, the ranking is entirely determined by whichever label has the larger α^(k) = a_k/(π^(k)(1−π^(k))), regardless of instance-level information. For label aggregation with summation ψ(Y^(1),…,Y^(K)) = Σ_k Y^(k) and ordinal costs c_{ȳȳ'} = 1(ȳ>ȳ')·|ȳ−ȳ'|, the optimal scorer is simply Σ_k η^(k)(x), which treats all labels symmetrically without hidden prior-dependencies. The paper proves Pareto-optimality for both (suitable) instantiations, shows that uniform-cost label aggregation can fail Pareto-optimality, and validates the dictatorship effect empirically on synthetic data and three real-world datasets (UCI Banking, HelpSteer, MSLR Web30k) using logistic/hinge surrogates.

## Results

On the MSLR Web30k dataset, label aggregation (LaA) achieves a Min AUC of 0.68 and a Diff AUC of 0.05, outperforming loss aggregation (LoA) which yields Min AUC 0.67 and Diff AUC 0.06. On the UCI Banking dataset (with 90% positive mortgage label), LaA achieves Min AUC 0.562 and Diff AUC 0.054, compared to LoA's Min AUC 0.555 and Diff AUC 0.071. Across all real-world datasets (Banking, HelpSteer, MSLR), label aggregation consistently produces a smaller gap between per-label AUCs, confirming the theoretical prediction that loss aggregation can exhibit 'label dictatorship' where one label dominates the ranking due to marginal skewness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two multi-label aggregation strategies—loss aggregation (weighted sum of per-label AUCs) and label aggregation (AUC on a combined ordinal label)—against single-label baselines that optimize only one objective. On MSLR, label aggregation Pareto-dominates loss aggregation with equal weights: it achieves a higher Min AUC (0.68 vs. 0.67) and a lower Diff AUC (0.05 vs. 0.06), indicating more balanced treatment of the Click and Relevance signals. On Banking, the gap is wider: LaA achieves Min AUC 0.562 vs. LoA's 0.555, and Diff AUC 0.054 vs. 0.071, with the advantage growing as label skewness increases. On HelpSteer, label aggregation yields the lowest per-label AUC difference across all five label-pair settings. The single-label baselines (optimizing AUC(Click) or AUC(Rel) alone) trivially maximize one objective but sacrifice the other, confirming that multi-label aggregation is necessary for balanced performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-objective learning-to-rank literature (which has primarily studied nDCG-based objectives and LambdaMART modifications) to the bipartite AUC setting, providing the first theoretical characterization of Bayes-optimal scorers for both loss aggregation (linear scalarization, building on Ruchte & Grabocka, 2021) and label aggregation (building on Svore et al., 2011; Carmel et al., 2020). The multipartite AUC theory of Uematsu & Lee (2015) is the key technical tool enabling the label aggregation analysis, while the standard bipartite AUC theory (Menon & Williamson, 2016) provides the single-label baseline. The paper departs from prior work by revealing that the two aggregation strategies, while both Pareto-optimal, have fundamentally different sensitivity to marginal label priors—a distinction not previously identified in the multi-objective ranking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'label dictatorship' finding provides practitioners with a concrete, theoretically-grounded reason to prefer label aggregation over loss aggregation when synthesizing multiple binary labels into a single ranking: even with uniform mixing weights, loss aggregation implicitly reweights labels by their marginal skew, potentially favoring one signal over another in ways unrelated to instance-level information content. This is particularly relevant in information retrieval, medical diagnosis, and recommendation systems where multiple label sources (e.g., relevance vs. engagement) must be balanced. Open questions include developing surrogate losses for these aggregation objectives, extending the analysis beyond AUC to metrics like nDCG, and studying whether directly optimizing the minimum per-label AUC (a fairness-inspired objective) outperforms both aggregation strategies.
