## Problem

Personalized federated learning methods require multiple communication rounds to iteratively refine local models, making them impractical in environments like LEO satellite constellations where satellites have limited contact windows and high mobility. Existing one-shot federated learning (OFL) methods produce a single global model rather than client-specific personalized models, and directly fine-tuning such a global model on local data fails under high data heterogeneity. The paper addresses how to deliver high-quality personalized models through only a single communication exchange between neighboring clients, without relying on a central server.

## Input / Output

Each client consumes its private local dataset of RGB images (350×350×3 satellite imagery for Wildfire/Hurricane, 32×32×3 for CIFAR/SVHN) plus up to Q=29 neighboring model parameter vectors received via one-shot inter-satellite links. The output is a personalized local model of the same architecture and size as the client's pre-existing model (not the K-times-larger ensemble), which produces class labels at inference. Notably, the method is fully peer-to-peer with no central server, and each pair of neighbors exchanges models at most once across the entire training horizon.

## Method

FOL is a four-stage distributed algorithm: (1) local pretraining, (2) one-shot collection of up to Q neighbor models, (3) model alignment via fine-tuning → alignment-aware structured pruning (using group-lasso regularization plus an L2 alignment term that retains filters coherent with the local model rather than merely locally important) → post-pruning fine-tuning, followed by top-K selection based on validation accuracy with cosine-similarity tie-breaking, and (4) regularization-based knowledge distillation where a weighted logit-level ensemble (teacher) is distilled into the local model (student) via KL divergence plus an L2 penalty anchoring the student to its pre-distillation parameters. The alignment-aware pruning is the key differentiator: it explicitly guides which filters to retain based on structural and parametric coherence with the target client's model, preventing both over-pruning of useful diverse features and excessive mismatch that would degrade ensemble quality. Two theoretical results are provided: a bound on the empirical risk discrepancy between student and teacher (Theorem 1), and a convergence guarantee for the distillation process under smoothness, strong convexity, and bounded stochastic gradient variance assumptions (Theorem 2).

## Results

On the Wildfire dataset (ψ=0.7), FOL (E=3) achieves 96.32% test accuracy on satellite #13, surpassing the best baseline Co-Boosting (90.31%) by 6.01%. Under high heterogeneity (ψ=0.1), FOL reaches 93.75% on Wildfire satellite #48, a 39.24% improvement over Co-Boosting (54.51%). On standard benchmarks, FOL (E=3) achieves 66.83% on CIFAR-10, 39.42% on CIFAR-100, and 82.85% on SVHN, outperforming the best baseline by 3.72%, 5.97%, and 9.27% respectively. The ensemble variant FOL-A consistently achieves the highest accuracy across all settings, reaching 97.99% on Wildfire and 98.20% on Hurricane at ψ=0.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOL consistently outperforms all three baselines (FedAvg, DENSE, Co-Boosting) across every dataset and heterogeneity level tested. FedAvg is the weakest baseline, achieving only 68.48% on Wildfire #48 and 67.82% on Hurricane #44 at ψ=0.7, trailing FOL by over 20 percentage points. DENSE and Co-Boosting, the two OFL-specific baselines, perform better than FedAvg but are still substantially outperformed: on Hurricane #44 (ψ=0.7), FOL reaches 95.81% versus Co-Boosting's 74.04% and DENSE's 73.05%, a gap of over 21%. Under extreme heterogeneity (ψ=0.1), the advantage widens dramatically—FOL achieves 93.75% on Wildfire #48 while Co-Boosting drops to 54.51% and DENSE to 52.08%, yielding the paper's headline 39.24% improvement. On CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, FOL maintains a consistent 3–9% lead over Co-Boosting, confirming generalizability beyond satellite imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOL carves new ground at the intersection of one-shot federated learning (OFL) and personalized federated learning (PFL), two lines of work that have developed largely in parallel. Prior OFL methods (DENSE, Co-Boosting, FedAvg) produce a single global model in one communication round, while PFL methods (FedBABU, Ditto, pFedMe) require multiple rounds to iteratively refine client-specific models. FOL departs from both by proposing a fully distributed, server-free paradigm where each client directly learns from up to Q neighbors via one-shot model exchange, using a novel four-stage alignment pipeline (fine-tuning, alignment-aware structured pruning with group-lasso regularization, post-pruning fine-tuning, and weighted ensemble distillation) to extract and integrate only the knowledge most relevant to its local data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored deployment gap: LEO satellite constellations, autonomous vehicle networks, and IoT devices where multi-round communication is infeasible and no central server exists. The 39.24% accuracy gain over FedAvg under extreme non-IID (ψ=0.1) highlights how poorly standard aggregation handles heterogeneous clients, though this figure is from a single satellite and the more typical improvements on CIFAR/SVHN are 3–9%. Key limitations include the memory overhead of storing Q=29 neighbor models locally, the computational cost of the multi-stage pruning pipeline per neighbor, and the evaluation on only a subset of 70 clients. Open questions include scalability to thousands of nodes, applicability to truly heterogeneous architectures (beyond same-architecture weight differences), and integration of formal privacy guarantees into the peer-to-peer exchange.
