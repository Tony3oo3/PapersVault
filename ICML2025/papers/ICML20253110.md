## Problem

Existing sequence parallelism methods (Megatron-LM, RingAttention, Megatron-SP, DeepSpeed-Ulysses) are 'embedded' approaches that shard along a single sequence dimension and are tightly coupled to specific module patterns, introducing substantial communication overhead. Multi-dimensional transformers (e.g., video generation models with separate temporal and spatial attention blocks) compute independently across multiple sequence dimensions, yet these methods cannot exploit this structure, resulting in unnecessary communication and limited scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a multi-dimensional sequence tensor X ∈ R^[B, S₁, S₂, …, S_K, C], where K sequence dimensions (e.g., temporal and spatial for video) are each processed by an independent transformer block. In experiments, the spatial dimension is fixed at 4096 tokens (from 1024×1024 video resolution after VAE and patch embedding) while the temporal dimension scales from 128 to 1024, yielding total sequence lengths of 0.5M–4M tokens. The output is the processed tensor with the same shape as the input, with each GPU holding only a 1/N shard of the active sequence dimension at any given stage.

## Method

DSP introduces three dynamic primitives—Switch (all-to-all, volume M/N), Split (no communication), and Gather (all-gather, volume M)—that reshard the sequence tensor between computation stages rather than within modules. The key mechanism is that after a transformer block completes along dimension i, a single all-to-all operation redistributes the tensor so the next block along dimension j receives its correct shard, completely decoupling parallelism from the module's internal logic. This reduces per-layer communication to 2M/N (two all-to-all operations for two 1D blocks) versus 8M for Megatron-LM/SP or 4M/N for DeepSpeed-Ulysses, and the volume decreases as N grows, enabling near-linear scaling. The approach is implemented as a high-level API requiring only four function calls, compatible with FSDP, Accelerate, DeepSpeed, and Megatron-LM frameworks, and is not constrained by the number of attention heads.

## Results

DSP achieves a per-layer communication volume of 2M/N, compared to 4M/N for DeepSpeed-Ulysses, 2M for Ring Attention, and 8M for both Megatron-LM and Megatron-SP. In end-to-end throughput on 128 H100 GPUs, DSP outperforms DeepSpeed-Ulysses by 32%–75% and other baselines by up to 10×. In weak scaling, DSP outperforms all baselines by more than 80.7% and maintains near-linear throughput (only 15% loss from 8 to 64 GPUs), while DeepSpeed-Ulysses and Megatron-SP are limited by the number of attention heads. Measured communication latency for an 8M-length sequence is 914 ms for DSP versus 5,828 ms (DeepSpeed-Ulysses), 8,412 ms (Megatron-LM), and 51,908 ms (Megatron-SP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSP outperforms all four baselines on every metric reported. Against DeepSpeed-Ulysses (the closest competitor), DSP halves the per-device communication volume (2M/N vs. 4M/N) and achieves 32%–75% higher end-to-end throughput; in weak scaling the gap widens to over 80.7%. Against Ring Attention, DSP's communication volume scales down with N (2M/N vs. fixed 2M), giving a growing advantage as the cluster grows. Against Megatron-LM and Megatron-SP, both of which require 8M per-device communication (via all-reduce or all-gather/reduce-scatter), DSP reduces communication by a factor of 4N and avoids the attention-head-count limitation that causes those methods to run out of memory at 4M sequence length. In measured latency (Table 9, 8M sequence), DSP's 914 ms is roughly 6× faster than DeepSpeed-Ulysses, 9× faster than Megatron-LM, and 57× faster than Megatron-SP. DSP is the only method that scales to 64 GPUs without being constrained by the number of attention heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established family of 'embedded' sequence parallelism methods (Megatron-LM, Megatron-SP, Ring Attention, DeepSpeed-Ulysses), all of which shard along a single fixed sequence dimension and require module-specific communication patterns. DSP reframes sequence parallelism as a dynamic, stage-level abstraction: rather than embedding parallelism logic inside attention or FFN modules, it switches the sharding dimension between computation stages via all-to-all resharding, exploiting the fact that multi-dimensional transformers process each sequence dimension independently. Conceptually, it repurposes the all-to-all primitive already used by DeepSpeed-Ulysses but applies it at a coarser granularity (between blocks rather than within attention), eliminating the need for head-dimension splitting and the associated constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners training or serving multi-dimensional transformers (video generation, 3D processing, protein structure), DSP offers a drop-in, framework-agnostic solution that reduces communication volume by 50–75% and improves throughput by up to 10× over existing methods, with the attractive property that per-device communication shrinks as the cluster grows (2M/N). The main limitation is scope: DSP is designed for multi-dimensional transformers and does not directly apply to single-sequence-dimension models like standard LLMs. Open questions include whether automated strategies can determine optimal switching points in heterogeneous architectures, and whether the approach extends to non-attention modules (Mamba, RWKV) in practice beyond the compatibility claim.
