## Problem

Safety alignment of LLMs often causes over-refusal, where models reject safe prompts that should be answered, but this issue lacks a large-scale, systematic benchmark. The only prior test suite, XSTest, contains just 250 hand-crafted prompts that have become too easy for modern models like Llama-3-70b, and there is no automated pipeline to generate over-refusal prompts at scale across diverse harmful categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250444/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes toxic seed prompts (generated per-category by Mixtral 8*7B) as input and produces three datasets: OR-Bench-80K (80,000 safe prompts across 10 categories like violence, privacy, hate, sexual), OR-Bench-Hard-1K (1,000 prompts rejected by at least 3 of the largest recent models), and OR-Bench-Toxic (600 toxic prompts for safety validation). Evaluation outputs are per-category and overall rejection rates on safe prompts (over-refusal) and acceptance rates on toxic prompts (safety), measured via keyword matching on the full 80K set and GPT-4-as-judge on the Hard-1K and Toxic subsets.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages: (1) toxic seed generation using Mixtral 8*7B (chosen for its fewer safety restrictions compared to GPT-4), producing 2,000 seeds per category; (2) rewriting each toxic seed into 5 borderline-safe prompts using few-shot prompting with Mixtral 8*7B, with explicit instructions to avoid fictional framing and safety keywords like 'legal' or 'safe'; (3) LLM ensemble moderation using GPT-4-turbo, Llama-3-70b, and Gemini-1.5-pro with chain-of-thought reasoning and majority voting to filter out remaining toxic prompts. The ensemble moderator achieves 93% accuracy (vs. 94% for human experts), and a secondary check using Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (without safety moderation) rescues prompts that were over-flagged due to keyword sensitivity. The key design insight is that rewriting toxic prompts into safe-but-topically-adjacent versions naturally produces prompts near the safety decision boundary where over-refusal occurs, enabling scalable generation without manual curation.

## Results

On OR-Bench-Hard-1K, over-refusal rates range dramatically across 32 models: Claude-2.1 rejects 99.8% of safe prompts (worst), while Llama-3.1-70B rejects only 3.0% (best). On OR-Bench-Toxic, the same Claude-2.1 accepts 0.0% of toxic prompts (safest), whereas Mistral-large-latest accepts 27.2% (least safe). The Spearman's rank correlation between toxic-prompt rejection (safety) and safe-prompt rejection (over-refusal) is 0.89, indicating that most models simply trade over-refusal for safety rather than breaking the trade-off. GPT-3.5-turbo-0301 shows 57.4% over-refusal on Hard-1K, reduced to 12.7% in GPT-3.5-turbo-0125, but at the cost of accepting 37.9% of toxic prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary prior benchmark for over-refusal is XSTest (Röttger et al., 2023), which contains only 250 hand-crafted prompts. The paper demonstrates that XSTest is now too easy for state-of-the-art models like Llama-3-70B, which answers nearly all 250 prompts correctly, whereas OR-Bench-Hard-1K still yields rejection rates of 37.7% for Llama-3-70B and 91.0% for Claude-3-opus. For the toxic-prompt component, OR-Bench-Toxic is validated against AdvBench (Zou et al., 2023), achieving a Spearman's rank correlation of 0.991 in model rankings, confirming that the two benchmarks measure safety consistently. OR-Bench-Toxic shows higher absolute acceptance rates than AdvBench (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo-0125: 37.9% vs. 12.7%), attributed to greater diversity including sexual and privacy topics that AdvBench underrepresents. The paper also compares its LLM ensemble moderator (GPT-4 + Llama-3-70B + Gemini-1.5-pro) against human expert labeling, achieving 93.0% accuracy vs. 94.0% for experts, with the ensemble outperforming individual human workers on the moderation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the over-refusal evaluation task from the small-scale, hand-crafted XSTest (250 prompts) to a fully automated, large-scale pipeline that generates 80,000 prompts across 10 categories. Conceptually, it repurposes the prompt-rewriting techniques from jailbreak literature (transforming harmful prompts into benign-looking ones) and combines them with an LLM ensemble moderator (GPT-4, Llama-3-70B, Gemini-1.5-pro) for quality control, drawing on the LLM-as-judge paradigm from MT-Bench and related work. Unlike concurrent PHTest, which targets specific models, OR-Bench is model-agnostic and continuously updatable. The paper also positions itself against safety benchmarks (AdvBench, SorryBench, ToxicChat) that measure harmful-content rejection, arguing that over-refusal is a distinct and under-measured failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OR-Bench fills a critical gap: XSTest is now trivially solved by SOTA models like Llama-3-70B, leaving the field without a meaningful large-scale over-refusal test. The strong Spearman correlation (0.89) between toxic-prompt rejection and safe-prompt rejection reveals that current safety alignment is largely a zero-sum trade-off, with few models breaking the Pareto frontier. Open questions include whether the binary refusal framing is too coarse (the authors note 'refusal is a false binary'), whether LLM moderators introduce systematic blind spots, and whether the benchmark will remain challenging as models improve. The finding that jailbreak defense methods (SmoothLLM, ICL) significantly worsen over-refusal also raises the question of how to evaluate defenses holistically.
