## Problem

Fairness in sequential decision-making has been studied primarily through meritocratic criteria (reward-proportional selection) rather than through attribute-based counterfactual reasoning within a causal structure. The closest prior work (Huang et al., 2022b) required a known safe action set and used discrete non-parametric models whose regret scales exponentially in the parent set size. This paper is the first to study constrained logistic bandits with a counterfactual fairness constraint that is a priori unknown (depending on the unobserved reward parameter) and must be learned online, without assuming any prior safe decision subset.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes contextual features X_t = {a_t, w_t, m_t} (specified attribute, confounded features, intermediate features) drawn from a fixed distribution, along with a decision set D of 20 arms. The fairness threshold τ and a user-chosen tightness parameter ε (optional) are set a priori. The algorithm outputs a selected decision d_t per round and provides high-probability upper bounds on cumulative regret R_+(T) and cumulative constraint violations V(T). Unusually, the constraint itself—|Δ_{d_t}(X_t)| ≤ τ—is a mixture of logistic functions of the unknown parameter θ*, so the learner never directly observes whether a constraint is violated and must estimate feasibility from reward feedback alone.

## Method

The CCLB algorithm combines a convex confidence set for θ* (constructed via regret-to-confidence-set conversion from Lee et al., 2024, yielding radius O~(√(n log(St)+S))) with a primal-dual online optimization loop. In the primal step, the learner selects an optimistic parameter θ̃_t from the confidence set and picks the action maximizing g(f(Z_d)ᵀθ̃_t) − φ_t(|Δ̂_d(X_t)|−τ), where the penalty term accounts for estimated counterfactual fairness violations. In the dual step, φ_{t+1} is updated by projected gradient descent onto [0, ρ] with step size 1/η = ρ/√T. The counterfactual features Z'_d are computed via do-calculus interventions on the specified attribute, and because both factual and counterfactual features lie in the same RKHS feature space, the same logistic regret analysis applies to both. A tightness parameter ε added to the dual update (effectively tightening the constraint to τ−ε) allows trading a small regret increase (ρ√T(2ε+ε²)) for zero cumulative constraint violations when ε is chosen above a problem-dependent threshold ε'.

## Results

The CCLB algorithm achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\rho n S\sqrt{T} + \rho n^2 S^2 \kappa_Z + \rho\sqrt{T})$ and a cumulative constraint violation bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(n S\sqrt{T} + n^2 S^2 \kappa_Z + \sqrt{T})$, both sublinear in horizon $T$. The leading regret term $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\rho n S\sqrt{T})$ matches the order of the unconstrained logistic bandit bound (Lee et al., 2024) up to the truncation factor $\rho$. By introducing a user-chosen tightness parameter $\epsilon$, the Zero_CCLB variant achieves zero cumulative constraint violations with only a $(1+\epsilon)^2$ multiplicative increase in the $\rho\sqrt{T}$ regret term. Empirically on a synthetic SCM with 20 arms, CCLB's penalized cumulative regret grows sublinearly while GLM-UCB and OFULog+ show near-linear growth, and $\epsilon$-CCLB's penalized regret growth rate approaches zero by $T=10{,}000$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Safe UCB-GLM (Amani et al., 2020), CCLB removes the need for a priori known safe actions (which Safe UCB-GLM requires) and replaces the linear dependence on the worst-case constant $\kappa$ (which can be arbitrarily large) with the problem-dependent $\kappa_Z$ and truncation parameter $\rho$. Against OFULog-r (Abeille et al., 2021) and OFULog+ (Lee et al., 2024), CCLB adds constraint handling while preserving the same leading-order $nS\sqrt{T}$ regret scaling (up to $\rho$), whereas the unconstrained baselines have no constraint guarantees at all. Against F-UCB (Huang et al., 2022b), CCLB replaces the exponential-in-parent-set dependence $\sqrt{|W|T}$ with a polynomial $nS\sqrt{T}$ dependence by using a parametric logistic reward model, though the fairness constraint becomes a non-linear mixture of logistic functions. Empirically, CCLB substantially outperforms both GLM-UCB and OFULog+ on penalized cumulative regret (where constraint-violating actions earn zero reward), with the gap widening over longer horizons; OFULog+ achieves lower unpenalized regret since it ignores constraints entirely, and $\epsilon$-CCLB trades slightly higher regret for near-zero constraint violations at large $T$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) logistic bandits with improved confidence sets (Abeille et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2024), (2) causal bandits with counterfactual fairness (Huang et al., 2022b), and (3) constrained bandits via primal-dual optimization (Amani et al., 2020; Zhou & Ji, 2022). The key departure from Huang et al. is the use of parametric (logistic) rewards rather than non-parametric discrete distributions, which dramatically improves dimensional dependence but makes the fairness constraint a non-linear mixture of logistic functions. The key departure from Amani et al. is the removal of the requirement for a priori known safe actions, replacing hard per-round feasibility with a soft cumulative constraint bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to handle constrained logistic bandits with a non-linear (mixture-of-logistic) fairness constraint without requiring prior knowledge of safe decisions, which is a natural and important relaxation for practical fair decision-making where the reward parameter is unknown. The regret bound's leading term matching the unconstrained logistic bandit rate (up to a constant ρ) is a clean theoretical guarantee, and the user-chosen tightness parameter ε provides a practical knob to trade regret for zero cumulative violations. Open questions include extending to unobserved confounders, distribution shifts over time, and budget constraints, all of which are common in real deployment settings but absent here.
