## Problem

Data assimilation for nonlinear state space models produces highly non-Gaussian posteriors, yet operational methods (EnKF, ETKF) assume Gaussianity and exact sampling methods (particle filters) degrade in high dimensions. The paper addresses how to maintain computationally tractable Gaussian posteriors while faithfully representing the non-Gaussian structure induced by nonlinear dynamics and observation operators, without accumulating Monte Carlo sampling errors over time steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252349/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time series of noisy observations o_{1:T}, which may be 44×44 pixel images (Moving MNIST), 2D mass coordinates (double pendulum), or 40 grid-point values (Lorenz96). For linear dynamics, only observations are needed; for nonlinear dynamics, (z_t, o_t) pairs are required during training but z_t is never needed at inference. The output is a Gaussian posterior over the latent state h_t (mean and covariance computed analytically each step), from which physical variable samples z_t are drawn via a learned nonlinear emission model, yielding both point estimates and calibrated uncertainty quantification.

## Method

DBF introduces latent variables h_t with strictly linear dynamics h_{t+1} = Ah_t (a learned Koopman operator parameterized as block-diagonal 2×2 rotation-scaling blocks) and a Gaussian inverse observation operator r(h_t|o_t) = N(f_θ(o_t), G_θ(o_t)) implemented by neural networks. Because both the predict step (linear) and update step (Gaussian IOO) preserve Gaussianity, the filtered posterior q(h_t|o_{1:t}) is computed via closed-form recursive Kalman-style updates with no Monte Carlo sampling, unlike DVAEs that require the cascade trick. Physical variables are recovered through a nonlinear emission model p(z_t|h_t) = N(z_t; φ(h_t), R). Training maximizes a factorized ELBO over the Markov chain: for linear dynamics this is unsupervised (observations only), while for nonlinear dynamics it is supervised using (z_t, o_t) pairs. The linear constraint on A also stabilizes training by preventing the exploding/vanishing gradient issues inherent to RNN-based DVAEs, and the block-diagonal structure gives O(d_h) arithmetic cost per time step.

## Results

On the Lorenz96 benchmark (F=8, N=40), DBF achieves RMSE of 1.08±0.15 under nonlinear observations at σ=1, compared to 4.69±0.14 (EnKF) and 4.57±0.25 (ETKF), and maintains an advantage across all noise levels for nonlinear observations. On the double pendulum, DBF achieves ω RMSE of 0.36±0.04 at σ=0.5, outperforming EnKF (1.17±0.22), ETKF (2.70±1.25), and PF (2.36±2.29). On the two-body Moving MNIST task, DBF achieves a 100% success rate (50/50) with position RMSE of 0.39±0.027, while EnKF, ETKF, and PF all achieve 0% success. DBF also produces well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, with a Jeffreys divergence of 0.02 from a unit Gaussian for normalized errors, versus 10.2 for EnKF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBF clearly outperforms all sampling-based methods (PF, PFF) across every setting, with PF achieving RMSE of 2.80–6.05 on Lorenz96 versus DBF's 0.53–1.65. Against EnKF and ETKF, DBF wins decisively under nonlinear observations (e.g., 1.08 vs 4.69 at σ=1) and high-noise direct observations (1.16 vs 1.73 at σ=5), but loses slightly in the low-noise direct observation case (0.53 vs 0.31 at σ=1) where posteriors remain near-Gaussian. Among DVAE baselines, DBF substantially outperforms DKF (which fails to train in most Lorenz96 settings), VRNN, and SRNN, which all plateau near the uninformative prior RMSE (~3.67) on Lorenz96. KalmanNet is competitive in the easiest Lorenz96 setting (0.60 vs 0.53) but degrades rapidly with noise and fails entirely on the double pendulum. On the Moving MNIST task, DBF is the only method achieving a 100% success rate, while EnKF, ETKF, and PF all fail (0%), and KalmanNet cannot train due to the high observation dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DBF builds directly on the inverse observation operator (IOO) introduced by Frerix et al. (ICML 2021) for variational data assimilation, and on the block-diagonal complex-eigenvalue parametrization of the Koopman dynamics matrix from Lusch et al. (Nature Communications 2018). Conceptually, it positions itself as a 'Bayes-Faithful' subclass of dynamical VAEs (VRNN, SRNN, DKF) where the posterior architecture mirrors the Markov structure of the underlying SSM: the IOO governs only the update step and the linear latent dynamics govern only the predict step, enabling closed-form recursive computation without the Monte Carlo cascade sampling required by RNN-based DVAEs. It also extends the classical Kalman Filter to nonlinear observations by replacing the fixed linear observation model with a learned Gaussian IOO, and extends KF-based learned methods (KalmanNet, EKFNet) by operating in a Koopman latent space to handle nonlinear dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DBF addresses a core practical bottleneck in operational data assimilation: the inability of Gaussian-assumption filters (EnKF, ETKF) and particle-based filters (PF, PFF) to handle highly non-Gaussian posteriors arising from nonlinear observation operators or strong noise, without incurring prohibitive sampling costs. The analytical recursive update eliminates cumulative Monte Carlo error and the linear-dynamics constraint provides training stability that RNN-based DVAEs lack, making the approach more scalable to high-dimensional physical systems. Key limitations include the need for a training phase (requiring either synthetic SSM data, reanalysis pairs, or a pretrained Koopman operator) and underperformance relative to EnKF/ETKF when posteriors are nearly Gaussian (e.g., Lorenz96 with low-noise direct observations). Open questions include scaling to the full dimensionality of atmospheric models and whether the finite-dimensional Koopman embedding remains sufficient for long-horizon chaotic dynamics.
