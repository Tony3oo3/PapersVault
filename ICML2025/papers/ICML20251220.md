## Problem

Existing AI benchmarks evaluate knowledge recall or static problem-solving but do not test the full iterative scientific process of planning observations under budget constraints, collecting data adaptively, and reasoning over accumulating evidence. Gravity-Bench-v1 fills this gap by presenting an environment-based benchmark where agents must discover hidden physics within a dynamic gravitational simulation, including out-of-distribution scenarios (modified gravity laws, drag forces) that require generalization beyond memorized textbook knowledge.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language physics problem (e.g., 'determine the mass of the largest star') and interacts with the environment through an observe tool that returns star Cartesian positions (t, x, y, z) at agent-chosen times, plus a Python interpreter for computation. Under the budget-obs-100 protocol, the agent is limited to 100 total observations (max 10 per call) within a fixed time window, forcing strategic planning; under full-obs, the entire dense simulation table is pre-loaded. The agent outputs a numeric value (scored against task-specific error thresholds ranging from 5% to 70%) or a true/false answer, with correctness determined by comparison to ground-truth simulation parameters or derived quantities.

## Method

The environment is powered by Rebound, a machine-precision N-body integrator (WHFast for standard gravity, IAS15 for modified forces), simulating 16 two-body stellar systems over ~10 orbits with symmetry-breaking features like displaced centers of mass and proper motion. Six simulations introduce out-of-distribution physics: three with linear drag (acc = -v/τ) and three with modified gravitational exponent F_G ∝ r^-(2+α), requiring compositional generalization. The agent operates in a ReAct-style scaffold with the observe tool and a LangChain-based Python interpreter (numpy, scipy, pandas). Expert reference solutions are produced by domain experts using uniform temporal sampling of 100 observations as a planning-free baseline, and task-specific success thresholds are calibrated from the performance gap between full-data and 100-uniform-observation expert runs, ensuring thresholds reflect inherent task difficulty rather than arbitrary cutoffs. The benchmark comprises 50 tasks (47 numeric, 3 true/false) instantiated across 206 task-simulation pairs, with an open-ended solution space that admits diverse planning and reasoning strategies.

## Results

Under the budget-obs-100 setting (100 observations max, testing planning ability), o4-mini-high achieves the highest score at 49.4%±2.6%, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 21.5%±2.5%, Claude 3.5 Haiku at 16.1%±2.3%, GPT-4o at 15.5%±2.1%, and GPT-4o-mini at 8.3%±1.5%. Under full-obs (complete data access, 5% error threshold), o4-mini-high reaches 73.9%±2.4%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 39.5%±3.2%, GPT-4o 36.1%±3.2%, Claude 3.5 Haiku 34.1%±3.1%, and GPT-4o-mini 26.7%±2.8%. All models show a significant performance drop under the observation budget, with o4-mini-high losing ~24 percentage points and GPT-4o-mini losing ~18 points, indicating that observational planning remains a major challenge for current AI agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates five frontier LLM agents (o4-mini-high, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude 3.5 Haiku, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini) as baselines on 206 task-simulation pairs. o4-mini-high dominates both evaluation settings, scoring 49.4% under budget-obs-100 (more than 2× the next-best Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 21.5%) and 73.9% under full-obs (nearly 2× Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 39.5%). Under the observation budget, all models severely underutilize their allocation: GPT-4o uses only 12.2 observations on average out of 100, and even Claude 3.5 Sonnet uses only 24.3, suggesting a systematic failure to plan iterative refinement. On out-of-distribution tasks (modified gravity, drag forces), o4-mini-high is the only model to consistently solve multiple OOD tasks under full-obs, while Claude 3.5 Sonnet solves one modified gravity task and GPT-4o-mini solves one OOD task in only 1 of 3 runs. The performance gap between full-obs and budget-obs-100 is largest for o4-mini-high (−24.5 pp) and GPT-4o-mini (−18.4 pp), confirming that the planning and adaptive observation scheduling required by the budget setting is the primary bottleneck for all current agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of N-body simulation environments and LLM agent evaluation. Prior discovery benchmarks (DiscoveryBench, DiscoveryWorld, AI Feynman) typically provide pre-collected datasets for analysis or focus on symbolic regression and rediscovery of known phenomena, decoupling data acquisition from reasoning. Gravity-Bench departs by making observation planning a first-class challenge: agents must strategically schedule observations under a finite budget within a partially observable, rigorously simulated environment, and must generalize to out-of-distribution physics (modified gravity exponents, drag forces) absent from standard training data. The ReAct-style agent scaffold and Rebound simulation engine are repurposed from their original contexts (general tool-use, astrophysical computation) into a unified evaluation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical gap in current AI agent capabilities: even the strongest model (o4-mini) drops from 74% to 49% when forced to plan observations rather than receiving full data, and all tested models systematically underutilize their observation budgets (e.g., GPT-4o uses only 12 of 100 allowed observations on average). This exposes a fundamental limitation in long-horizon planning and answer verification that is prerequisite for autonomous scientific discovery. Limitations include the narrow 2D scope, absence of measurement noise, and small task set (206 pairs), though the authors outline clear extensions (3D dynamics, visual observations, electromagnetism, RL adaptation). The benchmark's open-ended solution space and OOD physics scenarios make it a useful probe for whether future models achieve genuine scientific generalization versus pattern matching.
