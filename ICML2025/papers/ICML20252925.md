## Problem

Existing deep learning-based time-coarsened dynamics methods are restricted to a single molecular domain (e.g., peptides or proteins) within a fixed chemical environment, lacking transferability across different molecular scales and conditions. Additionally, hand-crafted representations tailored to specific domains impair the ability to recognize unfamiliar molecules such as proteins with unnatural amino acids. UniSim addresses this by proposing the first unified, transferable generative model for full-atom time-coarsened dynamics spanning small molecules, peptides, and proteins.

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial molecular conformation (3D atomic positions and element types) as input and generates the conformation at a coarsened timestep τ (e.g., 10–100 ps) that is orders of magnitude larger than the classical MD integration step (~1 fs). Inference proceeds autoregressively: each generated state becomes the initial state for the next iteration, producing trajectories of arbitrary chain length. A force guidance kernel, trained per chemical environment (solvent, temperature, forcefield), is applied during sampling to incorporate physics constraints without retraining the backbone, enabling rapid adaptation to new conditions via parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Method

UniSim comprises three stages built on an SO(3)-equivariant TorchMD-NET backbone. First, a unified atomic representation model is pretrained on multi-domain 3D molecular data (small molecules, peptides, proteins) using a multi-head scheme that handles scale discrepancy via gradient-environment subgraphs (randomly sampling a local neighborhood for large molecules) and captures domain-specific atomic patterns via an atomic embedding expansion that maps each element to multiple discrete chemical environments. Second, a vector field model trained with the stochastic interpolant framework learns the push-forward from X_t to X_{t+τ} by jointly predicting a velocity field v and a denoiser η_z, with the pretrained encoder serving as a frozen graph encoder. Third, a force guidance kernel ζ, implemented as a separate TorchMD-NET with residual connections to the frozen encoder, fits an intermediate forcefield ∇ε_t that modulates the SDE sampling process, allowing the model to adapt to different chemical environments while keeping the backbone parameters frozen.

## Results

On the PepMD peptide benchmark (14 test peptides), UniSim achieves the best JS distances on PWD (0.328), RG (0.330), and TIC-2D (0.731), along with the highest validity (VAL-CA 0.575) and competitive contact map error (0.157). On the ATLAS protein benchmark (14 test monomers), UniSim outperforms all baselines across every metric, achieving PWD 0.506, RG 0.554, TIC 0.542, VAL-CA 0.079, and CONTACT 0.173. On small molecules (MD22), the force guidance kernel reduces TIC JS distance from 0.408 to 0.368 and TIC-2D from 0.791 to 0.765. UniSim also achieves ~25× higher effective-sample-size per second compared to OpenMM MD on peptides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSim outperforms FBM (the closest competitor using a similar stochastic interpolant framework) on PWD (0.328 vs 0.361), RG (0.330 vs 0.411), TIC-2D (0.731 vs 0.736), and VAL-CA (0.575 vs 0.539), though FBM retains a slight edge on CONTACT (0.105 vs 0.157). Against Timewarp, UniSim achieves substantially better validity (VAL-CA 0.575 vs 0.028) and lower PWD/RG distances, while Timewarp's augmented normalizing flow approach struggles with structural validity. ITO achieves the best single TIC score (0.495) but is outperformed by UniSim on all other metrics, particularly PWD (0.328 vs 0.367) and VAL-CA (0.575 vs 0.160). Score Dynamics (SD) is clearly the weakest baseline, with PWD and RG JS distances roughly double those of UniSim (0.727/0.776 vs 0.328/0.330), reflecting its reliance on collective variables rather than full-atom dynamics. On the ATLAS protein benchmark, UniSim dominates all four baselines across every metric, with the largest margins over SD (VAL-CA 0.079 vs 0.001) and ITO (CONTACT 0.173 vs 0.428).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSim builds directly on the stochastic interpolant generative framework (Albergo et al., 2023) and the force-guided bridge matching approach of FBM (Yu et al., 2024), which established time-coarsened dynamics for peptides. The key departure is the introduction of a unified pretraining stage over multi-domain 3D molecular data (small molecules, peptides, proteins) using novel techniques—gradient-environment subgraphs to bridge scale discrepancies and atomic embedding expansion to capture domain-specific structural patterns—inspired by multi-task pretraining in DPA-2 (Zhang et al., 2024). Unlike prior time-coarsened dynamics methods (Timewarp, ITO, Score Dynamics) that are confined to a single molecular domain and fixed environment, UniSim positions itself as the first transferable model that can adapt to new chemical environments via a lightweight force guidance kernel with frozen backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-domain transferability demonstrated here—where a single pretrained backbone handles small molecules, peptides, and proteins with only a small force guidance kernel trained per environment—offers a practical path toward a general-purpose molecular dynamics simulator, reducing the need to train domain-specific models from scratch. However, the authors acknowledge that cumulative prediction errors limit validity for macromolecules (protein VAL-CA is only ~8%), and evaluation trajectories remain relatively short, leaving open whether the model can faithfully capture rare transitions over longer timescales. The gradient-environment subgraph and atomic embedding expansion techniques are broadly applicable to any multi-scale molecular representation learning task beyond dynamics simulation.
