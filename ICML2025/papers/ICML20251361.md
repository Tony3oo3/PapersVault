## Problem

Training-time preference optimization methods (RLHF, DPO) require gradient-based parameter updates and extensive retraining to align LLMs with human preferences, hindering rapid adaptation to evolving requirements. Existing inference-time alignment approaches rely on purely numerical reward scores to guide decoding, lacking interpretability. This paper asks whether an LLM can align with human preferences during inference—without any parameter updates—by interpreting reward signals as textual critiques and iteratively refining its own outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text query x, TPO first samples N candidate responses (default N=5) from a frozen policy model, scores each with a reward model (e.g., FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM-v0.1), and selects the highest- and lowest-scoring responses as the 'chosen' and 'rejected' pair. Over D iterations (default D=2 for benchmarks, up to 5 for analysis), the model generates textual critiques, improvement suggestions, and new candidate responses, caching all scored outputs. The final output is the single highest-scoring response from the accumulated cache, with a maximum context length of 4,096 tokens during the optimization phase to accommodate the growing critique history.

## Method

TPO translates the gradient descent loop into a purely textual framework with four components: variable definition (the model response v), loss calculation (a prompt P_loss that asks the LLM to critique the chosen vs. rejected response, producing a 'textual loss'), gradient computation (a prompt P_grad that converts the textual loss into concrete improvement suggestions, i.e., a 'textual gradient'), and variable optimization (a prompt P_update that generates N new responses incorporating the suggestions). The key insight is that the LLM's innate instruction-following and reasoning capabilities allow it to interpret numerical reward feedback as actionable textual critiques, effectively performing on-policy, on-the-fly alignment without touching model weights. Built on the TextGrad framework, TPO customizes only the loss prompt for preference optimization while reusing TextGrad's gradient and update prompts. Ablations confirm that both iterative depth (sequential revision) and contrastive pair selection (highest vs. lowest scoring) are essential: removing either component degrades performance, and TPO-D2-N5 outperforms Best-of-N with 30 or 60 samples by 65.2% and 57.5% win-rates respectively.

## Results

With only 2 TPO iterations and 5 samples per step (D2-N5), the unaligned Llama-3.1-70B-SFT achieves 70.5% WR on Arena-Hard, 71.2% on MATH-500, and 90.4% on XSTest, surpassing the RLHF-aligned Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (59.0%, 66.4%, 88.7%) on nearly all benchmarks. Under the ultra setting (D5-N20), the SFT model reaches 77.5% Arena-Hard WR and 71.8% MATH-500, exceeding Instruct on every metric. For aligned models, TPO lifts Mistral-Small-Instruct-2409 (22B) to 53.4% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 72.2% WR on Arena-Hard, approaching GPT-4-Turbo-level performance. TPO-D2-N5 (15 total samples) also outperforms Best-of-N with 30 or 60 samples, achieving 65.2% and 57.5% win-rates respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DPO baseline (Llama-3.1-70B-DPO, trained on 64k UltraFeedback pairs), TPO-SFT wins on every benchmark: Arena-Hard 70.5% vs. 50.4%, MATH-500 71.2% vs. 63.4%, and XSTest 90.4% vs. 89.8%. Against the stronger RLHF-aligned Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, TPO-SFT (D2-N5) surpasses it on Arena-Hard (70.5% vs. 59.0%), MATH-500 (71.2% vs. 66.4%), XSTest (90.4% vs. 88.7%), and HH-RLHF (0.1 vs. -0.5), but trails on AlpacaEval 2 LC (33.0% vs. 36.9%); the ultra setting (D5-N20) closes this gap (37.8% vs. 36.9%). Applied to the smaller Mistral-Small-Instruct-2409, TPO yields large absolute gains (e.g., Arena-Hard 53.8% → 72.2%, MATH-500 57.6% → 62.2%), confirming benefits for aligned models as well. Finally, TPO-D2-N5 (15 total generated responses) outperforms Best-of-N sampling with 30 or 60 responses, achieving 65.2% and 57.5% GPT-4-judged win-rates, demonstrating that iterative depth is more sample-efficient than pure breadth.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPO builds directly on the TextGrad framework, which formalizes textual 'gradient descent' by replacing numerical gradients with LLM-generated critiques. It departs from the dominant training-time preference optimization paradigm (RLHF, DPO, SimPO, KTO) by fixing model parameters and instead searching for an optimal contextual parameter φ at inference time. Among inference-time alignment methods, TPO distinguishes itself from Best-of-N sampling, tree-search approaches (TreeBoN, MCTS), and token/segment-level reward-guided decoding by performing iterative, whole-response revision driven by the policy model's own interpretation of reward-model feedback as textual loss and gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that two TPO iterations on an unaligned 70B SFT model surpass its RLHF-aligned Instruct counterpart on nearly all benchmarks—while consuming roughly 0.01% of the FLOPs required for DPO training—suggests a practical, lightweight alternative to expensive post-training pipelines. Key limitations include the dependence on strong instruction-following capability (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct fails under TPO) and reliance on a reward model as a proxy for human preferences, which may not capture all alignment dimensions. Open questions include whether fine-tuning smaller models specifically for TPO's textual loss/gradient tasks would extend the method's applicability, and how TPO behaves when the reward model itself is misaligned or adversarial.
