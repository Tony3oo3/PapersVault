## Problem

Existing brain-signal research focuses on reconstructing images from fMRI data, but no prior work explores using brain signals as one of multiple contexts for blended image generation alongside image and text prompts. This task introduces three unresolved challenges: extracting stable cross-subject brain embeddings under severe data scarcity, integrating implicit brain representations with image/text modalities without corrupting the diffusion latent space, and preserving each context's semantics during multi-modal fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image context (256×256 from MS-COCO), fMRI voxel data from a human subject (13,000–16,000 voxels normalized to 8,192 via adaptive max pooling), and an optional text prompt as inputs, and produces a single blended image that naturally integrates semantics from all provided contexts without requiring masks or multiple views. Generation runs in approximately 6 minutes on a single A100 GPU, enabling real-time single-image creation. For triplet contexts (image + brain + text), the image and brain embeddings are combined via linear interpolation to fit within the Versatile Diffusion model's capacity, while the text prompt is fed through a separate text flow.

## Method

MindCustomer builds on Versatile Diffusion and introduces three key mechanisms. First, an Image-Brain Translator (IBT), a lightweight 3-layer MLP trained with MSE loss to generate pseudo fMRI voxels from CLIP image embeddings, augments cross-subject training data so that a shared brain embedder (shallow subject-wise + deep shared MLP layers) can be trained with SoftCLIP and MSE losses to map voxels into CLIP latent space, with a ClipCap mapper providing additional semantic supervision. Second, to bridge the cross-modal semantic gap, the IBT transfers the image context into brain-embedding space before fine-tuning the diffusion model on this transferred representation, and then the frozen diffusion model is used to lightly optimize the brain context embedding toward the image context semantics, resolving semantic conflicts. Third, the final generation concatenates (dual context) or linearly interpolates (triplet context) the transferred image embedding and optimized brain embedding, feeding the result into the image flow of the fine-tuned VD while the text prompt drives the text flow. For new subjects, a Reset-Tuning strategy trains only the shallow embedder with as little as 10–20% of the subject's data while freezing the deep shared embedder.

## Results

On the NSD test set, MindCustomer achieves CLIP-I 0.563, DINOv2 0.654, and CLIP-IQA 0.892, outperforming both baselines on all three metrics. In a user study (220 ratings from 22 participants), MindCustomer scores 2.618/3 on generation quality and 1.868/3 on context consistency, versus 1.868 and 1.675 for the best baseline. For brain decoding (Table 7), MindCustomer achieves competitive results (SSIM 0.289, CLIP 94.5%) but trails UMBRAE on low-level metrics (PixCorr 0.157 vs. 0.283, SSIM 0.289 vs. 0.328) while matching MindBridge on high-level Inception (92.1% vs. 92.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindCustomer outperforms both custom baselines on all three generation metrics. Against Baseline-1 (which replaces the brain context with its original visual stimulus image for multi-image blending), MindCustomer gains +0.004 on CLIP-I, +0.024 on DINOv2, and a substantial +0.158 on CLIP-IQA, indicating significantly better perceptual quality. Against Baseline-2 (which uses MindEye to reconstruct the brain signal into an image before blending), the margins are larger: +0.037 CLIP-I, +0.042 DINOv2, and +0.062 CLIP-IQA, showing that direct brain-embedding fusion preserves more semantic content than a two-stage reconstruction-then-blend pipeline. In the user study, MindCustomer is preferred over Baseline-1 by a wide margin on both quality (2.618 vs. 1.868) and consistency (1.868 vs. 1.675). On the brain decoding sub-task (Table 7), MindCustomer is competitive but not dominant: UMBRAE leads on low-level reconstruction (PixCorr 0.283, SSIM 0.328) while MindBridge leads on high-level Inception (92.4%) and CLIP (94.7%); MindCustomer sits in between, suggesting its brain embedder is optimized for cross-modal fusion rather than pixel-accurate reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Versatile Diffusion (VD) as its generative backbone and adapts the ClipCap mapper as a semantic supervisor for brain embeddings, but its core contribution is a new fusion pipeline that sequentially fine-tunes the diffusion model on image contexts and then lightly optimizes brain embeddings against the frozen model to resolve cross-modal semantic conflicts. It departs from prior brain-decoding work (MindEye, UMBRAE, MindBridge), which treats fMRI-to-image as a single-modality reconstruction task, by repurposing brain signals as one of multiple co-equal contexts alongside image and text prompts. The Image-Brain Translator (IBT), a lightweight 3-layer MLP that simulates subject-specific fMRI from image inputs, is a novel data-augmentation mechanism not present in prior cross-subject decoding frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first exploration of brain-signal-blended multi-context generation, MindCustomer establishes a proof-of-concept that fMRI-derived semantics can be meaningfully integrated with visual and textual prompts in a diffusion pipeline, opening a path toward brain-computer-interactive creative tools. However, the practical impact is currently constrained by the need for 7-Tesla fMRI acquisition, the coarse granularity of brain signals relative to text or image prompts, and evaluation on a single dataset (NSD) with only four subjects. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or more diverse neural data, whether the simple linear-interpolation embedding fusion can be replaced by richer mechanisms (e.g., cross-attention), and whether the few-shot generalization to new subjects holds beyond the NSD protocol.
