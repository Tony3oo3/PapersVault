## Problem

Existing Domain Generalization (DG) methods rely on single-valued predictors, which inherently limit worst-case robustness across unseen domains and often force sub-optimal performance by discarding domain-specific information. No prior work formulates a learning objective that simultaneously guarantees per-domain, per-label recall on unseen domains while minimizing the size of the prediction set, leaving a gap between robustness guarantees and practical utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes features from multiple training domains—RGB image patches (Camelyon, FMoW, iWildCam) or BERT-embedded text (Amazon reviews)—and outputs a subset of the finite label space Y for each test instance. The prediction set size is a first-class optimization target: the goal is not to maximize accuracy with a singleton but to hit a predefined per-label recall threshold (e.g., 90%) in every domain while keeping the average set size as small as possible, making the output both informative and concise.

## Method

SET-COVER formulates a per-label constrained optimization: minimize the average prediction set size subject to per-domain, per-label recall constraints. The non-smooth 0-1 objective is relaxed into a differentiable surrogate using hinge losses, and the resulting Lagrangian is optimized via gradient descent on model parameters θ and periodic gradient ascent on Lagrange multipliers C that adaptively penalize domains/labels falling below the recall threshold. Theoretically, the paper introduces a multi-domain VC-dimension defined over the performance indicator 1_{L,γ}(h,e), proves that linear predictors have infinite VC-dimension without domain structure but finite VC-dimension under conditionally Gaussian domains with shared covariance structure, and derives sample complexity bounds of Θ(|Y|²(d+log(|Y|/δ))/ε²) training domains. Empirically, SET-COVER is compared against ERM, Robust Conformal, and two Pooling-CDFs variants on four WILDS benchmarks, consistently achieving comparable or better min-recall with significantly smaller prediction sets.

## Results

On the four WILDS benchmarks, SET-COVER achieves median min-recall of 0.96 (Camelyon), 0.91 (FMoW), 0.99 (iWildCam), and 1.0 (Amazon) while maintaining median average set sizes of 1.05, 1.10, 1.01, and 2.43 respectively. The key advantage over Robust Conformal is a substantially smaller prediction set size at comparable recall: e.g., on Amazon, SET-COVER achieves the same 1.0 recall with set size 2.43 versus 4.68 for Robust Conformal, and on iWildCam it matches 0.99 recall with set size 1.01 versus 2.00. On FMoW, SET-COVER also outperforms Robust Conformal on recall (0.91 vs. 0.89) and on the percentage of domains achieving ≥90% min-recall (72% vs. 43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SET-COVER is compared against four baselines: ERM (standard single-valued cross-entropy training), Robust Conformal (per-domain calibrated conformal thresholds), and two variants of Pooling CDFs (TrainC and CVC) from Dunn et al. (2018). Against Robust Conformal, SET-COVER achieves comparable or slightly lower median min-recall (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.98 on Camelyon) but with dramatically smaller prediction sets (1.05 vs. 1.79 on Camelyon; 2.43 vs. 4.68 on Amazon; 1.01 vs. 2.00 on iWildCam). On FMoW, SET-COVER actually surpasses Robust Conformal on both recall (0.91 vs. 0.89) and the fraction of domains meeting the 90% target (72% vs. 43%). Against the Pooling CDFs baselines, SET-COVER dominates on all three metrics across all four datasets, with the gap being most pronounced on FMoW (0.91 vs. 0.81–0.83 recall) and Amazon (set size 2.43 vs. 2.48–4.31). ERM, while producing singleton predictions (set size 1.0), fails to meet the 90% recall target in the majority of domains on FMoW (only 8% of domains) and Camelyon (63%), confirming that single-valued predictors are insufficient for worst-case domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—set-valued classification (Sadinle & Wasserman 2019, Wang & Qiao 2018), conformal prediction for distribution shift (Dunn et al. 2018, Gibbs & Candes 2021), and worst-case domain generalization (Eastwood et al. 2022)—into a unified framework where set-valued predictors are trained to meet per-domain recall thresholds while minimizing set size. It departs from prior set-valued DG work (Dunn et al. 2018) by targeting worst-case per-domain performance rather than average risk, and from single-valued DG methods by arguing that no single predictor can be optimal across all domains. The theoretical contribution introduces a hierarchical VC-dimension for meta-learning over domains, showing that standard linear hypotheses have infinite VC-dimension in the multi-domain set-valued setting without structural assumptions on the domain space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding (via hierarchical VC-dimension) for when set-valued domain generalization is learnable, with the notable negative result that unrestricted multi-domain settings are fundamentally harder than single-domain ones. Practically, SET-COVER offers a drop-in optimization procedure compatible with modern architectures that achieves near-singleton prediction sets while maintaining 90%+ per-domain recall on WILDS benchmarks. Key limitations include theoretical guarantees only for linear predictors under conditionally Gaussian domain assumptions, and the method's reliance on per-domain constraint enforcement that may not scale to very large numbers of domains. Open questions include whether the generalization theory extends to deep networks and whether the covariance-structure assumption is necessary.
