## Problem

Discrete graph diffusion models (e.g., DiGress) ensure valid graph topology at every denoising step but suffer from unbalanced dynamics: individual edit operations can abruptly perturb connectivity and clusterability, making the reverse process difficult to learn. Although graph topological information (e.g., spectrum) is naturally representable in continuous space, prior discrete-diffusion methods do not exploit this continuity to achieve smooth, stable topological transitions during generation.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes a clean discrete graph (one-hot adjacency matrix E ∈ R^{n×n×c_e} and node vector V ∈ R^{n×c_n}) and a sampled timestep t ∈ [0,1], producing a probabilistic adjacency matrix G^{θ_t} on the probability simplex as network input. At inference, starting from a prior (uniform or informative) probabilistic matrix, the model iteratively refines it over T steps and outputs a discrete graph with binary/categorical edge entries and node features. A key property is that T is arbitrary (continuous-time formulation), enabling 10× fewer steps than diffusion baselines while matching quality.

## Method

GraphBFN replaces the discrete latent of diffusion with a continuous count-based latent derived from sampling infinitely many times from a categorical noise distribution (via the central limit theorem), whose Bayesian aggregation parameter θ_t lives on the probability simplex. This yields a Probabilistic Adjacency Matrix P whose entries are edge-existence probabilities, allowing graph spectra (Laplacian eigenvalues) to be computed directly on intermediate states for smooth topological analysis and spectral conditioning. Training is simulation-free: the KL between sender and receiver distributions reduces to a weighted MSE between the true one-hot graph and the network's predicted categorical distribution (Eq. 12), optimized with a Graph Transformer. Three practical innovations are introduced: (1) Adaptive Flowback sampling that switches between vanilla BFN autoregressive sampling and direct Bayesian-flow resampling based on a prediction-change threshold ε to balance quality and diversity; (2) a generalized accuracy schedule α(t)=a+2(β(1)−a)t compatible with informative (low-entropy) priors; and (3) spectral-feature conditioning applied to the network's output distribution (with stop-gradient) to improve self-consistency on large graphs.

## Results

On the Planar dataset (|V|=64), GraphBFN achieves a V.U.N. score of 96.7%, substantially outperforming DiGress (75.0%), GBD (92.5%), and GruM (90.0%), while also attaining the best spectral MMD (0.0046) and orbit MMD (0.0002). On SBM (44≤|V|≤187), GraphBFN reaches 87.5% V.U.N., beating GruM (85.0%), GBD (75.0%), and DiGress (74.0%). On ZINC250k, GraphBFN achieves the best FCD (2.116) and validity (99.22%), and on QM9 with explicit hydrogen it matches or exceeds all baselines in molecule stability (94.7% Mol. Stab.) with only 50 sampling steps versus 500–1000 for diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphBFN consistently outperforms all discrete-diffusion baselines (DiGress, GruM, GBD) on both abstract graph datasets: on Planar it achieves 96.7% V.U.N. versus 75.0% (DiGress), 90.0% (GruM), and 92.5% (GBD); on SBM it reaches 87.5% versus 74.0%, 85.0%, and 75.0% respectively. On ZINC250k, GraphBFN attains the best FCD (2.116) and validity (99.22%), narrowly beating GBD (FCD 2.248, Valid 97.87%) and GruM (FCD 2.257, Valid 98.65%). On QM9 with implicit hydrogens, GraphBFN is competitive with GBD and GruM (FCD 0.101 vs. 0.093 and 0.108) but trails slightly on scaffold similarity (0.9386 vs. 0.9510 and 0.9449). On MOSES, GraphBFN surpasses DiGress in validity (88.5% vs. 85.7%), FCD (1.07 vs. 1.19), and SNN (0.59 vs. 0.52), though it reports lower novelty (89.0% vs. 95.0%), which the authors attribute to the learned distribution being very close to the training distribution. Compared to older baselines (SPECTRE, EDP-GNN, GDSS, MoFlow, GraphAF, GraphDF), GraphBFN dominates across nearly all metrics on every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphBFN adapts Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023) to discrete graph generation by replacing the discrete latent of prior discrete-diffusion methods (DiGress, GruM) with a continuous count-based latent projected onto the probability simplex, yielding a probabilistic adjacency matrix that smoothly interpolates between an uninformative prior and the data. This departs from both dequantization-based continuous diffusion (GDSS, EDP-GNN) and bounded-space beta diffusion (GBD) by enforcing simplex normalization across edge-type dimensions, which the authors argue is more natural for multi-class categorical edges. The spectral conditioning strategy builds on Martinkus et al. (2022) and Vignac et al. (2022) but applies it to the output distribution rather than the input, and the adaptive flowback sampling and generalized accuracy scheduling are novel practical contributions specific to the BFN framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× sampling efficiency (25–50 steps matching 500–1000 diffusion steps) is a practically significant gain for graph generation workloads, and the simplex-based representation offers a principled way to handle multi-class edge types that independent [0,1] models (e.g., GBD) cannot naturally express. The empirical correlation between spectral-gap smoothness and generation quality (Table 8) provides a diagnostic tool for understanding failure modes in discrete graph diffusion. Open questions include whether the smoothness advantage persists at much larger graph scales (beyond 200 nodes), whether the framework extends cleanly to 3D molecular or protein structures, and whether the adaptive flowback threshold can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
