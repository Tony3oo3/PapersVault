## Problem

Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for VideoQA produce answers through opaque, uncontrollable reasoning processes that cannot be traced or verified, making it impossible for users to identify where multi-step reasoning has gone astray. This lack of transparency is especially problematic for complex cognitive questions requiring compositional reasoning over multiple temporal visual cues, where incorrect intermediate steps silently propagate to wrong final answers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a video clip (336×336 pixels, 16 uniformly sampled frames) and a text question as input, and produces a final answer alongside a hierarchical language-centric logical tree and a fully traceable reasoning path. The tree's leaf nodes are simple perceptual questions answerable by the underlying MLLM, while internal nodes represent progressively more complex cognitive sub-questions. The framework is training-free and model-agnostic, wrapping around any existing MLLM (VideoLLaMA3, VideoChat2, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) without modifying its weights.

## Method

LTR is a two-stage divide-and-conquer framework. In the top-down Divide stage, the MLLM recursively decomposes the original question into sub-questions using RAG-assisted few-shot exemplars retrieved from AGQA-Decomp (256-dim embeddings via OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, cosine similarity), while a recursive perceptual checking step determines when sub-questions are simple enough to serve as leaf nodes. In the bottom-up Conquer stage, the framework first answers all leaf perceptual questions, then performs video-aided logical reasoning at each parent node (incorporating visual evidence to correct potential decomposition errors), and finally applies in-process answer verification that checks both logical consistency and visual coherence, triggering re-reasoning if conflicts are detected. The key design insight is that video content serves a dual role: guiding the hierarchical decomposition in Stage 1 and providing grounding plus error tolerance during Stage 2 reasoning, making the system more robust than rigid program-execution approaches like Neural Modular Networks.

## Results

On AGQA-Decomp, LTR consistently improves all four baseline MLLMs by +3.5–3.8% on main-question accuracy and +6.0–6.9% on sub-question accuracy, with the largest gains in compositional consistency (cF1 up +10.4–12.4%). On Causal-VidQA, Qwen2-VL+LTR reaches 70.2% Acc@All (vs. 68.0% baseline), and on NExT-QA, VideoLLaMA3+LTR achieves 84.8% Acc@All (vs. 83.0%). Improvements are most pronounced on cognitive reasoning sub-tasks (counterfactual, prediction, causal) at 2.4–4.2%, compared to 0.9–2.1% on descriptive/explanatory tasks, confirming the framework's strength in complex logical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTR is applied as a training-free plug-in on top of four MLLMs (VideoLLaMA3, VideoChat2, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) and consistently improves each by 3.5–3.8% on main-question accuracy and 6.0–6.9% on sub-question accuracy across AGQA-Decomp. On Causal-VidQA, Qwen2-VL+LTR (70.2% Acc@All) surpasses VoT (69.4%), the strongest interpretable baseline, while VideoLLaMA3+LTR (66.6%) also exceeds VoT's 69.4% on the counterfactual sub-task (47.5% vs. 58.6% for VoT on Acc@C). On NExT-QA, VideoLLaMA3+LTR (84.8% Acc@All) outperforms VoT (76.0%) by 8.8 points, and VideoChat2+LTR (81.4%) beats VoT by 5.4 points. On MVBench, LTR yields the largest gains on reasoning-intensive sub-tasks (e.g., counterfactual inference +5.0–7.5%, episodic reasoning +5.5–7.0%) versus modest gains on perceptual tasks (object existence +0–1.0%). The framework also substantially improves compositional consistency (cF1 +10–12%) over all baselines, a metric not addressed by prior interpretable methods like VoT or DSTN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the question-decomposition idea from Neural Modular Networks (NMNs) and AGQA-Decomp into a language-centric logical tree that guides existing MLLMs, rather than executing a predefined program. It departs from VoT, which reasons over fine-grained video representations without capturing the question's logical structure, and from DSTN/NMN-based approaches, which lack error tolerance because any program mistake is unrecoverable. The LTR framework is training-free and model-agnostic, using RAG-assisted recursive question division (top-down) followed by video-aided bottom-up tree reasoning, positioning itself as a soft, interpretable reasoning layer on top of any off-the-shelf MLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a real gap in VideoQA: existing MLLMs produce answers with opaque, uncontrollable reasoning paths, making errors hard to diagnose. By providing a fully traceable tree of sub-questions and intermediate answers, LTR enables users to pinpoint whether failures stem from perception or logic, which is valuable for both debugging and trust. Key open questions remain: performance on long videos is still constrained by the 16-frame sampling of baseline MLLMs, and the zero-shot dependency limits adaptability to domain-specific scenarios. The authors suggest integrating RL-based System-2 reasoning and dense video representations as future directions to close these gaps.
