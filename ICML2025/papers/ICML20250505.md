## Problem

While input-sparsity time discrepancy minimization was recently achieved for binary matrices (Jain et al., 2023), the general real-valued matrix case remained open: the best prior combinatorial algorithm (Larsen, 2023) required Õ(mn² + n³) time, which is far from input-sparsity time for sparse matrices. This paper asks whether input-sparsity time coloring is possible for arbitrary real-valued matrices, a question raised at the 2018 Discrepancy Theory workshop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250505/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a real-valued m×n matrix A (m ≥ n) and a failure probability δ, and outputs a binary coloring vector x ∈ {-1,+1}^n satisfying disc(A,x) = O(herdisc(A)·log n·log^1.5 m) with probability 1−δ. The runtime depends on nnz(A) (the number of non-zero entries) rather than the full matrix size mn, making it optimal for tall sparse matrices where m = poly(n). The algorithm is randomized and purely combinatorial in its base form, with an optional fast-matrix-multiplication variant for subcubic runtime on square matrices.

## Method

The algorithm has two main components. First, a fast hereditary-projection subroutine replaces Larsen's explicit computation of B_t = A(I−V^T V) with a JL-sketch-based norm estimation and an implicit leverage-score sampling procedure (IMPLICITLEVERAGESCORE) that approximates the top-k SVD of B_t^T B_t without ever materializing B_t, reducing the projection step to Õ(nnz(A) + n^ω) time. Second, a 'guess-and-correct' data structure with lazy batch updates and low-rank corrections implements the iterative Edge-Walk partial-coloring loop: Gaussian projections are precomputed in batches of size K = n^a, and as the subspace V_t changes adaptively, corrections are applied via at most K rank-1 updates, amortizing the per-iteration cost. A robustness analysis shows that constant-factor spectral approximations and approximate row-norm queries preserve correctness, and a concentration argument eliminates the need for the adaptive verification step that previously forced Ω(n³) time under the Online Matrix-Vector conjecture.

## Results

The paper achieves a combinatorial running time of Õ(nnz(A) + n³) for discrepancy minimization of real-valued m×n matrices, matching Larsen's (2023) approximation guarantee of O(herdisc(A)·log n·log^{1.5} m). With fast matrix multiplication (FMM), the runtime improves to Õ(nnz(A) + n^{2.53}), breaking the cubic barrier for square matrices. This is optimal for tall matrices where m = poly(n), as reading the input alone requires Ω(nnz(A)) time and computing the projection matrix requires Ω(n^ω) time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Larsen (2023), the paper's method achieves the same approximation guarantee O(herdisc(A)·log n·log^{1.5} m) but replaces the mn² term with nnz(A), yielding input-sparsity time for sparse matrices. The FMM-based variant further breaks the cubic barrier, improving from n³ to n^{2.53} for square matrices. Against Eldan & Singh (2018), the paper's purely combinatorial approach avoids the fundamental limitation of LP-based methods (which cannot achieve input-sparsity time for high-accuracy solvers) and is more practical due to the complexity of fast LP solvers. Compared to Bansal (2010), the improvement is dramatic: from mn^{4.5} (SDP-based) to nnz(A) + n³ (combinatorial), a reduction of over two orders of magnitude in the exponent for dense matrices. The paper also notes that even an ideal matrix multiplication exponent would only improve the FMM-based runtime to n^{2.5}, making the n^{2.53} result nearly optimal within this framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Larsen's (SODA 2023) combinatorial discrepancy-minimization algorithm, which itself reimplements the Edge-Walk partial-coloring subroutine of Lovett & Meka (2015). The key departure is replacing Larsen's explicit projection-matrix computations with implicit leverage-score sampling and a JL-sketch-based robust analysis, and circumventing the Online Matrix-Vector conjecture barrier via a 'guess-and-correct' data structure with lazy batch updates and low-rank corrections. It also builds on the input-sparsity-time result of Jain, Sah, and Sawhney (SODA 2023) for binary matrices, which the authors show does not generalize to real-valued entries due to the heavy-light decomposition's dependence on bounded entry magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first input-sparsity-time algorithm for discrepancy minimization on general real-valued matrices, resolving a major open problem from the 2018 Discrepancy Theory workshop and nearly closing the computational gap with the binary-matrix case. The subcubic runtime (n^2.53) for square matrices surpasses what LP-based approaches can achieve, suggesting combinatorial methods may be the right tool for this problem class. Limitations include the runtime's insensitivity to future matrix-multiplication improvements (the n^2.5 floor is inherent to the approach) and the fact that the n^ω term from explicitly computing the projection matrix remains unavoidable for real-valued inputs. The implicit leverage-score sampling and lazy-update data structure are flagged as potentially reusable tools for other iterative linear-algebra problems.
