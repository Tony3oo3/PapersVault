## Problem

Existing safety alignment methods (SFT, DPO, RLHF) teach LLMs to refuse harmful requests only at a superficial level, with safety signals implicitly embedded and easily diluted by competing objectives like tone and style. This leaves models vulnerable to adversarial and jailbreak attacks, yet prior work has offered no concrete architectural mechanism to achieve deeper, more robust safety alignment beyond data augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query concatenated with previously generated tokens as input, prepended by a special [CLS] token. It produces a standard text response alongside a binary safety classification (benign or malicious) read from the [CLS] token's output. During generation, the [CLS] token is dynamically re-inferred at selective steps (governed by an annealing schedule) to reassess the full context, and the decoding strategy can insert guiding refusal phrases with chain-of-thought justification when the classification flips to malicious for τ consecutive steps.

## Method

The paper introduces a BERT-inspired [CLS] token at the start of every sequence whose output is trained via a binary cross-entropy loss (with small coefficients λ₁=0.01, λ₂=0.1/0.01) to classify the input and generated context as benign or malicious, explicitly separating the safety signal from the next-token prediction objective. Two deployment mechanisms exploit this signal: (1) a Strategic Attention Mechanism that lets the [CLS] hidden state influence generation through dynamic attention rules—attending to query + initial r₁ tokens if initially malicious, the latest r₂ tokens if initially benign, or a window around a transition point Sₜ upon a benign-to-malicious flip; and (2) a Strategic Decoding Strategy that explicitly reads the classification label and inserts guiding refusal tokens (e.g., 'Sorry, I cannot fulfill your request because…') when the malicious label persists for τ≤3 consecutive steps, triggering a chain-of-thought justification. Attention is carefully isolated so that other tokens never attend to [CLS], preserving the original causal generation logic, and the [CLS] token skips auxiliary tokens (PAD, BOS, instruction prefixes). The entire pipeline adds less than 0.2× inference overhead via selective re-evaluation (annealing schedule) and achieves near-zero attack success rates across GCG, AutoDAN, DeepInception, Prefill, and decoding-parameter attacks on Llama2-7B and Mistral-7B.

## Results

The primary metric is Attack Success Rate (ASR, lower is better). Llama2-7B-CLS achieves near-zero ASR across most attack categories: 0.4% on AdvBench Prefill (vs. 92.7% for SFT and 39.62% for RLHF), 0.0% on GCG (vs. 41.0% SFT, 28.0% RLHF), and 0.0% on decoding attacks (vs. 98% SFT, 83% RLHF). The only notable exception is DeepInception, where SFT-DPO achieves 0% while the proposed method records 2.0%. On utility benchmarks, the enhanced Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2-CLS retains competitive scores (MT-Bench 7.38, GSM8K 41.77, MMLU 58.20) while dramatically improving safety over the original Mistral model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama2-7B-Chat (RLHF, Touvron et al. 2023), the proposed Llama2-7B-CLS achieves up to two orders of magnitude lower ASR: 0.4% vs. 39.62% on AdvBench Prefill, 0.0% vs. 28.0% on GCG, and 0.0% vs. 83% on decoding attacks. Against the state-of-the-art data augmentation method of Qi et al. 2024 (Llama2-7B-Chat-Aug), the proposed method wins on Prefill (0.9% vs. 2.8% at 5 tokens) and decoding attacks (0.0% vs. 11.3% on HEx-PHI), though both methods perform well on simpler attacks. In the cross-family comparison (Table 3), the enhanced Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2-CLS surpasses Llama2-7B-Chat on safety (e.g., 0.4% vs. 39.62% Prefill, 0.0% vs. 26.8% GCG) while retaining Mistral's superior utility (MT-Bench 7.38 vs. 6.32, GSM8K 41.77 vs. 22.97). The sole exception where the proposed method does not achieve the best result is DeepInception, where SFT-DPO records 0% ASR versus 2.0% for the proposed method (a single failure case out of 520 queries).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Robust Safety Alignment Hypothesis (RSAH) from Li & Kim (2024), which argued that safety alignment should enable models to re-evaluate reasoning direction at each generation step, by introducing a concrete mechanism: a BERT-style [CLS] token performing explicit binary safety classification. It departs from the data-augmentation approaches of Qi et al. (2024) and Yuan et al. (2024), which address superficiality by diversifying training examples but do not fundamentally change how safety signals are encoded. The key conceptual departure is making the safety decision an explicit, separable classification objective rather than an implicit byproduct of next-token prediction, combined with strategic attention and decoding rules that dynamically re-assess safety mid-generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, low-overhead (<0.2×) recipe for strengthening safety alignment that is compatible with existing SFT/DPO/RLHF pipelines and can be applied as a post-alignment enhancement to already-deployed models. However, validation is limited to 7B-parameter models (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B) and text-only inputs, and the pretraining-phase integration yielded only marginal gains, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or multimodal settings. A key open question is whether the explicit binary signal remains sufficient as adversarial attacks grow more sophisticated (e.g., multi-turn social engineering), and whether the approach can be integrated into production inference stacks without the added latency of periodic re-classification.
