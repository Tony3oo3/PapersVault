## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs determine channel and head importance by aggregating individual element scores (e.g., L2 norms or Taylor expansions), which ignores the interdependence among elements within a sub-module. Additionally, most methods apply a uniform pruning ratio across all layers, failing to account for varying redundancy at different depths, and lack a lightweight mechanism to recover performance loss after pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250807/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B, LLaMA-2-7B) and 32 calibration text samples from BookCorpus with sequence length 128 to compute importance scores and linear regression coefficients. It produces a pruned model with reduced attention heads and FFN channels, along with adjusted output matrix weights (A·W_O + B) that compensate for pruning-induced output shifts. No gradient computation is required; the entire pruning pipeline is post-training and operates on a single GPU.

## Method

SlimLLM introduces two holistic importance criteria: for MHA, it scores each head by the negative Pearson similarity between the full output and the output excluding that head's contribution, then applies a greedy search to find optimal head combinations that maximize output similarity; for FFN, it projects the down-projection matrix into a PCA-derived feature space of the output activations and computes channel importance as the L2 norm of eigenvalue-weighted projections, capturing both vector direction and magnitude. A simple per-dimension linear regression (O_i = A_i·O_i^pruned + B_i) is fitted on the calibration set to rescale and shift the output matrix, recovering performance at negligible cost. Finally, a non-uniform pruning ratio is assigned per layer via softmax of the expected cosine similarity between each layer's input and output, with the first and last layers skipped.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% pruning ratio with fine-tuning, SlimLLM achieves an average Commonsense Reasoning score of 62.41 (98.7% retention of the original 63.25), outperforming LoRAP (61.70) by 0.7%. At the more aggressive 50% pruning ratio with fine-tuning, SlimLLM reaches 53.16 average, surpassing LoRAP (52.17) by ~1% and LLM-Pruner (48.69) by 4.5%. On LLaMA2-7B at 20% pruning with tuning, SlimLLM scores 61.70 vs. LoRAP's 61.24. Inference latency at 50% pruning drops from 0.3008s to 0.1034s for prefill and reduces decoding latency by 28.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlimLLM consistently outperforms all three baselines (LLM-Pruner, LoRAPrune, and LoRAP) on LLaMA-7B across both 20% and 50% pruning ratios, both with and without fine-tuning. At 20% pruning with tuning, SlimLLM leads LoRAP by 0.7% in average score (62.41 vs. 61.70) and by a wider margin on individual datasets like BoolQ (74.71 vs. 72.94) and ARC-e (66.96 vs. 64.31). At 50% pruning without tuning, the gap widens substantially: SlimLLM achieves 50.10 average vs. LoRAP's 47.25 (+2.85%) and LLM-Pruner's 42.13 (+7.97%), with WikiText2 PPL of 37.89 vs. LoRAP's 56.96. On LLaMA2-7B, SlimLLM edges out LoRAP at 20% pruning (61.70 vs. 61.24 with tuning) and at 50% without tuning (46.93 vs. 46.09), though LoRAP slightly wins at 50% with tuning (52.31 vs. 52.02), which the authors attribute to differences in fine-tuning strategy alignment. LLM-Pruner and LoRAPrune are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing SlimLLM by 3–8% in average score at 50% pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlimLLM extends the structured pruning line initiated by LLM-Pruner and refined by LoRAPrune and LoRAP, but departs from the dominant paradigm of aggregating per-element importance scores (magnitude × activation norm) to evaluate sub-modules holistically. For attention heads, it replaces element-wise aggregation with Pearson similarity between the full output and the output minus a single head's contribution, plus a greedy search over head combinations. For FFN channels, it introduces a PCA-based feature space that captures both direction and magnitude of channel weight vectors, building on LoRAP's group importance formulation. The non-uniform layer pruning ratio draws on ShortGPT's observation that layer redundancy varies with depth, and the linear regression recovery is a simpler alternative to the gradient-based or LoRA-based fine-tuning used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal lies in the extremely low-cost performance recovery: a first-order linear fit per output dimension replaces expensive gradient-based tuning, making the entire pruning pipeline nearly gradient-free. The ablation showing that non-uniform layer pruning contributes roughly 8 percentage points (the largest single component) reinforces that where you prune matters as much as what you prune. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the LLaMA/Vicuna family, the linear regression being applicable only when output dimensions are preserved (so it cannot be used on the down-projection of FFN), and the greedy head search being omitted for FFN channels due to computational cost. Open questions include whether the PCA-based feature space importance generalizes to non-Transformer architectures and whether the linear regression trick can be extended to other parameter matrices.
