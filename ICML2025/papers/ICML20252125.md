## Problem

Transformers and state-space models (SSMs) are constrained to TC⁰ circuit complexity, meaning they cannot recognize all regular languages or simulate arbitrary finite state machines, limiting their ability to track state across sequences. Non-linear RNNs overcome this expressivity gap but require fully sequential processing, making large-scale parallel training infeasible. The paper asks how much sequential computation is actually needed to close the state-tracking gap while retaining most of the parallelism that makes modern sequence models scalable.

## Input / Output

The models consume token sequences (up to 2048 tokens for pretraining, 256 for synthetic word-problem tasks) and produce next-token probability distributions autoregressively. A key operational detail is the dual evaluation mode: during training, all tokens in a sequence are self-iterated simultaneously (exploiting the parallelism of the Mamba2 or Llama backbone), while during generation the model processes tokens sequentially with constant memory, passing only converged hidden states forward. The self-iteration depth is adaptive per token, bounded by a convergence threshold (ε=0.05) or a hard cap (24 for SSMs, 32 for transformers).

## Method

The paper proposes implicit SSMs (and implicit transformers) that inject the model's own output z_t back into the state-space recurrence, so that the transition matrix Λ(z_t, x_t) and input function u(z_t, x_t) depend on a self-iterated variable. In the fixed-point limit, this yields a non-linear, non-diagonal state-to-state transition (proven via the implicit function theorem), effectively making the model an RNN while retaining the parallelizable structure of the underlying SSM. Training uses a two-stage curriculum: a bounded phase with 4 self-iterations and 1 phantom gradient step (covering 80% of training), followed by a free phase with 24/32 self-iterations and 4 phantom gradient steps. The phantom gradient method (Geng et al., 2021) backpropagates through only a fixed number of iteration steps, decoupling memory cost from the number of forward iterations and enabling constant-memory training regardless of how many self-iterations are needed for convergence.

## Results

At 1.3B parameters trained on 207B D-PILE tokens, the implicit Llama (32+4) achieves a D-PILE perplexity of 7.24 and average downstream accuracy of 58.9%, while the implicit Mamba2 (24+4) reaches 7.70 perplexity and 56.2% average accuracy. Both implicit models consistently outperform their explicit counterparts (Llama†: 7.99 PL / 55.9%; Mamba2*: 8.28 PL / 55.3%) on perplexity and most downstream tasks. On the S₅ word problem, implicit Mamba2 generalizes to sequences of 128 tokens (trained on 32), whereas explicit Mamba2 fails beyond its training length. On CATBABI, a single-layer implicit Mamba2 outperforms a single-layer explicit Mamba2 on most of the 20 reasoning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-data Mamba2* baseline (207B D-PILE tokens), the implicit Mamba2 (24+4) reduces D-PILE perplexity from 8.28 to 7.70 and improves average downstream accuracy from 55.3% to 56.2%, with the largest gains on HellaSwag (+3.3 pp) and ARC-E (+2.8 pp); however, it underperforms on OpenBookQA (0.589 vs 0.612). Against the original Mamba2 trained on 300B PILE tokens, the implicit model still wins on perplexity (7.70 vs 8.40) and HellaSwag (0.627 vs 0.600) despite 30% less training data. The implicit Llama (32+4) shows an even larger margin over Llama† (same 207B tokens): perplexity drops from 7.99 to 7.24 and average accuracy rises from 55.9% to 58.9%, with consistent gains across all seven downstream tasks (e.g., HellaSwag +6.5 pp, ARC-C +3.8 pp). Notably, the 760M implicit Llama (32+4) is competitive with the 1.3B explicit Llama†, suggesting the implicit formulation provides a meaningful parameter-efficiency advantage. On the synthetic S₅ word problem, explicit Mamba2 requires 16 layers for length-32 sequences and fails to generalize beyond that length, while a single-layer implicit Mamba2 generalizes to length 128 via additional test-time self-iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three existing threads: Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019) for implicit fixed-point function definition, Mamba2/transformer backbones for parallelizable sequence processing, and phantom gradients (Geng et al., 2021) for constant-memory backpropagation through the fixed-point iteration. It departs from Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2019) and PonderNet, which also use self-iteration but require unrolled backpropagation or fixed-depth halting, by showing that the DEQ formulation applied to SSMs yields non-linear, non-diagonal state transitions equivalent to RNNs (Theorem 1). The theoretical grounding draws on circuit complexity results (Merrill et al., 2022, 2024) establishing that explicit SSMs and transformers are confined to TC⁰, while the practical curriculum (bounded then free self-iterations) is a novel training strategy not present in prior implicit-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean theoretical and empirical resolution of the parallelization-expressivity tradeoff for sequence models: implicit SSMs provably implement RNN-like transitions while retaining most of the parallelism of explicit SSMs, with only ~8 self-iterations needed at training time versus 256 sequential steps for an RNN. Scaling to 1.3B parameters on 207B tokens and demonstrating 97.6% token-match between simultaneous (training) and sequential (generation) modes makes this practically viable. Open questions include whether the 2–4× wall-clock overhead is justified for natural language (where 'hard' tokens are sparse), whether emerging neuromorphic hardware can amortize the fixed-point cost, and whether the modest benchmark gains over explicit baselines will widen as models scale further or as tasks demand more state tracking.
