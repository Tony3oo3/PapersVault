## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods model human preference through a single reward function (explicit or implicit), which cannot adequately represent the multifaceted, potentially conflicting factors (e.g., helpfulness vs. safety) that underlie holistic preferences. These factors vary across tasks and populations, and their relative weights are difficult to specify by hand. The paper asks whether a unified framework can automatically infer these implicit preference factors and their prompt-specific combinations from holistic pairwise annotations, without pre-defined sub-rewards or hand-crafted combination weights.

## Input / Output

During training, LPC consumes preference triples (x, y_w, y_l) from UltraFeedback, where x is a text prompt and y_w/y_l are the preferred and less-preferred completions. The posterior network takes the concatenated last-layer hidden states of (x, y_w) and (x, y_l) to infer a categorical distribution over a discrete codebook of K codes (K=32–64 optimal); the prior network takes only the prompt hidden state h_x to predict the same distribution. At inference, the prior samples a latent code z, and the policy model generates a text completion conditioned on both x and z, with z injected additively into hidden states before the LM head.

## Method

LPC introduces a discrete latent variable z over a learnable codebook E = {e_1, …, e_K}, where each code represents an implicit preference factor. A two-layer MLP posterior q(z|x, y_w≻y_l) infers the factor mixture from the observed preference pair, while a separate MLP prior p(z|x) predicts the mixture from the prompt alone; the two are coupled via a KL regularization term in the negative ELBO objective. The policy model π_θ(y|x,z) conditions generation on z by adding the Gumbel-softmax-weighted codebook embedding to hidden states before the language model head, with a linear schedule g∈[0,1] that shifts reliance from the posterior to the prior over training. The preference likelihood term in the ELBO can be instantiated as DPO, SimPO, or IPO, making LPC a drop-in extension of any offline RLHF objective. Implementation is lightweight: the policy, prior, and posterior share a single Transformer backbone, requiring only two forward passes per triple—identical to vanilla DPO.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback-based alignment setup, LPC consistently improves downstream benchmark averages across three base models and three alignment algorithms. For Mistral-7B, DPO w. LPC achieves 56.73 average (vs. 52.22 base), with gains of +5.6 on ARC-Challenge and +7.0 on TruthfulQA. For Llama3-8B, DPO w. LPC reaches 58.33 average (vs. 55.95 base), and for Llama3-8B-Instruct, SimPO w. LPC attains the highest average of 65.77 (vs. 64.24 base). Preference accuracy on UltraFeedback also improves by 1–2 points across all model–algorithm combinations (e.g., DPO on Mistral-7B: 71.9→73.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LPC-enhanced versions of DPO, IPO, and SimPO against their vanilla counterparts and the unaligned base models. DPO w. LPC is the most consistently effective combination, improving average scores by +4.5 (Mistral-7B), +2.4 (Llama3-8B), and +0.9 (Llama3-8B-Instruct) over base models, with the largest single-task gain being +7.0 on TruthfulQA for Mistral-7B. IPO w. LPC and SimPO w. LPC also outperform base models on average, and critically, they mitigate the performance deficits that vanilla IPO and SimPO exhibit on GSM8K (e.g., vanilla IPO drops below base on GSM8K for Mistral-7B, but IPO w. LPC partially recovers). On preference accuracy (Table 2), LPC improves all three methods by 1–2 points (e.g., DPO on Llama3-8B: 69.3→70.8; SimPO on Mistral-7B: 73.5→75.6). On AlpacaEval 2 (Table 4, Llama3-8B-Instruct), LPC lifts length-controlled win rates by +0.3 to +2.8 points (e.g., SimPO: 12.77→15.56 LC), with the largest gain for SimPO. The one area where LPC shows limited benefit is commonsense reasoning (ARC), where gains are modest (~1–3 points), suggesting its fine-grained preference modeling has less impact on capabilities shaped during pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPC extends the offline preference optimization lineage (DPO, SimPO, IPO) by introducing a variational inference layer with discrete latent codes that automatically discovers implicit preference factors from holistic pairwise annotations. It departs from multi-objective alignment approaches (reward model combination, policy interpolation, combination-aware learning) that require pre-defined sub-rewards and hand-crafted combination weights, instead learning both the factors and their prompt-specific importance end-to-end. The discrete codebook with Gumbel-softmax reparameterization draws from the VAE and discrete latent variable literature in NLG, but is the first application of this machinery to LLM preference alignment, distinguishing itself from concurrent work (Poddar et al., Yao et al.) that uses continuous latent variables or group-level distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPC offers a plug-and-play framework that can be integrated into any offline RLHF objective, reducing the need for fine-grained per-factor annotations that burden human annotators. The robustness to label noise (flipping experiment) and the T-SNE clustering by data source suggest the latent codes capture genuine preference structure, though the interpretability of individual codes remains limited. Key open questions include: whether the approach extends to online RLHF, whether it scales to larger models and more diverse training data beyond UltraFeedback, and whether the learned codes can be made more semantically interpretable. The modest but consistent gains across three base models and three alignment algorithms suggest the framework is sound but the ceiling for improvement may be constrained by the single-dataset evaluation.
