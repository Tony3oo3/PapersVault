## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods for dense prediction tasks fail to effectively integrate content and positional information at the patch level. Conventional contrastive learning applies fixed positional encodings regardless of crop location, discarding spatial context, while Masked Image Modeling masks only content embeddings, leaving positional information unexplored as a learning signal. This paper asks how to make positional encoding crop-aware and to jointly mask and predict both content and position embeddings to produce features that intertwine the two.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K at 480×480 resolution) that are patchified into N patches by a ViT backbone. A separate learnable vector field P ∈ R^{w×h×d} (default 50×50) provides positional embeddings sampled on a regular grid aligned to each crop's geometry. During pre-training, the output is a probability distribution over 65,536 learnable prototypes for both image-level [CLS] tokens and patch-level tokens; during fine-tuning, the ViT features feed into ViTDet (for COCO detection/segmentation) or a Segmenter linear decoder (for ADE20K semantic segmentation).

## Method

The method introduces two components on top of the DINOv2 self-distillation framework. First, positional encoding is represented as a smooth 2D vector field P that is cropped and interpolated in the same manner as the image, so each patch's position embedding reflects its true location within the original image rather than a fixed grid; a random scale-and-translation augmentation on P (scaling factor s ~ Beta(2,5)) prevents over-reliance on absolute positions and bridges the object-scale gap between ImageNet and COCO. Second, masked position prediction is added alongside standard MIM: position embeddings are independently masked with a [POSMASK] token using a cross-wise sampling pattern (rather than the box-wise pattern used for content), and the model must reconstruct the masked positions from the remaining content and position context. In practice, each image in a batch receives either content masking or position masking (50:50 split), and the total loss is L^image + L^content + λ_KoLeo·L^KoLeo + L^pos. At inference, standard ViT positional embeddings are used, requiring no architectural change.

## Results

On COCO object detection with ViT-B/16, the proposed method achieves 49.2 AP^Box and 43.8 AP^Mask using only 350 effective pre-training epochs, outperforming LOCA (48.3/43.0 at 600 epochs), MAE (48.1/43.2 at 1600 epochs), and DINOv2† (47.7/42.4 at 350 epochs). With ViT-S/16, it reaches 44.8 AP^Box and 39.8 AP^Mask, surpassing all baselines except FLSL (45.5/40.5), which the authors note has implementation issues in its official code. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the method achieves 48.4 mIoU (ViT-B/16), essentially matching LOCA's 48.5 while using fewer epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With ViT-B/16, the proposed method outperforms all 12 compared baselines on COCO detection: it beats LOCA by +0.9 AP^Box and +0.8 AP^Mask, MAE by +1.1 AP^Box and +0.6 AP^Mask, and DINOv2† (reproduced on ImageNet-1K) by +1.5 AP^Box and +1.4 AP^Mask, all while using only 350 effective epochs versus 600–1600 for those methods. With ViT-S/16, it surpasses every baseline except FLSL (45.5 vs. 44.8 AP^Box), which the authors flag as having bugs in its official implementation. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the method ties LOCA (48.4 vs. 48.5 mIoU for ViT-B/16) and slightly trails it, suggesting the positional encoding gains are most pronounced for instance-level tasks rather than pixel-level classification. Notably, DropPos underperforms MAE despite being designed for dense prediction, which the authors attribute to its aggressive 94% position mask ratio hindering visual pattern learning. The method also outperforms DINOv2-reg (52.2 AP^Box on LVD-142M) by a smaller margin when both are pre-trained on the same ImageNet-1K data, indicating the gap narrows with more pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINOv2/iBOT self-distillation framework (image-level contrastive loss + patch-level masked prediction) by fundamentally redefining how positional information enters the training loop. Unlike DropPos, which formulates position prediction as a 1D classification over patch indices, or LOCA, which predicts a query view's location via cross-attention, this work treats positional embeddings as a continuous vector field that is cropped alongside the image and then masked and predicted in the same embedding space as content. The position-and-scale augmentation (randomly shifting/scaling the positional field) is a novel mechanism to bridge the object-scale distribution gap between ImageNet and COCO, going beyond the fixed positional encodings used in all prior contrastive or MIM-based SSL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves the best COCO detection and instance segmentation results among ImageNet-1K-pretrained SSL methods with only 350 effective epochs (vs. 1600+ for iBOT/MAE), and the integration into DINOv2 requires only a few lines of additional loss computation, making it practically adoptable. However, the gains do not transfer to semantic segmentation (ADE20K), suggesting the benefits are specific to instance-level discrimination rather than general feature quality. Open questions include whether the vector-field positional encoding generalizes to non-ViT backbones, whether the 1.3× per-epoch training overhead is acceptable at scale, and whether the approach can be combined with the much larger LVD-142M pre-training regime used by official DINOv2 (where the gap to DINOv2-reg narrows to ~3 AP).
