## Problem

In federated heterogeneous distillation, the distillation temperature—a critical hyperparameter controlling knowledge transfer sharpness—is typically fixed or monotonically decreasing, failing to account for the compounded heterogeneity in both data distributions and model architectures across clients. The paper asks whether logits alone, as the minimal knowledge carrier, can effectively bridge model heterogeneity in FL without public datasets or additional information, and how to properly calibrate temperature to make this possible.

## Input / Output

Each client receives Z-score-standardized global logits (adjusted by per-category temperatures computed at the end of the previous round) and trains on local RGB images using heterogeneous CNN architectures (AlexNet, ShuffleNetV2, ResNet18, GoogleNet). The output is local logits (not model parameters) uploaded for aggregation, with the Z-score standardization bridging capacity gaps between heterogeneous models by normalizing logits to focus on relative class relations rather than absolute magnitudes. The framework supports three deployment modes—centralized FL, decentralized (DeL), and blockchain-FL—each with different communication topologies and validation protocols.

## Method

ReT-FHD introduces two dynamic temperature mechanisms on top of multi-level (intermediate-layer) knowledge distillation with Z-score-standardized softmax. First, Multi-level Elastic Temperature assigns each distillation layer a temperature τ_l = G_l(ΔZ_l) where ΔZ_l measures the Z-score divergence between the global logits and the next local layer's logits, using a logarithmic scaling function that increases temperature when inter-stage divergence is large (reducing knowledge density to promote learning from the teacher) and decreases it when divergence is small. Second, Category-Aware Global Temperature Scaling computes per-class temperatures  τ̃_c by modulating based on the deviation of each category's cross-entropy loss from the class mean, which theoretically (Theorem 4.3) balances gradient updates across categories to mitigate data heterogeneity. A Z-Score Guard mechanism, integrated with blockchain-based FL, validates uploaded logits against dynamic boundaries using Z-score coordinate analysis to detect and penalize label-flipping and model-poisoning attacks.

## Results

In the heterogeneous model FL setting (Table 1a), ReT-FHD achieves 72.76% on CIFAR-10 (Dir), 91.23% on CIFAR-10 (Pat), 30.77% on CIFAR-100 (Dir), 68.56% on CIFAR-100 (Pat), 33.16% on Flower102 (Dir), 72.91% on Flower102 (Pat), 17.24% on Tiny-ImageNet (Dir), and 42.35% on Tiny-ImageNet (Pat), outperforming all baselines by at least 2% across every setting. In the homogeneous model setting (Table 1b), ReT-FHD reaches 90.54% on CIFAR-10 (Dir) and 89.85% on CIFAR-10 (Pat). In the decentralized setting (Table 2), ReT-FHD achieves the highest accuracy on CIFAR-100 (64.11%) and is competitive on other datasets. Communication cost is minimal at 0.0003 MB upload / 0.0004 MB download per round, roughly 35,000× less than proxy-model methods (10.45 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReT-FHD consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Among proto-based methods, it surpasses FedTGP (the strongest proto baseline) by 6.01% on Flower102 (Dir) and 4.19% on CIFAR-10 (Dir); RethinkFL trails by 4.55% on Flower102 (Dir). Among proxy-model methods, the gap is even larger: ReT-FHD exceeds FedType by 9.63% on CIFAR-100 (Dir) and 14.05% on Flower102 (Dir), while HYDRA-FL lags by 19.03% on Flower102 (Pat) in the homogeneous setting. Among logits-based methods, FCCL (the closest competitor) is outperformed by 7.97% on CIFAR-10 (Dir) and 4.65% on CIFAR-100 (Dir). In the decentralized setting (Table 2), ReT-FHD achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-100 (64.11% vs. 60.12% for DeSA and 59.00% for L2C) but is slightly behind L2C on CIFAR-10 (88.80% vs. 90.14%). Under adversarial attacks (Table 7), ReT-FHD retains 38.75% on CIFAR-10, outperforming HYDRA-FL (36.94%), FCCL (31.18%), and FedDistill (20.75%), confirming that its multi-level elastic temperature strategy enhances robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated distillation paradigm initiated by FedMD (Li & Wang, 2019), which established logits as the minimal knowledge carrier for heterogeneous FL, and the multi-level distillation loss structure from HYDRA-FL (Khan et al., 2024) and One-for-All (Hao et al., 2024). It incorporates Z-score logit standardization from Sun et al. (2024) to bridge capacity gaps between heterogeneous architectures. The key departure is replacing the fixed or monotonically decreasing temperature schedules used in prior multi-level distillation with two dynamic mechanisms: a per-layer elastic temperature driven by inter-stage Z-score divergence, and a per-category global temperature scaling driven by cross-entropy disparity, both theoretically grounded in information entropy and gradient analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that logits alone—without proxy models, prototypes, or public datasets—can achieve competitive accuracy in heterogeneous FL while reducing communication by orders of magnitude (0.0003 MB vs. 10.45 MB for proxy-model methods), making it attractive for bandwidth-constrained edge deployments. The blockchain-integrated Z-Score Guard provides a lightweight security layer against label-flipping and model poisoning, though the claimed 44% attack mitigation rate in the abstract is not clearly substantiated in the reported experiments. Key open questions include compatibility with Transformer-based architectures (explicitly acknowledged as untested), the practical overhead of blockchain consensus in real deployments, and whether the theoretical guarantees (Theorems 4.1, 4.3) hold under more aggressive heterogeneity settings.
