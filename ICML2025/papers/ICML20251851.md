## Problem

Backpropagation requires storing full activation maps during the forward pass, creating a severe memory bottleneck that prevents practical on-device fine-tuning. While HOSVD-based activation compression (Nguyen et al., 2024) can reduce this memory, it incurs prohibitive per-step computational overhead from full tensor decomposition and lacks hard memory control, making it infeasible for resource-constrained edge devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251851/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (CNN or ViT), a user-specified memory budget B for activation storage across fine-tuned layers, and a small calibration batch for offline rank selection. It produces fine-tuned model weights alongside per-layer, per-mode low-rank projection matrices (U_{i,m}) that are updated via warm-start at each step. The rank selection is performed once offline using a recursive backtracking algorithm over a perplexity matrix, so the online training loop only requires a single subspace iteration per mode per layer.

## Method

ASI (Activation Subspace Iteration) replaces the expensive SVD step in HOSVD-based activation compression with a single subspace iteration using a warm-start initialization from the previous training step, justified by the short-term stability of activation maps under small parameter updates and Lipschitz-continuous nonlinearities. A novel activation perplexity measure—defined as the Frobenius norm difference between full-rank and compressed weight gradients—is evaluated at multiple explained variance thresholds to build a per-layer perplexity matrix. A recursive backtracking algorithm then selects the optimal per-mode truncation ranks for each fine-tuned layer under a strict memory budget constraint. During training, the compressed activations enable low-rank gradient computation in the backward pass, reducing both memory (up to 120×) and FLOPs (up to 1.86×) relative to vanilla training while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k fine-tuning (4 layers), ASI achieves up to 120.09× activation memory reduction and up to 1.86× fewer training FLOPs compared to vanilla training while maintaining comparable accuracy. For example, on MCUNet (4 layers), ASI uses 0.83 MB memory and 11.93 GFLOPs versus 19.52 MB and 19.88 GFLOPs for vanilla training, at 63.5% accuracy. On ResNet-18 (4 layers), ASI reaches 70.6% accuracy with 2.63 MB memory and 33.14 GFLOPs, outperforming HOSVD's 4048.56 GFLOPs by over 122×. On-device measurements on a Raspberry Pi 5 confirm ASI is 1.56× faster than vanilla training and 91.0× faster than HOSVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HOSVD (Nguyen et al., 2024), ASI achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy (e.g., 70.6% vs 70.5% on ResNet-18, 71.1% vs 71.1% on ResNet-34) while reducing FLOPs by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 33.14 vs 4048.56 GFLOPs on ResNet-18; 11.93 vs 2457.59 GFLOPs on MCUNet), a 252.65× reduction. Memory usage is nearly identical (0.83 vs 0.88 MB on MCUNet), confirming ASI matches HOSVD's compression ratio without the prohibitive decomposition cost. Against Gradient Filtering (Yang et al., 2023), ASI achieves substantially lower memory (0.83 vs 6.38 MB on MCUNet, 2.63 vs 7.00 MB on ResNet-18) and lower FLOPs (11.93 vs 19.89 on MCUNet), at a small accuracy cost (63.5% vs 64.4% on MCUNet). On the Raspberry Pi 5, ASI is 1.86× faster than gradient filtering and 91.0× faster than HOSVD, making it the only method that simultaneously reduces both memory and computation below vanilla training levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASI directly extends the HOSVD-based activation compression framework of Nguyen et al. (2024) by replacing the prohibitively expensive per-step HOSVD computation with a single warm-start subspace iteration borrowed from PowerSGD (Vogels et al., 2019). The rank selection strategy adapts the perplexity-based sensitivity analysis (STRS) from ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023), originally designed for weight compression, to the activation compression setting by redefining perplexity as the Frobenius norm difference between full-rank and low-rank weight gradients. The key conceptual departure is the observation that short-term stability of activation maps (supported by Lipschitz continuity of common nonlinearities) makes a single subspace iteration with warm-start sufficient, eliminating the need for full tensor decomposition at every training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASI makes activation compression practically deployable on resource-constrained devices, as demonstrated by real measurements on a Raspberry Pi 5 showing 91× speedup over HOSVD and 1.56× over vanilla training, while achieving up to 120× activation memory reduction. The brute-force backtracking rank selection algorithm is a significant scalability limitation that the authors acknowledge, and whether the activation stability assumption holds for larger models (e.g., LLMs with longer training) or more aggressive compression remains an open question. The TinyLlama 1B experiments suggest the approach generalizes beyond CNNs, but the accuracy drop (67.78→66.34 for 5 layers) and the fixed rank=20 (rather than budget-optimized) indicate the method has not yet been fully validated at scale.
