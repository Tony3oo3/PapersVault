## Problem

Contrastive losses such as InfoNCE maximize mutual information between multimodal representations but do not remove modality-specific information (nuisances), leading to a persistent representational misalignment known as the modality gap. This paper provides the first information-theoretic explanation of why this misalignment arises—namely, that representations remain sufficient but not minimal—and derives a principled regularization term to close the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images (224×224) and text captions, encoding each into a shared representation space via modality-specific encoders (e.g., a ViT-g/14 image encoder and a BERT-base text encoder in the real-world setup, or ResNet-20/MLP in toy experiments with discrete factor labels). The output is a set of aligned multimodal embeddings that support downstream tasks including image captioning (via a Q-Former with a frozen decoder LLM) and bidirectional image-text retrieval. In the toy experiments, one modality is the image and the other is a set of discrete scene factors, allowing controlled manipulation of the nuisance-to-essence ratio.

## Method

The paper decomposes each modality's input into an 'essence' (information shared across modalities) and 'nuisances' (modality-specific information), then formalizes the ideal representation as a minimal sufficient statistic of the essence. The key derivation shows that the nuisance mutual information I(Z_α; N_α) is upper-bounded by I(Z_α; X_α), which in turn is upper-bounded by the expected KL divergence between the two modalities' representation distributions for a positive pair. Under a spherical Gaussian assumption on the representation distributions, this bound reduces to a simple expected squared L2 distance between the mean embeddings, yielding the regularization term L_M. The final training objective is L = L_InfoNCE + βL_M, where β controls the compression–alignment trade-off; the term is modality-agnostic, requires no additional network, and is computationally negligible. The authors further show that this regularizer is equivalent to an adaptive temperature mismatch in InfoNCE and discover an 'Information Homeostasis' effect where trainable temperature partially counteracts the compression induced by large β.

## Results

On COCO image captioning, the proposed regularization term (βL_M) improves CIDEr from 91.7 (ITC+LM baseline) to 93.0 at β=0.1, and BLEU@4 from 28.6 to 29.4, while retrieval accuracy (I2T R@1) drops modestly from 64.2 to 63.0. At the lower β=0.01, captioning improves to CIDEr 92.3 with retrieval essentially unchanged (I2T R@1 64.0, T2I R@1 52.3). At β=0.3, both captioning and retrieval degrade sharply (CIDEr 90.5, I2T R@1 59.6), confirming the trade-off between minimality and sufficiency. In the toy experiments, the method demonstrates a clear negative correlation between nuisance information I(Z_α;N_α) and CKA alignment, and the regularization term effectively increases alignment while the temperature parameter exhibits an 'Information Homeostasis' effect that partially counteracts high β values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed βL_M regularization against the ITM (image-text matching) loss from UniTER (Chen et al., 2020b) as the primary baseline for encouraging alignment. The ITM baseline yields only a marginal captioning improvement (CIDEr 91.8 vs. 91.7) while substantially degrading retrieval (I2T R@1 drops from 64.2 to 61.4, T2I R@1 from 52.3 to 49.7). In contrast, the proposed method at β=0.01 achieves a larger captioning gain (CIDEr 92.3) while preserving retrieval accuracy almost exactly (I2T R@1 64.0, T2I R@1 52.3). At β=0.1, the proposed method achieves the best captioning score (CIDEr 93.0, BLEU@4 29.4) with a moderate retrieval cost (I2T R@1 63.0), whereas ITM's retrieval degradation is more severe. The authors also note that their regularization is modality-agnostic, computationally cheaper, and simpler to implement than ITM, which requires an additional binary classification head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) to multimodal contrastive representation learning, building on InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018) as the mechanism for maximizing mutual information between modality representations. It departs from prior work on the modality gap (Liang et al., 2022; Schrodi et al., 2024) by providing the first information-theoretic explanation rooted in the representation space rather than the input space, formalizing the gap as a consequence of modality-specific nuisances persisting in otherwise sufficient representations. The proposed regularization term L_M is related to multi-view IB losses (Federici et al., 2020; Tsai et al., 2020) but is derived specifically for the asymmetric multimodal setting and shown to be equivalent to an adaptive temperature adjustment in InfoNCE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework explaining why contrastive losses, despite maximizing mutual information, fail to produce aligned multimodal spaces—a long-observed but previously unexplained phenomenon. The proposed fix is simple, modality-agnostic, and computationally cheap, making it easy to adopt in existing pipelines. However, Theorem 2 is only informal and relies on infinite-set arguments, and the practical gains in captioning (up to ~1.3 CIDEr) come at a cost to retrieval accuracy, revealing a fundamental sufficiency-minimality trade-off. The 'Information Homeostasis' phenomenon—where encoders lower their temperature to counteract the regularization and preserve nuisances—is intriguing but left unexplored, and whether the approach generalizes beyond image-text pairs or to more than two modalities remains open.
