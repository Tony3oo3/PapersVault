## Problem

In the low-NFE regime (e.g., 4–8 steps), traditional ODE solvers for diffusion models fail because their local truncation error control via low-degree Taylor approximations is fundamentally inapplicable when step sizes are large. Prior work has focused on optimizing discretization schedules or learning solvers that minimize local error, but no method directly optimizes the solver to minimize the global error between the student's final output and a high-quality teacher trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250676/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

S4S takes a frozen pre-trained score network and a random noise latent x_T ~ N(0, σ̃²I) as input, and produces a single generated image after N forward passes through the score network (N = 4–8 NFEs in the main experiments). The method is data-free and training-free with respect to the underlying model: it learns only a small set of solver coefficients (e.g., 6–30 parameters for LMS solvers) in under an hour on a single A100, and can be plugged in as a black-box wrapper on top of any discretization schedule or architecture. Evaluation spans pixel-space models (CIFAR-10 32×32, FFHQ/AFHQv2 64×64) and latent-space models (LSUN-Bedroom/ImageNet 256×256, Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO 512×512).

## Method

S4S learns solver coefficients φ by minimizing a distillation objective L(φ) = E[d(Ψ_φ(x_T), Ψ*(x_T))] that matches the student solver's final output to a teacher solver's output (20 NFEs), using LPIPS as the differentiable distance. Two key design choices make this tractable: (1) time-dependent coefficients that vary at each discretization step, capturing the non-stationary difficulty of the diffusion ODE; and (2) a relaxed objective that allows the student to start from a nearby noise latent x_T' within an L2 ball of radius r·σ_T around x_T, enforced via projected SGD, which dramatically eases optimization when the student has few parameters. S4S-Alt extends this with alternating minimization: it iteratively optimizes the discretization steps (using the LD3 objective) and the solver coefficients separately, exploiting the full sampler design space. The method is initialized from existing solvers (iPNDM, UniPC) and requires no access to training data.

## Results

With S4S-Alt (jointly optimizing solver coefficients and discretization schedule), the paper achieves FID 6.35 on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFEs, 2.67 at 6 NFEs, and 2.39 at 8 NFEs, and FID 16.05 on MS-COCO at 4 NFEs, 11.17 at 6 NFEs, and 10.68 at 8 NFEs. The abstract highlights FID 3.73 on CIFAR-10 and 13.26 on MS-COCO at 5 NFEs, representing a 1.5× improvement over previous training-free ODE methods. As a standalone wrapper (S4S), the method uniformly improves FID over both UniPC and iPNDM across all tested discretization schedules (e.g., iPNDM-S4S achieves FID 9.30 vs. 10.93 for iPNDM on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFEs with LD3 schedule).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4S-Alt substantially outperforms all training-free ODE baselines: on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFEs, it achieves FID 6.35 versus 10.93 for the best traditional solver with LD3 schedule, 17.88 for DPM-Solver-v3, and 8.25 for the best S4S-only variant. On MS-COCO at 4 NFEs, S4S-Alt reaches FID 16.05 versus 20.22 (best traditional) and 23.90 (DPM-v3). Against BNS (Shaul et al., 2024), S4S-Alt matches or beats performance with fewer NFEs: S4S-Alt at 7 NFEs achieves FID 2.52 on CIFAR-10 while BNS requires 8 NFEs for FID 2.73, and on MS-COCO S4S-Alt at 6 NFEs (FID 11.17) far outperforms BNS at 12 NFEs (FID 20.67). Compared to training-based distillation methods, S4S-Alt at 7 NFEs (FID 2.52) is competitive with Progressive Distillation at 8 NFEs (FID 2.57) and iCT-deep at 1 NFE (FID 2.51), while requiring no training data. As a plug-in wrapper, S4S improves both UniPC and iPNDM across every discretization schedule tested (e.g., iPNDM-S4S: 9.30 vs. 10.93 FID on CIFAR-10 at 4 NFEs with LD3), confirming orthogonal gains over schedule optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

S4S builds on the DPM-Solver framework (Lu et al., 2022a) for formulating diffusion ODE solvers and on LD3 (Tong et al., 2024) for the relaxed training objective and learned discretization schedules. It departs sharply from the dominant paradigm of minimizing local truncation error (as in DPM-Solver-v3, GITS, AMED-Plugin, IIA, and Bespoke Solvers), arguing that this target is fundamentally wrong in the low-NFE regime where step sizes are large. The closest prior work, BNS (Shaul et al., 2024b), also minimizes global error but uses maximal-order solvers, a harder optimization objective, and simultaneous parameter updates; S4S instead uses lower-order solvers with time-dependent coefficients, a relaxed objective to ease optimization, and alternating minimization for stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S4S offers a lightweight, data-free, plug-in method that uniformly improves FID across six datasets and multiple architectures, achieving a 1.5× improvement over the best prior training-free ODE methods with only 5 NFEs. This is practically significant because it can be applied black-box on top of any existing discretization schedule or architecture without retraining the score network. Key limitations include: coefficients must be re-learned for each NFE count, only ODE (not SDE) solvers are considered, and performance still lags behind the best training-based distillation methods. Open questions include whether sample-level (rather than dataset-level) coefficients could further improve quality, and whether the global-error-matching philosophy extends to other generative model families.
