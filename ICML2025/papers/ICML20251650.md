## Problem

Signature-based machine learning methods for time series have been developed under the assumption of bounded-variation (piecewise-linear) paths, yet real data often arises from continuous-time stochastic processes with unbounded variation (e.g., Brownian motion). No general convergence theory existed to connect the empirical discrete-time expected signature estimator to its continuous-time theoretical value, leaving the probabilistic interpretation of expected-signature ML algorithms incomplete. Additionally, no variance-reduction technique was available for the expected signature estimator in the martingale setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N discretely-observed paths, each a piecewise-linear interpolation of a latent continuous-time stochastic process over a (possibly irregular, sample-dependent) partition of [0,T]. The output is the expected signature tensor φ(T) ∈ T((R^d)), a lower-dimensional feature representation that fully characterizes the data-generating distribution. Downstream, this tensor feeds into task-specific heads producing class labels (GPES classifier), derivative prices (signature-based pricing), or scalar regression values (SES model). The theory accommodates both independent repeated observations and chop-up sampling of a single long trajectory.

## Method

The paper establishes consistency and asymptotic normality of the expected signature estimator under a unifying set of continuity conditions (Aα–Aδ) on the latent process, using rough path theory to define signatures of unbounded-variation processes via canonical geometric lifts and an in-fill/long-span double asymptotic framework. The key in-fill result (Theorem 2.8) shows L^m convergence of discretized signatures to their continuous-time limits with explicit rates depending on the process regularity. The second contribution is a martingale correction: when the process is a martingale, the outermost Stratonovich integral in the signature is replaced by an Itô integral, yielding a mean-zero control variate S_c^I that, when subtracted with an optimally chosen coefficient c*_π, reduces the estimator variance by a factor (1 − ρ²) where ρ is the correlation between the original and correction terms. This correction is applied as a data transformation within existing signature-ML pipelines (GPES, SES, signature pricing) and empirically improves predictive accuracy even for non-martingale processes.

## Results

On the GPES time series classification task, the martingale correction (MC) yields a dramatic improvement on the OU dataset (62.20% → 88.26% accuracy, p < 0.001) and a substantial gain on Bidim (79.33% → 88.97%, p < 0.001), while the FBM result is statistically insignificant (95.62% vs 95.26%, p = 0.15). On the SES distributional regression task, the MC produces mild MSE reductions (e.g., 0.09 → 0.07 ×10⁻² on ideal gas r₂, 1.49 → 1.26 ×10⁻³ on rough volatility N=20) but none reach statistical significance (all p > 0.23). The pricing experiments (Figures 2–3) visually confirm that the corrected estimator has a materially narrower distribution than the classic estimator under both Bachelier and Heston dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GPES baseline (Triggiano & Romito, 2024), the martingale correction delivers a large and highly significant accuracy gain on OU (+26.06 pp, p < 0.001) and Bidim (+9.64 pp, p < 0.001), while on FBM it is essentially a tie (−0.36 pp, p = 0.15). Against the SES baseline (Lemercier et al., 2021), the correction yields small MSE reductions across both the ideal gas and rough volatility experiments (e.g., 1.49 → 1.26 ×10⁻³ at N=20 on rough volatility), but none of the pairwise t-tests reach conventional significance levels (p-values range from 0.23 to 0.79). Notably, the underlying processes in all five datasets are not martingales, yet the correction still helps, suggesting it acts as a beneficial data transformation even outside its strict theoretical regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical theory of expected signature estimation, building on prior work by Ni (2012), Passeggeri (2020), and Schell & Oberhauser (2023) that established convergence results for specific process classes (Brownian motion, fBm, bounded variation). It provides a unifying framework with general continuity conditions (Aα–Aδ) under which the discrete-time estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal, allowing irregular partitions and cross-sample dependency. The martingale correction (Section 2.2) is a novel control-variate modification inspired by the Stratonovich-to-Itô decomposition, applied to existing signature-based ML algorithms (GPES, SES, signature pricing) that were previously developed under the restrictive bounded-variation assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymptotic results give a rigorous probabilistic foundation for expected-signature-based ML methods, moving them beyond ad-hoc feature extraction toward well-defined statistical estimators of a continuous-time quantity. The martingale correction is a simple, drop-in modification that empirically yields large accuracy gains (e.g., 62%→88% on OU for GPES) even when the underlying process is not a martingale, suggesting it functions as a useful data transformation more broadly. Open questions include: whether the correction's benefit for non-martingales can be characterized theoretically (it introduces bias that may or may not be dominated by variance reduction), and whether the continuity conditions (Aα–Aδ) can be verified for a wider class of processes beyond those in Section 3.1.
