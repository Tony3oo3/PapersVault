## Problem

While rank collapse in depth (tokens converging across layers) and vanishing/exploding gradients in transformers at initialization were previously identified, the role of context length (width) in driving these pathologies remained unexplored. This paper reveals a previously unknown phenomenon—rank collapse in width—where all tokens converge to a single representation within a single attention layer as context length grows, caused by a spectral gap between the largest eigenvalue and the bulk of the attention matrix spectrum that is unique to softmax-based attention.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on randomly initialized softmax attention layers at initialization, taking a token sequence X_0 ∈ R^{T×d} (with T tokens of embedding dimension d, ratio γ = T/d ≤ 1 for the theoretical proof) and i.i.d. Gaussian Q, K, V matrices as input. The method produces a modified attention output X^⊥ = A^⊥(X_0)W^V where the rank-one outlier T^{-1}·1_{T×T} is subtracted from the attention matrix, along with rigorous spectral characterizations of both the original and modified attention matrices in the large-width limit (T, d → ∞). Empirical validation extends to non-isometric inputs from real tokenizers and to γ > 1 settings.

## Method

Using Random Matrix Theory, the paper proves that the softmax attention matrix at initialization is an i.i.d. Markov matrix whose spectrum exhibits a spectral gap: the largest eigenvalue is exactly 1 (from row-stochasticity) while the second largest singular value shrinks as O(T^{-1/2}), causing the gap to widen with context length and driving rank collapse in width (stable rank → 1) as well as accelerating depth collapse and gradient explosion (lower bound T^{L-1}). The proposed fix replaces A with A^⊥ = A − T^{-1}·1_{T×T} at each layer, removing the rank-one perturbation responsible for the outlier eigenvalue while preserving the bulk spectrum. Free probability theory then characterizes the resulting bulk as a free convolution of Marchenko-Pastur distributions, proving that stable rank scales linearly with T and gradient norms grow only linearly with depth rather than exponentially. The approach is validated empirically on BERT, RoBERTa, and TinyBERT, and is shown to be equivalent in spirit to recent practical fixes like centered self-attention (Ali et al., 2023) and the Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2025), which implicitly remove the same spectral outlier.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is Theorem 2.1, which proves that for softmax attention on orthonormal inputs, the largest singular value s₁(A) → 1 while s₂(√T·A) → 2σ, establishing a spectral gap that widens as context length T grows. Theorem 2.2 then shows the stable rank of the token covariance collapses to 1 at rate O(T⁻³) in a single layer, and Proposition 3.4 extends this to depth ℓ with rate O(T^{1−4ℓ}). The proposed fix (replacing A with A⊥ = A − T⁻¹·1_{T×T}) provably restores linear scaling of stable rank with T (Proposition 3.7) and reduces gradient norm growth from T^{L−1} to O(d) (Proposition 3.8). Empirically, on untrained BERT/RoBERTa encoders (d=784), the stable rank drops to ~1 within a few hundred tokens for vanilla attention, while the modified attention maintains stable rank scaling linearly with T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing architectures on a standard task; instead, it compares the proposed 'remove the gap' modification (A⊥) against several alternatives on the same untrained transformer encoders. Removing only the single dominant outlier (the proposed fix) performs nearly identically to the computationally expensive SVD-based removal of all spectral outliers (Figure 6), while being far cheaper. The fix consistently outperforms adding LayerNorm or skip connections alone at slowing rank collapse in depth (Figure 4b), and is effective even when combined with both. Setting the attention matrix to the identity (the highest possible stable rank, no spectral gap) still exhibits rank collapse in depth, confirming that depth collapse is inherent to repeated matrix multiplication and cannot be fully eliminated by attention modifications. The paper also shows that the Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2025) and Centered Self-Attention (Ali et al., 2023) can be reinterpreted as implicit spectral-gap removal, providing theoretical grounding for their empirical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on rank collapse in transformers (Dong et al., 2021; Noci et al., 2022) by identifying a previously unacknowledged failure mode—rank collapse in *width* (context length)—and rigorously attributing it to a spectral gap in softmax attention matrices via random matrix theory. It builds on the circular law for i.i.d. Markov matrices (Bordenave et al., 2011) and the free probability framework for deep network spectra (Pennington et al., 2017, 2018), but departs from Noci et al. by not assuming uniform attention a priori; instead, it proves *why* the attention matrix becomes effectively rank-one as width grows. The proposed fix (subtracting T⁻¹·1_{T×T} from the attention matrix) is shown to be mathematically equivalent to the empirical 'centering' of Ali et al. (2023) and the 'differential' mechanism of Ye et al. (2025), providing a unifying theoretical explanation for these ad-hoc solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of rank collapse in width as a distinct, attention-specific phenomenon (unlike depth collapse, which arises from any repeated matrix multiplication) reframes how the community should think about transformer initialization, especially as context lengths grow. The spectral gap diagnosis is general—it applies to ReLU and sigmoid attention variants as well—and the simple 'remove the outlier' fix is provably effective at initialization, though the paper explicitly notes it can only slow (not prevent) depth collapse. Key open questions include whether the fix transfers to training dynamics, how to handle the empirically observed emergence of *additional* spectral outliers in deep layers, and whether the initialization-only analysis captures the full picture once gradients flow. The paper also raises the broader question of whether the field has over-emphasized depth at the expense of width in theoretical analyses of transformers.
