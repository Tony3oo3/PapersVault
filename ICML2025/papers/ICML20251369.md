## Problem

Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) and its power-tuned variant (PPI++) provide valid, unbiased estimators for a single mean by combining limited gold-standard labels with abundant ML predictions, but they are constrained by their unbiasedness requirement and cannot fully exploit the information in the predictions. Modern applications (e.g., estimating galaxy fractions across spatial bins, product ratings across categories) require answering many parallel mean-estimation questions simultaneously, and no existing method combines PPI's variance reduction with empirical Bayes shrinkage to adaptively borrow strength across problems while respecting the quality of the ML predictor.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251369/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For each of m parallel problems, the method consumes a small labeled set (n_j covariate-outcome pairs), a larger unlabeled set (N_j covariates), and a pre-trained black-box predictor f applied to both sets. It also requires the second moments (σ²_j, τ²_j, γ_j) of the joint distribution of predictions and outcomes, or their sample-based estimates when these are unknown. The output is a vector of m compound mean estimates, where each estimate is a convex combination of the power-tuned PPI estimator and the prediction mean on unlabeled data, with the combination weight adapted per problem based on its estimated variance.

## Method

PAS operates in two stages. First, a within-problem power-tuning step computes the optimal λ*_j for each problem that minimizes the variance of the PPI estimator, yielding an unbiased PT estimator with variance σ̃²_j that is strictly lower than both the classical and vanilla PPI estimators. Second, an across-problem adaptive shrinkage step shrinks each PT estimator toward the prediction mean Z̃^f_j using per-problem weights ω_j = ω/(ω + σ̃²_j), where the global parameter ω is selected by minimizing CURE, a Correlation-Aware Unbiased Risk Estimate that explicitly accounts for the covariance between the shrinkage source (PT estimator) and target (prediction mean). This dual use of ML predictions—as both a variance-reduction device and a shrinkage target—allows PAS to provably achieve MSE no worse than either the classical estimator or the prediction mean, with asymptotic optimality of the tuning strategy as m → ∞.

## Results

On the Amazon Fine Food Reviews dataset with a fine-tuned BERT predictor, PAS achieves MSE of 3.287×10⁻³, reducing error by 86.5% over the classical estimator (24.305×10⁻³) and 47.7% over PPI++ (6.289×10⁻³). On Galaxy Zoo 2 with a ResNet50 predictor, PAS achieves MSE of 0.893×10⁻³, outperforming PPI (1.149×10⁻³) and PPI++ (1.026×10⁻³). In the synthetic model with a good predictor f₁(x)=x², PAS reaches MSE of 0.272×10⁻³ (vs. 3.142×10⁻³ for classical), while with a flawed predictor f₂(x)=|x| it achieves 2.496×10⁻³, still beating all baselines. PAS also attains the highest % Improved metric (80.8% on Amazon-tuned, 67.3% on Galaxy), indicating consistent gains across individual problems rather than improvements driven by a few easy cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAS dominates both PPI and PPI++ (PT) across all three real-world settings. On Amazon with the biased BERT-base predictor, PAS reduces MSE from 11.063 (PPI) and 10.633 (PT) to 8.517×10⁻³, a 23–29% improvement; with the more accurate BERT-tuned predictor, the gap widens to 57% over PPI and 48% over PT. On Galaxy Zoo 2, PAS achieves 0.893×10⁻³ MSE versus 1.149 (PPI) and 1.026 (PT), a 22% and 13% reduction respectively. The key mechanism is that PAS's adaptive shrinkage stage exploits the ML predictions as a contextual prior target, which PPI and PT cannot do since they enforce unbiasedness. PAS also outperforms the 'Shrink Avg' baseline (which shrinks toward the group mean rather than the prediction mean) on Amazon-tuned (3.287 vs. 6.280) and Galaxy (0.893 vs. 0.976), confirming that using the ML prediction as the shrinkage target is more informative than the grand mean. The UniPAS variant, which handles unknown second moments, is competitive with full PAS (e.g., 3.356 vs. 3.287 on Amazon-tuned), validating the practical applicability of the method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate statistical traditions: Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI/PPI++) for within-problem variance reduction using ML predictions, and empirical Bayes shrinkage (James-Stein, SURE) for across-problem information sharing. The key conceptual departure from prior PPI work is that PAS deliberately introduces bias through shrinkage toward the prediction mean, which PPI and PPI++ forbid by enforcing unbiasedness; this is only possible in the compound (multi-problem) setting where information can be borrowed across problems. The Correlation-Aware Unbiased Risk Estimate (CURE) is a new technical tool that extends Stein's SURE to handle the non-zero covariance between the shrinkage source (PT estimator) and target (prediction mean), a complication absent in classical James-Stein or standard SURE applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAS provides a principled, assumption-lean recipe for practitioners who have a black-box ML model and need to estimate many related means simultaneously — a setting common in genomics, astronomy, and NLP evaluation. The asymptotic optimality guarantee (Theorem 5.2) and the clean upper bound (Proposition 5.3) give strong theoretical justification, while the UniPAS variant handles the practically important case of unknown second moments. A key open question the authors flag is extending PAS from point estimation to valid confidence intervals with guaranteed coverage, which would require building on robust empirical Bayes interval methods. The framework is also limited to mean estimation with quadratic loss; extending to other functionals or loss structures remains open.
