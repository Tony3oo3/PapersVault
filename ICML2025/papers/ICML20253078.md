## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal large language models (MLLMs) typically stop at supervised fine-tuning, leaving truthfulness, safety, and human-preference alignment largely unaddressed. Existing alignment efforts target narrow domains such as hallucination reduction or conversational quality, and rely on small, machine-annotated datasets (often under 10K samples) that lack the diversity and granularity needed for comprehensive improvement. The paper asks whether a well-designed alignment pipeline can holistically enhance MLLMs across perception, reasoning, dialogue, and trustworthiness simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes images, videos, and text queries spanning three domains (image understanding, video understanding, and multimodal safety), with 30k representative queries drawn from 10M raw samples via CLIP-based KNN clustering to ensure category diversity. The MM-RLHF dataset outputs 120k ranked preference comparison pairs, each annotated with per-dimension scores (Helpfulness, Faithfulness, Ethical Considerations), an overall ranking, and free-text rationales. The Critique-Based Reward Model takes a query, a candidate response, and (during inference) a self-generated critique as input, producing both a natural-language critique and a scalar reward score; the aligned MLLM produces standard text responses.

## Method

The paper introduces three coupled components. First, the MM-RLHF dataset is built by generating responses with frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5-Sonnet, Qwen2-VL-72B, LLaVA-OV-72B), then having 50+ annotators score, rank, and explain each response over two months, with special strategies for handling ties (constructing positive samples from poor-quality ties and negative samples from high-quality ties). Second, the Critique-Based Reward Model replaces the standard scalar-only reward head with a two-head architecture: a language head trained to generate detailed critiques (using GPT-4o-augmented human annotations as targets to overcome brevity) and a scoring head trained with teacher-forcing on ground-truth critiques to assign pairwise rewards, jointly optimized via ℓ_Critique + ℓ_Score. Third, MM-DPO extends DPO with Dynamic Reward Scaling, where the per-sample β is modulated as β(δ) = β_ori·(1 + w(1 − e^{−kδ})) using the reward margin δ computed by MM-RLHF-Reward-7B, so that high-confidence comparison pairs receive proportionally larger gradient updates while the scaling remains bounded to prevent training instability.

## Results

On the MM-RLHF-RewardBench, MM-RLHF-Reward-7B achieves 0.85 ACC and 0.67 ACC+ overall, surpassing GPT-4o (0.74 ACC, 0.50 ACC+) and all open-source baselines (LLaVA-Critic Pointwise: 0.45/0.17; LLaVA-OV-7B: 0.24/0.07). On general MLLM benchmarks (Table 3), MM-RLHF-Reward-7B attains 50.15 average score, outperforming 72B open-source models like Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct (42.97) and InternVL2-26B (45.67), though trailing GPT-4o (62.40) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (62.50). For alignment, MM-DPO on LLaVA-OV-7B yields 97.90 on LLaVA-Wild, 69.30 on OCRBench, 86.11 on DocVQA, 61.60 on MathVista, and 4.08 on MMHal, with an average 11% gain in conversational ability and a 57% reduction in unsafe behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-DPO achieves the best score across all five benchmarks in Table 4, outperforming standard DPO by +2.7 on LLaVA-Wild, +3.0 on OCRBench, +0.39 on DocVQA, +0.7 on MathVista, and +0.20 on MMHal. Against the closest competitor MPO, MM-DPO leads by +2.6 (LLaVA-Wild), +3.1 (OCRBench), +0.23 (DocVQA), +0.5 (MathVista), and +0.36 (MMHal). LLaVA-Critic matches MM-DPO on LLaVA-Wild (97.90) but trails significantly on OCRBench (64.20 vs 69.30) and MathVista (58.74 vs 61.60), confirming that iterative self-generated DPO degrades on harder tasks. For the reward model, MM-RLHF-Reward-7B (50.15 avg) surpasses all open-source 72B models including Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct (42.97) and InternVL2-26B (45.67) on Table 3, but remains below closed-source GPT-4o (62.40) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (62.50). On the custom MM-RLHF-RewardBench, MM-RLHF-Reward outperforms GPT-4o by +0.11 ACC and +0.17 ACC+, while LLaVA-Critic (both pointwise and pairwise) lags far behind at 0.45/0.35 ACC. In the dataset comparison (Table 5), MM-RLHF dominates over LLaVA-RLHF, VLFeedback, RLAIF, LLaVA-Critic data, and MPO-Data on LLaVA-Wild, OCRBench, DocVQA, and MMHal, though MPO-Data slightly edges ahead on MathVista (63.40 vs 61.60) due to its math-focused composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Amini et al., 2024) to the multimodal setting, departing from prior MLLM alignment work (LLaVA-RLHF, VLFeedback, RLAIF-V, LLaVA-Critic) that targeted narrow domains like hallucination reduction or conversational quality. Conceptually, it draws on two threads: (1) critique-based reward modeling from the LLM literature (Yu et al., 2024d), adapted here to leverage fine-grained human annotations rather than model-generated critiques, and (2) dynamic beta adjustment from LLM DPO variants (beta-DPO, Wu et al., 2024a), but replacing unreliable implicit rewards with an external 7B reward model signal. The dataset itself represents a substantial scale-up over existing MLLM preference resources, which typically contain fewer than 10K samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 120k fine-grained human-annotated dataset and the two new benchmarks (RewardBench, SafetyBench) provide much-needed infrastructure for comprehensive MLLM alignment research, moving the field beyond single-task optimization. The finding that small MLLMs (<7B) cannot effectively self-improve through iterative sampling challenges the LLM-domain assumption that self-generated data plus reward models suffice, suggesting human annotation remains essential for multimodal alignment at scale. Open questions include whether the rich per-dimension annotation granularity can be exploited by more advanced optimization algorithms, how to integrate high-resolution data to close the remaining gap on real-world benchmarks, and whether the reward model can bootstrap semi-automated annotation to scale the dataset further.
