## Problem

Error feedback (EF) methods for communication compression in distributed optimization have been largely restricted to smooth, unconstrained settings, yet real-world problems frequently involve non-smooth objectives (e.g., PPO clipping, ReLU) and safety constraints (e.g., federated RL for robotics). The paper demonstrates that vanilla compressed gradient descent fails to converge and that the state-of-the-art EF21 actually diverges on simple non-smooth convex problems with Top-K compression, exposing a critical gap between theory and practice. The paper asks: what are the fundamental limits of compressed first-order methods in the non-smooth regime, and can one design a provably convergent method that also enforces safety constraints?

## Input / Output

Each worker i computes local subgradients of the objective f_i and constraint g_i at the current iterate, and sends a single float (the constraint value g_i(x^t)) to the server each iteration at negligible cost. The worker-to-server subgradient messages are compressed via a contractive operator C_i (e.g., Top-K preserving K largest-magnitude coordinates), while the server-to-worker model update is compressed via a separate operator C_0 (EF21-P style). The output is an averaged iterate x̄^T over iterations where the constraint violation is below a threshold c, with guaranteed bounds on both the suboptimality gap f(x̄^T)−f(x*) and the constraint violation g(x̄^T), each at rate O(RM/√(δ_s δ T)).

## Method

Safe-EF extends EF14 with two key mechanisms: (1) a primal switching subgradient rule that dynamically selects between the objective subgradient f_i'(x^t) and the constraint subgradient g_i'(x^t) at each iteration depending on whether the aggregate constraint violation g(x^t) exceeds a threshold c, and (2) bidirectional compression where workers apply C_i to their error-feedback-corrected subgradient messages and the server applies C_0 to the model update (w^{t+1}−x^t) before broadcasting. The convergence proof builds on a virtual-iterates construction (x̂^t = w^t − γe^t) and shows that appropriate choices of stepsize γ and threshold c guarantee at least T/2 'good' iterations (where constraints are satisfied), ensuring the averaged output is well-defined and achieves the O(RM/√(δ_s δ T)) rate. This matches a new lower bound of Ω(RM/√(δT)) for unidirectional compression, confirming optimality up to constants. In the stochastic extension, the method requires a batch size Õ(σ²_fv/(nc²)) for constraint estimation and achieves high-probability bounds with only logarithmic dependence on failure probability.

## Results

The paper establishes a lower bound of Ω(RM/√(δT)) for non-smooth convex distributed optimization with contractive compressors (Theorem 4.1), and proves Safe-EF achieves a matching upper bound of O(RM/√(δ_s·δ·T)) for both suboptimality and constraint violation (Theorem 4.2). In the stochastic setting, Safe-EF achieves high-probability bounds of Õ((MR + σ_fv/√N_fv)(1+log(1/β))/√(δT)) (Theorem 5.4). In the humanoid robot experiment, Top-K compression with Safe-EF achieves approximately 2000× communication reduction compared to uncompressed CGD while maintaining constraint satisfaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safe-EF outperforms all five baselines across the experimental settings. On the synthetic non-smooth problem (Figure 2), Safe-EF converges faster than CGD, EF21, EF21M, and EControl for moderate heterogeneity (s∈{0.1, 1.0}); under high heterogeneity (s=10.0), EControl is initially faster but Safe-EF catches up by the end of training. In the humanoid robot experiment (Figure 4), EF21 diverges entirely due to non-smoothness of the PPO objective, EF14 violates the safety constraint, and Parallel-CRPO (uncompressed) fails to converge within the same communication budget, while Safe-EF satisfies the constraint with only a slight performance reduction. The CGD baseline is shown to be fundamentally non-convergent in the non-smooth setting with Top-K compression (Example 1.1), making it an unsuitable comparison. The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 4.1) confirms that no first-order algorithm with unidirectional compression can beat the O(1/√(δT)) rate, and Safe-EF matches this bound, establishing its optimality in the worst case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the error feedback (EF) lineage from EF14 (Seide et al., 2014) and EF21 (Richtárik et al., 2021) into the non-smooth convex regime, where prior EF analyses have been restricted to smooth objectives. It departs from the dominant smooth-compression literature by establishing the first lower bounds for non-smooth distributed optimization with contractive compressors, and by showing that EF21—despite its state-of-the-art smooth guarantees—actually diverges on a simple non-smooth convex problem with Top-1 compression. The safety constraint mechanism draws on the switching subgradient approach (Polyak, 1967; Lan & Zhou, 2020), while the bidirectional compression component leverages the EF21-P variant (Gruntkowska et al., 2023), combining these into a unified algorithm that prior work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap between theory and practice in federated learning by providing the first provably optimal (matching lower bound) compressed gradient method for non-smooth constrained problems, with direct applicability to safe federated reinforcement learning (e.g., humanoid robot fleets). Key open questions remain: extending guarantees to non-convex objectives (where Safe-EF empirically works but lacks theory), improving the suboptimal O(1/ε⁴) sample complexity in the stochastic setting, and whether the √(δₛδ) compression dependence can be tightened. The finding that convergence does not improve with more workers in the worst case (matching the lower bound) also tempers expectations for scaling federated training in the non-smooth regime.
