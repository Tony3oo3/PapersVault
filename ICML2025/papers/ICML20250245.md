## Problem

Existing audio-visual quality assessment methods target compression and transmission distortions, making them ill-suited for AI-generated audio-visual content (AGAVs) that exhibit unique distortions such as unrealistic audio and A/V content inconsistency. While large multimodal models (LMMs) show promise for AIGC evaluation, they have not been adapted to produce human-like numerical quality scores for AGAVs, and no large-scale AGAV quality assessment dataset existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input configurations: audio+video (AGAV), audio+text prompt (TTA), or music+text prompt (TTM). Video is sampled at 1 fps into an image sequence, and audio is segmented to match frame count, converted to 128-bin fbank spectrograms, and encoded via BEATs. For AGAV, the output is three sequential numerical scores (audio quality, A/V content consistency, overall A/V quality) regressed from the LLM's last hidden states; for TTA/TTM, two scores are produced. In the selection task, the model compares multiple AGAV candidates for the same video and outputs the index of the best one.

## Method

AGAV-Rater is built on VideoLLaMA2 and trained in two stages. First, 50,952 instruction-response pairs are automatically constructed from VGGSound, AudioCaps, and MusicCaps using two text-defined levels (excellent/bad) assigned by swapping audio with mismatched-category audio or reversing audio to simulate unnaturalness; the model is pre-trained with cross-entropy loss to develop basic quality perception without human annotation. Second, the model is fine-tuned on the AGAVQA-MOS subset (3,088 AGAVs with 9,264 human MOS ratings) using a PLCC loss that directly regresses the LLM's last hidden states to continuous numerical scores, avoiding information loss from discretizing MOS into text levels. A multi-dimensional instruction prompts the model to evaluate audio quality, then consistency, then overall quality sequentially—mimicking the observed human rating order—and quality-level masking prevents the model from over-relying on previously predicted scores when generating subsequent ones.

## Results

On the AGAVQA-MOS subset, AGAV-Rater achieves SRCC of 0.7909 (Audio Quality), 0.7553 (Content Consistency), and 0.7458 (Overall Quality), outperforming the strongest baseline VAST (0.7640, 0.6811, 0.7094) by up to 7.4% on content consistency. On the unseen AGAVQA-Pair subset, AGAV-Rater reaches 78% optimal-AGAV selection accuracy, substantially exceeding the best alignment baseline VAST (64%) and all closed-source LMMs (≤30%). On TTA and TTM datasets, AGAV-Rater also leads, achieving SRCC of 0.7390/0.7330 (TTA) and 0.8322/0.7719 (TTM) on audio quality and content consistency respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGAV-Rater outperforms all 20 baselines across every dimension on all four evaluation datasets. The closest competitor is VAST (audio-video alignment), which trails by 2.7% SRCC on Audio Quality, 7.4% on Content Consistency, and 3.6% on Overall Quality on AGAVQA-MOS; on AGAVQA-Pair, AGAV-Rater's 78% selection accuracy exceeds VAST's 64% by a wide margin. Traditional AQA methods (NISQA, PAM) and AVQA methods (GeneralAVQA, DNN-SND) are significantly weaker on the Content Consistency dimension, confirming they cannot capture AIGC-specific audio-visual mismatches. Open-source audio-visual LMMs (PandaGPT, NextGPT, VITA-1.0, VideoLLaMA2) perform near-random or worse, with NextGPT achieving SRCC as low as 0.0076 on Content Consistency, demonstrating that off-the-shelf LMMs are ill-suited for quality scoring without targeted training. Closed-source LMMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5/2.0, Reka) also struggle on AGAVQA-Pair, with best accuracy of only 30% (GPT-4o+Video Caption), far below AGAV-Rater's 78%. On TTA and TTM, AGAV-Rater edges out CLAP and VAST by 1.4–3.5% SRCC, showing consistent but narrower margins on these smaller, single-modal-consistency tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMM-based quality assessment paradigm (established for images and videos by Q-Align, AIGV-Assessor, and related work) to the audio-visual domain, specifically targeting AI-generated content produced by video-to-audio (VTA) methods. It builds directly on VideoLLaMA2 as its architectural backbone, leveraging its joint audio-video encoding and language comprehension capabilities. The key departure from prior AVQA work (DNN-RNT, GeneralAVQA, etc.) is the shift from compression/transmission distortions to AIGC-specific distortions such as A/V content inconsistency and unnatural audio, and the introduction of a two-stage training strategy that pre-trains on automatically labeled text-defined quality levels from real-world datasets before fine-tuning on human-annotated MOS scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AGAVQA-3k dataset (3,382 samples from 16 VTA methods) is the first benchmark for evaluating AI-generated audio-visual quality, filling a gap that will grow as VTA tools become commercially widespread. The practical demonstration that AGAV-Rater can select higher-quality outputs from ElevenLabs (80% user preference) suggests immediate applicability in production pipelines. Limitations include the relatively small subjective panel (15 subjects), the modest size of the AGAVQA-Pair subset (75 pairs), and evaluation limited to short clips; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, more diverse VTA architectures, and whether the pre-training strategy scales to other AIGC modalities.
