## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods modify a large fraction of model parameters (often 50–100%) and rely heavily on the remaining dataset to preserve retained knowledge, incurring high computational cost and raising data-privacy concerns. This paper asks whether unlearning can be achieved by altering only a minimal, principled subset of weights while entirely eliminating the need for the remaining dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SEMU takes a pretrained model (ResNet-18, VGG-16, DDPM, or Stable Diffusion), a forgetting dataset D_f, and optionally a remaining dataset D_r (which can be set to empty). It outputs a modified model where each layer's weight matrix is updated by a low-rank term U_{i,r} R_i V_{i,r}^T; the only trainable parameters are the small r×r matrices R_i (typically <1–2% of total parameters), with r determined per-layer by an explained-variance threshold γ ∈ [0,1]. The method is applied to both classification (random and class-wise forgetting) and conditional diffusion generation (class and concept unlearning).

## Method

SEMU identifies the critical weight subspace for unlearning via SVD of the forgetting-loss gradient. For each layer, the gradient G_i is first projected perpendicular to the existing weight matrix A_i (to avoid disrupting retained knowledge), then decomposed as G_i = U Σ V^T. A single hyperparameter γ selects the smallest rank r such that the cumulative explained variance of the top-r singular values exceeds γ, yielding a truncated SVD that is provably optimal under the Frobenius norm (Eckart–Young–Mirsky). Each layer is then reparameterized as A_i + U_{i,r} R_i V_{i,r}^T, where R_i is an r×r matrix initialized to zero and is the sole trainable component. Fine-tuning proceeds with random-labeling cross-entropy (classification) or diffusion noise-prediction loss (generation), updating only the R_i matrices. This design confines all parameter changes to a low-dimensional subspace aligned with the forgetting gradient, achieving unlearning with <1–2% of weights modified and no dependence on D_r.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 (10% random forgetting), SEMU achieves a Testing Accuracy of 74.14% (vs. 74.13% for full retraining) while modifying only 1.18% of model parameters, compared to 100% for all standard baselines and 50% for SalUn. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, SEMU reaches 94.22% TA with just 0.54% of parameters altered. For class-wise forgetting on CIFAR-10, SEMU achieves 99.83% Unlearning Accuracy with only 0.87% trainable parameters. In image generation (DDPM, CIFAR-10), SEMU attains 95.60% UA with 1.2% parameters, and on Stable Diffusion (Imagenette) it achieves 83.80% average UA while significantly outperforming FMN (42.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMU's primary advantage over all baselines is parameter efficiency: it modifies only 0.54–1.18% of weights versus 100% for FT, RL, GA, IU, BE, BS, and ℓ₁-sparse, and 50% for SalUn. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18, 10% forgetting), SEMU matches the Retrain baseline on Testing Accuracy (74.14% vs. 74.13%) while SalUn degrades TA by 6.34 points (67.79%); however, SalUn achieves higher Unlearning Accuracy (27.53% vs. SEMU's 2.53%), indicating more aggressive forgetting at the cost of general performance. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), SEMU reaches 94.22% TA (vs. Retrain 94.26%) with 0.54% params, outperforming all 100%-parameter methods on TA preservation. For image generation on DDPM, SEMU (95.60% UA, 1.2% params) is slightly behind SalUn (99.20% UA, 50% params) and ESD (100% UA) on unlearning accuracy but uses ~40× fewer parameters. On Stable Diffusion (Imagenette), SEMU (83.80% avg UA) trails SalUn (99.82%) and ESD (99.40%) but dramatically outperforms FMN (42.54%), and SEMU's generated samples are semantically closer to the original model (CLIP score 0.280 vs. SalUn's 0.131 on NSFW prompts; MSE 0.025 vs. 0.101). Critically, SEMU operates without the remaining dataset, whereas SalUn collapses when remaining data is unavailable (TA drops from 93.66% to 12.47% on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMU builds directly on the gradient-based machine unlearning paradigm established by SalUn (Fan et al., 2024), which selects a subset of weights by gradient magnitude for targeted fine-tuning. The key departure is replacing this heuristic top-k selection with a principled SVD projection of the gradient matrix, yielding an orthogonal low-rank subspace where only a small r×r matrix R is trainable per layer. This is conceptually distinct from Kodge et al. (2024), which also uses SVD but for estimating feature-space mutual information between forget/retain classes rather than for gradient disentanglement. The paper further introduces a remaining-dataset-free formulation, departing from the standard MU setup (Bourtoule et al., 2021; Kurmanji et al., 2023) that relies on D_r to preserve accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: modifying <1% of parameters while eliminating the need for the remaining dataset addresses two of the most cited barriers to deploying MU in real-world settings (privacy restrictions on training data access, and computational cost of large-scale fine-tuning). The dramatic collapse of SalUn without remaining data (Table 7: RA drops from ~99% to ~13%) versus SEMU's stability highlights a previously underappreciated fragility in gradient-saliency-based methods. Open questions include whether the SVD-based approach generalizes to LLMs and VLMs (acknowledged as future work), and whether the lack of remaining-data constraint on critical directions limits performance on complex downstream tasks.
