## Problem

Potential-Based Reward Shaping (PBRS) requires well-designed state potential functions to accelerate RL, but the standard approach of computing optimal state values from offline data assumes no unobserved confounding (NUC). When offline data is generated by behavioral policies with hidden confounders (e.g., wind direction in a grid world), naively estimated value functions can be severely biased—penalizing states that are actually beneficial—and lead to suboptimal or even harmful shaping signals. This paper asks: how can one robustly extract informative potential functions from confounded offline data that still guarantee policy invariance and improve online learning efficiency?

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple offline trajectory datasets (state, action, reward, next-state tuples) collected by potentially different behavioral policies, along with a known scalar reward upper bound b. It produces a set of potential functions φ_h(s) that upper-bound the true optimal interventional state values while satisfying a 'conservative optimism' constraint (V*_h(s) ≤ φ_h(s) ≤ H). These potentials are then fed into a modified Q-UCB online learner that outputs an optimal interventional policy. A key structural detail is that the algorithm skips unvisited state-action pairs entirely and, when multiple datasets are available, takes the pointwise minimum of the per-dataset upper bounds to obtain the tightest valid estimate.

## Method

The core contribution is the Causal Bellman Optimal Equation (Thm. 3.1), which extends the standard Bellman optimality equation by replacing unknown interventional transition and reward terms with optimistic partial-identification bounds: for actions not taken in the offline data (probability P_h(¬x|s)), the method assigns the worst-case reward b plus the maximum next-state value, yielding a valid upper bound on the true optimal value. Algorithm 2 computes these bounds via backward value iteration, skipping unvisited state-action pairs and clipping the optimistic bonus at ω_h = (H−h)·b to prevent over-estimation. The derived potentials are then used in a modified Q-UCB (Algorithm 1) that incorporates PBRS through shaped rewards y'_h = y_h + φ_{h+1}(s_{h+1}) − φ_h(s_h), zero Q-value initialization, a potential-scaled UCB bonus b_t = c√(H·φ_m²·ι/t), and value clipping at the potential function. The theoretical analysis (Thm. 4.5) establishes a gap-dependent regret bound that decomposes into two terms: pseudo-suboptimal state-action pairs (where the shaping function cuts unnecessary exploration) enjoy an H^5 dependence, while remaining pairs match the vanilla Q-UCB's H^6 bound, demonstrating a provable sample-efficiency gain from the causal shaping.

## Results

The main evaluation metric is the optimal policy ratio (fraction of states where the learned policy is optimal) across four windy MiniGrid environments. The proposed Shaping+Causal Bound method achieves 0.76, 0.70, 0.81, and 0.90 on Windy Empty World, LavaCross (easy), LavaCross (hard), and LavaCross (maze) respectively, substantially outperforming No Shaping Q-UCB (0.00, 0.00, 0.00, 0.03) and BCQ (0.79, 0.50, 0.67, 0.50) in three of four environments. In the Windy Empty World where confounding provides little extra signal, all shaping baselines perform comparably (~0.72–0.76), and BCQ slightly edges out the proposed method (0.79 vs. 0.76). Cumulative regret curves (Fig. 4) confirm that the causal upper bound shaping yields the fastest convergence in the LavaCross environments where confounded behavioral values would otherwise mislead the agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla Q-UCB (Jin et al., 2018), the proposed method wins decisively in all four environments: the unshaped Q-UCB achieves near-zero optimal ratio (0.00–0.03) because it assumes rewards in [0,1] and initializes overly optimistically, while the proposed Q-UCB Shaping uses zero initialization and achieves 0.70–0.90. Against BCQ (Fujimoto et al., 2019), the proposed method wins in all three LavaCross environments (0.70 vs. 0.50 easy, 0.81 vs. 0.67 hard, 0.90 vs. 0.50 maze) but is slightly behind in Windy Empty World (0.76 vs. 0.79), where the uniform wind distribution provides little confounding signal to exploit. Notably, combining BCQ with the causal shaping (Shaping+BCQ) actually degrades performance in the LavaCross environments (0.31, 0.22, 0.03), suggesting that BCQ's conservative offline estimates conflict with the optimistic shaping signal. The shaping baselines using min/max/avg behavioral values from confounded data all underperform the causal bound, confirming that naive off-policy value estimates introduce harmful bias into the potential function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: potential-based reward shaping (PBRS, Ng et al. 1999) and confounding-robust off-policy evaluation (Zhang & Bareinboim 2024; Manski 1989). Prior PBRS work either relied on hand-designed potentials or assumed the no-unobserved-confounding (NUC) condition when learning potentials from offline data; prior confounded off-policy work focused on policy evaluation rather than shaping. The paper's key departure is applying Manski-style partial identification bounds to the Bellman optimality equation to produce *upper-bound* potentials that are valid even under unobserved confounding, then plugging them into a modified Q-UCB (Jin et al. 2018) to obtain the first gap-dependent regret bound for PBRS in model-free online learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'conservative optimism' condition (Def. 4.1) gives practitioners a concrete, checkable criterion for when a learned shaping function will provably accelerate online learning rather than mislead it—a gap in the PBRS literature where shaping quality was typically assessed only empirically. The regret bound showing that shaping reduces horizon dependence from H^6 to H^5 for pseudo-suboptimal state-action pairs provides a clean theoretical account of *why* good potentials help. Limitations include the tabular, finite-horizon setting, the requirement of a known reward upper bound b, and empirical validation only on small MiniGrid worlds; scaling to function approximation, continuous spaces, and infinite-horizon MDPs remains open.
