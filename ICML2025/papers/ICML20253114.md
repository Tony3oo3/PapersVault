## Problem

Existing nonparametric contextual bandit theory assumes bounded context supports with pdf bounded away from zero, yet practical applications in healthcare, dynamic pricing, and recommender systems often involve unbounded or heavy-tailed context distributions. The core challenge is that unbounded contexts require the algorithm to simultaneously balance the bias-variance tradeoff in reward estimation (adapting to local sample density) and the exploration-exploitation tradeoff (adapting to the suboptimality gap), which no prior method achieves with a single algorithm.

## Input / Output

At each step t, the algorithm receives a context vector X_t ∈ X (unbounded support, arbitrary dimension d) and the full history of previous contexts, actions, and rewards. It computes per-action UCB estimates using nearest neighbor distances among past samples for each action, then selects the action A_t maximizing the UCB. For the MNIST experiment, contexts are 28×28 grayscale images (784-dimensional) with 10 discrete actions (digit labels) and binary rewards; for synthetic experiments, contexts are 1D or 2D vectors drawn from uniform, Gaussian, t₄, or Cauchy distributions with 2 actions.

## Method

The paper proposes two kNN-UCB algorithms. The first uses a fixed k with an improved UCB of the form (1/k)ΣY_i + b + L·ρ_{a,t}(x), where the L·ρ bias term adapts to local suboptimality gap, improving over prior kNN-UCB for large margin parameter α. The second (main contribution) uses an adaptive k selected data-drivenly: k_t(x) = max{j : L·ρ_{a,t,j}(x) ≤ √(ln T / j)}, which equates the bias term L·ρ with the variance term √(ln T/j) at each point. This means larger k is used where samples are dense (high pdf, small suboptimality gap) and smaller k where samples are sparse (low pdf, large suboptimality gap), without requiring knowledge of the unknown pdf or margin parameter. The adaptive method achieves a regret bound of Õ(T^{1−min{(α+1)β/(α+(d+2)β), β}}) under heavy-tailed contexts, matching the newly derived minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: the adaptive kNN-UCB method achieves an expected regret of Õ(T^{1-min{(α+1)β/(α+(d+2)β), β}}) under heavy-tailed contexts, matching the minimax lower bound Ω(T^{1-min{(α+1)β/(α+(d+2)β), β}}) up to logarithmic factors. For bounded contexts, the adaptive method achieves Õ(T^{1-(α+1)/(d+2)}), which is minimax optimal across all parameter regimes (including d ≤ α+1 where the fixed-k method is suboptimal). Empirically, on MNIST (28×28 images, 10 actions), the adaptive kNN method achieves lower cumulative regret than standard kNN-UCB across all tested k values, and on synthetic 1D/2D distributions (uniform, Gaussian, t₄, Cauchy), it significantly outperforms UCBogram, ABSE, and fixed-k kNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against UCBogram (Rigollet & Zeevi, 2010) and ABSE (Perchet & Rigollet, 2013) on synthetic 1D and 2D distributions (uniform, Gaussian, t₄, Cauchy) with T=1000 steps averaged over 100 trials. The adaptive kNN-UCB method significantly outperforms both UCBogram and ABSE across all four distribution types in both 1D and 2D settings, with the gap widening for heavier-tailed distributions (Cauchy, β=0.5 in 1D; β=1/3 in 2D). On MNIST, the adaptive kNN method outperforms standard kNN-UCB with various fixed k values, though bin-splitting baselines (UCBogram, ABSE) are not compared there due to their poor adaptivity to local intrinsic dimension. Theoretically, the adaptive method is the first to achieve the minimax lower bound for heavy-tailed contexts, while UCBogram and ABSE have no guarantees in this regime. The fixed-k method (kNN-UCB1) improves over Guan & Jiang (2018) for α>0 by achieving Õ(T^{1-(α+1)/(d+2)}) instead of Õ(T^{(d+1)/(d+2)}), but remains suboptimal when d≤α+1 or β≤1/d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonparametric contextual bandit literature (Rigollet & Zeevi, 2010; Perchet & Rigollet, 2013; Guan & Jiang, 2018) from bounded context supports to unbounded, heavy-tailed context distributions. The fixed-k kNN-UCB method builds directly on Guan & Jiang (2018) but improves the UCB construction by adding a Lipschitz bias term L·ρ that adapts to local suboptimality gaps. The adaptive k selection mechanism is inspired by Zhao & Lai (2021b) for i.i.d. heavy-tailed classification, but is fundamentally different because it must simultaneously balance bias-variance (from heavy tails) and exploration-exploitation (from the sequential bandit structure), a dual challenge absent in the i.i.d. setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical treatment of heavy-tailed contexts in contextual bandits, closing a gap between theory and practical applications in healthcare, dynamic pricing, and recommender systems where contexts are naturally unbounded. The key open question is whether the adaptive kNN approach can be extended to Hölder smoothness (beyond Lipschitz), dynamic regret settings, and more complex sequential decision problems like reinforcement learning, as the authors note. A limitation is that the minimax analysis is for two actions, and the dependence on |A| is not characterized, leaving the multi-action regime theoretically unresolved.
