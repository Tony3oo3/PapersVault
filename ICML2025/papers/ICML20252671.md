## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods rely on concatenation or Kronecker products to combine modality representations, oversimplifying the interaction structure and neglecting the joint distribution of latent embeddings. This leads to biased fused representations and poor generalizability, particularly when modalities are heterogeneous (e.g., EHR time series, chest X-rays, clinical notes). Additionally, practical datasets often contain missing modalities, and standard imputation strategies (zero-filling, mean imputation) ignore the learned marginal distributions, further degrading downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three types of clinical inputs: EHR time series (17 variables sampled every 2 hours over 48 hours, yielding 76-dimensional vectors per time step), chest X-ray images resized to 224×224 pixels, and unstructured text (clinical notes or radiology reports capped at 512 words). Each modality is encoded into a shared latent space via a modality-specific encoder (two-layer LSTM, ResNet-34, or TinyBERT respectively) followed by a projection layer. The model outputs binary class predictions (in-hospital mortality or 30-day readmission) and, when a modality is missing, generates a pseudo representation by sampling from the learned per-modality GMM, which is then fed into the fusion and classification pipeline as if it were a real observation.

## Method

CM² models the joint distribution of modality embeddings using a copula, with each marginal assumed to follow a K-component Gaussian mixture model whose parameters (means, diagonal covariances, mixture weights) are learned end-to-end via gradient backpropagation. The copula parameter α is treated as a trainable scalar, and the joint log-likelihood is optimized through stochastic variational inference: the ELBO combines the negative copula log-likelihood (weighted by λ_cop), the negative marginal log-likelihoods, and a task-specific cross-entropy loss. Sklar's theorem guarantees that, given the continuous GMM marginals, the copula uniquely determines the joint distribution, enabling separate optimization of marginals and dependence structure. For missing modalities, the learned GMM serves as a generative model: a pseudo embedding is sampled from the marginal and passed through the same fusion module (two-layer LSTM) and MLP classifier, with the copula parameter α encoding cross-modality information into the marginal so that generated representations reflect the joint distribution rather than an isolated unimodal prior.

## Results

On the totally matched MIMIC-IV dataset, CM² achieves 0.827 AUROC and 0.492 AUPR for In-Hospital Mortality (IHM) prediction, and 0.737 AUROC and 0.466 AUPR for 30-day Readmission (READM) prediction, outperforming all seven baselines. On MIMIC-III, CM² reaches 0.854 AUROC / 0.513 AUPR (IHM) and 0.754 AUROC / 0.445 AUPR (READM), exceeding the best baseline (DrFuse) by 1.9% in AUROC for IHM. Under the partially matched (missing modality) setting on MIMIC-IV, CM² attains 0.858 AUROC / 0.527 AUPR (IHM) and 0.771 AUROC / 0.486 AUPR (READM), again leading all competitors. The tri-modal extension (EHR + CXR + radiology report) on MIMIC-IV yields 0.824 AUROC / 0.471 AUPR (IHM) and 0.730 AUROC / 0.444 AUPR (READM), maintaining its lead over baselines that degrade more sharply with added modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CM² outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset, task, and evaluation metric. The closest competitor is DrFuse (disentangled representation learning, AAAI 2024), which CM² surpasses by 0.9–1.9% AUROC and 3.2–3.6% AUPR on MIMIC-IV (totally matched), and by 0.1–0.3% AUROC and 0.9–1.1% AUPR under the partially matched setting. MMTM and Unified, which rely on simple concatenation or zero-filling for missing modalities, trail CM² by 2.5–7.8% AUROC on MIMIC-III IHM. DAFT (dynamic affine feature map transform) and MedFuse (LSTM-based fusion with global representation for missing data) are intermediate, with CM² leading by 1.2–1.9% AUROC on MIMIC-IV. The two additional transformer-based baselines, LSMT and Interleaved, achieve 0.803 and 0.800 AUROC respectively on MIMIC-IV IHM, both below CM²'s 0.827. Notably, under the tri-modal setting, baselines degrade more substantially (e.g., DrFuse drops from 0.818 to 0.808 AUROC) while CM² maintains a consistent lead, suggesting the copula-based alignment scales more gracefully with additional modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of multimodal fusion (concatenation, Kronecker products, cross-attention) by reframing modality alignment as a probabilistic distribution-matching problem. It draws on copula variational inference (Tran et al., 2015; Smith & Loaiza-Maya, 2023) and copula-based generative modelling (Tagasovska et al., 2019), but is the first to apply copula models as an explicit alignment mechanism in multimodal learning, grounded in Sklar's theorem for identifiability. The GMM marginals and stochastic variational inference for the copula parameter α are standard tools repurposed here, while the gradient-preserving sampling for missing-modality imputation extends the probabilistic framework to a practical clinical setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic fusion strategies, showing that explicitly modelling the joint distribution via a copula yields modest but consistent gains over cosine/KL alignment and attention-based baselines on MIMIC datasets. The natural handling of missing modalities through GMM sampling is a practical advantage in clinical settings where data incompleteness is the norm. However, the reliance on a single copula parameter α and the acknowledged non-convexity of the joint log-likelihood limit scalability to more complex dependence structures, and the evaluation is confined to healthcare data with at most three modalities, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to higher-dimensional or more heterogeneous multimodal settings.
