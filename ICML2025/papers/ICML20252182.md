## Problem

Existing deep AVR solvers rely on task-specific architectures, inductive biases, and hyperparameters, meaning each new AVR task (RPM, VAP, Odd-One-Out, SVRT) typically requires retraining or redesigning the model. This paper asks whether a single conditional generative framework can unify multiple AVR tasks so that one model, trained once on a multi-task mixture, can solve all of them—including unseen task types at test time—without any task-specific retraining or architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252182/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a problem panel of N context images (128×128 RGB) from any AVR task format (e.g., 8 of 9 images for RPM, 5 of 6 for VAP, 4 of 5 for O3, or 7 of 8 for SVRT) and optionally a candidate panel for selective tasks. It outputs a predictability estimate p(x | I_{-i}^p) over the discrete patch codebook, which is then interpreted by a lightweight, training-free judgment function: argmax over candidates for RPM/VAP, argmin over panel positions for Odd-One-Out, or pairwise comparison for SVRT. For generative tasks, the same predictability is sampled autoregressively to produce a full 128×128 target image.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is proving (Propositions 3.3–3.5) that RPM/VAP answer selection, Odd-One-Out identification, and SVRT categorization all reduce to estimating the conditional probability of a target image given the remaining panel images, unifying selective and generative AVR under a single predictability estimator. The instantiation UCGS-T uses a VQ-VAE image encoder/decoder to compress each panel image into M discrete patch codes, a patch encoder (12-layer Transformer decoder with K learnable CLS tokens) to extract image-level visual concepts, a concept encoder that groups concepts by type and uses a shared Transformer decoder to predict target concepts from context, and an autoregressive patch decoder that generates target patches conditioned on the predicted concepts. Training is multi-task supervised learning on RAVEN and PGM with a combined VQ-VAE reconstruction loss and cross-entropy patch prediction loss; the image encoder/decoder are pretrained then frozen. Because the judgment functions (argmax, argmin, pairwise comparison) are closed-form and task-specific but parameter-free, a single trained model handles all four AVR task types and generalizes zero-shot to unseen task formats.

## Results

UCGS-T achieves 64.6% selection accuracy on RAVEN and 38.1% on PGM (in-distribution RPM tasks), substantially outperforming the strongest ablation baseline TF+Patch (21.0% and 15.6% respectively). On in-distribution zero-shot tasks, UCGS-T reaches 33.6% on O3-ID, 35.8% on VAP-ID, and 84.6% on SVRT-ID. On out-of-distribution zero-shot tasks (G1-set, VAP, SVRT), UCGS-T scores 30.4%, 28.8%, and 52.8%, respectively, all above random guessing (22.5%, 25.0%, 50.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest ablation baseline TF+Patch, UCGS-T wins by a large margin on ID tasks (64.6% vs. 21.0% on RAVEN; 38.1% vs. 15.6% on PGM) and ID-ZS tasks (35.8% vs. 15.5% on VAP-ID; 84.6% vs. 71.8% on SVRT-ID), but the gap narrows on OOD-ZS tasks where both hover near random guessing. Against GPT-4o (evaluated on subsets), UCGS-T dominates on ID and ID-ZS tasks (e.g., 64.6% vs. 12.6% on RAVEN) but GPT-4o outperforms on OOD-ZS tasks (52.9% vs. 30.4% on G1-set; 64.2% vs. 52.8% on SVRT), likely due to GPT-4o's exposure to diverse visual concepts during pretraining. In the task-specific solver comparison (Table 2), UCGS-T (64.6% RAVEN, 38.1% PGM) outperforms all generative RPM solvers trained without rule annotations: RAISE (54.5%, 14.0%), ALANS (50.1%), GCA (37.3%, 31.7%), PrAE (13.6%), and NVSA (11.5%). ANP-based baselines perform near or below random on compositional RPM tasks, indicating they struggle with the multi-concept reasoning required by RAVEN and PGM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VQ-VAE's discrete representation learning for image encoding/decoding and VideoGPT's autoregressive discrete token generation for the patch decoder, but repurposes both within a unified conditional generative framework for abstract visual reasoning. It departs from the dominant task-specific paradigm in AVR (where each benchmark like RAVEN, VAP, O3, or SVRT requires a dedicated architecture or hyperparameter tuning) by proving that all these tasks reduce to estimating the predictability of a target image given context, enabling a single multi-task-trained model to handle them all. The work extends earlier conditional-generation approaches to AVR (Pekar et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2024) by formalizing the unification through explicit propositions and introducing a hierarchical patch→concept→panel reasoning architecture rather than operating on monolithic image representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified predictability framework offers a principled path toward a single AVR solver that generalizes across task formats without retraining, which is practically valuable for deploying reasoning systems across diverse cognitive tests. However, the absolute accuracy (64.6% on RAVEN) remains well below state-of-the-art selective solvers, and OOD-ZS performance hovers near random guessing (~30% on G1-set and VAP), limiting immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the framework scales to real-world scenes with richer visual concepts, whether the performance gap to selective solvers can be closed, and whether the zero-shot transfer to genuinely novel abstract rules (beyond in-distribution concepts) is achievable.
