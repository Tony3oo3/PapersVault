## Problem

Graph contrastive learning (GCL) relies on data augmentations to create positive/negative pairs, but existing heuristic methods (random edge dropping, node masking) lack theoretical grounding and often disrupt critical graph topology, leading to unstable and unreliable performance. The paper identifies that these predefined augmentations are merely point estimations of a theoretically defined 'positive-incentive noise' (π-noise) that should reduce task entropy, and that they frequently fail as strong estimators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,S) with node feature matrices and edge connectivity as input, and produces node-level or graph-level embeddings for downstream linear-probe evaluation. The noise generator outputs two components: a per-edge Bernoulli dropping probability (topological noise) and a per-node Gaussian perturbation vector (attribute noise), both conditioned on the node's features. The augmented graph view is fed through the same GNN encoder as the original graph to form contrastive pairs.

## Method

PiNGDA introduces a Gaussian auxiliary variable whose variance is set to the inverse of the contrastive confidence κ(u,ε), converting the InfoNCE loss into an information-entropy quantity that bridges GCL with the π-noise framework. The key theoretical result shows that standard GCL with a fixed augmentation is equivalent to maximizing mutual information under a Dirac-delta (point-estimation) assumption on the noise distribution, explaining why heuristic augmentations are unreliable. Instead of point estimation, PiNGDA learns a parametric noise distribution p_ψ(ε|u) by jointly optimizing the contrastive loss in expectation over the learned noise. The topological noise generator is a two-layer MLP producing per-edge dropping probabilities sampled via Gumbel-Softmax for differentiability, while the attribute noise generator uses two MLPs to predict the mean and diagonal covariance of a Gaussian perturbation applied via the reparameterization trick. The final loss (Eq. 15) is the expected negative log-contrastive-ratio over the learned noise, trained end-to-end with the GNN encoder.

## Results

On node classification across seven benchmark datasets, PiNGDA achieves the best accuracy on 5 of 7 datasets (Cora 86.25%, CiteSeer 72.44%, PubMed 87.34%, Wiki-CS 82.07%, Amazon-Photo 93.29%) and a best average rank of 1.4. On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv (169K nodes), PiNGDA reaches 68.72% accuracy, within 0.7% of the supervised GCN (69.42%), while several unsupervised baselines (DGI, GMI, GAT, GREET) run out of memory. On graph classification (Table 2), PiNGDA achieves top or near-top accuracy on all five TUDataset benchmarks, e.g., 89.71% on MUTAG and 75.51% on DD, with notably lower variance than competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiNGDA outperforms all non-learnable self-supervised baselines (DGI, GMI, GCA, BGRL, GREET, SGRL, GRACEIS, GOUDA, GASSL) on the majority of node-classification datasets, with the largest margins over GCA (+1.05% on Cora, +0.27% on PubMed) and GRACEIS (+1.68% on Cora, +2.79% on ogbn-arxiv). Among learnable methods, PiNGDA consistently beats AD-GCL (which only learns topology) by 2.37% on Cora, 3.70% on CiteSeer, and 3.11% on PubMed, and surpasses JOAO by 3.48% on Cora and 14.06% on Amazon-Photo, where JOAO's similarity-driven policy degrades severely. On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv, PiNGDA (68.72%) trails only the supervised GCN (69.42%) while matching or exceeding all unsupervised methods that do not OOM. On graph classification, PiNGDA edges out AD-GCL on MUTAG (89.71 vs. 89.70) and DD (75.51 vs. 75.10) but is slightly behind on NCI1 (69.35 vs. 69.67) and PROTEINS (73.21 vs. 73.81), while clearly surpassing InfoGraph, GraphCL, and RU-GIN across all five TUDataset benchmarks. As a plug-in augmentation, PiNGDA improves GRACE by up to +0.89% (PubMed) and Sp²GCL by up to +1.67% (CiteSeer), confirming its generalizability across GCL frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Positive-incentive Noise (Pi-Noise) framework from Li (2022) and its vision-contrastive-learning instantiation (Zhang et al., 2024) to the graph domain, providing a theoretical bridge between standard GCL and noise-driven augmentation. It departs from both heuristic augmentation methods (random edge/node dropping in DGI, GMI, GCA) and prior learnable-augmentation approaches (AD-GCL, JOAO) by reframing the augmentation problem as learning a full noise distribution p(ε|u) rather than selecting from or optimizing within a fixed set of operations. The key conceptual move is showing that any predefined GCL augmentation is merely a Dirac-delta point estimate of Pi-Noise (Eq. 10), which explains the observed instability of heuristic methods and motivates the learned noise generator with Gumbel-Softmax (topology) and reparameterization (attributes).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between standard GCL loss and point-estimation of Pi-Noise gives practitioners a principled explanation for why heuristic augmentations are unreliable, and the PiNGDA framework is drop-in compatible with existing GCL models (validated on GRACE and Sp²GCL). Limitations include testing only up to ogbn-arxiv (~170K nodes), a node-level derivation that is stated but not fully worked out for graph-level contrast, and the added parameter overhead of the noise generators. Open questions include whether the learned noise distributions generalize across graph scales and domains, whether alternative noise families (beyond Bernoulli/Gaussian) yield further gains, and whether the Pi-Noise lens can unify augmentation design across modalities beyond graphs.
