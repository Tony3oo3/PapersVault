## Problem

The original Skew Spectrum (Kondor & Borgwardt, 2008) provides a rigorous group-theoretic graph invariant but is restricted to simple weighted/directed graphs and offers no principled mechanism to trade computational cost against expressivity. As real-world graph data increasingly involves node/edge features, multilayer structures, and hypergraphs, a generalization that handles these richer structures while maintaining provable invariance and controllable complexity is needed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes graph data encoded as a vector-valued function f: S_n/S_{n-2} → C^d, where each component f_i corresponds to one orbit (e.g., adjacency entries, node features, edge features, or hypergraph incidence). The output is a collection of sparse matrix entries indexed by group elements from the double coset transversal S_{n-2}\S_n/S_{n-2} and four irreducible representations (partitions (n), (n-1,1), (n-2,2), (n-2,1,1)), yielding up to C(7,k-2)·7·d^k non-zero scalar features for the doubly-reduced k-Spectrum. The embedding is permutation-invariant: isomorphic graphs produce identical feature vectors regardless of node numbering.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-layer generalization of the Skew Spectrum grounded in harmonic analysis on the symmetric group. First, Multi-Orbit Spectra replace scalar functions with vector-valued functions (direct sums over orbits) and substitute the pointwise product with a tensor product, introducing cross-orbit interference terms that capture correlations between, e.g., adjacency and node labels. Second, k-Correlation Spectra extend the triple correlation (k=3, the original Skew Spectrum) to arbitrary order k, with the invariant defined as the Fourier transform over the last entry of the k-correlation, enabling a principled expressivity–complexity tradeoff. Third, Doubly-Reduced k-Spectra apply two heuristics: (i) projecting the irrep onto the trivial representation of S_{n-2} via τ_{S_{n-2}}, which enforces a fixed sparsity pattern and reduces computation to O(n^3), and (ii) restricting the group-element arguments G_{k-2} to combinations of the 7 double-coset representatives, limiting entries to C(7,k-2) and capping k at 9. A divide-and-conquer dynamic programming algorithm (Algorithm 2) precomputes the intermediate sums in O(d^{k-2}n^3 + d^{k-1}n^2) operations, making the full doubly-reduced k-Spectrum computable in O(d^{k-1}n^3 + d^k n^2) — matching the original Skew Spectrum's cost for k=3 while adding expressivity for k>3.

## Results

On QM7 atomization energy regression, the 2-Orbit Skew Spectrum with XGBoost achieves 18.28 MAE, a 37% improvement over the Single-Orbit Skew Spectrum (29.15 MAE) and outperforming Laplacian eigenvalues (23.52 MAE). On the synthetic 4-family classification task, the 2-Orbit representation achieves 100% accuracy with a Random Forest, while the Single-Orbit concatenation achieves only 50%. For expressivity, the concatenated Doubly-Reduced k-Spectra (k∈[3,9]) form a complete invariant on all 7-node graphs (zero collisions), outperforming both Laplacian eigenvalues and 1-WL, though 1-WL retains an advantage on 8-node chordal graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 2-Orbit Skew Spectrum consistently outperforms the Single-Orbit Skew Spectrum across all four regression models on QM7, with the largest gain under XGBoost (18.28 vs. 29.15 MAE) and Elastic Net (58.60 vs. 114.68 MAE). It also beats Laplacian eigenvalues under XGB (18.28 vs. 23.52) and GBR (27.12 vs. 26.93, marginal), while Laplacian eigenvalues remain competitive under EN and Linear. Against the 1-WL test, the concatenated k-Spectra achieve zero collisions on all 7-node graphs (a complete invariant), whereas 1-WL still produces collisions; however, on 8-node chordal graphs, 1-WL yields fewer collisions than the concatenated spectra, confirming the two methods capture complementary structural properties. In the GNN integration experiment (Appendix D), concatenating k-Spectra features to the HGP-SL baseline (Zhang et al., 2019) on PROTEINS improves average accuracy from 0.508 (dropout 0.5) / 0.518 (dropout 0.2) to higher values, with k=4 generally outperforming k=3 and multi-orbit variants outperforming single-orbit variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes the Skew Spectrum of Kondor & Borgwardt (2008), extending it along three axes: (1) multi-orbit functions to handle attributed graphs, multilayer graphs, and hypergraphs that the original could not represent; (2) k-correlation spectra that trade computational cost for expressivity, building on the Fourier-analytic framework of Clausen & Hühne (2017) for efficient transforms on S_n/S_{n-2}; and (3) a doubly-reduced variant that recovers the original's O(n³) complexity while capturing higher-order structure. The paper positions k-Spectra as complementary to the Weisfeiler-Lehman hierarchy, noting that k-correlations count edge-level substructures while k-WL refines node-tuple representations, and proposes concrete integration strategies with GNN pipelines (post-concatenation, aggregation, pooling).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key open question is whether a finite k_max exists such that k-correlations up to k_max form a complete invariant for all n-node graphs; the paper conjectures k_max ∈ O(n²) but does not prove it. Practically, the doubly-reduced k-Spectra offer a principled way to increase expressivity at the same computational cost as the original Skew Spectrum, which is valuable for settings where the 1-WL test (and hence standard GNNs) provably fails. However, the experimental evidence is preliminary: QM9/ZINC results show the multi-orbit spectra are competitive but not consistently best, and the GNN integration on PROTEINS uses only 3–30 node graphs with mixed improvements, leaving scalability to larger, real-world graphs untested.
