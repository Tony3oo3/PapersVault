## Problem

Data mixing for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs remains underexplored relative to pre-training, and existing pre-training data-mixing methods do not transfer to SFT because proxy-model weights may not generalize across model scales and loss parameterizations that ignore domain interactions produce unbalanced weight distributions at larger data budgets. This paper asks: can we derive optimal domain weights for SFT that are scale-dependent, account for cross-domain transfer effects, and provably prevent any domain from underperforming?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM, K multi-domain training sources, per-domain held-out validation sets, and a target data budget N₀ (in tokens). It first runs small-scale perturbation experiments (varying one domain's token count across 5 ratios while fixing others) to fit five per-domain parameters, then outputs a single optimal weight vector w* on the K-dimensional probability simplex. The weight vector is budget-conditional: re-solving the convex program at a different N₀ yields different weights, and the same fitted parameters can be reused across model sizes within a class.

## Method

The core contribution is a per-domain loss parameterization L̃_i = C_i·(N_i + k_i·|D_\i|^α_i)^(−β_i) + E_i that unifies effective data from transfer (Hernandez et al., 2021) with the power-law scaling of fine-tuning (Zhang et al., 2024), capturing both in-domain data and cross-domain knowledge transfer. Parameters are fitted via perturbation experiments using Huber loss with a Trust Region constraint ensuring transferred effective data does not exceed the actual out-of-domain token count. The resulting optimization over the simplex is proven convex (composition of a concave inner function with a convex, non-increasing outer function), and solved with SLSQP. A key theoretical result (Theorem C.1) shows that the power-law structure enforces diminishing marginal returns, guaranteeing that no domain is assigned zero weight unless its marginal gain is strictly dominated by all others—providing a built-in 'no domains left behind' property without explicit balance constraints.

## Results

On the Tulu3 re-weighting experiment, the proposed method achieves the lowest average validation PPL across all three model sizes: 2.150 (Llama3.1-8B), 2.080 (Llama3.1-70B), and 2.133 (Qwen2.5-32B), outperforming Original (2.162/2.127/2.165), Equal_T (2.817/2.244/2.246), and Equal_I (2.773/2.212/2.244). On downstream tasks for Tulu3, the method achieves the best average score for Llama3.1-8B (50.4) and Llama3.1-70B (63.8), and is within 0.5 points of the best for Qwen2.5-32B (68.0 vs. 68.5 for Equal_I). In the controlled 3-domain mixing experiment, the method's overall PPL is only 0.66% higher than the best grid-search PPL on average across all models and data budgets (5M, 20M, 200M tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines for SFT collection re-weighting (Original, Equal_T, Equal_I) and against exhaustive grid search (21 valid mixtures) for the controlled 3-domain experiment. Against grid search, the method's overall PPL is only 0.66% higher on average, with per-domain PPL gaps typically under 0.07 for Llama models but reaching 0.212 for IF on Qwen2.5-0.5B at 200M tokens. Against the Tulu3 baselines, the method wins on average PPL for all three models, with the largest margin over Equal_T (e.g., 2.150 vs. 2.817 for Llama3.1-8B, a 23.5% reduction). On downstream tasks, the method achieves the best average for Llama3.1-8B (50.4 vs. 50.1 Original, 49.1 Equal_T, 47.7 Equal_I) and Llama3.1-70B (63.8 vs. 61.1 Original, 63.6 Equal_T, 60.2 Equal_I), but trails Equal_I by 0.5 points on Qwen2.5-32B (68.0 vs. 68.5). A notable weakness is IFEval for Llama models, where the method scores 6+ points below Original weights, likely due to repeated sampling of the small precise-IF domain causing overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data mixing optimization literature from pre-training (DoReMi, DoGE, Data Mixing Laws, RegMix, Aioli) to supervised fine-tuning, where it was previously underexplored. It combines two established ideas—effective data from transfer (Hernandez et al., 2021) and the power-law scaling for fine-tuning (Zhang et al., 2024)—into a novel SFT-specific loss parameterization, and borrows the perturbation-experiment design from AutoScale (Kang et al., 2024) for parameter fitting. The key departure from prior work is that the resulting optimal weights are explicitly model- and scale-dependent, in contrast to the scale-invariance assumptions in DoReMi/DoGE and the prompt-embedding-based invariance in SMART, the only prior SFT-specific method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-cost procedure (a handful of small-scale perturbation runs plus a convex solve) for practitioners to determine SFT data mixtures without expensive grid search, and the 'no domains left behind' guarantee is valuable for general-purpose model training. However, the Orca experiments reveal that lower validation PPL does not reliably translate to better downstream performance when domains are defined by data source rather than task, and the IFEval degradation for Llama models highlights a tension between balanced weighting and task-specific overfitting from repeated sampling. Open questions include whether the parameterization can be extended to directly model downstream task performance and whether synergy with synthetic data generation can overcome the repeated-sampling limitation.
