## Problem

Benchmark Data Contamination (BDC) inflates LLM performance estimates, and while many mitigation strategies (paraphrasing, translation, choice permutation, etc.) have been proposed to update existing benchmarks, no rigorous framework exists to assess whether these strategies actually work. Existing evaluation practices—accuracy drop and accuracy matching—rely solely on aggregate accuracy, which can be misleading: a strategy may match overall accuracy while fundamentally altering which questions the model gets right, thereby changing the benchmark's evaluation objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an uncontaminated LLM (verified via three independent BDC detection methods: Min-K% Prob, Sharded Rank Comparison Test, and TS-Guessing), a manually contaminated version (produced by full-parameter fine-tuning on the benchmark under mild or intensive recipes), the original benchmark, and an updated benchmark produced by a given mitigation strategy. It outputs binary question-level evaluation vectors (one bit per question indicating correct/incorrect) for three configurations—clean model on original, clean model on updated, and contaminated model on updated—from which fidelity and resistance scores are derived as 1 minus the normalized Hamming distance between the relevant vector pairs.

## Method

The paper introduces two novel metrics—fidelity (degree to which the clean model's per-question correctness is preserved between original and updated benchmarks) and contamination resistance (degree to which the contaminated model's per-question correctness on the updated benchmark matches the clean model's)—both computed via normalized Hamming distance over binary evaluation vectors rather than scalar accuracy. The controlled pipeline first verifies that all 10 LLMs (3B–34B parameters) are uncontaminated on all 5 benchmarks using three detection methods from distinct categories, then manually introduces contamination via two fine-tuning recipes (mild: benchmark mixed with 20K OpenOrca samples for 1 epoch; intensive: benchmark-only for 3 epochs), with three validation checks confirming effective memorization without general capability degradation. Twenty mitigation strategies spanning semantic-preserving (16 strategies including single perturbations and combinations like Clean-Eval, ITD, MPA) and semantic-altering (4 LLM-generated strategies) categories are each applied independently, and one-sided paired hypothesis tests determine whether resistance improvements over the vanilla (no-update) baseline are statistically significant at the 0.05 level.

## Results

Across 10 LLMs (3B–34B) and 5 benchmarks, no mitigation strategy simultaneously achieves high fidelity (>0.9) and statistically significant contamination resistance over the vanilla (no-update) baseline. Among semantic-preserving strategies, MPA achieves the highest resistance (e.g., 0.921/0.912 mild/intensive on Arc-C) but the lowest fidelity (0.719), while minor edits like synonym replacement maintain fidelity >0.90 yet fail to significantly improve resistance. Semantic-altering strategies (e.g., Remember-Understand Extension) reach ~0.97 resistance on Arc-C and MMLU but drop fidelity to ~0.66–0.77, roughly 0.15 below semantic-preserving counterparts. One-sided paired tests confirm that no single strategy yields a significant resistance gain over vanilla across all five benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 20 mitigation strategies drawn from six prior works against the vanilla (no-update) baseline using question-level fidelity and contamination resistance. MPA (Zhu et al., ICML 2024) achieves the highest resistance among semantic-preserving strategies (e.g., 0.921 mild on Arc-C vs. 0.923 vanilla) with statistically significant gains on MMLU, TruthfulQA, and RepliQA, but at the cost of the lowest fidelity (0.686 on MMLU). ITD (Zhu et al., 2024) and Clean-Eval (Zhu et al., 2023) offer moderate resistance improvements with better fidelity (0.846 and 0.893 on Arc-C, respectively), yet none of these strategies significantly outperforms vanilla across all five benchmarks simultaneously. Minor-edit strategies from Wang et al. (2021) (Irrelevant Context, Typographical Perturbation) and Synonym Replacement (Zhu et al., 2023) preserve fidelity above 0.90 but fail to produce a statistically significant resistance gain over vanilla on any benchmark. Translation-based strategies from Li et al. (2024, C2Leva) show a notable pattern: Translation (Chinese) and (French) achieve very high resistance on RepliQA (0.966/0.954) but sacrifice fidelity to ~0.76–0.80 on MMLU. Semantic-altering strategies from Ying et al. (NeurIPS 2024) (Mimicking, Remember-Understand/Application/Analysis Extension) reach ~0.97 resistance on Arc-C and MMLU—clearly beating all semantic-preserving methods—but drop fidelity to 0.66–0.77, roughly 0.15 below the best semantic-preserving strategies, and require LLM-generated answers that risk introducing errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing a rigorous evaluation framework for BDC mitigation strategies, departing from prior assessment methods (accuracy drop, accuracy matching) that rely solely on aggregate accuracy. Rather than introducing a new mitigation technique, it systematically assesses 20 existing strategies—spanning semantic-preserving updates (Clean-Eval, ITD, MPA, translation, synonym replacement) and semantic-altering updates (mimicking, extension strategies from Ying et al.)—using two novel question-level metrics: fidelity and contamination resistance, computed via normalized Hamming distance. The controlled pipeline also departs from prior work by verifying uncontaminated status via three independent detection methods before manual contamination, and by testing two distinct contamination recipes (mild and intensive) to ensure robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that no existing strategy simultaneously achieves high fidelity and high contamination resistance—challenges the widespread assumption that surface-level modifications (paraphrasing, synonym replacement, choice permutation) meaningfully reduce BDC impact. This has direct practical implications: benchmark developers and evaluation practitioners may be investing effort in strategies that provide no statistically significant improvement over the vanilla (unmodified) benchmark. The open question is how to design strategies that break memorization without altering the evaluation objective, and whether the proposed fidelity-resistance framework can serve as a standard for evaluating future mitigation approaches. Limitations include focus on 3B–34B models and multiple-choice/open-ended benchmarks, leaving frontier-scale models and more complex evaluation tasks unaddressed.
