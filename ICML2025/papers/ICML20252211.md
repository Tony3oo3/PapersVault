## Problem

Existing W2 convergence bounds for score-based generative models require strong log-concavity of the data distribution and stringent regularity conditions on the score function or its estimator, limiting applicability to broad classes of real-world data. This paper closes that gap by establishing W2 convergence under only weak log-concavity and one-sided log-Lipschitz assumptions on the data, with no explicit regularity imposed on the score or its estimator.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data distribution π_data on R^d characterized by weak log-concavity constants (α, M) and a one-sided log-Lipschitz constant L_U, together with a trained score network approximating the modified score ∇log p̃_t. It produces synthetic samples via Euler-Maruyama discretization of the backward OU SDE, accompanied by a fully explicit W2 convergence bound in which every constant is transparently expressed in terms of α, M, L_U, the second moment m_2, dimension d, step size h, and score estimation error ε—unlike prior results that leave constants implicit or arbitrarily rescaled.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits the regularizing effect of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward process: weak log-concavity of the data distribution is shown to evolve into full log-concavity over time, with the critical transition time ξ(α,M) derived explicitly via a PDE-based analysis of the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation governing the log-density. The backward SDE drift is characterized as alternating between contractive and non-contractive regimes, with the transition point T(α,M,0) given in closed form. The W2 error is decomposed via synchronous coupling into three components—discretization (√h·√d·√m_2·T), initialization (e^{−T}·W2(π_data, π_∞)), and score approximation (εT)—each bounded using regularity properties propagated along the HJB flow. A key distinction from prior work is that the score function's Lipschitz and weak-convexity properties are derived directly from the data assumptions through the OU semigroup, eliminating the need for separate regularity hypotheses on the score or its neural estimator.

## Results

The main result (Theorem C.1) establishes a fully explicit W2 convergence bound: W2(π_data, L(X_T*)) ≤ e^{3Lη(α,M,9L²h/2)} [e^{-T} W2(π_data, π_∞) + 4ε·T(α,M,0) + √(2h)(√(m_2+d) + 6L√d)·T(α,M,0)], where the constant depends exponentially on the data parameters α, M, L_U. The bound decomposes into three error terms: an initialization error decaying as e^{-T}, a score-estimation error linear in εT, and a discretization error scaling as √h·√d·√m_2·T. The paper further shows that weak log-concavity of the data distribution transitions to full log-concavity at time ξ(α,M), and the backward drift switches from non-contractive to contractive at time T(α,M,0), providing explicit formulas for both transition points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. In terms of theoretical contributions, the paper relaxes assumptions relative to prior W2 convergence results: unlike Gao et al. (2023), Bruno et al. (2023), Tang & Zhao (2024), and Strasman et al. (2024), which require strong log-concavity of the data distribution, this work only requires weak log-concavity (κ_U(r) ≥ α − f_M(r)/r) and one-sided log-Lipschitz continuity. Unlike Lee et al. (2022; 2023) and Chen et al. (2022a), which impose smoothness or boundedness conditions on the score function or its estimator, this paper derives score regularity directly from the data assumptions via the HJB equation, requiring no additional regularity on the score network. The resulting bound matches the standard dependencies on T, ε, d, h, and m_2 found in KL-divergence analyses (Conforti et al., 2023a; Chen et al., 2023), but is expressed in W2 distance and derived via coupling techniques rather than information-theoretic arguments. The Gaussian mixture example (Proposition 4.1) demonstrates that the assumptions are satisfied by a broad and practically relevant class of distributions, with explicit constants α_{p_n} = 1/max(σ_i²) and √M_{p_n} = 2n·Σ‖μ_i‖/σ_i².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PDE-based analysis of SGMs initiated by Conforti et al. (2023a) and Berner et al. (2022), which connected the log-density of the forward process to a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, from KL-divergence bounds to W2-distance bounds via coupling techniques. It builds directly on Conforti (2024)'s weak log-concavity framework and controlled coupling, and on Conforti et al. (2023b)'s propagation of weak convexity along the OU flow, to derive W2 convergence under only weak log-concavity and one-sided log-Lipschitz assumptions on the data. The key departure from prior W2 results (De Bortoli et al. 2021a, Lee et al. 2023, Gao et al. 2023, Bruno et al. 2023, Strasman et al. 2024) is the elimination of explicit regularity requirements on the score function or its estimator, which are instead derived as consequences of the data distribution's properties through the HJB equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that weak log-concavity suffices (and is satisfied by Gaussian mixtures), this work substantially broadens the class of distributions for which rigorous W2 convergence guarantees hold, moving beyond the strongly log-concave regime that dominated prior analysis. The regime-switching characterization—identifying the precise time at which the backward drift transitions from non-contractive to contractive—provides actionable guidance for designing noise schedules and neural architectures in practice. A notable limitation is the exponential dependence of the constant on the weak convexity parameters (α, M, L_U), which the authors flag as an open problem for future improvement to polynomial dependence. The framework also naturally extends to early-stopping regimes via Gaussian kernel convolution, connecting to practical SGM implementations.
