## Problem

Satisfiability Modulo Counting (SMC) integrates symbolic Boolean constraints with probabilistic marginal constraints, but no efficient exact solver exists. Approximate solvers (SAA, XOR-SMC) may return solutions that violate constraints, while naively combining a SAT solver with a probabilistic inference solver causes excessive back-and-forth invocations and exhaustive enumeration on unsatisfiable instances. The paper addresses the need for an exact solver that detects probabilistic conflicts early, before all variables are assigned.

## Input / Output

The solver takes a CNF Boolean formula φ over decision variables x and probabilistic predicates b, a set of unnormalized probability distributions (given as Bayesian networks or MRFs in UAI file format) over x and latent variables y, and scalar thresholds q_j. Distributions are first compiled into smooth and decomposable probabilistic circuits via ACE. The output is a SAT/UNSAT verdict together with a complete variable assignment for x and b that satisfies both the Boolean formula and all marginal probability constraints (e.g., Σ_y P(x,y) ≥ q).

## Method

KOCO-SMC extends the CDCL framework by introducing the Upper-Lower Watch (ULW) algorithm for probabilistic constraint propagation. After compiling each distribution into a smooth and decomposable probabilistic circuit, ULW maintains an upper bound UB(v) and lower bound LB(v) at every circuit node, updated incrementally via bottom-up traversal whenever a new variable is assigned. For unassigned variables, leaf bounds take the max/min over both truth values; for marginalized-out variables, bounds are set to 1. If the root's UB falls below the threshold q, a conflict is immediately detected and a learned clause (the negation of the responsible partial assignment) is added to the Boolean formula, pruning the search space without requiring a full assignment. This early conflict detection avoids the exhaustive enumeration that plagues sequential SAT-plus-inference pipelines, particularly on unsatisfiable instances.

## Results

On a 1,350-instance benchmark (UAI 2010–2022 probabilistic models × 3-coloring CNFs × 3 thresholds), Koco-SMC solves 45% of instances within 10 seconds and 85% within 3 hours, whereas the best exact-solver baselines (Lingeling + UAI10/HBFS/D4/ADDMC/SSTD) solve at most 40% within 3 hours. Under a 10-minute limit, Koco-SMC returns verified exact solutions for the majority of instances, while approximate baselines (Gibbs-SAA, BP-SAA, XOR-SMC) produce incorrect or unverifiable answers on a non-trivial fraction. The ULW ablation shows a 10× speedup over Koco-SMC without ULW at high thresholds (q ≈ 10⁶), confirming that early conflict detection is the primary source of the efficiency gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five exact-solver baselines (Lingeling/MiniSAT/CaDiCal paired with UAI10, HBFS, D4, ADDMC, or SharpSAT-TD), Koco-SMC dominates on solving speed: it reaches 80% completion in 20 minutes while the best baseline reaches only ~40% in 3 hours, a gap that widens as the threshold q approaches the UNSAT boundary where baselines must exhaustively enumerate all assignments. Against the three approximate baselines (Gibbs-SAA, BP-SAA, XOR-SMC), Koco-SMC wins on solution quality—every returned answer is a verified exact solution—whereas XOR-SMC occasionally returns assignments that violate the probabilistic constraint and SAA-based methods can time out or produce unverifiable outputs. The ULW ablation (Koco-SMC without ULW) confirms that the bound-tracking mechanism, not the underlying SAT solver, is responsible for the 10× speedup at high thresholds. On the two real-world case studies (supply-chain design on 20/28/44-node networks and Hamiltonian-path package delivery on 8/18/24-node LA road maps), Koco-SMC finds the optimal plan in significantly less wall-clock time than all exact baselines, with no approximate baseline able to guarantee correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

KOCO-SMC extends the Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) framework from modern SAT solvers (Silva & Sakallah, 1996; Een & Sörensson, 2003) by adding a novel propagation step for probabilistic constraints. Where prior exact SMC approaches naively alternate between a SAT solver and a separate probabilistic inference solver in a sequential loop, this paper integrates both by compiling distributions into smooth and decomposable probabilistic circuits (via ACE, Darwiche & Marquis, 2002) and tracking upper/lower bounds of marginal probabilities under partial assignments through the ULW algorithm. This departs from approximate SMC methods (SAA-based sampling, XOR-SMC) that sacrifice exactness for speed, and from prior bound-tracking work (Dubray et al., 2024; Ge & Biere, 2024) that operates on DPLL-style search rather than CDCL with learned clauses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that early conflict detection via bound tracking on probabilistic circuits can reduce solving time by orders of magnitude for UNSAT SMC instances, where naive exact solvers must exhaustively enumerate all assignments. However, the experimental scope is limited: only one probabilistic constraint is tested, Boolean formulas are restricted to 3-coloring grid problems, and the knowledge compilation step introduces a fixed overhead that makes KOCO-SMC slower than baselines on easy (low-threshold) instances. Open questions include scaling to multiple interacting probabilistic constraints, more diverse Boolean formula structures, and whether the compilation overhead can be amortized in incremental or online settings.
