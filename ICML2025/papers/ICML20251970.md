## Problem

Existing theoretical convergence guarantees for diffusion models rely on worst-case bounds that are too loose to explain their practical efficiency, while empirical evaluation is limited to FID in Inception feature space. No prior work provides exact, closed-form error decompositions for the four error sources (initialization, truncation, discretization, score approximation) in data space. This paper fills that gap by restricting the data distribution to Gaussian, where the score is linear and all solutions become analytically tractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target centered Gaussian distribution specified by its covariance matrix Σ (e.g., the empirical covariance of CIFAR-10 at 3×32×32 or an ADSN microtexture at 3×256×256), a noise schedule β(t), and either the exact linear score or a learned U-Net score. Beyond producing samples, it outputs exact 2-Wasserstein distances computed directly in data space via the closed-form Gaussian W₂ formula, enabling per-eigenvalue error attribution and ablation across discretization schemes, truncation times, and initialization choices.

## Method

Under the Gaussian assumption, the score ∇log p_t(x) = −Σ_t⁻¹x is linear, which allows the authors to derive closed-form solutions for both the backward SDE (Proposition 2) and the probability flow ODE (Proposition 3, which is the OT map between p_T and p_{T−t}). They prove that all six studied discretization schemes (EM, EI, DDPM, Euler, Heun, RK4) preserve Gaussianity, so the covariance eigenvalues evolve via simple scalar recurrences (Table 4), making the 2-Wasserstein distance computable at every step. A key theoretical result (Proposition 4) shows the continuous SDE sampler always achieves lower W₂ error than the continuous ODE sampler, and the ablation study confirms Heun's method as the best discrete scheme at standard truncation ε=10⁻³. Finally, an empirical study with a learned U-Net score on ADSN microtextures reveals that score approximation dominates all other errors and that the stochastic EM sampler is more resilient to score mismatch than deterministic Heun's method.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 Gaussian (NFE=500, ε=10⁻³), Heun's method and RK4 achieve the lowest 2-Wasserstein error (0.18 and 0.17 respectively), substantially outperforming Euler-Maruyama (0.40), Exponential Integrator (0.41), and DDPM (0.53). The continuous SDE is provably better than the continuous ODE (Proposition 4), with the SDE initialization error being orders of magnitude smaller. On the ADSN microtexture with a learned U-Net score, the score approximation dominates all other errors: EM yields W₂=15.6 vs. 5.16 with the exact score, while Heun degrades far more severely to W₂=56.7 vs. 3.73, showing that stochastic sampling is more resilient to score approximation than deterministic schemes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six discretization schemes (EM, EI, DDPM for the SDE; Euler, Heun, RK4 for the ODE) on the CIFAR-10 Gaussian. Heun's method (Karras et al., 2022) and RK4 achieve the lowest Wasserstein error (0.16–0.19 at NFE=500, ε=10⁻³), confirming Karras's empirical recommendation with a theoretical justification. DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) performs worst among all schemes at 0.53 (NFE=500, ε=10⁻³), roughly 3× worse than Heun. The Exponential Integrator (De Bortoli, 2022) is more stable than EM but still lags behind Heun/RK4 (0.41 vs. 0.18). A key finding is that the SDE sampler is provably more resilient to initialization error than the ODE sampler (Proposition 4), and in the learned-score setting (Table 2), this translates to EM (W₂=15.6) being far more robust to score approximation than Heun (W₂=56.7), reversing the exact-score ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021b) and DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), extending the theoretical convergence analysis line (Chen et al., 2023b; Lee et al., 2024; De Bortoli et al., 2021) by restricting the data distribution to Gaussian, where the score is linear and exact solutions become tractable. It connects to the optimal transport literature (Lavenant & Santambrogio, 2022; Khrulkov et al., 2023) by showing the probability flow ODE solution is precisely the OT map between consecutive marginals, while highlighting that the standard N₀ initialization breaks the OT property. Unlike prior convergence bounds that rely on worst-case L² score errors and compactness assumptions, this work derives exact closed-form Wasserstein errors for all four error sources (initialization, truncation, discretization, score approximation) under the Gaussian assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact error decomposition provides a principled, data-space alternative to FID-based evaluation and confirms via rigorous analysis that Heun's method is the preferred ODE sampler, aligning with empirical recommendations from Karras et al. (2022). The key limitation is the Gaussian restriction: generalizing to multimodal distributions (e.g., GMMs) faces open problems in deriving exact backward SDE solutions and computing closed-form Wasserstein distances. The empirical finding that stochastic EM outperforms deterministic Heun's method under score approximation error (Section 5) suggests that the interplay between discretization order and score quality is underexplored, pointing to an important open question for practical diffusion model design.
