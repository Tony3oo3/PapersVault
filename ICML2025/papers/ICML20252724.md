## Problem

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is widely deployed in safety-critical domains like healthcare and legal Q&A, yet the retrieval component remains vulnerable to poisoning attacks where adversaries inject crafted documents that are reliably retrieved for specific queries. Existing defenses, such as examining the ℓ₂-norm of document embeddings, have been shown to be ineffective, and the underlying mechanism enabling such attacks in dense retrievers was not previously understood.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a set of attacker-specified text queries and targeted information (e.g., PII, adversarial treatment recommendations, synthetic legal content) and produces poisoned documents formed by token-level concatenation of query and target, which are then injected into a corpus (PubMed, StatPearls, Textbook, or ALQA). The defense takes an anchor set of clean document embeddings associated with protected queries and produces a shrinkage-regularized Mahalanobis distance score for each candidate document, with a threshold at the 95th quantile of anchor scores to classify documents as poisoned or clean. The attack is evaluated by top-K retrieval success rate (K=1 or 2) across 225 combinations of corpus, retriever, query set, and target type.

## Method

The paper identifies an 'orthogonal augmentation property' of dense retrievers: when two documents p and q have near-orthogonal embeddings, the embedding of their concatenation [q⊕p] shifts from f(q) in a direction approximately perpendicular to f(q), preserving the inner-product similarity between q and [q⊕p]. This explains why appending semantically unrelated targeted information to a query still yields high retrieval similarity, enabling universal poisoning across diverse target types. The defense exploits the complementary observation that clean retrieved documents are also near-orthogonal to their queries (average angle ~70° for Contriever), so poisoned documents—which stay close to the query—become perpendicular to clean documents. The proposed detector computes a shrinkage-regularized Mahalanobis distance s(X) = (X−μ)ᵀΣ_β⁻¹(X−μ) using an anchor set of clean document embeddings, where Σ_β = (1−β)S + (β/d)Tr(S)I_d regularizes the sample covariance to handle high-dimensional instability, achieving near-perfect detection (>98%) across all tested attack configurations.

## Results

Across 225 combinations of 3 corpora (Textbook, StatPearls, PubMed), 3 retrievers (MedCPT, SPECTER, Contriever), 5 query sets, and 5 target information types, the universal poisoning attack achieves top-2 retrieval success rates ranging from 0.58 to 1.0, with most configurations exceeding 0.80. The attack remains robust under GPT-4 query paraphrasing, maintaining ~0.80 top-2 success rates. The proposed Mahalanobis-distance-based defense achieves >98% detection rates in nearly all cases, while the ℓ₂-norm baseline fails with <50% detection (except under Contriever, where it reaches ~95% due to length sensitivity). Applied to the PoisonedRAG attack (Zou et al., 2024), the defense achieves a filtering rate exceeding 95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed Mahalanobis-distance-based defense against the ℓ₂-norm defense (Deng et al., 2024) across all 225 attack configurations. The ℓ₂-norm baseline fails to detect poisoned documents, achieving less than 50% detection rate in nearly all cases with MedCPT and SPECTER retrievers. The sole exception is under Contriever, where the ℓ₂-norm reaches ~95% detection due to Contriever's sensitivity to document length (longer documents yield larger embedding norms). The proposed method consistently outperforms the ℓ₂-norm, achieving >98% detection rates across virtually all corpus-retriever-query combinations. Additionally, the defense is evaluated against the PoisonedRAG attack (Zou et al., 2024), a state-of-the-art RAG poisoning method, and achieves a filtering rate exceeding 95%, demonstrating broad applicability beyond the paper's own attack. The key differentiator is that the Mahalanobis distance captures the anisotropic distribution of clean document embeddings, whereas the ℓ₂-norm assumes isotropic data and thus cannot distinguish the orthogonal-shift pattern characteristic of poisoned documents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RAG security work initiated by PoisonedRAG (Zou et al., 2024) and Pandora (Deng et al., 2024), which demonstrated that poisoning retrieval corpora can manipulate LLM outputs. However, it departs from those efforts in a key conceptual way: prior attacks inject query-relevant context to steer generation, whereas this paper shows that *any* information—semantically irrelevant PII, adversarial recommendations, jailbreak prompts—can be universally injected and retrieved, reframing the vulnerability as a fundamental property of dense retrievers rather than a content-specific weakness. The theoretical explanation via the 'orthogonal augmentation property' draws on geometric intuitions from embedding-space analysis, while the defense repurposes shrinkage-regularized Mahalanobis distance from high-dimensional anomaly detection into the RAG corpus-screening context, replacing the ℓ₂-norm heuristic shown ineffective by Deng et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAG systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains (healthcare, legal, finance) over publicly writable corpora like PubMed and Wikipedia, the demonstrated universality of the poisoning vulnerability—consistent across all three retrievers, three corpora, five query sets, and five target types—represents a concrete and actionable security risk that current deployment practices do not mitigate. The orthogonal augmentation property provides a reusable diagnostic: any dense retriever exhibiting this behavior is susceptible, enabling practitioners to audit their own systems. Open questions include whether the vulnerability persists under sparse retrieval (BM25), multi-hop or iterative retrieval pipelines, and whether the Mahalanobis-based defense generalizes to multi-lingual or multimodal RAG settings; the paper also notes that dynamic pruning methods for LLMs may interact with retrieval robustness in unexplored ways.
