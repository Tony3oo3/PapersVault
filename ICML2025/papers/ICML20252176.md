## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) either target tabular environments or require strong closure conditions from the compatible function approximation framework, which parametric policy classes in large-scale settings typically do not satisfy. This paper establishes best-in-class convergence of PMD for general (non-complete) policy classes by replacing closure conditions with a strictly weaker, algorithm-independent variational gradient dominance (VGD) assumption, yielding rates that are independent of the state-space cardinality.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a discounted MDP with finite action set and a general convex policy class Π ⊂ Δ(A)^S (not necessarily the complete class). It consumes Q-function estimates (with bounded critic error ε_crit), an action regularizer (Euclidean or negative entropy), a learning rate η, and ε-greedy exploration parameter, and produces a sequence of policies π^1, …, π^{K+1} along with explicit sub-optimality bounds V(π^K) − V⋆(Π) that scale as O(K^{-2/3}) for Euclidean regularization and O(K^{-2/7}) for negative entropy, with error floors determined by ε_vgd and √ε_stat. Crucially, the bounds do not depend on |S|, unlike prior gradient-based analyses that scale with state-space size.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel analysis that casts PMD as a Bregman proximal point algorithm in a non-Euclidean space, where the proximal operator uses a regularizer that adapts to the local smoothness of the value function. The key insight (Lemma 2) is that the value function is locally smooth w.r.t. a norm ‖·‖_{L²(μ^π),∘} induced by the current policy's occupancy measure μ^π, and this norm assigns the decision set Π a diameter independent of |S|—in contrast to the standard L² norm which gives diameter |S|. The analysis then proceeds in two stages: first establishing this local smoothness (requiring ε-greedy exploration to bound the smoothness constant by O(1/√ε)), and second proving a general convergence theorem for constrained non-convex optimization with locally smooth objectives under VGD (Theorem 3). The VGD condition is shown to be a strict relaxation of closure: closure implies VGD but not conversely, and the paper provides a concrete MDP example where VGD holds, PMD converges to the in-class optimum, yet closure-based analyses yield an error floor of at least 10H.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes best-in-class convergence of PMD under only the Variational Gradient Dominance (VGD) condition, without requiring closure, realizability, or parametric assumptions. With Euclidean regularization, the sub-optimality gap is O(C*²H³A^{3/2}/K^{2/3} + (C*H + AH²K^{1/6})√ε_stat + ε_vgd); with negative entropy regularization, it degrades to O(C*²H³A^{3/2}/K^{2/7} + (C*H + A²H³K^{4/7})√ε_stat + ε_vgd). Crucially, these rates are independent of the state-space cardinality |S|, unlike prior gradient-based convergence results that scale with |S| and are thus only useful in tabular settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are between convergence guarantees under different structural assumptions rather than empirical benchmarks. The paper's key advantage over all prior PMD convergence results (Xiao 2022, Lan 2023, Yuan et al. 2023, Ju & Lan 2022, Alfano et al. 2023) is that it requires neither closure conditions nor realizability, and imposes no parametric structure on the policy class. The trade-off is a slightly slower rate: 1/K^{2/3} (Euclidean) or 1/K^{2/7} (negative entropy) versus 1/K for prior works that assume closure. However, the paper demonstrates (Appendix A.3) that in a concrete MDP example where VGD holds but closure fails, prior closure-based analyses yield an error floor of ≥10H, while this work's bound converges to zero. Additionally, the rates here are independent of |S|, whereas prior gradient-based results (Agarwal et al. 2021, Bhandari & Russo 2024, Xiao 2022) that are applicable in the general policy class setting produce bounds that scale with state-space cardinality, rendering them useful only in tabular environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established observation (Shani et al. 2020; Tomar et al. 2020; Xiao 2022) that on-policy PMD is equivalent to a mirror descent step on the value function gradient with a dynamically weighted Bregman proximity term, and on Xiao's (2022) proximal point analysis technique for projected gradient descent. It departs from the dominant closure-based framework (compatible function approximation, bounded approximation/transfer error) by replacing these algorithm-dependent conditions with the strictly weaker, algorithm-independent Variational Gradient Dominance (VGD) condition from Bhandari & Russo (2024). The key technical novelty is a local smoothness result for the value function w.r.t. an occupancy-measure-induced norm, which enables a non-Euclidean proximal point analysis yielding rates independent of state-space cardinality—unlike prior Euclidean-smoothness-based analyses (Agarwal et al. 2021; Xiao 2022) that scale with |S|.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing best-in-class convergence of PMD without any closure condition, and the paper provides a concrete MDP example where VGD holds, closure fails, and prior closure-based analyses yield vacuous error floors (≥ H) while the new analysis guarantees convergence to zero. The local-norm smoothness framework is a reusable analytical tool for non-Euclidean optimization in MDPs. Key limitations include the requirement of policy class convexity (absent closure), a smooth action regularizer (degrading the negative-entropy rate from K^{-2/3} to K^{-2/7}), and ε-greedy exploration whose O(1/√ε) smoothness constant is shown to be tight. Open questions include whether geometric rates are achievable under VGD alone, whether non-smooth regularizers can be accommodated, and whether the exploration requirement can be replaced by a weaker local smoothness condition.
