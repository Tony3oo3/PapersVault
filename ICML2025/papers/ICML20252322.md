## Problem

Existing offline black-box optimization methods are confined to single-task, fixed-dimensional settings and cannot unify heterogeneous search spaces (continuous, categorical, integer, permutation) across domains, forcing practitioners to collect large task-specific datasets for every new problem. While recent string-based language model approaches have shown promise for universal online BBO, they have not addressed the unique challenges of offline BBO—particularly overfitting to limited historical data—and the geometric properties of learned latent spaces that directly impact optimization stability remain under-explored.

## Input / Output

Each design is encoded as a JSON dictionary string (e.g., {"x0":0,"x1":1}) prepended with task-level metadata (name, brief description, and objective specification), then tokenized via SentencePiece. The offline dataset consists of design-score pairs collected without any online evaluations. The method produces final designs by maximizing the trained surrogate's output through model-inner Bayesian optimization (BoTorch qEI for continuous spaces, GP-UCB with EA for categorical spaces), yielding 128 candidate solutions per task.

## Method

UniSO unifies heterogeneous offline BBO tasks through string-based representation and offers two regressor variants: UniSO-T (a T5-based seq2seq model predicting P10-encoded score tokens via next-token prediction) and UniSO-N (a T5-Small embedder feeding an MLP regressor trained with MSE on normalized scores). Two key regularizations address the vanilla models' failure to distinguish tasks and produce smooth latent spaces: (1) a contrastive loss that aligns input-embedding cosine similarity patterns with metadata-embedding similarity patterns, encouraging distinct clusters for dissimilar tasks while keeping related tasks proximate; and (2) a per-task Lipschitz loss that enforces local smoothness between embedding distances and score differences, with task-size-balanced weighting. A MetaBalance-inspired gradient scaling mechanism automatically balances the main loss against both auxiliary losses during training, and for UniSO-N a two-stage procedure first fine-tunes the embedder with the combined regularization losses before training the regressor with frozen embeddings.

## Results

On 9 tasks spanning Design-Bench and SOO-Bench, improved UniSO-T achieves the best average rank of 2.222 across all methods, outperforming the strongest single-task numeric-input expert (BN+BO, rank 3.111). On Ant Morphology, improved UniSO-T scores 455.658±39.188 versus 241.350±288.922 for BN+BO, and on GTOPX 6 it reaches −48.244±5.766 versus −49.660±6.170. In zero-shot generalization to three unseen tasks (RobotPush, Rover, LunarLander), both improved UniSO-T and UniSO-N significantly exceed the best score in the offline dataset D(best), and few-shot fine-tuning further surpasses a z-score-normalized single-task expert. Against 21 state-of-the-art single-task offline BBO methods, improved UniSO-T achieves an average rank of 9.8, outperforming several recently proposed methods and achieving the best result on TF-Bind-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-task numeric-input experts (BN+BO and BN+Grad), improved UniSO-T achieves a better average rank (2.222 vs. 3.111 for BN+BO) across all 9 tasks, winning outright on Ant, D'Kitty, TF Bind 10, and GTOPX 6, while BN+Grad wins on Superconductor and TF Bind 8. UniSO-N (both vanilla and improved) consistently underperforms the numeric-input experts, with average ranks of 4.778 and 4.111 respectively, suggesting that mapping from LM embeddings to numerical scores remains challenging. In the broader comparison against 21 state-of-the-art single-task offline BBO methods (referred from Tan et al., 2025, ICLR 2025), improved UniSO-T achieves an average rank of 9.8, outperforming several recently proposed methods specifically designed for single-task offline BBO and achieving the best result on TF-Bind-10, though it still trails the top-performing specialized methods overall. The paper also demonstrates that UniSO-N trained from scratch outperforms the pre-trained T5 embedder variant, and that pre-trained LMs exhibit harmful attention biases toward grammar tokens rather than numerical tokens, which explains the performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the string-based LLM-for-BBO line (OmniPred for token-targeted regression, Nguyen et al. for embedding-based regression) from the online setting to universal offline BBO, where no online evaluations are permitted. It introduces two novel regularization mechanisms—contrastive metadata alignment (inspired by contrastive learning) and a Lipschitz smoothness loss (adapted from Lee et al., 2023)—to address the task-discrimination and latent-space geometry problems that arise when these architectures are applied to offline data. The loss-balancing strategy draws on MetaBalance, and the overall pipeline (surrogate training + model-inner BO search) follows the standard forward approach in offline BBO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic framework for universal offline BBO across heterogeneous search spaces, demonstrating that string-based representations can unify continuous, categorical, and mixed-type problems under a single model. A notable finding is that pre-trained language models introduce harmful attention biases toward grammar tokens rather than numerical tokens, causing from-scratch training to outperform pre-trained initialization—a counterintuitive result for a field that relies heavily on LM pre-training. However, the approach still trails SOTA single-task offline BBO methods (average rank 9.8/21), and experiments are limited to 9 tasks with small T5-Scale models, leaving open whether the framework scales to larger, more diverse task suites and whether the pre-training harm persists with stronger LMs.
