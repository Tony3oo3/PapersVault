## Problem

The MAP topic-word assignment problem in LDA is NP-hard even for a single document, and all existing inference methods (Gibbs, variational) provide only local solutions with no approximation guarantees. Furthermore, standard out-of-sample inference used in causal-inference pipelines lacks guarantees that undermine downstream validity, and learned topics have no formal interpretability guarantee tying them to known keywords. The paper asks whether combinatorial optimization breakthroughs in submodular maximization can yield fast, near-optimal, and interpretable topic models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text corpus of documents, a (potentially very large) candidate topic set Φ generated by coherence-based functions (one topic per vocabulary word or n-gram), and a user-chosen sparsity upper-bound κ on the average number of topics per document. It outputs an ordered set of topic-to-document links E (|E| ≤ κ|D|) that induces MAP topic-word assignments for every word, plus per-document topic labels. Unlike standard LDA, the sparsity is enforced as an explicit cardinality constraint rather than through the Dirichlet prior α, and the candidate set can be orders of magnitude larger than the final number of topics selected.

## Method

The core insight is that under the limit α→∞, the LDA MAP objective reduces to a set function f(E) over topic-document links that is monotone submodular, enabling a 1−1/e approximation guarantee via Greedy. FASTGREEDY-E-LDA exploits LDA's conditional independence across documents to reduce per-iteration complexity from O(ℓκ|Φ||D|²) to O(log|D| + ℓ|Φ|) through memoization of per-word log-probabilities and a max-heap over documents' best remaining links. A parallel variant adapts the FAST submodular maximization algorithm (Breuer et al., 2020) to achieve near-optimal solutions in logarithmic parallel time (adaptivity). Interpretability is built in by generating the candidate topic set via coherence functions (UMass, Exp-UMass, Co-occurrence), so every selected topic is formally defined as a known function of a keyword. For causal inference, an out-of-sample variant of the objective enforces SUTVA and treatment-independence while retaining the same approximation guarantees.

## Results

On log posterior (Table 1, values ×10⁶), E-LDA uniformly outperforms all Gibbs baselines across all 90 experiments (3 datasets × 3 baselines × 10 runs, p < 10⁻¹⁰): e.g., on Congress E-LDA achieves −1.78 vs. Gibbs −2.05, and on Reuters −2.52 vs. −2.80, while using the baselines' own topics and matching their sparsity. On topic coherence (UMass, top-5 words), E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE achieves 2.53–4.20× higher mean coherence than Gibbs, and E-LDA EXP-UMASS achieves 1.18–1.67× higher. Against neural/LLM baselines, E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE outperforms BERTopic by 3.04–4.70×, AVITM by 5.96–10.17×, FASTopic by 2.24–5.84×, and ETM by 1.19–2.36× in mean topic coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MALLET Gibbs LDA (and its Bayesian and sparse variants), E-LDA achieves uniformly higher log posterior values on all three datasets while reusing the Gibbs-learned topics and matching their sparsity, confirming that the combinatorial MAP assignment is strictly better than the locally-modal Gibbs assignments. Against BERTopic, E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE outperforms by 3.04–4.70× in mean UMass coherence, and E-LDA EXP-UMASS by 1.46–2.72×. Against AVITM, the margins are the largest: E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE leads by 5.96–10.17× and EXP-UMASS by 3.08–7.09×. Against ETM (designed specifically to improve coherence), E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE still leads by 1.19–2.36×, while EXP-UMASS is competitive (better on Reuters, slightly worse on NewsGroups). Against FASTopic, E-LDA CO-OCCURRENCE outperforms by 2.24–5.84× and EXP-UMASS by 1.48–2.67×. In all comparisons, E-LDA's advantage grows as coherence is computed over more top words (h* = 5 → 25), indicating that E-LDA topics capture richer semantic structure beyond a handful of keywords.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes LDA's MAP topic-word assignment problem as a monotone submodular maximization problem, drawing a direct analogy to exemplar clustering and data summarization — areas that have already benefited from combinatorial optimization breakthroughs. It builds on the FAST parallel submodular maximization algorithm (Breuer et al., 2020) and the classic 1-1/e greedy guarantee (Nemhauser et al., 1978), adapting them to exploit LDA's document-independence structure for exponential speedups over vanilla greedy. The key conceptual departure from prior LDA work (Gibbs sampling, variational inference) is the shift from gradient-based local optimization to a combinatorial, non-gradient approach with global approximation guarantees, a connection the authors note was 'surprisingly undiscovered' in the topic modeling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical algorithm with provable near-optimal guarantees for LDA MAP topic-word assignment, which is the primary inference task in social science applications. The interpretability guarantee (each topic is a known function of a keyword) and the enforcement of document independence (SUTVA) for out-of-sample inference address two long-standing practical pain points that have limited LDA's use in causal inference frameworks. Open questions include whether the submodular reformulation extends cleanly to other topic model families (the paper mentions generalization but does not develop it), whether the coherence-based topic generation is truly circular when coherence is also the evaluation metric, and how the approach scales to very large vocabularies where the candidate set |Φ| becomes enormous.
