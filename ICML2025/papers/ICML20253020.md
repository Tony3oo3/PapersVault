## Problem

Existing data poisoning attacks against multimodal models (AtoB, ShadowCast) rely on random selection of concept pairs for misassociation and random instance selection for injecting poisoning noise, which often yields suboptimal efficacy or outright failure due to dilution by the large number of benign instances. The paper addresses the gap of providing a systematic, principled framework for selecting which concepts to misassociate and which specific instances to poison, given a limited attacker budget.

## Input / Output

MP-Nav consumes an open-sourced dataset of (image, text) pairs, pre-trained multimodal encoders (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32), and an attacker budget η specifying the number of instances to manipulate. It produces a poisoned training set D_p in which selected instances have been modified (text captions swapped for AtoB, or images perturbed via PGD for ShadowCast), along with the identified source-target concept pair and the ranked list of robust instances. The encoders are used only for embedding computation and similarity scoring; the actual poisoning manipulation is delegated to the underlying attack method.

## Method

MP-Nav is a plug-and-play module with two complementary selection components. At the concept level, it computes mean image and text embeddings for every concept in the dataset, constructs a full cosine-similarity matrix across all concept pairs, and selects the pair with the highest cross-modal similarity score as the source-target misassociation candidate. At the instance level, it computes a fused embedding for each instance (a weighted combination of image and text embeddings), measures each instance's cosine proximity to its concept center, and selects the top-η instances closest to the center as 'robust' targets for poisoning. The key insight is that semantically similar concepts are inherently easier to misassociate in the embedding space, and center-proximal instances exert disproportionate influence on the learned concept representation, so poisoning them resists dilution by the surrounding benign majority. The module is attack-agnostic: it feeds its selected concepts and instances into existing attack pipelines (AtoB for text-image retrieval, ShadowCast for VQA) without altering their core perturbation or pairing mechanisms.

## Results

On the COCO dataset for the AtoB (TIR) attack, MP-Nav's combined Concept+Instance selection (boat→kit) achieves Hit@10 of 0.677 and MinRank of 14.602, compared to the baseline's (boat→dog) Hit@10 of 0.505 and MinRank of 15.705, representing a 34% relative improvement in Hit@10 and a 4.8× improvement in Hit@1 (0.048 vs. 0.010). On Flickr-PASCAL, MP-Nav (sheep→cow) consistently outperforms the baseline (sheep→aeroplane) across all attacker budgets. For the ShadowCast (VQA) attack on the Biden-Trump dataset, MP-Nav's instance-level selection significantly enhances attack success at small poisoning budgets (n ≤ 20), though gains diminish at larger budgets. Model utility is preserved across all settings, with R@10 scores on COCO and Flickr-PASCAL remaining within ±0.1 of the clean model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AtoB baseline (Yang et al., 2023), MP-Nav wins decisively on all four poisoning efficacy metrics on COCO: the combined Concept+Instance configuration improves Hit@1 from 0.010 to 0.048, Hit@5 from 0.214 to 0.418, Hit@10 from 0.505 to 0.677, and reduces MinRank from 15.705 to 14.602. The concept-level selection alone (switching target from 'dog' to 'kit') already yields substantial gains (Hit@10: 0.610 vs. 0.505), while instance-level selection on the original concept pair also helps (Hit@10: 0.618 vs. 0.505), confirming both components contribute independently. Against the ShadowCast baseline (Xu et al., 2024) on the Biden-Trump VQA task, MP-Nav's instance-level selection provides meaningful improvements at small poisoning budgets (n ≤ 20 poisoned instances), but the overall trend is less consistent than in the TIR setting, with the authors noting that optimization procedures and data quality introduce large uncertainties. On the Food101 dataset, MP-Nav's concept selection (pho→ramen, similarity 0.268) outperforms randomly chosen pairs (cupcake→ramen at 0.126, icecream→ramen at 0.176). In all settings, model utility (R@10 on GQA and retrieval benchmarks) is preserved at levels comparable to the clean model, confirming the attacks remain stealthy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two prior data poisoning attacks against multimodal models: AtoB (Yang et al., 2023) for text-image retrieval and ShadowCast (Xu et al., 2024) for visual question answering, both of which rely on random selection of concept pairs and instances for misassociation. MP-Nav departs from this by introducing a principled, embedding-space-based selection strategy at both the concept level (choosing semantically similar pairs via cosine similarity of mean embeddings) and the instance level (selecting center-proximate 'robust' samples), drawing an analogy to findings in the unimodal backdoor attack literature (Xia et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2023) that not all training samples contribute equally to attack efficacy. The module is explicitly positioned as plug-and-play, meaning it wraps around existing attack procedures rather than proposing a new attack mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical security gap: current multimodal models deployed in search engines, VQA assistants, and other critical applications remain vulnerable to small-budget poisoning when the attacker can strategically choose what to poison. However, the evidence is mixed—the AtoB improvements on COCO are moderate (Hit@10: 0.505→0.677), the ShadowCast results show large uncertainties and the authors concede MP-Nav 'cannot definitely improve' those attacks, and Table 5 reveals cases (e.g., skateboard→person) where MP-Nav's concept selection actively degrades performance. Open questions include whether the center-proximity heuristic generalizes beyond CLIP-style contrastive encoders, how defenses might detect or neutralize such targeted poisoning, and whether the approach extends to more than two modalities or to generative multimodal models like LLaVA at scale.
