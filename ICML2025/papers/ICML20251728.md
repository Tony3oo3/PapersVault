## Problem

While SCAFFOLD's convergence in deterministic settings is well established, the impact of stochastic gradient updates on its performance remained poorly understood. In particular, no prior analysis demonstrated that SCAFFOLD achieves linear speed-up in the number of clients under stochastic gradients without relying on artificial global step sizes, and the residual bias introduced by stochasticity was uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes N clients each providing local stochastic gradients (with bounded sixth moments), an initial global parameter θ⁰, and zero-initialized control variates ξ₍c₎⁰=0. The primary output is a non-asymptotic convergence rate E[‖θᵀ−θ⋆‖²] that explicitly decomposes into a variance term scaling as γσ⋆²/(Nμ) (yielding linear speed-up) and higher-order bias terms in O(γ^{3/2}+γ³H). Additionally, the paper characterizes the full covariance structure of the stationary distribution and gives a first-order expansion of the residual bias, which scales as γ/N times the local gradient covariance.

## Method

The key insight is to view SCAFFOLD's global parameters and all N control variates jointly as a time-homogeneous Markov chain on a constrained subspace, equipped with a weighted norm ‖·‖_Λ that balances the global parameter against the control variates with weight γ²H²/N. The paper proves this Markov chain is geometrically ergodic in 2-Wasserstein distance with rate (1−γμ/4)^H per round, using a contraction argument based on co-coercivity and monotonicity of the gradient. The critical technical step is a careful analysis of the pairwise covariances between global parameters and control variates in the stationary distribution, showing that the global parameter variance scales as γ/N while individual control variate variances do not—linear speed-up emerges from the cross-client covariance structure. A synchronous coupling argument then converts the stationary-regime covariance bound into a non-asymptotic rate. Finally, first-order expansions in the step size reveal a non-vanishing bias of order γ/N arising purely from stochastic gradient noise, analogous to the bias of homogeneous FedAvg.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.9, Corollary 4.10) shows that SCAFFOLD requires O(log(1/ε)) communication rounds and O(1/(Nε²)) local stochastic gradients per client to reach E[‖θ^T − θ*‖²] ≤ ε², establishing the first linear speed-up for SCAFFOLD with general (non-quadratic) objectives. The stationary variance of the global iterate scales as γ/(Nμ)·σ*² (Theorem 4.8), while a residual bias of order γ/N persists (Theorem 5.3). Numerically, SCAFFOLD consistently outperforms FedAvg on heterogeneous linear and logistic regression, with the stationary error decreasing as N grows from 10 to 1000 (and saturating at N=10000 for logistic regression, consistent with the theory's higher-order terms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Karimireddy et al. (2020), the original SCAFFOLD analysis, this paper achieves the same O(log(1/ε)) communication complexity but reduces local iterations from O(1/ε²) to O(1/(Nε²)), i.e., a linear speed-up in the number of clients N that was previously absent. Compared to Mishchenko et al. (2022) (ProxSkip) and Hu & Huang (2023), which achieve acceleration but with local iterations that do not decrease with N, this paper is the first to combine acceleration with linear speed-up for general objectives. Against Mangold et al. (2024) (SCAFFLSA), which already achieved O(1/(Nε²)) local iterations, the key improvement is extending the result from quadratic objectives to general μ-strongly convex and L-smooth functions (requiring only bounded third and fourth derivatives). In the empirical comparison (Figure 1), SCAFFOLD outperforms FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) across all settings (N ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 10000}) on both linear and logistic regression, with the gap widening as N increases, confirming the theoretical linear speed-up up to saturation at N ≈ 1000 for the non-quadratic logistic case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov chain analysis framework developed for constant-step-size SGD (Dieuleveut et al., 2020) and FEDAVG (Mangold et al., 2025) to the SCAFFOLD algorithm, showing that the joint state of global parameters and control variates forms a geometrically ergodic Markov chain. It generalizes the quadratic-only linear speed-up result of Mangold et al. (2024, SCAFFLSA) to general strongly-convex and smooth objectives. The analysis departs from prior SCAFFOLD convergence results (Karimireddy et al., 2020; Mishchenko et al., 2022; Hu & Huang, 2023), which established communication complexity bounds but did not reveal the 1/N variance scaling or the residual stochastic bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first proof of linear speed-up for SCAFFOLD with general objectives is practically significant: it means each client's computational cost decreases as more clients join, a fundamental promise of federated learning that was previously unproven for this algorithm. The discovery that SCAFFOLD retains a non-vanishing O(γ/N) bias from stochasticity—despite its control variate mechanism eliminating heterogeneity bias—challenges the assumption that SCAFFOLD is a complete bias correction and opens a direction for designing improved algorithms. Key open questions include whether SCAFFOLD can achieve the √κ accelerated communication rate (as in ProxSkip) while preserving linear speed-up, and whether the residual stochastic bias can be eliminated.
