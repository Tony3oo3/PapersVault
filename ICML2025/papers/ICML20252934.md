## Problem

Advanced long-reasoning models (e.g., OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1) exhibit a uni-modal behavior where even trivial questions receive unnecessarily long reasoning traces, inflating inference costs and carbon footprints. The paper addresses how to enable models to adaptively allocate inference budgets based on query difficulty—spending more compute on hard problems and less on easy ones—rather than applying a fixed reasoning length to all queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252934/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a math problem prompt and an inference budget parameter q+ that caps the maximum fraction of responses allowed to be extended (voting) responses. It produces either a standard step-CoT response (a single trial) or an extended sequential voting response (up to 8 trials with early stopping when an answer appears 3 times), followed by a boxed final answer. The model autonomously decides which response type to generate, and the budget constraint is enforced at the population level across a batch of prompts rather than per individual query.

## Method

IBPO (Inference Budget-Constrained Policy Optimization) formulates adaptive reasoning as a constrained RL problem by partitioning the response space into two groups—standard CoT (G_circ) and extended voting (G_plus)—and imposing a density constraint that caps the expected fraction of extended responses at q+. The core mechanism is a bi-level optimization: the inner level solves an integer linear program (ILP) that maximizes a margin reward r_Δ (the expected-correctness advantage of one response group over the other) subject to the budget cap and CGPO feasibility constraints (correctness + empirical KL), while the outer level performs a weighted SFT update via KL projection of the ILP solution onto the parametric policy. The margin reward naturally encourages assigning extended responses to hard queries (where voting has higher expected correctness) and standard responses to easy queries, saving budget for harder problems. Built on CGPO, the practical algorithm replaces CGPO's Best-of-N selection with the ILP solution, reducing the entire update to a weighted cross-entropy loss with weights from the ILP—a clean generalization of RAFT and RFT. Training proceeds in two stages: SFT to teach the model both SCoT and voting response formats, followed by RL with IBPO (using SciPy's MILP solver per batch) to optimize the budget allocation policy.

## Results

On MATH500, ASV-IuB achieves 4.14% absolute pass@1 improvement (8.08% relative) at 2.16× inference budget and 5.74% absolute improvement (11.2% relative) at 4.32× budget, relative to LLaMA 3.1 8B Instruct. These gains are approximately 2× those of self-consistency (majority voting) under the same budgets. The method also demonstrates difficulty-adaptive allocation: at q+=25%, only 2% of level-1 (easiest) problems receive extended voting responses, while levels 4–5 receive substantially higher allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCoRe (Kumar et al., 2024), ASV-IuB at q+=50% achieves 4.14% improvement at 2.16× cost, closely matching SCoRe's 4.4% at 2×; at q+=75%, ASV-IuB reaches 5.74% at 4.32×, slightly below SCoRe's ~6% at 5–10× but at substantially lower inference cost. Against RISE (Qu et al., 2024), RISE+Iteration achieves higher absolute gains (9.7–10.4%) but at a fixed 5× cost, whereas ASV-IuB offers a tunable cost-accuracy tradeoff. Against S3C (Yan et al., 2024), ASV-IuB's 4.14–5.74% improvement exceeds S3C's 0.44–3.18% range across models. Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2024) and STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) show negligible or negative improvements (0.0 to −14.2%), confirming that SFT alone does not enable effective self-correction. Relative to self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022), the paper reports that ASV-IuB's improvements are approximately 2× those of majority voting under the same inference budgets, as visualized in Figure 2 where ASV-IuB points lie above the hypothetical OSS-to-SV-SFT efficiency boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBPO positions itself as a principled constrained-RL generalization of iterative weighted SFT methods (RAFT, RFT), showing that those successful algorithms can be interpreted as projecting empirical solutions onto a parametric policy space without explicit budget constraints. The group-density constraint draws from the resource-allocation and knapsack-bandit literature, while the base RL algorithm is CGPO, a generalized Best-of-N with feasibility constraints. The paper departs from prior heuristic difficulty-adaptive methods (Adaptive-Consistency, Adaption-of-Thought, difficulty-adaptive self-consistency) by replacing hand-crafted routing rules with an optimization-derived allocation, and from prior self-correction RL work (SCoRe, RISE) by explicitly modeling inference cost as a constraint rather than an implicit trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, solver-based mechanism for controlling inference spend without reward-hacking vulnerabilities, and the authors argue it generalizes to group fairness and MoE expert balancing. However, the evaluation is narrow: a single 8B model, a single math benchmark, a 10k-prompt training set, and a demonstrative (not state-of-the-art) construction of the extended-response group. The q+=75% failure due to distribution shift and the modest absolute gains (4–6% on MATH500) limit immediate practical impact, and scaling to larger models, longer reasoning chains, or non-math tasks remains open.
