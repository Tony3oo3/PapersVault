## Problem

Unpaired domain translation (UDT) suffers from content misalignment because infinitely many translation functions can achieve perfect distribution matching. The Diversified Distribution Matching (DDM) criterion resolves this by enforcing a unified translation function across multiple conditional distribution pairs, but was only implemented with GANs, which are numerically unstable and do not recover transport trajectories. Adapting flow matching (FM) to DDM is nontrivial because FM learns a velocity field rather than an explicit translation function, so the structural constraints that guarantee identifiability cannot be directly imposed.

## Input / Output

The method takes unpaired source and target domain samples (e.g., 256×256 CelebAHQ and Bitmoji face images) along with auxiliary conditional labels (e.g., gender) that partition each domain into Q non-overlapping sub-distributions. For the swarm navigation task, input is a LiDAR point cloud (34,183 points of Mt. Rainier) with Gaussian source/destination clusters per swarm. The output is a unified time-dependent velocity field v_t that, when integrated, yields both the translated target sample and the full continuous transport trajectory; for images, all FM operations are performed in the latent space of a Stable Diffusion VAE.

## Method

DFM introduces a bilevel optimization loss where the lower level learns a private vector field v_t^(q) for each conditional pair via standard FM loss, and the upper level enforces consensus by minimizing ‖v_t^(q) − v_t‖² across all q, provably recovering the DDM criterion. To make this tractable, the method exploits the non-overlapping support structure of conditional distributions: it first trains a unified nonlinear interpolant I_θ(x,y,t) = (1−t)x + ty + t(1−t)γ_θ(x,y,t) to be non-intersecting across all q via a repulsive pairwise loss, then trains the unified velocity field with a simple sum of FM losses using the frozen interpolant. This two-stage decomposition eliminates the bilevel structure while preserving identifiability guarantees under the Sufficiently Diverse Condition. The key insight is that non-intersecting interpolants prevent the trajectory 'reflection' failure mode that causes standard linear-interpolant FM to swap translations between conditional pairs.

## Results

On the CelebAHQ-to-Bitmoji face translation task, DFM achieves the best FID of 22.20 and the best DreamSim content-alignment score of 0.59, outperforming all FM-based baselines and matching or exceeding DDM-GAN (FID 26.20, DreamSim 0.58) while training in 6.17 hours versus DDM-GAN's 13.60 hours. On synthetic 2D Gaussian data, DFM reduces EMD from ~9.6 (FM-cond, FM-cond-OT) to 0.31 and translation error from ~9.9 to 2.42, demonstrating correct per-conditional-distribution transport. On the Mt. Rainier swarm navigation task, DFM achieves the lowest surface-adherence error (SA = 0.32) compared to FM-OT (1.09) and MFM-OT (0.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFM outperforms all FM-based baselines on both FID and DreamSim: it beats FM-cond (FID 22.65, DreamSim 0.60) by a small margin and substantially outperforms plain FM (FID 43.23) and FM-OT (FID 44.60), confirming that simply adding auxiliary variables with linear interpolants (Eq. 9) is insufficient for DDM satisfaction. Against the GAN-based DDM-GAN, DFM achieves a lower FID (22.20 vs. 26.20) and better DreamSim (0.59 vs. 0.58) while training roughly 2× faster, and avoids the severe convergence instability documented in Fig. 7. Diffusion-based baselines SDEdit and EGSDE are far worse on FID (63–70) and much slower at inference (27–66 s vs. ~5 s). On synthetic data, DFM reduces EMD by ~30× and translation error by ~4× relative to FM-cond and FM-cond-OT, which suffer from the trajectory-reflection failure mode. On swarm navigation, DFM achieves the best surface adherence (SA 0.32) versus MFM-cond-OT (0.43) and FM-cond-OT (1.08), showing that non-intersecting interpolants are critical for multi-swarm collision avoidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the Diversified Distribution Matching (DDM) criterion for translation-identifiable unpaired domain translation (Shrestha & Fu, ICLR 2024), which was only implemented via GANs, and flow matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023; Albergo et al., 2023), which learns velocity fields rather than explicit translation functions. The key conceptual departure is that DDM's identifiability guarantee relies on constraining the translation function itself, but FM only parameterizes its velocity—making a direct port non-trivial. The paper resolves this gap with a bilevel optimization loss and private nonlinear interpolants, positioning DFM as the first ODE-based framework that provably recovers the DDM criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFM offers a more stable, trajectory-preserving alternative to GAN-based DDM, which is practically important for applications requiring intermediate transport states (single-cell evolution inference, robot swarm navigation). The paper's limitations are clearly stated: the method is restricted to one-to-one translations and relies on non-overlapping supports of conditional distributions, both of which limit applicability to real-world scenarios where categories overlap. Open questions include extending the bilevel loss to overlapping supports and enabling identifiable one-to-many mappings, which would broaden the method's utility in image translation and other generative tasks.
