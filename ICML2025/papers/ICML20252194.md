## Problem

Most in silico molecular optimization methods assume an online setting where candidate molecules are immediately evaluated by an oracle, but in real-world drug discovery the oracle is a wet lab experiment taking weeks to months. Existing offline model-based optimization (MBO) methods only use the offline dataset to train a score-regression proxy, failing to directly exploit the structural information in the dataset, and the multi-objective setting further complicates weight selection when no online feedback is available to guide scalarization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of molecules (in SMILES format) paired with their true multi-objective property scores (e.g., GSK3β, JNK3, QED, SA), and takes pairs of parent molecules as input to StitchNet. It produces novel stitched molecules that combine desirable properties from both parents, a diverse Pareto-optimal molecule set, and a fine-tuned REINVENT generative model. Unusually, the proxy outputs pairwise rankings (which molecule is better) rather than absolute scores, and the generative model is updated via IPO preference loss rather than gradient ascent on proxy-predicted rewards.

## Method

MolStitch is a three-stage offline MBO framework. Stage 1 pre-trains StitchNet (a sequence-to-sequence model) on ZINC via rule-based crossover to internalize chemical grammar, then self-supervised fine-tunes it with chemical feedback from the offline dataset. Stage 2 trains a rank-based proxy using a pairwise ranking loss (sigmoid of score difference) instead of score regression, simplifying the proxy's task to classification. Stage 3 performs offline MBO: priority sampling from a Dirichlet distribution generates diverse scalarization weights for multi-objective trade-offs, StitchNet produces stitched molecules stored in a buffer, the rank-based proxy selects winners/losers from pairs, and the REINVENT generative model is fine-tuned with an IPO (Identity Preference Optimization) loss that penalizes excessive confidence in the preference margin, regularized by KL divergence to a reference model.

## Results

On the molecular property optimization (MPO) task under the full-offline setting, MolStitch achieves the best HV and R2 across all three objective configurations: HV 0.579 / R2 0.698 (GSK3β+JNK3), HV 0.403 / R2 1.649 (GSK3β+JNK3+QED), and HV 0.352 / R2 2.953 (GSK3β+JNK3+QED+SA). On the docking score optimization task, MolStitch attains the highest HV for all five target proteins (e.g., 0.560 for parp1, 0.575 for 5ht1b), outperforming the next-best method (BootGen) by 1.6–3.0 HV points. The advantage grows with the number of objectives, with MolStitch improving HV by up to 29% over the best baseline (Saturn, 0.281) in the four-objective setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolStitch outperforms all 15 baselines across every objective configuration in the MPO task. Among molecular optimization methods, the closest competitors are Saturn (HV 0.531 for 2-objective, 0.281 for 4-objective) and GeneticGFN (HV 0.309 for 3-objective), both of which use genetic-algorithm-style crossover; MolStitch improves HV by 9.2% (2-obj) and 25.3% (4-obj) over Saturn. Among offline MBO methods, BootGen (HV 0.540 for 2-obj) and RaM (HV 0.249 for 4-obj) are the strongest, with MolStitch achieving 7.2% and 41.4% HV gains respectively. In the docking score optimization task (Table 2), MolStitch leads all five proteins, beating the next-best BootGen by 1.6 HV points on parp1 (0.560 vs 0.544) and 3.0 points on 5ht1b (0.575 vs 0.545). The vanilla gradient-ascent baseline (Grad, HV 0.494 for 2-obj) and ensemble-based methods (Ensemble Proxy, HV 0.500) trail MolStitch by 8–15% HV, confirming that the combination of StitchNet, rank-based proxy, and IPO preference optimization is critical rather than any single component.

## Relation to Prior Work

MolStitch sits at the intersection of offline model-based optimization (MBO) for molecular design, trajectory stitching from offline RL, and preference optimization from LLM alignment. It departs from conventional offline MBO (which relies on score-regression proxies and gradient ascent) by replacing the proxy with a rank-based pairwise classifier and replacing generative-model-based data augmentation with a dedicated StitchNet that recombines molecular fragments. The preference-optimization fine-tuning stage directly adapts DPO and IPO losses from LLM alignment (Rafailov et al., 2023; Azar et al., 2024) to the molecular generation setting, while the Dirichlet-based priority sampling for multi-objective weight selection extends scalarization techniques used in MOMO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in drug discovery: wet-lab oracle evaluations take weeks to months, making online iterative optimization infeasible. By demonstrating that a fully offline pipeline (stitching + rank-based proxy + preference optimization) can consistently outperform both online molecular optimization methods and prior offline MBO approaches across 2–4 objectives and 5 protein targets, it provides a viable workflow for refining generative models between experimental batches. Open questions include whether the rank-based proxy's OOD reliability holds at larger chemical scales, how the framework behaves when the offline dataset is very small, and whether the StitchNet's fragment-recombination strategy generalizes beyond SMILES-based autoregressive generation to graph- or 3D-based representations.
