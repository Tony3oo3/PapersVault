## Problem

RL training is inherently unstable due to moving targets, high gradient variance, and in RLHF/RLAIF, additional noise from reward model prediction errors and diverse human/AI preferences. These factors cause advantage sign confusion—where the policy is pushed to increase or decrease an action's probability in the wrong direction—closely analogous to noisy labels in supervised classification. The paper asks whether the symmetric cross-entropy (SCE) robustness mechanism from noisy classification can be transplanted into policy-gradient RL to stabilize training.

## Input / Output

For control tasks, the method consumes environment states (RGB frames for Atari, low-dimensional state vectors for MuJoCo/Box2D) and outputs a full action probability distribution over all discrete actions; for RLHF tasks, it takes text contexts and generates continuations (IMDB sentiment) or summaries (TL;DR). A critical requirement is access to the complete action probability vector π_θ(s) for the current state, not merely the sampled action's probability, which forces discretization of continuous action spaces into 11 bins. The reverse RL loss term sums over all non-sampled actions, adding a 10–20% training-time overhead on Atari but negligible cost on continuous and LLM tasks.

## Method

The symmetric RL loss L_srl = α·L_rl + β·L_rev augments the standard A2C or PPO actor loss with a reverse RL loss (RA2C/RPPO) that sums the probabilities of all non-sampled actions weighted by the advantage and a constant Z=−1, mirroring how SCE adds reverse cross-entropy to standard cross-entropy. Gradient analysis with respect to action logits shows the two loss components produce aligned gradient directions, with the reverse term acting as an accelerator whose magnitude peaks at π=0.5—precisely where predictions are most ambiguous and most vulnerable to sign-flip noise. For PPO, the off-policy nature of multi-epoch updates combined with advantage normalization over small mini-batches causes >5% (Atari) to >10% (MuJoCo) advantage sign flips per update, explaining why SPPO outperforms SA2C and shows consistent gains across a wide range of β values. The approach is validated on 22 Atari games, 9 MuJoCo/Box2D environments (with and without injected reward noise), and two RLHF tasks using GPT-2, GPT-J, and Qwen2-0.5B with LoRA adapters, where SPPO improves reward scores and GPT-4-judged win rates over PPO.

## Results

On Atari games (22 environments, 5 seeds), SPPO achieves 16/22 wins over PPO in noise-free settings and 19/22 wins under 10% Binary Symmetric Channel reward noise. On RLHF tasks, SPPO outperforms PPO on IMDB positive sentiment (RM Score 0.92 vs. 0.89; Perplexity 40.60 vs. 41.09) and TL;DR summarization (RM Score 6.13 vs. 5.94; GPT-4-judged Win Rate 52.50% vs. 43.25%). On MuJoCo/Box2D continuous control with Gaussian reward noise, DSPPO consistently surpasses both PPO and DPPO across all 9 environments (e.g., Ant: 2095 vs. 601 for PPO; Walker2d: 3523 vs. 1270). SPPO also demonstrates robustness to hyperparameter choice, with average improvements of 10–25% over PPO across β ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 5.0, 10.0, 25.0} under 0% noise, and 22–167% under 10% noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, SPPO wins decisively: 16/22 Atari games in noise-free settings and 19/22 under 10% reward noise (Table 10), with particularly large gains on games where PPO struggles (e.g., Gopher: 7765 vs. 478; WizardOfWor: 4442 vs. 940 under noise). On RLHF tasks, SPPO improves RM Score by 0.03 on IMDB and 0.19 on TL;DR, and boosts the GPT-4-judged win rate from 43.25% to 52.50% on TL;DR summarization. Against A2C, SA2C shows only marginal improvements—tied or slightly worse in noise-free settings (e.g., Alien: 771 vs. 913; Centipede: 3574 vs. 3588) but consistent gains under reward noise (e.g., RiverRaid: 2511 vs. 1609; TimePilot: 3288 vs. 2232). The paper attributes SPPO's stronger gains to PPO's off-policy updates and advantage normalization with small mini-batches, which cause >5% (Atari) to >10% (MuJoCo) advantage sign flips that the reverse RL loss specifically counteracts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Symmetric Cross Entropy (SCE) from noisy-label classification (Wang et al., 2019) to the RL setting, adding a reverse cross-entropy term to the standard A2C and PPO policy losses. The conceptual bridge is that advantage sign confusion in PPO—caused by off-policy updates and advantage normalization with small mini-batches—behaves analogously to label noise in supervised learning, where the 'label' (advantage sign) may be wrong. Unlike prior robust-RL work that addresses perturbed rewards via confusion matrices (Wang et al., 2018) or ensembles, this approach operates at the loss-function level and requires no architectural changes, making it a lightweight drop-in modification to existing PPO/A2C implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPPO offers a practical, low-overhead stabilization technique for PPO that is robust across hyperparameters, which is valuable given that PPO is the dominant algorithm in RLHF pipelines for LLM alignment. However, the RLHF experiments use small models (GPT-2, GPT-J 6B, Qwen2-0.5B) and the method requires discretizing continuous action spaces, limiting direct applicability to large-scale LLM fine-tuning or high-dimensional continuous control. Open questions include whether the symmetric loss composes well with DPO/KTO-style preference optimization (which avoids explicit advantage estimation), whether the benefit persists at the scale of 70B+ parameter models, and whether the reverse loss term can be derived more principledly rather than by analogy to SCE.
