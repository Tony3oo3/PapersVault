## Problem

Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art image synthesis but lack a controllable semantic representation space, making them incompatible with existing counterfactual generation frameworks that rely on the abduction-action-prediction procedure of structural causal models. Prior diffusion-based counterfactual methods overlook the nuances of guidance and representation learning unique to diffusion, and no prior work addresses high-level semantic identity preservation or demonstrates principled tradeoffs between faithful causal control and identity preservation at Pearl's L3 rung.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (28×28 for Morpho-MNIST, 64×64 for CelebA-HQ, 192×192 for EMBED), the observed values of its parent variables in the SCM (e.g., digit class and slant for Morpho-MNIST; smiling, mouth open, and eyeglasses for CelebA-HQ; triangular/circular markers, breast density, and cancer status for EMBED), and a counterfactual intervention specifying which parent to set to a new value. It outputs a single counterfactual image (or a Monte Carlo average over M semantic posterior samples), with the exogenous noise decomposed into a spatial term u, a semantic term z, and per-timestep dynamic guidance tokens ∅₁:ₜ that are jointly inferred during abduction.

## Method

The paper introduces three diffusion-based causal mechanisms within a DSCM framework, distinguished by their exogenous noise decomposition and abduction procedure. The spatial mechanism uses DDIM generative transitions for prediction and DDIM inversion for abduction, treating the fully-noised image u as spatial exogenous noise that encodes low-level structure. The semantic mechanism adds a fixed Gaussian latent z learned via a CNN encoder trained with a variational objective (β-regularised KL plus denoising loss), enabling high-level identity preservation during counterfactual generation. Amortised anti-causal guidance reframes classifier-free guidance through a causal lens, using a modified noise estimator ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t) + ω(ε_θ(x_t, c̃_sem, t) − ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t)) to boost intervention faithfulness. Dynamic semantic abduction introduces Counterfactual Trajectory Alignment (CTA), a per-timestep linear update of guidance tokens that aligns the inverse DDIM trajectory with the guided generative trajectory, improving composition and identity preservation without the computational cost of full per-timestep optimisation.

## Results

On Morpho-MNIST with a simple DSCM (d→x), the guided semantic mechanism (ω=1.5) achieves the best composition (0.342×10⁻² L1) while the guided spatial mechanism (ω=3) achieves the highest effectiveness (99.95% classifier accuracy), both substantially outperforming VCI (92.48% acc, 2.05×10⁻² comp) and DiffSCM (17.02% acc). On the complex Morpho-MNIST DSCM with do(d) interventions, guided mechanisms reach 99.84–99.90% digit-class accuracy versus 90.04–94.62% for VCI and 94.92–97.27% for VAE, while maintaining comparable MAPE on slant, thickness, and intensity. On CelebA-HQ, the semantic mechanism (ω=2, p_∅=0.1) achieves F1(g)=96.86 and F1(s)=94.93 with LPIPS IDP of 0.096, improving identity preservation over the spatial counterpart (LPIPS 0.171) at a small effectiveness cost. On EMBED, the method removes 95.16±1.34% of triangular and 91.69±1.06% of circular skin markers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffSCM (Sanchez & Tsaftaris, 2022), the proposed methods dominate on effectiveness: DiffSCM achieves only 17.02% digit-class accuracy under do(d) on Morpho-MNIST, while all guided variants exceed 97%, indicating DiffSCM's counterfactuals barely reflect the intervention. Against VCI (Wu et al., 2024), the guided mechanisms achieve substantially higher digit-class accuracy (99.84–99.90% vs. 90.04–94.62%) under both do(s) and do(d), suggesting VCI struggles with global edits even when local MAPE metrics are competitive. Against the VAE baseline (Pawlowski et al., 2020), the proposed methods improve MAPE on slant, thickness, and intensity by 20–50% while boosting digit-class accuracy by 2–5 points. Against HVAE (De Sousa Ribeiro et al., 2023), the guided semantic mechanism matches or exceeds HVAE's composition (0.940 vs. 0.008×10⁻² at ω=1.5) while achieving 2.3–4.5 points higher digit-class accuracy. The key trade-off revealed is that increasing ω improves effectiveness (intervention faithfulness) at the cost of composition and reversibility (identity preservation), with the semantic mechanism consistently outperforming the spatial mechanism on IDP at matched ω.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep structural causal model (DSCM) framework for counterfactual image generation—previously instantiated with VAEs, HVAEs, GANs, and normalizing flows—by replacing the image-generating mechanism with a diffusion model (specifically DDIM). It builds on DiffAE's encoder-decoder architecture for semantic latent spaces but trains it end-to-end with a variational regularizer rather than post-hoc, and reframes classifier-free guidance through a causal (anti-causal score) lens to improve intervention faithfulness. The dynamic abduction component (Counterfactual Trajectory Alignment) directly extends Mokady et al.'s per-timestep guidance token optimization from the image-editing setting to the counterfactual prediction step, modeling guidance tokens as exogenous noise terms within the SCM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantification of the tradeoff between causal control (effectiveness) and identity preservation (composition/reversibility) in diffusion-based counterfactuals, showing that hyperparameters popular at the interventional L2 level (large ω, small p_∅) actively degrade L3 soundness. This has practical implications for medical imaging and face modelling where spurious changes are unacceptable. Key open questions include identifiability of the semantic latent (the unconditional VAE prior makes the model non-identifiable), scalability beyond small datasets, and whether the Markovian SCM assumption holds under noisy labels or low-resolution inputs—limitations the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
