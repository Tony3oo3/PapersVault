## Problem

Diffusion Probabilistic Models require hundreds of sampling steps for quality generation, and existing acceleration methods face a trade-off: training-free solvers degrade sharply below 10 NFE due to accumulated truncation errors, while training-based distillation methods demand 100+ A100 GPU hours, store additional model parameters, and disrupt the ODE trajectory's interpolation capability between disconnected modes. The paper asks whether truncation errors of existing fast solvers can be corrected with negligible parameter count and training cost while preserving the original trajectory structure.

## Input / Output

At inference, PAS takes an initial noise sample x_T, a frozen pre-trained noise-prediction network ε_θ, a chosen ODE solver φ (e.g., DDIM or iPNDM), a polynomial time schedule, and a small dictionary of trained coordinate vectors (4 scalars per corrected step, typically 1–5 steps, yielding ~4–20 parameters total). It outputs a generated image at the target resolution (32×32 to 512×512). The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing solver without modifying the pre-trained model, and the PCA computation per step takes ~0.06 s versus ~30 s per NFE in Stable Diffusion, making the overhead negligible.

## Method

PAS exploits the observation that each sampling trajectory lies in a ~3D subspace of the high-dimensional image space. At each step, it constructs a matrix from the trajectory history and current direction, applies SVD (via torch.pca_lowrank) to extract 4 orthogonal unit basis vectors via Schmidt orthogonalization, and represents the sampling direction as a linear combination of these basis vectors with 4 scalar coordinates. Training uses SGD on these coordinates (initialized with the current direction's norm on the first coordinate, zeros elsewhere) against ground-truth trajectories from a 100-NFE Heun solver, with L1 loss. An adaptive search strategy, motivated by the 'S'-shaped cumulative truncation error profile, selectively corrects only the high-curvature steps (where L2 improvement exceeds a tolerance τ), reducing the number of stored coordinate sets to 1–5 and total parameters to approximately 10. A theoretical analysis based on Gaussian score structure approximations explains why trajectories are low-dimensional and geometrically consistent across samples, enabling a single set of learned coordinates to generalize to all samples in a dataset.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), PAS reduces DDIM's FID from 15.69 to 4.37 at NFE=10 using only 12 learnable parameters and sub-minute training on a single A100 GPU. On FFHQ (64×64), DDIM+PAS achieves FID 5.61 at NFE=10 (vs. 18.37 for DDIM), and on LSUN Bedroom (256×256), it improves from 11.42 to 6.23. On Stable Diffusion v1.4 (512×512), DDIM+PAS achieves FID 14.23 at NFE=10, surpassing DPM-Solver-v3 (15.32), UniPC (16.15), and DPM-Solver++ (15.93) at the same NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAS consistently outperforms all trajectory-regularity baselines (AMED, GITS, TP) on CIFAR-10 and FFHQ: at NFE=10, DDIM+PAS achieves FID 4.37 vs. AMED (11.33), GITS (10.37), and TP (8.78). Against state-of-the-art fast solvers, DDIM+PAS at NFE=10 on CIFAR-10 (FID 4.37) beats DPM-Solver++ (5.16), DEIS-tAB3 (5.64), and UniPC (5.31), while iPNDM+PAS (FID 2.84) surpasses iPNDM (3.69) and all other solvers. On Stable Diffusion v1.4, DDIM+PAS (FID 14.23 at NFE=10) outperforms DPM-Solver-v3 (15.32), UniPC (16.15), and DPM-Solver++ (15.93), which use their optimal configurations. The improvement over iPNDM is smaller in absolute terms (e.g., 3.69→2.84 on CIFAR-10) because iPNDM already has low truncation error, but PAS still provides consistent gains across all four datasets. Notably, PAS requires only 4–12 learnable parameters (vs. millions in distillation-based methods) and 0–2 minutes of training on CIFAR-10, making it orders of magnitude cheaper than progressive distillation or consistency models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work observing that diffusion sampling trajectories lie in low-dimensional subspaces (Zhou et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Wang & Vastola, 2023/2024), but departs from those methods by replacing neural-network-based corrections with a PCA-derived basis and learning only a handful of scalar coordinates. It positions itself as a lightweight alternative to training-based distillation methods (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models, DMD) that require 100+ A100 GPU-hours and millions of additional parameters, and as a complement to existing fast ODE solvers (DDIM, iPNDM, DPM-Solver, DEIS, UniPC) rather than a replacement. The adaptive search strategy, motivated by the empirically observed 'S'-shaped cumulative truncation error, is a novel element not present in prior trajectory-regularity methods like AMED or GITS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme parameter efficiency (~10 learnable scalars) and sub-minute training cost on a single GPU make this a genuinely practical plug-and-play correction that could be adopted alongside existing solvers without significant infrastructure changes. It preserves the original ODE trajectory and thus retains interpolation capability between disconnected modes, a property lost by distillation-based methods. Open questions include whether the heuristic theoretical analysis (Gaussian score structure approximation) can be made rigorous, whether the approach extends to one-step sampling as the authors speculate, and whether it generalizes to video or audio generation where trajectory geometry may differ. The method also requires access to ground truth trajectories (from a high-NFE teacher solver) during training, which adds a dependency on the base model's quality.
