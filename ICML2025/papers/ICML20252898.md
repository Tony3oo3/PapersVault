## Problem

Existing CTR prediction models follow a discriminative paradigm that performs explicit interactions directly on raw ID embeddings, leading to two critical issues: embedding dimensional collapse (where low-cardinality fields constrain the representational capacity of all fields) and information redundancy (where interacted embeddings remain highly correlated). The paper asks whether reformulating CTR models under a generative 'feature generation' paradigm—where an encoder first constructs new hidden representations before any interaction occurs—can mitigate both issues while improving prediction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same multi-field categorical features (hundreds of user, item, and context-side features) as standard CTR models, each represented by a learned ID embedding of dimension d. The key structural difference is that the encoder receives the concatenation of all N feature embeddings as a single input vector of size Nd, and the decoder produces N generated embeddings (one per feature) from each encoded representation, following an 'all-predict-all' scheme. The final output is a scalar CTR probability via sigmoid, but the intermediate generated embeddings also serve as the new interaction inputs, replacing the raw ID embeddings used in the discriminative paradigm.

## Method

The Supervised Feature Generation (SFG) framework introduces an encoder-decoder structure around existing CTR interaction modules. The encoder is a field-wise single-layer non-linear MLP: it concatenates all N feature embeddings, applies a field-specific projection matrix W_{F(i)} ∈ R^{Nd×d}, and passes through ReLU to produce a hidden representation z_i for each feature i. The decoder maps z_i back to the original embedding space via a projection matrix W (whose structure—diagonal, full, or field-pair-wise—corresponds to the interaction function of the base model, e.g., FM, FmFM, or DCN V2), generating v_{i→j} for every target feature j. A classifier then computes relevance between generated and target embeddings across all feature pairs, and the entire pipeline is optimized with a supervised binary cross-entropy loss on the click/non-click label rather than a self-supervised reconstruction loss. This design avoids direct products between raw ID embeddings (preventing Interaction-Collapse), produces sample-specific decorrelated representations (reducing redundancy), and can be seamlessly grafted onto any existing feature interaction model by simply replacing the raw embedding input to the interaction function with the encoder output.

## Results

The Supervised Feature Generation (SFG) framework achieves an average 0.272% AUC lift and 0.435% Logloss reduction across all seven base models on Criteo and Avazu, with all improvements statistically significant (p < 0.05). Gains are largest for explicit feature interaction models (0.428% AUC, 0.689% Logloss) and more modest for DNN-based models (0.116% AUC, 0.181% Logloss). Notably, generative CrossNet V2 (0.81540 AUC on Criteo) outperforms discriminative DCN V2 (0.81387 AUC) by 0.106% AUC, and the framework narrows the inter-architecture AUC gap on Criteo from 1.151% (DIS) to 0.364% (GEN). Online A/B testing at Tencent's advertising platform yielded a 2.68% GMV lift and 2.46% CTR lift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between each model's discriminative (DIS) and generative (GEN) reformulation, and the SFG framework wins on every single model–dataset pair across both AUC and Logloss. The largest absolute gains appear for FM (+0.872% AUC on Criteo, +0.383% on Avazu) and FmFM (+0.440% AUC on Criteo), confirming that simpler explicit-interaction models benefit most from the generative encoder. Among DNN-based models, xDeepFM shows the strongest Avazu improvement (+0.207% AUC), while DCN V2 achieves the best absolute AUC under the generative paradigm (0.81472 on Criteo, 0.79342 on Avazu). In the supplementary comparison (Table 5), generative DCN V2 also outperforms feature-refinement baselines FiGNN (0.81352 AUC), GFRL (0.81427), and FRNet (0.81431) on Criteo, and similarly leads on Avazu (0.79342 vs. 0.79156/0.79296/0.79313). The generative paradigm additionally narrows the performance spread across architectures: the AUC gap between the strongest (DCN V2) and weakest (FM) model shrinks from 1.151% (DIS) to 0.364% (GEN) on Criteo, indicating that the encoder's representation learning partially compensates for architectural differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes CTR prediction from a discriminative 'feature interaction' paradigm to a generative 'feature generation' paradigm, drawing conceptual inspiration from autoregressive generation (next-token prediction), masked image modeling (MAR), and next-scale prediction (VAR), but adapting the generative structure to the unordered, multi-field categorical nature of CTR data via an 'all-predict-all' co-occurrence formulation. It builds on the Interaction-Collapse Theory (Guo et al., 2024) to motivate why direct interactions between raw ID embeddings are problematic, and repurposes the encoder-decoder structure from autoencoders, but with a key departure: replacing the self-supervised generative loss (standard in next-item prediction) with a supervised loss leveraging the click label. The encoder design is positioned as a generalization of gating mechanisms in FiBiNET, PEPNet, and FinalMLP, offering a representation-learning interpretation rather than an attention/weighting one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's broad applicability—seamlessly reformulating FM through DCN V2 with only a field-wise single-layer MLP added—makes it a low-cost, high-impact drop-in improvement for any existing CTR pipeline, as evidenced by its production deployment at Tencent yielding a 2.68% GMV lift. The diagnostic analyses (singular value spectra for dimensional collapse, Pearson correlation matrices for redundancy) provide reusable tools for evaluating embedding quality in CTR models. Open questions include whether the supervised loss is truly optimal versus hybrid self-supervised+supervised objectives, whether the simple MLP encoder remains optimal at much larger feature scales (hundreds of fields, higher-dimensional embeddings), and whether the paradigm extends naturally to multi-task or sequential CTR settings. The paper is limited to two standard datasets (Criteo, Avazu), leaving open how the approach performs on industrial-scale data with thousands of features.
