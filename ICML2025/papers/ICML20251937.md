## Problem

Offline value function learning is critically dependent on the quality of state-action representations, and poorly learned representations can cause temporal-difference methods to diverge under the deadly triad (off-policy updates, function approximation, bootstrapping). While bisimulation-based algorithms have shown promise for shaping representations in control, it remained unclear whether this class of methods can actually stabilize value function learning. This paper answers that question affirmatively by introducing KROPE and providing theoretical guarantees of stability.

## Input / Output

KROPE operates as an auxiliary task alongside FQE, consuming pairs of transition tuples (s, a, s', r) from an offline dataset D and the target policy π_e to shape the penultimate-layer features of FQE's action-value encoder into d-dimensional Euclidean representations. These learned representations are then frozen and fed into LSPE, which iteratively solves for linear weights θ to produce the final action-value estimates q̂^π_e(s,a). The two-stage linear evaluation protocol isolates the effect of representation quality from the value function learning algorithm itself.

## Method

KROPE defines a kernel k^π_e over state-action pairs that captures π-bisimilarity: it combines a short-term reward-similarity term k₁ with a discounted long-term term k₂ that measures similarity between next state-action distributions under π_e via independent coupling. The kernel is parameterized as a dot product of learned representations, and the algorithm minimizes a semi-gradient loss (with a target network) that enforces the fixed-point equation E[ΦΦᵀ] = E[K₁] + γE[P^π_e Φ (P^π_e Φ)ᵀ]. Theoretically, KROPE representations are proven to (1) stabilize LSPE by ensuring the spectral radius of the update matrix is less than 1, and (2) be Bellman complete under a reward-injectivity assumption. Empirically, KROPE is used as an auxiliary loss to FQE, introducing only one additional hyperparameter (the auxiliary task weight), and achieves lower and more stable MSVE than six baselines across 10/13 datasets.

## Results

On 13 offline datasets (4 DM Control + 9 D4RL), KROPE achieves the lowest normalized squared value error (MSVE) on 10/13 datasets, with 0.0±0.0 error on CartPoleSwingUp, CheetahRun, WalkerStand, cheetah-medium, cheetah-med-expert, and hopper-med-expert. KROPE is the only algorithm that produces MSVE ≤ 1 across 100% of runs on all instances where it converges, and it is the only method that avoids divergence on hopper-random (0.1±0.0) and walker-random (0.5±0.1). On Garnet MDPs (40 state-actions), KROPE maintains a spectral radius < 1 (stability) up to d=30 and achieves near-perfect q^π_e-consistency (Pearson correlation ≈ 1) between representation orthogonality and action-value difference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KROPE outperforms all six baselines on the majority of datasets where it converges. Against FQE (the primary baseline), KROPE avoids divergence on 10/13 datasets where FQE diverges on 10/13, and achieves strictly lower error on CartPoleSwingUp (0.0 vs. Div.) and hopper-random (0.1 vs. Div.). Against the other bisimulation-based methods, KROPE consistently beats DBC (which diverges on 12/13 datasets) and ROPE (which diverges on 9/13); on the 4 datasets where both KROPE and ROPE converge (CartPoleSwingUp, FingerEasy, WalkerStand, cheetah-random), KROPE matches or beats ROPE (e.g., 0.0 vs. 0.2 on CartPoleSwingUp). Against BCRL+EXP and BCRL, KROPE avoids divergence on cheetah-medium, cheetah-med-expert, hopper-random, and walker-random where both BCRL variants diverge, though BCRL+EXP achieves comparable error on cheetah-med-expert (0.2 vs. 0.0 for KROPE). Against the rank-regularization baselines DR3 and BEER, KROPE avoids divergence on hopper-random (0.1 vs. 0.8 for DR3) and walker-random (0.5 vs. 1.0 for DR3), while BEER diverges on 10/13 datasets. The one notable exception is walker-med-expert, where ROPE achieves 0.1±0.0 while KROPE diverges, and FingerEasy, where ROPE (0.1) and BCRL+EXP (0.6) outperform KROPE (0.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the π-bisimulation representation learning line (Castro 2020; Castro et al. 2021, 2023; Zhang et al. 2021), which defines state-action similarity via immediate rewards and next-state similarity under a fixed policy, but repurposes it from control toward offline policy evaluation. The key methodological departure is adopting the kernel perspective from Castro et al. (2023) to define a KROPE kernel over state-actions, which enables a clean spectral-radius proof that the resulting representations stabilize LSPE. Prior work (Pavse & Hanna 2023a) empirically showed bisimulation mitigates FQE divergence but offered no theoretical explanation; this paper fills that gap by proving both non-expansiveness and Bellman completeness of KROPE representations. It also positions against BCRL (Chang et al. 2022) as an alternative Bellman-complete approach, finding KROPE more robust to hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why bisimulation-based representations stabilize offline value function learning, giving practitioners a principled tool (KROPE) that achieves stable OPE on 10/13 datasets where baselines like FQE, DBC, and BCRL diverge. A notable open question is that KROPE's semi-gradient learning objective can still diverge (observed in 3 of 13 datasets), and the authors identify a Legendre-Fenchel-based minimax reformulation as a potential path to a provably convergent variant. The finding that representation learning with KROPE may be more robust than value function learning with FQE (requiring pairs of bad transitions rather than single bad transitions to diverge) suggests a practical strategy: invest in shaping representations rather than in the value function itself.
