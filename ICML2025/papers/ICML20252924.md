## Problem

Existing multi-agent LLM frameworks handle social or game simulation but cannot model the complexity of collaborative research activities like multi-author paper writing and multi-reviewer peer review. Prior research-automation efforts are limited to single tasks (idea generation, code experimentation) or single-agent workflows, leaving open whether an LLM-based system can realistically simulate the full collaborative research community process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252924/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For paper reading and writing, the system consumes paper abstracts and researcher publication profiles; for review writing, it additionally ingests full paper texts. The community graph encodes three edge types—authorship (agent-data), citation (data-data), and review expertise (agent-data)—and the simulation proceeds in three sequential stages. Outputs are standardized to condensed bullet-point formats: papers are summarized via five structured questions (problem, importance, difficulty, prior work gap, key approach/results), and reviews are decomposed into bullet-point strengths, bullet-point weaknesses, and a numerical score, enabling iterative message passing without unbounded text growth.

## Method

The paper introduces the agent-data graph, a heterogeneous graph where agent nodes carry LLM functions (prompt templates + profiles) rather than learned embeddings, and data nodes carry text attributes. On this graph, TextGNN performs message passing entirely in text space: each layer concatenates neighborhood text into LLM prompts, replacing the embedding-space aggregation of standard GNNs. The research simulation is a 2-layer TextGNN—layer 1 initializes agent hidden states by reading cited papers (Eq. 6), and layer 2 generates paper content (Eq. 7) and review content (Eq. 8) by aggregating over both agent and data neighbors. Evaluation is framed as a node-masking prediction task: a target paper or review is hidden, the simulator reconstructs it from its neighborhood, and similarity is measured via embedding cosine distance over the 5Q-decomposed or bullet-point-decomposed representations, yielding the RESEARCHBENCH benchmark (1,000 paper + 200 review tasks from NeurIPS/ICLR 2024).

## Results

On RESEARCHBENCH (1,000 paper writing + 200 review writing tasks from NeurIPS/ICLR 2024), RESEARCHTOWN (AGG-global) achieves an overall embedding-based similarity of 67.51 for paper writing (73.79 easy, 67.85 medium, 60.89 hard) using text-embedding-3-large. For review writing, it scores 51.51 on strengths and 47.17 on weaknesses, with an average score deviation (ΔS) of 1.55. The framework also demonstrates robustness: adding more agents improves paper writing from 49.0 (1 agent) to 52.7+ (2+ agents), and including related-work citations boosts similarity from 65.2 to 66.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external multi-agent frameworks; instead, it ablates four aggregation strategies as internal baselines. For paper writing, RESEARCHTOWN (AGG-global, 67.51) outperforms AGG-data (65.30) by 2.21 points overall and by a larger 4.87 points on the hard subset (60.89 vs. 56.02), showing that multi-researcher collaboration adds value beyond simply reading cited papers. AGG-agent (55.24) and AGG-self (46.08) are substantially weaker, confirming that both data context and agent profiles are necessary. For review writing, all four strategies perform within ~1 point of each other (strengths: 51.23–51.66; weaknesses: 46.75–47.62), with AGG-agent slightly best for strengths and AGG-data slightly best for weaknesses, suggesting that the paper itself dominates review generation. Across generation models (Table 3), GPT-4o-mini achieves the highest paper-writing similarity (67.51), followed by Deepseek-v3 (65.33) and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (65.30), while Deepseek-v3 produces the smallest review score deviation (ΔS = 0.81 under AGG-global).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-agent LLM simulation and graph-based reasoning. Prior multi-agent LLM frameworks (SOTOPIA, S3, WarAgent) target social or game simulation, while research-automation efforts (ResearchAgent, AI Scientist, MLAgentBench) handle isolated tasks like idea generation or code experimentation with single-agent workflows. The key conceptual departure is the agent-data graph, where agent nodes carry LLM functions rather than embeddings, and the TextGNN framework that performs message passing in text space rather than embedding space—repurposing the GNN aggregation paradigm for multi-agent text generation. The paper also extends text-attributed graph research by moving from node/link prediction to full node reconstruction and generation of non-existent nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RESEARCHTOWN provides the first principled, graph-structured framework for simulating collaborative research activities (reading, writing, reviewing) as a unified process, and RESEARCHBENCH offers a scalable, objective node-masking evaluation that avoids the subjectivity and cost of human review. The demonstration that LLMs can generate plausible interdisciplinary research directions (NLP × astronomy, NLP × criminology) suggests a path toward automated idea discovery across field boundaries. Key limitations include the bullet-point output format (not full papers), the gap in novelty/feasibility versus real-world work, and incoherent outputs when combining too many disparate domains. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger, more heterogeneous communities, whether it can generate truly novel (not merely similar) ideas, and how to incorporate multimodal research artifacts like code and experiments.
