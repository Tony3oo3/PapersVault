## Problem

Pseudolabeling-based SSL methods rely on heuristic thresholding and unreliable confidence scores (e.g., softmax outputs prone to overconfidence and miscalibration), with no principled mechanism to explicitly control the quality-quantity tradeoff of pseudolabels. This paper asks: can we learn confidence scores and thresholds from first principles to maximize the number of pseudolabeled points while guaranteeing the pseudolabeling error stays below a user-specified tolerance ε?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252440/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled set (N_l = 40–2500 points), a large unlabeled pool (50K–600K RGB images at 32×32), and a portion of validation data split into calibration data (D_cal, 1K–3K points) for training the confidence function and threshold-estimation data (D_th, 1K–3K points) for threshold selection. It produces a trained SSL classifier (WRN-28-2 backbone), a learned 2-layer neural confidence function that takes the backbone's last two layer representations as input, per-class or joint thresholds in [0,1], and pseudolabels with masks for the unlabeled pool. The confidence function is re-learned every F iterations as the backbone evolves.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating pseudolabeling as a constrained optimization: maximize pseudolabeling coverage subject to pseudolabeling error ≤ ε, solved over a flexible space of 2-layer neural confidence functions and threshold vectors. In practice, population-level coverage and error are replaced by finite-sample estimates on calibration data with differentiable sigmoid surrogates for the 0-1 mask, yielding a non-convex but differentiable objective (P1) solved by SGD. Thresholds are then estimated separately on held-out validation data by scanning candidate thresholds and selecting the smallest one where the estimated error plus a statistical safety margin (C₁·σ̂) stays below ε, providing a principled guarantee on pseudolabel quality. The learned scores and thresholds are plugged into existing SSL pipelines (FixMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch, AdaMatch) in place of their original confidence/thresholding components, and an optional pseudolabel accumulation step reuses prior pseudolabels for points that fall below the current threshold. The error tolerance ε acts as an explicit knob: lower ε yields higher-quality but fewer pseudolabels, and the paper shows a decreasing ε schedule across training is optimal.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 250 labels, Fixmatch+Ours achieves 93.10% top-1 accuracy, improving over Fixmatch (90.8%) by ~2.3% and over Freematch (92.26%) by ~0.8%. The gains are most dramatic on the harder CIFAR-100 (2500 labels) setting, where Fixmatch+Ours reaches 68.76% versus Fixmatch's 59.09% (~9.7% improvement) and Freematch's 63.13% (~5.6% improvement). On SVHN (250 labels), improvements are marginal (e.g., Fixmatch+Ours 96.65% vs. Fixmatch 97.57%), consistent with SVHN being an easier task. The method also outperforms calibration-focused baselines (Fixmatch+MR, Fixmatch+BaM) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PabLO consistently outperforms all six baselines on CIFAR-100, the hardest setting: Fixmatch+Ours (68.76%) beats Fixmatch (59.09%) by ~9.7%, Freematch (63.13%) by ~5.6%, Softmatch (61.43%) by ~7.3%, and Adamatch (58.08%) by ~10.6%. On CIFAR-10, improvements are more modest but still positive: Fixmatch+Ours (93.10%) exceeds Fixmatch (90.8%) by 2.3% and Freematch (92.26%) by 0.8%, while Softmatch+Ours (93.14%) and Adamatch+Ours (93.06%) also surpass their respective baselines by 1.4% and 1.7%. The calibration-focused variants (Fixmatch+MR, Fixmatch+BaM, Freematch+MR, Freematch+BaM) are all outperformed by the corresponding +Ours methods on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, indicating that learning task-specific confidence scores is more effective than generic calibration regularizers. On SVHN, where the task is easier and baselines already saturate near 97%, PabLO provides only marginal or no improvement, with Fixmatch+Ours (96.65%) slightly below Fixmatch (97.57%). Even when baselines are given the additional calibration data used by PabLO for training (Table 5), they still fall significantly short on CIFAR-100 (e.g., FixMatch+train+cal at 64.77% vs. Fixmatch+Ours at 68.76%), confirming that the gains come from the learned scores and thresholds rather than extra labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the confidence-function learning framework from Vishwakarma et al. (2024) on auto-labeling to the pseudolabeling-based SSL setting, replacing heuristic or calibration-based score-and-threshold choices (as in FixMatch, FreeMatch, DASH, FlexMatch) with a principled optimization that maximizes pseudolabeling coverage subject to an explicit error tolerance. It departs from prior calibration-focused approaches (BaM, Margin Regularization) by arguing that calibration is neither necessary nor sufficient for good pseudolabeling—what matters is the ability to segregate correct from incorrect predictions at a target error level. The method is integrated as a drop-in replacement for the pseudolabeling component in FixMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch, and AdaMatch, preserving their consistency regularization and other regularizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a principled knob (error tolerance ε) to trade off pseudolabel quality against quantity, which is a fundamental tension in SSL that prior methods handle only heuristically. The ~10% top-1 accuracy gain on CIFAR-100 (the hardest setting) demonstrates that better pseudolabel selection can substantially close the gap to fully supervised performance. A key limitation is the reliance on held-out validation data (N_cal + N_th) for learning scores and estimating thresholds, which may be costly in practice; the authors note that 500 points suffice but acknowledge this as an open problem. Future work could explore reducing validation data requirements via generative models or data augmentation, and extending the framework to settings where even small validation sets are unavailable.
