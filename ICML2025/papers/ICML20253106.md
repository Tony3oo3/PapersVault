## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), the dominant training-time safety alignment method for LLMs, exhibits two systemic flaws identified through gradient analysis: its effective learning rate for safe responses diminishes as the preference margin grows (premature saturation in refusal learning), and it lacks explicit out-of-distribution regularization, leaving models vulnerable to novel jailbreak attacks such as prefilling, adversarial suffix, and multi-turn exploits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253106/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes safety tuning triples (harmful prompt, safe refusal, harmful response) where the prompt is optionally augmented by prepending a random-length prefix of the harmful response to simulate partial unsafe generation. In W-DOOR, a proxy reward model assigns per-token weights to the safe response, concentrating gradient updates on critical refusal transition tokens (e.g., 'cannot', 'Sorry') rather than treating all tokens uniformly. The model produces a standard autoregressive text response—either a refusal or a helpful answer—whose safety is evaluated via an LLM-judge computing attack success rate across multiple adversarial settings.

## Method

DOOR decomposes DPO into two complementary objectives: (1) robust refusal training, which applies cross-entropy SFT on safe responses augmented with partial harmful prefixes to teach the model to revert to refusal even mid-harmful-generation, and (2) targeted unlearning via Negative Preference Optimization (NPO), which penalizes harmful outputs relative to a reference model without the catastrophic degradation of naive gradient ascent. Gradient analysis shows DOOR maintains a constant learning rate of 1 for safe-response logits (versus a diminishing rate in DPO) and introduces an additional term that uniformly reduces all response logits, serving as implicit OOD regularization. W-DOOR further refines the refusal objective with a reward-based token-level weighting: a proxy reward model (approximated by a DPO-aligned policy) assigns higher weights to tokens where the ideal policy diverges most from the reference, enabling the model to decisively 'snap back' to refusal at critical transition points. A retain loss on benign utility data is mixed in with a tunable ratio to preserve general capabilities while enhancing safety.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B, W-DOOR achieves the lowest Prefilling ASR (0.034) and Multi-turn ASR (0.447) among all training-based methods, while maintaining HellaSwag accuracy of 0.573 (vs. 0.577 for the original model). On Gemma-2-2B, W-DOOR further reduces Prefilling ASR to 0.005 and Multi-turn ASR to 0.347, compared to 0.346 and 0.554 for the unaligned model. DPO, by contrast, shows a Prefilling ASR of 0.210 (Llama-3-8B) and the highest over-refusal rate on XSTest (0.456), while also degrading HellaSwag to 0.564. The gap between W-DOOR and DPO on Prefilling ASR is roughly 6× on Llama-3-8B and 12× on Gemma-2-2B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, W-DOOR substantially outperforms on every safety metric: Prefilling ASR drops from 0.210 to 0.034 (Llama-3-8B) and from 0.060 to 0.005 (Gemma-2-2B), Multi-turn ASR improves from 0.521 to 0.447, and HellaSwag accuracy is preserved (0.573 vs. 0.564), while DPO also exhibits the highest over-refusal rate (XStest 0.456). Compared to SFT with data augmentation (Qi et al.), W-DOOR achieves lower Prefilling ASR (0.034 vs. 0.071) and Multi-turn ASR (0.447 vs. 0.511) on Llama-3-8B, and better HellaSwag retention (0.573 vs. 0.564), though SFT has a slightly lower XStest refusal rate (0.396 vs. 0.440). Against Representation Rerouting (RR), W-DOOR wins decisively on Prefilling ASR (0.034 vs. 0.338) and Multi-turn ASR (0.447 vs. 0.213), while RR achieves lower GCG (0.045 vs. 0.093) and AutoDAN (0.000 vs. 0.088) ASR, indicating complementary strengths. Against Tampering Attack Resistance (TAR), W-DOOR dominates on all safety metrics (e.g., Prefilling 0.034 vs. 0.536, AutoDAN 0.088 vs. 0.578) and HellaSwag (0.573 vs. 0.522), suggesting TAR provides minimal practical defense under the evaluated attack suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes the DPO loss (Rafailov et al., 2024) into two complementary objectives—robust refusal training via SFT with harmful-prefix data augmentation and targeted unlearning via NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a)—motivated by a gradient-level analysis showing DPO's learning rate for safe responses saturates prematurely and its OOD generalization is uncontrolled. The data augmentation strategy aligns with Qi et al. (2024), while the token-level weighting mechanism draws on KL-regularized reward principles from Xu et al. (2024b) and Qiu et al. (2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from DPO's relative-preference framing by arguing that safety alignment requires absolute reinforcement of refusal tokens rather than merely widening the safe-vs-harmful margin, and it positions DOOR/W-DOOR as a drop-in replacement within existing preference-optimization pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient decomposition provides a clean, actionable explanation for why DPO underperforms in safety settings, and the dual-objective formulation is a practical, low-overhead improvement that practitioners can adopt without changing their training infrastructure. However, multi-turn jailbreaks (Crescendo) remain largely unsolved by all tested methods, and the token-level weighting relies on an approximate reward model whose quality is sensitive to the reference policy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (only 2B and 8B tested), whether more sophisticated augmentation (beyond uniform-length prefix concatenation) can reduce the over-refusal side effect, and whether the observed correlation between KL divergence magnitude and robustness implies a fundamental limit on how far alignment can push internal representations without degrading utility.
