## Problem

Existing face-to-speech (F2S) methods can replicate speaker identity from a face image but fail to generate speech with rich emotional prosody, producing over-smoothed, emotionally flat output. More fundamentally, no prior method can simultaneously customize both identity and emotion from visual cues alone without relying on any speech prompt, leaving a gap for applications like virtual character dubbing and assisting individuals with expressive language disorders.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single face image (from which identity is extracted via ArcFace+FaceNet and emotion via Poster2) and a text prompt (phonemized to IPA, then encoded by SpeechT5) as input, and produces a 24 kHz emotional speech waveform decoded from 12-level RVQ tokens (codebook size 1024) via a neural codec decoder. A frame-level duration predictor estimates the output length from the text embedding before generation begins, and the entire pipeline requires no reference speech at inference time.

## Method

DEmoFace is a discrete diffusion framework built on a 12-level RVQ codec, where a Multimodal DiT (MM-DiT) with 12 transformer blocks (768 channels, 12 heads) predicts per-level concrete scores. Identity and emotion conditions are injected via AdaLN (concatenated with timestep), while text is aligned through cross-attention with rotary position embeddings; 12 linear heads output scores for each RVQ level. Training uses a coarse-to-fine curriculum that progressively introduces higher-frequency RVQ levels every 3 epochs, combined with a DSE loss and a cross-modal alignment loss (cosine + L1 + L2) that maps face identity embeddings to GE2E speaker embeddings. At inference, an Enhanced Predictor-Free Guidance (EPFG) modulates the concrete score by multiplying a compositional term (per-condition guidance scales w₁, w₂, w₃) with a joint term (all-condition scale w₀), derived from an EBM energy-function decomposition, enabling effective multi-conditional generation and attribute decoupling. The Euler sampler runs 96 steps, achieving an RTF of 0.49 at batch size 1.

## Results

In the visual-guided setting (no audio input), DEmoFace achieves EmoSim 0.6965 and SpkSim 0.6679, outperforming Face-TTS (0.5230 / 0.1968) by 17.35% and 47.11% respectively, while also improving MCD from 8.46 to 6.86 (−18.96%) and RMSE from 118.96 to 101.18 (−14.95%). In the acoustic-guided setting, DEmoFace* (using speech speaker embeddings instead of face embeddings) achieves the best scores across all metrics: EmoSim 0.7921, SpkSim 0.7990, MCD 6.5505, and WER 19.72%. Subjective evaluation confirms a 38% relative improvement in MOS_nat (3.17 vs. 2.30 for EmoSpeech and 2.28 for Face-TTS), with DEmoFace also leading in both MOS_id (3.20) and MOS_emo (3.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only visual-guided baseline Face-TTS, DEmoFace wins decisively on every metric except WER (20.78% vs. 17.47%), with the largest gains in SpkSim (+47.11% relative) and EmoSim (+17.35% relative), confirming that the discrete diffusion + EPFG framework captures identity and emotion far better than the prior face-styled diffusion approach. Remarkably, the visual-guided DEmoFace also surpasses all acoustic-guided baselines (EmoSpeech, FastSpeech2, V2C-Net, HPM, StyleDubber) on SpkSim and MCD, bridging the cross-modal gap using only heterogeneous face features. When given the same acoustic speaker embedding (DEmoFace*), the method dominates all acoustic-guided baselines by large margins: +3.1% EmoSim over EmoSpeech (0.7921 vs. 0.7667), +41.1% SpkSim over EmoSpeech (0.7990 vs. 0.5677), and −33.2% WER (19.72% vs. 29.59%). In subjective evaluation, DEmoFace leads all three MOS dimensions (nat, id, emo) over both EmoSpeech and Face-TTS, with the most pronounced gap in naturalness (3.17 vs. 2.30/2.28). The only metric where DEmoFace trails is WER in the visual-guided setting, where Face-TTS benefits from over 10× more training data (400 h LRS3 vs. 31.33 h combined).

## Relation to Prior Work

DEmoFace sits at the intersection of three research threads: discrete diffusion models for token generation (building on the DSE objective and concrete score formulation from Lou et al., 2024), the DiT transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and predictor-free guidance for conditional generation (Nisonoff et al., 2024). It departs from prior face-to-speech work (Goto et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2023) that only captures speaker identity from a face, and from emotional TTS methods (EmoDiff, V2C-Net) that require speech prompts or discrete emotion labels, by jointly extracting both identity and emotion from a single face image. The RVQ-based codec tokenization follows the MaskGCT/VALL-E lineage but replaces autoregressive decoding with parallel discrete diffusion, and the EPFG extends single-condition PFG to a multi-conditional setting via an EBM-inspired energy decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that high-quality emotional speech can be synthesized from purely visual cues without any vocal reference, which is relevant for virtual character dubbing and accessibility for individuals with expressive language disorders. The EPFG mechanism for decoupling multiple conditioning signals in discrete diffusion is a transferable technique applicable beyond speech. However, the authors acknowledge that visual-voice biases in the training data lead to 'average-sounding' speech rather than faithful voice reconstruction, and the 31-hour dataset limits pronunciation accuracy and diversity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse datasets and whether the curriculum learning strategy generalizes to other hierarchical token structures.
