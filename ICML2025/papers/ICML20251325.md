## Problem

Existing data reconstruction attacks in split inference have primarily targeted smaller CNN models like ResNet18, leaving the privacy risks of large vision foundation models (e.g., CLIP-ViT, DINOv2) in split inference settings largely unexplored. This paper investigates whether these widely deployed transformer-based encoders are vulnerable to reconstruction from deep-layer intermediate representations and proposes a new attack that exploits the rich image priors of pre-trained latent diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251325/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes as input the intermediate representation (IR) from a specified split layer of a white-box client model (token sequences for ViTs at 224×224 resolution, or feature maps for CNNs) and a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) serving as the image prior. It produces a reconstructed 224×224 RGB image approximating the original input. The optimization operates in the LDM's latent space rather than pixel space, with the client model applied to Tweedie-estimated clean images for guidance computation, and an ℓ₂ regularization constraining the decoded image to the [-1,1] range.

## Method

DRAG formulates reconstruction as guided diffusion in the LDM's latent space: starting from pure noise z_T, it iteratively denoises toward z_0 while steering the trajectory using a guidance signal computed as the gradient of the IR-matching distance d_H(f_c(x̂₀), h*) with respect to z_t, where x̂₀ is the single-step denoised estimate via Tweedie's formula. The guidance is refined with gradient clipping and an Adam optimizer to handle the non-convexity of f_c, and a self-recurrence mechanism (projecting z_{t-1} back to z_t via small-step DDPM and repeating k=16 times per timestep) ensures sufficient optimization at each denoising step. The noise direction is aligned with the guidance using the spherical Gaussian constraint from DSG, balancing the image prior against the IR-matching objective via a guidance strength parameter w. DRAG++ extends this by initializing the diffusion process from a coarse reconstruction produced by a trained inverse network, projected onto the editable manifold by adding noise at a small timestep, yielding improved perceptual quality at deep split points.

## Results

On CLIP-ViT-B/16 at the deepest split point (Layer 12), DRAG achieves DINO similarity of 0.9331, LPIPS of 0.1857, and MS-SSIM of 0.6735, substantially outperforming rMLE (DINO 0.6524), LM (DINO 0.4248), and GLASS (DINO 0.4553). At Layer 9, DRAG reaches DINO 0.9652 versus 0.7159 (rMLE), 0.9063 (LM), and 0.6740 (GLASS). The advantage is consistent across DINOv2-Base and CLIP-RN50, with DRAG maintaining DINO > 0.90 at the deepest split points where all baselines drop below 0.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAG decisively outperforms all three baselines at deep split points (Layer 6–12 for ViT, Block 3–5 for ResNet). Against rMLE (He et al., 2019), DRAG gains +0.28 DINO at Layer 12 on CLIP-ViT-B/16 (0.9331 vs. 0.6524) and +0.28 LPIPS improvement (0.1857 vs. 0.5900). Against LM (Singh et al., 2021), DRAG wins at Layer 6 and beyond (e.g., DINO 0.9331 vs. 0.4248 at Layer 12), while LM retains a narrow edge at the two shallowest split points (Layer 0–3). Against GLASS (Li et al., 2023), which uses a domain-specific StyleGAN prior, DRAG wins at every split point on CLIP-ViT-B/16 and DINOv2-Base, with the gap widening sharply at depth (DINO 0.9331 vs. 0.4553 at Layer 12). Notably, DRAG uses a single domain-agnostic prior (Stable Diffusion v1.5) whereas GLASS requires a separate GAN per dataset, yet DRAG generalizes better across MSCOCO, FFHQ, and ImageNet-1K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data reconstruction attack (DRA) literature in split inference—previously focused on CNN models like ResNet18 (He et al., 2019; Singh et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023)—to Vision Transformer foundation models (CLIP-ViT, DINOv2). Methodologically, it repurposes the guided diffusion framework from UGD (Bansal et al., 2024) and the spherical Gaussian constraint from DSG (Yang et al., 2024), combined with Tweedie-based posterior estimation (Chung et al., 2023), as a domain-agnostic image prior for inverting intermediate representations, replacing the GAN-based prior used by GLASS. The inverse network component from He et al. (2019) is integrated as a coarse initialization (DRAG++), and the LDM latent-space adaptation (Rombach et al., 2022) enables efficient high-resolution reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that widely deployed vision foundation models in split inference remain vulnerable to high-fidelity reconstruction even under existing defenses (DISCO, NoPeek), with DRAG achieving DINO similarity >0.9 at deep layers where prior methods collapse below 0.5. The ViT-specific token shuffling defense analysis reveals a unique architectural property (token order invariance) that both enables and limits this defense, opening a new axis for privacy research in transformer-based SI. Limitations include evaluation on only 10 images per dataset and a white-box assumption, leaving open whether the attack generalizes to black-box or query-free settings with foundation models, and whether stronger defenses (e.g., differential privacy on IRs) can close the gap.
