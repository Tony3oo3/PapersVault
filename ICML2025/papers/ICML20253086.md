## Problem

While textual RAG poisoning attacks (e.g., PoisonedRAG, TrojanRAG) have been studied, no prior work has explored knowledge poisoning for Vision-Language RAG (VLRAG) systems that retrieve from multimodal image-text databases. This paper asks whether a single injected poison image-text pair can reliably manipulate the response of a VLRAG system for a target query, and how to craft such a sample to satisfy both retrievability and inducibility in the joint vision-language embedding space.

## Input / Output

The attacker specifies a target query (text t_t + image i_t), a desired target response r_t, and has white-box access to the retriever's image encoder E_i and text encoder E_t. For the class-targeted variant, the attacker additionally collects H images (H=30 by default) from the target class to estimate a class-center embedding. The output is a single poison image-text pair (t_p, i_p): the text t_p is generated by prompting an LLM (e.g., GPT-4) with a fixed instruction template, and the image i_p is produced by applying a bounded perturbation (ε=16) to a source image via signed gradient descent over the retriever's joint embedding space.

## Method

The paper proposes three progressively stronger attacks. PoisonedEye-B (black-box) simply sets the poison image equal to the target image and uses an LLM-generated instruction template as the poison text, requiring no model access. PoisonedEye-S (white-box) initializes the poison image from the target image and optimizes a perturbation δ via PGD to minimize the L2 distance between the normalized query embedding (E_i(i_t)+E_t(t_t)) and the poison embedding (E_i(i_p)+E_t(t_p)) in the retriever's joint space. PoisonedEye-C (white-box) generalizes to an entire class by estimating the class-center embedding from H collected images and optimizing the poison image to minimize distance to the class-center-based query embedding, so the poison is retrievable for any image from that class. The key empirical finding is that inducibility is nearly guaranteed once the poison text is retrieved (PSR ≈ RSR-K), making retrievability the sole bottleneck that the image optimization addresses.

## Results

Under the class query targeted setting, PoisonedEye-C achieves an average RSR-1 of 86.91%, RSR-K of 92.41%, and ARD of 0.7906 across two retrievers (Siglip-so400m, CLIP ViT-H) and three datasets (ImageNet-1k, Places-365, Country-211), substantially outperforming the baseline PoisonedEye-B (49.69% RSR-1, 58.20% RSR-K) and the single-query PoisonedEye-S (75.62% RSR-1, 82.79% RSR-K). The Poison Success Rate (PSR) closely tracks RSR-K, indicating that whenever the poison sample is retrieved, the LVLM is highly likely to produce the target response (e.g., 92.63% PSR for PoisonedEye-C on Places-365 with Siglip-so400m and LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B). Even the simplest baseline (PoisonedEye-B, which only sets the poison image equal to the target image without optimization) achieves 49.69% top-1 retrieval success, demonstrating that VLRAG systems are inherently vulnerable to a single injected sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external attack methods but instead presents three progressively stronger variants of its own framework. PoisonedEye-B (the baseline, adapted directly from PoisonedRAG's text-only approach with the poison image set equal to the target image) achieves 49.69% average RSR-1, establishing that even a naive single-sample injection is effective. PoisonedEye-S improves retrieval by optimizing the poison image via signed gradient descent to minimize the L2 distance to the target query embedding, boosting average RSR-1 to 75.62% (+25.93 pp over B). PoisonedEye-C further extends the attack to an entire class of queries by estimating the class center embedding from H=30 collected images, reaching 86.91% average RSR-1 (+11.29 pp over S). In terms of defenses, RoCLIP reduces the original attack PSR from 92.63% to 0.00%, but an enhanced attack that jointly minimizes query-to-poison and image-to-text distance bypasses RoCLIP, recovering 54.52% PSR. Random crop reduces PSR to 45.20% and noise to 64.65%, indicating that no single defense fully neutralizes the attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the textual RAG poisoning line of work initiated by PoisonedRAG (Zou et al., 2024) to the vision-language setting, where the knowledge database contains image-text pairs rather than pure text. While PoisonedRAG, TrojanRAG, and AgentPoison all manipulate only the textual content of the database, PoisonedEye must additionally craft an adversarial image to satisfy the retrievability property in a multimodal embedding space (CLIP/SigLIP). The image optimization step borrows the standard signed gradient descent (PGD) framework from adversarial robustness literature, but applies it to the joint image-text embedding geometry of a contrastive retriever rather than to a classification network's input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that poison success rate closely tracks retrieval success rate (PSR ≈ RSR-K) simplifies the defense problem: securing the retrieval stage is nearly sufficient, since the LLM is highly susceptible to the crafted instruction text once the poison sample is retrieved. However, the paper also shows that the RoCLIP defense (re-matching images to their most similar text) can be bypassed with a minor loss modification, and that even random cropping leaves ~45% attack success, leaving the field without a robust countermeasure. Open questions include whether effective data-filtering mechanisms can detect the subtle perturbations (ε=16) used here, whether the attack generalizes to VLRAG systems with different retrieval architectures (e.g., cross-attention retrievers), and how to defend against the class-targeted variant that requires only a single poison sample to affect an entire image category.
