## Problem

Existing image tokenizers, whether 2D grid-based or 1D (e.g., TiTok), encode every image into a fixed number of tokens regardless of its semantic complexity, forcing autoregressive generators to always produce the full token sequence even when a simple condition (like a single class label) could be satisfied with far fewer tokens. This rigid, resolution-dependent token budget wastes compute and prevents adaptive, content-aware generation.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images, compresses them through a 16-channel VAE (8× downsampling) and 2×2 patchification (16× total), and outputs an ordered 1D sequence of 1–256 FSQ-quantized discrete tokens (64,000 vocabulary). Any prefix of this sequence—e.g., just the first 8 tokens—can be independently decoded into a plausible image via the rectified flow decoder. For generation, the AR model additionally consumes an ImageNet class label or a text prompt (via FLAN-T5-XL cross-attention) and autoregressively predicts the token sequence, which is then decoded to a 256×256 image.

## Method

FlexTok is a two-stage autoencoder: a ViT encoder with learnable register tokens resamples 2D VAE latent patches into a 1D sequence, which is discretized via 6-dimensional FSQ (levels [8,8,8,5,5,5]). Two mechanisms induce a coarse-to-fine ordering: (1) nested dropout that randomly truncates the register sequence to powers of two (1, 2, 4, …, 256) during training, and (2) a causal attention mask restricting each register to attend only to earlier registers. The decoder is a rectified flow model conditioned on the (possibly truncated) quantized registers and noised VAE patches, trained with a flow-matching loss plus a REPA cosine-similarity loss against DINOv2-L features to accelerate convergence and improve semantics. In Stage 2, a LlamaGen-style causal Transformer (49M–3B parameters) is trained to predict the FlexTok token sequence autoregressively from a class or text condition, enabling generation where the number of tokens produced can adapt to the complexity of the conditioning signal.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k class-conditional generation, FlexTok d18-d28 achieves a generation FID (gFID) of 1.86 and reconstruction FID (rFID) of 1.45 at 32 tokens, outperforming all fixed-length baselines at the same token budget. A key differentiator is that a single FlexTok model handles all sequence lengths from 1 to 256 tokens, whereas baselines require separate models per token count. For text-conditioned generation on COCO, FlexTok-based AR models outperform a matched 2D grid tokenizer in CLIPScore at 2–128 tokens, with the full 256-token sequence yielding the best text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 32 tokens, FlexTok d18-d28 (rFID 1.45, gFID 1.86) substantially outperforms TiTok-L (rFID 2.21, gFID 2.77), the closest 1D baseline at the same token budget, and also beats the larger TiTok-B (64 tokens, gFID 2.48) and TiTok-S (128 tokens, gFID 1.97) on gFID. Against 2D grid tokenizers, FlexTok d18-d28 improves gFID over LlamaGen (3.06) by 1.2 points and over Taming VQ-GAN (5.20) by 3.3 points; however, Open MAGVIT-v2 retains a slight rFID advantage (1.17 vs. 1.45) at the cost of a much larger codebook (262K vs. 64K). For text-conditioned generation, FlexTok outperforms a matched 2D grid flow-matching tokenizer in CLIPScore at 2–128 tokens, though the 2D grid model achieves a marginally better gFID at the full 256-token budget. In reconstruction comparisons (Figure 4), FlexTok produces semantically coherent images down to a single token, whereas ALIT and TiTok degrade significantly at low token counts and require separate models per configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexTok builds directly on TiTok's register-token architecture for 1D image resampling and FSQ for the discrete bottleneck, but departs from both TiTok and 2D-grid tokenizers (LlamaGen, MaskGIT) by making the token sequence length content-dependent rather than fixed to image resolution. The ordering mechanism combines nested dropout (Rippel et al., 2014) with causal attention masks to induce a coarse-to-fine hierarchy, while a rectified flow decoder (replacing TiTok's MSE-trained decoder) enables plausible reconstruction at any truncation length. Concurrent adaptive tokenization works (ElasticTok, ALIT, One-D-Piece) address variable length but are limited to minimum token counts of 32–256, require multi-stage training, or do not evaluate generative performance; FlexTok unifies these concerns in a single end-to-end model that handles 1–256 tokens and is validated for AR generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that AR image generation can use adaptive compute budgets: simple conditions (ImageNet classes) are satisfied with 8–16 tokens while complex text prompts benefit from the full 256, enabling early stopping without a separate model per resolution. The emergent coarse-to-fine 'visual vocabulary'—where high-level semantics appear in the first few tokens without explicit supervision—suggests a natural interface between language-model-style next-token prediction and image content hierarchy. Open questions include whether this extends to higher resolutions, text rendering, and video/audio domains, and whether the AR model can learn a principled stopping criterion rather than relying on fixed token budgets. The 256×256 resolution and the observation that long sequences (>128 tokens) require substantially larger AR models to maintain quality are current limitations.
