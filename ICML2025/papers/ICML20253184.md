## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on adaptive density control to distribute Gaussian primitives, but existing densification strategies fail to balance reconstruction quality and efficiency: they either produce blurred regions from too few primitives or redundancy from too many. The paper identifies that this imbalance stems from treating all spatial regions uniformly, ignoring the fact that human perception is far more sensitive to local structural details in certain areas than others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and an SfM-derived point cloud, from which it precomputes binary perceptual sensitivity maps via Sobel edge detection followed by threshold-based binarization and smoothing. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation where each primitive carries an additional learnable perceptual sensitivity parameter ε_i (sigmoid-constrained to [0,1]) alongside standard geometry, color, and opacity, enabling both novel view rendering and sensitivity-aware density control. The framework is designed to produce fewer Gaussians than vanilla 3DGS while matching or exceeding perceptual quality metrics.

## Method

Perceptual-GS introduces a dual-branch rendering framework where, in addition to the standard RGB branch, a sensitivity branch renders 2D sensitivity maps by alpha-blending each Gaussian's learned ε_i, supervised by BCE loss against precomputed binary sensitivity maps. After a 500-iteration warm-up, perceptual sensitivity-guided densification selectively splits or clones Gaussians whose learned sensitivity exceeds high (τ_h=0.9) or medium (τ_l=0.3) thresholds, gated by a per-Gaussian weight constraint to avoid over-densification within objects. A scene-level sensitivity metric β determines whether to use split (texture-rich scenes) or clone (simpler scenes) operations. For scenes with sparse initial point clouds—detected by the proportion γ of large Gaussians exhibiting medium sensitivity—scene-adaptive depth reinitialization refines primitive distribution. An opacity decline mechanism (power function x^1.2) is applied during cloning to reduce regional opacity, facilitating pruning of redundant primitives and further constraining the total Gaussian count.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Perceptual-GS achieves the best PSNR (28.01 dB), SSIM (0.839), and LPIPS (0.172) among all compared methods, while using only 2.69M Gaussians (vs. 3.14M for vanilla 3DGS and 5.23M for Pixel-GS). On the large-scale BungeeNeRF dataset, Perceptual-GS attains 27.86 dB PSNR and 0.095 LPIPS with 4.97M Gaussians at 89 FPS, whereas Pixel-GS and Taming-3DGS both suffer CUDA OOM failures. Across all four datasets, Perceptual-GS consistently achieves the best or near-best perceptual quality (LPIPS, SSIM) with the fewest Gaussian primitives among quality-focused methods, demonstrating a superior quality-efficiency trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, Perceptual-GS wins on all three quality metrics across all four datasets (e.g., +0.30 dB PSNR and −0.030 LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360) while also reducing the Gaussian count from 3.14M to 2.69M. Compared to Pixel-GS, Perceptual-GS achieves higher PSNR (+0.16 dB) and lower LPIPS (0.172 vs. 0.176) on Mip-NeRF 360 with roughly half the Gaussians (2.69M vs. 5.23M); on BungeeNeRF, Pixel-GS OOMs in one scene while Perceptual-GS completes all eight. Against Mini-Splatting-D, Perceptual-GS leads on PSNR for Mip-NeRF 360 (+0.50 dB) and BungeeNeRF (+2.28 dB), though Mini-Splatting-D retains a slight LPIPS edge on Tanks & Temples (0.140 vs. 0.151) and Deep Blending (0.211 vs. 0.231). Taming-3DGS marginally outperforms Perceptual-GS on PSNR for Tanks & Temples (24.04 vs. 23.90) and Deep Blending (30.14 vs. 29.94), but OOMs on two BungeeNeRF scenes where Perceptual-GS succeeds. When integrated with CoR-GS for sparse-view (24-view) settings, Perceptual-GS improves PSNR by +0.16 dB and reduces LPIPS by 0.060, confirming its generalizability as a plug-in module.

## Relation to Prior Work

Perceptual-GS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) by introducing a perceptual sensitivity parameter to each Gaussian primitive and a dual-branch rendering pipeline that maps 2D sensitivity maps onto 3D primitives. It departs from prior densification strategies (Pixel-GS, Mini-Splatting-D, Taming-3DGS) that refine gradient-based selection criteria, instead grounding the densification decision in human visual perception (contrast sensitivity, JND, SSIM-inspired local structure analysis). The approach also draws on foveated rendering literature (Franke et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025a) but shifts the perceptual adaptation from rendering-time selection to training-time distribution, and incorporates scene-adaptive depth reinitialization inspired by Mini-Splatting (Fang & Wang, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quality-efficiency trade-off in 3DGS is a practical bottleneck for VR/AR and digital twin applications where both visual fidelity and real-time rendering speed are required; Perceptual-GS addresses this by achieving SOTA perceptual quality (LPIPS, SSIM) with fewer Gaussians and higher FPS across four datasets, including large-scale BungeeNeRF scenes where competitors hit OOM. The modular design—demonstrated by successful integration with Pixel-GS and CoR-GS—suggests the perceptual sensitivity framework could become a drop-in component for future 3DGS variants. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted Sobel-based sensitivity extraction could be replaced by learned perceptual models, whether the approach extends to dynamic (4D) scenes, and whether the fixed thresholds (τ_h, τ_l, τ_ω) generalize across scene types without manual tuning.
