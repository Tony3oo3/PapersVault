## Problem

Behavior-regularized offline RL constrains policies to stay close to the data-collection policy to prevent exploitation of out-of-distribution actions, but existing formulations assume explicit policy parameterizations (Gaussian, deterministic) where KL divergence is directly computable. Extending this framework to diffusion-based policies is non-trivial: the log-probability of generated actions is intractable, making the regularization term ambiguous, and it is unclear how to simultaneously improve a diffusion policy while enforcing the behavior constraint without backpropagating through the entire denoising chain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (state, action, next_state, reward) tuples and, at deployment, a single environment state s_t. It outputs a continuous action a_t by running a conditional diffusion model for N denoising steps (N=5 on D4RL, N=50 on synthetic 2D tasks); at evaluation it generates 10 candidate actions in parallel and selects the one with the highest Q-value. A separate behavior diffusion p^ν is first pretrained on the dataset via standard denoising loss and then frozen to serve as the regularization anchor throughout training.

## Method

BDPO reframes the KL regularization from the marginalized action distribution to the full diffusion generation path, exploiting the Markov property to decompose it into a sum of per-step KL divergences between the actor's and behavior's Gaussian reverse transition kernels; each term reduces to ‖μ_π − μ_ν‖²/(2σ_n²), yielding a closed-form penalty. To optimize efficiently, the method employs a two-timescale actor-critic: the upper level updates Q^π with standard TD backups in the environment MDP (accumulating pathwise KL penalties at the next state), while the lower level maintains diffusion value functions V_n^{π,s}(a^n) that perform TD updates across intermediate diffusion steps, so policy improvement only requires a single-step reverse diffusion rather than backpropagating through all N steps. The paper proves that the pathwise KL-regularized objective is equivalent to the standard KL-regularized RL objective (same optimal policy), and in the continuous-time limit the discrete pathwise KL converges to the Girsanov-theorem expression. Practically, an ensemble of K=10 value networks with lower-confidence-bound targets provides pessimism on OOD actions, and (s, a^n, n) triplets are sampled off-policy from the dataset with n ~ U[1,N] and a^n ~ q_{n|0}(·|a).

## Results

On the D4RL locomotion benchmark (9 tasks), BDPO achieves a total normalized score of 856.7, outperforming the next-best method DAC (836.4) by ~2.4% and the non-diffusion baselines CQL (698.5) and IQL (749.7) by 22.6% and 14.3% respectively. On the antmaze navigation benchmark (6 tasks), BDPO scores 519.6, surpassing IDQL-A (474.9) by 9.4% and DAC (459.9) by 13.0%. The largest individual gains appear on halfcheetah-medium-v2 (71.2 vs. 59.1 for DAC) and antmaze-large-diverse-v0 (84.0 vs. 67.9 for IDQL-A), where the multi-modal action distribution and sparse-reward navigation challenge unimodal policies most severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDPO achieves the highest locomotion sum (856.7) and antmaze sum (519.6), beating the strongest diffusion-based competitors DAC (836.4 / 459.9) and DTQL (798.3 / 441.4) by 2.4% / 13.0% and 7.3% / 17.8% respectively. Against non-diffusion baselines, the gap is even larger: CQL (698.5 / 303.6) and IQL (749.7 / 378.0) trail by 18.5% / 41.5% on the two sums. BDPO does not win every individual task: DAC edges it on hopper-medium-v2 (101.2 vs. 100.6) and walker2d-medium-v2 (96.8 vs. 93.4), while SfBC slightly leads on antmaze-umaze-diverse-v0 (85.3 vs. 83.0, though BDPO's std of ±12.4 overlaps). The most decisive wins are on halfcheetah-medium-v2 (+12.1 over DAC) and antmaze-large-diverse-v0 (+28.7 over IDQL-A), confirming that the pathwise KL regularization and two-timescale value functions are most beneficial when the optimal policy is strongly multi-modal or the reward signal is sparse. Runtime is competitive: BDPO's total training time (303 min at N=50) is comparable to DAC (285 min) and roughly half that of Diffusion-QL (560 min), since BDPO avoids back-propagating through the full diffusion path.

## Relation to Prior Work

BDPO extends the behavior-regularized offline RL framework (Wu et al., 2019; Haarnoja et al., 2018) to diffusion-based policy parameterizations, where prior work was limited to unimodal (Gaussian or deterministic) actors. The key conceptual departure from Diffusion-QL (Wang et al., 2023) and DAC (Fang et al., 2024) is the pathwise KL decomposition: rather than backpropagating through the entire diffusion path or formulating optimization as noise regression, BDPO computes the KL penalty analytically at each single-step reverse transition and introduces diffusion value functions at intermediate steps. This connects to the SDE/score-based formulation (Song et al., 2021) via a Girsanov-theorem-based continuous-time limit, unifying the discrete and continuous perspectives of the regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BDPO provides the first principled, analytically tractable way to impose behavior regularization on full diffusion policies in offline RL, resolving a gap that limited prior diffusion-based offline RL methods to either one-step actors or expensive full-path backpropagation. The two-timescale actor-critic with diffusion value functions offers a reusable architectural pattern for any setting where a generative process is embedded within an MDP. Open questions include whether the off-policy sampling of (s, a^n, n) triplets can be tightened, how the method scales to very high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., multi-joint robotics), and whether the pathwise KL formulation generalizes to other sequential generative models beyond diffusion.
