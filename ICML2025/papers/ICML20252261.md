## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs are not trained for context-augmented generation, making them incompatible with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that require grounding answers in retrieved documents. While a few knowledge-based VQA datasets exist, they are limited to images linkable to Wikipedia passages, rely on template-based question construction, and exhibit very low question diversity (e.g., <1% unique questions in InfoSeek), leading to poor generalization when used for fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

Each training sample consists of an RGB image (sourced from LAION-400m, WIT Wikipedia images, or synthetic COCO-Counterfactuals), a context document (either GPT-4-generated or Wikipedia-sourced), and a natural-language question, producing a single-word or short-phrase answer. On average, each image-context pair is associated with 7 QA pairs (7.1 for GPT-4-generated contexts vs. 5.7 for Wikipedia contexts). Two filtered subsets are provided: SK-VQA_IR (excluding contexts that directly reference the image) and SK-VQA_IR+CAP (further requiring the answer to appear explicitly in the context).

## Method

The core pipeline prompts GPT-4 (gpt-4o-2024-05-13) to generate both a context document and multiple QA pairs in a single inference step for a given image, with the context generation explicitly conditioned on the downstream task of producing questions that require reasoning over both the image and the context. This joint generation strategy ensures contextual relevance and avoids the mismatch that arises when contexts are sourced independently (e.g., from Wikipedia). Images are drawn from three diverse sources—LAION, WIT, and COCO-Counterfactuals—decoupling the dataset from the Wikipedia-linking constraint of prior work. Two heuristic filters (Image Reference and Context Answer Presence) create progressively stricter subsets. For evaluation, the authors fine-tune LLaVA and PaliGemma on ~200K samples from SK-VQA and compare against models fine-tuned on InfoSeek and Enc-VQA, demonstrating that SK-VQA-trained models achieve superior out-of-domain generalization across all tested benchmarks and also perform well in a simulated RAG setting using CLIP-based retrieval.

## Results

SK-VQA contains over 2 million QA pairs with 1,928,336 unique questions (11× more than Enc-VQA's 175,000), making it the largest KB-VQA dataset to date. In zero-shot evaluation, the best model (LLaVA-v1.6-34B) achieves only 50.02% on SK-VQA versus 77.73% on Enc-VQA and 83.75% on ViQuAE, confirming SK-VQA as a substantially harder benchmark. For fine-tuning, LLaVA-7B trained on SK-VQA achieves 47.35 on InfoSeek, 63.89 on Enc-VQA, and 77.60 on ViQuAE (out-of-domain), outperforming models trained on InfoSeek (38.68, 69.90, 43.69) and Enc-VQA (31.39, 55.59, 21.60) across all out-of-domain settings. In a simulated RAG setup with retrieved contexts, SK-VQA-trained PaliGemma-3B surpasses the zero-shot baseline and both baselines in all 9 out-of-domain cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SK-VQA against two existing KB-VQA datasets (InfoSeek and Enc-VQA) as training resources, and against ViQuAE as an additional evaluation benchmark. For LLaVA-7B fine-tuning, SK-VQA-trained models outperform InfoSeek-trained models on all three out-of-domain datasets (Enc-VQA: 63.89 vs 38.68; ViQuAE: 77.60 vs 69.90; SK-VQA_IR: 68.30 vs 43.69), while InfoSeek-trained models only excel in-domain (68.68 on InfoSeek). Similarly, SK-VQA-trained models dominate Enc-VQA-trained models out-of-domain (InfoSeek: 47.35 vs 31.39; ViQuAE: 77.60 vs 55.59; SK-VQA_IR: 68.30 vs 21.60), whereas Enc-VQA-trained models only win in-domain (88.75 on Enc-VQA). The same pattern holds for PaliGemma-3B and LLaVA-1.6-13B, confirming that SK-VQA's diversity (11× more unique questions, broader image sources, GPT-4-generated contexts) translates into superior generalization. In the RAG ablation, SK-VQA-trained PaliGemma-3B beats both baselines and the zero-shot baseline in all 9 out-of-domain evaluation cases, whereas InfoSeek-trained PaliGemma degrades below zero-shot in 2 of 3 settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior KB-VQA datasets (ViQuAE, InfoSeek, Encyclopedic-VQA) that rely on template-based question construction and Wikipedia-linked images, instead using GPT-4 to jointly generate context documents and QA pairs for arbitrary images from LAION, WIT, and COCO-Counterfactuals. It repurposes the synthetic data generation paradigm—well-established in text-to-image and NLP RAG contexts—for the first time in multimodal KB-VQA, enabling fully synthetic image-context-QA triples. The paper positions itself within the broader multimodal RAG literature (RA-CM3, MuRAG, REVEAL, Wiki-LLaVA) but focuses on adapting the generator rather than the retriever, filling a gap where existing MLLMs were never trained for context-augmented generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SK-VQA provides the largest and most diverse KB-VQA resource to date (2M+ QA pairs, 11× more unique questions than Enc-VQA), and its public release makes it a likely standard benchmark for evaluating context-augmented MLLMs. The finding that synthetic image-context pairs (COCO-CFs + GPT-4) can match or exceed real Wikipedia pairs for fine-tuning suggests synthetic data generation may reduce dependence on curated real-world resources. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond English, whether the GPT-4 dependency limits reproducibility, and whether the dataset's synthetic contexts adequately capture the retrieval challenges of real RAG systems where irrelevant passages must be filtered.
