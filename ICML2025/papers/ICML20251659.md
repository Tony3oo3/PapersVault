## Problem

Genomic foundation models (GFMs) such as DNABERT-2 are too large and computationally expensive for deployment on resource-constrained devices (edge devices, mobile phones, IoT systems), and applying standard efficiency techniques like LoRA fine-tuning or post-training quantization to these models causes severe performance degradation due to outlier values in the attention mechanism. No existing GFM architecture simultaneously supports robust quantization, rapid low-rank adaptation, and practical deployment without requiring prohibitively expensive retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw DNA sequences tokenized via SentencePiece with BPE (vocabulary size 4096), with input lengths ranging from 70 to 1000 nucleotides. ALiBi positional encoding is used instead of learned embeddings, enabling the model to process sequences longer than those encountered during pre-training. The output is a class label for genomic classification tasks (epigenetic mark prediction, promoter detection, transcription factor binding, etc.), evaluated via Matthews Correlation Coefficient across 27 datasets spanning 7 tasks and 4 species.

## Method

GERM replaces the standard softmax attention in the transformer with a Softmax_1 activation function (exp(S)/(1+Σexp(S_i))) derived from associative memory (Hopfield) models, which naturally suppresses outlier attention weights by allowing attention heads to effectively 'do nothing' for irrelevant tokens. This outlier-free mechanism is applied during both pre-training (full retraining, 200K steps) and fine-tuning, yielding a 92.14% reduction in average kurtosis and 82.77% reduction in max infinity norm. To avoid the cost of full retraining, GERM-T introduces a small-step continual learning strategy that resumes training with the outlier-free architecture for only 40K additional steps from an existing DNABERT-2 checkpoint, achieving near-optimal quantization robustness at a fraction of the computational cost. The paper also provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem A.2) showing that under non-singularity assumptions, low-rank adapters of sufficient rank can exactly represent the target model when using Softmax_1, establishing the expressiveness of LoRA in this outlier-free setting.

## Results

On 27 genomic classification datasets spanning 7 tasks and 4 species, GERM achieves an average MCC of 57.27 under LoRA fine-tuning (vs. 50.91 for DNABERT-2), representing a 37.98% average performance improvement in low-rank adaptation. In post-training quantization, GERM reduces the average performance drop from 38.63% (DNABERT-2, SmoothQuant W8A8) to just 4.82%, and under W4A4 OmniQuant, GERM retains 49.42 MCC versus 2.94 for DNABERT-2. GERM also reduces average kurtosis by 92.14% and maximum infinity norm by 82.77% compared to DNABERT-2, directly addressing the outlier problem that hinders both quantization and adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline DNABERT-2, GERM wins decisively across all evaluation settings: it improves LoRA MCC from 50.91 to 57.27 (a 37.98% average performance improvement), reduces the W8A8 SmoothQuant performance drop from 38.63% to 4.82%, and under W4A4 OmniQuant retains 49.42 MCC versus DNABERT-2's 2.94. The compromise variant GERM-T (40K continual learning steps) also outperforms DNABERT-2, achieving a 20.01% average LoRA improvement and a 31.42% reduction in quantization performance drop, though it lags behind full GERM at 4-bit quantization due to residual outliers. On the Nucleotide Transformer (NT) architecture, GERM achieves a 52.01% average improvement in low-rank adaptation and 67.69% in post-training quantization over the vanilla NT approach, confirming generalizability beyond DNABERT-2. Compared to the outlier-removal techniques of Bondarenko et al. (2024) (clipped softmax and gated attention), GERM achieves a 2.59% MCC improvement at 4-bit and 1.99% at 8-bit quantization, demonstrating that the Softmax₁-based outlier-free layer is more effective than post-hoc attention modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the outlier-free Hopfield attention mechanism (Softmax_1) from Hu et al. (2024a) to the genomic foundation model domain, replacing the standard softmax in DNABERT-2's transformer blocks. It departs from the original OutEffHop work in two key ways: (1) it introduces GERM-T, a small-step continual learning strategy that avoids the computationally prohibitive retraining-from-scratch requirement of the original method, and (2) it provides a theoretical expressiveness guarantee for LoRA under Softmax_1, extending the analysis of Zeng & Lee (2024) to the outlier-free setting. The paper also positions itself against the broader quantization literature (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, Outlier Suppression) by arguing that architectural outlier removal is more fundamental than post-hoc quantization calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for biomedical labs operating on resource-constrained hardware: GERM enables 5-minute fine-tuning on a single RTX 2080 Ti and maintains usable accuracy under 8-bit quantization where DNABERT-2 collapses. However, GERM-T still suffers significant degradation at 4-bit quantization and under QLoRA/LoftQ, indicating that partial outlier removal via continual learning is insufficient for the most aggressive compression regimes. An open question is whether outliers can be eliminated entirely without any retraining, and whether the Softmax_1 mechanism generalizes to non-transformer genomic architectures (e.g., HyenaDNA, Mamba-based Caduceus) that the authors acknowledge but do not test.
