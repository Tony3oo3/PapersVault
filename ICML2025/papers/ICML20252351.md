## Problem

Constrained reinforcement learning methods achieve strong performance in autonomous systems but enforce safety only as soft constraints, offering no formal guarantees, while formal methods like HJ reachability and CBFs provide rigorous safety assurances at the cost of overly conservative, suboptimal control. The paper addresses the co-optimization of safety and performance as a state-constrained optimal control problem, where the resulting HJB-PDE is intractable for high-dimensional systems due to the curse of dimensionality in grid-based solvers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a known nonlinear dynamics model f, a failure set encoded via a scalar function g(x), a running cost l(x), a terminal cost φ(x), and a time horizon T as inputs, and produces a state-feedback policy π*(t,x), the optimal value function V(t,x), a probabilistic safety guarantee (a δ-level on the auxiliary value function with confidence 1−β_s and violation rate ε_s), and a probabilistic performance error bound (a ψ-level with confidence 1−β_p and violation rate ε_p). The augmented state x̂=[x,z]ᵀ includes an auxiliary cost variable z whose dynamics are ż=−l(x), and the policy is extracted by minimizing the Hamiltonian over the control set U at each state.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-stage pipeline: (1) an epigraph reformulation (Altarovici et al., 2013) eliminates hard state constraints from the SC-OCP, yielding an auxiliary value function V̂ that satisfies a coupled HJB-PDE; (2) a physics-informed neural network (3-layer MLP with sine activations, 256 neurons/layer) learns V̂ by minimizing PDE residual and boundary-condition losses under a curriculum schedule that progressively expands the time sampling window from [T,T] to [0,T], with adaptive loss re-balancing to stabilize training; (3) a conformal prediction-based safety verification searches for the tightest δ-level such that the sub-δ level set of V̂ is safe under the induced policy with probability ≥1−ε_s at confidence 1−β_s, using a Beta-distribution CDF bound; (4) a second conformal prediction procedure quantifies performance degradation by bounding the normalized gap between V_θ and the rollout cost V_{π_θ} over safe states. The final policy is recovered by binary search on z in the epigraph constraint V̂_θ(t,x,z)≤δ followed by argmin of the Hamiltonian, yielding a 2 ms inference time across all tested systems.

## Results

Across three experiments of increasing dimensionality (2D boat navigation, 8D pursuer-evader, 20D multi-agent), the proposed method achieves 100% safety rate while incurring the lowest cumulative cost among all baselines. The performance gap widens with system complexity: in the 20D multi-agent setting, MPPI incurs 148% higher cost, MPPI-NCBF 192% higher, and SAC-Lag 164% higher than the proposed method. Conformal prediction verification yields δ-levels of 0, −0.04, and −0.09 (with 1−10⁻¹⁰ confidence) and ψ-levels of 0.136, 0.137, and 0.068 for the three experiments, confirming tight safety and performance guarantees. Online inference takes only 2 ms across all systems, while offline training scales sublinearly with dimensionality (122 min for 2D vs. ~390 min for grid-based Level Set solvers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all baselines on the joint safety-performance objective. MPPI achieves the best performance among baselines but at the cost of safety (72–90% safety rate) and 18–148% higher cost; its finite-horizon, sample-based approach leads to deadlocks in the multi-agent setting. Safety-filtering methods (MPPI-NCBF, MPPI-C3BF) guarantee 100% safety but are overly conservative, incurring 42–192% higher cost because the filter operates independently of the performance objective. Constrained RL methods (SAC-Lag, PPO-Lag, CPO) enforce safety only as soft constraints, resulting in the lowest safety rates (66–76%) while also performing poorly on cost (7.5–101% higher in 2D/8D; 164% higher in 20D). The gap between the proposed method and all baselines widens monotonically with system dimensionality, confirming that the epigraph-based HJB-PDE formulation with PIML scaling is essential for high-dimensional co-optimization. In the 2D boat experiment, the learned value function also closely matches the ground truth from the Level Set Toolbox (MSE = 0.36 over a range of 0–14.76), validating the PIML approximation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established threads: the epigraph reformulation of state-constrained optimal control (Altarovici et al., 2013), physics-informed neural networks for solving HJB PDEs (Raissi et al., 2019; Bansal & Tomlin, 2021), and conformal prediction for distribution-free uncertainty quantification (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2022; Vovk, 2012). The key departure from DeepReach (Bansal & Tomlin, 2021) is that the epigraph formulation encodes both safety and performance in a single auxiliary value function, rather than solving a safety-only reachability problem. The conformal prediction layer is a novel addition that converts the learned (approximate) value function into one with explicit probabilistic safety and performance guarantees, addressing a gap that purely data-driven PIML approaches leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path to deploying safe, high-performance controllers in high-dimensional autonomous systems (demonstrated up to 20D) with 2ms inference, where grid-based HJB solvers are intractable and CRL methods lack formal safety guarantees. The probabilistic guarantees via conformal prediction (e.g., 99.9% safety with 1−10⁻¹⁰ confidence) are a meaningful step toward certifying learned controllers for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include extending to unknown or stochastic dynamics, rapid online adaptation when the environment changes, and whether the conformal verification step can be made more efficient for very large state spaces. The dramatic widening of the performance gap over baselines with dimensionality (7.5% in 2D to 148–192% in 20D) suggests the approach's advantage grows precisely where it is most needed.
