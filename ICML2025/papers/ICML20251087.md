## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion acceleration methods that reduce per-step computation via step-wise or token-wise sparsity achieve speedups but suffer from significant fidelity degradation (high LPIPS and FID) relative to unmodified baselines. The authors attribute this gap to two shortcomings: fixed or pre-searched sparsity patterns cannot adapt to the varying denoising trajectory of each prompt, and these methods ignore the underlying ODE formulation and its numerical solver, leading to uncorrected approximation errors.

## Input / Output

SADA is a training-free, plug-and-play accelerator that wraps around any ODE-based generative pipeline (diffusion or flow-matching). It takes a text prompt (and optionally a conditional image for ControlNet) as input and produces a generated image or audio clip as output, operating entirely in the latent space of the underlying model. The method requires no architectural modifications or fine-tuning and is applied at inference time on top of existing schedulers (EDM, DPM-Solver++) with 50 steps by default, though it also supports fewer-step settings (25, 15).

## Method

SADA introduces a unified stability criterion (Criterion 3.4) that measures the local dynamics of the denoising trajectory via the second-order difference of the ODE velocity field y_t, dynamically selecting between step-wise and token-wise sparsity at each timestep. When the trajectory is stable, SADA applies step-wise cache-assisted pruning using a third-order Adams-Moulton estimator that leverages precise gradient information from the chosen ODE solver, or a multistep-wise Lagrange interpolation scheme for larger effective step sizes in stable regions. When unstable, it switches to token-wise cache-assisted pruning, partitioning tokens into unstable (full computation) and stable (approximated via cached representations) sets at each transformer layer. The key distinction from prior work is that SADA directly bridges numerical ODE solvers with sparsity-aware optimizations, using the solver's gradient to both guide the sparsity decision and correct approximation error, yielding error-bounded reconstructions (O(Δt) for x_0^t) without any additional training.

## Results

SADA achieves consistent ≥1.8× speedups with LPIPS ≤ 0.100 and FID ≤ 4.5 across SD-2, SDXL, and Flux using both EDM and DPM-Solver++ schedulers at 50 steps. On SDXL with DPM-Solver++, SADA reduces FID from 8.48 (DeepCache) to 3.51 (59%↓) while maintaining 1.86× speedup. On Flux.1-dev with flow matching, SADA achieves the best FID of 1.95 and LPIPS of 0.060 at 2.02× speedup, substantially outperforming TeaCache (FID 4.89, LPIPS 0.216 at 2.00×). SADA also generalizes to MusicLDM (~1.81× speedup, ~0.01 spectrogram LPIPS) and ControlNet (~1.41× speedup) without modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADA outperforms all three baselines on quality metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID) in every configuration tested. Against DeepCache, SADA improves FID by 59% on SDXL/DPM++ (3.51 vs 8.48) and by 60% on Flux (1.95 vs 4.89), while also achieving higher speedup in most settings (1.80× vs 1.43× on SD-2/DPM++; 1.86× vs 1.74× on SDXL/DPM++). Against AdaptiveDiffusion, SADA achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.094 vs 0.100 on SD-2/DPM++; 0.084 vs 0.125 on SDXL/DPM++) and higher speedup (1.80× vs 1.45×; 1.86× vs 1.65×), though AdaptiveDiffusion marginally edges out SADA on raw speedup in two Euler-scheduler settings (1.89× vs 1.81× on SD-2; 2.01× vs 1.85× on SDXL) at the cost of significantly worse FID (7.58 vs 4.26; 6.11 vs 3.76). Against TeaCache on Flux, SADA matches the speedup (2.02× vs 2.00×) while dramatically reducing FID (1.95 vs 4.89) and LPIPS (0.060 vs 0.216), demonstrating that SADA's stability-guided sparsity allocation preserves far more perceptual fidelity at equivalent computational savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SADA extends the adaptive diffusion acceleration paradigm initiated by AdaptiveDiffusion (Ye et al., 2024), which uses a third-order finite-difference criterion to decide when to skip denoising steps, and the token-pruning framework from Bolya & Hoffman (2023) and Kim et al. (2024) that reduces per-step attention cost. The key departure is twofold: (1) SADA unifies step-wise and token-wise sparsity decisions under a single stability criterion derived from the second-order difference of the ODE velocity field, rather than treating them as independent heuristics; and (2) it replaces naive noise reuse with principled Adams-Moulton and Lagrange interpolation approximations that explicitly leverage the gradient information from the chosen numerical ODE solver. The authors claim this is the first paradigm to directly bridge numerical ODE solvers with sparsity-aware architecture optimizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SADA provides a practical, training-free, plug-and-play acceleration that consistently achieves ≥1.8× speedup with LPIPS ≤ 0.10 and FID ≤ 4.5 across U-Net (SD-2, SDXL) and DiT (Flux) backbones, both EDM and DPM++ solvers, and even generalizes to audio (MusicLDM) and conditional generation (ControlNet) without modification. This makes it immediately deployable in production pipelines where inference cost is the bottleneck. Open questions include whether the stability criterion degrades under very aggressive step reduction (speedup drops to ~1.25× at 15 steps), how it interacts with emerging few-step or one-step consistency models, and whether the per-prompt adaptivity can be further exploited for batch-level scheduling in high-throughput serving.
