## Problem

MoE LLMs face prohibitive storage and memory costs from storing multiple expert weights, but existing compression methods create a fundamental dilemma: expert pruning irreversibly loses specialized knowledge, while expert merging assumes high functional overlap that contradicts the observed moderate CKA similarity (0.3–0.5) between experts. No prior method simultaneously achieves high compression ratios, preserves expert diversity, and operates without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250795/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained MoE expert weight matrices (e.g., 8×7B for Mixtral, 64 experts for Qwen2-57B-A14B) and 512 calibration samples from WikiText-2 to compute Fisher importance scores and activation statistics. It outputs a single shared base weight matrix W_b (further pruned via semi-dynamic structured pruning) plus per-expert low-rank factor pairs (ΔU_i ∈ R^{m×k}, ΔV_i ∈ R^{k×n}) from truncated SVD, where k is determined by the target compression ratio. At inference, only the top-k routed experts' delta pairs are activated alongside the shared base weight, yielding a structured, acceleratable compressed model.

## Method

D²-MoE decomposes each expert weight W_i into a shared base W_b and a delta ΔW_i = W_i − W_b, then compresses each component differently. The base weight is computed via Fisher-weighted averaging (weighting each expert by its Fisher information matrix to prioritize high-sensitivity experts over simple averaging), then further reduced by a two-phase semi-dynamical structured pruning: a static phase prunes columns with consistently low L2-norm × activation-norm scores across all inputs, and a dynamic phase re-evaluates remaining columns per input batch to adapt to the current distribution. Delta weights are compressed via truncation-aware SVD (from SVD-LLM), where the delta matrix is first scaled by the Cholesky factor of the activation Gram matrix before decomposition, and small singular values are truncated to yield compact ΔU_i and ΔV_i factors. The key empirical insight is that delta weights exhibit strong low-rank structure (concentrated singular value energy), making them far more compressible than the original expert weights, while the moderate inter-expert CKA similarity justifies retaining per-expert deltas rather than fully merging.

## Results

On Mixtral-8x7B at 60% compression, D²-MoE achieves 0.52 average accuracy across 7 reasoning benchmarks, substantially outperforming NAEE (0.36) and MoE-I² (0.36) by 16 percentage points. At 40% compression, D²-MoE reaches 0.57 average accuracy (vs. 0.48 for NAEE and 0.49 for MoE-I²) with WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.28 (vs. 6.44 and 6.74). The method maintains this advantage across all four evaluated MoE models (Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base, Phi-3.5-MoE, Qwen2-57B-A14B), achieving up to 80% maximum compression ratio while remaining train-free and structured.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²-MoE consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model and compression ratio tested. Against the two primary MoE-specific baselines, at 60% compression on Mixtral-8x7B, D²-MoE achieves 0.52 average accuracy versus 0.36 for both NAEE (Lu et al., 2024) and MoE-I² (Yang et al., 2024), a 16-point gap that widens with compression ratio. Against general-purpose compressors at 20% on Mixtral-8x7B (Table 3), D²-MoE (0.60 avg) beats SparseGPT (0.56), MoE-Compress (0.54), ASVD (0.51), MC-SMoE (0.33), and LoSparse (0.32); the latter two suffer catastrophic perplexity degradation (WikiText-2 > 900) due to their unstructured or hybrid approaches. On Qwen2-57B-A14B at 40%, MoE-I²'s perplexity explodes to 24.90 on WikiText-2 while D²-MoE maintains 8.19, highlighting the fragility of inter-expert pruning at scale. In inference throughput (Table 4), D²-MoE also leads: at 80% compression it reaches 313.29 tokens/sec with only 290 TFLOPs, outperforming NAEE by 12.5% and MoE-I² by 6.5%, while LoSparse requires 4.6× more TFLOPs for lower throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces delta decomposition into MoE compression, a concept previously explored only in the context of compressing fine-tuned model variants (GPT-Zip, BitDelta, Delta-CoMe). It builds directly on SVD-LLM's truncation-aware SVD for compressing the delta weights and on Fisher-weighted averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022) for computing the shared base weight, but departs from both prior MoE compression paradigms—expert pruning (NAEE, MoE-I²) and expert merging (EEP, MC-SMoE, HC-SMoE)—by preserving expert-specific information as low-rank deltas rather than discarding or collapsing it. The semi-dynamical structured pruning, which splits sparsity into a static phase and an input-adaptive dynamic phase, is a new technique not present in prior MoE or dense-model compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (DeepSeek-V3, MiniMax-01, Qwen2) become the dominant scaling strategy for LLMs, their parameter footprint creates a critical deployment bottleneck; D²-MoE offers a train-free, structured path to 60–80% compression while retaining expert diversity, which prior pruning and merging methods sacrifice. The empirical finding that delta weights exhibit strong low-rank structure (validated via singular-value energy retention) provides a principled justification for the decomposition and opens the door to further compression via shared V matrices and merged U matrices (explored in the appendix). Open questions include whether the approach integrates cleanly with quantization or knowledge distillation, how it scales to MoE models with hundreds of experts, and whether the calibration-data dependency (512 samples) can be eliminated.
