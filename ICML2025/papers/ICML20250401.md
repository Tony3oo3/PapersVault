## Problem

When foundation models are replaced by newer or larger versions, all downstream fine-tuned models must be retrained from scratch, incurring repeated training costs. Existing inference-time tuning (emulated fine-tuning, EFT) avoids retraining by mixing outputs from the old pretrained model, its fine-tuned model, and the new pretrained model, but this requires running three models at every inference step. PRT addresses this by replacing the implicit reward (log-density ratio of two models) with an explicit, trainable reward model, reducing inference to only two model evaluations.

## Input / Output

The reward model r_θ(x;θ) takes the same input as the underlying pretrained model—an image for vision tasks or a token sequence for language tasks—and produces an L-dimensional vector of reward values, one per class or vocabulary token. The PRT model is constructed in logit space by adding the reward vector to the pretrained model's log-probabilities, then applying softmax to yield a class probability distribution or next-token distribution. A key constraint is that the source pretrained model (used during reward training) and the target pretrained model (used at inference) must share the same label space or vocabulary, though their architectures and pretraining data may differ freely.

## Method

PRT reparameterizes fine-tuning as reward learning: instead of optimizing the pretrained model's parameters, it trains a separate reward model r_θ(x,y) by minimizing the same cross-entropy loss used in standard fine-tuning, where the model output is the closed-form solution of KL-regularized reward maximization, π_θ(y|x) ∝ π_pt(y|x)·exp(r_θ(x,y)/λ). In practice this is computed as v = log_softmax(f(x;θ_pt)) + r(x;θ) followed by softmax(v), with λ=1 since the reward model absorbs the scaling. At inference, the pretrained model is simply swapped to any target model with the same vocabulary, and the PRT model is reconstructed from the frozen reward and the new pretrained model's logits. The paper provides a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound showing that PRT models generalize well when the target pretrained model is close to the source in KL divergence, and introduces an optional entropy-maximization regularization on the reward's exponential distribution to further improve cross-model generalization.

## Results

On GSM8k, PRT matches or exceeds EFT accuracy across model sizes, e.g., 77.23% vs. 71.01% at 14B and 62.37% vs. 53.37% at 3B (Table 4), while also showing lower variance (±0.20% vs. ±3.16% at 14B). In terms of inference cost, PRT reduces per-token latency by ~30% compared to EFT (e.g., 27.8 ms vs. 39.8 ms for Llama2-7B→13B; 23.1 ms vs. 30.4 ms for Llama3.2-3B) and peak GPU memory by ~10–15% (e.g., 2.18 GB vs. 2.42 GB for ResNet-50→ViT-B-16). On vision tasks (8 datasets including CUB, Cars, Aircraft, CIFAR-100), PRT consistently reaches accuracy close to the fine-tuned oracle while using only one auxiliary model at inference instead of two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Emulated Fine-Tuning (EFT; Mitchell et al., ICLR 2024), which performs inference-time tuning by mixing outputs from three models (old pretrained, old fine-tuned, new pretrained) to compute an implicit reward. PRT matches EFT at the smallest target size (0.5B: 26.69% vs. 26.79% on GSM8k) but clearly outperforms it at all larger sizes, gaining +5.8 pts at 1.5B, +9.0 pts at 3B, +5.2 pts at 7B, and +6.2 pts at 14B. The accuracy advantage grows with model scale, suggesting the explicit reward generalizes better to stronger pretrained backbones. On inference cost, PRT requires only two forward passes (target pretrained + reward model) versus three for EFT, yielding ~30% lower per-token latency (e.g., 27.8 ms vs. 39.8 ms for Llama2-7B→13B) and ~10–15% lower peak GPU memory. On vision classification across 8 datasets, PRT tracks EFT closely in accuracy while halving the number of models needed at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the inference-time tuning framework of Mitchell et al. (2024) (Emulated Fine-Tuning) and the parallel proxy-tuning work of Liu et al. (2024a), both of which reinterpret fine-tuning as KL-regularized reward maximization with an implicit reward defined as the log density ratio between fine-tuned and pretrained models. The key departure is replacing this implicit reward—which requires running both the old pretrained model and its fine-tuned counterpart at every inference step—with an explicitly trained reward model, reducing inference from three models to two. The training objective is further connected to Bradley-Terry reward learning in RLHF, and the generalization analysis draws on PAC-Bayes theory, positioning PRT as a principled alternative to standard fine-tuning rather than merely a decoding trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRT offers a practical path to reducing the repeated fine-tuning cost that arises whenever foundation models are updated (e.g., LLaMA-2→3, CLIP on larger datasets), halving inference overhead while maintaining comparable accuracy across vision and language tasks. The PAC-Bayes analysis and the KL-divergence bound (Proposition 3.2) provide theoretical grounding for how much the inference model's behavior shifts when the underlying pretrained model changes, which is important for deployment reliability. Open questions include: the entropy-maximization regularization is not yet practical since the optimal coefficient is task- and model-dependent; the approach requires the same vocabulary or label space across models; and the reward model is assumed to share the pretrained model's architecture, which limits generality. Whether PRT can be combined with parameter-efficient methods like LoRA in a fully portable way (Table 6 shows partial success) remains an active direction.
