## Problem

Existing sharpness measures (including adaptive sharpness) fail to correlate with generalization for transformers because they do not account for the full GL(h) symmetry in the attention mechanism, which creates continuous orbits of equivalent parameters where the network function is identical but Euclidean quantities like gradient norm and Hessian trace vary arbitrarily. The paper asks whether a principled, general recipe exists for constructing sharpness measures that are invariant to arbitrary continuous parameter symmetries, not just the element-wise re-scaling that adaptive sharpness corrects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices of each attention head (query/key and value/output projection pairs) plus all non-attention parameters, along with training data batches and a perturbation radius ρ. Perturbations are constrained to horizontal vectors (orthogonal to the symmetry orbit) with norm measured under a Riemannian metric rather than the Euclidean norm, and the weight update follows a geodesic path (including a second-order Christoffel correction) rather than a straight-line offset. The output is a single scalar geodesic sharpness value per model, which is then correlated via Kendall's τ with the generalization gap (test minus train error) across a collection of independently trained models.

## Method

The core contribution is defining sharpness on the Riemannian quotient manifold obtained by quotienting out the GL(h) symmetry of attention layers. Concretely, the paper (i) equips the parameter space with a symmetry-compatible Riemannian metric (two variants: an 'inv' metric using (GᵀG)⁻¹ and a 'mix' metric using HᵀH), (ii) identifies the vertical space tangent to the symmetry orbit and projects onto its orthogonal complement (the horizontal space), and (iii) defines worst-case sharpness as the maximum loss increase along geodesics of initial velocity norm ≤ ρ in the horizontal space. Since analytical geodesics are unavailable for the attention quotient, they are approximated via a second-order Taylor expansion involving Christoffel symbols, which encode the curvature induced by the symmetry. A key theoretical result is that dropping the Christoffel (curvature) terms and using the Riemannian norm recovers adaptive sharpness exactly, showing that the higher-order geometric corrections are what restore correlation with generalization. In practice, the optimization over horizontal vectors is performed with a modified Auto-PGD algorithm, and non-attention parameters (feedforward, layer norm, convolutional) are handled with the standard adaptive-sharpness element-wise normalization.

## Results

The paper evaluates the Kendall rank correlation coefficient (τ) between sharpness and generalization gap across three settings. On diagonal networks, geodesic sharpness achieves τ = −0.83 (inv metric) and −0.86 (mix metric), improving over adaptive sharpness (τ = −0.68). On ImageNet ViT-B/32 models (72 fine-tuned CLIP classifiers), geodesic sharpness reaches τ = −0.71/−0.70 versus −0.41 for adaptive sharpness. On MNLI BERT models (35 fine-tuned models), adaptive sharpness finds essentially no correlation (τ = 0.06), while geodesic sharpness recovers τ = 0.28 (inv) and 0.38 (mix). Notably, the sign of the correlation is negative on diagonal nets and ImageNet (sharper models generalize better) but positive on MNLI (flatter models generalize better).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against adaptive sharpness (Kwon et al., 2021), geodesic sharpness consistently achieves stronger |τ| across all three experimental settings: from −0.68 to −0.83/−0.86 on diagonal networks, from −0.41 to −0.71/−0.70 on ImageNet, and from a near-zero 0.06 to 0.28/0.38 on MNLI. The most dramatic improvement is on MNLI, where adaptive sharpness fails to detect any meaningful correlation while geodesic sharpness recovers a clear positive one. Against relative sharpness (Petzka et al., 2021), the paper reports on MNLI that relative flatness also fails to find a significant correlation with generalization, whereas geodesic sharpness does (Figure 6). The paper also verifies reparameterization invariance empirically: geodesic sharpness remains constant across an orbit of symmetry-equivalent parameters, while adaptive sharpness varies by orders of magnitude depending on the scale factor applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the line of work on symmetry-aware sharpness measures (adaptive sharpness for GL(1) re-scaling, Rangamani et al.'s quotient manifold for CNNs) to the higher-dimensional GL(h) symmetries inherent in transformer attention. It departs from prior approaches by replacing the Euclidean norm-ball with a geodesic ball on the Riemannian quotient manifold, showing that adaptive sharpness emerges as a first-order (curvature-ignoring) approximation of the proposed geodesic sharpness. The framework draws on Riemannian optimization on fixed-rank matrix spaces (Luo et al., 2023) for concrete metrics and geodesic equations, and adapts Auto-PGD (Croce & Hein, 2020) to optimize over horizontal perturbations rather than input perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper restores a measurable correlation between sharpness and generalization for transformers, where adaptive sharpness previously found none (τ=0.06 on MNLI), providing a principled geometric explanation for the failure. However, the surprising finding that the sign of the correlation flips across tasks (negative on ImageNet ViTs, positive on MNLI BERTs) limits immediate practical utility for sharpness-based regularization and opens a new question about the interplay between data distribution and parameter symmetries. The framework is general and could serve as a diagnostic probe even where full quotient computation is intractable, but the moderate correlation magnitudes (|τ|≈0.38–0.71) and sign ambiguity mean it is not yet a drop-in replacement for existing sharpness regularizers.
