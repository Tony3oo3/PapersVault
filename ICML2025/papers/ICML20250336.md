## Problem

Existing federated multi-view clustering (FedMVC) methods share model parameters or full intermediate features to mitigate data heterogeneity across clients, but this inevitably exposes clients to privacy attacks such as gradient inversion and model inversion. The paper tackles the unresolved trade-off: how to share enough information for the server to mine high-quality global clustering structures while minimizing the privacy risk of the shared content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250336/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds one view type of N samples (e.g., 28×28 MNIST digits, 16×16 USPS digits, 1750-dim visual + 79-dim textual BDGP features, or 40/59/20-dim GIST/HOG/LBP scene descriptors). Clients upload only low-dimensional clustering-related features Z_c^m (and optionally local clustering assignments Q^m in incomplete scenarios) to the server, which concatenates them and runs K-means to produce global K-cluster assignments Y distributed back to all clients. In incomplete settings, the sample overlap rate φ controls how many samples appear across all clients, and non-overlapping samples are handled either via the collaborative alignment loss or purely through model generalization.

## Method

ESFMC splits each client's latent features Z^m into clustering-related Z_c^m and sample-related Z_x^m via two adaptive projection layers, guided by an information-bottleneck objective that maximizes I(Z_x; X) (reconstruction fidelity), maximizes I(Z_c; Y) (cluster discriminability), and minimizes I(Z_x; Z_c) (mutual independence), so that only task-relevant features leave the client. A model-data mutual information constraint I(w^m; X^m) regularizes local training, and the posterior is sampled with stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD), which simultaneously provides client-level (ε,δ)-differential privacy. The server requires no public data or pre-trained models—it simply concatenates the uploaded Z_c^m vectors and applies K-means. For incomplete scenarios, a collaborative alignment loss L_a^m aligns each client's Z_c^m with a mapping of the server-aggregated global clustering assignments Q, ensuring consistency across non-overlapping samples while sharing only the compact assignment matrix rather than raw features.

## Results

On four general-scenario datasets, ESFMC achieves the best clustering performance among all federated methods: Caltech (ACC 91.50, NMI 84.54, ARI 83.45), HW (ACC 98.02, NMI 95.34, ARI 95.57), MNIST-USPS (ACC 97.24, NMI 93.19, ARI 94.02), and Synthetic3d (ACC 97.32, NMI 87.67, ARI 91.23). In incomplete scenarios (overlapping rate φ=0.5), ESFMC also leads on BDGP (ACC 94.9, NMI 84.9, ARI 87.8) and Scene (ACC 40.8, NMI 42.4, ARI 26.2). Notably, on MNIST-USPS, ESFMC (ACC 97.24) approaches the centralized upper bound of DSIMVC (ACC 98.08) while operating in a privacy-preserving federated setting with only clustering-related features shared.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four federated baselines, ESFMC dominates across all datasets: on Caltech it outperforms the strongest federated method FCUIF (ACC 89.14) by 2.36 points and FedDMVC (89.07) by 2.43 points; on HW it beats FCUIF (97.85) by 0.17 points; on MNIST-USPS it surpasses FCUIF (82.92) by 14.32 points and FedDMVC (84.56) by 12.68 points; on Synthetic3d it leads FCUIF (63.67) by 33.65 points. Against centralized methods (which serve as upper bounds since they access all data without privacy constraints), ESFMC is competitive: it exceeds AGDIMC on Caltech (91.50 vs 90.14) and HW (98.02 vs 97.35), and approaches DSIMVC on MNIST-USPS (97.24 vs 98.08 ACC) while operating under strict privacy constraints. In incomplete scenarios (Table 2), ESFMC leads all methods on BDGP (ACC 94.9 vs. DSIMVC 94.1, FCUIF 93.3) and Scene (NMI 42.4 vs. FCUIF 37.6, FedDMVC 34.3), with the gap widening on the more challenging Scene dataset where all methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) and the PAC-Bayes posterior inference framework (Wang et al., 2021) to introduce a feature-splitting mechanism into federated multi-view clustering. Prior FedMVC methods (FedDMVC, FCUIF, FedMVFCM) address feature heterogeneity by sharing complete embedded features or model parameters between clients and the server, which the paper argues inevitably leaks private information. ESFMC departs from this 'share more to improve performance' paradigm by decomposing latent features into clustering-related and sample-related components via an information-theoretic objective, sharing only the former. The collaborative alignment strategy for incomplete scenarios extends the method beyond the fully-overlapping assumption common in prior FedMVC work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in federated learning—balancing utility of shared information against privacy risk—by providing both a practical mechanism (feature splitting) and formal guarantees (generalization bounds tied to model-data mutual information, client-level differential privacy via SGLD). However, the method is designed for cross-silo settings with a limited number of clients (one per view), and experiments in Appendix E.3 show performance degrades sharply when the number of clients grows large, limiting applicability to cross-device scenarios. The semi-honest threat model and the reliance on histogram-based estimation of joint distributions for the decorrelation term are practical limitations. Open questions include whether the feature-splitting principle generalizes to supervised federated tasks, whether it can be combined with stronger cryptographic primitives for malicious settings, and whether the information-theoretic decomposition can be made differentiable end-to-end without the histogram approximation.
