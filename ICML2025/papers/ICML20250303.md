## Problem

Speculative Decoding (SD) loses its speed advantage in long-context inference because memory-bound KV cache operations negate the computational benefits of smaller draft models. Additionally, RAG and long-context LLMs exhibit complementary strengths, but existing methods lack a principled mechanism to integrate both during decoding while preserving the theoretical guarantees of lossless acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RAPID takes a long context document (up to 128K+ tokens) and a query prompt as input. The long context is segmented into 512-token chunks, embedded via BGE-M3, and top-k segments are retrieved by cosine similarity to form a compressed retrieval context (bounded between 4K and 1/24 of input length) for the RAG drafter, while the target LLM processes the full context. The output is a generated text sequence produced through iterative draft-then-verify steps of γ=10 tokens per round.

## Method

RAPID introduces two key components. First, a RAG drafter replaces the traditional smaller draft model: it operates on a shortened retrieval context rather than the full long context, enabling same-scale or even larger LLMs to serve as efficient drafters (self-speculation and upward-speculation settings). Second, a retrieval-augmented target distribution modifies the acceptance criterion by incorporating an inference-time knowledge distillation step: the RAG drafter is positioned as teacher and the target LLM as student, and a logits shift proportional to η·T·(q(x)−p(x)) is added to the target's unnormalized logits before re-normalization. This enriched distribution makes the acceptance criterion more receptive to high-quality candidates from potentially stronger RAG drafters while preserving the theoretical guarantee that rejected tokens are resampled from an adjusted residual distribution that maintains the original target distribution.

## Results

On InfiniteBench, RAPID with self-speculation improves LLaMA-3.1-8B from 39.33 (LC) to 42.83 and LLaMA-3.1-70B from 45.07 to 50.62, while achieving 2.10× and 2.69× decoding speedups respectively. Upward-speculation with a 70B RAG drafter further boosts LLaMA-3.1-8B to 49.98 on InfiniteBench (surpassing the 70B LC baseline of 36.2% on LongBench v2) at 1.14× speedup. On LongBench v2 (Overall, CoT), RAPID achieves 34.2% for LLaMA-3.1-8B (vs. 30.4% LC) and 40.2% for LLaMA-3.1-70B (vs. 36.2% LC). On the multi-turn dialogue task (MT-Bench-101 adapted), RAPID scores 4.21 quality (GPT-4 judge) vs. 2.82 for the target LLM, with 76.94% acceptance rate and 1.7× throughput speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPID consistently outperforms naive Speculative Decoding (SD) on both accuracy and speedup: for LLaMA-3.1-8B, RAPID achieves 42.83 vs. 39.64 on InfiniteBench and 2.10× vs. 1.63× speedup, with the gap widening at longer contexts where SD's acceptance rate drops. Against MagicDec (KV-cache compression via StreamingLLM), RAPID wins decisively on quality (42.83 vs. 37.35 for LLaMA-3.1-8B) and speed (2.10× vs. 0.71×), as MagicDec's compressed draft model degrades speculation quality. In the appendix comparison, RAPID also surpasses MInference (sparse attention) on decoding throughput (2.10× vs. 0.62× speedup) and accuracy (34.2% vs. 30.9% on LongBench v2 CoT), though MInference reduces prefill time more aggressively (9.10s vs. 26.37s). TriForce (hierarchical SD with chunk-wise attention) is noted to underperform RAPID because attention-based retrieval captures attention sinks rather than semantically relevant content, yielding lower acceptance rates. The key differentiator is that RAPID's RAG drafter operates on short retrieved contexts (≤16K), avoiding the memory-bound KV cache bottleneck that limits all other long-context SD variants, while the retrieval-augmented target distribution enables knowledge transfer from potentially stronger drafters that vanilla SD cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPID reimagines Speculative Decoding (Chen et al., 2023; Leviathan et al., 2023) for long-context inference by replacing the traditional smaller draft model with a RAG-based drafter operating on compressed retrieval contexts. This departs from prior long-context SD extensions (MagicDec, TriForce) that rely on KV cache compression, which the authors argue weakens draft quality. The retrieval-augmented target distribution draws on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) but inverts the teacher-student roles at inference time, positioning the RAG drafter as teacher and the long-context target as student—a mechanism not present in prior SD or RAG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAPID addresses a practical bottleneck (memory-bound KV cache operations in long-context inference) with a drop-in decoding method that simultaneously accelerates and improves generation quality, validated across two model families and multiple benchmarks. The upward-speculation paradigm—using larger models as drafters to accelerate smaller targets—opens a new design space for inference-time model composition. Open questions include how the method behaves with multi-document or cross-lingual retrieval, whether the η hyperparameter can be made adaptive per-token, and whether the emergent capability finding (RAPID succeeding where both target and draft fail) generalizes beyond the specific benchmarks tested.
