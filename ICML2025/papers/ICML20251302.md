## Problem

Existing molecular generative models are task-specific, requiring expensive architectural modifications or retraining to handle different drug discovery scenarios such as de novo generation, fragment-constrained completion, hit generation, and lead optimization. The closest prior work, SAFE-GPT, unifies these tasks under an autoregressive GPT framework but inherits the limitations of left-to-right decoding: it is computationally slow, struggles to exploit the permutation-invariant nature of molecular fragments, and requires costly reinforcement learning finetuning for goal-directed tasks.

## Input / Output

GenMol takes a partially masked SAFE sequence as input, where SAFE represents molecules as an unordered sequence of BRICS-decomposed fragment blocks joined by dot tokens (preserving attachment points), making the representation permutation-invariant on fragment order. The model outputs a complete SAFE sequence by iteratively unmasking tokens via parallel decoding, with the number of tokens unmasked per step (N), softmax temperature (τ), and randomness (r) jointly controlling the quality-diversity trade-off. For goal-directed tasks, the input additionally includes a fragment vocabulary (top-V scored fragments) and a seed molecule, and the output is a set of generated molecules scored by a target property oracle.

## Method

GenMol uses a BERT architecture trained with the NELBO objective of masked discrete diffusion (MDLM), generating SAFE sequences through non-autoregressive bidirectional parallel decoding that exploits the permutation-invariance of the SAFE representation. The key exploration mechanism is fragment remasking: rather than masking individual atom/bond tokens, an entire fragment chunk is replaced with mask tokens and regenerated by the diffusion process, aligning the unit of exploration with chemically meaningful substructures. This is combined with a dynamic fragment vocabulary that is updated each generation iteration, enabling exploration beyond the initial fragment set. Molecular Context Guidance (MCG) calibrates predictions by extrapolating between the denoiser's output on the current partially-masked input and a more-corrupted version (with an additional γ fraction of tokens masked), analogous to autoguidance in continuous diffusion but adapted to the MDLM/CTMC formulation. Confidence sampling (from MaskGIT) determines which N tokens to commit at each step based on Gumbel-perturbed prediction scores, balancing generation quality against sampling speed.

## Results

On de novo generation, GenMol (N=1, τ=0.5, r=0.5) achieves 100% validity, 99.7% uniqueness, and 84.6% quality (vs. SAFE-GPT's 54.7%), with a 21.1 s sampling time. On goal-directed hit generation (PMO benchmark, 23 tasks), GenMol achieves a sum AUC top-10 of 18.362, outperforming the best prior method f-RAG (16.928) and winning 19 of 23 tasks. On goal-directed lead optimization (30 tasks across 5 targets), GenMol successfully optimizes 26/30 seeds, whereas baselines frequently fail under the stricter δ=0.6 similarity constraint. On fragment-constrained generation, GenMol improves quality by 8–21 percentage points over SAFE-GPT across all five sub-tasks (linker design, scaffold morphing, motif extension, scaffold decoration, superstructure generation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenMol decisively outperforms SAFE-GPT (Noutahi et al., 2024) on de novo generation (84.6% vs. 54.7% quality, 100% vs. 94% validity) and on all five fragment-constrained sub-tasks (e.g., 34.8% vs. 14.3% quality on superstructure generation), while also sampling 2.5× faster. Against graph-based baselines JT-VAE and DiGress, GenMol achieves substantially higher quality (84.6% vs. 45.2% and 36.8%) with dramatically shorter sampling times (21 s vs. 97 s and 1242 s). On the PMO hit-generation benchmark, GenMol (sum AUC 18.362) beats f-RAG (16.928), Genetic GFN (16.213), Mol GA (14.708), REINVENT (14.196), Graph GA (13.751), and all 20+ methods from the Gao et al. (2022) benchmark (best: LSTM HC at 12.223), winning 19 of 23 tasks; however, f-RAG retains a narrow edge on albuterol_similarity (0.977 vs. 0.937) and isomers_c7h8n2o2 (0.955 vs. 0.942), and Genetic GFN wins sitagliptin_mpo (0.634 vs. 0.584). On lead optimization, GenMol succeeds in 26/30 tasks with docking scores up to −12.9 kcal/mol (5ht1b), while RetMol and Graph GA frequently fail (marked '–') under the δ=0.6 similarity constraint, particularly on parp1 and braf targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenMol builds directly on the SAFE fragment representation (Noutahi et al., 2024) and the MDLM masked discrete diffusion framework (Sahoo et al., 2024), replacing the autoregressive GPT decoder of SAFE-GPT with a BERT-based bidirectional denoiser. It adapts confidence sampling from MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) for discrete token unmasking and extends the autoguidance principle (Karras et al., 2024) to the discrete diffusion setting via MCG. The fragment scoring and dynamic vocabulary update follow f-RAG (Lee et al., 2024a), but the key departure is using fragment-level remasking under diffusion as a Gibbs sampling mechanism for chemical space exploration, rather than the token-level or graph-level mutations used in prior fragment-based methods (graph VAEs, RL, genetic algorithms).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single pretrained checkpoint handles de novo generation, fragment-constrained completion, goal-directed hit generation, and lead optimization without task-specific finetuning, reducing the engineering burden in drug discovery pipelines. The fragment remasking strategy aligns exploration with chemists' intuition (modifying functional groups rather than individual atoms), and the MCG derivation is the first to bring autoguidance to masked discrete diffusion. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that unmasking more than one token per step (N>1) degrades quality, leaving the speed-quality trade-off unresolved. The paper also notes the dual-use risk of generating harmful compounds, suggesting toxicity-aware filtering as a mitigation.
