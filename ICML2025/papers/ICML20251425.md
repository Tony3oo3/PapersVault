## Problem

Existing DGSS methods either rely on domain randomization that cannot create new contextual dependencies between scene instances, or use diffusion models as data generators whose expanded data still cannot cover the near-infinite distributional variations in target domains. The paper asks whether the scene distribution priors embedded in diffusion models can be directly leveraged to enhance segmentation generalization, rather than merely generating additional training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251425/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (cropped to 512×512 during training) and produces pixel-level semantic segmentation masks with per-pixel class probabilities via a Mask2Former decoder. During training, a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 model extracts diffusion features at two timesteps (t_w=0 for weak noise, t_s=100 for strong noise) to guide agent query optimization; during inference, the diffusion model is entirely omitted, keeping the pipeline efficient and general across backbones (ResNet50, MiT-B5, DINOv2-L).

## Method

QueryDiff introduces learnable agent queries that aggregate hierarchical features from the segmentation backbone through progressive cross-attention merging, forming a compact instance-level representation that serves as the interface to diffusion knowledge. A novel diffusion consistency loss (DCL) decouples scene distribution semantics from low-level visual details in diffusion features by enforcing that agent-query-reorganized weak-noise features match strong-noise features, which inherently lack fine-grained appearance information. The optimized agent queries then refine multi-scale decoder features via cross-attention and dilated depthwise separable convolutions before mask prediction. This design avoids the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling and prevents irrelevant visual details from interfering with the segmentation model's domain-invariant learning.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-real setting (GTA5→Cityscapes/BDD100K/Mapillary), QueryDiff with DINOv2-L achieves 66.7% average mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA Rein (64.3%) by 2.4 points. With MiT-B5, it reaches 60.2% average mIoU, outperforming DGInStyle (57.8%) by 2.4 points. On the real-to-real setting (Cityscapes→BDD100K/Mapillary), QueryDiff with DINOv2-L achieves 71.0% average mIoU versus Rein's 68.8%. On the normal-to-adverse setting (Cityscapes→ACDC), QueryDiff with DINOv2-L reaches 79.9% average mIoU, improving over Rein (77.6%) by 2.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QueryDiff consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every backbone family and generalization setting. Against the strongest prior SOTA, Rein (DINOv2-L), QueryDiff gains +2.4 mIoU on synthetic-to-real (66.7 vs. 64.3) and +2.2 on real-to-real (71.0 vs. 68.8). Among ResNet-based methods, QueryDiff (RN50) surpasses the strongest non-diffusion baseline FAMix (RN101) by 2.4 mIoU and the diffusion-based DIDEX (RN101) by 4.2 mIoU on synthetic-to-real. In the Transformer category, QueryDiff (MiT-B5) beats DGInStyle (MiT-B5, also diffusion-based) by 2.4 mIoU on synthetic-to-real and 2.9 on real-to-real, and outperforms HRDA (MiT-B5) by 4.3 mIoU. On the normal-to-adverse setting (Table 2), QueryDiff (DINOv2-L) achieves 79.9% average mIoU, exceeding Rein (77.6%) by 2.3 points, while QueryDiff (MiT-B5) at 67.7% surpasses HGFormer (Swin-L) at 65.8% by 1.9 points. Notably, QueryDiff with the lighter RN50 backbone (51.7% avg) already exceeds methods using the heavier RN101 backbone, demonstrating the efficiency of the agent-query design.

## Relation to Prior Work

QueryDiff builds directly on Rein (Wei et al., 2024), which introduced learnable queries for lightweight fine-tuning of vision foundation models in DGSS, and extends it with a diffusion-guided optimization stage. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of using diffusion models as data generators (DIDEX, DatasetDM, DGInStyle) by instead extracting scene-distribution priors from frozen diffusion features and transferring them into the segmentation model via agent queries. The agent query mechanism itself draws from the DETR/Mask2Former lineage of learnable queries, but is repurposed here as a bridge between segmentation backbone features and diffusion-model knowledge rather than as a detection or mask prediction head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion model priors can be distilled into a segmentation model without the iterative sampling cost or the data-generation overhead that plagues prior diffusion-based DGSS methods, offering a more efficient path to domain generalization. The diffusion consistency loss provides a principled mechanism for separating domain-invariant scene semantics from domain-specific visual appearance, a problem that has been addressed only heuristically in prior work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond urban-scene segmentation to other domains, whether the fixed timestep choices (t_w=0, t_s=100) transfer across diffusion model versions, and whether the added training complexity (frozen diffusion model in the loop) is justified given the moderate absolute gains over strong VFM-based baselines.
