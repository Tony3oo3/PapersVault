## Problem

In Contextual MARL (CMARL), even minor variations in task configurations—such as agent positions, types, or initial states—cause pre-trained policies to fail, yet existing MARL methods rely on fixed heuristic identity assignments that cannot adapt to these contextual shifts. This forces agents to learn separate policies for each variant, severely limiting sample efficiency and generalization. The paper addresses the absence of a mechanism to automatically assign consistent, context-aware agent identities across related problem variants.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the global environment state s and the local observations z = {z¹, ..., zⁿ} of all n agents, arranged as a sequence of length n+1 fed into the Transformer encoder. It produces three outputs: (1) a contextual state representation ĉ that augments s to form ŝ = (s, ĉ) for the mixing network, (2) a permutation of discrete identities id^a ∈ {1,...,n} assigned autoregressively with a uniqueness constraint enforced via dynamic masking, and (3) identity-conditioned transformed Q-values via an affine hypernetwork. The identity assignment is stochastic (sampled, not greedy) to prevent premature convergence, and the action regulator's weight matrix is constrained to be positive to preserve the IGM condition.

## Method

CAID comprises three modules built on a causal Transformer architecture. The Contextual State Encoder (a Seq2Seq-style Transformer encoder) maps the input sequence [s, z¹, ..., zⁿ] to a compact contextual representation ĉ, collapsing semantically similar but superficially different states into a shared low-dimensional space. The Agent Identity Decoder (a causal Transformer decoder inspired by Pointer Networks) autoregressively assigns each agent a unique identity via softmax over masked logits, where a dynamic mask M zeros out already-assigned identities at each decoding step; identities are sampled rather than greedily selected to maintain diversity. The Action Regulator is a two-layer hypernetwork that takes the discrete identity id^a as input and generates weights w and bias b for an affine transformation of each agent's Q-values, with |w| enforced to preserve the Individual-Global-Max condition. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a standard TD loss, using Straight-Through Gradients to backpropagate through the discrete identity sampling, and the contextual state ŝ replaces the raw state in the mixing network of any compatible value decomposition method (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN).

## Results

On PyTSC (traffic signal control), CAID achieves the best performance across all three city networks: on Jinan it reduces queue length to 463.39 (vs. 563.13 for QMIX, a ~18% reduction), delay to 0.471 (vs. 0.507), and travel time to 329.28 s (vs. 338.15 s). On SMACv2, CAID outperforms baseline QMIX across all 12 tested scenarios and achieves the fastest initial convergence rate among all compared algorithms, with particularly strong gains in the Terrain and Protoss series. On VMAS, CAID variants consistently outperform their original counterparts (VDN, QMIX, QPLEX) in most scenarios, confirming the framework's plug-and-play compatibility with existing value decomposition methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAID (on QMIX) outperforms the baseline QMIX across all 12 SMACv2 scenarios, all VMAS tasks, and all three PyTSC city networks, with the largest PyTSC gain being a ~100-unit reduction in Jinan queue length (463.39 vs. 563.13). Against Weighted QMIX, QPLEX, RIIT, COLA, and VMIX on SMACv2, CAID wins in most scenarios, with particularly clear advantages in the Terrain and Protoss series; in the Zerg series, CAID's gains are somewhat constrained by QMIX's weaker credit assignment. On VMAS, CAID-VDN outperforms VDN even though the contextual state is unused (VDN lacks a mixing network), isolating the contribution of the agent identity decoder. In the appendix comparison with dynamic role assignment methods, CAID outperforms ROMA on SMACv2, while RODE is inapplicable to SMACv2 due to environment modifications in its original implementation. Overall, CAID consistently achieves the fastest initial convergence rate among all tested algorithms, indicating improved sample efficiency in addition to higher final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAID extends Contextual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) from the single-agent to the multi-agent setting, formalizing the problem as Contextual MARL (CMARL) over Dec-POMDPs. Architecturally, it combines a Seq2Seq-style causal Transformer (for context encoding and autoregressive identity decoding, drawing on Pointer Networks) with a hypernetwork-based Action Regulator, all wrapped around existing value decomposition methods (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN). This departs from prior MARL generalization approaches (REFIL, ROMA, RODE, COLA) that use heuristic or role-based identity assignment, by learning context-aware identities end-to-end via straight-through gradients on discrete identity tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAID addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying MARL: policies trained on one task configuration fail when agent positions, types, or targets shift, and retraining per variant is infeasible. The plug-and-play design (compatible with any value decomposition method, CTDE-friendly at execution time) makes it broadly applicable. Open questions remain around dynamic agent populations, zero-shot generalization to truly unseen contexts, and whether the learned identities capture a more informative latent structure than the current discrete permutation space. The ablation showing VDN (which has no mixing network and thus ignores the contextual state) still benefits purely from identity reassignment highlights that consistent agent identification is a non-trivial source of performance.
