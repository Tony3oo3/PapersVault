## Problem

All-to-all communication in expert-parallel MoE training and inference can consume over 40% of total runtime, yet prior systems treat each token's k routed experts as independent dispatch targets, ignoring that co-activated experts on the same device require only a single token replica. The paper identifies this 'collaborative communication' structure as an untapped optimization axis and asks how to systematically reduce the average number of token replicas (C_T) while preserving model quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes token embeddings (ORI state), dense expert weight matrices, a pre-computed collaboration graph P (normalized co-activation frequencies from a profiling dataset), and a fixed expert placement L. It produces MoE layer outputs in the ORI state, a rescheduled expert placement that minimizes inter-device collaboration, and—when collaboration pruning is applied—a fine-tuned model whose routing is restricted to N_d devices. Internally, tokens flow through three tensor states (ORI < SFD < EPD in token count), with two Bidirectional ReIndex Matrices (BRIM_0 for dispatch/combine, BRIM_1 for sparse GEMM) mediating all state transitions on GPU HBM.

## Method

Occult is an algorithm-system co-design with three components. First, a custom Triton-based sparse matrix multiplication kernel uses BRIM data structures to perform dispatch, expert computation, and combine without materializing full k-replica tensors, splitting the MoE aggregation into intra-collaboration (pre-all-to-all) and inter-collaboration (post-all-to-all) stages. Second, a farthest-point-sampling-inspired clustering algorithm (Algorithm 1) partitions the collaboration graph P into N_d sub-graphs to reschedule expert placement, reducing C_T by ~20% while preserving exact results. Third, collaboration pruning restricts each token's routing to N_d devices by either selecting top-k experts within those devices (routing-score-based) or replacing out-of-range experts with the most similar in-range alternative from a cosine-similarity table built on router logits (expert-similarity-based), followed by fine-tuning to recover quality. The 2-device pruning variant achieves C_T = 2 with comparable or superior benchmark accuracy to standard top-k training, while 1-device pruning reaches the theoretical minimum C_T = 1 at a small quality cost.

## Results

On OLMoE with 2^14 prompt tokens (prefilling, 4 GPUs), Occult reduces all-to-all communication latency from 24.50 s (standard expert parallelism) to 5.82 s (2-GPU collaboration pruning), a 4.2× reduction. The rescheduled expert placement alone cuts C_T from 3.68 to 1.98 and latency from 15.93 s to 9.22 s (~20% improvement over MegaBlocks). For training at batch size 8, Occult (2-device pruning) achieves 1.54×, 2.65×, ~9×, and ~20× speedup over MegaBlocks, FSDP, DeepSpeed, and Tutel respectively. Collaboration pruning with 2 devices maintains comparable or superior downstream accuracy to standard fine-tuning across 23 benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tutel (Hwang et al., 2023), Occult achieves 8.66× speedup on OLMoE prefilling and ~20× speedup on training (batch size 8), making it the most outperformed baseline. Against MegaBlocks (Gale et al., 2023), Occult is 1.70× faster on OLMoE prefilling and 1.54× faster on training, with the gap widening for Qwen-MoE and DeepSeek-MoE where MegaBlocks' block-sparse approach is less effective. Against vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023), Occult is 1.86× faster on OLMoE prefilling and 1.51–1.60× faster on other models, though vLLM remains competitive for small-batch decoding where communication is not the bottleneck. Against FSDP (Zhao et al., 2023) and DeepSpeed (Rajbhandari et al., 2022), Occult achieves 2.65× and ~9× training speedup respectively at batch size 8, primarily due to superior memory efficiency enabling larger batch sizes under the same 48 GB HBM budget. Notably, Occult also supports the largest batch size under a fixed memory budget, a practical advantage over all compared training frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the expert parallelism paradigm established by GShard and Switch Transformers, where tokens are dispatched to distributed experts via all-to-all communication, and draws on MC-MoE's insight that router logits capture expert similarity for the pruning criterion. It departs from prior MoE systems (Tutel, MegaBlocks, DeepSpeed-MoE) that focus solely on kernel-level or scheduling optimizations by introducing an algorithm-system co-design: reframing all-to-all as 'collaborative communication' and jointly optimizing expert placement and routing policy. A key departure is the demonstration that pure system-level optimization (dynamic per-batch expert placement) is infeasible because the maximal connected subgraph of the collaboration graph converges to a complete graph as batch size grows, necessitating the algorithmic pruning component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With all-to-all communication consuming over 40% of runtime in large-scale MoE training, Occult's 1.5×+ speedups over current SOTA frameworks (vLLM, MegaBlocks, Tutel) across prefilling, decoding, and fine-tuning are practically significant for deploying MoE-LLMs on communication-constrained hardware. The finding that 2-device collaboration pruning can match or exceed standard top-k routing quality is important because it enables a strict communication budget (C_T = 2) without quality loss, making MoE deployment more predictable. Open questions include workload balancing across devices, generalization to multi-node NVLink/InfiniBand topologies, and whether collaboration pruning interacts with known issues like biased or harmful model outputs.
