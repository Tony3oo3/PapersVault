## Problem

Existing molecular relational learning methods for solvation free energy prediction assume IID training and test data, yet real-world solute-solvent systems exhibit substantial distribution shifts across diverse solvent environments. Prior invariant learning approaches (e.g., CGIB, CMRL) identify core substructures but neglect the solvent-dependent nature of solute properties and the complex non-covalent coupling effects that govern molecular behavior, leading to spurious correlations and poor generalization to unseen solvents or scaffolds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of molecular graphs (solute G₁ and solvent G₂) as input, encoded by separate 3-layer GCN/MPNN backbones into node-level representations. An interaction map I = h₁·h₂ᵀ (of size N₁×N₂) is constructed via matrix multiplication of the two node representation matrices, and the solute representation is augmented with solvent-interaction features before a Set2Set readout. The output is a scalar solvation free energy (or log-solubility for MolMerger), and the model additionally consumes a one-hot solvent environment vector that is interpolated via mixup during training.

## Method

RILOOD decomposes the predictor Φ into a conditional variational encoder f and a multi-granularity context-aware learner g, jointly optimized under a min-max objective over environments. The Mixup-enhanced CVAE (MCVAE) interpolates between solvent-conditioned representations using Beta-distributed mixing coefficients and an uncertainty-weighted reconstruction loss, learning a latent z that captures environment-invariant solute structure. The Multi-granularity Context-Aware Refinement (MCAR) module computes global interactions via scaled dot-product self-attention over a concatenated [z, H₂] embedding and local interactions via a PReLU transformation, then fuses them through a Hadamard product to produce a context-aware invariant feature Ĥ_inv. A contrastive mutual-information loss (InfoNCE-style) maximizes I(Ĥ_inv; Y) while constraining Ĥ_inv to be a sufficient statistic of H₁, and the full training objective combines the prediction loss, MCVAE ELBO, and MI loss with tunable weights α and β.

## Results

On the three primary OOD datasets (MNSolv, CompSolv, QM9Solv) under both solvent and scaffold splits, RILOOD achieves the lowest RMSE across all six settings. Headline numbers include 0.6784 RMSE on MNSolv-Solvent (vs. 0.7022 for the best no-interaction baseline GAT), 0.3660 on CompSolv-Solvent (vs. 0.3886 for CGIB), and 0.7001 on QM9Solv-Solvent (vs. 0.7227 for MixUp). The largest relative gains appear under scaffold splits, where RILOOD reduces RMSE by up to ~20% over the best interaction-based baseline (e.g., 1.0780 vs. 1.3478 on MNSolv-Scaffold). In the IID setting (Table 4), RILOOD also achieves the best RMSE on 7 of 9 datasets, including 0.246 on QM9Solv and 0.292 on CombiSolv.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RILOOD outperforms all 10 baselines across every OOD setting in Table 1. Among no-interaction GNNs, GAT is the strongest (e.g., 0.7022 on MNSolv-Solvent), but RILOOD still improves by ~3.4%; GIN and GCN lag further behind. Among interaction-based methods, the closest competitors vary by dataset: CGIB leads on CompSolv-Solvent (0.3886) and CompSolv-Scaffold (0.5476), MixUp leads on QM9Solv (0.7227/0.7490), and ERM leads on MNSolv-Scaffold (1.3478). RILOOD beats CGIB by 5.8% on CompSolv-Solvent and 4.9% on CompSolv-Scaffold, beats MixUp by 3.1% and 6.7% on QM9Solv, and beats ERM by 20% on MNSolv-Scaffold. Notably, CGIB and CMRL (both substructure/causal-based invariant learning methods) suffer severe degradation on QM9Solv-Solvent (1.4525 and 1.4425 respectively) and MNSolv-Scaffold (2.2118 and 2.1524), while RILOOD remains stable, confirming the paper's claim that substructure-only invariance is insufficient for solvent-dependent properties. MolMerger performs worst among interaction methods on CompSolv (2.1115) and MNSolv-Scaffold (1.6225), and GroupDRO is consistently outperformed by simpler baselines like MixUp and ERM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CIGIN (Pathak et al., 2020) as its message-passing backbone for solute-solvent interaction encoding, extending it with a CVAE-based conditional modeling module and a multi-granularity attention refinement layer. It departs from prior invariant learning approaches in MRL—specifically CGIB (Lee et al., 2023a) and CMRL (Lee et al., 2023b)—by arguing that substructure-level invariance is insufficient because solute properties are solvent-dependent and involve complex coupling effects. The environment inference strategy draws on ZIN (Lin et al., 2022) for learning latent environment partitions without explicit labels, while the mixup augmentation and uncertainty-constrained regression loss adapt standard techniques to the molecular pair setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: OOD generalization in molecular relational learning has been largely unexplored, yet real-world solvation prediction requires robustness to unseen solvents and scaffolds. The consistent improvements across six datasets and two shift types (solvent, scaffold) provide practical evidence that multi-granularity interaction modeling plus invariant learning is more robust than substructure-only approaches. Key limitations include reliance on discrete solvent categories (only 5 in QM9Solv), consideration of a single spurious factor in synthetic experiments, and the acknowledged tension between broad generalization and domain-specific precision. Open questions include whether the framework scales to continuous solvent property spaces and how it performs under multiple simultaneous spurious correlations.
