## Problem

LaMAGIC's float-input adjacency-matrix formulation (FM) suffers from low sensitivity to numeric precision (0.67 success rate at tolerance 0.01 vs. 0.93 for its text-based counterpart) and quadratic O(|V|²) token length that causes error accumulation and context-window overflow in autoregressive generation. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a new circuit formulation that simultaneously improves numeric precision sensitivity and reduces token complexity to O(|V|).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a voltage conversion ratio and power conversion efficiency as raw floats (bypassing the tokenizer via a shared linear projection into embedding space) plus an ordered list of component vertices (three fixed ports VIN/VOUT/GND and user-specified devices like Sa, Sb, C, L). It outputs a sparse edge list where each hyperedge is a comma-separated set of node tokens (e.g., <VIN> <Sa> 0 <VOUT>), followed by a single duty-cycle token chosen from five discrete options. The O(|V|) output length arises because each vertex participates in at most two hyperedges, bounding total vertex incidences by 2|V|.

## Method

SFCI (Succinct Float-Input Canonical Formulation with Identifier) combines three key design choices: (1) each device node token is split into a component-type token (<Sa>) and a numeric identifier (0, 1, …), enabling the model to recognize shared component types while distinguishing individual instances; (2) the sparse edge-list representation replaces the O(|V|²) adjacency matrix, reducing token length to O(|V|) and eliminating the many <no_edge> tokens that dilute attention; (3) natural-language descriptions (e.g., 'Duty cycle', 'voltage') are stripped from the input so the model focuses on the raw numeric-to-topology mapping, and duty cycle is encoded as a single classification token (<duty_0.3>) rather than a five-token selection sequence. The model is a Flan-T5-base encoder-decoder (248M params) trained via supervised fine-tuning with random vertex-order permutations as data augmentation, using a shared linear projection layer in place of the standard word embedding to handle float inputs. Ablations confirm that retaining component-type tokens in the output and keeping the duty-cycle prefix in the input both contribute meaningfully to generation accuracy.

## Results

On 3,4,5-component circuits, SFCI achieves a success rate of 0.90 at a tight tolerance of 0.01 (vs. 0.67 for FM) and 1.00 at tolerance 0.1, with voltage MSE of 0.0006 and efficiency MSE of 0.0002 — roughly 10× lower than both FM and PM. On 6-component circuits finetuned with only 2,000 samples, SFCI reaches a success rate of 0.84 at tolerance 0.1 (vs. 0.76 for FM) and achieves up to 58.5% lower MSE across all data budgets. SFCI also converges 23% faster in training steps (6,886 vs. 8,943 for SFM) due to its O(|V|) token length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LaMAGIC's three formulations, SFCI dominates on both success rate and MSE: at tolerance 0.01 it improves success rate from 0.67 (FM) to 0.90, and its voltage MSE (0.0006) is ~10× lower than FM (0.0061) and PM (0.0065). Against the RL-search method of Fan et al. (2021), SFCI achieves 0.91 vs. 0.41 success rate at tolerance 0.01 and a voltage MSE of 0.00008 vs. 0.313 — a roughly 4,000× reduction. Against OpenAI o1 with 100-shot prompting, SFCI's voltage MSE (0.00008) is ~7,500× lower than o1's (0.602), confirming that SFT with tailored formulations is essential for this task. On 6-component transferability, SFCI outperforms FM by 8 percentage points in success rate (0.84 vs. 0.76 at tolerance 0.1) and by up to 58.5% in MSE, while CF and PM lag further behind, validating that the sparse O(|V|) representation with component-type identifiers is critical for scaling to larger circuits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct follow-up to LaMAGIC (Chang et al., 2024), which first framed analog power-converter topology generation as a sequence-to-sequence problem solvable by supervised finetuning of a transformer LM. While LaMAGIC introduced three circuit formulations (CF, PM, FM), this work identifies their specific weaknesses—quadratic token length, poor numeric-precision sensitivity, and weak component-type recognition—and proposes two refined formulations (SFM, SFCI) that combine the sparse canonical encoding of CF with the float-input encoding and component-type tokens of the matrix-based variants. The paper also positions itself against earlier search-based methods (RL, Bayesian optimization) and against few-shot prompting of general-purpose reasoning models (o1), arguing that task-specific SFT with a well-designed formulation is necessary for reliable one-shot topology generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(|V|) token-length guarantee of SFCI is practically important for scaling topology generation to circuits with many nodes, where quadratic-length matrix formulations become intractable within fixed context windows. The ablation findings—that a seemingly redundant duty-cycle prefix acts as useful implicit regularization, and that retaining component-type tokens in the output improves accuracy despite lengthening the sequence—offer transferable design insights for anyone encoding structured graphs as LM sequences. However, the evaluation is confined to a single small model (Flan-T5-base, 248M), a single circuit domain (power converters with five component types), and a single GPU, leaving open whether the formulation advantages persist at larger scale or in more complex transistor-level design spaces that the paper only sketches conceptually.
