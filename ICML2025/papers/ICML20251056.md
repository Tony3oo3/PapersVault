## Problem

Training-free guidance of pre-trained diffusion models for Bayesian inverse problems relies on a Dirac-mass approximation of the intractable likelihood gradient, which requires heuristic rescalings and model-specific adjustments (especially for latent diffusion) and offers no principled way to improve quality with more compute. This paper asks whether a more structured approximation of the intermediate posterior distributions can both eliminate these heuristics and provide a natural compute-scaling mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251056/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy or incomplete observation y, a pre-trained diffusion prior (pixel-space DDPM on FFHQ/ImageNet at 256×256, latent-space LDM on FFHQ, or an audio diffusion model on 4-channel instrument tracks), and a closed-form forward model g_0(y|x). It outputs samples from the posterior π_0^y(x_0) ∝ g_0(y|x_0)p_0(x_0). A key practical detail is that the number of Gibbs iterations R and the number of gradient steps G for the variational fit are independent compute-scaling knobs: increasing R improves mixing without changing memory, while increasing G sharpens individual samples.

## Method

MGDM constructs a weighted mixture of t−1 density approximations of each smoothed posterior π_t^y, where each component uses a likelihood approximation g_t^s(y|x_t) = ∫ g_s(y|x_s) p_{s|t}(x_s|x_t) dx_s derived from a Chapman-Kolmogorov identity. Because the scores of these mixture components are intractable, the paper introduces a data-augmented joint distribution π̄_{0,s,t}^y(x_0, x_s, x_t) whose x_t-marginal equals one mixture component, and samples from it via a three-step Gibbs update: (i) fit a Gaussian variational approximation to the conditional π̄_{s|0,t}^y(·|x_0, x_t) using G gradient steps with the reparameterization trick, (ii) re-noise x_s to x_t via the exact forward transition, and (iii) denoise x_s to x_0 using M DDPM steps of the pre-trained model. Only step (i) depends on the observation y; the other two are standard diffusion operations. The algorithm proceeds sequentially from t=T down to t=2, sampling the mixture index s from a uniform distribution at each step, and the final x_0 output approximates a posterior sample. Increasing the number of Gibbs rounds R at each timestep provides a principled inference-time compute scaling axis that the authors show yields substantial LPIPS/SI-SDR improvements, particularly on challenging tasks like phase retrieval.

## Results

On pixel-space FFHQ (256×256, σ_y=0.05), MGDM with a single Gibbs step achieves the best LPIPS on 7 out of 10 tasks, including 0.09 on SR(×4), 0.09 on Motion Deblur, 0.11 on Phase Retrieval, and 0.12 on HDR. On ImageNet, MGDM leads on 8 out of 10 tasks, with particularly large margins on JPEG dequantization (0.38 vs. 0.60 for DPS) and Phase Retrieval (0.55 vs. 0.62 for DPS). On the slakh2100 source separation task, MGDM with R=6 Gibbs steps achieves 17.35 mean SI-SDR I, outperforming all training-free baselines (DPS: 13.77, MSDM: 16.48) but falling slightly short of the supervised DEMUCS_512 (17.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGDM consistently outperforms the gradient-based guidance methods DPS, PGDM, DDNM, DIFFPIR, and REDDIFF across nearly all 10 image-restoration tasks on both FFHQ and ImageNet, with the largest margins on nonlinear tasks (e.g., JPEG: 0.14 vs. 0.34 for DPS; Phase Retrieval: 0.11 vs. 0.40 for DPS). Against the Gibbs-sampling-inspired PNP-DM, MGDM wins on 9/10 FFHQ tasks and 8/10 ImageNet tasks, with PNP-DM only competitive on SR(×4) and Gaussian Deblur. Compared to DAPS, MGDM achieves lower LPIPS on 8/10 FFHQ tasks, though DAPS edges out MGDM on HDR (0.10 vs. 0.12) and Phase Retrieval on latent FFHQ (0.25 vs. 0.29). On the latent-space FFHQ setting, MGDM outperforms both RESAMPLE and PSLD on 7/10 tasks, losing only on Gaussian Deblur (0.18 vs. 0.16), Motion Deblur (0.22 vs. 0.20), and HDR (0.16 vs. 0.12) to RESAMPLE. For audio source separation on slakh2100, MGDM (R=6) achieves 17.35 mean SI-SDR I, beating MSDM (16.48), ISDM (17.27), DPS (13.77), PGDM (14.92), and DDNM (14.96), but remains slightly below the supervised DEMUCS_512 (17.73); increasing gradient steps at R=1 closes this gap to 17.85.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGDM extends the training-free diffusion guidance framework for Bayesian inverse problems (DPS, PGDM, DDNM, etc.) by replacing the single Dirac-mass likelihood approximation with a weighted mixture of multiple likelihood approximations derived from a simple identity relating g_t to g_s at earlier noise levels. Rather than the standard gradient-based (Langevin) sampling used by prior methods, MGDM introduces a data-augmentation scheme that makes Gibbs sampling tractable, with the Gaussian variational approximation from Moufad et al. (2024) handling the intractable conditional update. This positions the work as a principled alternative to both the heuristic rescaling approaches in DPS/PGDM and the SMC-based asymptotically exact methods (Wu et al., 2023; Trippe et al., 2023), offering a middle ground with favorable compute scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gibbs-sampling perspective provides a natural and effective compute-scaling axis: increasing the number of Gibbs iterations R yields substantially larger gains than increasing gradient steps G, a finding that gives practitioners a clear lever for quality-vs-runtime tradeoffs. The method works seamlessly off-the-shelf with both pixel-space and latent-space diffusion priors without task-specific adaptation, and matches or exceeds supervised source-separation baselines. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to ODE-based samplers (DDIM), whether the vector-Jacobian product overhead can be eliminated, and whether observation-driven index sampling or better weight sequences can close the remaining gap between latent and pixel-space performance.
