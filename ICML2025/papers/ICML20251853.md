## Problem

Policy regret minimization in Markov games against adaptive adversaries was previously only achievable in tabular settings with small discrete state-action spaces and restrictive consistency assumptions on the opponent. This paper asks whether sample-efficient algorithms with O(√T) policy regret can be designed when state and action spaces are large or continuous, requiring function approximation to scale beyond tabular methods.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a value function class F, an adversary behavior model class Ψ, a policy class Π_A, and hyperparameters T (total episodes) and K (number of batches), and at each episode outputs a learner policy π^t selected via optimistic planning over the current confidence sets. A key structural constraint is that the adversary is m-memory bounded and stationary, so the learner must commit to the same policy for m consecutive episodes (a 'warm-up' phase) before the adversary's response stabilizes. Confidence sets F^t and Ψ^t are only updated at batch boundaries, meaning the policy and models remain frozen within each batch of ⌊T/K⌋ episodes.

## Method

BOVL (Batching and Optimism based on Value and Likelihood fitting) partitions the episode budget into K batches, holding a fixed policy within each batch to observe consistent responses from the m-memory bounded adversary. Within each batch, the algorithm jointly maintains optimistic confidence sets: value functions are selected via squared Bellman loss fitting, while adversary models are selected via log-likelihood (total variation) loss, both with data-dependent radii α and β. The core technical contribution is a novel analysis that controls the number of batches with large in-batch empirical error using ℓ2-type Eluder conditions for both the value function class and the adversary class, extending Xiong et al.'s technique from squared loss to total variation loss without requiring unavailable lower-tail bounds on log-likelihood. This yields a policy regret bound of Õ(V̄(m+H)√(d_E·γ·T)) that is independent of state and action space cardinalities, and strictly improves over prior tabular bounds by a factor of √(HSA/(Bd*)) when specialized to the tabular case.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) establishes that BOVL achieves policy regret PR(T) = Õ(V̄(H+m)√(d_E γ T)), where V̄ is the reward scale, m is adversary memory, H is episode length, d_E is the ℓ₂-type Eluder dimension, and γ is the covering-type complexity. Instantiated to linear models, the bound becomes V̄(m+H)(d_in ∨ d_adv)√((H+B)T); for the tabular case it reduces to V̄(m+H)SA√((H+B)³T). This is an optimal Õ(√T) rate and is independent of the cardinalities of the state and action spaces, making it applicable to continuous high-dimensional domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is APE-OVE from Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2024a), which is restricted to tabular Markov games with consistent adversaries. In the tabular setting, BOVL's bound V̄(m+H)SA√((H+B)³T) strictly improves over APE-OVE's mV̄√(HSAB)(SAB(H+√S)+H²)√(T/d*) by a factor of √(HSA/(Bd*)), where d* is the minimum positive visitation probability—a quantity that can be arbitrarily small in large-state problems, making the improvement potentially very large. Beyond the tighter bound, BOVL generalizes from tabular to general function approximation (linear and beyond), replaces the restrictive consistency assumption with a more natural Lipschitz adversary condition, and eliminates the need for knowledge of d*. The algorithmic design is also simpler: BOVL uses standard squared-loss fitting and log-likelihood-based updates rather than APE-OVE's complex layerwise exploration with infrequent-transition marking and reward truncation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the policy regret minimization framework for Markov games established by Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2024a) from tabular settings to general function approximation, replacing their strong consistency assumption on adversaries with a more natural Lipschitz condition. The algorithmic design draws on the batching mechanism from Arora et al. (2012b) for handling adaptive opponents and the optimistic MLE framework from Liu et al. (2023) for opponent modeling, while the core analytical tool—the ℓ₂-type Eluder condition for controlling in-batch errors—is adapted from Xiong et al. (2023), with a key extension from squared loss to total variation loss that avoids unavailable lower-tail bounds on log-likelihood. Compared to the layerwise exploration of Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2024a), BOVL is conceptually simpler (standard squared-loss fitting plus log-likelihood updates) yet yields tighter regret bounds even in the tabular special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithmic framework achieving Ō(√T) policy regret in Markov games with function approximation, resolving three of four open problems from the foundational tabular work and making the theory applicable to large-scale and continuous environments where tabular guarantees are impractical. The Lipschitz adversary assumption is a natural and broadly applicable relaxation of consistency, and the TV-loss batching technique generalizes to online density estimation with function approximation. Key open questions include extending guarantees to unbounded-memory or non-stationary adversaries, replacing Lipschitz assumptions with alternative complexity measures, designing adaptive batching for non-stationary settings, and validating the framework empirically on large-scale benchmarks.
