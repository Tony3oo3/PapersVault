## Problem

Instruction-based image editing requires simultaneously modifying target regions per instruction while preserving irrelevant content, yet existing automated metrics (LPIPS, CLIPScore, DINOScore, etc.) evaluate the entire image holistically without separating editing-relevant from irrelevant regions. This leads to biased evaluations that favor either excessively preserved or excessively modified results, contradicting human judgment. The paper identifies that no existing metric jointly utilizes all three elements—original image, edited image, and instruction—while accounting for the distinct requirements of different image regions.

## Input / Output

BPM takes three inputs: the original image I_origin, the edited image I_edit, and a text editing instruction T_edit. It produces a hierarchical two-tier score: a Region-Aware Score S_region (binary position and size alignment indicators) and a Semantic-Aware Score S_semantic (continuous modification compliance and preservation scores), combined into a single BPM score via a weighted sum (α=0.7 for semantic, 0.3 for region). The method is training-free and runs at ~1.5 seconds per image, using CLIP-ViT 14/B for feature extraction, Grounding DINO + Grounded SAM for object localization, and Gemma-2-9bit-SimPO for instruction parsing.

## Method

BPM explicitly disentangles the image into editing-relevant and irrelevant regions before evaluation. An LLM first parses the instruction to extract the source object o₁, target object o₂, and required size/position change states; Grounding DINO and Grounded SAM then locate these objects in both the original and edited images, yielding bounding boxes and instance masks. The Region-Aware Judge applies rule-based functions to verify whether the edited object's size and position changes conform to the instruction (e.g., area ratio thresholds for size, center-coordinate and IoU checks for position). The Semantic-Aware Judge computes a directional CLIP similarity—cosine similarity between the difference of cropped image features and the difference of source/target text embeddings—to assess modification compliance within the edited region, while computing 1−L2 distance on the union-masked irrelevant regions to measure content preservation. As a secondary contribution, the editing region mask M_all = M_origin ∪ M_edit is injected into the diffusion editing process via a modified classifier-free guidance formulation (element-wise product with the mask), restricting instruction guidance to the relevant region and reducing unnecessary modifications elsewhere.

## Results

On the Human Alignment Test for local edits (MagicBrush), BPM achieves an average alignment score of 0.799, outperforming GPT-4o (0.754), L2 (0.702), LPIPS (0.683), DINOScore (0.674), CLIP-I (0.644), and CLIPScore (0.452). For global edits (PIE-Bench), BPM reaches 0.829 average alignment, again the highest among all metrics. In the Ground Truth Test, BPM favors the well-edited (ground truth) image in 87% of cases, compared to 78% for GPT-4o, 44% for CLIPScore+CLIP-I, and 0–17% for individual preservation metrics (LPIPS, L2, CLIP-I, DINOScore). BPM also runs at 1.5 s/image, substantially faster than GPT-4o at 11.4 s/image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPM outperforms all individual preservation metrics (LPIPS 0.683, L2 0.702, CLIP-I 0.644, DINOScore 0.674) and the modification metric CLIPScore (0.452) on the local-edit Human Alignment Test, with the largest gap over CLIPScore (+0.347). Against the combined balanced baseline CLIPScore+CLIP-I (0.641), BPM gains +0.158, demonstrating that simply summing a preservation and a modification score is insufficient without explicit region disentanglement. BPM also surpasses the VLM-based GPT-4o evaluator (0.754) by +0.045 on local edits and by +0.099 on global edits (0.829 vs. 0.730), while being ~7.6× faster (1.5 s vs. 11.4 s per image). In the Ground Truth Test, BPM's 87% GT-favoring rate exceeds GPT-4o (78%) and dramatically outperforms preservation metrics that favor excessively-preserved images (LPIPS 0%, DINOScore 2%, CLIP-I 13%, L2 17%) and CLIPScore which favors excessively-modified images (13% GT, 83% EM). For mask quality, BPM's editing-region masks receive a human rating of 4.01/5, substantially higher than GPT-4o bounding boxes (2.66) and DiffEdit attention maps (1.45), confirming superior localization for both evaluation and downstream editing guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP directional similarity (Kim et al., 2022) for measuring semantic instruction compliance and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) for a training-free editing enhancement, but departs fundamentally from prior evaluation metrics by explicitly disentangling the image into editing-relevant and irrelevant regions rather than scoring the whole image holistically. Where prior work (LPIPS, CLIPScore, DINOScore, GPT-4o-based evaluators) treats the image as a monolithic unit, BPM introduces a two-tier hierarchy—region-aware (size/position alignment) and semantic-aware (directional CLIP similarity for modification, L2 for preservation)—that separately addresses the dual requirements of instruction-based editing. The LLM-based instruction parsing and Grounding DINO + Grounded SAM localization pipeline is a practical engineering contribution that enables this region-level decomposition without task-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ground Truth Test reveals a stark failure mode in existing metrics (LPIPS: 100% bias toward over-preservation; CLIPScore: 83% toward over-modification), establishing a clear motivation for region-disentangled evaluation. The finding that naively combining CLIPScore + CLIP-I (0.641) substantially underperforms BPM (0.799) demonstrates that holistic scoring cannot substitute for explicit region separation. Open questions include whether the binary (0/1) region-aware scores are too coarse for fine-grained ranking, how the method generalizes to multi-object or spatially complex instructions beyond the six MagicBrush categories, and whether the 3-annotator user study provides sufficient statistical power. The mask-guided editing enhancement (70% preservation improvement) suggests the localization pipeline has independent utility beyond evaluation.
