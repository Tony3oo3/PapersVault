## Problem

Many-shot in-context learning (ICL) with hundreds of demonstrations significantly outperforms few-shot ICL, but acquiring the required labeled data is prohibitively expensive. Simply using unlabeled samples as demonstrations yields marginal and unstable gains, and existing pseudo-labeling approaches do not address which unlabeled samples to label or how to adaptively select demonstrations per query. MAPLE tackles both challenges: selecting the most impactful unlabeled samples for pseudo-labeling and tailoring the demonstration set to each test query.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled set (e.g., 20 samples), a large unlabeled pool, and a test query as input, and produces a predicted label or answer. A pseudo-labeling budget P (20–100) controls how many unlabeled samples receive model-generated labels, and a fraction α (default 0.75) controls what proportion of the resulting candidate pool is used as demonstrations per query. The LLM (Gemini 1.5 Flash or Pro, 1M-token context) consumes the selected demonstrations concatenated with the query, while a Contriever encoder computes relevance scores for graph construction.

## Method

MAPLE constructs a labeled-unlabeled k-NN graph using Contriever embeddings, then selects unlabeled samples for pseudo-labeling via a node-influence score derived from Theorem 3.2: s(V_L, v) = log P̃_S(v, V_L) − log d · L̄_S(v, V_L), which balances the geometric mean of shortest-path counts (robustness) against the average shortest-path distance (efficiency) from each unlabeled node to the labeled set. At inference, a per-query graph is built over the candidate pool (labeled + pseudo-labeled samples plus the test query), and the same influence score ranks all candidate nodes relative to the test node; the top α fraction is selected as demonstrations. This two-stage influence-based design—first for pseudo-labeling selection, then for adaptive per-query demonstration selection—ensures that only the most informative and relevant samples are used, reducing noise from irrelevant or low-quality pseudo-labeled examples.

## Results

On Banking77 with Gemini 1.5 Pro, MAPLE achieves 80.3% average accuracy across pseudo-labeling budgets (20–100), outperforming RAG (78.3%) and Random (77.5%). On the Date reasoning task, MAPLE reaches 68.0% with Gemini Pro and 55.8% with Gemini Flash, consistently exceeding both baselines. On GPQA (graduate-level QA), MAPLE attains 44.3% average accuracy with Gemini Pro versus 42.9% for RAG and 42.3% for Random. Across all three tasks and both LLM variants, MAPLE's advantage grows with more pseudo-labeled demonstrations, reaching up to +2.4% over RAG on Banking77 (Pro) and +2.4% on GPQA (Flash).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPLE is compared against Random (uniform sampling of unlabeled samples for pseudo-labeling) and RAG (Contriever-based semantic similarity retrieval, Izacard et al. 2021) as the two primary pseudo-labeling selection baselines. MAPLE wins on every task–model combination: on Banking77 it gains +1.5% over RAG and +2.2% over Random with Gemini Flash, and +2.0% over RAG and +2.8% over Random with Gemini Pro. On Date, MAPLE leads RAG by +2.2% (Flash) and +1.0% (Pro), while on GPQA it outperforms RAG by +2.8% (Flash) and +1.4% (Pro). The gap is most pronounced at higher pseudo-labeling budgets (80–100 samples), where RAG and Random degrade or plateau while MAPLE continues to improve, suggesting that influence-based selection becomes increasingly critical as the candidate pool grows. The paper also reports that MAPLE outperforms a RAG-Adapt variant (RAG + adaptive demonstration selection) across all eight evaluated datasets in Figure 3, confirming that the influence-based sample selection for pseudo-labeling is the dominant factor rather than the downstream demonstration selection alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPLE repurposes the node influence concept from graph neural network literature (Xu et al., 2018; Huang & Zitnik, 2020; Wang & Leskovec, 2020) — originally developed for semi-supervised graph classification — to the problem of demonstration selection in in-context learning. It builds on the many-shot ICL line of work (Agarwal et al., 2024; Baek et al., 2024) and the adaptive demonstration selection literature (Rubin et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2023), but departs from prior selection methods by framing both pseudo-labeling sample selection and per-query demonstration selection as influence maximization on a relevance graph, rather than relying on embedding similarity or learned scoring functions. The Contriever encoder (Izacard et al., 2021) serves as the backbone for graph edge weights, connecting the method to dense retrieval practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAPLE addresses a practical bottleneck in many-shot ICL: the high cost of obtaining hundreds of labeled demonstrations. By pseudo-labeling only the most graph-influential unlabeled samples, it reduces labeling cost while maintaining or improving performance, making many-shot ICL more accessible in resource-constrained settings. However, the paper reveals that performance degrades as pseudo-labeled demonstrations increase (noise accumulation), and the KV Cache efficiency gains from fixed demonstrations are modest for API-based models. Open questions include how the interaction between labeled and pseudo-labeled samples affects ICL quality, whether the influence-based selection generalizes to multimodal or agentic settings, and whether the graph construction overhead becomes prohibitive at very large scale.
