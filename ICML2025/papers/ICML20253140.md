## Problem

Prior work on model immunization demonstrated empirical success in making text-to-image models harder to fine-tune on harmful tasks, but lacked a precise definition of what constitutes an immunized model and did not characterize when immunization is theoretically possible. This paper addresses that gap by providing a condition-number-based framework that links immunization effectiveness to the geometry of data covariance matrices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-training dataset (with labels), a harmful dataset (features only, no labels needed for the regularizer computation), and an initialized feature extractor θ₀. It produces an immunized feature extractor θ_I such that the Hessian condition number for linear probing on the harmful task is inflated while that on the pre-training task is reduced or maintained. For deep-net experiments, only the final layers (last two conv blocks for ResNet18, final transformer block for ViT) are updated while the rest remain frozen at θ₀.

## Method

The paper formulates immunization as controlling the condition number of the Hessian H(θ) = θᵀKθ for linear probing, showing via Proposition 3.2 that Hessian singular values depend on the angular alignment between θ's singular vectors and the data covariance's eigenvectors. The key technical contribution is a novel differentiable regularizer R_ill(S) = 1/(½k‖S‖²_F − ½(σ_min)²) that upper-bounds 1/log(κ(S)) and is guaranteed to monotonically increase the condition number under gradient descent, complementing the existing R_well regularizer that decreases it. Algorithm 1 jointly minimizes the supervised loss on the pre-training task, R_ill applied to the harmful-task Hessian, and R_well applied to the pre-training-task Hessian, with gradients normalized by the respective data covariance inverses. The framework is validated on linear models (House Prices regression, MNIST binary classification) and extended empirically to ResNet18 and ViT on ImageNet with Stanford Cars and Country211 as harmful tasks, using a relative immunization ratio (RIR) metric to quantify effectiveness.

## Results

On the House Price regression task, the proposed method achieves a Relative Immunization Ratio (RIR) of 356.20±5.49, substantially outperforming Optκ (92.58±4.49), IMMA (2.00±0.19), and R_ill Only (1.24±0.02). On MNIST binary classification (averaged over 90 digit pairs), the method achieves RIR = 70.04±3.28 with low variance, matching Optκ's average (69.73) but with far greater robustness (Optκ has ±54.00 std). On deep nets pre-trained on ImageNet, the method achieves RIR_θ0 of 34.52 (ViT/Cars) and 41.34 (ViT/Country211), while maintaining or even improving ImageNet test accuracy (e.g., ViT accuracy increases from 81.78% to 82.79% for Cars).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: R_ill Only (minimizing only the ill-conditioning regularizer), IMMA (Zheng & Yeh, 2024, a bi-level optimization for immunizing text-to-image models), and Optκ (directly minimizing the difference in condition numbers). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on RIR across every setting. On House Price, it achieves RIR = 356 vs. 93 for Optκ and ~2 for IMMA; on MNIST, it matches Optκ's average RIR (~70) but with 16× lower variance, indicating far greater robustness to initialization. On deep nets (Table 3), the advantage is most pronounced: on ViT/Country211, the method achieves RIR_θ0 = 41.3 vs. 16.5 for Optκ, 0.85 for IMMA, and 1.12 for R_ill Only. IMMA and R_ill Only consistently fail to immunize effectively (RIR ≈ 1 or below), as they increase the condition number on both the harmful and pre-training tasks simultaneously. Notably, the proposed method also preserves or improves ImageNet test accuracy on ViT (81.78% → 82.79–83.17%), whereas all baselines degrade ResNet18 accuracy by 4–6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Nenov et al. (2024), which introduced a differentiable regularizer R_well for minimizing the condition number of a matrix with monotonic decrease guarantees under gradient descent. The authors design R_ill as the natural counterpart for maximizing the condition number, and together these two regularizers form the core of their immunization algorithm. Conceptually, the paper departs from Zheng & Yeh's (2024) IMMA formulation—which treats immunization as a bi-level optimization without a precise definition of 'immunized'—by reframing the problem through the lens of Hessian condition numbers, yielding a concrete, measurable definition of an immunized model and a first-order algorithm with provable monotonicity guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The condition-number framework provides the first principled, quantitative definition of model immunization and a clear evaluation metric (RIR), which should help standardize comparisons across immunization methods. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond linear probing to full fine-tuning or generative model adaptation (the original IMMA setting), and whether the monotonicity guarantees can be extended to the joint update of both regularizers plus the supervised loss. The empirical success on ResNet18 and ViT despite the linear-only theory is encouraging but leaves a significant theoretical gap that future work must close.
