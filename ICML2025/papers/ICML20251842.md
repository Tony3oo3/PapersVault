## Problem

Existing neural audio codecs apply residual vector quantization uniformly across the full frequency band, ignoring the well-established psychoacoustic principle that different spectral regions carry distinct perceptual information (intelligibility, articulation, speaker identity). This full-band quantization leads to suboptimal bitrate allocation and fails to exploit the perceptual masking effects that allow masked frequency regions to be encoded with fewer bits, limiting both compression efficiency and perceptual fidelity.

## Input / Output

MUFFIN takes 24 kHz mono audio waveforms (speech, music, environmental sounds) as input, truncated to 10-second clips during training with 1-second random segments per batch. It produces a sequence of discrete tokens at 75 Hz (300 tokens/s, 2.7 kB/s) in the default configuration, or at 25 Hz (150 tokens/s) and 12.5 Hz (100 tokens/s) in high-compression variants, along with a reconstructed 24 kHz waveform. The latent representation operates at 75 Hz after 320× temporal downsampling, and each token is a 9-bit index into an EMA-updated codebook, with four codebooks total (three for frequency bands plus one residual).

## Method

MUFFIN is a fully convolutional autoencoder (46.1M params, striding 2-4-5-8) whose core innovation is Multi-Band Spectral Residual Vector Quantization (MBS-RVQ): rather than splitting the input waveform into bands, it applies FFT to the encoder's latent representation to isolate three frequency sub-bands (0–18.75, 18.75–37.5, 37.5–75 Hz of the 75 Hz latent), quantizing each sequentially with residuals passed forward in an RVQ fashion. This design is justified by a perceptual entropy bound (Theorem 3.1) showing that multi-band modeling reduces the minimum required bitrate by exploiting psychoacoustic masking. The architecture replaces Leaky ReLU with a modified snake activation (adding learnable amplitude β and bias γ) to preserve high-frequency periodic structure while maintaining Lipschitz continuity, and uses multi-receptive-field fusion plus ConvNeXt-style inverted bottlenecks to mimic transformer self-attention and feed-forward layers within a purely convolutional framework. Training combines a 64-bin multiscale mel-spectrogram L1 loss, HingeGAN adversarial losses (MS-STFT, MPD, MSD discriminators), and per-band VQ-VAE commitment losses, all with straight-through gradient estimation.

## Results

On LibriTTS Test-Clean, MUFFIN achieves the best reconstruction quality across all metrics: STFT distance 1.555, MEL distance 0.692, PESQ 2.996, STOI 0.954, UTMOS 4.017, and ViSQOL 4.516, outperforming the retrained HiFi-Codec baseline (STFT 1.618, PESQ 2.712, UTMOS 3.831) and all other NACs. The high-compression MUFFIN▲ variant at 12.5 Hz (0.9 kB/s) still achieves UTMOS 4.074 and STOI 0.932, surpassing Mimi at the same frame rate (UTMOS 2.966, STOI 0.620). On the zero-shot IEMOCAP emotional speech set, MUFFIN variants maintain UTMOS scores above the ground truth (1.903–2.026 vs. GT 1.859), demonstrating robustness to unseen expressive content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUFFIN outperforms all three primary baselines on every reported metric for speech reconstruction. Against the retrained HiFi-Codec baseline (same architecture family, same training data), MUFFIN improves STFT distance from 1.618 to 1.555, PESQ from 2.712 to 2.996, and UTMOS from 3.831 to 4.017 on Test-Clean, with consistent gains on Test-Other. Against DAC, the gap is larger: MUFFIN reduces STFT distance by 0.204 (1.555 vs. 1.759) and boosts UTMOS by over 1.0 point (4.017 vs. 2.951). Against EnCodec, MUFFIN achieves a 0.401 lower STFT distance and a 1.748 higher UTMOS. In the extreme-compression regime at 12.5 Hz, MUFFIN▲ dominates Mimi (the prior SOTA at that rate) across all metrics, with STFT 1.663 vs. 2.488, PESQ 2.360 vs. 1.715, and UTMOS 4.074 vs. 2.966, confirming that the multi-band spectral quantization preserves perceptual quality even under 1920× downsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUFFIN builds directly on the HiFi-Codec architecture (same striding, training losses, and overall autoencoder design) but departs from the full-band RVQ paradigm used by SoundStream, EnCodec, and DAC by performing multi-band spectral quantization within the encoder's latent space rather than at the input. This latent-space band splitting avoids the latency penalty of per-band encoding in prior multi-band systems (Penguins, Gull, spectral codecs) while drawing on psychoacoustic principles (critical bands, frequency masking) that underpinned legacy codecs like MP3/AAC but had been largely unexplored in neural audio coding. The modified snake activation extends the periodic activation line from Kumar et al. (2024) and Evans et al. (2024) by adding a learnable bias term to stabilize variance, and the perceptual entropy bound (Theorem 3.1) provides an information-theoretic justification absent from prior neural codec work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing neural audio coding through a psychoacoustic lens, MUFFIN offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc full-band quantization, with the practical benefit of naturally disentangling speaker identity (Codebook 3) from speech content (Codebooks 1–2) without any supervision—enabling more controllable downstream generation. The 12.5 Hz SOTA variant is particularly relevant for speech-LLM integration where low frame rates reduce sequence length. Open questions include the notable degradation on emotional speech (IEMOCAP), the limited disentanglement for purely instrumental music, and whether the psychoacoustic band boundaries generalize beyond the 24 kHz / 320× downsampling configuration used here.
