## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors either rely on computationally expensive reconstruction with specific generative models (e.g., DDIM in DIRE), which overfits to generator-specific artifacts and fails to generalize to unseen generators, or leverage high-level semantic features from pretrained vision-language models that risk obsolescence as generative models improve photorealism. The paper asks: can we find a computationally simple, generator-free forgery artifact that captures universal low-level noise patterns independent of semantic content?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (H×W×3, pixel values in [0,255]) and produces a binary real/synthetic classification probability via a lightweight ResNet (1.4M parameters). Crucially, the detector does not consume the original image directly; instead it receives the pixelwise decomposition residual — a 3-channel image of identical spatial dimensions computed by subtracting the quantized-and-reconstructed version from the original. This residual has much smaller magnitude than the input (‖R_pid(x)‖ ≪ ‖x‖), effectively isolating noise from visual content.

## Method

PiD computes a pixelwise decomposition residual by (1) mapping each pixel vector from RGB to YUV color space via an invertible 3×3 matrix M_t, (2) applying a quantization function (rounding or truncation) to the projected vector, (3) mapping back to RGB via M_t^{-1}, and (4) taking the difference between the original and reconstructed pixel as the residual R_pid(x) = x − Q(x×M_t)×M_t^{-1}. This residual is fed to a simple customized ResNet trained with standard cross-entropy loss. The key insight is that generative models optimize high-level semantic content (principal components) while overlooking low-level quantization noise; the color-space transformation before quantization is critical — ablations show the YUV conversion error contributes most to generalization, outperforming pure frequency-domain compression or simple diagonal scaling. The approach requires no generative model for reconstruction, no pretrained backbone, and adds negligible inference overhead (~33 ms vs. 2425 ms for DIRE).

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (trained on SDv1.4), PiD achieves a new state-of-the-art average accuracy of 98.0% across 8 generative models, surpassing the best reconstruction-based method LARE² (86.2%) by 11.8%, the best low-level method NPR (88.6%) by 9.4%, and the best pretraining-based method C2P-CLIP (95.8%) by 2.2%. On UniversalFakeDetect (trained on ForenSynths/ProGAN), PiD achieves 96.22% mAcc and 98.24% mAP, outperforming FatFormer (95.89% mAcc) and NPR (93.21% mAcc). On the Self-Synthesis 9-GAN dataset, PiD reaches 94.7% mean accuracy, exceeding NPR (93.2%) by 1.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiD decisively outperforms reconstruction-based methods on GenImage: DIRE (71.2% mAcc) and LARE² (86.2%) both suffer severe generalization failure on non-SD generators (e.g., DIRE drops to 50.4% on Midjourney, 52.3% on ADM), while PiD maintains 95–99% across all 8 generators. Among frequency/low-level methods, PiD surpasses NPR (88.6%), FreqNet (86.8%), and LGrad on GenImage, with the largest gaps on ADM (96.3% vs. 76.9% for NPR) and BigGAN (98.3% vs. 84.2% for NPR). Against pretraining-based methods, PiD beats C2P-CLIP (95.8%) by 2.2% and FatFormer (88.9%) by 9.1% on GenImage mAcc; on UniversalFakeDetect, PiD (96.22% mAcc) edges out FatFormer (95.89%) and C2P-CLIP (95.70%). On the Self-Synthesis 9-GAN dataset, PiD (94.7% mean Acc, 99.4% mAP) surpasses NPR (93.2%, 96.6%) and UnivFD (77.6%, 98.8%), with near-perfect scores (100% Acc) on AttGAN, BEGAN, and RelGAN. Early baselines (CNN-Spot, Patchfor, Co-occurrence, Freq-spec, F3Net) all fall below 72% mAcc on GenImage, confirming that pixelwise decomposition residuals capture forgery signals invisible to prior representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of residual-based AIGI detection (DIRE, LARE², DRCT) and frequency/gradient-based methods (FreqNet, NPR, LGrad), but departs sharply from both by replacing heavy reconstruction generators or Fourier filters with a single pixelwise color-space transformation followed by quantization. The conceptual lineage traces back to the observation that JPEG compression residuals capture noise patterns, which the authors generalize into a generator-free, parameter-free operation (YUV mapping + rounding). Unlike CLIP-based detectors (UnivFD, FatFormer, C2P-CLIP) that rely on large pretrained backbones for semantic reasoning, PiD operates entirely in the pixel domain with a lightweight ResNet, arguing that low-level quantization artifacts are more universal than high-level semantic inconsistencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PiD achieves 98% accuracy on GenImage with only 1.4M parameters and ~33 ms inference, compared to DIRE's 2425 ms and 23.5M parameters, making real-time deployment feasible. The finding that a trivial pixelwise operation (color-space rotation + rounding) outperforms diffusion-model reconstruction by 11.8% challenges the assumption that generative-model-specific priors are necessary for detection. Open questions include robustness to real-world compression pipelines (multiple JPEG generations, HEIC), extension to video and 3D content, and whether the YUV-specific structure or the quantization step itself is the critical ingredient—ablations suggest the latter, but the full interaction with downstream network training remains underexplored.
