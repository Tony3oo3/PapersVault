## Problem

Existing LLM model selection strategies—routing (pick one model per query) and cascading (run models sequentially until quality is sufficient)—lack formal optimality proofs, do not characterize when they are effective, and cannot be combined. Routing is final after the first decision, cascading must follow a fixed model order, and prior cascading work relies on threshold heuristics without optimality guarantees. The paper asks: what is the provably optimal strategy for each paradigm, and can they be unified into a single more flexible framework?

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the method consumes a text query, per-model ex-ante quality estimates (predicted accuracy before running), post-hoc quality estimates (updated after observing prior model outputs, modeled as random variables with estimated variance), per-model cost estimates (monetary or latency-based), and scalar tradeoff parameters λ_j and γ. It outputs either a stop signal (return the best answer so far) or the index of the next model to run, chosen as the cheapest model within the selected supermodel. The iterative nature means the input at step j depends on which models were already computed, making the strategy stateful across steps.

## Method

The paper formulates routing as a linear program maximizing expected quality under a cost budget, solved via Lagrangian relaxation: the optimal strategy selects the model maximizing q̂_i(x) − λĉ_i(x), with ties broken by a convex combination (parameter γ) between the cheapest and most expensive tied model. Cascading is reinterpreted as sequential routing over 'supermodels' (prefix sequences of models), where at step j the strategy routes between M_{1:j−1}, …, M_{1:k} using updated quality/cost estimates. The key novelty, cascade routing, generalizes both by routing at each step over ALL supermodels consistent with the models already computed, enabling dynamic reordering (e.g., running a large model first, then a small one). To handle the exponential number of supermodels, the authors prove that the max-quality function is submodular and use a negative marginal gain lemma to prune any supermodel containing a model that hurts the quality-cost tradeoff, reducing the search space in practice.

## Results

On RouterBench (11 models, low noise), cascade routing achieves 87.24% AUC, outperforming routing (83.25%), the threshold-based cascade baseline (84.48%), and the authors' new optimal cascade (84.47%). On SWE-Bench with 10 models, cascade routing reaches 54.12% AUC versus 40.47% for routing and 38.52% for the baseline cascade, a 14% absolute gain. Across all RouterBench noise levels and model counts, cascade routing improves over the best baseline by 1–4% AUC (13–80% relative to linear interpolation), with gains widening as more models are available and narrowing under higher estimation noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cascade routing consistently outperforms both routing and the threshold-based cascade baseline across all RouterBench configurations, with the largest gap (up to 8% AUC) appearing in the 11-model, low-noise setting where routing scores 83.25% and the baseline cascade 84.48% versus 87.24% for cascade routing. On SWE-Bench, the advantage is even more pronounced: cascade routing (54.12%) beats routing (40.47%) by 14 points and the baseline cascade (38.52%) by 16 points, while the authors' new optimal cascade (53.20%) also substantially surpasses the threshold baseline, confirming that the theoretical improvement over prior cascading translates to practice. On Minerva Math + LiveCodeBench, where ex-ante quality estimation is accurate, routing performs well (47.46%) but cascade routing still leads (48.55%), and the new cascade nearly matches routing (46.76%) despite poor post-hoc estimates. On classification and open-form reasoning benchmarks with noisy quality estimators, cascade routing maintains a smaller but consistent edge (up to 1.2% AUC) over all baselines, mirroring the high-noise RouterBench results. The key finding is that cascade routing is most robust: it leverages low σ_ante when ex-ante estimates are good (routing-like regime) and low σ_post when post-hoc estimates are good (cascading-like regime), whereas each individual paradigm degrades sharply when its critical estimator is noisy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the routing formulation of Chen et al. (ICML 2022) from deterministic to probabilistic strategies, proving optimality where prior work could only show near-optimality. For cascading, it subsumes the threshold-based strategies used by FrugalGPT, Data Shunt, and all subsequent cascading work as special cases of a more general optimal strategy, showing via Corollary 1 that the threshold approach is only optimal under restrictive conditions (query-independent costs, query-independent future quality, and supermodel quality equal to last-model quality). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of cascade routing, which unifies both paradigms by allowing dynamic re-routing at each step over all supermodels, with a submodularity-based pruning lemma (negative marginal gain) to handle the exponential supermodel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably optimal strategies for both routing and cascading, filling a theoretical gap that has limited the field's ability to reason about when and why model selection helps. The identification of quality estimation accuracy (ex-ante for routing, post-hoc for cascading) as the critical bottleneck—rather than model size diversity—reframes where future effort should be directed. Open questions include scalability to very large model pools (the exponential supermodel space, even with pruning), applicability to multi-turn or agentic settings where cost and quality are path-dependent, and whether the framework extends naturally to settings without any reliable quality signal (e.g., open-ended creative tasks).
