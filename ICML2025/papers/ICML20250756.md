## Problem

Preconditioned subgradient methods for robust low-rank matrix recovery have established linear convergence rates independent of the condition number, but only in the known-rank asymmetric setting (Tong et al., 2021b) or the overparameterized symmetric setting (Zhang et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023). No convergence guarantees existed for preconditioned approaches applied to asymmetric matrices of unknown rank in the presence of gross corruptions, because the regularized preconditioners become non-invertible when the true singular values include zeros. This paper closes that gap by providing the first linear convergence rate for an overparameterized preconditioned subgradient method on asymmetric matrices with outliers.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a measurement vector y ∈ R^p obtained from linear measurements y_i = (1/p)⟨A_i, X_★⟩ + s_i of an unknown low-rank matrix X_★ ∈ R^{m×n}, where A_i are Gaussian measurement matrices and s_i are sparse outlier corruptions. The user specifies an overestimated rank d ≥ r (the true rank) and a single regularization parameter λ > 0 that ensures invertibility of the preconditioners. The output is the recovered matrix X̂ = L_t R_t^T, with factors L ∈ R^{m×d} and R ∈ R^{n×d} whose product approximates X_★; the ℓ1 loss makes the method robust to arbitrary sparse corruptions without requiring knowledge of the outlier locations.

## Method

OPSA performs alternating quasi-Newton-type subgradient updates on the Burer-Monteiro factors L and R, using regularized preconditioners (R_t^T R_t + λI)^{-1} and (L_t^T L_t + λI)^{-1} that remain well-defined even when d > r and Σ_★ is singular. A Polyak-type step size η_t = [L(L_t R_t^T) − L(L_★ R_★^T)] / γ_t drives the iterations, where γ_t is a preconditioned subgradient norm. The key theoretical innovation is a novel distance metric dist(F, F_★) that incorporates (Σ_★ + λI)^{1/2} weighting to handle the non-invertibility of the true singular values in the overparameterized asymmetric case, enabling contraction analysis via new perturbation bounds on matrix norms. Under rank-d restricted Lipschitz continuity and restricted sharpness conditions, OPSA achieves linear convergence at rate (1 − 0.12/χ²)^{t/2} independent of the condition number κ(X_★), with iteration complexity O((δ_{2d}^+/δ^0)² log(1/ε)) for robust matrix sensing under mixed-norm RIP and S-outlier bound conditions.

## Results

OPSA achieves a linear convergence rate of (1 − 0.12/χ²)^(t/2) in the proposed distance metric, where χ = L/μ is the ratio of the restricted Lipschitz constant to the restricted sharpness constant. The rate is provably independent of the condition number κ(X⋆) of the ground-truth matrix, and the iteration complexity for matrix sensing is O((δ⁺₂d/δ⁻₂d)² log(1/ε)) in the noiseless case and O((δ⁺₂d/δ⁰)² log(1/ε)) with outliers. For Gaussian measurements, this simplifies to O(1/(1−2p_s)² log(1/ε)) iterations, where p_s is the outlier fraction. Empirically, OPSA maintains stable linear convergence even with condition numbers up to κ = 10,000, 5× rank overparameterization (d = 2r), and 10% outlier corruptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is ScaledSM (Tong et al., 2021b), the state-of-the-art preconditioned subgradient method for robust matrix sensing. In direct head-to-head comparisons (Figures 2–3), OPSA consistently maintains linear convergence across all tested overparameterization levels (d = 5, 10, 20, 40 for r = 5 or 10), while ScaledSM fails to converge entirely when the rank is significantly overestimated (e.g., d = 40 with r = 5). This is the key empirical advantage: ScaledSM requires the exact rank r, whereas OPSA tolerates substantial rank overestimation without losing its linear rate. In the supplementary video background subtraction experiments (Figures 8–9), OPSA with a 5× overestimated rank (d = 10 vs. true rank 2) and only 30% of observations produces crisp foreground/background separations on the shoppingmall and restaurant datasets, visually comparable to the full-observation AccAltProj (Cai et al., 2019) baseline which uses the exact rank. OPSA also demonstrates robustness to condition numbers up to κ = 10,000 and outlier densities up to 10%, where ScaledSM's convergence degrades as corruption increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: preconditioned subgradient methods for robust low-rank recovery (Tong et al., 2021b), overparameterized matrix factorization for unknown-rank recovery (Ma & Fattahi, 2021; Zhang et al., 2023), and preconditioned approaches for asymmetric matrices (Cheng & Zhao, 2024). The key departure is that prior preconditioned methods required the true rank r to be known, while prior overparameterized methods either restricted to symmetric matrices or assumed smooth losses. OPSA is the first to combine overparameterization (d ≥ r), asymmetric factorization, and a non-smooth ℓ₁ loss under a unified preconditioned subgradient framework, requiring a novel distance metric that handles the non-invertibility of Σ_★ when d > r.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first linear convergence guarantee for overparameterized asymmetric low-rank matrix recovery with gross corruptions, independent of the condition number of the target matrix—a gap that left practitioners without theoretical backing when the true rank is unknown and data is corrupted. The iteration complexity analysis via mixed-norm RIP and S-outlier bounds makes the guarantees concrete for Gaussian measurement settings. Limitations include the requirement for a sufficiently close spectral initialization (whose tolerance tightens as κ grows) and the fact that the rate constant χ = L/μ degrades with overparameterization. Open directions include extending the framework to LoRA fine-tuning of foundation models and to weaker initialization conditions.
