## Problem

Context-based meta-RL methods learn task latents to distinguish training tasks but generalize poorly to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks. Existing virtual task (VT) construction approaches, such as LDM, suffer from two key limitations: generated VTs fail to accurately capture task characteristics, and they only generate reward samples, making them ineffective in environments where state transition dynamics also vary across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of N_c state-action-reward-next-state transitions (contexts) collected from both on-policy exploration and off-policy RL episodes per task, stored in separate replay buffers. It produces 10-dimensional task latent vectors that encode task identity, and policy actions conditioned on these latents. Additionally, the task decoder generates virtual contexts (rewards and next states) for interpolated VTs, which are fed back into the RL training loop alongside real contexts.

## Method

TAVT combines three key mechanisms: (1) a Bisimulation-metric-based encoder-decoder loss that trains task latents to preserve pairwise task distances computed from reward and transition dynamics, augmented with an on-off latent loss that stabilizes on-policy latents by anchoring them to off-policy counterparts; (2) a WGAN-based task-preserving sample generation module where the task decoder acts as a generator producing virtual contexts for Dirichlet-interpolated VT latents, with a task-preserving loss ensuring the encoder recovers the intended VT latent from generated contexts; and (3) a state regularization technique that blends generated next states with real training-task next states (coefficient ε_reg=0.1) to mitigate Q-function overestimation bias in state-varying environments. The RL policy (SAC) is trained jointly on real and virtual contexts, and a novel exploration policy π_exp = π(·|s, z_on^α) leverages VT latents to cover a broader task space during meta-testing.

## Results

On MetaWorld ML1 environments, TAVT achieves near-perfect average success rates across all six setups, ranging from 0.92 (Push-OOD-Extra) to 0.99 (Reach-OOD-Extra), consistently outperforming all 8 baselines. On MuJoCo OOD environments, TAVT approaches the '-oracle' upper bound (trained on both training and test tasks) in Cheetah-Vel-OOD, Ant-Goal-OOD, and Walker-Mass-OOD, while remaining robust in challenging extrapolation settings like Ant-Dir-2 where other methods struggle. In state-varying environments (Walker-Mass-OOD, Hopper-Mass-OOD), TAVT significantly outperforms LDM, which fails to adapt due to its reward-only virtual task construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAVT outperforms all 8 baselines across all 6 ML1 environments. The most dramatic gaps appear against PEARL-based methods: CCM collapses on Push tasks (0.18 on Push, 0.15 on Push-OOD-Extra) while TAVT maintains 0.98 and 0.92 respectively; PEARL and MIER similarly underperform on Push variants (0.55–0.61 vs. TAVT's 0.92–0.98). Among on-policy methods, LDM (the closest competitor using virtual tasks) trails TAVT by 0.16–0.27 on OOD tasks (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.92 on Push-OOD-Extra), and RL² degrades sharply on extrapolation (0.38 on Push-OOD-Extra vs. TAVT's 0.92). Amago is competitive on Reach-OOD-Inter (0.93 vs. 0.96) and Push-OOD-Inter (0.98 vs. 0.98, a tie) but falls behind on Reach (0.71 vs. 0.98) and Push-OOD-Extra (0.83 vs. 0.92). On MuJoCo, TAVT approaches oracle-level performance in Cheetah-Vel-OOD, Ant-Goal-OOD, and Walker-Mass-OOD, while LDM fails entirely in state-varying environments (Walker/Hopper-Mass-OOD) due to its inability to model task-dependent transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAVT extends the PEARL framework for context-based meta-RL by replacing its reconstruction-based task encoder training with a Bisimulation-metric-based approach that captures both reward and state-transition differences between tasks. It builds on LDM's virtual task construction via Dirichlet interpolation of task latents but departs critically by generating full dynamics (rewards and next states) rather than rewards alone, and by using a WGAN to ensure generated virtual contexts resemble real ones. The on-off latent loss and state regularization are novel stabilizing mechanisms not present in prior virtual-task methods, addressing instability in on-policy latents and overestimation bias in state-varying environments respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAVT addresses a practical gap in meta-RL: most context-based methods assume test tasks come from the same distribution as training tasks, yet real-world deployment often involves OOD tasks where even state dynamics shift. The consistent outperformance across 12 diverse environments (including state-varying MuJoCo tasks where LDM fails entirely) demonstrates that metric-based task representation combined with adversarial sample generation is a viable recipe for OOD generalization. However, the method assumes parametric task distributions (e.g., velocity or mass scaling) and does not extend to non-parametric task spaces like MetaWorld ML10/ML45, leaving a significant open question about whether the Bisimulation-metric approach can be combined with task decomposition methods to handle semantically distinct tasks.
