## Problem

Existing weighted multi-modal clustering methods suffer from three key limitations: learned modality weights lack trustworthiness guarantees, weight learning is isolated per-modality (relying on single-modal objective values rather than inter-modality correlations), and most methods require hand-tuned regularization parameters to control weight distributions. This paper addresses these gaps by reframing modality weight learning as a peer-review process where modalities mutually assess each other's contributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251624/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m multi-modal feature matrices Y^j ∈ R^{n×d^j} (with potentially different feature dimensions per modality) and the number of clusters |T| as input, converting them into joint probability distributions via a Bag-of-Words model. It outputs a final clustering assignment p(t|x) and per-modality weights w^k. Notably, the weight learning process is entirely parameter-free (no regularization hyperparameters), and the only tunable parameter is the IB balance β, which can be set to +∞ for a fully parameter-free variant with only ~2% performance degradation.

## Method

PTIB iteratively treats each modality as an 'author' while the remaining modalities act as 'reviewers', computing a peer-review score μ_i^k as the normalized mutual information between the local IB-based clustering results of the reviewer and author modalities. To ensure reliability, a trustworthy score σ_i^f is computed by measuring the cluster distortion (based on binary entropy of major-category probability, with a 'symmetry crisis' fix for clusters below 50% purity) between each reviewer's local clustering and the final clustering result, updated in a self-supervised manner each iteration. The final modality weight is the inner product of the peer-review and trustworthy score vectors, with a special two-modal case that bypasses peer-review scores to avoid modality prejudice. Optimization proceeds via a sequential k-means-like draw-and-merger algorithm that minimizes a Jensen-Shannon-divergence-based merger cost under the IB objective, requiring no additional weight parameters.

## Results

PTIB achieves the best Accuracy and NMI on all eight multi-modal datasets. Headline numbers include 99.80% Acc / 99.30% NMI on 20NG, 93.33% Acc / 96.46% NMI on COIL20, and 61.17% Acc / 82.86% NMI on COIL100. The method also offers a parameter-free variant (β→∞) that retains within ~2% of the tuned version on most datasets, with the largest drop being −6.87% Acc on COIL20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTIB outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset for both Acc and NMI. The closest competitors vary by dataset: on 20NG, TIM (99.40/98.08) is second, with PTIB gaining +0.40 Acc and +1.22 NMI; on COIL20, MLAN (87.22/94.35) is second, with PTIB gaining +6.11 Acc and +2.11 NMI; on Event, SMVAGC-SF (54.76/36.97) is second, with PTIB gaining +5.48 Acc and +8.39 NMI; on Soccer, MCMLE (56.07/50.06) is second, with PTIB gaining +6.79 Acc and +2.55 NMI. On the more challenging 17Flowers, 75Flowers, COIL100, and MMI datasets, PTIB leads SMVAGC-SF or MCMLE by 3.8–6.1% Acc and 4.7–6.1% NMI. Among IB-based baselines (MfIB, DMIB, MVIB), PTIB consistently surpasses them, confirming that the peer-review and trustworthy scoring mechanism adds value beyond standard mutual-information weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck (IB) framework for multi-modal clustering (Tishby et al., 1999; Lou et al., 2013) by introducing a peer-review-inspired mechanism for modality weight learning. It departs from existing weighted MMCs (e.g., MLAN, GMC, DMIB) that learn weights in isolation from single-modal objective values, instead computing weights through inter-modal interaction: normalized mutual information between local clusterings serves as a 'peer-review score,' and a cluster-uncertainty-based 'trustworthy score' (measuring how well a modality's local clustering aligns with the final result) gates the reliability of each reviewer. The optimization reuses the draw-and-merger k-means-like procedure from Lou et al. (2013) and Hu et al. (2020), with the merger cost theorem directly inherited from the latter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in weighted MMCs—no prior method assesses whether learned modality weights are trustworthy—and does so in a parameter-free, self-supervised manner, which is practically appealing for real-world deployment where tuning regularization parameters is burdensome. However, the method is limited to fully aligned, complete multi-modal data and requires the number of clusters in advance, restricting applicability to incomplete or partially observed settings. The peer-review metaphor, while creative, ultimately reduces to standard NMI and binary entropy computations, so the conceptual contribution is more in framing than in new mathematical machinery. Open questions include whether the trustworthiness mechanism generalizes to deep multi-modal representations or to incomplete/unaligned data.
