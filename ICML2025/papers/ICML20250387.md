## Problem

Neural operators can accelerate gradient-based PDE-constrained optimization, but face two critical challenges: (1) training data from random sampling fails to capture the complex, correlated structures near the optimal region, and (2) prediction and gradient errors accumulate across optimization iterations, causing the trajectory to enter out-of-distribution regions and derail. No prior work simultaneously addresses data-efficient training on optimization trajectories and derivative accuracy of the learned operator.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a design parameter tensor λ (spatial coordinates, control variables such as density or shape parameters, and binary masks for active domains) defined on arbitrary unstructured meshes with variable node counts, plus an optional reference solution u_r and transformation vector φ (the coordinate or density difference between reference and query). It outputs the predicted PDE solution field (e.g., pressure, velocity, displacement, magnetic flux) and, via autodifferentiation, the sensitivity ∂J/∂λ used for gradient-based updates. The RNO formulation allows the reference to be dropped at inference time, reducing to a standard neural operator mapping λ → u.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Virtual-Fourier (VF) layer, which generalizes Fourier-based integral operators to irregular meshes by (i) projecting N mesh points to M fixed virtual sensors via a learned point-wise linear map and softmax-weighted aggregation, (ii) applying a standard truncated Fourier layer in the virtual space, and (iii) projecting back to the physical mesh. This preserves the simple derivative structure of Fourier layers (d/dx F(z) = F(dz/dx)) while achieving permutation equivariance on arbitrary grids, unlike transformer-based layers whose softmax attention introduces complex derivative bias. Training uses a sensitivity loss that supervises the autodifferentiated gradient of the predicted objective against ground-truth sensitivities from adjoint solvers, combined with a reference-query RNO formulation that pairs nearby points along optimization trajectories (analogous to contrastive learning) and applies reference dropout (ratio 0.3) for flexible inference. At optimization time, a hybrid algorithm periodically calls the numerical solver to recalibrate the RNO's reference, suppressing error accumulation, while noise injection and multi-reference averaging further smooth the gradient signal.

## Results

On four PDE-constrained optimization benchmarks (Microreactor2D, Fuelcell2D, Inductor2D, Drone3D), the proposed R-VF model achieves the lowest relative l2 error across nearly all solution components and sensitivity metrics. For example, on Microreactor2D, R-VF reduces sensitivity error (Ls) to 1.70e-1 versus 2.02e-1 (R-PA) and 2.05e-1 (R-LA), and on Drone3D it achieves 5.05e-1 versus 5.66e-1 and 6.48e-1. The sensitivity error improvement over the second-best RNO variant ranges from 3.8% to 17.5% across datasets. Even in the degenerate no-reference setting (R-VF w/o ref), performance often surpasses all vanilla neural operators, indicating the RNO training procedure itself suppresses overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Virtual-Fourier (VF) layer against two transformer-based baselines: linear attention (LA) from GNOT (Hao et al., 2023) and physics attention (PA) from Transolver (Wu et al., 2024), all wrapped in the RNO framework. R-VF consistently outperforms both R-LA and R-PA on operator accuracy (solution field prediction) across all four datasets, with the largest gaps on Drone3D (e.g., u1: 7.64e-2 vs. 9.60e-2 for R-PA and 1.08e-1 for R-LA). On sensitivity (derivative) accuracy, R-VF achieves a 3.8%–17.5% reduction in relative l2 error over the second-best RNO variant, with the most dramatic improvement on Microreactor2D (Ls: 1.70e-1 vs. 2.02e-1 for R-PA, a ~16% reduction). The advantage is consistent across 5 random seeds (Table 4 in Appendix E), confirming it is not a seed-dependent artifact. Notably, even the no-reference variant (R-VF w/o ref) often beats the vanilla LA and PA operators, suggesting the RNO pairwise training procedure itself provides a regularization benefit independent of the reference input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Reference Neural Operators (RNO, Cheng et al. 2024) from their original shape-optimization setting to a broader class of PDE-constrained optimization problems, and generalizes Fourier-based operator layers (FNO, Li et al. 2020) to arbitrary irregular meshes via the Virtual-Fourier layer. Conceptually, it departs from prior surrogate-modeling work for PDECO (Hwang et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2021, Xue et al. 2020) by treating the optimization trajectory itself as the training data source and by explicitly supervising the operator's derivative (sensitivity) rather than just its forward prediction. The hybrid inference strategy—periodically recalibrating the neural operator with a numerical solver—differs from prior solver-in-the-loop methods (List et al. 2022, Um et al. 2020) in that the neural operator is trained offline and used as a standalone surrogate between solver calls, rather than being embedded within the solver iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error-accumulation problem identified here is fundamental to any iterative surrogate-assisted optimization and remains largely unaddressed in the neural operator literature; the hybrid recalibration strategy offers a practical, low-overhead remedy. The Virtual-Fourier layer is a potentially reusable architectural component that decouples derivative expressiveness from the inductive biases of softmax attention, which could benefit any differentiable operator-learning task. Key limitations include the requirement that the underlying PDE exhibit smooth (holomorphic) dependence on design parameters, the reliance on COMSOL-generated data for all four benchmarks, and the fact that the hybrid method can still converge to suboptimal solutions due to residual derivative errors. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-smooth or topology-altering optimizations, whether convergence guarantees can be established, and how the method scales to problems with thousands of design variables.
