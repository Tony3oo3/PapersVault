## Problem

Likelihood-based distribution matching methods rely on fixed or simple learnable priors (e.g., Gaussian, MoG) that distort the encoder's latent transformation and limit expressiveness. More expressive alternatives like LSGM require backpropagating through a diffusion U-Net to compute Jacobian terms, causing severe training instability at low noise levels. The paper asks whether one can use an expressive, learnable prior in distribution matching without ever computing its density or backpropagating through the score model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-domain inputs: grayscale digit images (MNIST/USPS at 28×28), RGB face images (CelebA/FairFace at 64×64×3), or tabular feature vectors (Adult dataset, 14 features). It produces a shared domain-invariant latent code (reduced dimensionality, e.g., 8-dim for tabular, 128-dim for images), which can be decoded back to the input space for reconstruction or cross-domain translation, or passed to a lightweight classifier for label prediction. For the geometry-preserving regularizer, CLIP ViT-H-14 embeddings of the input images serve as the semantic metric space, while a separate CLIP ViT-L-14 model is used for evaluation to avoid data leakage.

## Method

The core contribution is the Score Function Substitution (SFS) trick: since gradient-based DM training only requires the prior's score ∇_z log Q(z) and not its density, the cross-entropy term's gradient with respect to encoder parameters can be computed by detaching posterior samples from the computational graph and treating the score as a constant, yielding an equivalent gradient without any backpropagation through the score network. The prior is trained separately via denoising score matching on the encoder's marginal posterior, and the two are coupled through a simple alternating optimization loop (update encoder/decoder with the SAUB objective using the frozen score, then update the score with DSM for L steps). This avoids the Jacobian computation that destabilizes LSGM, reducing VRAM by up to 1.65× at 256-dim latents. On top of the SAUB objective, the paper adds a Gromov-Wasserstein structural preservation loss that compares pairwise distances in the data space against pairwise distances in the latent space, using CLIP semantic embeddings as the metric for high-dimensional image data (GW-SP) rather than pixel-level L2 (GW-EP).

## Results

On the MNIST→USPS domain adaptation task, SAUB with GW-SP (CLIP semantic space) achieves 96.1% accuracy, outperforming ADDA (89.4%), DANN (77.1%), and VAUB (40.7%); the reverse direction (USPS→MNIST) reaches 97.4% vs. 90.1% for ADDA. On the Adult fair classification benchmark, the method achieves near-zero demographic parity gap while maintaining higher accuracy than all non-adversarial baselines (FCRL, CVIB, VAUB) and the adversarial LAFTR-DP. For CelebA domain translation (black-to-blonde hair), GW-SP yields 9.0% Top-1 retrieval accuracy and 0.542 SSIM, substantially exceeding both no-GW (5.0%, 0.393) and GW-EP (4.0%, 0.428).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against domain adaptation baselines, SAUB with GW-SP dominates: it beats ADDA by 6.7 points (MNIST→USPS) and 7.3 points (USPS→MNIST), DANN by 19.0 and 24.4 points respectively, and VAUB by a large margin (55.4 and 52.1 points), indicating that the score-based prior is critical for effective distribution matching. In fair classification on the Adult dataset, SAUB achieves near-zero demographic parity gap at higher accuracy than FCRL, CVIB, VAUB, and the adversarial LAFTR-DP, with the score-based prior attributed to better semantic preservation in the latent space. Compared to LSGM (the closest score-based prior method), SAUB demonstrates superior training stability at low noise levels (σ_min=0.01) where LSGM exhibits diverging NLL, and achieves 1.44–1.65× lower VRAM usage at 128–256 latent dimensions by avoiding Jacobian backpropagation through the U-Net. For domain translation on CelebA, GW-SP in CLIP semantic space consistently outperforms both no-GW and GW-EP (Euclidean) across all retrieval and perceptual similarity metrics, while GW-EP actually degrades retrieval accuracy below the no-GW baseline, confirming that pixel-space distances are ineffective in high-dimensional image settings. On the synthetic nested-D dataset, score-based priors (both LSGM and SFS) achieve full latent class separation with as few as 20 samples, whereas Gaussian, MoG, and Vampprior priors require 100–500+ samples to reach comparable separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAUB framework (Gong et al., 2024) for non-adversarial distribution matching by replacing its fixed or simple learnable priors (Gaussian, MoG) with expressive score-based priors trained via denoising score matching. The key departure from the closest prior work, LSGM (Vahdat et al., 2021), is the Score Function Substitution (SFS) trick, which shows that gradient-based DM training only requires the prior's score function rather than its density, thereby eliminating the unstable Jacobian backpropagation through the diffusion U-Net. The paper further integrates Gromov-Wasserstein geometry-preserving regularization (Nakagawa et al., 2023) in CLIP semantic embedding space, a combination not previously explored in the DM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SFS trick provides a clean, theoretically grounded way to use expressive priors in variational DM without the computational and stability costs of LSGM, making score-based priors practically viable for domain-invariant representation learning. The demonstration that CLIP-based semantic metrics (GW-SP) substantially outperform pixel-space metrics (GW-EP) for geometry preservation in high-dimensional image data offers a practical recipe for incorporating pretrained semantic knowledge into DM pipelines. Limitations include reliance on relatively simple VAE and diffusion architectures, a basic alternating optimization scheme (with more sophisticated bi-level methods left to future work), and the fact that domain translation results are explicitly framed as proof-of-concept rather than competitive with SOTA image translation methods.
