## Problem

Diffusion models require many discretization steps to faithfully approximate the reverse process, making inference expensive. The standard denoiser approximates the posterior p_{0|t}(x_0|x_t) with a Dirac mass at its conditional mean, which is a poor approximation under coarse time discretization. No prior method learned the full posterior distribution using a simple, non-adversarial loss that naturally reduces to the classical diffusion regression objective.

## Input / Output

The generative network x̂_θ(t, x_t, ξ) takes a noisy sample x_t at time t, a Gaussian noise vector ξ, and optional conditioning (class label for conditional image generation; encoded visual/proprioceptive observations plus task description for robotics), and outputs a sample from the learned posterior p_{0|t}^θ(·|x_t). For images, inputs are 32×32×3 (CIFAR-10), 64×64×3 (CelebA, LSUN Bedrooms), or 64×64×3 latent codes (CelebA-HQ); ξ is concatenated to x_t along the channel dimension. For robotics, the model generates a chunk of 8 seven-dimensional actions, with ξ ∈ ℝ^{8×2} concatenated on the last dimension.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard MSE regression loss with a conditional generalized energy score S_{λ,β}, where β ∈ (0,2] controls the kernel and λ ∈ [0,1] interpolates between the regression loss (λ=0, β=2) and a strictly proper energy score (λ=1, β<2). The network x̂_θ(t, x_t, ξ) is trained to produce samples from the full posterior p_{0|t}(x_0|x_t) rather than just its mean, with the loss integrating the score over noise levels t and dataset samples. At sampling time, the DDIM-style update is modified to plug in a sample from the learned posterior (via a fresh ξ) instead of the conditional mean, enabling coarse time discretization. The hyperparameter λ controls a diversity-accuracy trade-off: λ<1 underestimates posterior variance (concentrating samples), which helps at higher step counts, while λ=1 preserves full variance for the few-step regime. Theoretical analysis shows the energy score is 'diffusion compatible' (recovers MSE as β→2), and Gaussian analysis characterizes the exact variance bias introduced by λ<1.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 and CelebA (conditional) and LSUN Bedrooms and CelebA-HQ latent (unconditional), distributional diffusion models with λ=1, β<2 achieve substantially lower FID than standard diffusion models at 2–16 denoising steps, with a ~3× FID gap in the conditional setting and ~1.5× in the unconditional setting. At 100 steps, standard diffusion models recover the best FID, while distributional models with λ∈(0,1) remain competitive. On the Libero-Long robotics benchmark, λ>0 yields the highest success rate at 2 and 16 diffusion steps, while λ=0 is best at 50 steps. In 2D mixture-of-Gaussians experiments, the distributional model matches the MMD of a 100-step diffusion model using only a handful of steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard DDIM diffusion model (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021a) trained with MSE regression, the distributional model clearly wins in the few-step regime: at 2–16 steps it achieves markedly lower FID on all four image datasets and higher success rates on Libero, while at 100 steps the standard diffusion model regains the edge (the Dirac approximation of p₀|ₜ becomes accurate). Against DPM-solver++ (Lu et al., 2022), the distributional approach outperforms it for more than 8 NFEs, whereas DPM-solver++ degrades as step count increases due to numerical instability. Against moment-matching distillation (Salimans et al., 2024), the distributional model achieves competitive FID across step counts without requiring a pre-trained teacher model or additional sampler hyperparameters, offering a simpler training pipeline. The paper also notes that even β<2 with λ=0 (no diversity term) can match or slightly beat the MSE-trained diffusion model, suggesting non-MSE losses are beneficial even without full posterior modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM framework for diffusion model sampling and the generalized scoring rule formalism of Gneiting & Raftery (2007), adapting the conditional energy score originally proposed for generative modeling by Bouchacourt et al. (2016, DISCONets) to the diffusion setting. It departs from prior work on learning the conditional posterior p_{0|t} (e.g., Xiao et al., 2022, which uses a GAN) by replacing adversarial training with a simple scoring-rule loss that interpolates continuously between standard MSE regression (β→2, λ→0) and a strictly proper distributional objective (β<2, λ=1). The paper explicitly positions itself as neither a distillation method nor a new numerical sampler, but as a training-objective modification that enables coarser time discretization at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a drop-in loss replacement that yields competitive FID at 2–16 denoising steps without requiring a pre-trained teacher model or additional sampler hyperparameters, which is appealing for settings where distillation is impractical. The λ parameter provides a useful continuous knob between distributional and regression objectives, and the finding that even β<2 with λ=0 (no diversity term) can match or slightly beat MSE suggests the field may be underexploiting non-MSE losses for diffusion training. Open questions include whether better architectures for incorporating the noise ξ (beyond channel concatenation) can close the gap at high step counts, whether the approach scales to large-scale text-to-image models, and whether combining it with modern distillation (e.g., moment-matching) yields further gains.
