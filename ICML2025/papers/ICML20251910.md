## Problem

Existing GFlowNet-based molecular generators rely on predefined fragment vocabularies as building blocks, which restricts the accessible chemical space to pockets around known fragments. Atom-based GFlowNets could in principle explore the full drug-like chemical space, but the resulting vast state space makes training prone to collapse, and prior atom-based approaches were limited to short trajectories that produce molecules too small to be drug-like. This paper asks whether a pretraining strategy using inexpensive molecular descriptors and expert offline trajectories can make atom-level GFlowNets trainable at drug-relevant molecular weights.

## Input / Output

The model takes conditional property ranges (e.g., QED ∈ [0.65, 0.80], TPSA ∈ [60, 100]) encoded via thermometer vectors, a task-specific reward function for finetuning, and optionally a Bemis-Murcko scaffold as a seed graph for lead optimization. It outputs chemically valid molecular graphs constructed atom-by-atom over a restricted set of drug-relevant heavy atoms (C, S, P, N, O, F, Cl, Br, I), with validity guaranteed by construction through valence-aware action masking. Trajectories are capped at 45 nodes and 50 edges, corresponding to molecular weights in the typical drug range of 200–600 Da.

## Method

A-GFN parameterizes forward and backward policies with a Graph Attention Network (with additive convolutional layers for counting) that produces per-node and per-edge logits over five constructive actions (addNode, addEdge, addNodeAttribute, addEdgeAttribute, stop) and their destructive counterparts. Pretraining uses a hybrid online-offline off-policy scheme: half the minibatch consists of expert trajectories deconstructed from ZINC250K molecules via the backward policy, and half are sampled online from the forward policy, with a goal-conditioned multiplicative reward over QED, TPSA, SAS, and ring count. A key regularizer combines the Trajectory Balance loss with an MLE loss over the offline dataset to balance exploration of novel chemical space against proximity to synthetically feasible (make-on-demand) libraries. For downstream finetuning, the Relative Trajectory Balance (RTB) objective treats the pretrained forward policy as a fixed prior and learns a posterior proportional to prior × task reward, which prevents the catastrophic forgetting observed when finetuning with plain TB and enables rapid adaptation to new property ranges or binding-affinity objectives.

## Results

In pretraining, A-GFN discovers 1,252 distinct high-reward modes and covers 196 unique scaffolds (n=200), compared to 0 modes and 113 scaffolds for the fragment-based GFlowNet, with diversity of 0.88 vs. 0.002. In the property-constrained optimization benchmark (logP, Preserved setting), A-GFN finetuned with RTB achieves 708 modes, RWTD of 0.793, hypervolume of 0.996, and top-100 reward of 0.980, substantially outperforming all baselines. For de novo binder generation across 5 protein targets, finetuning the hybrid A-GFN prior yields percentage improvements in hit ratio of 148.35% (fa7), 63.00% (braf), and 60.98% (jak2) over the best prior method (Saturn), with novel hit ratio gains up to 188.29% (braf).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-GFN finetuned with RTB dominates all four baselines on the property-constrained optimization benchmark: it achieves 708 high-reward modes vs. 0 (MolDQN), 139 (REINVENT), 51 (Frag GFlowNet), and 3 (Graph MCTS), with RWTD of 0.793 vs. 0.255, 0.545, 0.461, and 0.347 respectively. The top-100 reward of 0.980 for A-GFN FT w/ RTB exceeds REINVENT (0.759), Frag GFlowNet (0.536), Graph MCTS (0.404), and MolDQN (0.337) by wide margins. A-GFN trained from scratch (task-trained) collapses entirely, achieving 0 modes and rewards below 10⁻⁵, underscoring the critical role of pretraining. In de novo binder generation, the hybrid-prior A-GFN outperforms Saturn (the best prior method) on novel hit ratio across all 5 targets, with the largest gain of 188.29% on BRAF, while the offline-only prior underperforms on novelty due to RTB anchoring the policy to known scaffolds. REINVENT remains competitive on hypervolume (0.835 vs. 0.996) but is consistently outperformed on mode discovery and reward quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021) from fragment-based to atom-based molecular generation, using Trajectory Balance (Malkin et al., 2022) for training and Relative Trajectory Balance (Venkatraman et al., 2024) for finetuning. The key conceptual departure is introducing a pretrain-then-finetune paradigm to GFlowNets, borrowing the unsupervised pretraining philosophy from large-scale ML (Brown, 2020) and RL (Liu & Abbeel, 2021; Kim et al., 2024), but applying it to the GFlowNet setting with hybrid online-offline off-policy training on drug-like molecule datasets. The multi-objective reward scalarization follows Jain et al. (2023), and the goal-conditioned formulation builds on Roy et al. (2023), but the atom-level action space and the specific pretraining strategy with inexpensive molecular descriptors are novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of large-scale pretraining within the GFlowNet framework, establishing a practical pathway to make atom-based GFlowNets trainable at all (task-trained A-GFNs achieve 0 modes, while pretrained-then-finetuned versions reach 990+). The finding that RTB is essential to prevent catastrophic forgetting during finetuning—while TB finetuning loses all drug-likeness knowledge—provides a concrete recipe for GFlowNet adaptation. Open questions include scaling behavior with respect to expert data volume, model size, and pretraining duration, as well as whether the pretraining paradigm generalizes beyond molecular generation to other compositional object spaces. A notable limitation is that both finetuning and task-training struggle on complex tasks like LD50 toxicity, suggesting the current reward formulation is insufficient for harder pharmacological objectives.
