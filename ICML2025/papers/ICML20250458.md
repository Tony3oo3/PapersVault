## Problem

Information-theoretic generalization bounds for noisy iterative schemes like SGLD scale as O(T) or O(√T) with iteration count, becoming vacuous for long training runs even though the iterates converge to a Gibbs distribution with finite generalization. This paper asks whether noisy SGD in unbounded non-convex settings admits generalization and privacy bounds that remain finite (and decay as n→∞) regardless of the number of iterations, without requiring strong convexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250458/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a training dataset of n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution, a smooth non-convex loss with sub-Gaussian tails, and SGLD hyperparameters (stepsize η, temperature β, minibatch size). It produces time-uniform upper bounds on the KL and Rényi divergences between two parallel SGLD chains run on independently sampled datasets, which directly yield O(√(1/n)) generalization gaps and (ε,δ)-differential privacy guarantees. The key structural requirement is that the Gibbs distribution e^{-βF_n} satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality (isoperimetric condition), which is strictly weaker than strong convexity.

## Method

The core technique is an expansion-contraction template: each SGLD step is split into a gradient half-step (bounded expansion controlled by gradient sensitivity S_k) and a noise half-step (contraction via simultaneous Gaussian convolution). Under dissipativity, the paper establishes a uniform log-Sobolev inequality for all iterates—resolving an open question of Vempala & Wibisono (2019)—by showing dissipative gradient updates are approximately contractive, yielding sub-Gaussian iterates that Gaussian convolution upgrades to LSI via Chen et al. (2021). Without dissipativity, the paper instead exploits the regularizing effect of Gaussian convolution (log-Hessian lower bounds) to prove an approximate contraction theorem that only requires the target Gibbs distribution to satisfy LSI, combined with ergodicity in Wasserstein distance. The geometric recurrence D_q(X_{k+1}‖X'_{k+1}) ≤ γ·D_q(X_k‖X'_k) + γ·q·(βη/2)·S_k with γ<1 then yields time-independent bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes a time-independent O(√(1/n)) generalization bound for SGLD in non-convex, unbounded settings, where the KL divergence between two parallel SGLD chains satisfies D(X_k ‖ X'_k) ≤ βη(θ²M + D²)/(1−γ) · (1−γ^{k+1}), independent of the iteration count k. Under dissipativity, the uniform log-Sobolev constant is C_LSI ≤ 6C_P · 32(b + d + ηβ(LR)²) with C_P ≤ (4η/β)exp(32(b + d + ηβ(LR)²)), yielding bounded Rényi divergence and (ε,δ)-differential privacy. Without dissipativity but under ergodicity (target LSI), the contraction factor becomes γ = e^{−η/4βc_π} and the bound remains time-uniform with polynomial (rather than exponential) dimension dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The present work matches the O(1/√n) rate of Chourasia et al. (2021) and Ye & Shokri (2022), which were previously only available under strong convexity, by extending the expansion-contraction template to non-convex, unbounded settings. Compared to Futami & Fujisawa (2024), the closest prior work in the dissipative non-convex unbounded setting, the present result removes non-explicit dimension-dependent constants (C depending on d, b, m) from the bound, relying solely on stability-related quantities. Against Farghly & Rebeschini (2021), the present bound decays to zero as n→∞ for fixed step sizes, whereas their O(1/(n√η)+√η) bound does not. The Raginsky et al. (2017) bound has explicit K-dependence (ηK + exp(−ηK/c_π)), making it vacuous for large iteration counts, while the present bound is fully time-independent. Mou et al. (2018) and Wang et al. (2023)/Chien et al. (2024) are restricted to bounded domains (requiring projection steps), whereas the present work handles the unbounded case natively. The key technical advance over all prior work is establishing a uniform log-Sobolev inequality for all SGLD iterates without strong convexity, resolving an open question from Vempala & Wibisono (2019).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expansion-contraction analysis template of Chourasia et al. (2021) and Ye & Shokri (2022) from strongly convex to unbounded non-convex settings, leveraging the contraction result of Chen et al. (2022) and the LSI-stability properties of Gaussian convolution. It resolves an open question of Vempala & Wibisono (2019) by establishing uniform log-Sobolev inequalities for discrete Langevin iterates under dissipativity alone, and improves over Futami & Fujisawa (2024) by removing the dissipativity requirement in favor of a weaker ergodicity assumption while eliminating non-stability constants. The key conceptual departure from prior step-wise analyses (Pensia et al. 2018, Mou et al. 2018) is that the per-iteration expansion is shown to be geometrically dominated by the contraction, yielding time-independent bounds rather than O(T) or O(√T) divergences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a long-standing theoretical paradox where SGLD iterates had diverging generalization bounds despite converging to a Gibbs distribution with finite bounds, and establishes that early-stopping is not theoretically necessary for generalization in non-convex settings. The dissipative result matches the strongly-convex bound rate O(√(1/n)) using only stability-related constants, while the non-dissipative result trades this for polynomial dimension dependence at the cost of introducing non-stability terms. Open questions include whether the exponential-in-dimension LSI constant under dissipativity can be improved, whether the framework extends to other noisy iterative schemes (e.g., Adam with noise), and whether the pseudo-Lipschitz assumption can be further relaxed for Rényi (q>1) stability.
