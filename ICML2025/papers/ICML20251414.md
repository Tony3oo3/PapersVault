## Problem

LLM-based robotic planners struggle with closed-loop long-horizon planning due to unidirectional autoregressive dependency, lack of built-in error correction, and fixed forward processes that prevent dynamic compute allocation. Existing self-refinement approaches either rely on fragile prompting, require careful curation of process supervision, or depend on reinforcement learning with verifiers and reward models, making them complex and impractical for robotic domains where efficient verifiers are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251414/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner receives a natural language task instruction, a scene-graph-based environment description listing objects per room, and iterative feedback strings (format errors, invalid commands, execution failures, or unmet goal conditions) from either the real environment or a finetuned world model. It outputs a sequence of mid-level semantic actions drawn from a 40-action vocabulary, each paired with one or two object arguments (e.g., [PUTBACK] <plate> <sink>), with an average plan length of 10.8 actions. The draft plan is consumed and regenerated through multiple forward passes within each inner loop, and the feedback context is concatenated across outer-loop iterations rather than reset.

## Method

The paper formulates iterative self-refinement as a fixed-point problem x* = f_θ(x*, c) and trains the LLM planner via a Jacobian-free approximation of the implicit function theorem, reducing the equilibrium loss gradient to ∂L/∂θ ≈ ∂L/∂x* · ∂f_θ/∂θ. This converts training into a two-step supervised procedure: (1) solve the fixed point by iterative inference until convergence to obtain x*, then (2) finetune f_θ so that f_θ(x*, c) matches the ground-truth plan y, avoiding backpropagation through unbounded refinement steps. For closed-loop planning, a nested equilibrium solving scheme separates an inner loop (plan refinement with fixed feedback) from an outer loop (feedback update via environment or world model interaction), with previously converged equilibrium solutions reused as starting points to accelerate convergence. An equilibrium experience memory caches all intermediate fixed points for repeated sampling during training, and a lightweight world model (a finetuned LLM predicting feedback types from plan + scene context) supplies internal feedback when real environmental interaction is unavailable, with the two feedback sources alternated at a 1:1 ratio during inference.

## Results

On VirtualHome-Env with up to 10 corrections, the equilibrium planner achieves 56.45% SR and 76.63% GCR on the novel scene and task split, 77.89% SR and 87.07% GCR on the novel scene split, and 54.91% SR and 74.18% GCR on the novel task split. Without any correction, the method still achieves 40.32% SR and 65.40% GCR on the novel scene and task split, outperforming the supervised baseline (24.19% SR) by 14%. The method also demonstrates better scaling with inference-time computation than tree-based alternatives, achieving over 2× the success rate on plans longer than 20 actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tree-Planner (N=25 and N=50, both finetuned Llama 3 8B), the equilibrium planner wins on SR by 12–19% and GCR by 12–18% across all three test splits, while trading a small drop in executability (~3–4%). Against SELF-REFINE (prompting-based self-refinement on the same Llama 3 8B backbone), the method improves SR by ~13% and GCR by ~11% on the novel scene and task split, validating the equilibrium sequence modeling training objective over naive prompting. Against the GPT-3.5 API baselines (Local Replan, Global Replan, Tree-Planner), the method leads on all reported metrics by 10–25% in SR and GCR. In the no-feedback setting (Table 8), the method still outperforms SELF-REFINE by ~2% SR and ~8% GCR, confirming that the inner-loop introspection alone provides meaningful self-correction without any external signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the deep equilibrium model framework (Bai et al., 2019) and its Jacobian-free backpropagation approximation (Fung et al., 2022) from vision and image generation to the self-refinement process in LLM planners. While prior self-refinement methods for LLMs (Welleck et al., 2023; Madaan et al., 2023) relied on prompting or reinforcement learning with curated verifiers, this work reframes iterative plan refinement as a fixed-point problem whose gradient is obtained analytically via the implicit function theorem, reducing training to standard supervised finetuning. The nested equilibrium solving scheme with a world model for internal feedback extends the single-loop equilibrium formulation to closed-loop robotic planning, departing from tree-search-based alternatives like Tree-Planner (Hu et al., 2024) that require explicit backtracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a training recipe for self-refining planners that avoids the complexity of RL, process supervision, or handcrafted reward models—making it accessible to domains where efficient verifiers are unavailable, such as open-ended robotic planning. However, the evaluation is confined to VirtualHome-Env with Llama 3 8B, and the method requires ground-truth action sequences during training, limiting its applicability to domains without such annotations. Open questions include whether the equilibrium formulation scales to larger models or more complex multi-agent settings, whether visual inputs can be incorporated, and whether the fixed-point convergence properties hold under more stochastic sampling strategies. The paper also acknowledges that the current formulation ignores implicit reasoning steps, leaving a gap for combining planning with chain-of-thought reasoning.
