## Problem

Existing safe control methods (CBFs, probabilistic filtering, neural safety certificates) treat sensor measurements as external observations or bounded disturbances rather than as integral geometric components of the system. When constraint information is only locally available and uncertain—as with range sensors, force sensors, or depth cameras—these approaches either require global constraint knowledge or fail to exploit the geometric structure that local measurements inherently encode, limiting both learning efficiency and safety guarantees under realistic sensing conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253151/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes local sensor measurements (e.g., depth readings, force signals) with bounded uncertainty that scales with distance, the system state on a smooth manifold, and control inputs, and produces safe control actions, a learned continuous-time dynamics model (Neural ODE), and a measurement-aware safety certificate whose conservative margin automatically tightens or relaxes based on local measurement quality. A distinctive aspect is that the measurement uncertainty bounds (e.g., ||v_i|| ≤ α||x - x_i||) directly define the fiber geometry of the bundle, so the input sensing configuration shapes the entire geometric structure rather than merely perturbing a fixed model.

## Method

The core contribution is a fiber bundle E = M × Y over the state manifold M, where the fiber at each state x contains all admissible measurements y = h(x) + v with ||v|| ≤ δ_v, and a connection ∇ couples state and measurement dynamics through a feedback gain operator K(x). Measurement-adapted Control Barrier Functions (mCBFs) are defined as smooth functions on the total space E satisfying invariance under the bundle connection and Lipschitz continuity in the measurement direction, yielding probabilistic safety guarantees that degrade gracefully as 1 − exp(−c/δ_v²). Neural ODEs learn continuous-time dynamics on the bundle via bundle-valued learning operators that incorporate uncertainty-weighted residuals, while Soft Actor-Critic provides the policy backbone with safety-constrained parameter updates projected onto the safe policy set. Compatible Lie group actions enable dimension reduction while preserving safety through quotient-space dynamics, and the overall framework is validated across a soft-body worm robot (MPM), a 7-DOF Franka arm, and a quadrotor, achieving 96.3% success rate with 99.3% constraint satisfaction.

## Results

Across three robotic tasks (soft-body worm navigation, 7-DOF Franka arm manipulation, and quadrotor 3D navigation), the proposed measurement-induced bundle framework achieves a 96.3% success rate with 18.5 m average path length, 99.3% constraint satisfaction rate, and 0.17 average control magnitude. The closest baselines (DataFilter at 89.7% SR and AdaptSafe at 88.3% SR) trail by 6.6 and 8.0 percentage points respectively, while uncertainty-aware MPC methods (GPMPC, ALMPC) achieve comparable constraint satisfaction (99.0–99.7%) but produce substantially longer paths (26.2–26.9 m) and lower success rates (67.7–71.7%). Ablation confirms the mCBF component is the most critical: removing it drops success rate to 45.7% and constraint satisfaction to 72.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines on success rate, with the largest margins over physics-informed methods (PNDS 73.3%, GeoPath 74.0%, GEM 76.3%) and uncertainty-aware MPC approaches (GPMPC 67.7%, ALMPC 71.7%, SafeRL 70.3%, RobustSafe 70.0%). The closest competitors are DataFilter (89.7% SR) and AdaptSafe (88.3% SR), which also achieve 99.3% constraint satisfaction but with longer paths (21.7–21.8 m vs. 18.5 m) and higher control magnitudes (0.20–0.22 vs. 0.17). GPMPC marginally exceeds the proposed method on constraint satisfaction rate (99.7% vs. 99.3%) but at the cost of the lowest success rate (67.7%) and longest paths (26.9 m), reflecting its overly conservative trajectory planning. Learning-based safety methods (Neural-CBF, SafeLearn, SafetyNet, SafeTrack, StructCBF) cluster in the 75.7–86.7% SR range, confirming that fixed or learned barrier functions without explicit measurement-uncertainty modeling limit adaptability to novel obstacle configurations. The ablation study further shows that the mCBF component is the most critical architectural element: removing it collapses success rate to 45.7% and constraint satisfaction to 72.8%, while removing the fiber bundle structure alone reduces SR to 62.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—Control Barrier Functions (Ames et al., 2016), Neural ODEs for dynamics learning (Chen et al., 2018), and Ehresmann's fiber bundle geometry—into a single framework where measurement uncertainty is treated as an intrinsic geometric property rather than an external disturbance. It departs from prior CBF-based learning methods (Neural-CBF, SafeLearn, AdaptSafe) that assume perfect or bounded measurements by encoding the measurement-state relationship as a fiber bundle connection, and from geometric deep learning approaches (equivariant networks, Riemannian motion policies) by making safety guarantees the primary objective rather than representation quality. The measurement-adapted CBF (mCBF) is the key new object: a barrier function defined on the total bundle space whose Lipschitz dependence on the fiber coordinate yields probabilistic safety bounds that degrade gracefully with sensor noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled geometric language for the common practical problem of maintaining safety when sensors are noisy, local, and configuration-dependent—something that ad hoc robust CBF or tube-MPC approaches handle only approximately. The ablation revealing that mCBF removal causes the largest performance drop (96.3%→45.7% SR) suggests the safety certificate, not the learned dynamics, is the critical component, which reframes where effort should be invested in safe-learning pipelines. Key limitations include simulation-only validation (Genesis engine), smoothness assumptions on the state manifold, and the explicit avoidance of comparison with geometric deep learning baselines (Appendix F), leaving open whether the geometric framing yields advantages beyond what equivariant or Riemannian architectures already provide. Future work on multi-agent coupled uncertainties, data-driven fiber structure learning, and real-robot deployment would be needed to assess practical impact beyond the controlled simulation setting.
