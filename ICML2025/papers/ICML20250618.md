## Problem

While memorization in diffusion models has been extensively studied for image and text generation, its occurrence and impact in tabular data generation remain largely unexplored. This paper asks whether tabular diffusion models (e.g., TabSyn, TabDDPM) inadvertently replicate training samples, and if so, how this can be effectively mitigated without degrading synthetic data quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes mixed-type tabular data (numerical and categorical columns) and operates on pairs of same-class training samples, where 'same class' means sharing the same categorical target label. A custom mixed distance metric combines max-min-normalized L2 distance for numerical features with Hamming distance for categorical features to compute nearest-neighbor ratios. The output is an augmented training set (original samples plus feature-swapped composites) used to train diffusion models, which then produce synthetic tabular data of equal size to the training set for evaluation.

## Method

TabCutMix generates augmented samples by applying a binary mask M (each element drawn from Bernoulli(λ), λ~Unif(0,1)) to swap randomly selected feature segments between two same-class samples, preserving the class label. TabCutMixPlus enhances this by first clustering features via hierarchical clustering using type-appropriate correlation measures (Pearson for numerical-numerical, Cramér's V for categorical-categorical, squared ETA for mixed pairs), then performing swaps only within clusters to maintain feature coherence and reduce out-of-distribution generation. The paper also provides a theoretical analysis showing that under perfect score matching and an ideal SDE solver, the generated latent sample exactly replicates a training sample's embedding, explaining why memorization is an inherent risk. Memorization is quantified using a relative distance ratio criterion: a generated sample is flagged as memorized if its distance to the nearest training neighbor is less than 1/3 of the distance to the second-nearest neighbor.

## Results

Across three SOTA tabular diffusion models (TabSyn, TabDDPM, STaSy) and four datasets, TabCutMix reduces memorization ratio by 7.6%–19.7% relative (e.g., 16.16%↓ for TabSyn on Default, 19.71%↓ for STaSy on Adult), substantially outperforming Mixup (2.6%–4.5%↓) and SMOTE (4.5%–15.1%↓). TabCutMixPlus achieves slightly less memorization reduction (6.3%–17.6%↓) but preserves data quality more effectively: on TabSyn/Default, TCMP attains 77.17% MLE, 97.61% α-Precision, and 96.30% Trend Score versus 76.84%, 96.16%, and 96.51% for TCM. SMOTE causes severe quality degradation in some settings (e.g., Trend Score drops from 96.82% to 32.70% for TabSyn on Default), while TabCutMix/Plus maintain near-baseline quality across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabCutMix consistently outperforms both Mixup and SMOTE on memorization reduction across all model–dataset combinations: on TabSyn/Default it achieves a 16.16% relative reduction versus only 2.65% for Mixup and 6.93% for SMOTE; on STaSy/Adult it reaches 19.71% versus 4.37% (Mixup) and 11.91% (SMOTE). Mixup is the weakest baseline, sometimes even increasing memorization (e.g., STaSy+Mixup on Default shows −1.80% improvement, i.e., a slight increase). SMOTE provides moderate memorization reduction but severely degrades data quality in several settings—most notably Trend Score collapses from 96.82% to 32.70% (TabSyn/Default) and from 97.78% to 90.76% (TabDDPM/Adult), indicating it disrupts inter-feature correlations. TabCutMixPlus trades a small amount of memorization reduction (e.g., 12.48% vs. 16.16% on TabSyn/Default) for substantially better data utility: it recovers MLE (77.17% vs. 76.84%), α-Precision (97.61% vs. 96.16%), and β-Recall (44.46% vs. 40.69%) relative to TabCutMix, while keeping Trend Score near baseline (96.30% vs. 96.51%). Overall, TabCutMix is the best choice when memorization reduction is the sole priority, while TabCutMixPlus offers the best balance between privacy (memorization) and downstream data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization analysis framework from image and text diffusion models (Gu et al., 2023; Somepalli et al., 2023) to the tabular data domain, where the mixed-type (numerical + categorical) feature space requires a novel distance metric (Eq. 1) rather than the standard L2 norm. Methodologically, TabCutMix adapts the binary-mask feature-swapping mechanism of CutMix (Yun et al., 2019) from spatial image regions to tabular feature columns, while TabCutMixPlus introduces correlation-based hierarchical clustering (Pearson, Cramér's V, ETA) to preserve inter-feature coherence. The theoretical analysis directly ports the denoising score-matching memorization proof from Gu et al. (2023) to the latent diffusion setting of TabSyn, showing that perfect score recovery implies exact training-sample replication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic study of memorization in tabular diffusion models, this work establishes a quantitative detection criterion and demonstrates that memorization is prevalent (up to 80% on Magic) and scales with training epochs, raising concrete privacy concerns for sensitive domains like healthcare and finance. The practical limitation is that memorization reduction is moderate (6–19% relative improvement) and the evaluation covers only three diffusion architectures and four primary datasets, leaving open whether the findings generalize to other generative paradigms (GANs, VAEs, flow-based) or to very high-dimensional tabular settings. The OOD trade-off revealed by TabCutMix (39.47% OOD ratio on Default) and its partial mitigation via TabCutMixPlus highlights an unresolved tension between memorization reduction and distributional fidelity that future work must address more systematically.
