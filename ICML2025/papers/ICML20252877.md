## Problem

Existing RL post-training for LLMs relies on static, pre-curated prompt sets sampled in fixed schedules, regardless of their usefulness to the current training state. This creates two bottlenecks: training is inefficient because not all prompts contribute equally at every stage, and learning stops once the model saturates on the fixed distribution, preventing acquisition of new skills. The paper asks which prompts should be prioritized during RL and how to generatively create new useful prompts to sustain self-improvement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base instruction-tuned LLM (Gemma-2-9B-IT), a seed prompt set (10K prompts from UltraFeedback per iteration), and a fixed reward model (ARMORM-8B pointwise or pairwise variants) as inputs. It produces an improved LLM policy and a new evolved prompt distribution that can seed the next iteration. The solver step is algorithm-agnostic, accepting any preference optimization loss (DPO, RLOO, SPPO, SimPO, ORPO, OAIF) as a plug-in, while the creator step uses a separate generative model (Gemini-1.5-Pro or the model itself) to rewrite prompts via in-depth and in-breadth mutation templates.

## Method

eva (Evolving via Asymmetric Self-Play) casts RL post-training as a Stackelberg game between a creator policy π_φ that generates training prompts and a solver policy π_θ that generates responses, with the creator maximizing minimax regret and the solver minimizing it. The practical algorithm iterates three steps: (1) estimate each prompt's informativeness as the reward advantage (best-response reward minus baseline reward, using argmin for offline and argavg for online settings), (2) weighted-sample a 25% informative subset proportional to informativeness, and (3) evolve new prompts by applying mutation strategies (in-depth: adding constraints, deepening, concretizing, reasoning steps; in-breadth: persona shifts, domain mixing, abstraction) to the sampled subset. The solver then trains on a mixture of 80% evolved and 20% original prompts using standard preference optimization. For online RL, a prioritized generative buffer of fixed size (3200 prompts) maintains on-policy evolution per mini-batch through warm-up, mix-up, and bootstrap phases, enabling continual self-improvement beyond the initial human prompt set.

## Results

On Arena-Hard (the most challenging benchmark), eva improves Gemma-2-9B-IT's win-rate from 41.3% (base) to 60.1% with offline DPO (+8.5 pp) and to 62.4% with online RLOO at 3× prompts (+9.8 pp), surpassing claude-3-opus and nearing gemini-1.5-pro. Gains are consistent across all six RLHF algorithms tested: DPO (+8.5%), SPPO (+3.2%), SimPO (+8.4%), ORPO (+5.5%), RLOO (+9.8%), and OAIF (+9.6%). Notably, eva with 1× human prompts outperforms training with additional new human prompts on Arena-Hard (e.g., 60.1% vs. 59.8% for DPO), demonstrating that adaptive prompt evolution is more effective than simply scaling data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eva is evaluated as a plug-in on top of six existing RLHF algorithms, all of which serve as baselines. On Arena-Hard, eva+DPO reaches 60.1% vs. DPO's 51.6% (+8.5 pp), eva+SimPO reaches 60.7% vs. SimPO's 52.3% (+8.4 pp), eva+ORPO reaches 60.3% vs. ORPO's 54.8% (+5.5 pp), and eva+SPPO reaches 58.9% vs. SPPO's 55.7% (+3.2 pp). In the online setting, eva+RLOO at 3× prompts achieves 62.4% vs. RLOO's 52.6% (+9.8 pp), and eva+OAIF at 3× reaches 61.7% vs. OAIF's 52.1% (+9.6 pp). A key finding is that eva with only 1× human prompts consistently outperforms training with additional new human prompts on Arena-Hard across all four offline methods (e.g., 60.1% vs. 59.8% for DPO; 60.7% vs. 54.6% for SimPO), indicating that adaptive prompt evolution is more effective than naive data scaling. The largest relative gains appear with SimPO (+8.4 pp) and DPO (+8.5 pp), while SPPO shows the smallest gain (+3.2 pp), possibly because SPPO already incorporates some self-play dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

eva revives the classical asymmetric self-play paradigm (Sukhbaatar et al., 2017; Dennis et al., 2020) and regret-based environment design (Parker-Holder et al., 2022) in the context of LLM RL post-training, where prior work has been restricted to static, pre-curated prompt distributions. It departs from prompt-synthesis methods (EvolInstruct, Self-Instruct, WizardLM) that operate in SFT and evolve prompts uniformly, by introducing a reward-advantage-driven prioritization signal that selects which prompts to evolve during RL. The solver step is agnostic to the specific preference-optimization algorithm (DPO, RLOO, SPPO, SimPO, ORPO, OAIF), positioning eva as a plug-in layer on top of existing RLHF pipelines rather than a new optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 1× evolved prompts match or exceed 6× new human prompts on Arena-Hard suggests a significant data-efficiency gain for RL post-training, and the fact that this holds across six different RLHF algorithms without modification makes the technique broadly applicable. Key open questions include: (i) the fixed oracle reward model assumption becomes problematic as evolved prompts drift out-of-distribution, necessitating co-adaptive RM training; (ii) the creator policy is currently non-differentiable (heuristic evolve step), leaving room for learned prompt generators; and (iii) all results are on a single 9B model and single training dataset, so generality across model scales and domains remains untested. The paper also raises the broader question of whether self-generated curricula can substitute for human-curated data in scaling RL post-training.
