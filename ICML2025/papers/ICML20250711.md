## Problem

Prevalent link prediction research assumes abundant observed links (typically <15% unobserved), yet real-world networks such as protein interactomes have 89%–98% of links unverified. This paper tackles the data-scarce regime by exploiting hierarchical modularity—a structural prior that persists even under sparse observation—to reduce the sample complexity of link prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250711/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with a subset of observed edges and produces node embeddings as integer vectors in a discrete complete-tree (CT) space, where each coordinate corresponds to a leaf position in a complete b-ary tree (typically b=2, so each dimension is an integer index). Link prediction scores are then computed from the CT-space distance between node pairs via a strictly decreasing probability function that incorporates node degrees and a temperature parameter γ. Because the CT space is discrete, standard continuous-coordinate representations are replaced by integer leaf indices, enabling bit-operation acceleration for distance computation.

## Method

The paper introduces the complete-tree (CT) space, a discrete metric space where the distance between two leaf nodes equals the height of their lowest common ancestor in a complete b-ary tree, thereby encoding hierarchical permutation symmetry. Using a newly defined submetry group (a local analogue of the isometry group), the authors prove via PAC learning that the CT space yields a significantly lower sample complexity bound than Euclidean space, while a subspace approximation theorem guarantees expressivity for arbitrary latent tree structures. For optimization, since the CT space is discrete and gradients are undefined, leaf matching converts the embedding problem to an averaged greedy navigation over a Kleinberg-style small-world computation graph, where each node's representation is iteratively moved to the neighboring leaf that minimizes the link-prediction loss. This yields a scalable O(m_v log_b³(n_l) d) training procedure with polylogarithmic convergence epochs.

## Results

On the large-scale ogbl-collab dataset (Hit@100) and ogbl-ppa dataset (Hit@50) with extreme data scarcity (μ=0.02), leaf matching achieves 15.60±0.24% and 0.55±0.04% respectively, outperforming the SOTA graph transformer LPformer (15.55±1.63% and 0.27±0.03% with node features) without using any node features. In the data-efficiency experiment across Cora, Pubmed, and Citeseer, leaf matching consistently outperforms node2vec (Euclidean), Poincaré (hyperbolic), and node2ket (Hilbert) embeddings across all link observability levels (μ from 0.9 to 0.1), with the performance gap widening as data becomes scarcer. The convergence epoch scales sub-linearly with log(number of nodes), confirming the theoretical O(log_b(n_l)) convergence guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Leaf matching outperforms the SOTA graph transformer LPformer on both large-scale datasets without using node features: 15.60% vs. 15.55% (Hit@100) on ogbl-collab and 0.55% vs. 0.27% (Hit@50) on ogbl-ppa, with notably lower variance. Against GNN baselines, the gap is dramatic: SEAL achieves only 5.26% on Collab (and OOMs on PPA), while GAE scores near zero (0.04% and 0.00%). Among heuristic methods, the Katz Index (6.76% on Collab) is the strongest but still trails leaf matching by nearly 9 percentage points. In the data-efficiency comparison (Figure 7), leaf matching beats node2vec (Euclidean), Poincaré (hyperbolic), and node2ket (Hilbert) across all link observability levels on Cora, Pubmed, and Citeseer, with the advantage over node2vec growing substantially as μ drops from 0.9 to 0.1, empirically confirming the theoretical sample complexity advantage of the CT space over Euclidean space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard network embedding paradigm (node2vec in Euclidean space, Poincaré in hyperbolic space, node2ket in Hilbert space) by introducing a fundamentally different discrete metric space—the complete-tree space—designed to encode hierarchical modularity as an inductive prior. The optimization strategy builds directly on Kleinberg's small-world greedy navigation guarantees, reducing discrete embedding optimization to decentralized search on a generated computation graph. The link probability formula is adapted from Krioukov et al. and Jankowski et al., with a log-degree correction for the CT space's exponentially growing neighborhoods. Unlike prior tree-based approaches (HBDM, Clauset et al.) where tree structure is data-driven, the complete tree here is pre-determined, eliminating tree-selection search and shrinking the hypothesis space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most link prediction benchmarks assume <15% unobserved links, while real networks (protein interactomes at 89–98% unverified, large social networks) operate in extreme data scarcity where sample complexity dominates. The submetry-group framework for quantifying local permutation symmetry and deriving PAC-style sample complexity bounds is a novel theoretical tool that could generalize beyond link prediction to other structured prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the hierarchical modularity assumption holds universally (e.g., for non-hierarchical or flat networks), how the method extends to dynamic or attributed graphs, and whether the discrete CT space can be integrated into end-to-end differentiable pipelines for downstream tasks beyond link prediction.
