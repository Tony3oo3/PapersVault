## Problem

Existing LLM evaluations in social networking services focus on isolated tasks (e.g., misinformation detection, rumor analysis) without a systematic framework, standardized metrics, or datasets that capture the full diversity of social media interactions including emotional understanding, cultural context, and multi-user engagement. This paper addresses the absence of a comprehensive benchmark that defines, builds, and assesses LLM capabilities across the full spectrum of SNS-related tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250821/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance provides SNS note text (title, body, tags, comments), a search query, candidate category or tag lists, or a user question, with average instruction length of 793 words. Outputs are task-specific: single/multiple-choice category labels, 5-level relevance scores (−1 to 3), extracted entity lists, generated search queries, highlight word lists, reading comprehension answer lists, and binary yes/no judgments, with average answer length of 37 words. The 6,658 questions span subjective, single-choice, and multiple-choice formats drawn from a platform with over 300M users.

## Method

SNS-BENCH organizes 8 tasks under four capability dimensions—note comprehension, information retrieval, sentiment/intent analysis, and personalized recommendation—each with a task-specific metric (accuracy, F1, macro-F1, ANLS + Sentence-BERT semantic similarity, BLEU/ROUGE, or success-ratio-weighted composites). Data is curated through a five-step pipeline: collection from a 300M+ user platform, OCR-based preprocessing and filtering, manual annotation by 20 annotators (with auxiliary NER and FastText models for NER and highlight-word tasks), GPT-4-based quality scoring with FairEval positional-bias elimination, and expert review by 20 reviewers using majority-vote. The benchmark is evaluated on 25+ open- and closed-source LLMs (1.5B to 72B+ parameters) using the OpenCompass framework on 128 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, with uniform prompts across all models.

## Results

On SNS-BENCH (6,658 questions across 8 tasks), Claude-3.5-Sonnet achieves the highest average score of 61.98%, closely followed by GPT-4o-2024-11-20 (61.35%) and DeepSeek-V3 (61.90%, top open-source). Among open-source models, Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct reaches 61.27%, while smaller models like Qwen-2.5-1.5B score only 24.95%. The hardest tasks are Note-MRC (Complex) and Note-QueryGen, where even top models score below 50%, whereas Note-Gender and Note-Hashtag (Single-Choice) yield scores above 85% for leading models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source models consistently outperform open-source counterparts, with Claude-3.5-Sonnet (61.98%) edging out GPT-4o-2024-11-20 (61.35%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (61.23%). However, the gap between the top closed-source model (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) and the top open-source model (DeepSeek-V3, 61.90%) is negligible at only ~0.1%, indicating open-source models have nearly closed the performance gap. Among open-source models, DeepSeek-V3 leads across most tasks, particularly Note-MRC (Complex) at 60.97% and Note-Taxonomy at 67.27%, while Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct (61.27%) is a close second. Claude-3.5-Sonnet excels in Note-Hashtag (87.73%) and Note-NER (93.26%), whereas DeepSeek-V3 dominates Note-MRC (Complex) and Note-Taxonomy. All models struggle significantly on Note-QueryGen (best: 61.98% by Claude) and Note-MRC (Complex) (best: 60.97% by DeepSeek-V3), revealing that multi-step reasoning and novel query generation remain the hardest SNS capabilities for current LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first comprehensive multi-task benchmark specifically for evaluating LLMs in Social Networking Services, departing from prior work that addressed isolated SNS tasks (misinformation detection in MM-SOC, rumor detection, mental health signal extraction) or general tool-use benchmarks (API-Bank, Gorilla, ToolLLM) that overlook multi-user interaction dynamics. It repurposes well-established NLP evaluation tasks—classification, NER, reading comprehension, query generation—into a unified SNS-specific framework with domain-tailored metrics (ANLS, success ratio, macro-F1) and data sourced from a platform with over 300M users. The construction pipeline (GPT-4 quality scoring, FairEval for positional bias, 20-expert human validation) follows standard benchmark methodology but applies it to the underexplored SNS domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNS-BENCH provides the first systematic, reproducible evaluation of how well LLMs handle the full spectrum of social media interactions, revealing that even top models (Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o) plateau around 62% average and struggle disproportionately with complex multi-step reasoning (Note-MRC Complex) and novel query generation. The ~1% gap between the best closed-source and open-source models suggests the field is approaching a practical ceiling for current architectures on SNS tasks. Key limitations include text-only evaluation (no multimodal content), predominantly single-platform data that may not generalize globally, and metrics that may not fully capture cultural and contextual nuances of social communication. Open questions include whether multimodal SNS evaluation changes the difficulty landscape and whether benchmark performance translates to real-world deployment effectiveness.
