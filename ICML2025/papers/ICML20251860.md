## Problem

Diffusion models produce diverse, novel samples despite being trained to approximate a denoising objective whose optimal solution would only reproduce training data exactly. While prior work has observed that different architectures produce similar samples, the mechanistic reason for this shared generalization behaviour—specifically, what inductive bias causes network denoisers to deviate from the optimal empirical denoiser in consistent, architecture-independent ways—remains unexplained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251860/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy image z ∈ R^d at diffusion time t and the full training dataset D (no learned parameters), and produces a denoised image estimate E[x|z,t] of the same spatial resolution as the input (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for FFHQ/AFHQ). Unlike neural denoisers, PSPC is entirely training-free: it computes empirical patch posterior means directly from D using nearest-neighbour score estimators, then composites them spatially. For sampling, the denoiser is iterated along a PF-ODE trajectory from a Gaussian prior to produce a full generated image.

## Method

The paper identifies a shared local inductive bias in network denoisers by showing that gradient sensitivity heatmaps are concentrated in small spatial neighbourhoods around each output pixel, with the neighbourhood size inversely correlated with diffusion time t. Exploiting this, the proposed Patch Set Posterior Composite (PSPC) denoiser decomposes z into spatial patches via cropping matrices, computes an empirical posterior mean for each patch (a weighted average of dataset patches by their patch-level posterior probability), and composites the results into a full image normalized by patch overlap. Two variants are introduced: PSPC-Square uses fixed square patches with a time-varying size schedule, while PSPC-Flex uses adaptive, non-square patches constructed greedily from precomputed gradient sensitivity maps to better match the network's actual receptive field shape. The entire pipeline requires no training—only the dataset and a set of cropping matrices—yet produces denoiser outputs and PF-ODE samples that are substantially more similar to neural network outputs than the optimal denoiser, CFDM, or Gaussian baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PSPC-Flex achieves an average SSCD cosine similarity of 0.55 to network denoiser samples (NCSN++, DiT, DDPM++, U-ViT), substantially exceeding the optimal denoiser (0.34), Gaussian denoiser (0.34), and CFDM (0.23). This approaches the 0.6 threshold used by Zhang et al. (2023) to flag sample copying. In MSE comparisons against the DDPM++ network denoiser across forward and reverse processes on CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and AFHQv2, PSPC-Flex consistently achieves the lowest error among all non-network denoisers, outperforming the Gaussian denoiser particularly in the critical t ∈ [0.3, 3] range where network denoisers deviate most from the optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSPC-Flex outperforms all three non-network empirical baselines (optimal, CFDM, Gaussian) on both MSE against network denoisers and SSCD sample similarity across all three datasets. Against the optimal denoiser, PSPC-Flex achieves 0.55 vs. 0.34 average SSCD similarity to networks; against CFDM, 0.55 vs. 0.23; and against the Gaussian denoiser, 0.55 vs. 0.34. PSPC-Square is consistently second-best among non-network methods (0.46–0.51 similarity), confirming that adaptive non-square patches (PSPC-Flex) provide meaningful gains over fixed square patches. However, PSPC-Flex still lags behind actual network denoisers, which achieve 0.72–0.90 mutual similarity; the gap between PSPC-Flex (0.55) and the closest network (DiT at 0.59) is notable. The paper notes that PSPC-Flex samples, while structurally similar to network samples, suffer from compounding errors over the sampling trajectory that produce visible artifacts in final outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the observation (Zhang et al., 2023; Niedoba et al., 2024) that diffusion model denoisers deviate from the optimal empirical denoiser only at intermediate t, and that these deviations are qualitatively similar across architectures. It departs from prior mechanistic explanations (Kadkhodaie et al.'s harmonic bases, Scarvelis et al.'s spectral bias, Wang & Vastola's Gaussian approximation) by identifying a *local* inductive bias through gradient sensitivity analysis and showing that simple patch posterior composites (PSPC) replicate network behavior without any training. The concurrent work of Kamb & Ganguli (2024) proposes a similar patch-based denoiser, but PSPC extends the analysis to Transformer architectures (DiT, U-ViT) and introduces adaptive, non-square patches derived from gradient maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a fully training-free patch-based method more closely matches neural denoisers than the theoretically optimal denoiser does provides a concrete mechanistic account of diffusion generalization, with direct implications for attribution (patch posterior probabilities identify which training images influence a sample) and for training-free generation. However, PSPC samples still suffer from compounding errors that degrade final quality relative to DDPM++, and the method requires dataset-specific patch schedules tuned per dataset. Open questions include whether the gap between PSPC and network quality can be closed, whether the local inductive bias persists at higher resolutions (256×256+), and whether the mechanism extends to conditional or video diffusion settings.
