## Problem

Standard supervised learning assumes labels are low-dimensional and simpler than inputs, directing model capacity toward feature extraction rather than label prediction. However, modern tasks increasingly involve complex, high-dimensional labels (dense segmentation maps, full sentences, structured physical states), creating a predictive bottleneck where the feature-to-label transformation itself becomes the hard part. The paper asks whether label information can be repurposed as an additional learning input rather than serving solely as a prediction target.

## Input / Output

The model receives two inputs: the original data (image, graph node/edge features, or text hidden states) and a noise-perturbed version of the target label at a sampled timestep t, along with a sinusoidal timestep embedding. For continuous labels (N-body positions), Gaussian noise is added; for categorical labels (segmentation pixels, tokens), multinomial/categorical noise is applied. At inference, the label hint is initialized as pure noise and refined over multiple denoise-renoise steps, with the number of steps trading off runtime against prediction quality.

## Method

PCL defines a predictive consistency mapping f_θ(x, y_t, t) → y that recovers the true label from data input x and a noisy label hint y_t at noise level t. The training loss (Eq. 8) combines three terms: two task-specific losses (e.g., MSE or cross-entropy) measuring how well f_θ recovers y from two independently sampled noise levels t₁ and t₂ = αt₁, plus a consistency loss enforcing f_θ(x, y_{t₁}, t₁) ≈ f_θ(x, y_{t₂}, t₂), so all noise trajectories converge to the same target. This decomposes I(X_E; Y) into progressive increments I(X_E; Y | Y_t) + I(X_E; Y_t), letting the model learn partial label details at high noise and refine at low noise simultaneously. At inference, multi-step prediction iteratively denoises from pure noise, re-noises the intermediate prediction at a lower timestep, and repeats, progressively recovering finer label structure.

## Results

On constrained N-body simulation, PCL consistently reduces prediction error (×10⁻²) across all five task complexities and three GNN backbones; for example, GCN on the (0,10,0) configuration drops from 4.705 (SL) to 3.162 (PCL), a 33% reduction. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, ResNet50dilated+PPM improves mIoU from 41.49 to 48.56 and Score from 60.24 to 65.45 under PCL. For LLaMA-2-7B full fine-tuning on Alpaca-cleaned, PCL outperforms SL on all three INSTRUCTEVAL benchmarks: MMLU 47.10 vs. 46.22, CRASS 59.48 vs. 58.29, and BBH 34.75 vs. 33.38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is conventional supervised learning (SL) on identical network backbones, since PCL is proposed as a training paradigm rather than an architecture. PCL wins on every configuration tested: across all 15 backbone×task combinations in the N-body simulation, PCL achieves lower MSE than SL, with the largest gains on the most complex tasks (e.g., 33% error reduction for GCN on 0,10,0; 26% for GAT on 5,3,3). On semantic segmentation, PCL improves mIoU by 2.14 points (MobileNetV2) and 7.07 points (ResNet50+PPM) over SL. For LLM fine-tuning, PCL yields consistent but modest gains over SL (0.88 on MMLU, 1.19 on CRASS, 1.37 on BBH), suggesting the benefit is smaller when the label (next token) is relatively low-dimensional per step. Supplementary comparisons show PCL also outperforms label smoothing (best β=0.3: 3.256 vs. PCL's 2.795) and SL bagging ensembles (5-pass: 3.065 vs. PCL's 2.786) on the N-body task, confirming the gains are not merely from additional forward passes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the consistency mapping framework from generative consistency models (Song et al., 2023; Song & Dhariwal, 2024) for supervised prediction, inverting the direction of the noising process: instead of denoising data toward a distribution, it denoises labels toward a fixed target conditioned on the input. The key conceptual departure is that the diffusion/consistency machinery is applied to the label space rather than the data space, and the self-consistency constraint is replaced by a prediction-consistency constraint that anchors all trajectories to the ground-truth label. This positions PCL as an alternative to VAE-based latent-space prediction (Rombach et al., 2022; Hottung et al., 2021), which requires learning invertible label encoders, and to label-modification techniques (label smoothing, mixup, focal loss) that only alter the loss rather than the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCL offers an architecture- and loss-agnostic training paradigm that addresses a real bottleneck in complex-label prediction (segmentation, sequence generation, structured outputs) where the feature-to-label transformation is as hard as feature extraction. The multi-step inference provides a practical compute-accuracy tradeoff, and the noise-level parameter t gives interpretable control over prediction granularity (coarse structure vs. fine boundaries). However, the 2× training cost, the non-monotonic error accumulation in multi-step inference, and the modest gains on LLM fine-tuning (MMLU +0.88) temper the practical impact. Open questions include extending PCL to semi-supervised and weakly supervised settings, designing more natural label noising schedules, and whether sentence-level or structural hints could further improve NLP performance.
