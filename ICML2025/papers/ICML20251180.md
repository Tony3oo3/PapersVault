## Problem

Tensor parallelism (TP) for distributed LLM inference requires all-reduce sync-points after every self-attention output to maintain numerical parity across devices, creating a communication bottleneck that worsens as models scale to more GPUs. Existing work focuses on optimizing the communication operation itself (e.g., ring or tree all-reduce topologies) rather than eliminating sync-points, leaving a gap in directly reducing the number of synchronization barriers within the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA2 7B/13B/70B, OPT 6.7B/13B/30B/66B) deployed across 4 or 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, taking standard input text tokens and producing generated text tokens as in normal inference. The key system-level detail is that SPD modifies the decoder block structure in-place (removing the post-attention all-reduce) and optionally fine-tunes individual block parameters via block-to-block distillation using 128 calibration samples from WikiText2 (sequence length 2048), without altering the overall model architecture or the inference input/output interface.

## Method

SPD selectively removes the all-reduce sync-point after self-attention output in TP decoder blocks, requiring a redesigned block structure that disassembles the original residual connection into a block-input residual (added after the remaining MLP all-reduce) and a per-device attention-output residual (added before it), so the final output still reconstructs the full sum across devices. To minimize accuracy loss, the paper first identifies per-block sync sensitivity via progressive perplexity measurement, classifying blocks into in-sensitive (ISB), sensitive (SB), and extremely sensitive (ESB) categories. ISBs undergo zero-shot dropping; SBs receive block-to-block distillation where a TP block acts as teacher and the SPD block as student under MSE loss; ESBs additionally receive an attention head grouping initialization that scatters heads with diverse attention-score profiles evenly across devices and matches each head group to the MLP partition whose output norm best complements the residual, before distillation. This multi-tiered strategy achieves ~20% latency reduction with <1% accuracy regression on LLaMA2-70B over 8 GPUs.

## Results

For LLaMA2-70B on 8 GPUs with low-bandwidth (10 GB/s) interconnect, 70% SPD via zero-shot dropping alone achieves ~19.7% TTFT speedup with only 0.94% accuracy loss on zero-shot tasks. At 100% SPD, data transfer latency is reduced by over 46% across all system configurations, and speedup exceeds 20% for LLaMA2-13B and 70B under low-bandwidth settings. In 2-node (4+4 GPU) setups, SPD yields over 10% speedup at SPD percentages above 70% for both high- and low-bandwidth interconnects. Zero-shot dropping alone can safely remove sync-points from at least 44% of blocks (LLaMA2-7B) up to 84% (OPT-66B) with negligible accuracy impact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare SPD against external sync-point reduction or communication optimization methods (e.g., ring all-reduce, tree all-reduce, or ATP); instead, it benchmarks its own tiered strategies against the standard fully-synchronous Tensor Parallelism (0% SPD) baseline. Zero-shot dropping (ZS) alone recovers most of the latency benefit but causes >1% accuracy drop once the SPD budget exceeds the in-sensitive block boundary. Adding block-to-block distillation (ZS+B2B) recovers 20–28% of the lost accuracy on sensitive blocks (e.g., +28% on LLaMA2-13B 8-GPUs at 100% SPD). For the few extremely sensitive blocks present in smaller models, head grouping initialization (ZS+B2B+HG) provides an additional 2–3% accuracy recovery. OPT models show inherently lower sensitivity (≤1.3% max degradation), so the distillation and head-grouping stages yield smaller marginal gains compared to LLaMA2 models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tensor Parallelism (TP) as introduced in Megatron-LM (Shoeybi et al., 2019), but departs from the dominant line of work that improves the communication operation itself (ring/tree all-reduce topologies, ATP's dynamic parallel strategy). Instead, SPD takes a more radical system-level approach: it simply removes the all-reduce sync-point after self-attention output, then compensates for the resulting numerical divergence through a novel block design and sensitivity-aware strategies. The method also borrows concepts from model-level optimization (block skipping, head sparsity/redundancy findings) and applies them from a system perspective, bridging the gap between model compression and distributed serving that prior work has treated as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPD offers a practical, drop-in optimization for production LLM serving: ~20% latency reduction with <1% accuracy loss on LLaMA2-70B over 8 GPUs, with the benefit scaling further under low-bandwidth interconnects where communication is most painful. The finding that 44–84% of decoder blocks can be zero-shot dropped (with the fraction increasing with model size) suggests that much of TP's synchronization overhead is redundant, which could reshape how practitioners think about the communication-computation tradeoff in distributed inference. Open questions include generalization beyond LLaMA2/OPT architectures, interaction with speculative decoding or continuous batching, and whether the block design modifications remain compatible with future architectural changes (e.g., grouped-query attention, MoE layers).
