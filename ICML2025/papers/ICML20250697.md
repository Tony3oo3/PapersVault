## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods have been extensively studied for Transformer-based models, but their applicability to State Space Model (SSM) architectures like Mamba remains largely unexplored. The paper identifies two critical gaps: (i) no systematic benchmark exists comparing PEFT methods on SSM-based models, and (ii) while LoRA works well on linear projection matrices, it fails to improve performance when applied to SSM modules, and no existing method effectively tunes SSM-specific parameters.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pretrained SSM-based models (Mamba 130M/1.4B/2.8B, Jamba 319M/52B) on downstream tasks using text sequences (GLUE, DART, SAMSum, Spider) or flattened image pixels (CIFAR-10, CelebA, where 24×24×3 pixel values are serialized as space-separated integers). Outputs are task-specific: classification accuracy for NLU and vision, ROUGE/METEOR/BLEU for generation, and execution accuracy for text-to-SQL. Trainable parameters are constrained to below 1% for Mamba and 0.15% for Jamba, with the exception of prefix-tuning and LoRA/DoRA applied to both linear projections and SSM modules.

## Method

The paper proposes Sparse Dimension Tuning (SDT), a PEFT method specifically designed for SSM modules, motivated by a theoretical analysis (Lemma 2) showing that S4 state dimensions fall into three functional categories: redundant (zero), already-aligned (frozen), and misaligned (trainable). SDT uses a short warmup epoch to classify channel and state dimensions by the magnitude of changes in the discretized state matrix A, then selectively updates only the trainable dimensions of A, W_B, and W_C (for S6) while freezing the rest. Combined with LoRA applied to linear projection matrices (W_in, W_out), SDT achieves state-of-the-art PEFT performance. Theorem 1 provides a theoretical guarantee that SDT-P (the pruning variant) on SSM modules plus rank-rank LoRA on linear projections can match any smaller target model of the same architecture, with the number of trainable channels and states scaling with target model complexity. The practical SDT omits explicit pruning, treating pruned dimensions as parameters trained to zero, which reduces hyperparameters while maintaining effectiveness.

## Results

On Mamba-130M, LoRA applied to linear projection matrices achieves 81.2 average GLUE score, 70.9 METEOR on DART, and 61.0% CIFAR-10 accuracy, approaching full fine-tuning (80.5 GLUE, 71.0 METEOR, 60.0% CIFAR-10) with <1% trainable parameters. The proposed SDT combined with LoRA on linear projections further improves over LoRA alone on SSM modules: on Mamba-1.4B, SDT+LoRA reaches 84.5% Spider accuracy and 81.1 DART average versus 83.5% and 80.8 for LoRA+LoRA, while SDT+DoRA achieves 85.1% Spider and 52.1 ROUGE-1 on SAMSum versus 83.8% and 51.8 for DoRA+DoRA. On Jamba-Tiny-319M (GLUE), SDT+DoRA improves the average score from 67.9 to 69.2, with notable gains on QQP (72.7 vs. 67.8) and QNLI (79.6 vs. 77.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) is the strongest existing baseline: applied to linear projections it achieves 81.2 GLUE average on Mamba, but the paper shows that extending LoRA to SSM modules yields no further gain (80.3 vs. 81.2), motivating SDT. SDT+LoRA outperforms LoRA+LoRA on Spider (84.5% vs. 83.5%) and DART average (81.1 vs. 80.8) on Mamba-1.4B, while SDT+DoRA beats DoRA+DoRA on Spider (85.1% vs. 83.8%) and SAMSum ROUGE-1 (52.1 vs. 51.8). DoRA (Liu et al., 2024) performs comparably to LoRA on linear projections but slightly worse on SSM modules; SDT provides a consistent 1–1.3 point improvement over DoRA on Jamba GLUE (69.2 vs. 67.9 average). Prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) and prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) are clearly inferior, scoring 63.8 and 68.6 GLUE average respectively on Mamba, with prefix-tuning collapsing to 0.3% Spider accuracy on Jamba. BitFit (Zaken et al., 2022) achieves 76.8 GLUE on Mamba, trailing LoRA on linear projections by 4.4 points. Additional-Scan (Yoshimura et al., 2025), the only architecture-enhancement baseline, performs worst among SSM-targeting methods (62.4 GLUE, 15.8 DART BLEU on Mamba), confirming that simply adding state dimensions is ineffective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) literature, which has been developed almost exclusively for Transformer architectures, to the rapidly growing class of State Space Model (SSM) architectures such as Mamba and Jamba. It builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) for linear projection matrices and draws on the expressive-power framework of Zeng & Lee (2024) to develop a theoretical analysis of SSM parameter roles. The key conceptual departure from prior PEFT work is the finding that standard low-rank updates applied to SSM modules (the core sequential processing components) provide no benefit over tuning only the surrounding linear projections, motivating the novel Sparse Dimension Tuning (SDT) approach that operates at the granularity of individual channel and state dimensions rather than full weight matrices. Concurrent works (Yoshimura et al., 2025; Kang et al., 2025) explore other SSM-specific PEFT strategies, but this paper provides the most comprehensive benchmarking across architectures and tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSM-based models like Mamba and Jamba gain traction as alternatives to Transformers for long-sequence modeling, practical guidance on how to efficiently adapt them to downstream tasks is critical. The paper's negative result—that LoRA on SSM modules is ineffective and that input-injection methods (prompt/prefix tuning) are essentially useless on SSMs—saves practitioners from pursuing unproductive tuning strategies. SDT offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative for SSM modules, though its gains over LoRA-on-linear-projections alone are modest. Open questions include whether the warmup-based dimension selection is optimal, whether theoretical guarantees can be extended beyond linear activations and no-residual-connection assumptions, and whether the approach generalizes to even deeper SSM architectures or other sequence models.
