## Problem

While it is well known that ML models exploit spurious correlations, prior formalizations of simplicity bias were limited to low-dimensional or pairwise feature settings, leaving unclear what makes high-dimensional features 'easy' to learn and to what extent a trained model actually relies on spurious cues. The effect of over-parameterization on ERM-trained models (as opposed to group-reweighted objectives) was also poorly understood. This paper provides a rigorous, non-asymptotic statistical characterization of spurious correlations in high-dimensional ridge regression and random features models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2d-dimensional input z = [x, y] where x ∈ R^d is the core (predictive) feature and y ∈ R^d is the spurious feature, with labels depending only on x via g = x^T θ*_x + ε. The key analytical outputs are the spurious correlation C(θ̂) = Cov(f(θ̂, [x̃, y]), g) — measuring how the model output on an out-of-distribution sample (independent core x̃ paired with in-distribution spurious y) correlates with the true label — and the in-distribution test loss L(θ̂). The data covariance Σ ∈ R^{2d×2d} is decomposed into blocks, and the Schur complement S_x^Σ = Σ_yy − Σ_yx Σ_xx^{-1} Σ_xy encodes the conditional dependence between y and x.

## Method

The paper derives a sharp non-asymptotic characterization of C(θ̂_LR(λ)) via a deterministic quantity C^Σ(λ) = θ*^T Σ(Σ + τ(λ)I)^{-1} P_y Σ θ*, where τ(λ) is implicitly defined by a fixed-point equation involving the trace of (Σ + τI)^{-1}Σ. This is proved using the Gaussian sequence model of Han & Xu (2023) to obtain concentration bounds in the proportional regime n = Θ(d). Upper bounds on |C^Σ(λ)| reveal that the Schur complement's smallest eigenvalue controls the x–y correlation strength while the largest eigenvalue of Σ_yy captures the simplicity of y (few dominant directions = easier to learn). A trade-off is then proved: the regularization λ* minimizing test loss lies in an interval where C is positive and monotonically increasing. For over-parameterization, the random features model is shown to be pointwise equivalent to ridge regression with an effective regularization λ̃ = 2μ̃²d/(μ₁²n) + 2dλ/(μ₁²p), where μ₁ and μ̃² are Hermite coefficients of the activation, implying that even ridgeless (λ=0) over-parameterized models exhibit spurious correlations.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) provides a non-asymptotic characterization of spurious correlations C(θ̂_LR(λ)) for ridge regression in the proportional regime n=Θ(d), showing that C concentrates around a deterministic quantity C^Σ(λ) with high probability. Proposition 5.3 establishes a trade-off: the regularization λ* minimizing in-distribution test loss lies in an interval (0, λ_C] where spurious correlations are strictly positive and monotonically increasing (e.g., λ_C ≈ 1 for Gaussian data, λ_C ≈ 10 for Color-MNIST). Theorem 6.4 shows that an over-parameterized random features model (p = ω(n log⁴n)) is equivalent to linear regression with an effective regularization λ̃ = 2μ̃²d/(μ₁²n) + 2dλ/(μ₁²p), which is strictly positive even when λ=0, explaining why over-parameterized networks retain spurious correlations without explicit regularization (e.g., C(RF(0)) > 0 for tanh activation with λ̃ ≈ 0.05, while C(RF(0)) ≈ 0 for a near-linear activation φ₁ with λ̃ ≈ 0.005).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-baseline comparison table; its contributions are analytical characterizations rather than empirical benchmarks. The paper builds directly on Han & Xu (2023), whose non-asymptotic characterization of ridge regression solutions via the Gaussian sequence model is the technical foundation for Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 5.2. Compared to prior RF-to-linear-regression equivalence results (Goldt et al., 2020, 2022; Hu & Lu, 2023; Montanari & Saeed, 2022), Theorem 6.4 extends the equivalence from the level of training/test error to the level of the predictor itself (point-wise on a new test sample), which is necessary to transfer the spurious correlation analysis from linear regression to over-parameterized models. The paper also differs from prior simplicity bias analyses (Shah et al., 2020; Morwani et al., 2023; Pezeshki et al., 2021) by providing a covariance-based, model-agnostic quantification of simplicity via λ_max(Σ_yy) rather than architecture-specific complexity measures, and by connecting it to the Schur complement S_x^Σ that captures the correlation structure between core and spurious features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the non-asymptotic Gaussian sequence model framework of Han & Xu (2023) to characterize ridge regression solutions, and extends the random-features-to-regularized-linear-regression equivalence of Hu & Lu (2023) and Goldt et al. (2020, 2022) from the level of training/test error to the level of the predictor itself (Theorem 6.4). Conceptually, it departs from prior simplicity-bias analyses that relied on boolean functions, 1D features, or pairwise interactions (Shah et al., 2020; Pezeshki et al., 2021; Qiu et al., 2024) by providing a high-dimensional, covariance-spectrum-based characterization where simplicity is captured by λ_max(Σ_yy) and correlation by the Schur complement S_x^Σ. The spurious correlation metric C (Eq. 3) formalizes and unifies definitions from the fairness literature (Zliobaite, 2015) and the shortcut-learning survey (Ye et al., 2024) into a single regression-appropriate quantity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous, non-asymptotic characterization of how much spurious correlation a high-dimensional linear model learns, connecting it explicitly to data covariance structure and regularization strength. The key practical implication is the proven trade-off: the regularization that minimizes in-distribution test loss necessarily lies in a region where spurious correlations are positive and increasing, meaning that standard ERM with optimal ridge will always retain some spurious reliance. The RF result showing that over-parameterization introduces an effective regularization (even at λ=0) that sustains spurious correlations explains why neural networks exhibit this behavior without explicit regularization. Limitations include the focus on linear/RF models and Gaussian data for the main theorems, and the trade-off applies only to in-distribution performance; extending the framework to deeper non-linear architectures and designing ERM-beyond algorithms that selectively suppress spurious features remain open.
