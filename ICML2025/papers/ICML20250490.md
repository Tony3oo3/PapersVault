## Problem

Diffusion models excel at capturing complex data manifolds but inherently concentrate sampling in high-density regions, making them inadequate for discovery tasks that require exploring novel, low-density areas. This paper formalizes exploration as maximum entropy maximization over the approximate data manifold implicitly defined by a pre-trained diffusion model, and addresses the central challenge of doing so without explicit density estimation—a known intractable problem in high-dimensional settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250490/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5 with classifier-free guidance at scale w=8), a text prompt conditioning generation, and initial noise samples. It produces a fine-tuned diffusion model after K sequential mirror descent iterations, which generates images from lower-density regions while preserving semantic alignment. The fine-tuning operates on the classifier-free guided velocity field, and the iteration count K and per-step regularization coefficients {α_k} jointly control the exploration-versus-validity trade-off.

## Method

The key insight is that the gradient of the entropy first variation equals the negative score function (∇_x δH(p_T^π) = −s^π(·,T)), enabling exploration to be guided entirely by the diffusion model's own learned score without any explicit density estimation. The S-MEME algorithm performs sequential fine-tuning where each iteration k uses the previous model's score as the reward gradient in a KL-regularized linear fine-tuning subproblem, solved via Adjoint Matching (a first-order stochastic optimal control solver). Under idealized assumptions of exact score estimation and optimization, a single step provably achieves optimal entropy maximization via relative smoothness and strong convexity of the entropy functional (Theorem 5.2). Under realistic assumptions with noisy and biased oracles, almost-sure convergence to the optimal exploratory distribution is established through mirror flow analysis (Theorem 7.1), with the KL regularization implicitly constraining the fine-tuned model to remain on the approximate data manifold.

## Results

On the text-to-image task (Stable Diffusion 1.5, prompt 'A creative architecture'), S-MEME increases the FID distance from the pre-trained model from 0.0 to 19.15 after 3 iterations, while the cross-entropy metric Ĥ(p, p_T^pre) jumps from −1916.47 to 843.88, confirming the fine-tuned model samples from substantially lower-density regions. The CLIP score decreases modestly from 22.27 to 19.86, indicating preserved semantic faithfulness. In the illustrative 2D setting, S-MEME achieves a significantly higher Monte Carlo entropy estimate after K=4 iterations compared to the pre-trained model, with visibly more uniform density across the data support.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.5 model (p_T^pre), which serves as the reference distribution. S-MEME consistently increases the FID distance from the pre-trained model across all three iterations (10.25, 9.83, 19.15), demonstrating progressive exploration away from high-density regions. The cross-entropy metric Ĥ(p, p_T^pre) shifts from a large negative value (−1916.47) to positive values (564.72–843.88), confirming the fine-tuned model assigns higher probability to regions the pre-trained model considers low-density. The CLIP score drops by at most 2.4 points (22.27 → 19.86), showing that exploration does not severely degrade semantic alignment with the text prompt. The paper does not compare against other exploration or diversity methods (e.g., particle guidance, minority guidance), so no head-to-head comparison with competing exploration baselines is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends maximum state entropy exploration (Hazan et al., 2019) from discrete to continuous state spaces, with the critical twist that the admissible set is defined implicitly by a pre-trained diffusion model rather than explicitly. It builds on the recent line of diffusion fine-tuning via stochastic optimal control (Uehara et al., 2024; Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024), but departs from those works by optimizing a non-linear functional (entropy) rather than a linear scalar reward, made tractable through the key identity linking the entropy's first variation to the negative score. The convergence analysis draws on mirror descent theory (Lu et al., 2018) and the mirror flow framework (Hsieh et al., 2019), applied here for the first time to an entropy objective over probability measures with implicit generative-model constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The score-entropy connection (Eq. 12) provides a clean, scalable principle for self-guided exploration that bypasses intractable density estimation, which is a genuine practical bottleneck in high-dimensional generative settings. However, the convergence rate of O((log log k)^{-1}) is very slow, and the experimental validation is limited to a 2D toy problem and only 3 S-MEME iterations on SD-1.5 with proxy metrics (FID, CLIP) rather than direct entropy measurement. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex manifolds (e.g., molecular spaces), how it interacts with validity constraints beyond KL regularization, and whether the sequential fine-tuning can be accelerated or replaced by a single-step approximation that still preserves convergence guarantees.
