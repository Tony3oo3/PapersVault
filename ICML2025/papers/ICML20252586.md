## Problem

Existing ECG-language pretraining methods (e.g., MERL) align ECG signals with clinical reports only at the global level, overlooking the hierarchical multi-scale structure of ECG signals—waveform tokens, individual heartbeats, and overall rhythm. This limits their ability to capture fine-grained local patterns that are critical for diverse downstream diagnostic tasks and low-data generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second, 12-lead ECG recordings sampled at 500 Hz paired with up to 18 clinical text reports per recording (from MIMIC-IV-ECG, yielding 760,618 pairs after curation). The ECG encoder (Wav2Vec 2.0) produces token-level embeddings of shape L_t × D, which are hierarchically aggregated into beat-level and rhythm-level representations; the text encoder (12-layer BERT) produces word, sentence, and [CLS] embeddings. Outputs include global ECG/text embeddings for zero-shot retrieval, beat-level features for linear probing or fine-tuning classification, and autoregressively generated ECG report text via a 6-layer decoder.

## Method

MELP introduces three levels of cross-modal supervision aligned to clinically meaningful scales: (1) token-level ECG captioning, where an attention pooler (128 learnable queries) summarizes ECG token embeddings and a GPT-style decoder generates the paired report via next-word prediction (L_LM); (2) beat-level alignment, where 10 learnable tokens aggregate token features into beat embeddings, and an attention-weighted beat-sentence matching mechanism computes a local contrastive loss (L_Local) that links individual heartbeats to corresponding report sentences; (3) rhythm-level global contrastive loss (L_g) using InfoNCE on average-pooled beat embeddings and [CLS] text embeddings. Before joint multimodal training, a cardiology-specific language model is pretrained via masked language modeling on a 5.6 GB corpus (PubMed, Wikipedia, MIMIC-IV-ECG reports) to equip the text encoder with domain knowledge. The full model is trained by jointly optimizing L = L_g + 2·L_LM + 0.2·L_Local over 100 epochs with AdamW (lr=2e-4, cosine annealing) on 4× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On zero-shot ECG classification, MELP achieves an average AUC of 79.0%, outperforming the strongest multimodal baseline MERL (75.3%) by +3.7% across six tasks (CSN, PTBXL-Rhythm, PTBXL-Form, PTBXL-Sub, PTBXL-Super, CPSC2018). In linear probing with 100% training data, MELP reaches 96.91% AUC on PTBXL-Rhythm, 94.32% on CPSC2018, and 90.17% on CSN, ranking first or second in all 18 evaluation settings. With only 1% of training data, MELP's advantage is most pronounced, surpassing the second-best method by margins up to +7.38% (PTBXL-Rhythm) and +6.74% (CPSC2018). In cross-domain transfer learning, MELP achieves the best average AUC of 79.58%, edging out MERL (79.42%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MELP is compared against 13 unimodal SSL baselines and 1 multimodal baseline (MERL) on linear probing, zero-shot classification, and transfer learning. Against unimodal SSL methods, MELP dominates across all settings: ECGFM (McKeen et al., 2024) is the strongest unimodal competitor, reaching 92.70% AUC on PTBXL-Rhythm at 100% data, but MELP achieves 96.91% (+4.21%); HeartLang (Jin et al., 2025) reaches 90.34% on the same task, trailing MELP by 6.57%. At the 1% training ratio, MELP's lead over ECGFM widens to +7.38% on PTBXL-Rhythm (88.83% vs. 81.45%). The multimodal baseline MERL (Liu et al., 2024a) is the closest competitor overall, achieving 88.34% on PTBXL-Rhythm at 100% linear probing (vs. MELP's 96.91%) and 75.3% average zero-shot AUC (vs. MELP's 79.0%). In transfer learning, MELP and MERL are nearly tied on average (79.58% vs. 79.42%), with MELP winning 3 of 6 cross-domain settings. For ECG report generation, MELP (284M params) substantially outperforms PULSE (7B params) on BERTScore F1 (44.08 vs. 10.96) and ROUGE-L (18.50 vs. 8.15), though PULSE edges ahead on METEOR (13.76 vs. 11.28). MELP also surpasses all baselines on patient identification, achieving 49.67% R@1 vs. ECGFM's 49.18% and MERL's 16.12%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ECG-language pretraining line initiated by MERL (Liu et al., 2024a), which aligned global ECG embeddings with clinical text via contrastive learning, by introducing hierarchical supervision at three clinically meaningful scales (token, beat, rhythm). It builds on Wav2Vec 2.0 for the ECG encoder and MedCPT for the text encoder, adding a dedicated cardiology language pretraining stage inspired by HeartBERT. The key conceptual departure from prior multimodal ECG work (MERL, C-MELT, ECG-Chat) is the explicit multi-scale cross-modal alignment: a generative captioning loss at the token level, an attention-weighted beat-sentence contrastive loss at the beat level, and a global InfoNCE loss at the rhythm level, rather than a single flat alignment objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-scale design mirrors how cardiologists actually interpret ECGs—examining individual waveform components, then heartbeats, then overall rhythm—making the learned representations more clinically grounded and transferable. The consistent gains across zero-shot, linear probing, and cross-domain transfer settings (especially the large margins at 1% data) suggest the approach is robust and practically useful in label-scarce clinical settings. However, the token-level supervision lacks explicit clinical interpretability, and the text encoder (MedCPT) does not leverage recent LLM advances, leaving open whether instruction-tuned LLM backbones or externally sourced fine-grained waveform descriptions could further improve alignment and explainability.
