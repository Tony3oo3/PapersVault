## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to generate desired concepts through prompting alone, yet the conditions under which a concept becomes unreachable—and whether latent-space interventions can recover it—remain poorly understood. This paper asks: how do three common dataset limitations (concept scarcity, caption underspecification, and co-occurrence biases) affect concept reachability, and can steering intermediate activations bypass failures that prompting cannot overcome?

## Input / Output

The steering procedure takes a starting text prompt y_s, a collection Z of reference images containing the target concept combination, and a random seed x_T ~ N(0,I), and produces a generated image whose reachability is scored by trained classifiers against the target concept tuple. In the synthetic setting, images are 64×64 RGB with two colored shapes on a black background, and the concept tuple is (back_colour, back_shape, front_colour, front_shape). The optimized concept vector is added at a fixed layer during the DDPM denoising loop (T=1000 steps), so the output is a single image per seed whose correctness is determined by whether all four concept slots match the target.

## Method

The paper constructs a controlled synthetic dataset of 54,000 colored-shape images and systematically degrades it along three axes—reducing the proportion of images containing a given concept, removing semantic factors from captions, and tying two concepts in perfect co-occurrence—while training a 3.7M-parameter U-Net with a frozen T5Small text encoder. Steering is implemented in two spaces: (i) prompt-space, where a vector v_p is added to the text encoding before cross-attention, and (ii) h-space, where a vector v_h is added to the U-Net bottleneck output; in both cases the vector is optimized by minimizing the standard denoising loss with frozen U-Net weights over 5,000 steps. Reachability is measured as the fraction of 100 sampled images (averaged over 4 random seeds) whose classifier-assigned concept tuple matches the target. The key empirical findings are a sharp phase transition in reachability at roughly 1% concept presence, a strong dependence of h-space steering on the starting prompt's alignment with the target, and consistent superiority of prompt-space steering over prompting under scarcity and bias, while underspecified captions remain a hard limit for both methods.

## Results

Under balanced dataset conditions, both prompting and prompt-space steering achieve reachability accuracy above 0.9, while h-space steering accuracy diminishes as the number of modified concepts increases. A phase transition is observed: reachability remains high until the proportion of training images containing a concept drops below approximately 1% (p_X(f) ≈ 0.01), after which it declines sharply. When a concept is entirely absent from training (p_X(f) = 0), prompt-space steering still achieves over 50% accuracy for positionally OOD targets (e.g., s_2 = square), whereas prompting fails. Under caption underspecification, average reachability falls below 0.30 when fewer than two concepts are specified, though steering consistently outperforms prompting across all specification levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three control methods—direct prompting, prompt-space steering, and h-space (bottleneck-layer) steering—across three dataset obstacles. Under balanced conditions, prompt-space steering matches prompting (>0.9 accuracy), while h-space steering degrades with increasing concept modifications and exhibits a steerability bias toward targets where front and back shapes are identical. Under concept scarcity, prompt-space steering maintains the highest reachability below the ~1% threshold, outperforming both prompting and h-space steering; h-space steering shows the most variability and is sensitive to the choice of starting prompt. Under caption underspecification, prompt-space steering achieves the highest reachability among all methods, though all methods degrade substantially as specification decreases. Under tied-concept biases, prompt-space steering achieves moderately high accuracy in disentangling both tied concepts (e.g., blue and circle), while prompting fails to separate them; h-space steering performs best only when the starting prompt describes the target concepts. Overall, prompt-space steering is the most robust method across all three obstacle types, while h-space steering is effective but more fragile and dependent on prompt alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on activation steering in diffusion models (Kwon et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024a; Epstein et al., 2023) by shifting focus from *whether* steering can reach a concept to *under what dataset conditions* it can or cannot. It draws on the data-attribution and dataset-structure literature (Zhao et al., 2018; Chang et al., 2025) but departs from it by using a fully synthetic framework where influence relationships are known by design, enabling controlled isolation of scarcity, underspecification, and bias as independent obstacles. The h-space steering implementation directly follows Li et al. (2024a), while the prompt-space variant is a simpler analogue that the paper uses as a more stable baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical framework for model providers: rather than costly retraining or dataset curation, user-facing steering mechanisms can recover concepts that prompting cannot access, even under severely degraded training data. The phase-transition finding (reachability collapses sharply below ~1% concept prevalence) gives a concrete, actionable threshold for minimum dataset representation. Key limitations include the reliance on an auxiliary image collection Z for steering (hard to acquire in practice), the small model scale (3.7M parameters) limiting generalizability claims, and the fact that real-world obstacles (scarcity, underspecification, bias) co-occur simultaneously rather than in isolation. Open questions include steering methods that eliminate the Z dependency and whether the phase-transition threshold holds at the scale of billion-parameter models.
