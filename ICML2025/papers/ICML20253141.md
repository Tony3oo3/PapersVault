## Problem

Likelihood-based generative models (diffusion and autoregressive) trained via maximum likelihood estimation suffer from forward KL's mode-covering tendency, which forces the learned density to spread excessively under limited capacity, producing blurry samples and necessitating expensive guidance at inference. While GAN-type losses can counteract this, standard GANs require a separate discriminator network and alternating generator-discriminator optimization, creating engineering overhead incompatible with iterative sampling models. The paper asks whether the likelihood-based model itself can serve as the discriminator, eliminating the need for extra networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253141/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

DDO finetunes a pretrained likelihood-based model (EDM, EDM2, or VAR) without altering its architecture or inference protocol. At training time, it consumes real images from the dataset and fake images generated by a frozen reference model (50k samples per round for diffusion), computing the log-likelihood ratio between the target and reference models. At inference, the finetuned model takes the same inputs as the base model (Gaussian noise, optionally a class label) and produces synthetic images at the original resolution (32×32, 64×64, 256×256, or 512×512) with no additional NFE or guidance overhead.

## Method

The key insight is to implicitly parameterize the GAN discriminator as d_θ(x) = σ(log p_θ(x)/p_θ_ref(x)), where p_θ is the learnable target model and p_θ_ref is a frozen reference model, both initialized from the pretrained checkpoint. The training objective is the standard GAN binary cross-entropy loss applied to this implicit discriminator, with real samples drawn from p_data and fake samples from p_θ_ref, which by Theorem 3.1 has p_θ* = p_data as its optimum. For diffusion models, Jensen's inequality converts the multi-timestep likelihood ratio into a single-forward-pass approximation of the loss. A generalized objective introduces hyperparameters α (weighting the fake-sample term) and β (scaling the log-likelihood ratio), with β < 1 inducing an overshooting effect analogous to guidance methods. Multi-round self-play refinement iteratively replaces the reference model with the best model from the previous round, requiring less than 1% of pretraining epochs per round.

## Results

DDO achieves record-breaking guidance-free FID scores of 1.30 on CIFAR-10, 0.97 on ImageNet-64, and 1.26 on ImageNet 512×512, improving upon the EDM/EDM2 base models by 30–40%. On ImageNet 256×256, finetuning VAR-d30 with DDO reduces the guidance-free FID from 4.74 to 1.79 (2.6× improvement) and the CFG-enhanced FID from 1.92 to 1.73, while eliminating all sampling tricks. Each finetuning round requires less than 1% of pretraining epochs, and the full multi-round refinement (up to 28 rounds on ImageNet 512×512) still costs far less than retraining from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 512×512, DDO (FID 1.26 guidance-free) outperforms the 2× larger EDM2-XXL (1.91), VDM++ (2.99), and SiD2A (1.37, one-step GAN), while matching or beating all guidance-enhanced methods (e.g., EDM2-S+AG at 1.34, USiT-2B+CFG at 1.72) at half the inference cost. On ImageNet-64, the compact EDM2-S+DDO (0.97) surpasses the 4× larger EDM2-XL (1.58), SiD2A (1.11), PaGoDA (1.21), and even EDM2-S+AG (1.01). On CIFAR-10, DDO (1.30) beats all one-step GAN distillation methods including SiD2A (1.40) and CTM (1.73). For autoregressive models on ImageNet 256×256, DDO reduces VAR-d30's guidance-free FID from 4.74 to 1.79, outperforming CCA (2.54) and matching the CFG-enhanced baseline (1.90) at half the inference cost. Notably, DDO with DPM-Solver-v3 achieves FID 1.29 at only 25 NFE, demonstrating that the quality gains are robust to aggressive sampling acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDO bridges two previously separate lineages: the GAN discriminator loss (Goodfellow et al., 2014), whose optimal solution d*(x) = p_data/(p_data + p_θ) encodes a density ratio, and the DPO parameterization (Rafailov et al., 2024), which expresses a reward model as a sigmoid of the log-likelihood ratio between a target and reference policy. The key departure from both is that DDO uses the likelihood-based generative model itself as the implicit discriminator, eliminating the need for a separate discriminator network or joint adversarial training, while the Jensen's-inequality approximation for diffusion likelihoods follows the Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) trick. Unlike inference-time guidance methods (CFG, Discriminator Guidance, Autoguidance), DDO modifies the model at training time, and unlike prior GAN-augmented diffusion work (e.g., DMD2, SiD2A), it requires no architectural changes or auxiliary losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDO offers a drop-in post-training procedure that pushes already-SOTA diffusion and autoregressive models well beyond their MLE-trained limits with less than 1% of pretraining compute per round, achieving record guidance-free FIDs (1.30/0.97/1.26) that surpass even CFG-enhanced or autoguided baselines while halving inference cost. The theoretical connection (Theorem 3.3) showing that DDO with β<1 induces the same 'overshooting' distribution as guidance methods unifies two previously separate perspectives. Open questions include the sensitivity to hyperparameter grid search over α and β, whether the multi-round self-play can be made more automated, and whether the approach scales to text-to-image or video generation where the data distribution is far more complex and the reference model's coverage assumption may be harder to satisfy.
