## Problem

Common involutive MCMC kernels (RWMH, MALA, HMC) require a step size parameter that is difficult to tune, and for multiscale targets no single global step size works well across the state space. The closest prior method, autoMALA, was shown to be π-invariant but critically lacked proofs of irreducibility and aperiodicity, and in practice could get stuck near the mode or in the tails. This paper addresses both the theoretical gap and the practical robustness issue.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target density π(x), an initial state x, an initial step size θ₀, an auxiliary distribution m (e.g., Gaussian with mass matrix M), and a family of continuously differentiable involutions {f_θ}. At each iteration it produces a new state x_{t+1} and a locally-adapted step size θ_t selected by a doubling/halving procedure. A round-based outer loop additionally tunes θ₀ (via the median of selected log-step-sizes) and a diagonal preconditioner M from the inverse sample covariance, mixed with the identity for regularization.

## Method

AutoStep MCMC augments the state space to S = X × Z × Δ × Θ, where Δ holds randomized acceptance-ratio thresholds (a,b) ~ Unif and Θ holds the step size θ. The step size selector μ(x,z,a,b) uses a symmetric criterion—checking |ℓ(x,z,θ)| ∈ (|log b|, |log a|) rather than the asymmetric ℓ ∈ (log b, log a) from autoMALA—and finds θ via a doubling/halving loop starting from θ₀. This symmetric criterion is essential for proving irreducibility and prevents the chain from stalling near the mode or in the tails. The full kernel is a standard Metropolis-corrected involution on the augmented space, yielding π-invariance, irreducibility, and aperiodicity under mild assumptions (Assumptions 4.3–4.7). The expected cost per iteration scales as O(|log θ₀|) for strongly log-concave targets, making the method far more robust to the initial step size than fixed-step methods, which degrade at O(exp(−|log θ₀|)).

## Results

The primary metric is minimum KSESS (Kolmogorov-Smirnov effective sample size) per second across five benchmark models (mRNA 5D, orbital 12D, Kilpisjärvi 3D, funnel2 2D, funnel100 100D). AutoStep RWMH achieves competitive performance generally within the range of NUTS, slice sampling, adaptive RWMH, adaptive MALA, and delayed rejection HMC, with particular strength on the challenging funnel targets. AutoStep MALA shows slightly lower performance in some settings but exhibits better scaling to higher dimensions, as seen in the funnel100 results. For robustness, AutoStep methods degrade gracefully at O(|log θ₀|⁻¹) in KSESS per unit cost as the initial step size θ₀ varies over 14 orders of magnitude, compared to O(exp(−|log θ₀|)) decay for fixed-step RWMH/MALA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NUTS (Neal 2011), hit-and-run slice sampling (Neal 2003; Bélisle 1993), adaptive RWMH (Vihola 2012), adaptive MALA (Xu et al. 2020), and delayed rejection HMC (Modi et al. 2024), AutoStep RWMH and MALA are broadly competitive in min KSESS per second across all five benchmarks, with AutoStep MALA showing an edge on the 100D funnel where it scales better to higher dimensions. Against fixed-step RWMH (Hastings 1970) and fixed-step MALA (Rossky et al. 1978), AutoStep is dramatically more robust: when θ₀ is poorly chosen (e.g., 10⁻⁷ or 10⁷), fixed-step methods suffer near-total performance collapse (KSESS per unit cost drops by orders of magnitude), while AutoStep retains a meaningful fraction of its peak performance, degrading only logarithmically. The symmetric step size criterion used by AutoStep also outperforms the asymmetric criterion from autoMALA (Biron-Lattes et al. 2024), which exhibits very low acceptance probabilities near the mode and in the tails, causing the chain to get stuck—particularly problematic for Bayesian inference initialized in the tails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the locally-adaptive step size selection mechanism from autoMALA (Biron-Lattes et al., 2024) to the broader class of involutive MCMC methods (Tierney, 1998; Neklyudov et al., 2020), which includes RWMH, MALA, and HMC as special cases. Two key departures from autoMALA are: (1) a symmetric acceptance-ratio threshold that ensures irreducibility (the asymmetric criterion in autoMALA can trap the chain near the mode or in the tails), and (2) augmenting the state with the step size parameter, which substantially simplifies the invariance proof. The work is concurrent with Bou-Rabee et al. (2024) on Gibbs self-tuning, which introduces the same augmented-space technique but with a different adaptation scheme for HMC/NUTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a critical theoretical gap: autoMALA was shown π-invariant but not irreducible or aperiodic, and in practice it can get stuck—particularly problematic for Bayesian inference where initialization is often in the tails. The O(|log θ₀|) cost scaling (versus O(exp(−|log θ₀|)) for fixed-step methods) makes the sampler robust to poor initial step size choices without requiring expensive tuning. Open questions include a rigorous analysis of asymptotic variance and expected squared jump distance, and whether the approach extends to non-step-size tuning parameters (e.g., mass matrices, path lengths) with comparable guarantees. The method is competitive with NUTS and other state-of-the-art samplers but does not dramatically outperform them on the benchmarks tested.
