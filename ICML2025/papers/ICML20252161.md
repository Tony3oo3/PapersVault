## Problem

Pruning large language models at high sparsity ratios introduces a significant performance gap between the original dense model and the pruned sparse model, with perplexity degrading by orders of magnitude at 60–70% sparsity. Existing post-pruning recovery methods (re-training, knowledge distillation) are computationally expensive and require large datasets or teacher models, while prior low-rank approximation approaches fail to maintain a fixed sparsity pattern, making them incompatible with structured N:M pruning needed for hardware acceleration. This paper addresses the gap by recovering lost information through a low-rank component that complements the sparse matrix while strictly preserving its sparsity structure, in a fully data-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a dense weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} and a binary pruning mask P (derived from any pruning strategy: magnitude, Wanda, SparseGPT, or structured N:M), and produces an updated sparse weight matrix S' that maintains the exact sparsity pattern of P, plus a low-rank residual matrix L_k of target rank k (typically k=128). The refinement itself requires no calibration data; the only data dependency is in the initial pruning step (e.g., Wanda uses 128 C4 sequences). The low-rank component adds k(m+n) parameters per layer, amounting to roughly 4–5% additional parameters for LLaMA-7B at k=128, while the sparse matrix retains its original sparsity ratio.

## Method

The core algorithm iteratively refines the sparse matrix by decomposing the residual L^(t) = W − S^(t) via SVD and transferring the least significant singular components (those beyond a linearly increasing rank threshold r^(t) = ⌊1 + (k−1)/(T−1)·t⌋) into the sparse matrix through the mask P, while the top r^(t) components remain in the low-rank part. This progressive rank schedule ensures the most energy-concentrated singular directions are captured first, with subtler details incorporated in later iterations, over T=50 total steps. The binary mask P is applied at every update (S^(t+1) = S^(t) + P ⊙ {truncated SVD terms}), guaranteeing sparsity preservation throughout. The approach is distinguished from PCP-based baselines (which apply uniform nuclear-norm shrinkage to all singular values) and from zero-shot SVD (which never updates S) by its adaptive, rank-prioritized transfer of information from the low-rank residual into the sparse structure. Theoretical analysis establishes convergence to a fixed point, asymptotic monotonic error decrease, and a Frobenius-norm error bound of √(r−k)·σ_{k+1}(L^(t)).

## Results

On LLaMA-7B WikiText-2 perplexity (k=128, ~4.9% extra parameters), the proposed iterative low-rank refinement reduces perplexity by 53.9% over magnitude pruning at 50% sparsity (7.97 vs. 17.29), 92.0% at 60% sparsity (12.14 vs. 152.36), and 99.6% at 70% sparsity (200.09 vs. 48427.85). For structured 4:8 sparsity, perplexity drops from 16.83 to 8.86 (−47.4%), and for 2:4 from 42.53 to 10.74 (−74.7%). On LLaMA-3.1-8B, the method recovers GSM8K accuracy from 1.3% (magnitude 50%) to 6.5%, and improves HellaSwag from 56.4 to 66.8, though a gap to the dense model (49.8% GSM8K, 78.9% HellaSwag) remains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015a), the proposed method achieves dramatic perplexity reductions: 53.9% at 50% sparsity, 92.0% at 60%, and 99.6% at 70% on LLaMA-7B, with similar gains on LLaMA-13B (5.5% at 50%, 75.7% at 70%). Against Wanda (Sun et al., 2023), improvements are more modest but consistent: 4.7% perplexity reduction at 50% unstructured sparsity, 16.1% at 60%, 61.2% at 70%, and 9.7% at 4:8 structured sparsity on LLaMA-7B. Against SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023) at 2:4 structured sparsity on LLaMA-7B, the method improves average benchmark score from 24.3 to 25.4 (a 1.1-point gain), narrowing the gap to the dense model (30.3) from 19.8% to 16.2%. The method also outperforms its own zero-shot SVD and PCP-with-mask baselines across all sparsity levels, with the largest margins at high sparsity (e.g., 200.09 vs. 283.74 vs. 727.54 perplexity at 70% on LLaMA-7B), confirming that iterative rank-increasing refinement is superior to one-shot or uniform-shrinkage alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse-plus-low-rank matrix decomposition framework from Principal Component Pursuit (Candès et al., 2011) and Chandrasekaran et al. (2009, 2011), but departs from the standard PCP formulation by enforcing a fixed binary sparsity mask throughout the optimization, which prior low-rank refinement methods (LoSparse, SLoPE, OATS) do not maintain. The key methodological departure is replacing the convex nuclear-norm minimization with a forward-only iterative SVD procedure that progressively increases the retained rank, prioritizing large singular values first. Unlike re-training or knowledge distillation approaches for post-pruning recovery, the method is entirely data-free and operates layer-wise on weight matrices alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free, layer-wise, sparsity-preserving nature makes this approach broadly applicable to any pruning method (magnitude, Wanda, SparseGPT) and compatible with hardware-efficient structured N:M patterns, addressing a practical gap that re-training and distillation cannot fill without large datasets or teacher models. However, the absolute performance gap to dense models remains substantial on reasoning tasks (e.g., GSM8K drops from 49.8% to 6.5% on LLaMA-3.1-8B at 50% sparsity), and the method adds k(m+n) parameters per layer, slightly eroding the sparsity benefit. Open questions include automatic per-layer rank selection, whether the approach composes well with quantization, and whether the convergence guarantees hold under non-uniform sparsity patterns across layers.
