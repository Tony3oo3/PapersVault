## Problem

Modern autoencoders for latent diffusion models exhibit inordinate high-frequency components in their latent spaces, particularly as bottleneck channel size increases, which disrupts the coarse-to-fine spectral autoregression property that diffusion models rely on for efficient synthesis. While prior work has focused on reconstruction quality and compression ratios, the 'diffusability' of the latent space—the degree to which its spectral profile supports the diffusion process—has been largely unexplored. The paper further shows that standard KL regularization, intended to simplify the diffusion task, actually amplifies these harmful high-frequency components.

## Input / Output

The autoencoder accepts RGB images at 256×256 or video clips at 17×256×256 and produces compressed latent representations (e.g., 16 channels at 8×8 spatial compression for FluxAE, or 32 channels at 8×32×32 for LTX-AE). During the SE regularization step, both the input and the latent are bilinearly downsampled by a randomly sampled factor of ×2 or ×4, and the decoder must reconstruct the downsampled RGB from the downsampled latent. The downstream DiT backbone then operates on these latents with class-conditional generation, producing 256×256 images or 17×256×256 videos over 256 denoising steps.

## Method

The paper proposes scale equivariance (SE) regularization, a simple loss term that enforces consistency between downsampled latents and downsampled RGB in the decoder. Concretely, the training objective adds α·d(x̃, Dec(z̃)) to the standard reconstruction loss, where x̃ and z̃ are bilinearly downsampled versions of the input and latent (α=0.25), effectively requiring the decoder to produce the same output whether it receives full-resolution or downsampled latents. This suppresses spurious high-frequency components in the latent space without requiring explicit DCT transforms, and only needs 10K–20K fine-tuning steps with minimal code changes. The approach is validated across four autoencoders (FluxAE, CMS-AE, CogVideoX-AE, LTX-AE) in both image and video domains, reducing FID by 19% on ImageNet-1K 256² and FVD by at least 44% on Kinetics-700 17×256², while preserving reconstruction quality.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256², scale equivariance (SE) regularization reduces FID by 19% for DiT-XL/2 (12.21 → 9.85) and by 29% for DiT-B/2 (25.41 → 18.06) compared to vanilla FluxAE, while also improving FDD by ~17%. On Kinetics-700 17×256², SE-regularized video autoencoders achieve at least 44% lower FVD₁₀K: CogVideoX-AE drops from 650.40 to 252.26 (DiT-B/2) and LTX-AE from 854.47 to 389.56 (DiT-B/1). Reconstruction quality is preserved (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS remain comparable), and the method requires only 10K–20K fine-tuning steps with ~11% FLOPs overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla FluxAE (Black Forest Labs, 2023), SE regularization delivers the largest gains: 19% FID reduction for DiT-XL/2 (12.21→9.85) and 29% for DiT-B/2 (25.41→18.06), while also outperforming the fine-tuned-without-SE control (FluxAE+FT) by 8% FID. Against CMS-AE_I (Agarwal et al., 2025), the improvement is marginal (FID 11.69→11.85), largely because the fine-tuning pipeline itself degrades this high-compression autoencoder; SE still recovers most of the loss (13.59→11.85). For video, SE-regularized CogVideoX-AE (Hong et al., 2022) achieves 44–54% lower FVD₁₀K (650.40→252.26 for DiT-B/2; 268.26→135.15 for DiT-XL/2), and SE-regularized LTX-AE (HaCohen et al., 2024) achieves 54% lower FVD₁₀K (854.47→389.56). Compared to the original DiT paper (Peebles & Xie, 2022) which used SD-VAE-ft-MSE, the SE-regularized DiT-XL/2 at 400K steps (FID 9.85) already surpasses the original DiT-XL/2 at 7M steps (FID 12.03), and at 1M steps reaches FID 3.27.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework by introducing 'diffusability' as a distinct optimization target for autoencoders, separate from the previously emphasized reconstruction quality and compression ratio. It builds on the spectral autoregression interpretation of diffusion (Ning et al., 2024; Dieleman, 2024; Rissanen et al., 2023), showing that the coarse-to-fine generation property is undermined when latent spaces contain excessive high-frequency energy. The proposed scale equivariance regularization is conceptually related to concurrent work on equivariance in autoencoders (EQ-VAE, AF-LDM) but is motivated specifically by spectral alignment between latent and RGB spaces rather than spatial transformation invariance. The paper departs from the standard VAE training recipe by demonstrating that KL regularization, widely adopted to align latents with the Gaussian prior, actually amplifies harmful high-frequency components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a minimal-code-change regularization requiring only 10–20K fine-tuning steps yields 19% FID reduction on ImageNet and 44%+ FVD reduction on Kinetics-700 across multiple autoencoder architectures, making it highly adoptable for existing LDM pipelines. The spectral analysis framework (DCT-based frequency profiling) provides a diagnostic tool for practitioners to evaluate latent space quality beyond reconstruction metrics. Open questions include whether temporal scale equivariance can be made to work (it surprisingly did not), whether adversarial losses can fully recover the slight FID degradation in reconstruction, and how the approach generalizes to other diffusion parametrizations and discrete tokenizers. The finding that larger bottleneck channels exacerbate the high-frequency problem suggests a fundamental tension between reconstruction fidelity and diffusability that the field has not yet resolved.
