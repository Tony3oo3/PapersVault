## Problem

Long-context Transformer inference is bottlenecked by quadratic attention cost during prefill. Existing block-sparse attention methods (MInference, FlexPrefill) rely on expensive pattern-search or pooling-based block importance estimators whose overhead can negate the sparsity gains, creating a persistent accuracy-efficiency trade-off that limits practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252790/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

XAttention consumes the standard query Q ∈ R^{L×d}, key K ∈ R^{L×d}, and value V matrices from a Transformer layer and produces a sparse block mask M (a binary matrix over B×B blocks of the L×L attention map) followed by the attention output computed only on selected blocks. The method is applied during the prefill stage and is plug-and-play: it replaces the full attention computation without any retraining, operating on sequences up to 256k tokens with configurable block size B and stride S.

## Method

The key insight is that summing attention values along antidiagonals (lower-left to upper-right) within each B×B block provides a lightweight yet accurate proxy for block importance, because antidiagonals intersect every possible vertical and slash pattern that existing methods explicitly search for. Concretely, Q and K are reshaped along antidiagonal directions with stride S before computing approximate softmax scores, reducing the scoring cost to O(L²/S²) rather than O(L²). A threshold-based selection (find_blocks) retains the minimal set of blocks whose cumulative antidiagonal probability exceeds τ, yielding dynamic sparsity that adapts to input structure. An optional dynamic-programming module predicts per-head minimum thresholds (reducing τ by 10% per step) to further tighten the accuracy-efficiency frontier without manual tuning.

## Results

On RULER with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, XAttention (S=8) achieves an average accuracy of 88.47 across 4k–128k sequence lengths, surpassing full attention (87.52) and all sparse baselines. In attention computation, XAttention delivers up to 13.5× prefill speedup at 256k tokens (S=8, density 7.32%) and 9.8× (S=16, density 6.89%), with end-to-end prefill speedups of up to 5.12×. On Video-MME (Qwen2-VL-7B), XAttention scores 69.1% overall, outperforming MInference (68.4%) and FlexPrefill (67.7%) while matching full attention (69.2%). For video generation (HunyuanVideo on VBench), XAttention with τ=0.95 achieves PSNR 23.5, SSIM 0.822, and LPIPS 0.155 at 45.5% density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XAttention (S=8) outperforms all four baselines on RULER average accuracy: 88.47 vs. Full Attention 87.52 (+0.95), FlexPrefill 87.72 (+0.75), SeerAttention 87.23 (+1.24), and MInference 84.15 (+4.32). The gap over MInference is most dramatic at 128k context, where MInference collapses to 54.12 while XAttention retains 72.31. Against FlexPrefill, XAttention leads at 32k (90.75 vs. 88.14) and 64k (84.08 vs. 81.14), though FlexPrefill slightly edges ahead at 128k (74.67 vs. 72.31). On Video-MME, XAttention (69.1%) beats MInference (68.4%) and FlexPrefill (67.7%) by 0.7 and 1.4 points respectively, and matches full attention (69.2%) while using sparse attention. In efficiency, XAttention's pattern selection is up to 24.9× faster than MInference and 5.9× faster than FlexPrefill, and it achieves up to 13.5× attention speedup at 256k tokens, substantially exceeding the speedups reported by the other sparse methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

XAttention extends the block-sparse attention paradigm (Zaheer et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2024) by replacing the costly vertical-slash index search used in MInference and FlexPrefill with a simple antidiagonal summation as a block importance proxy. Unlike SeerAttention, which requires expensive pretraining of gate parameters, XAttention is entirely training-free and plug-and-play. The method also introduces a dynamic programming-based per-head threshold prediction that departs from the fixed-threshold approach in prior sparse attention work, allowing each head to adapt its sparsity level independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.5× attention speedup at 256K tokens with accuracy comparable to full attention makes long-context inference substantially more practical, and the training-free, plug-and-play design lowers the barrier to adoption across architectures (LLMs, VLMs, diffusion video generators). A key open question is whether the antidiagonal heuristic generalizes to non-causal attention settings beyond the limited video generation evaluation, and whether the DP-based threshold prediction (which requires a calibration pass) remains tractable for very large model ensembles. The method's reliance on a fixed stride and block size also raises questions about adaptivity to inputs with highly non-uniform attention structure.
