## Problem

Transcriptomics representations offer gene-level interpretability but weaker predictive power compared to microscopy imaging, yet collecting fully paired multimodal data is prohibitively expensive. Existing cross-modal distillation methods rely on supervised objectives requiring precise labels or large paired datasets, and no prior approach effectively leverages unsupervised cross-modal alignment for distillation in the low-data regime of weakly paired biological samples (same cell line and perturbation, but different biological replicates). The paper tackles how to transfer rich morphological knowledge into transcriptomics embeddings using only scarce weakly paired data while enabling unimodal transcriptomics inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes bulk transcriptomics gene expression profiles (130K HUVEC-CMPD samples) and cell painting microscopy images (20K HUVEC images), both spanning 1,700 chemical perturbations at three concentrations. Pairing is weak: one transcriptomics sample is randomly matched with one image sample sharing the same perturbation and concentration per epoch, and control (unperturbed) samples are excluded from pairing, used only for batch correction. The output is a transcriptomics-only enriched embedding (dimension d_T=768) produced by a lightweight adapter, evaluated via zero-shot retrieval of known gene-gene biological relationships and transcriptomic interpretability preservation scores on three OOD datasets.

## Method

Semi-Clipped adapts the CLIP contrastive loss for one-way cross-modal distillation: a frozen microscopy encoder (Phenom-1) provides fixed teacher embeddings, while a trainable 3-layer MLP adapter on the frozen transcriptomics encoder (scVI or scGPT) learns to align student embeddings into the teacher space, ensuring unidirectional knowledge transfer without mutual drift. No biological labels appear in the loss; pairing is determined solely by shared perturbation and cell-line metadata. PEA (Perturbation Embedding Augmentation) repurposes batch correction as a stochastic training-time augmentation: for each sample, a random subset of normalization steps (centering, scaling, PCA-based TVN) is randomly selected and applied using a randomly sampled number of control samples, introducing controlled distributional shifts that preserve biological signal while increasing training diversity. A fixed TVN correction is applied to teacher embeddings to focus them on relevant variation. The combination achieves state-of-the-art biological relationship recall on OOD datasets while maintaining transcriptomic interpretability, and GSEA reveals emergent enrichment in cell-cycle and post-translational modification pathways not captured by either unimodal model alone.

## Results

Semi-Clipped+PEA achieves the highest Known Biological Relationship Recall across all three OOD datasets: 23.05 on HUVEC-KO, 19.63 on LINCS, and 39.84 on SC-RPE1, compared to unimodal transcriptomics baselines of 16.51, 12.21, and 25.29 respectively. PEA alone improves relationship recall over no augmentation by 17% (HUVEC-KO), 55% (LINCS), and 20% (SC-RPE1), while combined with all other biological augmentations the gains reach 25%, 69%, and 26%. Transcriptomic Interpretability Preservation is maintained at 53.87, 93.15, and 37.56, closely matching or slightly exceeding the unimodal baseline (52.23, 93.35, 37.75), confirming that morphological knowledge is transferred without sacrificing gene-level interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semi-Clipped+PEA outperforms all label-based distillation baselines (KD, SHAKE, C2KD) on Known Relationship Recall across every OOD dataset, with the largest margins on SC-RPE1 (39.84 vs. 29.02 for KD+PEA and 30.81 for SHAKE+PEA). Among label-free methods, Semi-Clipped+PEA also beats VICReg+PEA on all three datasets (23.05 vs. 20.46, 19.63 vs. 18.12, 39.84 vs. 38.14), confirming that one-way distillation to frozen teacher embeddings is more effective than bidirectional alignment. PEA provides statistically significant gains (Wilcoxon p < 0.05) for every distillation method tested, with the largest relative improvement on LINCS (e.g., VICReg 12.96→18.12, a 40% increase). On Transcriptomic Interpretability Preservation, Semi-Clipped+PEA matches or slightly exceeds the unimodal baseline on HUVEC-KO (53.87 vs. 52.23) and LINCS (93.15 vs. 93.35), while VICReg+PEA and SHAKE+PEA show slight drops on SC-RPE1 (36.33 and 36.15 vs. 37.75 baseline). The paper also shows that label-free approaches (Semi-Clipped, VICReg) consistently outperform label-based ones (KD, SHAKE) in relationship recall, with label-based methods even falling below the unimodal baseline on LINCS and SC-RPE1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the CLIP contrastive alignment framework (Radford et al., 2021) into a one-way cross-modal distillation recipe (Semi-Clipped) where a frozen teacher modality (microscopy via Phenom-1) guides a trainable student adapter (transcriptomics via scVI or scGPT), following the frozen-encoder-with-adapter paradigm established by Fradkin et al. (2024). It departs from standard knowledge distillation (KL-divergence-based KD, SHAKE, C2KD) by replacing supervised output-matching with unsupervised contrastive alignment, and from prior multimodal alignment methods (VICReg, SigClip, DCCA) by freezing one modality to prevent mutual drift under data scarcity. The PEA augmentation repurposes TVN batch correction (Ando et al., 2017)—traditionally a post-processing step—into a stochastic training-time augmentation, a conceptual reframing not seen in prior biological data augmentation work (MWO, scVI denoising, MDWGAN-GP, scGFT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in computational biology: weakly paired multimodal datasets are too scarce for standard multimodal training, yet unimodal transcriptomics representations lack the predictive richness of morphological features. By enabling unimodal (transcriptomics-only) inference after multimodal training, the approach preserves gene-level interpretability while gaining morphological predictive power—a trade-off that matters for drug discovery pipelines. Key limitations include the reliance on random pairing within treatment groups (which may dilute subtle intra-group differences), the single-cell-line focus (HUVEC), and the need for large-scale paired data to validate broader applicability. Open questions include whether the frozen-teacher assumption holds when both modalities benefit from co-adaptation, and whether PEA's batch-correction-based stochasticity generalizes to other omics modalities beyond transcriptomics.
