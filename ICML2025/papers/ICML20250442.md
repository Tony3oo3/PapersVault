## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for Local GD on heterogeneous distributed objectives require stepsizes η ≤ O(1/K) to ensure monotonic objective decrease, a condition far removed from practical training where large stepsizes and non-monotonic loss are standard. It remained open whether vanilla Local GD with a constant (potentially large) stepsize could converge for a specific problem, and whether the resulting instability could actually accelerate convergence beyond the O(1/R) worst-case rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a distributed binary classification dataset partitioned across M clients with n points each in d dimensions, assuming the global dataset is linearly separable with maximum margin γ and all feature vectors have norm ≤ 1. The user specifies a learning rate η > 0 (no upper bound required), a communication interval K ≥ 1, and a total communication budget R. The output is a trained logistic regression weight vector w_R, with a last-iterate objective gap bound of Õ(M/(γ⁵R²)) achieved by choosing ηK = Θ̃(γ³R/M), which improves upon the O(1/R) rate of worst-case distributed convex optimization.

## Method

The analysis decomposes each Local GD round update into per-data-point contributions and defines a ratio β_{r,i}^m that measures how the local gradient magnitude at each step compares to the global gradient at the round's start. For the initial unstable phase, the authors adapt the split comparator technique from single-machine large-stepsize GD (Wu et al., 2024a) by upper- and lower-bounding β_{r,i}^m, showing that Local GD's trajectory can be related to GD's and that an unstable phase of Õ(ηKM) rounds is followed by a transition to stability. For the stable phase, they exploit the logistic loss property that local smoothness ‖∇²F(w)‖ ≤ F(w) decreases with the objective value, yielding a modified descent inequality that permits monotonic decrease even with arbitrarily large η. The key insight is that the gradient bias from taking K local steps before averaging is negligible once F(w_r) drops below O(γ/(ηKM)), and the resulting per-round descent rate of 1/(ηγ²K) combined with the optimal choice ηK = Θ̃(γ³R/M) produces the accelerated R^{-2} convergence.

## Results

The paper proves that Local GD for logistic regression with separable, heterogeneous data converges with any stepsize η > 0 and any communication interval K ≥ 1. The headline result (Corollary 4.3) shows that choosing ηK = Θ̃(γ³R/M) yields a last-iterate objective gap of Õ(M/(γ⁵R²)) after an initial unstable phase of Õ(ηKM) rounds. This R^{-2} rate improves upon the O(1/R) worst-case bounds for general smooth convex objectives. Experiments on MNIST (M=5, n=200) and a synthetic dataset confirm that larger η and K produce faster convergence despite non-monotonic objective decrease, and that local steps (K > 1) can stabilize training at larger stepsizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Local GD with constant stepsize achieves an R^{-2} convergence rate, strictly improving upon the worst-case O(1/R) bounds of both Woodworth et al. (2020b) and Koloskova et al. (2020), which require η ≤ O(1/K) and cannot exploit large stepsizes. Compared to the Two-Stage Local GD of Crawshaw et al. (2025), the proposed method improves the margin dependence from γ^{-6} to γ^{-5} while using a simpler single-phase algorithm (no stepsize switching). However, the proposed bound M/(γ⁵R²) is strictly worse than the single-machine GD rate of 1/(γ⁴R²) from Wu et al. (2024a) in both the M and γ factors, meaning the theory does not yet show an advantage of local steps over parallelized GD. The R^{-2} rate also beats the lower bound HB²/R + (Hζ*²B⁴)^{1/3}/R^{2/3} of Patel et al. (2024) for general convex distributed optimization, confirming that problem-specific structure enables acceleration beyond worst-case limits. Empirically, the paper finds that increasing K with tuned η can permit larger stepsizes without divergence (e.g., η=256 diverges at K=1 but converges at K=64 on MNIST), suggesting local steps may offer practical benefits not captured by the current theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-machine large-stepsize GD analysis of Wu et al. (2024a) to the distributed setting, adapting the split comparator and gradient potential techniques to handle the additional instability introduced by heterogeneous local updates across K local steps. It also builds on Crawshaw et al. (2025), which proved convergence for a two-stage Local GD variant requiring a small initial stepsize, by showing that vanilla constant-stepsize Local GD converges for any η > 0 without such a warmup phase. The key conceptual departure from prior distributed optimization theory (Woodworth et al., 2020b; Koloskova et al., 2020) is the removal of the η ≤ O(1/K) monotonicity condition, replacing worst-case smooth-convex guarantees with problem-specific analysis for separable logistic regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing Local GD converges for logistic regression without any stepsize restriction, achieving an R^{-2} rate that beats the R^{-1} worst-case lower bounds for distributed convex optimization. However, the bound is strictly worse than single-machine GD in terms of M and γ, and η and K appear only through their product, so the theory does not yet explain the empirical benefit of local steps observed in experiments. Open questions include whether Local GD with K > 1 can provably outperform parallelized GD, whether the γ dependence can be tightened via implicit bias analysis, and whether these techniques extend to Local SGD or two-layer networks.
