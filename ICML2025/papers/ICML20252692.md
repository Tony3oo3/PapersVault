## Problem

Despite a surge of LLM-based node classification methods, practitioners lack clear design guidelines for when and how to deploy LLMs versus traditional GNNs or LMs. Existing benchmarks such as GLBench suffer from inconsistent backbone architectures and implementation codebases, preventing fair cross-method comparisons. This paper asks: under which settings (supervision level, homophily, model size, prompt design) do LLMs genuinely outperform classic methods, and by how much?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All methods consume text-attributed graphs where each node carries raw text (paper titles/abstracts, product descriptions, user profiles) rather than pre-computed shallow embeddings like BoW or TF-IDF. The benchmark spans 14 datasets across academic, web-link, social, and e-commerce domains, with node counts ranging from 191 (Cornell) to 169,343 (arXiv) and homophily from 6.69% (Texas) to 85.28% (Computer). Three learning paradigms are evaluated: semi-supervised (official splits, ~0.3–10% labeled), supervised (60/20/20 split), and zero-shot (no labels, with intra-domain transfer for GFMs).

## Method

LLMNodeBed is a PyTorch-Geometric-based testbed that standardizes backbones (GCN for GNNs, RoBERTa-355M for LMs, Mistral-7B for LLMs) and codebases to enable apples-to-apples comparison of 8 LLM-based algorithms (ENGINE, GNN_LLMEmb, TAPE, GraphGPT, LLaGA, LLM Instruction Tuning, Direct Inference with 6 prompt variants, ZeroG) against 8 classic baselines (MLP, GCN, GAT, SAGE with shallow/LLM embeddings, GLEM, LM classifiers). Over 2,700 models are trained and evaluated, systematically varying the LLM backbone (Mistral-7B, GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-3B/7B/14B/32B, RoBERTa, SenBERT), prompt strategy (Direct, CoT, ToT, ReAct, w. Neighbor, w. Summary), and graph homophily. The key mechanistic insight is framed via mutual information decomposition I(G;Y_l) = I(E;Y_l) + I(X;Y_l|E): LLMs contribute most when structural information I(E;Y_l) is weak (semi-supervised, heterophilic graphs), while classic GNNs dominate when structure is highly informative (supervised, homophilic graphs). Findings include that LLM-as-Encoder offers the best cost-accuracy tradeoff, LLM-as-Predictor requires abundant labels, and incorporating neighbor summaries into zero-shot prompts yields 3–10% gains on homophilic datasets.

## Results

Across 14 datasets and 2,700+ model runs, LLM-based methods consistently outperform classic GNN/LM baselines in semi-supervised settings (e.g., GCN_LLMEmb and TAPE achieve 82–85% accuracy on Cora vs. 71–82% for GCN/GAT with shallow embeddings), while the advantage narrows in supervised settings where LLaGA leads on 5/10 datasets (e.g., 94.16% on Pubmed vs. 90.47% for GCN_ShallowEmb). In zero-shot, GPT-4o with a neighbor-summary prompt achieves 64.99% average accuracy, outperforming ZeroG (55.56%) and LLaMA-8B (53.27%), while LLaGA as a GFM performs poorly (28.53%). LLM-as-Encoder (GCN_LLMEmb) offers the best cost-accuracy tradeoff, training in ~13 minutes vs. ~6 hours for TAPE on Pubmed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In semi-supervised settings, LLM-as-Encoder (GCN_LLMEmb) and LLM-as-Explainer (TAPE) consistently beat classic GNNs with shallow embeddings (GCN, SAGE, GAT) by 3–10% across datasets, with the gap widening on lower-homophily graphs (e.g., Reddit: +4% for LLM encoders). In supervised settings, LLaGA (LLM-as-Predictor) achieves the best accuracy on 5/10 datasets (e.g., 94.16% on Pubmed vs. 90.47% for GCN_ShallowEmb), but the margin over strong baselines like TAPE (93.00%) is only ~1%. In zero-shot, GPT-4o with structural context (w. Summary) outperforms ZeroG (the strongest GFM) by ~9% average accuracy, while LLaGA as a GFM collapses to 28.53% due to its dataset-specific projector. LLM-as-Encoder (GCN_LLMEmb) is the most cost-efficient LLM-based method (~13 min training on Pubmed vs. ~6 h for TAPE), making it the recommended default when computational budget is limited. Among LLM backbones at similar scale, Mistral-7B outperforms Qwen-7B and LLaMA-8B for LLaGA (81.69% vs. 78.71% and 79.30% avg accuracy), and within the Qwen series, 7B/14B models offer the best performance-to-efficiency ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benchmarking effort initiated by GLBench (Li et al., 2024c) by standardizing backbone architectures and codebases across all methods, which GLBench left inconsistent. It builds on the taxonomy of LLM roles in graph learning (Encoder, Explainer, Predictor, Direct Inference, GFM) established by surveys (Li et al., 2023b; Jin et al., 2023a) and representative methods (ENGINE, TAPE, LLaGA, GraphGPT, ZeroG), but departs from prior work by adding heterophilic and e-commerce datasets, a supervised learning paradigm, and systematic ablations over model size, prompt type, and homophily. The mutual information decomposition (Eq. 1) provides a theoretical lens that prior empirical studies lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first actionable decision framework for practitioners: when to deploy LLMs (semi-supervised, heterophilic, or text-heavy settings) versus classic GNNs (supervised, homophilic settings), and which LLM role to choose given compute constraints. Key open questions remain: the limited exploration of LLMs on heterophilic graphs (only 4 small datasets available), the efficiency gap between LLM inference (one case per millisecond) and classic methods (thousands per millisecond), and whether GFMs can close the gap to frontier LLMs like GPT-4o. The benchmark itself (LLMNodeBed) is positioned as the standard reproducible testbed for this rapidly growing subfield.
