## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) typically rely on concept sets extracted from large language models or extensive image corpora, limiting their effectiveness in data-sparse and fine-grained classification scenarios. Text-based concept discovery also introduces modality gaps in the CLIP embedding space, reducing faithfulness of explanations. The paper asks whether interpretable CBMs can be built exclusively from dataset-specific visual concepts generated from very few images (as few as 50 per class).

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and requires only 50 randomly selected training images per class for concept proposal generation (all images for smaller datasets like CUB). At inference, a single RGB image is encoded by a frozen CLIP backbone, projected onto learned concept centroids to produce concept activations, and passed through a linear layer to yield a class label. Additionally, the framework outputs named concepts (via CLIP text-image alignment against a 10,000-word English corpus) and Grad-CAM attribution maps that localize which image regions activated each concept.

## Method

DCBM is a four-step pipeline that generates visual concepts entirely in the image domain. First, segmentation or detection foundation models (SAM, SAM2, GroundingDINO, Mask R-CNN, DETR) extract image regions as concept proposals from a small subset of training images, yielding multiple concepts at different granularities per image. Second, these proposals are embedded in the CLIP space and clustered via K-Means (typically 2048 clusters), with each cluster's median embedding serving as a single prototypical concept, reducing redundancy while preserving diversity. Third, a sparse linear CBM (single linear layer with L1 regularization) is trained to map concept activations—computed as normalized projections of the image embedding onto each concept centroid—to class labels via cross-entropy loss. Fourth, concepts are optionally named by matching their visual embeddings to the nearest text embedding from a common English word corpus. The key design choice is that all concepts are dataset-specific visual regions rather than predefined or text-derived, enabling fine-grained differentiation and OOD generalization without any pre-training on external corpora.

## Results

On the main CBM benchmark (CLIP ViT-L/14), DCBM achieves top-1 accuracy of 77.9% (SAM2) on ImageNet, 52.1% on Places365, 81.8% on CUB, 97.7% on CIFAR-10, and 85.4% on CIFAR-100, performing within 6% of the linear probe across all datasets. DCBM excels on the fine-grained CUB dataset, outperforming all text-based CBM baselines. On the OOD evaluation (ImageNet-R), DCBM-MASK-RCNN achieves the lowest error rate (44.6%) and the smallest IID-to-OOD gap (22.4%) compared to DN-CBM (55.2% error, 38.8% gap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCBM clearly outperforms DCLIP (Menon & Vondrick, 2023) on all comparable datasets: CUB 81.8% vs 63.5%, Places365 52.1% vs 40.5%, and ImageNet 77.9% vs 75.0%. Against LF-CBM (Oikarinen et al., 2023), DCBM wins on CUB (81.8% vs 80.1%), CIFAR-10 (97.7% vs 97.2%), CIFAR-100 (85.4% vs 83.9%), and Places365 (52.1% vs 49.4%). Compared to the strongest visual-domain baseline DN-CBM (Rao et al., 2024), DCBM trails on ImageNet (77.9% vs 83.6%) and Places365 (52.1% vs 55.6%) but achieves the best CUB accuracy (81.8–82.4% vs not reported) and substantially better OOD generalization on ImageNet-R (44.6% error vs 55.2%, with a 22.4% IID-OOD gap vs 38.8%). Against CDM (Panousis et al., 2023), DCBM wins on CIFAR-10 (97.7% vs 95.9%) and CIFAR-100 (85.4% vs 82.2%) but lags on ImageNet (77.9% vs 83.4%) and Places365 (52.1% vs 55.2%). Overall, DCBM's dataset-specific visual concepts give it a clear advantage on fine-grained and OOD tasks, while text-aligned and large-scale pre-trained CBMs retain an edge on general-purpose large-scale datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCBM extends the Concept Bottleneck Model framework (Koh et al., 2020) by replacing text-derived or autoencoder-derived concepts with visual regions extracted via segmentation and detection foundation models (SAM, SAM2, GroundingDINO, Mask R-CNN, DETR). It shares the visual-domain concept philosophy of DN-CBM (Rao et al., 2024) and concurrent works (Kowal et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024), but departs sharply in data-efficiency: instead of requiring large-scale pre-training corpora (CC3M) or full-dataset concept extraction, DCBM generates concepts from as few as 50 images per class. The activation computation follows Yuksekgonul et al. (2023), and the overall architecture remains a single linear layer, preserving the original CBM's interpretability guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCBM offers a practical, low-barrier path to interpretable classification in data-scarce or domain-specific settings where large-scale pre-training is infeasible, reducing concept-generation data needs by ~96% on ImageNet. The OOD generalization gap (22–27% vs. 38.8% for DN-CBM on ImageNet-R) suggests dataset-specific visual concepts transfer more robustly than task-agnostic ones, though absolute error rates remain high. Open questions include whether concept grounding (e.g., via slot attention or image tagging) can eliminate the spurious-correlation artifacts observed in qualitative analysis, and whether the framework extends naturally to post-hoc interpretability or regression tasks. The method's applicability is bounded by the expressiveness of the CLIP embedding space and the quality of the chosen segmentation model.
