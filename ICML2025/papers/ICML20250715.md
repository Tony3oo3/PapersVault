## Problem

Conformal prediction methods guarantee coverage of prediction sets but provide no formal control over their volume (Lebesgue measure), leaving set size to empirical validation. This paper proves that distribution-free volume optimality is fundamentally impossible for arbitrary prediction sets, then introduces a tractable restricted notion—optimality over unions of k intervals—that admits both theoretical guarantees and efficient algorithms.

## Input / Output

In the unsupervised setting, the method consumes n i.i.d. scalar observations and a target coverage level 1−α, producing a data-dependent prediction set that is a union of k intervals. In the supervised setting, it additionally requires a conditional CDF estimator F̂(y|x) (e.g., from quantile regression or density estimation) and a test covariate x, outputting a prediction set Ĉ(x) that is a union of k intervals with both marginal and approximate conditional coverage. The number of intervals k is a user-specified hyperparameter that controls the structural complexity of the output set; when k matches the number of modes of the conditional distribution, the restricted optimum equals the unrestricted optimum.

## Method

The core mechanism is a dynamic programming algorithm (Algorithm 1) that, given sorted data points, finds the minimum-volume union of k intervals covering a target fraction of points in O(n³k/γ) time, where γ is a discretization slack. To convert this into a valid conformal predictor, the paper constructs a nested system of sets {S_j} (each a union of k intervals with empirical mass j/m) and defines a conformity score q(y) as the number of sets in the nested system containing y; the final prediction set is then obtained via standard split conformal quantile selection. In the supervised setting, the DP is applied to quantiles of the estimated conditional CDF F̂(·|x) rather than raw data, and the resulting conformity score q(y,x) is plugged into the distributional conformal prediction framework of Chernozhukov et al. (2021), yielding approximate conditional coverage and conditional restricted volume optimality under a (k,∞)-norm accuracy assumption on F̂. The key insight is that restricting the prediction set family to bounded VC-dimension (unions of k intervals) overcomes the impossibility barrier while remaining practically interpretable and computationally tractable.

## Results

On the Romano et al. (2019) synthetic regression dataset (target coverage 0.7), Conformalized DP with k=5 intervals achieves an average prediction set volume of 0.4660 with empirical coverage 0.7152, dramatically outperforming the best baseline DCP-QR* (volume 1.8854, coverage 0.7054) by roughly 4×. On the Izbicki et al. (2020) 20-dimensional bimodal dataset, C-DP(k=2) achieves volume 3.6774 with coverage 0.7152, competitive with HPD-split (3.6084, 0.7014). In the unsupervised mixture-of-Gaussians setting, C-DP(k=3) yields volume 3.1888 versus 3.8747 for the best conformalized KDE bandwidth, approaching the theoretical optimum of 3.0178.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conformalized KDE (Lei et al., 2013) in the unsupervised setting, C-DP consistently produces smaller-volume prediction sets across all tested distributions (Gaussian, censored Gaussian, mixture of Gaussians, ReLU-transformed Gaussian), and is far less sensitive to its tuning parameter k than KDE is to bandwidth ρ. Against CQR (Romano et al., 2019) and DCP-QR/DCP-QR* (Chernozhukov et al., 2021) in the supervised setting, C-DP(k=5) on the Romano dataset reduces average volume from ~1.4–1.9 to 0.47 while maintaining or exceeding target coverage; even C-DP(k=1) (volume 1.09) beats all interval-based baselines. Against CD-split and HPD-split (Izbicki et al., 2022), which also output unions of intervals, C-DP wins decisively on the Romano dataset (0.47 vs. 1.71–1.76) but is roughly competitive on the Izbicki dataset where the conditional density is smooth and well-estimated (3.68 vs. 3.61 for HPD-split). The paper notes that when the conditional density is smooth and accurately estimable, density-based methods like HPD-split have an advantage, but C-DP remains competitive and does not require density estimation—only a conditional CDF estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributional conformal prediction (DCP) framework of Chernozhukov et al. (2021) and the nested conformal prediction equivalence of Gupta et al. (2022) by introducing a dynamic-programming-based conformity score that directly optimizes the volume of prediction sets restricted to unions of k intervals. It departs from prior volume-optimality results (Lei et al., 2013; Izbicki et al., 2020, 2022) that require consistent conditional density estimation, instead relying only on conditional CDF estimation—a strictly weaker assumption that also handles distributions without densities. The key conceptual departure is the impossibility theorem showing that unrestricted distribution-free volume optimality is unattainable, which motivates the new 'restricted volume optimality' formulation over a finite VC-dimension set family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result provides a clean theoretical explanation for why the conformal prediction literature has lacked volume-optimality guarantees, and the restricted formulation offers the first distribution-free volume guarantee that is actually achievable. Practically, the method achieves roughly 3× volume reduction over DCP-QR* on multimodal regression data (0.45 vs 1.29 average volume), and handles discrete/point-mass distributions where KDE-based methods fail entirely. Open questions include extending to higher-dimensional response spaces, integrating with cross-validation conformal frameworks (jackknife+, CV+), and adaptive selection of k without prior knowledge of the number of modes.
