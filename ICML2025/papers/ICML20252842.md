## Problem

Vector-Quantized Generative Models (VQGMs) rely on a codebook of discrete tokens to generate images, yet it remains unclear which tokens are critical for representing specific visual concepts. Naive frequency-based token selection picks up background and contextual elements rather than concept-discriminative features, obscuring how the model internally encodes concepts and hindering bias detection and targeted editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token-based embeddings E ∈ R^{d×m×m} (e.g., 256×16×16 for VQGAN) extracted directly from the generative process, the full codebook C ∈ R^{K×d}, a set of user-specified concept labels Y, and optionally a binary mask M_mask specifying which spatial regions to optimize. It produces per-token importance scores (TIS) as scalars, concept-relevant token subsets T* (Top-n per image, Top-k per concept), bias quantification via Cliff's δ and mean frequency comparisons, and locally edited images where only masked-region tokens are replaced.

## Method

CORTEX builds an Information Extractor Module (IEM) trained as a supervised classifier on VQGAN-generated synthetic images, mapping token-based embeddings to probability distributions over 1000 ImageNet concepts; its design implicitly follows the Information Bottleneck principle by forcing the network to retain only concept-discriminative token patterns. The sample-level explanation computes SmoothGrad saliency of each token position with respect to each concept, reduces the d-channel gradient to a scalar Token Importance Score via max-abs across channels, then selects Top-n tokens per image and Top-k most frequent tokens across images. The codebook-level explanation makes discrete token selection differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax, optimizing a token selection matrix P ∈ R^{m²×K} over the entire codebook space under a binary spatial mask, minimizing −f_{y_i}(E) + α‖E‖² to find globally optimal token combinations that maximize the target concept's activation. Three IEM architectures (CNN, ResNet with SE blocks, and a lightweight 12-layer Transformer) are trained and compared, with the CNN-based extractor achieving the highest Top-1 accuracy (53.07%) on the synthetic dataset.

## Results

On the concept-specific sample-level evaluation across 1,000 ImageNet classes, CORTEX's CNN-based extractor (CE) achieves a 46.6% accuracy drop on ResNet50 while masking only 42.2 tokens on average, compared to the frequency-based baseline's 41.9% drop with 64.2 masked tokens — a 15% larger effect with 34% fewer masked tokens. The codebook-level Token Selection method outperforms the Embedding Optimization baseline across all four pretrained models; for example, on ResNet18 with CE, it reduces the original label probability by 29.0% and increases the target label probability by 0.162, versus 22.4% and 0.127 for the baseline. In bias detection on DALL-E mini, white-doctor-associated tokens appear 8.55 times per image versus 2.32 for black-doctor tokens (Cliff's δ = 0.456), quantifying a ~4× systematic bias under neutral prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CORTEX against three baselines. (1) For sample-level explanation, a frequency-based baseline selects the Top-100 most frequent tokens in concept images without using the Information Extractor; CORTEX (CE/RE) consistently produces larger accuracy drops (e.g., −46.6% vs −41.9% on ResNet50) while masking 34% fewer tokens (42.2 vs 64.2), confirming that IEM-guided selection identifies more concept-critical tokens. (2) For image-specific evaluation, random token masking is used as a control; CORTEX's high-TIS tokens cause a steeper probability decline across all four pretrained models (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ResNet18, ResNet50). (3) For codebook-level explanation, an Embedding Optimization baseline directly optimizes continuous d-dimensional vectors in the target region then snaps to the nearest codebook token; CORTEX's Token Selection method (using Gumbel-Softmax) outperforms it on every model, e.g., achieving −0.290/0.162 (ΔP_Orig/ΔP_Targ) vs −0.224/0.127 on ResNet18 with CE, indicating that discrete token-level optimization better captures concept-relevant codebook entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends vision model interpretability from the pixel and feature-map level to the discrete token space of Vector-Quantized Generative Models, a representation that prior explainability methods (Grad-CAM, SmoothGrad, LRP, feature visualization) cannot directly address. The Information Extractor is grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000), repurposing it to train a classifier that compresses token embeddings while preserving concept-relevant information, and the codebook-level explanation leverages Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2016) to make discrete token selection differentiable for gradient-based optimization. The work sits at the intersection of VQGM research (VQ-VAE, VQGAN, DALL-E) and model explainability, filling a gap where the codebook's role in concept representation had remained opaque.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORTEX provides the first systematic framework for attributing visual concepts to specific codebook tokens in VQGMs, enabling both interpretability (understanding how concepts are encoded) and actionable applications (quantifying demographic bias, targeted local image editing). The bias detection results—showing white-doctor-associated tokens appearing ~4× more frequently than black-doctor tokens under neutral prompts—offer a concrete, token-level metric for auditing generative models beyond qualitative inspection. Key limitations include evaluation on a single VQGM (VQGAN) with moderate IEM accuracy (~53% top-1), and the bias analysis is demonstrated on DALL-E mini rather than frontier models. Open questions include whether token-level explanations transfer across VQGM architectures, whether the approach scales to video or vision-language models, and whether identified token combinations are stable across training runs.
