## Problem

Vision-based RL agents struggle to generalize zero-shot to novel environments, and current solutions rely on data augmentation or massive datasets that increase computational cost and may not achieve true extrapolative generalization. While disentangled representations have been theorized to facilitate compositional generalization, they have seen limited adoption in RL due to training instability and the difficulty of jointly learning disentanglement and a policy. The paper identifies a missing ingredient—associative memory mechanisms that map OOD latent variables back to in-distribution values—and formally shows that data augmentation is merely a form of weak disentanglement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes framestacked RGB observations of shape (3×64×64), but the framestack dimension k is folded into the batch dimension so the disentanglement encoder/decoder processes individual frames in R^{Bk×C×H×W} rather than stacked images, which the authors find is necessary for effective disentanglement. Temporal information is then recovered by reshaping the batch of per-frame latent vectors into R^{B×k×n_z} and passing through a 1D CNN to produce the final state representation z ∈ R^{B×e} used by the SAC actor and critic. The system outputs a continuous control action, a 12-dimensional quantized latent vector (each dimension mapped to one of 12 discrete codebook values), and a reconstructed image from the decoder.

## Method

ALDA (Associative Latent DisentAnglement) jointly learns a disentangled representation and an RL policy by modifying QLAE's latent quantization dynamics. Instead of the hard argmin used in standard QLAE, each latent dimension is mapped to its codebook via a Softmax separation function: z_{d_j} = Softmax(−β·L₁(f_θ(o)_j, v_j)) ⊙ v_j, which the authors show is equivalent to modern Hopfield network retrieval dynamics where codebook entries are stored memories and the encoder output is the query. This allows OOD latent dimensions to be softly projected back to the nearest in-distribution codebook value without affecting other dimensions, enabling zero-shot generalization. The training objective combines a commitment loss (keeping encoder outputs near codebook values) with a reconstruction loss, while omitting the quantize loss so codebooks remain fixed as task-optimized memories. The paper also provides a formal proof (Theorem 1) showing that any optimality-invariant Q-function under data augmentation requires conditional independence between task-relevant and task-irrelevant source approximations in the latent space, establishing data augmentation as a form of weak disentanglement.

## Results

ALDA is evaluated on four DeepMind Control Suite tasks (walker walk, cartpole balance, finger spin, ball in cup catch) under two out-of-distribution evaluation environments: 'color hard' (extreme RGB randomization of agent and background) and DistractingCS (camera shaking + random background video). ALDA outperforms all non-data-augmentation baselines (DARLA, SAC+AE, RePo) on both OOD environments while maintaining stable, high performance on the training environment despite strong weight decay (λ=0.1) on encoder/decoder. ALDA performs comparably to SVEA (which leverages 1.8M Places dataset images as overlays) on color hard, and matches SVEA on cartpole balance and finger spin on DistractingCS, despite only seeing images from the original task. Results are averaged over 5 seeds with 95% confidence intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALDA outperforms DARLA (Higgins et al., 2017b), the only prior method using disentangled representations for zero-shot RL generalization, on both color hard and DistractingCS across all four tasks; DARLA's two-stage β-VAE approach suffers from training instability and limited state coverage from random actions. ALDA also outperforms SAC+AE (Yarats et al., 2021b), which uses a deterministic autoencoder with reconstruction loss, on both OOD environments, suggesting that reconstruction alone without explicit disentanglement is insufficient for robust generalization. RePo (Zhu et al., 2023), a model-based method learning task-centric invariant representations, is also outperformed by ALDA on both OOD environments, supporting the paper's hypothesis that discarding task-irrelevant information is less effective than encoding it separately. Against SVEA (Hansen et al., 2021), which uses random image overlays from the 10M-image Places dataset, ALDA performs comparably on color hard and matches SVEA on cartpole balance and finger spin on DistractingCS; notably, when SVEA is trained with only the weaker 'color easy' augmentation instead of Places overlays, ALDA outperforms it on both training and color hard environments, suggesting that data augmentation's apparent success may partly stem from placing test data within the training distribution's support.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALDA builds on QLAE (Hsu et al., 2023) for latent quantization-based disentanglement and on modern Hopfield networks (Krotov & Hopfield, 2016; Ramsauer et al., 2021) for associative memory retrieval, combining them within a Soft Actor-Critic framework. It departs from DARLA's two-stage β-VAE approach by jointly learning the disentangled representation and policy, and from data augmentation methods (DrQ, SVEA, SADA) by providing a formal proof that augmentation constitutes only 'weak disentanglement'—a partial factorization rather than a full decomposition into independent sources. The key conceptual departure is the observation that QLAE's scalar codebook quantization is mathematically equivalent to a Hopfield retrieval step, which the paper makes explicit by replacing the argmin with a Softmax separation function to recover full associative dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal equivalence between data augmentation and weak disentanglement reframes a widely-used technique in principled terms and suggests that inductive biases toward disentanglement could replace expensive augmentation pipelines for generalization. However, the method is validated only on four DMControl tasks with a heuristically chosen latent dimensionality (12), and the disentangled representation does not explicitly capture temporal dynamics, leaving open how to handle tasks where temporal structure is critical. The DistractingCS results show significant performance degradation across all methods, suggesting that camera shaking and video backgrounds remain a hard open problem. Broader questions include whether this approach scales to more complex, high-dimensional environments and whether stronger learnable Hopfield models can further improve the association step.
