## Problem

Standard Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) assumes the simulator and real-world dynamics are identical, a premise that breaks down in real deployment due to the simulation-to-reality gap in transition models and reward distributions. While robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP) frameworks provide principled worst-case formulations for value iteration and policy optimization, no MCTS-based planning algorithm with non-asymptotic guarantees had been developed for the robust RL setting. This paper asks whether a search-based planning approach can balance exploration and exploitation under model ambiguity while preserving the O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rate of standard MCTS.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a root state s₀, nominal transition and reward models (P⁰, ν⁰), ambiguity budgets ρ_T and ρ_R defining f-divergence or Wasserstein balls around the nominal models, a discount factor γ, and a rollout policy π₀ for leaf-node approximation. It produces the greedy optimal action at the root after n rollouts, along with per-node robust value estimates V̂(s_h) and robust Q-value estimates Q̂(s_h, a) that converge to their worst-case counterparts. The ambiguity set metric (total variation, chi-squared, or Wasserstein) is a design choice that trades conservatism against nominal performance, and the robust Bellman backup at each node is computed in O(S log S) time via dual reformulations.

## Method

Robust-Power-UCT embeds distributionally robust optimization into the MCTS tree by replacing standard value backups with a robust power-mean operator: at each node, the value is computed as a p-th power mean of action Q-values, where each Q-value uses an empirical robust reward R̂^rob = min_{r∈R̂} E[R] and a worst-case transition operator σ_{P̂}(V) that minimizes expected next-state value over the ambiguity ball centered at the empirical transition estimate. Action selection employs an optimistic exploration bonus C·T_{s_h}(t)^{b_{h+1}/β_{h+1}} / T_{s_h,a}(t)^{α_{h+1}/β_{h+1}} with depth-dependent exponents chosen to satisfy a chain of conditions (Table 1) that propagate concentration rates from leaves to the root. The theoretical analysis recasts each tree node as a non-stationary multi-armed bandit, proving via induction on tree depth that value and Q-value estimates concentrate at prescribed (α_h, β_h) rates, yielding an O(n^{-1/2}) convergence bound at the root that matches standard MCTS. The robust backup operators for TV, chi-squared, and Wasserstein ambiguity sets are made tractable through dual reformulations and covering-number arguments over the reward space.

## Results

The paper establishes that Robust-Power-UCT achieves an O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rate for robust value estimation at the root node, matching the best-known rate for standard (non-robust) MCTS. On Frozen Lake, the Wasserstein-robust variant achieves 58% success rate when planning with p_slip=0.3 and executing at p_slip=0.1, compared to only 15% for the Stochastic-Power-UCT baseline. Under matching conditions (p_slip=0.3), robust variants still outperform the baseline (32–35% vs. 10%), indicating that explicit uncertainty accounting benefits performance even without model mismatch. In the American Option task, standard Power-UCT reward drops from ~5 to near 0 under probability mismatch, while robust variants maintain stable returns across the full execution range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Stochastic-Power-UCT (Dam et al., 2024b), the non-robust power-mean MCTS algorithm. Robust-Power-UCT with Wasserstein and Chi-squared ambiguity sets consistently and substantially outperforms this baseline across all three environments. On Frozen Lake, the gap is largest under model mismatch: at planning p_slip=0.3 and execution p_slip=0.1, Wasserstein achieves 58% success versus 15% for Stochastic-Power-UCT (a 3.9× improvement), and Chi-squared reaches 55%. Even under matching conditions (p_slip=0.3), robust variants achieve 32–35% versus 10% for the baseline. In the Gambler's Problem, Stochastic-Power-UCT converges to an aggressive single-bet strategy that fails catastrophically when the true win probability is lower than assumed, while robust variants adopt conservative sequential betting that preserves capital. In American Option pricing, standard Power-UCT reward collapses from ~5 to near 0 when the execution probability deviates from the planning assumption, whereas all robust variants maintain stable, risk-averse returns across the full range of execution probabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to embed distributionally robust optimization directly into the Monte Carlo Tree Search planning loop, bridging two previously separate lines of work: robust RL (Iyengar 2005; Liu et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2023) which used value iteration or Q-learning, and non-robust MCTS (Dam et al. 2024b) which provided power-mean backup operators and non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. The robust Bellman operator and ambiguity set constructions (TV, chi-squared, Wasserstein) are inherited from the distributionally robust RL literature, while the exploration bonus design and power-mean backup are adapted from Stochastic-Power-UCT. The key departure is recasting each MCTS node as a non-stationary bandit problem under worst-case backups, requiring new concentration analyses for the robust operator that go beyond the standard MCTS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that robust MCTS achieves the same O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rate as standard MCTS is a clean theoretical guarantee that robustness need not come at a sample-efficiency cost, which is reassuring for practitioners concerned about the sim-to-real gap in robotics and autonomous systems. However, the empirical validation is limited to three small-scale environments (Gambler's Problem, Frozen Lake, American Option) with only transition ambiguity tested, and the authors acknowledge the framework is 'overly conservative.' Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous state spaces, how the convergence constants depend on |S|, |A|, and tree depth H, and whether less conservative robust formulations (e.g., risk-sensitive or CVaR-based) can be integrated into the MCTS framework without losing the clean rate.
