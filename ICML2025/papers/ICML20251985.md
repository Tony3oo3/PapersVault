## Problem

Sparse Linear Regression (SLR) is NP-hard, and classical algorithms like LASSO provably fail when the design matrix is ill-conditioned (highly correlated columns) or when the sparsity level is low. Prior VAE theory only covered well-conditioned settings where PCA trivially solves the problem, leaving open whether VAEs can be adapted to handle the non-trivial ill-conditioned and low-sparsity regimes where no polynomial-time algorithm exists.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observation vector x ∈ R^d and a fat, full-rank design matrix Φ ∈ R^{d×n} (d < n) as input, and produces a κ-sparse coefficient vector ẑ along with its support (non-zero indices). The VAE is trained via SGD on x alone in a self-supervised manner; once the correct support is identified by the trainable diagonal matrix, the exact coefficient values are recovered by ordinary least squares. Experiments use d=100 observations and n=200 features (or n=71 for the Riboflavin dataset), with sparsity levels κ ranging from 2 to 60.

## Method

The core architecture is a linear encoder-decoder VAE where the decoder is p_θ(x|z) = N(x; Φ diag[w_x] z, γI) and the encoder has mean μ_z = W_z x with full-rank covariance SS^T. The trainable diagonal matrix diag[w_x] implicitly selects active columns of Φ during training, replacing the explicit ℓ₁ penalty used by LASSO. For ill-conditioned design matrices, a ridge-style preconditioning factor P = (ΦΦ^T + γI)^{-1} is embedded directly into the decoder (p_θ(x|z) = N(x; PΦ diag[w_x] z, γI)), where the same γ simultaneously controls decoder variance and preconditioning strength. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) proves that under the Restricted Isometry Property, the VAE loss has no bad local minima, and as γ→0 the global minimum yields the optimal sparse solution. For the ill-conditioned case (Theorem 4.4), any γ* that makes PΦ satisfy RIP guarantees all minima are global, and the VAE intrinsically learns this preconditioning factor during training.

## Results

On a standard Gaussian design matrix (n=200, d=100), the VAE-based method maintains a higher sparse support recovery rate than LASSO across all sparsity levels κ ∈ {2,…,60}, with LASSO's recovery dropping to zero for κ > 30 while the VAE retains meaningful recovery. On an ill-conditioned Gaussian random walk matrix, the preconditioned VAE (Theorem 4.4) sustains a higher recovery rate than SBL and augmented basis pursuit up to κ ≤ 20, where the other methods degrade. On the real-world Riboflavin dataset (d=100, n=71), the preconditioned VAE achieves the highest recovery rate for κ ≤ 10, outperforming all baselines under the challenging low-observation regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its VAE-based SLR solver against three baselines: LASSO, Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL), and augmented basis pursuit. Against LASSO, the VAE consistently wins on all three design matrices, with the gap widening at lower sparsity (larger κ); on the standard Gaussian matrix, LASSO's recovery rate falls to zero for κ > 30 while the VAE still recovers support. Against SBL, the preconditioned VAE outperforms on the ill-conditioned Gaussian random walk matrix, maintaining a higher recovery rate up to κ ≤ 20 where SBL degrades earlier, and also leads on the Riboflavin dataset for κ ≤ 10. Against augmented basis pursuit, the preconditioned VAE achieves superior recovery on both the Gaussian random walk and Riboflavin matrices at moderate sparsity levels. The VAE's advantage is attributed to its ability to jointly learn the mean and covariance of sparse coefficients (via the trainable diagonal matrix and encoder covariance SS⊤), whereas LASSO only constrains the ℓ₁ norm and SBL relies on EM iterations that can converge to spurious local minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the no-bad-local-minima result for VAEs from Simultaneous Sparse Regression (Wipf, 2023) to Sparse Linear Regression, a distinct and more practically relevant NP-hard problem. The core theoretical tool is Lemma 4.1 from Dai & Wipf (2019), which guarantees that linear VAEs with diagonal covariance achieve optimally sparse representations at γ→0. The key departure from prior work is the architectural choice of embedding a trainable sparsity-promoting diagonal matrix in the decoder (rather than an explicit ℓ₁ penalty as in LASSO) and, for ill-conditioned matrices, incorporating a ridge-based preconditioner P=(ΦΦᵀ+γI)⁻¹ directly into the VAE loss, unifying preconditioning and decoder variance in a single trainable parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant for domains where design matrices are inherently ill-conditioned (genomics, signal processing, privacy-preserving ML) and where LASSO provably fails. The empirical demonstration of higher recovery at low sparsity (larger κ) with fixed observations could reduce data collection overhead in biological applications. However, the theoretical guarantees are restrictive: the preconditioned matrix must satisfy RIP with δ<1, and the optimal solution requires γ→0, which is contingent on successful optimization. Open questions include whether a theoretical explanation exists for the observed low-sparsity advantage, whether the preconditioning mechanism generalizes to all ill-conditioned matrix structures (some of which are connected to lattice-hardness), and what sample complexity bounds apply to VAE-based SLR recovery.
