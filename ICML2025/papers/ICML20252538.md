## Problem

Existing dual-channel spoken dialogue models either learn a conditional distribution p(S^b|S^a) that breaks speaker-independence, require additional encoders or VAD modules, or rely on non-decoder-only architectures. No prior work has demonstrated a decoder-only, speaker-independent, VAD-free approach that directly models the joint distribution of both speakers while maintaining a single KV cache for efficient real-time inference.

## Input / Output

The model consumes dual-channel speech (two speakers recorded on separate channels) quantized into discrete RVQ tokens at 40 tokens/sec with codebook size 4096 and D residual depth levels, arranged as interleaved token pairs (s_t^a, s_t^b). At inference, streaming single-speaker user input is fed in fixed-size chunks of λ tokens, and the model generates the assistant's response tokens chunk-by-chunk, which are decoded by a multi-speaker HiFi-GAN vocoder. The pair-wise structure preserves strict time alignment between the two channels, a property lost in sequential turn-based generation.

## Method

NTPP replaces next-token prediction with next-token-pair prediction: at each time step t, the model jointly predicts (s_t^a, s_t^b) conditioned on all prior pairs, learning the joint distribution p(S^a, S^b) rather than a conditional. This is implemented in a decoder-only transformer with two key modifications: (1) a token pair embedding that concatenates both speakers' codebook embeddings and adds a one-hot channel embedding to distinguish speaker identity while sharing the same RoPE positional encoding, and (2) a pair-wise causal mask where the 2×2 diagonal blocks only unmask diagonal entries, preventing same-time-step cross-attention between s_t^a and s_t^b. For RVQ tokenizers, the 2D code array is flattened per time step, a cyclic depth embedding (sin/cos) identifies residual depth, and the causal mask is extended to 2D×2D blocks. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: ~140K hours of single-channel speech pretraining (textless, no ASR transcripts) followed by 2,200 hours of Fisher dual-channel fine-tuning, yielding speaker-independence, no VAD dependency, and a single KV cache.

## Results

On human evaluation (5-point MOS), NTPP achieves 3.95 (±0.04) Meaningfulness and 4.15 (±0.06) Naturalness overall, outperforming Moshi (3.90/3.95), SyncLLM (3.85/4.10), and dGSLM (1.38/3.85). On the out-of-distribution CANDOR test set, NTPP scores 4.05 (±0.04) Meaningfulness and 4.05 (±0.10) Naturalness, again leading all baselines. For turn-taking statistics on Fisher, NTPP (T=0.9) achieves the lowest mean absolute deviation in overlap occurrences (0.9/min) and overlap duration (1.9 s/min), while NTPP (T=0.5) achieves the lowest IPU duration deviation (2.9 s/min). NTPP also demonstrates the strongest speaker-independence, with near-zero metric variation under speaker-swapped inference on the training set (e.g., |ΔIPU| = 0.03) versus 0.43 for Moshi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTPP outperforms dGSLM across all evaluated dimensions: turn-taking statistics (lower |Δ| on all four event types), human evaluation (3.95 vs. 1.38 Meaningfulness; 4.15 vs. 3.85 Naturalness), and speaker-independence (near-zero variation vs. 0.05–0.21 for dGSLM). Against Moshi, NTPP achieves higher Naturalness (4.15 vs. 3.95) and Meaningfulness (3.95 vs. 3.90) overall, a substantially larger advantage on Fisher Naturalness (4.42 vs. 3.90), and far superior speaker-independence (e.g., 0.18 vs. 0.43 |ΔIPU| on test). NTPP also beats Moshi on inference latency, maintaining response times below the 220 ms perceptual threshold across all conversation rounds while Moshi degrades linearly. Compared to SyncLLM, NTPP edges ahead on both Meaningfulness (3.95 vs. 3.85) and Naturalness (4.15 vs. 4.10) overall, with a more pronounced gap on Fisher Naturalness (4.42 vs. 4.33). Against LSLM, NTPP achieves lower mean absolute deviations on all turn-taking event metrics (e.g., 1.3 vs. 2.2 for |ΔIPU| occurrences at T=0.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of dual-channel spoken dialogue generation initiated by dGSLM (Siamese encoder-decoder, joint distribution), LSLM (decoder-only but conditional distribution), and Moshi (RQ-transformer, conditional distribution). NTPP departs from all three by achieving speaker-independent joint distribution modeling p(S^a, S^b) within a pure decoder-only transformer, using a token-pair prediction objective with pair-wise causal masking that prevents same-timestep cross-channel attention. The approach repurposes standard LLM components (RoPE, KV cache, decoder-only attention) with minimal modifications—adding a channel embedding and adjusting the diagonal masking blocks—rather than introducing new architectural primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NTPP provides a practical, efficient recipe for real-time full-duplex spoken dialogue: a single KV cache, no VAD module, and no auxiliary encoder reduce both memory and latency, which becomes increasingly critical as conversation turns grow. The main open limitation is data scarcity—large-scale dual-channel speech corpora are far harder to obtain than single-channel audio, and the authors acknowledge this as the primary bottleneck for scaling. Future directions include synthetic dual-channel data generation and extending the paradigm to more than two speakers, which would be natural given the token-pair formulation generalizes to token-tuples.
