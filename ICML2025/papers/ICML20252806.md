## Problem

Existing contrastive multi-view clustering methods implicitly assume balanced class distributions, yet real-world multi-view data (e.g., wildlife monitoring, medical imaging) is predominantly imbalanced. This causes two coupled failures: the model cannot perceive the underlying imbalanced class structure, and minority samples suffer representation degradation because contrastive learning biases toward majority classes. PROTOCOL is the first systematic study addressing both challenges in an unsupervised multi-view setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N samples across V views, where each view v has its own feature dimensionality D_v (e.g., 784 and 256 for Hdigit; 64, 225, 144, 73, 128 for NUS-WIDE), along with the number of clusters K and an imbalance ratio R that controls the min/max cluster size ratio. It outputs a soft POT label matrix T ∈ R^{N×K} (each entry T_{i,k} is the probability of sample i belonging to class k) and a consensus representation U ∈ R^{N×d} that integrates all views. The imbalance ratio R is kept consistent across views, and the method operates entirely unsupervised without ground-truth labels.

## Method

PROTOCOL is a three-stage framework: (1) view-specific autoencoder pre-training for basic representation, (2) transformer-based structure-aware multi-view contrastive alignment, and (3) imbalanced learning via POT-guided contrastive learning. The key innovation is reformulating imbalanced self-labeling as a combined Partial OT + Unbalanced OT problem, where a progressive mass constraint λ (ramped via a sigmoid schedule) controls how many samples are assigned, and a weighted KL divergence on column marginals enforces the imbalanced class distribution. The transport plan is solved efficiently via a Sinkhorn-like scaling algorithm with a virtual cluster absorbing unassigned mass. A two-level rebalancing strategy then mitigates minority degradation: feature-level logit adjustment (η_i = −log p(T_i*)) boosts under-represented samples in the contrastive denominator, while class-level frequency-weighted prototypes (w_c = n_c/Σn_k) rebalance the class alignment loss.

## Results

Under the most severe imbalance (R=0.1), PROTOCOL achieves 89.2% ACC and 91.4% NMI on Hdigit, 84.6% ACC and 90.3% NMI on Fashion-MNIST, 79.1% ACC on Caltech-101, and 86.1% ACC and 93.0% NMI on CIFAR-10, outperforming the best baseline by 6.7% ACC on Hdigit and 4.8% ACC on Caltech. Under milder imbalance (R=0.5 and R=0.9), PROTOCOL maintains its lead, reaching 98.7% ACC on Fashion (R=0.5) and 99.3% ACC on CIFAR-10 (R=0.9). The method consistently dominates across all five datasets and all three imbalance ratios, with the largest margins appearing under severe imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROTOCOL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric across all three imbalance ratios. The closest competitor is SEM (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2023), which leads among baselines on Hdigit (82.5% ACC) and CIFAR-10 (85.1% ACC) at R=0.1, yet PROTOCOL still surpasses it by 6.7% ACC on Hdigit and 1.0% ACC on CIFAR-10. MFLVC (Xu et al., CVPR 2022) is the strongest baseline on Fashion-MNIST (80.4% ACC at R=0.1), where PROTOCOL gains 4.2% ACC. GCFAggMVC (Yan et al., CVPR 2023) is the best baseline on Caltech-101 (74.3% ACC) and NUS-WIDE (44.0% ACC) under severe imbalance, with PROTOCOL improving by 4.8% and 3.0% ACC respectively. The adversarial-robust methods AR-DMVC and AR-DMVC-AM (Huang et al., ICML 2024) and the incomplete-view method DSIMVC (Tang & Liu, ICML 2022) show the largest degradation under imbalance, with DSIMVC dropping to 13.2% ACC on NUS-WIDE at R=0.1. CSOT (Zhang et al., IEEE TIP 2024) and AECoDDC (Trosten et al., CVPR 2023) also trail PROTOCOL by 5–15% ACC depending on the dataset, confirming that none of the existing contrastive or OT-based MVC methods adequately handle class imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends contrastive multi-view clustering (CoMVC, GCFAggMVC, SEM, CSOT) to the imbalanced setting by borrowing the self-labeling-via-optimal-transport paradigm from single-view imbalanced clustering (SeLa, SLA, P²OT) and adapting supervised imbalanced classification tools (logit adjustment, class-sensitive learning) to an unsupervised multi-view context. The key conceptual departure is the joint use of partial OT (progressive mass constraint λ) and unbalanced OT (KL-relaxed marginals) to generate pseudo-labels that reflect the underlying class distribution, rather than assuming uniform cluster sizes as prior MVC methods do. The three-stage training pipeline (reconstruction → contrastive alignment → imbalanced refinement) builds directly on the GCFAggMVC/SEM architecture while inserting the POT mechanism as a bridge between representation learning and distribution-aware clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of class imbalance in multi-view clustering, a gap that limits practical deployment in domains like wildlife monitoring, medical imaging, and multi-sensor systems where rare classes are the norm. The two-level rebalancing strategy (feature-level logit adjustment + class-level weighted prototypes) is a practical recipe that could be grafted onto existing MVC pipelines. Limitations include: imbalance is artificially constructed by subsampling (not naturally occurring), all datasets are relatively small (≤50K samples), the method requires knowing K a priori, and no theoretical guarantees are provided for the POT label quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to incomplete multi-view clustering with imbalance, whether it scales to very large datasets, and whether the progressive λ schedule is optimal versus alternative curricula.
