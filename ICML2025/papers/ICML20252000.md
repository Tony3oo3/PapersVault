## Problem

Existing ensemble combination strategies for out-of-distribution generalization optimize weights on validation data that may not represent unseen test distributions, leading to suboptimal OoD performance. While Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) offers a principled alternative by optimizing worst-case risk over an uncertainty set, it treats all distributions within the set independently and produces overly pessimistic solutions by considering implausible shifts. The paper asks whether ensemble weights can be learned that generalize to unseen test distributions without test data access, while avoiding DRO's excessive conservatism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of m pre-trained ResNet-50 models (trained via DiWA with different hyperparameters) and training data from n distinct distributions (e.g., 4 domains in PACS, 6 in DomainNet, 16 years in FMoW), all as 224×224 RGB images. It produces ensemble weights w on the m-dimensional probability simplex, which are applied at inference to combine model predictions into a final class label. The distribution graph is constructed from Gaussian approximations (mean and covariance) of each training distribution, and the optimization uses in-distribution validation data for 100 (DomainBed) or 50 (WILDS) alternating gradient steps.

## Method

SRM constructs a weighted graph over training distributions where edge weights are 2-Wasserstein distances computed via a Gaussian approximation (reducing cubic to quadratic complexity). Closeness centrality on this graph yields a structural prior p that identifies globally influential distributions. The uncertainty set is defined as mixtures of training distributions whose mixture weights q stay within distance τ of p, replacing DRO's divergence ball with a structure-aware constraint. The resulting minimax problem (min_w max_q Σ q_e R_{P_e}(w) s.t. D(q‖p) ≤ τ) is solved via a KKT-based Lagrangian reformulation with alternating projected gradient updates on the simplex for w and q. This design interpolates between ERM (τ=0, uniform prior), weighted risk minimization (τ=0, centrality prior), and Group DRO (τ=∞), with a moderate τ balancing robustness against over-pessimism.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark (5 datasets), SRM achieves 66.5% average accuracy, outperforming Group DRO (66.2%), ERM (66.2%), and Uniform Ensemble (66.0%). The largest gain is on TerraIncognita (49.5% vs. 49.0% for DRO) and OfficeHome (71.0% vs. 70.5% for DRO). On the FMoW-WILDS benchmark, SRM achieves 51.6% average worst-region accuracy across three train-test splits, surpassing DRO (50.8%) and ERM (51.4%), with the most notable improvement in the Test Before 2004 setting (54.4% vs. 53.2% for DRO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRM consistently outperforms Group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2019) across all DomainBed datasets, with the largest margin on OfficeHome (+0.5%) and TerraIncognita (+0.5%), while tying on PACS (87.1% each). On FMoW-WILDS, SRM beats DRO in all three temporal splits, most dramatically in the Test Before 2004 extrapolation setting (54.4% vs. 53.2%, a +1.2% gap) and Test After 2016 (37.8% vs. 37.0%). Against ERM (Vapnik, 1998), SRM wins on every dataset and split, with gains of +0.3% to +0.7% on DomainBed and +0.2% to +1.2% on FMoW. Compared to Greedy Selection (Wortsman et al., 2022), SRM achieves higher accuracy on 4 of 5 DomainBed datasets (notably +0.9% on TerraIncognita and +0.5% on DomainNet) and outperforms it on all three FMoW splits. The paper also includes Uniform Prior and Laplacian Prior as ablation baselines; SRM matches or exceeds both, confirming that the centrality-based prior is the most effective structural signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRM extends Distributionally Robust Optimization (Shalev-Shwartz & Wexler, 2016) and its group-level instantiation (Sagawa et al., 2019) by replacing the standard divergence-ball uncertainty set with a structure-informed mixture constrained by a graph centrality prior. Rather than treating all distributions within a fixed radius as equally plausible, SRM builds a distributional graph over training environments and uses closeness centrality to anchor the uncertainty set toward globally representative distributions. This positions the work at the intersection of ensemble combination strategies (ERM, greedy selection, stacking) and robust optimization, unifying ERM, weighted risk minimization, and Group DRO as special cases of a single constrained minimax formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to mitigate the well-known over-pessimism of DRO in ensemble learning without requiring test data, and the unified view (τ=0 recovers ERM, τ=∞ recovers Group DRO) provides a useful interpretive lens for practitioners tuning robustness. However, empirical gains are modest (0.3–0.5% on DomainBed, ~1% on WILDS worst-region accuracy), and the advantage over DRO shrinks under severe corruption, suggesting the structural prior helps most in moderate-shift regimes. Open questions include scalability to very large ensembles or high-dimensional distributional graphs, whether the Gaussian approximation of Wasserstein distance remains valid for non-Gaussian feature distributions, and whether the centrality-based prior generalizes beyond the specific graph construction used here.
