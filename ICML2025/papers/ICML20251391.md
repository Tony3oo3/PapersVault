## Problem

State-of-the-art causal discovery methods that handle latent variables (e.g., FCI, RFCI) learn a global partial ancestral graph (PAG) and then enumerate all equivalent maximal ancestral graphs to determine whether one variable causes another. This global approach is computationally expensive and redundant when the goal is to identify the causal relationship between a single target pair of variables. No existing method locally identifies causal relationships between arbitrary variable pairs without assuming causal sufficiency or learning the full PAG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251391/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target variable pair (X, Y) and observational data O (tabular, with no assumption of causal sufficiency, i.e., latent variables may exist between observed variables). It outputs one of four causal relationship classifications: invariant non-ancestor, explicit invariant ancestor, implicit invariant ancestor, or possible ancestor. The algorithm operates exclusively on the Markov blanket of X (MB⁺(X)), requiring conditional independence tests only over subsets of MB⁺(X) rather than the full variable set, which is what makes it fully local.

## Method

The paper introduces necessary and sufficient local characterizations for each causal relationship type using two novel graph-theoretic constructs: the augmented parent set Pa*(X), defined via arrow-collider paths (which blocks all non-causal paths from X), and the augmented undirected neighbor set Ne*(X), defined via circle-collider paths (which blocks partially directed paths). The LocICR algorithm first locally learns the induced subgraph over MB⁺(X) using an iterative Markov-blanket-by-Markov-blanket procedure (extending Xie et al. 2024), then derives Pa*(X), Ne*(X), and clique-restricted Ne*(X_M) sets. It then applies a sequence of conditional independence tests: X⊥Y|Pa*(X) identifies invariant non-ancestry; X⊥̸Y|Pa*(X)∪Ne*(X) identifies explicit invariant ancestry; and X⊥̸Y|Pa*(X)∪Ne*(X_M) for all maximal cliques M identifies implicit invariant ancestry, with the remaining case being possible ancestry. The approach is proven sound and complete, matching the accuracy of global methods while requiring significantly fewer independence tests.

## Results

On four benchmark networks (MILDEW, ALARM, WIN95PTS, ANDES) with 35–223 vertices and 4–10 latent variables, LocICR achieves the highest Weighted F1 (WF1) score across all networks and sample sizes, outperforming seven competing methods. The number of conditional independence tests (nTest) required by LocICR is significantly lower than all global structure-based methods (PC-ITC, PC-IDA, RFCI-LVIDA, RFCI-Zhang, ICD-Zhang). On two real-world datasets (General Social Survey with 6 variables, Wille et al. gene expression with 33 genes), LocICR correctly identifies all tested causal relationships (invariant ancestor, invariant non-ancestor), matching domain-knowledge baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocICR outperforms all seven baselines on Weighted F1 across all four benchmark networks and sample sizes. Against global constraint-based methods (RFCI-Zhang, ICD-Zhang), LocICR achieves higher WF1 while requiring substantially fewer conditional independence tests, since it avoids learning the full PAG. Against global causal-effect-estimation methods (PC-IDA, RFCI-LVIDA), LocICR again wins on WF1 with lower nTest. Against the hybrid scoring method M3HC-Zhang, LocICR achieves superior WF1 (M3HC is excluded from nTest comparison as it is not purely constraint-based). Against the local method Local-ITC (Fang et al., 2022), LocICR wins on WP, WR, and WF1 despite Local-ITC using fewer CI tests in most settings; this gap is attributed to Local-ITC's assumption of causal sufficiency, which causes it to misidentify relationships when latent variables are present. Methods that assume causal sufficiency (PC-ITC, PC-IDA, Local-ITC) consistently show the weakest performance, confirming their inability to handle latent confounders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constraint-based causal discovery literature by bridging two previously separate lines: global structure learning (FCI, RFCI, M3HC) that learns a full PAG before inferring pairwise causal relations, and local structure learning (Fang et al. 2022, Xie et al. 2024) that avoids global learning but either assumes causal sufficiency or restricts to adjacent variables. The key theoretical departure is the introduction of the augmented parent set (via arrow-collider paths) and augmented undirected neighbor set (via circle-collider paths), which together yield necessary and sufficient local characterizations for invariant ancestor, invariant non-ancestor, and possible ancestor relations between arbitrary variable pairs. The algorithm LocICR operationalizes these characterizations using Markov blanket-based local learning, generalizing the MMB-by-MMB approach of Xie et al. (2024) from adjacent variables to the full MB⁺(X) neighborhood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale causal discovery: by avoiding global PAG construction and MAG enumeration, LocICR reduces the number of conditional independence tests substantially while maintaining completeness (identifying the same relationships as global methods). The theoretical contribution—showing that the local Markov property for MAGs, expressed through augmented parent sets, is sufficient to characterize all three causal relationship types locally—is a clean and reusable result. Limitations include reliance on oracle CI tests in the theoretical guarantees (practically approximated via TC for MB discovery), and the worst-case complexity still scales exponentially with MB size. Future work suggested by the authors includes incorporating background knowledge (data generation mechanisms, expert insights) to further reduce the search space.
