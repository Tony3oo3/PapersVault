## Problem

Existing Graph Neural ODE (GODE) models for dynamic graphs rely solely on latent-space correlations to capture inter-node relationships and model node state evolution through linear dynamics, failing to account for the contribution of graph topology to information flow and the nonlinear changes in node states (e.g., sudden traffic congestion under adverse weather). This limits their ability to model complex dynamic systems where both time-varying relationships and nonlinear evolution are critical.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of dynamic graphs with fixed topology but irregularly sampled, incomplete node features at non-uniform timestamps—each node has its own observation set, so at any given time only a subset of nodes is observed. It produces reconstructed node features at all unobserved time points (interpolation) and predicted features for future timestamps (extrapolation), with latent state representations available at any continuous time via the ODE solver. All observation times are normalized to [0,1] to handle misaligned sampling across nodes.

## Method

CSG-ODE is a VAE framework where the encoder computes a mixed edge weight by combining a learnable adaptive adjacency matrix (latent correlations) with an information-propagation-based importance weight derived from the Fréchet derivative of total graph communicability, then feeds density-adjusted graph snapshots into a GCRNN with node-specific convolution parameters to produce per-node posterior distributions. The decoder solves an ODE of the form ż = A₀z + ΣⱼAⱼfⱼ(MLPⱼ(z)) + g(c), where multiple subnetworks with nonlinear activations capture nonlinear state evolution and a GNN-based control term c encodes inter-node interactions. For high-stability scenarios, SCSG-ODE constrains all weight matrices to a shared learnable anti-symmetric matrix, and Theorem 3.4 proves that under commuting subnetwork weight matrices the system is insensitive to initial conditions. Training maximizes the ELBO jointly over encoder, ODE parameters, and decoder.

## Results

On the interpolation task (MSE ×10⁻²), CSG-ODE achieves the best performance across nearly all dataset–ratio combinations, e.g., 0.1550 on Springs (40%), 0.0439 on Motion-walk (40%), and 0.2526 on PEMS08 (40%). On the extrapolation task, CSG-ODE similarly leads, with MSE of 1.3495 on Springs (40%), 0.1393 on Motion-jump (40%), and 1.6607 on PEMS08 (40%), outperforming the strongest baseline LG-ODE by 9–50% depending on the dataset. The SCSG-ODE variant achieves near-optimal MSE (e.g., 0.0435 on Motion-walk interpolation at 40%) while halving the standard deviation across 5 runs (0.0056 vs. 0.0092 at 40%), confirming its theoretical stability advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSG-ODE outperforms all five baselines on the vast majority of dataset–ratio settings for both interpolation and extrapolation. Against the strongest GODE baseline LG-ODE, CSG-ODE reduces interpolation MSE by 41% on Springs (0.1550 vs. 0.2628 at 40%), 47% on Motion-walk (0.0439 vs. 0.0823), and 15% on PEMS08 (0.2526 vs. 0.2982); on extrapolation the gap widens to 9% on Springs (1.3495 vs. 1.4861) and 50% on Motion-jump (0.1393 vs. 0.2743). LG-CSODE, which shares CSG-ODE's ODE decoder but lacks the information-propagation weighting in the encoder, is the closest competitor yet still trails CSG-ODE on 14 of 15 interpolation settings and all 15 extrapolation settings. Weaker baselines—Latent-ODE (no node interactions), Weight-Decay (simple exponential decay), and Edge-GNN (edge-feature GNN without ODE)—are consistently 2–10× worse in MSE, confirming that both the graph-structured ODE dynamics and the information-propagation-based edge weighting are critical. The Friedman test (p < 10⁻⁴ across all settings) confirms the differences among the seven methods are statistically significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSG-ODE sits at the intersection of three research threads: the VAE+ODE framework for irregularly sampled time series (LatentODE, Rubanova et al. 2019), graph neural ODEs that embed GNN message-passing into the ODE function (LG-ODE, Huang et al. 2020), and the ControlSynth ODE architecture with multi-subnetwork nonlinear dynamics and a control term (Mei et al. 2024). The key conceptual departure from prior GODE work is twofold: (1) replacing purely latent-correlation-based edge weights with a hybrid that incorporates a Fréchet-derivative-based information propagation measure from network science, and (2) augmenting the linear ODE dynamics with multiple nonlinear subnetworks borrowed from ControlSynth ODE. The SCSG-ODE variant further departs by constraining weight matrices to anti-symmetric form, yielding a provable stability guarantee absent in prior GODE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete gaps in GODE modeling—time-varying inter-node relationships and nonlinear state evolution—that are particularly relevant for real-world systems like traffic networks where weather or events cause abrupt nonlinear shifts. The stability guarantee for SCSG-ODE, empirically confirmed by roughly halving the standard deviation across runs, offers a principled option for safety-critical or high-stability deployment scenarios. However, the O(N³) cost of the Fréchet derivative computation and the assumption of static graph topology limit scalability to large or topologically evolving graphs, and the paper acknowledges that unmodeled environmental factors and numerical ODE errors remain unaddressed. Open questions include whether the information propagation weighting generalizes to dynamic topologies and whether the stability guarantee holds when subnetwork depth exceeds 1.
