## Problem

Classical model-free reinforcement learning with general parameterized policies relies on sampling trajectories of random (geometric) length for policy gradient estimation, a procedure that is fundamentally incompatible with quantum systems requiring deterministic, fixed-length operations. No prior work had coherently embedded a full Natural Policy Gradient update into a quantum state or provided theoretical guarantees for quantum model-free RL with parameterized policies in infinite-horizon MDPs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes three quantum oracles: a transition oracle U_P that prepares superpositions over next states, an initial-state oracle U_ρ, and a policy unitary Π that coherently samples actions under π_θ. Each inner-loop iteration draws quantum samples of fixed-length-N trajectory superpositions |Ψ_θ⟩, from which the gradient and Fisher matrix estimators are computed in superposition via unitaries U_g and U_F. The output is a sequence of policy parameters {θ_k} whose average value achieves an optimality gap of at most √ε_bias + ε, with the total quantum oracle query count scaling as Õ(ε^{-1.5}).

## Method

The key insight is replacing the classical geometric-distribution trajectory sampling with a deterministic fixed-length truncation, which introduces an O(γ^N) bias that decays exponentially with truncation length N while remaining quantum-compatible. The algorithm uses a two-loop structure: a classical outer loop (K iterations) updates policy parameters via θ_{k+1} = θ_k + ηω_k, and a quantum inner loop (H iterations) solves the NPG subproblem by applying QVarianceReduce—a quantum mean estimation subroutine with quadratic sample-complexity speedup—to the truncated gradient and Fisher matrix estimators prepared in superposition. The quantum oracles U_F and U_g encode the per-trajectory estimators into quantum states, enabling quantum mean estimation to reduce variance to target levels Ûσ_g² and Ûσ_F² with Õ(1/σ) queries rather than Õ(1/σ²) classically. This yields an overall sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-1.5}) for quantum oracle queries, improving upon the classical lower bound of Õ(ε^{-2}), with the speedup arising entirely from the quantum inner loop while the outer loop retains classical scaling.

## Results

The proposed Quantum Natural Policy Gradient (QNPG) algorithm achieves a sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-1.5}) quantum oracle queries and an iteration complexity of O(ε^{-1}) for infinite-horizon discounted MDPs with general parameterized policies. This improves upon the classical lower bound of Õ(ε^{-2}) for MDP queries established by Mondal & Aggarwal (2024a). The speedup arises from quantum mean estimation in the inner loop, which reduces the per-iteration sample cost from O(1/ε) to O(1/√ε), while the outer loop retains its classical O(1/ε) structure. The truncation-based deterministic estimators introduce a bias of O(γ^N) that decays exponentially with trajectory length N = O(log(ε^{-1})).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical rather than empirical. QNPG improves the sample complexity from Õ(ε^{-2}) (the classical lower bound for model-free NPG with general parameterization, established by Mondal & Aggarwal 2024a) to Õ(ε^{-1.5}), a polynomial speedup of order ε^{-0.5}. This matches the best achievable rate for quantum stochastic convex optimization (Sidford & Zhang, 2024), which also uses a classical outer loop with a quantum inner loop. The paper explicitly notes that the outer loop cannot be further accelerated by quantum methods, making Õ(ε^{-1.5}) likely the best achievable rate for this double-loop structure. No empirical baselines are compared, as the contribution is entirely in the theoretical sample complexity guarantee under quantum oracle access assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) framework of Mondal & Aggarwal (2024a) into the quantum oracle setting, replacing the geometric-distribution trajectory sampling with a deterministic truncation-based estimator that is compatible with quantum superposition. The quantum inner loop leverages QVarianceReduce from Sidford & Zhang (2024) and the multivariate quantum mean estimation procedure of Cornelissen et al. (2022), while the outer loop retains the classical NPG convergence structure. Prior quantum RL work (Zhong et al., 2024; Ganguly et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2021a) was restricted to tabular policies or model-based settings; this paper is the first to coherently embed the full NPG update—including the Fisher information matrix—into a quantum state using only standard transition and initial-state oracles, enabling general parameterized policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes the first theoretical quantum speedup for model-free, parameterized, infinite-horizon RL, improving sample complexity from O~(ε^{-2}) to O~(ε^{-1.5}). The key trade-off—accepting an exponentially decaying truncation bias in exchange for quantum-compatible deterministic estimation—provides a template for porting other classical RL estimators to the quantum domain. Open directions explicitly noted include quantum actor-critic methods (where classical order-optimal results now exist) and constrained RL in the quantum setting. A practical limitation is the assumption of coherent quantum oracle access to the MDP, which remains far from current hardware capabilities, making the result primarily of theoretical significance for now.
