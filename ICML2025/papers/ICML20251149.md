## Problem

Existing feature attribution methods compute saliency maps in the pixel domain, which discards the multi-scale spatial and frequency structure inherent in signals. For 1D audio and 3D volumes, explanations are further distorted by projecting into 2D pixel grids. The choice of domain for attribution has been largely overlooked, despite signal processing theory indicating that meaningful features exist at multiple scales and are better captured in transformed domains that jointly encode spatial and frequency information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained classifier (any differentiable architecture) and a raw input signal—1D audio waveform, 2D RGB image, or 3D volume—and produces a multi-scale attribution map in the wavelet domain. Each wavelet coefficient at a specific spatial location and dyadic scale receives an importance score, so the output is naturally organized as a hierarchy of structural components (e.g., coarse edges, intermediate contours, fine textures) rather than a flat pixel grid. The procedure is fully post-hoc, requiring only a single backward pass through the classifier with no additional training or perturbation loops.

## Method

WAM computes the gradient of the classifier's class logit with respect to the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) coefficients of the input, rather than with respect to raw pixels. Concretely, given z = W(x), the saliency map is |∂f_c(W⁻¹(z))/∂z|, evaluated via the chain rule as the product of the input-space gradient and the Jacobian of the inverse wavelet transform. Two smoothing variants are provided: WAM_SG adds Gaussian noise to the input before the DWT and averages gradients over n samples (analogous to SmoothGrad), while WAM_IG integrates the gradient along the straight-line path from a zero baseline z₀ to z in wavelet space (analogous to Integrated Gradients), inheriting the completeness and sensitivity axioms. Because wavelets are defined for any square-integrable function in any dimension, the same formulation applies uniformly to 1D, 2D, and 3D signals without modality-specific adaptations. The choice of mother wavelet (Haar, Daubechies, Biorthogonal) controls which structural features—sharp edges, smooth textures, or symmetric contours—are emphasized in the explanation, while quantitative attribution scores remain invariant to this choice for images and volumes.

## Results

On ImageNet (EfficientNet), WAM_IG achieves a Faithfulness score of 0.370, Insertion of 0.447, and Deletion of 0.049, substantially outperforming all pixel-domain baselines (best baseline: GradCAM with Faithfulness 0.061). On AdrenalMNIST3D (3D Former), WAM_IG reaches Faithfulness 0.098, Insertion 0.719, and Deletion 0.621, again leading all competitors. On ESC-50 audio (ResNet), WAM_SG attains the best Insertion (0.449) and Faithfulness (0.197), while WAM_IG achieves the best Insertion (0.436), both surpassing Integrated Gradients (Insertion 0.267, Faithfulness 0.264) and SmoothGrad (Insertion 0.251, Faithfulness 0.184).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAM decisively outperforms all pixel-domain baselines on images and volumes. On ImageNet, WAM_IG achieves a Faithfulness of 0.370 versus 0.061 for GradCAM (the strongest baseline) and 0.000 for Integrated Gradients, while its Deletion score of 0.049 is roughly 2–6× lower than all baselines (0.113–0.303). On AdrenalMNIST3D, WAM_IG's Faithfulness of 0.098 is the only positive value among all methods (best baseline: Saliency at 0.009), and its Deletion of 0.621 is the lowest (best baseline: Saliency at 0.742). On audio (ESC-50), WAM_SG leads in Insertion (0.449 vs. 0.274 for GradCAM) and Faithfulness (0.197 vs. 0.184 for SmoothGrad), though Integrated Gradients retains a slight edge in Deletion (0.047 vs. 0.252). In supplementary comparisons (Tables 4–5), WAM also surpasses GradCAM++ (Faithfulness 0.370 vs. 0.050 on EfficientNet), LayerCAM (0.370 vs. 0.139 on ResNet), LRP (0.370 vs. 0.039), SRD (0.370 vs. 0.002), and Guided Backpropagation (0.370 vs. 0.000) on Faithfulness, confirming the advantage is not limited to the four primary baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends gradient-based feature attribution methods (SmoothGrad, Integrated Gradients) from the pixel domain to the wavelet domain, arguing that the choice of attribution domain has been systematically overlooked. It builds on a small line of wavelet-based explainability work (CartoonX, ShearletX, WCAM) that explored wavelets for perturbation-based or Sobol-based attributions, but departs by showing that standard gradient-based methods can be directly transposed to the wavelet domain while preserving their theoretical guarantees (completeness, sensitivity, implementation invariance). The key conceptual departure is treating wavelet coefficients as the natural basis features for attribution, unifying 1D audio, 2D images, and 3D volumes under a single modality-agnostic framework rather than projecting everything into 2D pixel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WAM provides a practical, post-hoc method that requires only a single backward pass and no additional training, yet yields multi-scale explanations that reveal both where and what a model focuses on. The ability to assess model robustness by simply summing wavelet coefficient importances per scale (recovering known adversarial-training results without complex frequency-filtering experiments) opens a lightweight diagnostic tool for practitioners. Limitations include inapplicability to point clouds, unsuitability of greedy coefficient extraction for listenable audio explanations, and the fact that the method is restricted to square-integrable signals (excluding text). Open directions include continuous/complex wavelets for audio, graph wavelets for unstructured data, and extension to video.
