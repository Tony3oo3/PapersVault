## Problem

State-of-the-art FD-based adaptive subgradient methods (e.g., S-ADA) achieve regret bounds with a linear dependence on the dimensionality d, yielding unsatisfactory guarantees for high-dimensional problems, while also suffering from O(τ²d) per-round time complexity. The paper asks whether both the dimension-free regret bound and the O(τd) time complexity can be achieved simultaneously, and extends the question to matrix-structured decisions where existing FD-Shampoo variants also retain dimension-dependent bounds.

## Input / Output

In each round t, the algorithm receives a gradient vector g_t ∈ R^d (or a gradient matrix G_t^X ∈ R^{m×n} for matrix-structured decisions) and outputs an updated decision x_{t+1} ∈ R^d (or X_{t+1} ∈ R^{m×n}). Rather than storing the full d×d gradient outer-product matrix, the method maintains a compact sketching matrix B_t ∈ R^{d×τ} with τ ≪ d, plus a scalar cumulative escaped-mass ρ_{1:t}, so the per-round space cost is O(τd) instead of O(d²). For the matrix case, two separate sketches L̂_t ∈ R^{m×τ} and R̂_t ∈ R^{n×τ} approximate the row and column preconditioners, reducing space to O(τ(m+n)).

## Method

FTSL integrates Frequent Directions with ADA-FULL under the primal-dual subgradient framework (rather than mirror descent) and adds back the cumulative discarded eigenvalues ρ_{1:t} as a scalar identity term to preserve positive-definite monotonicity of the preconditioner G̃_t = B_t B_t^⊤ + ρ_{1:t} I. The primal-dual update x_{t+1} = −η G̃_t^{−1/2} ḡ_t yields a dimension-free regret bound O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √ρ_{1:T}), eliminating the linear d-dependence that arises from Bregman-divergence trace bounds in the mirror-descent analysis. FTFSL accelerates FTSL by replacing the per-round SVD on B_t ∈ R^{d×τ} with an FFD strategy that maintains a factorization V_t M_t^{1/2} = B_t and performs SVD only on the small 2τ×2τ matrix M_t every τ+1 rounds, reducing time to O(τd) when τ ≤ O(√d) while preserving the same regret bound. For matrix-structured decisions, FTSL-Shampoo applies the same primal-dual + spectral-compensation idea to Shampoo's row/column preconditioners, achieving a dimension-free bound O(√r (tr(L_T^{1/4}) + (ρ_{1:T}^L)^{1/4})(tr(R_T^{1/4}) + (ρ_{1:T}^R)^{1/4})) with O(τ(m+n)) space.

## Results

On the Gisette dataset (5000 features, τ=50, T=2000) and Epsilon dataset (2000 features, τ=20, T=5000), FTFSL achieves the lowest training loss and highest testing accuracy among all compared methods, consistent with its superior dimension-free regret bound O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √ρ_{1:T}). In terms of runtime, FTFSL and Fast S-ADA exhibit significantly lower per-round time (O(τd)) compared to S-ADA (O(τ²d)), confirming the theoretical complexity improvement. The theoretical contribution is the removal of the linear dimensionality dependence d present in S-ADA's bound O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √(d(d−τ)ρ_{1:T})), yielding a tighter guarantee that scales favorably in high-dimensional settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTSL/FTFSL strictly dominates S-ADA (Feinberg et al., 2023) on the regret bound: FTSL achieves O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √ρ_{1:T}) versus S-ADA's O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √(d(d−τ)ρ_{1:T})), eliminating the linear dependence on dimensionality d that makes S-ADA's guarantee unsatisfactory in high-dimensional problems. FTFSL further improves upon S-ADA in time complexity, reducing it from O(τ²d) to O(τd) per round (when τ ≤ √d), which is empirically confirmed by significantly lower runtimes on both Gisette and Epsilon. Compared to ADA-FD (Wan et al., 2018), FTSL replaces the looser Σ√ρ_t term with the tighter √ρ_{1:T} (by adding back cumulative escaped mass under the primal-dual framework), while maintaining the same O(τd) space. Against ADA-FFD (Wan & Zhang, 2022), FTFSL achieves the same O(τd) time complexity but with a strictly better regret bound (√ρ_{1:T} vs. Σ√ρ_t). For matrix-structured decisions, FTSL-Shampoo improves upon S-Shampoo (Feinberg et al., 2023) by removing the m and n factors from the regret bound, going from O(√r(tr(L_T^{1/4}) + m(ρ^L)^{1/4})(tr(R_T^{1/4}) + n(ρ^R)^{1/4})) to O(√r(tr(L_T^{1/4}) + (ρ^L)^{1/4})(tr(R_T^{1/4}) + (ρ^R)^{1/4})), while preserving the same O(τ(m+n)) space complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of adaptive subgradient methods (ADA-FULL, Duchi et al. 2011) and matrix sketching via Frequent Directions (Ghashami et al. 2016). It directly extends the line from ADA-FD (Wan et al. 2018) and S-ADA (Feinberg et al. 2023), which both use FD to approximate the gradient covariance matrix but suffer from either loose regret bounds (∑√ρ_t) or a linear dependence on dimensionality d. The key conceptual departure is moving the analysis from the mirror descent framework (used by S-ADA) to the primal-dual subgradient framework, which is what eliminates the d-dependence in the regret bound. The FTFSL variant additionally borrows the fast-FD acceleration trick from Wan & Zhang (2022) and Chen et al. (2020) to reduce per-round time from O(τ²d) to O(τd).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-free regret bound O(tr(G_T^{1/2}) + √ρ_{1:T}) is a meaningful theoretical improvement for high-dimensional online learning, where prior FD-based methods degraded linearly with d. The extension to matrix-structured decisions (FTSL-Shampoo) addresses a practical gap for neural network training, where flattening parameters into vectors is memory-prohibitive. Open questions include whether the primal-dual framework advantage generalizes to non-convex settings beyond the online-to-batch conversion, and whether the √ρ_{1:T} term can be further tightened or made data-adaptive. The experimental validation, while confirming the theory, is limited to standard LIBSVM datasets and small-scale neural networks, leaving open whether the gains persist at the scale of modern deep learning.
