## Problem

Fixed-length skills in skill-based deep RL inevitably skip critical decision points (e.g., crossroads, subtask boundaries), limiting exploration and slowing policy learning in long-horizon tasks. While the option framework provides principled termination conditions, it is computationally intractable in high-dimensional continuous spaces. The paper asks whether a simple, learnable termination condition derived from task-agnostic demonstrations can identify these decision points without requiring knowledge of the downstream task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes task-agnostic expert trajectories (state-action sequences) collected across diverse tasks excluding the target task; for maze and block stacking these are agent-centered cropped RGB images plus velocity vectors, while kitchen uses 30-dimensional state vectors. The novelty module is pre-trained offline on these trajectories, and the downstream agent receives only the current state observation to select a skill embedding. Output is a variable-length action sequence (up to H=30 steps) terminated either by a learned Bernoulli termination signal β=1 or by reaching the maximum length, along with a high-level SAC policy over the skill latent space.

## Method

NBDI uses the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM) as a state-action novelty estimator, pre-trained on offline trajectories so that high prediction error marks state-action pairs that are rare or have many plausible successor actions. The key decomposition χ(s,a) = χ(s)·χ(a|s) combines state novelty (rare/hard-to-reach states) with conditional action novelty (states with many distinct actions, e.g., crossroads), and a threshold at the top ~1% percentile of novelty values defines critical decision points. A VAE with LSTM encoder jointly learns the skill embedding space, skill prior, and termination distribution p(β|s,a) by maximizing an ELBO that reconstructs both actions and termination points. In downstream RL, the agent operates on an SMDP where SAC selects skill embeddings and the learned termination distribution determines variable skill lengths, connecting to Sutton's termination improvement theorem by terminating when better alternatives are available.

## Results

NBDI achieves 24% success rate on Maze 30×30 and 25% on Maze 40×40, outperforming SPiRL (13% and 9%) by 84.62% and 177.78% respectively. In Sparse Block Stacking, NBDI stacks 1.12 blocks on average versus SPiRL's 0.67 (67.16% improvement), and in Kitchen it completes 3.67 subtasks versus SPiRL's 3.0 (22.33% improvement). Flat RL baselines (SAC, IQL+SAC) fail to solve any of the four tasks effectively, with SAC achieving 0.0 completed subtasks in Kitchen and ≤4% success in mazes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NBDI consistently outperforms SPiRL (fixed-length skill prior) across all four environments, with the largest gains in the 40×40 maze (177.78% improvement) where fixed-length skills most severely skip critical crossroad decision points. Against flat RL baselines (SAC, IQL+SAC), NBDI's advantage is overwhelming, confirming that temporal abstraction via skills is essential for these long-horizon sparse-reward tasks. Compared to Relative Novelty (Şimşek & Barto, 2004) and LOVE (Jiang et al., 2022) in Figure 6b, NBDI achieves higher success rates in both maze and kitchen; LOVE performs comparably to SPiRL in the maze but degrades significantly in the kitchen's higher-dimensional action space. In the meta-RL setting (Figure 9, Table 2), SiMPL+NBDI reaches 96% success by episode 100 versus SiMPL's 59.3%, and achieves superior sample efficiency on unseen target tasks. With suboptimal (stochastic BC) training data, NBDI still achieves 22–28% success in the maze while SPiRL drops to 0%, demonstrating robustness to data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the skill-based deep RL framework of SPiRL (Pertsch et al., 2021a), which extracts fixed-length skills from task-agnostic demonstrations, by introducing a variable-length termination condition learned from state-action novelty. The novelty estimator is the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM, Pathak et al., 2017), repurposed from its typical role as an online exploration signal into an offline pre-trained novelty detector. Conceptually, the approach bridges the classical option framework's termination conditions (Sutton, 1998; Şimşek & Barto, 2004) with modern deep RL skill extraction, while departing from prior variable-length methods like LOVE (Jiang et al., 2022) by avoiding model-based compression and instead using a simple prediction-error-based criterion that generalizes across environment configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fixed-length skills are a widely recognized limitation in skill-based RL, yet practical solutions for learning termination conditions in high-dimensional, continuous-action spaces have been scarce. NBDI provides a simple, plug-and-play mechanism (pre-train ICM on offline data, threshold the prediction error) that substantially improves downstream performance, particularly when the environment configuration changes significantly. Key limitations include sensitivity to dataset stochasticity (random-walk data degrades decision point detection) and the need for manual threshold tuning per environment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fully offline skill execution and whether the novelty-based termination can be learned end-to-end without a separate pre-training phase.
