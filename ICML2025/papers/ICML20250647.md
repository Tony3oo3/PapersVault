## Problem

Standard methods for overcoming catastrophic forgetting (regularization, rehearsal, gradient projection) all depend on backpropagation, which is infeasible under 'gradient bans' such as black-box LLM APIs, hardware without principled backprop, or non-differentiable systems. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether gradient-free (forward-pass-only) optimization can mitigate forgetting, leaving it unclear whether forward passes alone are sufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250647/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone (ImageNet-1K) and class-incremental task sequences (10 stages each) from CIFAR-100, CUB, ImageNet-A, and OmniBenchmark, with no replay buffer permitted in the main setting. Gradient estimation requires only two forward propagations per query (two-point finite difference on perturbed parameters), with a default query budget of q=1, eliminating the need for activation storage or reverse-mode AD. The output is updated model parameters evaluated on average accuracy, final-task accuracy, and a forgetting score (BWT) across all incremental stages.

## Method

ZeroFlow benchmarks seven forward-pass-based optimizers—ZO-SGD, ZO-SGD-Sign, ZO-SGD-Conserve, ZO-Adam, ZO-Adam-Sign, ZO-Adam-Conserve, and Forward-Grad—against their first-order counterparts under two SOTA CL schemes (EASE and APER). Gradient estimation uses Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA): parameters are perturbed by a random vector ξ, and the loss difference at θ+εξ and θ−εξ yields an unbiased gradient estimate with cost independent of parameter dimensionality. The key finding is that ZO methods match or exceed FO methods on forgetting metrics while reducing memory by ~5× and runtime by ~50%, revealing that the noise inherent in ZO exploration naturally promotes flat minima that resist forgetting. Three enhancements further improve performance: Hybrid ZO (coarse FO training followed by ZO refinement in flat regions), historical gradient reweighting to stabilize old-task updates, and random sparsification of ZO gradients to bound variance.

## Results

The headline finding is that zeroth-order (ZO) methods requiring only forward passes achieve performance comparable to or exceeding first-order (FO) backpropagation-based methods for overcoming catastrophic forgetting. For the APER model, ZO-SGD matches FO-SGD within 0.01–0.1% average accuracy across all four datasets (e.g., 82.33% vs 82.31% on CIFAR-100, 90.53% vs 90.56% on CUB). For EASE, ZO-SGD-Conserve surpasses FO-SGD on OmniBenchmark (77.07% vs 74.73% avg accuracy) and ZO-Adam outperforms FO-Adam on CUB (89.49% vs 84.44%). Memory usage drops from 12.08 GB (FO-SGD) to 2.41 GB (ZO-SGD), a ~5× reduction, while per-epoch runtime decreases by ~50% (59.3s → 32.4s on CIFAR-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the APER model, all ZO variants (ZO-SGD, ZO-SGD-Sign, ZO-SGD-Conserve) essentially match or marginally exceed FO-SGD across all four datasets, with differences within 0.1% average accuracy (e.g., ZO-SGD-Conserve achieves 90.62% on CUB vs 90.56% for FO-SGD). For EASE, plain ZO-SGD lags FO-SGD by up to 12.6 points on CIFAR-100 (78.62% vs 91.23%), but ZO-SGD-Conserve narrows the gap to 9.0 points and actually surpasses FO-SGD on OmniBenchmark (77.07% vs 74.73%). ZO-Adam with the EASE model outperforms FO-Adam by 5.05 points on CUB (89.49% vs 84.44%), suggesting adaptive learning rates benefit more from ZO's noisy gradient estimates in certain regimes. Forward-Grad (Baydin et al., 2022) is competitive with the best ZO variants, achieving 77.00% on OmniBenchmark with EASE, though it requires more memory (3.94 GB vs 2.41 GB) due to Jacobian-vector product computation. The Conserve variant (Kim et al., 2021) consistently provides the strongest ZO performance for EASE, while Sign (Liu et al., 2019) offers the best efficiency–accuracy trade-off, matching plain ZO-SGD in runtime while improving accuracy by 4–5 points on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of zeroth-order optimization (SPSA, ZO-SGD, ZO-AdaMM, SignSGD-ZO, CARS, Forward-Grad) and continual learning, where all prior methods (EASE, APER, MEMO, OGD, GPM, SAM-CL, C-Flat) assume full gradient access via backpropagation. While MeZO and DeepZero demonstrated ZO optimization for LLM fine-tuning and from-scratch training respectively, and DECL/DFCL explored API-based learning at the data level, no prior work systematically benchmarked gradient-free optimization specifically for mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The paper departs from the standard CL optimization literature by replacing the gradient feedback mechanism entirely with forward-pass-based gradient estimation, reframing forgetting mitigation as a derivative-free problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in LLMaaS, hardware-constrained, or non-differentiable settings, backpropagation is simply unavailable, yet all existing anti-forgetting methods depend on it. The demonstrated ~5× memory reduction and ~50% runtime savings make ZO-based CL viable where FO methods are infeasible, and the finding that ZO naturally explores flat minima (without explicit sharpness regularization) offers a new mechanistic understanding of why forward passes can manage the stability-plasticity trade-off. Key limitations include the reliance on pre-trained backbones (from-scratch ZO training remains impractical at scale), noticeable accuracy gaps on some dataset-method combinations (e.g., EASE+SGD on CIFAR-100 drops from 91.23 to 78.62), and evaluation restricted to ViT-B/16 with class-incremental learning. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to LLM continual pre-training, whether the query budget can be further reduced via adaptive strategies, and how ZO-based methods interact with parameter-efficient fine-tuning approaches.
