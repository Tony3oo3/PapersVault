## Problem

The minimax rate for estimating the Fisher information of a Gaussian-smoothed density f*φ_t was unknown, despite the smoothed setting being central to recent work on information-theoretic functionals in machine learning. A key open question was whether the debiasing strategies essential for the unsmoothed case (t=0) remain necessary once t>0, and how the rate scales with the smoothing parameter t. The paper reveals a surprising non-monotonicity: small amounts of Gaussian smoothing actually make estimation harder than no smoothing at all, with a critical threshold above which the problem becomes easier.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a compactly supported α-Hölder density f on [-1,1] that is bounded below by a universal constant c_d, together with a Gaussian smoothing parameter t>0. The primary output is an estimate of the Fisher information I(f*φ_t) = ∫(f*φ_t)′²/(f*φ_t) dx, with the sharp minimax rate 1/(√n·t^{3/4}) ∧ n^{-α/(2α+1)}/√t ∧ 1/(√n·t²) across three noise regimes. As a corollary via the I-MMSE and de Bruijn identities, the same machinery yields parametric-rate (1/√n) estimators for the mutual information I(X_t;μ), the smoothed entropy h(f*φ_t), and the unsmoothed entropy h(f), with error bounds that do not diverge as t→0.

## Method

The paper proves that simple plug-in estimators of the form ∫Υ(p̂, ∂_x p̂) dx with Υ(u,v)=v²/u are minimax-optimal for all t>0, in contrast to the unsmoothed case where debiasing is required. Three regime-specific estimators are constructed: for high noise (n^{-2/(2α+1)}<t<1), unbiased kernel estimators of p and ∂_x p are truncated to known bounds; for low noise (t≤n^{-2/(2α+1)}), kernel density estimates of f and f′ are convolved with φ_t and similarly truncated; for very high noise (t≥1), a decomposition isolates a moment term estimated unbiasedly from a residual plug-in term. Upper bounds are established via a first-order Taylor expansion of Υ with careful control of the remainder, exploiting the Gaussian structure to bound the truncated estimator errors. The matching lower bound uses Le Cam's two-point method with a two-parameter (ε,ρ) perturbation localized near the boundary of [-1,1], an idea from Dou et al. (2024) for score matching, where the functional separation is computed via a second-order Taylor expansion of Υ and the key quantity 2g(1)−E[g(μ)|X_t=x] is shown to be bounded away from zero.

## Results

The sharp minimax rate for estimating the Fisher information of a Gaussian-smoothed density f*φ_t is characterized as 1/(√n·t²) ∧ 1/(√n·t^{3/4}) ∧ n^{-α/(2α+1)}/√t, exhibiting three distinct regimes: very high noise (t≳1), high noise (n^{-2/(2α+1)}≲t≲1), and low noise (t≲n^{-2/(2α+1)}). Simple plug-in estimators achieve this rate for all t>0, in contrast to the unsmoothed case (t=0) where debiasing is required for optimality. As a corollary, plug-in estimators of mutual information I(X_t;μ) and entropy h(f*φ_t) achieve the parametric rate 1/√n via the I-MMSE and de Bruijn identities, with error bounds that do not diverge as t→0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Laurent (1997) for the unsmoothed case (t=0), the paper shows that plug-in estimators are suboptimal at t=0 (requiring second-order debiasing to achieve the rate 1/√n + n^{-4(α-1)/(4α+1)}), but become rate-optimal for any t>0 without debiasing. Compared to Goldfeld et al. (2020b), the entropy estimation bounds here do not blow up as t→0, whereas the constant in Goldfeld et al.'s bound (their Eq. 63) explicitly diverges in that limit. Compared to Cao et al. (2020), who propose a truncated plug-in estimator for Fisher information of smoothed densities, the present work establishes sharp minimax lower bounds (via a two-point construction with an extra tunable parameter ρ from Dou et al. 2024) and identifies the precise rate exponents, whereas Cao et al. only show polynomial decay in n with unclear exponents and offer no lower bounds. The paper also reveals a qualitative difference: the minimax rate for smoothed Fisher information actually blows up as t→0 (since the Fisher information itself diverges due to boundary discontinuities of f), and there exists a critical threshold of t below which smoothed estimation is harder than the unsmoothed case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Fisher information estimation literature (Laurent 1997, Birgé & Massart 1995, Bhattacharya 1967) to the Gaussian-smoothed setting proposed by Goldfeld et al. (2020b) to circumvent the curse of dimensionality in empirical measure convergence. The key conceptual departure is that while Laurent (1997) showed plug-in estimators are suboptimal for the unsmoothed case (requiring second-order debiasing), this paper proves plug-in estimators are minimax-optimal for any t > 0. The lower bound technique borrows a two-point construction with an extra tunable parameter ρ from Dou et al. (2024) on score matching, and the I-MMSE/de Bruijn identities (Guo et al. 2005, Stam 1959) are used to transfer Fisher information rates to mutual information and entropy estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that small amounts of Gaussian smoothing (t > 0 but small) make Fisher information estimation strictly harder than no smoothing at all is a notable caveat to the smoothing paradigm proposed by Goldfeld et al. (2020b), which was motivated in part to alleviate statistical difficulties. The parametric-rate estimators for mutual information and entropy that do not blow up as t → 0 improve upon the bounds in Goldfeld et al. (2020b) and have direct relevance to Information Bottleneck analysis in deep networks. Open questions include closing the gap in the lower bound for intermediate t (c < t < C), extending to the case where f is not bounded away from zero, and whether the plug-in optimality phenomenon extends to higher dimensions or other functionals.
