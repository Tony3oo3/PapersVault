## Problem

Serving thousands of LoRA adapters on a single GPU requires constant loading and offloading of adapter weights, which degrades throughput even in optimized systems like S-LoRA and vLLM. The fundamental bottleneck is that storing all adapters in GPU memory is infeasible at scale, and no prior work addresses this by reducing the per-adapter parameter count through joint compression across the collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of n LoRA adapter pairs {(A_i, B_i)} with individual ranks r_i (typically 16) and produces a shared pair of basis matrices (U, V) of rank r that are pre-loaded on the GPU, plus small per-LoRA scaling matrices Σ_i (either r×r full or r-dimensional diagonal) that are loaded on demand per batch. When clustering is used, each of k clusters receives its own (U_j, V_j) pair, and each LoRA is assigned to exactly one cluster. The compressed representation replaces the original B_i A_i product with U Σ_i V^T, reducing per-LoRA storage from O(r_i · (d_A + d_B)) to O(r²) or O(r) parameters.

## Method

The core technique is Joint Diagonalization (JD), which factorizes each LoRA product B_i A_i as U Σ_i V^T with U and V shared across all n LoRAs, solved via alternating minimization: given (U,V), each Σ_i = U^T B_i A_i V is computed in closed form; given {Σ_i}, U and V are updated as top-r eigenvectors of aggregated Gram matrices. Two variants exist—JD-Full (orthogonal U,V with full r×r Σ_i) and JD-Diag (unconstrained U,V with diagonal Σ_i, saving r² → r parameters per LoRA). For large collections (n ≥ 100), a k-means clustering step groups LoRAs into k clusters, each independently compressed with its own (U_j, V_j), so that the shared rank r need not grow with n. Theoretical analysis bounds reconstruction error via the singular values of the stacked LoRA matrix L = [vec(B_1 A_1) ... vec(B_n A_n)], showing error is small when LoRAs are clustered in the d²-dimensional space and large when they are near-orthogonal, motivating the clustering extension.

## Results

When serving over 1000 LoRAs on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, the proposed joint compression (25 clusters, rank 16 JD-Full) achieves a 1.6× throughput improvement over vLLM multi-LoRA with matched GPU memory, while maintaining 80% of the throughput of serving a single LoRA. Compressed LoRAs preserve at least 99% of the original LoRA's Rouge-L performance (relative performance ratio ≥ 0.99) when reconstruction loss is kept below 0.6, and in many settings the JD methods actually outperform the uncompressed LoRAs due to improved generalization. Across 1000 natural instruction tasks from Super-NaturalInstructions, the base model achieves 20.62±18.21 Rouge-L while LoRA fine-tuning reaches 67.80±30.15, and compressed LoRAs maintain performance close to the uncompressed level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vLLM multi-LoRA (Kwon et al., 2023), the proposed compression method consistently improves throughput at every tested scale from 4 to 1024 unique LoRAs, with the advantage growing as the number of LoRAs increases. At 1024 LoRAs, the compressed approach achieves 1.6× the throughput of vLLM multi-LoRA (which allows only 28 LoRAs on GPU at a time) while preserving ≥99% of the original LoRA task performance. The key mechanism is that compression reduces the per-LoRA parameter count, allowing more LoRAs to reside on GPU simultaneously without the constant load/offload cycle that degrades vLLM's throughput. Against TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023b), which consolidates all LoRA adapters into a single merged adapter, the proposed method significantly outperforms: TIES-merging 'significantly compromises performance' because it destroys per-task specialization, whereas joint diagonalization with clustering preserves task-specific scaling matrices (Σ_i) while sharing the basis (U, V). The paper also notes that individual SVD compression (k=n extreme) underperforms the joint methods while only slightly outperforming uncompressed LoRAs, confirming that the shared-basis structure is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's (A, B) matrix parameterization (Hu et al., 2021) and positions itself as orthogonal to existing multi-LoRA serving systems like S-LoRA, vLLM, and Punica, which optimize scheduling and memory management but still require loading/offloading adapters. The joint diagonalization formulation draws on classical matrix factorization and SVD theory, while the clustering extension borrows from k-means-style alternating optimization. Conceptually, the method bridges model compression (shared subspaces, reduced rank) and model merging (the theoretical lower bound corresponds to a fully merged model), connecting to recent work on LoRA merging (Shah et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024) and model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for LLM providers who must serve thousands of user-specific adapters: the paper demonstrates 1.6× throughput gains at 1000+ LoRAs while preserving 99%+ of original performance, integrated into a production serving stack (vLLM + Punica). The finding that moderate (~60%) reconstruction error does not degrade—and may slightly improve—downstream performance is counterintuitive and suggests joint compression acts as an implicit regularizer or soft merger, opening questions about whether lossy compression can systematically improve generalization. Open limitations include evaluation on a single base model (Mistral-7B) and single dataset, unclear scaling to hundreds of thousands of LoRAs, and preliminary (negative) privacy results that warrant deeper investigation.
