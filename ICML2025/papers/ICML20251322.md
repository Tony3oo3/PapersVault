## Problem

Conditional DDPMs for signal restoration rely on a standard Gaussian prior that discards useful statistical information inherent in the correlation between the degraded observation and the clean target signal, leading to slow convergence and sensitivity to reduced sampling steps. Existing remedies like PriorGrad handcraft data-dependent priors using domain-specific rules, which do not generalize to tasks lacking such recipes. The paper asks whether a more informative prior can be learned systematically and jointly with the diffusion model itself.

## Input / Output

For speech enhancement, the input is a 16 kHz noisy waveform and the output is an enhanced waveform; the noisy signal's mel-spectrogram serves as the DDPM conditioner. For image restoration, the input is a degraded image processed in overlapping 64×64 patches (hop size 16) and the output is the restored image. During training, the clean signal x₀ is additionally available to the Posterior Net, while at inference only the degraded signal y is used to query the Prior Net for sampling the initial latent noise ε from a learned zero-mean Gaussian with diagonal covariance.

## Method

RestoreGrad integrates a conditional DDPM into a VAE framework by identifying the terminal diffusion state x_T with the VAE latent variable ε (Proposition 3.1), yielding a unified ELBO with three loss terms: a Latent Regularization term that constrains the posterior covariance, a Denoising Matching term that trains the noise estimator with a Σ_post⁻¹-weighted norm, and a Prior Matching (KL) term that aligns the Prior Net's output distribution with the Posterior Net's. Two lightweight ResNet-20 encoders (93K params for audio, 0.27M for images, <2% of the DDPM) are jointly trained with the DDPM: the Prior Net ψ maps the degraded signal y to a prior covariance, and the Posterior Net φ maps both x₀ and y to a posterior covariance that is more informative during training. At inference, only the Prior Net is invoked to sample ε, and the DDPM denoises from this learned prior rather than from N(0,I), yielding 5–10× faster convergence and 2–2.5× fewer reverse steps at comparable or better quality.

## Results

On speech enhancement (VoiceBank+DEMAND), RestoreGrad achieves PESQ 2.51, CSIG 3.80, CBAK 3.00, COVL 3.14, and SI-SNR 14.74 after only 96 training epochs (4.6× fewer than CDiffuSE's 445), outperforming the fully-trained baseline on every metric. Even halving inference steps from 6 to 3, RestoreGrad retains PESQ 2.50 and SI-SNR 14.65, still exceeding CDiffuSE. On multi-weather image restoration (AllWeather), RestoreGrad trained for 887 epochs (2× fewer than WeatherDiff) and inferred with 10 steps (2.5× fewer) achieves PSNR 30.82/30.83/31.78 and SSIM 0.9159/0.9411/0.9394 on Snow100K-L, Outdoor-Rain, and RainDrop respectively, surpassing WeatherDiff on all three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RestoreGrad outperforms CDiffuSE (Lu et al., 2022) on all five SE metrics (PESQ 2.51 vs 2.44, SI-SNR 14.74 vs –) while requiring only 96 vs 445 training epochs, and also beats PriorGrad (Lee et al., 2022) on every metric (PESQ 2.51 vs 2.42, SI-SNR 14.74 vs 14.21). On CHiME-3 OOD evaluation, RestoreGrad matches CDiffuSE on PESQ (1.54 vs 1.55) but surpasses it on SI-SNR (8.45 vs 7.67) and COVL (2.16 vs 2.15), while clearly outperforming DOSE (Tai et al., 2023) on PESQ (1.54 vs 1.52) and CSIG (2.88 vs 2.71), and non-generative models Demucs (PESQ 1.54 vs 1.38) and WaveCRN (1.54 vs 1.43). For image restoration, RestoreGrad beats WeatherDiff (Özdenizci & Legenstein, 2023) on all three weather types with 2× fewer training epochs and 2.5× fewer inference steps (e.g., Snow100K-L PSNR 30.82 vs 30.09; Outdoor-Rain PSNR 30.83 vs 29.64; RainDrop PSNR 31.78 vs 30.71), and also surpasses TransWeather (Valanarasu et al., 2022) and All-in-One (Li et al., 2020) across all conditions. Against the weather-specific DTPM (Ye et al., 2024), RestoreGrad achieves comparable performance (e.g., Snow100K-L PSNR 30.82 vs 30.92; RainDrop PSNR 31.78 vs 32.72) without requiring a 55K-image pretraining stage. Among the numerous weather-specific baselines in Table 4 (RESCAN, HRGAN, AttentiveGAN, DesnowNet, PCNet, MPRNet, IDT, etc.), RestoreGrad as a single multi-weather model matches or exceeds most of them, confirming the generality of the learned-prior approach.
