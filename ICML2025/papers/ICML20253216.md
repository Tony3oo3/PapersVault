## Problem

Designing cluster-randomized spatial experiments requires balancing two opposing forces: spatial interference pushes toward assigning the same policy to neighboring regions, while spatial correlation pushes toward assigning different policies. Existing methods either assume weak interference (making them inapplicable to moderate-to-large interference), use minimax formulations that are overly conservative across covariance structures, or rely on computationally expensive integer programming. No prior approach simultaneously accommodates strong interference, adapts to the specific covariance function, and scales efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatial grid of R regions with a binary adjacency matrix, a covariate matrix O ∈ R^{R×d}, and iteratively estimated residuals to construct the covariance matrix Σ (not provided a priori). It outputs an optimal cluster partition (selecting the number of clusters m from 1 to R^{2/3} by evaluating the true MSE formula) and a doubly robust ATE estimate. A notable detail is that the edge-weight matrix ω = RW∘Σ⁺ − Σ can be indefinite, requiring a modification to standard graph cut: the algorithm checks whether the smallest Laplacian eigenvalue is zero and uses either the Fiedler vector or the first eigenvector accordingly.

## Method

The paper decomposes the DR estimator's MSE into four terms (design-agnostic, spatial correlation, first-order interference, second-order interference) and shows that interference and correlation drive the optimal design in opposite directions. The key technical contribution is a surrogate objective that upper-bounds the first-order interference term I₁ using the adjacency matrix and the positive part of Σ (avoiding explicit boundary specification), while capturing the spatial correlation term via between-cluster covariance, and omitting the negligible second-order term I₂ to keep the objective quadratic. This surrogate reduces to a graph cut problem with edge weights ω_{ii'} = RW_{ii'}Σ_{ii'}⁺ − Σ_{ii'}, solved via Laplacian eigenvectors (Fiedler vector or first eigenvector, depending on definiteness) followed by k-means. The full algorithm iterates: collect a batch of data, estimate the outcome regression g_i and covariance Σ from residuals, run graph cut for each candidate m, select the m minimizing the true MSE, and compute the DR ATE estimate—converging to near-oracle performance as more data accumulates.

## Results

In the city-scale ridesharing simulator (R=85 hexagonal regions), the proposed Causal Graph Cut (CGC) algorithm achieves an MSE approximately 3.5× lower than all benchmark methods, with MSE consistently decreasing as the number of repeated experiments increases. In synthetic environments spanning four spatial layouts and three covariance structures (constant, truncated constant, exponential), CGC and its oracle variant (OCGC) achieve substantially smaller MSEs than causal clustering (CC), three-net (TNET), and spectral clustering (SC) across all correlation strengths (ρ from 0.1 to 0.9). CGC consistently outperforms the naive global design (GD) by 54% and the individual design (ID) by 72% in relative MSE, and closely matches OCGC performance, validating the iterative covariance-estimation framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CGC against five baselines: causal clustering (CC, Viviano et al. 2023), three-net (TNET, Ugander et al. 2013), spectral clustering (SC, Leung 2022), global design (GD), and individual design (ID). CGC wins against all baselines in both synthetic and real-data simulator settings. Against CC, the key advantage is that CGC handles moderate-to-large interference without the weak-interference and minimax assumptions that make CC overly conservative; CGC's MSE is substantially lower across all ρ values. Against TNET and SC, CGC achieves lower MSE in all four spatial configurations and all three covariance structures, with the gap widening as spatial correlation increases. Against the two extreme designs, CGC improves MSE by 54% over GD and 72% over ID, confirming that the optimal design lies between the two extremes when both interference and correlation are present. CGC closely tracks the oracle OCGC (which assumes known Σ), demonstrating that the iterative estimation of the covariance matrix from repeated experiments is effective in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal clustering framework of Viviano et al. (2023) and the DR estimator of Yang et al. (2024) by replacing the minimax formulation and weak-interference assumption with a surrogate MSE objective that accommodates moderate-to-large spatial interference and adapts to specific covariance structures. The key methodological departure is the construction of a quadratic surrogate (balancing a boundary-interference upper bound against between-cluster covariance) that is amenable to classical spectral graph cut algorithms, avoiding the computationally intensive integer programming used by Zhao (2024b). The theoretical MSE decomposition (Theorem 1) formalizes the opposing roles of interference and correlation in driving optimal cluster boundaries, a structural insight not made explicit in prior design literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5× MSE reduction in the city-scale ridesharing simulator and the clean theoretical characterization of the interference–correlation trade-off provide a practical, computationally efficient tool for spatial A/B testing in marketplaces, environmental studies, and social networks. Key limitations include the omission of the second-order interference term I₂ (acknowledged as potentially non-negligible), the reliance on repeated experiments or prior knowledge of the covariance matrix Σ, and the neighborhood-interference assumption that restricts interference to local neighbors. Open questions include tractable handling of I₂, extension to fully general (non-neighborhood) interference structures, and adaptation to single-experiment settings without reliable covariance priors.
