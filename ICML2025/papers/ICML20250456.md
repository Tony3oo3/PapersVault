## Problem

Temperature Scaling (TS) calibration and Conformal Prediction (CP) are two widely used post-processing approaches for quantifying uncertainty in DNN classifiers, yet their interplay has never been systematically studied. Prior works apply TS before CP without investigating its effect, leaving practitioners unaware that TS calibration can paradoxically increase prediction set sizes for adaptive CP methods (APS, RAPS) while improving their class-conditional coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250456/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the logits vector z(x) ∈ ℝ^C produced by a trained DNN (ResNet, DenseNet, or ViT) on an RGB image, along with two separate labeled sample sets: a calibration set (10% of validation) for optimizing the TS temperature T*, and a CP set (5–20% of validation, disjoint from the calibration set) for computing CP thresholds. The output is a prediction set of candidate class labels C_α(x) with guaranteed marginal coverage 1−α, plus a calibrated confidence score from the temperature-scaled softmax. A key design choice is that the CP temperature T̂ can be decoupled from the calibration temperature T*, allowing independent control of set size versus conditional coverage.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical and empirical characterization of how varying the TS temperature T affects the prediction set sizes of APS and RAPS. The key mechanism is a 'gap function' g(z; T, M) that measures the difference between cumulative softmax probabilities with and without temperature scaling, and a 'bound function' b(T) that depends on the logit gap Δz = z₁ − z₂ and the number of classes C. Theorem 4.4 establishes that when Δz exceeds b(T), the sign of ∇_{z₁}g determines whether TS increases or decreases the prediction set size, explaining the observed non-monotonic trend: for T > 1, set sizes increase until a critical temperature T̃_c (where b(T) is minimized) and then decrease. The practical guideline separates calibration (using T* optimized for ECE/NLL) from CP (using a tunable T̂ scanned up to T_c), enabling users to trade off prediction set size against class-conditional coverage on a per-task basis.

## Results

The paper's headline finding is that applying Temperature Scaling (TS) calibration before adaptive CP methods (APS, RAPS) improves class-conditional coverage (TopCovGap) while paradoxically increasing prediction set sizes. For example, on CIFAR-100 with ResNet50 (T*=1.524), APS AvgSize increases from 5.31 to 9.14 and RAPS from 2.88 to 4.96, while TopCovGap drops from 12.6% to 9.0% (APS) and 11.7% to 7.9% (RAPS). The effect on LAC is negligible (AvgSize changes by <0.1). All methods maintain marginal coverage (MarCovGap ≤ 0.1%) regardless of TS, consistent with CP theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three CP methods (LAC, APS, RAPS) with and without TS calibration, plus Mondrian CP as a class-conditional baseline. LAC is essentially unaffected by TS: AvgSize changes by <0.1 and TopCovGap shifts by <1% across all datasets, confirming that TS's impact is specific to adaptive scoring. APS and RAPS both show the same qualitative pattern: TS improves TopCovGap (e.g., APS on CIFAR-100 ResNet50 drops from 12.6% to 9.0%) but increases AvgSize substantially (5.31→9.14 for APS; 2.88→4.96 for RAPS). The magnitude of the set-size increase scales with T*, being most dramatic for high-temperature models (CIFAR-100, T*≈1.5) and minimal for high-accuracy models (CIFAR-10, T*≈1.8 but accuracy >94%). In Appendix D, the paper shows that TS+RAPS (using the temperature minimizing TopCovGap) outperforms Mondrian CP (Vovk, 2012) on both TopCovGap and AvgSize, demonstrating practical superiority of the proposed guideline over a dedicated class-conditional CP method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established post-processing paradigms for DNN classifiers: Temperature Scaling calibration (Guo et al., 2017) and Conformal Prediction (Vovk et al., 1999/2005), specifically the adaptive methods APS (Romano et al., 2020) and RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2021). While prior works (Angelopoulos et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2022; Gibbs et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2023) routinely apply TS before CP, none investigates the effect of this step on CP performance. The paper also extends beyond Gupta et al. (2020), who studied prediction sets and calibration but only in the binary case, and provides a more complete picture than the concurrent Xi et al. (2024), which omits conditional coverage and explores only a limited temperature range that masks the non-monotonic behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete, theoretically-grounded mechanism to trade off prediction set size against class-conditional coverage in adaptive CP methods by tuning temperature, which is directly actionable in safety-critical applications. The theoretical explanation (Theorem 4.4) via the bound function b(T) rigorously accounts for the entire non-monotonic pattern, giving confidence that the observed behavior is structural rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include extending the theory to conditional coverage (currently only set sizes are analyzed), understanding the effect of other calibration methods beyond TS, and whether the quantile-sample assumption holds under distribution shift. The practical guideline (Figure 4) and its validation against Mondrian CP in Appendix D demonstrate immediate utility.
