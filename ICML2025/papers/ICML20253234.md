## Problem

Classical greedy subset selection algorithms (OMP, CoSaMP, ABESS) rely on correlation-based selection and Wald-T-statistic-based elimination criteria that, from an optimization perspective, capture only the partial one-step variation of the objective function after a feature enters or exits the active set. These criteria ignore the subsequent coefficient re-optimization on the updated support, thereby missing the interaction effects between the newly selected (or removed) feature and the remaining features. The paper asks: what are the truly optimal selection and elimination decisions that account for the complete objective change?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a design matrix X ∈ R^{n×p}, a response vector y ∈ R^{n×1}, and a target sparsity level K, operating iteratively with one feature entering or exiting the support set per step. It outputs the optimal support set S, the least-squares coefficient vector β on S, and the residual r = y − Xβ. A key practical detail is that the new criteria require no additional matrix inversions beyond the Cholesky decomposition already performed for coefficient updates at each step, so the per-iteration cost remains O(|S|³) and the overall complexity matches the original algorithms.

## Method

The paper formulates exact optimization subproblems for feature entry (P1: minimize ‖y−Xβ‖² subject to |supp(β)| = |S_{k−1}|+1 and S_{k−1} ⊂ supp(β)) and feature exit (Q1: same with |S_{k−1}|−1), which allow full coefficient re-optimization on the updated support rather than fixing coefficients as in one step of block coordinate descent. Solving (P1) via a forward matrix inversion lemma yields a closed-form selection criterion (8) whose denominator includes a projection matrix (I − X_S(X_S^T X_S)^{−1}X_S^T), capturing the correlation of candidate feature X_j with the residual in the noise subspace of the current active set. Solving (Q1) via a backward inversion lemma yields an elimination criterion (10) that accounts for how removing feature j reshapes the least-squares fit of all remaining features. These criteria are proved optimal (Theorems 4.1–4.2) and, when meta-substituted into existing algorithms, produce enhanced variants (OP, CoSaOP, OP-ABESS) that retain the original convergence guarantees (e.g., linear convergence rate 0.869 for CoSaOP) while delivering 3–7× improvements in recovery rates and ~0.1 R² gains in sparse regression without increasing computational complexity.

## Results

On the AudioSet compressed sensing task (Table 1), the enhanced algorithms achieve substantial NMSE reductions: CoSaOP improves over CoSaMP by 31–71% (e.g., 1.64E-03 vs. 2.36E-03 on Audio 1), and OP-(A)BESS improves over (A)BESS by 30–91% (e.g., 6.22E-04 vs. 1.41E-03 on Audio 1). On synthetic sparse data at 25% sampling rate, OP achieves nearly 3× more successful recoveries than OMP, while CoSaOP outperforms CoSaMP by at least 4× (up to ~7× at SNR=21). On sparse regression across six real-world datasets, OP and OP-(A)BESS deliver approximately 0.1 improvement in R², equivalent to the gain from selecting 10 additional features, without increasing computational complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three pairs of algorithms across compressed sensing and sparse regression. OP consistently outperforms OMP: on synthetic data at 25% sampling rate, OP achieves nearly 3× more successful recoveries, and on AudioSet it reduces NMSE by 5–19%. CoSaOP dominates CoSaMP across all settings, with 4–7× improvement in recovery rate on synthetic data and 31–71% NMSE reduction on AudioSet (e.g., 6.36E-04 vs. 1.87E-03 on Audio 2). OP-(A)BESS, the enhanced version of the state-of-the-art (A)BESS, achieves 30–91% NMSE reduction on AudioSet and delivers observable meta-gains even in near-saturation scenarios where (A)BESS already recovers almost perfectly. On sparse regression (six real-world datasets), all three enhanced algorithms outperform their classical counterparts, with OP and OP-(A)BESS showing ~0.1 R² improvement; notably, CoSaMP fails on this regression task while CoSaOP remains effective. All enhanced algorithms maintain the same computational complexity order as their originals, with only slight runtime increases from additional multiplication operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the classical correlation-based selection criterion (used in OMP, MP, LARS) and the Wald-T-statistic elimination criterion (used in CoSaMP, IHT, ABESS) through the lens of block coordinate descent, revealing that both capture only the first-step (partial) variation of the objective function after a support set update, while ignoring the subsequent coefficient re-optimization. By formulating exact optimization subproblems for feature entry and exit and solving them via forward and backward matrix inversion lemmas, the authors derive new 'objective-based' criteria that account for the full descent including feature interactions. The resulting meta-substitution framework is algorithm-agnostic: it upgrades OMP→OP, CoSaMP→CoSaOP, and (A)BESS→OP-(A)BESS without altering their structural logic, and extends naturally to Gradient Pursuit (Optimal Gradient Pursuit) for ultra-high-dimensional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The meta-substitution principle is broadly applicable to any greedy subset selection algorithm, offering a drop-in improvement that preserves existing theoretical guarantees (convergence rates, RIP-based bounds) while delivering 3×–7× gains in compressed sensing recovery and near-order-of-magnitude NMSE reductions on real audio signals, all at the same computational complexity. The theoretical results showing that classical criteria fundamentally fail under high feature correlation (Theorems 4.10–4.11) while the new criteria remain robust are practically important for real-world data where features are rarely orthogonal. Open questions include extending the criteria to general (non-least-squares) objective functions, developing statistical inference theory for the new criteria, and applying the framework to structured sparsity and arbitrary greedy algorithms beyond the linear model setting.
