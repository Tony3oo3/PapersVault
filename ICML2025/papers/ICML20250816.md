## Problem

Transformer self-attention's quadratic O(n²) complexity is prohibitive for whole slide images containing tens of thousands of patches, yet existing mitigations—linear attention approximations and fixed local-global patterns—sacrifice the full attention's ability to model context-dependent, long-range patch relationships. The paper addresses how to retain full self-attention's expressiveness while achieving near-linear computational cost for gigapixel WSI analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gigapixel WSI partitioned into non-overlapping patches, each encoded by the PLIP vision-language foundation model into a 512-dimensional feature vector. Under weak supervision (only slide-level labels available), it produces a single slide-level output: a class probability vector for biomarker/mutation/subtyping tasks or a discretized survival time interval for prognostic tasks. The framework is designed to handle sequences of 10,000+ patches within ~1 GB memory, compared to ~37 GB for full self-attention at 100k patches.

## Method

Querent replaces fixed or uniform attention patterns with a query-aware dynamic sparsification in three stages: (1) each spatial region of K patches is summarized by element-wise min/max metadata vectors projected through learnable networks; (2) for each query patch, an importance score is computed as the maximum absolute dot product between the projected query and each region's min/max metadata, selecting the top-K most relevant regions; (3) dense multi-head self-attention is computed only between the query and patches within those selected regions, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(NK). A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) proves the resulting attention matrix approximates full self-attention within a Frobenius-norm bound of (2 + B/√d)·ε under conditions on embedding dimension, region diameter, and spatial separation. The refined patch features are then aggregated via softmax-weighted attentive pooling for the final slide-level prediction.

## Results

Querent achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 11 WSI datasets. On classification tasks, it reaches 0.836 ACC / 0.848 AUC on BCNB-ER, 0.678 ACC / 0.706 AUC on TCGA-LUAD TP53, and 0.835 ACC / 0.956 AUC on UBC-OCEAN subtyping. On survival prediction across 8 TCGA cancer types, Querent attains the highest average C-Index of 0.670, outperforming the next-best methods (WiKG and MambaMIL at 0.647) by 2.3 points. Computationally, Querent reduces memory from 37 GB to 1 GB and FLOPs from 10,000 to 500 GFLOPs at 100k sequence length compared to full self-attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Querent outperforms all 13 baselines (including pooling, attention-based MIL, graph-based, SSM-based, and transformer-based methods) on every dataset and metric. The closest competitors are ABMIL and MambaMIL on BCNB-ER (0.825 ACC vs. Querent's 0.836), HistGen on TCGA-LUAD TP53 (0.668 ACC vs. 0.678), and ABMIL on UBC-OCEAN (0.823 ACC vs. 0.835). Among transformer-based baselines, TransMIL, HIPT, HistGen, RRT-MIL, and LongMIL all underperform Querent by 1–11 points in ACC depending on the task, with the largest gaps on UBC-OCEAN subtyping (e.g., LongMIL at 0.760 vs. Querent's 0.835). On survival prediction, Querent's average C-Index of 0.670 exceeds WiKG and MambaMIL (0.647) by 2.3 points, with the largest margin on LUSC (0.732 vs. 0.711 for WiKT) and KIRP (0.667 vs. 0.651). The consistent wins across all 11 datasets suggest the query-aware dynamic region selection provides a meaningful advantage over fixed local-global attention patterns and linear approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Querent positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work in computational pathology: MIL-based WSI aggregation (ABMIL, CLAM, DS-MIL) and transformer-based long-range dependency modeling (TransMIL, HIPT, HistGen, RRT-MIL, LongMIL). It departs from both linear-attention approximations (which sacrifice pairwise interaction fidelity) and fixed local-global attention patterns (which impose rigid spatial priors) by introducing a query-aware dynamic sparsification mechanism that uses region-level min-max metadata to adaptively select which regions each patch attends to. The theoretical contribution—a bounded Frobenius-norm approximation guarantee for the sparse attention relative to full self-attention—replicates the spirit of JL-lemma-based guarantees from the NLP literature but applies them to the region-partitioned WSI setting with an exponential spatial-decay assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making full-attention-quality modeling feasible for gigapixel WSIs (100k+ patches) where standard transformers are computationally intractable, with the efficiency gain (O(NK) vs O(N²)) enabling near-linear scaling in both memory and FLOPs. The theoretical guarantee, while relying on idealized assumptions (exponential attention decay, spatial separation of regions), provides a principled justification for the sparsification that prior heuristic local-global methods lack. Open questions include whether the min-max metadata strategy generalizes beyond PLIP features, whether the exponential decay assumption holds for all pathological contexts, and whether the approach extends to multi-resolution or multi-modal WSI settings. The method's reliance on a fixed pretrained encoder (PLIP) also limits its applicability as newer foundation models emerge.
