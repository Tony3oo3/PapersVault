## Problem

Existing robust training methods defend against specific, pre-specified attack types (e.g., ℓp balls of fixed radius) and rarely account for the fact that defenders encounter new attacks over time. Naively fine-tuning a model on newly discovered attacks degrades robustness on previously defended attacks, creating a fundamental tension between adapting to new threats and preserving old ones. The paper asks how to improve both initial training and fine-tuning to simultaneously maintain robustness across all known attacks as they are sequentially introduced.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNette) and outputs class labels. The key distinction is that during training, each image is consumed in both its clean form and its adversarially perturbed form, where the perturbation type (ℓ2, ℓ∞, StAdv, ReColor, or ImageNet-UA attacks) changes over time as new attacks are discovered. Evaluation is performed across all known attacks simultaneously, reporting per-attack accuracy, average accuracy, and union (worst-case) accuracy.

## Method

The paper proposes Regularized Continual Robust Training (CRT), a two-phase framework: initial adversarial training on known attacks followed by iterative fine-tuning as new attacks are sequentially introduced. The core theoretical insight (Theorem 3.1) shows that the robustness gap between any two attacks is upper-bounded by the sum of maximal ℓ2 distances between clean and perturbed logits for both attacks, motivating Adversarial ℓ2 Regularization (ALR), which penalizes the ℓ2 distance between a sample's logits and its furthest adversarially perturbed logits. ALR is incorporated into both the initial training objective and the fine-tuning objective, directly minimizing the theoretical bound. For fine-tuning, the method combines ALR with Croce & Hein's error-weighted attack sampling strategy, and the paper demonstrates that adversarial regularization (ALR, VR) is essential during fine-tuning while random noise-based regularization (UR, GR) is only beneficial during initial training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the attack sequence ℓ₂ → StAdv → ℓ∞ → ReColor, FT Croce + ALR achieves 34.92% Union(all) accuracy at the final time step, outperforming unregularized FT Croce (29.44%) by 5.48%. At time step 1 (ℓ₂, StAdv), FT Single + ALR achieves 55.04% Union(known) versus 41.05% for FT Single alone, reducing the ℓ₂ accuracy drop from 24.25% to 7.62%. Compared to training from scratch on all four attacks, CRT+ALR achieves 4.35% higher average accuracy (58.85% vs 54.50%) while using only 26.86 hours of training time versus 51.55–61.09 hours for AVG/MAX training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-from-scratch baselines (Tramèr & Boneh's MAX/AVG and Madaan et al.'s Random sampling), CRT+ALR achieves 58.85% average accuracy on CIFAR-10 versus 54.18% (MAX), 54.50% (AVG), and 54.27% (Random), while using roughly half the training time (26.86h vs 51.55–61.09h); however, MAX still achieves higher Union accuracy (37.44% vs 34.92%). Against the FT Croce fine-tuning baseline (Croce & Hein, 2022), adding ALR improves Union(known) accuracy by 7.7% at time step 1 and 6.72% at time step 2 with only ~1h additional overhead. Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), FT Single+ALR achieves 55.04% Union(known) versus 46.25% for FT Single+EWC(0.5) at time step 1, a 8.79% gap. Against TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019), ALR is more effective: for ℓ₂ initial attack, ALR achieves 48.23% StAdv accuracy versus TRADES's 7.04%, and for ℓ∞ initial attack, TRADES sacrifices ℓ∞ robustness (41.70% vs ALR's 51.49%) to gain ReColor accuracy. Against variation regularization (Dai et al., 2022), ALR and VR show comparable unforeseen robustness gains, but ALR better preserves accuracy on the initial attack (e.g., 70.29% ℓ₂ vs VR's 70.38% at λ=0.5, with ALR maintaining this across fine-tuning while VR degrades more). Random noise regularization (UR/GR) improves initial training generalization but actively hurts Union accuracy during fine-tuning, unlike ALR which consistently helps in both phases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multiattack robustness (MAR) literature (Tramèr & Boneh, 2019; Maini et al., 2020) and unforeseen attack robustness (UAR) work (Dai et al., 2022; Laidlaw et al., 2021), but departs from both by assuming the defender does not know all attacks a priori and can sequentially update the model as new attacks are discovered. The proposed adversarial ℓ2 regularization (ALR) is conceptually close to TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019) but replaces KL-divergence with Euclidean logit distance, motivated by a new theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) connecting robustness gaps between attacks to logit-space perturbation distances. The fine-tuning component extends Croce & Hein (2022)'s quick fine-tuning for ℓp unions to arbitrary attack types, and the problem setting of continual adaptive robustness (CAR) is formalized from a companion position paper (Dai et al., 2024b), making this the first dedicated defense for that setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded framework for patching deployed models against newly discovered attacks without expensive retraining, which is directly relevant to safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and content moderation. However, the approach does not outperform training from scratch on all datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNette), the choice of initial attack significantly affects downstream robustness, and the real-world scenario where the defender lacks access to the threat model or true labels remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether CRT can provably match or exceed from-scratch training, how to select optimal initial attacks, and whether the logit-space regularization principle extends to other continual learning properties like fairness or privacy.
