## Problem

Existing chemical reaction modeling methods extract separate representations for reactants and products before combining them, which loses the reaction-level information about which atoms and bonds actually change during the transformation. Additionally, while 3D molecular structure is widely exploited in single-molecule property prediction, its role in reaction modeling has remained largely unexplored. The paper asks whether a unified graph representation that simultaneously captures reaction transformations and 3D spatial structure can improve prediction of reaction conditions, yields, and types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes SMILES strings of all reactants and products, atomic mapping predictions from RXNMapper, and 3D atom coordinates computed via MMFF94 force-field optimization. These are assembled into a single Reaction Graph where nodes carry atom-type, charge, hybridization, and ring-size attributes, and edges carry bond-type, chirality, stereochemistry, and length attributes. Outputs are task-specific: condition prediction uses an iterative autoregressive decoder with beam search, yield prediction outputs both a mean and a variance (via Monte Carlo Dropout for uncertainty), and classification produces a multi-class probability vector over reaction types.

## Method

The core contribution is the Reaction Graph (RG), a unified graph that merges all reactant and product molecular graphs into one structure. Two novel edge types are introduced: (1) reaction edges, which connect the same atom in reactants and products based on atomic mapping, enabling GNN message passing to flow across the reactant–product boundary and capture what changes during the reaction; and (2) angular edges, which form shape-stable triangles with two adjacent bond edges to implicitly encode bond-angle information in a rotationally and translationally invariant manner without explicitly computing angles. Edge lengths are embedded via a learnable RBF kernel and concatenated with one-hot edge-type vectors, then projected into a per-edge message matrix following the MPNN framework. Vertex features are updated over T₁ message-passing iterations using a GRU-gated residual scheme, and a Set2Set-style LSTM with softmax attention aggregates vertex features into a single reaction-level vector over T₂ iterations. The representation is architecture-agnostic and is paired with task-specific output heads (iterative condition decoder, uncertainty-aware MLP for yield, or standard MLP for classification).

## Results

On the primary task of reaction condition prediction, Reaction Graph (RG) achieves a top-1 accuracy of 0.325 on USPTO-Condition and 0.392 on Pistachio-Condition, outperforming all 12 baselines across all top-k metrics. On reaction yield prediction, RG attains the highest R² on 6 of 8 datasets, with particularly large gains on the challenging B-H-4 split (0.68 vs. 0.56 for the next best) and Subgram (0.216 vs. 0.206). On reaction classification, RG reaches 99.9% accuracy on USPTO-TPL and 98.7% on the more complex Pistachio-Type, surpassing the prior best (T5Chem) by 66.6% in error reduction on USPTO-TPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RG decisively outperforms all baselines on both condition prediction datasets. Against fingerprint-based CRM, RG improves top-1 accuracy by 25.0% on USPTO (0.325 vs. 0.260) and 18.8% on Pistachio (0.392 vs. 0.330). Against the SMILES-based Transformer Parrot, RG gains 20.8% on USPTO and 12.0% on Pistachio. The largest margins are over 2D molecular-graph methods: RG surpasses AR-GCN by 122.6% and CIMG by 76.6% in top-1 on USPTO. Among graph-based reaction representations, RG improves over D-MPNN (CGR) by 63.6% on USPTO and 51.0% on Pistachio, and over Rxn Hypergraph by 52.6% and 36.1% respectively. On yield prediction, RG ties or beats UGNN on all B-H splits and S-M, with the most dramatic gain on B-H-4 (0.68 vs. 0.50, +36%), and leads on Gram (0.129 vs. 0.117) and Subgram (0.216 vs. 0.190). On classification, RG exceeds T5Chem by 0.4% ACC on USPTO-TPL (0.999 vs. 0.995) and by 1.1% on Pistachio-Type (0.987 vs. 0.976), while also outperforming D-MPNN and Rxn Hypergraph on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MPNN message-passing framework (Gilmer et al., 2017) and Set2Set aggregation (Vinyals et al., 2016) into a unified Reaction Graph that connects reactant and product atoms via reaction edges derived from atomic mapping, departing from prior approaches (CGR, Rxn Hypergraph) that model reactants and products separately or superimpose them. The key conceptual departure is the angular edge: rather than explicitly encoding bond angles (as in DimeNet/GemNet), the paper introduces a diagonal edge whose length implicitly encodes the angle through triangle geometry, arguing that reaction properties are conformation-invariant and thus only bond lengths and angles (not torsion angles or pairwise distances) are needed. This positions the work at the intersection of reaction representation learning and geometric deep learning, but with a deliberately minimal 3D encoding tailored to invariance rather than equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Reaction Graph provides a practical, architecture-agnostic representation that could benefit from future GNN backbone improvements, and the angular edge trick offers a reusable, low-cost way to inject 3D information into graph models without expensive equivariant operations. However, the method's performance is bounded by the quality of upstream components (MMFF94 for 3D coordinates, RXNMapper for atomic mapping), and the paper acknowledges that better 3D structure prediction would further enhance results. An open question is whether the conformation-invariance assumption (excluding torsion angles) holds universally across all reaction types, and whether the representation scales to multi-step reaction sequences or mechanistic modeling beyond single-step transformations.
