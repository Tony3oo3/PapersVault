## Problem

Existing prompt-based defenses for adversarial robustness operate on mixed patterns (pixel or broad frequency domains) that cannot explicitly capture specific semantic structures and textures. Since the phase spectrum encodes structural information and the amplitude spectrum encodes texture information, and the two have different impacts on model robustness, no prior method has disentangled and separately prompted on these spectra to stabilize predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252795/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, normalized to [0,1]) that may contain adversarial perturbations, along with the model's predicted class label used to select the corresponding class-specific prompts. The image is converted to the frequency domain via DFT, the selected phase and amplitude prompts are added to the respective spectra, and the result is inverse-transformed back to pixel space before being fed to the frozen target model, which outputs a class label.

## Method

PAP learns one phase-level prompt and one amplitude-level prompt per class (initialized from a randomly sampled natural example's spectra) and trains them with a composite loss combining adversarial/natural classification, pixel-level reconstruction, and a data-prompt mismatching term that penalizes misclassification when prompts from the wrong class are applied. A key mechanism is an adaptive weighting strategy: every 5 epochs, the amplitude-level prompt weight is updated by the ratio of robust accuracy under amplitude-only prompting to that under phase-only prompting, reflecting their differing contributions to robustness. At test time, instead of traversing all class prompts as in C-AVP, the method selects prompts directly from the model's predicted label, making inference efficient even on large-class datasets like Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes). The learned prompts are model-agnostic and can be transferred across architectures (ResNet18, WRN28-10, VGG19, Swin Transformer) without retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a naturally pre-trained ResNet18, PAP achieves 37.34% robust accuracy under AutoAttack (vs. 0.61% for C-AVP and 0.44% for Freq), 80.27% under C&W, and 66.22% under DDN. For adversarially pre-trained (AT) ResNet18, PAP reaches 52.31% AA, 66.66% C&W, and 60.29% DDN robust accuracy, substantially outperforming both baselines on C&W and DDN. On Tiny-ImageNet with WRN28-10, PAP improves C&W robustness from 5.83% (C-AVP) and 14.82% (Freq) to 42.14% under NAT pre-training. The method also transfers well across architectures, e.g., achieving 81.33% C&W robustness when applied to VGG19 and 70.29% to WRN28-10 on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAP decisively outperforms both baselines on C&W and DDN attacks across all pre-training settings. On CIFAR-10 with NAT ResNet18, PAP achieves 80.27% C&W robustness versus 1.93% (C-AVP) and 11.57% (Freq), and 66.22% DDN versus 1.05% and 4.60% respectively. For AT-pretrained models, PAP reaches 66.66% C&W and 60.29% DDN, compared to 11.00%/10.35% (C-AVP) and 35.67%/35.12% (Freq). On AutoAttack, PAP also leads: 52.31% vs. 45.79% (C-AVP) and 51.50% (Freq) under AT on CIFAR-10. The main trade-off is natural accuracy: PAP sacrifices ~7.7% clean accuracy on NAT CIFAR-10 (87.12% vs. 94.83%), while C-AVP and Freq preserve clean accuracy but offer negligible robustness gains. On Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), PAP's advantage is even more pronounced, e.g., 42.14% C&W vs. 5.83% (C-AVP) and 14.82% (Freq) under NAT, highlighting the efficiency benefit of selecting prompts by predicted label rather than traversing all 200 class prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based adversarial defense line initiated by C-AVP (pixel-domain class-level prompts) and Freq (frequency-domain masked prompting) by explicitly disentangling the frequency domain into its phase and amplitude spectra, each tied to distinct semantic patterns (structure vs. texture). The key conceptual departure is treating phase and amplitude as separate, independently weighted prompt channels rather than a monolithic frequency or pixel representation, grounded in signal-processing and cognitive-science literature. The weighting mechanism that dynamically adjusts amplitude-prompt strength based on relative robustness contributions, and the predicted-label-based prompt selection (replacing C-AVP's exhaustive traversal), are practical innovations layered on this spectral decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAP offers a computationally efficient defense (no model retraining, O(1) prompt lookup at test time) that substantially outperforms existing prompt-based baselines, particularly on L2-norm attacks (C&W, DDN) where prior methods collapse near zero. However, the method sacrifices natural accuracy on naturally pre-trained models (e.g., 94.83→87.12 on CIFAR-10), and the learned amplitude weights are small (0.25–0.33), suggesting the phase channel dominates and the weighting mechanism's contribution is modest. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet (not evaluated due to compute), whether the natural-accuracy trade-off can be resolved (the authors suggest contrastive learning), and whether the spectral decomposition generalizes beyond classification to detection or segmentation tasks.
