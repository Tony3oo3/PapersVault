## Problem

The implicit bias of gradient descent for homogeneous deep networks is well understood, but prior results do not extend to the more common non-homogeneous case (e.g., networks with residual connections or non-homogeneous activation functions). This gap was explicitly posed as an open problem by Ji & Telgarsky (2020), and no general framework existed to characterize what GD converges to in direction for such networks under exponential loss.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a near-M-homogeneous network (with polynomial bounds on the homogeneity error) trained on a binary classification dataset under exponential loss, starting from a point satisfying a 'strong separability' condition that is strictly stronger than standard separability when the network is non-homogeneous. The output is a triple of asymptotic guarantees: (i) a nearly monotonic increase in the normalized margin, (ii) directional convergence of the iterates while their norm diverges to infinity, and (iii) the limiting direction satisfies the KKT conditions of a margin maximization problem defined on the homogenization f_H of the network, rather than on f itself.

## Method

The key technical innovation is a 'near-M-homogeneity' condition (Definition 1) that bounds the deviation from exact M-homogeneity via polynomials p and q, together with a modified margin γ^GF = (φ(L(θ)) − p_a(||θ||)) / ||θ||^M whose offset term p_a absorbs the homogeneous error. A 'strong separability' assumption requires the empirical risk to fall below e^{−p_a(||θ_s||)}/n, which is necessary for the margin to grow and is shown to be satisfiable by any non-degenerate near-homogeneous network whose homogenization is weakly separable. Directional convergence is established via o-minimal definability and Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz desingularizing functions, extending the machinery of Ji & Telgarsky (2020) to the non-homogeneous setting. The limiting direction is then shown to satisfy KKT conditions of a margin maximization problem on the homogenization f_H(θ;x) = lim_{r→∞} f(rθ;x)/r^M, and the results are extended from gradient flow to GD with arbitrarily large stepsizes by controlling discretization error through a modified loss G(θ) = exp(p_a(||θ||))·L(θ).

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results for gradient flow (GF) and gradient descent (GD) on near-M-homogeneous networks under exponential loss. First, the modified margin γ^GF(θ_t) increases nearly monotonically and is an ε_t-multiplicative approximation of the normalized margin, where ε_t = O((log t)^(-1/M)) → 0. Second, the parameter norm diverges as ||θ_t|| ≈ (log t)^(1/M) while the risk decays as L(θ_t) ≈ 1/(t(log t)^(2-2/M)) for GF (and 1/(ηt(log ηt)^(2-2/M)) for GD). Third, the directional limit θ_* exists (finite arc length) and satisfies the KKT conditions of a margin maximization problem defined on the homogenization f_H of the network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper's contribution is to extend prior implicit bias results from homogeneous networks to non-homogeneous ones. Specifically, Lyu & Li (2020) proved margin improvement and KKT convergence for M-homogeneous networks, and Ji & Telgarsky (2020) proved directional convergence and alignment for homogeneous networks under o-minimal definability. The present paper recovers these results as special cases (when p_a = 0) and extends them to near-M-homogeneous networks, which include residual connections and non-homogeneous activations (e.g., Leaky ReLU, Softplus, GELU, Swish). The key additional requirement is a 'strong separability' condition (Assumption 2) that generalizes the standard separability condition and is shown to be satisfiable by any non-degenerate near-homogeneous network. The paper also extends from GF to GD with arbitrarily large stepsizes, going beyond Lyu & Li (2020) which required small stepsizes bounded by a function of the initial margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis of gradient descent from homogeneous networks (Lyu & Li, 2020; Ji & Telgarsky, 2020) to a broad class of non-homogeneous networks by introducing a 'near-homogeneity' condition that quantifies the deviation from exact homogeneity. It builds directly on the o-minimal structure and desingularizing function machinery of Ji & Telgarsky (2020) for directional convergence, and generalizes the margin improvement framework of Lyu & Li (2020) via a modified margin that subtracts a polynomial offset capturing the homogeneous error. The work also extends Cai et al. (2025), which handled only near-1-homogeneous networks, to general near-M-homogeneous networks and adds the directional/KKT convergence results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves an open problem explicitly posed by Ji & Telgarsky (2020) and provides the first general implicit bias characterization for networks with residual connections and non-homogeneous activations (e.g., Swish, GELU, Leaky ReLU), which are ubiquitous in modern architectures like ResNet, DenseNet, and VGG. The key limitation is that normalization layers and softmax attention violate the near-homogeneity condition, leaving transformer architectures out of scope. Open questions include whether the strong separability condition can be verified more constructively for deeper networks, and whether the homogenization-based margin maximization problem admits a more interpretable geometric characterization.
