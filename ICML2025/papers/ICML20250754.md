## Problem

Subscription platforms distribute revenue to creators using engagement-proportional rules (GLOBALPROP) that are inherently exploitable: adversaries can profitably create fake users or bribe real ones to inflate engagement. The paper asks whether revenue-division mechanisms can be designed to make such manipulation unprofitable by construction, reducing dependence on an arms race of ML-based fraud detection, and whether the current status quo rule even makes fraud detection computationally tractable.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a bipartite engagement matrix w ∈ R^{n×m} (non-negative interaction counts between n users and m artists), the revenue-share parameter α ∈ (0,1], and produces a payment vector φ ∈ R^m summing to αn. Each user's subscription fee is normalized to 1, so the platform retains (1−α)n and distributes αn. The engagement matrix is the sole data structure consumed; no metadata, timestamps, or user identities are used, which is precisely what makes the axiomatic guarantees (anonymity, neutrality) meaningful.

## Method

The paper formalizes three manipulation-resistance axioms—fraud-proofness (no profitable fake-user creation), bribery-proofness (no profitable engagement manipulation of existing users), and Sybil-proofness (no gain from splitting/merging artist identities)—plus two fairness axioms (engagement monotonicity and Pigou-Dalton consistency), and proves they are not equivalent. It shows GLOBALPROP fails fraud- and bribery-proofness and that identifying the artists benefiting most from fraud under GLOBALPROP is NP-hard. The key novel contribution is SCALEDUSERPROP, a user-additive rule where each user's contribution is capped at 1 via min(γ·Σ_j w_ij, 1) before proportional allocation within that user's engagement profile, with γ chosen so total artist payments equal αn. This capping limits the influence of high-engagement users (who are the primary fraud vector) while preserving the same axiomatic guarantees as USERPROP, and empirically yields lower maximum envy in pay-per-stream terms on both real (Music Listening Histories) and synthetic data.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result is that GLOBALPROP (the status quo rule used by major streaming platforms) fails both fraud-proofness and bribery-proofness, while USERPROP, USEREQ, and the proposed SCALEDUSERPROP all satisfy these axioms. Additionally, detecting potentially suspicious artists under GLOBALPROP is NP-hard (Theorem 3.5). Empirically, on the Music Listening Histories Dataset (~583K users, 439K artists, 27B listening events), SCALEDUSERPROP emerges as the fairest mechanism by the maximum envy (ME) metric, particularly for revenue share α not close to 1, while USEREQ is the least fair. On synthetic data (10K users, 1K artists), SCALEDUSERPROP again achieves the lowest ME, with USERPROP and USEREQ performing nearly identically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four rules: GLOBALPROP and USERPROP (from Alaei et al., 2022), USEREQ (from Bergantiños & Moreno-Ternero, 2024), and the proposed SCALEDUSERPROP. SCALEDUSERPROP matches USERPROP exactly on all manipulation-resistance axioms (fraud-proofness, bribery-proofness, Sybil-proofness) and on engagement monotonicity, while both fail strong Sybil-proofness and Pigou-Dalton consistency. The key differentiator is empirical fairness: on real-world data, SCALEDUSERPROP significantly reduces the top-100 artists' pay-per-stream (PPS) relative to GLOBALPROP even for α > 0.9, whereas USERPROP and USEREQ do not. USEREQ, which treats avid and casual listeners equally, is the least fair mechanism on real-world data. GLOBALPROP, despite being the industry standard, is the only rule that fails both fraud-proofness and bribery-proofness, and the paper further shows that fraud detection under GLOBALPROP is NP-hard, making it both vulnerable and hard to police.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the axiomatic study of revenue-division rules on subscription streaming platforms initiated by Alaei et al. (2022) and Bergantiños & Moreno-Ternero (2025), which characterized GLOBALPROP and USERPROP through fairness and efficiency lenses. The key departure is introducing manipulation-resistance axioms (fraud-proofness, bribery-proofness, Sybil-proofness) as first-class design criteria, motivated by a real-world $10M fraud case, rather than treating fraud as a detection problem. The proposed SCALEDUSERPROP rule is a novel user-additive mechanism that interpolates between GLOBALPROP and USERPROP via a per-user engagement cap, and the NP-hardness result for fraud detection under GLOBALPROP connects the mechanism-design literature to computational complexity in a way prior streaming-platform work had not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness of identifying fraud-benefiting artists under GLOBALPROP is a practically significant negative result: it implies that even with unlimited ML resources, the current industry-standard rule makes targeted fraud detection fundamentally intractable, strengthening the case for switching to manipulation-resistant mechanisms. SCALEDUSERPROP offers a concrete, implementable alternative that satisfies all three manipulation-resistance axioms while empirically reducing pay-per-stream disparity on real-world data. Open questions include extending the framework to freemium/ad-supported models, addressing AI-generated content manipulation, and whether the axiomatic guarantees hold under more realistic adversarial models (e.g., adaptive fraudsters who observe the rule). The paper explicitly scopes out AI-generated content, leaving a significant gap given the current flood of synthetic tracks on streaming platforms.
