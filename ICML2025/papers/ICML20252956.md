## Problem

Traditional message passing in GNNs maps node features into a Euclidean spatial domain with unit vector bases that is unrelated to graph topology, making it difficult to guarantee that learned representations capture topological structure. While recent work models message passing via differential equations, these approaches lack a systematic theoretical explanation of what properties the resulting node features possess and why the formulation is effective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A, and produces node embeddings that are provably constrained to a solution space spanned by eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian. For the signal filtering evaluation, 100×100 RGB images are converted into 2D regular 4-neighborhood grid graphs (10,000 nodes each), and the output is a filtered graph signal compared against ground-truth spectral filter outputs. The key structural constraint is that at any time step, node features are decomposed as a superposition of Laplacian eigenvector basis vectors rather than arbitrary Euclidean coordinates.

## Method

The paper formulates message passing as a system of hyperbolic PDEs (∂²X/∂t² = a²L̂X) and proves that the solution space is spanned by 2n eigenvectors of a block matrix whose lower block is a²L̂, meaning node features propagate along eigenvector directions that encode topological structure. To avoid the O(n³) cost of eigen-decomposition and to increase model flexibility, the Laplacian operator is approximated by a polynomial P(L,t) = Σ θ_{tk} p_k(L)X using orthogonal polynomial bases (Chebyshev, Bernstein, Jacobi), which also establishes a direct connection to existing spectral GNNs. Time is discretized via the forward Euler method, yielding a simple iterative update X(t_{m+1}) = (2I + τ²P(L,t_m))X(t_m) − X(t_{m−1}) that can be plugged into any spectral GNN as an enhancement wrapper. This paradigm allows existing spectral GNNs (SGC, APPNP, GPR-GNN, ChebNet, BernNet, etc.) to be augmented by constraining their solution space to the eigenvector basis while retaining their polynomial filter design.

## Results

On node classification, Hyperbolic-PDE GNN achieves the best accuracy on Cora (90.82%) and CiteSeer (81.88%), outperforming the strongest baselines (NFGNN at 89.82% and SpecFormer at 81.49%) by approximately 1% on both. On PubMed, it reaches 91.36%, competitive with UniFilter (91.44%), and on the heterophilic Actor dataset it scores 42.03%, close to OptBasisGNN (42.39%). In the signal filtering task, Hyperbolic-Cheb reduces squared error by over 95% across all seven filter types compared to vanilla ChebNet, and Hyperbolic-GCN improves high-pass filter fitting by 97.38% over GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyperbolic-PDE GNN achieves the best accuracy on Cora (90.82%) and CiteSeer (81.88%), surpassing the next-best methods NFGNN (89.82%) and SpecFormer (81.49%) by roughly 1 percentage point. On PubMed, UniFilter edges out the proposed method by 0.08% (91.44% vs. 91.36%), while on the heterophilic Actor dataset, OptBasisGNN leads by 0.36% (42.39% vs. 42.03%). The paper further demonstrates that the hyperbolic PDE paradigm can enhance existing spectral GNNs: in Table 5, Hyperbolic-SGC improves Actor accuracy from 28.81% to 41.49% (+13.68%) and Texas from 81.31% to 93.61% (+12.30%), while Hyperbolic-APPNP gains +6.21% on Photo and +4.56% on Computers. In the signal filtering task, Hyperbolic-Cheb reduces squared error by 95–99% across all seven filter types compared to vanilla ChebNet, and Hyperbolic-GCN improves high-pass fitting by 97.38% over GCN, showing the paradigm substantially boosts filter expressiveness even for simple monomial-based models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: PDE-based GNNs (PDE-GCN, GRAND, GRAND++, GraphCON) that heuristically model message passing as differential equations, and polynomial spectral GNNs (ChebNet, BernNet, JacobiConv, ChebNetII) that approximate arbitrary graph filters. The key departure is a formal proof that the solution space of the hyperbolic PDE system is spanned by Laplacian eigenvectors, providing the theoretical justification that prior PDE-based GNNs lacked. By approximating the solution space (rather than just the filter) with orthogonal polynomials, the paper establishes an explicit algebraic bridge between the PDE paradigm and classical spectral GNNs, allowing any existing polynomial-based spectral GNN to be 'upgraded' into a hyperbolic PDE formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eigenvector-basis characterization of the solution space offers a principled explanation for why PDE-based message passing captures topological structure, addressing a gap in interpretability that prior PDE-GNN papers left open. Practically, the framework provides a drop-in enhancement for a wide range of spectral GNNs (SGC, APPNP, GPR-GNN, ChebNet, BernNet, JacobiConv, ChebNetII) with consistent gains on both homophilic and heterophilic benchmarks. Limitations include the reliance on forward Euler discretization (introducing approximation error), the need to select polynomial type and order, and occasional performance degradation on certain base models (e.g., BernNet on Actor, ChebNetII on Photo). Open questions include whether the eigenvector interpretation extends to non-symmetric or directed Laplacians, how the framework scales to very large graphs where even polynomial-based convolutions become costly, and whether other PDE classes (parabolic, elliptic) yield similarly interpretable solution spaces.
