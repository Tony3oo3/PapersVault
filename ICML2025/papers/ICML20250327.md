## Problem

Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) models face severe memory constraints in resource-limited deployments due to their large expert parameter counts. Existing reduction methods either require retraining (TSEP, M-SMoE), lack scalability to large expert counts (O-prune), or rely on router-logits for expert grouping, which introduces task-specific biases and fails in task-agnostic settings. The paper addresses the gap of a retraining-free, task-agnostic, and scalable expert merging framework that preserves model performance across diverse downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained SMoE model (e.g., Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B with 60 experts/layer or Mixtral 8x7B with 8 experts/layer), a calibration dataset of 32 sequences of 2048 tokens (sampled from C4, MATH, or CodeQA), and a target expert count r per layer. It produces a compressed SMoE model where each layer's n experts are consolidated into r merged experts via frequency-weighted averaging, while the router weights remain entirely unchanged—inputs previously routed to any expert in a merged group are simply redirected to the corresponding merged expert, preserving router dimensionality.

## Method

HC-SMoE operates in two phases: hierarchical clustering of experts, followed by frequency-weighted merging within each cluster. For clustering, it computes each expert's representative vector as the mean of its outputs over the calibration dataset, then applies bottom-up agglomerative hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance and average linkage to iteratively merge the most functionally similar expert pairs until r clusters remain. This deterministic, iterative approach avoids K-means' initialization sensitivity and single-shot grouping's inability to adapt to evolving cluster structure. Within each cluster, experts are merged as a frequency-weighted sum of their weight matrices, where weights are normalized activation frequencies from the calibration data. Theoretically, the approximation error between the original and compressed model outputs is bounded by the weighted intra-cluster variance, and average-linkage HC provides a 3-approximation guarantee to the optimal clustering objective.

## Results

On Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat (60 experts), HC-SMoE with average linkage achieves 57.16% average zero-shot accuracy at 25% expert reduction (45 experts) and 52.23% at 50% reduction (30 experts), compared to the original model's 60.08%. On Mixtral 8×7B, HC-SMoE achieves 64.25% at 25% reduction (6 experts) and 57.29% at 50% reduction (4 experts) versus the original 67.48%. The paper reports improvements of 6.95% and 2.14% over the strongest baseline (F-prune) at the 50% and 25% reduction settings on Qwen, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HC-SMoE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both Qwen and Mixtral at both 25% and 50% expert reduction. Against O-prune (Lu et al., 2024), HC-SMoE leads by 9.44% on Qwen 30×2.7B (52.23% vs. 42.79%) and 6.62% on Mixtral 4×7B (57.29% vs. 57.28% — essentially tied, but HC-SMoE wins on individual tasks like BoolQ and MMLU). Against S-prune (He et al., 2024), the gap is even larger: 9.14% on Qwen 30×2.7B and 16.67% on Mixtral 4×7B (57.29% vs. 40.62%). Against M-SMoE (Li et al., 2024), which uses router-logit-based single-shot grouping, HC-SMoE achieves a dramatic 20.02% improvement on Qwen 30×2.7B (52.23% vs. 32.21%) and 24.78% on Mixtral 4×7B (57.29% vs. 32.51%), confirming that router-logit grouping is highly unreliable in task-agnostic settings. Against Fuzzy C-Means (Bezdek et al., 1984) as a soft-clustering alternative, HC-SMoE's hard hierarchical clustering yields 19.83% higher accuracy on Qwen 45×2.7B (57.16% vs. 32.40%), attributed to FCM's ambiguity in routing merged experts. Against ZipIt (Stoica et al., 2024), the paper's Fix-Dom adaptation outperforms the original ZipIt by 2.28% average on Mixtral 4×7B (56.37% vs. 54.09% with activation features) while being over 100× faster (7 min vs. 725 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expert-merging line initiated by M-SMoE (Li et al., 2024), which used router-logits for single-shot expert grouping and frequency-based merging. HC-SMoE departs from M-SMoE on two axes: it replaces router-logits with averaged expert outputs as the similarity metric (arguing that functional equivalence, not routing preference, should drive grouping), and it replaces single-shot grouping with deterministic hierarchical clustering using average linkage. The merging component also draws on ZipIt (Stoica et al., 2024), adapted into a 'fixed-dominant' variant for expert-level merging. The paper positions itself against the broader pruning literature (TSEP, O-prune, S-prune) by arguing that merging preserves information that pruning irreversibly discards, while remaining retraining-free and task-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SMoE architectures (Mixtral, Qwen-MoE, DeepSeek-MoE) become the dominant scaling paradigm, their memory footprint is a critical deployment bottleneck, and a retraining-free, task-agnostic compression method with a 3-approximation guarantee is practically valuable. The finding that clustering quality dominates merging strategy (frequency vs. average vs. fix-dom differ by <0.2%) is a useful simplification for practitioners. However, the router is left unmodified, so inference latency gains are limited to reduced expert computation rather than reduced routing overhead, and the method still requires a calibration dataset. Open questions include whether soft clustering can be made to work without router-weight interference, and whether the approach extends to dynamic (non-uniform) expert counts per layer without the frequency-based heuristics the paper identifies as unreliable.
