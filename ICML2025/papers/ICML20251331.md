## Problem

Despite CLIP's widespread success, the geometry of its latent embedding space remains poorly understood. Prior analyses focused on properties of the L2-normalized hypersphere (alignment, uniformity, modality gap) but overlooked the pre-normalized primary embedding, where the encoder's actual geometric structure resides. This paper asks: what is the true shape of CLIP's embedding before normalization, and what functional role does that shape play in contrastive learning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251331/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on CLIP's primary (pre-L2-normalization) encoder outputs at 512 dimensions (ViT-B/32) or 768 dimensions (ViT-L/14), using the MS-COCO validation set (~5k image-caption pairs) as the representative dataset. The method produces three types of outputs: (1) geometric characterizations including modality mean vectors, covariance matrices, and thin-shell parameters for each ellipsoid; (2) a per-instance conformity score computed as a linear function of cosine similarity to the modality mean; and (3) for vSLERP, interpolated images between a source and target embedding, controlled by a scalar parameter α that adjusts the effective center of interpolation.

## Method

The paper first establishes through statistical analysis that CLIP's primary embeddings for images and text each form a tilted ellipsoid shell (not a sphere), with mass concentrated in a thin band around the shell radius and centers significantly offset from the origin. It then proves that conformity—the expected cosine similarity of an instance to all others in the dataset—can be accurately estimated (Pearson r = 0.9998) by a simple linear function of cosine similarity to the modality mean vector, under the thin-shell assumption. The key mechanistic insight is that the non-origin-centered ellipsoid geometry optimizes the NT-Xent loss: shifting the embedding center toward the origin improves alignment for correctly classified pairs but degrades uniformity for misclassified (false-negative) pairs, with the trained CLIP position (α≈0) balancing both. Finally, the paper introduces vSLERP, a training-free interpolation that subtracts a scaled modality mean before performing standard SLERP and re-adds it afterward, enabling controlled semantic editing of a single object without optimization.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative finding is a near-perfect Pearson correlation of 0.9998 between true conformity (average cosine similarity to all other instances) and the proposed surrogate (cosine similarity to the modality mean) on MS-COCO, with fitted coefficients a=1.461, b=−0.002 for images and a=1.411, b=−0.008 for text. Image and text embeddings are shown to be 100% linearly separable using only two features (93 and 134), with a KL-divergence of ≈301 between their per-feature distributions. The loss-vs-offset experiment (Fig. 6) confirms that the NT-Xent loss is minimized at α≈0 (the trained, non-origin-centered position), and the conformity distribution matching between modalities achieves KL≈0.14 at that same offset, supporting the geometric explanation of the modality gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses the proposed conformity metric to compare generative and captioning methods rather than reporting standard benchmark scores. For image synthesis (Fig. 13, top), unCLIP-generated images exhibit lower conformity (higher diversity and detail) than Glide-generated images, though both fall short of the diversity seen in real MS-COCO images. For captioning (Fig. 13, bottom), ClipCap produces captions with higher conformity (more generic/common descriptions), while Caption Reward generates captions with lower conformity that even surpass the diversity of human MS-COCO annotations. These comparisons are qualitative in the sense that the paper reports distributional shifts in conformity histograms rather than a single scalar metric per method, but the ordering is consistent: Glide > unCLIP > real images for image conformity, and ClipCap > human > Caption Reward for caption conformity (higher conformity = less diverse).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work analyzing CLIP's latent space geometry (Liang et al., 2022; Schrodi et al., 2024; Fahim et al., 2024), which previously characterized the modality gap and narrow cone effect on the post-normalization unit hypersphere. The key departure is analyzing the pre-normalization primary embedding, where L2-normalization's information-destroying projection is avoided, revealing a double-ellipsoid structure that unifies and explains both the modality gap and narrow cone as geometric consequences rather than independent phenomena. The theoretical grounding draws on thin-shell theory from high-dimensional probability (Klartag, 2023; Paouris, 2006) and the alignment/uniformity decomposition of NT-Xent (Wang & Isola, 2020), while the false-negative mitigation argument connects to the broader contrastive learning literature (Byun et al., 2022; Li et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a geometric mechanism for the modality gap (optimizing conformity distribution matching between modalities) and shows the non-origin-centered ellipsoid position is loss-optimal, offering a principled explanation rather than an empirical observation. The conformity metric is practically useful for evaluating diversity of generative models (image synthesis, captioning), and vSLERP provides a training-free interpolation tool. Limitations include reliance on MS-COCO as a proxy for the full training distribution, analysis primarily on ViT-B/32 (with ViT-L/14 as secondary confirmation), and the 'optimality' claim being approximate. Open questions include whether the ellipsoid structure generalizes to other contrastive architectures (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based models) and whether explicitly regularizing toward this geometry during training could improve downstream performance.
