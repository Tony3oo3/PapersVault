## Problem

Overfitting in energy-based models (EBMs) trained in unsupervised or generative settings lacks clear diagnostic indicators, unlike supervised tasks where test accuracy directly signals the problem. While empirical regularization strategies exist, there is no analytical framework linking finite-data effects to training dynamics, no principled early-stopping criterion, and no counterpart of generalized cross-validation (GCV) for EBMs that could estimate test performance without a held-out set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of M samples of dimension N (real-valued vectors for the Gaussian EBM, binary spin configurations for the Boltzmann Machine) and operates on the empirical covariance matrix Ĉ^M derived from these samples. Outputs include the time-dependent coupling matrix J(t), closed-form RMT predictions of train/test energy, coupling error, and log-likelihood as functions of the aspect ratio ρ=M/N and population spectral density ν, a GCV-like deterministic relation between train and test energy for the spectral L1-regularized case, and corrected covariance matrices via shrinkage or polynomial eigenmode fitting. The analysis is conducted in the eigenbasis of Ĉ^M, exploiting the independent evolution of eigenmodes, and requires only the population spectrum and ρ for asymptotic predictions.

## Method

The paper projects the gradient-ascent training dynamics of a pairwise EBM onto the eigenbasis of the coupling matrix, showing that once eigenvectors align with those of the empirical covariance, each eigenvalue J_α evolves independently with a relaxation timescale proportional to (ĉ_α^M)^(−2), yielding an explicit solution via the Lambert W function. This spectral decomposition reveals a timescale separation where strong covariance modes are learned quickly while weak modes (dominated by finite-sample noise) converge slowly, producing a non-monotonic reconstruction error with an optimal early-stopping point. Asymptotic random matrix theory (Marchenko–Pastur self-consistent equations) is then applied to derive closed-form expressions for train/test energy, coupling error, and log-likelihood in the N,M→∞ limit at fixed ρ, and a deterministic GCV-like relation E_test=(1−ρ^(−1)E_train)^(−1)E_train is obtained for the spectral L1-regularized GEBM. To mitigate overfitting, the paper proposes rotationally-invariant shrinkage corrections (Bun et al.) and a novel polynomial-fit protocol that extrapolates eigenvalues to the M→∞ limit via downsampling, both validated against RMT predictions. The framework extends to binary Boltzmann Machines through a mean-field approximation of the correlation-coupling relation, and to arbitrary EBMs by deriving the neural tangent kernel dynamics of the score function under score-matching, recovering the GEBM result as a special case.

## Results

The paper derives an exact analytical solution for GEBM eigenvalue training dynamics (Eq. 6, involving the Lambert W function), showing each mode relaxes with a timescale proportional to (ĉ_α^M)^(−2), establishing a clear spectral-bias-like separation. Asymptotic RMT predictions for reconstruction error, test log-likelihood, and optimal stopping times match numerical trainings across system sizes N (Fig. 4), with all strategies exhibiting a 1/√ρ scaling of reconstruction error at large ρ = M/N. The paper also derives a GCV-like deterministic relation for GEBMs: E_test = (1 − ρ^(−1) E_train)^(−1) E_train (Eq. 9), providing a practical test-LL estimator. For the Boltzmann Machine extension, the approximate analytic eigenvalue evolution (Eq. 11) qualitatively captures the observed timescale separation, with stronger covariances (ĉ_α^M > 1) learned faster than weaker ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against the rotationally invariant estimator (RIE) shrinkage protocol of Bun et al. (2017). In Fig. 5(b), the RMT-based shrinkage correction (cyan) substantially reduces reconstruction error E_J relative to unregularized ML training (blue) across all ρ values, but residual non-monotonic behavior persists. The paper's proposed polynomial-fit eigenmode correction (yellow) achieves performance comparable to or slightly better than shrinkage, and both non-informative methods closely track the oracle strategies (optimal L2 regularization in red, C_val-pop in green) that require knowledge of the true population matrix. For the Boltzmann Machine (Fig. 6c), the polynomial-fit correction again outperforms standard ML training and matches the performance of optimal L2 regularization, while shrinkage formulas are not applicable to the binary-variable setting. All strategies converge to the same 1/√ρ asymptotic scaling at large ρ, confirming that the finite-data correction is fundamentally governed by the aspect ratio rather than the specific correction method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019; Advani et al., 2020) from Fourier/linear-regression settings to the eigenbasis of the empirical covariance matrix in energy-based models, showing that eigenmode learning timescales follow a (c_α)^{-2} law solvable via the Lambert W function. It builds on the empirical observations of non-monotonic training dynamics in RBMs (Agoritsas et al., 2023; Decelle et al., 2024) by providing the first closed-form analytical explanation rooted in random matrix theory (Bun et al., 2017; Ledoit & Wolf, 2004). The NTK extension to generic EBMs (Section 7) draws on the kernel-regime framework of Jacot et al. (2018) and Chizat et al. (2019), while the GCV counterpart (Eq. 9) parallels the leave-one-out arguments of Golub et al. (1979) and Furtlehner (2023) adapted to the EBM likelihood structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first analytical framework connecting finite-sample overfitting in EBMs to the spectral structure of the data covariance, yielding a practical GCV-like relation (Eq. 9) for test-LL estimation without ground truth and a simple polynomial-fit correction that matches RMT-based shrinkage performance. A key conceptual finding is that generation quality (covariance error) stabilizes well before parameter reconstruction error peaks, meaning EBMs can produce good samples even while their couplings are actively overfitting — a distinction that complicates model selection. Open questions include whether the NTK score-matching framework (Section 7) captures overfitting in complex EBMs like RBMs or CNN-based architectures, how early-stopping points relate to asymptotic spectral properties, and whether the polynomial-fit strategy generalizes beyond pairwise models to higher-order interactions.
