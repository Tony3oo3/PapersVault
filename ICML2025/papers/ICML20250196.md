## Problem

Tree-based surrogates are well-suited for Bayesian optimization over mixed feature spaces, but existing tree-kernel GP methods fit trees independently of the GP in a two-step procedure, yielding a fixed kernel that cannot capture uncertainty over the tree structure itself. BART performs fully Bayesian inference over trees but only samples deterministic functions, making it infeasible to construct standard acquisition functions that require a distribution over function values. No prior method jointly fits the tree kernel and the GP in a fully Bayesian framework that quantifies uncertainty over both function values and tree topology.

## Input / Output

BARK consumes sequential black-box function evaluations over mixed feature spaces (continuous, integer, and categorical variables) and produces 16 posterior samples of Gaussian processes, each defined by a forest of 50 trees with a sampled noise variance. The integrated UCB acquisition function is then optimized via mixed-integer linear programming (Gurobi, 100s time limit, 10% MIP gap), which encodes the piecewise-constant tree structure through binary variables and yields a globally optimal next query point. Unlike gradient-based AF optimizers, the MIP formulation naturally handles discrete and categorical inputs without one-hot encoding or alternating optimization heuristics.

## Method

BARK reinterprets the BART additive tree model as a GP with a forest kernel, where the kernel is defined by tree agreement (Eq. 2) and the GP marginal likelihood integrates over all leaf values rather than sampling them sequentially. MCMC (Metropolis-Hastings with grow/prune/change proposals) explores the posterior over tree structures, with a critical modification: split points are sampled uniformly from the domain subspace reaching each node rather than from observed data points, which restores the property that uncertainty grows with distance from observations and prevents over-exploitation near dense clusters. Kernel matrix updates exploit the low-rank structure of individual tree contributions via the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula, reducing per-proposal cost from O(N³) to O(N²L_t). The integrated UCB is a convex function of the per-sample GP means and standard deviations, enabling efficient global optimization over the mixture of piecewise-constant surrogates.

## Results

On regression benchmarks (Table 1), BARK matches BART performance closely: e.g., NLPD of 1.09 vs 1.10 on Abalone, 0.31 vs 0.54 on Concrete (where BARK is notably better), and 1.27 vs 1.27 on Student Performance. On Bayesian optimization, BARK achieves the lowest cumulative regret on TreeFunction, TreeFunctionCat, DiscreteAckley, SVRBench, CCOBench, and PestControl, outperforming all tree-based baselines (LeafGP, BART, SMAC, Entmoot). BARK also outperforms BARKPrior (sampling from the prior without MCMC), confirming that posterior inference over tree structure provides meaningful gains. The main failure case is DiscreteRosenbrock, where the shallow optimum favors smooth GP-RBF and Entmoot's distance-based uncertainty over BARK's piecewise-constant prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BARK consistently outperforms LeafGP (two-step forest kernel GP, Thebelt et al. 2022a) across all synthetic and applied BO benchmarks, demonstrating that jointly fitting the tree kernel with the GP via MCMC (capturing uncertainty over tree structure) is superior to the two-step fitting approach. BARK also beats BART on BO tasks (particularly TreeFunction and TreeFunctionCat) because BARK's GP formulation enables global MIP optimization of the UCB, whereas BART relies on grid-based evaluation that cannot find the global optimum. Against GP-RBF, BARK is competitive on applied benchmarks requiring alternating optimization (SVRBench, CCOBench, PestControl), but GP-RBF wins on XGBoostMNIST where full enumeration of categorical combinations is feasible, and on DiscreteRosenbrock where smoothness assumptions are advantageous. BARK outperforms SMAC and Entmoot on most benchmarks, though Entmoot's distance-based uncertainty gives it an edge on DiscreteRosenbrock. The comparison with BARKPrior (sampling tree kernels from the prior without MCMC) shows that proper posterior inference yields additional gains beyond what the tree-kernel structure alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

BARK sits at the intersection of three research threads: Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (BART), forest/tree kernels for Gaussian processes, and tree-ensemble-based Bayesian optimization. It departs from prior tree-kernel GP methods (Davies & Ghahramani, 2014; Thebelt et al., 2022a) by replacing the two-step fitting procedure (fit trees, then train GP) with a fully Bayesian joint MCMC inference over the tree structure and GP hyperparameters simultaneously. Relative to BART itself, BARK reinterprets the same probabilistic model through a kernel lens: instead of sampling deterministic leaf values, it samples entire GP distributions, and it modifies the splitting-rule prior from data-point-based to uniform-over-domain to improve exploration. The MIP-based acquisition function optimization framework is inherited from Thebelt et al. (2022a) and Mišić (2020), extended here to handle multiple MCMC samples of the kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BARK provides the first fully Bayesian treatment of tree-kernel GPs for BO, capturing uncertainty over the tree structure itself rather than just function values, which yields a richer posterior and better-calibrated acquisition functions. The practical impact is strongest for expensive black-box optimization over mixed (continuous + categorical + integer) feature spaces, where gradient-based methods struggle and global MIP optimization over piecewise-constant surrogates is natural. Key limitations include the O(N³) wall-time cost (roughly 50s fitting + 100s optimization per iteration), which restricts applicability to settings where function evaluations take minutes or longer, and the demonstrated failure of the Laplace-kernel approximation to provide meaningful regret bounds under the actual BART depth prior. Open questions include whether the MCMC mixing can be improved for larger datasets and whether the approach can be adapted to batch or asynchronous BO settings.
