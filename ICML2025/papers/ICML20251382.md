## Problem

Current mathematical LLMs achieve high scores on competition-style benchmarks through drill-based memorization of proof patterns, but this does not reflect genuine conceptual understanding of mathematical theorems. No existing benchmark specifically evaluates whether LLMs can reason about nuanced mathematical concepts through counterexamples — a fundamental pedagogical technique that distinguishes deep understanding from surface-level pattern matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input is a concise university-level mathematical statement drawn from four fields (algebra, topology, real analysis, functional analysis) that requires a binary True/False judgement. The expected output is not merely the judgement but a rationale that explicitly constructs or references specific counterexamples (or confirming examples) to justify the truth value. Evaluation combines macro F1 for the judgement with three LLM-as-judge metrics — Proportion of Examples, Strict Align, and Loose Align — that assess whether the model's generated examples are semantically consistent with reference counterexamples, validated at 97% extraction accuracy and 93.5% alignment accuracy against human raters.

## Method

The paper introduces COUNTERMATH, a 1,216-sample benchmark constructed via a two-stage annotation pipeline: crowd-sourced OCR extraction of statement-rationale pairs from four Chinese counterexample textbooks, followed by expert validation and GPT-4o-assisted English translation. For model improvement, the authors design a filter-based data engineering framework that uses GPT-4o to identify counterexample-based proofs from ProofNet and NaturalProofs (yielding 1,025 samples from 30,000+), then refines the rationales via GPT-4o rewriting to match COUNTERMATH's concise, example-centric style. The trained model is Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA (lr=1e-5, 2×L20 48GB GPUs) on these 1,025 samples. Evaluation employs GPT-4o as an automated judge that extracts examples from model outputs and assesses their alignment with reference solutions, a methodology validated against human evaluation. The key finding is that even top commercial models (OpenAI o1, GPT-4o) show limited counterexample-driven reasoning, while the 7B fine-tuned model improves both in-domain F1 and out-of-distribution benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, AIME 2024), demonstrating that counterexample-based conceptual reasoning generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Results

On COUNTERMATH, the strongest commercial model Deepseek-R1 achieves a Judgement F1(macro) of 80.7 with 86.8% example usage and 54.2% strict alignment, while Gemini2.5-pro reaches 77.0 F1 with the highest example proportion (90.8%) and strict alignment (65.1%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct leads with 41.8 F1, far below commercial models, and most 7B open-source models score between 22–38 F1. After fine-tuning Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct with only 1,025 counterexample samples (SFT+Hint), the model improves from 38.3 to 41.1 F1 on COUNTERMATH and surpasses the 72B base model on OOD benchmarks (e.g., MATH: 87.9 vs. 84.9; AIME 2024: 30.0 vs. 26.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 23 LLMs on COUNTERMATH, revealing a large gap between commercial and open-source models: Deepseek-R1 (80.7 F1) and Gemini2.5-pro (77.0 F1) substantially outperform the best open-source model Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct (41.8 F1). Among open-source 7B models, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (38.3 F1) and Eurus-2-7B-PRIME (37.5 F1) lead, while drill-trained models like WizardMath-7B (27.9) and MetaMath-Mistral-7B (31.0) show near-zero strict alignment (6.4% and 0.4%), indicating they rarely produce valid counterexamples. The paper's SFT approach (1,025 samples on Qwen2.5-Math-7B) improves F1 from 38.3 to 41.1 and, more notably, transfers to OOD benchmarks where the fine-tuned 7B model surpasses the 72B base on MATH (87.9 vs. 84.9), MMLU-college-Math (80.0 vs. 78.3), and AIME 2024 (30.0 vs. 26.7), though it remains slightly below the 72B on OlympiadBench (46.4 vs. 49.2). Notably, OpenAI o1-preview (60.1 F1) underperforms Deepseek-R1 and Gemini2.5-pro, and GPT-4o (59.0 F1) shows the lowest example usage (44.7%) among top commercial models, suggesting that even frontier reasoning models struggle with counterexample-driven conceptual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in mathematical LLMs—synthetic problem generation (WizardMath, MetaMath) and formal theorem proving (Lean 4, Draft-Sketch-Prove)—by arguing that both amount to 'drill-based' learning that fails to test deep conceptual understanding. It carves new ground by isolating counterexample-driven reasoning as a distinct evaluation axis, drawing on the pedagogical tradition of 'proof by counterexamples' in human mathematics education. The data engineering framework repurposes existing proof corpora (ProofNet, NaturalProofs) through LLM-based filtering for counterexample content, and the evaluation methodology introduces an LLM-as-judge pipeline for example extraction and alignment assessment that has no direct precedent in prior math benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a genuine conceptual-reasoning gap: even top proprietary models (o1 at F1=60.1, GPT-4o at 59.0) struggle substantially with university-level counterexample reasoning, and topology/real analysis are particularly weak areas likely underrepresented in training data. The finding that fine-tuning on only 1,025 counterexample samples improves OOD performance (MATH 80.5→87.9, AIME 20→30) suggests counterexample reasoning may be a transferable meta-skill rather than a narrow task-specific ability. Open questions include whether this gap persists with stronger RL training, whether the LLM-as-judge evaluation metrics generalize beyond the GPT-4o judge, and whether the 95%+ data retention rate from textbook extraction introduces subtle distributional biases.
