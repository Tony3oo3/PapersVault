## Problem

Existing 3D human pose-shape estimators from video lack uncertainty quantification with formal statistical guarantees, making it impossible to know when to trust predictions in safety-critical applications. While conformal prediction offers distribution-free coverage guarantees, it requires exchangeable data—an assumption violated by video sequences where permuting frames changes the underlying distribution. No prior work has provided reliable statistical error bounds for human reconstruction under non-exchangeability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252988/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T RGB frames (H×W×3×T, typically T=16) and outputs SMPL parameters: 6D joint poses θ ∈ R^{24×6×T} and shape coefficients β ∈ R^{10×T}, plus a conformal prediction set C_θ(X) = {Y : S_θ(X,Y) ≤ τ*} that certifies prediction quality. At test time, Monte Carlo Dropout can be applied to explicitly construct the set by sampling multiple hypotheses and checking set membership against the calibrated threshold, enabling multi-hypothesis aggregation that further improves accuracy.

## Method

CUPS builds on the GLoT Global-to-Local Transformer backbone and jointly trains a Deep Uncertainty Function S_θ(X,Y) ∈ [0,1] (an MLP over the decoupled global-local representation and SMPL parameters, passed through a sigmoid) end-to-end with the reconstruction model. Training uses ensemble augmentation: the intrinsic randomness of GLoT's frame masking generates H_train hypotheses per input, which are scored by a discriminator-style loss that pushes ground-truth scores high and predicted scores low, plus an adversarial loss that encourages the reconstructor to produce more realistic outputs. Post-training, the learned score serves as the conformity score for non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al., 2023), with feature-distance-based weights w_i = exp(-‖φ_pred − φ_GT‖²/T) to handle distribution shift. The paper further derives two practical upper bounds on the miscoverage gap: one for periodic changepoints (bounded by ρ^k) and a novel bound assuming Beta-distributed scores (bounded by √(2−2(1−2k/(n+k))^{k/2})), providing theoretical backing for the uncertainty quantification.

## Results

On 3DPW (trained on 3DPW), CUPS achieves 48.7 mm PA-MPJPE, 76.2 mm MPJPE, and 91.7 mm MPVPE, outperforming the strongest baseline GLoT by 1.9 mm, 4.5 mm, and 4.6 mm respectively. On MPI-INF-3DHP and Human3.6M, CUPS also leads on all metrics (e.g., 44.0 mm PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M vs. 46.3 mm for GLoT). In the harder setting where no method uses 3DPW for training (Table 2), CUPS still achieves the best PA-MPJPE (53.0 mm) and MPJPE (85.7 mm) on 3DPW, beating GLoT by 0.5 mm and 4.2 mm. Empirical conformal coverage with weighted calibration reaches 86–89% across all three test datasets, close to the target 90% (α=0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUPS outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of metrics across three datasets. Against the closest competitor GLoT (the architecture CUPS builds upon), CUPS improves PA-MPJPE by 1.9 mm on 3DPW, 2.3 mm on Human3.6M, and 0.2 mm on MPI-INF-3DHP, with MPJPE gains of 4.5 mm, 3.2 mm, and 1.1 mm respectively; the only metric where CUPS is slightly worse is 3DPW Accel (6.9 vs. 6.6). Against POCO, which also learns a confidence score, CUPS leads by 1.8 mm PA-MPJPE and 4.3 mm MPJPE on 3DPW. In the no-3DPW-training setting (Table 2), CUPS beats GLoT by 0.5 mm PA-MPJPE and 4.2 mm MPJPE, and outperforms all other baselines including SPIN (59.2 mm PA-MPJPE) and HMMR (72.6 mm). The paper also shows that with 25% bottom truncation (occlusion), CUPS degrades only modestly (e.g., 48.7→50.7 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW), still remaining competitive with untruncated baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUPS builds directly on the GLoT transformer architecture (Shen et al., 2023) for video-based 3D human pose-shape estimation, and integrates the non-exchangeable conformal prediction framework of Barber et al. (2023) to handle the inherent temporal dependencies in video data. The learned deep uncertainty function draws on the end-to-end nonconformity score learning paradigm from CHAMP (Zhang & Carlone, 2024) and the discriminator-style adversarial training from VIBE (Kocabas et al., 2020), but departs from both by coupling the uncertainty score to a conformal calibration step that provides distribution-free coverage guarantees. The key conceptual departure from prior uncertainty-aware pose estimators (POCO, 3DMB, diffusion-based methods) is the shift from probabilistic output modeling to a post-hoc statistical certification via conformal prediction, which requires no distributional assumptions on the error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled statistical guarantees (coverage bounds) for 3D human mesh reconstruction from video, which is critical for safety-critical deployments in robotics and AR/VR where knowing when to trust a pose estimate matters. The two practical miscoverage bounds (periodic changepoint and beta-distribution) offer actionable diagnostics for practitioners, though they remain somewhat loose in practice (empirical coverage ~88% vs. target 90%). Limitations include the significant GPU memory overhead of training-time ensemble augmentation (20 proposals) and the absence of explicit joint-level constraints, which could further tighten predictions. Open questions include whether the conformal framework extends naturally to longer sequences or multi-person settings, and whether the learned uncertainty score remains well-calibrated under severe distribution shift beyond what the calibration set captures.
