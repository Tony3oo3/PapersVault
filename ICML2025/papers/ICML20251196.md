## Problem

Existing pseudo-labeling strategies for unsupervised domain adaptation were developed for static data (images) and operate as black boxes that fail to explicitly model the two critical aspects of time series: temporal dynamics within sequences and selective channel-wise shifts between domains. This results in sub-optimal pseudo labels and no interpretable explanation of what is being adapted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251196/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multivariate time series (D channels × T timesteps) from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, segmenting each channel into non-overlapping patches of length m (8 for HAR tasks, 15 for PTB). It produces pseudo labels as a weighted aggregation of channel-wise class posteriors, where each posterior is computed via Bayes' rule from class-conditional likelihoods and a prior, and the weights are channel alignment scores derived from optimal transport distances between source and target transition matrices. An optional label distribution (weak supervision) can be incorporated as the prior in the Bayes formulation.

## Method

TransPL encodes time series patches with a Transformer encoder and quantizes them using a residual VQ structure with a small coarse codebook (n_c=8) and a larger fine codebook (n_f=64), where the coarse codes capture global temporal trends and the fine codes encode residual details. The coarse codes are treated as states in a discrete Markov chain, and three types of transition matrices are constructed: class-wise TMs from the labeled source (K×D×n_c×n_c) and channel-wise TMs from both source and target (D×n_c×n_c). Pseudo-labeling applies Bayes' rule per channel, computing class-conditional likelihoods as products of transition probabilities from the class-wise TM, then weighting each channel's posterior by an alignment score w_d computed via optimal transport (Earth Mover's Distance) between source and target channel-wise TMs, where the cost matrix encodes cosine distances between coarse code vectors. This design explicitly models temporal transitions through the Markov structure and channel shifts through the OT-based alignment, while remaining interpretable since each pseudo label can be traced back to specific transition patterns.

## Results

TransPL achieves the best adaptation performance across all four time series UDA benchmarks, with mean target-domain accuracy of 69.0% (UCIHAR), 64.0% (WISDM), 68.4% (HHAR), and 67.2% (PTB), and corresponding macro-F1 scores of 64.9%, 56.2%, 65.3%, and 74.0%. These represent average gains of 11.4% in accuracy and 12.2% in MF1 over the non-adapted baseline, and a consistent 3.0% accuracy / 2.9% MF1 advantage over the strongest baseline SHOT. In pseudo-labeling accuracy (Table 3), TransPL outperforms the best pseudo-labeling baseline by 6.1% accuracy and 4.9% MF1 on average, widening to 10.7% / 5.2% when weak supervision (true label distribution) is incorporated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransPL outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and metric. Among domain-adaptation methods, the closest competitor is SHOT (67.8/64.3 on UCIHAR), which TransPL exceeds by 1.2% Acc and 0.6% MF1; the gap widens to 6.8% Acc on HHAR (68.4 vs. 64.8) and 5.6% Acc on PTB (67.2 vs. 61.6). RAINCOAT is highly inconsistent, achieving the best MF1 on WISDM (63.0) but the worst accuracy there (47.3), while TransPL maintains balanced performance. Among pseudo-labeling baselines, T2PL (63.8/60.6 on UCIHAR) and SoftMax (62.4/57.4) are the strongest, yet TransPL leads by 5.2% and 6.6% accuracy respectively. In the weakly-supervised setting (Table 4), TransPL with the true label prior gains +2.2 to +7.3% accuracy across datasets, whereas CoDATS degrades on UCIHAR (−0.4%), HHAR (−0.6%), and PTB (−5.3%), confirming that TransPL's Bayes-rule integration of the prior is more robust than CoDATS's KL-divergence minimization. Discriminative sequential models (1D-CNN, LSTM, GRU) used as pseudo-labeling baselines in Table 3 are substantially outperformed (e.g., TransPL 71.0% vs. 1D-CNN 65.8% on UCIHAR), validating the transition-matrix approach over direct sequence classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

TransPL builds on VQVAE's residual quantization mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and optimal transport (Peyré et al., 2019), repurposing them for a novel pseudo-labeling pipeline in time series UDA. It departs from prior pseudo-labeling strategies (Softmax, SHOT, T2PL) that operate as black boxes derived from image domains, and from time series DA methods (CoDATS, SASA, RAINCOAT, CauDiTS) that align representations without explicitly modeling temporal state transitions or per-channel shifts. The key conceptual departure is treating VQ codes as states in a discrete Markov chain, where class-conditional transition matrices encode temporal dynamics and channel-wise transition matrices quantify domain shift via Earth Mover's Distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TransPL addresses a practical gap in time series UDA by providing interpretable pseudo-labels that reveal which temporal patterns and which channels drive the adaptation decision, rather than treating the process as a black box. The consistent 3% improvement over the strongest baseline (SHOT) across four diverse datasets (HAR and ECG) with a principled weak-supervision integration via Bayes' rule makes it a practical option for sensor-based applications. Key limitations remain: channels carrying class-relevant information that undergo strong domain shift are downweighted, and the coarse-fine code hierarchy is not mathematically enforced, leaving open questions about incorporating channel importance measures and formal regularization of the residual codebook structure.
