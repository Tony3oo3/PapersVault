## Problem

Existing KG-enhanced LLM reasoning methods—whether retrieval-based (requiring additional accurate retrievers) or agent-based (incurring high latency from iterative KG interactions)—still suffer from substantial hallucination, with leading methods like RoG exhibiting 33% hallucination errors. No prior approach simultaneously guarantees zero hallucination in reasoning paths, efficient single-pass inference, and state-of-the-art KGQA performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a knowledge graph, converting the KG into a KG-Trie (a trie-based index of tokenized reasoning paths within L hops of question entities) that is plugged directly into the LLM's decoding process as a prefix constraint. The KG-specialized LLM outputs K KG-grounded reasoning paths (delimited by special <PATH>/</PATH> tokens) paired with hypothesis answers via beam search in a single call; these are then fed to a general LLM that produces the final answer. Because the KG-Trie restricts every generated token to valid KG path prefixes, all reasoning paths are guaranteed to be grounded in the KG, yielding zero hallucination by construction.

## Method

GCR integrates KG structure directly into the LLM's autoregressive decoding: a KG-Trie built from BFS-retrieved reasoning paths (formatted as sentences, tokenized, and stored as a prefix tree) constrains each decoding step so only tokens forming valid KG path prefixes are permitted (Eq. 7), eliminating hallucinated paths without external retrieval or iterative agent interaction. A lightweight KG-specialized LLM (e.g., fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B on question–shortest-path–answer triples) generates K KG-grounded reasoning paths and hypothesis answers in a single beam-search call, exploiting parallel GPU computation. These K paths and hypotheses are then passed to a powerful general LLM (ChatGPT or GPT-4o-mini) via a FiD-style multi-document prompt for inductive reasoning to produce the final answer. The key architectural distinction is that KG constraints operate at the token-decoding level rather than through a separate retriever or multi-turn agent loop, enabling constant-time path exploration and zero-shot transfer to unseen KGs simply by swapping the KG-Trie without retraining.

## Results

GCR achieves state-of-the-art performance on both KGQA benchmarks: 92.6% Hit and 73.2% F1 on WebQSP, and 75.8% Hit and 61.7% F1 on CWQ (using Llama-3.1-8B as KG-specialized LLM with GPT-4o-mini as general LLM). This represents a 2.1% Hit improvement over the second-best method (GNN-RAG+RA) on WebQSP and a 9.1% Hit improvement on CWQ. GCR also achieves 100% faithful reasoning ratio (zero hallucination) and demonstrates zero-shot generalizability to unseen KGs, improving accuracy by 8.2% on FreebaseQA and 7.6% on CSQA over GPT-4o-mini alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCR outperforms all 22 baselines across both datasets. Against the strongest LLM-only baseline (ChatGPT+Self-Consistency, 83.5% Hit on WebQSP), GCR gains 9.1% Hit, demonstrating that KG grounding substantially exceeds pure LLM reasoning. Among graph reasoning methods, GCR surpasses the best (UniKGQA, 77.2% Hit on WebQSP) by 15.4% Hit. In the KG+LLM category, GCR beats the closest competitor GNN-RAG+RA (90.7% Hit on WebQSP, 68.7% Hit on CWQ) by 1.9% and 7.1% Hit respectively, while also achieving 100% faithful reasoning versus GNN-RAG+RA's unreported hallucination rate. Compared to agent-based methods like ToG (GPT-4: 82.6% Hit on WebQSP) and EffiQA (82.9% Hit), GCR improves by ~10% Hit while requiring only 2 LLM calls and 231 input tokens versus ToG's 11.6 calls and 7,069 tokens. The zero-shot generalization results further show GCR(GPT-4o-mini) outperforming GPT-4o-mini alone by 5% on FreebaseQA (94 vs 89) and 3% on CSQA (94 vs 91), confirming that the KG-Trie constraint transfers to unseen knowledge graphs without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained-decoding paradigm (originally applied to entity retrieval by De Cao et al., 2022) to knowledge graph reasoning, repurposing trie-based prefix constraints to enforce that LLM-generated reasoning paths are valid KG subpaths. It departs from both retrieval-based KG-enhanced methods (RoG, GNN-RAG, KD-CoT) that rely on external retrievers and agent-based methods (ToG, StructGPT) that iteratively query KGs, by embedding the KG structure directly into the autoregressive decoding loop via a KG-Trie index. The inductive reasoning stage borrows the FiD multi-document framework (Izacard & Grave, 2021) to aggregate multiple constrained paths, and the overall formulation generalizes Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022) by replacing free token generation with KG-structured path generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving 100% KG-grounded reasoning with only 2 LLM calls and ~231 input tokens, compared to 11.6 calls and ~7,000 tokens for agent-based baselines, making faithful KG reasoning feasible in latency-sensitive settings. Zero-shot transfer to unseen KGs (ConceptNet, medical KG) without retraining is a strong practical property for deployment. However, the 'zero-hallucination' guarantee is relative to the KG itself, not ground truth—KG incompleteness and incorrect facts remain unaddressed, as shown by the failure cases. Open questions include scaling KG-Trie construction to billion-edge graphs, handling temporal or dynamic KGs, and whether the approach extends to more complex multi-hop or compositional reasoning beyond 2-hop paths.
