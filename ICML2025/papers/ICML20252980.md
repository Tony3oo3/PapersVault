## Problem

Prior constitutional AI approaches rely on generic, predefined principles (e.g., 'avoid harmful or unethical content') whose precise meaning is situation-dependent, making them insufficient for nuanced tasks like cognitive reappraisal or instance-specific evaluation. Crafting expert-level, context-specific guidance for every query is resource-intensive and requires domain expertise (e.g., clinical psychologists for emotional support). SPRI addresses this by automatically generating tailored guiding principles in real-time for each individual input, requiring minimal or no human effort.

## Input / Output

SPRI takes a user text query T and optionally a small set of seed (instruction, principle) pairs as few-shot examples to stabilize principle generation. It produces two outputs: a set of context-situated principles K_final (capped at 128 words) and an aligned response R_final that adheres to those principles. The critic model scores both principles and responses on a 1–5 Likert scale, and the iterative loop terminates when a score of at least 4 is reached or after a maximum of four iterations. In the SFT setting, the framework outputs synthetic prompt–response pairs at scale (e.g., 10k–50k examples) for fine-tuning downstream models.

## Method

SPRI operates in two stages, each with a critique-refine loop between a base model M and a (potentially smaller) critic model C. In Stage I, M generates initial principles K_0 from the input T, and C evaluates their fitness to T using a chain-of-thought direct-assessment prompt that yields both qualitative feedback and a numerical score; M then refines the principles based on this feedback, repeating until the score reaches 4 or the iteration cap is hit. In Stage II, the finalized principles K_final are injected into M's prompt to guide response generation, and C evaluates the response against K_final as rubrics, with M iteratively revising the response based on the critic's feedback. The key distinction from prior work is that principles are synthesized per-query rather than drawn from a static set, and the same principles serve dual roles: guiding generation and providing evaluation criteria. The critic model need not be stronger or larger than the base model, and the framework is agnostic to the base model's size or architecture.

## Results

On TruthfulQA, fine-tuning base models with SPRI-generated data surpasses off-the-shelf models by an average of 24.76% and oracle-response fine-tuned models by 19.09% (e.g., Gemma-2-9B reaches 62.62% vs. 45.39% off-the-shelf). On BiGGen Bench, SPRI-generated rubrics achieve Pearson correlation of 0.472 (GPT-4o-mini) and 0.480 (Llama-3.1-70B), outperforming the best instance-agnostic baseline (MT-Bench rubric) by 12.1% on average while approaching oracle rubrics (0.550/0.556) within 0.07. On cognitive reappraisal, SPRI yields an average 6.1% improvement in alignment and 8.4% in empathy over the strongest vanilla baseline, approaching oracle-principle performance (e.g., 8.17 vs. 8.53 alignment for Llama-3.1-70B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRI consistently outperforms all instance-agnostic baselines across all three evaluation settings. Against Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023), SPRI wins by up to 11.0% in reappraisal alignment, 12.1% in BiGGen Bench Pearson correlation, and 1.8–3.5% on TruthfulQA. Against the MT-Bench rubric (Zheng et al., 2023) and FLASK rubric (Ye et al., 2024), SPRI achieves substantially higher Pearson correlations on BiGGen Bench (e.g., 0.472 vs. 0.416 and 0.358 for GPT-4o-mini). Against Self-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023) and Self-Align (Sun et al., 2023) for synthetic data generation, SPRI leads on TruthfulQA by 1.4–4.9% and 1.4–4.3% respectively. SPRI approaches but does not fully match the oracle baselines: on cognitive reappraisal, oracle principles (Zhan et al., 2024) still lead by ~0.4–0.6 alignment points, and on BiGGen Bench, oracle rubrics (Kim et al., 2025) exceed SPRI by an average 0.07 Pearson correlation. The 'seed principles' ablation (using generic, task-irrelevant principles) confirms that the gains come from context-specificity rather than the mere presence of principles in the prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPRI extends the Constitutional AI paradigm (Bai et al., 2022b; Sun et al., 2023) by replacing static, hand-crafted principle sets with principles that are dynamically generated per-query through an iterative critique-refine loop. The iterative refinement mechanism borrows from Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023), but is fundamentally distinguished by being guided by context-situated principles rather than generic self-feedback. The paper also positions itself against LLM-as-a-judge frameworks (Zheng et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2024) that rely on coarse-grained or domain-specific rubrics, showing that auto-generated instance-specific rubrics can approach the quality of human-curated ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPRI addresses a practical bottleneck in LLM alignment: the heavy reliance on domain experts to craft context-specific guidance (e.g., clinical psychologists for reappraisal, crowd-sourced annotators for evaluation rubrics). The framework's ability to approach oracle-level performance with minimal human input (a single seed example) makes it a scalable alternative for tasks requiring nuanced oversight. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly novel domains with no relevant seed examples, how the quality of the critic model bounds the ceiling of generated principles, and whether the multi-call overhead (4–6 model calls per response) is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment settings.
