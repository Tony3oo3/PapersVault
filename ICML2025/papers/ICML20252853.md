## Problem

Loss reweighting has become a common technique to mitigate over-forgetting in gradient-ascent-based LLM unlearning, yet the precise functional roles of existing reweighting strategies (e.g., NPO, WGA) remain ambiguous, making it unclear which design directions to pursue. This paper asks: what are the distinct goals that reweighting can serve, and how do the smoothness and granularity of the resulting weight distributions affect unlearning efficacy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252853/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-2-7B, Phi-1.5, or Zephyr-7B-beta) that has been fine-tuned on a full dataset, and takes as input the unlearn set (QA pairs to forget), an optional retain set (QA pairs to preserve), and the model's own token-wise conditional probabilities computed on-the-fly during each training step. The output is a set of updated LLM parameters that have reduced likelihood on the unlearn set while maintaining performance on the retain set. A key operational detail is that reweighting is applied at the token level (Eq. 4) rather than the instance level (Eq. 3), and the weights are dynamic—recomputed from the current model's likelihoods at every forward pass—rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

The paper identifies two complementary reweighting goals: Saturation (assign higher weights to tokens that are still sufficiently unlearned, i.e., high-likelihood tokens, to prevent over-forgetting) and Importance (assign higher weights to semantically critical tokens, which empirically correlate with low-likelihood tokens, to accelerate convergence). It then derives simplified, label-free proxies: SimSat uses w = p(y^k|y^{<k},x;θ)^β and SimImp uses w = (1−p(y^k|y^{<k},x;θ))^β′, where β controls the smoothness of the weight distribution. The proposed SatImp method multiplies both components—w = p^β1 · (1−p)^β2—shifting emphasis to middle-loss tokens and offering two hyperparameters to independently tune saturation and importance tendencies. Extensive ablations show that saturation-based reweighting outperforms importance-based on retention, their product yields further gains, soft token-wise reweighting beats hard sampling and coarser granularities, and an excessively peaked or overly uniform weight distribution degrades performance.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark with retain regularization, SatImp achieves the best retention (ES Re.) across all settings: 0.6079 (Phi-1.5, 1%), 0.5410 (Phi-1.5, 5%), and 0.4706 (Phi-1.5, 10%), while maintaining competitive unlearning (ES Un. 0.0464, 0.0427, 0.0407 respectively). On LLaMA-2-7B, SatImp reaches ES Re. 0.8034 (1%) and ES Un. 0.0579, outperforming all baselines on retention. On WMDP with forget-only regularization, SatImp achieves the best MMLU retention (0.3197) while matching GA-level unlearning accuracy (WMDP-Bio 0.2470, WMDP-Cyber 0.2456).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SatImp consistently outperforms all six baselines on retention (ES Re.) across TOFU settings. Against GA, SatImp dramatically improves retention (e.g., 0.6079 vs. 0.2098 on Phi-1.5 1%) while maintaining competitive unlearning (ES Un. 0.0464 vs. 0.0322), confirming that vanilla GA suffers severe over-forgetting. Against NPO, SatImp improves both retention (0.6079 vs. 0.4514) and unlearning (0.0464 vs. 0.0583) on Phi-1.5 1%, and on LLaMA-2-7B the gap widens further (0.8034 vs. 0.6692 retention). Against WGA (the closest competitor), SatImp gains ~2.4% ES Re. on Phi-1.5 1% (0.6079 vs. 0.5835) and ~4.3% on LLaMA-2-7B 1% (0.8034 vs. 0.7603), with comparable unlearning performance. Against SimNPO and DPO, SatImp dominates on both retention and unlearning metrics. On WMDP, SatImp achieves the best MMLU retention (0.3197) matching RMU (0.3193) while achieving the lowest WMDP-Cyber accuracy (0.2456), indicating superior unlearning-utility balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the gradient-ascent (GA) unlearning framework of Yao et al. (2023) and the probability-based token-wise reweighting of WGA (Wang et al., 2025b), whose saturation component is mathematically identical to WGA's weight function. Its key conceptual departure is proposing a unified taxonomy that categorizes all existing reweighting strategies into two complementary types—saturation (emphasizing insufficiently unlearned data) and importance (emphasizing critical tokens)—and demonstrating that NPO and SimNPO, previously interpreted as importance-based, are in fact saturation-based. SatImp then merges both perspectives into a single token-wise weight function, positioning itself as a generalization of WGA that also captures importance-driven reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The saturation/importance taxonomy provides a clear diagnostic lens for the LLM unlearning community, correcting a published mischaracterization of NPO's mechanism and clarifying why different reweighting strategies exhibit complementary strengths. The finding that weight smoothness and granularity critically affect the unlearn-retain trade-off offers practical guidance for method design. However, SatImp remains hyperparameter-sensitive (β₁, β₂), and token-wise reweighting inevitably causes over-forgetting at higher unlearning rates (5%, 10%), a limitation shared with WGA. Open questions include whether a theoretical analysis can explain why saturation dominates importance, whether the taxonomy generalizes beyond QA-style unlearning, and how to make the method robust to hyperparameter selection without manual tuning.
