## Problem

The discrete-time last-iterate convergence rate of Mean-Field Langevin Stochastic Descent-Ascent (MFL-SDA) for distributional minimax optimization remained unexplored. Existing discrete-time analyses either relied on PDE-based techniques connecting to continuous-time flows, or achieved suboptimal rates (e.g., O(ε^{-1-O(1/α)})) for averaged-iterate convergence, leaving open whether a nearly optimal O(1/ε log 1/ε) last-iterate guarantee comparable to Euclidean minimax optimization could be established.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on probability distributions μ₀, ν₀ over R^{d_Θ} and R^{d_Ω} (not finite-dimensional parameter vectors), with the convex-concave functional J providing Wasserstein gradients via first variations δJ/δμ and δJ/δν. Updates use two distinct step sizes η₁ < η₂ (two-timescale scheme) and inject Gaussian noise scaled by √(2ηᵢτ) at each step, with the KL regularization parameter τ controlling both the entropy penalty and the noise magnitude. The output is characterized by the Lyapunov gap L(μ_K, ν_K) converging to a bias R₂ = O(η₁), which translates via log-Sobolev inequalities into KL-divergence and W₂-distance bounds to the unique saddle point (μ*, ν*).

## Method

The paper introduces an elementary analysis framework that directly bounds per-step changes in a composite Lyapunov function L(μ,ν) = L₁(μ) + λL₂(μ,ν) (outer gap plus weighted inner gap) without invoking PDE or continuous-time flow arguments. The μ-update is analyzed via a fourth-order Taylor expansion of the value function E*(μ) = max_ν E(μ,ν), where smoothness of the Gibbs fixed-point operator K*⁺[μ] is established through log-Sobolev inequalities and bounded cross-variations of J. The ν-update exploits the log-Sobolev inequality (the distributional analogue of the PL condition) to contract the inner gap L₂ geometrically at rate (1−2η₂τα). Time-discretization errors from the injected Gaussian noise are controlled as O(η²) higher-order terms using integration by parts and the divergence theorem, rather than being absorbed into the continuous-time limit. The stochastic gradient extension follows by bounding additional O(η²) error terms from the fourth moments of gradient noise (Assumption 4), yielding the final O(1/ε log 1/ε) last-iterate rate that matches the complexity of mean-field Langevin SGD for distributional minimization.

## Results

The paper establishes a last-iterate convergence rate of O(1/ε log 1/ε) for the discrete-time MFL-SDA algorithm (Theorem 2), with the Lyapunov function decaying geometrically at rate (1 − 2η₁τα)^K to a bias R₂ of order O(η₁). For the exact-gradient MFL-DA variant (Theorem 1), the same geometric decay holds with bias R₁ = O(dη₁/(τ³α³)), yielding a sample complexity K = O(d/(ετ⁴α⁴) · log(1/ε)). Corollary 1 further translates this into convergence in KL-divergence and 2-Wasserstein distance to the saddle point (μ*, ν*), with the rate governed by the log-Sobolev constant α₁ of the Gibbs distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or dataset evaluations, so comparisons are made at the level of iteration complexity. The O(1/ε log 1/ε) last-iterate rate is nearly optimal relative to the O(1/ε) rate achievable for stochastic gradient descent-ascent under two-sided PL conditions in Euclidean space (Yan et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2020), with the extra log factor arising from time-discretization bias that cannot be absorbed in the distributional setting. The rate matches the O(1/ε log 1/ε) complexity of mean-field Langevin stochastic gradient descent for distributional minimization (Nitanda et al., 2022a; Suzuki et al., 2024) and the outer-loop iteration complexity of the double-loop MFL-ABR algorithm (Kim et al., 2024). Compared to the MFL-AG method of Kim et al. (2024), which achieves only an average-iterate rate of O(ε^{-1-O(1/α)}) in squared 1-Wasserstein distance, the present result provides a last-iterate guarantee in 2-Wasserstein distance that improves upon the conservative α = O(1/d) bound. The key methodological advantage is an elementary analysis framework avoiding PDE-based techniques, making the stochastic gradient extension straightforward and the perturbation analysis flexible enough to handle the two-timescale structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-time convergence analysis of Mean-Field Langevin Dynamics from single-objective minimization (Nitanda et al., 2022a; Suzuki et al., 2024) to the distributional minimax setting, building on Lu's (2023) continuous-time two-time-scale dynamics and Kim et al.'s (2024) MFLAG/MFL-ABR algorithms. The key methodological departure is from prior discrete-time analyses that relied on PDE-based techniques to connect discrete updates to continuous-time gradient flow; here, the authors use an elementary framework of Taylor expansions, integration by parts, and the divergence theorem to directly bound discretization errors, mirroring the structure of Euclidean minimax analyses (Yang et al., 2020). The two-time-scale alternating update scheme follows Yang et al. (2020) and Lu (2023), while the log-Sobolev inequality as a distributional PL condition is inherited from the MFLD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap by establishing the first nearly-optimal last-iterate convergence rate for discrete-time MFL-SDA, matching the O(1/ε log 1/ε) complexity of distributional minimization and the outer-loop of the double-loop MFL-ABR algorithm. The elementary analysis framework is more flexible than PDE-based approaches and extends naturally to stochastic gradient oracles, making it a practical tool for future algorithm design. Open questions include finite-particle approximation (propagation of chaos), whether a single-timescale variant can avoid the large time-scale ratio, and whether sharper bounds are possible for specific functional classes (bilinear, convex-concave compositions). The results have direct implications for training GANs and mean-field neural networks in the distributional minimax setting.
