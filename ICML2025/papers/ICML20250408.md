## Problem

Existing dataset pruning methods require training a model on the full dataset for many epochs (e.g., 200 for CIFAR, 90 for ImageNet) before computing per-sample importance scores, making the pruning overhead comparable to or exceeding the cost of a single full training run. This paper asks whether informative samples can be identified from only the early training stage (30–60 epochs) without sacrificing pruning quality, and how to prevent catastrophic accuracy drops at extreme pruning ratios where the retained subset becomes too small and biased.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled RGB image dataset and the target-class prediction probabilities from a partially trained CNN (ResNet-18/34/50 or VGG-16) at a single early checkpoint T (30 epochs for CIFAR, 60 for ImageNet), using a sliding window of J=10 consecutive epochs to compute per-sample statistics. It outputs a pruned coreset of size (1−r)·n selected via weighted sampling, where the sampling probability for each sample is a product of its DUAL score and a Beta PDF evaluated at its prediction mean, with Beta parameters that shift as a function of the pruning ratio r. No gradient information, feature vectors, or full training trajectory is required.

## Method

The DUAL score multiplies two per-sample terms computed over a sliding window: (a) 1 − P̄_k, the mean prediction error quantifying example difficulty, and (b) the standard deviation of target-class predictions over the window, quantifying prediction uncertainty. The key insight, supported by a moon-shaped scatter of prediction mean vs. standard deviation, is that at early epochs Dyn-Unc (uncertainty alone) scores easy samples highest, whereas DUAL targets the bottom-right region—samples with moderate difficulty and rising uncertainty—that will become the most informative by the end of training. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that for a linearly separable toy problem, the DUAL score's ranking flip (hard-but-useful sample overtaking an easy one) occurs strictly earlier than for pure uncertainty. To handle high pruning ratios, a Beta-distribution sampling strategy assigns each sample a selection probability proportional to its DUAL score weighted by a Beta PDF evaluated at its prediction mean; the Beta parameters α_r and β_r are chosen so that the distribution's mean shifts from the highest-scoring sample's prediction mean (at r≈0) toward 1 (at r→1), progressively including easier samples to maintain distributional coverage.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, DUAL+β sampling achieves a SOTA 60.0% top-1 accuracy at 90% pruning ratio, surpassing the previous best (CCS at 57.3%) by 2.7 points, while reducing total computation time to 66% of prior methods. On CIFAR-10, the method reaches 87.09% at 90% pruning (vs. CCS 85.92%, D² 86.11%), and on CIFAR-100 it achieves 54.54% (vs. CCS 54.23%, D² 50.14%), all while requiring only 30 epochs for score computation versus 200 for baselines. On CIFAR datasets, total time cost is reduced to just 15% of full-training-based methods while maintaining SOTA performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUAL+β sampling outperforms all 10 static baselines at every pruning ratio on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with the largest margins at 90% pruning: it beats CCS (85.92%→87.09% on CIFAR-10; 54.23%→54.54% on CIFAR-100) and D² (86.11%→87.09%; 50.14%→54.54%). Difficulty-based methods (EL2N, AUM) collapse catastrophically at high pruning ratios (EL2N drops to 38.74% on CIFAR-10 and 8.89% on CIFAR-100 at 90%), while uncertainty-based Dyn-Unc (59.67% / 34.57%) and TDDS (82.49% / 47.93%) remain competitive at moderate ratios but fall well behind DUAL+β at 90%. On ImageNet-1k, DUAL+β achieves 60.0% at 90% pruning versus CCS (57.3%) and D² (55.6%), and notably Dyn-Unc underperforms even random pruning (49.0% vs. 52.3%) due to insufficient training epochs. Against dynamic pruning methods (InfoBatch, IES) in Appendix B.5, DUAL achieves 70%+ accuracy at 50% pruning under 40% label noise where InfoBatch reaches only 51.24% and IES prunes merely 1.7% of samples. The key advantage is computational: DUAL requires only 30 epochs (CIFAR) or 60 epochs (ImageNet) for score computation versus 200/90 for baselines, reducing total time to 15% (CIFAR) or 66% (ImageNet) of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dyn-Unc (He et al., 2024), which measures prediction uncertainty via sliding-window standard deviation over the full training trajectory. The key conceptual departure is multiplying the uncertainty term by (1 − mean prediction) to incorporate example difficulty, yielding the DUAL score that can be computed after only 30–60 epochs rather than the full 200-epoch training required by Dyn-Unc, TDDS, CCS, and D². The pruning-ratio-adaptive Beta sampling extends the line of work on diversity-aware selection (CCS, BOSS, SIMS) by defining the Beta PDF over prediction mean rather than a difficulty score, and by using a non-linear parameterization that keeps the distribution nearly stationary at low pruning ratios. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) formalizes why the difficulty-weighted score identifies the most informative samples strictly earlier than pure uncertainty in a toy linearly-separable setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: on CIFAR the total pruning pipeline costs only 15% of a full training run while matching or exceeding SOTA, and on ImageNet-1K it achieves 60% accuracy at 90% pruning (vs. 57.3% for the prior best, CCS) at 66% of the compute. The demonstrated noise robustness—nearly optimally pruning mislabeled samples at a 10% pruning ratio and outperforming full-dataset training under 20–40% label noise—addresses a real-world data-quality concern that most score-based methods handle poorly. Cross-architecture transfer (scores from a 3-layer CNN or ResNet-18 generalizing to ResNet-50/VGG-16) further reduces the cost of score computation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unsupervised or self-supervised settings, whether the 30-epoch minimum can be reduced further, and how the method behaves on non-vision modalities where the 'easy-first' learning dynamics may differ.
