## Problem

Non-equilibrium sampling methods like annealed importance sampling (AIS) transport walkers from a simple base distribution to a complex target in finite time, but the resulting importance weights exhibit high variance when the walkers' distribution lags behind the evolving target. Existing learned-transport approaches to mitigate this lag either require backpropagation through the stochastic differential equation (as in CMCD) or introduce bias into the objective. NETS addresses this gap by providing an unbiased sampler with learned transport whose training objective avoids backpropagation entirely and provably bounds the KL divergence between the estimated and target distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized energy potential U_1(x) (known up to a partition function) and a simple base distribution (e.g., a standard Gaussian), and learns a drift network via a feed-forward neural network parameterizing both the velocity field b_t(x) and the free energy F_t. It produces unbiased samples from the target, expectation estimates for arbitrary test functions, and a free energy / partition function estimate via Jarzynski's equality. A key practical detail is that the diffusion coefficient ε_t is tunable post-training (ranging from 0 for pure transport to large values for near-independent sampling), and the method can be augmented with SMC-style resampling to push effective sample size toward 100%.

## Method

NETS augments the annealed Langevin SDE with a learned drift term b_t(x) that guides walkers toward the evolving target, reducing the lag that causes high-variance importance weights. The drift is trained via a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) objective that minimizes the squared residual of the Fokker-Planck equation, ∇·b_t − ∇U_t·b_t − ∂_t U_t + ∂_t F_t, which is off-policy (usable with any proposal distribution) and requires no backpropagation through the SDE simulation. The method generalizes Jarzynski's equality to the augmented dynamics, preserving unbiasedness through importance weights whose variance is dramatically reduced by the learned transport. The PINN objective is shown to provably bound the KL divergence between the sampler's output and the target distribution, and the diffusion coefficient ε_t can be adjusted after training to maximize effective sample size without retraining.

## Results

On the 40-mode GMM (d=2) benchmark, NETS-PINN with ε_t=4 achieves an effective sample size (ESS) of 0.979±0.002 and a 2-Wasserstein distance of 3.14±0.46, outperforming the best baseline (CMCD-LV: ESS 0.655, W2 4.01) by a wide margin. With SMC-style resampling, ESS reaches 0.993±0.004. On the 50-dimensional Mixture of Student-T, NETS-PINN-resample achieves MMD 0.030±0.000 and W2 400.1±59.3, roughly 3× better in MMD than the next-best method (DDS: MMD 0.131). On the 400-dimensional lattice φ⁴ theory at the phase transition, NETS is nearly 2 orders of magnitude more statistically efficient than plain AIS in terms of ESS over time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NETS outperforms all seven baselines on the 40-mode GMM in both ESS and W₂. The closest competitor, CMCD-LV (Vargas et al., 2024), achieves ESS 0.655 and W₂ 4.01, while NETS-PINN (ε_t=4) reaches ESS 0.979 and W₂ 3.14 — a ~50% improvement in ESS and ~22% reduction in W₂. FAB (Midgley et al., 2023) and pDEM (Akhound-Sadegh et al., 2024) show the weakest W₂ scores (12.0 and 12.20 respectively), indicating poor mode coverage. On the 50-dim Mixture of Student-T, NETS-PINN-resample achieves MMD 0.030, roughly 4× better than DDS (0.131) and 3× better than BAM (0.093), and W₂ 400.1 versus BAM's 1204.2. On Neal's funnel (d=10), NETS-PINN-resample matches the best MMD (0.027 vs. GAMI's 0.031) but trails slightly in W₂ (343.8 vs. GAMI's 105.6). The PIS baseline (Zhang & Chen, 2021) and CMCD-KL show the lowest ESS on the GMM (0.295 and 0.268), indicating severe weight degeneracy without learned transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

NETS sits at the intersection of non-equilibrium sampling (AIS, Jarzynski's equality) and learned dynamical transport (neural ODEs, score-based models, stochastic interpolants). The closest prior work is CMCD (Vargas et al., 2024), which also augments annealed Langevin dynamics with a learned drift, but derives its training objective via Girsanov's theorem and path integrals, requiring backpropagation through the SDE or a numerically unstable reference measure. NETS departs by deriving its PINN objective through Fokker-Planck equation manipulations, yielding a loss whose gradient can be estimated without differentiating through the sampling simulation, and by establishing that this objective provably bounds the KL divergence between the sampler and target. The method also connects to action matching (Neklyudov et al., 2023) and to the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2022), which provides a principled way to choose the interpolating potential U_t without access to target samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NETS offers a practically important combination: unbiased sampling with a post-training tunable diffusion coefficient, a training objective that requires no backpropagation through SDEs, and a provable KL-divergence bound that doubles as a convergence monitor. The two-orders-of-magnitude statistical efficiency gain over AIS on lattice φ⁴ theory (up to 400 dimensions) is compelling for statistical physics applications where HMC is the standard but struggles near phase transitions. Limitations include the need for 1500–2000 integration steps near critical points, the challenge of learning the drift in very high dimensions, and the fact that the strongest empirical comparisons are against AIS alone rather than against the best learned-transport baselines in all settings. Open questions include whether the post-training diffusion tuning can be automated, how the method scales beyond 400 dimensions, and whether the multimarginal and inertial extensions (Appendix D) can be exploited for further variance reduction.
