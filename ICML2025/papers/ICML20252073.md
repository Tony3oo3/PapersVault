## Problem

Neural networks encode knowledge in weights, making data unlearning, privacy auditing, and bias correction extremely difficult. Prior work separated knowledge representation (feature embeddings) from knowledge storage (visual memory), but the embedding model itself was still trained on real-world data, leaving the unlearning and privacy problem unsolved for the encoder. This paper closes that gap by training the embedding model exclusively on procedurally generated data, so that all real-world data resides solely in the easily editable memory database.

## Input / Output

At inference, a query RGB image is encoded by the procedurally-trained ViT into a fixed-length embedding, which is then matched via k-nearest-neighbour search against a database of pre-computed reference image embeddings (up to ~1.3M entries for ImageNet). For in-context segmentation, the system additionally accepts a prompt image paired with a binary prompt mask to guide retrieval. Outputs are a class label (majority vote over KNN neighbours), a per-pixel segmentation mask (via PCA clustering or prompt-guided search), or a binary similarity judgment on the NIGHTS benchmark. Critically, the embedding model has never seen any real image; all real-world semantics are carried entirely by the memory database.

## Method

The paper trains a Vision Transformer (ViT-S) using the DINO self-supervised local-to-global similarity objective, but on procedurally generated OpenGL shader images rather than natural photos. Two new data generation processes are introduced: Shaders KML, which extracts a spatial mixing mask from one shader via K-Means clustering in RGB space and uses it to blend two other shaders (replacing the constant mask of standard Mixup), and Shaders KML Mixup, which further applies pixel-space Mixup on top to suppress shortcut solutions. At test time, no fine-tuning or parametric classifier is used: the query embedding is simply matched to its k-nearest neighbours in the visual memory, and the majority label is returned. This design ensures that adding, removing, or auditing any real-world data sample requires only modifying the memory database, while the embedding weights remain fixed and free of real-data privacy exposure.

## Results

On KNN classification with visual memory, the best procedural model (Shaders KML Mixup) achieves 75.20% on Flowers102, 27.08% on CUB200, 48.70% on Food101, and 37.88% on ImageNet-1K. On the NIGHTS human visual similarity benchmark, the S.KML model reaches 82.4% alignment with human judgments, within 0.9% of the Places model. For zero-shot segmentation on COCO, the best procedural model (S.KML) achieves an R² of 55.9, within 10% of real-data models (Places: 62.1, ImageNet: 63.7). On MedMNIST, procedural models match or exceed the best result from the original paper (a normally trained ResNet) in 7 out of 10 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Places baseline (realistic, non-overlapping training data), the best procedural model (S.KML Mixup) wins on all three fine-grained classification datasets: Flowers102 by 15.7 points (75.20 vs 59.51), CUB200 by 8.0 points (27.08 vs 19.09), and Food101 by 0.9 points (48.70 vs 47.78). However, on ImageNet-1K the procedural model trails Places by 9.4 points (37.88 vs 47.30), and on COCO segmentation R² it is 6.2 points below (55.9 vs 62.1). Against the ImageNet target model (which has semantic overlap with evaluation sets), the procedural model is consistently worse, as expected, with gaps of 8.2–27.9 points across datasets. On MedMNIST, the procedural S.KML model matches or exceeds the best result from the original MedMNIST paper (a normally trained ResNet) in 7 of 10 datasets (e.g., Path: 99.30 vs 91.1, Derma: 82.15 vs 76.8), while trailing on OCT (92.12 vs 77.6) and OrganA (85.07 vs 70.3) where the ResNet was stronger. The authors attribute the fine-grained wins over Places to the absence of semantic overlap between Places and those datasets, meaning the Places model wastes capacity on irrelevant scene associations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: procedural-data representation learning (Baradad et al., 2021; 2022) and visual-memory-based perception (Geirhos et al., 2024; Nakata et al., 2022). Prior procedural-data work combined non-realistic embeddings with linear classifiers trained on real data, leaving real-data knowledge embedded in weights. This paper departs by pairing procedural embeddings with a KNN visual memory, so that all real-world data resides exclusively in the memory database rather than in the model weights. The training objective (DINO self-supervised learning on ViT) and the KNN inference mechanism are both inherited from prior work; the novelty is in their combination and in the new Shaders KML / KML Mixup data generation processes that improve embedding quality over prior Shaders Mixup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the clean separation it enables: all real data is in a single, editable database, making unlearning, privacy auditing, and differential-privacy computation straightforward operations on the memory rather than expensive weight retraining. The key limitation is that procedural models lack object-wholeness understanding—parts of the same real object receive dissimilar representations—causing incorrect KNN retrieval and a persistent ~10% gap on ImageNet-1K. Open questions include whether this gap can be closed by richer procedural data or by auxiliary object-part consistency objectives, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D where the memory storage cost becomes more acute.
