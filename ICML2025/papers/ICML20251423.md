## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting (CF) is widely observed in CNNs during continual learning, yet theoretical understanding of CF in non-linear models—particularly CNNs—remains limited. Existing analyses focus on linear regression or the Neural Tangent Kernel regime, leaving the feature-learning dynamics that drive CF in convolutional architectures unexplained. This paper fills that gap by providing the first theoretical characterization of CF in a two-layer CNN.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251423/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes P+3 non-overlapping image patches of dimension d, where three patches carry structured features (task-specific, general/robust, and random) and P patches carry Gaussian background noise. The network outputs a binary label via the sign of a sum of cubic activations over all patches. Theoretically, the paper produces conditions on feature signal strengths (α_u, α_v, α_ζ) that determine whether CF occurs, and empirically validates these on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet split into sequential binary tasks.

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient descent dynamics in a two-layer patch-wise CNN (cubic activation, fixed last layer) under a multi-view data model where each task contains a task-specific feature u^(τ), a shared general feature v, a random feature ζ^(τ) (which is the other task's task-specific feature), and background noise. By tracking the ODE evolution of inner products ⟨w_c, e⟩ for each feature direction e, the authors show that when α_v ≪ α_u, only the task-specific feature is captured in stage 1, and when α_ζ ≥ Ω(α_u), the random feature in task 2 drives a negative gradient that erases the learned u^(1) projection—formally establishing CF. The analysis further shows that replaying Ω(n) samples from task 1 restores the positive gradient on u^(1) and suppresses forgetting, providing a theoretical justification for replay-based methods.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical: CF occurs in a two-layer CNN when (1) the general/robust feature has a signal α_v ≤ o(α_u) relative to the task-specific feature, and (2) the task-specific feature from Task 1 appears as a random feature with strong signal α_ζ ≥ Ω(α_u) in Task 2. Empirically, on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet, the model predominantly extracts task-specific (non-robust) features across most task pairs, confirming condition (1) in practice. T-SNE visualizations on CIFAR-10 (Task-0 → Task-3) show significant feature overlap between tasks, supporting condition (2). Simulated experiments confirm that replaying at least Ω(n) data points from the previous task suppresses CF, while increasing α_v (task similarity) also reduces forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other continual learning methods; its contribution is a theoretical characterization of when and why CF occurs in two-layer CNNs, validated empirically. The 'comparisons' are internal: varying α_v and α_ζ in simulated data shows that CF is suppressed when α_v ≥ Ω(α_u) (strong general feature) or α_ζ ≤ o(α_u) (weak random feature), and occurs when both conditions for forgetting are met simultaneously. On real-world datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), the paper demonstrates that the model's extracted features are predominantly task-specific rather than shared, with T-SNE plots showing that features from Task 1 and Task 2 exhibit significant overlap in representation space—consistent with the theoretical condition α_ζ ≥ Ω(α_u). The replay-based method is validated as a mitigation: incorporating additional samples from the previous task into the second-stage training set restores accuracy on both tasks, matching the theoretical prediction that ñ ≥ Ω(n) replay samples are sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view data model framework introduced by Allen-Zhu & Li (2023) and Shen et al. (2022) from single-task CNN analysis to sequential task-incremental continual learning. Prior theoretical work on catastrophic forgetting has been largely confined to linear models (Evron et al., 2022; Goldfarb & Hand, 2023; Lin et al., 2023) or the NTK regime (Doan et al., 2021), while prior two-layer CNN analyses (Jelassi & Li, 2022; Kou et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2024) assume random initialization at the start of training. The key departure here is that at the beginning of a new task, the network weights carry learned structure from the previous task, a setting not addressed by any of the above works, making the gradient dynamics fundamentally different from single-task analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of catastrophic forgetting in a non-linear CNN, identifying two concrete mechanistic conditions (signal-strength hierarchy between task-specific and general features, and the role of cross-task random features) that explain when and why CF occurs. The practical implication that GD-trained CNNs preferentially learn the strongest-signal feature rather than the most robust one highlights a fundamental limitation of standard training and motivates the development of robustness-aware optimization. However, the analysis is restricted to a two-layer CNN with a cubic activation, two binary tasks, and a highly structured multi-view data model; whether these insights transfer to deeper architectures, multi-class settings, or the many tasks typical of real CL benchmarks remains an open question. The empirical validation on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet is suggestive (T-SNE clustering, singular-vector analysis) but indirect, as real images cannot be cleanly decomposed into the paper's feature/noise components.
