## Problem

VAE-based structural inference methods treat all edges as uniformly latent, ignoring partial domain knowledge such as known-present/absent edges, global sparsity, and node-degree constraints. Naively overwriting edges in the latent adjacency breaks gradient flow and creates contradictory KL signals against the variational prior, causing training instability or suboptimal inference. The paper asks how to integrate such heterogeneous prior knowledge into a VAE framework without distributional conflicts or gradient disruption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D tensor of node trajectories (N nodes × T timesteps × d features) split into past observations and future targets, plus optional structured priors: a set of known-present edges E⁺, a set of known-absent edges E⁻, a target sparsity fraction ρ, and per-node in/out-degree targets k_i. It outputs a latent directed adjacency matrix in [0,1]^{N×N} (or {0,1}^{N×N} via Gumbel-Softmax) and predicted future node states. The gating parameters α_e ∈ [0,1] are learned per-edge and serve as soft masks throughout both encoder and decoder message passing.

## Method

SGSI introduces three coupled mechanisms within a VAE-GNN framework. First, a learnable soft gate α_e = σ(θ_e) is applied element-wise to every edge message in the encoder's node-to-edge and edge-to-node layers, smoothly suppressing or activating edges while preserving differentiability. Second, a cloning-clamping operation copies the gating vector before forcing known-present edges to 1 and known-absent edges to 0 in the clone, so gradients still flow through the original α_e; known edges are simultaneously excluded from the KL divergence (treated as delta distributions with zero entropy). Third, adaptive L1 penalties softly enforce global sparsity (|Σα_e − ρE|) and per-node degree constraints (|outdeg(i) − k_i|), allowing data-supported deviations at a penalty cost. The theoretical justification frames known edges as consuming zero information-theoretic bits under the VIB objective, thereby reallocating compression capacity to uncertain edges and optimizing the I(X;Z) − βI(Z;Y) trade-off.

## Results

On the StructInfer Vascular Networks benchmark (15–100 nodes), SGSI with both in- and out-degree constraints (SGSI w. Both-deg.) achieves the highest AUROC across all 8 datasets, reaching 97.90% on VN_SP_15, 96.95% on VN_SP_30, 96.46% on VN_SP_50, and 94.81% on VN_SP_100. The largest gains over the best baseline appear on larger, harder graphs: 83.56% on VN_NS_50 (vs. 75.31% for ALaSI, the next-best) and 82.08% on VN_NS_100 (vs. 80.21% for SICSM). On the PEMS road-sensor networks (307–883 nodes), adding 20% known-present edges lifts AUROC from 70.82% to 77.15% on PEMS03 and from 74.20% to 79.07% on PEMS04, confirming that partial prior knowledge yields consistent 4–6% improvements even at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGSI with both in- and out-degree constraints outperforms all seven baselines on every VN dataset. The margin is most pronounced on the harder, larger NetSim-based graphs: on VN_NS_50, SGSI w. Both-deg. reaches 83.56% AUROC versus 75.31% for ALaSI (the strongest baseline there), a gap of over 8 points; on VN_NS_100 it achieves 82.08% versus 80.21% for SICSM. On the easier Spring-based graphs (VN_SP_15/30), SGSI w. Both-deg. edges out SICSM by a narrow 0.20–0.07 points, while on VN_SP_50 it surpasses iSIDG (95.72%) by 0.74 points. Without any constraints, plain SGSI is competitive but trails the best baselines on several datasets (e.g., 88.17% vs. 94.78% for SICSM on VN_SP_100), underscoring that the soft-gating mechanism's advantage is fully realized only when domain constraints are supplied. The paper also reports that naive adjacency overwriting (the only way to inject priors into NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, and RCSI) yields negative Δ AUROC at higher known-edge fractions, whereas SGSI's cloning-clamping approach consistently improves with more prior knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGSI builds directly on the VAE-based structural inference lineage initiated by NRI (Kipf et al., 2018) and extended by MPM, ACD, iSIDG, and RCSI, all of which treat every edge as uniformly latent. It also extends the same group's prior work on partial-knowledge integration (ALaSI for known-present edges via active learning, SICSM for fixed present edges via conjoined state-space models), but departs from both by providing a unified, gradient-safe mechanism for known-absent edges, global sparsity, and node-degree constraints simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is the cloning-clamping operation combined with KL-term exclusion for deterministic edges, framed through the Variational Information Bottleneck principle, which prior methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where domain experts' partial knowledge (known interactions, degree limits, sparsity) is systematically wasted or actively harmful when naively injected into VAE-based structural inference. The VIB-based justification—that deterministic edges consume zero information-theoretic 'bits' and thus free capacity for uncertain edges—provides a principled reason why the approach works, not just an empirical recipe. Open questions remain around scalability to very large heterogeneous graphs, automated constraint specification (currently requiring manual domain expertise), and whether the soft-gating paradigm transfers to dynamic or temporal graph inference. The dependence on accurate priors and additional hyperparameter tuning (β, λ_sparsity, λ_deg) limits plug-and-play applicability.
