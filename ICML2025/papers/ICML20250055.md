## Problem

Although tilted empirical risk minimization (TERM) has demonstrated strong empirical performance against outliers, class imbalance, and fairness violations, no theoretical generalization bounds existed for the tilted empirical risk under unbounded loss functions. This paper fills that gap by establishing generalization and robustness guarantees for TER under negative tilt (γ < 0) with only a bounded (1+ε)-th moment assumption on the loss, and further analyzes behavior under distributional shift caused by noisy training samples.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes i.i.d. training samples Z_i = (X_i, Y_i) from a data-generating distribution μ, a non-negative loss function ℓ(h, Z) with bounded (1+ε)-th moment for some ε ∈ (0,1], and a negative tilt parameter γ. It outputs a learned hypothesis h*, a high-probability upper bound on the tilted generalization error |gen_γ(h, S)| with convergence rate O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}), and a data-driven tilt value γ_data obtained by minimizing the theoretical bound. For the information-theoretic variant, the learning algorithm is specified as a Markov kernel P_{H|S}, and the bound is expressed in terms of the mutual information I(H;S) rather than hypothesis-space cardinality.

## Method

The tilted generalization error is decomposed into a population-risk gap R(h,μ) − R_γ(h,μ^{⊗n}) (bounded by |γ|^ε κ^{1+ε} via a moment inequality) and a non-linear concentration term controlled by Bernstein's inequality applied to exp(γℓ(h,Z_i)), whose variance is bounded by 2^ε|γ|^{1+ε}κ^{1+ε} under the (1+ε)-th moment assumption. Uniform bounds are obtained via a union bound over a finite hypothesis space, while information-theoretic bounds replace the cardinality term with I(H;S) using a coupling argument. For robustness under distribution shift, a functional derivative (à la Cardaliaguet et al.) bounds the tilted population risk gap between μ and a shifted distribution μ̃ in terms of total variation distance TV(μ,μ̃), yielding a robustness cost of order TV(μ,μ̃)/γ². The KL-regularized TERM problem is shown to have a tilted Gibbs posterior as its solution, and its expected tilted generalization error achieves the faster O(n^{-ε}) rate when γ = O(1/n). A data-driven tilt γ_data is obtained by minimizing the asymptotic upper bound, balancing the generalization term |γ|^ε against the distribution-shift term TV(μ,μ̃)/γ².

## Results

The paper establishes a convergence rate of O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}) for the tilted generalization error under unbounded loss functions with bounded (1+ε)-th moment (ε∈(0,1]), via both uniform and information-theoretic approaches. For the KL-regularized TERM problem, a faster O(n^{-ε}) rate is achieved for the tilted Gibbs posterior. Empirically, on logistic regression with Pareto outliers (unbounded second moment), the data-driven tilt γ_data achieves population risk of 0.01±0.000 at 70% outlier ratio, compared to 0.02±0.001 for ERM, while the grid-search optimal tilt γ* achieves 0.00±0.002.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is standard Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM), i.e., linear empirical risk with γ=0. Under Pareto outliers (which have unbounded second moment), TERM with the data-driven tilt γ_data consistently matches or outperforms ERM: at 17.58% outlier ratio, γ_data achieves 0.01±0.000 vs. ERM's 0.02±0.000, and at 70% outlier ratio, 0.01±0.000 vs. 0.02±0.001. The grid-search optimal tilt γ* achieves the lowest risk (0.00±0.003 at 17.58%) but requires exhaustive search, whereas γ_data is obtained in closed form from the theoretical bound minimization (Eq. 22) and remains competitive. Under Gaussian outliers (Table 1), the advantage of TERM over ERM is more pronounced at moderate outlier ratios (e.g., 35%: 0.20 vs. 0.30 for ERM), but the gap narrows at very high ratios. The paper also notes that TERM and data-driven TERM exhibit lower variance than ERM across runs, consistent with the theoretical robustness guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first generalization and robustness theory for the Tilted Empirical Risk (TER) proposed by Li et al. (2021), extending the information-theoretic framework of Xu & Raginsky (2017) from linear empirical risk to the non-linear log-sum-exponential risk. It departs from prior unbounded-loss generalization results (Cortes et al., 2019; Haddouche & Guedj, 2022) by achieving a tighter O(n^{-ε/(1+ε)}) rate without the log(n) factor, and from the Gibbs posterior analysis of Aminian et al. (2021a) by relaxing the sub-Gaussian loss assumption to bounded (1+ε)-th moments. The robustness analysis under distribution shift via total variation distance is novel, building on influence-function-style arguments but exploiting the exponential structure of TER to obtain bounds that are infeasible for standard ERM with unbounded losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the TERM literature by providing the first theoretical guarantees for tilted empirical risk under unbounded loss functions, and introduces a principled data-driven method for selecting the tilt parameter γ that balances robustness against outliers with generalization performance. The key practical insight is that negative tilt provably bounds distribution-shift cost via total variation distance—a property ERM lacks for unbounded losses—giving practitioners a theoretical justification for using TERM in noisy or adversarial training settings. Open questions include extending the analysis to positive tilt (for class imbalance), the overparameterized/mean-field regime, and removing the lower-bound sample-size requirement for unbounded losses. The experiments, while confirming the theory, are limited to simple logistic regression with synthetic outliers, leaving open whether the data-driven tilt selection transfers to deep learning or high-dimensional settings.
