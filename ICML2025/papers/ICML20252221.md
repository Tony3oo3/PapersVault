## Problem

The conventional wisdom in NLP holds that final-layer representations in large language models are the most useful for downstream tasks, yet no systematic, architecture-agnostic study has quantified whether intermediate layers might encode richer features. Additionally, existing representation quality metrics—information-theoretic, geometric, and invariance-based—have been developed in isolation without a unifying theoretical lens explaining why certain layers balance compression and signal preservation more effectively.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes hidden-state matrices Z ∈ R^{N×D} extracted from every layer of a model (transformer, SSM, or vision encoder) given text prompts from WikiText-103 or MTEB tasks, along with augmented variants produced via character swaps, keyboard substitutions, and word splits. For vision experiments, image patches from ImageNet serve as the input sequence. The output is a per-layer vector of seven representation quality metrics (prompt entropy, dataset entropy, effective rank, curvature, InfoNCE, LiDAR, DiME) plus downstream accuracy on 32 MTEB embedding tasks, enabling both diagnostic analysis and unsupervised layer selection.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified framework grounded in matrix-based (Rényi) entropy of the Gram matrix K = ZZ^T, showing that information-theoretic metrics (prompt/dataset entropy, effective rank), geometric metrics (curvature of token trajectories), and augmentation-invariance metrics (InfoNCE, LiDAR, DiME) are all facets of how eigenvalue variance is distributed across principal directions. Theoretical results establish that effective rank lower-bounds exp(S₁(Z)) and that dataset entropy upper-bounds mutual information, linking compression to InfoNCE loss. Empirically, the authors evaluate every layer of Pythia, Llama3, Mamba, BERT, LLM2Vec, and seven vision architectures on 32 MTEB tasks, finding intermediate layers outperform final layers by up to 16% and that the mid-depth entropy 'valley' is driven by autoregressive training objectives rather than data modality, with residual connections identified as the specific sub-layer responsible for the compression.

## Results

Across 32 MTEB embedding tasks, intermediate layers consistently outperform final layers by 2–16% in average accuracy, with the best-performing layer typically residing around mid-depth (~50% of network depth). This pattern holds across decoder-only transformers (Pythia, Llama3), state-space models (Mamba), and encoder-only models (BERT). Using DiME as an unsupervised layer-selection proxy yields a 3% average MTEB improvement for Pythia-410M without any task-specific labels. Distance correlation (dCor) between representation metrics and downstream accuracy is strongest for DiME, curvature, and InfoNCE, confirming the unified framework's predictive power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a single proposed method against external baselines; instead, it compares layer-wise representation quality across architectures and training objectives. Autoregressive models (Pythia, Llama3, AIM) exhibit a pronounced mid-layer entropy 'valley' and accuracy peak, whereas bidirectional models (BERT, LLM2Vec) and SSMs (Mamba) show flatter, more uniform layer-wise trends. In vision, the autoregressive AIM model replicates the language-model mid-depth bottleneck, while non-autoregressive models (ViT, CLIP, DINOv2, MAE, BEiT) show strictly increasing accuracy toward the final layer. CoT finetuning (Qwen 2.5-Math vs. Qwen 2.5) preserves higher prompt entropy with lower variance, indicating richer context retention. Finetuning Llama3 into LLM2Vec variants improves augmentation invariance (LiDAR) but shifts prompt entropy in opposite directions depending on whether the finetuning is supervised or unsupervised.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on matrix-based entropy (Giraldo et al., 2014) and the prompt-level entropy formulation of DiffeRank (Wei et al., 2024) to propose a unified theoretical framework connecting information-theoretic, geometric, and invariance-based representation metrics through the eigenvalue spectrum of the Gram matrix. It extends the growing line of layer-wise analysis in LLMs (Liu et al., 2019; Tenney et al., 2019; Jin et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2024) by covering a broader architectural range—decoder-only transformers, encoder-only transformers, state-space models, and bidirectional variants—and by providing theoretical theorems linking effective rank, dataset entropy, and InfoNCE to downstream performance. The paper also connects to the compression-generalization literature (Bordes et al., 2023; Park et al., 2024a) and the anisotropy analysis of Razzhigaev et al. (2024), positioning its entropy-based metrics as a common theoretical substrate for these previously separate diagnostic tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: unsupervised layer selection via DiME or InfoNCE can identify the best-performing intermediate layer without task-specific labels, yielding a 3% average MTEB improvement, which could reduce the cost of embedding model deployment. The conceptual finding that autoregressive training (rather than data modality) is the key driver of mid-layer compression—evidenced by the same entropy valley in both language and image autoregressive models—reframes how we think about why intermediate layers are useful and suggests that training objective design, not architecture choice, is the lever for controlling representation quality. Open questions include the mechanistic cause of the residual-stream compression, whether explicit fine-tuning can target and modulate mid-layer entropy, and how intrinsic dimensionality (noted but not studied here) further refines the picture.
