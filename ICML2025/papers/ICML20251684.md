## Problem

Existing Meta-Black-Box-Optimization (MetaBBO) methods face a dual bottleneck: massive joint configuration spaces (with 10+ hyperparameters) make it difficult for RL to learn effective dynamic algorithm configuration policies, and the online RL paradigm is extremely inefficient since each training trajectory requires hundreds of low-level optimization steps. No prior work has explored offline learning as a viable alternative for MetaBBO.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251684/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each optimization generation, the Mamba-based Q-Learner receives a 9-dimensional optimization state vector (6 population/objective statistics + 3 temporal progress features) concatenated with the 5-bit binary token of the previously selected action bin, and autoregressively outputs a 16-dimensional Q-value vector for the current hyperparameter dimension. The full configuration a_{1:K}^t is assembled by taking argmax over each dimension's Q-values sequentially, with a special 11111 start token initiating the sequence. Continuous hyperparameter ranges are uniformly discretized into M=16 bins for universal representation across all action dimensions.

## Method

Q-Mamba transforms the DAC task into a long-sequence decision process by decomposing the joint Q-function into K per-hyperparameter Q-functions, each updated via inverse maximization (Bellman backup for the last dimension, max over subsequent dimensions for earlier ones), thereby escaping the M^K curse of dimensionality. The Q-Learner is a Mamba-based recurrent architecture that processes the autoregressive sequence of [state, previous-action-token] pairs, leveraging the selective state space model for flexible long/short-term dependency capture and hardware-aware parallel scan (PrefixSum) for training acceleration over the full trajectory. Training uses a compositional Q-loss that combines standard TD-error Bellman updates on the decomposed sequence with a CQL-style conservative penalty (λ=1) pushing out-of-distribution action-bin Q-values toward zero, plus a weighted emphasis (β=10) on the last action dimension to anchor the inverse-maximization chain. The offline E&E dataset (10K trajectories) mixes 50% exploitation data from pre-trained MetaBBO baselines with 50% random-policy exploration data to balance quality and coverage.

## Results

On the CoCo BBOB test set (8 unseen problem instances, 19 runs), Q-Mamba achieves the highest accumulated performance improvement across all three low-level algorithms: 0.989 (Alg0, K=3), 0.997 (Alg1, K=10), and 0.995 (Alg2, K=16), where 1.0 denotes finding the global optimum. Q-Mamba trains in 13 hours, less than half the 25–28 hours required by online baselines (RLPSO, LDE, GLEET), while matching or exceeding their optimization performance. On the out-of-distribution neuroevolution (Mujoco continuous control) task, Q-Mamba generalizes zero-shot with comparable performance to online baselines despite being trained only on synthetic BBOB problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three online MetaBBO baselines, Q-Mamba wins on all three algorithms: it outperforms RLPSO by +0.003 (Alg0), +0.002 (Alg1), and +0.004 (Alg2), and beats GLEET by +0.027, +0.003, and +0.004 respectively, while training in roughly half the time (13h vs. 25–28h). Among offline baselines, Q-Mamba dominates the conditional imitation learning methods (DT, DeMa) by large margins (e.g., +0.321 over DT on Alg1), confirming that pure sequence imitation is insufficient for DAC. Against the Q-value-augmented offline methods QDT and QT, Q-Mamba achieves superior performance on Alg0 (+0.021, +0.016) and Alg2 (+0.003, +0.002) while training 7 hours faster (13h vs. 20h). The closest competitor is Q-Transformer, which shares the same Q-function decomposition scheme but uses a Transformer backbone; Q-Mamba edges it out on all three algorithms (e.g., +0.022 on Alg0, +0.002 on Alg1) and trains 3 hours faster, attributed to Mamba's selective state-space mechanism for long-sequence modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Meta-Black-Box-Optimization (MetaBBO) and offline reinforcement learning. It directly adopts the per-action-dimension Q-function decomposition from Q-Transformer (Chebotar et al., 2023) to handle the high-dimensional configuration space of BBO algorithms, and integrates the conservative regularization from CQL (Kumar et al., 2020) to mitigate distribution shift in offline learning. The key architectural departure is replacing the Transformer backbone used in Q-Transformer and prior MetaBBO methods (e.g., GLEET) with a Mamba selective state-space model, motivated by the long-sequence nature of DAC (hundreds of optimization steps × K hyper-parameters per step). The paper is the first to frame MetaBBO as an offline RL problem, departing from the online RL paradigm that dominates existing MetaBBO work (RLPSO, LDE, GLEET, ConfigX).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a near-2× reduction in training time (13h vs 25–28h) while matching or slightly exceeding online baselines, which is meaningful for BBO scenarios where each optimization step is expensive. The zero-shot transfer to neuroevolution on Mujoco continuous control tasks suggests the learned policy generalizes beyond synthetic benchmarks, though this is demonstrated on only one scenario. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Q-Mamba must be re-trained for each specific BBO algorithm; an open question is whether algorithm feature extraction could enable a single model to co-train across diverse algorithm structures. The broader implication is that offline RL pipelines may be a viable and more efficient paradigm for learning-based automated algorithm design, potentially accelerating the field beyond the current online-only paradigm.
