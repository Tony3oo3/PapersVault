## Problem

Existing pre-trained-model-based Domain-Incremental Learning methods do not address class imbalance, which manifests as both intra-domain imbalance (underfitting of few-shot classes) and cross-domain class distribution shifts (conflicting needs to preserve many-shot knowledge while improving few-shot classes via cross-domain transfer). Shared-prompt paradigms improve few-shot transfer but cause severe many-shot forgetting, while domain-specific paradigms reduce forgetting but block few-shot generalization—no existing method balances both.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of RGB image domains (e.g., 4–11 domains across Office-Home, DomainNet, CORe50, CDDB-Hard) with a fixed label space and imbalanced per-domain class distributions, in an exemplar-free setting. It outputs a single class label per image, with the classifier dimension fixed at |Y| throughout all incremental stages. The frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K or ImageNet-21K) extracts features, and only lightweight expert MLPs and a selector are updated per domain.

## Method

DCE employs a two-stage per-domain training pipeline. In stage one, three parallel frequency-aware expert MLPs are trained independently with differentiated losses—standard cross-entropy (many-shot biased), balanced softmax (frequency-corrected), and a novel inverse-distribution loss that doubly weights few-shot classes—thereby isolating task-specific knowledge and mitigating intra-domain imbalance. In stage two, a dynamic expert selector MLP is trained on synthetic pseudo-features generated by balanced Gaussian sampling from stored per-class means and OAS-shrinkage covariances, producing soft routing weights over all accumulated experts to enable cross-domain knowledge transfer without hard domain assignment. A visual prompt is learned once in the first task and frozen thereafter, keeping the backbone parameters fixed across all domains to ensure feature-space consistency.

## Results

DCE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four imbalanced DIL benchmarks. On CORe50, DCE reaches 80.1% average accuracy (Ā) and 84.8% final accuracy (A_B), outperforming the runner-up RanPAC (76.7% / 78.4%) by 3.4 and 6.4 points respectively. On CDDB-Hard, DCE attains 74.6% / 71.8%, surpassing CODA-Prompt (67.9% / 66.6%) by 6.7 and 5.2 points. On Office-Home and DomainNet, DCE achieves 84.5% / 84.4% and 64.2% / 63.4% (Ā / A_B), leading the next-best methods by approximately 2 and 5 points respectively. DCE also shows the most balanced performance across many-shot, medium-shot, and few-shot class groups, with particularly strong gains on few-shot classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCE outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and both metrics (Ā and A_B). Against shared-prompt methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt), DCE gains 7.3–17.8 points on CORe50 and 6.7–13.3 points on CDDB-Hard, while also reducing catastrophic forgetting on many-shot classes. Against the domain-specific prompt method S-iPrompt, DCE improves by 17.4 points on CORe50 (80.1 vs. 62.7) and 10.4 points on CDDB-Hard (74.6 vs. 64.2), demonstrating that its dynamic expert selector enables cross-domain knowledge transfer that hard-assignment methods cannot achieve. Compared to the training-free SimpleCIL baseline, DCE leads by 17.6 points on CORe50 and 9.1 points on CDDB-Hard, confirming that learned expert collaboration substantially outperforms static prototype matching. Exemplar-based methods (iCaRL, MEMO) trail DCE by 9.1 and 14.1 points on CORe50 respectively, and by 17.1 and 8.6 points on CDDB-Hard, indicating that DCE's exemplar-free design is both more accurate and more memory-efficient. On Office-Home and DomainNet (Table 1), DCE leads CODA-Prompt and RanPAC by ~2 points and S-iPrompt by ~5 points, with the largest few-shot class accuracy gains (e.g., 83.3% vs. 73.2% on DomainNet few-shot classes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of pre-trained-model-based Domain-Incremental Learning (DIL) and class-imbalanced learning. It builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., 2022) for the frozen-encoder feature space and on balanced meta-softmax (Ren et al., 2020) as one of three expert training objectives. The key conceptual departure is from existing PTM-based DIL methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, S-iPrompt), which treat domain shift as the sole challenge; DCE identifies that intra-domain class imbalance and cross-domain class distribution shifts impose distinct, complementary pressures on shared-prompt vs. domain-specific-prompt paradigms, and addresses both through a frequency-aware MoE architecture and a Gaussian-sampled dynamic expert selector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to systematically formalize and benchmark imbalanced DIL across four datasets, this paper gives the continual-learning community a concrete testbed and a practical method that simultaneously reduces many-shot forgetting and improves few-shot generalization. The dual-balance design (specialized losses per expert + probabilistic routing via synthetic features) is modular and could be adapted to other incremental settings. Open questions include whether the Gaussian class-feature assumption holds under severe domain shifts, whether the three-expert configuration scales to hundreds of classes, and whether the approach extends beyond vision to other modalities or to the exemplar-based setting.
