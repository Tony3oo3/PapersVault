## Problem

Existing RGB-X trackers process RGB and auxiliary modality (depth, thermal, event) through two parallel branches, maintaining two dispersed feature spaces. This design causes a severe imbalance between intra-modality and inter-modality spatial modeling (e.g., 92% vs. 0.3% parameter allocation in BAT) and leaves insufficient computational budget for dense temporal modeling, limiting robustness in challenging scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250657/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, the tracker receives a 256×256 RGB search image, a 256×256 X-modality search image (depth, thermal, or event), and 128×128 template images (both initial and dynamic) for each modality. A shared HiViT patch embedding converts all inputs into token sequences, and the model outputs a single bounding box per frame. For RGB-only training data, the same RGB image is fed into both the RGB and X interfaces to maintain input format consistency across the joint RGB-D/T/E training pipeline.

## Method

CSTrack replaces the dual-branch architecture with a single-branch compact spatiotemporal design. The Spatial Compact Module (SCM) first applies bidirectional cross-attention between RGB and X token sequences (augmented with 4 learnable queries per modality to preserve modality-specific information), then simply adds the two refined feature maps to produce a compact spatial feature whose token count is comparable to a single modality. This compact feature is processed by a one-stream HiViT-base backbone, enabling comprehensive intra- and inter-modality modeling without the overhead of parallel branches. The Temporal Compact Module (TCM) constructs a refined target distribution heatmap by equally weighting a correlation-based intermediate heatmap (from search-template similarity) and a Gaussian heatmap derived from the predicted bounding box, then selects the top-16 search tokens as a compact temporal feature stored in a length-4 sliding window buffer. A Temporal Guidance Module injects these dense temporal cues into the search feature via cross-attention before a CNN head predicts the final box. Training is two-stage: 150 epochs without TCM, then 50 epochs with TCM (backbone frozen), using joint training across RGB-D/T/E datasets supplemented by large-scale RGB-only datasets (LaSOT, GOT-10K, COCO, etc.).

## Results

CSTrack achieves new SOTA across all mainstream RGB-X benchmarks. On RGB-Thermal, it reaches 70.9 MSR / 94.0 MPR on RGBT234 and 60.8 SR / 75.6 PR on LasHeR, surpassing the previous best (IPL) by 5.2 MSR and 5.7 MPR. On RGB-Depth, it attains 65.8 F-score on DepthTrack and 77.4 EAO on VOT-RGBD2022, outperforming SDSTrack by 3.9 F-score and 4.6 EAO. On RGB-Event, it achieves 65.2 SR / 82.4 PR on VisEvent, exceeding OneTracker by 4.4 SR and 5.7 PR. The model uses 75M parameters and runs at 33 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSTrack outperforms all 37 compared baselines across every benchmark. On RGBT234, it surpasses the previous best IPL (Lu et al., 2024) by 5.2 MSR and 5.7 MPR, and SDSTrack (Hou et al., 2024) by 8.4 MSR and 9.2 MPR. On LasHeR, the margin over IPL is 5.5 SR and 6.2 PR. On DepthTrack, CSTrack leads SDSTrack by 3.9 F-score and 3.8 PR, and on VOT-RGBD2022 it exceeds SDSTrack by 4.6 EAO and 4.6 Rob. On VisEvent, it beats OneTracker (Hong et al., 2024) by 4.4 SR and 5.7 PR. On the modality-missing RGBT234-Miss benchmark, CSTrack surpasses IPL by 8.6 MSR and 7.8 MPR, demonstrating robustness even without a dedicated modality-missing module. The consistent and substantial margins (typically 4–9 points) across all three modality types (depth, thermal, event) indicate strong generalization of the compact spatiotemporal modeling approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSTrack departs from the dominant dual-branch paradigm in RGB-X tracking (ViPT, OneTracker, SDSTrack, BAT), where RGB and X modalities are processed in separate feature spaces with inter-modality interaction modules. Instead, it draws on the one-stream tracking framework (OSTrack) and HiViT's hierarchical patch embedding to fuse both modalities into a single compact spatial feature before backbone processing, motivated by the Attenuation Theory's coarse-to-fine information integration. For temporal modeling, it extends beyond the sparse dynamic templates of TATrack and STMT by introducing dense multi-step temporal features via a parameter-free target distribution heatmap, building on the sliding-window memory mechanism from recent trackers like AQATrack and HiPTrack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compact spatiotemporal representation offers a practical architectural simplification: a single 73M-parameter branch replaces dual 82–136M branches while achieving better accuracy and higher speed (35 FPS vs. 18–24 FPS), making it attractive for real-time multimodal tracking deployment. The joint training strategy across RGB-D/T/E modalities, augmented with large-scale RGB-only datasets, demonstrates that cross-modal knowledge transfer is effective even without modality-specific architectures. A key open question is whether this compact-fusion philosophy extends to fundamentally different auxiliary modalities such as text (RGB-L), which the authors acknowledge as future work but do not address.
