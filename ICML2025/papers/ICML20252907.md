## Problem

Diffusion-based planners such as Diffuser generate coherent trajectories but offer limited avenues for inference-time scaling: increasing denoising steps plateaus quickly, and independent random sampling fails to leverage information across samples. Meanwhile, MCTS provides strong inference-time scalability through iterative search but relies on forward dynamics models, is restricted to discrete action spaces, and suffers from exponential tree growth over long horizons. The paper asks how to combine the holistic generative strength of diffusion with the adaptive, scalable search of MCTS for continuous, long-horizon planning.

## Input / Output

The planner receives an initial state and a goal state (or, in the visual pointmaze, 64×64×3 RGB images of the initial and goal configurations encoded to 8-dimensional VAE latents) and produces a full state-action trajectory of length N (e.g., 500 or 1000 steps). The trajectory is internally partitioned into S subplans (e.g., S=5) that serve as tree nodes, each carrying its own noise-level schedule. A separate low-level module—a heuristic controller, value-learning policy, or inverse dynamics MLP—converts the planned positions into executable actions, so the diffusion planner operates on lower-dimensional representative states rather than raw high-dimensional actions.

## Method

MCTD restructures diffusion denoising into a tree-structured process through three mechanisms. First, Denoising as Tree-Rollout partitions the trajectory into S subplans and applies a causal, semi-autoregressive noise schedule so that earlier subplans are denoised faster than later ones, making each subplan a tree node and reducing tree depth from N to S. Second, Guidance Levels as Meta-Actions assign a per-subplan guidance scale (e.g., {0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2}) that controls whether that subplan is sampled from the exploratory prior or a goal-directed guided distribution, with UCT over these meta-actions balancing exploration and exploitation during tree selection. Third, Jumpy Denoising as Fast Simulation uses DDIM skip-level sampling (every C steps) to rapidly complete a partially denoised trajectory for reward evaluation without costly forward-model rollouts. The full MCTS loop—Selection via UCT, Expansion via one diffusion denoising step, Simulation via jumpy denoising, and Backpropagation updating node values and guidance schedules—is executed iteratively, with a Transformer backbone (x₀-prediction, 12 layers, 4 attention heads) handling the per-subplan denoising at heterogeneous noise levels.

## Results

On the OGBench maze navigation tasks, MCTD achieves near-perfect success rates (94–100%) across all maze sizes for both point-mass and ant robots, substantially outperforming Diffuser (0–58%) and Diffusion Forcing (24–90%). On the most challenging giant mazes, MCTD reaches 100% on pointmaze and 94% on antmaze, while Diffuser achieves 0% and Diffusion Forcing only 50% and 24% respectively. On the robot arm cube manipulation task, MCTD-Replanning with object-wise guidance improves two-cube success from 22% (MCTD) to 50%, and achieves 24% on four-cube tasks where all baselines score 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCTD decisively outperforms both baselines across all maze sizes. Against Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), the gap is most dramatic on giant mazes where MCTD achieves 100% (pointmaze) and 94% (antmaze) versus 0% for Diffuser, and remains substantial on large mazes (98% vs. 44% pointmaze, 98% vs. 14% antmaze). Against Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024a), MCTD wins on every setting: 100% vs. 50% on pointmaze-giant, 94% vs. 24% on antmaze-giant, and 98% vs. 74% on pointmaze-large. Diffusion Forcing is the closest competitor on medium mazes (65% pointmaze, 90% antmaze) thanks to its semi-autoregressive causal schedule, but its single-pass nature prevents it from refining suboptimal trajectory segments, causing performance to degrade sharply as maze complexity increases. The paper also shows that Diffuser-Random Search (same compute budget as MCTD) provides no improvement over one-shot Diffuser, confirming that systematic tree-based branching is essential rather than merely increasing the number of independent samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCTD sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: diffusion-based trajectory planning (Diffuser, Diffusion Forcing) and structured tree search (MCTS). It builds directly on Diffusion Forcing's semi-autoregressive causal denoising to enable tree-structured rollouts, and on DDIM's skip-step sampling to implement fast simulation, while adapting MCTS's four-step loop (Selection, Expansion, Simulation, Backpropagation) to operate over partially denoised sub-trajectories rather than individual states. The paper explicitly contrasts itself with Monte Carlo Guidance (MCG) from Chen et al. 2024a, arguing that MCG's passive guidance averaging lacks the explicit search, backtracking, and exploration-exploitation control that MCTD's tree structure provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCTD addresses a concrete and previously underexplored gap: how to make diffusion-based planners scale with inference-time computation in a principled way, analogous to how MCTS scales in discrete domains. The near-perfect success rates on giant mazes (where Diffuser scores 0%) and the complete failure of random search at matched compute budgets underscore that structured search, not just more sampling, is what unlocks long-horizon planning. Key limitations remain: the method is computationally expensive (System 2 deliberation), depends on a separate value-learning policy for execution that degrades with task complexity, and struggles with multi-object manipulation. Open questions include adaptive compute allocation, amortized search via meta-learning, and whether the framework extends to online or partially observable settings beyond the VAE-encoded visual pointmaze.
