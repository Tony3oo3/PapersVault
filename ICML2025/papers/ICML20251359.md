## Problem

State-of-the-art graph condensation methods based on trajectory matching (e.g., GEOM, SFGC) require repeatedly retraining a GNN from scratch (e.g., 200 times) to collect training trajectories, which dominates total condensation time (up to 452 hours on million-node graphs). This computational bottleneck severely limits scalability to large real-world graphs such as social networks or e-commerce platforms. The paper asks whether comparable condensation quality can be achieved without any repetitive retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251359/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original graph G = {X, A, Y} with N nodes, d-dimensional features, and C classes, and produces a structure-free condensed graph S = {X', I, Y'} with N' ≪ N nodes (condensation ratios as low as 0.025%). The adjacency matrix of the condensed graph is fixed to the identity (no edges), and Y' preserves the original class proportions via proportional sampling. The condensed features X' are designed so that a GNN trained on S (with self-loops) achieves comparable node classification accuracy on the original graph's test nodes.

## Method

GCPA is a two-stage framework that completely bypasses trajectory matching. The precomputation stage first performs K-hop graph diffusion (H^(k) = (1−α)Â·H^(k−1) + α·H^(0)) to enrich raw features with structural neighborhood information, then for each synthetic node uniformly samples M original nodes of the same class and averages their aggregated features to form the initial condensed features. The adaptation stage introduces an MLP module that refines these precomputed features via a residual connection (Z' = βX̂' + (1−β)·f_adapt(X̂')), trained with a contrastive loss combining class-wise alignment (pulling synthetic features toward same-class anchors from H) and an intra-class diversity term (pushing same-class synthetic features apart, weighted by γ). Notably, the precomputation stage alone (taking only seconds) already matches or surpasses 5 out of 9 baselines, and the full framework achieves SOTA on 4/7 datasets while being 96×–2,455× faster than GEOM.

## Results

Using GCN as the backbone, GCPA achieves SOTA node classification accuracy on 5 out of 7 datasets (CiteSeer 74.8%, PubMed 80.9%, Ogbn-products 70.2%, Flickr 47.1%, Reddit 93.0%), while trailing GEOM on Cora (82.9 vs. 83.6) and Ogbn-arXiv (67.7 vs. 68.8). The precomputation-only variant (Ours Pre.) already matches or surpasses 5 of 9 baselines in under 60 seconds. In terms of efficiency, GCPA is 96×–2,455× faster than GEOM (e.g., 2,985 s vs. 1,912,105 s on Ogbn-products), with the full adaptation stage adding only ~2,880 s on the largest dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest trajectory-matching baseline GEOM, GCPA wins on CiteSeer (+0.5), PubMed (+1.2), Ogbn-products (+0.4), Flickr (+0.1), and Reddit (+1.6), but trails on Cora (−0.7) and Ogbn-arXiv (−1.1). Against SFGC, GCPA is consistently superior across all 7 datasets, with the largest margin on Reddit (+3.0) and Ogbn-products (+2.3). Among gradient-matching methods (GCond, SGDD, EXGC) and distribution-matching methods (GCDM, SimGC, CGC), GCPA outperforms all of them on every dataset, typically by 1–5 percentage points. The coreset baseline K-Center is uniformly the weakest, trailing GCPA by 10–30 points. Notably, the precomputation-only variant (no adaptation) already beats K-Center, GCond, SGDD, GCDM, SimGC, and EXGC on most datasets, demonstrating that the one-time message-passing step captures most of the signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCPA departs from the dominant trajectory-matching paradigm (SFGC, GEOM) that has driven recent SOTA in structure-free graph condensation, instead replacing the costly iterative trajectory collection with a one-time graph diffusion pass for structural precomputation and a contrastive adaptation stage for semantic refinement. The structural precomputation draws on graph diffusion literature (Gasteiger et al., 2019; Frasca et al., 2020), while the adaptation loss borrows contrastive alignment ideas from GNN knowledge distillation (Joshi et al., 2022). A key conceptual departure is the equivalence proof (Appendix E) showing that SGC on precomputed features with an identity adjacency matrix is mathematically identical to SGC on raw features with the original graph structure, providing theoretical grounding for the structure-free design. The paper positions itself within the broader graph condensation lineage (GCond, GCDM, SimGC, EXGC, CGC) but explicitly targets the efficiency bottleneck that trajectory-based methods introduce through their multiplicative retraining factor T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96×–2,455× speedup over SOTA trajectory-based methods makes graph condensation practical for million-node graphs (e.g., Ogbn-products) where prior methods required hundreds of hours, opening the door to real-world deployment on social networks and e-commerce graphs. The finding that a seconds-long precomputation stage alone matches or surpasses 5 of 9 baselines suggests that the field may have over-invested in complex trajectory matching when simpler structural aggregation captures most of the necessary information. Limitations include evaluation restricted to node classification with structure-free outputs (no condensed edges), and the modest accuracy gap (−1.5% to +2.4%) relative to GEOM means the method trades a small amount of peak accuracy for dramatic efficiency. Open questions include whether richer precomputation techniques (beyond K-hop diffusion) can close the remaining accuracy gap, and whether the framework extends to other tasks like link prediction or graph-level classification.
