## Problem

Decentralized stochastic optimization methods suffer from a parallelism threshold well below that of centralized learning: even in highly connected topologies, adding more machines beyond a certain point degrades convergence rather than accelerating it. This gap persists across all prior decentralized methods (D-SGD, gradient tracking, momentum variants), leaving a fundamental question of whether decentralized systems can fully exploit parallelism to match centralized performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250582/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M machines holds a local data distribution D_i and samples one instance per round to compute a stochastic gradient at its local query point. The network topology is encoded in a symmetric, doubly stochastic gossip matrix P whose spectral gap ρ governs communication speed. The algorithm outputs the average of the query points across all machines (x̄_T), with a theoretical excess-loss guarantee of O(σD₁/√(MT) + D₁^{3/2}√(Lσ̃)/(ρT) + LD₁²/T); local iterates at each machine also converge with an additional O(MD₁σ̃/(ρ²T²)) term.

## Method

DAT-SGD extends the Anytime SGD framework to the decentralized setting by maintaining two sequences per machine: fast iterates w_t^i updated via local SGD with a scaled step (η·α_t), and slower query points x_t^i updated as a weighted average of the previous query point and the new iterate. Stochastic gradients are evaluated at the query points x_t^i rather than the iterates, and both sequences are synchronized with neighbors via gossip averaging each round. The key insight is that the slower evolution of query points tightens the consensus-distance recursion from O(η²/ρ) (as in D-SGD) to O(η²/ρ⁴) for the query-point bias, which after tuning the learning rate yields a network-dependent error term of O(1/(ρT)) instead of O(1/(ρ^{2/3}T^{2/3})). This raises the parallelism bound from O(ρ^{1/2}N^{1/4}) to O(ρ√N), matching the centralized limit for near-complete graphs and enabling linear speedup with more machines on sparse topologies.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a parallelism bound improvement from O(ρ^{1/2} N^{1/4}) (D-SGD) to O(ρ√N) (DAT-SGD), matching the centralized bound of O(√N) for near-complete topologies (ρ=Ω(1)). The convergence rate improves from O(σ²/(Mε²) + (σ√ρ+ζ)/(ρε^{3/2}) + 1/(ρε)) to O(σ²/(Mε²) + (√σ+√ζ)/(ρε) + 1/ε). Empirically, on Fashion MNIST with heterogeneous data (α=0.1), DAT-SGD consistently outperforms D-SGD and D² across ring and Base-2 graph topologies, with its largest accuracy drop occurring between M=8 and M=16, whereas D-SGD and D² degrade most sharply between M=4 and M=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-SGD (Koloskova et al., 2020), DAT-SGD achieves a strictly better parallelism bound across all topologies: O(N^{1/6}) vs. O(N^{1/8}) on ring, O(N^{1/4}) vs. O(N^{1/6}) on torus, and O(ρ√N) vs. O(ρ^{1/2}N^{1/4}) on near-complete graphs. Empirically on Fashion MNIST with heterogeneous data, DAT-SGD's performance degrades later (between M=8 and 16) compared to D-SGD (between M=4 and 8), confirming the theoretical parallelism improvement. Against D² (Tang et al., 2018), DAT-SGD consistently achieves higher test accuracy in the heterogeneous regime (α=0.1) on both ring and Base-2 graph topologies, with ring-topology results at M=8 matching or nearly matching D²'s results on the well-connected Base-2 graph. Notably, under homogeneous data (α=10), D-SGD and D² outperform DAT-SGD, suggesting the Anytime averaging mechanism is most beneficial when data heterogeneity is present.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAT-SGD combines two previously separate threads: the Anytime SGD framework (Cutkosky, 2019), which evaluates gradients at slowly-evolving weighted averages of past iterates, and the standard D-SGD gossip-averaging protocol (Koloskova et al., 2020). While Anytime SGD has been adapted to asynchronous (Aviv et al., 2021) and local (Dahan & Levy, 2024) training, this is the first application to decentralized gossip-based communication. The key departure from prior decentralized methods is that by evaluating gradients at averaged query points rather than at the current iterates, the consensus distance bias is fundamentally reduced, changing the parallelism scaling from O(ρ^{1/2}N^{1/4}) to O(ρ√N) without requiring gradient tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a persistent gap between centralized and decentralized learning: for near-complete topologies (ρ=Ω(1)), DAT-SGD recovers the centralized O(√N) parallelism bound, which no prior decentralized method achieved. This suggests that the consensus distance, long considered an inherent bottleneck in decentralized systems, can be mitigated through query-point design rather than requiring more complex mechanisms like gradient tracking. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex objectives (where empirical results on Fashion MNIST are mixed, with D-SGD and D² outperforming under homogeneous data), handling asymmetric gossip matrices, and integrating with gradient tracking to remove the heterogeneity dependence entirely.
