## Problem

Existing DTR methods rely on clinician-prescribed labels as ground truth even for deceased patients whose treatment patterns may not reflect optimal care, creating systematic label uncertainty that degrades model robustness. Additionally, most approaches use only structured EHR data while ignoring clinical notes, and none provide formal statistical guarantees on the reliability of their treatment recommendations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252168/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes timestamp-aligned sequences of 44 tabular vital/lab variables plus 5 static demographics (sequence length 8) alongside free-text clinical notes encoded via BioClinicalBERT, predicting the next-step treatment from a 25-class space (5×5 grid of IV fluid and vasopressor dosage bins). Beyond the point prediction, SAFER outputs a per-patient uncertainty score (KL divergence between two internal modules) and, at decision time, an FDR-controlled subset of recommended treatments selected via a Benjamini-Hochberg procedure over conformal p-values derived from those scores.

## Method

SAFER uses a Transformer architecture with masked self-attention within each modality (tabular and text) and bidirectional cross-attention to fuse them into a unified patient embedding, followed by a feedforward classifier. The key training innovation is a two-stage risk-aware fine-tuning: a teacher module f_θ is trained on all patients, then a student module f_φ is trained exclusively on survivors; the KL divergence between their output distributions serves as a per-patient uncertainty score that is provably higher for deceased patients (Theorem 4.1). A modified loss down-weights high-uncertainty (deceased) labels and adds a γ·κ² penalty to suppress overconfidence. At inference, conformal p-values are constructed from the uncertainty scores on a calibration set, and the BH procedure selects a subset of recommendations whose expected false-discovery rate is bounded by a user-specified α (Theorem 5.1), providing distribution-free safety guarantees without requiring knowledge of the true treatment label.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, SAFER achieves MI-AUC 0.9407, MA-AUC 0.8672, HR@3 0.8517, MRR@3 0.9017, and a counterfactual mortality rate reduction of 0.3891. On MIMIC-IV, it reaches MI-AUC 0.9356, MA-AUC 0.8755, HR@3 0.8713, MRR@3 0.8698, and mortality reduction of 0.4562. SAFER outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets, with the largest margins in counterfactual mortality reduction (e.g., 0.3891 vs. 0.0881 for Naive RL on MIMIC-III) and MRR@3 (0.9017 vs. 0.8891 for Naive RL on MIMIC-III).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFER dominates all seven baselines on every metric across both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV. Among sequential embedding methods, RETAIN is the closest competitor on MI-AUC (0.9257 vs. 0.9407 on MIMIC-III) and HR@3 (0.8324 vs. 0.8517), but SAFER leads by 1.5–4.5 points on MA-AUC and by 8.6 points on MRR@3. Among RL-based methods, Naive RL achieves the highest MRR@3 among baselines (0.8891 on MIMIC-III) yet suffers the worst classification scores (MA-AUC 0.6025) and lowest mortality reduction (0.0881), illustrating the class-imbalance problem the authors attribute to sparse reward signals. ACIL, the strongest RL baseline on MIMIC-IV, trails SAFER by 5.0 points on MA-AUC (0.7135 vs. 0.8755) and by 7.8 points on mortality reduction (0.3782 vs. 0.4562). SRL-RNN and ISL show intermediate performance but are consistently outperformed by SAFER by 3–10 points across classification and ranking metrics. The largest absolute gap appears in counterfactual mortality reduction, where SAFER achieves 0.3891 (MIMIC-III) and 0.4562 (MIMIC-IV) versus at most 0.3212 (ACIL) and 0.3782 (ACIL) respectively, underscoring the benefit of the risk-aware fine-tuning and multimodal fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFER extends the DTR literature along two axes that prior work has largely treated separately: multimodal fusion and statistical reliability. On the multimodal side, it builds on the growing body of work combining clinical notes with structured EHR (Lyu et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024) but is the first to integrate BioClinicalBERT-encoded notes into a sequential DTR pipeline via cross-attention. On the reliability side, it adapts the conformal p-value framework of Jin & Candès (2023) and the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR procedure to the treatment-recommendation setting, a domain where prior DTR methods (RETAIN, TAHDNet, SRL-RNN, ACIL) offer no formal error-rate guarantees. The risk-aware fine-tuning loss, which down-weights deceased-patient labels via a KL-divergence-based uncertainty score, departs from both the 'discard negative trajectories' approach (Sun et al., 2021) and the 'use all labels uniformly' approach of most baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first FDR-controlled treatment recommendation framework, SAFER gives clinicians a principled dial (α) to trade off recommendation coverage against the expected fraction of unsafe suggestions—a capability absent from all prior DTR methods. The ablation showing that removing clinical notes (SAFER-N) degrades performance across all metrics underscores that structured EHR alone is insufficient for capturing treatment-relevant context in sepsis management. However, the evaluation is limited to sepsis cohorts with a 5×5 discrete treatment space, the counterfactual mortality estimate relies on a single LSTM surrogate model under standard causal assumptions (SUTVA, ignorability, positivity), and the i.i.d. exchangeability assumption underlying conformal inference may be violated in longitudinal ICU data. Open questions include whether the FDR guarantee holds under temporal dependence, how the framework extends to continuous or combinatorial treatment spaces, and whether the KL-divergence uncertainty score generalizes beyond the binary survival/death label-ambiguity setting.
