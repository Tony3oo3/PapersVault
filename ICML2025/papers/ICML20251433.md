## Problem

Multimodal Reward Models (MM-RMs) trained on existing preference datasets exhibit a significant performance gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluation (91.4 vs 68.1 average accuracy), because they over-rely on text-only spurious correlations that hold within the training distribution but fail to generalize. This reliance on unimodal shortcuts undermines the reliability of MM-RMs in real-world deployment, where distribution shifts are inevitable, and can lead to reward hacking during RL training.

## Input / Output

The MM-RM takes an RGB image (segmented into up to 6 sub-images via a dynamic patch mechanism), a text query, and a text answer as input, and outputs a scalar reward score via a linear head replacing the language modeling head of InternVL2-8B. Training uses pairwise preference data under the Bradley-Terry model, where the model must assign a higher score to the chosen answer than the rejected one. At inference, the model scores individual (image, query, answer) triples, and pairwise comparison accuracy serves as the evaluation metric.

## Method

The paper proposes a Shortcut-aware MM-RM learning algorithm built on a dual-branch training architecture: a primary multimodal branch and an auxiliary text-only branch (shortcut-proxy) initialized identically but trained without visual input. The Shortcut-Failure Coefficient (SFC) is computed per sample as the ratio of the text-only branch's loss to the combined loss of both branches, quantifying how much a sample resists text-only prediction. The primary branch's Bradley-Terry loss is then reweighted by SFC values, upweighting samples where text-only shortcuts fail (indicating genuine multimodal understanding is needed) and downweighting easily text-discriminable samples. Gradients are detached during SFC computation so it acts purely as a weighting coefficient. At inference, the auxiliary branch is discarded and only the primary branch is deployed, incurring no additional overhead.

## Results

On cross-distribution generalization, the Shortcut-aware MM-RM improves average o.o.d. accuracy from 68.1 to 78.5 (+10.4) across six transfer scenarios, with only a marginal i.i.d. drop (91.4 → 90.2). The Shortcut-Failure Degradation (SFD) metric, which quantifies performance collapse when text-only shortcuts fail, is consistently reduced across all o.o.d. scenarios. On downstream Best-of-64 selection, the Shortcut-aware MM-RM outperforms the standard MM-RM on all three benchmarks (MM-Vet, LLaVA-Bench, MMHal-V) for every training dataset, e.g., 50.2 vs. 49.0 on MM-Vet and 84.7 vs. 80.5 on LLaVA-Bench when trained on VLFeedback. Model-scale analysis confirms consistent o.o.d. gains of +6.4 (2B) and +6.8 (4B) over standard MM-RMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard MM-RM trained with the vanilla Bradley-Terry loss (Equation 1) on the same multimodal preference data. The Shortcut-aware MM-RM consistently outperforms the standard baseline on all six o.o.d. cross-distribution transfer scenarios, raising average o.o.d. accuracy by 10.4 points (68.1 → 78.5) while sacrificing only 1.2 points in i.i.d. accuracy. A text-only RM (trained without images) serves as a diagnostic proxy: it matches the standard MM-RM on i.i.d. accuracy (within ~1–3 points, Table 1) but collapses to below-random on o.o.d. data (e.g., 47.8 on RLHF-V when trained on POVID), confirming that the standard MM-RM's o.o.d. failures stem from reliance on the same text-only shortcuts. On downstream Best-of-64 selection, the Shortcut-aware MM-RM wins on every benchmark–training-set combination, with the largest gains on LLaVA-Bench (+4.2 to +6.5 points) and MMHal-V (+0.28 to +0.28 points), while also showing stronger resistance to reward overoptimization as N scales (Figure 8). The method also generalizes across model sizes, with consistent o.o.d. improvements of +6.4 (2B) and +6.8 (4B) over the standard baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal reward modeling (Bradley-Terry-based training on vision-language preference data) and the broader spurious-correlation / shortcut-learning literature (Geirhos et al. 2020; Cadene et al. 2019 on unimodal bias in VQA). Rather than proposing a new architecture or a new preference dataset, it repurposes the standard MM-RM training loop by adding a text-only auxiliary branch whose per-sample loss ratio (the Shortcut-Failure Coefficient) drives dynamic reweighting of the primary branch. Conceptually, the reweighting mechanism echoes group-DRO (Sagawa et al. 2019) and worst-group accuracy, but operates at the sample level using a learned proxy rather than explicit group labels, and is explicitly modality-agnostic in design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-only RMs match multimodal RMs on i.i.d. data (≤2.8% gap) while collapsing on o.o.d. data is a practical warning for anyone deploying MM-RMs in RLHF or Best-of-N pipelines: the 'multimodal' model may be a text model wearing a vision encoder. The Shortcut-aware algorithm offers a drop-in training modification (no inference overhead, no dataset curation) that consistently closes the o.o.d. gap across three datasets and three model scales. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other spurious-correlation types (e.g., background bias, style bias) without redesigning the proxy, whether the slight i.i.d. trade-off (91.4→90.2) persists at larger scale, and whether the SFC signal remains informative when the text-only proxy is itself poorly calibrated.
