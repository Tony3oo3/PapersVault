## Problem

Existing One-Shot Federated Learning methods approximate each client's local loss with a single quadratic (unimodal) surrogate, ignoring the well-known multimodality of neural network loss landscapes. This can cause the server to reconstruct a suboptimal global model, especially under high data heterogeneity, because a secondary local minimum on one client may be more informative for the global optimum than that client's global minimum. The paper asks whether a principled Bayesian treatment that explicitly captures multiple modes of the local posteriors can yield a better global model in a single communication round.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a partition of a standard image classification dataset (28×28 grayscale FashionMNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100, or SVHN digit images) split via symmetric Dirichlet sampling with heterogeneity parameter α. The client trains M CNN models (LeNet or a deeper CNN) from different random initializations and transmits only the M MAP weight vectors and their Kronecker-factored (or Diagonal+Full) Laplace precision matrices to the server—no raw data or full Hessian is shared. The server returns an ensemble of M global weight vectors; at inference time the client averages the M model predictions to produce a class label.

## Method

FedBEns derives an exact expression (Proposition 3.1) showing that the global posterior equals the product of client posteriors divided by the prior raised to (C−1), enabling lossless one-shot aggregation. Each client trains M models via SGD from distinct random starts and fits a Laplace approximation (Kronecker-factored Hessian with temperature T=0.1) at each MAP solution, yielding a mixture-of-Gaussians local posterior. The server combines these mixtures into a multimodal global log-posterior (Eq. 5) and runs M independent SGD optimizations from median-initialized starting points to locate M modes of the global posterior. Final predictions use Bayesian model averaging over the M global models rather than a single MAP estimate. A zero-mean Gaussian diagonal prior (σ²=0.1) regularizes the aggregation, and a small server-side validation set (500 samples) selects the best server-optimizer checkpoint per ensemble component.

## Results

FedBEns with Kronecker Hessian factorization (M=5) achieves the highest average test accuracy across all heterogeneity levels on three image classification benchmarks. At high heterogeneity (α=0.05, 5 clients), it reaches 60.90% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FedFisher K-FAC (49.58%) by over 11 percentage points. At low heterogeneity (α=0.4), the gap narrows but FedBEns still leads with 77.16% vs. 68.32% for FedFisher K-FAC. The largest absolute gains appear on CIFAR-10 under high heterogeneity, where FedBEns achieves 51.43% versus 36.97% for FedFisher K-FAC and 28.44% for FisherMerge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedBEns (Kron, M=5) wins against all five baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level tested. Against the closest competitor FedFisher K-FAC (unimodal quadratic approximation via Fisher information), FedBEns leads by 11.3 pp on average at α=0.05 and 8.8 pp at α=0.4, with the largest single-dataset gap on CIFAR-10 at α=0.05 (51.43% vs. 36.97%). RegMean (model fusion via weight merging) trails FedBEns by 11.8 pp at α=0.05 and 8.7 pp at α=0.4. DENSE (data-free knowledge distillation via GANs) is outperformed by 18.9 pp at α=0.05 and 9.6 pp at α=0.4. The neuron-matching method OTFusion and the Fisher-weighted averaging method FisherMerge are the weakest baselines, losing to FedBEns by 25.6 pp and 22.5 pp respectively at α=0.05. Notably, even FedBEns with a single mixture component (M=1) matches or slightly exceeds the baselines, and M=2 already surpasses them, indicating that the multimodal posterior is the key driver of the gain rather than raw ensemble size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the exact posterior-combination formula from Bayesian federated learning (Al-Shedivat et al., 2021), the mixture-of-Laplace-approximations technique for capturing multimodal posteriors in deep networks (Eschenhagen et al., 2021), and the Bayesian marginalization principle for prediction (Wilson & Izmailov, 2020). It departs from the closest competitor, FedFisher (Jhunjhunwala et al., 2024), which also operates in the One-Shot FL setting but relies on a unimodal quadratic approximation of each local loss; FedBEns instead models each local posterior as a mixture of Laplace approximations and performs Bayesian model averaging at prediction time. Unlike earlier Bayesian FL methods (FedPA, FedEP) that require multiple communication rounds, FedBEns achieves the same exact posterior combination in a single round by transmitting mixture parameters rather than iterating on posterior estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-Shot FL is a practically important regime for communication-constrained or intermittently-connected client settings, and the paper demonstrates that the multimodal structure of neural network loss surfaces is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a source of substantial accuracy gains (up to ~10+ percentage points) that unimodal methods systematically miss. Key limitations include the linear-in-M communication and computation overhead per client and the quadratic-in-M server cost, though the authors show that even M=2 already surpasses all baselines, mitigating this concern. Open questions the authors flag include integrating differential privacy, handling dynamic client populations and distribution shift, and reducing ensemble cost via single-model ensemble mimicking (e.g., Havasi et al., 2021).
