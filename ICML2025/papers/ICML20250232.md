## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for large vision-language models require iterative gradient computation and parameter updates across the entire network, incurring high computational cost, demanding extensive hyperparameter tuning, and causing unintended degradation of unrelated concepts. No prior approach simultaneously achieves computational efficiency, robust unlearning, and targeted removal with minimal side effects across diverse model architectures (CLIP, Stable Diffusion, VLMs).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained vision-language model (CLIP ViT-B/32 through EVA01-g-14, SDv1.5/2.1, or LLaVA-1.5-7B), a forget set of 1,000–6,000 image-text pairs per target concept, and a retain set (~7,900 image-text pairs from LAION-400M). It produces an unlearned model where exactly one layer's weights are shifted along a fixed gradient direction by a scalar step size λ determined via binary search (S=10 steps), requiring only O(N_f + N_r) gradient computation and storing ~43 MB of gradient values for the Pareto-front layers.

## Method

SLUG computes a single forward-backward pass to obtain forget gradients (cosine embedding loss pushing positive pairs apart) and retain gradients (contrastive loss preserving alignment) for all layers simultaneously. It then scores each layer by importance (ratio of forget-gradient L2 norm to parameter L2 norm, a diagonal Fisher-information proxy) and gradient alignment (cosine similarity between forget and retain gradients), selecting layers on the Pareto front that maximize importance while minimizing alignment. The chosen layer is updated along the linearized forget-gradient direction θ_l* = θ_l^(0) − λ*∇L_forget, with λ* found by binary search that halts when forget accuracy reaches zero while retain accuracy remains high. This one-shot, single-layer update avoids iterative optimization entirely, reducing unlearning to a weight-arithmetic operation inspired by task arithmetic, and extends naturally to Stable Diffusion (via the CLIP text encoder) and VLMs (via the CLIP vision encoder) without architectural modification.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark (SDv1.5, 20 objects × 60 styles), SLUG achieves a Gap Ratio of 0.15%, the smallest among all 10 compared methods, indicating the closest overall performance to the hypothetical best method across all 9 metrics. SLUG requires only 39 seconds of compute time, 3.61 GB memory, and 0.04 GB storage—orders of magnitude less than iterative methods (e.g., SPM: 29,700 s; ESD: 6,163 s). On CLIP zero-shot identity unlearning, SLUG reduces forget accuracy to 0.0% (FA@1 and FA@5) while retaining 59.96% ImageNet top-1 accuracy (vs. 60.12% original), outperforming SSD (51.84%) and GAFT (55.52–60.13%). On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SLUG drops average forget accuracy from 99.50% to 2.8% across 10 celebrity identities while maintaining MMBench scores within ~3% of the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLUG dominates all baselines on efficiency: it requires 39 s compute time vs. 95 s (SEOT, the next fastest), 350 s (FMN), 667 s (SalUn), 6,163 s (ESD), and 29,700 s (SPM), with 3.61 GB memory and 0.04 GB storage—far below all competitors. On the unified Gap Ratio metric, SLUG achieves 0.15%, beating SEOT (0.46%), UCE (1.72%), FMN (1.81%), CA (2.44%), SalUn (2.78%), SHS (4.62%), EDiff (5.37%), ESD (18.17%), and SPM (84.73%). On CLIP identity unlearning (Table 1), SLUG matches SSD on forget accuracy (both 0.0% FA@1/FA@5) but substantially outperforms it on utility retention: 59.96% vs. 51.84% ImageNet top-1 and 58.32% vs. 35.96% CelebA top-1. SLUG also outperforms GAFT and SalUn on CelebA accuracy (58.32% vs. 25.71–55.22% and 26.11–55.81% respectively) while matching their forget accuracy. On UnlearnCanvas effectiveness, SLUG is competitive but not always best on individual metrics (e.g., ESD achieves 98.58% style UA vs. SLUG's 86.29%; SalUn achieves 99.59% object CRA vs. SLUG's 81.18%), yet SLUG's balanced performance across all metrics yields the lowest overall gap. Against adversarial attacks (Table 8), SLUG shows lower ASR than ESD and FMN on the 'Nudity' concept (16.90% vs. 20.42% and 88.03% without attack) but is not immune to whitebox attacks (ASR rises to 76.76% under P4D and 90.32% under UnlearnDiffAtk).

## Relation to Prior Work

SLUG departs from both iterative unlearning methods (fine-tuning, gradient ascent) and distributed saliency-based approaches (SalUn, SSD) by restricting the update to a single layer identified via a Pareto-optimal trade-off between gradient importance and gradient alignment. Conceptually, it repurposes the weight-arithmetic idea from task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the linearization of optimization trajectories, combined with Fisher-information-inspired layer importance scoring, into a one-shot unlearning procedure. Unlike prior saliency methods that threshold parameters across the entire network, SLUG's layer-level granularity introduces modularity: different concepts can be unlearned by updating different single layers without interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gains (39 s vs. thousands of seconds; 0.04 GB vs. several GB storage) make targeted unlearning practically deployable for large foundation models, addressing a key bottleneck for regulatory compliance (e.g., GDPR right-to-erasure). However, the paper acknowledges the absence of a theoretical explanation for why single-layer updates suffice, the lack of robustness to whitebox adversarial attacks, and the restriction to vision-language models (no LLM experiments). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to purely linguistic models, whether unlearned information can be fully certified as erased, and how the method behaves under repeated or overlapping unlearning requests.
