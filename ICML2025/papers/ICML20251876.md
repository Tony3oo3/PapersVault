## Problem

Active Feature Acquisition requires sequentially selecting which features to measure at test time, but existing approaches suffer from fundamental limitations: RL-based methods face training difficulties (sparse rewards, deadly triad), while greedy conditional mutual information (CMI) maximization makes myopic acquisitions that ignore the effects of future unobserved features and can reduce low-probability classes rather than disambiguating likely ones. The paper provides theoretical proofs of CMI's sub-optimality and introduces a supervised alternative that reasons over stochastic latent realizations.

## Input / Output

The model receives a partial feature vector where missing values are imputed (zero for continuous, a new category for categorical) alongside a binary mask vector indicating which features are genuinely observed. Each feature is independently encoded by its own MLP into l latent components, so the input dimensionality remains fixed regardless of how many features are observed. The output is a class probability distribution over C classes (via a predictor MLP on the concatenated latent vector) and, at acquisition time, a score for each unobserved feature computed by averaging gradient-based importance over multiple latent samples, weighted by predicted class probabilities.

## Method

SEFA uses a factorized stochastic encoder-predictor architecture where each feature has its own encoder producing a Gaussian latent distribution over l components, regularized by an information bottleneck KL term to retain only label-relevant information. Training is fully supervised (negative log-likelihood plus β-weighted KL divergence), with random feature dropout at each step to handle arbitrary observation subsets. The acquisition objective scores each unobserved feature i by computing the L2 norm of the predictor's gradient with respect to feature i's latent components, normalizing across features, averaging over multiple stochastic latent samples (capturing diverse possible unobserved feature values), and weighting by the current predicted class probabilities to focus on disambiguating likely classes. This design is non-greedy by construction because the expectation over latent samples implicitly considers how future feature observations would shift the latent distribution, addressing the myopia that plagues CMI-based methods.

## Results

SEFA achieves the best average acquisition metric across all 8 real-world and synthetic datasets in Table 2. On the synthetic datasets (Table 1), SEFA requires the fewest acquisitions to select all relevant features: 4.02 (Syn 1), 4.10 (Syn 2), and 5.08 (Syn 3), compared to 4.08/4.58/5.67 for the best CMI baseline (DIME). On the largest margin, SEFA outperforms the best baseline on MNIST by 2.1 points (0.761 vs 0.740 Opportunistic RL) and on California Housing by 1.5 points (0.676 vs 0.661 DIME). On the medical TCGA dataset, SEFA reaches 0.843 average accuracy versus 0.838 for the next-best method (Opportunistic RL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEFA outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset evaluated. The closest competitor is Opportunistic RL (Kachuee et al., 2019), which is second-best on 5 of 8 datasets but trails SEFA by 0.9–2.1 points (e.g., 0.740 vs 0.761 on MNIST, 0.838 vs 0.843 on TCGA). Among CMI-based methods, DIME (Gadgil et al., 2024) and GDFS (Covert et al., 2023) are the strongest, yet SEFA still leads by 0.3–1.5 points (e.g., 0.676 vs 0.661 on California Housing, 0.721 vs 0.703 on Fashion MNIST). Generative-model CMI estimators (ACFlow, EDDI, VAE) consistently underperform, with EDDI being the worst active method on nearly all datasets (e.g., 0.572 on MNIST, 0.634 on TCGA), confirming that inaccurate CMI estimation compounds the fundamental myopia of greedy CMI maximization. On the synthetic datasets where the optimal ordering is known, SEFA requires 4.02/4.10/5.08 acquisitions (Syn 1/2/3) versus 4.08/4.58/5.67 for DIME, approaching the theoretical minimum of 3/3/5.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEFA departs from the two dominant paradigms for Active Feature Acquisition: RL-based policy learning (Opportunistic Learning, GSMRL) and greedy conditional mutual information maximization (DIME, GDFS, ACFlow, EDDI). It borrows the information bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 1999; Alemi et al., 2017) to regularize a stochastic latent space, but repurposes it not for representation learning per se, but as the substrate for a gradient-based acquisition scoring function. The key conceptual departure is shifting the acquisition decision from the feature space (where CMI is computed or generative models are trained) to a factorized latent space where each feature has its own stochastic encoder, enabling the model to reason about unobserved features through latent diversity rather than explicit conditional distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical demonstration that greedy CMI maximization is provably sub-optimal (the indicator example) and that even as a short-term objective it can favor reducing unlikely class probabilities over disambiguating likely ones, which challenges a widely-used assumption in the AFA literature. Empirically, SEFA's consistent outperformance across 11 diverse datasets (synthetic, tabular, image, and medical) with well-supported ablations makes it a strong new baseline. Key limitations include restriction to classification (not regression) and higher inference-time memory due to multiple latent samples; extending the approach to continuous labels and very high-dimensional feature spaces (e.g., full genomics) remain open.
