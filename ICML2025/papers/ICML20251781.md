## Problem

Induction heads explain in-context learning only when the answer appears verbatim in the context via a match-and-copy mechanism, but practical LLM ICL requires inferring an underlying task from examples to answer queries whose answers are absent from the context. How this meta-learning capability is implemented by internal circuits, and how those circuits are acquired during training, remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251781/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an alternating sequence of N items and N labels (all drawn from the same task τ) followed by a single query item x_q, and must predict the correct label for x_q. Items and labels are represented as (P+D)-dimensional vectors combining a 65-dimensional positional one-hot code with a 63-dimensional content vector drawn from class-specific Gaussian means plus noise. Critically, the query item is excluded from the context with probability 1 (p_B=0), forcing the model to infer the task rather than retrieve a matching pair, and the same task τ is consistent across all N example pairs within a single context.

## Method

The paper introduces an In-Context Meta-Learning (ICML) setting with T=3 tasks over K=64 classes and L=32 labels, where each task defines a distinct (x,ℓ) mapping, and trains a 2-layer, single-head, attention-only transformer (128-dim, causal mask, MLP classifier) via SGD. The key finding is that training proceeds through three abrupt phases, each marked by a distinct circuit: Phase 1 (NCC) uses bigram attention in both layers to predict from weights alone (~33% accuracy); Phase 2 (SCC) introduces label attention in layer 1 while layer 2 retains bigram attention, enabling the model to eliminate candidate labels present in context (~75% accuracy); Phase 3 (FCC) uses chunk-example attention in layer 1 to bind (x,ℓ) pairs into single tokens and label attention in layer 2 to read off the inferred task (100% accuracy). Three attention-based metrics (Bigram, Label Attention, Chunk Example) are proposed to quantify each circuit, and their transitions align precisely with accuracy jumps. A theoretical derivation shows SCC accuracy equals p·1 + (1−p)·0.5 where p is the probability that at least one of the two candidate labels appears in context, confirmed empirically. In multi-head settings, different heads specialize in parallel (one on NCC, another on FCC), smoothing the accuracy curve while hiding the same abrupt circuit transitions. The authors further validate that the FCC pattern (chunk example in early layers, label attention in later layers) appears in GPT2-XL on a 2-shot SST2 sentiment task.

## Results

In the In-Context Meta-Learning (ICML) setting with T=3 tasks, the 2-layer attention-only transformer exhibits three distinct learning phases: Phase 1 (Non-Context Circuit) plateaus at 30–40% accuracy, Phase 2 (Semi-Context Circuit) reaches ~75%, and Phase 3 (Full-Context Circuit) achieves 100%. The theoretical accuracy for the SCC phase, given by p·1 + (1−p)·0.5 where p = 1 − C(K−2,4)/C(K−1,4), matches empirical accuracy closely across K ∈ {8, 16, 32}. In a pretrained GPT2-XL evaluated on SST2 (2-shot), chunk-example attention peaks in early layers while label attention dominates middle-to-late layers, mirroring the FCC circuit structure observed in the toy model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method-comparison paper, so no formal baselines are benchmarked. The key contrast is with prior induction-head research (Olsson et al., 2022; Reddy, 2023), which identifies a single-phase match-and-copy circuit; the present work shows that when task inference is required (T≥2), learning decomposes into three sequential circuit phases rather than one abrupt transition. The SCC phase's random-label robustness (RLA rises in Phase 2) provides a circuit-level explanation for the puzzling finding of Min et al. (2022b) that LLMs maintain high ICL accuracy even under random label permutations. In the multi-head setting, the distinct phases visible in single-head accuracy curves disappear, yet circuit metrics reveal that hidden circuit transitions still occur internally, bridging the gap between toy-model phase transitions and the smooth loss curves observed in large-scale LLM training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction head framework (Olsson et al., 2022; Reddy, 2023) from single-phase copy tasks to a multi-task In-Context Meta-Learning setting where the model must infer the underlying task from examples rather than simply retrieving a matching label. It builds directly on Reddy's (2023) two-layer attention-only transformer and data representation, and on Chan et al.'s (2022) analysis of how data distributional properties affect ICL emergence. The work also connects to the task vector literature (Hendel et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2024) by providing a circuit-level mechanism for how such abstract task representations are formed during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of three distinct circuit phases (NCC → SCC → FCC) provides a mechanistic account of how meta-learning capability is acquired incrementally, going beyond the single-phase story of induction heads. The SCC's label-focused attention offers a concrete explanation for the puzzling random-label robustness observed by Min et al. (2022b), and the multi-head parallel circuit discovery bridges the gap between abrupt toy-model phase transitions and the smooth loss curves seen in LLM training. Limitations include the primarily toy-scale setting (2-layer, attention-only, small vocabulary) and the GPT2-XL validation being limited to static attention pattern analysis rather than training dynamics; whether these specific circuits persist in deeper, more heterogeneous LLM architectures remains open.
