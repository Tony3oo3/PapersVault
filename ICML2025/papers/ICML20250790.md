## Problem

Topological Data Analysis extracts powerful global shape descriptors (e.g., persistent homology) from point clouds, but standard ML tasks like classification and clustering require point-level features. Existing point-level methods (PCA, diffusion maps, geometric descriptors) are blind to higher-order topology, while TDA tools produce only a global summary with no per-point assignment. The paper asks: how can we faithfully transfer global topological structure to individual points in a way that is robust to noise and heterogeneous sampling?

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite point cloud X ⊂ R^n along with a maximum homology dimension d (typically d = n−1) and an interpolation coefficient λ ∈ (0,1) that controls how localised versus smooth the resulting features are. It produces a |X| × |F| feature matrix where each column corresponds to a selected persistent homology class and each row encodes how strongly a point participates in that topological feature. The features are computed using α-filtrations for ambient dimension ≤ 3 and Vietoris-Rips filtrations for higher dimensions, with Z/3Z coefficients to preserve simplex orientation information.

## Method

TOPF operates in four steps: (1) compute persistent homology with generators on an α/VR-filtration; (2) select the most significant features via a heuristic that finds the largest drop in persistence duration ratios; (3) for each selected feature, build the simplicial complex at an interpolated filtration step t = b^(1−λ)·d^λ between its birth and death, lift the Z/3Z generator to a real vector, and project it into the harmonic space ker(L_k) of the simplicial Hodge Laplacian by solving a least-squares problem that removes the gradient and curl components; (4) normalise the harmonic representative and aggregate simplex values to points by averaging over all k-simplices containing each point. The key insight is that the Hodge decomposition guarantees a unique harmonic representative per homology class, which smoothly distributes topological information across all contributing simplices rather than concentrating it on the small generator support, yielding features that are both topologically faithful and robust to local perturbations.

## Results

On the Topological Clustering Benchmark Suite (7 datasets), spectral clustering on TOPF vectors achieves a mean Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 0.86, outperforming all classical clustering algorithms and the only other topological method TPCC (mean ARI 0.58) by a wide margin. TOPF runs in a mean 15.1 seconds versus 298.9 seconds for TPCC, a ~20× speedup, while not requiring prior knowledge of the topological scale. The only dataset where TOPF underperforms is 4Circles+Grid (ARI 0.70 vs. DBSCAN 0.92), which the authors attribute to suboptimal scale selection under fixed hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TPCC (Grande & Schaub, ICML 2023), the closest topological competitor, TOPF achieves a mean ARI of 0.86 vs. 0.58, winning decisively on 4Spheres (0.81 vs. 0.52), Ellipses (0.95 vs. 0.47), Halved Circle (0.71 vs. 0.18), and Spaceship (0.92 vs. 0.56), while roughly tying on 2Spheres2Circles (0.94 vs. 0.97) and SphereinCircle (0.97 vs. 0.98) where the data is noise-free and directly sampled from simple manifolds. TOPF is also ~20× faster than TPCC in mean runtime (15.1s vs. 298.9s), with the gap most extreme on 2Spheres2Circles (20.7s vs. 1662.2s). Against DGCNN (Wang et al., 2019), a pretrained neural network, TOPF wins on 6 of 7 datasets (mean 0.86 vs. 0.64), demonstrating that unsupervised topological features can match or exceed supervised deep learning without application-specific training data. Against WSDesc (Li et al., 2022), another pretrained descriptor, TOPF dominates with mean ARI 0.86 vs. 0.39. Against PAM+ISOMAP (Tenenbaum et al., 2000), a classical manifold-learning baseline, TOPF wins on 5 of 7 datasets (0.86 vs. 0.58 mean ARI). The sole exception is 4Circles+Grid, where DBSCAN (0.92) and spectral clustering (0.90) outperform TOPF (0.70), which the authors attribute to the dataset's grid structure being better captured by density-based methods and to TOPF's fixed hyperparameters selecting suboptimal scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPF directly extends the authors' own TPCC method (ICML 2023), replacing its expensive eigenvector computation of the Hodge Laplacian with a persistent-homology-driven pipeline that projects homology generators into harmonic space via least-squares. Conceptually, it also builds on the harmonic cohomology reparametrisation of De Silva & Vejdemo-Johansson (2009) and the harmonic persistent homology framework of Basu & Cox (2022), but departs from both by targeting robust point-level features rather than global reparametrisation or pooled summaries. The paper positions itself against local topological signatures (Carrière et al., 2015) by explicitly relating each point to *global* topological features, and against topological autoencoders (Moor et al., 2020) by extracting rather than preserving topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOPF bridges a concrete gap: persistent homology yields powerful global shape descriptors, but standard ML pipelines need per-point features, and prior tools (PCA, diffusion maps, local signatures) are blind to higher-order topology. The introduced Topological Clustering Benchmark Suite (TCBS) is the first dedicated benchmark for this setting, giving the community a reproducible yardstick. Key limitations remain: TOPF only produces meaningful features on data with persistent-homology-quantifiable structure, runtime degrades on very large or high-dimensional point clouds, and feature selection relies on a heuristic that may fail without domain knowledge. Open questions include efficient computation of optimal simplicial weights and integration of TOPF features into broader supervised or self-supervised learning pipelines.
