## Problem

State-of-the-art shape-generative models in 3D geometry and engineering design rely on large supervised datasets that are scarce or unavailable in these domains. The paper asks whether a shape-generative neural field can be trained purely from user-specified design objectives and constraints (e.g., interface attachment, topology, smoothness) without any training data, and whether it can produce multiple diverse feasible solutions rather than a single optimum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes spatial coordinates x ∈ ℝⁿ (2D or 3D) and, in generative mode, a latent code z concatenated to the input, producing a scalar SDF value at each point. The implicit zero-level set defines the shape boundary, and the sign defines inside/outside. In generative mode, sampling k i.i.d. latent codes per training iteration yields a batch of diverse shapes, and the trained model allows continuous traversal of the latent space to interpolate between feasible solutions. The SDF property is enforced via an eikonal constraint |∇f|=1, and surface integrals (smoothness, diversity) are estimated via Newton-projected boundary point sampling.

## Method

GINNs represent shapes as implicit neural fields (MLPs with softplus, SIREN, or WIRE activations) approximating signed distance functions, and train them via constrained optimization solved with an adaptive augmented Lagrangian method that automatically balances multiple heterogeneous constraint losses without manual tuning. The key novelty is a diversity constraint that aggregates pairwise boundary-based shape distances (a modified chamfer-like metric integrating f_j² over ∂Ω_i) using a concave minimal-aggregation measure, which prevents mode collapse and induces a structured latent space. Topology constraints (connectedness, number of holes) are enforced through a differentiable persistent homology loss on the discretized field, while smoothness is controlled via surface integrals of curvature expressions (mean curvature or surface strain) computed in closed form from the gradient and Hessian of the SDF. For generative mode, a conditional neural field (input concatenation of z) maps latent codes to distinct shapes, and the expected constraint violations and objectives are optimized over the latent distribution.

## Results

On the jet engine bracket problem, GINN achieves a surface strain (curvature) of 144 compared to 442 for the FeniTop topology optimization baseline, a 3× improvement on GINN's optimization objective, while both methods satisfy all shared geometric constraints (connectedness, interface fit, design region) to comparable precision. For diversity, 196 shapes sampled from the GINN's 2D latent space yield a diversity metric of 0.167, exceeding the 0.099 diversity of 196 human-expert shapes from the simJEB dataset (estimated 14 expert-human-years of collective effort). The GINN also achieves a compliance of 0.344 versus 0.99 for the TO shape, though compliance is not GINN's optimization target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GINN against two baselines. Against FeniTop (classical SIMP topology optimization), GINN achieves a 3× lower surface strain (144 vs. 442), its optimization objective, while both methods satisfy all shared geometric constraints (connectedness = 1, interface chamfer = 0.00, design region volume outside = 0.00). The TO shape achieves lower compliance (0.99 vs. 0.344), its own optimization target, confirming each method excels at its respective objective. Against the simJEB human-expert dataset, GINN produces higher shape diversity (0.167 vs. 0.099) from 196 samples, matching or exceeding the diversity accumulated over an estimated 14 expert-human-years of design work. The authors note that a fully fair head-to-head comparison is not yet possible because GINN currently omits the PDE-based elasticity constraint that classical TO incorporates, which they plan to address in follow-up work.

## Relation to Prior Work

GINNs extend the constraint-based training paradigm of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) from PDE solving to geometric shape generation, but with a substantially richer constraint set (differential, integral, geometrical, and topological) and a generative objective. The optimization backbone is the adaptive augmented Lagrangian method (Basir & Senocak, 2023), which handles multiple constraints with different scales without manual tuning. The generative aspect draws on conditional neural fields and Boltzmann generators, but departs from both by using a geometric diversity constraint over shape boundaries rather than entropy or invertibility assumptions. Unlike classical topology optimization, which produces a single optimal shape, GINNs optimize a model that generates a diverse set of feasible shapes, bridging topology optimization and generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that shape-generative models can be trained purely from design constraints without any data, which is significant for engineering and design domains where large shape datasets are unavailable. The emergent structured latent space—where traversing latent directions produces interpretable geometric changes—is a notable byproduct of the diversity constraint that could enable downstream design exploration. Key limitations include the absence of a PDE solver in the training loop (the bracket problem uses a smoothness objective rather than structural compliance), the reliance on a single GPU with limited problem scale, and the lack of comparison to full topology-optimization pipelines. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex multi-physics constraints, whether more sophisticated conditioning mechanisms (beyond input concatenation) improve latent structure, and how to integrate second-order optimizers for the ill-conditioned curvature losses.
