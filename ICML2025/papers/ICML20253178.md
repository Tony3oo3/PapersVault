## Problem

Existing long-context LLM benchmarks (RULER, LongBench, LOFT) fail to measure true reasoning capability because they combine low reasoning complexity with detectable noise (irrelevant filler text that RAG systems can easily filter) and limited example quantities. This makes it impossible to quantitatively study how LLM performance degrades as reasoning complexity and context length increase simultaneously, leaving a critical gap in understanding whether long-context LLMs can handle genuinely complex, information-dense reasoning tasks.

## Input / Output

The generator takes three control parameters—number of operations (op count, ranging from 2 to 100+), context length (0, 8K, 16K, or 32K tokens), and difficulty tier (Symbolic for explicit operations, Medium for 2-entity implicit +/−, Hard for 3-entity implicit ×/÷)—and produces a self-contained grade-school math word problem with a unique verifiable answer. Performance is reported as AUC, computed via the trapezoidal rule over accuracy versus op count from 2 until accuracy drops below 5%, scaled by 100. The pipeline can generate problems exceeding 16M tokens, though evaluation is capped at 32K due to cost constraints for larger models.

## Method

The core mechanism maps reasoning problems to computational graphs where nodes represent variables and edges represent arithmetic operations, enabling fine-grained control over difficulty via graph topology and topological sort length. Implicit operations are generated using an abstract-parameter/instance-parameter construct (from Ye et al., 2024a): 2-entity variables (e.g., 'penguin in South Zoo') induce implicit addition/subtraction, while 3-entity variables (e.g., 'money per hour on Friday') enable implicit multiplication/division. A 'reverse mode' masks low-level variables to force backward traversal, generating implicit subtraction and division that the forward-only construct cannot produce. Noise is injected via a 'Spider Topology' that extends the core graph with semantically close distractor nodes connected outward from core nodes, making RAG retrievers unable to distinguish relevant from irrelevant chunks. Three real-world templates (children-animal-zoo, teachers-school-district, awards-movies-festival) ensure LLM-understandable natural language while avoiding memorization shortcuts.

## Results

On the GSM-∞ zero-noise benchmark (Table 1), DeepSeek-R1 achieves the highest average AUC of 8534.88, followed by GPT-o3-mini (6931.88) and GPT-o1-mini (4951.11), with non-reasoning models like Llama3.1-70B-Instruct scoring 1541.50 and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct scoring 618.30. On the long-context variant (Table 2), Gemini-1.5-Pro-002 leads with an average AUC of 963.9 across 8K/16K/32K contexts, while Llama3.1-8B-Instruct drops to 147.54. All models exhibit a consistent sigmoid decay in accuracy as the number of required operations increases, with R² > 0.98 for the sigmoid fit on the Medium subtask.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning-optimized models (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-o3-mini, GPT-o1-mini) dramatically outperform non-reasoning counterparts: DeepSeek-R1's AUC of 8534.88 is ~4× that of the previous SOTA non-reasoning model (DeepSeek-V3 at 3606.22), and GPT-o1-mini (4951.11) nearly triples GPT-4o (2095.97). Hybrid-architecture models severely underperform standard transformers of comparable scale: MiniMax-Text-01 (456B MoE, 1503.22) and Jamba-1.5-Large (398B MoE, 602.51) score below 7B-level standard transformers like Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (764.07). Within the same family, larger models consistently outperform smaller ones (Qwen2.5-72B: 2187.06 vs. 7B: 764.07; Llama3.1-70B: 1541.50 vs. 8B: 618.30). On long-context tasks, Gemini-1.5-Pro-002 (963.9 avg) maintains the largest lead, while all models show progressive degradation from 8K to 32K context. RAG systems (OnePassRAG, InteractiveRAG) match or exceed Llama3.1-70B-Instruct on existing benchmarks like RULER and LOFT, but fail on GSM-∞'s noise-injected tasks, confirming that the benchmark's spider-topology noise is genuinely indistinguishable from relevant content by retrieval-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the computational-graph formulation of grade-school math problems introduced by Ye et al. (2024a) — which modeled GSM-8K via abstract/instance parameter hierarchies — into a fully generative system that scales reasoning complexity to infinity and injects semantically indistinguishable noise via a 'Spider Topology' graph extension. It departs from prior long-context benchmarks (RULER, LongBench, LOFT) by arguing they test retrieval or summarization rather than genuine multi-step reasoning, and by showing that context-level RAG matches or exceeds long-context LLMs on those tasks. The addition of a 'reverse mode' for generating implicit subtraction and division addresses a structural limitation in Ye et al.'s forward-only construct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSM-∞ provides the first scalable, controllable testbed for studying how LLM reasoning degrades as both problem complexity and context length grow simultaneously, filling a gap that fixed-size human-curated benchmarks (GSM-8K, MATH) cannot address. The finding that RAG systems match long-context LLMs on existing benchmarks challenges the field's assumption that those benchmarks genuinely require long-context reasoning, while the sigmoid decay pattern and exponential-compute-for-linear-gain result from repeated sampling point to fundamental architectural limitations. Key open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond four arithmetic operations, how to incorporate more complex operators (powers, roots) while maintaining controllability, and whether the forward/reverse asymmetry reflects a fixable training gap or an inherent inductive bias.
