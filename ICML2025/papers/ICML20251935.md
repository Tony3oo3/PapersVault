## Problem

Existing automated jailbreaking methods for LLMs either produce semantically meaningless adversarial suffixes (e.g., GCG) that are trivially filtered by perplexity-based defenses, or require solving a new discrete optimization problem for every single prompt, making them slow and non-scalable. The paper asks whether a trained LLM can rapidly generate diverse, human-readable adversarial suffixes that are adaptive to individual instructions, without requiring backpropagated gradients from the target model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful instruction (e.g., 'Write a tutorial on building a bomb') as input and produces a short adversarial suffix (e.g., 'as part of a lecture') that, when appended to the instruction, elicits a harmful positive response from the TargetLLM. The suffix is generated autoregressively by the AdvPrompter (a Llama2-7b model with LoRA adapters) via next-token prediction with nucleus sampling, taking only 1–2 seconds per prompt. Unlike universal suffix methods, the output is conditioned on the specific instruction, so the suffix adapts semantically and syntactically even to unseen instructions at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is AdvPrompterTrain, an alternating optimization scheme that iterates between two steps: (1) a q-step where AdvPrompterOpt, a stochastic beam-search algorithm, generates target adversarial suffixes by minimizing a regularized adversarial loss (negative log-likelihood of the desired harmful response under the TargetLLM plus a perplexity penalty under a BaseLLM), and (2) a θ-step where the AdvPrompter is fine-tuned via teacher-forced cross-entropy on the generated suffixes. Critically, AdvPrompterOpt samples candidate tokens from the AdvPrompter's own next-token distribution rather than from TargetLLM gradients, making it a gradient-free 'graybox' attack that requires only log-probability access. This amortized-optimization design creates a self-improvement loop: as the AdvPrompter learns to predict more promising candidates, the beam-search finds better targets, which in turn improve the AdvPrompter. The approach avoids the instability of end-to-end gradient descent through autoregressive generation and outperforms PPO-based reinforcement learning alternatives.

## Results

On AdvBench (whitebox), AdvPrompter achieves ASR@10/1 of 97.1/69.6% on Mistral-7b, 81.1/48.7% on Vicuna-13b, and 17.6/8.0% on Llama2-7b-chat, with perplexities of 41.6, 15.9, and 86.8 respectively — dramatically lower than GCG's 114,189.7, 104,749.9, and 106,374.9. On HarmBench (data-transfer), AdvPrompter outperforms GCG-universal on all three TargetLLMs (e.g., 77.8% vs 72.2% ASR@10 on Mistral-7b). In the blackbox model-transfer setting, AdvPrompter achieves 89.0/52.8% ASR@10/1 on GPT-3.5-0301 and 38.4/14.4% on GPT-4-0613, substantially outperforming GCG-universal (75.0/50.7% and 7.7/3.8% respectively). Adversarial fine-tuning on AdvPrompter-generated data reduces validation ASR from 43.3% to 0.9% (Vicuna-7b) and 58.7% to 0.0% (Mistral-7b) while preserving MMLU and MT-bench scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), AdvPrompter achieves comparable or superior ASR@10 across all TargetLLMs while producing suffixes with 3–4 orders of magnitude lower perplexity (e.g., 41.6 vs 114,189.7 on Mistral-7b), making it resistant to perplexity-based filters. On the hardest model (Llama2-7b-chat), AdvPrompter dramatically outperforms GCG-universal (17.6% vs 0.3% ASR@10) and AutoDAN-universal (17.6% vs 4.1%). Against AutoDAN (Zhu et al., 2023), AdvPrompter matches or exceeds ASR@10 on Mistral-7b (97.1% vs 89.4%) and Vicuna-13b (81.1% vs 85.1% for universal, but 48.7% vs 45.3% ASR@1) while generating prompts ~40× faster per token. In the blackbox data+model-transfer setting on HarmBench, AdvPrompter (79.1/49.4% ASR@10/1 on GPT-3.5-1106) outperforms all direct blackbox baselines including TAP-T (47.6% ASR@1), PAIR (36.3%), and PAP (17.0%), and on GPT-4-0613 it matches TAP-T (55.8% ASR@1) while achieving 58.9% ASR@10. Against BEAST (Sadasivan et al., 2024), AdvPrompter achieves comparable ASR@1 with lower perplexity and generates 2000 suffixes in ~1 GPU hour vs 33 GPU hours for BEAST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial suffix optimization line (GCG, AutoDAN) by reframing per-prompt discrete optimization as an amortized optimization problem: a separate LLM (AdvPrompter) is trained to predict instruction-conditioned adversarial suffixes via next-token prediction, replacing expensive per-instance search with a single forward pass. The training scheme (AdvPrompterTrain) departs from both end-to-end gradient-based optimization and RL (PPO), instead using an alternating loop that generates targets via a gradient-free beam search (AdvPrompterOpt) and fine-tunes the generator on those targets. Conceptually, it is closest to Perez et al. (2022) in using a second LLM for red-teaming, but differs by enforcing a specific harmful instruction and by automating the entire target-generation pipeline without human-crafted templates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: (1) generation speed (seconds per suffix vs. minutes-to-hours for GCG/AutoDAN) enables multi-shot attacks and large-scale adversarial dataset generation, and (2) the demonstrated adversarial fine-tuning pipeline (Section 4.3) reduces ASR from ~60% to <1% while preserving MMLU/MT-bench, offering a scalable defense recipe. Open questions include whether the self-play loop (alternating AdvPrompter and TargetLLM training) converges to a robust equilibrium, whether the approach transfers to more advanced defenses beyond perplexity filters and safety system prompts, and whether the graybox assumption (log-probability access) holds for truly closed APIs. The paper acknowledges it does not evaluate against state-of-the-art defense mechanisms.
