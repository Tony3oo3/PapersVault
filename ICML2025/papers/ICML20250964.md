## Problem

The self-repellent random walk (SRRW) achieves near-zero sampling variance by modifying transition kernels based on past visit frequencies, but this kernel-level modification requires pre-computing transition probabilities for all neighbors at each step, destroying the lightweight on-demand nature of Metropolis-Hastings. Additionally, SRRW's proof of correctness critically depends on time-reversibility of the base chain, excluding a broad class of non-reversible MCMC samplers that offer better mixing. The paper asks whether the self-repellent effect can be preserved without these computational and structural constraints.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a connected undirected graph G(X,E), an unnormalized target distribution μ̃ (e.g., μ̃_i=1 for uniform, μ̃_i=|N(i)| for degree-based), a user-specified base MCMC sampler (reversible or non-reversible), and a self-repellency strength α≥0. It outputs a sequence of samples {X_n} from the true target μ and an unbiased estimator ψ_T(f) for graph attributes. A key design constraint is that only unnormalized quantities μ̃_i and x̃_i are needed at each step, and the LRU cache variant reduces memory from O(|X|) to O(r|X|) by tracking only a fraction r of states, approximating missing visit counts via local neighborhood averages.

## Method

The core insight is to decouple the self-repellent mechanism from the transition kernel and embed it in the target distribution instead. The history-driven target π[x] ∝ μ_i(x_i/μ_i)^(-α) is proven (Lemma 3.1) to be the unique distribution satisfying four design conditions: scale invariance, local dependence on only (x_i, μ_i), fixed point at μ, and history-dependent exploration bias. This means any existing MCMC sampler—MH, MTM, MHDA, 2-cycle chains—can be used unchanged by simply replacing μ with π[x] in its acceptance ratio, requiring only the same O(1) local computation per proposal as the base sampler. Theoretically, the empirical measure update is cast as stochastic approximation with controlled Markovian dynamics, yielding almost-sure convergence to μ and an O(1/α) variance reduction (Theorem 3.3). A cost-based CLT (Theorem 3.5) further shows HDT-MCMC achieves at least a factor of 2/E[|N(i)|] smaller cost-based variance than SRRW under the same computational budget, with the advantage growing on dense graphs.

## Results

On the facebook graph (4,039 nodes, avg. degree 43.6) at n=15,000 steps, HDT-MTM achieves the lowest TVD (~0.283–0.288) and NRMSE (~0.047–0.098) among the three HDT variants, while HDT-MHRW and HDT-MHDA achieve TVD ≈ 0.371 and 0.365 respectively. Under a fixed computational budget B, HDT-MHRW consistently outperforms SRRW in both TVD and NRMSE, with the gap widening on denser graphs (facebook avg. degree 43.6 vs. p2p-Gnutella04 avg. degree 7.4), consistent with the theoretical 2/E[|N(i)|] cost-based variance advantage. The LRU cache scheme with only 10% of states tracked still yields >10% TVD reduction over the base MHRW, and the framework achieves O(1/α) variance reduction relative to the base sampler.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRRW (Doshi et al., 2023), HDT-MHRW wins on both TVD and NRMSE under a fixed computational budget B, with the advantage growing with average neighborhood size (facebook avg. degree 43.6 shows a larger gap than p2p-Gnutella04 avg. degree 7.4), matching the theoretical 2/E[|N(i)|] cost-based variance bound from Lemma 3.6. Against the standard MH random walk (MHRW, Hastings 1970), HDT-MHRW achieves consistently lower TVD and NRMSE at α=5, confirming the O(1/α) variance reduction. Against MTM with locally balanced weights (Chang et al., 2022), HDT-MTM outperforms the base MTM in both TVD and NRMSE, and unlike SRRW which cannot be combined with MTM due to prohibitive P_ij computation over K intermediate candidates, HDT integrates seamlessly at no extra cost. Against MHDA (Lee et al., 2012), HDT-MHDA achieves lower TVD and NRMSE than the base non-reversible MHDA, demonstrating that the HDT framework extends the self-repellent benefit to non-reversible samplers—a capability SRRW fundamentally lacks due to its time-reversibility requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the self-repellent random walk (SRRW) framework of Doshi et al. (2023), which achieved near-zero variance by modifying transition kernels based on visit history. The key conceptual departure is relocating the self-repellent mechanism from the kernel to a history-driven target distribution π[x], which eliminates the need to precompute transition probabilities for all neighbors and removes the time-reversibility constraint. The convergence and CLT proofs build on stochastic approximation theory (Delyon et al., 1999; Fort, 2015), the same framework used in SRRW's analysis, but extended to handle non-reversible chains on augmented state spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HDT framework provides a universal 'drop-in' variance reduction that works with any existing MCMC sampler (reversible or non-reversible) at the same per-step computational cost as the base method, making it broadly applicable to graph sampling in network science, distributed optimization, and statistical physics. The LRU cache scheme demonstrates practical scalability, maintaining >10% TVD reduction while tracking only 10% of states. A key open question is extension to exponentially large configuration spaces (e.g., Ising/Potts models, energy-based models), which the authors explicitly defer to future work, limiting current applicability to moderate-sized graphs.
