## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of permutation-based SGD (e.g., Random Reshuffling, IGD) are largely restricted to the large epoch regime where K ≥ κ, the condition number. In the practically relevant small epoch regime (K < κ), it was an open question whether permutation-based SGD can converge faster than with-replacement SGD, and the convergence behavior of IGD—the simplest deterministic permutation-based method—was poorly understood. This paper reveals that IGD can be surprisingly slow in this regime, even when all component functions are strongly convex, and dramatically worse when nonconvex components are allowed.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite-sum objective F(x) = (1/n)Σf_i(x) that is μ-strongly convex and L-smooth with L-smooth components, an initialization point x_0, a constant step size η, and K epochs of IGD (identity permutation each epoch). The output is tight upper and lower bounds on the optimality gap F(x_n^K) − F(x*), expressed using ≲ and ≳ notation that hides polylogarithmic factors and applies to all valid K values (not just asymptotic K→∞). Lower bounds are established via explicit function constructions (2D, 3D, or 4D) that are dimension-aggregated across three step-size regimes (small, moderate, large) to guarantee the bound holds for any constant η.

## Method

The paper constructs lower-bound functions for IGD by partitioning the step-size range into three regimes and designing separate 1D or 2D functions for each, then aggregating them across dimensions so the combined function satisfies the stated bound for any η. The key geometric insight (Theorem 3.3) is to define component functions by rotating a base quadratic so their minimizers form a regular n-gon; with a carefully chosen initialization, the IGD iterates preserve this rotational symmetry and trace a circular trajectory at constant distance from the optimum, yielding a lower bound of Ω(LG²/μ² · min{1, κ²/K⁴}). For nonconvex components (Theorem 3.5), concave directions in the components cause the optimality gap to grow exponentially as (1 + L/(2μnK))^n ≈ exp(κ/(2K)). On the positive side, Theorem 3.7 leverages the Herding algorithm to show the existence of a fixed permutation σ that achieves rate Õ(H²L²G*²/(μ³n²K²)), outperforming with-replacement SGD whenever n ≥ H²κ²/K—establishing the first result that a permutation-based method can beat with-replacement SGD in the small epoch regime.

## Results

In the small epoch regime (K ≲ κ), IGD exhibits a lower bound of G²/(μK) even when all components share the same Hessian (Theorem 3.1), which is slower than with-replacement SGD's G²/(μnK) by a factor of n. When components are merely strongly convex (no identical Hessian), the lower bound worsens to (LG²/μ²)·min{1, κ²/K⁴} (Theorem 3.3), and with nonconvex components it becomes (G²/L)·(1+L/(2μnK))^n ≈ (G²/L)·exp(κ/(2K)) (Theorem 3.5), growing exponentially in κ/K. In the large epoch regime (K ≳ κ), tight matching upper and lower bounds of LG²/(μ²K²) (convex) and L²G²/(μ³K²) (nonconvex) are established, with the nonconvex penalty reduced to only a factor of κ. Theorem 3.7 further shows that a Herding-based fixed permutation achieves H²LG*²/(μ³n²K²), outperforming with-replacement SGD whenever n ≥ H²κ²/K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to with-replacement SGD (rate G²/(μnK), Liu & Zhou 2024b), IGD is strictly slower by a factor of n in the small epoch regime (G²/(μK) vs. G²/(μnK)), even under the favorable identical-Hessian assumption. Against Random Reshuffling, whose small-epoch lower bound is G²/(μnK) (Safran & Shamir 2021), IGD's worst-case rate is again n times worse, confirming that the deterministic identity permutation is the worst-case ordering. In the large epoch regime with convex components, IGD matches the tight rate LG²/(μ²K²), while RR achieves a faster LG²/(μ²nK²) by a factor of n (Liu & Zhou 2024a; Cha et al. 2023). With nonconvex components in the large epoch regime, IGD's rate L²G²/(μ³K²) is slower than the optimal permutation-based rate L²G²/(μ³n²K²) (GraB, Lu et al. 2022a) by a factor of n². The Herding-based permutation (Theorem 3.7) is the first result showing a permutation-based method can beat with-replacement SGD in the small epoch regime, achieving H²LG*²/(μ³n²K²) versus G²/(μnK) whenever n ≥ H²κ²/K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of permutation-based SGD from the well-studied large epoch regime (K ≳ κ) to the practically critical small epoch regime (K ≲ κ), focusing specifically on Incremental Gradient Descent (IGD), the simplest deterministic variant. It builds directly on Safran & Shamir (2021), who first identified the small epoch regime as an open challenge and showed RR cannot beat with-replacement SGD there, and on Mishchenko et al. (2020) and Liu & Zhou (2024a), whose upper bounds for permutation-based SGD require K to be large relative to κ. The paper also leverages the Herding algorithm from Bansal & Garg (2017) in a novel way to construct a fixed permutation that achieves faster convergence than with-replacement SGD in the small epoch regime, complementing the adaptive GraB approach of Lu et al. (2022a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The small epoch regime is the practically relevant setting for training large models on ill-conditioned landscapes, yet convergence guarantees were largely absent. The finding that IGD exhibits exponential dependence on κ/K when nonconvex components are present (versus polynomial for convex components) reveals a sharp qualitative distinction between small and large epoch behavior that was previously unrecognized. Key open questions remain: establishing tight bounds for RR in the small epoch regime, and designing a practical (not oracle-dependent) permutation-based method that provably outperforms with-replacement SGD in this regime. The paper's lower bound constructions are for specific function classes, so generalization to broader settings is an important next step.
