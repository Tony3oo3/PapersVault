## Problem

Existing DC-oriented AFL bidding strategies assume the entire team of data owners must be recruited in a single auction session before FL training begins, but in practice DCs recruit DOs incrementally over multiple training sessions. No prior method addresses the resulting multi-session budget pacing problem—strategically dispersing a fixed total budget across sessions while simultaneously optimizing per-DO bids under competition from other DCs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252342/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The InterBPA consumes a state vector comprising the last S' sessions' allocated budgets, per-DO payments, and recruited-DO reputations, plus current-session metadata (number of available DOs C_s, remaining total budget B, session index s), and outputs a single scalar session budget B_s. The IntraBMA then operates within that session, taking as input the remaining DO count, remaining session budget, and the current DO's reputation (computed via GTG-Shapley contributions and a Beta Reputation System), and outputs a bid price for each DO under a second-price sealed-bid auction with DO-side reserve prices. The DC forfeits future auctions once its total budget is exhausted.

## Method

MBOS-AFL employs a two-level hierarchical RL architecture with DQN-based agents: an Inter-session Budget Pacing Agent (InterBPA) that decides how much of the total budget to commit to each session (updated once per session, with reward equal to the mean reputation of recruited DOs), and an Intra-session Bidding Agent (IntraBMA) that sets individual bid prices for each DO auction (updated per auction, with reward equal to the utility of the won DO). The inter-session action B_s serves as the initial state for IntraBMA, coupling the two levels. Both agents use 3-layer fully connected networks (64 nodes/layer), replay buffers of 5,000 transitions, ε-greedy exploration annealed from 1.0 to 0.05, RMSprop at lr=0.0005, and target networks refreshed every 20 steps, with discount factor γ=1 to reflect the DC's time-agnostic utility objective. The key design choice is separating the coarse-grained pacing decision from the fine-grained bidding decision, enabling the DC to adaptively conserve budget in sessions with low-quality DO candidates while spending aggressively when high-reputation DOs are available.

## Results

Across six benchmark datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, EMNIST-Digits, EMNIST-Letters, Kuzushiji-MNIST) and five budget levels (100–800), MBOS-AFL consistently outperforms all seven baselines on both #data acquired and total utility. On average, MBOS-AFL achieves 12.28% higher utility and 14.52% more data samples than the best baseline (RLB), and 1.23% higher FL model test accuracy. Under Non-IID data with noise, MBOS-AFL yields 1.49% and 1.72% higher model accuracy over the best baseline at 10% and 25% noise levels, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MBOS-AFL dominates all seven baselines across every dataset and budget setting. Against the strongest single-session baseline RLB (Tang & Yu, IJCAI 2023), MBOS-AFL achieves 12.28% higher utility and 14.52% more data on average; for example, at budget 400 on CIFAR-10, MBOS-AFL acquires 53,672 samples (utility 22.71) versus RLB's 42,731 (utility 19.63). The Fed-Bidder variants (FBs, FBc) are the next-best baselines but remain static bidding strategies that cannot adapt to multi-session dynamics; MBOS-AFL outperforms them by roughly 15–20% in utility. Bidding Machine (BM) and LinearForm (Lin) are further behind, with BM's per-auction price-density prediction prone to overfitting and Lin's linear form lacking adaptivity. The simplest baselines (Const, Rand, Bmub) trail by 20–30% in utility, confirming that utility-aware and learning-based bidding is essential. Under Non-IID noisy conditions (Table 2), MBOS-AFL's advantage in FL model accuracy narrows to 1.49–1.72% over RLB, but it still wins on every dataset–budget–noise combination tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the forward auction-based federated learning (AFL) bidding literature, which has focused on single-session budget-constrained bidding (e.g., Fed-Bidder, RLB), to the multi-session setting where a DC recruits DOs incrementally across repeated FL training sessions. Architecturally, it builds on Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (Pateria et al., 2021) and DQN (Mnih et al., 2015), decomposing the problem into an inter-session budget pacing agent and an intra-session bidding agent. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of budget pacing as an explicit optimization objective across sessions, rather than assuming the entire DO team must be assembled before training begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to address multi-session budget pacing in forward AFL, which is practically relevant for continual FL settings (e.g., banks with continuously updated user data) where DOs are recruited over multiple training rounds. Limitations include reliance on a relatively simple VGG11 backbone, evaluation on only six image classification datasets, and simulation-based experiments without real auction market data. Open questions include scalability to larger numbers of competing DCs, generalization to other auction mechanisms beyond SPSB, and whether the HRL decomposition remains effective when session dynamics are highly non-stationary.
