## Problem

While UNet-based diffusion models have well-studied cross-attention mechanisms for interpretability, the internal representations of newer multi-modal diffusion transformers (DiTs) like Flux and Stable Diffusion 3 remain largely unexplored. It is unclear whether DiT attention layers encode uniquely interpretable features that can localize textual concepts within images, and whether their representations transfer to downstream vision tasks like segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (encoded to VAE latent space with Gaussian noise at a chosen timestep, typically 500/1000) and a user-specified set of single-token text concepts (e.g., "cat", "sky") that need not appear in any generation prompt. It produces a per-concept saliency map as a spatial grid of scalar scores, which can be thresholded at the mean to yield a binary segmentation mask. The method is open-set: concepts are arbitrary and do not affect the image's appearance, and it generalizes from images to video by averaging over the frame dimension.

## Method

ConceptAttention injects a set of concept embeddings (initialized from T5 token embeddings) into each multi-modal attention (MMATTN) layer of a pretrained DiT, processed in a one-directional fashion: concept tokens attend to both image patches and other concept tokens, while image and prompt tokens ignore the concepts entirely, preserving the generated image. The key discovery is that computing saliency maps via a dot-product in the attention *output* space (softmax(o_x · o_c^T)) yields significantly sharper and more transferable maps than the standard cross-attention maps (softmax(q_p · k_x^T)), as confirmed by ablations showing output-space projections outperform cross-attention and value-space projections. No additional training is required; the method repurposes the existing text-side projection matrices (K_p, Q_p, V_p) and the modulation/projection/MLP residual stream for the concept tokens. Final saliency maps are obtained by averaging the per-layer output-space similarities across the last 10 of 18 MMATTN layers in Flux.

## Results

On ImageNet-Segmentation, ConceptAttention with the Flux DiT backbone achieves 83.07% pixel accuracy, 71.04% mIoU, and 90.45% mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline (DINO self-attention at 81.97% Acc / 69.44% mIoU / 86.12% mAP) by 1.1% Acc, 1.6% mIoU, and 4.3% mAP. On PascalVOC (single-class), ConceptAttention (Flux) reaches 87.85% Acc, 76.45% mIoU, and 90.19% mAP, a margin of 7.1% Acc and 12.1% mIoU over DINO. On the multi-class PascalVOC split, ConceptAttention achieves 86.99% Acc and 51.39% mIoU, nearly doubling the mIoU of Flux raw cross-attention (27.04%) and DAAM (10.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptAttention outperforms all 15 zero-shot interpretability baselines across both ImageNet-Segmentation and PascalVOC, spanning CLIP-ViT methods (LRP, Rollout, GradCAM, TextSpan, TransInterp, CLIP-as-RNN), DINO self-attention variants (DINOv1, DINOv2, DINOv2-Reg, iBOT), UNet-based diffusion methods (DAAM on SDXL/SD2, OVAM), and raw cross-attention from Flux and SD3.5 DiTs. The largest margins appear over UNet-based methods: DAAM (SDXL) trails by 4.6% mIoU on ImageNet and 20.5% mIoU on multi-class PascalVOC, while DAAM (SD2) lags by 23.4% mIoU. Against the strongest single-model baseline, DINO self-attention, ConceptAttention leads by 1.6% mIoU on ImageNet and 12.1% mIoU on PascalVOC. Notably, ConceptAttention also surpasses its own architecture's native cross-attention maps (Flux CA: 59.90% mIoU vs. 71.04%), confirming the paper's central finding that the attention output space yields sharper saliency than raw cross-attention. On the multi-class PascalVOC task, the gap widens dramatically: ConceptAttention's 51.39% mIoU is 1.9× that of Flux cross-attention and 4.7× that of DAAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpretability of diffusion models from UNet-based architectures (where cross-attention maps between prompt and image tokens have been the standard tool, as in DAAM) to the newer multi-modal DiT architectures (Flux, SD3) that process text and image in a unified attention mechanism. It builds on the zero-shot segmentation evaluation protocol established by Chefer et al. (2021) and Gandelsman et al. (2024) for CLIP ViTs, and on the observation that self-attention in diffusion models encodes layout information (Liu et al., 2024). The key departure is the discovery that linear projections in the attention *output* space—rather than the cross-attention space—yield substantially sharper saliency maps, a property uniquely enabled by the dual-stream MMATTN design of DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first evidence that multi-modal DiT representations are directly transferable to downstream vision tasks like segmentation without any fine-tuning, positioning DiTs as potentially powerful interpretable image encoders. The no-training requirement and cross-architecture generality (Flux, SD3.5, CogVideoX video) make the technique immediately practical for auditing and controlling generative models. Open questions include whether the output-space advantage persists in single-stream DiT variants, how the method behaves with multi-word or compositional concepts, and whether the observed limitation with semantically overlapping concepts (e.g., sun vs. sky) can be addressed through concept disambiguation.
