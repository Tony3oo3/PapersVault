## Problem

Existing student performance prediction methods rely on pairwise graph modeling or temporal sequences, which cannot capture higher-order interactions among multiple students and questions simultaneously. Moreover, prior work has not effectively integrated LLM-derived semantic features with hypergraph structural representations, and available hypergraph neural networks lack multi-scale spectral mechanisms to jointly model shared learning patterns and individual distinctions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251337/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two input streams: (1) binary student–MCQ response records (correct/incorrect), which are organized into a signed hypergraph where each question node is connected to all students who answered it correctly via a positive hyperedge and to all students who answered incorrectly via a negative hyperedge; and (2) MCQ textual content (stem, options, answer, explanation), which is processed by ChatGPT to extract up to five weighted knowledge-point keywords, then embedded via GloVe word vectors and combined into a per-question semantic vector. The output is a binary score (via sigmoid) predicting the sign of an unobserved hyperedge, i.e., whether a given student will answer a given question correctly or incorrectly, trained with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Method

EduLLM's core novelty is FraS-HNN, a spectral framelet-based signed hypergraph neural network that decomposes node signals on the signed hypergraph Laplacian into multi-frequency components using Haar-type low-pass and high-pass filters. The signed hypergraph Laplacian is eigendecomposed, and framelet decomposition operators project the feature matrix onto low-pass (capturing shared learning patterns) and high-pass (capturing individual distinctions) subspaces at multiple scales; spectral filters are approximated via Chebyshev polynomials to avoid explicit eigendecomposition at scale. Multiple convolution layers are stacked with initial residual connections and learnable identity-mapping coefficients to mitigate over-smoothing. LLM-induced semantic embeddings (ChatGPT-extracted keywords weighted and averaged through GloVe vectors) serve as initial node features for question nodes, synergizing with the structural hypergraph representations. The final prediction concatenates student and question embeddings and passes them through an MLP with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Results

EduLLM achieves the highest Binary-F1 score across all five datasets, ranging from 0.712 (Sydney19351) to 0.945 (Law). The largest gains over the strongest baseline (LLM-SBCL) are on Sydney23146 (+6.9 pp, 0.829 vs 0.760) and Law (+3.7 pp, 0.945 vs 0.908). EduLLM also exhibits the lowest standard deviations (≤0.016) among all methods, indicating superior stability across 10 repeated runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EduLLM outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset. Against unsigned GNNs (GCN, GAT), the gains are substantial (e.g., +12.7 pp on Biology, +12.8 pp on Cardiff), confirming that ignoring edge signs severely degrades performance. Among signed-graph methods, SGCN trails EduLLM by 4.1–13.8 pp, while the stronger signed-bipartite baselines SBGNN, SBCL, and LLM-SBCL are closer but still consistently below EduLLM by 1.8–11.7 pp. In the HNN-module ablation (Table 4), replacing FraS-HNN with HGNN, HyperGCN, AllDeepSets, AllSetTransformer, ED-HNN, or SheafHyperGNN yields 2.8–13.6 pp lower Binary-F1, with SheafHyperGNN being the closest competitor (e.g., 0.914 vs 0.945 on Law). The consistent advantage of EduLLM over LLM-SBCL (which also uses LLM embeddings but on signed bipartite graphs) isolates the contribution of the signed hypergraph formulation and framelet-based spectral convolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signed graph neural network line (SGCN, SBGNN, SBCL) by elevating the structural representation from pairwise signed edges to signed hyperedges, enabling higher-order modeling of multi-student interactions with a single question. The core architectural novelty, FraS-HNN, draws on framelet theory from multi-resolution signal processing and applies it to the spectral domain of signed hypergraph Laplacians, a combination not previously explored in the hypergraph learning literature (which has focused on spatial/vertex-based convolutions like HGNN, HyperGCN, and SheafHyperGNN). The LLM-based semantic extraction module is adapted from Wang et al. (2024b), but the paper's contribution lies in integrating those embeddings within a signed hypergraph framework rather than a signed bipartite graph one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical treatment of tight framelet systems on signed hypergraphs (Theorem B1), giving rigorous guarantees for multi-frequency decomposition and reconstruction that prior spatial HNNs lack. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the LLM embeddings are pre-encoded by a prior work (so the LLM component cannot be independently varied), the robustness test substitutes Gaussian noise for genuine LLM diversity, and the signed hypergraph formulation is explicitly incompatible with standard knowledge-tracing and cognitive-diagnosis datasets. The near-parity between EduLLM and its signed-graph variant (EduLLM-SG) in Table E-2 raises open questions about whether the hypergraph inductive bias provides meaningful gains beyond what a well-designed signed graph convolution can achieve, particularly on sparse data.
