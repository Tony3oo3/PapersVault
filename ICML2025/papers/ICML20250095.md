## Problem

Standard RLHF optimizes the win rate of a single sample from the aligned model against the base model, but in practice inference-time procedures such as Best-of-N sampling or Best-of-N jailbreaking are used to decode from the model. This train/test mismatch makes the standard RLHF objective sub-optimal for the actual inference-time win rate. The paper asks whether a language model can be aligned to be optimally served under a known inference-time procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, an uncalibrated Bradley-Terry reward model, a base SFT policy, and a specification of the inference-time procedure (e.g., Best-of-N with a given N). It draws K=100 offline rollouts per prompt from the reference model to compute empirical calibrated reward scores, then trains a new policy via PPO. The output is an aligned language model whose responses, when passed through the target inference-time procedure, maximize win rate against the base model's inference-time outputs while maintaining a controlled KL divergence from the base policy.

## Method

InfAlign proves that for any inference-time procedure, the optimal aligned policy is the solution to a standard KL-regularized RLHF problem with a specific reward transformation (Lemma 1, Theorem 1). The practical solver, InfAlign-CTRL, operates in three steps: (1) calibrate the reward model's scores per-prompt using K samples from the reference policy, yielding a calibrated reward that is provably uniform on [0,1] regardless of the base policy or reward model; (2) apply a transformation function Φ to the calibrated reward, chosen based on the inference-time procedure (positive exponential tilting e^{tu} for Best-of-N, negative for Worst-of-N); (3) solve the resulting RLHF problem with PPO. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that for calibrated inference-time procedures, the resulting win rate and KL divergence are independent of the specific reward model and base policy, enabling transferable transformation design via simple toy models.

## Results

InfAlign-CTRL achieves 3–8% higher inference-time win rates compared to the best SOTA baselines across three tasks. Specifically, with exponential reward transformation, it gains up to 3% on Best-of-4 win rate for Anthropic Helpfulness, up to 8% on Best-of-4 for Reddit TL;DR summarization, and up to 5% on Worst-of-4 for Anthropic Harmlessness. For standard win rate (no inference-time procedure), InfAlign-CTRL with identity transformation achieves better win-rate–KL trade-offs on helpfulness and harmlessness and is on-par with SOTA on summarization. The exponential transformation parameter t=10 is optimal for BoN while t=−10 is optimal for WoN, consistent with analytical predictions from the toy model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four main baselines: Uncalibrated RLHF (PPO with raw reward), BoNBoN (Gui et al., 2024), BoND (Sessa et al., 2024), and IPO (Azar et al., 2023). On standard win rate, InfAlign-CTRL (identity transform) outperforms all four on Anthropic Helpfulness and Harmlessness, and is on-par on Reddit summarization, attributed to the calibration step reducing reward hacking. On Best-of-4 win rate, InfAlign-CTRL with exponential transformation (t=10) outperforms all baselines by up to 3% (helpfulness) and 8% (summarization), while IPO and BoNBoN/BoND—which are near-optimal for standard win rate—are suboptimal for inference-time BoN. On Worst-of-4 (jailbreak defense), InfAlign-CTRL with negative exponential transform (t=−10) outperforms all baselines by up to 5%. Additionally, InfAlign-CTRL matches GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on standard win-rate–KL trade-offs without the cost of online rollouts, and outperforms GRPO on Best-of-4 inference-time evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF alignment framework (Ouyang et al., 2022) and the win-rate-optimal objectives of IPO (Azar et al., 2023) and BoN distillation (Gui et al., 2024; Sessa et al., 2024) by explicitly accounting for inference-time procedures such as Best-of-N sampling. The key conceptual departure is identifying a train/test mismatch: prior methods optimize the training-time policy's standard win rate, but at deployment the model is decoded through a different procedure (BoN, WoN, CoT), making the standard RLHF objective suboptimal. The paper shows this gap can be closed by solving the same KL-regularized RLHF problem with a transformed reward, where the identity and log transformations recover IPO and BoN distillation as special cases, and exponential tilting yields new objectives tailored to BoN/WoN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As inference-time compute (BoN, tree search, CoT) becomes the dominant scaling axis for LLM deployment, aligning models for the actual inference procedure rather than raw sampling is increasingly important. The 3–8% inference-time win-rate gains over SOTA baselines are meaningful in the alignment context, and the model-agnostic calibration step (Lemma 4) provides a reusable component that also mitigates reward hacking. Limitations include reliance on continuous-model assumptions for the main theorems, testing only on PaLM-2 S with two specific inference-time procedures (BoN, WoN), and the computational overhead of K offline rollouts per prompt for calibration. Open questions include extending the framework to more complex inference-time procedures (CoT, tree search, self-consistency) and multi-objective alignment with transformed rewards.
