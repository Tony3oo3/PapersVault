## Problem

Training flexible neural network priors on variant effects from functional annotations is bottlenecked by the need to invert and compute log-determinants of large linkage disequilibrium (LD) matrices at every gradient step. Prior methods were therefore restricted to small parametric priors or had to sacrifice statistical efficiency by fitting summary statistics (LD score regression) rather than the full marginal likelihood. This paper shows that larger models only improve predictions when trained with the full likelihood, not with summary statistics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 165 functional genomics annotations (ENCODE ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, FANTOM CAGE) as exact per-position values in a 256-base-pair window around each variant, plus 9 coding variant effect predictions (ESM2, SIFT, PROVEAN, etc.), variant frequency, minor allele frequency, and LD score. It outputs a per-variant effect size prior f_θ,m (the variance of the variant's effect on the phenotype) and the phenotype marginal likelihood. A key practical constraint is that the banded LD approximation means the network only computes f_θ for ~10⁴ variants per mini-batch window rather than all ~10⁷ variants in the genome.

## Method

DeepWAS reformulates the marginal likelihood using the Woodbury identity and matrix determinant lemma to express all expensive linear algebra in terms of a well-conditioned matrix B_θ = I + (1/σ_N²) F_θ^{1/2} W F_θ^{1/2}, where W is a precomputed LD-derived matrix independent of θ. This allows iterative algorithms—stochastic Lanczos quadrature for log-determinants and conjugate gradients for matrix solves—to replace O(M³) Cholesky decompositions with O(M²K) operations, where K is a small number of iterations enabled by the favorable conditioning. The genome is partitioned into 2700 windows of ~1M variants using a banded LD approximation, and each window serves as a mini-batch for stochastic gradient descent. The architecture is a 52M-parameter transformer-CNN hybrid (adapted from Enformer) that maps the 256-position annotation window to a scalar effect enrichment, multiplied by a frequency-dependent term (freq·(1−freq))^0.7. GPU-accelerated backpropagation through the iterative solvers is handled by the COLA library, reducing per-step cost from hours on CPU to minutes on a single A100.

## Results

On UK Biobank held-out chromosomes (6, 7, 8), the full DeepWAS transformer model (52M parameters) achieves a 3.02% likelihood increase per person for BMI, 11.70% for height, and 0.694% for asthma, compared to a null model with no genetic effect. The improvement is largest for asthma (lowest SNP-heritability at 10–16%), where the flexible prior has the most room to help. Critically, when the same transformer is trained with LD score regression (LDSR) instead of DeepWAS, prediction quality does not improve over smaller models and actually decreases for BMI, demonstrating that full-likelihood training is essential for larger models to benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DeepWAS (full-likelihood training) against LDSR (summary-statistics fitting) across model sizes from constant to 52M-parameter transformer. The headline finding is that the transformer trained with DeepWAS substantially outperforms all other configurations on all three traits, while the same transformer trained with LDSR shows no improvement over the linear prior and actually degrades for BMI. Against the generalized linear model baseline from Li et al. (2024), the DeepWAS transformer achieves lower RMSE in recovering the true f in semi-synthetic simulations (Figure 4) and higher held-out likelihood on real data. Against the linear functional-annotation prior of Finucane et al. (2015), the transformer with DeepWAS provides a clear gain (e.g., 11.70% vs. a smaller gain for the linear model on height), confirming that flexible models capture more of the variant-effect structure. The comparison with LDSR (Bulik-Sullivan et al., 2015) is the most striking: larger models only help when the full likelihood is optimized, revealing that the statistical efficiency lost by fitting summary statistics becomes a bottleneck as model capacity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepWAS extends the functionally informed prior framework for GWAS (Finucane et al. 2015; Li et al. 2024) by replacing the summary-statistic fitting objective (LD score regression) with direct full-likelihood optimization of a large neural network. The key technical departure borrows from Gaussian process regression (Gardner et al. 2018): a Woodbury-identity reformulation converts the ill-conditioned LD matrix inversion into operations on a well-conditioned matrix, which is then solved with iterative algorithms (stochastic Lanczos quadrature and conjugate gradients) via the COLA library. Previous flexible-prior methods (Lu et al. 2016; Fabiha et al. 2024) were restricted to small models trained on limited labeled data, while DeepWAS scales to 52M-parameter transformer-CNN hybrids trained on millions of variants through banded LD mini-batching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that larger models only improve predictions under full-likelihood training (not LDSR) provides a concrete justification for the added computational complexity and suggests that the field has been leaving predictive power on the table by using summary statistics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across populations (only European UKBB was tested), whether multi-phenotype or multi-component priors further improve performance, and whether pre-trained genomic foundation models (Enformer, Nucleotide Transformer) can be effectively integrated. The accelerated linear algebra pipeline (COLA + Woodbury reformulation) is broadly reusable for any Bayesian model requiring repeated inversion of large structured matrices.
