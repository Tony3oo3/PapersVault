## Problem

Reward models trained with the Bradley-Terry loss are prone to over-optimization, overfitting to the training distribution and losing alignment capability on unseen prompts or response styles. The root cause of this degradation has not been clearly identified, and existing mitigations (ensembling, meta-learning, data augmentation) address symptoms rather than the underlying mechanism. This paper pinpoints the specific source of over-optimization and proposes a targeted, lightweight regularization to fix it.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a prompt-response text pair (x, y) and produces a scalar reward score r(x, y) computed as a dot product between a linear projection head W_p and the last hidden state of a backbone language model. Training consumes triplets (x, y_w, y_l) with pre-annotated chosen/rejected labels, and the resulting score is used downstream in RLOO-based RLHF as a proxy for human preference to guide policy optimization. The projection head is initialized with N(0, 1/(H+1)) and the backbone can be either encoder-only or decoder-only, with hidden dimension H.

## Method

The paper shows that BT loss minimization drives excessive growth in the norm dispersion of hidden states ||h(x,y)|| while the projection head norm ||W_p|| remains near its initialization value of 1, causing over-confidence analogous to the logit magnitude problem in classification. To counteract this, they propose batch-wise sum-to-zero regularization (BSR), which adds a penalty (1/2|B|)² Σᵢ Σⱼ r(xᵢ, yᵢⱼ)² to the BT loss, forcing the batch reward sum toward zero and selectively constraining extreme reward outliers. The BSR gradient is proportional to r(x,y) itself, so it penalizes high-magnitude rewards without removing the norm signal entirely (unlike logit normalization, which underperforms in all scenarios). They further define four generalization scenarios (in-domain, prompt-disjoint, response-disjoint, mutual-disjoint) to systematically assess robustness, and demonstrate that BSR consistently improves alignment with a gold preference model across all scenarios, reduces generation length by 40% in RLOO training while improving AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate by 7%, and surpasses state-of-the-art 8B RMs on RM-Bench hard tasks by over 5%.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-3B, RM_BT-BSR achieves the best alignment to the gold preference model (ArmoRM) across all four generalization scenarios: 0.8541 in-domain accuracy, 0.7202 prompt-OOD Kendall's τ, 0.5991 response-OOD τ, and 0.6106 mutual-OOD τ, consistently outperforming plain BT (0.8496 / 0.705 / 0.5917 / 0.587). On RM-Bench with Llama-3.1-8B, BSR (λ=10⁻³) improves Hard Acc from 47.8% to 52.1% (+4.3 pp) while maintaining 70.2% overall accuracy. In the final RLHF evaluation on AlpacaEval 2.0, the BSR-trained policy achieves a 9.02% length-controlled win rate with 1,337 average response length, versus 8.41% and 2,180 for the BT-trained policy—a 40% reduction in verbosity with a 7% win-rate gain over the SFT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSR consistently outperforms plain BT across all four generalization scenarios and both model families, with the gap widening as model size increases (e.g., +1.5 pp on prompt-OOD τ for Qwen2.5-3B vs. +0.4 pp for Qwen2.5-1.5B). BT-DR (Yuan et al., 2024), which adds separate logistic losses to boost the margin, underperforms BT in every scenario—confirming the paper's hypothesis that stronger margin boosting worsens hidden-state norm dispersion. BT-Norm (Wei et al., 2022), which explicitly normalizes logits, underperforms all other methods in every scenario, indicating that completely removing norm information is too aggressive. BT-Hinge (Cortes, 1995) occasionally matches or slightly exceeds BT on response-OOD and mutual-OOD (e.g., 0.6210 vs. 0.6195 for Llama-3.2-3B), supporting the role of norm growth in over-optimization, but still trails BSR. Against the state-of-the-art Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B checkpoint (Liu et al., 2024a), the BSR-trained 8B RM achieves a higher AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate (9.02% vs. 8.41%) while reducing average response length by 40% (1,337 vs. 2,180 tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bradley-Terry model as the standard reward modeling objective and connects the over-optimization problem (formalized by Gao et al., 2023) to the over-confidence phenomenon in neural classifiers identified by Wei et al. (2022). Rather than proposing yet another architectural or data-level mitigation (ensembling, meta-learning, data augmentation, or constrained RLHF), the paper diagnoses the root cause as excessive hidden-state norm dispersion and addresses it with a single batch-level regularization term. The four-scenario generalization framework (in-domain, prompt-disjoint, response-disjoint, mutual-disjoint) extends the binary in-domain/OOD split used in prior over-optimization studies, enabling finer-grained attribution of where RMs fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivially simple sum-to-zero penalty on batch rewards consistently improves robustness across model families, sizes, and all four generalization scenarios makes BSR a low-cost drop-in for any BT-based RM pipeline. The 40% reduction in generation length alongside a 7% AlpacaEval win-rate gain is practically significant for deployment, as it directly counters the verbosity bias that plagues LLM-as-a-judge evaluations. Open questions include whether the norm-dispersion diagnosis and BSR transfer to non-BT reward formulations (e.g., SimPO, DPO implicit rewards), whether the optimal λ is stable across dataset scales, and whether the effective-rank diagnostic can be used as an early stopping criterion during RM training.
