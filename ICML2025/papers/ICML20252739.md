## Problem

Experimental recordings show that hippocampal CA1 neurons conjunctively encode both spatial position and accumulated task evidence during the accumulating tower task, yet the decision itself depends only on evidence, not position. Existing RL models optimize task performance but do not explain which specific architecture of grid-place cell interactions in the entorhinal-hippocampal circuit is necessary to produce this conjunctive coding, leaving the mechanistic role of the hippocampus in spatially embedded decision-making unresolved.

## Input / Output

The agent perceives a limited 5-position field of view showing binary tower presence on the left and right sides of a virtual T-maze corridor, which is projected through the dorsal visual stream to a lateral entorhinal cortex (LEC) sensory vector. The model outputs a three-way action probability (left, right, forward) from a cortical RNN policy, and internally generates hippocampal place field firing rates and grid cell state vectors that can be directly compared to experimental recordings in E×Y (evidence × position) space. The sensory input format supports ablation studies (e.g., simulating optogenetic inhibition of one hemisphere) by treating left and right fields as independently weighted channels.

## Method

The paper proposes Vector-HaSH+, a multi-region brain model that couples a prestructured entorhinal-hippocampal circuit (inheriting the bipartite grid-cell-to-place-cell scaffold from Chandra et al., 2025) with a cortical RNN policy trained via REINFORCE. The key architectural degree of freedom is how grid cells in the medial entorhinal cortex encode task variables: in the joint integration model, each grid module's 2D toroidal attractor space uses one axis for position velocity and the other for evidence velocity, producing a wiggling activation pattern; in the disjoint model, separate modules encode position and evidence independently. The hippocampal readout is a ReLU-gated linear combination of grid cell projections (fixed random weights) and non-grid LEC sensory projections (learned heteroassociative weights via pseudo-inverse updates). The paper counterfactually tests five model variants (M1–M5) that systematically vary whether grid cells encode evidence, whether sensory input projects to the hippocampus, and whether grid coding is joint or disjoint, then evaluates each variant on learning efficiency, exploration speed, and qualitative alignment with experimental place field observations (conjunctive E×Y tuning, choice-specific sequences, evidence-space firing fields). The winning architecture (M5) requires both jointly tuned grid cells and non-grid LEC sensory input to the hippocampus to simultaneously achieve efficient RL learning and reproduce the conjunctive hippocampal representations observed by Nieh et al. (2021).

## Results

On the accumulating tower task, the proposed model M5 (joint grid-cell encoding of position and evidence, with LEC sensory input to HPC) achieves 99.00% success rate and 27.65 steps/episode at its optimal learning rate (1e-4). Models with jointly tuned grid cells (M3: 97.00%, M5: 99.00%) dramatically outperform those where grid cells encode position only (M1: 51.67%, M2: 51.67%, i.e., chance level for a binary decision). RNN-only baselines with matched parameter counts (M0: 68.67%, M0+: 81.67%) still underperform M5 by 17–30 percentage points in success rate, confirming that the structured entorhinal-hippocampal scaffold is essential for efficient learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baselines are RNN-only agents (M0, M0+) with matched parameter counts (~26,755 trainable weights). Even when M0+ receives explicit position and evidence velocity inputs, it reaches only 81.67% success versus M5's 99.00%, a 17.3-point gap that persists across all tested learning rates (Appendix H). Against the counterfactual M3 (joint grid encoding but no LEC sensory projection to HPC), M5 achieves comparable success (99.00% vs. 97.00%) but with more variable learning; however, M5 navigates the maze faster in later training, reflecting the benefit of sensory context for exploration. The critical comparison is M5 (joint) vs. M4 (disjoint grid encoding): both reach near-ceiling success at LR 1e-4, but M5 learns more efficiently in the main-text protocol (Fig. 3A) and uniquely reproduces the experimentally observed conjunctive E×Y place fields and choice-specific sequences from Nieh et al. (2021), which M4 fails to generate. Models M1 and M2, where grid cells encode position only, are stuck at chance (~50%), demonstrating that evidence must be integrated into the grid cell attractor dynamics for the task to be solvable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Vector-HaSH (Chandra et al., 2025), a biologically grounded heteroassociative memory scaffold built on continuous attractor grid-cell dynamics (Burak & Fiete, 2009), by coupling it with a cortical RNN policy trained via REINFORCE to solve the accumulating tower task. It departs from prior deep RL approaches to this task (Mochizuki-Freeman et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024) that optimize performance without structured neural architecture, and from generative models like the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (Whittington et al., 2020) that lack mechanistic interpretability. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of counterfactual model variants (M0–M5) that systematically test whether grid cells encode position alone, position jointly with evidence, or position and evidence in disjoint modules, directly targeting the conjunctive hippocampal representations observed by Nieh et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper generates a specific, falsifiable prediction—that grid cells in the medial entorhinal cortex must conjunctively encode both self-position and accumulated evidence for efficient spatially embedded decision-making—which directly motivates new neurophysiological experiments and challenges the prevailing view that grid cells are primarily spatial. It also demonstrates that structured, content-addressable memory circuits (grid-cell scaffolds) confer learning efficiency advantages over unstructured RNN baselines, offering a concrete inductive bias for brain-inspired RL. Open questions include whether the joint-versus-disjoint distinction holds in biological grid cells, whether CA3 recurrence or additional cortical inputs (MEC, LEC directly to cortex) alter the conclusions, and whether the framework generalizes beyond the 1D accumulating tower task to richer spatial or abstract cognitive domains.
