## Problem

Tensor completion (TC) via low-rank decomposition requires solving least-squares subproblems at each ALS step, but masking the design matrix to only observed entries destroys the Kronecker or Khatri-Rao product structure that enables fast tensor decomposition (TD) solvers. This structural loss makes it unclear how to extend sublinear-time TD algorithms to the TC setting, leaving a gap between the rich theory of structured regression and the practical TC problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed tensor X ∈ R^{I₁×⋯×I_N} with an observation mask Ω ⊆ [I₁]×⋯×[I_N] (a random p-fraction of entries revealed) and produces a low-rank decomposition (CP, Tucker, or TT) whose factors implicitly define the completed tensor. Crucially, the running time is sublinear in the full tensor size I = ∏I_n because leverage score sampling only touches Õ(R) rows of the structured design matrix, and the lifted response vector b^(k) is computed lazily—only entries corresponding to sampled rows are ever materialized.

## Method

The paper introduces a lifting procedure called mini-ALS that restores the algebraic structure lost in TC: unobserved entries are promoted to free variables, converting the unstructured regression min_x ‖A_Ω x − b_Ω‖₂ into the structured problem min_{x, b_Ω̄} ‖Ax − b‖₂ with the same optimal x. The key theoretical result (Lemma 3.5) shows that alternating minimization between x and b_Ω̄ in this lifted problem exactly simulates a preconditioned Richardson iteration with preconditioner A^T A for the original TC regression. The main convergence theorem (Theorem 3.7) then proves that the structured solve in each mini-ALS step can be performed approximately (via leverage score sampling) with per-step error Ûε < 1/β², yielding a final solution with controllable relative error in O(β/(1−√Ûε·β) · T·log β/ε) time. This allows existing fast TD ALS algorithms for CP (Khatri-Rao), Tucker (Kronecker), and TT decompositions to be plugged in as blackbox subroutines, yielding the first sublinear-time TC algorithms for all three decomposition types.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that approximate-mini-ALS achieves up to 100x speedup over direct ALS methods for CP completion on real-world tensors (CARDIAC-MRI: 256×256×14×20, HYPERSPECTRAL: 1024×1344×33) while maintaining comparable reconstruction error (RRE). A key finding is that mini-ALS running time is sublinear in the number of observed entries |Ω| (due to leverage score sampling), and interestingly *decreases* as the observation ratio p increases for ε < 0.1, because the lifted structured matrix AᵀA becomes a better preconditioner (β→1 as p→1). The accelerated variant with adaptive step sizes further reduces iterations by extrapolating the trajectory of iterates via a geometric series.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PARAFAC (Tomasi & Bro, 2005), which the paper identifies as equivalent to running exactly one mini-ALS iteration per ALS step. PARAFAC is faster per ALS round but converges at a slower rate, requiring more ALS rounds to reach comparable RRE. The 'direct' baseline (solving the normal equation (PᵀP)⁻¹Pᵀq in each step) has running time linear in |Ω|, while mini-ALS and accelerated-mini-ALS are sublinear in |Ω|. Mini-ALS matches direct method solution quality as ε→0 (recovering the exact Richardson iteration), and accelerated-mini-ALS achieves better solution quality than mini-ALS for a fixed ε while always running faster, especially at low observation ratios. In the coupled matrix warm-up experiment, approximate-mini-ALS (using leverage score sampling with 1% row sampling) achieves comparable MSE to direct and mini-ALS while being substantially faster, and can even achieve lower MSE than the other methods around step 15 of ALS due to its stochastic convergence path.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the lifting trick from experimental design (Healy & Westmacott, 1956) and fast leverage-score-sampling-based tensor decomposition algorithms (Fahrbach et al., 2022; Bharadwaj et al., 2023, 2024). The key conceptual departure is proving that alternating minimization on the lifted problem is exactly a preconditioned Richardson iteration (Lemma 3.5), which had not been previously established. This positions the work as a principled generalization of Tomasi & Bro's (2005) PARAFAC-with-missing-values algorithm, which corresponds to running exactly one mini-ALS iteration per ALS step, whereas the present method runs mini-ALS to convergence and inherits Richardson iteration convergence guarantees. The approximate version (Theorem 3.7) is the main theoretical novelty, showing how much error in the structured sub-solve is tolerable while preserving convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by reducing TC to a sequence of structured TD sub-problems solvable in sublinear time via leverage score sampling, the method achieves ~100× speedups over direct ALS on real-world tensors (CARDIAC-MRI, HYPERSPECTRAL) while matching reconstruction quality. The framework is modular—any future fast TD algorithm can be plugged in as a blackbox subroutine—making it a durable contribution to the TC algorithmic toolkit. Open questions include formal analysis of the accelerated (adaptive step-size) variant, extension to regularized TC objectives, and whether the incoherence/β bounds are tight in practice for highly structured tensors. A notable limitation is that the speedup depends on the observation rate p being large enough for β to remain small; for very sparse observations the Richardson iteration may require many more steps.
