## Problem

Causal language models with discrete audio tokens (e.g., AudioGen) lag significantly behind state-of-the-art diffusion models for text-to-audio generation, despite the LLM framework's advantages in streaming, scalability, and multi-modal integration. The paper asks whether a decoder-only causal LM can achieve diffusion-level audio fidelity by addressing two bottlenecks: the information loss from discrete tokenization and the limited context utilization of standard next-token prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252830/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt, encoded into 78 conditioning tokens via concatenated CLAP and FLAN-T5 embeddings, and generates a 10-second 16 kHz audio waveform. Internally, audio is represented as 256 continuous-valued tokens of dimension 128, obtained by encoding a 64-band Mel-spectrogram through a pre-trained VAE (from AudioLDM) with 4× spatial compression and 4×4 patchification. At inference, the sampled continuous tokens are decoded back to a Mel-spectrogram by the VAE decoder and synthesized into a waveform by a pre-trained Hifi-GAN vocoder.

## Method

The paper proposes two key changes to the standard causal LM pipeline. First, it replaces discrete audio tokens with continuous-valued VAE latents and swaps the cross-entropy loss for a token-wise diffusion loss implemented by a small MLP diffusion head, while keeping the Transformer decoder backbone unchanged. Second, it introduces Masked Next-Token Prediction (MNTP), a novel training task where tokens are randomly dropped to form a shorter sequence and the model learns to predict the next visible token on this sparse sequence, augmented with a target positional embedding that indicates which future position to predict. This makes MNTP a generalized form of causal LM where the model learns to predict any future token given any subset of past tokens, effectively injecting the sparse-context learning benefit of masked language modeling into a strictly causal architecture. The 193M Base and 462M Large models are initialized from image MAR pre-trained weights and trained on only AudioCaps and WavCaps, achieving SOTA-level FAD and FD scores that rival much larger bidirectional diffusion models while remaining streamable and LLM-compatible.

## Results

On the AudioCaps evaluation set, AudioMNTP Base (193M) achieves FAD 1.68, KL 1.16, IS 9.67, and CLAP 0.336, outperforming the 285M AudioGen Base (FAD 2.84, KL 2.14) by 20% and 40% relative improvements on FAD and KL respectively. Scaling to AudioMNTP Large (462M) yields FAD 1.22 and KL 1.17, achieving a 33% relative FAD improvement over the 1B AudioGen Large (FAD 1.82) and matching or surpassing SOTA diffusion models like AudioLDM2-AC-Large (FAD 1.42) and Tango 2 (FAD 2.69) on FAD while remaining competitive on KL and CLAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discrete-token causal LM baselines, AudioMNTP Large (462M) achieves a 33% relative FAD improvement over AudioGen Large (1B, FAD 1.82→1.22) and a 41% improvement over AudioGen Base (285M, FAD 2.84→1.68 for Base), while using far fewer parameters and training data (only AC+WC vs. AS+AC+8+ datasets). Against the SOTA bidirectional diffusion models, AudioMNTP Large achieves the best FAD (1.22) among all models in Table 1, surpassing AudioLDM2-AC-Large (1.42) and Tango 2 (2.69), though Tango 2 retains a lead on CLAP (0.375 vs. 0.341) due to its preference-optimization alignment. In human evaluation (Table 2), AudioMNTP significantly outperforms AudioGen on both REL and OVL in speech and non-speech categories, and approaches Tango 2 on non-speech REL (3.79 vs. 3.95) while achieving the highest OVL in the speech category (3.69 vs. 3.15 for Tango 2). The paper also demonstrates that under causal (left-to-right) decoding, AudioMNTP outperforms both AudioNTP and the causally-decoded AudioMAR on most metrics, confirming that MNTP's training objective specifically benefits streaming inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: causal audio language modeling (AudioGen, UniAudio) which uses discrete tokens and cross-entropy loss, and latent diffusion models (AudioLDM 2, Tango 2) which model continuous-valued tokens but rely on bidirectional architectures. Drawing on MAR (Li et al., 2024b) for continuous-valued tokens with token-wise diffusion loss, the authors transplant this into a standard Transformer decoder, and further introduce masked next-token prediction (MNTP) as a novel training task that imports the sparse-context learning principle of MLM into a strictly causal framework. The key departure from MAR is the use of a unidirectional decoder with classic next-token prediction rather than iterative bidirectional masked generation, making the approach directly compatible with LLM infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that audio generation becomes a native capability of causal LLMs without external vocoders, enabling seamless integration into multi-modal LLMs and real-time streaming applications. The MNTP task is a clean, generalizable training objective that could transfer to other continuous-token modalities (speech, music, video). However, the evidence is limited to AudioCaps evaluation with small training data (AudioCaps + WavCaps only), and the models (193M/462M) are far smaller than the diffusion baselines they match, leaving open whether the approach scales to billions of parameters and larger corpora like AudioSet. Speech generation quality, while improved, still lags behind reference audio, and the paper acknowledges that further scaling is needed to fully close the gap with SOTA diffusion systems.
