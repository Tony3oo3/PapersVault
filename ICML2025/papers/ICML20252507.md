## Problem

High-resolution image perception in MLLMs is bottlenecked by the need to downsample images to fixed encoder resolutions, causing loss of fine-grained detail. Existing remedies—cropping, HR visual encoders, and hierarchical tree search—either still require downsampling or suffer from inefficient top-down search that fails on small objects. The paper asks whether retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), a proven long-context technique for text LLMs, can be directly adapted to enhance MLLM perception of HR images without any training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary high-resolution RGB image (up to 8K, e.g., 7680×4320) and a natural-language text query. The image is partitioned into fixed-size crops (e.g., 448×448) matching the retriever's encoder resolution, and only query-relevant crops are retained to synthesize a new, lower-resolution image that replaces the original as input to the MLLM. The output is a standard text answer; the framework is training-free and model-agnostic, plugging into any MLLM that accepts a single image and text prompt.

## Method

RAP is a training-free, two-component framework. First, VisRAG computes cosine similarity between the query embedding and each image-crop embedding to retrieve the top-K relevant crops. Second, a Spatial-Awareness Layout reconstructs these crops into a new image by compressing a binary selection matrix (removing all-zero rows and columns) so that the relative spatial positions of retained crops are preserved—critical for cross-instance spatial reasoning. To adaptively choose K, RE-Search performs an A*-guided search over a tree where each node corresponds to a different retention ratio (25%, 50%, 75% of crops); the cost function blends retrieval similarity (g) with the MLLM's self-reported confidence that the current image suffices to answer the query (h), with a depth-dependent weight that down-weights confidence at shallow levels where the model cannot yet perceive the full image. Search terminates when confidence exceeds a threshold (τ=0.6), yielding a task-adaptive crop count without requiring task-type labels.

## Results

On V*Bench, RAP applied to LLaVA-v1.5-7B achieves 91.1% overall accuracy (up from 48.7% baseline), a 42.4-point gain, and LLaVA-v1.5-13B reaches 89.8% (from 47.1%), a 42.7-point gain. On HR-Bench 8K, RAP boosts LLaVA-v1.5-13B from 37.8% to 59.3% overall accuracy (+21.5 points) and LLaVA-ov-0.5B from 42.8% to 63.5% (+20.7 points). On HR-Bench 4K, LLaVA-v1.5-13B w/ RAP reaches 60.1% overall, surpassing GPT-4o (59.0%) and QWen-VL-max (58.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP on LLaVA-v1.5-7B achieves 91.1% on V*Bench, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (66.0%), LLaVA-v1.6-7B (61.8%), and LLaVA-HR-X-7B (56.5%). On HR-Bench 4K, RAP on LLaVA-v1.5-13B (60.1% overall) surpasses GPT-4o (59.0%) and QWen-VL-max (58.5%), while RAP on LLaVA-ov-0.5B (61.3%) edges past InternVL-1.5-26B (60.6%). On HR-Bench 8K, RAP on LLaVA-v1.5-13B (59.3%) beats GPT-4o (55.5%) and QWen-VL-max (52.5%), and RAP on LLaVA-ov-0.5B (63.5%) exceeds InternVL-1.5-26B (57.9%). Against search-based methods, RAP outperforms Zoom Eye by 6.0–6.8 points and DC² by 18.9–18.8 points on HR-Bench 8K (LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B), while also achieving higher throughput (4.2 vs. 3.3 vs. 2.1 samples/min). RAP does not surpass InternVL-1.5-26B on HR-Bench 4K FSP (73.8 vs. 69.5 for LLaVA-v1.5-7B w/ RAP, but 74.3 vs. 69.5 for LLaVA-v1.5-13B w/ RAP), and FCP scores remain relatively modest across all models, indicating cross-instance spatial reasoning is still a bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAP reframes high-resolution image perception as a long-context retrieval problem, directly borrowing the RAG paradigm from text LLMs (via VisRAG for crop-query similarity) rather than following the three established HR-perception lineages: cropping-based methods (LLaVA-v1.6, LLaVA-ov), HR visual encoders (LLaVA-HR, ConvLLaVA), and hierarchical top-down search (DC², Zoom Eye, SEAL). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the sequential, layer-by-layer search of prior methods with a flat retrieval step plus a spatial-awareness layout that preserves relative crop positions, and an A*-inspired adaptive search (RE-Search) over crop-count rather than image hierarchy. The Spatial-Awareness Layout and RE-Search are novel algorithmic contributions, but the underlying retrieval mechanism is inherited wholesale from VisRAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, model-agnostic framework that plugs into any MLLM with a ViT-based encoder, RAP offers a low-cost path to substantially better HR perception without retraining, which is practically significant given the rapid proliferation of new MLLM architectures. The empirical finding that preserving relative spatial positions among retrieved crops (rather than ordering by retrieval score) is critical for cross-instance tasks, and that the optimal number of crops is task-dependent, provides actionable design principles for future HR-perception systems. Open questions include the reliance on an external retriever (VisRAG or SigLIP) as a quality bottleneck, whether internal model representations could replace explicit retrieval, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D perception where spatial layout becomes more complex.
