## Problem

Existing speech-text multimodal LLMs fine-tune the LLM backbone when aligning speech modalities, which causes catastrophic forgetting given the limited scale of available spoken Q&A data (far below the million-hour level needed to match text pretraining). This results in a measurable intelligence gap between spoken and text question answering. Freeze-Omni addresses this by achieving effective speech-text alignment and low-latency duplex dialogue while keeping the LLM parameters completely frozen throughout training.

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming speech as mel-filter bank features (25 ms window, 10 ms shift) processed in variable-size chunks by a streaming encoder that outputs 12.5 Hz frame-rate representations. Output is streaming 24 kHz PCM speech generated from 40 Hz single-codebook speech tokens (codebook size 1024) decoded by a TiCodec-based codec. The duplex design uses an acoustic VAD to trigger chunk-wise ingestion, and an LLM-side classification head predicts three states (continue listening, interrupt-and-regenerate, end-of-speech) on the last frame of each encoder chunk to manage turn-taking.

## Method

Freeze-Omni decouples speech understanding and generation into two independently trained 3-stage pipelines, both with the LLM (Qwen2-7B-Instruct) frozen. For speech input: a CTC-trained streaming encoder is connected to the LLM via a down-sampling adapter (stage 2), then a shared prompt embedding is trained on a small multi-round Q&A set (stage 3) to enable speech-to-text generation. For speech output: a single-codebook codec is trained on speech alone (stage 1), a NAR+AR transformer decoder is trained on text-speech pairs using frozen LLM embedding vectors as semantic input (stage 2), and a novel NAR prefix decoder is fine-tuned on LLM hidden-state kv-caches to align the speech decoder with the LLM's actual output distribution (stage 3). Duplex capability is added by a lightweight classification layer on the LLM's last-layer hidden states, trained jointly with the text-generation loss in stage 3 to predict chunk-level dialogue states. The key architectural insight is that only the encoder, adapter, prompt embeddings, and speech decoder (~470 M parameters total) are trainable, preserving the backbone LLM's full text intelligence while enabling end-to-end speech-to-speech dialogue with ~745 ms statistical latency.

## Results

On spoken question answering, Freeze-Omni (7B) achieves 44.73% on Web Questions, 72% on LlaMA-Questions, and 53.88% on Audio Trivia QA, with only a small gap to its text-only backbone Qwen2-7B-Instruct (45.13%, 77.67%, 63.93%). For speech input understanding, Freeze-Omni achieves 2.15% CER on AISHELL-1 and 3.24% WER on LibriSpeech test-clean, comparable to VITA-1.5. The end-to-end statistical latency averages 745 ms (median 753 ms, 90th percentile 1020 ms), yielding roughly 1.2 s total latency including network overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Freeze-Omni substantially outperforms all prior speech-to-speech dialogue models on spoken question answering: it beats GLM-4-Voice (9B) by 12.5 points on Web Questions (44.73 vs. 32.2), 7.3 points on LlaMA-Questions (72 vs. 64.7), and 14.8 points on Audio Trivia QA (53.88 vs. 39.1), despite using far less speech training data. Against Moshi (7B), the gap is even larger—18.1 points on Web Questions and 31.1 points on Audio Trivia QA. Compared to its own text-only backbone Qwen2-7B-Instruct, Freeze-Omni loses only 0.4 points on Web Questions, 5.7 on LlaMA-Questions, and 10.1 on Audio Trivia QA, confirming near-parity intelligence across modalities. On ASR (speech input), Freeze-Omni matches or slightly edges VITA-1.5 (2.15 vs. 2.16 CER on AISHELL-1; 3.24 vs. 3.4 WER on LibriSpeech test-clean) and clearly surpasses Mini-Omni2 (3.24 vs. 4.7 WER on test-clean) and wav2vec 2.0-base (3.24 vs. 6.0 WER on dev-clean). The key takeaway is that freezing the LLM preserves its text-modality intelligence almost entirely while enabling competitive speech understanding and generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Freeze-Omni sits within the rapidly growing line of speech-to-speech dialogue models (Moshi, GLM-4-Voice, Mini-Omni2, LLaMA-Omni) that connect speech encoders and decoders to a backbone LLM. Its key conceptual departure is that all prior works fine-tune at least some LLM parameters during speech-text alignment, whereas Freeze-Omni keeps the LLM entirely frozen, relying instead on a chunk-wise streaming speech encoder with prompt-embedding training and a prefix kv-cache fine-tuning strategy for the speech decoder. The speech decoder architecture draws directly from VALL-E's NAR+AR token generation paradigm and TiCodec's single-codebook design, while the streaming encoder follows standard CTC-based ASR training with dynamic chunking from WENET.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-LLM approach offers a practical path to adding speech capabilities to any existing LLM without risking catastrophic forgetting of its text intelligence, which is especially valuable when large-scale spoken Q&A data is unavailable. The demonstrated near-parity between speech-modality and text-modality Q&A accuracy (e.g., 44.73 vs. 45.13 on Web Questions) supports the claim that modality alignment need not degrade reasoning. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 7B backbone, a single speaker, and 60K Q&A pairs, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, multi-speaker settings, or more complex dialogue scenarios. Future work on multi-voice support, emotion understanding, and richer instruction-following in the speech decoder would strengthen the practical applicability.
