## Problem

MLLMs exhibit a substantial performance gap on Multimodal Sentiment Analysis (MSA) under the zero-shot paradigm, raising doubt about whether they can perceive sentiment as competently as supervised models. While In-Context Learning (ICL) offers a promising few-shot alternative, the configuration of ICL demonstrations—specifically their retrieval, presentation, and distribution—has not been systematically investigated for the multimodal and affective nature of MSA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image-text post (optionally with an aspect term for aspect-level MSA) and a sequence of n retrieved demonstration pairs, all templatized into a single multimodal ICL sequence prefixed by a task-descriptive prompt. The MLLM (IDEFICS-9B or Open-Flamingo2-9B) generates a single sentiment polarity token (Positive, Neutral, or Negative) as the next-token prediction. Demonstrations are drawn from a support set comprising 1% of the training set (or the full training set for extended experiments), and the annotated data requirement per test sample remains constant regardless of support set size.

## Method

The paper conducts an empirical study optimizing three factors for ICL demonstration configuration in MSA. For retrieval, it devises weighted similarity strategies (WIT for post-level with α:β=2:8 prioritizing text; WITA for aspect-level with α:β=7:3 and (α+β):γ=2:8 prioritizing aspect similarity) that outperform unweighted aggregation by properly balancing modality contributions. For presentation, it shows that original image+text inputs are optimal, as auxiliary modalities (MLLM-generated captions, diffusion-model-generated images) impair the 'Task Learning' effect by increasing input complexity without net information gain. For distribution, it discovers an inherent MLLM predictive bias favoring positive/neutral over negative predictions, and demonstrates that the Category Balanced protocol (equal demonstrations per sentiment class) effectively counteracts this bias on datasets where negative samples are underrepresented, while the Unlimited protocol (no distribution constraint) is preferable otherwise. The three optimized strategies are combined per-dataset, yielding average accuracy improvements of 15.9% over zero-shot and 11.2% over random ICL across six MSA datasets.

## Results

On six MSA datasets (MVSA-S, MVSA-M, TumEmo, Twitter-15, Twitter-17, MASAD), the proposed ICL configuration achieves a mean accuracy of 69.1% with IDEFICS-9B (1% training set as support), improving 15.9% over the zero-shot paradigm (55.0%) and 11.2% over random ICL (59.7%). With the full training set as support, the mean rises to 70.9%, surpassing SOTA few-shot models (68.3%) while remaining below SOTA fully-supervised models (78.8%). The same strategy generalizes to Open-Flamingo2-9B, yielding 67.4% mean accuracy (1% support) and 68.6% (full support).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ICL configuration (WIT/WITA retrieval + original image-text presentation + dataset-specific distribution protocol) consistently outperforms all three ICL baselines across all six datasets for both IDEFICS and Open-Flamingo. Against RICES (Yang et al., 2022), which retrieves by unweighted image-text similarity, the method gains +4.2% mean accuracy (69.1 vs. 64.9 for IDEFICS, 1% support), with the largest margin on MVSA-S (+8.6%) and MASAD (+5.5%). Against SQPA (Li et al., 2024), which assigns pseudo-labels to support samples, the improvement is +9.0% mean (69.1 vs. 60.1), as SQPA degrades notably on aspect-level datasets (Twitter-17: 50.4 vs. 62.0). Against MMICES (Chen et al., 2024), which retrieves images first then texts, the gain is +5.2% mean (69.1 vs. 63.9), with the advantage concentrated on post-level datasets (MVSA-S: +7.3%, TumEmo: +2.9%). The method also surpasses SOTA few-shot models (68.3% mean) when using the full training set as support (70.9%), while still trailing SOTA fully-supervised models (78.8%) by roughly 8–9 points, indicating that fine-tuning remains irreplaceable for peak performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL configuration literature from unimodal NLP and general multimodal tasks (image captioning, VQA) to the specific domain of Multimodal Sentiment Analysis. It builds directly on the RICES retrieval strategy (Yang et al., 2022) and the broader ICL framework established by GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020) and adapted to MLLMs via Flamingo-style architectures (IDEFICS, Open-Flamingo). The key departure is identifying that MSA-specific factors—aspect-term similarity, modality weighting, and sentiment distribution bias—require tailored optimization strategies that generic ICL configurations do not capture, and that modality conversion techniques (captioning, text-to-image) that help in other multimodal tasks actually degrade ICL performance here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical evidence that MLLMs can perceive sentiment competitively with supervised models when ICL demonstrations are properly configured, closing a perceived capability gap. The discovery of an inherent predictive bias (favoring positive/neutral over negative) in MLLMs is a useful diagnostic for the field, though the paper acknowledges it likely stems from pre-training data curation rather than architecture. Key limitations include evaluation only on 9B-scale models and the persistent gap versus fully-supervised models, leaving open whether findings transfer to larger MLLMs or whether the bias can be addressed at the training level. The practical takeaway—use weighted similarity with modality-specific priorities, keep inputs to original image+text, and choose distribution protocol based on dataset class balance—gives practitioners a concrete recipe.
