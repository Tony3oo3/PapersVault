## Problem

Convex Markov games (cMGs) extend Markov decision processes to multi-agent settings with convex utilities over occupancy measures, enabling rich strategic interactions, yet no algorithmic solution with provable convergence to Nash equilibria existed. The fundamental challenges include the absence of Bellman consistency (ruling out dynamic programming), inherent nonconvexity of best-response mappings, and the complexity of infinite-horizon optimization. The paper asks: do independent policy gradient methods converge in two-player zero-sum cMGs?

## Input / Output

Each player independently receives the cMG game parameters (finite state space S, action spaces A and B, transition kernel P, continuous utility functions F₁ and F₂ over occupancy measures, discount factor γ, and initial state distribution ρ) along with their own policy parameterization. Players use ε-greedy stochastic trajectory sampling to compute inexact gradient estimates without exchanging private policy information, with the regularization coefficient μ=O(ε) controlling the systematic bias in the minimizing player's gradient. The output is a pair of stationary Markovian policy profiles (x*, y*) constituting an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium, with sample and iteration complexity polynomial in 1/ε, |S|, |A|+|B|, and 1/(1−γ).

## Method

The approach proceeds in two steps. First, a simple nonconvex regularization (subtracting μ/2·‖λ₂(x,y)‖² from the utility) exploits the hidden-convexity structure of cMG utilities—where each player's payoff is concave in the state-action occupancy measure—to transform the min-max problem into a nonconvex-proximal Polyak-Lojasiewicz (NC-pPL) objective, ensuring the best-response mapping becomes Lipschitz continuous in the opponent's policy. Second, the paper establishes the first global convergence guarantees for stochastic nested (Nest-PG) and alternating (Alt-PGDA) gradient descent-ascent methods under NC-pPL and two-sided pPL conditions over constrained domains, with careful timescale separation of step-sizes. The algorithms use independent policy gradient estimators derived via the chain rule through occupancy measures, with ε-greedy exploration to bound variance, and tolerate inexact (biased) gradients arising from the regularization term that couples both players' policies.

## Results

The paper provides the first provable convergence guarantees for policy gradient methods in two-player zero-sum convex Markov games. For the general (hidden-convex) case, Nest-PG (Algorithm 1) requires O(1/ε^{11/2}) iterations while Alt-PGDA (Algorithm 2) requires O(1/ε^6) iterations to reach an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium. Under the stronger hidden-strongly-concave (HSC) assumption, both algorithms achieve logarithmic convergence: O(log(1/ε)) iterations. Numerical experiments on an iterated rock-paper-scissors-dummy game confirm that exploitability decays toward a small positive value determined by the regularization coefficient μ, with more inner-loop iterations yielding more stable convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to provide any provable convergence guarantees for policy gradient methods in zero-sum convex Markov games, so there are no direct algorithmic baselines to compare against. The prior state of the art offered no convergence guarantees at all for this problem class, as the absence of Bellman consistency and inherent nonconvexity blocked all standard dynamic-programming-based approaches. The numerical experiments (Figure 1) compare the effect of regularization strength μ and inner-loop length T_in on exploitability decay, showing a clear trade-off: larger μ accelerates convergence but increases the residual exploitability floor, while more inner-loop iterations (T_in = 100 vs. 10) produce smoother and more stable exploitability decrease. The Alt-PGDA single-loop method is highlighted as particularly practical, requiring only three simple ingredients (regularization, alternating updates, and step-size separation) without the nested-loop overhead of Nest-PG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first algorithmic solution for computing Nash equilibria in zero-sum convex Markov games (cMGs), a framework recently defined by Gemp et al. (2024) who proved equilibrium existence but offered no computation method. It builds on the hidden-convexity analysis of Fatkhullin et al. (2023) and the hidden-convex-hidden-concave game convergence results of Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis et al. (2021), extending both from single-objective or unconstrained settings to constrained multi-agent min-max problems. The optimization core departs from Nouiehed et al. (2019), who established convergence for two-sided PL min-max games only in the unconstrained case, by providing the first guarantees for stochastic nested and alternating GDA under NC-pPL and two-sided pPL conditions over constrained domains. The policy gradient estimator adapts the general-utility RL framework of Zhang et al. (2020) to the multi-agent cMG setting where Bellman consistency fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant because cMGs subsume both convex MDPs and conventional Markov games, and the paper demonstrates that the standard value-iteration subroutine at the heart of all prior MG equilibrium algorithms is fundamentally inapplicable here—necessitating entirely new algorithmic tools. The simple regularization-plus-alternating-GDA recipe is practical and the NC-pPL/pPL-pPL optimization results are of independent interest beyond cMGs. Key open questions include extending to multi-player cMGs, general-sum settings, equilibrium selection, and whether the approach can handle the richer trajectory-level preferences that finite-trial cMGs support. The empirical validation is limited to a single toy game (iterated rock-paper-scissors-dummy), leaving the practical scalability of the method to larger cMG instances untested.
