## Problem

Existing dynamic (non-uniform) LLM compression methods assign per-layer importance scores and greedily allocate compression, implicitly assuming that per-layer errors sum linearly to total model error. The paper demonstrates this monotonicity assumption fails in practice—pruning more blocks can yield lower perplexity than pruning fewer—meaning scoring-based approaches can select strictly sub-optimal compression profiles, especially at higher compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM, a global compression constraint (e.g., target model size or average sparsity), and a calibration dataset (Fineweb-Edu, 8M tokens) as input. It first builds a level database by independently compressing each layer/block to every available compression option (e.g., multiple sparsity levels via SparseGPT, or bit-widths via GPTQ). The output is a per-layer compression profile—an n-tuple assigning one compression level to each unit—that satisfies the global constraint while minimizing KL-divergence from the original model's output distribution on calibration data.

## Method

EvoPress formulates dynamic compression as a constrained optimization over the space of per-layer compression assignments and solves it with a (1+λ)-evolutionary algorithm featuring two key modifications. First, a level-switch mutation operator randomly increases one unit's compression level and decreases another's by a matching step size, preserving the global constraint while maintaining high locality between parent and offspring. Second, an aggressive multi-step selection progressively evaluates offspring on increasing token counts (e.g., 512 → 8192 → 65536 tokens) while reducing the number of survivors at each stage, drastically cutting total evaluation cost. The fitness function is KL-divergence between the offspring and original model outputs, and the algorithm is heavily exploitation-focused (elitism, single parent, minimal mutation). The paper proves convergence in O(k(n-k)/λ) generations for linear fitness functions under an ℓ₁ constraint, and empirically validates the approach across depth pruning, unstructured sparsity, and quantization on Llama-2/3, Mistral, and Phi-3 models.

## Results

At 70% unstructured sparsity, EvoPress achieves WikiText-2 PPL of 14.42 / 28.76 / 13.83 and zero-shot task average of 53.8 / 50.8 / 59.8 on Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Phi-3-Medium-14B respectively, outperforming uniform sparsity (PPL 23.08 / 85.84 / 16.66) and OWL (PPL 17.22 / 48.07 / 15.66) by large margins. For 3-bit quantization on Llama-3-8B, EvoPress reaches WikiText-2 PPL 7.49 and task average 64.3, versus 12.19 / 60.2 for uniform GPTQ and 29.00 / 61.3 for the DP (SPDY-style) baseline. For depth pruning on Mistral-7B, EvoPress maintains functional models even at 50% sparsity (PPL ~48) while all baselines collapse entirely beyond 31.25% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoPress consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every compression type and model tested. Against OWL (the strongest prior method for non-uniform sparsity), EvoPress reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 48.07 to 28.76 on Llama-3-8B at 70% sparsity and improves zero-shot task average from 48.4 to 50.8. Against the DP/SPDY-style quantization baseline, EvoPress achieves PPL 7.49 vs. 29.00 on Llama-3-8B at 3 bits, a 3.9× improvement, while also beating uniform GPTQ (12.19). For depth pruning, EvoPress dominates all four scoring-based baselines (Shortened Llama, ShortGPT, Weight Subcloning, and Gromov's sliding-window cosine similarity): at 37.5% sparsity on Llama-2-7B, EvoPress achieves PPL 17.98 while ShortGPT and Weight Subcloning reach 70.94 and Cosine Similarity (Window) reaches 192.07. The iterative search methods SLEB and BlockPruner are also outperformed (Appendix G). Notably, EvoPress is the only method that produces functional submodels at 50% depth sparsity, where all baselines collapse to PPL >145.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in dynamic LLM compression, where prior methods (SPDY, OWL, ShortGPT, Shortened Llama, Weight Subcloning) assign per-layer importance scores and solve a knapsack or greedy allocation problem under an implicit error-monotonicity assumption. EvoPress instead formulates non-uniform compression as a general black-box optimization over compression profiles and applies a (1+λ)-evolutionary algorithm with level-switch mutation and multi-step selection, drawing on stochastic drift analysis from evolutionary computation theory for its convergence guarantee. It is the first framework to unify depth pruning, unstructured sparsity, and quantization under a single search procedure, whereas prior work treated each compression type independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of non-monotonicity in compression error (pruning more blocks can improve perplexity) challenges a foundational assumption underlying most prior dynamic compression methods, suggesting their solutions are systematically suboptimal. EvoPress's convergence guarantee and practical efficiency (hours on a single GPU) make it a viable drop-in replacement for scoring-based allocation in production LLM compression pipelines. Open questions include combining heterogeneous compression types (e.g., sparsity + quantization) in a single search space, extending to finer-grained structured pruning, and whether the smooth-fitness-landscape property that enables fast convergence persists under more aggressive or multi-objective compression settings.
