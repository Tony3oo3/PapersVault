## Problem

In RLHF and DPO pipelines, the gradient of the MLE-based reward modeling loss is misaligned with the gradient of the true oracle objective (maximizing human values under KL regularization), because the standard uniform sampling of response pairs does not correct for the nonlinear sigmoid mismatch between the two objectives. This misalignment is especially costly in iterative and online RLHF settings where preference data is collected on-the-fly and annotation budgets are limited.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt x, the current policy π_θ, and a fixed reference policy π_ref, PILAF generates a pair of responses (y^a, y^b) that are sent to a preference oracle (human labeler or proxy reward model) for pairwise annotation. The per-token generation rule for the interpolated policies is softmax of (1+β)h_θ − βh_ref (for y^a) and softmax of (1−β)h_θ + βh_ref (for y^b), where h_θ and h_ref are the respective logits and β is the KL regularization coefficient. The annotated pairs then feed into a standard DPO update to produce the next iteration of the aligned policy.

## Method

PILAF (Policy-Interpolated Learning for Aligned Feedback) replaces uniform response sampling with a mixture: with probability 1/2 both responses are drawn from π_θ (vanilla), and with probability 1/2, y^a is drawn from π_θ^+ ∝ π_θ·exp{r_θ} and y^b from π_θ^- ∝ π_θ·exp{−r_θ}, where r_θ is the DPO implicit reward β·log(π_θ/π_ref). The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that under this sampling, the MLE gradient becomes a first-order approximation of the oracle objective gradient, because the sampling density ratio exactly cancels the sigmoid derivative σ′, converting the nonlinear difference σ(Δr⋆)−σ(Δr_θ) into the linear difference Δr⋆−Δr_θ. A complementary statistical result (Theorem 4.3) shows the asymptotic covariance of the parameter estimate is inversely proportional to the Hessian of the oracle objective, concentrating estimation error along directions where the objective is least sensitive. In practice, the normalizing constants Z_θ^± are dropped (replaced by 1), making the algorithm a simple logit interpolation that requires no additional hyperparameters beyond the existing β.

## Results

In the online DPO setting (Table 3), PILAF achieves the best overall objective J of -6.42, outperforming Vanilla (-7.11), Best-of-N (-6.77), Hybrid (-8.96), and VPO (-7.09). PILAF attains the highest reward (-4.88) with a moderate KL divergence (15.42), whereas Vanilla and VPO reach similar reward levels but at substantially higher KL (21.50 and 22.31 respectively). In the iterative DPO setting (Table 2), PILAF achieves J = -12.30 versus Vanilla's -13.68, and reaches baseline endpoint rewards roughly 40% faster in training steps, translating to reduced annotation and computational costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PILAF outperforms all four baselines on the composite objective J in both iterative and online DPO settings. Against Vanilla DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), PILAF achieves a 0.63 improvement in J (-6.42 vs. -7.11) in online DPO while reducing KL from 21.50 to 15.42, indicating a better reward-KL trade-off. Compared to Best-of-N (Xiong et al., 2024), PILAF achieves higher reward (-4.88 vs. -5.54) and better J (-6.42 vs. -6.77) despite a slightly higher KL, and requires only 3 forward passes per pair versus 8. Against Hybrid sampling (Xie et al., 2024), PILAF dominates on all three metrics (J: -6.42 vs. -8.96; Reward: -4.88 vs. -6.42; KL: 15.42 vs. 16.46). Versus VPO (Cen et al., 2024), PILAF achieves comparable reward (-4.88 vs. -4.91) but with substantially lower KL (15.42 vs. 22.31), yielding a 0.67 improvement in J. In iterative DPO, PILAF reaches the baselines' final reward levels approximately 40% faster, directly reducing annotation and compute costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), modifying its response sampling scheme rather than its loss function. It departs from prior sampling heuristics—vanilla on-policy sampling, best-of-N (Xiong et al., 2024), hybrid policy/reference sampling (Xie et al., 2024), and VPO's exploration bonus (Cen et al., 2024)—by providing the first theoretically optimal sampling distribution derived from first principles. The key conceptual bridge is to policy gradient methods in RL: the authors show that interpolating the policy and reference logits with coefficient β exactly cancels the sigmoid nonlinearity that causes the MLE gradient to misalign with the oracle objective gradient, a connection not previously exploited in the preference-learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PILAF offers a drop-in, hyperparameter-free modification to DPO's data collection that reduces annotation and compute costs by over 40% while improving the reward-KL trade-off, making it practically relevant for any team running iterative or online preference optimization. The theoretical guarantees (gradient alignment and inverse-Hessian-aligned statistical error) provide a principled foundation for future sampling design beyond DPO. However, the evaluation is limited to 8B models with a reward-model proxy for human labels, and the method's extension to other paradigms (KTO, IPO) or to real human annotation remains open. Whether the 40% savings persist at larger scale or with genuinely noisy human labelers is an important unresolved question.
