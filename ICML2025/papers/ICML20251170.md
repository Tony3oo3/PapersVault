## Problem

Adapting natural-image vision foundation models (e.g., DinoV2, MAE) to new visual domains such as satellite, medical, or wildlife imagery has traditionally required expensive full pre-training from scratch on the target domain, precluding knowledge transfer from the source. The paper asks whether parameter-efficient self-supervised pre-training can bridge large domain shifts without retraining the entire model, and whether the standard LoRA low-rank assumption holds under such shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained ViT-L weights from a source domain (DinoV2 or MAE on natural images) and an unlabeled dataset of target-domain images (RGB satellite at 224×224, 13-band Sentinel-2 at 98×98, temporal sequences, or medical/wildlife/agricultural images). It produces a domain-adapted ViT encoder W_T* = W_S + Δ_T, where Δ_T consists of only 1–2 fully unfrozen transformer blocks, LoRA matrices on Q/V attention weights, and layer-normalization parameters (~18.7M out of 303.3M parameters). For multi-spectral inputs, patch-embedding and positional-encoding weights are additionally unfrozen since they cannot be inherited from RGB pre-training. The resulting model is then fine-tuned with LoRA or linear probing to produce class labels, segmentation masks, or bounding boxes.

## Method

ExPLoRA partitions the weight update as W_T^(τ) ≈ W_S + Δ_T + Δ^(τ), where Δ_T is a structured update learned via extended unsupervised pre-training on the target domain and Δ^(τ) is the task-specific fine-tuning update. Concretely, it initializes a ViT with source weights W_S, fully unfreezes 1–2 transformer blocks (typically the last block), applies LoRA of rank r to the Q and V attention matrices in all remaining frozen blocks, and unfreezes all layer-normalization layers. The model is then trained with the same SSL objective as the source (DinoV2's global+patch contrastive loss or MAE's reconstruction loss) on unlabeled target-domain images. This structured parameter space Θ(U, r) is key: the ablations show that simply unfreezing more blocks without LoRA, or applying high-rank LoRA to all matrices including MLPs, performs worse than the targeted combination. The approach requires only ~6% of the original ViT parameters and 8–10× less compute than full domain-specific pre-training, while matching or exceeding fully pre-trained baselines on satellite, medical, wildlife, and agricultural benchmarks.

## Results

On fMoW-RGB, ExPLoRA with DinoV2 initialization (D-[L]-r64) achieves 79.28% top-1 accuracy with LoRA-r8 fine-tuning, outperforming fully pre-trained ScaleMAE (77.80%) and SatMAE (77.78%) while using only 18.7M trainable parameters (6% of the ViT-L encoder) and 100 GPU hours versus 960+ for full pre-training. Linear probing accuracy reaches 77.48% (last 4 blocks), a +8.2% improvement over the prior SoTA of 69.20% (CrossScaleMAE). On multi-spectral fMoW-Sentinel, ExPLoRA (M-[1,L]-r32) achieves 60.15% top-1 accuracy, within 1.3% of fully pre-trained SatMAE (61.48%) while requiring 320 vs. 1150 GPU hours and <10% of the trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExPLoRA (D-[L]-r64 + LoRA-r8) achieves 79.28% top-1 on fMoW-RGB, outperforming fully pre-trained ScaleMAE (77.80%) and SatMAE (77.78%) by 1.5–1.7% while using 6% of their trainable parameters and 8× less pre-training compute. Against PEFT-only baselines on natural-image pre-trained weights, ExPLoRA beats AdaLoRA-r8 (78.87%), Adapter+ (78.16%), DinoV2+LoRA-r8 (78.08%), and SA2VP (77.53%) by 0.4–1.8%. On multi-spectral fMoW-Sentinel, ExPLoRA (M-[1,L]-r32) reaches 60.15%, within 1.3% of fully pre-trained SatMAE (61.48%) but requiring 320 vs. 1150 GPU hours, and substantially outperforming direct MAE fine-tuning (51.61%) and GDA (55.23%). On WILDS benchmarks, ExPLoRA outperforms DinoV2 on Camelyon17 (94.41% vs. 93.27% linear probing) and iWildcam (68.07% vs. 67.10% LoRA), and on GlobalWheat detection (82.7% top-1, 54.5 AP vs. DinoV2's 82.3%, 52.1 AP). On VisDA-2017 UDA, ExPLoRA initialization improves SSRT to 90.9% mean accuracy (vs. 89.9% with DinoV2 init and 88.8% with ImageNet init), establishing a new SoTA for that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExPLoRA extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) paradigm—specifically LoRA—from the supervised fine-tuning phase into the unsupervised pre-training phase itself. Prior domain-adaptation work for satellite imagery (SatMAE, ScaleMAE, GFM) either pre-trains the full model from scratch or performs full-parameter continual pre-training, while GDA uses non-mergeable scaling vectors. ExPLoRA departs from both by selectively unfreezing 1–2 ViT blocks and applying LoRA to the remaining frozen blocks during extended pre-training, preserving the source-domain weights as a strong initialization. The method is explicitly complementary to existing PEFT techniques (LoRA, VPT, adapters) used at fine-tuning time, and it differs from unsupervised domain adaptation by requiring no source-domain labels or shared label space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: ExPLoRA matches or exceeds fully pre-trained domain-specific foundation models while using 8–10× less compute and 16× fewer trainable parameters, making domain-specific foundation models accessible to researchers without large GPU clusters. The finding that full pre-training from scratch (1200 GPU hours) yields *worse* linear-probing accuracy (54.29%) than ExPLoRA (100 GPU hours, 77.48%) challenges the assumption that more pre-training compute monotonically improves representations. Open questions include whether alternative PEFT mechanisms could replace the unfrozen-block strategy, whether the approach transfers to LLMs, and why linear probing degrades on small-domain-gap settings (iWildcam), suggesting a potential overfitting regime that needs further characterization.
