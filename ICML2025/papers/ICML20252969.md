## Problem

Existing stability and generalization analyses of Decentralized SGD impose restrictive assumptions such as Lipschitzness of the loss and bounded gradient variance, and their bounds fail to capture fast rates under low-noise conditions. This paper removes these assumptions and derives sharper on-average model stability bounds that involve training errors, thereby establishing a direct connection between optimization progress and generalization performance for both smooth and nonsmooth convex problems.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes m agents each holding n local samples drawn i.i.d. from a common distribution, a doubly stochastic communication matrix W with spectral gap 1−√λ, and a convex loss function that is either L-smooth or has (α,L)-Hölder continuous gradients. The outputs are closed-form stability bounds (involving the sum of expected training errors across iterations), convergence rates for the averaged iterate, and excess risk bounds that are minimax optimal O(1/√(mn)) in the general case and fast O(1/(mn)) under a low-noise condition where the optimal training risk vanishes.

## Method

The key technical novelty is a new error decomposition for the neighboring consensus error ‖θ̄⁽ᵗ⁾−θₖ⁽ᵗ⁾−θ̄⁽ᵗⁱʲ⁾+θₖ⁽ᵗⁱʲ⁾‖² that exploits the co-coercivity of convex smooth gradients: the inner product ⟨θₖ⁽ᵗ⁾−θₖ⁽ᵗⁱʲ⁾, ∇ℓ(θₖ⁽ᵗ⁾)−∇ℓ(θₖ⁽ᵗⁱʲ⁾)⟩ lower-bounds the squared gradient difference by (1/L)‖∇ℓ(θₖ⁽ᵗ⁾)−∇ℓ(θₖ⁽ᵗⁱʲ⁾)‖², which cancels the dominant term in the consensus error recursion. This yields stability bounds controlled by the self-bounding property (‖∇ℓ‖² ≤ 2Lℓ), so the final bounds involve the training risk E[R_S(θ̄⁽ᵗ⁾)] rather than a uniform Lipschitz constant. For nonsmooth problems with Hölder-continuous gradients (α∈[0,1)), the co-coercivity is generalized and an additional term b^((1+α)/(1−α))·η_t^(2/(1−α)) appears, capturing the effect of the smoothness parameter. The step-size requirement η_t ≲ (1−λ)/L is mild and satisfied by the standard η_t ∝ 1/√T schedule, and the resulting excess risk bounds interpolate between O(1/√T) and O(1/T) depending on whether the optimal training risk is small.

## Results

For convex and smooth problems, the paper derives minimax optimal excess risk bounds of order O(1/√(mn)) without requiring Lipschitzness or bounded variance assumptions, and achieves fast rates of order O(1/(mn)) under a low-noise condition (where R(θ*) + ‖θ*‖²/(ηT) ≲ 1/(ηT)). For convex and nonsmooth (Lipschitz) problems, the paper provides the first optimal stability-based generalization bounds, also yielding minimax optimal O(1/√(mn)) excess risk. The stability bounds (Corollary 4.4) involve training errors Σ E[R_S(θ̄^(t))], establishing a direct connection between optimization progress and generalization, with the key improvement over prior work being the removal of the non-vanishing GηT/(1−λ) and λ terms present in earlier analyses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Richards & Rebeschini (2020) and Sun et al. (2021), the paper removes the G-Lipschitzness assumption entirely and eliminates the non-vanishing GηT/(1−λ) term that prevents convergence in Sun et al.'s bound. Against Zhu et al. (2022), the paper removes the persistent λ term (which does not vanish as mn→∞) and avoids the Gaussian weight difference assumption. Versus Le Bars et al. (2024), the paper drops both the bounded variance assumption (σ) and the Lipschitzness assumption (G), and its stability bound involves Σ E[R_S(θ̄^(t))] which is controllable by optimization theory, enabling fast O(1/(mn)) rates in low-noise settings that Le Bars et al.'s bound cannot achieve. Against Taheri & Thrampoulidis (2023), the paper's bound replaces the η²√T/(1−λ) factor with the sharper η^{3/2}/(√(mn)(1−λ)) + η/(m√n), and additionally analyzes D-SGD (more computationally efficient) rather than DGD, with the improvement being significant under the recommended parameter regime η ≍ 1/√(mn), T ≍ mn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-average model stability framework of Lei and Ying (2020) to the decentralized setting, building on the D-SGD algorithm of Lian et al. (2017) and the co-coercivity property from Hardt et al. (2016). The key departure from prior stability analyses (Richards & Rebeschini 2020; Sun et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2022; Le Bars et al. 2024; Taheri & Thrampoulidis 2023) is a new error decomposition that exploits co-coercivity to offset neighboring consensus errors, rather than bounding them crudely as two separate consensus terms. This analytical trick removes the Lipschitzness and bounded-variance assumptions that were standard in prior work, and the self-bounding property of Srebro et al. (2010) is used to express stability in terms of training errors, linking optimization progress to generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax-optimal excess risk bounds of order O(1/√(mn)) for D-SGD on nonsmooth problems and the first fast rates of O(1/(mn)) under a low-noise condition, both without Lipschitzness assumptions. The stability bounds involving training errors establish a concrete connection between optimization quality and generalization, which is useful for designing early-stopping rules in decentralized training. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonconvex settings (e.g., overparameterized neural networks) and developing high-probability bounds, which the authors identify as natural next steps.
