## Problem

Existing physically-embodied dynamical systems for machine learning suffer from two critical limitations: (1) low expressivity, as they use flat architectures with only linear, homogeneous node interactions, restricting their ability to represent complex nonlinear functions; and (2) inefficient training, because while inference exploits natural annealing on the hardware, the training process to construct the energy landscape still runs on digital GPUs, decoupling training from inference and leaving the most computationally expensive phase unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251596/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are physically clamped as capacitor voltages on the device: spatial-temporal graph node values (128 hidden nodes), PDE initial conditions or coefficient fields on 16×16 to 64×64 grids, or attention kernel input-output pairs extracted from GPT-2's forward pass on LAMBADA. Outputs emerge as stabilized capacitor voltages after natural annealing, producing predicted node values, PDE solutions, or attention kernel outputs. A key design choice is setting output self-reaction strengths r_m = 1, which simplifies both training and inference by making the ground-truth clamp directly proportional to the target signal.

## Method

EADS replaces the flat, homogeneous dynamical system with a three-stage hierarchical pipeline: a linear input-to-hidden projection (Eq. 4), a nonlinear inter-hidden coupling stage using ReLU implemented via diodes (Eq. 5), and a linear hidden-to-output projection (Eq. 6), all realized as a resistively-coupled capacitor network. For training, EC-Train exploits Kirchhoff's current law: when output nodes are clamped to ground truth, the residual current (I_m^in − I_m^R) directly yields the error signal δ_m, eliminating the need for digital loss computation. Gradients for output weights Q are computed as δ_m·h_i; for inter-hidden weights J, the Adjoint Sensitivity Method introduces auxiliary adjoint nodes a_i that evolve backward in time (Eq. 9–11) to accumulate gradients without requiring hidden-state ground truths; and for input-to-hidden weights P, gradients are δ_i·x_j. All parameter updates are performed on-device by routing feedback currents to programmable resistors, enabling continuous, instant training within each natural annealing cycle.

## Results

On six spatiotemporal prediction datasets (PEMS04, PEMS08, PM2.5, PM10, NYC Taxi, Texas Covid), EADS achieves the lowest test MAE across all datasets, with an average 23.91% MAE reduction over the best GPU-based baseline and up to 16.29% reduction on Texas Covid (18.45 vs. 22.04 for NP-GL). On PDE solving (Burgers' equation and Darcy Flow), EADS achieves the best or near-best MAE (e.g., 1.32×10⁻⁵ on Burgers S1=256 vs. 1.41×10⁻⁵ for FNO). EADS delivers ~300× average training speedup and ~1000× inference speedup over GPU-based baselines on graph learning, and ~1000× speedups on PDE solving. On LLM attention kernel approximation (GPT-2 small, LAMBADA), EADS incurs only 1.54–1.95 PPL degradation vs. the original GPT-2 (35.13), while achieving ~800× training speedup over NP-GL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EADS outperforms all five spatiotemporal prediction baselines (Graph WaveNet, MTGNN, DDGCRN, MegaCRN, NP-GL) on every dataset in Table 1, with the largest gap on Texas Covid (18.45 vs. 22.04 for NP-GL, a 16.3% reduction) and NYC Taxi (2.46 vs. 3.03 for NP-GL). Against the closest dynamical-system baseline NP-GL, EADS achieves an average 8.08% MAE reduction, demonstrating the benefit of its hierarchical architecture and heterogeneous dynamics. On PDE solving, EADS matches or slightly beats FNO (the strongest operator-learning baseline) on Burgers' equation (1.32×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.41×10⁻⁵ at S1=256) and Darcy Flow (4.31×10⁻⁶ vs. 4.72×10⁻⁶ at 16×16), while substantially outperforming GNO, FCN, and NP-GL. In the LLM attention kernel experiment, EADS achieves an average PPL of ~36.9 across all 12 GPT-2 kernels (vs. 35.13 for the original GPT-2), outperforming NP-GL by ~1.49 PPL points on average (e.g., 36.67 vs. 37.79 on kernel 1). In terms of efficiency, EADS achieves ~356× training speedup over NP-GL and ~1000× inference speedup over GPU baselines on graph learning, ~1000× speedups on PDEs, and ~800× training / ~100× inference speedups on LLM kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the real-valued dynamical system framework introduced by Wu et al. (NP-GL, ICLR 2024) and the BRIM hardware embodiment (Afoakwa et al., HPCA 2021) in two fundamental directions. First, it replaces the flat, homogeneous node dynamics with a brain-inspired hierarchical pipeline (input projection → nonlinear inter-hidden coupling → output projection), drawing on the multi-stage processing observed in cortical circuits. Second, and more radically, it moves training from offline GPU-based statistical estimation to an on-device procedure (EC-Train) that exploits Kirchhoff's current law and the adjoint sensitivity method from optimal control theory to compute gradients directly from intrinsic electrical signals, eliminating the train/inference hardware decoupling that has constrained prior dynamical-system ML work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the simulation results translate to physical hardware, EADS could shift the cost structure of ML from training-dominated to inference-dominated, with orders-of-magnitude reductions in both. The on-device training paradigm is the more consequential contribution conceptually: it aligns with how biological systems learn and removes the need for a separate, expensive digital training loop. Key open questions include whether the FEA-simulated speedups hold on actual resistive-capacitive hardware at scale, how the system handles larger hidden-layer sizes and more complex nonlinearities, and whether the single-kernel LLM replacement generalizes to full-model substitution. The modest accuracy gains over NP-GL (~8% MAE) suggest the expressivity improvements, while real, may not yet be the bottleneck relative to training efficiency.
