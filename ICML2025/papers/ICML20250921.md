## Problem

High-frequency continuous control (e.g., 500 Hz torque control) is essential for safety-critical robotics, but direct end-to-end RL at such frequencies suffers from poor exploration and long-horizon credit assignment, while fixed PD controllers limit expressiveness to simple state-goal tracking and require per-task manual tuning. The paper asks whether a learned bi-level system can simultaneously achieve efficient exploration, complex high-frequency interaction behavior, and automated controller tuning.

## Input / Output

The high-level policy ρ (operating at ~31.25 Hz) consumes the full proprioceptive state and outputs a stochastic latent action a_k (e.g., target joint position or velocity). The low-level policy β (operating at 500 Hz) receives the proprioceptive state plus the held latent action a_k and outputs deterministic joint torques; it executes G=16 low-level steps per high-level step. The low-level network is deterministic to reduce fitness variance during ES optimization, while the high-level actor is a diagonal-covariance Gaussian.

## Method

EvoControl alternates between optimizing the high-level policy with PPO (fixed low-level) and the low-level policy with Evolution Strategies (PGPE, fixed high-level) in K discrete training stages. The key architectural insight is that the low-level policy β is parameterized as a convex combination α·β_PD + (1−α)·β_NN, where α anneals from 1 to 0 over training, so learning begins with a semantically grounded PD controller and gradually transitions to a fully learned neural network. ES optimizes the full episodic return R as a black-box fitness function, sidestepping the long-horizon credit-assignment problem that plagues policy gradients at 500 Hz. Theoretically, the paper proves (Proposition 2.1) that there exist CTMDPs where finer discretization strictly increases optimal expected cumulative reward, providing a formal motivation for high-frequency control.

## Results

On 11 standard MuJoCo continuous-control environments at 500Hz, EvoControl (Full State) achieves normalized evaluation returns of 368 (Ant), 263 (Hopper), 262 (Pusher), 163 (Walker2D), 157 (HalfCheetah), and 123 (Humanoid), all substantially exceeding the best non-EvoControl baseline (normalized to 100). On the safety-critical Reacher task with random obstacles, EvoControl (Target+Proprio.) reaches 237 vs. 100 for the best fixed PD controller and 0 for direct high-frequency torque control. EvoControl also demonstrates robustness to mistuned PD gains (Kp from 0.001 to 10.0), maintaining scores above 94 across all settings while fixed PD controllers drop to 0 at Kp=0.001.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoControl is compared against two families of baselines: (1) fixed PD controllers (PD Position, PD Position Delta, PD Integrated Velocity, PD Position & Kp) from Aljalbout et al. (2024), and (2) direct end-to-end torque control at both 500Hz and 31.25Hz from Chen et al. (2023). EvoControl decisively outperforms all fixed PD controllers on 10 of 11 environments, with the largest margins on Ant (368 vs. 100), Hopper (263 vs. 100), and Pusher (262 vs. 100). Against direct high-frequency (500Hz) torque control, EvoControl wins on every environment where the baseline achieves non-zero performance (e.g., HalfCheetah 157 vs. 17.2, Hopper 263 vs. 1.4), and the high-frequency torque baseline fails entirely on Ant, Inverted Double Pendulum, Inverted Pendulum, and Walker2D. Against direct low-frequency (31.25Hz) torque control, EvoControl still wins on most environments (e.g., Ant 368 vs. 54.5, Hopper 263 vs. 69.2, Pusher 262 vs. 53.0), confirming that the bi-level structure with a learned fast controller provides advantages beyond simply increasing control frequency. On the safety-critical Reacher task, EvoControl (Target+Proprio.) achieves 237 vs. 100 for the best fixed PD and 0 for direct 500Hz torque control, highlighting the importance of learned high-frequency reactive behavior for collision avoidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoControl sits at the intersection of hierarchical reinforcement learning and evolutionary strategies, departing from both the fixed-PD-controller paradigm (Song et al., 2019; Aljalbout et al., 2024) and end-to-end direct torque control (Peng & Van De Panne, 2017; Chen et al., 2023). It builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) for the high-level policy and PGPE (Sehnke et al., 2010) within the broader ES framework (Salimans et al., 2017) for the low-level policy, but the key conceptual departure is the annealing schedule that transitions the low-level controller from a fixed PD law to a learned neural network, grounding the latent action space semantically before freeing it. Unlike prior HRL work that typically uses gradient-based methods at both levels or focuses on discrete skill/subgoal decomposition (Sutton et al., 1999; Nachum et al., 2018), EvoControl uniquely pairs a gradient-based high-level optimizer with a gradient-free low-level optimizer to address the long-horizon credit assignment problem inherent in 500Hz control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in safety-critical robotics: fixed PD controllers cannot react to unmodeled collisions or disturbances, while direct high-frequency torque control fails to learn effectively due to exploration and credit assignment difficulties. The formal result (Proposition 2.1) that higher-frequency action can strictly improve optimal return, combined with the sim-to-real validation on a Franka Emika Panda at 200Hz, strengthens the case for learned high-frequency controllers in deployment. Key limitations include the continued dependence on an initial PD controller for grounding, higher computational cost than pure PPO, and the theoretical construction being somewhat artificial (two-state CTMDP). Open directions include nested hierarchies, direct cross-level information flow, and whether the ES component can be replaced by more sample-efficient gradient-free methods as they mature.
