## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) for online vision-language navigation remains underexplored, and the existing entropy-minimization paradigm (e.g., FSTTA) suffers from overfitting to failure patterns and limited exploration in the sequential decision-making setting. The paper asks what properties a TTA framework must have for online VLN—flexibility to different navigation outcomes, interactivity with external feedback, and a balance between plasticity and stability—and shows that none of these are satisfied by prior approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the agent receives a stream of episodes, each consisting of a natural language instruction and a 360° panoramic initial view, and after each episode an oracle (human or LLM such as GPT-4o) returns a single binary scalar (+1 for success, −1 for failure). The method freezes the language and visual encoders of a pre-trained VLN policy (HAMT, DUET, BEVBert, or EPTNav) and updates only the cross-modal encoder onward, producing an adapted policy that outputs a sequence of navigation actions (move, turn, stop) per episode. The binary episodic feedback is the sole training signal—no ground-truth goal positions, step-wise rewards, or pre-defined maps are available at test time.

## Method

FEEDTTA applies the REINFORCE policy gradient to maximize cumulative binary episodic feedback, where the reward is 0 at all intermediate steps and the binary feedback F at the final step, yielding a score function J(θ) = E[Σ γ^(T−t−1) F]. The key technical contribution is Stochastic Gradient Reversion (SGR): for each update, a random subset of parameter dimensions is selected via a Bernoulli(p) mask, and the gradients in that subset are multiplied by a negative coefficient α < 0 (reversing their direction), while the remaining gradients are scaled by 1/(αp + (1−p)) to preserve the expected gradient magnitude. This counterfactual reasoning partially simulates the opposite feedback outcome, smoothing the abrupt gradient distribution shifts caused by the binary signal and thereby alleviating non-stationarity. The paper proves that SGR scales the expected absolute gradient by a factor (1 − p² − αp) ≤ 1, reducing catastrophic forgetting while maintaining plasticity, and introduces the Adaptive Success Rate (ASR) metric to measure sample-wise transitions (preserved and converted successes) before and after adaptation.

## Results

On REVERIE (the primary benchmark), FEEDTTA applied to DUET achieves SR 66.49 and SPL 45.38 on Val Unseen, a +19.51 and +11.65 absolute improvement over the DUET baseline (46.98 / 33.73). On Val Seen, DUET+FEEDTTA reaches SR 84.19 and SPL 75.54, surpassing all offline-training SOTA methods (BEVBert: SR 51.78, KERM: SR 50.44, VLN-VER: SR 55.98 on Val Unseen). On R2R, DUET+FEEDTTA improves SPL from 60 to 65 on Val Unseen, and on R2R-CE, BEVBert+FEEDTTA improves SR from 68 to 73 and SPL from 60 to 63 on Val Seen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEEDTTA decisively outperforms the two TTA baselines (FSTTA and Tent) across all three datasets and both target policies. On REVERIE Val Unseen with DUET, FEEDTTA achieves SR 66.49 vs. FSTTA's 54.15 (+12.34) and Tent's 47.55 (+18.94); on Val Seen, the gap is SR 84.19 vs. 75.48 (FSTTA) and 71.89 (Tent). Notably, Tent actually degrades HAMT's performance on Val Unseen (SR 30.56 vs. 32.95 baseline), while FEEDTTA consistently improves. FEEDTTA on DUET also surpasses all offline-training SOTA methods on REVERIE Val Unseen (SR 66.49 vs. VLN-VER 55.98, BEVBert 51.78, KERM 50.44), demonstrating that a single stream of online adaptation can exceed passive offline training. On R2R and R2R-CE, FEEDTTA yields more modest but consistent gains over FSTTA (e.g., R2R Val Unseen SPL 65 vs. 62 for FSTTA on DUET; R2R-CE Val Seen SR 73 vs. 69 for FSTTA on BEVBert), with the paper attributing the smaller margins to the denser step-wise training signal already embedded in R2R-trained policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant entropy-minimization TTA paradigm (Tent, FSTTA) by replacing it with a feedback-based REINFORCE policy gradient that learns from binary episodic success/failure signals. It draws conceptual inspiration from RLHF/RLAIF in the LLM literature but adapts the feedback mechanism to the sequential, embodied setting of VLN, where the oracle provides a single scalar per episode rather than per-token preferences. The SGR regularization is a novel contribution that exploits the binary structure of the reward to smooth non-stationary gradient updates, and the method is applied as a plug-in on top of existing offline-trained VLN policies (HAMT, DUET, BEVBert, EPTNav) without modifying their architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical setup—requiring only a yes/no judgment from a human or LLM after each navigation episode—makes TTA for embodied agents far more deployable than approaches needing dense step-wise rewards or ground-truth maps. The finding that a single stream of online adaptation can surpass state-of-the-art offline training on REVERIE suggests that active, interactive adaptation may be more effective than passive generalization for distribution-shift scenarios in robotics. Open questions include the reliability of LLM oracles (currently ~72% accuracy), whether the approach extends to truly novel task types at test time, and how the method scales to longer-horizon or multi-agent navigation.
