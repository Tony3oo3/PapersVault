## Problem

Trilevel learning problems with three nested optimization levels are NP-hard even to find feasible solutions, and existing approaches require first-order information at every level. When gradients are inaccessible (e.g., black-box LLM APIs, proprietary models) and data is distributed across nodes for privacy, no method existed to solve trilevel zeroth-order optimization with convergence guarantees. This paper is the first to address this gap, providing both an algorithm and non-asymptotic convergence analysis for distributed trilevel zeroth-order learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only black-box function evaluations of the three-level objectives f_{1,j}, f_{2,j}, f_{3,j} at each of N distributed workers—no gradients or sub-gradients are accessed at any level. Data remains local to each worker; only scalar function values and updated variable vectors are exchanged with the central master node. The output is a set of optimized variables {x_{1,j}, x_{2,j}, x_{3,j}} and consensus variables {z_1, z_2, z_3} that constitute an ε-stationary point of the reformulated penalty problem, with iteration complexity O(1/ε²). In the LLM experiments, the concrete output is backdoor trigger prompts; in the robust HPO experiments, it is robust hyperparameter configurations.

## Method

DTZO constructs a cascaded zeroth-order polynomial approximation of the trilevel problem: an inner-layer relaxation approximates the level-3 optimality constraint, and an outer-layer relaxation approximates the level-2 optimality constraint, both expressed as collections of nonlinear polynomial inequalities. The key novelty is the zeroth-order cut—a cutting plane generated entirely from smoothed function evaluations (evaluating φ_in or φ_out at a point plus a Gaussian perturbation of radius μ) rather than from gradients, yielding a nonlinear cut of the form aᵀx² + bᵀx + cᵀz² + dᵀz + e ≤ ε. In the distributed algorithm, workers update local variables via zeroth-order gradient estimators (two-point Gaussian smoothing of f_{1,j}), while the master updates consensus variables and periodically generates new zeroth-order cuts every T iterations, pruning inactive cuts to control complexity. The constrained problem is converted to an unconstrained one via an exterior penalty method, and the paper proves O(1/ε²) iteration complexity under L-smoothness and boundedness assumptions, with a tunable parameter T₁ trading off approximation quality against computational cost.

## Results

On the robust hyperparameter optimization task (Table 2), DTZO achieves the best average clean+adversarial accuracy across all 7 datasets, ranging from 0.6351 (Crop) to 0.8513 (USPS). The closest competitor, AFTO+ZO (trilevel first-order method with zeroth-order estimator), trails by 0.03–0.06 in absolute accuracy (e.g., 0.7927 vs. 0.7501 on MNIST; 0.7007 vs. 0.6448 on Fashion-MNIST). In the black-box LLM backdoor attack experiments on GLUE (SST-2, COLA, MRPC), DTZO outperforms FedRZO_bl on both Attack Success Rate (ASR) and clean Accuracy (ACC) across Qwen-1.8B-Chat, Qwen2-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B (Figures 1–3). The theoretical iteration complexity is O(Σᵢ dᵢ⁶ / ε²), matching the O(1/ε²) rate of prior first-order trilevel methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTZO outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in the robust hyperparameter optimization benchmark. Against the closest trilevel competitor AFTO+ZO (Jiao et al., 2024, AAAI), DTZO gains 0.03–0.06 in absolute accuracy (e.g., 0.7927 vs. 0.7501 on MNIST, 0.8513 vs. 0.7987 on USPS), attributed to its native trilevel zeroth-order design versus AFTO's first-order trilevel framework patched with a zeroth-order estimator. Against the bilevel zeroth-order method FedRZO_bl (Qiu et al., 2023, NeurIPS), DTZO leads by a wider margin of 0.05–0.12 (e.g., 0.7927 vs. 0.5405 on MNIST), reflecting the advantage of handling three nested levels. The single-level and bilevel first-order methods (FedZOO, FEDNEST+ZO, ADBO+ZO) all cluster in the 0.49–0.74 range and are consistently outperformed by 0.05–0.25. In the black-box LLM experiments, DTZO surpasses FedRZO_bl on both ASR and ACC across SST-2, COLA, and MRPC using Qwen-1.8B-Chat, Qwen2-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B, confirming that the trilevel structure (trigger design + prompt optimization + model adaptation) is essential for effective backdoor attacks on opaque LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cutting-plane-based distributed nested optimization framework (Jiao et al., 2023; 2024) by replacing gradient-based cuts with novel zeroth-order cuts generated purely from function evaluations, and extends distributed bilevel zeroth-order methods (FedRZO_bl, Qiu et al., 2023) from two to three optimization levels. It also departs from first-order trilevel learning methods (AFTO, Sato et al., 2021) by eliminating all gradient requirements at every level, and introduces the 'soft constraint' concept to justify why lower-level problems can be relaxed. The exterior penalty method for handling the cascaded polynomial constraints follows standard constrained optimization practice but is applied here in a novel trilevel zeroth-order context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for trilevel zeroth-order optimization, opening a problem setting that is practically relevant for black-box LLMs, federated learning with opaque models, and other scenarios where gradient access is restricted at all levels. The O(1/ε²) iteration complexity with d^6 dimension dependence is consistent with existing zeroth-order literature but the high dimension dependence limits scalability to very high-dimensional problems. Open questions include whether the soft constraint assumption holds universally across trilevel applications, whether the dimension dependence can be improved, and how the method performs when only partial zeroth-order constraints apply (grey-box settings).
