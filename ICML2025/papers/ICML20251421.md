## Problem

Existing methods for modeling multi-region brain communications either ignore temporal delays entirely (non-delay models like MR-SDS, mp-srSLDS) or assume static/discretely changing delays (DLAG, MRM-GP), failing to capture the continuously evolving nature of inter-region communication. Moreover, GP-based delay models incur O(T³) cost, while SSM-based approximations rely on sequential O(T) inference, making them impractical for long-duration, large-scale neural recordings.

## Input / Output

The method takes smoothed multi-region spike train recordings (e.g., 72 V1 + 22 V2 neurons at 20 ms bins, or 202 neurons across 5 visual cortex sub-areas at 10 ms bins) and produces per-pair continuous temporal delay functions θ_ij(t), MN latent variables decomposed into across-region and within-region groups, and directed meso-scale brain networks whose edge lengths encode communication speed. The observation model is a block-diagonal Factor Analysis mapping MN latent dimensions to D observed neurons, with the delay parameters evolving continuously over time rather than switching between discrete states.

## Method

ADM establishes a universal connection between arbitrary temporally stationary GPs and SSMs by expressing the SSM transition matrices and process noise as functions of the kernel K(τ) via least-squares regression, removing the separability constraint required by prior Markovian GP approaches. The across-region latent variables are modeled with a time-varying MOSE kernel whose delay parameter θ_ij(t) evolves continuously, and the resulting multi-order SSM is converted to a first-order Markovian form using a controllable canonical structure. Inference proceeds via a parallel scan Kalman EM algorithm: the E-step runs a parallelized Kalman filter and RTS smoother in O(log T) time, while the M-step updates kernel parameters by gradient descent and FA parameters in closed form. The length scale l is held constant across time to constrain delay evolution and prevent misattribution of signal variability to delay fluctuations.

## Results

On the V1-V2 monkey spike train dataset, ADM achieves the highest test log-likelihood among all compared methods (DLAG, MRM-GP), attributed to its ability to model continuously time-varying temporal delays. On the five-region Allen Institute Neuropixels dataset, ADM outperforms mDLAG in test log-likelihood with optimal hyperparameters m_a=3, m_w=1, P=5. Computationally, the parallel scan-based Kalman EM achieves O(log T) time complexity, significantly outperforming the O(T) sequential inference of MRM-GP and the O(T³) cost of DLAG, with the parallel version being substantially faster than sequential updates especially as T increases (up to T=600 bins tested).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADM outperforms DLAG (Gokcen et al., 2022) on the V1-V2 dataset in test log-likelihood, with the gap attributed to DLAG's static delay assumption versus ADM's time-varying delays; DLAG also suffers from O(T³) computational cost making it impractical for long recordings. Against MRM-GP (Li et al., 2024b), ADM again achieves higher test log-likelihood on V1-V2, as MRM-GP restricts delays to discrete phase changes tied to hidden state transitions rather than continuous temporal delays. On the five-region dataset, ADM surpasses mDLAG (Gokcen et al., 2024a) in test log-likelihood, with the improvement linked to mDLAG's assumption of static communication patterns. The paper does not directly compare against mp-srSLDS (Glaser et al., 2020) in the main experiments, noting it as a non-delay model that cannot capture temporal delays. In all comparisons, ADM's advantage stems from its continuous time-varying delay mechanism combined with the O(log T) parallel scan inference, which no baseline combines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markovian Gaussian Process (MRM-GP) framework of Li et al. (2024b) by replacing its discrete, synchronously-switching phase delays with continuous, time-varying temporal delays, while retaining the GP-SSM structure. It also generalizes the GP-SSM connection beyond the separable-kernel restriction that constrained prior Markovian GP methods (Solin et al., 2016; Loper et al., 2021; Dowling et al., 2021), establishing a universal mapping from any temporally stationary GP to a controllable canonical form SSM. For inference, it adopts the parallel scan formulation of Särkkä & García-Fernández (2020) to replace the sequential Kalman EM used by MRM-GP, reducing time complexity from O(T) to O(log T). The factor analysis and MOSE kernel structure are inherited directly from DLAG (Gokcen et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to recover continuously evolving inter-region delays without discrete hidden states addresses a genuine gap: prior delay models (DLAG, mDLAG) assume static delays, and MRM-GP assumes all communication subspaces switch delays simultaneously, which is biologically implausible. The O(log T) parallel scan inference makes long-duration multi-region recordings (e.g., Neuropixels sessions) tractable, and the universal GP-SSM connection has applicability beyond neuroscience to any time-series problem requiring scalable GP inference. Limitations include cubic scaling in the number of regions N and SSM order P (though these are typically small), validation restricted to visual cortex, and the assumption of a shared length scale across time. Open questions include whether frequency-domain techniques (Parnichkun et al., 2024; Gokcen et al., 2024b) can eliminate the cubic bottleneck, and whether the framework generalizes to non-visual, higher-order cognitive tasks with more complex communication dynamics.
