## Problem

Predictive models that generate actionable videos (World Simulators) lack a principled categorization within the broader family of predictive models, and existing benchmarks evaluate video outputs only from aesthetic or feature-similarity perspectives, failing to assess physical rule adherence, 3D scene consistency, and whether generated videos can actually drive embodied actions. This paper introduces a hierarchical taxonomy (S0–S3) and the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate S3 World Simulators from an embodied perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds text instructions (and optionally a first-frame RGB image) to video generation models, which produce short (~16-frame) or long (~48–64-frame) video clips. The Explicit Perceptual Evaluation scores each clip across 6–7 scenario-specific dimensions (e.g., Trajectory, Perspectivity, Embodied Interaction, Safety) on a 1–2 scale for Minecraft or 1–5 scale for driving/robotics, yielding a per-dimension score and an overall average. The Implicit Manipulative Evaluation converts generated videos into real-time control signals via pre-trained video-to-action policies, producing task-level outputs such as MineRL item counts and travel distance, CARLA driving scores (RC, IS, DS, collision counts), and CALVIN sequential task success rates over 5-task chains.

## Method

WorldSimBench employs a dual evaluation framework. For Explicit Perceptual Evaluation, the authors construct the HF-Embodied Dataset (35,701 video-instruction-score-reason tuples across three scenarios) via fine-grained human annotation along a hierarchical dimension checklist (Visual Quality, Condition Consistency, Embodiment), then fine-tune Flash-VStream with LoRA into a Human Preference Evaluator that outputs per-dimension scores aligned with human perception. For Implicit Manipulative Evaluation, three closed-loop simulation pipelines (MineRL+Steve-1 for Minecraft, CARLA+LMdrive for driving, CALVIN+Susie for robot manipulation) translate the World Simulator's generated future videos into executable actions in real time, with the agent's task performance serving as an implicit measure of video actionability. The key design insight is that no single ground truth exists for actionable videos, so the framework combines human-aligned perceptual scoring with closed-loop functional testing to jointly capture visual fidelity and physical executability.

## Results

In Explicit Perceptual Evaluation, OpenSora achieves the highest overall score in AD (4.40/5), ModelScope leads in OE (1.91/2), and DynamicCrafter tops RM (4.14/5). In Implicit Manipulative Evaluation, OpenSora achieves the best OE average (27.80), Open-Sora-Plan leads in AD with a Driving Score of 31.05, and Open-Sora-Plan achieves the highest RM average task length (2.95/5). The Human Preference Evaluator (HPE) substantially outperforms GPT-4o as an evaluator, achieving 89.4% accuracy in OE vs. 72.8%, and PLCC of 0.60 (AD) and 0.43 (RM) vs. 0.28 and 0.07 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Human Preference Evaluator (HPE) consistently outperforms GPT-4o across all three scenarios: 89.4% vs. 72.8% accuracy in OE, 0.60 vs. 0.28 PLCC in AD, and 0.43 vs. 0.07 PLCC in RM; in zero-shot settings, GPT-4o even shows negative correlation (−0.04 PLCC) when evaluating Lavie in AD, while HPE maintains 0.49. Among the 7 video generation models, no single model dominates all scenarios: OpenSora leads in AD (4.40 overall), ModelScope in OE (1.91), and DynamicCrafter in RM (4.14), but DynamicCrafter underperforms Open-Sora-Plan in closed-loop OE and long-horizon RM tasks, revealing a gap between perceptual quality and actionability. In Implicit Manipulative Evaluation, Open-Sora-Plan achieves the best AD Driving Score (31.05) and RM average task length (2.95), while OpenSora leads in OE (27.80 AVG). In RM, all three tested video generation models (Open-Sora-Plan, DynamicCrafter, EasyAnimate) substantially outperform UniPi (Susie), which achieves only 0.56/0.16/0.08 success rates on tasks 1/2/3, confirming that current video generation models surpass prior latent video diffusion approaches for embodied control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video generation evaluation lineage (VBench, EvalCrafter at S2) by introducing a new S3 stage focused on actionable, physically-grounded video generation. The Human Preference Evaluator is built by fine-tuning Flash-VStream with LoRA, while the closed-loop evaluation pipeline adapts action spaces from Steve-1 (Minecraft), LMdrive (CARLA), and Susie (CALVIN). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior feature-similarity-based evaluation by proposing a dual framework combining human-aligned perceptual scoring with implicit video-to-action task completion, positioning World Simulators as a distinct capability tier above standard video generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldSimBench provides the first systematic benchmark for evaluating video generation models as World Simulators, with the HF-Embodied Dataset (35,701 annotated tuples) serving as a reusable resource for both evaluation and preference alignment (demonstrated via DPO). The key finding that current models still fail to capture physical rules and that image conditioning can degrade performance in open-ended environments highlights a significant gap before video generators can reliably drive embodied agents. Open questions include extending evaluation beyond the three tested scenarios, scaling to longer-horizon tasks, and whether the HPE can serve as a real-time reward signal for training better World Simulators.
