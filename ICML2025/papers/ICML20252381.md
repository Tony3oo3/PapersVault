## Problem

Sliced Wasserstein (SW) distance reduces the computational burden of optimal transport by projecting measures onto 1D lines, but this projection discards topological information, especially in high-dimensional spaces. The paper asks whether one can replace 1D lines with richer geometric domains that still admit closed-form OT solutions, thereby preserving more structural information without sacrificing computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252381/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability measures in R^d (with n and m support points respectively) and a splitting map α: R^d → Δ_{k-1}, and produces a scalar distance value. In practice, L tree systems are sampled (each containing k lines), and the measures are projected onto each tree system via a Radon Transform variant, yielding discrete measures on the tree. The final distance is the average of tree-Wasserstein distances over the L sampled tree systems, with computational complexity O(Lkn log n + Lkdn), matching SW when the total number of projection directions (Lk) is held constant.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the 1D projection lines in SW with connected systems of lines equipped with a tree structure (called tree systems), which naturally form metric spaces with tree metrics. A splitting map α distributes the mass at each point across the k lines of a system, and a novel Radon Transform on Systems of Lines projects high-dimensional measures onto these tree systems while preserving total mass. The paper proves this transform is injective and that the induced metric on the tree system is a valid tree metric, guaranteeing a closed-form OT solution via the edge-sum formula. TSW-SL is then defined as the integral of tree-Wasserstein distances over a distribution of sampled tree systems, estimated by Monte Carlo. A MaxTSW-SL variant replaces the average with a max over tree systems, analogous to MaxSW, and is computed via gradient ascent on the tree parameters.

## Results

On the Swiss Roll gradient flow task, TSW-SL reduces the Wasserstein distance to 3.65e-8 at 2500 iterations, compared to 1.05e-3 for SW and 3.57e-5 for SWGG. On 25 Gaussians, TSW-SL achieves 3.03e-7 versus 2.20e-2 for SW. For generative modeling, TSW-SL improves FID from 9.62 to 8.90 on CelebA (L=500) and from 2.90 to 2.83 on CIFAR-10 denoising diffusion, while maintaining comparable per-iteration runtime (~0.010s vs 0.006s for SW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSW-SL substantially outperforms SW (Bonneel et al., 2015) on gradient flows: on Swiss Roll it reduces the Wasserstein distance by ~4 orders of magnitude (3.65e-8 vs 1.05e-3) and on 25 Gaussians by ~5 orders (3.03e-7 vs 2.20e-2), while incurring only ~40% more per-iteration time. Against MaxSW (Deshpande et al., 2019), MaxTSW-SL achieves 5.00e-7 vs 3.45e-3 on Swiss Roll and 5.19e-7 vs 8.48e-2 on 25 Gaussians, a dramatic improvement. TSW-SL also beats SWGG (Mahey et al., 2023) and LCVSW (Nguyen & Ho, 2023) on both gradient flow datasets. For GANs, TSW-SL (L=100, k=5) achieves FID 8.90 vs SW's 9.62 on CelebA and IS 10.74 vs 10.56, and for denoising diffusion on CIFAR-10, TSW-SL-DD reaches FID 2.83 vs SW-DD's 2.90 and DDGAN's 3.64. The authors note that TSW-SL is positioned as a direct replacement for SW (same number of projection directions) rather than a competitor to more recent SW variants like SWGG or LCVSW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sliced Wasserstein (SW) framework by replacing its one-dimensional projection lines with connected systems of lines endowed with tree structures, called tree systems. It builds directly on the closed-form tree Wasserstein distance (Le et al., 2019; Indyk & Thaper, 2003) and the classical Radon Transform (Helgason, 2011), introducing a novel Radon Transform on Systems of Lines with a splitting map that distributes mass across multiple lines. The MaxTSW-SL variant parallels MaxSW (Deshpande et al., 2019), and the overall approach is positioned as a geometric generalization of SW rather than a modification of its sampling or projection mechanism, distinguishing it from prior SW variants that improve line selection or projection strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution—proving that tree systems are metrizable by tree metrics and that the associated Radon Transform is injective—provides a rigorous foundation for a new family of efficient OT metrics that capture more topological structure than 1D projections while maintaining SW-level computational cost. However, the paper explicitly frames TSW-SL as a 'simple alternative' and does not claim superiority over recent SW variants (SWGG, LCVSW, etc.), leaving open the question of whether incorporating those advanced techniques into the tree-system framework would yield further gains. The gradient flow results on 2D datasets show dramatic improvements over vanilla SW, but the GAN and diffusion model improvements are modest, and the practical advantage in high-dimensional generative tasks remains to be established against the strongest baselines.
