## Problem

Naively trained AIGI detectors exhibit an 'asymmetry phenomenon': they rapidly overfit to the limited fake patterns in training data, collapsing the learned feature space into a low-ranked structure that severely limits generalization to unseen forgery methods. The paper identifies this low-rank constraint as the root cause of poor cross-method and cross-dataset generalization, and asks how to expand the feature space using pre-trained vision foundation models without distorting their rich representations during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252812/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (or 8 sampled frames per video for training, 32 for inference) and produces a binary real/fake probability score via a linear classification head. The SVD-based orthogonal decomposition is applied to the linear layers within self-attention modules across all transformer layers of a pre-trained ViT backbone (default: CLIP ViT-L/14), requiring only 0.19M trainable parameters. For video-level evaluation, frame-level probabilities are averaged to yield a single video-level AUC.

## Method

Effort decomposes each pre-trained linear weight matrix W via SVD into a frozen rank-r principal component W_r (preserving pre-trained semantic knowledge) and a learnable residual ΔW = W − W_r (adapting to forgery patterns), guaranteeing explicit orthogonality between the two subspaces. Two regularization losses are added: an orthogonality constraint penalizing deviation of concatenated singular vectors from identity, and a singular value constraint preserving the Frobenius norm of the residual to prevent overfitting. The key insight is that fake images are hierarchically derived from real ones, so the orthogonal decomposition enables discrimination within semantically-aligned subspaces (e.g., among dogs only), reducing effective model complexity and tightening generalization bounds. This yields ~1000× fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning while achieving higher effective feature-space rank (159/160 principal components retained vs. 57 for FFT), directly addressing the low-rank collapse that causes poor generalization.

## Results

On the deepfake detection benchmark (Table 1), Effort achieves 0.917 average AUC on cross-dataset evaluation and 0.940 on cross-method evaluation, using only 0.19M trainable parameters (~1000× fewer than LSDA or ProDet which use ~100M). On the UniversalFakeDetect synthetic image benchmark, Effort attains 99.41% mAP and 95.19% mAcc across 19 generative model subsets. On the GenImage benchmark (diffusion-model-centric), Effort reaches 91.1% mAcc, outperforming the prior SOTA DRCT (ICML 2024) by 1.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Effort consistently outperforms all 13 deepfake detection baselines across both cross-dataset and cross-method protocols. Against the strongest recent baselines, it improves cross-dataset AUC by 3.9% over CDFA (ECCV'24, 0.878→0.917) and 8.9% over ProDet (NeurIPS'24, 0.828→0.917), while cross-method AUC gains are even larger: +15.9% over CDFA (0.781→0.940) and +7.3% over ProDet (0.867→0.940). On the UniversalFakeDetect synthetic image benchmark, Effort achieves 95.19% mAcc and 99.41% mAP, surpassing FatFormer (CVPR'24) by 4.33% mAcc and UniFD (CVPR'23) by 13.81% mAP. Against adapter-based methods (LoRA, MoE-LoRA, Dual-Adapter), Effort achieves 0.940 vs. 0.796 average cross-dataset AUC, with the gap attributed to explicit orthogonality preservation. Notably, Effort uses only 0.19M trainable parameters compared to 87–133M for the strongest baselines (CDFA, LSDA, ProDet), a ~500–700× reduction, while simultaneously achieving the best detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work using vision foundation models (CLIP, BEIT-v2, SigLIP) for AIGI detection, most directly building on UniFD (Ojha et al., 2023), which freezes the CLIP encoder and tunes only a linear probe. It departs from both full fine-tuning and LoRA-based adaptation (Kong et al., 2023; Kong et al., 2024) by repurposing SVD to explicitly construct two orthogonal subspaces—one frozen to preserve pre-trained semantic knowledge, one adapted for forgery patterns—thereby guaranteeing orthogonality that neither FFT nor LoRA enforces. The conceptual departure is the 'asymmetry phenomenon' diagnosis: the paper identifies that naive detectors collapse into low-ranked feature spaces dominated by a few fake patterns, and reframes detection as exploiting a hierarchical prior (fake is derived from real) rather than a symmetric binary classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.19M trainable parameters achieving SOTA-level generalization (vs. ~100M for LSDA, ProDet) makes robust AIGI detection far more accessible, and the orthogonality-via-SVD principle is a transferable design choice for any PEFT setting where preserving pre-trained structure matters. The PCA-based rank analysis (FFT drops effective rank from 160→57, SVD maintains 159) provides a concrete, measurable diagnostic for feature-space collapse that the broader PEFT community can adopt. Key open questions include whether the single-class treatment of all fake methods limits scalability to the ever-growing diversity of generative models, and whether the incremental-learning extension (one SVD branch per fake method) the authors outline can prevent catastrophic forgetting across forgery types.
