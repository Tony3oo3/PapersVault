## Problem

While adversarial detection methods are well-developed for unimodal classifiers, defense strategies for multimodal alignment systems (CLIP, BLIP) remain underexplored. This paper asks whether the topological structure of the image-text embedding alignment carries distinctive, measurable signatures when adversarial perturbations are present, and whether those signatures can be leveraged for more sensitive detection than existing statistical tests.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of images (50 for CIFAR-10/100, 100 for ImageNet) and their corresponding text class-label embeddings from a frozen CLIP or BLIP model, operating on the logits/embeddings extracted before the alignment step rather than raw pixel inputs. A hold-out set Z of clean embeddings (1000–3000 samples) is required to compute per-sample topological features via backpropagation. The output is a binary two-sample test decision (reject or accept H₀ that the batch is clean) at a controlled 5% Type-I error rate, along with the scalar TP and MK loss values that quantify the degree of topological misalignment between the image and text point clouds.

## Method

The core contribution is two Topological-Contrastive (TC) losses—Total Persistence (TP) and Multi-scale Kernel (MK)—that measure the topological discrepancy between the Vietoris-Rips persistence diagrams of the image-embedding point cloud and the text-embedding point cloud. The key empirical finding is that these losses change monotonically as the fraction of adversarial samples in a batch increases, across 5 CLIP models, 3 BLIP models, 3 datasets, and 6 attack methods. For detection, the TC loss is backpropagated to yield per-sample gradient features capturing each image's contribution to global topological distortion; these features are then fed into a modified MMD test (TPSAMMD/MKSAMMD) using a topological-contrastive deep kernel that combines logit similarity with topological-feature similarity. A Poisson Cluster Process model provides theoretical justification: adversarial scattering of logits away from clean cluster centers increases the minimum spanning tree length, which equals the 0-dimensional total persistence, explaining the observed monotonic TP increase.

## Results

On ImageNet with CLIP-ViT-B/32 under PGD at ε=1/255, TPSAMMD achieves a 3.6% test-power gain over SAMMD, rising to 14.3% at ε=4/255. For BLIP-ViT-B/129 at ε=1/255, the gain reaches 16.7%, while at ε=4/255 both methods saturate at 100% test power (0% gain). All methods maintain Type-I error below the 5% threshold. The advantage of TPSAMMD over SAMMD is most pronounced at small perturbation magnitudes, where topological features provide the strongest discriminative signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TPSAMMD and MKSAMMD against four two-sample tests: SAMMD (Gao et al., 2021), Mean Embedding (Jitkrittum et al., 2016), SCF (Chwialkowski et al., 2015), and C2ST (Liu et al., 2020). TPSAMMD consistently outperforms SAMMD, with gains of 3.6–16.7% test power at ε=1/255 and up to 14.3% at ε=4/255 on CLIP models; the gap narrows to 0% for BLIP at ε=4/255 where both reach 100%. MKSAMMD also improves over SAMMD in many configurations but is generally slightly weaker than TPSAMMD. The ME and SCF baselines perform considerably worse than all SAMMD-based methods, particularly in the challenging 50-sample batch setting the authors adopt (versus the 500-sample batches used in prior work). All methods maintain Type-I error at or below 5%, confirming that the topological features improve detection power without inflating false positives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAMMD framework (Gao et al., 2021) by augmenting its semantic-aware kernel with topological features derived from persistent homology, specifically Total Persistence (Divol & Polonik, 2019) and the Multi-scale Kernel (Reininghaus et al., 2014). Conceptually, it departs from prior multimodal adversarial defenses—which have focused on prompt mutations, LoRA-based weight protection, or hidden-state analysis—by reframing adversarial disruption as a measurable topological misalignment between image and text embedding point clouds. The Poisson Cluster Process modeling of logits provides a theoretical bridge between the adversarial scattering assumption and the observed increase in 0-dimensional persistence, a connection not present in prior TDA or adversarial detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As CLIP and BLIP models are deployed in safety-critical applications, the lack of principled multimodal adversarial defenses is a significant gap; this work offers an interpretable, monotonic signal (TP/MK losses) that scales with adversary proportion, which is more informative than a binary detection score. However, the practical detection gains over SAMMD are modest (a few percentage points in some settings, zero in others), and the computational cost of constructing Vietoris-Rips filtrations for each batch limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether topological features can inform training-time robustness (not just detection), whether the approach extends to video-text or audio-text modalities, and whether the batch-processing approximation for independence introduces systematic biases at scale.
