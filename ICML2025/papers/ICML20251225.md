## Problem

While diffusion models and flow matching have been extended to data-to-data translation, no method grounded in electrostatic theory had been applied beyond the noise-to-data setting. The Poisson Flow Generative Model (PFGM) used electrostatic fields for generation but could not handle unpaired distribution-to-distribution transfer. This paper asks whether the physics of a capacitor—two charged plates producing a field whose lines connect them—can serve as a unified, provably correct transport mechanism for both generative and translation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two distributions accessible only by i.i.d. samples (e.g., Gaussian noise and 32×32 RGB images, or two colored-digit distributions) and embeds them as charge densities on parallel D-dimensional hyperplanes separated by distance L in (D+1)-dimensional space. Output is produced by integrating a learned ODE along electric field lines from the source plate (z=0) to the target plate (z=L), yielding samples from the target distribution. The interplate distance L is a critical hyperparameter: too small makes field lines nearly straight (easy to approximate), too large increases curvature and training volume, degrading quality.

## Method

EFM models two distributions as positive and negative charge densities on the plates of a (D+1)-dimensional capacitor. A neural network f_θ approximates the normalized electrostatic field direction at intermediate points, trained by minimizing MSE against Monte Carlo estimates of the true field computed from samples of both distributions. Inference integrates the ODE d x̃/dt = E(x̃) (reparameterized by z for a natural stopping condition) from z=0 to z=L to transport samples. The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.1, which proves via Gauss's theorem and flux conservation that the stochastic map defined by following field lines (with forward/backward line selection probabilities proportional to local field flux) transfers P to Q almost surely. In practice, only forward-oriented field lines are used, and the stochastic continuation past z=L is approximated by simply stopping at the first intersection.

## Results

This is a proof-of-concept paper with no quantitative evaluation metrics (no FID, IS, or precision/recall reported). The method is validated qualitatively across three settings: a 2D Gaussian-to-Swiss-Roll distribution transfer, Colored MNIST image-to-image translation (digit 3 → digit 2), and noise-to-image generation on MNIST (32×32) and CIFAR-10 (32×32). The interplate distance L is shown to be a critical hyperparameter: L=6 yields accurate Swiss Roll recovery while L=30 degrades quality due to increased field-line curvature and a larger required training volume. Training completes in under 10 hours on a single GTX 1080 Ti GPU for all image experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All comparisons in this paper are qualitative (side-by-side image samples), as no quantitative metrics are reported. Against Flow Matching (FM) on Colored MNIST 3→2 translation, the authors note that FM 'does not always accurately translate the shape and color of the initial digits 3,' while EFM's space-conditional electric-field matching produces visually more faithful translations (Fig. 10). Against PFGM on noise-to-image generation, EFM and PFGM produce comparable results on MNIST (Fig. 11) and CIFAR-10 (Fig. 12), with EFM using a simpler two-plate capacitor setup versus PFGM's hemisphere-based formulation. Three additional image-translation baselines—Cycle-GAN, DDIB, and DSBM—are included in Appendix D (Fig. 23) for the Colored MNIST task, but no quantitative ranking is provided. Overall, the paper positions EFM as a theoretically grounded alternative (with a provable distribution-transfer guarantee via Theorem 3.1) rather than claiming empirical superiority over existing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the electrostatic generative modeling paradigm introduced by PFGM (Xu et al., 2022, 2023), which placed a single data distribution on one plate and a uniform distribution on a hemisphere at infinity for noise-to-data generation. EFM generalizes this to a two-plate capacitor where both plates carry arbitrary data distributions, enabling data-to-data translation — a setting previously addressed only by SDE-based methods (Diffusion Bridge Matching) or ODE-based methods (Flow Matching). The key conceptual departure from Flow Matching is that EFM learns a space-conditional vector field (the electrostatic field) rather than a time-conditional velocity field, and the transport is defined by following field lines rather than integrating a learned ODE over a fixed time interval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is a provable distribution transfer guarantee (Theorem 3.1) grounded in Gauss's theorem and flux conservation, which is a clean theoretical result absent from most flow-based generative methods. However, the empirical validation is limited to qualitative comparisons on small datasets (32×32 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10) without standard quantitative metrics (FID, IS), and the Swiss Roll experiment shows clear degradation at larger interplate distances L. Open questions include: the optimal choice of L, handling of field lines that cross the target plate boundary (z > L), the instability of the 1/||x−x'||^D term in high dimensions, and whether the method can scale to larger, higher-resolution image generation tasks. The practical implementation also simplifies the theoretical stochastic map by always stopping at the first plate intersection, which the authors acknowledge may lead to incorrect target distribution recovery.
