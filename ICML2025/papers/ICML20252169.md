## Problem

Diffusion-based probabilistic HMR methods (e.g., ScoreHypo) generate multiple plausible meshes but suffer from misalignment with 2D image cues and poor robustness on in-the-wild images, because end-to-end diffusion training avoids 3D reprojection loss and relies on noisy pseudo-labels. No existing approach aligns the diffusion generator directly with image observations in a way that is robust to imperfect annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252169/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces M (10–200) plausible 3D human mesh predictions parameterized by SMPL pose (24×3) and shape (10) vectors, with evaluation via min-MPJPE and min-PVE over the candidate set. The HMR-Scorer component consumes UVD coordinates of 29 projected body keypoints plus multi-scale image features (global ViT-Base tokens and local pixel-aligned features sampled at reprojected 2D joint positions) to output a scalar quality score in [0,1].

## Method

ADHMR is a two-stage framework. First, HMR-Scorer is trained as a reward model that maps UVD keypoint coordinates and multi-scale image features through a transformer encoder with cross-attention (global features as key/value, keypoint features as query), using a RankNet-style probabilistic ranking loss over four HMR metrics (PVE, MPJPE, PA-PVE, PA-MPJPE) to learn relative quality ordering. Second, HMR-Scorer automatically ranks M predictions from the frozen HypoNet diffusion base model to synthesize a preference dataset of winner/loser pairs, which is then used to fine-tune the denoiser via the Diffusion-DPO (DDPO) objective operating on the full denoising trajectory. The key insight is that DPO optimizes relative prediction quality rather than absolute reconstruction error, making it robust to noisy pseudo-labels in in-the-wild data, while the scorer eliminates the need for manual preference annotation. As a secondary contribution, HMR-Scorer is applied to filter low-quality pseudo-labeled training samples (threshold τ=0.6), improving several SOTA HMR models even with fewer training data.

## Results

On the in-the-wild 3DPW benchmark, ADHMR (ITW) with M=200 predictions achieves PVE 60.5 mm, MPJPE 52.6 mm, and PA-MPJPE 30.8 mm, outperforming the base HypoNet model (M=200) by 11.4 mm in PVE and 9.2 mm in MPJPE. Notably, ADHMR (ITW) with only M=10 predictions (MPJPE 61.3 mm) surpasses the base model with M=200 predictions (MPJPE 61.8 mm), demonstrating a 20× efficiency gain. On Human3.6M, ADHMR (M=200) achieves MPJPE 35.8 mm and PA-MPJPE 24.1 mm, improving over the base model by 1.6 mm and 1.2 mm respectively. For HMR-Scorer evaluation, the proposed scorer achieves PLCC 0.63 and SRCC 0.62 on GTA-Human, outperforming the ScoreHypo reward model (PLCC 0.52, SRCC 0.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADHMR consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across both 3DPW and Human3.6M. On 3DPW, ADHMR (ITW, M=200) reduces MPJPE from 61.8 mm (HypoNet base) to 52.6 mm, a 9.2 mm improvement, and beats the best prior probabilistic method HuManiFlow (M=100, MPJPE 65.1 mm) by 12.5 mm. The most striking result is that ADHMR (ITW) with only M=10 predictions (MPJPE 61.3 mm) matches or exceeds the base model with M=200 (MPJPE 61.8 mm), indicating the preference optimization substantially improves per-prediction quality. Among deterministic methods, ADHMR (ITW) surpasses Zolly (MPJPE 65.0 mm) and HMR2.0a (MPJPE 70.0 mm) by 12.4 mm and 17.4 mm respectively on 3DPW. On Human3.6M, ADHMR (M=200) achieves MPJPE 35.8 mm, outperforming the best deterministic baseline HMR2.0a (44.8 mm) by 9.0 mm. For the HMR-Scorer evaluation (Table 1), the proposed scorer achieves PLCC 0.63/SRCC 0.62 on GTA-Human, surpassing the ScoreHypo reward model (0.52/0.49) and its own ablations (HMR-Scorer-2D: 0.59/0.58; HMR-Scorer-P: 0.30/0.28), confirming that UVD-coordinate input with multiscale image features is critical for accurate quality assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADHMR builds directly on the diffusion-based HMR base model HypoNet (ScoreHypo, Xu et al. 2024), which generates multiple plausible mesh predictions via a denoising process but suffers from 2D–3D misalignment due to the absence of reprojection loss in early denoising steps. The method adapts the Direct Preference Optimization framework (Rafailov et al. 2024; Wallace et al. 2024) from NLP and text-to-image alignment to the HMR domain, but departs from prior DPO applications by replacing human-annotated preference pairs with an automatically constructed preference dataset generated by a purpose-built reward model (HMR-Scorer). This scorer, which uses UVD joint coordinates and multi-scale ViT image features with pixel-aligned local sampling, is itself a new architectural contribution not present in prior HMR or score-prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that preference optimization can improve diffusion-based HMR without requiring clean 3D ground truth, making it applicable to in-the-wild settings where pseudo-labels are noisy. The HMR-Scorer's ability to assess prediction quality without 3D annotations opens a practical path for automated data curation, as shown by improved performance of three SOTA models trained on fewer, cleaner samples. A notable open question is whether the DPO-based alignment generalizes beyond SMPL to more expressive body models (SMPL-X, full-body with hands/face) and whether the preference signal degrades when the base model's prediction diversity is very low. The approach also raises the broader question of whether learned reward models can substitute for explicit geometric losses in other 3D reconstruction tasks.
