## Problem

Existing long-term time series forecasting methods either ignore frequency-domain information entirely or process different frequency components independently, leaving cross-frequency interactions unmodeled. Additionally, while timestamps are increasingly used as auxiliary features, their inherent periodic structure—specifically intra-period dependencies and inter-period correlations—remains insufficiently exploited by prior embedding or attention-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series window of length τ=96 (N variables) and future timestamp information as input, with the time series normalized via instance normalization before processing. Timestamps are decomposed into five hierarchical features (MinuteOfHour, HourOfDay, DayOfWeek, MonthOfYear, SeasonOfYear) and reshaped into 2D tensors using a fixed period length P=24. The output is a prediction of τ' future steps (τ' ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}) in the original scale after inverse normalization.

## Method

CFPT is a dual-branch architecture combining a Cross-Frequency Interaction (CFI) branch and a Periodic-Aware Timestamp Modeling (PTM) branch. The CFI branch applies DFT to split the signal into high- and low-frequency components, processes each through separate linear projections, then uses a coupling network of L layers where each layer applies multiplicative (element-wise × exp(MLP)) and additive (MLP) interactions between the two frequency representations, before reconstructing the prediction via IDFT. The PTM branch maps hierarchical timestamp features into the N-dimensional variable space, reshapes them into 2D tensors of shape N×P×⌈τ'/P⌉, and applies 2D convolution with kernel k×k to simultaneously capture intra-period dependencies and inter-period correlations. The two branch outputs are fused via a fixed weighted sum (γ·CFI + (1−γ)·PTM), and the model is trained end-to-end with L2 loss under instance normalization.

## Results

CFPT achieves state-of-the-art long-term forecasting performance on 6 out of 7 benchmark datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, ECL, Weather) for both MSE and MAE, with lookback window τ=96 and horizons {96, 192, 336, 720}. Headline averaged results include MSE 0.374 / MAE 0.393 on ETTm1, MSE 0.164 / MAE 0.259 on ECL, and MSE 0.240 / MAE 0.267 on Weather. The only dataset where CFPT does not rank first is Traffic, where iTransformer achieves a lower MSE (0.428 vs. 0.470) and MAE (0.282 vs. 0.289). CFPT's advantage is most pronounced on ECL, where it outperforms the second-best method (TimeXer, MSE 0.171) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFPT outperforms all 8 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets for both MSE and MAE, with the sole exception being Traffic, where iTransformer (MSE 0.428, MAE 0.282) and TimeXer (MSE 0.466, MAE 0.287) both edge out CFPT (MSE 0.470, MAE 0.289). The largest margins are on ECL, where CFPT (MSE 0.164) beats the next-best TimeXer (0.171) by ~4% and significantly outperforms frequency-based methods like FilterNet (0.201) and FEDformer (0.214). On the ETT series, CFPT consistently leads by small but steady margins over TimeXer and FilterNet (e.g., ETTm1: 0.374 vs. 0.382 vs. 0.384 MSE), while the gap widens substantially against transformer-based baselines like iTransformer (0.407) and PatchTST (0.387). Linear baselines DLinear and RLinear are clearly outperformed across all datasets, with DLinear showing particularly poor results on ETTh2 (MSE 0.559 vs. CFPT's 0.364). The results suggest that CFPT's dual-branch design—combining cross-frequency interaction with periodic-aware timestamp modeling—provides the most consistent advantage on datasets with strong periodic structure (ECL, ETT series) while remaining competitive on the high-dimensional Traffic dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFPT carves new ground at the intersection of two underexplored directions in time series forecasting: cross-frequency interaction modeling and periodic-aware timestamp exploitation. On the frequency side, it departs from prior methods that either ignore frequency (NoFreq), treat all frequencies uniformly (Unified-Freq), discard high frequencies (Only Low-Freq), or combine them via weighted summation (Weighted-Freq) by introducing explicit coupling layers that model bidirectional interactions between high- and low-frequency components. On the timestamp side, it repurposes the 2D convolution idea from TimesNet (which applies it to data reshaped by period) and redirects it toward timestamp features, using period-based slicing to capture intra-period and inter-period structure. The overall architecture is a dual-branch design that integrates these two complementary signals through a simple weighted sum, rather than building on a single prior backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, lightweight framework (modest parameter counts, ~0.8s/iter) that achieves SOTA on 6 of 7 standard benchmarks, offering practitioners a strong baseline that explicitly leverages both spectral structure and calendar periodicity. A key limitation is the fixed period length P=24, which restricts applicability to datasets with non-daily periodicity; the authors acknowledge that adaptive period detection is needed for broader deployment. The finding that future timestamps become increasingly important over longer horizons (gap widens from 96 to 720 steps) is a useful practical insight for forecasting system design. Open questions include whether the coupling mechanism generalizes to more than two frequency bands and whether the 2D timestamp convolution can be made period-agnostic.
