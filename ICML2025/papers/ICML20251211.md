## Problem

Computing the exact (unregularized) Wasserstein barycenter of discretized probability distributions in dimensions d>1 remains a major computational bottleneck: existing Wasserstein gradient descent methods require solving n optimal transport problems per iteration, while entropic-regularized Sinkhorn-type algorithms introduce visually undesirable blurring at high resolutions. No scalable algorithm with provable convergence guarantees existed for the unregularized barycenter on large grids (e.g., 1024×1024 images).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251211/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n absolutely continuous probability density functions discretized on a fixed m-point grid over a compact domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d (demonstrated on 2D images up to 1024×1024 and 3D volumes at 200×200×200) and produces the Wasserstein barycenter density on the same grid. The densities must be bounded away from zero and infinity (0 < a ≤ μ_i(x) ≤ b < ∞) for the convergence theory to apply, and the algorithm is limited to compact domains in 2D or 3D.

## Method

The paper recasts the unregularized barycenter problem as a nonconvex-concave minimax optimization and proposes the Wasserstein-Descent H¹-Ascent (WDHA) algorithm, which alternates between two geometrically distinct updates: a Wasserstein gradient descent step on the barycenter ν (pushing mass along the map id − τ∇φ̄) and a homogeneous Sobolev H¹ gradient ascent step on each Kantorovich potential φ_i (using the inverse Laplacian (−Δ)⁻¹ to compute the H¹ gradient). The critical design choice is the H¹ gradient for the dual update, which is an isometric dual embedding of the OT earthmoving cost ‖μ−ν‖²_{H^{-1}} = ∫‖∇φ‖², ensuring stability and enabling an O(1/T) convergence rate to stationary points matching Euclidean nonconvex-strongly-concave GDA theory. In practice, the expensive projection onto the strongly-convex/smooth function class F_{α,β} is replaced by a second convex conjugate (φ)**, yielding O(m log m) per-iteration time and O(m) space complexity—dramatically better than the O(m³) time and O(m²) space of LP-based OT solvers.

## Results

On the ground-truth uniform disk barycenter problem (1024×1024 grid), WDHA achieves a Wasserstein distance of 3.758×10⁻⁸ to the true barycenter, orders of magnitude better than CWB (2.98×10⁻⁴) and DSB (1.2×10⁻⁴). WDHA also attains the lowest barycenter functional value (9.001×10⁻²) and lowest L² distance (0.4869). On the 1024×1024 synthetic shapes experiment, WDHA runs in 676 s versus 3,731 s (CWB) and 7,249 s (DSB) while achieving the smallest functional value (74.5791). On 100 high-resolution 500×500 handwritten digit-8 images, WDHA completes in 3,299 s compared to 10,808 s (CWB) and 11,186 s (DSB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WDHA dominates both entropic-regularized baselines on the primary 2D ground-truth benchmark: its Wasserstein distance to the true barycenter (3.758×10⁻⁸) is roughly 4–5 orders of magnitude smaller than CWB (2.98×10⁻⁴) and DSB (1.2×10⁻⁴), and it also achieves the lowest L² distance and barycenter functional value. In terms of runtime on 1024×1024 grids, WDHA is approximately 5.5× faster than CWB and 10.7× faster than DSB, and the gap widens on the 500×500 handwritten-digit experiment (3,299 s vs. ~11,000 s). Visually, WDHA produces sharp-edged barycentric shapes, whereas CWB and DSB exhibit blurring that persists even after thresholding 10% of the mass. The one notable exception is the 1D truncated-Gaussian experiment in the appendix, where DSB achieves a smaller average W₂ distance (1.225×10⁻⁵) than WDHA's Algorithm 3 (7.610×10⁻⁵), suggesting that entropic regularization can be advantageous in very low-dimensional settings where the unregularized problem is already well-conditioned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the H1-gradient ascent framework of Jacobs & Léger (2020) for solving the Kantorovich dual problem, and on the nonconvex-concave minimax convergence theory of Lin et al. (2020) for Euclidean GDA algorithms. It departs from prior Wasserstein barycenter methods (Zemel & Panaretos, 2019; Chewi et al., 2020) that require computing n optimal transport maps per iteration, and from entropic-regularized Sinkhorn-type approaches (CWB, DSB) that introduce blurring artifacts. The key conceptual contribution is unifying two distinct optimization geometries—Wasserstein gradient for the primal barycenter and homogeneous Sobolev H1 gradient for the dual potentials—into a single primal-dual coordinate descent-ascent algorithm, which had not been previously combined in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WDHA algorithm achieves O(m log m) per-iteration complexity for exact (unregularized) Wasserstein barycenter computation, a substantial improvement over O(m^3) LP-based solvers, making high-resolution (1024×1024) barycenter computation practical. The O(1/T) convergence rate to a stationary point, matching the Euclidean nonconvex-concave minimax rate, provides the first such guarantee for an exact barycenter algorithm. Limitations include restriction to 2D/3D compact domains, a technical assumption of uniformly bounded iterates that remains unproven, and the fact that the practical Algorithm 4 (using double convex conjugate instead of projection) loses the strong convexity/smoothness guarantees. The divergence of the L2-descent variant (Appendix C.4) underscores that the Wasserstein geometry is not merely a design choice but essential for stability.
