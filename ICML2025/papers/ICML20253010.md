## Problem

Subset selection under partition constraints for 'close-to-submodular' objectives (e.g., weakly DR-submodular or (γ,β)-weakly submodular functions) is hindered by existing distorted local-search methods that require prior knowledge of hard-to-estimate structural parameters (submodularity ratio, DR ratio) and incur prohibitive query complexities of Õ(1/ε⁶) and Õ(1/ε³). The paper asks whether a parameter-free, query-efficient algorithm can match the same approximation guarantees with significantly fewer function evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set function f: 2^V → R₊, a partition {V₁,…,V_K} of the ground set V into non-overlapping communities, and per-community budgets B_k (with 0 < B_k ≤ |V_k|), and produces a subset S satisfying |S ∩ V_k| ≤ B_k for all k. Unusually, the continuous relaxation learns a multinoulli (categorical) distribution per community rather than independent per-element probabilities, and the rounding from the continuous solution back to a discrete subset is lossless for any set function—no submodularity assumption is needed to preserve the expected function value.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multinoulli Extension (ME), which replaces the standard multilinear extension by learning one multinoulli distribution Multi(p_k) per community V_k and performing B_k independent draws from each, thereby inherently respecting the partition constraint and enabling lossless rounding for arbitrary set functions. The Multinoulli-SCG algorithm maximizes the ME via a two-step continuous greedy procedure: at each iteration it finds the optimal ascent direction by solving a discrete subset selection over the partition constraint, then updates the probability vector along that direction. To avoid expensive exact gradient computation, it employs a stochastic path-integrated differential estimator that approximates the Hessian of the ME through sampled random elements, requiring only O(1/ε²) function evaluations in total. Theoretical analysis establishes that the ME inherits weak DR-submodularity and upper-linearizability from the original set function, yielding (1−e^{−α})OPT−ε or φ(γ,β)OPT−ε approximation guarantees without requiring knowledge of α, γ, or β.

## Results

On video summarization (7 videos from VSUMM and web sources), Multinoulli-SCG achieves Top-1 utility on 5/7 videos and Multinoulli-SGA on 6/7, outperforming all three baselines. In terms of query efficiency, Multinoulli-SCG requires approximately 2 orders of magnitude fewer function evaluations than Distorted-LS-G (e.g., log₁₀ queries ≈ 8.5 vs. ≈ 10.4 on V1), while Multinoulli-SGA requires ~4 fewer orders (≈ 6.5). On the Bayesian A-optimal design (Boston Housing), Multinoulli-SGA achieves the best objective (61.745), followed by Multinoulli-SCG (61.456), Standard Greedy (61.295), and Distorted-LS-G (61.285). On the adversarial coverage maximization instance, Multinoulli-SCG matches Distorted-LS-G at the optimal value (e.g., 89.00 for n=50,k=10) while using ~2 fewer orders of magnitude in queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard Greedy (Khashayar & Manuel, 2019), both Multinoulli-SCG and Multinoulli-SGA consistently achieve higher utility across all 7 video summarization videos (e.g., 50.17 vs. 46.48 on V1) and on the Housing dataset (61.456/61.745 vs. 61.295), while using comparable or fewer queries. Against Residual-Greedy (Chen et al., 2018a), the proposed methods again dominate on video summarization (e.g., 54.44 vs. 52.02 on V5) and Housing (61.456/61.745 vs. 61.104). Against the strongest baseline Distorted-LS-G (Thiery & Ward, 2022), Multinoulli-SCG matches or slightly exceeds its utility on video summarization (e.g., 50.17 vs. 50.08 on V1; 47.20 vs. 46.79 on V7) and on Housing (61.456 vs. 61.285), while requiring roughly 2 orders of magnitude fewer function evaluations (log₁₀ ≈ 8.5 vs. ≈ 10.4). On the adversarial coverage instance, Multinoulli-SCG ties Distorted-LS-G at the optimal value (89.00 for n=50,k=10) with ~2 fewer orders of queries, whereas Multinoulli-SGA and Standard Greedy both get trapped at the local optimum (49.43/49.49 vs. 89.00). Multinoulli-SGA, despite its weaker worst-case guarantee (α²/(1+α²) vs. 1−e^(−α)), empirically matches or beats Multinoulli-SCG on video summarization and Housing while using the fewest queries of all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the continuous greedy framework of Calinescu et al. (2011) and the stochastic path-integrated differential estimator of Fang et al. (2018), but departs from the multilinear extension by introducing a Multinoulli E that structurally encodes partition constraints and provides lossless rounding for any set function, not just submodular ones. It also departs from the distorted local-search line (Thiery & Ward 2022; Lu et al. 2022) by eliminating the need for prior knowledge of submodularity or DR ratios and reducing query complexity from Õ(1/ε⁶) or Õ(1/ε³) to O(1/ε²), matching the information-theoretic lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lossless rounding property of the Multinoulli E for non-submodular functions resolves a gap that had been explicitly flagged as open by Thiery & Ward (2022), and the parameter-free O(1/ε²) guarantee is practically significant for black-box or expensive-expensive objectives. However, the stationary-point guarantee (α²/(1+α²)) is strictly suboptimal compared to the SCG guarantee (1−E^{−α}), and the empirical is limited to three small-scale applications (video summarization, coverage, A-optimal design on 506 points). Open questions include whether the E extends to richer constraint structures (general matroids, knapsacks, multiple partition layers) and whether the SCG's O(r³n²/ε²) query bound can be tightened for large-n regimes.
