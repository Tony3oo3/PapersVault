## Problem

Existing conditional mean independence (CMI) tests for multivariate responses and covariates suffer from three simultaneous limitations: performance degrades in high-dimensional settings, theoretical size guarantees are unavailable under general nonparametric assumptions, and power is trivial against local alternatives converging at the parametric n^{-1/2} rate. No prior method achieves all three desirable properties—high-dimensional robustness, valid asymptotic size, and nontrivial local power—simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253011/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. triples (X_i, Y_i, Z_i) where X ∈ R^{d_X} is the covariate whose predictive contribution is being tested, Y ∈ R^{d_Y} is the (potentially multivariate) response, and Z ∈ R^{d_Z} is the set of given covariates; in imaging applications X and Z are grayscale images (48×48 or 224×224) with specific regions masked. The output is a U-statistic test statistic T̂_n calibrated via B=1000 Rademacher wild-bootstrap replications, yielding a p-value and a reject/fail-to-reject decision at a user-specified level γ. The computational cost scales quadratically in sample size n and linearly in total dimensionality (d_X + d_Y + d_Z).

## Method

The paper introduces a population CMI measure Γ* expressed as an expectation of a product U(X,X')·V(Y,Y')·K_Z(Z,Z'), where U involves the conditional mean embedding g_X(z)=E[K_X(X,·)|Z=z] in an RKHS and V involves the centered response Y−g_Y(Z). To estimate g_X without directly fitting an RKHS-valued regression, a generative moment matching network (GMMN) is trained to sample from P_{X|Z}, and the CME is then approximated by Monte Carlo averaging of kernel evaluations over generated samples. A DNN estimates g_Y=E[Y|Z]. Two-fold sample splitting with cross-fitting ensures the plug-in statistic T̂_n is asymptotically equivalent to the oracle version, and the multiplicative structure of Γ* provides double robustness: the test remains valid as long as the product of the two nuisance estimation errors decays faster than n^{-1/2}, even when each individual error decays slowly. A Rademacher wild bootstrap calibrates the rejection threshold, and the resulting test achieves correct asymptotic size and nontrivial power against local alternatives outside an n^{-1/2}-neighborhood of H_0.

## Results

In the linear regression simulation (Example A1, d_X = d_Z = 25), the proposed test T̂_n achieves empirical sizes of 8.8%, 7.0%, and 5.4% at n = 200, 400, 800 (nominal 5%), and reaches 100% size-adjusted power at n = 800 under both sparse and dense alternatives. In the nonlinear simulation (Example A2), T̂_n achieves 100% power at n = 800 for sparse alternatives and 99.2% for dense alternatives. On the FER2013 facial expression task, T̂_n correctly identifies the nose and top-left corner as non-discriminative regions (p-values > 5%) while rejecting H0 for all other regions, consistent with VGG test accuracies. On UTKFace age estimation, T̂_n correctly identifies TL, nose, and mouth as non-critical regions while rejecting H0 for eyes and face.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cai et al. (2024) pMIT variants, T̂_n achieves substantially better size control: pMIT_M with DNN is severely oversized (13.2%–21.0% vs. T̂_n's 5.4%–8.8%), and T̂_n outperforms all pMIT variants in size-adjusted power at n = 800 (100% vs. 66.4%–100%). Against Lundborg et al. (2024) PCM, T̂_n dominates on both size (PCM is 0% — completely undersized — while T̂_n is near nominal) and power (T̂_n reaches 100% at n = 800 vs. PCM's 99.8% for sparse and 100% for dense in A1, but PCM's power is unreliable given its size distortion). Against Williamson et al. (2023) VIM, T̂_n has comparable size but substantially higher power (e.g., 100% vs. 52.8% at n = 800 for sparse A1; 99.2% vs. 31.2% for dense A2). Against Dai et al. (2022) DSP, T̂_n has far superior size control (DSP is 0% undersized) but DSP achieves slightly higher power at small n in the nonlinear Example A2 (e.g., 74.6% vs. 19.2% at n = 200 for sparse); however, T̂_n catches up to 100% by n = 800. Overall, T̂_n is the only method that simultaneously achieves accurate size, high power, and the ability to detect local alternatives at the n^{-1/2} rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CMI testing literature by combining three previously separate threads: Daudin's (1980) population measure for conditional association, the GMMN generative framework (Li et al., 2015) for estimating conditional mean embeddings, and the doubly robust U-statistic construction from Zhang et al. (2024) for conditional independence testing. The key departure from prior CMI tests is the multiplicative-form sample statistic that inherits double robustness from the population measure, allowing slow nonparametric estimation errors (from DNNs or GNNs) to not compromise asymptotic size control. Unlike kernel-smoothing-based tests that suffer the curse of dimensionality, or DNN-based tests (Williamson et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2022) that require ad hoc debiasing or sample splitting to handle degeneracy, this approach achieves size guarantees through the structural double robustness of the statistic itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first CMI test to simultaneously achieve high-dimensional performance, theoretical size guarantees, and nontrivial power against n^{-1/2} local alternatives — properties that no single prior method provides (see Table 1). The double robustness property is practically important: it means the test remains valid even when the conditional generator (GMMN) is only a rough approximation of P_{X|Z}, which is realistic for complex data like images. Open questions include extending to conditional quantile independence, developing variable selection with false discovery rate control, and whether the quadratic computational cost in n (from the U-statistic form) limits scalability to very large datasets. The imaging applications demonstrate practical utility but are limited to small subsamples (n=2000) due to computational constraints.
