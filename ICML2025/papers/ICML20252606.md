## Problem

In multi-player multi-armed bandits with heterogeneous rewards and collisions, maximizing total reward forces some players to sacrifice, creating unfairness. Prior max-min fairness algorithms (Bistritz et al., 2020; Leshem, 2025) suffer from regret bounds of O(log T log log T) with constants exponential in N and 1/Δ, lack lower bounds in N and K, and rely on restrictive assumptions (bounded rewards, K=N, distinct per-player means). This paper closes the gap by proving a tight O((N²+K)log T/Δ) regret upper bound matching a new Ω(max{N²,K}log T/Δ) lower bound, while relaxing those assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a fully decentralized setting with N players and K arms (N≤K) over T rounds; each player i observes only its own scalar reward r_{i,k}(t) (drawn from a 1-sub-Gaussian distribution with mean μ_{i,k}) and a binary collision indicator C_i(t) per round. Players periodically exchange reward estimates with each other (bounded communication of O(log T) phases), and the output at each round is a collision-free matching m(t) assigning one distinct arm to each player, collectively converging to the max-min fair matching m* that maximizes min_i μ_{i,m*_i}.

## Method

The Decentralized Fair Elimination (DFE) algorithm runs in exponentially growing phases, each consisting of three stages: (1) an elimination stage where all players compute a lower-bound max-min value γ̲_s from LCB indices and remove player-arm pairs whose UCB falls below γ̲_s or that cannot participate in any matching with all UCBs above γ̲_s; (2) an exploration stage using a novel assignment algorithm that covers all surviving pairs in at most N²+K matchings (versus NK naively) by constructing a base matching, then per-pair matchings for arms in the base, and a round-robin schedule for the remaining K−N arms with collision-avoiding fallbacks; (3) a communication stage where players exchange empirical means. The key insight is that elimination guarantees sub-optimal pairs are never re-explored in later phases, removing the forced exploration that caused the O(log T log log T) bound in prior ETC-based methods and yielding the optimal O(log T) dependence. The exploration assignment is shown to be order-optimal via a matching lower bound in the regret analysis.

## Results

The DFE algorithm achieves a regret upper bound of O((N²+K) log T / Δ), matching the derived lower bound of Ω(max{N², K} log T / Δ), which is optimal in all parameters T, N, K, and Δ. This significantly improves upon the previous best upper bound of O(exp(1/Δ) + K³ log T log log T) from Leshem (2025). In experiments with T=500,000 across (N,K) = (4,4), (10,10), and (10,15), DFE reduces cumulative regret by more than 90% compared to My Fair Bandit and more than 80% compared to the Leshem algorithm, while also converging substantially faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DFE against two prior max-min fair bandit algorithms: My Fair Bandit (Bistritz et al., 2020) and the Leshem algorithm (Leshem, 2025). DFE outperforms both baselines across all three experimental settings (N,K) = (4,4), (10,10), and (10,15), reducing cumulative regret by over 90% versus My Fair Bandit and over 80% versus Leshem. Both baselines use an explore-then-commit (ETC) method at each epoch, which forces continued exploration of already-identified sub-optimal arms, causing them to suffer regret continuously even after sufficient information is gathered. DFE avoids this by using an elimination mechanism that permanently removes sub-optimal player-arm pairs, allowing the algorithm to stop incurring regret on those pairs. Additionally, DFE's novel exploration assignment (Algorithm 2) covers all non-eliminated pairs in at most N²+K rounds rather than the naive NK rounds, further reducing unnecessary exploration regret. The theoretical improvement is also substantial: DFE removes the exponential dependence on 1/Δ and the log log T factor present in prior bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the max-min fairness MP-MAB formulations of Bistritz et al. (2020) and Leshem (2025), both of which use a phased explore-then-commit (ETC) structure in the decentralized heterogeneous setting. The key departure is replacing ETC with a phased elimination mechanism: sub-optimal player-arm pairs are identified via UCB/LCB thresholds and permanently removed, eliminating the need for the growing exploration lengths that forced the O(log T log log T) regret in prior work. The novel exploration assignment algorithm (achieving N²+K rounds instead of NK) and the tight Ω(max{N²,K} log T / Δ) lower bound are new contributions that close the gap left open by earlier analyses, which only bounded regret in T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching the upper and lower bounds on all parameters (N, K, Δ, T), this paper provides a complete characterization of the max-min MP-MAB problem, resolving a gap that had persisted since the initial formulation. The elimination-based exploration technique is identified as potentially generalizable to other heterogeneous MP-MAB variants with collisions, offering a reusable methodological tool. Open questions highlighted by the authors include robustness to non-stationary environments, incentive compatibility (whether players will actually follow the algorithm), and extension to settings with a central server or different collision models. The practical relevance is primarily in wireless resource allocation and multi-agent scheduling where fairness guarantees are needed.
