## Problem

On-device federated fine-tuning of LLMs must simultaneously handle computational resource heterogeneity (users with different device capacities requiring different model sizes) and data heterogeneity (users with distinct topic preferences and linguistic patterns). Prior methods address only one of these challenges: HetLoRA and FlexLoRA handle varying LoRA ranks but assume homogeneous data, while PCL and pFedMoE handle data heterogeneity but require identical model architectures across all users. No existing solution provides token-level personalized collaboration that adapts to both constraints.

## Input / Output

Each user's model receives text tokens as input and produces next-token predictions, with a per-layer, per-token router (single-layer MLP) deciding how to weight the output of a shared generalist LoRA expert against a variable number of local specialist LoRA experts. The number of specialists n_i differs across users to match their computational budgets, while the generalist is always present and aggregated server-side. A small validation set (sampled from the target distribution, potentially different from the training distribution) is used exclusively for router updates, decoupling routing decisions from expert training.

## Method

CoMiGS formulates the Mixture-of-Experts learning objective as a bi-level optimization problem: the upper level minimizes validation loss over routing parameters Φ, while the lower level minimizes training loss over expert parameters Θ (comprising shared generalist LoRA modules and user-specific specialist LoRA modules). This hierarchical structure reflects the natural leader-follower relationship where the router assigns tokens to experts and experts refine their specializations under the router's guidance. The problem is solved via alternating minimization, where expert parameters are updated on training data (with generalist weights aggregated across users via a trusted server) and routing parameters are updated less frequently on a separate validation set, ensuring the router adapts to the target distribution rather than the training distribution. The paper provides convergence guarantees showing linear convergence under a contraction condition (λ₁·λ₂ < 1) and global linear convergence for linear experts with sufficient regularization. The approach adds only 1.25% computational and memory overhead over FedAvg while halving communication costs, since only generalist weights are exchanged.

## Results

On in-distribution tasks with GPT-2-124M, CoMiGS-1G1S achieves test perplexity of 47.19 on Multilingual Wikipedia (vs. 52.27 for PCL and 54.53 for FedAvg) and 21.79 on SlimPajama (vs. 23.27 for FedAvg). On Llama 3.2-1B, CoMiGS-1G1S reaches 18.37 perplexity on Common Corpus (vs. 19.65 for PCL and 21.95 for FedAvg). On out-of-distribution AG News, CoMiGS-1G1S (33.53 GPT-2; 16.31 Llama) is competitive but slightly trails FedAvg (31.84; 15.86), while the ablation CoMiGS-2G (both generalists) achieves 31.18 on GPT-2, showing the method closely tracks the best-performing configuration across scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMiGS-1G1S outperforms FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) on all in-distribution tasks (e.g., 47.19 vs. 54.53 on Multilingual; 21.79 vs. 23.27 on SlimPajama; 18.37 vs. 21.95 on Common Corpus) but trails it on out-of-distribution AG News (33.53 vs. 31.84 on GPT-2; 16.31 vs. 15.86 on Llama). Against PCL (Wagner et al., 2024), CoMiGS wins on every dataset, with the largest gap on Multilingual (47.19 vs. 52.27) and Common Corpus (18.37 vs. 19.65), attributed to token-level routing versus client-level collaboration weights. pFedMoE (Yi et al., 2024) is consistently outperformed across all settings (e.g., 47.19 vs. 57.45 on Multilingual; 21.79 vs. 25.40 on SlimPajama), which the authors attribute to pFedMoE updating routing and expert parameters simultaneously on training data rather than using a separate validation set. FDLoRA (Qi et al., 2024) is beaten on in-distribution tasks (21.79 vs. 22.71 on SlimPajama; 18.37 vs. 22.11 on Common Corpus) but is competitive on out-of-distribution AG News (33.53 vs. 33.61). For heterogeneous model setups (Table 2), CoMiGS-1GXS outperforms both HetLoRA (Cho et al., 2023) and FlexLoRA (Bai et al., 2024) across all configurations, e.g., 46.48 vs. 57.76 (HetLoRA) and 77.71 (FlexLoRA) on Multilingual with (2,2,4,4) experts. In the extended comparison (Table 5), CoMiGS also surpasses FFA-LoRA (Sun et al., 2024) and FedSA (Guo et al., 2024), which do not use routing at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mixture-of-Experts architecture (Fedus et al., 2022) and LoRA-based federated fine-tuning of LLMs, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it introduces a bi-level optimization formulation where the router is optimized on a separate validation set (reflecting the target distribution) while experts are optimized on training data, in contrast to standard MoE training where both are updated jointly on the same data. Second, it splits experts into shared 'generalists' (aggregated across users) and local 'specialists' (kept per-user), which no prior federated LLM method combines: HetLoRA/FlexLoRA handle model heterogeneity but not data heterogeneity, while pFedMoE/FDLoRA handle data heterogeneity but require identical architectures. The alternating minimization solver draws on bilevel optimization theory (Chen et al., 2021) and provides convergence guarantees under contraction assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMiGS is the first framework to simultaneously address both computational resource heterogeneity and data heterogeneity in collaborative on-device LLM fine-tuning, a gap the authors explicitly identify as unsolved. The practical overhead is minimal (+1.25% compute/memory, −50% communication vs. FedAvg), making it deployable on edge devices. However, the method requires a per-user validation set for router updates, has no robustness guarantees against adversarial attackers in the aggregation step, and is limited to LoRA-based fine-tuning of relatively small models (≤1B parameters). Open questions include whether the bi-level formulation scales to larger MoE architectures, whether differential privacy can be integrated into the generalist aggregation, and whether the validation-set requirement can be relaxed in settings where users cannot afford to hold out data.
