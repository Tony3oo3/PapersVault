## Problem

Existing sub-Gaussian thinning algorithms like Kernel Halving and Compress provably outperform uniform subsampling, but their guarantees are restricted to specific kernel classes and exhibit pessimistic dependence on the ambient data dimension. The paper asks whether one can obtain strong thinning guarantees for arbitrary kernels and distributions by exploiting the approximate low-rank structure that is common in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250254/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The thinning framework takes a collection of n_in points (data points in R^d, query/key/value vectors for attention, stochastic gradients, or two sample sets for hypothesis testing) and returns a subset of n_out ≥ √n_in points. Quality is measured via kernel MMD (a Mahalanobis distance between input and output probability vectors weighted by the kernel matrix) or kernel max seminorm (the maximum absolute row of K(p_in − p_out)). The sub-Gaussian parameter ν of the thinning algorithm, combined with the eigenvalue decay of the kernel matrix, determines the achievable error bound.

## Method

The core contribution is a low-rank analysis (Theorem 1) showing that for any (K, ν, δ)-sub-Gaussian thinning algorithm, the squared MMD is bounded by O(ν²[e²r + e log(1/δ′)] + λ_{r+1}(1/n_out − 1/n_in)), where r is a freely optimizable approximate-rank parameter and λ_{r+1} is the (r+1)-th eigenvalue of the kernel matrix. This yields O(ν) MMD for finite-rank kernels and adapts to intrinsic dimension for kernels with rapid eigenvalue decay (e.g., Gaussian kernels on manifolds). The paper instantiates this analysis in three applications: Thinformer, which defines a new key-value attention kernel k_att and uses KH-COMPRESS to subselect key-value pairs, achieving n^{-a} error decay (vs. n^{-a/2} for KDEformer) at O(dn^{1+a}) runtime; LKH-SGD, which uses linear-kernel halving to reorder stochastic gradients and replaces the Θ(d) dimension penalty with an ε-rank term, matching minimax lower bounds; and a low-rank analysis of Compress Then Test, proving near-linear-time two-sample tests with deep kernels that match quadratic-time MMD power up to logarithmic inflation factors.

## Results

On T2T-ViT ImageNet classification, Thinformer achieves 82.18% Top-1 accuracy (best among all approximations) while running 9x faster than exact attention (2.06ms vs 18.48ms per layer). On BigGAN image generation, Thinformer achieves the best FID (30.54) and IS (57.12) while running 2.1x faster than exact attention. In the Higgs boson two-sample test, CTT matches the detection power of a full quadratic-time MMD test in a fraction of the runtime, dominating both SUBSAMPLING and W-BLOCK baselines across the entire time-power trade-off curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thinformer outperforms all four practical attention baselines on both accuracy and speed: it beats Performer (80.56%) by 1.62 points in Top-1 accuracy while running 23% faster, beats Reformer (81.47%) by 0.71 points while running 3.8x faster, and beats both KDEformer (82.00%) and ScatterBrain (82.05%) by 0.13–0.18 points while running 2.6–3.3x faster. On BigGAN, Thinformer achieves the best FID (30.54) and IS (57.12), surpassing KDEformer (FID 30.70, IS 56.91) and dramatically outperforming ScatterBrain (FID 38.47, IS 36.84) and Reformer (FID 72.23, IS 19.14). Theoretically, Thinformer's error bound decays as n^{-a} versus n^{-a/2} for KDEformer and n^{-a/6} for HyperAttention at the same subquadratic runtime, and it eliminates the KDEformer penalty parameter τ. For SGD training, LKH-SGD strongly outperforms random reshuffling and the theoretically justified CD-GraB: SBW variant in convergence speed, while matching CD-GraB: Greedy on test accuracy. In two-sample testing, CTT dominates both SUBSAMPLING (wild-bootstrap MMD) and W-BLOCK across the entire time-power trade-off curve for Higgs boson detection, matching full MMD power at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the kernel thinning line of work (Dwivedi & Mackey, 2024; Shetty et al., 2022) and the Compress Then Test framework (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2023), but introduces a fundamentally different analytical lens: a low-rank decomposition of the kernel matrix that yields quality guarantees for ANY kernel and ANY distribution, rather than the distribution-specific or kernel-restricted analyses of prior work. The Gram-Schmidt walk (Bansal et al., 2018) is repurposed to derive two new thinning algorithms (GS-THIN, GS-COMPRESS) with the smallest sub-Gaussian constants, and the Harvey & Samadi (2014) herding construction is adapted to translate thinning into SGD reordering rules. The key conceptual departure is showing that the pessimistic dimension dependence in prior guarantees (e.g., Θ(d) in SGD, polynomial in d for Gaussian kernels) can be replaced by an effective rank or ε-rank parameter that adapts to the data structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low-rank framework provides a single, general tool that simultaneously improves guarantees across three disparate applications—attention approximation, SGD acceleration, and two-sample testing—resolving the open dimension-gap problem in permuted SGD (Cha et al., 2023) and delivering the first non-asymptotic power guarantees for deep kernel testing. Practically, Thinformer achieves the best ImageNet accuracy (82.18%) while running ~9× faster than exact attention in T2T-ViT, and LKH-SGD outperforms random reshuffling with no hyperparameters to tune. Open questions include whether the low-rank assumption is too restrictive for truly high-rank kernels (e.g., very deep random features), and whether the ε-rank parameter in SGD can be estimated or bounded more tightly in practice without access to the full gradient matrix.
