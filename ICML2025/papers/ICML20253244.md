## Problem

Existing digraph GNNs capture only unidirectional edge relationships (hitting time) during message passing, neglecting the mutual path asymmetry between nodes. In directed graphs, the path from node A to B can differ substantially from B to A, and this asymmetry encodes critical relationship strength information, yet no prior method directly encodes this mutual dependency into node representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed graph G=(V,E,X) with N nodes, M edges, and d-dimensional node features, along with a small set of labeled nodes for semi-supervised training. It first rewires the graph by adding at most two bidirectional edges per node (based on feature-similarity ordering) to guarantee irreducibility and aperiodicity, then computes a dense N-by-N commute time matrix. The output is per-node class predictions via a final MLP+Softmax head; the dense commute matrix is the primary memory bottleneck, scaling quadratically in N.

## Method

CGNN introduces a digraph Laplacian (DiLap) defined as the divergence of the gradient operator on directed graph signals, incorporating the transition matrix P and the Perron vector to preserve both local connectivity and global structural importance. A key lemma establishes that the fundamental matrix Z (which encodes all hitting and commute times) equals the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the sparse Weighted DiLap, enabling efficient computation via randomized truncated SVD in O(q|E|) time rather than O(N^3) dense inversion. Commute times are then converted to proximity weights via exp(-C), masked by the directed adjacency matrix for incoming and outgoing edges separately, and used to modulate neighbor contributions in a DirGNN-style message passing scheme. A feature-similarity-based graph rewiring step ensures the Markov chain is irreducible and aperiodic while adding minimal edges, preserving the original graph semantics and sparsity.

## Results

CGNN achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy on 6 out of 8 directed graph benchmarks, with headline improvements of +2.64% over DirGNN on Squirrel (77.83% vs. 75.19%) and +4.17% over the best baseline on Citeseer (71.59% vs. 67.60%). On the two remaining datasets, CGNN is competitive: it trails DiGCL by 0.45% on CoraML (77.08% vs. 77.53%) and DirGNN by 1.06% on Snap-Patents (72.89% vs. 73.95%). The overall time complexity is O((Ld²+q)|E|), effectively linear in edge count with q=5, making it feasible for large-scale graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGNN outperforms its direct architectural parent DirGNN (Rossi et al., 2023) on 6 of 8 datasets, with the largest gains on Citeseer (+5.02%), Squirrel (+2.64%), and Roman-Empire (+1.64%), while DirGNN retains a narrow edge on Snap-Patents (73.95% vs. 72.89%). Among digraph-specific baselines, CGNN surpasses DiGCL (Tong et al., 2021) on all datasets except CoraML where DiGCL leads by 0.45%, and beats DUPLEX (Ke et al., 2024) by up to +20.23% on Squirrel. General GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) and non-local GNNs (APPNP, MixHop, GPRGNN, GCNII) are consistently outperformed, with GPRGNN being the closest general baseline on AM-Photo (90.23% vs. 90.42%). The paper notes that on citation networks (CoraML, Snap-Patents) where mutual path dependencies are absent due to temporal ordering, commute-time weighting provides little benefit, explaining the two losses. Overall, CGNN's advantage is most pronounced on heterophilic graphs (Squirrel, Chameleon, Roman-Empire) where asymmetric mutual relationships carry the most discriminative signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGNN extends DirGNN (Rossi et al., 2023), which introduced direction-aware message passing by separating incoming and outgoing neighbor aggregation, by adding commute-time-based proximity weights to modulate neighbor contributions. The theoretical backbone draws on Li & Zhang's (2012) digraph Laplacian and fundamental matrix theory, but the paper's DiLap formulation is novel in being derived from graph signal processing principles (divergence of gradient on digraphs) rather than the spectral or in-degree-based constructions of Chung (2005) or Singh et al. (2016). The key conceptual departure from prior digraph GNNs (MagNet, DiGCN, DiGCL, DUPLEX) is shifting focus from unidirectional edge directionality to mutual path dependencies, and the feature-similarity-based graph rewiring for ensuring irreducibility is a practical alternative to the dense PageRank-style teleportation matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine gap in digraph GNNs: existing methods capture unidirectional edge direction but ignore the asymmetry of mutual paths, which carries important relationship-strength information (e.g., follower-to-celebrity vs. celebrity-to-follower). The commute time weighting provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to address this, with the DiLap-based computation maintaining sparsity via randomized SVD. However, the dense O(N²) commute time matrix remains a significant scalability bottleneck, and the paper itself notes the method is most beneficial in networks with prevalent mutual relationships (social, web) rather than citation networks. A promising open direction is local, neighborhood-restricted commute time computation to avoid the global dense matrix.
