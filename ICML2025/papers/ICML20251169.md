## Problem

Existing clipart animation methods like AniClipart model spatial deformations via Bezier trajectories and ARAP but lack a mechanism for temporal noise correction, producing abrupt transitions and geometric distortions during complex motions. Meanwhile, general-purpose T2V/I2V diffusion models fail on clipart due to the statistical mismatch between natural video and flat clipart styles. The paper addresses the intertwined gap of temporal consistency and geometric integrity in free-form character animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static clipart image (resized to 256×256, sourced from AniClipart or Freepik) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired motion. It detects keypoints via UniPose, constructs a skeleton, and optimizes 8–11 cubic Bezier control points per keypoint over 24 frames. Output is a 24-frame animated video rendered through DiffVG (for SVG inputs) or pixel warping within mesh triangles (for bitmaps), with the full pipeline running in ~40 minutes on a single V100 GPU.

## Method

FlexiClip decomposes the per-frame mesh Jacobian into a spatial component (from Bezier keypoint posing) and a temporal corrective component, then models the temporal Jacobian as a continuous-time pfODE whose drift is parameterized by a 3-layer MLP conditioned on attention-encoded windows of spatial and past temporal Jacobians. A flow matching loss inspired by GFlowNet detailed balance compares the score functions of frames rendered with the full Jacobian versus the spatial-only Jacobian, progressively reducing accumulated temporal noise while preserving local structure. Training combines Video SDS from a pretrained ModelScope T2V model (guidance scale 50, UNet Jacobian omitted per DreamFusion) with the flow matching loss (λ=15), optimizing Bezier parameters, attention networks, and ODE parameters end-to-end over 700 Adam steps. The key insight is that treating temporal correction as a deterministic continuous-time flow (rather than per-frame discrete optimization) prevents drift and enables smooth, locality-preserving deformations even under rapid non-rigid motion.

## Results

On the AniClipart/Freepik clipart benchmark (30+ images, 256×256), FlexiClip achieves the best visual identity preservation (CLIP score 0.9563) and text-video alignment (X-CLIP score 0.2102), outperforming the closest competitor AniClipart (0.9401 / 0.2075) and all T2V/I2V baselines. On animation quality, FlexiClip yields higher motion vibrancy (MV 25.33 vs 20.87), lower temporal inconsistency (TC 8.14 vs 8.51), lower deformation smoothness error (DS 13.76 vs 18.49), and higher animation energy (AE 113.44 vs 75.23) than AniClipart, though geometric deviation is slightly higher (GD 52.34 vs 50.98) due to the absence of ARAP. In a 30-participant user study over 55 clipart images, FlexiClip is preferred 94.90% of the time for identity preservation, 94.54% for text-video alignment, and 93.81% for smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiClip outperforms all 10 baselines on both CLIP and X-CLIP scores. Against the closest clipart-specific method AniClipart, FlexiClip gains +0.0162 CLIP and +0.0027 X-CLIP, and additionally wins on MV (+4.46), TC (−0.37), DS (−4.73), and AE (+38.21), though it trails slightly on GD (+1.36) because it omits ARAP deformation. Against the sketch-animation method Gal23, FlexiClip leads by +0.1168 CLIP and +0.0237 X-CLIP. Among general-purpose T2V/I2V models, the strongest competitor is LTXVideo (CLIP 0.9325, X-CLIP 0.2054), which FlexiClip surpasses by +0.0238 and +0.0048 respectively; DynamiCrafter is the weakest baseline, trailing FlexiClip by 0.1532 CLIP and 0.0370 X-CLIP. In the user study, FlexiClip is preferred over AniClipart by roughly 11–18 percentage points across all three subjective criteria, and over the best I2V model (LTXVideo) by 33+ points, confirming that the temporal Jacobian + pfODE + flow-matching pipeline delivers perceptually smoother and more text-aligned animations than both prior clipart methods and general video diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiClip extends AniClipart's framework of Bezier-curve keypoint trajectories with ARAP-style mesh deformation, but departs from it by replacing discrete per-frame optimization with a continuous-time temporal correction mechanism. Specifically, it reformulates the pfODE from Lim et al. (2023) and the dimension-preserving flow rescaling from de Albuquerque & Pearson (2024) into a temporal Jacobian ODE that incrementally corrects spatial deformations, and introduces a GFlowNet-inspired detailed-balance loss to constrain the correction to temporal noise only. The video SDS distillation pipeline follows DreamFusion-style gradient omission (Poole et al., 2022), and keypoint/skeleton extraction reuses UniPose, making the core novelty the temporal modeling layer rather than the spatial posing or rendering pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visible failure mode in clipart animation (temporal jitter and geometric distortion during rapid pose changes) with a principled continuous-time formulation, and the ablations confirm both the temporal Jacobian and flow matching loss contribute meaningfully. However, the scope remains narrow: 2D clipart only, 24-frame clips, 40-minute per-clip optimization on a V100, and dependence on a specific pre-trained T2V model (ModelScope). Open questions include whether the pfODE-based temporal correction generalizes to 3D mesh animation or longer sequences, whether the GFlowNet flow matching loss can be replaced by cheaper alternatives, and whether the approach scales to real-time or interactive settings. The modest quantitative gains over AniClipart (CLIP +0.016, X-CLIP +0.003) suggest the method's value is more in qualitative smoothness than in large metric jumps.
