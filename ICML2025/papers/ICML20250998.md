## Problem

While the expressiveness and learnability of transformers for regular language recognition have been studied, the training dynamics of how transformers actually learn these tasks remain largely uncharacterized. In particular, the even pairs problem has never been analyzed theoretically, and prior parity check studies either examined only the attention layer in isolation or analyzed a fixed number of training steps without proving full convergence. This paper fills that gap by providing a complete two-phase characterization of joint attention-and-linear-layer training dynamics, including convergence rates and implicit bias.

## Input / Output

The model consumes binary sequences of tokens {a, b} of variable length L ≤ L_max, where each token at position ℓ is embedded as a one-hot vector (e_{2ℓ-1} for 'a', e_{2ℓ} for 'b'), ensuring all token embeddings are mutually orthogonal. The one-layer transformer produces a single scalar whose sign (±1) is the predicted label: +1 for even pattern count, −1 for odd. For even pairs, the label depends only on whether the first and last tokens match (O(1) check); for parity check, it requires counting all 'b' tokens across the full sequence (O(L) check), making it fundamentally harder for a single attention pass.

## Method

The paper analyzes a one-layer transformer (softmax attention with scaling factor λ followed by a linear layer) trained via a two-phase gradient descent schedule: Phase 1 uses a scaled learning rate ηλ for the attention matrix W to drive rapid, mutually reinforcing growth of both layers, while Phase 2 reverts to standard GD where the attention layer stabilizes and the linear layer converges in direction to the max-margin hyperplane with logarithmic norm growth and O(1/t) loss decay. The key analytical tool is higher-order Taylor expansion to precisely track the coupling between attention and linear layer gradients during Phase 1, with the scaling factor λ chosen as Ω(L_max²) to bound attention perturbations. For the parity check problem, the paper introduces two CoT-based solutions: a zero-shot truncated CoT inference that reuses an even-pairs-trained transformer iteratively (mimicking a 2-state finite automaton), and a teacher-forced CoT training procedure that augments the CoT loss with an even-pairs regularization loss to prevent gradient vanishing and stabilize the two-phase dynamics.

## Results

The paper establishes a two-phase training dynamics for a one-layer transformer on even pairs and parity check tasks. In Phase 1 (t ≤ t₀ = O(1/(ηL_max))), the first token score grows linearly as Θ(ηt) while non-leading token scores decrease as −Θ(η²t²), and attention scores grow as Ω(η²t²/L), making the attention layer's outputs linearly separable. In Phase 2, the linear layer norm grows logarithmically (‖u_t‖ ≥ Ω(log t)) and converges in direction to the max-margin hyperplane, while the attention layer remains stable (‖W_t − W_{t₀}‖ ≤ O(1)). The loss converges sublinearly at rate O(L_max‖u*‖²/(η√t)), and experiments with L_max=6, L_0=4, η=0.1, λ=2 confirm the predicted token score and attention score dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no head-to-head empirical comparisons against competing methods. The closest prior works are Kim & Suzuki (2024b), which analyzed only the attention layer for parity check via CoT, and Wen et al. (2024), which studied three training steps without establishing full convergence. The present paper extends both by providing a complete two-phase characterization of joint attention-and-linear-layer training, proving convergence of the full process, and identifying the implicit bias (max-margin solution). The even pairs problem is studied for the first time theoretically. The experimental validation (Figures 2–3) uses synthetic binary sequences with L_max=6 and confirms the theoretical predictions on token score growth, attention score separation, and loss decay, but does not benchmark against alternative architectures or training methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on transformer training dynamics for regular language recognition, building most directly on Kim & Suzuki (2024b), who showed that CoT enables transformers to solve parity but analyzed only the attention layer in isolation. It also goes beyond Wen et al. (2024), which studied sample complexity for parity but analyzed only three training steps without establishing full convergence. The key departure is the joint analysis of attention and linear layer training dynamics using higher-order Taylor expansions to handle gradient coupling, combined with implicit bias arguments for the margin-maximization phase. The even pairs problem is introduced as a new theoretical target, while parity check is extended from prior attention-only analyses to full joint training with CoT under teacher forcing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of how a one-layer transformer learns regular language recognition tasks, revealing a universal two-phase structure (rapid feature alignment followed by max-margin convergence) that connects to SVM-like implicit bias. The zero-shot transfer from even pairs to parity check via truncated CoT inference is a practical insight suggesting that structural connections between seemingly different regular languages can be exploited without retraining. Limitations include the restriction to one-layer transformers, binary alphabets, and small sequence lengths (L_max=6 in experiments), leaving open whether these dynamics persist in multi-layer architectures or with more complex regular languages. The analytical techniques for handling joint attention-linear layer coupling could inform future studies of deeper transformer training.
