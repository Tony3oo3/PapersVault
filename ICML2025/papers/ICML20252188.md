## Problem

Teacher-guided language modeling for smaller student LLMs typically assumes the teacher and student share the same vocabulary, which prevents students from leveraging state-of-the-art or domain-specialized teacher models that use different tokenization schemes. This vocabulary mismatch causes two concrete failures: token sequences diverge (the same text is split into different numbers of tokens), and logit distributions become incomparable across different vocabulary spaces, making standard KL-divergence or Wasserstein-based distillation ineffective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a student model (TinyLlama 1.1B, 32K vocab), a frozen teacher model (7B-scale, e.g., Qwen2.5-Math with 150K vocab, DeepSeekMath with 100K vocab, or Mistral-ProXMath with 32K vocab), and a raw text corpus (OpenWebMath, ~15B tokens). The teacher and student may share as little as 6.32% of their vocabulary. The output is a continually pretrained student model; during training, the teacher is used only to compute per-token losses on the same text, not to generate new data or produce aligned logits. The method requires character-level offsets from both tokenizers, which are standard outputs of BPE/BBPE tokenizers.

## Method

VocAgnoLM addresses vocabulary mismatch through two components. First, Token-level Lexical Alignment uses character-level start/end offsets to establish a one-to-many mapping from each student token to the minimal contiguous range of teacher tokens that covers the same text span, computed via two binary searches per student token in O(N log M) time. Second, Teacher Guided Loss avoids the intractable problem of aligning logit distributions across different vocabulary spaces by instead using the teacher's per-token cross-entropy losses as a reweighting signal: for each student token, the difference between its own loss and the aggregated loss of its mapped teacher tokens determines whether it passes a top-k threshold (40%), and only selected tokens (plus unmapped special tokens) contribute to the training gradient. This token-level loss-based guidance replaces distribution-level distillation, enabling fine-grained supervision even when the teacher and student vocabularies are nearly disjoint.

## Results

Using TinyLlama 1.1B as the student and Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct (only 6.32% vocabulary overlap) as the teacher, VocAgnoLM achieves an average score of 29.3 (AVG†, w/o *) and 28.6 (AVG‡, w/o SAT) across 9 math benchmarks, a 46% improvement over naive continual pretraining (21.5) and a 33% improvement over the ULD logit-distillation baseline (21.8). With the strongest teacher (Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct), the method reaches 11.3 on GSM8K, 7.6 on MATH, and 60.7 on MAWPS. Performance scales monotonically with teacher quality: from 24.2 (Mistral-ProXMath) to 24.9 (DeepSeekMath) to 25.8 (Qwen2.5-Math) to 29.3 (Qwen2.5-Math-Inst) on AVG†.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KLD (same-vocabulary distillation with Llemma), VocAgnoLM wins on AVG† (22.9 vs. 22.5) and AVG‡ (22.8 vs. 21.7), with the gap widening as the teacher strengthens. Against ULD (Wasserstein-distance-based cross-vocabulary logit distillation), VocAgnoLM achieves a consistent and substantial advantage across all four different-vocabulary teachers; the largest gap appears with Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct, where VocAgnoLM scores 29.3 vs. ULD's 21.8 on AVG† (a 33% relative improvement). Against Rho-1 (token-level data selection requiring a same-vocabulary oracle), VocAgnoLM with DeepSeekMath matches its AVG† score (24.9 vs. 24.8) while using a teacher with a completely different 100K-token vocabulary, demonstrating that vocabulary-agnostic guidance can rival same-vocabulary fine-grained selection. Overall, VocAgnoLM outperforms every baseline in every comparison setting, and the margin grows with teacher strength, confirming that the method effectively transfers knowledge regardless of vocabulary overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends teacher-guided language modeling (MiniLLM, Rho-1, LightPaff) to the cross-vocabulary setting, where prior methods either assume identical vocabularies (KLD, Rho-1) or attempt to align logit distributions across different tokenizers via Wasserstein distance (ULD) or optimal transport. The key departure is replacing distribution-level alignment with a character-offset-based token-level lexical mapping (one-to-many) combined with teacher token loss as the guidance signal, rather than trying to make incompatible logit spaces comparable. This builds on Rho-1's token-level importance weighting but generalizes it to arbitrary teacher-student vocabulary pairs without requiring a shared reference model or new corpus construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any newly released strong teacher model can immediately be used to guide a student regardless of tokenizer, removing a real bottleneck in the LLM ecosystem. However, validation is limited to a single 1.1B student (TinyLlama), a single domain (math), and a single 15B-token corpus (OpenWebMath), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, broader domains, or longer training regimes. The finding that chunk-based alignment degrades below random at fine granularity (64 chunks) while lexical alignment maintains 100% student coverage is a useful diagnostic for future alignment work. A key open question is whether the top-k loss-based filtering generalizes beyond the 40% threshold tuned on a 2B-token subset.
