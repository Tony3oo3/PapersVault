## Problem

Black-box models for dynamical systems fail to capture physical relationships between variables and generalize unreliably outside the data support. Existing physics-informed approaches are limited: symplectic/Hamiltonian methods only model conservative systems, and diffeomorphism-based methods are restricted to first-order dynamics, unable to handle self-crossing trajectories, force exchange, or energy dissipation. This paper addresses the gap of learning general second-order non-conservative dynamics with physical interpretability, uncertainty quantification, and robust generalization to unseen states.

## Input / Output

The method takes system states in position-velocity form {q, q̇} and converts them to canonical contact coordinates {q, p, s} (position, momentum, Lagrangian action), where s is not directly observable and is estimated by enforcing consistency between Maupertuis' principle and Noether's theorem. The system is treated as autonomous (no explicit time input), and the model predicts the next state at t_{k+1} from the current state at t_k, iterated to reconstruct full trajectories. For robotics, the output is a reference dynamic trajectory tracked by a low-level Cartesian impedance controller at 10 Hz, with the ensemble also providing per-state uncertainty estimates that drive safety-aware behavior.

## Method

GCF defines a latent contact Hamiltonian dynamics H_g with desirable inductive properties (e.g., periodic orbits, attractor convergence, or energy-based stopping) on a (2d+1)-dimensional contact manifold, then adapts it to target dynamics via a contactomorphism φ_r that preserves the contact form η′ (unlike generic diffeomorphisms, which destroy the conjugate-variable structure of second-order systems). Each contactomorphism is a composition of K=12 parametrized contact Hamiltonian flows, where each flow integrates a vector field generated by H = ½pᵀM(p)p + V(q) + F(q)s, with M, V, F implemented as Random Fourier Features Networks and integrated via a contact splitting integrator that is analytically invertible. An ensemble of N contactomorphisms (N=5 for control tasks) quantifies predictive uncertainty, which reshapes the Riemannian metric to produce uncertainty-aware geodesics that steer trajectories toward data-rich regions. For safety, obstacle distributions are mapped into the latent space and incorporated as energy penalty terms in an optimal control formulation that locally deforms the geodesic flow. Training is supervised, minimizing a weighted loss over both ambient and latent space reconstruction errors across B trajectories.

## Results

On the 60-dimensional Spring Mesh reconstruction task, GCF achieves a DTWD of 0.50±0.19, a 57% reduction over the best baseline (MLP at 1.71±0.56) and a 60% reduction over DHNN (1.24±0.62). On the quantum system, GCF reaches 0.29±0.04 DTWD, outperforming EF (0.72±0.12) and NCDS (0.70±0.11) by ~60%. On the WRAP-AND-PULL robotic task, GCF (both stable and safe variants) achieves 0.61–0.62 DTWD, roughly 3× better than EF (1.79) and 7× better than DHNN (4.25), while also maintaining near-constant energy consumption under added load (Δ=0.03 J for safe GCF vs. 1.21–1.82 J for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCF consistently outperforms all four baselines across the majority of tasks. Against Euclideanizing Flows (EF, Rana et al. 2020), GCF achieves a 60× reduction on Spring Mesh (0.50 vs. 30.9 DTWD) and a 3× reduction on DigiLeTs handwriting (0.70 vs. 2.22), while matching EF on the simpler first-order LASA dataset (0.44 vs. 0.43). Against Neural Contractive Dynamical Systems (NCDS, Mohammadi et al. 2024), GCF wins on Spring Mesh (0.50 vs. 24.9), Quantum (0.29 vs. 0.70), and DigiLeTs (0.70 vs. 2.79), though NCDS achieves a higher convergence ratio on LEAF_2 (0.94 vs. 0.69) due to its contraction guarantee on first-order dynamics. Against Dissipative HNN (DHNN, Sosanya & Greydanus 2022), GCF wins on every task where both are applicable: Spring Mesh (0.50 vs. 1.24), LASA (0.44 vs. 0.65), DigiLeTs (0.70 vs. 0.82), and WRAP-AND-PULL (0.61 vs. 4.25). HNN (Greydanus et al. 2019) is only applicable to even-dimensional conservative systems, so it is excluded from quantum and handwriting experiments; where it is applicable (Spring Mesh), GCF still wins (0.50 vs. 1.49). The key structural advantage is that EF and NCDS are limited to first-order dynamics and cannot model self-crossing trajectories or second-order interactions, while DHNN lacks convergence guarantees and fails to generalize beyond the training data manifold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffeomorphism-based dynamics learning paradigm (Rana et al. 2020; Mohammadi et al. 2024) by replacing generic diffeomorphisms with contactomorphisms, which preserve the contact structure encoding the interplay between conjugate variables in second-order systems. It builds on Riemannian metric learning for geodesic-based generalization (Beik-Mohammadi et al. 2023) by incorporating ensemble uncertainty into the metric reshaping, and on contact Hamiltonian mechanics (Bravetti et al. 2017; Zadra 2023) by using a fixed latent contact Hamiltonian as an inductive bias rather than directly parametrizing the target Hamiltonian. The key departure from prior contact Hamiltonian learning (Zadra 2023) is that GCF does not learn the contact Hamiltonian of the target system directly, but instead learns a coordinate transformation (contactomorphism) from a simple, physically-motivated latent dynamics, thereby inheriting stability and energy-conservation properties by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCF provides a unified geometric framework that handles conservative, dissipative, and second-order dynamics within a single architecture, addressing a fragmentation in prior work where symplectic methods (HNN) cannot model dissipation and dissipative methods (DHNN) lack convergence guarantees. The ensemble-based uncertainty-aware geodesics and the energy-based safety stop (Eq. 7c) offer practical mechanisms for robot control in interaction tasks, demonstrated on a real Franka Emika robot. Limitations include the need to estimate the non-observable Lagrangian action s, the computational cost of the ensemble (N=5 contactomorphisms with K=12 layers each), and evaluation primarily on low-to-moderate dimensional systems (up to 60D). Open questions include whether the contact structure advantage persists at much higher dimensions, and whether the Maupertuis-based coordinate conversion generalizes to systems without a clear potential energy decomposition.
