## Problem

Traditional counterfactual explanations ignore causal dependencies among features, producing unrealistic or infeasible recommendations, while causal approaches like Causal Algorithmic Recourse require combinatorial search over feature subsets and are computationally expensive. Backtracking counterfactuals offer a more principled causal paradigm but depend on a user-specified conditional distribution P_B that is difficult to choose and expensive to integrate over. The paper seeks a single, efficient formulation that preserves causal consistency while remaining tractable and actionable.

## Input / Output

The method requires a fully known structural causal model with an invertible (bijective) generation function F mapping latent noise U to observed X, a classifier h(·), and a target output class y^CF. Because F is invertible, the latent vector u = F^{-1}(x) is uniquely determined by the observed input, eliminating the need for probabilistic posterior inference. The output is a single deterministic counterfactual pair (x^CF, u^CF) rather than a distribution, making the explanation directly actionable; in practice, categorical features like gender are held fixed to avoid non-actionable modifications.

## Method

BRACE formulates a single constrained optimization that jointly minimizes the observed-space distance d_X(x, x^CF) (ℓ₁ norm for sparsity) and the latent-space distance d_U(u, u^CF) (ℓ₂ norm for smoothness), weighted by a trade-off parameter λ, subject to h(x^CF) = y^CF and the causal consistency constraint x^CF = F(u^CF). The key insight is that under the Bijective Generation Mechanism, U is deterministic given X, so the probabilistic backtracking distribution P_B(U^CF|U) is unnecessary and can be replaced by a simple distance term in the latent space. This single objective unifies four existing paradigms: λ=0 recovers classical Counterfactual Explanations, λ→∞ recovers Deep Backtracking Explanations, a specific exponential P_B recovers Backtracking Counterfactual Explanations, and under convexity assumptions the method provably outperforms Causal Algorithmic Recourse (Theorem 6.1). Theorem 5.1 further establishes that backtracking counterfactuals generalize interventional counterfactuals whenever F is invertible, providing the theoretical bridge between the two causal reasoning paradigms.

## Results

On the German Credit Dataset loan-approval scenario, BRACE (λ=1) reduces the monthly repayment increase from 46.3% (achieved by both Counterfactual Explanations and Causal Algorithmic Recourse) to 37.8%, and BRACE (λ=1.2) further reduces it to 25.0%, while still flipping the prediction from high-risk to low-risk. BRACE modifies only two features (loan amount and duration) compared to three features modified by Deep Backtracking Explanations, yielding sparser and more locally interpretable counterfactuals. A sensitivity analysis adding Gaussian noise (σ=5) to the causal function coefficients shows the counterfactuals remain stable, with age unchanged and explanations staying sparse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Counterfactual Explanations (Wachter et al., 2017) and Causal Algorithmic Recourse (Karimi et al., 2021), BRACE wins on actionability: both baselines only reduce the repayment duration (48→32.8 months), increasing the monthly payment by 46.3%, whereas BRACE jointly adjusts loan amount and duration, limiting the monthly increase to 37.8% (λ=1) or 25.0% (λ=1.2). Against Deep Backtracking Explanations (Kladny et al., 2024), BRACE wins on sparsity and local interpretability: Deep Backtracking alters all three mutable features (age, loan amount, duration), producing a counterfactual that departs significantly from the original observation, while BRACE changes only two features and keeps age fixed. The paper also proves (Theorem 6.1) that under convexity and linearity assumptions, BRACE yields a counterfactual strictly closer to the observed input than Causal Algorithmic Recourse at the same latent-space distance, and it avoids the exponential 2ⁿ combinatorial search over intervention subsets that CAR requires.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRACE sits at the intersection of backtracking counterfactuals (Von Kügelgen et al., 2023) and the bijective generation mechanism (Nasr-Esfahany et al., 2023), exploiting the invertibility of the reduced-form mapping F to replace the probabilistic backtracking distribution P_B with a deterministic latent-space distance. This conceptual move allows the authors to unify four previously distinct paradigms—classical counterfactual explanations, causal algorithmic recourse, backtracking counterfactual explanations, and deep backtracking explanations—under a single optimization parameterized by λ. The key departure from prior work is Theorem 5.1, which shows that under bijective generation, interventional counterfactuals are a strict special case of backtracking counterfactuals, thereby subsuming the interventional framework (Karimi et al., 2021) within the backtracking one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification under a single λ parameter offers a clean theoretical lens for understanding how existing counterfactual methods relate, and the elimination of combinatorial search over intervention subsets makes the approach more scalable than causal algorithmic recourse. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single 4-feature tabular dataset with a logistic regression classifier, leaving open whether the method's advantages persist for deep neural networks or high-dimensional inputs. The bijective generation assumption and the requirement of a fully known SCM are strong constraints that limit real-world applicability, and the paper itself flags the need to relax convexity assumptions and test on complex models as key future directions.
