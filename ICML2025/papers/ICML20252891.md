## Problem

Existing inverse protein folding methods perform poorly on biomolecular complexes containing non-protein components (ligands, nucleotides, metal ions) and on dynamic complexes that adopt multiple structural states. While AlphaFold3 enabled structure prediction for such complexes, no generative model had combined all-atom structural context with the ability to handle multi-state ensembles and property-guided design in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252891/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ADFLIP takes protein backbone coordinates (N, Cα, C, O per residue) plus all-atom coordinates of non-protein components (ligands, nucleotides, or metal ions) as structural input, optionally across N conformational states from an NMR ensemble. It outputs a full amino acid sequence (20 standard residues per position) along with predicted side chain conformations via PIPPACK. During generation, side chains are progressively incorporated as additional structural context, and the model operates on PDB structures resolved at better than 3.5 Å with protein length under 6,000 residues.

## Method

ADFLIP uses discrete flow matching (following Campbell et al., 2024) to define a probability flow from a fully masked sequence to the target sequence, with a multi-scale GNN denoiser that alternates between local atom-level attention (with frame averaging, inspired by AlphaFold3's IPA) and global residue-level message passing (inspired by LigandMPNN). A key design choice is progressive side chain incorporation: as amino acids are sampled at each denoising step, PIPPACK predicts their conformations, which are then fed back as structural context for subsequent steps, enabling the model to reason about side-chain–ligand interactions. An adaptive purity-based sampling scheme prioritizes high-confidence positions first, reducing compute on easy residues. For dynamic complexes, predictions from multiple conformational states are ensembled at each step. A training-free classifier guidance mechanism estimates property predictions for intermediate masked states by taking the expectation of an external regressor (e.g., DSMBind) over the denoiser's predicted clean-sequence distribution, enabling plug-and-play optimization of designed sequences for desired properties without retraining.

## Results

On the all-atom inverse folding benchmark (interaction residues within 5 Å of non-protein atoms), ADFLIP achieves the best recovery rate across all three complex types: 62.2% for ligand-binding residues, 50.2% for nucleotide-binding residues, and 75.7% for metal-ion-coordinating residues, with corresponding perplexities of 3.57, 4.86, and 2.61. In foldability assessment (Chai-1 refolding, TM-score ≥ 0.5), ADFLIP reaches 100% foldability for small-molecule complexes (RMSD 1.15 Å), 83.5% for nucleotide complexes (RMSD 5.55 Å), and 85.1% for metal-ion complexes (RMSD 2.27 Å). On the NMR ensemble benchmark, using multiple conformational states improves ligand recovery from 41.5% (single structure) to 50.1% and reduces average RMSD from 9.10 Å to 7.21 Å.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADFLIP outperforms all three baselines on both perplexity and recovery rate across every complex type. Against the closest competitor LigandMPNN (which also uses all-atom non-protein context), ADFLIP gains +3.0 pp on ligand recovery (62.2% vs 59.2%), +4.1 pp on nucleotide recovery (50.2% vs 46.1%), and +6.4 pp on metal-ion recovery (75.7% vs 69.3%). The backbone-only methods ProteinMPNN and PiFold are substantially worse, particularly on nucleotide complexes where they trail ADFLIP by 10–12 pp in recovery rate. On foldability (Table 2), ADFLIP also edges out LigandMPNN: 100% vs 98.8% for small molecules, 83.5% vs 79.4% for nucleotides, and 85.1% vs 83.7% for metal ions, with consistently lower RMSD. On the NMR ensemble benchmark, ADFLIP (Multiple) surpasses LigandMPNN by +9.5 pp on ligand recovery (50.1% vs 40.6%) and +5.7 pp on nucleotide recovery (40.3% vs 32.6%), while also achieving lower RMSD (7.21 Å vs 8.34 Å).

## Relation to Prior Work

ADFLIP extends the inverse protein folding paradigm established by ProteinMPNN (random-order autoregressive generation) and PiFold (one-shot prediction) by adopting discrete flow matching as the generative framework, following the recent formulations of Campbell et al. (2024) and Gat et al. (2024). Its multi-scale GNN denoiser draws architectural inspiration from AlphaFold3's invariant point attention and LigandMPNN's residue-neighborhood encoding, but introduces a key departure: progressive side-chain incorporation during sampling, so that predicted side chains serve as structural context for subsequent denoising steps. The training-free classifier guidance mechanism repurposes flow matching's marginalization property to plug in arbitrary pre-trained regressors (e.g., DSMBind) without retraining, and the ensemble sampling across NMR conformations generalizes the task from single-structure to multi-state inverse folding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADFLIP demonstrates that discrete flow matching is a practical and flexible framework for all-atom protein sequence design, achieving state-of-the-art recovery rates for ligand-, nucleotide-, and metal-ion-binding residues while handling dynamic complexes through ensemble conditioning. The training-free guidance opens a path toward property-optimized protein design without the cost of retraining structure predictors, though the current fixed-backbone constraint limits how much affinity improvement is achievable. Key open questions remain: how to obtain biologically relevant conformational ensembles for arbitrary complexes, whether integrating backbone refinement during sampling (as in RNAflow) would substantially improve nucleotide-binding design, and whether ADFLIP-designed sequences can be experimentally validated. The modest but consistent gains over LigandMPNN (e.g., 75.7% vs 69.3% metal-ion recovery) suggest the flow-matching formulation and side-chain context are genuinely useful, but the field still lacks a unified benchmark for dynamic, multi-component protein design.
