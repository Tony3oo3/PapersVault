## Problem

GPTQ and the underlying OBQ framework perform 'symmetric calibration,' where each layer's quantization objective uses the input from the previous *quantized* layer rather than the full-precision model's input. This causes quantization error to accumulate systematically across network depth, degrading accuracy especially at low bit-widths (W2A4) and in very large models. The paper identifies this asymmetry as a fundamental bias and asks how to correct for it without resorting to expensive fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251450/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires three inputs per layer: the full-precision weight matrix W, the calibration input X obtained by forwarding 128 samples through the already-quantized preceding layers, and the full-precision input X̃ obtained by forwarding the same samples through the unquantized model. The difference ΔX = X̃ − X captures the accumulated asymmetry. Output is per-channel asymmetric quantized weights (optionally with per-token asymmetric activation quantization applied before weight calibration). The algorithm processes one transformer block at a time on a single GPU, with only one copy of the block in GPU memory.

## Method

GPTAQ reformulates the OBQ objective from ‖(w+Δw)X − wX‖² to ‖(w+Δw)X − wX̃‖², introducing a residual r = wX̃ − wX that captures input deviation. Solving the resulting constrained optimization via Lagrangian yields a closed-form weight update with two terms: the standard GPTQ quantization-error correction and a new residual-error correction term W_{:,q}P_{q,:}. Four efficiency optimizations make this practical at scale: (1) arbitrary column ordering enables parallel processing of all output channels; (2) neuron decomposition of the residual reduces the correction term from O(mnk) to O(mn) by precomputing a matrix P; (3) Cholesky reformulation of the inverse Hessian replaces iterative Gaussian elimination with stable matrix slicing, and a masking theorem (P = ((ΔXXᵀL) ⊙ M_U)Lᵀ) allows P to be computed in a single fused operation; (4) lazy-batch updates defer cross-column corrections to improve GPU utilization. The entire implementation adds only ~20 lines over GPTQ.

## Results

On LLaMA3-70B W4A4 quantization, GPTAQ reduces WikiText2 perplexity from 9.44 (QuaRot+GPTQ) to 6.93, and improves zero-shot average accuracy from 62.4% to 69.1%. On the extreme W2A4 setting for LLaMA3-8B, GPTAQ achieves 17.9 perplexity versus 102 for GPTQ. For the 405B LLaMA3.1 model, GPTAQ achieves 3.48 perplexity (vs. 5.82 for GPTQ) on a single A100 GPU. On vision transformers (DeiT-S/B), GPTAQ improves ImageNet top-1 accuracy by 0.7–1.0% over GPTQ at W4A4 and by 8.4–1.2% at W2A4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPTAQ consistently outperforms GPTQ across all tested models and bit widths: on LLaMA3-70B W4A4, perplexity drops from 9.44 to 6.93 (26% reduction) and average zero-shot accuracy rises from 62.4% to 69.1%. Against finetuning-based methods (OmniQuant, QLLM, DuQuant), the finetuning-free QuaRot+GPTAQ matches or exceeds their perplexity while requiring orders of magnitude less GPU time (e.g., 0.3 GPU-hours vs. 2.1–15 GPU-hours for LLaMA2-7B). Compared to SpinQuant+GPTQ (which requires learned rotations via finetuning), QuaRot+GPTAQ achieves comparable or better average accuracy at a fraction of the cost (0.3 vs. 4.0 GPU-hours on LLaMA3-8B). On vision transformers, GPTAQ beats GPTQ by 1.0% on DeiT-S W4A4 (72.8% vs. 71.9%) and by 8.4% on DeiT-S W2A4 (46.8% vs. 38.4%), while also outperforming RepQ-ViT, PTQ4ViT, APQ-ViT, and PD-Quant. For weight-only 3-bit quantization, GPTAQ achieves 63.8% average accuracy on LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, surpassing AWQ (61.3%) and GPTQ (62.5%). On the 405B LLaMA3.1 model, GPTAQ reduces perplexity from 5.82 (GPTQ) to 3.48, halving the gap to FP16 (1.44).

## Relation to Prior Work

GPTAQ extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon → Optimal Brain Compression → GPTQ lineage by identifying and correcting a fundamental asymmetry in how GPTQ calibrates layers: GPTQ matches each quantized layer's output to the *quantized* previous layer's output (symmetric calibration), while GPTAQ matches to the *full-precision* model's output (asymmetric calibration). The theoretical derivation follows the same Lagrangian/OBS framework as GPTQ, but introduces a residual correction term (W_{:,q} P_{q,:}) that accounts for accumulated input-activation deviation from prior layers. The implementation reuses GPTQ's Cholesky-based Hessian handling and block-parallel structure, adding only ~20 lines of code, and composes naturally with rotation-based incoherence methods like QuaRot and SpinQuant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because GPTQ is the de facto standard for post-training quantization (5,300+ models on HuggingFace), a drop-in replacement that requires minimal code changes and improves performance—especially at extreme low-bit (W2A4) where GPTQ often fails catastrophically—has immediate practical impact. The W2A4 perplexity reductions of 20–90% over GPTQ (e.g., 444→39.1 on LLaMA3-70B) open up previously impractical deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the asymmetric calibration benefit persists at higher bit widths (W8A8), whether rotation preprocessing is always necessary for the full benefit, and how the method interacts with emerging hardware-aware mixed-precision schemes.
