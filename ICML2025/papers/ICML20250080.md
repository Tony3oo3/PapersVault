## Problem

Score-based generative models in Euclidean spaces rely on Gaussian smoothing and the Tweedie-Miyasawa formula to ease sampling and recover clean data, but this paradigm has no direct analogue for binary data on the Boolean hypercube where Gaussian noise is inapplicable. The paper addresses how to derive a denoising-through-score-functions framework, discrete Langevin samplers with convergence guarantees, and multi-measurement noise reduction specifically for Bernoulli noise on {-1,1}^d.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250080/cropped_page8_idx16.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of binary vectors in {-1,1}^d and a noise parameter α (which sets the Bernoulli flip probability via σ(2α)) to learn a score function via logistic regression, then produces binary samples in {-1,1}^d through discrete Langevin MCMC with step size η (typically set to 1/α). A key structural detail is that the noisy distribution q_α(y) admits an analytical extension to all of R^d via an exponential tilt, so the score function is well-defined beyond the hypercube vertices—this is essential for multi-measurement denoising where the averaged observation ȳ_{1:m} lies in [-1,1]^d rather than {-1,1}^d.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces Gaussian noise with isotropic Bernoulli sign-flip noise y = x ∘ ε, yielding a noisy distribution q_α(y) ∝ Σ_x p(x) exp(α x^T y) that spreads mass across the hypercube as α decreases. A binary Tweedie-Miyasawa formula is derived: E[x|y] = (1/α) ∇log q_α(y), and the optimal denoiser under Hamming loss is sign(E[x|y]). For sampling, two discrete Langevin MCMC algorithms are proposed—a one-stage and a two-stage (Gibbs-based) variant—both with proven exponential contraction in Wasserstein distance where the rate depends on α, and the two-stage sampler achieves a tighter distance-to-stationary-distribution bound (O(d·α√d) vs. O(d·√α·d)) at high noise. The multi-measurement extension accumulates m independent noisy observations at fixed α, reducing effective noise to mα in the TMF, enabling a discrete analogue of the walk-jump sampling strategy without continuous-time SDEs.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical guarantees for discrete Langevin sampling on the Boolean hypercube: the one-stage sampler contracts at rate (1 − ½e^(−2/η−α)) with a stationary-distribution error bounded by 2d(2dαe^(2α) + √(dαe^(2α))), while the two-stage sampler contracts at rate (1 − ½e^(−2/η−2α)) with error bounded by 12d√(α²d) = 12dα√d, requiring only α ≪ 1/√d (vs. α ≪ 1/d for one-stage). On binarized MNIST, α = 0.5 with step-size η = 1/α yields a practical sweet spot where the sampler mixes rapidly (visible within 100 steps) while denoising quality remains acceptable; at α = 2 the algorithm breaks down. Multiple measurements (m = 3, 5) reduce the effective noise to mα, improving denoising Wasserstein distance by a factor of e^(−mH(α)) per coordinate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external generative-modeling baselines; its comparisons are internal and theoretical. The two-stage discrete Langevin sampler outperforms the one-stage variant in distance to the target distribution at high noise (small α), with the advantage scaling as dα√d vs. d√(αd), meaning the two-stage method only requires α ≪ 1/√d rather than α ≪ 1/d. Against the Gaussian-noise alternative (Section 4), the binary Bernoulli approach achieves the same denoiser function E[x|y] = (1/α)∇log q_α(y) but with a significantly better log-concavity threshold: the Gaussian mixture case requires α ≤ 1/d for log-concavity guarantees (Saremi et al., 2024), while the two-stage binary sampler operates effectively at α ≪ 1/√d. On binarized MNIST, the method demonstrates fast mixing with essentially no tuning (step-size fixed at 1/α), though qualitative artifacts appear at the highest noise level tested (α = 0.25), where digits such as '5' flip to '3' and '3' to '8' after denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the score-based denoising-and-sampling paradigm (Tweedie-Miyasawa formula, Langevin MCMC, multi-measurement accumulation) from the Euclidean/Gaussian setting of Saremi et al. (2024) and Saremi & Hyvärinen (2019) to the Boolean hypercube, replacing Gaussian noise with Bernoulli sign-flip noise. The one-stage discrete Langevin kernel builds directly on Zhang et al. (2022), while the two-stage sampler adapts the Gibbs/Langevin structure of Lee et al. (2021) to the binary case. A key departure from the broader discrete-diffusion literature (Hoogeboom et al., Austin et al., Campbell et al.) is the use of a single fixed noise scale with discrete measurement accumulation rather than a continuous-time forward/backward diffusion process, eliminating the need for a noise schedule or SDE discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exponential-convergence-in-Wasserstein-metric guarantees for discrete Langevin-like MCMC algorithms, establishing a clean theoretical parallel: high Bernoulli noise (small α) makes sampling easy in the same way that large Gaussian σ maps to log-concave sampling. The multi-measurement extension shows that effective noise decreases at rate α/m without any continuous-time process, offering a simpler alternative to discrete diffusion for binary generative modeling. Limitations include validation only on small-d synthetic mixtures and binarized MNIST, the two-stage sampler's advantage being restricted to the high-noise regime, and the framework being confined to binary data. Open questions include extension to other exponential-family noise models, sharper denoising bounds under strong priors, and whether Metropolis-Hastings corrections can tighten the stationary-distribution gap.
