## Problem

Existing watermarking methods are designed for static, unstructured data (plain text, images) and cannot handle the spatial and temporal variations inherent in dynamic knowledge graphs, where node/edge updates, isomorphic relabeling, and structural perturbations routinely occur. This leaves AI-generated KGs without a mechanism for intellectual property protection, traceability, or integrity verification against adversarial post-editing attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251951/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge graph G=(V,E) (with entity embeddings of dimension 4096 produced by RotatE) and a watermark signature S drawn from N(0, σ²I). The graph is first encoded into a latent vector Z_0 via an RGAT-based VAE, then DDIM-inverted to recover the initial noise Z_T^INV. The output is a watermarked KG G^w reconstructed from the modified latent, plus a detection P-value computed under a noncentral χ² distribution that requires the secure key (S, M) for verification.

## Method

KGMark embeds the watermark in the frequency domain of the DDIM initial noise vector Z_T^INV via a Fourier transform, using a Learnable Adaptive Watermark Mask Matrix (LAWMM) that is optimized per-graph to minimize reconstruction loss while preserving detectability. To defend against isomorphism, vertices are canonically reordered by degree and clustering coefficient before encoding; to defend against structural variations, the watermark is redundantly embedded across both a Community Layer (spectral profile of detected communities) and a Vertex Layer (edge-weight distributions around high-centrality nodes). Detection inverts the watermarked graph back to Z_T^INV via DDIM, computes a residual vector R against the reference signature, and evaluates a likelihood-ratio test statistic under a noncentral χ² distribution with degrees of freedom |M|. The 'sample-then-embed' strategy with a correction term αS·M ensures the watermark aligns with the latent space equilibrium, balancing transparency (cosine similarity ≥ 0.94 at 75 DDIM steps) against robustness (AUC ≈ 0.95 under 50% triple deletion).

## Results

KGMark achieves watermark detection AUC of 0.9991 (AliF), 0.9987 (MIND), and 0.9976 (Last-FM) on clean samples, with average AUC of 0.9697, 0.9762, and 0.9707 respectively across all attack scenarios. Under 50% triple deletion, KGMark retains AUC of 0.9031–0.9576, while the best baseline (GaussianShading) drops to 0.7850–0.8846. Most strikingly, under isomorphism variation (IsoVar), KGMark maintains AUC of 0.9842–0.9977 whereas all baselines collapse to 0.5708–0.6551, demonstrating a >30-point advantage in structural invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KGMark outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and attack scenario. Against the strongest baseline GaussianShading (Yang et al., 2024b), KGMark gains +10.0, +10.6, and +7.9 points in average AUC on AliF, MIND, and Last-FM respectively. The gap is most dramatic under isomorphism variation: KGMark retains AUC ≥ 0.984 while all baselines fall below 0.66, a >30-point margin that reflects KGMark's graph-alignment and redundant community-level embedding. Classical DCT/DWT methods (DctQim, DwtDct) suffer the largest degradation under triple deletion (AUC as low as 0.6993 on MIND at 50%), whereas KGMark stays above 0.90. TreeRing (Wen et al., 2023), a node-level diffusion watermark adapted to graphs, is competitive on adversarial attacks but drops sharply under relation alteration (0.7392 on AliF at 50%) and IsoVar (0.5763–0.6257), confirming that node-level strategies lack the structural invariance that KGMark's dual community/vertex encoding provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

KGMark adapts the diffusion-model watermarking paradigm (TreeRing, Gaussian Shading) from images to the knowledge graph embedding (KGE) space, but departs fundamentally by operating in the Fourier domain of the DDIM-inverted latent vector rather than in pixel or node-feature space. It builds on classical signal-processing watermarking (DwtDct, DctQim) for the frequency-domain embedding idea, and on RotatE/RGAT-based VAEs for encoding KGs into a latent space amenable to diffusion. The key conceptual departure is treating spatial (isomorphism) and temporal (dynamic update) variations as first-class design constraints, addressed via graph alignment, community-level redundant embedding, and a learnable adaptive mask matrix (LAWMM) — none of which have analogues in prior image or text watermarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework for watermarking KGEs, filling a gap the authors identify as 'unsolved and looming' as synthetic KGs proliferate in recommendation, semantic search, and GraphRAG pipelines. Open questions include compatibility with samplers beyond DDIM, scaling to very large or multi-relational KGs, and the tension between relational-model expressiveness (RGAT) and adversarial susceptibility noted in the discussion. A notable limitation is that the W/O LAWMM ablation sometimes matches or exceeds full KGMark in detectability, suggesting the transparency-robustness trade-off is not yet fully resolved, and the evaluation is confined to three mid-scale datasets on a single GPU.
