## Problem

Existing LLM knowledge distillation methods incur high training costs because they treat all samples uniformly, ignoring that easy samples require no further supervision. Moreover, when the distillation dataset is dominated by difficult samples, standard KD losses (KL, RKL) suffer from gradient explosion or vanishing and fail to emphasize the hardest examples, leading to unstable optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250875/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a teacher-student LLM pair (e.g., LLaMA2-7B → Sheared-LLaMA2-2.7B, Qwen2.5-7B → Qwen2.5-0.5B) and instruction-following or task-specific datasets (Dolly, SAMSum, GSM8K). The student is trained to generate text responses that match the teacher's token-level probability distributions. Unusually, the effective training set shrinks each epoch via a linear or cosine decay schedule, so the model sees progressively fewer but harder samples over the course of training.

## Method

DA-KD combines two components. First, Difficulty-Aware Data Updating (DiffUp) computes a Distillation Difficulty Score (DDS) as the ratio of student cross-entropy loss to teacher cross-entropy loss per sample, then applies Stratified Data Updating (SDU) to progressively reduce the dataset while mixing high-DDS and low-DDS partitions to preserve diversity and prevent forgetting. Second, the Bidirectional Discrepancy Loss (BDL) replaces standard KL divergence by blending teacher and student distributions in both the numerator and denominator: D_BDL = KL((1−λ)p + λq_θ ‖ λp + (1−λ)q_θ). Gradient analysis shows this bounds the per-token gradient coefficient C(x) to a finite range determined solely by λ, preventing explosion/vanishing, while making C(x) monotonically increasing with the teacher-student probability ratio q_θ/p, thereby amplifying gradients on the hardest samples. With λ=0.9, BDL combines KL and reverse-KL behaviors to stabilize convergence on difficult samples.

## Results

On task-agnostic instruction following (Table 1), DA-KD achieves the highest average ROUGE-L across all model pairs: 28.11 on Llama2-2.7B (surpassing the 7B teacher at 27.89), 26.15 on Llama2-1.3B, 33.01 on Qwen2.5-1.5B (surpassing the 7B teacher at 32.86), and 30.44 on Qwen2.5-0.5B. On task-specific benchmarks (Table 2), DA-KD achieves 40.05 ROUGE-L on SAMSum and 54.66% zero-shot accuracy on GSM8K for Qwen2.5-1.5B, both exceeding the 7B teacher. In terms of efficiency, DA-KD requires only 1,963 iterations and 106.35 minutes of training time, compared to 3,570 iterations and 408.24 minutes for GKD (a 74% reduction in GPU time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-KD outperforms all five baselines across every model pair and dataset. Against the strongest baseline KD-RKL (MiniLLM), DA-KD gains +0.41 avg ROUGE-L on Llama2-2.7B (28.11 vs 27.70) and +3.50 on Llama2-1.3B (26.15 vs 23.86). Against GKD, the gap is +1.26 on Llama2-2.7B and +0.85 on Qwen2.5-1.5B, while DA-KD also uses 74% less training time (106 vs 408 minutes). Against Distillm, DA-KD leads by +1.50 on Llama2-1.3B and +2.60 on Qwen2.5-0.5B, with roughly half the training cost. Notably, DA-KD surpasses the 7B teacher model on both Llama2-2.7B (28.11 vs 27.89) and Qwen2.5-1.5B (33.01 vs 32.86) in average ROUGE-L, and on Qwen2.5-1.5B task-specific SAMSum (40.05 vs 39.70). The largest absolute gains appear on the Unnatural Instructions benchmark, where DA-KD leads KD-RKL by up to +4.70 (Llama2-1.3B: 36.09 vs 32.39), suggesting the difficulty-aware data selection is particularly effective on harder instruction types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM knowledge distillation lineage (Hinton 2015 → MiniLLM's reverse KL → GKD's generalized JSD → Distillm's skew divergences) by addressing two underexplored axes: data efficiency and loss stability under difficult samples. It departs from prior data-selection-for-KD work (Li et al. 2021, Lan et al. 2025), which relied on teacher-only or student-only signals, by introducing a teacher-student discrepancy score (DDS) to dynamically curate the distillation set. The proposed Bidirectional Discrepancy Loss (BDL) is conceptually closest to Distillm's skew divergences but differs in blending both distributions symmetrically in numerator and denominator, yielding provably bounded gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners: DA-KD cuts training iterations by 55% and GPU time roughly in half relative to SOTA KD methods while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making large-scale LLM distillation substantially cheaper. The observation that a 1.5B student can surpass its 7B teacher (4.7× compression) suggests that difficulty-aware data curation can extract more signal per token than uniform training. Key limitations include dependence on teacher quality (misidentified difficulty if the teacher is wrong) and persistent gaps on mathematical reasoning for small students; the optimal λ for BDL remains heuristic, and whether the framework scales to 70B+ teachers or multi-turn generation is untested.
