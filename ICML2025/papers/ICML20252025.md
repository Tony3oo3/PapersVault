## Problem

Existing GNN explanation methods (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, LRI) were designed for 2D molecular graphs where edges are explicit chemical bonds, but 3D GNNs construct dense edge sets via cut-off distances and learn proximity-dependent interactions. Applying these methods to 3D GNNs produces chemically uninterpretable subgraphs of 'random edges' and suffers from optimization inconsistency due to the binary-to-continuous mask relaxation, which compounds severely with the rapid edge growth in 3D representations.

## Input / Output

RISE takes a 3D molecular graph defined by node coordinates P ∈ R^{n×3}, node features X, and initial uniform radii R for edge construction, along with the target property value Y. It outputs a vector of per-atom radii of influence M^r ∈ [0,1]^n, which implicitly defines a directed proximity graph (DPG) as the explanatory subgraph: an edge v_i→v_j is retained iff d_ij < M_i^r. The budget B = ρ·‖R‖₁ constrains the total radius sum, and unlike edge-mask methods, the output is a compact n-dimensional vector rather than an n×n matrix, making the subgraph structure emerge from spatial geometry rather than explicit edge selection.

## Method

RISE reformulates 3D GNN explanation by replacing edge masks with per-node radii of influence, motivated by the finding that 3D GNNs learn proximity-dependent message passing (shorter edges carry more importance). The optimization directly targets continuous radius masks M^r, eliminating the binary-to-continuous relaxation and its associated inconsistency; the edge mask M_ij is computed differentiably via a sigmoid of (M_i^r − d_ij) with a large sharpness parameter k. The budget is enforced exactly through a softmax-sigmoid parameterization (Eq. 8) of the radii, removing the need for L1 sparsity or entropy regularization terms that existing methods require. This design ensures the optimized radii directly define a valid DPG subgraph, and under small budgets, the radii naturally shrink to preserve only chemical bonds (e.g., C–H at 1.095 Å, C–C at 1.530 Å), yielding chemically interpretable explanations that edge-mask methods cannot produce.

## Results

On the QM9 dataset with SchNet as backbone, RISE achieves the lowest MAE across nearly all properties and budgets. For example, at budget ρ=0.5, RISE achieves MAE of 0.422 on μ (vs. 0.859 for GNNExplainer, 1.684 for PGExplainer, 4.127 for LRI-Bernoulli) and 0.207 on ε_LUMO (vs. 0.209 for GNNExplainer, 1.418 for PGExplainer, 1.401 for LRI-Bernoulli). On the GEOM dataset, RISE consistently achieves the best MAE across all configurations, e.g., 0.082 with SchNet at ρ=0.6 vs. 0.102 for GNNExplainer. RISE is the only method that produces chemically interpretable explanatory subgraphs, precisely extracting chemical bonds under small budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISE is compared against three baselines: GNNExplainer (edge-level soft masks), PGExplainer (parameterized edge masks via MLP), and LRI-Bernoulli (node-level Bernoulli masking, the only prior 3D-specific explainer). RISE decisively outperforms PGExplainer and LRI-Bernoulli on virtually all properties and budgets; for instance, on μ with SchNet at ρ=0.3, RISE achieves MAE 0.596 vs. 1.884 (PGExplainer) and 6.087 (LRI-Bernoulli). Against GNNExplainer, RISE wins on μ, α, and ε_LUMO across all budgets, but is essentially tied on ε_HOMO (0.333 vs. 0.334 at ρ=0.3). On the GEOM dataset, RISE achieves the best MAE in every configuration, e.g., 0.082 vs. 0.102 (GNNExplainer) and 0.071 (PGExplainer) with SchNet at ρ=0.6. Beyond quantitative metrics, RISE is the only method that extracts chemically interpretable subgraphs (precisely chemical bonds), while all baselines produce fragmented, uninterpretable edge sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-level GNN explanation framework established by GNNExplainer (Ying et al., 2019) and PGExplainer (Luo et al., 2020) from 2D molecular graphs to 3D geometric GNNs, identifying two fundamental differences—representation (dense cut-off-based edges vs. explicit chemical bonds) and learning (proximity-dependent message passing vs. bond-based propagation)—that prior methods overlook. It positions itself against LRI (Miao et al., 2023), the only prior 3D-specific explainer, arguing that LRI addresses only the representational difference through node-level Bernoulli masking while ignoring the learning-dynamics difference. The key conceptual departure is reformulating subgraph extraction as optimization of per-atom radii of influence over a directed proximity graph, eliminating the binary-to-soft mask relaxation that plagues existing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RISE is the first explanation method for 3D GNNs that produces chemically interpretable substructures (exact chemical bonds) rather than arbitrary edge subsets, addressing a practical need in molecular science where explanations must align with known chemistry. The consistency guarantee—no soft-mask relaxation, exact budget enforcement by construction—removes a class of hyperparameter sensitivity issues inherent in prior explainers. However, the method is validated only on small molecules (QM9, GEOM) with three backbone architectures; whether the radius-of-influence formulation generalizes to macromolecules with long-range interactions (e.g., proteins) remains an open question the authors explicitly flag.
