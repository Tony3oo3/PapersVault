## Problem

Existing subgraph federated graph learning methods focus on structural aspects but neglect how graph signals propagate across the spatial and spectral domains. Specifically, edge disconnections between clients cause label signal disruption that degrades semantic knowledge, while spectral heterogeneity across subgraphs induces spectral client drift that undermines global generalizability. This paper is the first to empirically identify and jointly address both challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a subgraph (partitioned via Louvain community detection) with node feature vectors and a local adjacency matrix; the global model is frozen and used to extract reference inference features. The method produces node class labels via a standard cross-entropy head on ACM-GCN. Unusually, the training loop also consumes a server-side global prototype repository (4 prototypes per class, aggregated from top-K SALC-ranked nodes) and a frozen global GNN's feature matrix for spectral alignment, both broadcast to clients each round.

## Method

S²FGL combines two complementary strategies. NLIR selects structurally and semantically representative nodes via a Structure-Aware Label Centrality score (combining PPR-based structural prominence and label influence propagation), builds a global prototype repository, and injects semantic knowledge by aligning local and global GNN similarity distributions over the repository via a KL-divergence distillation loss. FGMA reconstructs GNN-aware graph spectra by building a sparse similarity Laplacian from top-k cosine-similar node features, performs eigendecomposition, projects features onto low- and high-frequency eigenvectors, and aligns local projections with frozen global projections via MSE, encouraging a shared spectral signal propagation paradigm. The total training objective is L_CE + λ₁·L_FKD + λ₂·L_FGMA, balancing classification, semantic reinforcement, and spectral alignment.

## Results

S²FGL achieves the best node classification accuracy across all six datasets: 83.4% on Cora, 76.0% on CiteSeer, 88.6% on PubMed, 74.8% on Texas, 79.0% on Wisconsin, and 80.5% on Minesweeper. Compared to the FedAvg baseline, S²FGL yields improvements of +1.5 to +2.0 percentage points on homophilic datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed) and +0.9 to +2.0 points on heterophilic datasets (Texas, Wisconsin, Minesweeper). The method also exhibits notably lower variance (e.g., ±0.2 on Cora vs. ±0.7 for FedAvg), indicating more stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²FGL outperforms all 10 baselines on all six datasets. On homophilic graphs, the closest competitors are FedFa (82.7% on Cora) and FedSage+ (75.2% on CiteSeer), with S²FGL leading by 0.7 and 0.8 points respectively. On heterophilic graphs, S²FGL achieves the largest margin on Texas (+0.5 over FedStar at 74.3%) and matches FedSage+ on Wisconsin (both 79.0%), while on Minesweeper it edges out FGGP by just 0.1 points (80.5 vs. 80.4). Traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) consistently underperform FGL-specific baselines, confirming that graph-aware strategies are essential. The FGL baselines (FedStar, FedPub, FGSSL, FedGTA, FGGP) cluster within 0.5–1.0 points of each other, whereas S²FGL extends beyond this cluster by 0.7–2.0 points, demonstrating the added value of its spatial (NLIR) and spectral (FGMA) components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the subgraph-FL branch of Federated Graph Learning, where prior methods (FedSage+, FedStar, FedPub, FGSSL, FGGP, FedGTA) have focused on structural remedies such as identifying similar collaborators, enhancing structural knowledge exchange, or retrieving generic information under structural shifts. The key departure is a signal-propagation lens: the authors argue that existing FGL work neglects how graph signals propagate in the spatial domain (label signal disruption from edge loss) and the spectral domain (spectral client drift from eigenvalue heterogeneity across subgraphs). NLIR repurposes prototype-based federated knowledge distillation and Personalized PageRank for representative node selection, while FGMA applies Laplacian eigendecomposition and frequency-band projection in a novel cross-client alignment loss—neither component directly extends a single prior method but rather combines established tools (PPR, KL-divergence distillation, spectral projection) in a new federated-graph context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By empirically diagnosing two previously unarticulated failure modes in subgraph-FL (declining Structure Inertia Score and divergent eigenvalue distributions across clients), the paper gives the FGL community a concrete vocabulary and measurement toolkit for signal-propagation issues that structural-only methods cannot address. The targeted-effectiveness analysis (NLIR gains scale with SIS drop; FGMA gains scale with spectral KL divergence) strengthens the causal story beyond a generic SOTA bump. Limitations include evaluation only on small-to-medium citation and grid graphs with a single GNN backbone (ACM-GCN) and Louvain partitioning, leaving open whether the spectral alignment generalizes to large-scale, dynamic, or multi-relational graphs and to other GNN families. Future work could explore whether the frequency-band alignment loss transfers to inter-graph FGL or to tasks beyond node classification (link prediction, graph classification).
