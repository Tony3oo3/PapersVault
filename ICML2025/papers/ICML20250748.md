## Problem

Existing agnostic boosting algorithms require significantly more labeled samples than ERM to achieve the same excess error guarantee: (log|H|)/ε⁴ for Kanade & Kalai (2009) and (log|H|)/ε³ for Ghai & Singh (2024), versus the optimal (log|H|)/ε² for ERM. This paper identifies unlabeled samples as a previously unexplored resource that can close this gap, showing that a vanishing fraction of the total samples need to be labeled.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a fixed set of S = O(VC(B)/γ²ε²) labeled samples drawn once and reused across all T = O(1/γ²ε²) boosting rounds, plus T·U = O(VC(B)/γ⁴ε⁴) unlabeled feature samples drawn fresh each round, and a γ-agnostic weak learner oracle. The output is an improper binary classifier (a majority vote over the sign of the ensemble at each round, selected by empirical correlation on a held-out labeled set) that achieves ε-excess correlation over the best-in-class hypothesis with probability 1−δ. The labeled samples are never relabeled or reweighted, which is what enables their reuse without uniform convergence arguments.

## Method

The key innovation is a new bivariate potential φ(z,y) = ψ(z) − yz built on the Huber loss, whose derivative decomposes into a feature-only term ψ′(z)h(x) estimable from unlabeled data and a label-only term −yh(x) estimable from labeled data. This separability means the labeled portion of the gradient does not depend on the current ensemble H_t, allowing the same labeled dataset to be reused across all boosting rounds without the uniform convergence or martingale arguments required by prior work. The algorithm performs randomized relabeling where the probability of flipping a label is p_t(x) = (1−ψ′(H_t(x)))/2, so the more confident the ensemble is, the closer the pseudo-label is to uniform random. Unlike the Madaboost potential, this potential curves upward between −1 and 0, meaning it does not uniformly downweight misclassified points—a property the authors show is incompatible with the required separability. A further refinement using a Pseudo-Huber loss and selective data reuse from Ghai & Singh (2024) reduces unlabeled samples to O(log|B|/γ³ε³) while keeping labeled samples at the ERM-optimal rate.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed algorithm achieves labeled sample complexity O(log|H|/ε²), matching ERM for the first time in agnostic boosting, while requiring only O(log|H|/ε⁴) unlabeled samples (reducible to O(log|H|/ε³) with data reuse). Empirically, on six UCI datasets with 50–90% of labels dropped, the method achieves an average 50-fold cross-validated accuracy of 0.89 (no noise), 0.88 (5% noise), 0.84 (10% noise), and 0.81 (20% noise), consistently outperforming the Potential-based Agnostic Booster (PAB) baseline by 0.03–0.05 in average accuracy across all noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: the Potential-based Agnostic Booster (PAB) of Kanade & Kalai (2009). The proposed method wins on 5 out of 6 datasets at every noise level, with the most dramatic gains on Sonar (0.92 vs. 0.78 at 0% noise; 0.89 vs. 0.68 at 5% noise) and German (0.86 vs. 0.79 at 0% noise; 0.87 vs. 0.76 at 10% noise). Waveform is essentially a tie across all noise levels (e.g., 0.89 vs. 0.89 at 0%). The advantage grows with noise: the average gap widens from +0.05 (no noise) to +0.03 (20% noise), though PAB degrades more sharply on Sonar and German under noise. The authors attribute the improvement to PAB's inability to reuse samples across boosting rounds, which limits its effective number of iterations when labeled data is scarce (50–90% of labels are dropped in these experiments).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the potential-based agnostic boosting framework of Kanade & Kalai (2009) by introducing a novel decomposable potential function (based on the Huber loss) whose derivative splits into a label-dependent part estimable from labeled data and a label-independent part estimable from unlabeled data. It layers the selective data-reuse technique of Ghai & Singh (2024) onto the unlabeled component to further reduce sample requirements, and adapts the RL boosting meta-algorithm of Brukhim et al. (2022) to exploit reward-free trajectories. The key departure from prior work is that the new potential intentionally violates the 'conservative relabeling' property of Madaboost (it downweights misclassified points), which prior theory treated as essential, yet still yields valid descent guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the long-standing gap between agnostic boosting and ERM sample complexity (from (log|H|)/ε³ or ε⁴ down to (log|H|)/ε²) by showing that polynomially many unlabeled samples suffice, with only a vanishing ε-fraction needing labels. This is particularly relevant in distribution-specific settings (uniform, Gaussian marginals) where unlabeled data is free and weak learners are readily available via L₁-approximation. Open questions include whether the ε² labeled sample complexity is achievable without any unlabeled data in polynomial time, whether oracle complexity can be improved from 1/ε² toward log(1/ε), and whether per-round adaptive step sizes can make the algorithm more practical.
