## Problem

Differentially private cut sparsification on n-node graphs has been stuck at O(n^{1.5}) additive error for all polynomial-time algorithms, even when allowing (1+γ) multiplicative error and approximate DP. While an exponential-time algorithm achieves Õ(n) additive error, closing this gap was identified as a prominent open problem. This paper breaks the n^{1.5} barrier by presenting the first polynomial-time (ε,δ)-DP algorithm achieving n^{1.25+o(1)} additive error with (1+γ) multiplicative error.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a non-negative weighted undirected graph G=(V,E,w) on n nodes, DP parameters (ε,δ), and a multiplicative approximation parameter γ∈(0,1), and outputs a non-negative weighted sparse synthetic graph H with Õ(n/γ²) edges. The guarantee holds simultaneously for all 2^n cuts with high probability: |w_G(S)−w_H(S)| ≤ γ·w_G(S) + n^{1.25+o(1)}·polylog(1/δ)/(ε·γ^{0.5}). The output graph is guaranteed to have non-negative edge weights and near-linear size, making it suitable for downstream applications like max-cut and minimum-bisection.

## Method

The core technique is a private expander decomposition that partitions V into sets V_1,…,V_k where each induced subgraph G[V_i] has sparsity at least ψ = n^{0.5+o(1)}·polylog(1/δ)/(αε), and the total weight of inter-component edges is ψ·n^{1+o(1)}. The sparse inter-component edges are privatized using the Elias et al. (2020) algorithm (yielding Õ(n^{1.25}/√α) additive error), while each dense expander subgraph is privatized using the Upadhyay et al. (2021) Laplace-noise-based algorithm, whose Õ(|C|√n) additive error is subsumed by the (1+α) multiplicative factor thanks to the expander's high sparsity. The private expander decomposition itself is obtained by white-box privatizing the Nanongkai–Saranurak recursive decomposition, replacing their unweighted sparse-cut subroutine with Li–Saranurak's weighted version and using the Upadhyay et al. mechanism as the privacy-preserving oracle for finding sparse cuts at each recursion level. A final non-private Benczur–Karger sparsifier is applied as post-processing to reduce the output to Õ(n/γ²) edges while preserving the cut approximation.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.1) is a polynomial-time (ε,δ)-DP algorithm that outputs a sparse synthetic graph with Õ(n/γ²) edges, approximating all cuts with (1+γ) multiplicative error and n^{1.25+o(1)}·polylog(1/δ)/(ε·γ^{0.5}) additive error. This breaks the long-standing n^{1.5} additive error barrier for polynomial-time private cut sparsification with multiplicative error. The key intermediate result is a private expander decomposition (Theorem 3.1) that partitions vertices into ψ-expanders with total inter-component edge weight ψ·n^{1+o(1)} for ψ ≥ n^{0.5+o(1)}·polylog(1/δ)/ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gupta et al. 2012 (the prior best polynomial-time method with multiplicative error), this work improves the additive error from O(n^{1.5}/ε) to n^{1.25+o(1)}/ε while maintaining the same (1+γ) multiplicative approximation and polynomial runtime. Against Arora & Upadhyay 2019, whose bound is Õ(√n·|S|/ε) per cut, the new method is strictly better in the worst case since |S| can be Θ(n), making their bound Õ(n^{1.5}). Against Elias et al. 2020 / Liu et al. 2024 (which achieve Õ(√(mn)/ε) with no multiplicative error), the new method trades a (1+γ) multiplicative factor for a lower additive error of n^{1.25+o(1)}/ε on dense graphs where m ≈ n². The only method with a better additive error is the exponential-time algorithm of Elias et al. 2020 achieving O(n log n/ε), which this work does not match in error but surpasses in runtime (polynomial vs. exponential). The paper also shows downstream improvements for Max-Cut, Maximum-Bisection, Max-k-Cut, and Minimum-Bisection, all inheriting the n^{1.25+o(1)} additive error improvement over prior DP algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the expander decomposition framework of Nanongkai & Saranurak (2017) and the weighted variant of Li & Saranurak (2021), but critically departs from prior DP graph work by privatizing the decomposition algorithm in a white-box manner rather than applying it as a black box to a private synthetic graph (which provably fails to preserve expander structure). The resulting private expander decomposition is then combined with two existing DP subroutines — the Õ(√(nm)) additive-error mechanism of Elias et al. (2020) for sparse inter-component edges and the Õ(|S|√n) mechanism of Upadhyay et al. (2021) for dense intra-component edges — to achieve a unified n^{1.25+o(1)} additive error guarantee. This positions the work as the first polynomial-time algorithm to break the O(n^{1.5}) barrier established by Gupta, Roth, and Ullman (2012), closing part of the gap to the near-optimal Õ(n) bound achievable only in exponential time by Elias et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Breaking the n^{1.5} additive error barrier resolves a problem explicitly identified as 'a prominent open problem' in the DP literature, and the private expander decomposition subroutine is of independent interest for other graph algorithm applications. The result immediately improves DP guarantees for max-cut, maximum-bisection, max-k-cut, and minimum-bisection. However, a significant gap remains between the achieved n^{1.25+o(1)} error and the near-optimal Õ(n) bound, and the authors pose open questions about whether the additive error can be further reduced and for what range of edge counts m ≫ n the √(mn) error can be polynomially improved with multiplicative approximation allowed.
