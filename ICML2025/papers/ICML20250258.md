## Problem

Maximum entropy RL (MaxEnt-RL) provides principled exploration but traditionally relies on Gaussian policy parameterizations that severely limit representational capacity. Diffusion models offer a far more expressive alternative, yet their marginal entropy is intractable to compute, blocking direct integration into the MaxEnt objective. Existing diffusion-based RL workarounds (DIPO, QSM, DACER, QVPO) circumvent this by adding artificial Gaussian or uniform noise to generated actions, effectively falling back to Gaussian exploration rather than exploiting the diffusion model's capacity for non-Gaussian exploration patterns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous observation vector (up to 223 dimensions in DMC dog tasks) as input and produces a continuous action vector (up to 39 dimensions in MYO hand tasks) as output. The diffusion policy operates in an unbounded latent space, with a tanh squashing function applied at the final diffusion step (n=0) to map actions into the bounded range (-1, 1). The score network uses Fourier features to encode the diffusion timestep, and the prior distribution is N(0, 2.5) rather than the standard N(0, 1), with 16 diffusion steps as the default configuration.

## Method

DIME derives a lower bound on the MaxEnt objective by expressing the intractable marginal entropy of the diffusion policy as an expectation over the denoising trajectory (Eq. 15), then casts the policy improvement step as a KL divergence between the learned denoising process and a noising process whose marginal is the exponentiated Q-function. This yields a policy iteration scheme with provable monotonic improvement and convergence to the optimal diffusion policy. Crucially, the resulting training objective (Eq. 27) requires only samples from the diffusion model itself via the reparameterization trick, eliminating the need for samples from the intractable target distribution exp(Q)/Z that would be required by standard score matching. The practical algorithm combines this with CrossQ (batch renormalization, no target network) and distributional Q learning, while auto-tuning both the entropy temperature α and the diffusion coefficient β end-to-end to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Results

DIME is evaluated on 13 high-dimensional continuous control environments (up to 39-dim actions, 223-dim observations) across the Gym, DMC, and MYO benchmark suites, reporting IQM with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over 10 seeds. DIME significantly outperforms all six diffusion-based baselines on every environment and outperforms the Gaussian-policy SOTA methods (CrossQ, BRO) on 10 out of 13 environments. On the high-dimensional dog-run task, DIME achieves substantially higher returns than all baselines, while on humanoid-run it matches BRO's asymptotic performance but trains in ~4.5h versus BRO's ~8.5h, using a UTD ratio of 2 versus BRO's 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six diffusion-based baselines (QSM, Diff-QL, Consistency-AC, DIPO, QVPO, DACER), DIME consistently achieves higher returns or success rates across all 13 environments; DIPO is the strongest diffusion baseline on Ant-v3 but is still outperformed by DIME. Against CrossQ (Gaussian policy, Batch Renormalization, no target network), DIME performs comparably on Ant-v3 but clearly outperforms it on Humanoid-v3, all four dog tasks, and most MYO hand tasks. Against BRO (Gaussian policy, UTD=10, network resets, optimistic exploration), DIME wins on the dog environments and MYO suite tasks, while BRO achieves slightly higher average performance on humanoid-run and humanoid-walk; however, DIME reaches comparable asymptotic performance on humanoid-run over 3M steps while requiring 5× fewer gradient updates (UTD=2 vs. 10) and roughly half the wall-clock training time. Overall, DIME's advantage is most pronounced in high-dimensional action spaces (dog: 38-dim, MYO: 39-dim) where the expressivity of the diffusion policy and its non-Gaussian exploration patterns provide the largest gains over Gaussian-parameterized methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIME extends the MaxEnt-RL / SAC framework (Haarnoja et al., 2018b) by replacing the Gaussian policy with a diffusion policy, casting the policy improvement step as an approximate inference problem over the full diffusion trajectory rather than a single action. The theoretical foundation draws on recent advances in approximate inference with diffusion models (Berner et al.; Richter & Berner), which provide the entropy lower bound that makes the MaxEnt objective tractable for diffusion policies. Practically, DIME adopts CrossQ's batch-renormalized, target-network-free Q-function and distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017), while departing from prior diffusion-RL methods (DIPO, QSM, DACER, QVPO) that rely on ad-hoc Gaussian or uniform exploration noise rather than a principled entropy objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIME provides the first principled, provably convergent policy iteration scheme for diffusion-based MaxEnt-RL, eliminating the need for hand-crafted exploration noise that plagues prior diffusion-RL methods. The practical algorithm requires fewer design choices and a lower update-to-data ratio than the SOTA Gaussian-based BRO while matching or exceeding its performance on high-dimensional tasks, making it an attractive option for practitioners. Open questions include extending beyond the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward process to general learned bridges (preliminary results are provided), exploring alternative divergences (e.g., log-variance), and scaling to more sophisticated critic architectures such as transformers.
