## Problem

Discrete Langevin dynamics, adapted from continuous-domain MCMC, frequently gets trapped in local optima when applied to combinatorial optimization because discrete local optima can have large gradients pointing to infeasible regions (unlike continuous optima where gradients vanish). This makes standard noise-based exploration insufficient for escaping, and existing discrete LD/diffusion solvers inherit this limitation without adequately accounting for the structural differences between continuous and discrete optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} encoding the problem structure (MIS, MCl, or MCut formulated as QUBO) along with hyperparameters (initial temperature τ₀, regularized step size d, parallel runs K, total steps T, penalty coefficient β). It produces a binary solution vector x ∈ {0,1}^N and the corresponding objective value (independent set size, clique size, or cut size). For RLSA the exact energy gradient ∇H(x) is required in closed form; for RLNN a 5-layer GCN with 128 hidden dimensions approximates the per-coordinate flipping probabilities, removing the need for an analytic gradient.

## Method

RLD regularizes the locally-balanced proposal by constraining the expected Hamming distance between the sampled and current solutions to a fixed value d, preventing the transition kernel from collapsing to a tiny neighborhood as temperature anneals. In RLSA, this is implemented by setting the effective step size α = τ/(Δ_(d)+ε) where Δ_(d) is the d-th largest element of Δ = (2x−1)⊙∇H(x), so that exactly d coordinates flip on average per step; when Δ_(d) < 0 (at a local optimum), the update reverses the gradient direction to escape rather than descend, acting as a discrete analog of normalized gradient descent. In RLNN, a GCN predicts per-node flipping probabilities trained with a local unsupervised loss combining the EGN-style energy expectation with a squared penalty on the deviation of the expected Hamming distance from d, eliminating the need for long-horizon return estimation or labeled data. Both solvers run K independent parallel chains, and RLSA requires only 5–20% of the runtime of the prior SOTA SA method iSCO while matching or exceeding its solution quality.

## Results

On the MIS problem, RLSA achieves set sizes of 19.97, 40.19, 44.10, and 375.31 on RB-[200-300], RB-[800-1200], ER-[700-800], and ER-[9000-11000] respectively, outperforming the SOTA SA baseline iSCO (19.29, 36.96, 42.18, 365.37) while using only 5–20% of its runtime (e.g., 35s vs. 2.71m on RB-[200-300], 1.66m vs. 1.10h on ER-[9000-11000]). On MCl, RLSA reaches 40.53 on RB-[800-1200] (vs. iSCO's 40.35) in 1.27m vs. 11.37m. On MCut, RLSA achieves 2955.81 on BA-[800-1200] (vs. iSCO's 2919.97) in 1.45m vs. 4.18m. RLNN, trained unsupervised, matches or exceeds SOTA NN baselines (DiffUCO, DIMES) on most datasets while completing training in under 3 hours versus up to 2 days for DiffUCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA SA baseline iSCO, RLSA wins on every dataset: it improves MIS size by 0.68–3.21 (e.g., 40.19 vs. 36.96 on RB-[800-1200]) while reducing runtime by 80–98% (e.g., 1.85m vs. 11.26m on RB-[800-1200], 1.66m vs. 1.10h on ER-[9000-11000]). Against the exact OR solver KaMIS, RLSA is within 0.13–2.96 in set size but runs 10–200× faster (e.g., 35s vs. 1.40h on RB-[200-300]). RLNN outperforms the RL baseline DIMES on ER-[700-800] (43.34 vs. 42.06) and ER-[9000-11000] (363.34 vs. 332.80), and matches or exceeds the SOTA unsupervised diffusion model DiffUCO on RB graphs (19.52 vs. 19.24 on RB-[200-300]) while training in under 3 hours versus up to 2 days. On MCl, RLSA edges out iSCO (40.53 vs. 40.35 on RB-[800-1200]) in 1.27m vs. 11.37m; on MCut, RLSA leads all non-OR methods (2955.81 on BA-[800-1200] vs. iSCO's 2919.97 and DiffUCO's 2947.53). The ablation confirms that removing the Hamming-distance regularization degrades RLNN substantially (e.g., 18.64 vs. 19.52 on MIS RB-[200-300]), validating the core contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete Langevin sampler (Zhang et al., 2022) by adding a Hamming-distance regularization constraint to the locally-balanced proposal framework (Zanella, 2017), ensuring a constant expected number of coordinate flips per step. The key conceptual departure from prior discrete MCMC work (GWG, PAFS/iSCO) is the observation that discrete local optima retain large gradients pointing to infeasible regions—unlike continuous optima where gradients vanish—making standard noise-based exploration insufficient. For the neural solver (RLNN), the training loss builds on the unsupervised EGN objective (Karalias & Loukas, 2020) but replaces the full-trajectory reinforcement learning used by DiffUCO with a local one-step objective, and the regularization term parallels curiosity-driven exploration in RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% runtime reduction over the SOTA SA method (iSCO) while matching or exceeding solution quality makes RLSA a practically attractive drop-in replacement for gradient-guided SA solvers on QUBO-type problems. The local training objective for RLNN (hours vs. days for DiffUCO) lowers the barrier to unsupervised neural CO solvers. However, the framework is restricted to binary variables and three specific problems (MIS, MCl, MCut), with no theoretical guarantees for the regularization's effect on convergence or mixing. Open questions include generalization to integer/categorical variables, problems with global constraints (e.g., TSP), and a formal analysis of when the Hamming-distance constraint is optimal versus alternative kernel choices.
