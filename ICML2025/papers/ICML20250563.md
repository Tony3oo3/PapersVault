## Problem

Generating novel enzymes for target molecules in zero-shot settings is fundamentally difficult because no positive training samples exist for the target substrate, and existing conditional generation methods (ligand-conditioned antibody design, EC-number-conditioned sequence generation) cannot be repurposed for small-molecule substrates. Additionally, no comprehensive evaluation framework or benchmark dataset existed for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250563/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The only required input is the target molecule's SMILES string; the method internally retrieves structurally similar substrates from a relational database (using Tanimoto similarity on Morgan fingerprints), fetches their associated enzymes, and aligns them into an MSA via ClustalW. The molecule is additionally encoded as a graph embedding via a GAT and appended as an extra token to each MSA row. The output is a single protein sequence (gaps removed) representing a novel enzyme predicted to catalyze the target molecule, with no requirement that it resemble any known enzyme for that substrate.

## Method

SENZ combines three components: (1) substrate-indexed retrieval that queries a substrate-enzyme relational database by molecular structural similarity rather than protein sequence similarity, exploiting the observation that enzymes catalyzing similar substrates share catalytic properties; (2) an order-agnostic autoregressive discrete diffusion generator with an MSA transformer backbone that denoises a fully masked sequence placed atop the retrieved MSA, progressively unmasking k positions per step; and (3) discriminator-guided training where a frozen pre-trained enzyme-substrate classifier scores the Gumbel-softmax 'soft' protein output, and the gradient of (1 − score) with respect to generator parameters steers the denoising distribution toward catalytically effective sequences. The total loss combines a reconstruction term (KL divergence to the training distribution) and the guidance term, balancing natural protein structure with substrate-specific catalytic capability.

## Results

On the full test set of 389 substrates, SENZ achieves the highest average log₁₀(k_cat) of 0.380, surpassing the test-set ground truth (0.363) and all neural-network baselines (ZymCTRL 0.375, ProGen2 0.352, LigandMPNN 0.342, ProtGPT2 0.322, NOS 0.224). On foldability (pLDDT), SENZ scores 62.8, matching ZymCTRL (62.5) and substantially exceeding ProtGPT2 (55.2), ProGen2 (55.5), LigandMPNN (31.0), and NOS (23.1). On the seven targeted design tasks (Table 2), SENZ outperforms ground-truth natural enzymes on 5 of 7 substrates, reaching log₁₀(k_cat) = 1.288 for leukotriene A4(1−) versus 0.371 for the ground truth. SENZ also achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance to the test set on BLASTp identity (20.8) and cluster-count difference (1.74), indicating generated proteins cluster similarly to natural enzymes per substrate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENZ outperforms all five neural-network baselines on catalytic capability (log₁₀ k_cat): it beats ZymCTRL (0.375), ProGen2 (0.352), LigandMPNN (0.342), ProtGPT2 (0.322), and NOS (0.224), and even exceeds the ground-truth test-set average (0.363). On foldability (pLDDT), SENZ (62.8) is essentially tied with ZymCTRL (62.5) but substantially outperforms ProtGPT2 (55.2), ProGen2 (55.5), LigandMPNN (31.0), and NOS (23.1); however, the rule-based retrieved enzymes achieve a much higher pLDDT of 85.9, reflecting a known activity–stability trade-off. On novelty and clustering, SENZ's Wasserstein distance to the test set on BLASTp identity (20.8) and absolute cluster-count difference (1.74) are the best among all generative methods, far surpassing ProtGPT2 (31.5 / 8.58), ProGen2 (26.7 / 8.47), NOS (36.5 / 8.59), and LigandMPNN (33.6 / 8.52), indicating that SENZ generates proteins that cluster per-substrate like natural enzymes rather than collapsing into a generic distribution. On the seven targeted design tasks (Table 2), SENZ achieves the highest log₁₀ k_cat on 5 of 7 substrates, including a 3.5× improvement over ground truth on leukotriene A4(1−) (1.288 vs. 0.371), while ZymCTRL and LigandMPNN show the strongest per-substrate performance among baselines but still trail SENZ on average. ZymCTRL's competitive k_cat (0.375) comes at the cost of requiring a ground-truth EC number as input, which is unavailable in the zero-shot setting SENZ targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENZ builds on the autoregressive discrete diffusion framework (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) and the MSA Transformer backbone (Rao et al., 2021), repurposing them for a fundamentally different conditioning signal: a small-molecule substrate rather than a protein sequence or EC label. It departs from prior conditional protein generation (ZymCTRL, LigandMPNN, NOS) by introducing a substrate-indexed retrieval step that queries a relational database using molecular Tanimoto similarity, rather than relying on protein-protein sequence similarity or structural docking. The guided training with a frozen enzyme-substrate discriminator, applied through Gumbel-softmax differentiable sampling, extends the guided diffusion paradigm from NOS (which scores antibody-antigen binding) to the enzyme-catalysis setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first formal definition, dataset, and multi-metric evaluation protocol for zero-shot substrate-specified enzyme generation, filling a gap where prior methods either required EC labels, protein ligands, or structural templates. The demonstrated trade-off between catalytic capability and foldability as retrieval depth increases, and the finding that generated enzymes can outperform natural ground-truth enzymes in predicted kcat, suggest computational design may surpass evolutionary optimization for specific substrates. Key limitations include in silico-only evaluation (no wet-lab validation), restriction to small-molecule substrates, and reliance on a single kcat predictor (UniKP) as the primary functional metric, leaving open whether the predicted catalytic advantage translates to experimental reality.
