## Problem

In Continual Generalized Category Discovery (C-GCD), models must incrementally learn new classes from unlabeled data streams that mix known and novel categories, yet existing methods suffer severe catastrophic forgetting without a unified explanation of why. The paper identifies that covariance misalignment between old and new class distributions—quantified via Bhattacharyya distance—irreversibly biases decision boundaries, and that prior approaches either over-regularize (limiting plasticity) or under-regularize (causing forgetting) without a principled stopping criterion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two phases: an offline phase with a small labeled set (e.g., 50% of classes, 80% of samples per class) and an online phase with unlabeled streams where each session contains both novel and previously seen categories. A DINO pre-trained ViT-B/16 backbone extracts features, reduced to 384 dimensions via PCA. The output is a class-conditional multivariate normal distribution (mean vector and full covariance matrix) per class, used for classification via Mahalanobis-distance nearest-class-mean, yielding a predicted class label for each input image.

## Method

VB-CGCD models each class as a multivariate normal distribution fitted via stochastic variational inference (SVI) on the ELBO, which naturally suppresses pseudo-label noise by penalizing outliers through the KL term. The backbone is fine-tuned only during the offline phase using a combined cross-entropy and contrastive loss (BOFT), then frozen for all online sessions to prevent feature drift. In each online session, k-means clustering assigns initial pseudo-labels, a self-correcting re-labeling step refines them by merging old and new class distributions into a unified MVN-NCM classifier, and SVI re-fits the distributions. The key mechanism is a covariance-driven early stopping rule that halts training when the new class's covariance determinant aligns with the average of old classes' determinants (ratio R < 0.01), preventing the Bhattacharyya distance from growing asymmetrically and thus preserving old-class accuracy while maximizing new-class plasticity.

## Results

VB-CGCD achieves an average 15.21% improvement in final overall accuracy (S5) over the strongest baseline (Happy) across four datasets: 81.23% on CIFAR-100, 74.52% on TinyImageNet, 87.32% on ImageNet-100, and 66.44% on CUB-200. The method also attains the lowest forgetting rate (M_f) among all baselines on three of four datasets (6.94% on C100, 5.72% on Tiny, 5.68% on IN100) while simultaneously achieving the highest novelty learning rate (M_d) (79.14%, 68.12%, 87.04%, 56.46% respectively). In the extreme 10%-labeled, 9-session benchmark, VB-CGCD reaches 67.86% final accuracy versus 38.55% for Happy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VB-CGCD outperforms all four baselines on every dataset in final overall accuracy. Against the strongest competitor Happy (Ma et al., 2024), VB-CGCD gains +20.46% on CIFAR-100 (81.23 vs 60.77), +19.59% on TinyImageNet (74.52 vs 54.93), +8.74% on ImageNet-100 (87.32 vs 78.58), and +12.07% on CUB-200 (66.44 vs 54.37). The gap over GM (Zhang et al., 2022) and MetaGCD (Wu et al., 2023) is substantially larger, with those methods suffering severe forgetting (final accuracies dropping below 50% on most datasets). PromptCCD (Cendra et al., 2024) maintains moderate stability but lags VB-CGCD by 10–27% in final accuracy. Notably, VB-CGCD also achieves the best novelty learning rate (M_d) on all four datasets while keeping the forgetting rate (M_f) near the supervised upper bound, whereas Happy falls below the lower forgetting bound on CUB, indicating insufficient new-class learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nearest-class-mean (NCM) classifier from prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017) by replacing Euclidean distance with a covariance-aware Mahalanobis metric, and further replaces direct statistical estimation of class distributions (as in FeCAM, Goswami et al., 2023) with stochastic variational inference for noise-robust fitting. It departs from prior C-GCD methods (GM, MetaGCD, PromptCCD, Happy) that rely on regularization, knowledge distillation, or frozen backbones to combat forgetting, instead providing a unified Bayesian analysis that identifies covariance misalignment between old and new classes as the root cause of catastrophic forgetting. The covariance-driven early stopping criterion is a novel practical mechanism not present in prior variational continual learning work (VCL, GVCL, VAR-GP), which regularize parameter updates via KL divergence but do not model per-class feature distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a theoretical diagnosis (Bhattacharyya distance analysis showing how covariance growth irreversibly skews decision boundaries) and a practical remedy (early stopping at covariance alignment) for a problem that has been treated largely heuristically in C-GCD. The new 10%-labeled, 9-session benchmark (b10t9) exposes a significant weakness in current SOTA (Happy drops to 38.55% final accuracy), making it a useful stress test for future methods. Key limitations include sensitivity to initial clustering quality in high-dimensional spaces and degraded covariance estimation under extreme data scarcity (CUB-200 with 25 samples/class), suggesting that hierarchical Bayesian priors or deep clustering integration are natural next steps.
