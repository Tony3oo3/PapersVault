## Problem

Existing Neural ODE methods model time series with static parameters, capturing only surface-level state dynamics while ignoring the deeper driving rules encoded in how those parameters evolve over time. Although ANODEV2 introduced time-evolving parameters, it treated their dynamics as a generic ODE without revealing the intrinsic linear structure beneath the apparent nonlinearity, leaving the fundamental governing modes of the system unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250089/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete time series trajectory {x(t)} over an input window and produces a continuous predicted trajectory at arbitrary future times via ODE integration. The initial Koopman observable w(t₀) is either drawn from a centered Gaussian (when only an initial point is given) or estimated by an RNN-based IKOE module from the first N_input time steps (when a sequence is given). Beyond the predicted trajectory, the framework outputs the eigenvalue spectrum of the learned Koopman matrix A, whose real parts encode evolution speed and imaginary parts encode intrinsic frequencies, enabling spectral interpretability of the system's dominant modes.

## Method

KoNODE introduces a three-level hierarchical structure: the observed state x(t) evolves under a neural ODE with time-varying parameters θ(t); θ(t) is modeled as a neural-network observation h(w(t); ψ) of a latent trajectory w(t); and w(t) follows a linear ODE dw/dt = Aw where A is constrained to a block-diagonal form of 2×2 blocks [αᵢ βᵢ; −βᵢ αᵢ] representing conjugate complex eigenpairs. This Koopman-linear constraint encodes the intrinsic linear dynamics that drive the apparent nonlinear parameter evolution, following the Koopman operator theory. Training uses a custom adjoint method that backpropagates through both coupled ODEs (state and Koopman observable) simultaneously, with gradients for A, ψ, and w₀ computed via integral expressions derived from the Lagrangian KKT conditions. The framework is trained end-to-end in a supervised manner, and theoretical error bounds (scaling as O(m^{-r}) in Koopman dimension and O(1/√N) in data size) guarantee that the finite-rank approximation introduces only minor additional error over standard NODEs.

## Results

On the Non-linear Oscillator (system modeling), KoNODE achieves a test MSE of 0.085±0.031, outperforming the second-best LEADS (0.120±0.049) by ~29%. On the Damped Oscillator Family (system family modeling), KoNODE reaches 0.810±0.009 (×10⁻²), beating ANODEV2 (0.895±0.126) and LEADS (0.907±0.073). On real-world robot motion prediction, KoNODE achieves the lowest MSE across all three tasks: Imitation C (0.017±0.003), Imitation cube pick (0.022±0.001), and Imitation S (0.005±0.002). On multivariate forecasting (Table 2), KoNODE reports the best MSE on 5 of 6 datasets (CUP, WEATHER, ETTH2, ETTM1, ETTM2), with Koopa leading only on ETTH1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KoNODE achieves the best test MSE across all five evaluation tasks in Table 1, outperforming every baseline. Against the closest competitors on the Non-linear Oscillator, KoNODE (0.085) beats LEADS (0.120) and CoDA (0.123) by 29–31%, while methods with static parameters (NODE, MoNODE, OINNs) lag by 5–7×. On the Damped Oscillator Family, KoNODE (0.810) edges out ANODEV2 (0.895) and LEADS (0.907), confirming that Koopman-driven parameter evolution outperforms ANODEV2's Green's-function-based approach for generalizing to unseen system parameters. On robot motion prediction, KoNODE dominates HBNODE (0.021/0.030/0.015) and OINNs (0.022/0.042/0.016) on all three tasks, with particularly large margins on Imitation S (0.005 vs. 0.010 for CoDA). In the multivariate forecasting comparison (Table 2), KoNODE leads on 5/6 datasets; Koopa (Liu et al., 2023) wins on ETTH1 (0.605 vs. KoODE's 0.678), while KoNODE remains competitive, and both Koopman-based methods outperform Transformer baselines (Autoformer, PatchTST) and SSM-based SST on most datasets. The paper also reports that KoNODE converges in 109 s (10 epochs) versus NODE's 216 s (48 epochs), indicating faster convergence despite higher per-iteration cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

KoNODE extends the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) by building on ANODEV2's (Zhang et al., 2019) insight that ODE parameters should evolve over time, but departs fundamentally in how that evolution is modeled: rather than treating parameter dynamics as a separate ODE coupled via Green's functions (as ANODEV2 does, primarily for image data), KoNODE models θ(t) as a nonlinear observation of an intrinsic linear system governed by Koopman operators. This creates a three-level hierarchy—observed state dynamics, parameter dynamics, and Koopman linear dynamics—that is conceptually distinct from both standard NODEs and prior Koopman-based forecasting methods like Koopa (Liu et al., 2023), which apply Koopman operators directly to the state rather than to the ODE parameters. The adjoint-based optimization and spectral analysis tools are inherited from the NODE and Koopman theory literatures respectively, but their integration into a unified hierarchical training framework is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-level hierarchical structure offers a principled way to extract interpretable system properties (dominant frequencies, stability, periodicity) from the learned Koopman spectrum, moving NODEs beyond black-box predictors toward mechanistic understanding of dynamic systems. The consistent performance gains across synthetic oscillators, robot motion, and multivariate forecasting datasets suggest the Koopman parameterization is a broadly useful inductive bias for long-horizon prediction. However, the per-iteration cost is higher than vanilla NODE, the practical guidance for choosing the Koopman dimension m remains somewhat heuristic (Prop. 3.3 gives a range but not a precise rule), and the framework has not yet been tested on irregularly sampled data or very high-dimensional systems—both noted as future work by the authors.
