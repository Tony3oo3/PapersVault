## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs suffer from quadratic attention complexity, making long-context inference and training prohibitively expensive. Existing KV cache compression methods (e.g., StreamingLLM) are training-free, creating a mismatch between training and inference behavior, and they discard all non-local, non-sink tokens—including separator tokens that the paper shows carry disproportionate attention weight and encode compressed segment information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text token sequence and produces autoregressively generated tokens while maintaining a compressed KV cache that retains only initial (attention-sink) tokens, separator tokens (punctuation such as '.', ',', '?', '!', ';', ':', '\t', '\n'), and a sliding window of n neighboring tokens. In the streaming variant, the KV cache is partitioned into four blocks (Initial, Separator, Past Window, Local Window) with a fixed total capacity c, and compression is triggered periodically when runtime usage reaches c, moving separator KV into a dedicated Separator Cache and discarding other past-window entries.

## Method

SepLLM introduces a data-dependent sparse attention mask in which each token attends only to a small set of initial tokens, all preceding separator tokens, and the closest n neighboring tokens, rather than all preceding tokens as in vanilla attention. The key insight, validated through attention-map visualization on Llama-3-8B, is that punctuation tokens receive disproportionately high attention scores because they naturally summarize the semantic content of the segment they terminate; by retaining only these separators' KV pairs, the model preserves segment-level information while discarding redundant content tokens. The architecture is integrated into both training-from-scratch and post-training pipelines (using a FlexAttention-based Sep-Attention kernel for hardware efficiency), eliminating the train-inference gap of prior training-free methods. For streaming applications, a Tailored Streaming Design manages four cache blocks with periodic compression, achieving bounded KV cache usage (average ≈ (w+c+a+s)/2) over arbitrarily long sequences. The paper further proves a universal approximation theorem showing that encoder-based SepLLM with 2 heads, hidden dimension 1, and FFN dimension 4 can approximate any continuous sequence-to-sequence function to arbitrary precision.

## Results

In the training-free setting on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, SepLLM(n=256) achieves 77.18% on GSM8K-CoT (vs. 77.79% Vanilla) and 64.68% overall on MMLU (vs. 65.72% Vanilla) while using only 47.36% and 44.61% of the KV cache, respectively. In the training-from-scratch setting (Pythia-160m), SepLLM(n=128) reduces LAMBADA perplexity from 34.83 to 30.16 and improves SciQA accuracy from 81.50% to 82.60% while using only 32.27% of the KV cache. In streaming applications on PG19, SepLLM(s=64) maintains a perplexity of 33.9 at 4M tokens versus 36.1 for StreamingLLM with the same KV capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SepLLM consistently outperforms all four compared baselines at matched KV cache budgets. Against StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2024b), SepLLM(n=256) gains +7.51 points on GSM8K-CoT strict (77.18 vs. 69.67) and +2.58 on MMLU overall (64.68 vs. 62.10) at comparable KV usage; even when StreamingLLM's window is enlarged to n=380 to match KV budgets, it still trails by 6.29 points on GSM8K. Against H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), SnapKV (Li et al., 2024), and PyramidKV (Zhang et al., 2024) on GSM8K-CoT at ~47% KV, SepLLM leads by 0.91, 0.68, and 1.36 points (flexible) and by 2.12, 3.56, and 5.16 points (strict), respectively. In streaming PG19 evaluation, SepLLM(s=64) achieves lower perplexity than StreamingLLM at every tested length from 1M to 4M tokens (e.g., 33.9 vs. 36.1 at 4M). The paper also shows that a fixed-interval variant (FixLLM) significantly underperforms SepLLM, confirming that separator tokens specifically—not arbitrary periodic tokens—carry the compressed segment information.

## Relation to Prior Work

SepLLM builds directly on StreamingLLM's (Xiao et al., 2024) framework of retaining attention sinks and local window tokens for streaming inference, but introduces a fundamentally different sparsity principle: data-dependent retention of separator tokens (punctuation) as natural segment compressors. Unlike prior KV cache compression methods (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV) that use learned or heuristic importance scores to select tokens, and unlike earlier sparse attention work (BigBird, Longformer, SparseBERT) that applies fixed masks to BERT-style encoders, SepLLM leverages the semantic structure of natural language itself—where punctuation inherently delimits and summarizes segments—to define its attention mask. The paper also departs from the training-free-only paradigm of most KV compression work by integrating the sparse attention mechanism into both from-scratch training and post-training, closing the train-inference gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that separator tokens function as natural information compressors for their preceding segments offers a new lens on how LLMs encode and retrieve contextual information, potentially informing future architecture design beyond just inference acceleration. The plug-and-play nature (applicable training-free, from-scratch, or via post-training) and the >50% KV cache reduction with maintained performance make this practically significant for deploying long-context LLMs under memory constraints. Open questions include whether the separator-compression hypothesis generalizes to languages with different punctuation conventions, whether the universal approximation guarantee (proven only for encoder-based SepLLM) extends to the decoder-only setting used in practice, and whether the benefit persists as models scale further beyond 40B parameters.
