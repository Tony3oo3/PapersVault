## Problem

Pretrained LLMs are optimized for next-token prediction and linguistic coherence, so their internal representations do not inherently separate truthful from hallucinated content. Existing hallucination detection methods that rely on default LLM embeddings or require extensive labeled data fail to achieve reliable separation, and no prior work has explored steering LLM representations specifically for hallucination detection rather than hallucination mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a concatenated sequence of an input prompt and a single LLM generation (produced via beam search with 5 beams) and applies the TSV at a fixed intermediate layer during a forward pass. It outputs a continuous truthfulness score S(x') computed as the normalized von Mises-Fisher probability of the final-layer last-token embedding belonging to the truthful class, which is thresholded to yield a binary hallucination label. The entire detection requires only a single forward pass (O(m²) complexity), unlike consistency-based baselines that need multiple samples.

## Method

The Truthfulness Separator Vector (TSV) is a single learnable vector (≈4K parameters) added to the residual stream at an intermediate layer during inference, reshaping the representation space without modifying model weights. Training uses a two-stage semi-supervised framework: first, TSV is trained on a small labeled exemplar set (N=32) by maximizing a von Mises-Fisher MLE objective that pulls steered embeddings into compact clusters around class prototypes; second, unlabeled LLM generations are pseudo-labeled via an entropic-regularized optimal transport problem (solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm) that respects the imbalanced class distribution, followed by confidence-based selection of the most reliable samples to augment the exemplar set. At inference, the truthfulness score is computed from the learned class prototypes, and removing the TSV restores the model's original generation capability. This approach achieves near-fully-supervised performance (84.2% vs. 85.5% AUROC on TruthfulQA) with only 32 labeled examples and 8×–800× fewer trainable parameters than PEFT alternatives.

## Results

On the TruthfulQA benchmark with LLaMA-3.1-8b, TSV achieves 84.2% AUROC using only 32 labeled exemplars, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline HaloScope (70.6%) by 13.6% and the fully-supervised SAPLMA (78.2%) by 6.0%. The method reaches performance nearly matching the fully-supervised oracle (85.5% AUROC) while requiring orders of magnitude fewer labels. On Qwen-2.5-7b, TSV achieves 87.3% on TruthfulQA and 82.0% on SciQ, consistently leading all 11 baselines across all four datasets. TSV uses only 4K trainable parameters (0.00005% of the base model), compared to 3.4M for LoRA and 32K for LoReFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSV decisively outperforms all 11 baselines across both model families and all four datasets. The most notable margin is against HaloScope (the strongest internal-state-based method), where TSV gains +13.6% AUROC on TruthfulQA with LLaMA-3.1-8b (84.2% vs. 70.6%) and +6.0% on Qwen-2.5-7b (87.3% vs. 81.3%). TSV also surpasses the fully-supervised SAPLMA† by +6.0% on TruthfulQA (84.2% vs. 78.2%) despite using only 32 labeled examples versus full labels. Against logit-based methods (Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Semantic Entropy) and consistency-based methods (SelfCKGPT, EigenScore, Lexical Similarity), TSV leads by 10–40% AUROC depending on the dataset, with the largest gap on NQ Open where most baselines hover near chance (50–63%) while TSV reaches 76.1%. Verbalized methods (Verbalize, Self-evaluation) and CCS perform poorly on TruthfulQA (50–68%), where TSV achieves 84.2%. The gap to the fully-supervised oracle (TSV†) is only 1.3% on TruthfulQA, confirming that 32 exemplars suffice to approach the supervised upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the activation-engineering / steering-vector framework (Im & Li, 2025) to a new objective: reshaping LLM latent space for hallucination *detection* rather than the previously explored goal of *mitigating* hallucinated generations (Li et al., 2024; Marks & Tegmark, 2024). It departs from prior internal-state-based detectors (HaloScope, SAPLMA, CCS) that rely on default LLM embeddings by introducing a learnable Truthfulness Separator Vector that explicitly restructures the representation geometry. The pseudo-labeling stage borrows the entropic-regularized optimal transport formulation (Sinkhorn, Cuturi 2013) from the semi-supervised clustering literature, adapting it to respect imbalanced class proportions in LLM generations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—4K trainable parameters, 32 labeled exemplars, no model fine-tuning, plug-and-play at inference—makes hallucination detection accessible for deployed LLM systems where annotation budgets are tight and model weights are frozen. The near-parity with a fully-supervised oracle (84.2% vs 85.5% AUROC on TruthfulQA) and strong cross-dataset transfer (79.8% on TriviaQA when trained on TruthfulQA) suggest the learned separation is capturing a general truthfulness signal rather than dataset-specific artifacts. Open questions include extending to token/phrase-level fine-grained detection, long-form generation, and whether the single-vector steering generalizes to multi-class or multi-domain truthfulness settings beyond binary QA.
