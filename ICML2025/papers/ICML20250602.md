## Problem

Existing mean field game and control models based on graphons, Lp graphons, and graphexes require the expected average degree to diverge to infinity, excluding realistic sparse networks such as power law graphs with coefficient above two (e.g., the internet, coauthorship, and biological networks). This paper addresses the open problem of learning optimal cooperative policies on such very sparse agent networks where the average degree is finite but the degree variance may diverge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250602/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse graph sequence (e.g., Chung-Lu, Barabási-Albert, or configuration model) with finite expected average degree, a finite state space, and a finite action space as input. It produces a policy ensemble where each degree class k has its own policy π^k, plus a unified policy π^∞ for all agents exceeding a threshold k*, along with the corresponding per-degree mean field distributions. The graph topology is consumed through local weak convergence properties rather than explicit edge-level interactions, and the two-systems approximation collapses all high-degree agents into a single infinite mean field to keep computation tractable.

## Method

The core contribution is the Local Weak Mean Field Control (LWMFC) model, which uses local weak convergence of graph sequences (rather than graphon or graphex convergence) to define a limiting system with separate mean fields per degree class. A two-systems approximation splits agents into low-degree (≤k*) and high-degree (>k*) groups: high-degree agents share a unified neighborhood distribution derived from a size-biased degree heuristic, while low-degree agents sample neighborhoods from a multinomial distribution over that unified state. Two learning algorithms are provided: LWMFC Policy Gradient, which solves the resulting single-agent MFC MDP via PPO where the high-level action is the full policy ensemble, and LWMFMARL, which directly interacts with the real graph using policy gradient RL without requiring model knowledge. Theoretical results establish mean field convergence, objective convergence, and optimal policy transfer from the limiting system to all sufficiently large finite systems under Assumption 2.2.

## Results

On eight real-world networks, LWMFC achieves average expected total variation Δμ of 0.19–5.00% across four problems (SIS, SIR, Color, Rumor), substantially outperforming LPGMFG (4.88–39.83%) and GXMFG (0.99–32.62%). The most dramatic gains appear on the Color problem, where LWMFC reaches 0.19–1.05% versus 34.18–39.83% for LPGMFG and 4.91–32.62% for GXMFG. On synthetic Chung-Lu graphs, LWMFC and LWMFMARL policy gradient outperform IPPO on all four problems at N=860 and N=1598 (e.g., SIS: −9.11 vs −19.70; Rumor: 1.35 vs 0.25), while remaining competitive at smaller sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LWMFC dominates both LPGMFG (Fabian et al., AISTATS 2023) and GXMFG (Fabian et al., ICLR 2024) across all eight real-world networks and all four problems. The gap is most extreme on the Color problem, where LWMFC achieves 0.19–1.05% total variation versus 34–40% for LPGMFG and 5–33% for GXMFG, reflecting the inability of graphon/graphex models to capture the high fraction of low-degree nodes in sparse topologies. GXMFG is consistently closer to LWMFC than LPGMFG (e.g., SIS on Enron: 4.73% vs 24.77% for LPGMFG vs 3.39% for LWMFC), but still trails by 2–4× on most settings. Against IPPO (Papoudakis et al., NeurIPS 2021) on synthetic CL graphs, LWMFC and LWMFMARL win on all four problems at N=860 and N=1598 (e.g., Rumor at N=860: 1.35/1.47 vs 0.25 for IPPO), while IPPO is only marginally better in two of eight smaller-graph settings (SIR at N=167 and N=406). The extensive approximation LWMFC* provides a modest additional accuracy boost over LWMFC (e.g., 0.90% vs 1.60% on Flixster SIS) but is computationally infeasible for Color and Rumor at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean field games/control lineage from graphon MFGs (Caines & Huang, 2019) through Lp graphon MFGs (Fabian et al., 2023) and graphex MFGs (Fabian et al., 2024) by replacing the graph convergence framework entirely. All prior graph-based mean field models require the average degree to diverge to infinity, excluding power law networks with coefficient above two; here, the authors instead leverage local weak convergence (Lacker et al., 2023) to handle graph sequences with finite expected degree but potentially diverging variance. The two-systems approximation and PPO-based learning algorithms are novel algorithmic contributions, while the theoretical backbone (MF convergence, objective convergence, optimal policy transfer) is built directly on the local weak convergence machinery of Lacker et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Many empirically important networks (internet topology, coauthorship graphs, biological networks) follow power laws with coefficient between 2 and 3, placing them outside the scope of all existing mean field graph models; LWMFC is the first framework to rigorously cover this class. The dramatic accuracy gaps on real networks (e.g., ~40× lower total variation on the Color problem vs. Lp graphons) confirm that the diverging-average-degree assumption is not merely a technicality but fundamentally limits approximation quality. Open questions include extending to infinite state/action spaces, partial observability, and bounded rationality, as well as understanding when the two-systems approximation's multinomial neighborhood assumption breaks down for non-locally-tree-like topologies.
