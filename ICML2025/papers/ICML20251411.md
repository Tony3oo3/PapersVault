## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models frequently hallucinate syntactically coherent yet visually ungrounded content, but it remains unclear how hallucination emerges and propagates internally during generation. While prior work has examined attention patterns and logit-space divergence, the internal token-level dynamics that drive visual information loss over the course of generation have not been systematically characterized.

## Input / Output

VISTA is a training-free inference-time intervention that takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please help me describe the image in detail') and produces a text description or short VQA answer (Yes/No). It operates on top of any standard decoding strategy (greedy, beam search with beam size 5, or nucleus sampling with top-p=0.9) and requires only a single additional forward pass per image to compute the per-image Visual Steering Vector, which is then cached. Maximum generation length is 512 tokens for open-ended captioning tasks.

## Method

VISTA combines two complementary modules motivated by three empirical observations from token-logit ranking analysis: (1) gradual visual information loss, (2) early excitation of semantically meaningful tokens in penultimate layers, and (3) hidden genuine information retaining high rankings even when not decoded. The Visual Steering Vector (VSV) module constructs a per-image steering vector by contrasting the residual stream of a positive context (system + visual + query tokens) against a negative context (system + query tokens only), then injects this vector into every layer's residual stream at each generation step with L2 normalization to maintain stability. The Self-Logits Augmentation (SLA) module applies the LM head to hidden states from w=5 layers preceding the final layer (via the logit lens), averages those augmentation logits, and blends them with the final-layer logits using a mixing coefficient γ=0.3. Together, VSV counteracts visual information dilution in activation space while SLA promotes decoding of semantically meaningful tokens identified through early-layer excitation, and the two modules operate synergistically without requiring any external supervision or model retraining.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (open-ended image captioning, 500 MSCOCO images, max 512 tokens), VISTA achieves approximately 40% relative hallucination reduction over vanilla decoding across all four architectures and three decoding strategies. Under greedy decoding, VISTA reduces CHAIR_S from 46.4→20.4 (LLaVA-1.5), 35.2→19.8 (MiniGPT-4), 56.8→31.4 (Shikra), and 38.0→27.4 (InstructBLIP), with corresponding CHAIR_I reductions of roughly 40–45%. On POPE (targeted VQA), VISTA improves average accuracy by up to +6.4% under nucleus sampling (MiniGPT-4: 60.56→66.96), and on MME it boosts overall scores by up to +224 points (MiniGPT-4 under nucleus: 845.30→1069.37), confirming that hallucination mitigation does not degrade general capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA outperforms all four baselines across every architecture and decoding strategy where they are jointly available. Against PAI (the closest competitor, also training-free), VISTA achieves lower CHAIR_S on LLaVA-1.5 (20.4 vs. 22.8), MiniGPT-4 (19.8 vs. 29.2), and Shikra (31.4 vs. 40.8) under greedy decoding, with the largest gap on MiniGPT-4 (−9.4 CHAIR_S). DoLa, which contrasts logits across layers, is only supported on LLaVA-1.5 and Shikra; VISTA beats it on both (20.4 vs. 45.4 on LLaVA-1.5; 31.4 vs. 60.0 on Shikra), and notably DoLa actually *increases* hallucination on Shikra relative to vanilla. VCD (visual contrastive decoding) is available for LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP; VISTA dominates on both (20.4 vs. 47.4; 27.4 vs. 45.8). OPERA, tailored for beam search, is beaten by VISTA on all four architectures (e.g., 17.4 vs. 45.2 on LLaVA-1.5). VISTA's advantage is most pronounced under nucleus sampling, where PAI degrades substantially (CHAIR_S 30.2 on LLaVA-1.5) while VISTA maintains strong performance (24.0), and where VCD actually worsens hallucination (60.8 vs. 53.2 vanilla on LLaVA-1.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA draws on two distinct lineages: (1) activation-space steering vectors from LLM interpretability (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023), repurposed here as a per-image visual grounding signal rather than an amortized concept vector, and (2) the logit lens (Gurney, 2023), which enables reading intermediate-layer hidden states through the LM head. It departs from prior contrastive decoding methods (DoLa, VCD, OPERA) that operate in logits space or attention space by intervening earlier in the residual stream, and from PAI (Liu et al., 2024g) which modifies attention weights by instead adding a contrastive steering vector that avoids amplifying accumulated language priors. The token-ranking diagnostic framework, while built on the logit lens, introduces a new empirical lens for tracking visual information flow across both time and depth during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISTA's training-free, decoding-agnostic design makes it immediately deployable on existing LVLMs without retraining, and the ~40% hallucination reduction with only 1.27× latency overhead is practically significant for production systems. The mechanistic findings—particularly that hidden genuine tokens retain high rankings even when not decoded—suggest LVLMs perceive more visual information than they express, pointing toward inference-time interventions as a viable path rather than requiring architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn visual dialogue, whether it benefits from stronger vision encoders, and whether the per-image steering vector computation can be further amortized for high-throughput settings.
