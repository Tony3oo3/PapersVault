## Problem

One-step video generation remains largely unsolved: existing distillation methods are limited to low-resolution (512×512 or 640×352) videos of at most 16 frames and still require 4 diffusion steps. Direct adversarial training of large diffusion transformers (billions of parameters) is highly unstable and prone to collapse, making it unclear whether a single forward pass can produce high-resolution, multi-second video at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251511/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by a text encoder) and a Gaussian noise latent z ∈ R^{t'×h'×w'×c'} as input, and produces a single generated sample in one forward pass: either a 1024px image or a 1280×720, 24fps, 2-second (49-frame) video in latent space. On a single H100 GPU the DiT forward pass takes 2.65 s for the video latent; with 8× H100 GPUs the full pipeline (text encoder + DiT + VAE decoder) completes in ~2 s, achieving real-time generation. The model always operates at the final diffusion timestep T, collapsing the entire denoising trajectory into one evaluation.

## Method

APT fine-tunes a pre-trained MMDiT (8B parameters, 36 transformer layers) with a standard GAN objective directly against real data, rather than distilling from a teacher's outputs. The generator is first initialized via consistency distillation (MSE loss, CFG 7.5) to provide a stable starting point, then trained adversarially; the discriminator shares the same MMDiT backbone but adds cross-attention-only blocks at layers 16, 26, and 36 that aggregate multi-layer features into a single scalar logit. Two key stabilizing designs are introduced: (1) a timestep ensemble where the discriminator receives inputs at a randomly sampled and shifted timestep t rather than t=0, and (2) an approximated R1 regularization that replaces the intractable second-order gradient with a finite-difference penalty ‖D(x,c) − D(N(x,σI),c)‖², which is critical to prevent collapse under FSDP/FlashAttention. Training proceeds in two stages—1024px images (128–256 H100s, 350 EMA updates) then 1280×720 video (1024 H100s, 300 updates)—using RMSProp at 5e-6/3e-6 learning rate in BF16.

## Results

In user studies, APT is the only one-step method to outperform its own 25-step diffusion baseline on visual fidelity (+37.2%), while all other one-step methods show degradation (ranging from -7.7% to -94.4%). Against state-of-the-art one-step image generators, APT ranks 2nd in absolute preference (trailing FLUX-Schnell) and 1st in relative preference (adjusted for base model strength), winning on visual fidelity and structural integrity but losing on text alignment. For video, APT 1-step achieves a VBench total score of 82.00, nearly matching the 25-step diffusion baseline (82.15), and generates a 2-second 1280×720 24fps video in 6.03s on a single H100 (1.97s on 8× H100, i.e., real-time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX-Schnell, APT wins on visual fidelity (+35.7%) but loses substantially on structural integrity (−21.5%) and text alignment (−28.1%), yielding a net −4.6% average — the only baseline APT does not beat overall. Against SDXL-Lightning, APT wins on all three criteria (+34.1%, +14.1%, +11.4%; net +19.9%), making it the most consistent win. Against SDXL-Turbo and SD3.5-Large-Turbo, APT achieves large margins on visual fidelity (+68.9% and +97.8% respectively) but still trails on text alignment (−7.9% and −16.7%). Against SDXL-DMD2, SDXL-Hyper, and SDXL-Nitro-Realism, APT consistently wins on visual fidelity (+24.6% to +43.6%) and structural integrity (+4.1% to +16.7%) while losing modestly on text alignment (−4.9% to −11.8%). On the base-model comparison (Table 3), APT's underlying diffusion model is weaker than FLUX and SD3.5-Large on all criteria but slightly stronger than SDXL, which the authors note inflates APT's relative advantage over SDXL-family baselines. For video (Table 4), APT 1-step beats its own 25-step diffusion on visual fidelity (+10.4%) but degrades on structural integrity (−38.5%) and text alignment (−8.3%); the 2-step variant narrows the structural gap to −31.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion distillation paradigm (where a pre-trained teacher generates targets and the student mimics them) by instead performing adversarial post-training directly on real data, using the diffusion model only for weight initialization. The closest prior work is UFO-Gen, which also trains adversarially on real data, but uses a DiffusionGAN-style corrupted-input discriminator and sub-1B convolutional models; APT scales this to 8B-parameter MMDiT transformers for both images and videos. The generator is initialized via consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023) to avoid the instability of training a diffusion model adversarially from scratch, and the discriminator incorporates an approximated R1 regularization (Roth et al., 2017) adapted to work around FSDP/FlashAttention limitations on higher-order gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of one-step high-resolution video generation (1280×720, 24fps, 2s) at real-time speed on 8×H100, a significant practical milestone for video generation deployment. The finding that adversarial post-training on real data can surpass the diffusion teacher in visual fidelity (resolving CFG-induced over-exposure) challenges the assumption that distillation quality is inherently bounded by the teacher. Key open questions include whether the structural integrity degradation (attributed to generator under-capacity in mode transitions) can be addressed, whether longer videos are feasible, and whether the approximated R1 generalizes to other large-scale adversarial training settings. The paper also provides an important caution that standard automatic metrics (FID, PFID, CLIP) can be misleadingly optimistic for one-step models, reinforcing the need for human evaluation.
