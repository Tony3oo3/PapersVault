## Problem

Existing synthetic data pipelines for image-text pairs are ad hoc, domain-specific, and lack fine-grained control over the generation process. They typically support either image or text augmentation but not both simultaneously, and they cannot apply user-specified policies (e.g., removing objects, enforcing safety constraints, or targeting long-tail classes) to the synthesis, limiting their utility for diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a real or synthetic RGB image (224×224 for CLIP pretraining) along with an optional original caption and user-defined control policies that specify operations on visual tags (remove, add, replace) or generation style (cinematic, realistic, isometric). It produces synthetic captions (averaging ~60 words vs. ~8 in original captions), synthetic images via diffusion, or paired image-text samples. The closed-loop design allows any output to be re-fed as input, enabling iterative synthesis and automatic quality filtering by re-extracting visual tags and checking tag-presence ratios against a threshold.

## Method

CtrlSynth decomposes an image's visual semantics into fine-grained tags (objects, attributes, relations) using a hybrid vision tagging model combining CatLIP multi-label classification with Florence-2 captioning and Qwen2-7B tag extraction. A text controller then formulates LLM instructions from these tags plus user policies, and an LLM (Mistral-Nemo) recomposes the tags into diverse synthetic captions; an image controller similarly steers a diffusion model (SDXL) to generate images from the synthetic text under style or weighting policies. The key architectural insight is separating image-to-text generation into a decompose-then-recompose step, which provides a large control space for manipulating individual visual elements rather than treating the caption as an atomic unit. The closed-loop structure enables self-filtering: synthetic outputs are re-tagged by the VTM and discarded if fewer than a threshold fraction of original tags are preserved, requiring no external heuristics. Four synthesis paths (SP1–SP4) support text-only, text-faithful, joint image-text, and image-only generation, with the default CtrlSynth-mix setting combining synthetic captions with synthetic images for CLIP pretraining.

## Results

CtrlSynth improves zero-shot classification accuracy by +7.7% on average across 20 vision datasets (CC3M: 19.4→27.1) and +2.5% on CC12M (33.9→36.6). On image-text retrieval (recall@1), gains are +23.4% on CC3M (13.7→37.1) and +9.0% on CC12M (45.4→54.4). Compositional reasoning on SugarCrepe improves by +4.5% (CC3M) and +3.0% (CC12M), with the largest gains in SWAP attribute (+14.8%) and REPLACE relation (+4.3%). For long-tail tasks, tail-class accuracy improves by +21.3% on ImageNet-LT (13.8→35.1) and +16.2% on Places-LT (8.2→24.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard CLIP baseline (Radford et al., 2021), CtrlSynth consistently wins across all evaluated tasks: +7.7% average zero-shot classification on CC3M, +23.4% retrieval recall, +4.5% SugarCrepe compositional reasoning, and +21.3% long-tail tail-class accuracy. Against VeCLIP (Lai et al., 2024), which rewrites captions using LLMs, CtrlSynth achieves 4.8% higher average VTAB accuracy and 7.9% higher ImageNet-1K accuracy at the 3M setting (28.24 vs. 23.48 average; 23.82 vs. 15.98 ImageNet-1K), and maintains a 1.0% edge at 200M scale. Against LaCLIP (Fan et al., 2023), which uses language rewrites for CLIP training, CtrlSynth outperforms by 3.4% average on 15 common datasets and 2.3% on ImageNet-1K at 3M (28.0 vs. 24.6 average; 23.8 vs. 21.5 ImageNet-1K), and by 0.9% average and 2.5% on ImageNet-1K at 400M scale. The paper notes that a fully fair comparison with VeCLIP and LaCLIP is not possible due to different experimental setups, but the consistent advantage across both 3M and large-scale (200M/400M) settings supports the superiority of the decompose-recompose approach over direct caption rewriting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CtrlSynth carves new ground by unifying image and text synthesis into a single closed-loop pipeline, whereas prior work handles them separately: caption-rewriting methods (VeCLIP, LaCLIP, ReCap) improve text quality but offer no image generation, while diffusion-based augmentation (DiffuseMix, EDA, StableRep) generates images without controllable text synthesis. The key conceptual departure is the decompose-recompose paradigm: rather than treating an image as a monolithic input to a captioning or generation model, CtrlSynth first extracts structured visual tags (objects, attributes, relations) and then applies user-defined edit policies (remove, add, replace) before recomposing via an LLM or diffusion model. This positions the work as orthogonal to training-method improvements (SLIP, FLIP, CLIPA) and as a more flexible successor to ad-hoc synthetic data pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners who need to improve CLIP models without collecting new data: 23.4% retrieval gains, 16–21% long-tail accuracy improvements, and 5% compositional reasoning gains are all achieved with a training-free, modular pipeline that can be assembled from off-the-shelf models. The closed-loop self-filtering mechanism (re-running VTM on synthetic outputs to verify tag alignment) is an elegant alternative to hand-crafted heuristics and scales without manual tuning. Open questions include whether synthetic data inherits and amplifies biases from the original dataset (acknowledged but not explored), how to automatically generate per-sample image editing instructions, and whether the pipeline's benefits compound with deduplication and pruning techniques at web scale.
