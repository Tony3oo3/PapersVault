## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) rely on 3D full attention whose quadratic complexity in context length makes generation of even a few seconds of 720p video take tens of minutes on high-end GPUs, with attention accounting for 51–80% of end-to-end latency. Existing sparse attention methods designed for LLMs fail on video DiTs because video tokens exhibit fundamentally different sparsity structures (spatial and temporal locality) that token-level heuristics cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252719/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SVG is a training-free, drop-in replacement for the 3D full attention module in pre-trained video DiTs. For text-to-video it consumes a text prompt; for image-to-video it additionally takes a reference RGB image. The evaluated configurations produce 720p videos: CogVideoX-v1.5 operates on 11 latent frames with 4080 tokens per frame (~45k total context), while HunyuanVideo operates on 33 frames with 3600 tokens per frame (~120k total context). The output is a temporally consistent video at the same resolution and duration as the dense baseline.

## Method

SVG exploits two inherent sparse attention patterns in video DiTs: Spatial Heads attend to block-wise tokens within nearby frames (preserving spatial structure), and Temporal Heads attend to slash-wise tokens at the same spatial position across frames (preserving temporal consistency). An online profiling strategy samples only 1% of query tokens per head, computes full, spatial-masked, and temporal-masked attention, and selects the pattern with the lowest MSE against full attention—achieving near-oracle classification with ~3% overhead. To make the non-contiguous temporal sparsity hardware-friendly, SVG applies a layout transformation that transposes the token-major tensor into a frame-major layout, converting the stride-L pattern into a contiguous block that Tensor Cores can exploit. Customized Triton/FlashInfer kernels for block-sparse attention, fused QK-norm/RoPE (up to 16× faster than PyTorch), and optional FP8 quantization complete the system, yielding up to 2.33× end-to-end speedup with PSNR > 29.

## Results

SVG achieves up to 2.28× end-to-end speedup on CogVideoX-v1.5-T2V (232s vs. 528s baseline) and 2.33× on HunyuanVideo-T2V with FP8 (968s vs. 2253s), while maintaining PSNR of 29.99 and 29.45 respectively. On CogVideoX-v1.5-I2V, SVG reaches 28.17 PSNR with a 2.23× speedup. All baselines (DiTFastAttn, MInference, PAB) achieve PSNR below 26, confirming that SVG is the only method that preserves near-lossless quality while delivering the highest speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVG dominates all three baselines on both quality and efficiency across every model tested. On CogVideoX-v1.5-T2V, SVG achieves 29.99 PSNR versus 23.20 (DiTFastAttn), 23.80 (Temporal-only), 22.45 (MInference), and 22.49 (PAB) — a gap of 6–7.5 dB in PSNR. The speedup advantage is equally clear: SVG's 2.28× outpaces DiTFastAttn (1.56×), Temporal-only (1.61×), MInference (1.48×), and PAB (1.41×). On HunyuanVideo-T2V, SVG reaches 29.55 PSNR (29.45 with FP8) while the best baseline (Temporal-only) only reaches 25.85 PSNR, and SVG's 1.92× (2.33× with FP8) speedup exceeds MInference's 1.59× and DiTFastAttn's 1.82×. MInference, designed for LLM token-level sparsity, fails to capture the slash-wise temporal pattern in video attention, causing the largest quality degradation. PAB, a caching-based method, reuses attention results across layers and similarly suffers a PSNR drop below 23.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVG departs from the LLM sparse-attention lineage (H2O, MInference, DuoAttention) by identifying that video DiTs exhibit fundamentally different sparsity structures—spatial heads attending within frames and temporal heads attending across frames at fixed spatial positions—rather than the token-level 'heavy hitter' patterns found in text. It is orthogonal to prior efficient-diffusion techniques (step reduction, caching, quantization) and can be stacked with them, as demonstrated by the FP8 combination. The hardware-efficient layout transformation (token-major to frame-major transpose) draws on the broader sparse-compilation literature (SparseTIR, PIT) but applies it to a novel video-specific sparsity pattern that prior hardware-aware sparse attention work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video DiTs are among the most compute-hungry generative models (HunyuanVideo requires ~1 hour on A100 for 5 seconds), and SVG's training-free 2.3× speedup with PSNR > 29 makes near-real-time video generation substantially more accessible without retraining. The clean spatial/temporal head taxonomy provides a reusable analytical lens for future video architectures. Open questions include whether the two-category classification generalizes to newer or more heterogeneous video DiTs, how to adaptively tune sparsity ratios per head and per denoising step, and whether the layout transformation extends to other non-contiguous sparsity patterns beyond the stride-L temporal case.
