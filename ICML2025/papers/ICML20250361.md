## Problem

Standard Batch Normalisation degrades in federated learning because local statistical parameters (means, variances) computed on heterogeneous, Non-IID client data do not match the ideal global statistics needed for evaluation, creating an external covariate shift that existing alternatives (GN, LN, FN) fail to resolve by ignoring global distributional information entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250361/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) partitioned across K clients with Dirichlet-controlled label heterogeneity, producing class labels. A distinguishing feature is that each client works with small local batches (as low as 2) and only model parameters—not raw data—are exchanged with the central server, so the normalisation layer must function with stale global statistics and partial client participation per round.

## Method

HBN introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, it decouples the update of BN statistical parameters from learnable parameters: each client computes local means and variances using the frozen global model before local SGD, and the server aggregates them via a distributed formula (weighted mean plus a between-group variance correction term) to recover unbiased global statistics without extra communication rounds. Second, during forward normalisation, a per-channel learnable hybrid distribution factor α (initialised to 0) adaptively blends current batch statistics with the stored global statistics through a sigmoid-weighted mixture, allowing each client to calibrate the local-vs-global balance according to its own degree of distributional divergence. The global statistics are frozen during gradient backpropagation, and α is kept client-local (not communicated), making HBN a drop-in replacement for the BN layer within any FedAvg-based pipeline.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (φ=0.6, B=4), HBN achieves 78.22% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FixBN (77.34%) and standard BN (75.82%). On CIFAR-100 (φ=0.1, B=4), HBN reaches 46.52%, a 3.12-point gain over BN (43.40%) and 4.37 points over FixBN (42.15%). On Tiny-ImageNet (φ=0.05, B=64), HBN attains 24.69% versus 21.55% for BN and a catastrophic 3.76% for FBN. HBN consistently leads across all heterogeneity levels, batch sizes, and client counts tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBN outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting in Table 1. Against the closest competitor FixBN, HBN gains +0.88 pp on CIFAR-10 (φ=0.6), +1.10 pp on CIFAR-100 (φ=0.1), and +1.17 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (φ=0.05). Standard BN, while competitive in moderate-heterogeneity settings, degrades sharply under severe Non-IID conditions (e.g., CIFAR-100 φ=0.1: 43.40% vs. HBN's 46.52%). GN and LN, which avoid batch statistics entirely, suffer the most on Tiny-ImageNet (14.89% and 14.84% at φ=0.05 vs. HBN's 24.69%), confirming that discarding global information is costly. FBN performs catastrophically on Tiny-ImageNet (3.76–4.90%), likely because its running-average global statistics become unreliable under high heterogeneity and partial client participation. FedFN shows moderate gains over BN on CIFAR-100 but still trails HBN by 2.72 pp at φ=0.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Batch Normalisation (Ioffe, 2015) but repositions it for federated learning, where the standard BN's running-statistics mechanism produces biased global estimates due to Non-IID client data. It departs from two prior lines of work: (1) methods that replace BN with per-sample normalisation (GN, LN, FedFN), which avoid batch dependence but discard global distributional information, and (2) methods that patch BN statistics in FL (FixBN, FBN, FedTAN), which either freeze statistics at a fixed stage or incur 3× communication overhead. The key conceptual departure is separating the update of statistical parameters from learnable parameters to recover unbiased global estimates, then introducing a learnable hybrid distribution factor that adaptively blends local batch statistics with global statistics per client.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BN is the default normalisation layer in most CNNs, and its silent degradation in federated settings has been a practical pain point; HBN offers a drop-in plugin that requires only one extra communication round and no architectural changes, making it immediately adoptable across existing FL frameworks (FedProx, Scaffold, FedSAM, etc.). Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with CNNs and a Dirichlet-based heterogeneity model; whether the approach transfers to NLP, video, or more complex heterogeneity patterns (e.g., feature-level shifts) remains open. The paper also does not explore interaction with privacy mechanisms like differential privacy, which could further constrain statistical parameter sharing.
