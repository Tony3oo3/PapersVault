## Problem

Standard autoregressive image generation models operate on a single-scale sequence of dense image tokens, making it difficult to capture global context for early token prediction and resulting in sub-optimal autoregressive modeling. This single-shot design conflates global structure construction with local detail refinement into one stage, unlike human perception which proceeds hierarchically from coarse to fine.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253148/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes either a class label (ImageNet) or a text prompt encoded via CLIP (MS-COCO) as conditioning context, and produces 256×256 RGB images. Images are encoded by a pre-trained VAE into continuous-valued latent tokens at two resolutions: 128×128 (low-resolution, ~16 tokens) and 256×256 (high-resolution, ~64 tokens). Generation proceeds in two phases—first predicting the few low-resolution tokens, then using their Transformer outputs as conditional pivots to predict the full high-resolution token sequence—requiring 32 steps for Phase 1 and only 4 steps for Phase 2 at inference.

## Method

Hi-MAR builds a top-down hierarchical masked autoregressive framework on top of MAR's continuous-valued tokenization. In Phase 1, a scale-aware Transformer (with adaLN-Zero blocks that inject resolution information via sinusoidal embeddings) performs bidirectional masked prediction over low-resolution tokens, and an MLP-based diffusion head reconstructs them. In Phase 2, the Transformer outputs (conditional tokens) from Phase 1—rather than raw visual tokens—are concatenated with context tokens and masked high-resolution tokens to avoid the training-inference discrepancy of conditioning on ground-truth low-res tokens. A novel Diffusion Transformer head replaces the per-token MLP head, using self-attention across all masked and unmasked tokens to model inter-token dependencies during denoising. This combination of hierarchical pivoting, scale-aware conditioning, and global-context diffusion prediction yields a 0.38 FID improvement over MAR at comparable parameter count while requiring only 54% of the computational cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Hi-MAR-B achieves FID 1.93 (w/ CFG) and 2.11 (w/o CFG), improving over MAR-B by 0.38 FID. At the largest scale, Hi-MAR-H reaches FID 1.52 (w/ CFG) and 1.55 (w/o CFG), outperforming MAR-H (1.55/2.35) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27/9.62). On MS-COCO 256×256 text-to-image, Hi-MAR-S achieves FID 4.77, beating the best competitor AutoNAT-S (5.36) by 0.59. Hi-MAR also leads on T2I-CompBench compositional metrics (e.g., Color 0.3862 vs. 0.3626 for U-ViT-S/2) while requiring only 54% of MAR's computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-MAR outperforms all 23 baselines on FID across both ImageNet and MS-COCO. Against the closest masked-AR competitor MAR, Hi-MAR-B improves FID by 0.38 (1.93 vs. 2.31 w/ CFG) and 1.37 (2.11 vs. 3.48 w/o CFG) on ImageNet, and by 1.59 on MS-COCO (4.77 vs. 6.36). Against diffusion models, Hi-MAR-B (1.93) beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and U-ViT-H/2 (2.29) with fewer parameters (244M vs. 675M/501M). Against multi-scale AR models, Hi-MAR-B (1.93) surpasses VAR-d24 (2.09) and LlamaGen-XXL (2.34) despite using continuous tokens rather than VQ-based discrete tokens. On MS-COCO text-to-image, Hi-MAR-S (FID 4.77) beats AutoNAT-S (5.36) and U-ViT-S/2-Deep (5.48), and on T2I-CompBench it leads on Color (0.3862), Shape (0.2782), Texture (0.3945), and Complex composition (0.2313) over both U-ViT-S/2 and AutoNAT-S. The only metric where Hi-MAR does not lead is Precision under w/ CFG at the L/H scales (0.79/0.80 vs. MAR's 0.81), a minor trade-off for the substantial FID gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hi-MAR builds directly on MAR (Li et al., 2024), which established continuous-valued masked autoregressive modeling without vector quantization, and extends it with a two-phase hierarchical structure that first predicts low-resolution tokens as global-structure pivots before refining dense high-resolution tokens. It departs from prior multi-scale AR approaches like VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and Muse (Chang et al., 2023) by operating entirely in continuous token space and by using Transformer-internal conditional tokens (rather than raw or ground-truth low-resolution tokens) as cross-phase guidance, thereby avoiding the training-inference discrepancy. The scale-aware Transformer blocks borrow the adaLN-Zero conditioning mechanism from DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and the Diffusion Transformer head adapts the self-attention-based denoising paradigm of DiT/U-ViT into an autoregressive prediction head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting explicit global-structure awareness into masked autoregressive models via a low-resolution pivot phase yields meaningful FID gains (0.38 over MAR at base scale) while reducing inference cost to 54%, suggesting that hierarchical decomposition is a viable path to closing the quality gap between AR and diffusion models for image generation. The finding that only 4 autoregressive steps suffice in the second phase once global structure is established has practical implications for deployment latency. Open questions include whether the two-scale hierarchy generalizes to more resolution levels or to video generation, and whether the training-inference gap is fully eliminated or merely mitigated by the conditional-token pivot design.
