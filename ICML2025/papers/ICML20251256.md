## Problem

Traditional conditional density estimation methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality and struggle to adapt to data supported on lower-dimensional manifolds. While unconditional deep generative models have received theoretical analysis, the statistical properties of likelihood-based *conditional* deep generative models—particularly when the response is singular (supported on a manifold) and corrupted by full-dimensional noise—remain unexplored. This paper provides the first convergence rate guarantees for such models, showing they can circumvent the curse of dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a predictor vector X ∈ R^p and a latent random seed Z ∈ R^d (with known distribution) as joint input to a deep neural network generator G(Z, X) mapping to R^D. The observed response Y = G(Z, X) + ε is full-dimensional in R^D but concentrates on a d-dimensional manifold when d < D, making the underlying distribution singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. The method produces both the smoothed conditional density p̂(y|x) (evaluated in Hellinger metric) and, via deconvolution, the intrinsic conditional distribution on the manifold (evaluated in Wasserstein metric), with rates depending only on intrinsic dimension and smoothness rather than the ambient dimension D.

## Method

The approach parameterizes the conditional generator G*(Z, X) using sparse or fully connected ReLU deep neural networks and trains via a conditional VAE that maximizes the log-likelihood of the convolution model Y = G(Z, X) + ε. The key theoretical insight is that under a composite structure assumption on the generator, the sieve MLE achieves Hellinger convergence rates of order n^{-(β* − t*α)/(2β* + t*)} log²(n), where t* and β* are the intrinsic dimension and smoothness—completely independent of the ambient dimension D. For manifold-supported data with small noise, the paper introduces a calibrated Gaussian perturbation strategy that recovers optimal Wasserstein rates for the deconvolved intrinsic distribution. The entropy analysis (Lemma 1) bridges the covering number of the neural network function class to the bracketing entropy of the induced distribution class, enabling the application of classical sieve MLE theory to this conditional, potentially singular setting.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution establishes a Hellinger convergence rate of ε*_n ≍ n^{-(β*−t*α)/(2β*+t*)} log²(n) for the sieve MLE, where β* and t* are the intrinsic smoothness and dimension of the true generator, and α governs the noise decay rate. Numerically, on full-dimensional regression tasks (FD1–FD3), the Sieve MLE achieves MSE of 0.038 for the conditional mean on FD1, dramatically outperforming CKDE (1.005) and FlexCode (1.166). On the MNIST image generation task, both sparse and fully connected architectures achieve W₁ distances of ~1.89–1.96 at zero noise, slightly below the 2.02 baseline between two test images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Sieve MLE (conditional VAE) against two established conditional density estimation methods: CKDE (Hall et al., 2004) and FlexCode (Izbicki & Lee, 2017). On FD1, the Sieve MLE dominates both baselines by an order of magnitude on both mean (0.038 vs. 1.005 and 1.166) and standard deviation (0.028 vs. 0.989 and 1.200) MSE. On FD2, the Sieve MLE wins on mean MSE (0.194 vs. 0.264 and 0.395) and ties CKDE on SD MSE (0.284 vs. 0.285), while FlexCode fails catastrophically on SD (5.83). On FD3, the Sieve MLE wins on mean MSE (0.234 vs. 0.297 and 1.342) but loses to CKDE on SD MSE (1.639 vs. 0.633), though the authors note the gap narrows with larger training sets. Overall, the Sieve MLE wins 5 out of 6 comparisons, with the sole loss being the FD3 standard deviation where the multiplicative noise structure poses additional difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconditional sieve MLE framework of Chae et al. (2023) to the conditional distribution regression setting, where the generator depends on both a latent variable and covariates. It builds on the Noise Outsourcing Lemma from Zhou et al. (2022) to justify the existence of a conditional generator, and leverages the entropy-based convergence machinery of Wong & Shen (1995) and Ghosal & van der Vaart (2017) to derive rates. The key technical departure from the unconditional case is the need to control entropy with respect to two sources of smoothness (across regressors and responses) and to handle a dynamic manifold structure that varies with the covariate value, making the analysis substantially more intricate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical theory showing that likelihood-based conditional deep generative models achieve convergence rates depending only on intrinsic dimension and smoothness, providing a rigorous explanation for why such models circumvent the curse of dimensionality in distribution regression. The practical recommendation to inject small noise when data lies very close to a manifold (Corollary 2) is a non-obvious but actionable insight validated empirically on MNIST. Open questions include extending the analysis to flow-matching and score-based conditional generative models, controllable generation via penalized optimization, and hypothesis testing within this framework.
