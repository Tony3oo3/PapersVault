## Problem

Existing meta-learning frameworks for dynamic graphs (e.g., ROLAND, WinGNN) update model parameters between snapshots using fixed weights or fixed learning rates, ignoring the structural differences that actually distinguish one snapshot from the next. This paper asks how to capture the high-order topological changes between adjacent snapshots and use them to adaptively modulate the meta-learning parameter update, rather than treating all temporal transitions uniformly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251419/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of discrete-time dynamic graph snapshots G_1, ..., G_T (each with node set V_t and edge set E_t), partitioning nodes into landmarks via an ε-net and the remainder into witnesses to build Dowker complexes at a chosen scale δ. It outputs predicted links between adjacent snapshots (standard link prediction) and, internally, a scalar topology-adaptive learning rate r derived from the difference of 50×50 zigzag persistence images (ZPIs) computed over a sliding window of w snapshots. The ZPIs are the only topological signal fed into the meta-learning adaptor; the underlying GNN backbone (GCN+GRU, as in ROLAND) remains unchanged.

## Method

TMetaNet introduces Dowker Zigzag Persistence (DZP), which replaces the expensive Vietoris-Rips complex with a Dowker complex built on an ε-net landmark subset, reducing the ZP computation from O(m log²N) to O(m log²n') where n' is the (much smaller) landmark count. A zigzag filtration is then constructed over the bidirectional inclusion sequence D(G_t) → D(G_t ∪ G_{t+1}) ← D(G_{t+1}), yielding persistence diagrams that track k-dimensional topological features across snapshots while naturally accommodating landmark changes via interleaving. The Topological Learning Rate Adaptor computes the element-wise difference ΔZ between adjacent ZPIs, passes it through a residual CNN and fully connected layers to produce a scalar r, and applies it in the weight update w_{t+1} = w_t − η·r·∇_w L(w_t). A stability theorem (Theorem 4.5) guarantees that the bottleneck distance between DZP diagrams is bounded by a constant times the structural metric between the underlying dynamic graphs, ensuring the learned learning rate is robust to noise. The framework is compatible with both ROLAND's live-update and WinGNN's chronological training protocols.

## Results

Under the ROLAND (live update) setting, TMetaNet achieves an average 3.29% ACC and 5.52% MRR improvement over the best baseline across six datasets, with the largest single gain being 8.14% ACC on Bitcoin-Alpha. Under the WinGNN setting, improvements are more pronounced: 3.27% average ACC and 24.08% average MRR, with a peak of 74.70% MRR improvement on Reddit-Body (28.93 vs. 16.56). All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05) except on Reddit-Body under the ROLAND setting, where the dataset's high homogeneity limits topological signal gains. TMetaNet also demonstrates up to 31.1% robustness gains under edge-flipping noise attacks compared to WinGNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMetaNet outperforms all five baseline families across nearly all dataset-metric combinations. Against the strongest meta-learning baselines, TMetaNet beats ROLAND by 8.14% ACC on Bitcoin-Alpha (86.84 vs. 83.18) and WinGNN by 8.14% ACC and 12.55% MRR on Bitcoin-Alpha under the WinGNN setting (89.92 vs. 83.15 ACC; 38.93 vs. 34.59 MRR). The most dramatic win is on Reddit-Body MRR under the WinGNN setting, where TMetaNet achieves 28.93 versus WinGNN's 16.56 (74.70% relative improvement), while DeGNN fails entirely (OOM). Against non-meta-learning baselines (GCN-L/G, EvolveGCN), TMetaNet leads by 20–40+ points in MRR on most datasets, confirming that the meta-learning framework itself provides the bulk of the gain. The one exception is Reddit-Body under the ROLAND setting, where ROLAND (91.63 ACC) slightly edges TMetaNet (89.59 ACC), which the authors attribute to the dataset's structural homogeneity limiting the topological signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: meta-learning-based dynamic GNNs (ROLAND's live-update training, WinGNN's window gradient aggregation) and topological data analysis (persistent homology, zigzag persistence). The key departure from prior meta-learning work is replacing fixed parameter-update weights or learning rates with a topology-adaptive learning rate derived from Dowker Zigzag Persistence (DZP) diagrams of adjacent snapshots. On the TDA side, the paper departs from standard Vietoris-Rips-based zigzag persistence by substituting the computationally expensive VR complex with the lighter Dowker complex (using ε-nets for landmark selection), and introduces a seed-based ε-net algorithm to maintain landmark consistency across time steps. The theoretical stability guarantee (Theorem 4.5) extends the ε-interleaving framework of Ye et al. (2023) to the Dowker complex setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for making meta-learning parameter updates sensitive to structural (not just temporal) changes in dynamic graphs, with a provable stability bound that links graph perturbation to topological feature perturbation. This could inform more robust adaptation in settings where graph topology shifts non-uniformly (e.g., financial fraud networks, infrastructure monitoring). However, open questions remain: the running time overhead is substantial (up to 237% in the ROLAND setting), scalability to very large graphs is addressed only heuristically (sampling by degree centrality), and the ablation showing that 'Dist' (statistically matched random images) recovers much of the 'Topo' gain raises questions about how much of the improvement is truly topological versus a learned regularization effect. Generalization beyond link prediction and to continuous-time dynamic graphs is unexplored.
