## Problem

Existing offline RL methods for adversarial Markov games either rely on pessimism-based value functions requiring infinite samples for deterministic games, or use model-based approaches that need full game dynamics. It remains unexplored whether model-free, policy-constrained learning dynamics can find mixed-strategy Nash equilibria offline, since policy constraints that sacrifice optimality in single-agent MDPs (where optimal policies are pure) may behave differently in adversarial games where optimal policies are inherently mixed.

## Input / Output

CED consumes a finite offline dataset of non-repetitive transitions (s, a, b, r, s′) from a deterministic two-player zero-sum Markov game, along with a discount factor γ and iteration count K. The dataset must satisfy uniform coverage (all state-action tuples (s,a,b) appear) for the theoretical guarantee; under non-uniform coverage the method still improves the behavior policy. The output is a min-player mixed-strategy policy ν that is unexploitable with respect to the converged max-player policy; running CED a second time with player roles swapped yields a joint policy (μ̂, ν̂) constituting a potential mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium.

## Method

CED inherits the iterative structure of Exploitability Descent (compute Q, update max-player μ, compute best-response ν) but introduces three key modifications for the offline setting. First, the Q-value is updated by applying the Bellman operator to the previous Q_{k−1} rather than solving the current Bellman equation directly, using only the offline transition set. Second, the max-player update follows an ED-like gradient step but is projected onto the feasible region defined by a direct policy constraint D(μ(s), μ_β(s)) ≤ δ, keeping μ close to the behavior policy. Third, the min-player ν is computed as a KL-regularized best response against the precomputed Q^{μ_β,ν_β} (not the current Q), yielding a unique closed-form softmax solution that makes μ's update follow a deterministic gradient rather than an arbitrary generalized gradient. This last property is the critical insight: it enables a last-iterate convergence proof (Theorem 5.2) and, via a projected-update argument (Lemma 5.5), guarantees that the stationary ν is unexploitable (Theorem 5.6) whenever μ converges to an interior point of the constrained policy space.

## Results

In the soccer game, CED achieves a significantly lower NashConv than the pessimism-based VI-LCB-Game baseline under the same offline dataset with uniform coverage. CED-learned policies achieve win rates of 91.8–92.4% against behavior policies under both uniform and non-uniform data coverages, and the non-uniform-coverage policy still reaches 45.8% against the uniform-coverage NE policy (near the 50% theoretical ceiling for symmetric zero-sum games). In the large-scale robotic combat game, CED(BC_init) achieves 52% win rate against OSP(BC_init) at 3000 iterations, and CED(RD_init) achieves 67% against OSP(RD_init) under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VI-LCB-Game (pessimism-based, model-based), CED achieves a significantly lower NashConv in the infinite-horizon soccer game under the same uniform-coverage dataset, demonstrating that a model-free approach can outperform a provably-efficient pessimistic method in practice. Against ED (Exploitability Descent, model-based), CED converges to the NE policy in the tree-form game while ED exhibits continual oscillations around the local optimum due to following a generalized gradient. Against Behavior Cloning (BC), CED consistently improves upon the BC-approximated behavior policy in the robotic combat game, with CED(BC_init) reaching 52% win rate over OSP(BC_init) at 3000 iterations. Against Offline Self-Play (OSP), CED outperforms OSP under both BC and random initializations: CED(RD_init) achieves 67% win rate over OSP(RD_init) at 3000 iterations, and even randomly-initialized CED (45%) nearly matches BC-initialized OSP at the same iteration count. Overall, CED wins or ties in all head-to-head comparisons across all four game environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

CED sits at the intersection of two research threads: game-theoretic equilibrium-learning dynamics (specifically Exploitability Descent from Lockhart et al., 2019) and policy constraint methods from offline RL (direct constraints à la Siegel et al., 2020; KL-divergence penalties à la Wu et al., 2019). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that while policy constraints are known to sacrifice optimality in single-agent offline RL (where optima are pure strategies), they are benign in adversarial games where the Nash equilibrium is inherently a mixed strategy. This allows CED to be model-free and offline, unlike ED which requires the full game model, and unlike pessimism-based offline game methods (Cui & Du, 2022; Yan et al., 2024) which require infinite samples for stochastic games or knowledge of the game horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-free offline RL algorithm with a convergence guarantee for finding mixed-strategy Nash equilibria in deterministic two-player zero-sum Markov games, filling a gap between model-based ED and sample-inefficient pessimism-based methods. The insight that policy constraints need not hurt equilibrium quality in adversarial settings could inform the design of other constrained multi-agent learning algorithms. However, the guarantees are limited to two-player zero-sum games with deterministic transitions and uniform data coverage, and the extension to imperfect-information games (where ED is also applicable) remains an open problem requiring new counterfactual value estimation techniques. The practical relevance is further tempered by the modest scale of experiments, with the largest test being a 6-robot combat game on a 100-node graph.
