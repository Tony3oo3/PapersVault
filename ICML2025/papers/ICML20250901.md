## Problem

Existing safety research for generative AI focuses almost exclusively on the text domain, leaving a distinct lack of open frameworks for vision-specific content moderation. Current visual safeguards rely on rigid NSFW classifiers or closed-source tools that cannot handle context-dependent policies or provide interpretable, category-level assessments. This paper addresses the need for a flexible, open VLM-based framework that can assess image safety against arbitrary, modifiable policies.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image alongside a structured safety policy prompt that defines nine risk categories (hate, violence, sexual content, nudity, criminal planning, weapons/substance abuse, self-harm, animal cruelty, disasters) with explicit 'should not' and 'can' guidelines per category. The output is a JSON object with three fields: a binary safety rating (safe/unsafe), a category label from the taxonomy (or NA), and a free-text rationale explaining the assessment relative to the policy. Policies can be dynamically modified by declaring specific categories as non-violating, which the model must respect in its rating and rationale.

## Method

LlavaGuard fine-tunes pre-trained VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B/7B and Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) on a 5,466-sample multimodal safety dataset built from SMID and web-crawled images, annotated with safety ratings, categories, and guided rationales. The key training innovations are: (1) policy augmentation, where unsafe samples are re-labeled as safe by declaring the violated category non-violating, teaching the model to adapt to different regulatory contexts; (2) guided rationale generation using Llava-34B with conditioned prompts that inject prior knowledge about the correct rating, category, and policy exceptions, yielding rationales scoring 9.1/10 vs. 3.8/10 for unguided generation; and (3) a Policy Exception Score (PES) metric defined as the harmonic mean of the Policy Exception Rate and balanced accuracy, measuring both safety classification quality and policy adaptability. Training runs 3 epochs at lr 2e-5 with cosine scheduling on 5 A100 GPUs, with the 0.5B model achieving 347% faster inference than the 7B variant.

## Results

On the held-out test set (824 samples), LlavaGuard-7B achieves 90.84% balanced accuracy, 91.39% recall, 87.97% precision, and 89.85% Policy Exception Score (PES), substantially outperforming all baselines. The smaller LlavaGuard-0.5B model reaches 88.70% accuracy and 87.10% PES while being 347% faster in inference (0.075s/sample on a single A100). In real-world applications, LlavaGuard identifies 20k unsafe images (1.5%) in ImageNet's 1.3M images and flags 20% of 11k StableDiffusion-generated images as safety violations, with 88% human agreement on ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LlavaGuard-7B (90.84% accuracy) outperforms the strongest external baseline, GPT-4o (72.92%), by 17.9 points, and the best open-source VLM, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (70.84%), by 20 points. On the Policy Exception Score (PES), which measures flexible policy adherence, LlavaGuard-7B (89.85) and LlavaGuard-0.5B (87.10) dramatically surpass all competitors—GPT-4o reaches only 77.29, while purpose-built safeguards like ImageGuard (27.00) and Siglip2Guard (36.71) show near-complete inability to adapt to modified policies. LlamaGuard-3-11B is effectively non-functional for image safety, with 0.56% recall (misclassifying nearly all unsafe images as safe). Among the paper's own QwenGuard variants, LlavaGuard-7B edges ahead on accuracy (+1.13%) and PES (+5.28), while LlavaGuard-0.5B matches QwenGuard-3B on accuracy but leads by 2.36 PES points. Non-generative baselines (NSFW filters at ~50% accuracy, Q16 at 69.70%) are far inferior, confirming the advantage of VLM-based approaches for multi-category, policy-flexible safety assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-domain safety taxonomy and safeguard paradigm (LlamaGuard, DecodingTrust) to the visual modality, building on LLaVA-OneVision and Qwen2.5-VL as base architectures. It departs from prior vision safety tools (NSFW classifiers, Q16, NudeNet) that offer rigid, fixed-policy classification by introducing a VLM-based framework that generates policy-adaptive assessments with natural-language rationales. The data pipeline builds on the SMID dataset but augments it with web-crawled images and guided rationale generation to address category imbalance and reasoning quality. Unlike LlamaGuard-3-Vision (which targets human-AI conversations) or ImageGuard (which overfits to a single policy), LlavaGuard explicitly targets flexible, multi-policy image moderation as its core capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open, end-to-end framework for VLM-based vision safety that combines a customizable taxonomy, annotated dataset, and trained models, making it a practical resource for dataset auditing and T2I moderation at scale. The finding that a 0.5B model outperforms 72B VLMs and GPT-4o on safety assessment suggests task-specific fine-tuning can substitute for raw scale in narrow safety tasks, reducing deployment costs. Key limitations include the small dataset (5.5k samples), synthetically generated rationales without human verification, absence of synthetic/AI-generated content in training, and a taxonomy that does not yet cover bias or fairness. Open questions include whether the policy-flexibility advantage persists as taxonomies grow more complex, how to handle adversarial evasion, and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image contexts.
