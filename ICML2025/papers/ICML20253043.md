## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of actor-critic methods for decentralized MARL are limited to linear function approximation and stationary-point convergence guarantees, leaving a significant gap with the practical use of deep neural network critics that are inherently nonlinear and non-convex. This paper asks whether one can develop a DNN-based actor-critic algorithm for fully decentralized MARL that provably converges to global optimality at a finite-time rate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253043/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a multi-agent MDP defined over a communication graph G=(N,E), where each agent observes the global state s, takes a local action a^i, and receives a local reward r^i; agents communicate only with graph neighbors via a doubly stochastic consensus matrix A. The output is the optimal joint policy parameter vector θ* (DNN weights for each agent's actor) along with a consensual Q-function approximation W_K obtained after K inner-loop TD iterations and t_gossip consensus rounds. A restart kernel P(s,a,s')=γP(s'|s,a)+(1−γ)I{s'=s0} is assumed, which simplifies the policy gradient to a stationary-distribution expectation and is central to the convergence proof.

## Method

The proposed method uses a double-loop structure: an inner critic loop (K iterations) where each agent performs local TD-learning on a fully connected ReLU network (width m, depth D) with projection onto a ball B(B) for non-expansiveness, followed by t_gossip rounds of gossiping to reach near-consensus on critic weights; and an outer actor loop (T iterations) where agents sample Markovian batches of length M, compute consensual TD-errors via gossiping, and update policy parameters along normalized gradient directions with step-size α_t=α/t. The key technical insight for handling the nonlinearity–decentralization interplay is maintaining a pseudo-centralized parameter V that collects local rewards to form the global reward r̄, enabling a centralized TD-error that bridges the gap between the averaged local critic W̄ and the true optimal W*_θ — a step impossible under linear approximation where the consensus and nonlinear operators commute. The policy gradient uses the advantage function (consensual TD-error) rather than the raw Q-value, which the authors show empirically yields substantially better performance. Under standard assumptions (Lipschitz continuity, Fisher-information non-degeneracy, universal approximation), the algorithm achieves E[J(θ*)−J(θ_T)]=O(1/T) with sample complexity O(N²/ε³) for ε-optimality.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 5.10, which establishes that the proposed DNN-based actor-critic algorithm converges to global optimality at a rate of O(1/T), where T is the total number of outer-loop iterations. The sample complexity to achieve an ε-global-optimal solution is O(N²/ε³), and the communication complexity is O((1/ε)·log(N^{3/2}/ε)). In the Simple Spread ablation experiment, the TD-error-based policy gradient (the proposed approach) yields steadily increasing rewards over episodes, while the Q-value-based alternative (used in prior work) shows no meaningful learning and even decreasing rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is the first to combine nonlinear (DNN) function approximation with global optimality convergence in decentralized MARL, a combination no prior work achieves. Compared to Hairi et al. (2022) and Chen et al. (2022), which both achieve O(1/T) rates with linear approximation, this paper matches the convergence rate while upgrading to nonlinear DNN critics; however, Hairi et al. only guarantee stationary convergence (‖∇J(θ)‖² → 0) rather than global optimality. Against Zeng et al. (2022), which achieves only O(T^{-2/5}) stationary convergence with linear approximation, the proposed method is strictly superior in both rate and guarantee strength. The deep MARL methods of Foerster et al. (2016) and Omidshafiei et al. (2017) use nonlinear networks but provide no finite-time convergence guarantees at all. In the empirical ablation on Simple Spread, the proposed TD-error-based actor update significantly outperforms the Q-value-based update used in Lowe et al. (2017) and Gaur et al. (2024), with the latter showing no learning progress. The LLM alignment experiment (multi-agent RLHF) further demonstrates practical efficacy, with all agents showing consistent reward improvement through the gossiping mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of research: single-agent DNN actor-critic theory (Gaur et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2019) and decentralized MARL actor-critic theory (Hairi et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2018). Prior decentralized MARL results relied on linear function approximation in the critic, while prior DNN actor-critic results were limited to single-agent settings. The key departure is introducing a pseudo-centralized parameter technique to handle the non-interchangeability of nonlinear DNN updates and consensus operations, a challenge absent in the linear-approximation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time global optimality guarantee for DNN-based actor-critic in fully decentralized MARL, closing a significant theory-practice gap where empirical methods use deep networks but theory only covered linear approximations or stationary points. The O(1/T) rate matching the best linear-approximation results suggests nonlinearity need not degrade convergence, which is reassuring for practitioners. Limitations include the restart kernel assumption, requirement for global state/action observation, and restriction to fully connected ReLU networks; extending to partial observability, competitive settings, or alternative architectures (e.g., transformers) remains open. The LLM alignment experiment, while promising, is small-scale and does not yet demonstrate scalability to production-level multi-agent RLHF.
