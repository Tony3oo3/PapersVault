## Problem

While LLM-based forecasters have emerged as a promising approach to time series forecasting, no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate whether models can effectively integrate essential textual context with numerical data. Existing context-aided forecasting benchmarks do not guarantee that the provided context is actually necessary for accurate predictions, leaving it unclear whether models genuinely leverage textual information or merely perform well on the numerical component. This paper addresses this gap by introducing a benchmark where every task is designed so that accurate forecasts are impossible without understanding the accompanying natural language context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252664/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance provides a numerical time series history (ranging from sub-hourly to monthly frequency, with varying lengths) paired with a manually crafted natural language context spanning five types: intemporal, future, historical, covariate, and causal information. The model must produce a probabilistic forecast distribution over the future horizon, evaluated via the proposed RCRPS metric, which weights errors differently within a task-specific region of interest and applies a penalty term for constraint violations (e.g., predicting negative values for a non-negative quantity). Forecasts are generated as 25 independent samples per instance, enabling distributional evaluation without requiring closed-form densities.

## Method

The paper introduces three core contributions. First, the CiK benchmark comprises 71 manually designed forecasting tasks across seven real-world domains (climatology, economics, energy, mechanics, public safety, transportation, retail), each requiring integration of both numerical history and essential textual context, with memorization mitigated via live data sources, derived series, or minor transformations. Second, the Region of Interest CRPS (RCRPS) scoring rule extends the standard CRPS by assigning higher weight to errors within context-sensitive time windows and adding a constraint-violation penalty term (scaled by β=10), enabling fair cross-task aggregation via a normalization factor. Third, DIRECT PROMPT is a simple prompting strategy that instructs an LLM to output a structured probabilistic forecast for all required timestamps in a single call, which surprisingly outperforms the more complex autoregressive LLMP approach and all quantitative baselines, with Llama-3.1-405B-Inst achieving the best aggregate RCRPS of 0.159.

## Results

On the CiK benchmark (71 tasks, 7 domains), Llama-3.1-405B-Inst with DIRECT PROMPT achieves the best average RCRPS of 0.159±0.008, significantly outperforming all other methods. GPT-4o (DIRECT PROMPT) attains the best average rank (4.381) but a higher RCRPS (0.274) due to occasional catastrophic failures. LLM-based methods with context improve substantially over their no-context counterparts (e.g., Llama-3.1-405B-Inst improves by 67.1%), while purely numerical models (Chronos-Large: 0.326, Lag-Llama: 0.327, ARIMA: 0.475) are consistently outperformed by context-aware LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DIRECT PROMPT method with Llama-3.1-405B-Inst (RCRPS 0.159) outperforms all 11+ baselines by a wide margin, beating the next-best LLM method (Llama-3-70B LLMP at 0.236) by 33% and the best TS foundation model (Chronos-Large at 0.326) by 51%. GPT-4o (DIRECT PROMPT) achieves the best average rank (4.381) but its RCRPS (0.274) is inflated by catastrophic failures on a small number of tasks, making it worse in aggregate than Mixtral-8x7B (LLMP) at 0.262. Among LLMP variants, base (non-instruction-tuned) Llama-3-70B (0.236) substantially outperforms its instruction-tuned counterpart (0.539), confirming that instruction tuning degrades autoregressive numerical forecasting. All LLM-based methods with context significantly outperform purely numerical models (TSFMs and statistical methods), but without context, this advantage largely disappears—e.g., Llama-3.1-405B-Inst (DIRECT PROMPT) degrades from 0.159 to 0.473 without context, falling below Chronos-Large. No single method dominates all five context types: Llama-3.1-405B-Inst wins on 3/5 types, while Llama-3-70B (LLMP) is best on causal information (0.360) and covariate information (0.193).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CRPS scoring rule (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007) into RCRPS by adding a region-of-interest weighting and a constraint-violation penalty, and positions its DIRECT PROMPT method as a simpler alternative to the autoregressive LLMP approach (Requeima et al., 2024). It departs sharply from prior context-aided forecasting benchmarks (Time-MMD, TGTSF, SysCaps, TS-Insights, Dual-Forecaster) by guaranteeing that every task's textual context is *essential* for accurate forecasting, rather than merely potentially helpful. The evaluation spans the full spectrum from classical statistical models (ARIMA, ETS) through time series foundation models (Lag-Llama, Chronos, Moirai, TimeGEN) to LLM-based and multimodal forecasters (UniTime, Time-LLM, ChatTime), providing the first systematic comparison under a context-essential protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CiK is likely to become the standard reference benchmark for context-aided probabilistic forecasting, given its 71 hand-crafted tasks across 7 real-world domains, its novel RCRPS metric, and its open-source availability. The finding that no single method excels across all five context types, combined with the catastrophic failure modes of even top LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o on scientific notation), signals that the benchmark is far from solved and opens clear research directions. Key limitations include the restriction to univariate series and natural-language-only context, the residual memorization risk despite mitigation strategies, and the high inference cost of LLM-based methods that limits practical deployment at scale.
