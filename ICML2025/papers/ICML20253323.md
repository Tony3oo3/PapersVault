## Problem

Current LLM reasoning paradigms—next-token prediction, RLHF with task-specific reward models, and prompt-based refinement—produce brittle, task-bound solutions that fail to generalize across domains, are vulnerable to trivial input perturbations, and cannot adapt their reasoning strategy to the structure of each problem. The paper argues that LLMs are trained to solve individual tasks rather than to learn *how* to reason, and that no existing approach provides a principled mechanism for self-awareness, dynamic strategy selection, cross-instance error pattern detection, and efficient knowledge consolidation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a task/question together with two knowledge priors: Θ_I (inherent, long-term world knowledge from pre-training) and Θ_E (task-specific, working-memory context such as retrieved facts or intermediate steps). Rather than producing a single fixed reasoning chain, the system outputs a distribution over latent reasoning skills (the strategy F), a stepwise reasoning process A = [(s_i, a_i)]_i^T refined through iterative monitoring and regulation, and updated meta-level priors. The reasoning strategy is explicitly a meta-level planner—a distribution over skills like planning, retrieval, and tool execution—rather than a direct solution, and the final output can incorporate verifiable grounding from external solvers (calculators, logic engines, compilers).

## Method

The paper proposes a bi-level Bayesian inference architecture with five interacting modules: Self-awareness (assesses task solvability via capability- and mission-aware checks, then initializes a skill-distribution strategy F), Monitoring (samples k candidate steps at each reasoning step and selects via a reward model Q_t conditioned on F, Θ_I, Θ_E), Evaluation & Regulation (reviews the full chain using internal critic signals and external tools, then applies corrections), Meta-reflection (updates F and the knowledge priors via Bayesian posterior updates, drawing on inverse planning to approximate cumulative rewards), and Memory (stores priors and past reasoning episodes). Task-level learning updates p(F|O, Θ_I, Θ_E) ∝ p(O|F)·p(F|Θ_I, Θ_E) iteratively until cumulative reward is maximized, while meta-level learning updates p(Θ_I, Θ_E|O, F) by marginalizing over F, strengthening priors that yield cross-task success and adjusting those that produce contradictions. The framework is explicitly a position paper: it formalizes the architecture, identifies six concrete limitations in existing self-awareness, monitoring, evaluation, and meta-optimization methods, and proposes six actionable research directions (unified meta-reasoning benchmarks, neuro-symbolic solvability assessment, adaptive MoE-based skill selection, self-play for dynamic meta-rewards, latent-space reasoning, and interpretable meta-knowledge consolidation) without implementing a full system.

## Results

This is a position paper that proposes a conceptual Bayesian Meta-Reasoning framework for LLMs and does not report quantitative experimental results. The paper identifies four open problems (lack of self-awareness, limited adaptivity, reward hacking in RL, and inefficient knowledge internalization) and six specific limitations in existing reasoning approaches, then outlines six actionable research directions. No benchmarks are evaluated and no performance metrics are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not empirically compare its proposed framework against baselines. Instead, it critically surveys and identifies gaps in existing approaches across five modules: (1) Self-awareness — existing methods lack a multi-view solvability framework integrating capability and mission awareness; (2) Reasoning strategy initialization — current methods propose a single 'optimal' strategy rather than adaptive combinations of latent skills; (3) Monitoring/rewards — existing reward signals (ORM, PRM, LLM self-evaluation) are imperfect proxies that are stationary and fail to capture multi-dimensional criteria; (4) Evaluation and regulation — feedback is predominantly sample-specific and fails to capture generalized error patterns; (5) Meta-reflection — existing meta-learning approaches (MAML, LoRA composition, multi-agent RL) lack explainability and integrated bi-level optimization. The paper positions its Bayesian framework as addressing all five gaps simultaneously through a unified bi-level inference and learning process, but leaves empirical validation to future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes Bayesian inference frameworks for LLMs (Xie et al., 2021; McCoy et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2025b) with meta-learning principles from MAML (Finn et al., 2017) and cognitive science meta-reasoning theories (Dual-Process Theory, Metacognitive Reasoning) to propose a unified bi-level Bayesian architecture. It departs from existing LLM reasoning approaches—CoT/ToT prompting, RLHF/DPO/GRPO post-training, and iterative self-reflection (Reflexion, Self-Refine)—by arguing these treat reasoning as a task-specific output rather than a learnable meta-process, and that no prior work has formalized meta-reasoning as a Bayesian learning problem with explicit priors over knowledge and strategies. The paper positions itself as a conceptual bridge between the Bayesian LLM reasoning line and the emerging meta-reasoning line (Yoran et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024a), which it critiques for relying on prompting heuristics rather than new learning paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a position paper, its primary value is in identifying six concrete limitations across self-awareness, monitoring, evaluation, and meta-reflection, and translating them into six actionable research directions (multi-view solvability, adaptive skill selection via MoE, self-play meta-rewards, latent-space reasoning, interpretable meta-knowledge consolidation). The key open question is whether the proposed Bayesian bi-level updates are computationally tractable at LLM scale, and how to design the 'overarching meta-reward' that goes beyond sample-wise annotations without falling into reward hacking. The framework's practical impact depends on whether the conceptual architecture can be instantiated efficiently; the paper acknowledges added complexity and computational overhead as potential criticisms, and no implementation or empirical validation is provided.
