## Problem

Existing combinatorial multi-armed bandit (CMAB) research focuses almost exclusively on online exploration, overlooking the prohibitive costs and ethical constraints of active data collection in domains like healthcare, recommendation, and autonomous driving. This paper introduces the first offline learning framework for CMAB (Off-CMAB), addressing two open questions: how to design a sample-efficient algorithm when the action space is combinatorially large, and how much offline data is necessary to find a near-optimal action under varying dataset quality.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of n samples, each consisting of a combinatorial action (super arm), the set of triggered arms, and the observed outcomes for those triggered arms, along with an α-approximation oracle and a confidence parameter δ. A key distinction from standard offline bandit settings is that the dataset may be out-of-distribution: the data collection distribution can assign zero probability to the optimal action or even to feasible actions, and the probabilistic triggering model means not all arms in a super arm are observed in any given sample. The output is a single combinatorial action (super arm) with a high-probability suboptimality gap bound that depends on the dataset size, action size, and a novel data coverage coefficient.

## Method

The CLCB algorithm applies the pessimism principle at two levels: it first constructs high-probability lower confidence bounds (LCBs) for each base arm by subtracting a confidence penalty √(log(4mn/δ)/(2N_i)) from the empirical mean, then feeds these LCBs into an α-approximation oracle that solves the combinatorial optimization problem under the pessimistic estimates. The theoretical analysis introduces two novel data coverage conditions—infinity-norm and 1-norm triggering probability modulated (TPM) coverage—that quantify dataset quality by reweighting per-arm data requirements according to each arm's triggering probability under the optimal action, rather than requiring uniform coverage. Under these conditions, CLCB achieves a near-optimal suboptimality gap of Õ(K*√(C*∞)/n) that matches a derived lower bound up to logarithmic factors. The framework handles nonlinear reward functions, probabilistic (non-full) feedback, and out-of-distribution data, and is instantiated for cascading bandits (learning to rank), LLM caching, and influence maximization with node-level feedback, yielding improved guarantees over prior work in each setting.

## Results

On the cascading bandit (learning to rank) task, CLCB reduces suboptimality gaps by 47.75% over CUCB-Offline and 20.02% over EMP on synthetic data, and consistently achieves the lowest gap on the real-world Yelp dataset across K=4 and K=8. For the LLM cache task, CLCB-LLM-C achieves at least 1.32× improvement over LFU and LEC on synthetic data, and reduces inference costs by up to 36.01% (vs. LFU) and 20.70% (vs. LEC) on the real-world SciQ dataset using GPT-4-o. Theoretically, CLCB achieves a suboptimality gap of Õ(K*√(C∞*/n)) under infinity-norm TPM data coverage, matching the derived lower bound Ω(K*√(C∞*/n)) up to a logarithmic factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CUCB-Offline (Chen et al., 2016), an offline adaptation of the online CUCB algorithm, CLCB reduces suboptimality gaps by 47.75% on synthetic cascading bandit data and consistently outperforms it on the Yelp dataset across all tested list lengths. Against EMP (Liu et al., 2021), which simply selects the top-K arms by empirical mean reward, CLCB achieves a 20.02% gap reduction on synthetic data, demonstrating that the pessimistic LCB construction is critical under limited offline samples. For the LLM cache task, CLCB-LLM-C outperforms both LFU (least-frequently-used eviction) and LEC (lowest-estimated-cost eviction) from Zhu et al. (2023), achieving at least 1.32× improvement on synthetic data and up to 36.01% and 20.70% cost reduction on the SciQ dataset, respectively. The theoretical improvement over Zhu et al. (2023) is a factor of Õ(√(k²/C₁)) for the offline setting, attributed to tighter triggering-probability analysis and direct use of empirical means for full-feedback arms. For influence maximization, the variance-adaptive LCB construction improves upon Chen et al. (2021) by a factor of Õ(V⁴/(k²d_max²)·η) while also relaxing their assumptions on seed sampling and activation probabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online CMAB-T framework (Chen et al., 2016; Wang & Chen, 2017) to the offline setting, where no active exploration is possible. It adapts the pessimism principle from offline RL (Jin et al., 2020a) to combinatorial action spaces, constructing base-arm LCBs that are fed into a combinatorial oracle. The TPM data coverage conditions generalize uniform coverage assumptions from offline RL (Chen & Jiang, 2019b) by reweighting per-arm data needs via triggering probabilities, avoiding the 1/p_min factor that plagued earlier online CMAB analyses. For specific applications, it improves upon Zhu et al. (2023) for LLM caching and Chen et al. (2021) for influence maximization by leveraging variance-adaptive confidence intervals and tighter triggering-probability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide near-optimal suboptimality guarantees for offline CMAB, matching a derived lower bound up to logarithmic factors, which establishes the fundamental sample complexity of the problem. The practical relevance is timely, particularly for LLM caching where the framework improves existing results by a factor of O~(sqrt(k²/C₁)) and handles out-of-distribution data that excludes optimal actions. Open questions include extending to variance-adaptive algorithms for tighter guarantees and bridging to offline RL with combinatorial action spaces, which the authors identify as a natural next step. A limitation is that the framework requires an α-approximation oracle, and the data coverage conditions, while less restrictive than uniform coverage, still impose non-trivial requirements on the logging policy.
