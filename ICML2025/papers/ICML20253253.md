## Problem

Existing differentially private synthetic data generation methods via pre-trained language model APIs (Private Evolution) struggle in data-scarce settings: they require thousands of private samples for reliable distribution estimation, produce unavoidable low-quality noisy samples that degrade downstream performance, and rely on a single PLM whose suitability varies across tasks. No prior work addresses all three challenges simultaneously while maintaining formal DP guarantees.

## Input / Output

WASP consumes a small private dataset (M=100 samples for single-party, M=300 across L=10 federated parties), K black-box PLM APIs (6 open-source or 3 closed-source), and task/category label descriptions. It outputs a DP synthetic dataset of N=6,000 text samples with guaranteed (ε,δ)-DP (sample-level or user-level), which is then used to fine-tune a BERT-based STM for downstream classification or QA. Critically, private samples are never directly exposed to PLMs; they only influence generation through differentially private voting histograms with added Gaussian noise.

## Method

WASP is an iterative (T=5 rounds) multi-PLM framework with three core mechanisms. First, a differentially private Top-Q voting mechanism replaces the standard Top-1 vote: each private sample casts Q weighted votes (with decaying weights 1, 1/2, …, 1/2^(Q−1)) to both its Q nearest and Q furthest synthetic samples, producing Nearest and Furthest histograms that are perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ = 4√(2log(1.25/δ_iter)·(T−1))/(ε√L), sensitivity Δ=4) to guarantee (ε,δ)-DP. Second, cross-PLM contrastive in-context learning constructs prompts containing both high-quality (top Nearest histogram) and low-quality (top Furthest histogram) samples drawn from any PLM, instructing the model to generate text closer to high-quality and further from low-quality examples. Third, dynamic PLM importance weighting assigns each PLM a weight proportional to the aggregate nearest-histogram scores of its generated samples, so that PLMs producing samples more similar to the private distribution generate more samples in subsequent iterations. The entire process requires no additional API queries compared to single-PLM baselines and extends naturally to federated settings via secure aggregation of local histograms.

## Results

On the primary single-party setting (ε=4.0, 100 private samples, 6,000 synthetic samples, 6 open-source PLMs), WASP achieves the best downstream STM accuracy across all 6 classification tasks: 89.52% on IMDb, 63.91% on Yelp-Category, 61.21% on Yelp-Rating, 34.99% on Openreview-Category, 37.10% on Openreview-Rating, and 88.75% on Banking77. In the federated setting (L=10, 300 total private samples), WASP further improves to 89.65% (IMDb) and 89.63% (Banking77). On the SQuAD generation task (F1), WASP reaches 11.40, outperforming the best baseline (Aug-PE with Flan-T5) at 9.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WASP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task. Against Aug-PE (the strongest single-PLM baseline, using Flan-T5), WASP gains +0.52% on IMDb, +2.52% on Yelp-Rating, and +7.50% on Banking77, with the largest margins on harder multi-class tasks (e.g., +1.68% on Openreview-Rating). Against DP-SGD+Gen, WASP leads by +0.38% on IMDb and +3.06% on Yelp-Rating, while also being applicable to closed-source PLMs where DP-SGD fine-tuning is infeasible. Against FuseGen (zero-shot, no private data), WASP improves by +0.45% on IMDb but by a much larger +3.25% on Yelp-Rating and +10.00% on Banking77, demonstrating the value of private-sample-guided weighting. In the federated setting (Table 2, L=10), WASP beats Pre-Text by +0.63% on IMDb, +0.42% on Yelp-Rating, and +7.88% on Banking77, confirming that the multi-PLM fusion and Top-Q voting advantages transfer to distributed scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

WASP extends the Private Evolution (PE) line of work (Aug-PE for text, Pre-Text for federated settings) by replacing single-vote private-sample guidance with a differentially private Top-Q voting mechanism and adding contrastive in-context learning that uses both high- and low-quality synthetic samples as demonstrations. It also builds on FuseGen's multi-PLM fusion for zero-shot data generation, but departs from it by introducing dynamic, private-sample-informed PLM importance weighting and an iterative DP-protected feedback loop rather than a single zero-shot pass. The combination of these three mechanisms—Top-Q voting, cross-PLM contrastive prompting, and adaptive PLM weighting—within a unified iterative framework is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing PE methods require thousands of private samples for reliable distribution estimation, while real-world data sources often provide only a few hundred. WASP's PLM-agnostic design removes the need for practitioners to pre-select the best model for a given task, and its extension to federated settings with secure aggregation broadens applicability to multi-party scenarios like healthcare. Limitations include evaluation primarily on classification tasks (plus one QA task), reliance on black-box PLM APIs without privacy guarantees for their training data, and modest absolute improvements over strong baselines. Open questions include whether the contrastive and weighting mechanisms transfer to non-classification generation tasks and whether more fine-grained sample-level weighting could further close the gap to the OnlyPrivate upper bound.
