## Problem

End-to-end speaker diarization models rely on permutation-invariant loss (PIL), which requires a specialized loss function at the output layer and cannot be seamlessly integrated into multi-task ASR or multimodal LLM pipelines that use standard cross-entropy training. This forces diarization and ASR to remain separate, cascaded systems that are difficult to jointly optimize for domain-specific applications. The paper asks whether the speaker permutation problem can be resolved through a sorting-based objective that is compatible with standard token-level cross-entropy loss.

## Input / Output

Sortformer takes 90-second multi-speaker audio segments (up to 4 speakers) and produces a K×T binary matrix of frame-wise speaker presence labels via sigmoid outputs, where K is the number of speaker bins and T is the number of frames. In the integrated MS-ASR system, the ASR encoder embeddings are augmented with speaker supervision through additive sinusoidal kernels, and the decoder emits a sorted serialized transcript where each word is prefixed with a generic speaker token (<spk0>, <spk1>, …) ordered by the speaker's first-segment arrival time. No word-level timestamps are required; they are approximated via a syllable-duration model when unavailable.

## Method

Sortformer is a 123M-parameter Transformer encoder (18 layers, hidden size 192) on top of a NEST backbone, with four sigmoid output bins for multi-label speaker presence classification. The key innovation is Sort Loss: instead of searching over all K! permutations as PIL does, ground-truth speaker rows are sorted by the onset of each speaker's first segment, and the model is trained to produce outputs in the same arrival-time order, effectively performing neural sorting through the Transformer's positional embeddings and self-attention. A hybrid loss (α·L_Sort + (1−α)·L_PIL) further improves standalone diarization accuracy. For multi-speaker ASR integration, the Sortformer's frame-wise speaker presence matrix is projected onto ASR encoder states via additive sinusoidal kernels, and the entire system is trained with a single standard cross-entropy loss on sorted speaker-and-word tokens, making multi-speaker ASR training functionally equivalent to mono-speaker ASR training.

## Results

On the LibriSpeechMix benchmark, Sortformer-MS-Canary (293M) achieves WER of 2.26% (1-mix), 4.61% (2-mix), and 9.05% (3-mix), yielding a 30% relative error reduction on 2-mix and 25% on 3-mix over the MS-Canary baseline without speaker supervision (6.55% and 12.14% respectively). For stand-alone diarization, Sortformer with hybrid loss and post-processing achieves 14.76% DER on DIHARD3-eval (≤4 speakers), 5.87%/8.46%/12.59% on CALLHOME (2/3/4 speakers), and 6.86% on CH109, outperforming all end-to-end baselines on CALLHOME and CH109. The runtime overhead of integrating Sortformer into the ASR pipeline is only 0.78% (297.9s → 300.2s for 42.5K seconds of audio).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sortformer-MS-Canary (293M) outperforms all multi-speaker ASR baselines on LibriSpeechMix 2-mix and 3-mix: it beats SOT-ASR (11.2→4.61 on 2-mix, 24.0→9.05 on 3-mix), SOT-ASR-SQR (8.7→4.61, 20.2→9.05), DOM-SOT (5.56†→4.61, 9.96†→9.05), and MT-LLM (5.2→4.61, 10.2→9.05), despite being 28× smaller than MT-LLM. On 1-mix (single speaker), the 1B Canary ASR (1.65%) and 170M Canary (2.19%) slightly outperform Sortformer-MS-Canary (2.26%), reflecting a minor degradation when speaker supervision is unnecessary. For stand-alone diarization (Table 1), Sortformer-Hybrid-Loss with post-processing achieves 5.87% DER on CALLHOME 2-speaker, beating AED-EEND (6.18%), WavLM-L+EEND-VC (6.46%), and EEND-GLA-Large (7.11%), though EEND-GLA-Large retains a slight edge on DIHARD3 (13.64% vs. 14.76%). The single most compelling comparison is against the MS-Canary ablation: adding the 123M Sortformer module reduces 2-mix WER from 6.55% to 4.61% and 3-mix from 12.14% to 9.05%, confirming that permutation-resolved speaker supervision is the key driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sortformer extends the EEND (End-to-End Neural Diarization) paradigm of Fujita et al. (2019) and Horiguchi et al. (2022a), which frame diarization as frame-wise multi-label classification with Permutation Invariant Loss (PIL). The key departure is replacing PIL with a Sort Loss that resolves speaker permutation by enforcing arrival-time ordering, eliminating the need for a specialized loss function at the output layer. For the ASR side, the paper builds on the Canary architecture (Puvvada et al., 2024) and the SOT-style serialized output training (Kanda et al., 2020b), but departs from SOT by using sorted speaker tokens rather than speaker-change-point tokens, and introduces sinusoidal kernel injection to bridge diarization outputs with ASR encoder states. The NEST encoder (Huang et al., 2025) serves as the shared feature backbone, and adapter-based fine-tuning (Wang et al., 2024) is adopted for the 1B-parameter experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing multi-speaker ASR training to the same cross-entropy loss paradigm as mono-speaker ASR, removing the need for word-level timestamps and complex permutation-handling mechanisms. This makes speaker tagging a drop-in capability for foundational STT models and multimodal LLMs, with only 0.78% runtime overhead from integrating the 123M-parameter Sortformer. Open questions include whether the arrival-time sorting assumption degrades gracefully with more than four speakers, how the approach extends to streaming settings, and whether the hybrid loss weighting (α=0.5) generalizes across domains without retuning. The paper does not explore target-speaker ASR or multi-task scenarios (translation, summarization) that the authors identify as future directions.
