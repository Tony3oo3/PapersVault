## Problem

Existing TTS models cannot perform true end-to-end optimization of perceptual metrics like word error rate and speaker similarity because they either contain non-differentiable components (e.g., duration predictors) or require iterative sampling over 128+ steps, making backpropagation through perceptual losses computationally infeasible. Prior distillation attempts to reduce sampling steps consistently degrade speech quality, leaving no framework that simultaneously achieves fast inference and direct metric optimization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes IPA phoneme sequences, a speaker prompt audio (encoded to a 64-channel latent at 40 Hz via a DAC-based VAE autoencoder with 1200× downsampling from 48 kHz), and random noise of shape [64, L], where L is estimated from phoneme count times the prompt's speaking rate. A binary prompt mask (covering 0–50% of the sequence) preserves the prompt region during both training and inference. The output is a 48 kHz speech waveform decoded from the 40 Hz latent, requiring only 4 sampling steps (vs. 128 for the teacher) with a real-time factor of 0.07 on a V100 GPU.

## Method

DMOSpeech distills a 450M-parameter DiT teacher (128-step latent diffusion) into a 4-step student generator via improved Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2), where the student is conditioned on discrete noise levels {1.0, 0.75, 0.50, 0.25} and re-noises its own predictions between steps. A conditional multimodal Conformer discriminator (trained with LSGAN loss) receives stacked features from the student score network along with text embeddings, prompt mask, and noise level, enforcing strong conditioning on both text and speaker identity. The key enabler for direct metric optimization is that the 4-step student provides a short, fully differentiable path from noise to output, allowing gradients from a latent CTC-based ASR loss (for WER) and a latent ECAPA-TDNN speaker verification loss (for speaker similarity) to flow back through all model components. Training uses an alternating schedule (5 score-model updates per generator update, equal-rate discriminator updates) with the CTC and SV losses delayed for 5k and 10k iterations respectively to stabilize early generation. The distillation induces beneficial mode shrinkage that narrows the conditional output distribution toward high-probability regions, improving prompt adherence and correcting teacher artifacts like sentence cutoffs.

## Results

DMOSpeech (4-step distilled) achieves a WER of 1.94 and speaker similarity (SIM) of 0.69, surpassing the ground truth SIM of 0.67, while reducing inference time to an RTF of 0.07 (13.7× faster than the 128-step teacher at RTF 0.96). In subjective evaluation against end-to-end baselines, it scores MOS-N 4.27, MOS-Q 4.45, SMOS-V 4.35, and SMOS-S 4.16, significantly outperforming all autoregressive baselines. On the Seed-TTS generalization test, DMOSpeech achieves SIM 0.687 (en) / 0.757 (zh) and WER 1.78 (en) / CER 1.43 (zh) at RTF 0.06, matching or exceeding F5-TTS and MaskGCT while being 5–20× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest end-to-end diffusion baseline DiTTo-TTS, DMOSpeech wins on WER (1.94 vs. 2.56), SIM (0.69 vs. 0.62), and RTF (0.07 vs. 0.16), while achieving comparable naturalness (MOS-N 4.27 vs. 4.28) and higher voice/style similarity (SMOS-V 4.35 vs. 4.16; SMOS-S 4.16 vs. 4.07). Against the non-E2E model NaturalSpeech3, DMOSpeech achieves higher SIM (0.69 vs. 0.67) and much faster inference (RTF 0.07 vs. 0.30), with a marginally higher WER (1.94 vs. 1.81) and higher subjective naturalness (MOS-N 4.42 vs. 4.24). Against all autoregressive baselines (VoiceCraft, CLaM-TTS, XTTS), DMOSpeech dominates on every metric: WER is 2.5–3.3× lower, SIM is 0.08–0.20 higher, and RTF is 5–16× faster. Against StyleTTS-ZS, DMOSpeech leads on naturalness (4.42 vs. 4.40), voice similarity (4.49 vs. 4.34), and style similarity (4.30 vs. 4.20). On the cross-dataset Seed-TTS evaluation, DMOSpeech matches or exceeds F5-TTS (SIM 0.687 vs. 0.647 en; 0.757 vs. 0.741 zh) and MaskGCT (SIM 0.687 vs. 0.717 en; 0.757 vs. 0.752 zh) while running 5–20× faster (RTF 0.06 vs. 0.32/1.21).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DMD2 distillation framework (Yin et al., 2024a) and end-to-end latent diffusion TTS architectures (SimpleTTS, DiTTo-TTS), adapting them with a conditional multimodal discriminator and multi-step sampling schedule for speech. The key departure from prior distillation work (progressive distillation, consistency models, rectified flow) is that those methods focused solely on reducing sampling steps while consistently degrading quality; here, the authors reframe the resulting mode shrinkage as beneficial for strongly conditional generation and exploit the reduced sampling steps to enable direct gradient-based optimization of perceptual metrics (CTC loss for WER, SV loss for speaker similarity) — something previously infeasible due to non-differentiable components or prohibitively expensive backpropagation through hundreds of denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of true end-to-end metric optimization in TTS, opening a path toward aligning speech synthesis directly with human auditory preferences without RLHF. The mode shrinkage insight — that diversity reduction from distillation improves quality in strongly conditional tasks — has implications beyond TTS for any conditional generative model. Limitations include sensitivity to batch size (96 required for stable score estimation), the need for careful loss scheduling to avoid training collapse, and the observation that SV loss alone degrades naturalness, suggesting the metric-optimization balance is non-trivial. Open questions include whether this framework scales to multilingual settings, larger datasets, and whether more differentiable reward models can replace the current CTC/SV losses.
