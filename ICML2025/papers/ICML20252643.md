## Problem

Leveraging abundant unlabeled EEG data to improve performance on scarce labeled data, and transferring diagnostic knowledge from expensive high-density (HD) EEG to affordable low-density (LD) EEG, remain largely unsolved. Prior graph pre-training methods for EEG have applied either contrastive or generative (masked autoencoder) paradigms in isolation, and no approach has integrated both within a unified graph framework while simultaneously enabling HD-to-LD distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252643/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes EEG time series converted into graph representations: alpha-band Power Spectral Density (PSD) values serve as node features, and Pearson correlation between electrodes forms the adjacency matrix. The HD graph (e.g., 64 or 128 electrodes) and LD graph (e.g., 16 or 32 electrodes) are related as parent and subgraph, with the LD node set being a strict subset of the HD node set. The model outputs binary class predictions (MDD vs. healthy, ASD vs. healthy, male vs. female, mild vs. severe depression) evaluated via AUROC and accuracy.

## Method

EEG-DisGCMAE introduces a unified pre-training paradigm (GCMAE-PT) that simultaneously performs graph contrastive pre-training and graph masked autoencoder pre-training: contrastive augmented pairs are reconstructed via a masked autoencoder, and the reconstructed samples are then contrasted against a shared key queue containing both HD and LD keys from teacher and student models, creating explicit mutual supervision. During fine-tuning, a novel Graph Topology Distillation (GTD) loss uses KL divergence on node similarity kernels to align the student's learned topology with the teacher's, specifically identifying positive pairs (nodes 1-hop or 2-hop connected via deleted electrodes in the HD graph) and negative pairs (nodes connected in LD but not in HD) to correct the student's erroneous edges introduced by electrode removal. The total fine-tuning loss combines cross-entropy, logit distillation, and GTD loss, enabling a lightweight student (1.3M params) trained on LD data to approach the performance of a large teacher (5.7M params) trained on HD data.

## Results

On the HBN MDD task (HD), the best configuration (Ours-Large GFormer) achieves 87.4% AUROC / 87.8% ACC, outperforming the strongest baseline GraphMAE2 (83.5/85.7) by ~4 AUROC points. Under low-density (LD) input, the same model reaches 84.8/86.9, surpassing GraphMAE2 (81.3/83.0) by ~3.5 AUROC points. Notably, the tiny model (1.3M parameters, DGCNN backbone) achieves 84.8/85.4 on HD MDD, matching or exceeding larger pre-training baselines (5.7M parameters) while using less than a quarter of the parameters. Across all four tasks (HBN MDD, HBN ASD, EMBARC Sex, EMBARC Severity) and both HD/LD densities, EEG-DisGCMAE consistently ranks first.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEG-DisGCMAE outperforms all 13 baselines across every task and density setting. Against the strongest graph pre-training baselines, the large GFormer model gains ~4 AUROC over GraphMAE2 (87.4 vs 83.5 on HBN MDD HD) and ~5 AUROC over GRACE (87.4 vs 83.7), while the tiny DGCNN model (1.3M params) already surpasses GraphMAE2 (83.5 AUROC) and GPT-GNN (83.3 AUROC) on HBN MDD HD. On the LD setting, the advantage persists: Ours-Large GFormer reaches 84.8 AUROC vs GraphMAE2's 81.3, a 3.5-point gap that reflects the effectiveness of the GTD loss in recovering information lost from removed electrodes. Among non-pre-training baselines, GFormer (80.4/83.6) and EEGNet (80.6/82.9) are the strongest on HBN MDD HD, but both trail the proposed method by 7+ AUROC points. The tiny model's competitive performance with far fewer parameters (1.3M vs 5.7M) demonstrates that the unified GCMAE-PT pre-training and GTD distillation compensate for model capacity, achieving a superior performance-efficiency tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate graph self-supervised pre-training paradigms—graph contrastive pre-training (GCC, GraphCL, GRACE) and graph masked autoencoder pre-training (GraphMAE, GPT-GNN, GraphMAE2)—into a single GCMAE-PT framework, drawing an analogy to CMAE in computer vision but extending it to the graph domain for the first time. It further introduces a Graph Topology Distillation (GTD) loss that leverages the structural relationship between high-density and low-density EEG graphs (where LD is a subgraph of HD with removed nodes) to distill topological knowledge, building on graph distillation work like GCRD and VQ-Graph. The joint teacher-student pre-training via a shared MoCo-style key queue (He et al., 2020) is a novel integration that ensures both models are co-optimized as distillers before downstream fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: making affordable, portable low-density EEG devices achieve performance close to expensive high-density systems could substantially broaden access to EEG-based clinical diagnostics. However, a key limitation is that all experiments use the 10-20 EEG system, so the method has not been validated on truly heterogeneous electrode configurations across different EEG standards—a critical barrier to building a general-purpose EEG graph foundation model. Open questions include whether the GTD loss generalizes to non-subgraph density reductions (e.g., different spatial layouts) and whether the unified pre-training paradigm transfers to other graph domains beyond EEG.
