## Problem

Existing exploration methods for autonomous agents rely on random, uninformed exploration and slow gradient-based updates, requiring infeasibly many interactions to adapt. Humans, by contrast, make a few carefully directed attempts and quickly adjust their behavior based on what they have already tried. The paper asks: can we train agents offline from expert demonstrations to perform fast, semantically informed exploration that adapts in-context over the space of expert behaviors, without any online gradient updates?

## Input / Output

The policy takes as input the current state (proprioceptive vector or 128×128 RGB image encoded via ResNet-34), a downsampled history of past observations (each state encoded as a single transformer token), a scalar coverage-to-go value that controls the degree of exploration, and optionally a task label (language command or one-hot task encoding). It outputs continuous control actions in chunks (e.g., 4–20 steps depending on the setting). The coverage value is computed at training time as 1/tr((Λ(h)+λI)⁻¹) over the future trajectory, and at deployment the user sets it to regulate how exploratory the agent should be relative to its collected history.

## Method

Behavioral Exploration (BE) trains a long-context transformer-based diffusion policy via a modified behavioral cloning objective: the standard BC loss is augmented by conditioning the action prediction on a history of past observations and a scalar coverage-to-go value, so the model learns which expert actions are most exploratory given what has already been seen. The coverage metric is defined as the inverse trace of the regularized inverse feature matrix over visited states, restricted to the subspace spanned by the behavior policy's features (via SVD), ensuring exploration stays within the space of coherent expert behaviors. A key theoretical result (Proposition 4.2) shows that conditioning on the event that the future trajectory achieves maximum coverage relative to the history yields an optimal policy for maximizing behavior-policy coverage. At deployment, the agent simply feeds its online-collected history into the context window and sets the coverage conditioning value, enabling fast in-context adaptation with no gradient updates—exploration is regulated by a single scalar, and the policy naturally avoids repeating previously attempted behaviors.

## Results

On D4RL Antmaze (medium + large), BE reaches 55.6% of goals and covers 24.1 regions, substantially outperforming all RL-based exploration baselines (SUPE: 19.5% goals, 17.0 regions; HILP: 12.8% goals, 18.9 regions; RND and ExPLORe: 0% goals, ~3.5 regions). On D4RL Kitchen, BE achieves 92.2% task completion, comparable to SUPE (95.6%) and HILP (91.3%) but far exceeding BC (30.6%). On Libero 90 with a hidden task, BE achieves 82.4% success after 15 attempts versus 59.0% for BC and 26.7% for SUPE, and with task conditioning reaches 99.3% success. On real-world WidowX reaching tasks, BE succeeds ~40% more often than BC or OpenVLA and requires ~2 fewer attempts per trial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BE dominates all RL-based exploration baselines on Antmaze: it reaches 55.6% of goals versus 19.5% for SUPE, 12.8% for HILP, and 0% for both RND and ExPLORe, while also covering 24.1 regions versus 17.0 (SUPE), 18.9 (HILP), and ~3.5 (RND/ExPLORe). On Kitchen, BE (92.2%) is slightly below SUPE (95.6%) but comparable to HILP (91.3%), while RND and ExPLORe fail almost entirely (~8%). On Libero with a hidden task, BE (82.4%) substantially outperforms SUPE (26.7%), BC (59.0%), and all BC variants, demonstrating that in-context behavioral exploration is far more effective than gradient-based online RL or naive action noise for selecting diverse task-solving behaviors. On the real-world WidowX reaching tasks, BE outperforms both BC and OpenVLA by ~40% in success rate and ~2 fewer attempts, despite OpenVLA having ~100× more parameters and being trained on a much larger dataset. The key distinction is that BE achieves all gains through purely offline training plus in-context adaptation at deployment, whereas SUPE, HILP, ExPLORe, and RND all require expensive online gradient-based RL updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion policy architecture of Dasari et al. (2024) as its backbone, but departs fundamentally from standard behavioral cloning by conditioning the policy on a history of past observations and a coverage metric, enabling in-context adaptation without gradient updates. Conceptually, it draws from in-context learning in LLMs (Brown et al., 2020) and return-conditioned policies (Decision Transformers, etc.), but repurposes the 'return' signal as a coverage-over-behavior-space measure rather than a reward-to-go. It positions itself in contrast to RL-based exploration methods (RND, ExPLORe, HILP, SUPE) that require online gradient updates, and to meta-RL that assumes access to a reward function, instead requiring only offline demonstration data and a supervised training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a minimal modification to the BC objective—adding history and coverage to the conditioning—yields a policy that explores over expert behaviors and adapts online in-context, outperforming both standard BC and complex RL-based exploration methods across simulated and real-world settings. This suggests that in-context adaptation can substitute for expensive online gradient-based updates in exploration, which could simplify deployment pipelines for robotic systems. Open questions include whether BE can explore beyond the demonstration space when the solution requires novel behaviors, whether it can be combined with LLMs for language-based exploration, and how it scales with state-of-the-art generalist robot policies like π0 or GR00T.
