## Problem

Quantizing depth prediction DNNs to INT8 for on-device deployment causes severe depth precision loss because high dynamic range depth (e.g., 0–10 m at 1 cm accuracy needs 10 bits) cannot be faithfully represented in 8 bits, producing false edges on planar surfaces and loss of spatial detail. Existing work either keeps the final layer in float precision (computationally expensive at high resolution) or relies on mixed-precision quantization schemes that many edge hardware platforms (Qualcomm Hexagon DSP, Coral Edge TPU) do not support. The paper addresses this by changing the structure of the signal the DNN predicts rather than modifying the quantization process itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB stereo pair at 384×512 pixels and produces a disparity/depth map at 192×256 pixels. Unusually, the quantized DNN outputs two channels (Hilbert curve x and y components) in b-bit precision (e.g., INT8) rather than a single depth channel; a CPU-side post-processing step using a 256×256 lookup table then reconstructs the depth at b + log₂L bits (e.g., INT10–INT11 for a 3rd-order Hilbert curve). The post-processing involves no addition or multiplication, only table lookups, keeping the overhead minimal.

## Method

The core idea is to encode high dynamic range depth q as a point (x(q), y(q)) on a 2D Hilbert curve of order p, where both components are bounded in [0,1] and can be represented in low-bit precision with minimal quality loss. The DNN (DispNet or DPT) is modified to replace its single disparity head with two identical heads predicting the Hilbert components, and is trained with a composite loss combining a standard depth loss on the reconstructed q with a Hilbert curve loss that penalizes deviation from the curve manifold. Standard quantization (PTQ or QAT) is applied unchanged; on-device, the two b-bit Hilbert components are mapped back to depth via a precomputed LUT that projects each (x,y) to the nearest point on the curve, effectively compressing quantization error by the curve length L and increasing effective bit-width by log₂L bits. The Hilbert curve was chosen over other space-filling curves (Peano, Gosper) for its uniform coverage of the unit square, non-self-intersection, and fine-grained control over curve length via the order parameter p.

## Results

On ScanNet v2 stereo matching, the modified h3DispNet in W8A8 format on DSP achieves Abs Rel 0.93% and S_C 0.81, compared to 2.03% and 0.58 for the original DispNet in the same W8A8/DSP setting. For DPT, h2DPT W8A8 DSP reduces Abs Rel from 4.18% to 1.12% and S_C from 0.52 to 0.62. The modified W8A8 models outperform the original W8A16 models in depth quality while running 1.5× faster and consuming 35–54% less energy. Quantization error is reduced by up to 4.6× on DSP as a side effect of the Hilbert curve representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DispNet (Mayer et al., 2016) and DPT with MobileViTv3-S encoder (Ranftl et al., 2021), both evaluated in W8A8 format on Qualcomm Hexagon DSP. Against DispNet, h3DispNet W8A8 DSP wins decisively on every metric: Abs Rel drops from 2.03% to 0.93%, EPE from 0.69 to 0.24 px, D1 from 5.35% to 1.26%, and S_C rises from 0.58 to 0.81. Against DPT, h2DPT W8A8 DSP reduces Abs Rel from 4.18% to 1.12% and S_C from 0.52 to 0.62, while h3DPT achieves the best S_C (0.70) and EPE (0.32 px) among all modified DPT variants. Critically, the modified W8A8 models surpass the original W8A16 models in depth quality (e.g., h3DispNet Abs Rel 0.93% vs. W8A16's 1.78%) while being 30–54% faster and consuming 34–35% less energy, making the Hilbert curve approach strictly superior to simply using higher activation precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard quantization pipeline (QAT/PTQ) by instead modifying the *structure of the predicted signal*: depth is re-encoded as two 2D Hilbert curve components, each fitting in INT8 range, so that a fully W8A8 model can reconstruct higher-precision depth via a simple LUT post-processing step. It builds directly on DispNet and DPT architectures, replacing their single disparity/depth head with two Hilbert-component heads, and on the mathematical machinery of space-filling curves (Bader, 2012). Unlike prior work that keeps the final layer in float precision (Jiang et al., 2022) or resorts to mixed-precision schemes (Borrego-Carazo et al., 2021), this approach maintains homogeneous 8-bit quantization across all layers while recovering effective INT10–INT11 depth precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables fully W8A8 deployment on edge DSPs (e.g., Qualcomm Hexagon) with depth quality matching or exceeding W8A16 baselines at 1.5× lower latency and power, which is practically significant for autonomous driving, robotics, and AR/VR on constrained hardware. An unexpected side effect—quantization error reduction of up to 4.6×—suggests that signal-structure-aware output representations can complement (not replace) quantization-aware training, opening a new axis of optimization. Key limitations include the need for full retraining, applicability only to one-stage (non-iterative) architectures, and sensitivity to the correlation structure between Hilbert component errors, which nullifies the error-reduction benefit for tasks like direct keypoint regression. Integrating the approach into iterative GRU-based stereo matchers and exploring higher-dimensional space-filling curves are natural next steps.
