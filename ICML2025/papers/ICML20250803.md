## Problem

Existing dynamical system forecasting methods learn environment-specific dynamics but cannot isolate the shared invariant mechanism across environments, especially when environmental effects change entire function forms (e.g., adding friction vs. adding external power) rather than merely altering coefficients. Prior invariant learning techniques (IRM, VREx) were designed for categorical prediction tasks and lack a principled definition of invariance in function spaces, making them inapplicable to ODE-based dynamical systems where the invariant object is a derivative function, not a scalar label.

## Input / Output

The method takes a past trajectory matrix X_p ∈ R^{d×T_c} (the first T_c time steps of a d-dimensional state trajectory observed in one environment) and initial conditions X_0 as input. It produces two neural-network-parameterized derivative functions: the full function f̂ capturing all dynamics, and the invariant function f̂_c capturing only the environment-independent mechanism. The invariant function is then passed through a numerical integrator with X_0 to generate the predicted invariant trajectory X̂^c, which is evaluated against ground-truth invariant trajectories X^c (simulated by removing environment-specific terms) using NRMSE. A key structural detail is that the hypernetwork outputs m-dimensional parameter vectors representing the derivative network, not the trajectory directly.

## Method

DIF (Disentanglement of Invariant Function) formulates the problem via a structural causal model where the full derivative function f is composed of an invariant component f_c (caused by exogenous variable c) and an environment-specific component f_e (caused by exogenous variable e). The architecture is an encoder-decoder hypernetwork: a transformer encoder maps X_p to a hidden representation, two MLPs separately produce invariant and environment embeddings (ẑ_c, ẑ_e), these are summed to form the full function representation, and a decoder MLP maps it to m-dimensional neural network parameters. The core theoretical contribution is the invariant function learning principle (Theorem 3.1): the true f_c is recovered by maximizing conditional mutual information I(h_θc(X_p); f | X_0) subject to the independence constraint h_θc(X_p) ⊥ e. This is made implementable by showing the mutual information maximization reduces to MSE minimization on invariant trajectories (Proposition 3.3), while the independence constraint is enforced via adversarial training with an environment discriminator g_φ that classifies environments from the invariant embedding, trained in a min-max fashion to make f̂_c indistinguishable across environments.

## Results

On the three multi-environment ODE datasets, DIF's invariant function f̂_c achieves NRMSE of 0.3561 (ME-Pendulum), 0.6194 (ME-Lotka-Volterra), and 0.0652 (ME-SIREpidemic) for invariant trajectory prediction, substantially outperforming the full function f̂ on the same target (0.7884, 0.7919, 0.9867 respectively). On ME-Pendulum, over 75% of DIF's 80+ random hyperparameter candidates outperform the best results of MAML and CoDA, and over 93.75% outperform IRM and VREx. On ME-Lotka-Volterra, DIF's median still outperforms nearly all candidates from competing methods. Symbolic regression of the extracted invariant functions recovers near-exact physical laws, e.g., 0.99ω_t and −0.97α²sin(θ_t) for the ideal pendulum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIF outperforms all four baselines (MAML, CoDA, IRM, VREx) on all three datasets in terms of invariant trajectory NRMSE, as shown by the boxen-plot distributions in Figure 4. Among the baselines, the invariant learning methods (IRM, VREx) are generally more stable and outperform MAML on 2 of 3 datasets, while consistently beating CoDA across all datasets. However, even the best candidates from IRM and VREx remain suboptimal compared to DIF's median, confirming that standard invariant learning principles (IRM, VREx) do not transfer directly to the function-space setting. On ME-Pendulum, the gap is most pronounced: over 75% of DIF candidates beat the best MAML/CoDA results and over 93.75% beat the best IRM/VREx results. On the more challenging ME-Lotka-Volterra dataset, DIF's median still surpasses nearly all competing candidates, though the absolute NRMSE is highest (0.6194) due to the greater complexity of the four modified predator-prey relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2016) to implement a function-that-returns-a-function architecture, and draws conceptual inspiration from invariant causal prediction (Peters et al., 2016) and invariant risk minimization (Arjovsky et al., 2019). However, it departs sharply from prior invariant learning, which assumes categorical outputs and cannot define invariance in function spaces, and from prior meta-learning for ODEs (CoDA, LEAD, MetaphysiCa), which only vary function coefficients across environments rather than function forms. The key conceptual departure is formulating invariant function learning as a causal disentanglement problem in function space, with a new information-theoretic principle (Theorem 3.1) that provides existence and uniqueness guarantees for the invariant function, something neither IRM nor VREx can offer in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes invariant function learning as a principled task for scientific discovery, showing that one can extract the underlying physical law (e.g., the ideal pendulum equation) from observations entangled with diverse environmental effects, with symbolic regression confirming near-exact recovery of the true equations. The causal formulation and theoretical guarantee are clean and fill a genuine gap: prior invariant learning methods provably cannot handle this setting due to the categorical assumption violation. However, the scope is limited to low-dimensional ODE systems (2–3 states), the symbolic regression does not always perfectly recover equations (notably on ME-SIREpidemic), and extension to PDEs, high-dimensional graph-structured systems, or image/video-based physics discovery remains open. The practical impact for the broader DL community is currently narrow, but the task formulation and causal framework could become foundational if the community adopts multi-environment function learning as a standard scientific discovery benchmark.
