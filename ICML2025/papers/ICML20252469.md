## Problem

Decentralized optimization on Riemannian manifolds remains challenging because existing Euclidean diffusion strategies rely on linear operations (vector addition, matrix decomposition) that have no direct analogue on curved spaces, and prior manifold methods either depend on specific Euclidean embeddings (extrinsic) or lack the diffusion strategy's proven advantages for continuous adaptation. This paper fills the gap by introducing a fully intrinsic diffusion adaptation algorithm on general manifolds with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees under constant step size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252469/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of K agents sequentially observes streaming data samples x_{k,t} (e.g., n-dimensional vectors or 784-pixel MNIST images) and communicates only with its neighbors on a fixed graph whose topology is encoded in a left-stochastic weighted adjacency matrix C (generated via the Metropolis rule). The algorithm produces a consensus solution on the target manifold: for PCA, a point on the Grassmann manifold G_n^p representing the top-p principal subspace; for GMM inference, a point on the product manifold ∏S_n^{++} × R^{M-1} encoding covariance matrices and mixing coefficients. In practice, exact exponential maps in the updates are replaced by cheaper retractions for computational efficiency.

## Method

The algorithm alternates two fully intrinsic steps at each agent: an adaptation step that performs one R-SGD update on local data to produce an intermediate estimate φ_{k,t} = exp_{w_{k,t-1}}(-μ·∇̂J_k(w_{k,t-1})), and a combination step that aggregates neighbors' intermediate estimates on the tangent space of φ_{k,t} by descending along the Riemannian gradient of a geodesic-distance consensus penalty, yielding w_{k,t} = exp_{φ_{k,t}}(α·Σ_ℓ c_{ℓk}·exp^{-1}_{φ_{k,t}}(φ_{ℓ,t})). The key insight is that the combination step's gradient has the closed form -Σ_ℓ c_{ℓk} exp^{-1}_{φ_{k,t}}(φ_{ℓ,t}), making the entire procedure independent of any ambient Euclidean embedding and applicable to arbitrary manifolds. The theoretical analysis establishes network agreement (all agents converge to the consensus submanifold) after O(μ^{-1}) iterations and a non-asymptotic convergence bound for a streaming average of iterates, with the curvature of the manifold entering through a geometric constant ζ that depends on the lower sectional curvature bound κ_min. The framework is instantiated on the Grassmann manifold for online distributed PCA and on a product of SPD matrices for GMM parameter estimation, with numerical validation on synthetic and MNIST data.

## Results

The paper evaluates its Riemannian diffusion adaptation algorithm on two tasks—distributed PCA (Grassmann manifold) and GMM inference (SPD product manifold)—using both synthetic data and MNIST, with K=20 agents over a connected graph. Performance is measured via Mean Square Deviation (MSD) for PCA and average log-likelihood difference (ALL* − ALL) for GMM, reported as learning curves (Figures 2–5) rather than tabular values. The proposed method consistently achieves lower steady-state MSD and ALL difference than all decentralized baselines, approaching the centralized solution that has access to the full dataset. Theoretical guarantees show network agreement within O(μ⁻¹) iterations and non-asymptotic convergence to a neighborhood of the optimum whose radius scales as O(μ) for sufficiently small step sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-cooperative R-SGD baseline (each agent optimizes independently on local data), the proposed diffusion adaptation method achieves a significant reduction in both MSD (PCA) and ALL difference (GMM), demonstrating the clear benefit of inter-agent information exchange. On the PCA task, the method also outperforms DRSGD (Chen et al., ICML 2021), an extrinsic consensus algorithm operating on the Stiefel manifold, confirming the advantage of the intrinsic Grassmannian formulation. For GMM inference, the proposed method beats ECGMM (an extrinsic consensus SGD extended to the SPD product manifold with projection), which is the only other online decentralized baseline available. In all experiments, the centralized R-SGD (using data from all K agents simultaneously) achieves the lowest MSD/ALL difference, as expected given its full-information access; the proposed method narrows this gap while remaining fully decentralized, with each agent using only locally observed data and communicating solely with neighbors. The paper notes that the GMM log-likelihood is not guaranteed to be geodesically convex for multiple components, yet the algorithm still performs well, suggesting robustness beyond the stated theoretical assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean diffusion adaptation framework (Chen & Sayed, 2012) to general Riemannian manifolds by replacing vector addition with exponential maps and the Euclidean gradient with the Riemannian gradient. The adaptation step draws directly on R-SGD (Bonnabel, 2013), while the combination step descends along the geodesic-distance consensus penalty from Riemannian consensus theory (Tron et al., 2012). The convergence proof leverages the Riemannian iterate-averaging scheme of Tripuraneni et al. (2018) to construct a Lyapunov function. The only prior diffusion-on-manifolds work (Wang et al., 2024b) relied on inner-loop optimization and lacked theoretical analysis, making this the first fully intrinsic, theoretically grounded Riemannian diffusion strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete non-asymptotic convergence analysis for a diffusion-type strategy on general manifolds with constant step size, which is essential for continuous adaptation in streaming settings. The curvature-dependent constant ζ provides a principled way to understand how manifold geometry affects convergence rates, a tool that could inform algorithm design for other Riemannian problems. A key limitation is the reliance on the exponential map in the theory (computationally expensive), while experiments use cheaper retractions without corresponding guarantees. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-geodesically-convex objectives (as in multi-component GMMs), scaling to larger networks, and whether the O(μ⁻¹) transient phase can be shortened.
