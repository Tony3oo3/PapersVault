## Problem

Online KD and self KD can only moderately improve the generalization of DNNs because the teacher and student update in lockstep, providing a constantly shifting learning target. The paper asks whether introducing a temporal gap between teacher and student updates—inspired by the well-established spacing effect in biological learning—can yield a more effective KD paradigm for generalization without additional training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252297/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100/1K) and outputs class labels via a standard classification head. The key I/O detail is temporal: the teacher network consumes a block of s epochs of mini-batches first, then the student network processes the same cached batches while the teacher is frozen, creating an asynchronous data-flow pattern not present in standard online or self KD.

## Method

Spaced KD introduces a hyperparameter s (space interval, in epochs) that controls how far ahead the teacher trains before distilling to the student. Concretely, the teacher updates for s steps on consecutive mini-batches, then its parameters are frozen while the student performs KD updates over those same batches, after which the cycle repeats. The paper proves via Hessian trace analysis that this spaced teacher yields a lower expected Hessian trace at convergence than a synchronously updated online teacher, implying a flatter loss minimum and better generalization. Empirically, the spacing effect is most effective when applied in the later stages of training (a 'critical period'), and the optimal interval s≈1.5 epochs is relatively insensitive across architectures and datasets. The approach is plug-and-play, compatible with various KD loss functions and advanced KD methods (TSB, CTKD, LSKD) without modifying their architectures.

## Results

Spaced KD improves top-1 accuracy over online KD by an average of 2.14% across CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-100, with gains ranging from +1.19% (ResNet-101/CIFAR-100) to +2.65% (ResNet-50/ImageNet-100). On Tiny-ImageNet, the largest gains are observed: +2.31% for ResNet-50 and +2.47% for ResNet-101. For self KD, Spaced KD achieves up to +3.44% (ResNet-101/Tiny-ImageNet) and +3.86% (DeiT-Tiny/Tiny-ImageNet). On ImageNet-1K, Spaced KD improves ResNet-18 by up to 5.08% over online KD (53.67% vs 48.59% at 80 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spaced KD consistently outperforms online KD (DML, Zhang et al. 2018) across all 14 dataset-architecture combinations, with average gains of +1.38% on CIFAR-100, +2.14% on Tiny-ImageNet, and +2.17% on ImageNet-100. Against self KD (Zhang et al. 2019), Spaced KD achieves larger absolute gains (up to +3.86% on DeiT-Tiny/Tiny-ImageNet), suggesting the spacing effect is particularly beneficial when the teacher and student share the same architecture. When combined with more advanced KD methods, Spaced KD still provides consistent improvements: over TSB (Li et al. 2022) by +1.12% to +3.23%, over CTKD (Li et al. 2023) by +1.71% to +0.42%, and over LSKD (Sun et al. 2024) by +1.02% to +0.38% across CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. Notably, a naive variant that maintains a constant s-step gap between teacher and student (Table 11) shows no significant improvement over online KD, confirming that the periodic freezing-and-distilling mechanism is essential rather than merely the temporal offset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends online KD (Zhang et al., 2018) and self KD (Zhang et al., 2019) by introducing a temporal spacing interval between teacher and student updates, drawing an explicit analogy to the biological spacing effect in memory formation. Unlike adaptive distillation methods (ESKD, ATKD, SHAKE) that adjust teacher-student capacity from a spatial/architectural level, Spaced KD operates purely from a temporal level and is architecture- and algorithm-agnostic. The theoretical framing connects KD to ensemble learning at temporal scales (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2020) and links the spacing mechanism to convergence toward flatter minima in the loss landscape (Keskar et al., 2016; He et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spaced KD offers a simple, plug-and-play modification to existing KD pipelines that consistently improves generalization across architectures (ResNet, DeiT, PiT) and datasets without additional training cost, making it practically attractive. The finding that the spacing effect is most beneficial in the later stage of training—contrary to the intuition that capacity-gap-driven KD should matter most early—opens a new temporal perspective on KD design. Key open questions include the theoretical justification for the empirically optimal interval (s=1.5), adaptive strategies for both interval and timing, and whether the spacing principle transfers to other learning paradigms such as curriculum learning, continual learning, and reinforcement learning.
