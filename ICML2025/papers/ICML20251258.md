## Problem

Steerable convolutions capture local equivariant features effectively but cannot model long-range spatial dependencies, while existing equivariant transformer methods either operate on point clouds (imposing inappropriate inductive biases on grid-structured volumetric data) or incur prohibitive memory costs. This paper addresses the gap by designing a steerable self-attention mechanism that is SE(d)-equivariant and operates directly on dense volumetric grids in d dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251258/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The architecture consumes grid-structured volumetric data: 28×28 grayscale digits (Rotated MNIST), 3D voxelized point clouds (ModelNet10), 578×770 RGB dermoscopic images (PH2), and 240×240×155 multi-modal MRI volumes (BraTS). Internally, all features are represented as complex-valued vectors on a discrete spatial grid, indexed by irreducible representations of SO(d) (integer frequencies in 2D, Wigner D-matrices of degree ℓ in 3D). Outputs are class labels for classification tasks and per-pixel/per-voxel probability maps for segmentation, obtained by taking the magnitude of the constant-representation channel.

## Method

The steerable transformer inserts self-attention blocks on top of a steerable convolutional encoder, operating entirely in Fourier space. The key mechanism is a steerable positional encoding built from spherical harmonics modulated by a learnable inverse-square radial function, which encodes relative spatial structure while satisfying the equivariance constraint P^(ρ)(Rx+t, Ry+t) = ρ(R)P^(ρ)(x,y) (Theorem 1). Queries, keys, and values are complex-valued matrices per irrep, with scores computed via conjugate-transpose dot products and softmax applied to the absolute value. Non-linearities use ReLU on the L2 norm of Fourier vectors (preserving equivariance), and layer normalization divides by the aggregate norm across all irreps. The full architecture is a hybrid: steerable convolution blocks extract local equivariant features, two steerable transformer blocks capture global dependencies, and a final norm enforces invariance before classification or segmentation heads.

## Results

On Rotated MNIST, the steerable transformer (k=4) achieves 98.82% accuracy, outperforming the steerable convolution baseline (98.72%) with fewer parameters (1.13M vs 1.18M). On ModelNet10, the steerable transformer (ℓ=3) reaches 90.40% (z-rotation) and 86.80% (SO(3) rotation), improving over the convolution baseline (90.13% / 86.62%). For segmentation, the steerable transformer yields a 90.61% Dice score on PH2 (vs 89.31% for convolution) and improves BraTS scores across all three tumor categories (e.g., enhancing tumor: 75.01 vs 73.29; tumor core: 54.89 vs 51.07; whole tumor: 76.37 vs 74.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Rotated MNIST (Table 2), the steerable transformer with k=8 achieves 0.97% error, substantially outperforming all equivariant attention baselines: GE-ViT (Xu et al., 2023) at 1.99%, GSA-Nets (Romero & Cordonnier, 2021) at 2.03%, and α-R4 CNN (Romero et al., 2020) at 1.69%. It also beats equivariant convolution methods like Harmonic Net (Worrall et al., 2017; 1.69%), P4CNN (Cohen & Welling, 2016; 2.28%), and TI-Pooling (Laptev et al., 2016; 1.2%), though it trails E2CNN (Weiler & Cesa, 2019; 0.69%) and Weiler et al. (2018b; 0.71%), which use a higher Fourier cutoff of 16 versus the paper's cutoff of 8. On ModelNet10 (Table 3), the steerable transformer achieves 91.1% accuracy with 0.92M parameters, outperforming ECC (Simonovsky & Komodakis, 2017; 90.8%) and 3D ShapeNets (Wu et al., 2015; 83.5%), but falling short of Cubenet (Worrall & Brostow, 2018; 94.6%), ORION (Sedaghat et al., 2016; 93.8%), and Rot-SO-Net (Li et al., 2019; 94.5%); notably, the paper applies data augmentation only at test time, whereas competing methods use both train- and test-time augmentation. For the segmentation tasks (PH2 and BraTS), the paper does not provide direct comparisons against external baselines in the main tables, reporting only the steerable convolution vs. steerable transformer comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Transformer (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) and steerable convolution (Kondor & Trivedi, 2018; Worrall et al., 2017) frameworks by designing a self-attention mechanism that operates in Fourier space on group-representation features, preserving SE(d) equivariance. It departs from prior equivariant attention work (Romero & Cordonnier, 2021; Xu et al., 2023) by retaining a steerable convolutional encoder rather than using standalone attention, and from 3D equivariant transformers (Fuchs et al., 2020; Liao & Smidt, 2023) by targeting dense volumetric grids rather than point clouds. The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which gives necessary and sufficient conditions on positional encodings for equivariance, leading to a spherical-harmonic-based relative positional encoding with learnable radial modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled way to combine local (convolutional) and global (attention) representations in equivariant networks for volumetric data, which is directly relevant to medical imaging applications like tumor segmentation where rotation/translation equivariance is essential. However, the empirical gains are modest (typically 0.1–1.7 points across four datasets), and the authors acknowledge that memory constraints forced low Fourier cutoffs, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher-frequency representations or larger datasets. A key open question is whether the consistent but small improvements persist at scale, and whether the architectural overhead justifies the benefit compared to simply using deeper steerable convolutions.
