## Problem

Standard state space models (SSMs) fitted to observational neural data capture only statistical associations and cannot be causally interpreted, since observational dynamics are confined to a low-dimensional task manifold. This limits the ability to test causal hypotheses about circuit mechanisms or predict responses to novel perturbations. The paper addresses how to build identifiable models that recover true latent dynamics and generalize to unseen interventional conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes time-series neural recordings (spike counts or calcium traces) from N neurons over T timesteps across K trials, together with interventional stimulation signals u_t ∈ R^M specifying which stimulation channels are active at each time point. It outputs a D-dimensional latent trajectory x_t, a linear dynamics matrix A ∈ R^{D×D}, a sparse intervention matrix B ∈ R^{D×M} (regularized with a centered Laplace prior), and reconstructed neural activity. A key property is that once fitted on a small set of canonical interventions, the model can predict observation distributions for novel, unseen interventions that act on latents in already-separated blocks.

## Method

iSSM modifies the standard SSM dynamics equation to x_{t+1} = 1{Bu_t=0} ⊗ Ax_t + Bu_t + ε_t, so that when a latent node is perturbed, its dependence on all upstream latents is severed (columns of A corresponding to perturbed nodes are zeroed) and the intervention directly sets the latent value. The emission model is a generic nonlinear function f_θ mapping latents to observations, with a non-conjugate noise model (Gaussian or Poisson). Inference uses variational inference with an LSTM recognition network and the reparameterization trick, with the interventional structure applied directly to the approximate posterior during training to maintain causal consistency. The theoretical contribution establishes block identifiability of A and f_θ under bounded completeness of the observation model, piecewise-linear injective mixing, and a faithfulness assumption on latent dynamics; full identifiability up to permutation and coordinate-wise scaling follows when interventions satisfy the unordered pairs condition, enabling extrapolation to all unseen interventions.

## Results

Across all experiments, iSSM consistently outperforms the standard SSM baseline in both observation reconstruction accuracy and latent identifiability. In synthetic motor cortex simulations (RD and DA models), iSSM recovers the true latent variables and dynamics matrix A, while SSM cannot distinguish between the two models from observational data alone. On the mice ALM photostimulation dataset (179 neurons, 77 trials), iSSM achieves higher reconstruction accuracy than SSM across all tested latent dimensions on both training and test splits. On the macaque dlPFC micro-stimulation dataset (96 electrodes, 6 sessions), iSSM again outperforms SSM in reconstruction accuracy on both training and test sessions, and the consistency score of the inferred intervention matrix B across random initializations is substantially lower for iSSM, confirming better identifiability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard (observational) State Space Model (SSM), which models neural dynamics without incorporating interventional structure. iSSM wins on every metric across all four experimental settings: (1) In 2D synthetic RD/DA simulations, only iSSM recovers the true latents and distinguishes between the two dynamical models, with reconstruction correlation improving as more interventions are added (Fig. 2E). (2) In high-dimensional simulations (up to 50 observation dimensions), both models achieve high observation reconstruction, but only iSSM identifies the true 2D latents (Fig. 3C). (3) On the mice ALM dataset, iSSM achieves higher training and test reconstruction accuracy than SSM across all latent dimensions, and its inferred B matrix shows lower Euclidean distance across random initializations (better consistency/identifiability) (Fig. 4D,E). (4) On the macaque dlPFC dataset, iSSM outperforms SSM in reconstruction accuracy on both training and test sessions across all latent dimensions, with the gap widening at higher dimensions (Fig. 5E), and again shows superior consistency of B across initializations (Fig. 5F). The paper does not report a single headline numerical gap (e.g., 'iSSM achieves 0.92 vs. SSM 0.78 correlation'), as results are presented as curves in figures rather than a summary table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard state space models (SSMs) for neural dynamics by embedding them within Pearl's causal inference framework, where interventions are modeled as structural equation modifications (disconnecting intervened nodes from their parents) rather than the additive perturbations used in prior SSM work. The identifiability theory builds directly on Balsells-Rodas et al. (2023) for switching dynamical systems and on bounded completeness results from the econometrics literature, while the variational inference procedure adapts Archer et al. (2015) with an interventional correction to the approximate posterior. The key departure from prior causal representation learning is the move from static to temporal models with observation noise, and from full identifiability to block identifiability under a single intervention, with full identifiability recovered under the unordered pairs condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical identifiability guarantees for neural dynamics models fitted on interventional data, showing that causal perturbations can distinguish between dynamical models (e.g., rotational dynamics vs. dynamic attractor) that are provably indistinguishable from observational data alone. This opens a path toward causal hypothesis testing in neuroscience that goes beyond correlational SSM fitting. Key limitations include the restriction to linear latent dynamics, the assumption that interventions directly affect latents (not yet validated experimentally at single-neuron resolution), and reliance on approximate variational inference with LSTM recognition networks. Open questions include extending the theory to nonlinear dynamics, validating the latent-intervention assumption in vivo, and scaling to larger, more heterogeneous perturbation protocols.
