## Problem

Existing protein design methods approximate the joint distribution P(structure, seq) through marginal factorizations—either P(structure|seq)·P(seq) or P(backbone)·P(seq|backbone)—which fail to capture the interdependence between sequence and structure and cannot model explicit side-chain interactions. Prior all-atom generative models like ProtPardelle still require post-hoc sequence redesign and side-chain refinement, indicating that their learned representations do not adequately encode the structure-sequence relationship. The paper asks whether directly learning P(all-atom) can eliminate these marginalization steps and produce self-consistent all-atom proteins with correct sequences in a single generative pass.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian-noise 3D point cloud of shape N×14×3 as input, where the atom14 representation fixes every residue to exactly 14 atom slots (backbone atoms N, Cα, C, O, Cβ plus 9 side-chain positions), padding missing side-chain atoms with virtual coordinates at the Cα position to prevent sequence leakage. It outputs denoised all-atom coordinates (N×14×3) and a 20-class amino acid sequence (N×20 logits) from a single linear SeqHead applied to aggregated atomic embeddings. The model is trained on crops up to 128 residues but generalizes to sequences of 400+ residues, and sampling uses 200 Euler ODE steps with optional stochastic noise injection.

## Method

Pallatom is an EDM-based diffusion model that directly learns the score function over all-atom coordinates, using a dual-track transformer architecture with residue-level global attention and atomic-level local attention. The key architectural unit is the AtomDecoder, which fuses residue-level and atomic-level features through 'traversing' embeddings (stable skip connections that avoid the information redundancy identified in naive residual broadcasting), predicts coordinate updates, and performs a differentiable recycling step that feeds partially denoised distances back into pair embeddings via TriangleAttention. The atom14 representation resolves the variable-atom-count problem by normalizing all residues to 14 atoms with virtual atoms at Cα, and a SeqHead linearly decodes the 20 amino acid types from mean-pooled atomic embeddings. Training combines an aligned MSE loss (Kabsch-aligned), cross-entropy sequence loss, smooth LDDT, residue/atom-level distogram losses, and intermediate per-unit supervision with progressive weight decay, all under the EDM preconditioning framework.

## Results

On the CO-DESIGN 1 benchmark (all-atom co-generation), Pallatom achieves 85.03% designability (DES-aa), substantially outperforming Multiflow (62.74%), ProteinGenerator (43.14%), and ProtPardelle (30.00%). It also generates the highest structural diversity (291 clusters) and sequence diversity (1466 clusters), along with the most novel structures (NOV-str = 0.719, lower is better). On the PMPNN 1 backbone benchmark, Pallatom reaches 89.89% designability with 318 structural clusters, surpassing RFdiffusion (78.29%, 165 clusters) and Multiflow (84.69%, 184 clusters), though ProteinGenerator edges ahead on DES-bb(w) at 93.14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pallatom decisively outperforms all all-atom baselines on the CO-DESIGN 1 benchmark: it achieves 85.03% DES-aa versus 62.74% for Multiflow, 43.14% for ProteinGenerator, and 30.00% for ProtPardelle, while also generating 2–20× more structural and sequence diversity clusters. On novelty (lower TM-score to PDB), Pallatom (0.719) produces the most novel structures, beating RFdiffusion (0.805), ProteinGenerator (0.791), Multiflow (0.753), and ProtPardelle (0.747). In the PMPNN 1 backbone mode, Pallatom (89.89% DES-bb(w)) surpasses RFdiffusion (78.29%) and Multiflow (84.69%) but trails ProteinGenerator (93.14%), which benefits from a dedicated backbone-design training objective. Across all methods, Pallatom consistently achieves the highest structural diversity (318 clusters vs. 184 for Multiflow, 165 for RFdiffusion, 151 for ProteinGenerator), indicating that its all-atom formulation does not sacrifice exploration for accuracy. The paper also notes that ProtPardelle's DES-bb(wo) of 80.23% is heavily inflated relative to its pLDDT-constrained DES-bb(w) of 30.00%, highlighting the importance of quality constraints in designability assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pallatom builds directly on the EDM diffusion framework (Karras et al., 2022) for score-matching and on AlphaFold3's dual-track (residue-level + atom-level) architecture, adapting its AtomFeatureEncoder, TriangleAttention, and smooth LDDT loss for a generative rather than predictive setting. It departs from ProtPardelle (Chu et al., 2024), the prior all-atom protein generator, by replacing the atom73 superposition representation with a fixed-size atom14 virtual-atom scheme and by substituting ProtPardelle's simple U-ViT backbone with a multi-unit AtomDecoder featuring differentiable recycling. Relative to backbone-only generators (RFdiffusion, MultiFlow, CarbonNovo), Pallatom extends the generative target from Cα/CA traces to full side-chain coordinates, arguing that P(all-atom) subsumes both P(backbone) and P(seq|backbone) in a single joint distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 85% all-atom designability (vs. 30% for ProtPardelle) and the elimination of a separate ProteinMPNN redesign step make end-to-end all-atom protein design practically viable for enzyme engineering and binding-pocket design. The ablation showing that injecting explicit sequence information (hybrid14) *degrades* performance challenges the assumption that more conditioning is always beneficial, suggesting the all-atom coordinate space already carries sufficient sequential signal. Key limitations include restriction to monomeric proteins ≤128 residues during training, a lower novelty score than backbone-only competitors, and the near-complete absence of all-β structures; extending the framework to multichain complexes, small-molecule co-design, and wet-lab validation remain open.
