## Problem

Autoregressive mesh generation relies on coordinate-level tokenizers (e.g., MeshXL, MeshAnything V2, EdgeRunner), but there is no efficient theoretical metric to compare their quality without expensive training runs. Additionally, existing tokenizers leave redundancy in the serialized coordinate sequences that could be further compressed to allow more faces within a fixed context window.

## Input / Output

The model takes a point cloud of 8,192 sampled points as conditioning input (encoded by a frozen-then-finetuned encoder) and autoregressively generates a token sequence that decodes into a 3D triangle mesh. Coordinates are discretized at 7-bit resolution (128 levels per axis), and the serialized sequence is constrained to a 9,000-token context window, which directly limits the maximum number of faces the model can produce.

## Method

The paper introduces Per-Token-Mesh-Entropy (PTME = H_s/l × C_R), a training-free metric that quantifies sequence learnability by combining substring entropy, average token length, and compression ratio. Building on this, the Rearrange & Merge Coordinates (RMC) technique first reorders the coordinate sequence into 9-element groups (all x's, then all y's, then all z's per face) to align same-axis values, then applies BPE via SentencePiece to merge high-frequency coordinate patterns into composite tokens (vocabulary up to 8192). This is plug-and-play on top of existing tokenizers (RAW, AMT, EDR) and is modeled by a 24-layer autoregressive Transformer with cross-attention conditioning on the point cloud. The key insight is that rearrangement increases co-occurrence probabilities of adjacent coordinates, enabling BPE to achieve substantially lower PTME than naive merging (e.g., EDR+RMC reaches 21.2% compression ratio vs. 50.5% for EDR alone).

## Results

The best configuration, EDR+RMC, achieves a compression ratio of 0.212 (i.e., 21.2% of the original sequence length), a PTME of 2.231, a Hausdorff distance of 0.280, and a Chamfer distance of 0.123 on point-cloud-conditioned mesh generation. The RMC variant consistently outperforms both the baseline Merge Coordinates (MC) and the rearrangement-only (RAC) variant across all three base tokenizers (RAW, AMT, EDR). The paper further reports a strong Pearson correlation (r=0.965, p=0.0004) between PTME and Chamfer Distance for EDR+RMC under varying vocabulary sizes, validating PTME as a training-free proxy for generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAW (MeshXL), EDR+RMC reduces the compression ratio from 1.000 to 0.212, lowers PTME from 6.742 to 2.231, and improves Chamfer distance from 0.326 to 0.123 (a 62% reduction). Against AMT (MeshAnything V2), EDR+RMC halves the compression ratio (0.495→0.212), reduces PTME by 33% (3.349→2.231), and cuts Chamfer distance by 44% (0.219→0.123). Against EDR (EdgeRunner), the RMC enhancement achieves a 58% reduction in compression ratio (0.505→0.212), a 29% PTME improvement (3.139→2.231), and a 38% Chamfer distance improvement (0.198→0.123). Notably, the naive Merge Coordinates (MC) baseline actually *worsens* PTME relative to the unmodified tokenizer (e.g., RAW+MC: 6.943 vs. RAW: 6.742), confirming that BPE's cross-axis limitation makes direct merging counterproductive without the rearrangement step. The rearrangement-only variant (RAC) has negligible impact on all metrics, isolating the gain to the merging step enabled by the rearrangement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the autoregressive coordinate-level mesh generation paradigm, building directly on three tokenizer designs: MeshXL's raw coordinate serialization (RAW), MeshAnything V2's adjacent mesh tokenization (AMT), and EdgeRunner's half-edge directional representation (EDR). Its conceptual departure is twofold: it imports the BPE/SentencePiece subword tokenization machinery from NLP into the 3D mesh domain, and it introduces a Shannon-entropy-based metric (PTME) that decouples tokenizer evaluation from the expensive training loop that has been the only practical assessment method. The paper explicitly positions itself as a plug-and-play layer on top of existing tokenizers rather than a new generation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PTME offers a training-free, theoretically grounded way to compare mesh tokenizers, which could accelerate tokenizer design iteration in the autoregressive mesh generation community. The 21.2% compression ratio achieved by EDR+RMC is practically significant, directly translating to more faces within a fixed context window. However, the evaluation is confined to 7-bit (128-level) vertex quantization; the authors themselves note that at industry-standard 1024-level quantization, the frequency of repeated coordinate patterns drops sharply, likely diminishing the merging benefit. Open questions include whether dynamic or adaptive merging strategies (analogous to byte-level latent approaches) can close this gap, and whether PTME generalizes to non-coordinate tokenizations such as VQ-VAE-based schemes.
