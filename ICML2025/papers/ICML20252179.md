## Problem

Distributionally robust Markov games (RMGs) address sim-to-real gaps in multi-agent reinforcement learning, but two critical challenges remain open: (1) existing uncertainty sets use (s,a)-rectangularity that models other agents' uncertainty separately per joint action, contradicting behavioral economics evidence that humans apply a single risk metric to the integrated expected outcome of others; and (2) all prior sample-efficient algorithms for RMGs suffer from the curse of multiagency, with sample complexity scaling exponentially in the number of agents via the product of action spaces.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a generative model (simulator) that can draw i.i.d. samples from the nominal transition kernel P^0 for any (s, a) pair, along with per-agent uncertainty radii σ_i ∈ (0,1] and the full game structure (state space S, per-agent action spaces A_i, horizon H, reward functions r). The output is not a single joint policy but a distribution ξ over joint product policies (one per time step), constituting an ε-approximate robust CCE where no agent can improve its worst-case value by more than ε through unilateral deviation. The generative model only provides access to the nominal environment, not the worst-case kernels within the uncertainty sets.

## Method

The paper introduces fictitious uncertainty sets satisfying an others-integrated (s,a_i)-rectangularity condition: each agent's uncertainty set decomposes into independent balls (under TV distance) around the expected nominal transition P_{h,s,a_i}^{π_{-i}} conditioned on other agents' current policies, rather than around the nominal kernel for each joint action. The Robust-Q-FTRL algorithm performs backward induction over time steps, running K iterations of online learning at each step: it constructs empirical models via N-sample estimation over (s,a_i) pairs, estimates robust Q-functions by solving Iyengar's dual (a one-dimensional maximization over a clipped value function), updates Q-estimates with rescaled linear learning rates, and applies FTRL to update policies. The key technical challenge is that the robust value function is highly nonlinear in the transition kernel (unlike the linear payoffs in standard MGs), so the authors design a variance-style optimistic bonus term through non-trivial decomposition of auxiliary statistical errors, yielding a sample complexity of Õ(SH^6 ΣA_i / ε^4 · min{H, 1/min σ_i}) that is linear in the sum of action spaces rather than their product.

## Results

The paper's main result is a sample complexity bound for learning an ε-approximate robust CCE in fictitious RMGs. Robust-Q-FTRL requires Õ(SH⁶ Σᵢ Aᵢ / ε⁴ · min{H, 1/min σᵢ}) samples, which scales linearly in the sum of individual action spaces (ΣAᵢ) rather than exponentially in the product (∏Aᵢ). This is the first algorithm to break the curse of multiagency for RMGs regardless of the uncertainty set formulation. A matching (up to polynomial factors) information-theoretic lower bound of Ω(SH³ maxᵢ Aᵢ / ε² · min{H, 1/min σᵢ}) is also established.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior RMG algorithms, both of which suffer from the curse of multiagency. P2MPO (Blanchet et al., 2024) has sample complexity scaling as (∏Aᵢ)³, making it intractable even for moderate numbers of agents; Robust-Q-FTRL replaces this with a linear ΣAᵢ dependency, a dramatic improvement. DR-NVI (Shi et al., 2024b) achieves SH³∏Aᵢ/ε² · min{H, 1/min σᵢ}, still exponential in the number of agents when action spaces are equal-sized; the proposed method's ΣAᵢ scaling breaks this exponential barrier. The trade-off is a slightly worse ε-dependency (ε⁻⁴ vs. ε⁻²) and a higher H-dependency (H⁶ vs. H³), but the multi-agent scaling improvement is the central contribution. The paper also notes that prior methods impose restrictive conditions on σᵢ (e.g., Ma et al., 2023 requires σᵢ ≤ max{ε/(SH²), p_min/H}), which can fail to maintain desired robustness, whereas the proposed approach handles σᵢ ∈ (0,1] without such restrictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Q-FTRL adaptive sampling framework for standard Markov games (Li et al., 2022) and the distributionally robust RL literature (Iyengar, 2005; Shi et al., 2024b), but departs from prior RMG work by replacing the (s,a)-rectangularity uncertainty sets used by Shi et al. (2024b) and Blanchet et al. (2023) with a new 'fictitious' others-integrated (s,a_i)-rectangularity condition inspired by behavioral economics. The key conceptual departure is that each agent's uncertainty reference point now integrates other agents' expected behavior into the nominal transition kernel, rather than treating each joint action's transition independently. This structural change, combined with a tailored variance-style bonus for the nonlinear robust value function, is what enables the first polynomial-in-all-parameters sample complexity for RMGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to break the curse of multiagency in robust MARL regardless of uncertainty set formulation, showing that the exponential dependence on the joint action space (∏A_i) in all prior RMG algorithms is not fundamental but an artifact of the (s,a)-rectangularity structure. The behavioral-economics motivation for the fictitious uncertainty set makes the formulation more realistic for human-in-the-loop multi-agent systems. Limitations include the requirement of a generative model, restriction to TV distance and finite-horizon tabular settings, and the ε⁴ dependence (vs. ε² in standard MGs) reflecting the added difficulty of robustness. Open questions include extending to other divergences (KL, Wasserstein), non-generative model access, and function approximation settings.
