## Problem

Adversarial text prompts against diffusion models are detectable by simple text filters (perplexity, keyword, LLM, embedding), reducing attack success rates by 58–96%. However, the image conditioning input in Image-to-Image (I2I) diffusion models has been largely unexplored as an attack surface. This paper asks whether rejecting adversarial prompts truly ensures safety, and answers negatively by demonstrating that adversarial perturbations on the input image alone can induce NSFW generation while leaving the text prompt completely benign and undetectable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image and a benign text prompt as input and produces a single adversarial image that is visually near-identical to the original (constrained by an L_p noise bound ε, tested at 32/255, 64/255, and 128/255). At inference, this adversarial image is fed into an I2I diffusion model (e.g., InstructPix2Pix or SDv1.5-Inpainting) alongside the unchanged benign prompt, causing the model to generate NSFW content. The generator is trained once on a dataset of 1,800 image-text pairs and then generalizes to unseen images and prompts without per-image optimization.

## Method

AdvI2I first extracts an NSFW concept vector c by averaging the difference between CLIP text embeddings of contrastive prompt pairs (NSFW vs. non-NSFW), then shifts any benign prompt embedding by α·c to form a target NSFW embedding. A pre-trained VAE is repurposed as an adversarial image generator g_ψ, trained so that the latent feature at the final diffusion timestep (t=1) produced by the adversarial image with the benign prompt matches the latent feature produced by the original image with the NSFW-shifted prompt, subject to a clipping constraint keeping the output within ε of the input. The key insight is that the NSFW concept is transferred from the text embedding space into pixel-space perturbations, making the attack invisible to text-based filters. AdvI2I-Adaptive further adds a safety-checker loss that minimizes cosine similarity between the VAE-decoded output and predefined NSFW concept vectors, plus Gaussian noise injection during training, enabling the attack to bypass both post-hoc safety checkers and pre-processing Gaussian-noise defenses while maintaining ASRs above 70%.

## Results

On InstructPix2Pix, AdvI2I achieves 81.5% ASR for nudity and 80.0% for violence without defense, substantially outperforming the MMA baseline (68.5% and 71.5% respectively). Under the Safety Checker (SC) defense, AdvI2I's ASR drops to 18.0% (nudity) and 32.5% (violence), but AdvI2I-Adaptive recovers to 70.5% for both concepts. On SDv1.5-Inpainting, AdvI2I reaches 82.5% (nudity) and 81.0% (violence) without defense, with AdvI2I-Adaptive maintaining 72.0% and 71.5% ASR under SC. The attack generalizes to unseen images (≥63.5% ASR) and unseen prompts (≥68.5% ASR), and transfers to SDv2.0/SDv2.1 with ASRs of 80.5% and 84.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MMA-Diffusion (Yang et al., 2024a), a multimodal attack that combines adversarial text prompts with image perturbations to evade safety checkers. AdvI2I outperforms MMA by 13.0% (nudity) and 8.5% (violence) in ASR on InstructPix2Pix without defense, and by 40.5% (nudity) and 33.5% (violence) on SDv1.5-Inpainting, demonstrating that image-only adversarial perturbations are more effective than the combined text+image approach. However, under the Safety Checker defense, MMA retains higher ASR than vanilla AdvI2I (e.g., 64.5% vs. 18.0% for nudity on InstructPix2Pix), since MMA was explicitly designed to evade post-hoc safety filters. AdvI2I-Adaptive closes this gap, surpassing MMA under SC by 6.0% (nudity) and 5.0% (violence) on InstructPix2Pix, and by 32.5% (nudity) and 25.5% (violence) on SDv1.5-Inpainting. The Attack VAE baseline, which only exploits the VAE encoder-decoder without the full diffusion process, achieves ASRs below 23%, confirming that the diffusion denoising process is critical for effective adversarial image attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on diffusion models from the text-prompt channel to the image-conditioning channel in I2I models. It builds on concept extraction via contrastive prompt pairs (Tsai et al., 2023) to obtain an NSFW direction in embedding space, then repurposes this by training a VAE-based generator to transfer that concept into pixel-space perturbations. Unlike prior adversarial prompt attacks (QF-Attack, Ring-A-Bell, MMA-Diffusion) that are detectable by text filters, AdvI2I operates entirely through the image input, making it orthogonal to all text-based defenses. The adversarial image generator design draws from targeted perturbation generation (Naseer et al., 2021) but substitutes a pre-trained VAE for U-Net/ResNet to preserve visual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the entire suite of current I2I safety mechanisms—Safe Latent Diffusion, negative prompts, post-hoc safety checkers, and Gaussian noise preprocessing—can be simultaneously bypassed by a single adaptive adversarial image generator, maintaining ~70% ASR. This exposes a structural blind spot: defenses designed around the text modality are irrelevant when the attack vector is the image. The SDv3.0 result (34% ASR vs. 82% on SDv1.5) suggests that thorough training-data curation may be the most effective long-term defense, but this is expensive and imperfect. Open questions include whether image-side defenses analogous to text filters exist, whether the attack generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the defensive application (embedding benign concepts to suppress harmful generation) can be made practical.
