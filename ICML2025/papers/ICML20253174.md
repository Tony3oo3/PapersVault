## Problem

Existing computational methods for cyclic peptide design are limited to a single cyclization type (e.g., head-to-tail or disulfide-linked) and rely on residue-level representations that cannot naturally encode the covalent bonds and non-canonical residues required for cyclization. Additionally, the scarcity of 3D structural data for protein–cyclic peptide complexes makes it difficult to train generative models directly on target data. This paper addresses the need for a unified, structure-based generative framework that can produce all four types of cyclic peptides (head-to-tail, head-to-side, side-to-tail, side-to-side) from a 3D receptor structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a 3D receptor structure, a 2D cyclization chemical graph (atoms as nodes, bonds as edges, with no 3D coordinates), the desired number of residues, and the cyclization type. It outputs the full all-atom 3D coordinates of the cyclic peptide, its amino acid sequence, and the complete peptide chemical graph. A key detail is that the cyclization graph provides only topological connectivity, so the model must infer the 3D geometry of the cyclic closure; free-residue side chains are represented in the Atom73 format (a superposition over all 20 canonical amino acids) while cyclization-constrained atoms are tracked in a separate state.

## Method

CPSDE comprises two models trained jointly via a routed sampling loop. ATOMSDE is a score-based generative model built on a harmonic SDE whose forward process uses the Laplacian of the ligand's chemical graph to define anisotropic, bond-aware noise—bonded atoms receive correlated perturbations rather than independent Gaussian noise—ensuring geometric constraints are respected throughout denoising. The denoiser is an SE(3)-equivariant network that processes both a k-nearest-neighbor graph over the protein–ligand complex and the ligand chemical graph, trained with a reconstruction loss on protein–ligand docking data (small molecules and peptides). RESROUTER, an SE(3)-equivariant classifier with the same backbone, predicts residue types from the denoised structure after stripping free-residue side chains from the input graph to prevent label leakage. During inference, routed sampling alternates between ATOMSDE (denoising coordinates) and RESROUTER (updating residue types) at each SDE step, reassembling the chemical graph after each sequence update and using an Atom73 superposition state with time-alignment to handle the fact that different side-chain atoms are sampled at different steps.

## Results

On 100 protein targets, CPSDE (Mix) achieves the best Stability (−580.67 avg, −527.80 med) and Affinity (−55.71 avg, −48.42 med) among all co-design methods, while also attaining the highest Diversity (0.79). RFDiffusion, a non-co-design method, shows the strongest absolute energy scores (Stability −633.51, Affinity −70.30) but the lowest Diversity (0.55). Among the four cyclization types, head-to-tail (Stability −568.04, Affinity −50.86) and head-to-side (−564.81, −49.81) outperform side-to-tail and side-to-side, likely reflecting the greater prevalence of C–N bonds in training data. MD simulations confirm that the top designed cyclic peptide H2T-6 achieves a binding free energy of −24.02 kcal/mol (MM-PBSA), surpassing the ground-truth linear peptide (−19.00) and PepFlow's output (−7.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPSDE (Mix) outperforms all three co-design baselines on both Stability and Affinity: it beats ProteinGenerator (−580.67 vs −576.39 Stability; −55.71 vs −46.98 Affinity), PepFlow (−580.67 vs −576.16; −55.71 vs −47.88), and PepGLAD (−580.67 vs −359.44; −55.71 vs −45.06), while also achieving the highest Diversity (0.79) tied with PepGLAD. Against RFDiffusion, CPSDE trades some absolute energy performance (Stability −580.67 vs −633.51; Affinity −55.71 vs −70.30) for substantially higher Diversity (0.79 vs 0.55), which the authors attribute to RFDiffusion's tendency to generate repetitive α-helical patterns. The ablation in Appendix H.5 further shows that removing the harmonic SDE degrades both Stability (−534.38 vs −568.04) and Affinity (−39.43 vs −50.86), confirming the contribution of the graph-aware anisotropic noise schedule. In the SMYD2 case study, the top CPSDE cyclic peptide H2T-6 achieves a MM-PBSA binding free energy of −24.02 kcal/mol, outperforming both the ground-truth linear peptide (−19.00) and PepFlow's linear peptide (−7.26), with a lower RMSD (3.05 Å) than PepFlow (4.59 Å) over 100 ns MD simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPSDE builds directly on Protpardelle's (Chu et al., 2024) Atom73 representation and superposition-based routed sampling for all-atom protein design, extending both to the cyclic peptide setting by adding explicit bond modeling and a harmonic SDE whose anisotropic noise is governed by the chemical graph Laplacian. The structure-denoising component (ATOMSDE) draws on the generative docking lineage (DiffDock, EquiBind) and the harmonic prior idea from Jing et al. (2023) and Stark et al. (2023), while the residue-type predictor (RESROUTER) follows the inverse-folding paradigm of ProteinMPNN and PiFold. The key departure from prior peptide design methods (RFDiffusion, PepFlow, PepGLAD) is the shift from residue-frame representations to all-atom-and-bond modeling, which is what makes cyclization constraints (disulfide bonds, thioester bonds, non-canonical residues) tractable within a single generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPSDE is the first generative method to handle all four cyclization types (head-to-tail, head-to-side, side-to-tail, side-to-side) from 3D target structures, addressing a gap that residue-level methods cannot fill due to their inability to represent covalent cyclization bonds. The MD simulation case studies (SMYD2, SET8) showing designed cyclic peptides outperforming the natural linear reference in both stability and binding affinity provide encouraging but preliminary evidence of practical drug-discovery utility. Open questions include wet-lab validation, scalability to polycyclized or very long peptides, and whether the current 100-target evaluation generalizes to structurally diverse binding sites. The method's reliance on a pretrained docking model and a separate sequence predictor also raises questions about end-to-end differentiability and whether joint training could further improve quality.
