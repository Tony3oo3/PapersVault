## Problem

Existing SNN-based Transformers focus on single-image spatial tasks and fail to exploit SNNs' inherent temporal encoding for video-based vision tasks. Additionally, the dot-product attention score used in prior spike-driven Transformers is theoretically ill-suited for binary spike vectors, as it indiscriminately ignores zero elements in the query, producing identical scores for structurally different keys.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253251/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips of shape T×H×W×3 (e.g., 32 frames at 224×224 for classification, 8 or 32 frames at 256×256 for pose tracking) or event streams split into 3T temporal segments converted to binary event frames. Outputs are task-specific: a single class label for video classification, SMPL body parameters (shape β∈R^{T×10}, pose θ∈R^{T×72}, translation d∈R^{T×3}) for pose tracking, and per-pixel semantic category maps for video segmentation. All inputs undergo temporal spiking over T LIF time steps before entering the network.

## Method

SpikeVideoFormer is a Conv+ViT architecture where spike-driven CNN blocks (separable convolutions with LIF neurons) extract downsampled spatial features, followed by spike-driven spatiotemporal Transformer blocks using a novel Spike-Driven Hamming Attention (SDHA). SDHA replaces the dot-product score with normalized Hamming similarity, justified by a JL Lemma on Binary Embedding showing that for sufficiently large channel dimension D, Hamming similarity approximates a monotone transform of cosine similarity with high probability. The computation is made efficient by mapping {0,1}→{−1,1} via bit-shifts, absorbing the 1/(2D) scaling into the neuron threshold, and rearranging the matrix product to O(ND²) per attention layer. For space-time modeling, the paper adopts joint attention (reshaping T×N tokens into TN and applying SDHA), which achieves O(TND²) complexity—linear in both temporal and spatial length—outperforming hierarchical and factorized alternatives empirically. The model is pre-trained on ImageNet-1K with T=1 then fine-tuned with T=4, and transferred to downstream video tasks.

## Results

On Kinetics-400 video classification, SpikeVideoFormer (55.9M params) achieves 79.8% top-1 and 94.0% top-5 accuracy, surpassing the prior SNN state-of-the-art Meta-SpikeFormer by 4.3% and matching Swin-B (80.6%) within 0.8% while delivering ×3 efficiency. On MMHPSD human pose tracking (T=8, 55.8M), it achieves 39.8 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming all SNN baselines by >4 mm and nearly matching the ANN GLoT (39.9 mm) at ×14 lower power. On CityScapes video semantic segmentation (17.8M), it reaches 73.1% mIoU with Integer-LIF=4, exceeding SpikFormer (65.0%) and Meta-SpikeFormer (65.9%) by over 7%, while on VSPW it achieves 37.9% mIoU, surpassing all prior SNN methods by >5.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SNN baselines, SpikeVideoFormer consistently wins: it outperforms Meta-SpikeFormer by 4.3% top-1 on Kinetics-400, by 4.4–8.3 mm PA-MPJPE on MMHPSD pose tracking, and by 7.2–7.8% mIoU on CityScapes VSS. Against ANN baselines, it matches or exceeds them on most tasks: it beats I3D-101 by 2.1% on Kinetics-400, nearly matches GLoT (39.8 vs 39.9 mm PA-MPJPE) on pose tracking at ×14 lower power, and surpasses FCN and PSPNet on CityScapes VSS by 11.6% and 2.9% mIoU respectively. It trails the strongest ANN methods slightly—Swin-B and ViViT by 0.8% on Kinetics-400, SegFormer and CFFM by 1–2% on CityScapes VSPW—but delivers ×3 to ×16 efficiency gains. On ImageNet-1K pre-training, SpikeVideoFormer (79.9%) surpasses ViT (79.7%), marking the first SNN to exceed a Transformer-based ANN on this benchmark, while remaining below RSB (81.8%). On ImageNet-C robustness, it performs comparably to VOneResNet50, showing competitive resilience under corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spike-driven Transformer lineage (SpikFormer, Meta-SpikeFormer) from single-image spatial attention to video-based space-time attention, adapting the three attention decompositions (joint, hierarchical, factorized) originally proposed for ANN video Transformers (ViViT). The key conceptual departure is replacing the dot-product attention score with normalized Hamming similarity, justified theoretically via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma on binary embeddings, which provides the first principled adaptation from real-valued cosine similarity to binary spike-driven similarity. The Conv+ViT backbone architecture and LIF neuron model are inherited from prior spike-driven Transformer work, while the joint space-time attention design and O(T) temporal complexity are novel contributions specific to this paper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first spike-driven video Transformer with linear temporal complexity, opening a path toward energy-efficient video processing on neuromorphic hardware where SNNs' addition-only computation provides 5–16× efficiency gains over ANNs while matching accuracy. The theoretical grounding of Hamming attention via the JL lemma resolves a previously ad-hoc design choice (empirical threshold scaling) and clarifies why dot-product attention is fundamentally ill-suited for binary spike vectors. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos and higher resolutions, whether it extends to generative video tasks (the authors mention this as future work), and whether the joint-attention superiority observed here holds under different spiking rates or neuron models. The >15% gap over prior SNNs on pose tracking and segmentation suggests that the attention score function, not just architecture depth, was the bottleneck for SNNs on video tasks.
