## Problem

Current positional encoding methods for temporal link prediction are limited in three ways: they rely on fixed, pre-defined functions that cannot adapt to complex attributed graphs; the few learnable approaches (e.g., Dwivedi et al., 2022) only capture static structural information, ignoring time-evolving topology and features; and they require transformer attention mechanisms with O(n²) or O(n³) complexity to fully exploit the encoding, making them unaffordable on large-scale temporal graphs.

## Input / Output

L-STEP consumes a temporal graph snapshot sequence with node features (d_N-dim), edge features (d_E-dim), and a query (node pair, timestamp). For each node it retrieves only L most recent positional encodings, K most recent interactions, and 1-hop neighbors within a time window t_gap, avoiding any all-pairs computation. The output is a scalar probability in (0,1) indicating whether the queried link exists at the given timestamp, produced by a 2-layer MLP over concatenated node representations.

## Method

L-STEP's core innovation is the Learnable Positional Encoding (LPE) module, which approximates the current Laplacian-based positional encoding by applying a Discrete Fourier Transform to the sequence of L past positional encodings, filtering in the frequency domain with a complex-valued learnable matrix W_filter, and applying inverse DFT followed by weighted sum-pooling. After prediction, the approximate encoding is refined via an MLP that incorporates neighbor positional encodings and time encodings, then stored for future retrieval—eliminating the need to recompute Laplacian eigendecomposition (O(|V|³)) at each timestamp. The Node-Link-Positional Encoder aggregates node features, edge features with time encodings, and neighbor positional encodings through simple 1-layer MLPs (no attention), and a final 2-layer MLP link predictor outputs the probability. A positional encoding loss (Eq. 12) additionally constrains linked nodes to have similar encodings while pushing unlinked nodes apart. Theoretically, the paper proves that under a 'slowly changing' graph assumption, the DFT-based approximation error is bounded by a term depending on L and the filter eigenvalues, and shows the overall complexity scales as O(n(t_gap + K + L log L))—linear in nodes—versus O(|E|) for attention-based SOTAs like DyGFormer and FreeDyG.

## Results

In the transductive setting with random negative sampling, L-STEP achieves an average rank of 1.85 (AP) and 1.69 (ROC-AUC) across 13 datasets, outperforming all 9 baselines. The most dramatic gains appear on UN Vote (73.40 AP vs. 65.72 for the next-best TGN) and UN Trade (75.84 AP vs. 66.46 for DyGFormer), where L-STEP leads by 8+ points. On the large-scale TGB benchmark, L-STEP ranks 2nd on tgbl-review (Test MRR 0.411) and 3rd on tgbl-coin (Test MRR 0.711), confirming scalability to graphs with 350K–640K nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-STEP outperforms all 9 baselines on 11 out of 13 datasets in AP (transductive, random NSS), with the largest margins on UN Vote (+7.7 over TGN), UN Trade (+9.4 over DyGFormer), and Can. Parl. (+0.9 over DyGFormer). The two closest competitors are DyGFormer (avg. rank 2.77) and FreeDyG (avg. rank 3.85); L-STEP trails FreeDyG only on MOOC (86.94 vs. 89.61) and Social Evo. (92.22 vs. 94.91), and is marginally behind on Reddit (99.37 vs. 99.48). In the inductive setting (Table 5), L-STEP maintains its lead with avg. rank 2.08 (AP) and 1.54 (ROC-AUC), again trailing only on Reddit and Social Evo. On the TGB large-scale benchmark, L-STEP ranks 2nd on tgbl-review (0.411 MRR vs. GraphMixer's 0.521) and 3rd on tgbl-coin (0.711 vs. TNCN's 0.762), showing that the efficiency gains of avoiding dense attention come at a modest accuracy cost on very large graphs. Against PINT (Souza et al., 2022), L-STEP wins on both Enron and UCI in both transductive and inductive settings (e.g., Enron transductive AP: 93.96 vs. 81.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learnable positional encoding concept introduced by Dwivedi et al. (ICLR 2022) from static structural information to time-evolving topological and feature information, using Discrete Fourier Transform as the mechanism for temporal evolution. It departs from the dominant paradigm in temporal link prediction (DyGFormer, FreeDyG, TGN, TGAT) that relies on attention mechanisms or complex recurrent architectures, instead showing that a simple MLP-based model with well-designed learnable positional encoding can match or exceed transformer-based SOTA. The Node-Link-Positional Encoder integrates node features, edge features, and evolving positional encodings through lightweight MLP transformations, avoiding the O(n²) dense attention cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that temporal link prediction at scale no longer requires expensive attention mechanisms; L-STEP's linear-in-nodes complexity makes it viable for large-scale temporal graphs where transformer-based methods become prohibitive. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that the DFT-based positional encoding approximates future snapshots under a 'slowly changing' assumption provides a principled foundation, though this assumption limits applicability to rapidly evolving graphs. Open questions include whether the 1-hop neighborhood restriction can be relaxed, how the method behaves under distribution shift, and whether the learnable filter can be made more interpretable. The strong and consistent gains across 13 diverse datasets and the TGB large-scale benchmark suggest broad practical relevance.
