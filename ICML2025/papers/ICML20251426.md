## Problem

Li et al. (NeurIPS 2023) introduced the group maximin share (GMMS) fair allocation problem for scheduling jobs among groups of machines and proved a 2-approximation for identical machines within groups, but left the general unrelated-machines (heterogeneous) case as an open problem. This paper resolves that open problem and further improves the homogeneous-case approximation from 2 to 4/3.

## Input / Output

The input consists of m indivisible jobs and n groups, where group G_i contains g_i machines each with an additive cost (processing time) function over jobs; the group structure is fixed and groups are disjoint. The output is a two-level allocation: first, a partition of all jobs into n bundles (one per group), and second, within each group, an assignment of its bundle to the g_i machines. The fairness guarantee is that every machine's load is at most α times its group's GMMS value, where the group cost of a bundle is the minimum makespan achievable by scheduling those jobs on the group's machines.

## Method

For the heterogeneous setting, the algorithm generalizes the LP framework of Lenstra et al. (1990) to multiple groups: it solves a linear program to obtain a fractional allocation where each agent's cost is bounded by the group's GMMS, then eliminates 'share cycles' in an auxiliary multi-graph (where edges represent shared fractional items) via a cost-preserving item-exchange procedure, and finally rounds by iteratively selecting agents with at most one fractional item, yielding a 2-GMMS integral allocation. A matching hard instance proves no algorithm can achieve better than (2−1/n)-GMMS. For the homogeneous setting, the algorithm exploits the ordered-instance reduction and a greedy two-phase bag-filling procedure: items are classified as large (>2/3 GMMS), medium (1/3–2/3 GMMS), or small (<1/3 GMMS) per group, large/medium items are distributed round-robin across groups, and small items fill remaining capacity, guaranteeing each machine's load is at most 4/3 GMMS.

## Results

In the heterogeneous (unrelated machines) setting, the paper proves a 2-GMMS fair allocation can be computed in polynomial time via LP rounding, and shows this is asymptotically tight: no algorithm can achieve better than (2 − 1/n)-GMMS for n groups. In the homogeneous (identical machines) setting, the approximation ratio is improved from 2 (Li et al., 2023) to 4/3. Additionally, the paper shows that exact GMMS allocations do not exist for any n ≥ 3, even when each group contains a single agent (reducing to standard chore allocation with additive costs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Li et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who introduced the GMMS problem and proved a 2-approximation for the homogeneous (identical machines) setting. This paper improves that bound to 4/3, a significant tightening. For the heterogeneous (unrelated machines) setting, which was left as an open problem by Li et al., this paper provides the first polynomial-time algorithm achieving a 2-approximation, closing the gap. The 2-approximation is shown to be asymptotically optimal via a hard instance with lower bound (2 − 1/n). The LP-based fractional allocation technique generalizes the classical Lenstra et al. (1990) approach for unrelated machine scheduling to the multi-group setting, while the polynomial-time computation of GMMS values relies on techniques from Barman & Krishnamurthy (2020). No empirical baselines are compared, as the contributions are entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves an open problem left by Li et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who introduced the group maximin share (GMMS) fair allocation problem and proved a 2-approximation for identical machines but left the unrelated-machines case unresolved. The algorithmic core generalizes the LP rounding framework of Lenstra et al. (1990) for scheduling unrelated parallel machines to a multi-group setting, introducing an auxiliary graph with 'share cycles' whose elimination yields the integral allocation. For the homogeneous case, the paper improves upon Li et al.'s 2-approximation to 4/3 using a structured bag-based greedy algorithm on ordered instances, building on the ordered-instance reduction technique from Barman & Krishnamurthy (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymptotically tight 2-approximation (matching the 2−1/n lower bound) settles the heterogeneous GMMS problem and identifies a natural class of subadditive valuations—between additive and general subadditive—for which constant-approximation MMS algorithms exist. The 4/3 improvement for homogeneous machines is a meaningful tightening, though the optimal ratio in that setting remains open. Key open questions include whether related machines are strictly harder than identical ones, whether the heterogeneous bound can be improved for small n, and whether the homogeneous case with multiple agents per group differs fundamentally from the single-agent-per-group case.
