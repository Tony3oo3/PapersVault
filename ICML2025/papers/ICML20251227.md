## Problem

Feature-mapping topology optimization (FMTO) produces manufacturable designs by parameterizing voids with predefined geometric features, but existing approaches are severely limited in the diversity of feature types they support, the range of parameters each feature can express, and the requirement for analytically differentiable signed distance functions. In particular, current methods cannot represent closed chains of linear and arc segments connected tangentially (as engineers naturally create in CAD), cannot compute gradients at segment contacts, and lack the ability to seamlessly morph one feature type into another.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D FEM-discretized design domain with specified boundary conditions, external loads, and a volume fraction target, along with an initial set of geometric features parameterized by 15-element shape codes (one-hot feature-type label plus type-specific geometric parameters such as semi-axis ratios, vertex coordinates, and corner radii). It outputs an optimized pseudo-density field over the mesh, the updated high-level feature parameters (scale, offset, rotation angle, and latent vector per feature), and a manufacturable void topology composed of smooth closed contours. A key structural detail is that the volume constraint is folded into the objective as a soft penalty (active only above threshold) rather than a hard constraint, and all design variables are optimized in unconstrained logit space via sigmoid reparameterization.

## Method

The core architecture is a variational autoencoder with a shape encoder (fully-connected layers with batch norm and LeakyReLU), a Heaviside decoder using the DeepSDF decoder architecture, and a separate reconstruction decoder. A 15-element shape code encodes the feature type (ellipse, triangle, quadrangle) via a one-hot segment plus type-specific geometric parameters; the encoder maps this to a shared latent vector z_m that unifies all feature types in a single space. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and true sigmoid-approximated Heaviside values plus KL divergence regularization, with the reconstruction decoder trained separately on shape-code reconstruction. A critical design choice is training the latent representation through the Heaviside decoder (rather than the reconstruction decoder), which shapes the latent space around geometric contours rather than abstract parameter values, enabling smooth morphing between feature types. During topology optimization, the pretrained Heaviside decoder is frozen and used to evaluate pseudo-densities at element centroids; gradients flow through the network via adjoint differentiation, a Kreisselmeier-Steinhauser function provides a smooth maximum over overlapping features, and a refactoring mechanism (re-encoding z_m through the reconstruction-then-encoder pipeline every 5 iterations) prevents the latent vector from drifting outside the valid manifold.

## Results

On six classical 2D elasticity topology optimization tasks, the proposed Neural Heaviside SDF (NHSDF) method achieves the best compliance among all feature-mapping topology optimization (FMTO) methods while using less material (lower volume fraction). For example, on Task 1 (cantilever beam), NHSDF reaches a compliance of 3.91×10⁻⁵ at vf=0.255, compared to 4.39×10⁻⁵ (Ellipses) and 5.85×10⁻⁵ (TreeTOp), approaching the free-form SIMP lower bound of 3.78×10⁻⁵. On the square-domain tasks (Tasks 5 and 6), NHSDF outperforms both TopoDiff (1.55 vs. 1.70 and 2.34 vs. 2.44) and NTopo (1.55 vs. 1.60 and 2.34 vs. 2.31), while matching NTopo exactly on Task 3 (1.63×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among FMTO baselines, NHSDF consistently outperforms both Ellipses and TreeTOp on every task where all three are available: e.g., on Task 1, NHSDF (3.91) beats Ellipses (4.39) by ~11% and TreeTOp (5.85) by ~33%; on Task 3, NHSDF (1.63) beats Ellipses (1.74) by ~6% and TreeTOp (3.73) by ~56%. Against free-form neural methods, NHSDF ties NTopo on Task 3 (both 1.63×10⁻³) and outperforms it on Tasks 5 and 6 (1.55 vs. 1.60; 2.34 vs. 2.31). NHSDF also outperforms TopoDiff on both square-domain tasks (1.55 vs. 1.70 on Task 5; 2.34 vs. 2.44 on Task 6). The SIMP method remains the lower bound that no FMTO method can match, as free-form approaches can create arbitrarily thin features; however, NHSDF comes closest to SIMP among all FMTO methods, particularly on Task 1 (3.91 vs. 3.78, a gap of only ~3.4%). Notably, NHSDF achieves these results while using less material (lower volume fraction) than competing FMTO methods in most cases, e.g., vf=0.255 vs. 0.297 for Ellipses on Task 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends feature-mapping topology optimization (FMTO) by replacing explicit, hand-crafted SDF parameterizations (ellipses, B-splines, half-spaces) with a variational autoencoder that learns a unified latent space for diverse geometric features. The architecture directly repurposes the DeepSDF decoder (Park et al., 2019) as the Heaviside function approximator and the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2022) to enable smooth transitions between feature types, building on the earlier observation that neural networks can approximate Heaviside functions in level-set topology optimization (Deng & To, 2021). The optimization formulation itself remains rooted in the SIMP framework (Bendsøe & Kikuchi, 1988) with adjoint-based sensitivity analysis, but the key departure is treating the latent vector as the optimizable shape parameter rather than explicit geometric attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in FMTO: the limited and rigid set of geometric features available, which constrains design flexibility and manufacturability. By unifying ellipses, triangles, and quadrilaterals in a single latent space, the approach opens a path toward CAD-integrated topology optimization with minimal human intervention. However, significant limitations remain: transitions between feature types are corrupted by the reconstruction decoder, offset variables exhibit oscillatory behavior requiring artificial damping, and the method is demonstrated only in 2D with three feature types. Extending to 3D, adding more feature types, and resolving the initialization sensitivity (a known issue in FMTO) are critical open questions before the approach can be broadly adopted.
