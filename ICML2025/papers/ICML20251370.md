## Problem

Existing code editing approaches treat transformation knowledge as latent and implicit, internalizing it into model weights through brute-force finetuning. This fails to decouple the modular, reusable editing procedures from specific training samples, resulting in suboptimal performance, poor robustness to semantics-preserving code transformations, and limited generalization to unseen code lengths or CWE categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-edit code sample x_i as primary input; in prompting mode it additionally receives the discovered meta-rule set R, and in iterative mode it consumes coarse-grained execution feedback (compilation/execution errors). The output is structured as a sequence: first a functional specification s_i (natural-language description of the program's intended behavior plus input/output constraints), then per-sample editing rules R_i (a subset of the meta-rule set in 'switch from X to Y' format), and finally the post-edit code y_i. The augmented training set D* consists of quadruples (x_i, s_i, R_i, y_i), where the meta-rule set R is a concise, task-specific collection of rules discovered inductively from the full training corpus.

## Method

EDITLORD employs a language model G as an inductive learner to distill a concise meta-rule set R from training code pairs through an iterative refinement algorithm that applies three operations—ADD, MERGE, and PRUNE—while enforcing a 'balanced' criterion (neither too generic nor too specific) to keep rules modular and reusable. For each training pair, G also extracts a functional specification s_i capturing the shared input-output semantics that the edit must preserve. The augmented training set D* = {(x_i, s_i, R_i, y_i)} is then used to finetune a separate LM M to model the joint conditional P(y_i, s_i, R_i | x_i) = P(s_i|x_i)·P(R_i|x_i,s_i)·P(y_i|x_i,s_i,R_i), forcing the model to reason about editing steps before generating code. Beyond finetuning, the meta-rule set directly augments zero-shot prompts and guides iterative refinement with execution feedback, and the explicit rule structure enables human experts to intervene by adding or removing rules, yielding up to 35.5% additional improvement.

## Results

EDITLORD outperforms state-of-the-art finetuned baselines across all three code editing tasks and all three LM backbones (DeepSeek-Coder 1.3B/6.7B, GPT-4o mini), with average improvements of 23.3% (performance optimization), 12.7% (decompilation), and 27.6% (security hardening). On GPT-4o mini, EDITLORD achieves 72.5% OPT@8 vs. 61.5% for the finetuned baseline in performance optimization, 67.2% correctness vs. 52.7% in decompilation, and 29.3% Correct&Sec@10 vs. 22.1% in security hardening. EDITLORD also demonstrates substantially better robustness (up to 58.1% less degradation under semantics-preserving transformations) and length generalization (up to 24.87% less drop on longer unseen code).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDITLORD is compared against three task-specific finetuned baselines: Shypula et al. (2024) for performance optimization, Tan et al. (2024) for decompilation, and Fu et al. (2022) for security hardening. On performance optimization, EDITLORD consistently wins across all three models, e.g., improving OPT@8 from 61.5% to 72.5% (GPT-4o mini) and Speedup@8 from 3.0 to 3.8 (DeepSeek-Coder 1.3B). On decompilation, EDITLORD achieves the largest gains in compilability (97.7% vs. 86.3% for GPT-4o mini) and correctness (67.2% vs. 52.7%), though the margin narrows for DeepSeek-Coder 6.7B (90.1% vs. 89.6% compilability). On security hardening, EDITLORD wins on Security@k and Correct&Sec@k across all models (e.g., 40.3% vs. 28.5% Security@10 for GPT-4o mini), but slightly underperforms the finetuned baseline on Correct@10 for DeepSeek-Coder 6.7B (41.3% vs. 45.5%). Overall, EDITLORD achieves the best joint performance when considering both functional correctness and editing quality simultaneously, with average improvements of 23.3%, 12.7%, and 27.6% across the three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDITLORD departs from the dominant paradigm of treating code editing as an implicit end-to-end mapping learned through brute-force finetuning (Shypula et al. 2024, Tan et al. 2024, Fu et al. 2022), instead making the transformation steps explicit through an inductively learned meta-rule set. Conceptually, it extends the bootstrapping-of-symbolic-reasoning line of work (Zelikman et al. 2022, Kim et al. 2023, Huang et al. 2023b) where LMs synthesize per-sample reasoning chains, by distilling a concise, composable, and reusable rule set that is shared across samples rather than generated independently for each. The framework is also complementary to iterative refinement with execution feedback (Huang et al. 2024, Peng et al. 2025b), positioning the explicit rules as a structured prior that guides both finetuning and prompting-based editing modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit rule representation addresses a practical gap in code editing: it makes the editing process interpretable and enables human experts to intervene, yielding up to 35.5% additional improvement. The substantial robustness gains (58.1% less degradation under semantics-preserving transformations) and length generalization improvements suggest that decoupling modular editing knowledge from specific training samples is a meaningful design principle for code LMs. Key open questions remain: EDITLORD cannot formally guarantee functional correctness of all edits (treating it as a soft constraint), and integrating fine-grained per-statement execution profiles into the rule-guided iterative loop is left for future work. The framework's reliance on a strong oracle LM (GPT-4o mini) for rule discovery also raises questions about scalability to domains where such oracles are unavailable.
