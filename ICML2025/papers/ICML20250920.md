## Problem

While score-based diffusion models (DiffCSP, MatterGen) and conditional flow matching (FlowMM) have each set successive state-of-the-art results for inorganic crystal generation, it remains unclear whether either framework is definitively superior or whether the optimal approach depends on the dataset. The paper addresses this by introducing stochastic interpolants (SIs) as a unifying framework that subsumes both diffusion and flow matching as special cases, thereby opening a much broader design space of tunable interpolation paths and sampling schemes for materials generation.

## Input / Output

For crystal structure prediction (CSP), the model receives a fixed chemical composition and generates fractional coordinates in [0,1)^{3×N} and lattice vectors in R^{3×3}; for de novo generation (DNG), it additionally generates the discrete atomic species sequence via progressive unmasking from an all-masked initial state. The base distribution for lattice vectors is an informative log-normal fitted to the empirical distribution of lattice lengths in each dataset (rather than a fixed Gaussian), and fractional coordinates use a uniform base distribution on the periodic torus. The number of atoms N in the generated structure is determined by the target structure during training, and the model must learn the joint correlated distribution over all three components simultaneously.

## Method

OMatG learns a time-dependent velocity field b^θ(t,x) (and optionally a denoiser z^θ(t,x)) via a CSPNet equivariant graph neural network encoder, trained to match the drift of a stochastic interpolant x(t,x₀,x₁,z) = α(t)x₀ + β(t)x₁ + γ(t)z that bridges a base distribution to the target crystal distribution. The key design freedom is the choice of interpolant functions (linear, trigonometric, encoder-decoder, VP/VE score-based diffusion) and sampling scheme (ODE vs. SDE), which can be optimized independently for fractional coordinates (with periodic boundary conditions handled via shortest-path geodesics on a 4D torus) and lattice vectors. Atomic species are generated through discrete flow matching, where a neural network predicts the unmasking probability for each atom given the full interpolated structure, coupled to the continuous SI flow via a shared loss. The paper's central empirical finding is that the optimal interpolant and sampling scheme differ across datasets and degrees of freedom, and that this per-component tuning yields state-of-the-art match rates, stability, and S.U.N. rates across all benchmark datasets.

## Results

On Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP), OMatG's best variant (VP SBD with ODE sampling) achieves 83.06% match rate on perov-5, surpassing DiffCSP (53.08%) and FlowMM (53.63%) by a factor of ~1.6. On MP-20, the linear ODE variant reaches 69.83% match rate (vs. 57.82% DiffCSP, 66.22% FlowMM), and on MPTS-52 it achieves 27.38% (vs. 15.79% and 22.29%). For De Novo Generation (DNG) on MP-20, OMatG's Linear(SDE) variant attains a 22.48% S.U.N. rate (stable, unique, novel) and 46.18% stability rate, outperforming MatterGen-MP (20.30%, 44.79%), DiffCSP (15.95%, 43.43%), and FlowMM (11.73%, 41.20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffCSP (score-based diffusion), OMatG wins decisively on CSP match rate across all four datasets (e.g., 83.06% vs. 53.08% on perov-5; 69.83% vs. 57.82% on MP-20) and on DNG S.U.N. rate (22.48% vs. 15.95%), though DiffCSP achieves a lower RMSE on MP-20 (0.0627 vs. 0.0741) when structures do match. Against FlowMM (Riemannian flow matching), OMatG improves CSP match rate on MP-20 (69.83% vs. 66.22%) and MPTS-52 (27.38% vs. 22.29%), and substantially outperforms on DNG S.U.N. rate (22.48% vs. 11.73%) and combined validity (83.40% vs. 76.94%). Against MatterGen-MP (diffusion-based, Nature 2025), OMatG achieves a higher S.U.N. rate (22.48% vs. 20.30%) and stability rate (46.18% vs. 44.79%), but MatterGen produces structures much closer to their relaxed configurations (RMSD 0.1038 Å vs. 0.6357 Å) and has slightly higher combined validity (83.89% vs. 83.40%). Overall, OMatG establishes new state-of-the-art match rates on all CSP benchmarks and the best S.U.N. rate for DNG, with the caveat that different OMatG interpolant variants excel on different datasets, underscoring the value of the flexible SI design space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to implement and extend the Stochastic Interpolants (SI) framework (Albergo et al., 2023) for inorganic crystal generation, unifying score-based diffusion models (DiffCSP, MatterGen) and conditional flow matching (FlowMM) as special cases within a single tunable framework. It adapts the CSPNet equivariant GNN encoder from DiffCSP and couples continuous SI flows over fractional coordinates and lattice vectors with discrete flow matching (Campbell et al., 2024) for atomic species. The key methodological departure from prior work is the extension of SI paths to a four-dimensional torus to handle periodic boundary conditions, and the systematic optimization of interpolant choice per degree of freedom (coordinates vs. lattice vectors) and per dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OMatG establishes new state-of-the-art results across four benchmark datasets for both CSP and DNG, and provides an open-source, flexible framework where practitioners can tune interpolants, sampling schemes, and latent variables to match their specific dataset. The finding that the optimal interpolant varies by dataset and by degree of freedom challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that a single generative paradigm (pure diffusion or pure flow matching) is universally optimal. Open questions include whether symmetry constraints can be incorporated into the SI framework, how different interpolants influence the discovery of materials with specific target properties, and whether the match-rate/RMSE tradeoff observed here is fundamental to unconstrained flow-based generation of symmetric structures.
