## Problem

Existing rehearsal learning methods for the AUF (Avoiding Undesired Future) problem rely on threshold-based strategies (P_AUF ≥ τ) rather than directly maximizing the AUF probability, which fails when τ exceeds the achievable maximum or yields suboptimal actions when it does not. Moreover, the AUF probability lacks an analyzable functional form in general, making direct optimization intractable and forcing reliance on expensive sampling or cubic-complexity feasible-region construction. The paper asks: under what conditions can P_AUF be explicitly formulated and efficiently optimized to find the truly optimal alteration?

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed variables x, estimated structural model parameters θ (obtained via least-squares estimation on observational data), a desired region S specified as a convex polytope S(M,d), and element-wise alteration bounds [z_left, z_right]. The desired region must either satisfy the CARE condition (be a canonical rectangle w.r.t. the noise covariance CΣC^T) or admit an inner CARE embedding; for |Y|=1, any interval qualifies. The output is a single optimal alteration vector z^ξ within the bounds that maximizes P_AUF, along with the corresponding AUF probability value. When |Y|=1, the solution is produced in closed form with O(|z^ξ|) time complexity rather than the O(|z^ξ|^3·L) of the iterative solver.

## Method

The paper introduces the CARE (CAnonical REctangle) condition, requiring the desired region S to be a canonical rectangle w.r.t. the noise covariance matrix CΣC^T, under which P_AUF decomposes into a product of Gaussian CDF differences and its negative log becomes a convex function of the alteration z^ξ. A projection-Newton algorithm (Alg. 1) then solves the resulting convex optimization with box constraints, using a partial diagonal Hessian approximation and Armijo stepsize rule, and is proven to achieve superlinear (at least quadratic) convergence to the optimal alteration. For regions not satisfying CARE, an inner CARE embedding constructs the largest canonical-rectangle subset of S, providing a strict lower bound on P_AUF whose log is still convex and optimizable as a surrogate. When |Y|=1, the gradient of the convex objective reduces to a scalar equation k_2^T z = (b_1−b_2)/2, yielding a closed-form solution (Alg. 2) that iterates over vector elements in O(|z^ξ|) time, eliminating the need for iterative optimization entirely.

## Results

On the real-world Bermuda dataset (|Y|=1), the proposed method achieves 82.76% AUF probability, outperforming the best rehearsal baseline AUF-MICNS (76.19%) and QWZ23 (73.47%), while reducing decision-making time from 23.61 ms (MICNS) and 567.7 ms (QWZ23) to just 0.91 ms via the closed-form solution. On the synthetic Manage dataset (|Y|=2), the method reaches 99.01% AUF probability versus 96.96% (MICNS) and 94.22% (QWZ23), with comparable runtime (5.86 ms). RL baselines (DDPG, SAC, PPO) perform near-random levels (≤16% AUF probability) due to the lack of environment interactions in the AUF setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two rehearsal-learning baselines (QWZ23 and AUF-MICNS) and three RL methods (DDPG, SAC, PPO). On the Bermuda dataset, the proposed method wins on all three metrics: AUF probability (82.76% vs. 76.19% for MICNS and 73.47% for QWZ23), 100-round success frequency (83.26 vs. 76.69 and 72.62), and decision time (0.91 ms vs. 23.61 ms and 567.7 ms), with the time advantage stemming from the O(|z|) closed-form solution when |Y|=1. On the synthetic Manage dataset (|Y|=2), the method also leads in AUF probability (99.01% vs. 96.96% for MICNS and 94.22% for QWZ23) but is slightly slower than MICNS (5.86 ms vs. 4.02 ms) since the projection-Newton algorithm with O(|z|³·L) complexity is used instead of the closed form. All three RL baselines (DDPG, SAC, PPO) perform at or near random-action levels on both datasets, confirming that interaction-based methods are ill-suited to the AUF setting where immediate decisions must be made without environment feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rehearsal learning framework introduced by Qin et al. (2023) and the AUF problem formulation by Zhou (2022), building directly on the Structural Rehearsal Model (SRM) and its linear structural equations. It departs from prior rehearsal learning methods (QWZ23, AUF-MICNS) that rely on threshold-based feasibility (P_AUF ≥ τ) by instead directly maximizing P_AUF through a newly established CARE condition that renders the problem a convex optimization. The projection-Newton algorithm and the closed-form solution for |Y|=1 represent a methodological departure from the sampling-based and cubic-complexity approaches in prior work, while the inner CARE embedding generalizes the framework to non-CARE scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CARE condition provides a practical and verifiable assumption under which the AUF problem becomes analytically tractable, and the closed-form O(|z|) solution for |Y|=1 is significant given that most existing rehearsal learning experiments operate in this regime. However, the approach is limited to linear structural equations with Gaussian noise, and the inner CARE embedding is only constructively demonstrated for circular regions, leaving arbitrary desired regions as an open challenge. The broader question of how to handle non-linear SRMs or non-Gaussian noise distributions remains unaddressed, and the practical prevalence of the CARE condition in real-world decision systems beyond the two tested datasets warrants further validation.
