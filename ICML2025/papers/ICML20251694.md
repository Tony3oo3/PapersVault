## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning models fail to scale: increasing network size beyond a critical threshold causes performance degradation due to optimization pathologies such as plasticity loss, capacity collapse, and gradient interference. While the SimBa architecture (residual connections + layer normalization) improved scalability to ~17M parameters, further scaling in any dimension still leads to sharp performance drops. The paper asks whether static network sparsity alone, without dynamic interventions or algorithmic modifications, can break through this scaling barrier.

## Input / Output

The method operates on environment state vectors (67-dim for Humanoid, 223-dim for Dog tasks in DMC) or RGB images (for visual RL via DrQ-v2), producing continuous actions for control tasks or discrete actions for Atari-100k. The key structural detail is that a binary mask matrix M^l ∈ {0,1}^{n^l × n^{l-1}} is applied element-wise to each layer's weight matrix at initialization, yielding effective weights W_eff^l = M^l ⊙ W^l that remain fixed throughout training. Sparsity ratios per layer follow Erdős-Rényi scaling (1 − (n^{l-1}+n^l)/(n^{l-1}·n^l) for fully-connected layers), ensuring the overall network sparsity S is achieved while preserving connectivity proportional to layer dimensions.

## Method

The core technique is one-shot random pruning: before any training begins, a predetermined fraction of weights in each layer is randomly zeroed out using Erdős-Rényi sparsity ratios, and the resulting sparse topology is frozen for the entire training run. This static sparse training (SST) is applied on top of the SimBa architecture (residual blocks with layer normalization) using SAC or DDPG, with the critic network scaled to 4× the width and 2× the depth of the actor. The paper demonstrates that at equal learnable parameter counts, larger sparse networks outperform smaller dense ones, and at equal total model size, sparse networks beat dense counterparts. Analysis via stable-rank, dormant neuron ratios, gradient norms, parameter norms, simplicity bias scores, and gradient covariance matrices reveals that sparsity simultaneously prevents capacity collapse, preserves plasticity, constrains parameter growth, promotes simpler solutions, and reduces gradient interference—mechanisms that collectively explain the scaling unlock.

## Results

On the four hardest DMC tasks (Humanoid Walk, Humanoid Run, Dog Run, Dog Trot) using SimBa architecture with SAC and DDPG, dense networks degrade beyond ~17M parameters, while sparse networks at ~109M total parameters (with ~92% sparsity) achieve up to 40% higher episode returns than their dense counterparts. At matched learnable parameter counts, larger sparse networks consistently outperform smaller dense ones, demonstrating superior parameter efficiency. Diagnostic analyses show sparse networks maintain Srank near the theoretical upper bound of 256 (vs. capacity collapse in large dense networks), preserve gradient norms and low dormant ratios throughout training, and raise simplicity bias scores by an additional 0.3–0.4 beyond SimBa's architectural improvements (5.8→6.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against dense SimBa networks (Lee et al., 2024), the current state-of-the-art DRL architecture: sparse networks clearly win at all scales beyond ~17M parameters, with the gap widening as model size increases (up to 40% higher returns at ~109M params). Against Reset-based plasticity restoration (Nikishin et al., 2022), sparse networks achieve comparable or better performance without any Reset intervention, and Reset actually slightly harms sparse network performance. In visual RL (DrQ-v2 baseline, Yarats et al., 2022), combining 0.8 sparsity with 4× wider critic networks significantly outperforms dense width scaling alone, while dense 4× critics suffer severe plasticity deterioration (high dormant neuron fractions). In streaming RL, both one-shot static pruning and SparseInit (Elsayed et al., 2024) improve sample efficiency over dense baselines, though static pruning maintains fixed sparsity throughout training whereas SparseInit gradually densifies. On Atari-100k (DER baseline, Van Hasselt et al., 2019), 3× wider IMPALA CNN with 0.4 or 0.8 sparsity generally outperforms the dense 3× variant, extending findings to discrete action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SimBa architecture (Lee et al., 2024), which integrates layer normalization, residual connections, and observation normalization as the current state-of-the-art for DRL network scaling. It departs from the dominant line of sparsity research in DRL—dynamic sparse training (SET, RigL, gradual magnitude pruning, Neuroplastic Expansion, Soft MoEs)—by isolating static sparsity via one-shot random pruning at initialization as a standalone, topology-fixed feature. The conceptual lineage draws from the 'unreasonable effectiveness of random pruning' in supervised learning (Liu et al., 2022) and the Erdős-Rényi sparsity initialization from network science (Mocanu et al., 2018), but applies them in a fundamentally different context: not for compression or training acceleration, but as a scaling enabler on top of already-advanced architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single one-time preprocessing step (random pruning) that is algorithm-agnostic and requires no changes to training dynamics, effectively eliminating the need for Reset operations that previously enabled scaling. Open questions include whether specialized sparsity structures (e.g., structured pruning, learned topologies) can push scaling further, whether theoretical frameworks can explain why static sparsity counteracts multiple pathologies simultaneously, and whether the benefits fully transfer to very long-horizon or multi-agent settings. The paper's evaluation is concentrated on DMC continuous control; the Atari-100k and streaming RL results, while positive, are noted as preliminary and may not fully capture the scaling benefits in those regimes.
