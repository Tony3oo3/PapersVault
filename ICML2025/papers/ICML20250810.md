## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs is inherently limited by its generative, token-level objective: it increases the likelihood of good tokens but does not suppress the likelihood of bad ones, so the model may still generate incorrect answers with high probability. The standard remedy—applying preference optimization (PO) after SFT—requires human-labeled preference pairs or a trained reward model, both of which are resource-intensive and limit scalability. The paper asks whether classical discriminative learning principles can replace this two-stage SFT→PO pipeline entirely, using only standard supervised input-output pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised (prompt, response) pairs and a set of self-generated negative responses sampled offline from the base model, optionally conditioned on adversarial system prompts (e.g., 'You are an unhelpful assistant') to elicit low-quality outputs. No human preference annotations, reward model scores, or explicit winning/losing pair labels are required. The output is a fine-tuned LLM producing text responses, evaluated on mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH) and general capability benchmarks (MMLU, TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC, IFEval, AlpacaEval2).

## Method

DFT replaces SFT's generative likelihood P_g(y|x) with a discriminative likelihood P_d(y|x) = exp(s_θ(y,x)/τ) / Σ_{y'∈Y} exp(s_θ(y',x)/τ), where the scoring function s_θ is the (optionally length-normalized) generative log-likelihood of the LLM itself. The numerator recovers the standard SFT objective, while the denominator actively suppresses the scores of all possible bad outputs, shifting the paradigm from token prediction to data-level prediction. The intractable partition function over all texts Y is estimated via importance sampling from the base model's distribution and optimized using a finite-sum coupled compositional optimization (FCCO) framework with per-sample moving-average estimators for numerical stability. DFT2 further approximates by dropping the importance-sampling weights, yielding a log-sum-exp loss over a fixed pool of self-generated negatives—structurally distinct from the pairwise logistic loss in DPO, SimPO, or SPIN, because log-sum-exp assigns higher gradient weight to the highest-scoring (most dangerous) negatives rather than treating all negatives equally.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback general-language setting (7 benchmarks), DFT achieves an average score of 62.84 and DFT2 achieves 62.56, both outperforming standard SFT (61.79) and competitive with the best SFT→PO pipelines (SFT→KTO at 63.04, SFT→DPO at 62.98 under the improved UF→UF pipeline). On the mathematical reasoning setting, DFT reaches 79.15% on GSM8K and 28.34% on MATH, surpassing the SFT baseline MetaMath-Mistral-7B (77.7% / 28.2%). DFT also leads on IFEval (51.20) over SFT→DPO (47.69) and SFT→SimPO (43.81) in the UF→UF pipeline, and on GSM8K (48.22) over SFT→KTO (45.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFT (62.84 avg) is competitive with the strongest two-stage SFT→PO baselines under the improved UF→UF pipeline: it trails SFT→KTO (63.04) by 0.20 and SFT→DPO (62.98) by 0.14, but clearly beats SFT→SimPO (62.06) by 0.78. Against the standard UC→UF pipeline, DFT outperforms all PO methods by 1.8–7.0 points on average, with the largest gap over SFT→RRHF (49.85). On individual benchmarks, DFT leads on GSM8K (48.22 vs. 47.54 for SFT→DPO and 45.41 for SFT→KTO) and IFEval (51.20 vs. 47.69 for SFT→DPO and 43.81 for SFT→SimPO). In the self-play comparison (Table 2), DFT (62.84) substantially outperforms SPIN (57.80), SimPO (51.40), DPO-P (57.33), and KTO (59.53), and edges out ORPO (61.97) and SimPO-SFT (60.13), confirming that the log-sum-exp discriminative objective is more effective than pairwise losses when using self-generated negatives. On the mathematical reasoning task, DFT (79.15% GSM8K, 28.34% MATH) and DFT2 (78.77%, 28.62%) both surpass the SFT baseline MetaMath-Mistral-7B (77.7%, 28.2%) without any preference data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes LLM fine-tuning through the lens of discriminative probabilistic modeling, drawing a direct analogy to the generative-vs-discriminative distinction in classical supervised learning (e.g., Naive Bayes vs. logistic regression). It builds on the standard SFT objective (Ouyang et al., 2022) as its first term but augments it with a log-sum-exp penalty over self-generated negatives, optimized via the finite-sum coupled compositional optimization (FCCO) framework of Wang & Yang (2022) with moving-average estimators. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant SFT→PO pipeline (DPO, SimPO, KTO, etc.) by eliminating the need for human preference labels or reward models, and from self-play methods like SPIN by replacing the pairwise logistic loss with a log-sum-exp loss that adaptively weights harder negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DFT achieves competitive alignment in a single training stage without any preference data or reward model, reducing the annotation and infrastructure burden of the standard SFT→PO pipeline. However, the performance gains over SFT are modest (e.g., 62.56 vs. 61.79 average on seven benchmarks), and DFT does not clearly surpass the best SFT→PO methods under the improved UF→UF pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex reasoning tasks, how sensitive it is to the quality of self-generated negatives, and whether the discriminative framing can be extended to multi-turn or tool-use settings where the output space is even more structured.
