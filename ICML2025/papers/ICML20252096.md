## Problem

LLM-as-a-Judge models generate chain-of-thought rationales for evaluation, but the absence of human-annotated reasoning traces means the required structure and components of effective evaluation CoTs remain understudied. Prior approaches either constrain reasoning to hand-designed components (criteria lists, verification questions, scoring rubrics) that must be manually tailored per domain, or intertwine planning with reasoning in a single undifferentiated trace. This paper asks: can an LLM learn to both plan and reason for evaluation in an unconstrained, self-improving manner using only synthetic data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252096/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a user instruction x and a pair of responses (a, b), and produces a structured CoT of up to 2048 tokens (temperature 0 at inference) decomposed into three sequential components: an unconstrained evaluation plan z generated conditioned only on the instruction (not the responses, ensuring it encodes a general recipe), a step-by-step execution e that follows the plan to analyze both responses, and a final binary verdict y (A or B). The plan and execution are treated as latent variables in the generative process, and the model is trained to optimize over both jointly rather than treating the CoT as a monolithic sequence.

## Method

EvalPlanner decouples evaluation into an unconstrained planning stage (the model generates a domain-adaptive recipe of criteria, reference answers, unit tests, etc. conditioned only on the instruction) and an execution stage (the model follows the plan step-by-step to compare responses and produce a verdict). Training proceeds in a self-improving loop: (1) SFT on a subset of correct CoTs to teach the plan+execution+verdict format, (2) DPO on preference pairs where 'chosen' CoTs lead to the correct verdict and 'rejected' CoTs do not, and (3) a second DPO iteration on fresh instructions with CoTs sampled from the updated model. Synthetic data is constructed by sampling 5 plans and 8 executions (4 per response order) per instruction, yielding 22K total preference pairs from WildChat and MATH prompts. Ablations show that unconstrained plans outperform constrained alternatives (criteria lists, verification questions) by ~2–3 points, and the second DPO iteration with fresh data improves accuracy by 1.6 points over a single iteration on the same data volume.

## Results

On RewardBench, EvalPlanner achieves a new SOTA of 93.9% overall accuracy for generative reward models (with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as seed), using only 22K synthetically generated preference pairs—far fewer than baselines trained on 80K–680K human-annotated pairs. On PPE, EvalPlanner (Llama-3.3-70B) reaches 67.9% overall, outperforming DeepSeek-GRM-27B (62.2%) by 5.7 points, with particularly strong gains on PPE Correctness (70.2% vs. 59.8%). On FollowBenchEval, which tests multi-level constraint satisfaction, EvalPlanner (Llama-3.3-70B) achieves 65.4% position-consistent accuracy, a 13-point improvement over the prior best (Skywork-Critic at 52.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvalPlanner outperforms all nine baselines on RewardBench. Against zero-shot LLM judges (Llama-3.1-70B at 84.0%, Llama-3.3-70B at 85.4%), EvalPlanner gains roughly 8–9 points. Among reward models with critiques, it beats SynRM (87.3%) by 6.6 points, CLoud (87.6%) by 6.3 points, and Critic-RM-Rank (90.5%) by 3.4 points. Among SOTA generative reward models, EvalPlanner surpasses Self-Taught Evaluator (90.0%, 20K pairs) by 3.9 points, SFR-Llama-3.1-70B-Judge (92.7%, 680K pairs) by 1.2 points, Skywork-Critic (93.3%, 80K pairs) by 0.6 points, and LMUnit (93.4%, 84K pairs) by 0.5 points—all while using only 22K synthetic pairs. On PPE, EvalPlanner (67.9%) outperforms DeepSeek-GRM-27B (62.2%) by 5.7 points overall and by up to 10.4 points on the IFEval subtask (64.3% vs. 53.9%). On FollowBenchEval, EvalPlanner (Llama-3.3-70B) leads Skywork-Critic by 13.2 points (65.4% vs. 52.2%), and on RM-Bench it leads by 8.0 points (82.1% vs. 74.1%), while on JudgeBench it is within 0.5 points of Skywork-Critic (56.6% vs. 57.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

EvalPlanner extends the Self-Taught Evaluators paradigm (Wang et al., 2024c) by decomposing the evaluation Chain-of-Thought into an explicit planning stage and a separate execution stage, rather than generating a monolithic reasoning trace. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as the optimization mechanism but applies it iteratively over both plans and executions in a self-training loop, using only synthetically generated preference pairs. The key departure from prior LLM-as-a-Judge work (Critic-RM-Rank, Skywork-Critic, Self-Taught Evaluators) is the use of unconstrained, task-adaptive evaluation plans instead of hand-designed components like fixed criteria lists or verification questions, and the explicit decoupling of planning from reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-efficiency result is practically significant: achieving SOTA on RewardBench (93.9) with only 22K synthetic preference pairs versus 680K+ human-annotated pairs for prior SOTA suggests that the plan-execution decomposition provides a more information-dense training signal. The finding that unconstrained plans outperform structured alternatives (criteria lists, verification questions) challenges the prevailing assumption that evaluation rubrics must be manually designed per domain. Open questions include whether further iterative DPO rounds yield continued gains, whether the approach transfers to non-pairwise evaluation settings, and whether the synthetic data pipeline inherits and amplifies biases from the seed model. The FollowBenchEval benchmark, while small (205 samples), addresses a real gap in evaluating multi-constraint instruction following.
