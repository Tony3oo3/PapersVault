## Problem

GNN-based collaborative filtering suffers from two intertwined limitations: neighborhood aggregation biases representations toward high-degree (head) nodes, underrepresenting tail items, and Euclidean embedding space cannot faithfully encode the hierarchical, exponentially-expanding structure inherent in long-tail interaction graphs. Prior work has addressed either local structure bias or embedding distortion in isolation, but no method simultaneously tackles both within a unified architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252831/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a bipartite user-item interaction graph G=(V_u, V_i, E) where users and items are identified solely by IDs (no side features), and produces a learned ranking function F(u,i|G,Θ) that scores each user-item pair for recommendation. Embeddings are initialized in Euclidean space and projected into a hyperbolic manifold of curvature K via the exponential map at the north pole; all subsequent computation (convolution, attention, normalization, loss) is performed in hyperbolic space. The final prediction uses a hyperbolic margin-ranking loss that separates positive and negative user-item pairs by a distance margin in the manifold.

## Method

Hgformer combines two modules operating entirely in hyperbolic space: (1) LHGCN, a parameter-free graph convolution that aggregates neighbor messages via the hyperbolic centroid without projecting to tangent space, avoiding the information loss inherent in HGCF-style tangent-space round-trips; and (2) a Hyperbolic Transformer using cross-attention (rather than self-attention) to model only user-item interactions, respecting the bipartite structure and avoiding noisy intra-group signals. To achieve linear O(M+N) complexity instead of quadratic O(M·N), the paper introduces a Hyperbolic SoftMax (HSM) similarity function based on the Minkowski inner product and approximates it via positive random features φ(·) with a provable unbiased estimation (Theorem 3.1) and bounded approximation error (Theorem 3.2). Local and global embeddings are fused by a weighted average in tangent space before being mapped back to the manifold, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a hyperbolic margin-ranking loss.

## Results

Hgformer achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six datasets (Amazon Book/CD/Movie, Douban Book/Movie/Music) on Recall@10, Recall@20, NDCG@10, and NDCG@20. The most substantial gains appear on Amazon Book, where Hgformer reaches Recall@10 of 0.0901 and NDCG@10 of 0.0573, improving 17.6% and 18.1% over the second-best model HICF (0.0766, 0.0485). On Amazon CD, Hgformer attains Recall@10 of 0.0977 and NDCG@20 of 0.0678, outperforming HICF by 18.3% and 16.9% respectively. All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hgformer outperforms all ten baselines on 22 out of 24 dataset-metric combinations, with the sole exceptions being Douban Movie NDCG@10 (HRCF: 0.1342 vs. Hgformer: 0.1322) and NDCG@20 (HRCF: 0.1456 vs. Hgformer: 0.1447), where HRCF edges ahead by a narrow margin. The strongest competitors are the hyperbolic GNN baselines HICF and HRCF, which Hgformer surpasses by 15–19% on Amazon datasets and 3–7% on Douban datasets. Traditional GNNs (LightGCN, NGCF) are consistently outperformed by 20–50% in Recall@10, confirming the benefit of hyperbolic geometry for long-tail CF. The graph transformer baselines (SGFormer, NodeFormer, Hypformer) perform poorly in CF because they compute self-attention over all node pairs, introducing noisy intra-group signals in the bipartite user-item graph; Hypformer in particular drops to 0.0313 Recall@10 on Douban Music versus Hgformer's 0.1386. Ablation results further show that removing LHGCN causes the largest performance drop, while replacing LHGCN with HGCF (which projects to tangent space) degrades results on 4 of 6 datasets, validating the fully-hyperbolic convolution design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hgformer sits at the intersection of three research threads: parameter-free graph convolution for CF (LightGCN), hyperbolic geometric representation for long-tail recommendation (HGCF, HICF, HRCF), and linear-complexity graph transformers (SGFormer, NodeFormer, Hypformer). Its key departures are: (1) LHGCN eliminates the tangent-space projection that HGCF and its variants require, performing aggregation entirely on the hyperbolic manifold via hyperbolic centroid; (2) it replaces the self-attention used by Hypformer (designed for node classification) with a cross-attention mechanism tailored to the bipartite user-item structure of CF; and (3) it achieves linear complexity through a positive random feature map applied to a hyperbolic kernel, rather than Hypformer's time-like value recalibration trick. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first hyperbolic graph transformer in the collaborative filtering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By simultaneously addressing degree-bias in local message passing and embedding distortion from Euclidean geometry, Hgformer provides a principled recipe for mitigating the long-tail problem in CF that prior methods tackled only partially. The theoretical bound on the random-feature approximation error (Theorem 3.2) gives practitioners a concrete knob (number of random features m) to trade off accuracy vs. speed, which is practically valuable for deployment. Open questions include whether the approximation error bound remains tight at the scale of industrial datasets (millions of users/items), whether the cross-attention design generalizes to multi-relational or heterogeneous interaction graphs, and whether the hyperbolic curvature K can be learned per-dataset rather than fixed. The tail-item improvements, while consistent, were validated on datasets up to ~211K users, leaving scalability to truly massive graphs untested.
