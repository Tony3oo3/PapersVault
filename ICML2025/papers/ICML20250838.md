## Problem

Structure-augmented RAG methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG) that target sense-making or associativity suffer significant performance degradation on basic factual QA tasks, and no existing system achieves robust performance simultaneously across all three memory dimensions—factual recall, multi-hop association, and large-scale discourse understanding. This paper identifies that the root causes are LLM-generated noise in retrieval corpora and entity-centric query parsing that discards contextual signals.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a corpus of passages as input. During offline indexing, an LLM extracts OpenIE triples from each passage to build a schema-less knowledge graph containing phrase nodes, passage nodes, relation edges, synonym edges, and context edges. During online retrieval, the system produces a ranked list of top-5 passages, which are then fed as context to a QA LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct or GPT-4o-mini) to generate the final answer. The KG is managed via python-igraph, and the embedding model (NV-Embed-v2) scores both passages and triples for seed node selection.

## Method

HippoRAG 2 extends HippoRAG's Personalized PageRank retrieval with three key refinements. First, dense-sparse integration adds passage nodes to the KG connected via 'contains' edges to their constituent phrases, embedding full contextual information alongside sparse conceptual representations. Second, deeper contextualization replaces NER-based query-to-node matching with query-to-triple matching, where the entire query is embedded and matched against KG triples to capture richer relational context. Third, recognition memory uses an LLM (tuned via DSPy MIPROv2) to filter the top-5 retrieved triples, removing irrelevant ones before they serve as PPR seed nodes. Reset probabilities are assigned to both phrase nodes (from filtered triple ranking scores) and passage nodes (from embedding similarity scaled by a weight factor of 0.05), and PPR then propagates probability mass across the graph to rank passages for downstream QA.

## Results

HippoRAG 2 achieves an average F1 of 59.8 across seven RAG benchmarks, outperforming the strongest dense retriever NV-Embed-v2 (57.0) by 2.8 points. The largest gains appear on multi-hop QA: +9.5 F1 on 2Wiki (71.0 vs. 61.5), +2.9 on MuSiQue (48.6 vs. 45.7), and +3.1 on LV-Eval (12.9 vs. 9.8), while also matching or slightly exceeding NV-Embed-v2 on simple QA (NQ: 63.3 vs. 61.9) and discourse understanding (NarrativeQA: 25.9 vs. 25.7). On retrieval (passage recall@5), HippoRAG 2 reaches 78.2 average, surpassing NV-Embed-v2 (73.4) by 4.8 points, with the largest margin on MuSiQue (74.7 vs. 69.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HippoRAG 2 outperforms the strongest dense retriever NV-Embed-v2 on 6 of 7 datasets, with the most substantial margins on multi-hop tasks: +9.5 F1 on 2Wiki, +2.9 on MuSiQue, and +3.1 on LV-Eval, while matching or slightly exceeding it on simple QA and discourse understanding. Against the prior HippoRAG, improvements are dramatic on tasks where the original method was weak: +13.5 F1 on MuSiQue (48.6 vs. 35.1), +12.0 on HotpotQA (75.5 vs. 63.5), +4.5 on LV-Eval, and +9.6 on NarrativeQA, confirming that the deeper passage integration and recognition memory fix HippoRAG's context-loss limitations. All other structure-augmented RAG methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, LightRAG) underperform even the simplest dense retrievers on average, with LightRAG collapsing to 6.6 F1 due to LLM-generated noise in its retrieval corpus. Among simple baselines, GTR (T5-base) at 50.4 F1 and BM25 at 47.7 are far behind the 7B embedding models, which cluster between 54.9 and 57.0. The paper also shows HippoRAG 2 is robust to retriever choice, consistently adding ~5 F1 over direct dense retrieval regardless of whether GTE-Qwen2-7B, GritLM-7B, or NV-Embed-v2 is used as the underlying encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

HippoRAG 2 is a direct extension of HippoRAG (Gutiérrez et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced a neurobiologically inspired framework combining OpenIE-constructed knowledge graphs with Personalized PageRank (Haveliwala, 2002) for multi-hop retrieval. The key departures are three: dense-sparse integration (adding passage nodes to the KG to preserve context), query-to-triple linking (replacing entity-centric NER with full-query-to-triple matching), and recognition memory (LLM-based filtering of retrieved triples). The paper also positions itself against other structure-augmented RAG methods—RAPTOR (hierarchical summarization), GraphRAG (community-based summarization), and LightRAG (dual-level KG retrieval)—arguing that their use of LLM-generated summaries or expanded corpora introduces noise that degrades performance on simpler factual tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most important contribution is a comprehensive evaluation revealing that all prior structure-augmented RAG methods suffer significant performance drops on basic factual QA compared to strong dense retrievers, a cost previously unmeasured because each method was only evaluated on its target task. HippoRAG 2 demonstrates that a well-designed graph-based retrieval system can simultaneously improve associative memory (7% over NV-Embed-v2) while maintaining or slightly improving factual and sense-making performance, suggesting the structure-vs-simplicity tradeoff is not fundamental. Open questions include the precision of the triple filter (18% of queries yield empty filtered sets), scalability to very large or rapidly evolving corpora, and whether the simulated continual learning experiment (4-segment NQ/MuSiQue) captures the true difficulty of open-ended knowledge integration.
