## Problem

Choosing pretraining data is one of the most impactful decisions in LLM development, yet the methods used to make this decision at small scale (single-scale ranking or scaling-law extrapolation) have never been validated against counterfactual outcomes across many datasets. Without a suite of comparable models trained on diverse data recipes at a common target scale, it is impossible to measure whether a small-scale prediction method actually identifies the best data or merely predicts the magnitude of performance for a few models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251713/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The prediction pipeline takes evaluations from small-scale models (as small as 4M parameters, trained on one of 25 data recipes) and produces a pairwise ranking of data recipes, scored by decision accuracy—the fraction of all recipe pairs where the small-scale prediction correctly identifies the winner at the 1B target scale. Compute cost is measured as a percentage of the target 1B budget in FLOPs (6ND), and the target 'gold' ranking is based on mean OLMES accuracy over 3 random seeds. A key design choice is that small-scale predictions can use continuous proxy metrics (e.g., CORRECT PROB, TOTAL PROB) while the target scale uses discrete cloze-formulation accuracy, decoupling the input metric from the output metric.

## Method

DATADECIDE pretrains 1,050 transformer models (25 data recipes × 14 scales from 4M to 1B parameters × 3 seeds) at a fixed 100:1 token-to-parameter ratio (5× Chinchilla optimal), using OLMo's model ladder for hyperparameter selection. Two prediction strategies are compared: (1) ranking single-scale experiments at a fixed small size, and (2) fitting 8 variants of two-step scaling laws (compute→loss→accuracy) across multiple small scales and extrapolating to 1B. The paper introduces decision accuracy as the evaluation metric, formalized as the fraction of pairwise recipe comparisons where the predicted ordering matches the target-scale ordering. Key findings: single-scale ranking at 150M parameters achieves ~80% decision accuracy and no scaling-law variant exceeds this compute-to-accuracy frontier; continuous likelihood metrics (CORRECT PROB, TOTAL PROB) at small scale outperform or match discrete accuracy as predictors, enabling >80% predictability on code tasks (MBPP, HumanEval) with just 0.01% of target compute; and task predictability is governed by the interplay of run-to-run variance and spread of performance across recipes.

## Results

Ranking single-scale experiments at 150M parameters achieves ~80% decision accuracy for predicting the best pretraining data recipe at the 1B target scale, with a roughly log-linear relationship between compute spent and decision accuracy. None of the 8 scaling law variants (best: 3-parameter fit with 5.6% relative / 2.6% absolute prediction error) exceed the compute-to-decision-accuracy frontier set by single-scale ranking. Using continuous proxy metrics (CORRECT PROB, TOTAL PROB) at small scale makes MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, MBPP, and HumanEval >80% predictable at 1B with only 0.01% of the target compute, and lifts code tasks (HumanEval, MBPP) from trivial to ~80% decision accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two families of prediction methods: (1) scaling law extrapolation following Bhagia et al. (2024), implemented in 8 variants (2-param, 3-param, 5-param, single-step, with/without helper points and checkpoint filtering), and (2) single-scale ranking as used by DCLM (Li et al., 2024). The headline finding is that single-scale ranking at 150M parameters (~80% decision accuracy) sets a compute-to-accuracy frontier that none of the 8 scaling law variants surpass, even when using up to 14 model sizes for fitting. The best scaling law variant (3-parameter with helpers and >50% checkpoints, 5.6% relative error) achieves comparable but not superior decision accuracy to simply ranking one small model. In terms of prediction error, the 3-parameter variants cluster tightly (5.6–6.5% relative error), while the 5-parameter (N,D) and single-step variants are dramatically worse (42–231% relative error), suggesting that the simpler compute-based formulation is more robust for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the scaling laws literature (Kaplan et al. 2020; Hoffmann et al. 2022) and its extension to downstream task prediction via two-step fitting (Bhagia et al. 2024; Gadre et al. 2024), as well as the single-scale ranking approach popularized by DCLM (Li et al. 2024). It departs from prior data-comparison suites (Pythia with 2 recipes, PALOMA and Brandfonbrener et al. with 6 recipes) by scaling to 25 data recipes across 14 model sizes with 3 random seeds, and introduces 'decision accuracy' as a metric that directly measures whether small-scale predictions identify the correct winner among data pairs—something prior work could not validate without observing the full counterfactual. The OLMo model ladder provides standardized hyperparameters across scales to avoid confounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DATADECIDE is the most extensive open suite for studying pretraining data decisions, releasing 1,050 models, all corpora, and evaluations, enabling the community to iterate on prediction methods without repeating ~820K H100 GPU hours of pretraining. The practical finding that a single 150M-parameter ranking achieves ~80% pairwise decision accuracy and that no scaling law variant improves on this frontier directly informs how practitioners should allocate compute for data selection. Open questions include whether conclusions hold at different token-parameter ratios, whether new scaling law formulations can break the single-scale frontier, and whether the 25-recipe coverage is representative of future data curation choices. The proxy-metric finding (continuous likelihoods making code tasks predictable at 0.01% of target compute) opens a path to cheaper evaluation for tasks where discrete accuracy is too noisy at small scale.
