## Problem

Direct preference alignment algorithms such as DPO maximize the implicit reward gap between chosen and rejected responses but ignore the absolute quality scores already available from judge models during AI feedback. This causes three concrete failures: unnecessary unlearning of high-quality rejected responses, indiscriminate reinforcement of low-quality chosen responses, and poor generalization to optimal responses that are sparse in the preference data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253033/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference pairs (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) annotated with scalar judge-model reward scores (e.g., GPT-4 scores on a 1–10 scale in UltraFeedback) and outputs a reward-augmented dataset of double the original size, where each pair is split into two goal-conditioned sub-pairs. At inference, the LLM receives a system prompt specifying the target quality score (set to the maximum, e.g., 10) prepended to the user instruction, and generates a single text response conditioned on achieving that quality level. The approach also extends to multi-dimensional reward vectors (e.g., 5 attributes from ArmoRM) and to binarized datasets by substituting DPO implicit rewards for judge scores.

## Method

The core contribution is a data relabeling procedure that constructs a reward-augmented preference dataset: for each original pair (x, y_w, y_l) with judge scores r_w and r_l, two goal-conditioned pairs are created using the goal-conditioned reward R(x,y,g) = −(g − r(x,y))²—one with goal g = r_w (preserving the original ranking) and one with goal g = r_l (reversing the ranking)—doubling the dataset without any algorithmic change. The reward-conditioned policy π(y|x,g) is implemented as a standard LLM with a system prompt such as 'generate responses of score g,' and any off-the-shelf direct alignment algorithm (e.g., DPO) is applied directly to the augmented dataset. At inference, the model is conditioned on the optimal goal g* = r_max to extrapolate to the highest-quality responses. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) establishing global convergence to the optimal policy with suboptimality decaying at O(N^{−1/2}), and ablation studies confirm that the gains stem from quality-aware conditioning rather than mere dataset expansion.

## Results

Across five model families (Mistral-7B, Qwen2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, SPPO), DPO on reward-augmented UltraFeedback consistently outperforms both base models and standard DPO on UltraFeedback. On academic benchmarks (Table 3), the average accuracy improves by 0.8–1.6 points over standard DPO (e.g., Qwen2-7B: 53.74 → 54.54; Llama-3.1-8B: 53.50 → 54.37; SPPO: 58.77 → 59.55). On instruction-following benchmarks, the gains are larger: Qwen2-7B-Instruct jumps from 19.35% to 27.58% AlpacaEval 2.0 Win Rate, and SPPO improves from 40.58% to 66.41% Win Rate. When conditioning on 5-dimensional ArmoRM rewards on on-policy Llama-3-8B data, the method achieves SOTA AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate of 56.57, surpassing SimPO (53.70) and OpenChat (17.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AlpacaEval 2.0 with on-policy Llama-3-8B data conditioned on 5-dimensional ArmoRM rewards, the proposed method achieves LC Win Rate 56.57 and Win Rate 52.19, outperforming SimPO (53.70 / 47.50) by ~3 points and OpenChat/Conditioned-RLFT (17.48 / 11.36) by a very large margin. In the conditional fine-tuning comparison (Figure 4, offline UltraFeedback, Zephyr-SFT base), the method achieves the best AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate across varying verbosity preference profiles, surpassing DPA, SteerLM, and (Info)NCA. On the SPPO model (already trained on UltraFeedback), a second round of DPO with reward-augmented data yields LC Win Rate 60.97 and Win Rate 66.41, whereas standard DPO on the same data degrades performance to 52.75 / 40.58, demonstrating that the method extracts additional utility from the preference data rather than merely benefiting from increased dataset size. The Half-RA ablation (reward augmentation on only half the data) still outperforms full standard DPO, confirming the gains are not solely due to data expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and the broader family of direct preference alignment algorithms, but departs from them by introducing a goal-conditioned reward framework at the data level rather than the algorithm level. It is conceptually related to conditional LLM fine-tuning (SteerLM, DPA, Conditioned-RLFT) and to goal-conditioned supervised learning in RL (Yang et al., 2022; Ghosh et al., 2019), but differs by requiring no algorithmic modifications—only a data relabeling step that duplicates each preference pair with reversed labels under different reward goals. The theoretical analysis extends the offline RLHF convergence literature (Liu et al., 2024b; Cen et al., 2024) to the goal-conditioned setting, providing a global convergence guarantee that prior goal-conditioned RL work with supervised learning did not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's simplicity (a data relabeling step followed by standard DPO) makes it immediately adoptable by practitioners working with any preference dataset that includes reward scores, and the consistent improvements across six model families and multiple benchmarks suggest broad practical utility. The 'Half RA' ablation—where half the data with reward augmentation outperforms the full original dataset—indicates the method extracts more signal per sample rather than merely adding volume. Open questions include whether the system-prompt conditioning mechanism is optimal (vs. learned embeddings or architectural conditioning), how the approach behaves with very noisy or miscalibrated judge scores, and whether the N^{-1/2} convergence rate is tight in practice for the discrete, high-dimensional response space of LLMs.
