## Problem

Long-horizon multi-stage robotic manipulation tasks with sparse stage-wise rewards are extremely difficult for RL due to the combinatorial state-action space and lack of informative feedback between subgoals. While the natural multi-stage structure of these tasks (e.g., grasp → lift → place) offers a decomposition opportunity, existing demonstration-augmented methods either require large demonstration sets to cover behavior space, rely on offline reward learning that struggles on unseen states, or fail to exploit the stage structure for online dense reward generation within a world model.

## Input / Output

The agent receives multi-modal observations consisting of one or two RGB camera images (128×128 for ManiSkill/Robosuite, 224×224 for Meta-World) plus proprioceptive state, along with a small offline demonstration set of 5–100 episodes. At inference, the method outputs continuous robot actions (4–25 dimensional depending on the embodiment) via MPPI planning through the learned latent-space world model. The learned dense reward is structured per-stage: each stage discriminator contributes a bounded scalar in [−β, β] (β ≤ 1/3) added to the sparse stage indicator, ensuring rewards never cross between stage regions.

## Method

DEMO³ builds on TD-MPC2 and introduces a series of stage-specific discriminators {δ_k}, one per task stage, each trained with binary cross-entropy to predict whether a latent state will lead to the next stage transition. The discriminator outputs are combined with the sparse environment reward via a tanh-bounded additive term to produce a dense reward that guides both world model training (replacing the sparse reward in the TD-MPC2 reward prediction loss) and MPC planning. Training follows a bi-phasic scheme: Phase 1 pretrains the policy and encoder via behavioral cloning on the demonstration set with early stopping, and Phase 2 performs interactive MBRL where the agent anneals from the frozen BC policy to world-model planning while sampling 50% of each update batch from the demonstration dataset to maintain distributional coverage. The discriminators are updated online alongside the world model at every step, with the total loss jointly optimizing reconstruction, value estimation, latent dynamics, and all stage discriminator objectives, so the encoder itself is shaped by the reward-learning signal.

## Results

Across 16 sparse-reward manipulation tasks in four domains (ManiSkill3, Meta-World, Robosuite, ManiSkill Humanoids), DEMO³ achieves an average 40% improvement in data-efficiency over state-of-the-art baselines, with the gap widening to 70% on the most difficult long-horizon tasks. In the hardest domain (ManiSkill3), DEMO³ reaches 75% average success rate after 500k interaction steps, outperforming the second-best baseline by 50%. On the two most challenging tasks (Peg Insertion and Stack Cube), DEMO³ is the only method to reliably solve both within the interaction budget. Wall-time is competitive at 5.19 hours per 100k steps, second only to TD-MPC2 (4.84h) and far faster than LaNE (20.40h) or MoDem (8.37h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEMO³ outperforms all three baselines across the four domains. Against MoDem (Hansen et al., 2023), which uses a similar demonstration-augmented MBRL pipeline but lacks online reward learning, DEMO³ achieves substantially higher success rates, particularly on long-horizon tasks where the learned dense reward provides critical intermediate feedback. Against LaNE (Zhao et al., 2024), a model-free method relying on a pre-trained feature extractor for latent-space exploration, DEMO³ wins on average but LaNE is competitive specifically in the Robosuite domain; notably, LaNE does not transfer to the other three domains while DEMO³ does. Against TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), the pure MBRL backbone without any demonstrations or learned rewards, DEMO³ shows a dramatic advantage—TD-MPC2 struggles to achieve meaningful performance from sparse rewards alone, while DEMO³ leverages as few as 5 demonstrations to reach high success rates. In wall-time, DEMO³ is only marginally slower than TD-MPC2 (5.19h vs. 4.84h per 100k steps) due to the additional discriminator computation, but is 2–4× faster than the other demonstration-augmented baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEMO³ unifies three previously separate threads of demonstration-augmented RL: MoDem's bi-phase training scheme (behavioral cloning pretraining followed by interactive MBRL), TD-MPC2's latent-space world model with MPPI planning, and DrS's stage-discriminator approach to densifying sparse multi-stage rewards. The key departure from DrS is moving reward learning from an offline, pre-computed stage to an online process integrated within the world model's training loop, so the dense reward co-evolves with the dynamics model and policy. Unlike MoDem, which uses demonstrations only for policy seeding and replay-buffer oversampling, DEMO³ additionally leverages them to train stage discriminators that reshape the reward signal used for both world model learning and planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for robotics: practitioners need only provide coarse stage indicators (e.g., 'object grasped') and a handful of demonstrations rather than engineering dense, hand-crafted reward functions, and the paper shows that even 2 stages of granularity approach dense-reward performance. Limitations include simulation-only evaluation, a fixed 50% demonstration sampling ratio that may not be optimal, and dependence on the task having a natural multi-stage decomposition. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real hardware under domain shift, how it handles tasks without clear stage structure, and whether more sophisticated replay strategies (e.g., prioritized sampling) could further reduce the demonstration requirement.
