## Problem

Auction theory's most influential results—Myerson's optimal auction, the Bulow-Klemperer theorem, and anonymous price auction guarantees—all depend on the regularity of bidder value distributions, yet no efficient test exists to verify whether a given dataset satisfies this assumption. The original testable learning framework of Rubinfeld & Vasilyan (2023) cannot be applied directly because learning the optimal mechanism for an irregular distribution is information-theoretically infeasible, making the standard soundness condition unsatisfiable for revenue maximization.

## Input / Output

The tester and learner each receive m = Õ(log(1/δ)/α²) i.i.d. samples from a product distribution over n bidders, along with a distortion parameter α ∈ (0,1) controlling the Kolmogorov-Smirnov radius. Upon tester acceptance, the learner outputs Myerson's optimal auction computed with respect to a *constructed* regular distribution E′ (not the input distribution D), guaranteeing Rev(M, D) ≥ (1 − O(√(nα)))·OPT(D′) for every regular D′ within α-KS distance of D. When the tester rejects, no revenue guarantee is provided—the framework simply certifies that the input is too far from any regular distribution to support meaningful guarantees.

## Method

The core contribution is the Regularized Testable Learning framework, which relaxes the original testable learning soundness condition: instead of requiring the learner to match the optimal revenue of the input distribution itself, it only requires matching the optimal revenue of the best *close regular* distribution. The tester (Algorithm 1) constructs a quantile-shifted empirical distribution using DKW concentration bounds, takes the convex envelope of its link function to obtain a regular distribution, and then verifies that this regular distribution lies within an α-KS ball of the empirical distribution. The learner (Algorithm 2) similarly constructs a regular distribution E′ that is stochastically dominated by both the input D and every close regular D′, then outputs Myerson's optimal auction for E′. Revenue monotonicity via first-order stochastic dominance bridges the gap: since D ≽ E′, the revenue of the output mechanism on D is at least OPT(E′), which in turn is within (1−O(√(nα))) of OPT(D′) for any close regular D′.

## Results

The paper provides a tester-learner pair with sample complexity m = Õ(log(1/δ)/α²) that, when the tester accepts, guarantees the learner's mechanism achieves (1 − O(√(nα)))·OPT(D′) revenue for any regular distribution D′ within KS-distance α of the input. For the Bulow-Klemperer setting, the same tester certifies that Rev_{n+1}(VA, D) ≥ (1 − O(√(nα)))·OPT_n(D′). For anonymous price auctions, the guarantee is Rev(APA(p*), D) ≥ (1/e)(1 − O(√(nα)))·OPT(D′). A matching single-bidder lower bound (Lemma 11) shows no mechanism can achieve better than (1 − Ω(√α))·OPT(D′), confirming the √α dependence is tight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets; all results are stated as theorems with sample complexity and approximation guarantees. The key comparison is conceptual: prior work on learning irregular auctions (Guo et al. 2019; Roughgarden & Schrijvers 2016) requires sample complexity that scales with an upper bound on the distribution's support, whereas this paper's framework drops that dependence entirely, yielding guarantees for unbounded distributions (e.g., mixtures of Gaussians). The original testable learning framework of Rubinfeld & Vasilyan (2023) is shown to be inapplicable here (Theorem 3 proves no nontrivial tester-learner pair exists under the conventional definition), motivating the regularized relaxation. The framework subsumes the sample complexity results of Guo et al. (2021) as a special case when the input distribution is itself regular (α → 0), while extending guarantees to the irregular regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the testable learning framework of Rubinfeld & Vasilyan (2023) to mechanism design, but first proves that the original framework is inapplicable to revenue maximization (Theorem 3), motivating a new 'Regularized Testable Learning' variant that relaxes the soundness condition to compare against nearby regular distributions rather than the input distribution itself. The learning and testing algorithms build directly on the quantile-shift and convex-envelope techniques of Guo et al. (2019, 2021) for learning optimal auctions, augmented with a conditional KS-distance check. The revenue guarantees leverage Myerson's (1981) virtual value characterization, the Bulow-Klemperer (1996) competition theorem, and the Hartline-Roughgarden (2009) anonymous price result, all of which assume regularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result (Theorem 3) is a clean negative result showing that no tester-learner pair can simultaneously satisfy soundness and completeness for revenue maximization under the original testable learning definition, which is a fundamental barrier for applying testable learning to mechanism design. The regularized framework provides the first nontrivial revenue guarantees for learning single-item auctions from unbounded, irregular distributions, removing the support-size dependence present in prior work (Guo et al. 2019; Roughgarden & Schrijvers 2016). Open questions include extending the framework to multi-bidder lower bounds, testing the product-distribution and i.i.d. assumptions, and applying the generic formulation (Appendix G) to other mechanism design or learning settings.
