## Problem

Existing proximal decentralized optimization (PDO) methods like SONATA exploit functional similarity among nodes to reduce communication rounds, but they require solving proximal subproblems with high accuracy at each node, incurring significant computational overhead that grows with the number of rounds. Additionally, prior analyses bound communication complexity by the maximum dissimilarity δ_max across nodes, which can be up to √n times larger than the average dissimilarity δ. The paper asks whether a PDO method can achieve state-of-the-art communication complexity without sacrificing computational efficiency and with dependence on the more favorable average similarity δ.

## Input / Output

The method operates on n nodes each holding a local loss function f_i, connected by an undirected graph with Metropolis-Hastings edge weights, and is parameterized by a proximal step size λ, a gossip averaging depth M, and (for the accelerated variant) an acceleration parameter γ. It produces optimized model parameters along with provable communication and computational complexity bounds that hold in both convex and strongly-convex settings. A key output distinction is that the complexity depends on the average second-order similarity δ (defined via the averaged Hessian deviation) rather than the per-node maximum δ_max, yielding tighter guarantees when node functions are broadly similar but a few outliers exist.

## Method

SPDO introduces a stabilization step inspired by the Hybrid Projection Proximal-point Method: after approximately solving the proximal subproblem for x_i, it computes an auxiliary variable v_i via a closed-form update that blends the current iterate, the previous v_i, and the local gradient. This stabilization ensures that when the subproblem is solved exactly, the method recovers standard PDO/SONATA, while allowing the subproblem to be solved to a fixed (round-independent) accuracy, eliminating the growing computational cost of Inexact-PDO. The accelerated variant, Accelerated-SPDO, layers Monteiro–Svaiter acceleration on top and replaces standard gossip with accelerated gossip averaging (a two-step linear iteration with parameter γ tuned to the spectral gap ρ). The convergence analysis for all variants (PDO, SPDO, Accelerated-SPDO) is refined to depend on the average similarity δ rather than δ_max, and is extended to the convex case where prior PDO analyses were absent.

## Results

In the strongly-convex setting, Accelerated-SPDO achieves O(√(δ/(μ(1−ρ))) log(L/δ) log(1/ε)) communication complexity and O(√(L/μ) log(1/ε)) computational complexity, which are state-of-the-art among decentralized optimization methods. The non-accelerated SPDO achieves O(δ/(μ(1−ρ)) log(L/δ) log(1/ε)) communication and O(√(δL)/μ log(1/ε)) computation, matching Inexact-PDO's communication while improving its computation by removing the log-log factor. On MNIST with logistic regression (25 nodes, ring topology), experiments confirm that SPDO converges faster than Gradient Tracking when data heterogeneity is low (Dirichlet α=0.5 vs α=0.1), while Gradient Tracking remains unaffected by similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Gradient Tracking (Koloskova et al., 2021), SPDO achieves strictly better communication complexity when local functions are similar (δ ≪ L), since Gradient Tracking's O(L/(μ(1−ρ)²)) bound is independent of δ and cannot exploit data similarity. Compared to SONATA/Exact-PDO (Sun et al., 2022), SPDO matches the communication complexity while replacing the infeasible exact subproblem solve with an inexact one requiring only O(√(L/δ)) gradient steps per round, and further improves the dependence from δ_max to the (potentially √n times smaller) average dissimilarity δ. Compared to Inexact-PDO (the paper's own refined analysis of PDO), SPDO achieves identical communication complexity but removes the log log(1/ε) factor in computation by using a stabilization trick (Hybrid Projection Proximal-point idea) that keeps the subproblem accuracy requirement constant across rounds rather than tightening it. Compared to (Inexact) Accelerated SONATA (Tian et al., 2022), Accelerated-SPDO achieves the same or better communication (δ vs δ_max, and no extra log(δ/μ) factor) and strictly better computation: O(√(L/μ) log(1/ε)) vs O(√(δ_max L)/μ log(δ/μ) (log(1/ε))²), and additionally works in the convex case where Accelerated SONATA was only analyzed for strong convexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proximal decentralized optimization (PDO) framework introduced by Li et al. (2020) and instantiated as SONATA by Sun et al. (2022), which combines proximal-point updates with gradient tracking to exploit second-order similarity among local loss functions. The key departure is a 'stabilization' modification inspired by the Hybrid Projection Proximal-point Method (Solodov & Svaiter, 1999) that allows inexact subproblem solutions without the accuracy requirements growing over iterations, and a refined analysis that replaces the maximum dissimilarity δ_max with the average dissimilarity δ. The accelerated variant builds on Monteiro-Svaiter acceleration (2013) and accelerated gossip averaging (Liu & Morse, 2011), positioning itself against Inexact Accelerated SONATA (Tian et al., 2022) as the state-of-the-art baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from δ_max to δ in the communication complexity bound is practically significant since δ can be up to √n times smaller, directly translating to fewer communication rounds in heterogeneous networks. The stabilization trick also resolves a subtle but important issue: a naive adaptation of inexact proximal methods to the decentralized setting fails to recover the exact method when subproblems are solved perfectly, which would break convergence. However, the experimental validation is limited to MNIST with logistic regression on 25 nodes in a ring topology, leaving open whether these theoretical gains materialize in more complex settings (non-convex losses, larger networks, deeper models). The paper also does not address stochastic or non-convex variants, which are the dominant regimes in modern decentralized deep learning.
