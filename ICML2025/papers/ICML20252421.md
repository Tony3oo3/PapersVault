## Problem

The Sliced-Wasserstein distance is widely used as an optimization objective in generative modeling and variational inference, yet the behavior of its gradient descent dynamics remains largely uncharacterized. In particular, it is unknown what critical points the SW objective admits, whether gradient descent can get stuck at non-global-minimizer critical points, and whether such 'bad' critical points are stable or unstable. This paper provides the first rigorous critical point analysis for the SW objective, bridging the gap between the discrete particle optimization problem and its continuous measure-space limit.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target probability measure ρ on R^d (assumed to be a density or, more generally, atomless) and a discrete particle configuration X = (X_1, …, X_N) ∈ (R^d)^N not on the generalized diagonal Δ_N. It produces an explicit gradient formula for the SW objective, a barycentric characterization of Lagrangian critical points (a vector field v_μ = 0 condition involving 1D optimal transport barycenters integrated over the unit sphere), and a stability certificate showing that critical points supported on segments are unstable. The key structural constraint is that ρ must be without atoms so that 1D projections are atomless almost everywhere, enabling the use of Power cells and quantile functions.

## Method

The paper develops a multi-layered analysis: (1) an explicit gradient formula for the SW objective on discrete measures, yielding a descent lemma that guarantees loss decrease for step sizes λ ∈ (0, 2Nd) and a non-collapse property ensuring particles remain separated; (2) a barycentric characterization of Lagrangian critical points via the condition v_μ(x) = x/d − ∫_{S^{d−1}} γ̄_θ(⟨x|θ⟩)θ dθ = 0 μ-a.e., where γ̄_θ is the barycentric projection of the 1D optimal transport plan between the θ-projections of μ and ρ; (3) a proof that weak limits of sequences of discrete critical points remain critical, connecting the particle-based optimization to the continuous measure-space problem; and (4) an instability theorem (in d=2) showing that any critical point containing a segment is unstable under a symmetric splitting perturbation, with the energy decreasing as SW₂²(μ^t, ρ) ≤ SW₂²(μ, ρ) − Ct² for arbitrarily large C. The instability proof exploits the explicit closed-form quantile functions available for projections of segment-supported measures.

## Results

The paper establishes that the SW₂² objective is 1/(Nd)-semiconcave, yielding a descent lemma guaranteeing that a gradient step with step-size λ ∈ (0, 2Nd) strictly decreases the loss (Proposition 3.2). It proves that stable critical points of the SW objective cannot concentrate on line segments in d=2: for any measure μ containing a segment S with aḤ¹|S ≤ μ, the perturbation μᵗ = ½(τ₋ₜn#μ + τₜn#μ) satisfies SW₂²(μᵗ, ρ) ≤ SW₂²(μ, ρ) − Ct² for any C > 0 (Proposition 5.2). Numerical experiments with N=100 particles confirm the predicted local maximum under alternating perturbations for three target measures (Gaussian, sliced-uniform, and shell), and show that step-sizes near λ = dN yield the fastest initial convergence while step-sizes exceeding 2dN cause divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without standard method-vs-method benchmarking. The key comparison is with prior theoretical frameworks: the paper extends Sarrazin's (2022) Lagrangian critical point theory from the standard Wasserstein distance W₂ to the Sliced-Wasserstein distance SW₂, proving that the barycentric characterization (Definition 4.2) is equivalent to the directional-derivative definition (Definition 4.1) under compact support and atomlessness (Corollary 4.8). It also extends Bonnotte's (2013) differentiability result for SW from the strong assumption of absolute continuity to the milder assumption of being without atoms (Proposition 4.7c). The descent lemma (Proposition 3.2) parallels and generalizes the Lloyd's algorithm analysis of Mérigot et al. (2021), showing that the optimal step-size λ = Nd for SW₂² mirrors the single-step barycenter update in the W₂ setting. The instability result (Proposition 5.2) is novel and has no direct counterpart in the W₂ literature, as it exploits the specific structure of 1D projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lagrangian critical point framework developed by Sarrazin (2022) for the standard Wasserstein distance to the Sliced-Wasserstein setting, and builds on Bonnotte's (2013) characterization of stationary points of the SW Wasserstein gradient flow. It also connects to the Lloyd's algorithm analysis of Mérigot et al. (2021), showing that the optimal step-size for SW gradient descent (λ = Nd) mirrors the single-step convergence guarantee of Lloyd's algorithm for W₂. The paper departs from prior work by providing the first rigorous critical point analysis for SW optimization dynamics, filling a gap explicitly noted in the literature where 'the behavior of its optimization dynamics remain largely unknown.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical foundation for understanding when and why SW-based optimization algorithms succeed or fail, which is crucial given SW's widespread use in generative modeling and variational inference. The key open question—whether stable critical points of SW must equal the target ρ—remains unresolved, and the instability result (Proposition 5.2) is limited to 2D and uses a perturbation technically outside the Lagrangian framework. Generalizing the instability analysis to higher dimensions and to fully Lagrangian perturbations, and proving that absolutely continuous critical points must equal ρ, are identified as important future directions that would significantly strengthen the theoretical guarantees for SW-based methods.
