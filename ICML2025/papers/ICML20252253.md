## Problem

In online learning with safety constraints, the learner must select actions that satisfy an unknown constraint function at every single round while minimizing regret. Prior work either assumed the constraint was known, restricted to linear constraints with specific feedback models, or relaxed per-timestep safety to long-term aggregate satisfaction. This paper addresses the general problem of arbitrary decision spaces, loss functions, and constraint classes with per-round safety guarantees, and identifies the fundamental complexity that governs whether safe learning is possible.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a context x_t and must output an action a_t that is guaranteed safe (f*(a_t, x_t) ≤ 0) before observing the outcome. The only information about the unknown constraint comes through noisy safety feedback z_t drawn from a signal distribution p_signal(f*(a_t, x_t)), which can be continuous (e.g., f* + Gaussian noise) or discrete (e.g., a coin flip or Bradley-Luce-Shepherd choice among m constraints). The learner is given an initial non-empty safe set A_0(x) per context and two black-box oracles: an online regression oracle for estimating f* and an online learning oracle for minimizing loss over given constraint sets. The output is a sequence of actions that are safe with high probability (1−3δ) and achieve sublinear regret relative to the best safe policy in hindsight.

## Method

The algorithm maintains a version space F_t of candidate safety functions via an online regression oracle, then constructs an optimistic set O_t (actions that could be safe under some f ∈ F_t) and a pessimistic set P_t (actions safe under all f ∈ F_t). It passes O_t to an online learning oracle to obtain a loss-minimizing distribution p̃_t, then applies a mapping M that converts p̃_t into a distribution over P_t, balancing the loss gap against the expected width Δ_{F_t}(a_t, x_t) (a measure of information gain about the constraint). The key theoretical contribution is the complexity measure V_κ, which captures the per-step tension between loss minimization and constraint information acquisition; a matching lower bound shows that when V_κ is bounded away from zero, no safe algorithm can achieve sublinear regret. For linear constraints, the mapping is a simple scaling transformation that shrinks each action toward the origin to guarantee safety, yielding O(√T) regret with the Vovk-Azoury-Warmuth forecaster and online gradient descent.

## Results

The paper establishes a general regret bound of Regret_T ≤ inf_κ {Σ_t V_κ(t) + κ inf_α {αT + 20·Reg_OR·E(F_0,α)/α}} + Reg_OL + √(2T log(1/δ)), where V_κ is a novel per-step complexity measure balancing loss minimization against information gain on the safety constraint. For the linear constraint setting, this yields the first O(√T) regret guarantee: Regret_T = O((d/b)·log(T/d)·√(T·log(δ^{-1}))). A matching asymptotic lower bound shows that whenever the average V_κ(t) is bounded away from zero, no safe algorithm can achieve sublinear regret, establishing the necessity of the complexity measure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's O(√T) regret for linear constraints improves upon Chaudhary & Kalathil (2022), who achieved O(T^{2/3}) regret using an explore-then-exploit (SO-PGD) strategy, by replacing the fixed exploration phase with a per-step scaling transformation that simultaneously balances optimistic exploration and pessimistic constraint satisfaction. This matches the rate of the concurrent work by Hutchinson et al. (2025) but provides a more general framework that extends to arbitrary decision spaces, loss functions, and constraint classes via oracle-based meta-algorithms. Compared to the long-term constraint literature (Mahdavi et al., 2012; Yu & Neely, 2020), which achieves O(√T) regret but only O(1) or O(T^{3/4}) cumulative constraint violation, this paper enforces per-timestep safety with high probability at the cost of the V_κ complexity term. The lower bound (Theorem 4.4) distinguishes this work from prior results by showing that the V_κ term is not an artifact of the analysis but is fundamentally necessary for per-timestep constraint satisfaction, whereas it vanishes entirely under the relaxed long-term constraint objective (Lemma 4.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the safe online learning problem from specific settings (linear bandits, convex optimization with linear constraints) to a fully general framework with arbitrary decision spaces, loss functions, and constraint classes. It builds on the regression oracle paradigm of Foster et al. (2018) and the eluder dimension machinery of Russo & Roy (2013) to construct a meta-algorithm that converts an unconstrained online learning oracle into a safe one via optimistic/pessimistic version spaces. The scaling-based mapping for linear constraints is a randomized variant of the doubly-optimistic method from Hutchinson et al. (2024, 2025), but the key departure is the introduction of the complexity measure V_κ, which explicitly quantifies the per-step tension between loss minimization and constraint information gain — a tradeoff that prior safe-learning algorithms handled heuristically (explore-then-exploit, directional optimism) without a unifying theoretical characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general-purpose framework and matching upper/lower bounds for online learning with unknown per-timestep safety constraints, showing that the novel complexity measure V_κ is both sufficient for sublinear regret and asymptotically necessary. This gives the field a clear diagnostic: safe learning is fundamentally hard precisely when the per-step loss-information tradeoff cannot be resolved, and the measure cleanly separates per-timestep from long-term constraint satisfaction (where V_κ vanishes). Limitations include the non-constructive nature of the general result (relying on existence of oracles), the requirement of a non-empty initial safe set, and the fact that the optimal mapping M involves a saddle-point optimization that may be computationally expensive. Open questions include whether the framework extends to stochastic (rather than adversarial) contexts, whether the initial safe set requirement can be relaxed, and whether constructive algorithms matching the general bound exist beyond the linear and finite-action instantiations.
