## Problem

Spatio-temporal Gaussian processes (STGPs) offer linear-in-time inference via state-space formulations but are highly sensitive to outliers, which are common in financial and weather data. The recently proposed robust conjugate GP (RCGP) framework addresses this but suffers from three practical failures: unreliable performance when the prior mean is misspecified, poor uncertainty quantification, and a shrinking hyperparameter that must be hand-tuned based on an unknown outlier proportion. This paper resolves all three issues while preserving the computational efficiency of STGPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observations on a spatial grid of n_s points in d_s dimensions across n_t time steps, along with kernel hyperparameters (lengthscale, amplitude, smoothness, noise level). At each time step, the weight function is updated adaptively using the current filtering posterior predictive, so the centering and shrinking parameters are data-driven rather than fixed a priori. The output is an exact Gaussian posterior predictive (mean and variance) at any spatio-temporal query point, with computational cost O(n_t · n_s³) time and O(n_t · n_s²) memory—matching standard STGPs rather than the O(N³) cost of batch RCGPs.

## Method

The core contribution is a state-space reformulation of RCGPs where the inverse multi-quadric weight function is made adaptive: at each time step k, the centering function is set to the GB filtering predictive mean γ_k = f̂_k and the shrinking function to the predictive variance c_k² = diag(Ŝ_k), eliminating sensitivity to the prior mean and removing the need to hand-select c. The weighted score-matching loss is shown to be quadratic in the state vector, yielding exact Kalman filter and Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother updates with modified precision and mean terms (involving J_w and ∇_y log w²), so inference remains conjugate and exact. Robustness is established by proving the posterior influence function is bounded under the IMQ weight with the proposed adaptive hyperparameters. Hyperparameter optimization uses a weighted log-likelihood objective φ_GB that down-weights outlier time steps, preventing the standard leave-one-out criterion from overfitting to contaminated observations.

## Results

On the N=46,800-point financial dataset with induced outliers, ST-RCGP achieves RMSE 0.145±0.002 and NLPD −0.62±0.01, substantially outperforming STGP (RMSE 0.54, NLPD 13.9) while matching the accuracy of BayesNewton methods (MEP: RMSE 0.15, NLPD −0.57) at roughly 3× lower computational cost (9.4 s vs. ~29 s total; 0.20 ms vs. ~0.61 ms per step). In well-specified settings (Table 1), ST-RCGP maintains near-STGP efficiency (EWR 0.903 vs. 1.0) with comparable RMSE (0.19) and NLPD (5.7 vs. 6.5), confirming no statistical-efficiency penalty. On UK temperature data with focussed outliers, STGP's NLPD explodes to 41.62 in November, while ST-RCGP stays at 2.44.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-RCGP decisively outperforms the standard STGP on all outlier-contaminated benchmarks: on the 46,800-point financial data, STGP's RMSE degrades from 0.19 (clean) to 0.54, while ST-RCGP holds at 0.145; on UK temperature data, STGP's NLPD jumps to 41.62 in November versus ST-RCGP's 2.44. Against the vanilla RCGP, ST-RCGP wins on robustness (adaptive centering avoids the prior-mean sensitivity that causes RCGP to center on outliers during the DJIA flash crash), on uncertainty quantification (coverage plots in Figure 4 show RCGP is miscalibrated), and on computational cost (linear-in-time SSM vs. cubic batch inversion). Compared to the Relevance Pursuit (RP) method of Ament et al. (2024), ST-RCGP matches predictive quality on the DJIA crash but runs at STGP-level cost, whereas RP costs K× a standard GP. Against the three BayesNewton approximate methods (MEP, MVI, MLa), ST-RCGP achieves the best RMSE (0.145 vs. 0.15–0.17) and best NLPD (−0.62 vs. −0.39 to −0.57) while being roughly 3× faster in both total and per-step time, and does so exactly rather than approximately.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the robust and conjugate GP (RCGP) framework of Altamirano et al. (2024), which uses generalised Bayes with a weighted score-matching loss to achieve outlier robustness while preserving conjugacy, and the state-space model (SSM) formulation of spatio-temporal GPs (Solin, 2016; Hartikainen & Särkkä, 2010), which provides linear-in-time Kalman filtering/smoothing. The key departure from vanilla RCGPs is the introduction of *adaptive* weights indexed by time—centering at the GB filtering predictive mean and shrinking at its variance—which simultaneously resolves three documented failure modes of RCGPs (sensitivity to prior mean, unreliable uncertainty, and manual hyperparameter selection). The robustness guarantee is inherited from the bounded posterior influence function analysis of Altamirano et al. (2024), now extended to the sequential setting via Proposition 3.3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ST-RCGP provides a practically important recipe: outlier-robust spatio-temporal GP inference at the same O(n_t · n_s³) cost as standard STGPs, matching the accuracy of non-conjugate Student-t methods (BayesNewton's MEP/MVI/MLa) while being exact rather than approximate. This is relevant for any application involving spatio-temporal data with measurement anomalies—financial time series, weather forecasting, sensor networks—where robustness is needed but computational budgets preclude expensive non-conjugate inference. Open questions include scaling to large spatial grids (the method still costs O(n_s³) per step), parallelisation via scan algorithms, and extending the framework beyond Gaussian likelihoods to classification or count data.
