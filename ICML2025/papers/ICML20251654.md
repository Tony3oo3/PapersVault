## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) may over-rely on learned visual language priors from training data rather than performing genuine visual reasoning, yet existing benchmarks use internet-sourced images that inadvertently reinforce those same priors. This paper asks whether current VLMs can override strong textual assumptions when confronted with deliberately out-of-distribution visual evidence, and how to train them to do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251654/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For evaluation, the model receives a synthesized out-of-distribution image (generated by DALL-E-3 or Flux) paired with a question that embeds a distractor fact (ViLP^F) or stands alone (ViLP^P), and must produce a single-word answer judged by a synonym/plural-matching pipeline without LLM-based scoring. For Image-DPO training, the input is a question-image-answer triplet where the image is either clean (I_w) or corrupted via Gaussian blur, pixelation, or semantic editing (I_l), while the question and answer remain identical across the pair, forcing the model to discriminate based on visual quality rather than textual content.

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled contributions. First, the ViLP benchmark constructs 900 QIA triplets (300 questions × 3 answers) governed by three formal criteria: the question alone must strongly favor a 'prior' answer, the image must shift the correct answer to a rare 'test' answer, and the prior answer must become clearly wrong given the image; images are synthesized with DALL-E-3 and Flux to be out-of-distribution, and distractor facts are prepended to amplify language priors. Second, Image-DPO is a self-improving preference-optimization objective that, unlike standard DPO which varies the answer while fixing the image, corrupts the image (Gaussian blur, pixelation, semantic editing via InstructPix2Pix and Grounded-SAM) while keeping the question and answer fixed, so gradients push the model to attend to the vision branch. A scalable data pipeline repurposes seed images from COCO, Visual Genome, and Text2VQA through VLM-guided instruction generation and SDXL-based image synthesis to produce large-scale QIA triplets. The authors prove (Proposition 1) that the Image-DPO loss optimizes an upper bound on the KL-constrained RL fine-tuning objective, providing theoretical grounding for the vision-focused preference signal.

## Results

On the ViLP benchmark, humans achieve 98.33% accuracy on ViLP^F-Score, while the best VLM (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) reaches only 70.00%, and GPT-4o scores 66.17%. For the proposed Image-DPO method, LLaVA-1.5-7B improves from 29.67% to 34.17% on ViLP^F-Score (+4.5 points), and Cambrian-8B improves dramatically from 8.67% to 20.83% (+12.16 points). On general VQA benchmarks, Image-DPO also yields consistent gains: LLaVA-1.5-7B improves on NaturalBench (NB_Q: 37.7→39.79, NB_I: 43.8→45.47) and MM-Vet (31.1→32.3), though CHAIR scores slightly decrease (CHAIR_S: 49.1→45.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Image-DPO outperforms all five VLM self-improvement baselines on every ViLP metric. On ViLP^F-Score (the primary metric), Image-DPO achieves 34.17%, beating HADPO (33.00%, +1.17), EOS (31.33%, +2.84), V-DPO (29.50%, +4.67), RLHF-V (29.50%, +4.67), and SIMA (27.83%, +6.34). The Text-DPO ablation (31.34%) confirms that the improvement stems from the image-corruption objective itself rather than merely training on perturbed data. On ViLP^F-Prior, Image-DPO also leads at 75.00%, matching RLHF-V's 75.00% but exceeding all others. The gains are most dramatic for Cambrian-8B, where Image-DPO lifts ViLP^F-Score from 8.67% to 20.83% (+12.16), far surpassing any baseline's improvement on that model. On general benchmarks (Table 3), Image-DPO consistently improves NaturalBench and MM-Vet scores while slightly degrading CHAIR hallucination metrics, suggesting a trade-off between visual grounding and hallucination suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) by shifting the preference signal from the answer axis to the image axis: instead of training a VLM to prefer good answers over bad ones for a fixed image (Text-DPO), Image-DPO corrupts the image while holding the Q&A pair constant, and proves the resulting objective optimizes an upper bound on the standard RL fine-tuning loss. The self-improvement data pipeline builds on the self-rewarding LLM paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024) but repurposes it for vision by generating multiple images per question via SDXL, InstructPix2Pix, and Grounded-SAM rather than generating multiple textual answers. On the benchmark side, ViLP departs from prior VQA diagnostic datasets (Winoground, Whoops!, HallusionBench) by using image-generation models (DALL-E-3, Flux) to synthesize out-of-distribution visuals and by explicitly engineering strong language priors via distractor facts, rather than relying on internet-sourced images that inadvertently align with training distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViLP provides a focused, reproducible diagnostic for a persistent and practically important failure mode—VLMs answering from text priors rather than visual evidence—and the ~32-point human-vs-GPT-4o gap quantifies how far current models remain from genuine visual reasoning. Image-DPO offers a self-improving training recipe that requires no human preference labels and yields consistent (if modest, ~4–5 point) gains across LLaVA and Cambrian, suggesting a practical path for open-source VLMs to reduce prior reliance. Key limitations include the small benchmark size (300 questions), the reliance on cartoon-style synthetic images (ablations show photorealistic images increase difficulty further), and the modest absolute improvements, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse visual domains. The counterintuitive finding that stronger models (GPT-4o) benefit from distractor facts while weaker ones are misled also raises open questions about how instruction-following and prior-reliance interact across model scales.
