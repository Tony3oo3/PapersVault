## Problem

Existing self-supervised time series methods cannot simultaneously capture long-term dynamic evolution and subtle local patterns in a unified manner: masked autoencoders suffer pre-training/fine-tuning inconsistency, contrastive methods limit fine-grained temporal variation, and autoregressive methods overfit noise while their MSE objective implicitly assumes a unimodal Gaussian distribution that poorly reflects real-world multimodal time series behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252479/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape C×L, which are instance-normalized per channel and partitioned into non-overlapping patches of length P (equal to stride S to preserve autoregressive causality). During pre-training, each patch independently receives Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled diffusion step, and the model outputs denoised patch reconstructions. For downstream tasks, the pre-trained encoder produces temporal representations that are mapped to forecasted values (via a flatten head) or class labels (via a max-pooling head).

## Method

TimeDART unifies autoregressive optimization with a patch-level denoising diffusion process. A causal Transformer encoder processes non-overlapping patch embeddings left-to-right, while a cross-attention denoising decoder takes noisy patch embeddings as queries and encoder outputs as keys/values, with a self-only mask ensuring each noisy patch j only attends to encoder position j (which already aggregates causal context from patches 1 to j−1). Noise is independently added to each patch at a different diffusion step to prevent the sequence from retaining invariant statistics and to avoid task oversimplification. The standard MSE objective is replaced by a diffusion loss (equivalent to the ELBO), which rationalizes the squared error through gradual noise addition and removal, enabling the model to express a richer multimodal belief over time series. A cosine noise scheduler is used for smoother transitions, and clean and noisy patches share the same embedding layer to help the model distinguish intentional noise from inherent data anomalies.

## Results

On the forecasting task across 12 datasets, TimeDART achieves the best MSE in 83.3% of 24 evaluation metrics (MSE and MAE across 4 prediction horizons per dataset), with a 6.8% average MSE reduction over random initialization and 3% over the strongest self-supervised baseline. Representative numbers include MSE 0.346 on ETTh2, 0.257 on ETTm2, 0.163 on Electricity, and 0.133 on PEMS04. On classification, TimeDART reaches 92.47% accuracy on HAR, 97.12% on Epilepsy, and 82.69% on EEG, improving average accuracy by ~5.7% over random initialization and surpassing the supervised FormerTime baseline on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeDART outperforms all four self-supervised baselines (SimMTM, PatchTST-SSL, TimeMAE, CoST) on the majority of forecasting datasets, with the largest margins on Exchange (MSE 0.359 vs. 0.451 for SimMTM) and PEMS08 (0.201 vs. 0.215 for CoST). Against supervised methods, TimeDART beats DLinear on every dataset (e.g., 0.133 vs. 0.290 on PEMS04) and matches or exceeds PatchTST on most, though PatchTST-SSL edges out TimeDART on ETTm1 (0.342 vs. 0.344). On classification, TimeDART surpasses the supervised FormerTime on all three datasets (HAR: 92.47% vs. 88.16%, Epilepsy: 97.12% vs. 93.15%, EEG: 82.69% vs. 81.02% accuracy). Notably, even TimeDART with random initialization (no pre-training) remains competitive with supervised PatchTST on several datasets, underscoring that the vanilla Transformer backbone is not the limiting factor. The ablation confirms both the autoregressive mechanism and the diffusion denoising process are essential: removing either degrades performance, and removing both yields results worse than random initialization on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeDART unifies two previously separate threads in time series modeling: autoregressive pre-training (as in Timer, Liu et al. 2024c) and denoising diffusion (as in DDPM, Ho et al. 2020; TimeGrad, Rasul et al. 2021). It departs from standard autoregressive self-supervised methods by replacing the MSE reconstruction loss with a diffusion-based ELBO loss, motivated by the observation that MSE implicitly assumes a Gaussian data distribution that causes prediction collapse. The patch-level independent noise addition strategy and the cross-attention denoising decoder with a self-only mask are novel architectural choices that distinguish TimeDART from both masked autoencoder approaches (TimeMAE, SimMTM) and prior diffusion-based time series models (CSDI, TimeDiff), which focused on probabilistic forecasting rather than representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining autoregressive structure with diffusion denoising yields consistently better self-supervised representations than either paradigm alone, validated across nine datasets spanning energy, transportation, finance, and biomedical domains. The ablation finding that removing the autoregressive mechanism degrades performance below random initialization underscores that causal temporal structure is not merely helpful but essential for meaningful representation learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model sizes and longer sequences, whether the diffusion component's benefit persists at scale, and how the method generalizes to streaming or online settings. The reliance on a 2-layer encoder and moderate dataset sizes limits confidence in the method's behavior at the scale of foundation models.
