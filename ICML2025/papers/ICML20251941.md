## Problem

Existing permutation equivariant neural networks have not considered symmetric tensors as inputs, and prior work on learning from symmetric tensors has focused exclusively on equivariance to Euclidean groups (SO(n), SE(3), etc.) rather than to the permutation group S_n. No complete characterisation of all linear permutation equivariant functions between symmetric power spaces of R^n existed, leaving a gap for applications in statistics, graph theory, and materials science where symmetric tensors are the natural input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a k-order symmetric tensor T ∈ (R^n)^{⊗k} (i.e., T_{i_1,...,i_k} = T_{i_{π(1)},...,i_{π(k)}} for all π ∈ S_k) and produces an l-order symmetric tensor in (R^n)^{⊗l}. The weight matrix is represented in its 'unrolled' form as a map from the full tensor power space (R^n)^{⊗k} to (R^n)^{⊗l}, where entries are filled by propagating the symmetric tensor's values across all index permutations. The output can be a scalar (l=0, invariant task), a vector (l=1, equivariant task), or a higher-order symmetric tensor (l>1).

## Method

The paper provides a complete characterisation of Hom_{S_n}(S^k(R^n), S^l(R^n)) using two bases: an orbit basis indexed by (k,l)-bipartitions with at most n blocks (derived from S_n orbits on S[n]^l × S[n]^k), and a more computationally efficient diagram basis derived from the spherical partition algebra SP_k^l(n), where each diagram basis element D_π is a sum of orbit basis elements over a partial order. The dimension of the space is p_n(k,l), the number of (k,l)-bipartitions with at most n blocks. Crucially, the authors introduce 'map label notation' to avoid storing large weight matrices in memory: each basis matrix's action on an input tensor is expressed as a compact set of index equations (e.g., D_π(T)_i = Σ_j T_{i,j} + T_{j,i} − T_{i,i}), and when n ≥ k+l, a single set of map labels works for all dimensions n without regenerating weight matrices. The approach is validated on two synthetic tasks (S_12-invariant and S_8-equivariant), showing high data efficiency over standard MLPs and generalisation to unseen tensor sizes.

## Results

On the S₈-equivariant diagonal extraction task, the proposed SymmPermEquiv model achieves a test MSE of 0.0035, compared to 0.0447 for a standard permutation-equivariant MLP (PermEquiv) and 0.6486 for a plain MLP. On the S₁₂-invariant task (computing f(T) = Σᵢⱼ T_{i,j,i} on 3-order symmetric tensors), the model demonstrates substantially higher data efficiency than a standard MLP, as shown by its learning curve in Figure 3. The model also generalises to unseen tensor sizes: when trained on n=8, it achieves test MSE of 0.0048 at n=16 and 0.0088 at n=32, with only a modest degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three methods on the S₈-equivariant diagonal extraction task: a standard MLP, a standard permutation-equivariant model (PermEquiv), and the proposed symmetric-tensor-aware model (SymmPermEquiv). SymmPermEquiv achieves a test MSE of 0.0035, outperforming PermEquiv (0.0447) by a factor of approximately 12.8× and the plain MLP (0.6486) by a factor of approximately 185×. The key advantage is that SymmPermEquiv explicitly encodes the symmetric tensor structure, reducing the number of free parameters and providing a stronger inductive bias. On the S₁₂-invariant task, the learning curve (Figure 3) shows the proposed model converges to lower loss with far fewer training samples than the MLP baseline, confirming the data-efficiency claim. Additionally, the model trained on 8×8×8 tensors generalises to 16×16×16 and 32×32×32 tensors with only a small increase in MSE (0.0048 and 0.0088 respectively), suggesting the map label notation enables size-agnostic inference without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: permutation equivariant neural networks (Deep Sets, Maron et al., Ravanbakhsh et al.) and equivariant learning on symmetric tensors (Gao et al., Lou & Ganose, Wen et al., Garanger et al.). The key departure is that prior symmetric-tensor work focused exclusively on Euclidean group equivariance (SO(n), SE(3)), while prior permutation equivariant work treated sets or graphs as inputs rather than symmetric tensors. The paper's algebraic machinery draws on MacMahon's bipartition theory and the recently introduced spherical partition algebra (Bastias et al., 2024), and it recovers the Deep Sets characterisation as a corollary, confirming consistency with the existing permutation equivariant literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete characterisation via bipartitions provides a principled, finite-dimensional parameterisation of all linear permutation equivariant maps between symmetric powers, which is a foundational result for building equivariant architectures on symmetric tensor data (e.g., elasticity tensors, hypergraph adjacency tensors, covariance structures). The map label notation is a practical contribution that eliminates the need to store exponentially large weight matrices, enabling the same set of equations to work across different tensor dimensions. However, the empirical validation is limited to two synthetic toy tasks, and the paper does not address nonlinear equivariant functions, scalability of the bipartition count for large k and l, or comparison against other equivariant architectures on real-world problems—these remain important open questions.
