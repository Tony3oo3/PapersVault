## Problem

Selecting the best LMM for a new deployment domain typically requires labeled benchmarks, which are expensive to curate and may not match the target distribution. The paper demonstrates that the standard Accuracy-on-the-Line approach—using performance on one existing benchmark to predict performance on another—exhibits highly variable and often weak cross-dataset correlations for LMMs, making it unreliable for model selection in label-scarce settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes image+text VQA or MCVQ prompts and the token-level logits (softmax probabilities) produced by each LMM during autoregressive generation, requiring no ground-truth answers. For self-consistency variants, it additionally requires T=5 stochastic inferences per sample at temperature 0.7. The output is a single scalar score per model (e.g., mean NLL_max across all samples), validated against true benchmark accuracy via Spearman's ρ and weighted Kendall's τ_w to confirm ranking fidelity.

## Method

The paper proposes three families of label-free proxy scores for ranking LMMs: (1) output-probability-based scores computed from token-level softmax distributions—NLL or normalized entropy at the first token, penultimate token, maximum, or mean across all generated tokens; (2) self-consistency scores measuring agreement across stochastic samples via unigram BLEU or BERTScore; and (3) ATC, which thresholds uncertainty on a labeled proxy set to estimate target-domain accuracy. The key finding is that NLL_max and NLL_avg (aggregating over all generated tokens) achieve the strongest and most stable correlation with true performance (ρ up to 0.93), outperforming both AoL and entropy-based variants, because they capture the least-confident token's probability without being confounded by vocabulary-size differences across models. The approach requires only access to model outputs (logits and generated tokens), making it applicable to API-based or closed-source LMMs without architectural introspection.

## Results

Across 9 visual question answering benchmarks and 45+ LMMs, NLL_max (maximum negative log-likelihood over generated tokens) achieves the strongest average Spearman's rank correlation of ρ=0.76 with true model performance, followed closely by NLL_avg (ρ=0.76). On individual benchmarks, NLL_max reaches ρ=0.94 on ChartQA, ρ=0.92 on TextVQA, and ρ=0.91 on AI2D. The accuracy-based baseline AoL achieves only ρ=0.60 on average, while ATC reaches ρ=0.63, confirming that uncertainty-derived scores substantially outperform proxy-benchmark accuracy for unsupervised model ranking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two external baselines: AoL (Miller et al., 2021), which uses a model's accuracy on one benchmark to predict its ranking on another, and ATC (Garg et al., 2022), which thresholds uncertainty on a labeled proxy set to estimate target-domain accuracy. NLL_max decisively outperforms AoL, achieving an average ρ of 0.76 versus AoL's 0.60, and surpasses it on 7 of 9 benchmarks (e.g., ρ=0.92 vs 0.69 on TextVQA; ρ=0.91 vs 0.73 on AI2D). AoL's only competitive result is on RealWorldQA (ρ=0.70), where NLL_max still reaches ρ=0.63 with a higher τ_w. ATC is more variable: it achieves the best single-benchmark score on GQA (ρ=0.95) but collapses to ρ=0.28 on ChartQA and ρ=0.39 on MMMU, yielding an average of only 0.63. The paper attributes ATC's instability to the sensitivity of its threshold to the choice of proxy dataset, whereas NLL_max requires no proxy and remains stable across all task types (MCVQ and VQA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts uncertainty-based classifier ranking techniques from Tu et al. (2024b) and Hu et al. (2024) to the fundamentally different setting of large multimodal models, which generate open-ended token sequences rather than fixed-class predictions. It positions itself against the Accuracy-on-the-Line (AoL) phenomenon (Miller et al., 2021) and Average Thresholded Confidence (ATC, Garg et al., 2022), showing that while these methods work for conventional classifiers, AoL is unreliable for LMMs due to the lack of accessible in-distribution metrics and the variability of cross-benchmark performance correlations. The paper does not introduce a new uncertainty estimation method; rather, it provides the empirical insight that existing softmax-based uncertainty scores (NLL, entropy) and self-consistency measures (BLEU, BERTScore) already encode sufficient signal for unsupervised LMM ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LMMs proliferate across dozens of architectures and training recipes, practitioners face a practical bottleneck: selecting the best model for a new deployment domain without expensive manual annotation. This paper demonstrates that a simple NLL_max score computed from model logits achieves ρ≈0.92 correlation with true performance, offering a label-free, low-compute selection tool. Key open questions remain: whether internal model states (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023) can provide stronger signals, how methods perform for API-only models without logit access, and whether the observed text-prompt-dominance over image features in cross-dataset correlation can inform better benchmark design. The finding that AoL fails for LMMs also cautions against assuming transferability of model-selection heuristics across model families.
