## Problem

Existing knowledge editing (KE) methods fine-tune LLMs on single training instances using standard cross-entropy loss, which treats all tokens in the target equally. This causes heterogeneous token overfitting (HTO): tokens that were already well-fitted by pre-training get overfitted faster than genuinely new tokens, degrading the edited model's ability to reason about the new knowledge (portability). Prior work identified KE overfitting but left its token-level mechanism unexplored and lacked a principled, model-agnostic mitigation.

## Input / Output

OVERTONE is a drop-in training loss that replaces the standard CE objective in any existing KE method (FT-M, LoRA, MELO, WISE). It consumes the same editing pair (x, y) and pre-trained parameters, plus three hyperparameters: λ (mixing weight between the delta target and the model's own filtered prediction), ε (per-token early-stopping threshold on KL divergence), and n (top-nσ filtering threshold for removing noisy logits). The output is the edited parameter set with the same structure as the base KE method, requiring no architectural changes or additional preference data.

## Method

OVERTONE replaces each token's delta target δ_{y_i} with an adaptive smoothed distribution π_tar that mixes δ_{y_i} with a top-nσ-filtered version of the model's own current prediction π_flr, weighted by λ. The loss is a per-token clipped forward KL divergence D_KL[π_tar ‖ π_θ] with a floor ε, which acts as token-level early stopping once the model is close enough to the target. A skip mechanism (Eq. 3) discards the mixture whenever the filtered distribution would assign higher probability to a non-target token, preventing conflicts with outdated internal knowledge. Theoretically, OVERTONE generalizes CE (reducing to it when ε=0, λ=1), yields a gradient direction closer to the ideal retraining direction via influence-function analysis, and is shown to be an unbiased estimator of a DPO objective with an additional KL penalty—without requiring any preference data pairs.

## Results

On LLaMA 2-7b-chat, OVERTONE dramatically improves portability and locality across all four training-based KE methods. For example, LoRA on ZsRE sees portability nearly triple (23.34 → 61.16) and locality jump from 30.44 to 87.2, while on Wiki_counterfact its locality surges from 9.99 to 57.85. All OVERTONE-enhanced methods surpass the analytic baselines ROME and MEMIT on average score across all four single-editing benchmarks. On the more complex MQuAKE multi-hop reasoning task, LoRA's multi-hop score improves from 39.0 to 48.0 (LLaMA 2) and from 35.0 to 40.0 (LLaMA 3) with OVERTONE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OVERTONE-enhanced training-based methods (FT-M, LoRA, MELO, WISE) against both their unenhanced versions and the analytic baselines ROME and MEMIT. Without OVERTONE, the four training-based methods are generally comparable to or below ROME/MEMIT on average; with OVERTONE, all four consistently surpass both analytic baselines across all four single-editing benchmarks and both LLMs. The largest gains appear in portability (e.g., LoRA on LLaMA 2 ZsRE: 23.34 → 61.16) and locality (e.g., LoRA on Wiki_counterfact: 9.99 → 57.85), confirming that HTO was the primary bottleneck. On MQuAKE multi-hop reasoning, OVERTONE-enhanced LoRA and FT-M also improve over their base versions (e.g., LoRA multi-hop: 39.0 → 48.0 on LLaMA 2), though absolute multi-hop scores remain modest. In continual editing, OVERTONE enables LoRA and FT-M to reach performance on par with specialized continual-editing methods (MELO, WISE), whereas vanilla LoRA was previously considered unsuitable for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the knowledge editing (KE) literature where methods like ROME, MEMIT, LoRA, FT-M, MELO, and WISE update LLM parameters to incorporate new knowledge while preserving locality. It departs from prior overfitting-mitigation work (Zhang et al. 2024a, Zhong et al. 2023) that identified portability degradation but treated it as a monolithic phenomenon; here the authors decompose it into a token-level mechanism (heterogeneous token overfitting) and address it with an adaptive per-token smoothing objective. The method repurposes top-nσ logit filtering (Tang et al. 2024, originally an inference-time technique) into the training loop, and draws a formal connection to DPO (Rafailov et al. 2024) without requiring preference pairs, while grounding the update-quality claim in influence-function analysis (Koh & Liang 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVERTONE is a plug-and-play training paradigm that improves portability and locality across all four tested KE methods and two LLMs with negligible O(|V|) overhead, making it immediately adoptable by the KE community without architectural changes. The identification of HTO as the root cause of portability loss provides a principled lens for future work, and the DPO connection suggests potential transfer to machine unlearning and other selective-update settings. Open questions include whether HTO worsens with longer editing texts, how it interacts with specialized losses in more complex KE methods, and whether it can be unified with other overfitting forms (answer-level, neighboring-knowledge). The authors also call for more rigorous experimental design (multiple runs per instance) in the KE community.
