## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for the Deep Ritz Method (DRM) and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) rely on standard Rademacher complexity, yielding slow n^{-1/2} rates, or on peeling arguments that require strong convexity of the objective and are restricted to specific PDEs (e.g., Schrödinger with zero Dirichlet boundary). This paper asks whether sharper, fast-rate generalization bounds can be derived for both DRM (Poisson and Schrödinger with Neumann boundary) and PINNs (general linear second-order elliptic with Dirichlet boundary) without imposing strong convexity, and whether the dimension dependence can be made explicit and polynomial.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input the PDE coefficients (source term f ∈ L^∞, potential V ∈ L^∞ with V_min > 0, boundary data g), n Monte Carlo samples drawn uniformly from the domain Ω = (0,1)^d (and N₂ samples from ∂Ω for PINNs), and a parameterized neural network function class (two-layer ReLU with m neurons for Barron-space solutions, or deep ReLU with width O(N log N) and depth O(L log L) for Sobolev-space solutions). The output is not a numerical PDE solution but a high-probability upper bound on the energy excess (DRM) or the PINN loss, with explicit rates in n, d, and the function-class complexity parameters (m, N, L, K). For the Poisson equation, the bound involves a log M factor from the L^∞ norm of f and the Barron norm of the solution.

## Method

For the DRM, the key technical obstacle is that the expectation of the empirical loss does not equal the population variational functional for the Poisson equation (due to the (∫u dx)² term), which blocks direct application of local Rademacher complexity. The authors introduce a novel error decomposition that isolates this discrepancy and controls it via Hoeffding's inequality and the fact that ∫u* dx = 0, then apply a peeling argument with ρ₀ = 1/n to obtain fast rates. For the Schrödinger equation, where the expectation does match the energy functional, they use a different decomposition to invoke local Rademacher complexity directly. For PINNs, the framework is recast as multi-task learning (interior PDE residual task + boundary condition task), and the authors derive a new Talagrand-type concentration inequality via the entropy method that improves the constants of Yousefi et al. (2018); combined with the non-negativity of the loss, this yields a non-exact oracle inequality without requiring strong convexity. The approximation side shows that two-layer ReLU networks with controlled weights approximate B²(Ω) functions in H¹ norm at rate m^{-(1/2 + 1/(3d))} with a dimension-independent constant, and deep ReLU networks approximate W^{k,∞} functions at near-optimal Sobolev rates. In the over-parameterized regime, convex hull entropy (extending the Vaart–Wellner theorem to H¹) recovers meaningful n^{-1/2 + 1/(2(3d+1))} rates.

## Results

For the DRM on the Poisson and Schrödinger equations with Neumann boundary conditions, the paper achieves a generalization error rate of $(d/n)^{(3d+2)/(2(3d+1))}\log n$ when solutions lie in Barron space $\mathcal{B}^2(\Omega)$, and $n^{-(2k-2)/(d+2k-2)}(\log n)^6$ for solutions in $W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)$. For PINNs on general linear second-order elliptic PDEs with Dirichlet boundary conditions, the rates are $(1/n)^{(3d+2)/(2(3d+1))}\log n$ (Barron space $\mathcal{B}^3$) and $n^{-(2k-4)/(d+2k-4)}\log n$ (Sobolev space $W^{k,\infty}$, $k>3$). In the over-parameterized regime, the DRM achieves a rate of $n^{-(1/2+1/(2(3d+1)))}$ for Barron-space solutions, with dimension dependence only polynomial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon Duan et al. (2021) for the DRM by tightening the Sobolev-space rate from $n^{-(2k-2)/(d+4k-4)}$ to $n^{-(2k-2)/(d+2k-2)}$, effectively halving the denominator's dependence on $k$. For Barron-space solutions, the rate $(d/n)^{(3d+2)/(2(3d+1))}\log n$ dominates the $n^{-1/3}$ rate of Lu et al. (2021c) for all $d\geq 1$, and unlike Lu et al. (2021c) the constant is dimension-independent and the Barron space is strictly larger than the spectral Barron space. For PINNs, the rate $n^{-(2k-4)/(d+2k-4)}\log n$ matches Lu et al. (2021b) but removes the strong convexity requirement and extends from the single-task Schrödinger equation with zero Dirichlet BC to general linear second-order elliptic PDEs with Dirichlet BC via a multi-task learning framework. Compared to Jiao et al. (2021), the PINN rate improves from $n^{-(2k-4)/(d+4k-8)}$ to $n^{-(2k-4)/(d+2k-4)}$. The Talagrand-type concentration inequality for MTL (Theorem 3.2) yields better constants than Theorem 1 in Yousefi et al. (2018) by a factor of 2 in the Rademacher term and 4 in the variance term when $\alpha=1$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of neural-network-based PDE solvers initiated by Lu et al. (2021c) for the DRM and Jiao et al. (2021) for PINNs, replacing their standard Rademacher complexity arguments with localized (peeling) techniques to obtain sharper convergence rates. It departs from Lu et al. (2021b) by removing the strong convexity requirement and by handling the Poisson equation, where the expectation of the empirical loss does not equal the population energy functional—a mismatch that necessitates a novel error decomposition. For PINNs, the paper reframes the problem as multi-task learning and derives a new Talagrand-type concentration inequality via the entropy method, improving the constants in Yousefi et al. (2018) and extending the analysis from the single-task Schrödinger equation to general linear second-order elliptic PDEs with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The refined bounds (e.g., n^{-(2k-2)/(d+2k-2)} for Sobolev solutions vs. the prior n^{-(2k-2)/(d+4k-4)}) provide tighter theoretical guarantees that the DRM and PINNs can overcome the curse of dimensionality when solutions lie in Barron or Sobolev spaces, giving practitioners more confidence in the statistical efficiency of these methods. However, the analysis is restricted to specific PDE classes (Poisson, Schrödinger with Neumann; general elliptic with Dirichlet), assumes bounded coefficients, and does not address optimization error or the practical difficulty of computing higher-order Sobolev norms in the loss. Open questions include extending the framework to time-dependent PDEs, non-convex loss landscapes, and PINN variants like XPINNs where cross-region sample correlations break the standard MTL structure.
