## Problem

Existing fast incomplete multi-view clustering methods treat graph construction, anchor learning, and graph partition as separate sequential stages, which degrades anchor quality and prevents mutual reinforcement among these components. Additionally, most approaches ignore the structural relation between the anchor graph and the similarity matrix in symmetric nonnegative matrix factorization, forcing an extra graph partition step that increases both computation and memory cost for large-scale datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes v incomplete multi-view matrices {X_p} where each view p has dimension d_p × n_p (n_p ≤ n), along with binary indicator matrices A_p ∈ {0,1}^{n×n_p} marking which samples are available per view. It directly outputs a binary cluster assignment matrix F ∈ {0,1}^{k×n} (one-hot per column) without requiring a separate spectral clustering step. The internal anchor graph Z ∈ R^{m×n} with m << n replaces the full n×n similarity matrix, keeping space complexity at O(mn) rather than O(n²).

## Method

FIML formulates a single joint objective (Eq. 3) that simultaneously optimizes a shared nonnegative anchor graph Z (column-sum-to-1), view-specific orthogonal projection matrices B_p serving as consensus anchor guidance, a centroid matrix G (orthogonal), a one-hot cluster assignment F, and adaptive view weights α_p. The key structural insight is relating Z to the SNMF similarity matrix by dropping the symmetry constraint (justified since m << n), so each entry Z_{ij} directly encodes the similarity between anchor i and sample j, enabling the factorization Z ≈ GF to yield the final partition without an additional graph partition step. An alternating optimization algorithm solves each subproblem in closed form: B_p via SVD of a Hadamard-product matrix, Z via KKT conditions yielding z_i = max(y_i + σ_i·1, 0), G via SVD of ZF^T, F via nearest-centroid assignment, and α_p via Cauchy-Schwarz. The overall time complexity is O((m²d + mnd + mnk + mk²)t), which is nearly linear in n since m << n and m << k.

## Results

FIML achieves the best ACC across all six datasets, reaching 78.84% on ORL, 91.40% on 20 Newsgroups, 93.00% on WebKB, 78.30% on STL-10, 98.90% on MNIST, and 99.50% on CIFAR-100. On the large-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-100), most baselines fail with out-of-memory errors, while FIML completes in 18.4 s and 35.0 s respectively. The ablation study confirms that the unified framework (integrating graph construction, anchor learning, and graph partition) yields substantial gains over a separated pipeline, e.g., 78.84% vs. 70.60% ACC on ORL and 99.50% vs. 90.40% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIML outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, F1-score, Purity). The strongest competitor is FIMVC-VIA (Liu et al., 2022), which uses view-independent anchors; FIML leads it by 2.54 pp on ORL (78.84 vs. 76.30), 1.70 pp on NGs (91.40 vs. 89.70), and 0.60 pp on CIFAR-100 (99.50 vs. 98.90). IMVC-CBG (Wang et al., 2022) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing FIML by 9.34 pp on ORL and 6.50 pp on CIFAR-100. Traditional methods such as BSV (Ng et al., 2001) and MIC (Shao et al., 2015) are far behind, with BSV scoring only 24.30% ACC on ORL and 41.20% on NGs. On the large-scale MNIST and CIFAR-100 datasets, seven of the ten baselines (BSV, MIC, MKKM-IK, APMC, PIC, EEIMVC, V³H) fail entirely due to out-of-memory errors, while FIML completes in 18.4 s and 35.0 s respectively—faster than FIMVC-VIA (20.2 s / 47.0 s) and IMVC-CBG (552 s / 815 s). DAIMIC (Hu & Chen, 2018) is competitive on NGs (89.50% ACC) but collapses on STL-10 (23.00%) and WebKB (N/A), highlighting its sensitivity to dataset characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIML sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: anchor-based scalable clustering (building on Wang et al.'s consensus anchor guidance for multi-view subspace clustering) and symmetric nonnegative matrix factorization for direct graph partition (building on Qin et al.'s block-diagonal guided SNMF). The key conceptual departure is that prior incomplete multi-view clustering methods treat anchor selection, graph construction, and spectral partition as sequential, decoupled stages, whereas FIML formulates all three as jointly optimized variables in a single objective. Additionally, the paper explicitly bridges the anchor graph with the SNMF similarity matrix—a connection that prior anchor-based incomplete multi-view methods (e.g., FIMVC-VIA, IMVC-CBG) ignored by performing graph partition directly on the anchor graph without relating it to the factorization structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in scalability: FIML achieves near-linear time complexity O(n) and handles large-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-100) where many competing methods fail with out-of-memory errors, while simultaneously improving clustering accuracy. The ablation showing that the unified framework substantially outperforms the separated pipeline (e.g., MNIST ACC jumping from 84.6% to 98.9%) provides evidence that joint optimization of anchors, graph, and partition is not merely a convenience but a substantive source of information. Open questions include whether the SNMF-based partition step generalizes to settings with very high missing ratios (>80%), whether the orthogonal constraint on G is necessary or merely sufficient, and whether the approach extends naturally to deep or contrastive representations of views.
