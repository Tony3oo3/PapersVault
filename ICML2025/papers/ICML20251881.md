## Problem

MLLMs are known to degrade under distribution shifts (domain-specific images, linguistic variations, joint modality shifts), yet existing analyses provide only coarse empirical evaluations without a formal framework to characterize the underlying causes of performance degradation. This paper asks whether a theoretical framework can be derived to quantify the maximum risk of MLLMs under distribution shifts and connect performance gaps to measurable distributional discrepancies in input and output spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal queries (RGB images and text) along with model-generated and reference responses, projecting them into embedding spaces via frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 and XLM-RoBERTa-Base encoders. A CLUB-based MI estimator is trained once on an integrated dataset spanning all shift scenarios (~5,000–9,000 samples), then reused to compute EMI scores and EMID values for each ID-OOD pair without retraining. The upper bound is constructed from RJSD estimates of visual/textual marginal divergences and output distribution discrepancies, with a scale-adjusted form (replacing the intractable entropy term with a constant ≈4) for practical Pearson correlation analysis.

## Method

The core contribution is Effective Mutual Information (EMI), defined as I(P_X ⊗ P_θ) − I(P_XY), which subtracts the ground-truth input-output MI from the model's MI to remove domain-dependent scale, analogous to excess risk in classification. The paper proves EMI is closely related to the LLM-judge relative preference score via a logit Bradley-Terry preference model (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.4), establishing EMI as a theory-grounded, 138× faster alternative to GPT-4-based evaluation. For distribution shift analysis, EMID (the EMI difference between ID and OOD) is upper-bounded by JS divergences of visual and textual marginal distributions plus output discrepancy terms (Theorem 4.5), with a more general bound incorporating conditional dependency shifts between modalities (Theorem 4.6). The framework is validated across 61 shift scenarios (34 synthetic, 27 natural) with six MLLMs, showing strong Spearman/Pearson correlations between EMI and RP scores and between EMID and its upper bound. The EMID upper bound is also instantiated as a regularization term during instruction tuning, encouraging intermediate representations to shrink toward a Gaussian prior and improving shift robustness while preserving ID performance.

## Results

Across 61 distribution shift scenarios (34 synthetic, 27 natural) and four LLaVA-series MLLMs, EMI exhibits strong Spearman rank correlation with the LLM-judged relative preference (RP) score, ranging from ρ = 0.457 to 0.794 (all p < 0.014). The empirical EMID correlates significantly with its theoretical upper bound (Theorem 4.5), with Pearson r between 0.550 and 0.807 (all p < 0.003). On the medical domain (LLaVA-Med), the EMID-to-upper-bound correlation reaches r = 0.930. Using EMI as a training-time regularizer improves OOD robustness (e.g., joint shift RP score from 59.6 to 60.8) while preserving ID performance (72.7 unchanged).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the LLM-as-a-judge relative preference (RP) score from Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which relies on a black-box judge model (e.g., GPT-4o). EMI is validated as a theoretically grounded alternative: it achieves strong Spearman correlations with RP (ρ = 0.794 for LLaVA v1.5 7B on synthetic shifts, down to 0.457 for LLaVA NeXT 13B on natural shifts), all statistically significant. Against GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024) as the judge, EMI provides a 138× per-instance speedup (0.039s vs. 5.49s) and remains 3× faster even after including one-time MI estimator training (14.6s vs. 2008.5s total for four models on LLaVA-Bench COCO). The framework also extends to advanced MLLMs: Qwen2.5-VL-7B shows ρ = 0.767 (EMI↔RP) and r = 0.672 (EMID↔UB), while InternVL2.5-7B shows weaker but still significant correlations (ρ = 0.375, r = 0.810), confirming generality across model architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first information-theoretic framework for formally characterizing MLLM performance under distribution shifts, a problem that prior work (Zhang et al. 2024a, Zhou et al. 2024, Zhai et al. 2024) addressed only through coarse empirical evaluations without theoretical grounding. Conceptually, it repurposes the excess risk / effective robustness paradigm (Taori et al. 2020) to the generative MLLM setting by defining Effective Mutual Information (EMI) as MI minus a domain-dependent baseline, and connects it to the widely-used relative preference score via a logit Bradley-Terry preference model. The distribution-shift analysis draws on information-theoretic approaches to domain adaptation (Federici et al. 2021, Shui et al. 2022) but extends them from discriminative classifiers to autoregressive multimodal generators, and from KL-based bounds to Jensen-Shannon divergence bounds over both marginal and conditional multimodal distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, theory-grounded alternative to expensive LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (138× faster than GPT-4o scoring), and the EMID upper bound doubles as a practical regularizer that modestly improves OOD robustness during instruction tuning. Key limitations include: EMI captures only input-output relevance (not faithfulness, safety, or other quality facets), the derived bounds are acknowledged as not tight and no lower bound is provided, and the simplified scenario (Theorem 4.5) assumes consistent conditionals across ID/OOD, which may not hold for complex spurious-correlation shifts. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-faceted evaluation, tightening the bounds, and analyzing shifts driven by spurious correlations that fall under the more general Theorem 4.6.
