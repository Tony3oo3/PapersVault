## Problem

Replay-based class-incremental learning methods achieve strong performance but require storing raw exemplars, leading to high memory consumption. Existing memory-efficient replay methods compress images in the high-dimensional pixel space (e.g., downsampling, patch masking), which is inefficient because natural images have low intrinsic dimensionality and strong local pixel correlations. Tensor decomposition has not been explored as a compression mechanism for CIL exemplar storage.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images of varying resolutions (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet-100) treated as third-order tensors of shape C×H×W, and outputs class labels via a standard classification head. During replay, stored CP factor matrices are reconstructed back into images that are fed through the same backbone as raw exemplars. The method is designed as a plug-and-play module that slots into existing CIL baselines (iCaRL, FOSTER, DER, MEMO, DyTox) without modifying their architecture or training pipeline, operating under a fixed memory budget M (e.g., 500, 1k, 2k, 20k samples).

## Method

The core mechanism is CP (CANDECOMP/PARAFAC) tensor decomposition, which factorizes each image tensor into R rank-1 components represented by three factor matrices of sizes C×R, H×R, and W×R, achieving a compression ratio of (C+H+W)R/(C×H×W) well below 1. A two-stage hybrid exemplar selection strategy balances quality and quantity: the first stage uses herding to select representative raw samples (proportion ε), while the second stage randomly selects remaining samples, decomposes them, and stores only the factors, discarding any whose reconstruction relative error exceeds a threshold τ. An adaptive training strategy additionally trains the model on CP-reconstructed versions of current-task data so it learns to tolerate the minor reconstruction errors introduced by decomposition and 8-bit quantization. The approach is resolution-agnostic and requires no additional network modules, distinguishing it from methods that rely on autoencoders or MAE for reconstruction.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (M=2k, Base0 Inc10), TDMER plugged into DER achieves 73.69% average accuracy and 63.21% last accuracy, improving over the DER baseline by +2.64 and +3.57 points respectively. On ImageNet-100 (M=2k, Base0 Inc10), DER w/ TDMER reaches 79.75% average and 73.26% last accuracy, outperforming the strongest competing memory-efficient method MAE-CIL (79.54% avg, 70.29% last). The largest single improvement is +8.99% last accuracy for iCaRL on CIFAR-100, and gains grow with the number of incremental tasks and shrink under tighter memory budgets (e.g., MEMO gains +10.09% last accuracy at M=500 vs. +4.31% at M=2k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDMER consistently improves all four backbone CIL methods it is plugged into. On CIFAR-100 (M=2k, Base0 Inc10), the gains are largest for iCaRL (+6.39% avg, +8.99% last) and smallest for the already strong DER (+2.64% avg, +3.57% last); FOSTER and MEMO fall in between (+3.94/+4.01 and +3.10/+4.31 respectively). Against dedicated memory-efficient replay methods on ImageNet-100 (M=2k, Base0 Inc10), DER w/ TDMER (79.75% avg, 73.26% last) outperforms MAE-CIL (79.54% avg, 70.29% last), CIM (77.94% avg), and MRDC (76.02% avg). On the ViT-based DyTox baseline (CIFAR-100, N=10), TDMER lifts average accuracy from 77.10% to 80.21% and last accuracy from 64.53% to 70.56%, surpassing MAE-CIL (79.12% avg, 68.40% last). Gains are amplified under tighter memory budgets: at M=500 on CIFAR-100, MEMO's last accuracy improves by 10.09% versus only 4.31% at M=2k, confirming the method's value when storage is most constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the memory-efficient replay line of CIL methods (MECIL, CIM, MAE-CIL), which compress stored exemplars to fit a fixed memory budget. It departs from these pixel-level compression approaches by instead exploiting the low intrinsic dimensionality and local pixel correlation of natural images through CP (CANDECOMP/PARAFAC) tensor decomposition, storing compact factor matrices rather than compressed pixel arrays. The exemplar selection strategy builds on the herding mechanism from iCaRL, extending it with a second random-selection stage gated by a reconstruction-error threshold to balance quality and quantity. Conceptually, the paper repurposes a classical multilinear algebra tool (CP decomposition) as a plug-and-play compression module for replay-based CIL, a connection not previously explored in the incremental learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means any existing replay-based CIL method (iCaRL, FOSTER, DER, MEMO, DyTox) can be enhanced without architectural modification, and the method generalizes across resolutions from 32×32 to 224×224 without re-derivation. A key open question is whether the rank parameter and ε proportion can be selected automatically rather than tuned per dataset, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal incremental settings where tensor structure is richer. The paper also leaves open whether the modest gains over MAE-CIL (which requires training a separate autoencoder) justify the added tensor decomposition overhead at inference time, particularly as memory budgets grow large.
