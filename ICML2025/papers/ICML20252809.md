## Problem

Post-hoc explanations (e.g., LIME) are widely demanded for trust and regulatory compliance but fail operationally in adversarial settings where model owners have incentives to manipulate them. Model confidentiality compounds the problem by enabling model swapping between prediction and explanation generation, and non-deterministic explanation parameters allow adversaries to make discriminatory predictions appear benign. The paper asks whether cryptographic techniques can guarantee that a specific committed model is used consistently and that the explanation is computed correctly, all while keeping model weights secret.

## Input / Output

The system takes a customer's input point x, a one-time KZG commitment to the model weights W (fixed-point, 4 decimal places for ZKP compatibility), public LIME hyperparameters (sampling type, kernel type, bandwidth σ, explanation length K, neighborhood size n), and a verifier-supplied random value r_v. It produces the model's prediction f(x), a top-K LIME explanation (feature indices and weights), and a Halo2 SNARK proof (~13 KB for NNs) certifying correct computation. Verification completes in ~0.12 seconds on the customer's side, while proof generation takes up to ~1.5 minutes for standard LIME on a 64-core server.

## Method

ExpProof is a two-phase protocol: (1) a one-time offline commitment phase where the model owner binds to weights W and sampling randomness r_p via KZG commitments, and (2) an online phase where, for each query, the prover outputs a prediction, LIME explanation, and a ZK proof. The key technical insight is avoiding the expensive LASSO optimization inside the ZKP circuit by instead verifying optimality through the duality gap: the prover supplies a dual-feasible solution and the circuit checks that primal minus dual objective ≤ ε, leveraging the closed-form primal/dual of LASSO. To handle ZKP-incompatible operations, the system uses Poseidon hash for verifiable random sampling, lookup tables for the exponential kernel and inverse CDF (Gaussian sampling), and ezkl's built-in inference verification circuit for model predictions. The paper also introduces BorderLIME, which replaces the original radial-search border-finding algorithm with a parallelizable line search parameterized by a pre-committed stability radius δ, reducing the number of required inference proofs. The entire pipeline is implemented as a single Halo2 relation in ezkl (~3.7k lines of Rust), with the most computationally expensive component being the inference proofs, which scale polynomially with model depth.

## Results

For Neural Networks with standard LIME (G+N variant, n=300), ExpProof achieves a maximum proof generation time of ~1.5 minutes, verification time of ~0.12 seconds, and proof size of ~13 KB across the Adult, Credit, and German Credit datasets. For Random Forests, the corresponding overheads are ~2.02 minutes proof generation, ~0.14 seconds verification, and ~16.20 KB proof size. BorderLIME incurs roughly 4× the proof generation time of standard LIME (4.85 min vs. 1.17 min for NNs) due to the additional border-point search, while verification remains under 0.3 seconds. In the ablation study, inference proofs dominate total ZKP cost and scale polynomially with the number of hidden layers (42 s for 2 layers → 319 s for 40 layers), while sampling+kernel and LASSO verification remain nearly constant (~24 s and ~60 s respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external explanation systems; instead it benchmarks four internal LIME variants (Gaussian/Uniform sampling × Exponential/No kernel) and Standard LIME vs. BorderLIME. On explanation fidelity (prediction similarity), all four standard LIME variants produce statistically indistinguishable results for NNs (overlapping error bars), with Gaussian sampling showing a slight edge over Uniform on the high-dimensional Credit dataset. BorderLIME (G+N) matches or exceeds standard LIME fidelity, particularly when the input is far from the decision boundary, confirming that anchoring the explanation at the nearest opposite-class point yields more meaningful local explanations. On ZKP overhead, Gaussian sampling adds ~20–30% proof generation time over Uniform (due to the inverse-CDF lookup), and the exponential kernel adds a similar increment (due to the exponential lookup table), making G+N the most ZKP-efficient variant. BorderLIME roughly quadruples proof generation time relative to standard LIME (4.85 min vs. 1.17 min for NNs; 8.46 min vs. 2.02 min for RFs) because the line-search border-point algorithm requires additional inference proofs, though verification time increases only modestly (0.11→0.30 s for NNs). The ablation confirms that inference verification is the dominant and scaling bottleneck, while LASSO duality-gap checking and sampling/kernel verification remain constant regardless of model depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LIME (Ribeiro et al., 2016) as the core explanation algorithm, creating ZKP-amenable variants by replacing non-linear operations (exponential kernel, Gaussian sampling) with lookup-table verifications, and on Laugel et al.'s (2018) radial border-point search, which is reformulated as a parallelizable line search (BorderLIME) using a stability-radius parameter. It departs from the broader ZKP-for-ML literature, which has focused on verifying inferences (Sun et al., 2024; Kang et al., 2022), training (Garg et al., 2023), or fairness properties (Yadav et al., 2024a), by identifying explanation verification as a distinct and previously unaddressed problem. It also contrasts with consistency-check-based explanation auditing (Bhattacharjee & von Luxburg, 2024; Dasgupta et al., 2022), which the authors argue is infeasible for individual customers and places the verification burden asymmetrically on the verifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExpProof provides the first cryptographic protocol that simultaneously guarantees model uniformity, explanation correctness, and model confidentiality in adversarial settings, addressing a gap identified by Bordt et al. (2022) where explanations fail as trust tools. The demonstrated feasibility (≤1.5 min proof generation, 0.12 s verification, 13 KB proof for small NNs) suggests the approach could become practical as ZKP hardware accelerates, though the polynomial scaling with model depth limits near-term applicability to large models. Key open questions include: preventing model-level manipulation (fairwashing) which requires ZKP of training, determining optimal stability radii for BorderLIME, and designing explanation algorithms natively optimized for ZKP efficiency rather than retrofitting existing ones.
