## Problem

Retraining a model with its own predicted hard labels is a simple empirical trick that often improves accuracy under noisy labels, yet no prior work had provided a theoretical guarantee that this procedure provably increases model accuracy. The paper also identifies a practical gap: label differential privacy (DP) training inherently uses noisy labels, and there was no lightweight post-processing step to recover accuracy without additional privacy cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250482/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained model checkpoint (obtained from initial training on a dataset with noisy labels) and the original training set of (feature, noisy-label) pairs. It produces a retrained model with improved population accuracy. For consensus-based retraining, the effective training set is reduced to only those samples where the model's predicted hard label matches the given noisy label (e.g., ~11–76% of the full set depending on noise level), and the method applies as a post-processing step on top of any label DP mechanism at zero additional privacy cost.

## Method

The theoretical contribution analyzes full retraining in a linearly separable binary classification setting (Gaussian mixture model with positive margin γ) where labels are randomly flipped with probability p < 1/2. The key insight is that the initial model's predicted labels constitute a non-uniform noisy label source—samples far from the decision boundary are predicted more accurately—so retraining with these predicted hard labels yields a classifier whose error bound depends on (1−2p′) rather than (1−2p)², provably improving accuracy when p is close to 1/2 and n lies in a specific range relative to d. The empirical contribution introduces consensus-based retraining: retraining only on the subset of samples where the predicted label agrees with the given noisy label, which acts as a filter producing a smaller but substantially more accurate training set. This is demonstrated to improve label DP training (using Ghazi et al.'s prior-based method or randomized response) by 6–12% across CIFAR-10/100, DomainNet, and AG News Subset, and remains effective even when combined with noise-robust losses or forward/backward correction.

## Results

Consensus-based retraining (retraining only on samples where the predicted label matches the given noisy label) consistently outperforms both full retraining and the label DP baseline across all four datasets. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 and ε=3 label DP, consensus-based RT achieves 29.98% test accuracy versus 23.53% for the baseline (a 6.45-point gain). On CIFAR-10 with ε=1, it reaches 63.84% versus 57.78% baseline (+6.06 points). On AG News Subset with ε=0.5, a small BERT model improves from 69.21% to 80.95% (+11.74 points), and on DomainNet with ε=3, accuracy jumps from 23.60% to 36.30% (+12.70 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the prior-based label DP method of Ghazi et al. (2021) (used for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and DomainNet) and randomized response (Warner, 1965) (used for AG News Subset). Consensus-based RT wins on every single comparison across all four datasets and all tested ε values. On CIFAR-100 (ε=3), it beats the Ghazi et al. baseline by 6.45 points (29.98% vs. 23.53%) and full RT by 5.56 points. On AG News (ε=0.5), it beats the randomized response baseline by 11.74 points (80.95% vs. 69.21%). The gains are largest at low ε (high label noise), where the consensus set is smallest (e.g., ~11% of training data for CIFAR-100 at ε=3) yet yields the most dramatic accuracy improvements, confirming that the filtering effect of consensus-based selection is the key mechanism. Full RT also consistently beats the baseline but by a smaller margin, validating that the consensus filtering step provides substantial additional benefit beyond simply retraining on all predicted labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on the linear classifier framework from Carmon et al. (2019) for its theoretical analysis and applies retraining as a post-processing step on top of the label DP training algorithm of Ghazi et al. (2021). It deliberately distinguishes itself from self-training (which operates in the semi-supervised setting with confidence-based sample selection) and self-distillation (which uses soft labels and requires stronger assumptions like population access or uniform inner products within classes). The consensus-based filtering idea is conceptually similar to Tang et al. (2022), who use unsupervised pseudo-labels for consensus, but this paper shows that matching the model's own predicted label to the given noisy label is sufficient without any unsupervised component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result proving that retraining with predicted hard labels provably improves population accuracy under label noise, filling a gap where the practice was widely used but uncharacterized. Practically, consensus-based retraining offers a zero-privacy-cost post-processing step that yields 6–12% accuracy gains across vision and language datasets under label DP, making it immediately applicable to any existing label DP pipeline. Key limitations include the theory being restricted to linear classifiers with uniform (randomized-response) noise, and the absence of a theoretical analysis for the empirically superior consensus-based variant. Open questions include extending the theory to non-uniform noise models, non-linear classifiers, and larger-scale settings.
