## Problem

In current text-to-image generation, human preference alignment (e.g., DPO fine-tuning) and test-time sampling (e.g., Classifier-Free Guidance) are optimized and applied independently, despite both operating on a shared conceptual framework of preferred versus dispreferred distributions. This isolation leaves their mutual reinforcement unexplored, resulting in suboptimal text-image alignment and aesthetic quality that neither component can achieve alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image (1024×1024 resolution in experiments). During sampling, the text prompt embedding is linearly combined with a null-prompt embedding to form a proxy prompt (ĉ = −αc + (1+α)∅), which is fed to the dispreferred model, reducing the required forward passes from three to two. The framework is model-agnostic, operating on both diffusion-based backbones (SD1.5, SDXL) and flow-matching backbones (DiT-based In-house T2I).

## Method

CHATS initializes two separate models from the same reference model: a preferred model (θ⁺) and a dispreferred model (θ⁻). Training uses a reformulated DPO objective (Eq. 9) that maximizes the sum of reward scores from both models rather than their difference, preventing the dispreferred model from collapsing; the resulting per-step loss (Eqs. 13–14) compares each model's noise/velocity prediction against the frozen reference. At sampling time, a proxy-prompt-based strategy (Eq. 15–17) combines both models' outputs: the preferred model is queried with the original prompt at scale (1+s), while the dispreferred model is queried with the proxy prompt at scale s, effectively steering generation toward preferred modes and away from dispreferred ones. The hyperparameter α (default 0.5) controls how much conditional information from the dispreferred model is retained versus suppressed, and the method achieves strong results with only 7,459 high-quality preference pairs from the OIP dataset.

## Results

On HPS v2 (the primary aesthetic metric), CHATS achieves 27.74 on Anime and 26.43 on Photo with SD1.5, 32.96 on Anime and 29.62 on Photo with SDXL, and 30.41 on Anime and 29.30 on Photo with In-house T2I, consistently outperforming both the Standard baseline and Diffusion-DPO across all six prompt groups. On GenEval, CHATS achieves 44.11% overall with SD1.5, 60.12% with SDXL, and 66.42% with In-house T2I, surpassing Diffusion-DPO by 2.12, 0.85, and 4.20 percentage points respectively. On DPG-Bench, CHATS reaches 64.44% (SD1.5), 76.08% (SDXL), and 83.72% (In-house T2I) overall, again leading both baselines. Notably, CHATS narrows the aesthetic gap between SD1.5 and SDXL from 8.83 (Standard) to 4.41, demonstrating robustness across model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHATS is compared against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024), the current state-of-the-art DPO method for text-to-image generation, as well as the unmodified Standard versions of each model. CHATS wins on every single benchmark and model combination: on HPS v2, it outperforms Diffusion-DPO by 4.34–6.10 points on SD1.5, 1.25–1.34 points on SDXL, and 3.78–4.37 points on In-house T2I. On GenEval, CHATS leads Diffusion-DPO by 2.12% (SD1.5), 0.85% (SDXL), and 4.20% (In-house T2I) overall, with particularly large gains on Counting (+10.18% over Diffusion-DPO on SD1.5) and Color attribution. On DPG-Bench, CHATS achieves the highest overall scores across all three models (64.44%, 76.08%, 83.72%). The margin is largest on the weaker SD1.5 model, suggesting CHATS's dual-model architecture is especially beneficial when the base model has more room for improvement. A key differentiator is data efficiency: CHATS trained on the small 7,459-pair OIP dataset outperforms Diffusion-DPO trained on the much larger 851K-pair PaP v2 dataset, whereas Diffusion-DPO performs better on PaP v2 than on OIP, indicating CHATS's two-model design is more robust to subtle preference signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHATS sits at the intersection of two previously independent lines of work: DPO-based preference alignment for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, Wallace et al. 2024) and classifier-free guidance (CFG, Ho & Salimans 2022) for sampling. The paper's key conceptual move is recognizing that both DPO and CFG operate on a shared preferred/dispreferred distribution structure, and that prior methods optimize them in isolation. By deploying two separate models—one for the preferred distribution and one for the dispreferred/unconditional distribution—and coupling them through a proxy-prompt-based sampling strategy, CHATS is the first framework to jointly exploit both mechanisms rather than treating alignment and guidance as orthogonal stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the data efficiency: CHATS achieves state-of-the-art results with only 7,459 high-quality preference pairs (OIP), roughly 100× fewer than the 851,293 pairs in PaP v2 used by Diffusion-DPO, suggesting that small curated datasets can drive strong preference optimization when the method properly separates preferred and dispreferred modeling. The main limitation is a modest throughput cost from requiring two forward passes per step instead of one, though the authors note this is mitigated by model distillation (e.g., into FLUX.1-dev-style single models). An open question is whether the two-model decomposition generalizes to other alignment objectives beyond DPO (e.g., RLHF with explicit reward models) or to video generation, where the preferred/dispreferred structure may be less cleanly separable.
