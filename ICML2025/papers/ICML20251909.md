## Problem

Existing guidance methods for diffusion models either require training additional models (classifier guidance, classifier-free guidance) or rely on restrictive approximations of the diffusion posterior that limit them to linear inverse problems or pixel-space models. This paper asks whether guidance can be formulated as a unified variational control problem that is training-free, task-agnostic, and applicable to both pixel-space and latent-space diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251909/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For inverse problems, the method takes a noisy measurement y = A(x_0) + σ_y·z (where A is a known or partially known degradation operator such as blur, downsampling, or masking) and produces a reconstructed 256×256 image; for blind deblurring it additionally infers the unknown blur kernel. For style guidance, it takes a text prompt and a reference style image and produces a generated image via Stable Diffusion 1.4 that simultaneously adheres to the prompt (measured by CLIP score) and the reference style (measured by Gram-matrix distance in CLIP feature space). All experiments use pretrained diffusion models without any fine-tuning.

## Method

The paper formulates diffusion guidance as a stochastic optimal control problem: at each reverse-diffusion timestep t, a variational control u_t is optimized to minimize a cost combining a terminal term (a task-specific loss Φ evaluated at the Tweedie estimate x̂_0^t) and a transient KL-divergence term that penalizes deviation from the unguided diffusion posterior. The key instantiation, NDTM, applies the control non-linearly by perturbing the noisy state as x_t + γ·u_t before querying the pretrained denoiser, making the transient cost non-linear in u_t and enabling richer steering than the linear score-addition used by classifier guidance. For DDIM, the objective reduces to a tractable upper bound with three terms: a control-magnitude penalty ‖u_t‖², a score-deviation penalty ‖ε_θ(x̄_t,t) − ε_θ(x_t,t)‖², and the terminal cost, all optimized greedily per timestep with a few Adam steps. The framework provably subsumes classifier guidance and path-integral control as the linear-control special case, and extends naturally to continuous-time SDEs and flow-matching models by replacing the transient cost with the corresponding score or velocity-field deviation.

## Results

On non-linear deblurring (the paper's headline task), NDTM achieves LPIPS 0.046 / FID 14.2 / KID 0.0004 on FFHQ and LPIPS 0.163 / FID 34.3 / KID 0.0032 on ImageNet, outperforming the next-best baseline (RB-Modulation) by 28% in LPIPS and 29% in FID on FFHQ. On linear inverse problems (4× super-resolution, 90% inpainting), NDTM matches or beats all baselines, e.g. LPIPS 0.158 / FID 28.75 on ImageNet SR vs. DPS's 0.195 / 30.67. For blind image deblurring, NDTM improves LPIPS from 0.147 (DMPlug) to 0.103 (Gaussian) while running 7× faster (7.2 min vs. 51.2 min per image). On style guidance with Stable Diffusion, NDTM achieves CLIP score 31.34 and Style Score 475.6, beating both FreeDOM (30.86 / 508.3) and MPGD (30.21 / 498.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDTM decisively outperforms DPS on non-linear deblurring (LPIPS 0.046 vs. 0.752 on FFHQ) and on ImageNet linear tasks (e.g., SR FID 28.75 vs. 30.67), though DPS remains competitive on FFHQ inpainting (LPIPS 0.058 vs. 0.059). RED-diff is beaten by a wide margin on non-linear deblurring (FID 14.2 vs. 64.6) but is competitive on linear tasks where its variational formulation is well-suited. MPGD is outperformed on non-linear deblurring (LPIPS 0.046 vs. 0.636) and on style guidance (Style Score 475.6 vs. 498.9), but is competitive on linear inpainting. RB-Modulation, which the paper shows is a special case of NDTM (γ=1, w_s=0, w_c=0), is beaten on non-linear deblurring (LPIPS 0.046 vs. 0.064) but slightly edges out NDTM on FFHQ super-resolution FID (18.22 vs. 18.99). DDRM and C-ΠGDM are limited to linear problems; NDTM outperforms both on ImageNet SR (FID 28.75 vs. 52.76 and 39.96) and inpainting, while C-ΠGDM fails entirely on inpainting. On blind image deblurring, NDTM beats DMPlug by 30% in LPIPS (0.103 vs. 0.147 Gaussian) while being 7× faster. FreeDOM is outperformed on style guidance on both CLIP score (31.34 vs. 30.86) and Style Score (475.6 vs. 508.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion model guidance as a variational optimal control problem, building directly on the Control-as-Inference framework of Kappen et al. (2012) and the path integral control formulation of Kappen (2005, 2008). The key conceptual departure is showing that classifier guidance, DPS, and a broad class of posterior-approximation methods are all special cases of *linear* control (control added additively to the score), while the paper's Non-linear Diffusion Trajectory Matching (NDTM) inserts the control non-linearly into the denoiser input, yielding a strictly more expressive family. This unifying lens also subsumes recent control-theoretic guidance methods like RB-Modulation (Rout et al., 2025) as degenerate parameter settings, positioning DTM as a general framework rather than a single algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, training-free recipe for guiding any pretrained diffusion model (pixel- or latent-space) toward satisfying arbitrary differentiable terminal costs, which is practically important for inverse problems and style transfer where task-specific retraining is infeasible. The non-linear control generalization yields consistent and substantial gains over linear-control baselines across diverse tasks, suggesting that the field's reliance on score-additive guidance has left performance on the table. Open questions include extending the framework to non-differentiable terminal costs (e.g., discrete constraints), providing theoretical guarantees on the greedy per-step optimization, and integrating with fast samplers to reduce the per-step inner-loop overhead.
