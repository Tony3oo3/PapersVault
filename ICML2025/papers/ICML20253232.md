## Problem

Existing online CMDP algorithms achieve sublinear regret under stochastic constraints but fail when constraints are adversarial—unknown, time-varying, and potentially designed to challenge the learner. No prior method handles anytime adversarial constraints with strong (non-cancellation) constraint violation bounds while avoiding restrictive assumptions like Slater's condition or a known strictly safe policy, leaving a gap for safe decision-making in genuinely adversarial environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253232/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a finite-horizon episodic CMDP with bandit feedback: at each step the agent observes only the reward and cost for the chosen action (stochastic setting) or the full cost vector revealed post-episode (adversarial setting), while the transition kernel, reward, and cost functions remain unknown. The output is a policy parameterized as an occupancy measure over state-action-step triples, from which a stochastic policy is reconstructed via conditional normalization. The theoretical guarantees are stated in terms of strong constraint violation (sum of per-episode positive violations, disallowing cancellation) rather than the weaker aggregate violation used in prior work.

## Method

OMDPD combines three key mechanisms: (1) UCB-style optimistic estimates for transitions, rewards, and costs that maintain a confidence set of plausible models, ensuring the optimal policy remains feasible under estimation; (2) a surrogate objective built on an exponential Lyapunov function Φ(x)=exp(βx)−1 whose drift-plus-penalty structure (à la Neely) adaptively amplifies constraint penalties during high-violation regimes while bounding long-term drift; and (3) a two-phase optimistic OMD update (Rakhlin & Sridharan) that first predicts the next occupancy measure using the current gradient, then refines it with a predicted gradient, yielding an adaptive learning rate dependent on cumulative gradient variation. The dual variable λ_k accumulates only positive constraint violations, and the exponential Lyapunov drift couples the violation bound to the regret bound, producing Õ(√K) guarantees for both without Slater's condition. When a generative model provides accurate transition and reward estimates, the estimation error vanishes and the regret improves to O(1) for fixed rewards.

## Results

OMDPD achieves Õ(√K) regret and Õ(√K) strong constraint violation (disallowing cancellation) for online CMDPs under both stochastic and adversarial cost functions, without requiring Slater's condition or a known safe policy. This is the first result to provide optimal-order guarantees under anytime adversarial constraints. Furthermore, when a generative model (perfect simulator) is available for accurate reward and transition estimates, the regret bound improves to O(1) for a fixed reward function. In simulation on a synthetic 5-state, 3-action, 5-step CMDP over K=3000 episodes, cumulative violation grows sublinearly, consistent with the theoretical O(√K) rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMDPD is the only method in the comparison that handles adversarial (time-varying, unknown) cost functions with strong constraint violation; all five baselines (Efroni 2020, Müller 2023, Stradi 2024c, Müller 2024, Kitamura 2024) are limited to stochastic constraints and report 'N/A' for adversarial violation. On the stochastic constraint setting, OMDPD matches the best-known Õ(√K) regret and violation rates of Efroni et al. (2020), Müller et al. (2023), and Stradi et al. (2024c), while strictly outperforming Müller et al. (2024) at Õ(K^0.93) and Kitamura et al. (2024) at Õ(K^{6/7}). Critically, OMDPD is the only method that does not require Slater's condition (a strictly feasible solution) or a known safe policy, whereas all baselines require at least one of these. The additional O(1) regret guarantee under a generative model (Remark 5.2) has no counterpart in any of the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimistic online mirror descent framework of Rakhlin & Sridharan (2013) and the drift-plus-penalty Lyapunov analysis of Neely (2010), combining them with UCB-based optimistic estimation for episodic CMDPs. It departs from prior online CMDP work (Efroni et al. 2020, Müller et al. 2023, Stradi et al. 2024c) in two key ways: (1) it handles adversarially chosen cost functions rather than only stochastic ones, and (2) it eliminates the need for Slater's condition or a known strictly feasible policy, which all prior methods with strong violation guarantees require. The algorithm unifies stochastic and adversarial cost settings under a single surrogate objective with an exponential Lyapunov potential, positioning it as the first method to achieve optimal Õ(√K) regret and strong constraint violation under anytime adversarial constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of Slater's condition is practically significant because in adversarial settings an adversary can make the feasibility slack arbitrarily small, rendering prior guarantees vacuous. The O(1) regret improvement with access to a generative model highlights estimation error as the fundamental bottleneck, guiding future work toward better model estimation. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single small synthetic CMDP (5 states, 3 actions, 5 steps), leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees translate to realistic safety-critical domains. Future directions mentioned include multi-agent extensions and partial observability, while the infinite-horizon and continuous-state settings remain unaddressed.
