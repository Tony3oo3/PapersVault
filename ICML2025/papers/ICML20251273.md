## Problem

The max-min eigenvalue augmentation problem—selecting at most k PSD matrices to maximize the minimum eigenvalue of their sum with a base matrix—captures Bayesian E-optimal design and maximum algebraic connectivity augmentation. Existing approximation results either restrict augmentation matrices to rank-one form or focus on the k ≥ n regime, leaving the practically important k < n setting with general-rank matrices unaddressed. This paper provides the first constant-factor approximation guarantee for simple randomized rounding in that setting, conditioned on the optimal eigenvalue increase being sufficiently large.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base PSD matrix M ∈ S_+^n, m general-rank PSD augmentation matrices A_1,…,A_m ∈ S_+^n, and an integer k < m. It solves the SDP relaxation (2) to obtain a fractional solution z_sdp ∈ [0,1]^m, then independently rounds each coordinate to 1 with probability [z_sdp]_i. The output is a binary vector z_round ∈ {0,1}^m; feasibility (‖z_round‖_0 ≤ k) is not guaranteed by the rounding itself but holds with probability ≥ 1/2 via a Poisson binomial median argument, yielding a joint success probability of 29/64 for both feasibility and the (1/4)OPT eigenvalue guarantee.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new intrinsic-dimension matrix concentration inequality (Theorem 3.1) that lower-tails the minimum eigenvalue of a sum of independent random PSD matrices over a restricted set of directions S(E[X], α), where the set size is measured by intdim(E[X], α) = tr(E[X])/α rather than the ambient dimension n. The proof decomposes the unit sphere into two orthogonal subspaces spanned by high- and low-eigenvalue eigenvectors of E[X], applies a one-sided approximation reformulation of the standard matrix Chernoff bound (Theorem 2.5) on the high-eigenvalue subspace, and the intrinsic-dimension Chernoff bound for the maximum eigenvalue (Theorem 2.2) on the low-eigenvalue subspace, then combines them via a Cauchy–Schwarz decomposition of x = x_1 + x_2. The approximation guarantee (Theorem 4.1) follows by instantiating the inequality with α = (1/2)INC and ε = 1/2, yielding the condition INC ≥ 2^14 e^2 R ln k (where R = max_i tr(A_i)) under which simple randomized rounding achieves λ_min ≥ (1/4)OPT with probability ≥ 61/64. This condition depends on R ln k rather than W ln n (as the standard Chernoff bound would give), which is the key advantage in the k < n regime.

## Results

The main guarantee (Theorem 4.1) shows that simple randomized rounding of the SDP relaxation achieves λ_min(M + Σ z_round,i A_i) ≥ (1/4)·OPT with probability at least 61/64, provided the optimal increase satisfies INC ≥ 2^14·e^2·R·ln(k), where R = max_i tr(A_i). Adding the feasibility constraint ‖z_round‖_0 ≤ k (via Corollary 4.3), the joint probability drops to > 29/64. The key technical contribution is a new intrinsic-dimension matrix Chernoff inequality for the minimum eigenvalue (Theorem 3.1), which yields the ln(k) dependence rather than the ln(n) dependence that would follow from the standard matrix Chernoff bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The implicit comparison is between the new intrinsic-dimension concentration inequality (Theorem 3.1) and the existing matrix Chernoff inequality for λ_min (Theorem 2.4, from Tropp 2015). Using Theorem 2.4 directly would require INC = Ω(W·ln n) where W = max_i λ_max(A_i), which is uninformative in the k < n regime of interest. The new result requires only INC = Ω(R·ln k) where R = max_i tr(A_i), which is the relevant scaling when k < n. For rank-one augmentation matrices (the classical E-optimal design and algebraic connectivity settings), R = W, so the advantage reduces to ln k vs. ln n. The paper also notes that the existing intrinsic-dimension Chernoff inequality (Theorem 2.2) applies only to λ_max, not λ_min, and that the techniques used to prove it do not extend to the minimum eigenvalue case — a gap this paper fills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tropp's matrix concentration inequalities (2015), specifically combining the intrinsic-dimension Chernoff bound for the maximum eigenvalue (Theorem 2.2) with a reformulated one-sided approximation inequality for the minimum eigenvalue (Theorem 2.5, derived from Theorem 2.4) to fill a known gap: no intrinsic-dimension analog existed for the minimum eigenvalue. It also departs from prior max-min eigenvalue augmentation work (E-optimal design, algebraic connectivity augmentation) by dropping the rank-one assumption on augmentation matrices and targeting the k < n regime, which the authors note remains unexplored for Bayesian E-optimal design. The feasibility argument leverages a 1968 result on Poisson binomial medians (Jogdeo & Samuels) that the authors note they could not find in more recent references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intrinsic-dimension concentration inequality for the minimum eigenvalue (Theorem 3.1) is a clean, self-contained result of independent interest that completes a natural gap in the matrix Chernoff inequality toolkit. The approximation guarantee's dependence on R ln k (rather than W ln n) is the right scaling for the k < n regime, making the result practically relevant for settings where one can only add a small number of general-rank augmentations. Open questions include whether the constant-factor approximation is tight, whether the ε-dependence in Theorem 3.1 can be improved, and whether the method performs well empirically — none of which are addressed here. The paper is purely theoretical with no computational experiments.
