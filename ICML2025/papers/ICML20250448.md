## Problem

Supervised GRN inference methods achieve high benchmark scores but exploit gene-specific dataset biases (e.g., class imbalance of known interactions per gene) rather than learning true regulatory mechanisms from scRNA-seq data, and existing benchmarks fail to test generalization to interactions between completely unseen genes. Meanwhile, unsupervised methods like DeepSEM lack a principled mechanism to incorporate informative biological priors such as gene embeddings or partially known interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a scRNA-seq expression matrix X ∈ R^{P×N} (P genes × N cells), d-dimensional BioBERT embeddings h_i for each gene's textual description, and optionally a partially observed binary adjacency matrix Y from ChIP-seq. It outputs a weighted adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{P×P} encoding directed regulatory effects; in InfoSEM-BC, A is factorized as A = A^e ⊙ σ(A^l) where A^l is constrained to rank h ≪ P (motivated by the number of transcription factors), and the final binary network is obtained by thresholding σ(A^l).

## Method

InfoSEM extends DeepSEM's β-VAE generative framework by replacing the weakly informative Laplace prior on the adjacency matrix with structured informative priors. In InfoSEM-B, the prior mean for each interaction effect a_ik is set to a linear function [h_i, h_k]w^T of concatenated BioBERT embeddings, making functionally similar genes share similar regulatory profiles while preserving directionality. In InfoSEM-BC, the adjacency matrix is decomposed into magnitude A^e (embedding-informed Laplace prior) and logit-probability A^l (Gaussian prior centered at logit(0.95) or logit(0.05) for known positive/negative edges, uniform otherwise), with A^l constrained to low rank h via a factorized MAP estimate. Training maximizes the ELBO under variational Bayes, with full-rank Dirac posteriors for A and A^e and a rank-h factorized posterior for A^l, avoiding the discriminative cross-entropy loss that causes supervised methods to memorize class-imbalance shortcuts.

## Results

On the proposed unseen-genes benchmark with cell-type-specific ChIP-seq targets, InfoSEM-BC achieves the best AUPRC across all four BEELINE datasets (hESC 0.331, hHEP 0.499, mDC 0.322, mESC 0.408) and best Hit@1% on 3 of 4 datasets. InfoSEM-B (without known interactions) improves over DeepSEM by 25% on average (cell-specific) and 52% (non-cell-specific), while InfoSEM-BC further improves over DeepSEM by 31% and 66% respectively. Overall, InfoSEM outperforms existing models without informative priors by 38.5% on average, and integrating GT labels as priors adds a further 11.1% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the new unseen-genes benchmark, InfoSEM-BC outperforms all baselines on AUPRC across all four datasets, beating the best supervised method (scGREAT) by 10–33% and the best unsupervised baseline (DeepSEM) by 25–31% on average. InfoSEM-B, which uses no known interaction labels, still surpasses DeepSEM by 25% (cell-specific) and 52% (non-cell-specific) on average, and achieves top-2 AUPRC on 3 of 4 datasets even against supervised methods that use GT labels. A striking finding is that trivial baselines (one-hot LR, MatComp) that use no scRNA-seq data at all match or exceed supervised methods (scGREAT, GENELink) on the standard seen-genes benchmark (e.g., one-hot LR achieves AUPRC 0.600 vs. scGREAT 0.642 on hESC), revealing that supervised models exploit gene-specific class-imbalance biases rather than true regulatory mechanisms. On the unseen-genes benchmark, these trivial baselines drop to near-random performance (AUPRC ~0.21–0.35), while supervised methods suffer a 42% average drop (cell-specific) and 79% (non-cell-specific), sometimes falling below the random baseline. GRNBoost2, the strongest classical unsupervised method, is consistently outperformed by both InfoSEM variants on all four datasets for both AUPRC and Hit@1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoSEM is a direct extension of DeepSEM (Shu et al., 2021), a β-VAE-based generative model that learns GRN structure in a latent space via a structural equation model. The key departure is replacing DeepSEM's weakly informative Laplace prior on the adjacency matrix with two types of informative priors: a linear model over BioBERT gene embeddings for interaction effects, and a Gaussian prior on interaction logits conditioned on known ChIP-seq labels. This positions InfoSEM between the unsupervised methods (GRNBoost2, GENIE3, DeepSEM) that cannot incorporate external knowledge and the supervised methods (scGREAT, GENELink) that use GT labels for direct cross-entropy supervision, which the paper argues leads to exploitation of gene-specific class-imbalance biases rather than learning true regulatory mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is the demonstration that state-of-the-art supervised GRN methods largely exploit dataset biases (node-degree class imbalance) rather than scRNA-seq signal, as evidenced by trivial baselines matching their performance on standard benchmarks and a 42–79% collapse on unseen genes. The proposed unseen-gene evaluation framework is a practical and biologically motivated shift that better reflects real-world applications like biomarker discovery and rare cell-type studies. Limitations include evaluation restricted to four BEELINE datasets, reliance on gene-name-only BioBERT embeddings (not full functional descriptions), and a low-rank constraint on the interaction-probability matrix that may not capture all TF-TG structure. Open questions include whether richer foundation-model embeddings (e.g., from multi-omics or perturbation data) further improve the prior, and whether the approach scales to genome-wide networks with thousands of TFs.
