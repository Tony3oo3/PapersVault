## Problem

Existing Test-Time Training (TTT) methods for 3D point cloud classification, such as MATE, rely on computationally expensive backpropagation during adaptation and focus on surface-level reconstruction via masked autoencoders, making them highly sensitive to surface corruptions and too slow for real-time deployment. The paper asks whether a more abstract geometric representation can make pre-trained features robust enough to adapt without any gradient-based weight updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250087/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and tokenizes it via Farthest Point Sampling into M centers, each paired with K nearest neighbors to form local neighborhoods P_local ∈ R^{M×K×3}. It produces two outputs: a class label over K categories (via softmax) and a skeletal representation consisting of M skeletal points (M×3) and their corresponding radii (M×1). During test-time adaptation, the input is an unlabeled corrupted point cloud, and the model adapts using only these unlabeled samples without accessing any source data or class prototypes.

## Method

SMART-PC uses a shared PointMAE encoder with two branches: a skeletal branch that predicts skeletal points and radii directly from refined decoder features (bypassing the convex-combination approach of prior skeletal methods), and a classification branch that sums encoder and decoder features before a multi-layer classification head. Training jointly optimizes a skeletal loss (point-to-sphere, Chamfer-distance sampling, and radius regularization) in a self-supervised manner alongside standard cross-entropy classification. The key insight is that skeletal pre-training forces the encoder to learn abstract, noise-resistant geometric features, so that during online test-time adaptation the model needs only to update BatchNorm running statistics (mean and variance) without any backpropagation. This backpropagation-free strategy achieves 59.52 FPS on ModelNet40-C versus 10.79 FPS for MATE, while matching or exceeding MATE's accuracy across all three benchmark datasets.

## Results

On the three corrupted benchmark datasets, SMART-PC achieves top-1 accuracy of 72.9% (ModelNet40-C), 47.4% (ScanObjectNN-C), and 67.1% (ShapeNet-C) in online adaptation mode, outperforming MATE (70.6%, 36.9%, 69.1%) on two of three datasets. The backpropagation-free variant (updating only BatchNorm statistics) retains competitive accuracy at 70.8%, 46.7%, and 65.9% while achieving 59.52 FPS versus MATE's 10.79 FPS. In source-only (no adaptation) settings, SMART-PC-SO leads with 61.7%, 38.7%, and 64.5% average accuracy, surpassing MATE-SO by 7.3, 4.2, and 8.0 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART-PC outperforms MATE (the primary 3D TTT baseline) in online mode on ModelNet40-C (72.9% vs 70.6%) and ScanObjectNN-C (47.4% vs 36.9%, a +10.5 pp gain), but trails on ShapeNet-C (67.1% vs 69.1%). In standard mode, SMART-PC edges out MATE on all three datasets (63.1 vs 63.0, 39.6 vs 36.9, 64.4 vs 63.1). Against 2D TTT methods (TENT, SHOT, T3A, DUA, TTT-Rot), SMART-PC achieves substantially higher accuracy across all datasets, with gaps of 10–40 pp. The backpropagation-free variant (SMART-PC-Online†) matches or exceeds MATE-Online on ModelNet40-C (70.8 vs 70.6) and ScanObjectNN-C (46.7 vs 36.9) while running at 59.52 FPS versus MATE's 10.79 FPS. Against diffusion-based methods (CloudFixer, DDA), SMART-PC is competitive on ModelNet40-C (72.9% vs 77.2% for CloudFixer-Online) but those methods are not evaluated on the other two datasets. BFTT3D, another backprop-free method, is outperformed by SMART-PC on all three datasets in both accuracy and FPS (59.52 vs 6.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART-PC builds directly on two lines of work: the skeletal representation framework from Point2Skeleton (Lin et al., 2021), which defines the point-to-sphere, sampling, and radius-regularization losses, and the MATE test-time training framework (Mirza et al., 2023), which established the online/standard adaptation paradigm for 3D point clouds using a masked autoencoder. The key departure is replacing MATE's surface-reconstruction objective with skeletal prediction, which the authors argue produces more abstract, corruption-resistant features. This in turn enables a second departure from MATE: eliminating backpropagation during online adaptation entirely, updating only BatchNorm running statistics rather than all network weights, a strategy previously explored in 2D (Nado et al., 2020; Li et al., 2018) but not in the 3D TTT context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the 5.5× speedup in adaptation (59.52 FPS vs. MATE's 10.79 FPS) while matching or exceeding accuracy, making real-time 3D adaptation feasible for latency-sensitive applications like autonomous navigation. The finding that skeletal pre-training is sufficient to make BatchNorm-only adaptation competitive with full backpropagation-based methods suggests that the quality of pre-trained features, not the adaptation mechanism, is the primary determinant of robustness. Limitations include evaluation only on classification with a single backbone (PointMAE), batch size 1 for fair comparison (larger batches improve accuracy substantially), and a notable underperformance on ShapeNet-C in online mode (67.1% vs. MATE's 69.1%). Extending the framework to segmentation, detection, and other backbones remains open.
