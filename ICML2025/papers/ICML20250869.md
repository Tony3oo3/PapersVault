## Problem

Standard flow matching transports individual points between distributions, but in domains like 3D shape generation and spatial transcriptomics, each data sample is itself a probability distribution (a point-cloud or a Gaussian). Existing generative models either ignore this distributional geometry or rely on voxelization, which cannot scale to high-dimensional or variable-sized realizations. This paper asks how to perform flow matching directly over the Wasserstein space of probability distributions, respecting their intrinsic geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a source distribution (either a Gaussian parameterized by mean and covariance, or a point-cloud of arbitrary size) and optionally a class label for conditional generation, and produces a target distribution of the same type. A key distinction from prior point-cloud generative models is that WFM handles variable-sized point-cloud realizations (e.g., MNIST digits with differing pixel counts, or 16-nearest-neighbor gene-expression clouds in 254 dimensions) via entropic OT, whereas competing methods require uniform-sized, low-dimensional inputs. For the Bures-Wasserstein variant, the source is sampled from a product of Gaussian and Wishart distributions matched to the target's statistics.

## Method

WFM lifts Riemannian flow matching to the Wasserstein space by regressing a neural vector field onto the optimal velocity field along McCann interpolations (constant-speed geodesics given by pushforwards of OT maps). For Gaussian families on the Bures-Wasserstein submanifold, closed-form OT maps and Riemannian velocity fields yield a fully analytic training objective solved by a fully-connected network; for general distributions, entropic OT (Sinkhorn iterations) approximates the OT map, and a transformer with multi-head self-attention serves as the vector field, exploiting the natural alignment between permutation equivariance of attention and the equivariance of Wasserstein geometry. The paper proves that Wasserstein geodesics constitute valid conditional probability flows (Proposition 3.1), establishing the training objective as a proper FM loss. Generation proceeds via simulation-free training followed by numerical integration of the learned flow at inference time, using Riemannian exponential-map updates for Gaussians and Euler steps on point-clouds for general distributions.

## Results

On the 3D shape datasets (Table 3), WFM achieves state-of-the-art Chamfer Distance on airplane (69.88) and car (53.41), while remaining competitive on EMD across all three classes. For Gaussian-based generation (Table 2), Riemannian BW-FM consistently outperforms Frobenius FM and BW-FM (Euclidean) on average minimum W2^2 distance across all six datasets, with the advantage growing in dimension (e.g., 1.31 vs 3.21 on 32D scRNA-seq). WFM also demonstrates unique capability on generation of variable-sized and high-dimensional distributions (Table 4), where all competing baselines are inapplicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six 3D point-cloud generative models: PointFlow (ICCV 2019), SoftFlow (NeurIPS 2020), DPF-Net (ECCV 2020), Shape-GF (ECCV 2020), PVD (ICCV 2021), and PSF (CVPR 2023). WFM wins on Chamfer Distance for airplane (69.88 vs best baseline 71.11 from PSF) and car (53.41 vs 54.55 from PVD), but trails PVD on chair CD (57.62 vs 56.26) and EMD (57.93 vs 53.32). On EMD metrics, WFM is competitive but not always best, losing to PSF on airplane (64.44 vs 61.09) and PVD on chair (57.93 vs 53.32). All baselines rely on voxelization or uniform-sized point-cloud representations, which precludes them from handling variable-sized realizations or high-dimensional distributions. WFM uniquely handles variable-sized point-clouds (MNIST, Letters) and high-dimensional spatial transcriptomics (MERFISH, XENIUM) where all six baselines report NA, making direct comparison impossible in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

WFM is a direct instantiation of Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2023), lifting the FM framework from Euclidean or finite-dimensional Riemannian manifolds to the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space of probability measures. The key algorithmic ingredients are entropic optimal transport (Pooladian & Niles-Weed, 2021) for approximating OT maps between empirical point-clouds, and transformer architectures whose permutation equivariance aligns with the symmetry of Wasserstein geometry. The paper distinguishes itself from Fisher FM and Categorical FM (which use Fisher-Rao geometry over the simplex), from Meta FM (which requires pre-coupled distribution pairs), and from prior shape-generation methods (PointFlow, SoftFlow, PSF) that are restricted to uniform-sized point clouds and cannot scale to high dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WFM is the first framework capable of generative modeling where each sample is itself a distribution, enabling applications in spatial transcriptomics (synthesizing cellular microenvironments) and shape generation with variable-sized realizations that prior voxelization-based methods cannot handle. The theoretical contribution—proving Wasserstein geodesics constitute valid conditional flows—provides a clean foundation, though it follows relatively directly from the RFM template. Key limitations include the quadratic computational complexity in point-cloud size (from both Sinkhorn iterations and transformer attention), the gap between classical OT theory and entropic OT approximations, and the curse of dimensionality in OT map estimation rates. Open questions include whether the entropic regularization bias degrades generation quality in high dimensions, and whether the framework can be extended to unbalanced or multi-modal transport settings.
