## Problem

Most feedback-driven training for language models relies on sequence-level annotations (e.g., a single scalar score per output), leaving the richer information in span-level error annotations underutilized for direct model training. This paper asks whether fine-grained, span-level error information—such as MQM annotations marking specific error locations, categories, and severities—can be directly exploited to finetune machine translation models more effectively than sequence-level methods like DPO or quality-filtered SFT.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a source sentence (English or Chinese) and produces a translated sentence (German or English) via greedy decoding from a 602M-parameter Transformer encoder-decoder. During training, the method additionally consumes span-level MQM error annotations on the target translations, specifying which character spans contain errors, their category (fluency, accuracy, etc.), and severity (major, minor, minor punctuation), which are converted into per-token weights that drive the loss computation.

## Method

TWA assigns each token in a target translation a weight based on its annotation: negative (scaled by MQM severity) for tokens within an error span, zero for tokens following the first error span (off-trajectory), and positive for all other non-error tokens. For error spans, TWA applies a span-level unlikelihood loss −|w|·log(1−p_span), where p_span is the joint probability of all tokens in the span; this lets the model learn which specific tokens within the span to penalize rather than uniformly suppressing all of them. For non-error tokens preceding the first error, TWA uses standard cross-entropy (maximum likelihood), while off-trajectory tokens after the first error receive zero loss to avoid noisy signal from contexts the model should assign near-zero probability. The overall sequence loss is the sum of all span-level losses, and the method requires no auxiliary reward model, no preference pair construction, and no changes to test-time decoding.

## Results

On En→De (submissions + references), TWA achieves 2.882 Metric-X, 0.513 COMET, and 0.672 BLEURT, outperforming SFT (3.159/0.491/0.662), DPO (3.564/0.442/0.660), and Filter+SFT (2.950/0.499/0.670). On Zh→En (submissions + references), TWA scores 3.965 Metric-X, 0.290 COMET, and 0.653 BLEURT, again leading all baselines. TWA is the only method consistently in the top statistical cluster across all six language-pair × data-source settings and all three metrics, with the largest gains on Metric-X (the metric trained on MQM data) and the smallest on BLEURT (fully held out from checkpoint selection).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWA outperforms DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) across nearly all settings and metrics. On En→De with submissions+references, TWA improves Metric-X from 3.564 to 2.882 (−19%) and COMET from 0.442 to 0.513 (+16%), while on Zh→En the COMET gap is even more dramatic (0.113 → 0.290, more than 2.5×). The sole exception is Zh→En submissions-only on Metric-X, where DPO (4.072) marginally beats TWA (4.091), but DPO is significantly worse on COMET (0.113 vs. 0.277) and BLEURT (0.615 vs. 0.651) in that setting, suggesting DPO exploits a Metric-X idiosyncrasy rather than genuine quality improvement. TWA also beats SFT (which ignores annotations entirely) and Filter+SFT (which discards all sequences containing any error), demonstrating that span-level negative signal provides useful information beyond what sequence-level filtering can capture. The sequence-level analogue TWA-seq is the closest competitor, occasionally sharing the top cluster with TWA on Zh→En, but TWA's span-level granularity yields consistent and significant advantages, particularly on Metric-X.

## Relation to Prior Work

TWA repurposes MQM span-level error annotations—previously used only to train auxiliary metric models (MetricX, COMET) or to construct DPO preference pairs—as a direct training signal for MT models via a span-level unlikelihood loss. Conceptually, it sits between sequence-level preference optimization (DPO) and token-level negative training (TNT), occupying a middle ground where the model learns *which* tokens within an annotated error span to penalize rather than being told explicitly. It also departs from fine-grained RLHF (Wu et al., 2023), which requires an online reward model, by operating purely on offline annotated data with no auxiliary model during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that span-level annotations, which are often collected at comparable cost to sequence-level labels, can be directly exploited for finetuning without the overhead of training reward models or constructing preference pairs. A notable open question is the inconsistent benefit of ignoring off-trajectory tokens (large gain for En→De, none for Zh→En), suggesting the method's handling of post-error context is not yet principled. More broadly, as MT systems approach or exceed reference quality, annotation-based training signals like MQM become increasingly important, and TWA provides a simple template for leveraging such signals in other text generation tasks where fine-grained error localization is feasible.
