## Problem

Existing Boltzmann generators (BGs) cannot scale beyond the smallest dipeptides in Cartesian coordinates because exactly equivariant normalizing flows lack expressivity, and continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) rely on simple E(n)-GNN vector fields that produce poor proposal-target overlap. This results in low effective sample size during self-normalized importance sampling, making equilibrium sampling of tri-, tetra-, and hexa-peptides intractable for prior methods.

## Input / Output

SBG takes a small biased dataset of molecular conformations (e.g., 10^5 samples from a 1 µs MD trajectory) and a classical force-field energy function E(x) with its gradient ∇E(x) as input, operating on all-atom Cartesian coordinates in R^{n×3}. It produces uncorrelated samples from the target Boltzmann distribution along with their importance weights, enabling computation of arbitrary observables via weighted averages. The energy function is known analytically but the partition function Z is intractable, so sampling must proceed through reweighting rather than direct density evaluation.

## Method

SBG pairs a TarFlow architecture (a transformer-based, exactly invertible, non-equivariant masked autoregressive flow) with inference-time annealing. During training, SE(3) equivariance is enforced softly via random rotation augmentation and center-of-mass noise rather than hard architectural constraints, removing the tension between equivariance and invertibility that limits prior equivariant flows. At inference, proposal samples are transported toward the target Boltzmann distribution via a continuous-time annealed Langevin SDE with a linear energy interpolant between the proposal negative log-likelihood and the physical energy, with importance weights updated through Jarzynski's equality. This yields two variants: AIS (no resampling) and SMC (adaptive resampling when ESS drops below a threshold). A provably beneficial center-of-mass energy adjustment (Proposition 1) corrects for the training augmentation, and an energy thresholding strategy with a theoretical bias bound (Proposition 3) discards high-energy samples before annealing to improve numerical stability.

## Results

On trialanine, SBG AIS achieves ESS of 0.052, E-W2 of 0.797, and T-W2 of 0.450, substantially outperforming the ECNF++ baseline (ESS 0.003, E-W2 2.206, T-W2 0.962). The advantage grows with system size: on hexa-alanine, SBG AIS reduces E-W2 from 10.668 (ECNF++) to 1.021 and T-W2 from 1.902 to 1.431, while ESS improves from 0.006 to 0.034. On the decapeptide chignolin, SBG SMC achieves E-W2 of 3.571 versus 12.046 for plain SNIS, representing the first successful equilibrium sampling of a 10-residue peptide in Cartesian coordinates. SBG is also over an order of magnitude faster in GPU hours on the hexapeptide system compared to ECNF++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior Boltzmann generator baselines: SE(3)-EACF (Midgley et al., 2023a) and ECNF (Klein & Noe, 2024), plus an improved ECNF++ variant. SE(3)-EACF is only evaluated on alanine dipeptide where it achieves ESS < 10⁻³ and E-W2 of 108.2, making it effectively unusable; it is omitted from larger systems due to computational cost. ECNF (original) achieves ESS 0.119 and E-W2 0.419 on alanine dipeptide but cannot scale to trialanine. The strongest baseline, ECNF++, is competitive on alanine dipeptide (ESS 0.275, T-W2 0.189) but degrades sharply on larger systems: on trialanine its E-W2 is 2.206 vs. SBG AIS's 0.797, and on hexa-alanine its E-W2 reaches 10.668 vs. SBG AIS's 1.021. SBG wins decisively on all metrics for trialanine, tetrapeptide, and hexa-alanine, with the gap widening as system size increases, confirming that the annealing-based inference scaling is the key differentiator for scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Boltzmann generator framework (Noe et al., 2019) along two complementary axes: (1) replacing the equivariant continuous normalizing flows (ECNF, SE(3)-EACF) that underpin prior SOTA BGs with a non-equivariant transformer-based finite flow (TarFlow) that enforces SE(3) symmetry softly via data augmentation, and (2) adding inference-time scaling through continuous-time annealed importance sampling and sequential Monte Carlo, building on the Jarzynski-equality-based transport framework of NETS (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2025). The key conceptual departure is that exact invertibility of finite flows (enabling cheap log-likelihood evaluation) is what makes inference-time annealing tractable, whereas CNFs require expensive divergence simulation and ODE differentiation at every Langevin step. The paper also provides theoretical grounding (Propositions 1, 3, 4) for practical heuristics like center-of-mass energy adjustment and energy thresholding that were previously used without formal justification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBG achieves the first uncorrelated equilibrium sampling in Cartesian coordinates for tri-, tetra-, and hexa-peptides, and makes significant progress toward decapeptide (chignolin) sampling—systems where prior BGs were intractable. The practical efficiency gain is substantial: SBG is over an order of magnitude faster than ECNF++ on hexapeptides, and the inference-time scaling capability (annealing more Langevin steps) offers a knob for quality-vs-compute tradeoffs absent in standard BGs. Key limitations include the restriction to small peptides with classical force fields, the incompatibility of the annealing approach with CNFs (limiting use of modern flow-matching training), and the fact that the decapeptide results, while promising, show only partial target overlap. Future directions the authors identify include hybrid CNF-to-invertible distillation, consistency-based objectives, and autoregressive models with exact likelihoods.
