## Problem

Uniform-precision post-training quantization of LLMs suffers severe accuracy degradation at ≤3-bit, while existing element-wise mixed-precision methods (e.g., PB-LLM, SpQR) require storing per-element bitmaps or code indices that are hardware-unfriendly and add storage/computation overhead. The paper identifies that salient weights in LLMs exhibit structured spatial clustering along channels (driven by activation outlier channels), yet this observation has been largely overlooked by prior quantization research.

## Input / Output

The method consumes FP16 pre-trained LLM weights and a small calibration set (128 samples × 2048 tokens from WikiText2) to compute per-layer Hessian matrices. It outputs group-wise mixed-precision quantized weights where each group of 128 consecutive elements along the input channel is assigned a distinct bit-width (e.g., 1-bit, 2-bit, or 3-bit for a 2-bit average), along with per-group scales, zero-points, and a 2-bit-per-group precision index array. The packed representation is compatible with modified AutoGPTQ CUDA kernels for GPU inference, with weights vertically packed into integers per group to avoid padding.

## Method

SliM-LLM introduces two complementary components operating at different granularities. First, Salience-Determined Bit Allocation (SBA) computes per-group average salience using the SparseGPT Hessian-based formula, ranks groups, and then performs a double-pointer search over the number of (N−1)-bit and (N+1)-bit groups (constrained to be equal in count) to minimize KL divergence between the original and quantized layer outputs. Second, Salience-Weighted Quantizer Calibration (SQC) identifies ~1% locally salient weights within each group via a 3σ threshold, then searches a scaling parameter τ over [1−λ, 1+λ] (λ=0.1, 50 candidates) to jointly optimize the shared scale and zero-point that minimize combined L2 reconstruction error of both salient and non-salient subsets. The framework is plugged into GPTQ's Hessian-based error compensation pipeline (SliM-LLM) or OmniQuant's learnable quantizer (SliM-LLM+), preserving structured group-wise dequantization without element-wise bitmaps.

## Results

At 2-bit quantization on WikiText2, SliM-LLM reduces perplexity from 152.31 (GPTQ) to 14.58 on LLaMA-7B (a 90% improvement) and from 20.44 to 8.87 on LLaMA-13B (56% improvement). At 3-bit, SliM-LLM achieves 6.40 PPL on LLaMA-7B, marginally outperforming GPTQ (6.55) and AWQ (6.46). The gradient-based extension SliM-LLM+ further reduces 2-bit LLaMA-7B perplexity to 9.68, surpassing OmniQuant (9.72) and AffineQuant (13.51). On zero-shot benchmarks, 2-bit SliM-LLM+ achieves 51.02% average accuracy on LLaMA-7B, compared to 39.65% for GPTQ and 49.11% for OmniQuant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SliM-LLM dramatically outperforms GPTQ at 2-bit, reducing LLaMA-7B perplexity from 152.31 to 14.58 (90% reduction) and LLaMA-13B from 20.44 to 8.87 (56% reduction). Against element-wise mixed-precision baselines, SliM-LLM beats PB-LLM (14.58 vs 24.61 on LLaMA-7B) and LLM-MQ (8.87 vs 12.17 on LLaMA-13B) while remaining hardware-friendly with structured group-wise precision. Compared to codebook-based QuIP, SliM-LLM achieves lower perplexity (14.58 vs 29.74 on LLaMA-7B, 8.87 vs 12.48 on LLaMA-13B) without requiring codebook storage. At 3-bit, SliM-LLM is competitive with GPTQ and AWQ, achieving the best perplexity across all tested LLaMA variants (e.g., 7.16 vs 8.19 for GPTQ on LLaMA-3-8B). The gradient-based SliM-LLM+ slightly edges out OmniQuant at 2-bit (9.68 vs 9.72 on LLaMA-7B, 7.17 vs 7.93 on LLaMA-13B) and substantially outperforms AffineQuant (9.68 vs 13.51). In the SBA vs. ILP ablation, SBA's KL-divergence-based double-pointer search outperforms HAWQ-V2's ILP formulation (14.58 vs 17.55 on LLaMA-7B 2-bit), confirming the superiority of the proposed allocation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SliM-LLM builds directly on GPTQ's Hessian-based error compensation pipeline and OmniQuant's learnable quantizer, but departs from both the element-wise mixed-precision line (SpQR, PB-LLM, LLM-MQ) and the layer-wise allocation line (HAWQ, APTQ) by introducing a structured group-wise mixed-precision strategy. The key conceptual departure is the observation that activation outliers induce spatially clustered salience in weight matrices (formalized in Theorem 1), which justifies assigning different bit-widths to contiguous groups rather than individual elements. This positions SliM-LLM as a hardware-friendly middle ground: it captures the accuracy benefits of fine-grained mixed precision while retaining the deployment simplicity of uniform group-wise quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical path to 2-bit LLM deployment that avoids the bitmap storage and irregular memory access overheads of element-wise mixed-precision methods, demonstrated through a working AutoGPTQ-based GPU inference pipeline. The group-wise mixed-precision strategy is modular and can be plugged into existing QAT, PEFT, and codebook-based compression frameworks, broadening its applicability. Key limitations include the lack of mature hardware support for 1-bit operations and the fact that current deployment tools still cannot fully exploit mixed-precision computing, leaving room for further efficiency gains. An open question is whether the salience-clustering phenomenon driven by activation outliers generalizes uniformly across all model architectures and training regimes.
