## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods are limited to coarse-grained binary classification (normal vs. abnormal) and fail to provide fine-grained categorization of specific anomaly types or natural-language explanations of what went wrong. This lack of interpretability hinders practical deployment in high-stakes settings such as security surveillance, healthcare monitoring, and autonomous driving, where operators need to understand not just that an anomaly occurred but what type it is and why.

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed video clips as input, uniformly sampling frames at 16-frame intervals for visual encoding and n frames for VLM caption generation. It produces four outputs simultaneously: per-frame binary anomaly scores for temporal localization, a fine-grained anomaly category label (e.g., abuse, robbery, explosion), a natural-language explanation of the anomaly generated by an LLM, and frame-level anomaly degree scores derived from either the coarse classifier or the fine-grained alignment map (whichever performs better).

## Method

Ex-VAD comprises three modules operating in sequence. First, the Anomaly Explanation Generation Module (AEGM) uses BLIP-2 to produce frame-level captions, refines them via image-text cosine-similarity matching to remove VLM hallucinations, and then prompts LLaMA-3 to synthesize a coherent video-level anomaly explanation. Second, the Multimodal Anomaly Detection Module (MADM) encodes video frames with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder augmented by an LGT-Adapter for temporal modeling, encodes the generated explanation with the CLIP text encoder, fuses the two feature streams by addition, and passes them through an FFN-FC-Sigmoid head for binary anomaly scoring. Third, the Label Augment and Alignment Module (LAAM) uses GPT-4 to expand each anomaly label into multiple descriptive sentences, selects the top-k most semantically similar via cosine similarity to form enriched label embeddings, and computes a frame-by-label alignment map for fine-grained classification. Training combines binary cross-entropy (with a Top-k MIL mechanism), multi-class cross-entropy over the alignment map, and a contrastive loss that pushes the normal-class embedding away from all anomaly-class embeddings.

## Results

On fine-grained WSVAD, Ex-VAD achieves 10.15% AVG mAP on UCF-Crime and 28.23% AVG mAP on XD-Violence, outperforming the strongest baselines VADCLIP (6.68% and 24.70%) and STPrompt (6.48% and 23.44%) by 3.47 and 3.53 points respectively. On coarse-grained WSVAD, Ex-VAD reaches 88.29% AUC on UCF-Crime and 86.52% AP on XD-Violence, surpassing VADCLIP (88.02% AUC, 84.51% AP) and STPrompt (88.08% AUC, 83.97% AP). Ex-VAD is the only method that simultaneously supports fine-grained classification and anomaly explanation, while also maintaining competitive coarse-grained detection accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ex-VAD outperforms all weakly-supervised fine-grained baselines on both datasets: on UCF-Crime it leads VADCLIP by 3.47 points (10.15 vs 6.68 AVG mAP) and STPrompt by 3.67 points; on XD-Violence it leads VADCLIP by 3.53 points (28.23 vs 24.70) and STPrompt by 4.79 points. For coarse-grained detection, Ex-VAD achieves the best UCF-Crime AUC (88.29%) and XD-Violence AP (86.52%), edging out VADCLIP (88.02% AUC, 84.51% AP) and STPrompt (88.08% AUC, 83.97% AP). Among training-free methods, VERA achieves the highest coarse-grained scores (88.2 AP, 86.6 AUC) but lacks fine-grained capability, while LAVAD trails significantly (62.01 AP, 80.28 AUC). VADOr, which fine-tunes a VLM for explainability, matches Ex-VAD's UCF-Crime AUC (88.1 vs 88.29) but does not support fine-grained classification. Ex-VAD is the only method combining fine-grained detection with anomaly explanation, making it uniquely positioned for practical deployment where both precision and interpretability are required.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ex-VAD builds directly on VADCLIP's CLIP-based fine-grained WSVAD framework (LGT-Adapter for temporal modeling, alignment maps for category matching) and on LAVAD's demonstration that VLM+LLM pipelines can produce anomaly descriptions without training. The key departure is unifying these two previously separate threads: prior fine-grained methods (VADCLIP, STPrompt) lack explanations, while prior explainable methods (LAVAD, VERA, VADOr) either forgo fine-grained classification or require heavy fine-tuning of large models. Ex-VAD repurposes the LLM-generated explanation text as a dual-purpose signal—both an interpretable output and a textual feature fused with visual features for detection—rather than treating explanation as a post-hoc add-on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explainability and detection accuracy are complementary rather than a trade-off: the anomaly explanation text generated for interpretability also improves fine-grained mAP by ~50% relative over VADCLIP on UCF-Crime. This dual-use pattern (LLM text as both explanation and detection feature) is a practical recipe for deploying VAD in high-stakes settings where operators need to understand *why* an anomaly was flagged. Open questions include the reliance on external LLM inference (LLaMA-3, GPT-4) at test time, which complicates real-time deployment, and whether the approach generalizes to open-world anomaly categories not present in the training label set. The ablation showing that raw VLM captions *degrade* performance (86.76→86.33 AUC) before cleaning is a useful caution for practitioners integrating VLM outputs into downstream pipelines.
