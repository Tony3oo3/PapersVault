## Problem

Deep RL agents for continuous control suffer from primacy bias, where experience replay causes overfitting to early transitions, degrading sample efficiency and generalizability. Existing reset mechanisms only partially address this because older samples still dominate the replay buffer's sampling distribution. The paper asks whether a combined 'forget and grow' strategy—inspired by infantile amnesia in neuroscience—can more effectively mitigate this bias.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from the environment (varying dimensionality across MuJoCo, DMControl, Meta-World, and HumanoidBench tasks) and outputs a continuous action sampled from a max-entropy stochastic policy. Training operates at a high replay ratio of 10 with a 1M-transition buffer, and the critic network is modularized into 2–4 residual blocks of 512-dim dense layers with ELU activations and LayerNorm, while the actor remains a simple 3-layer MLP.

## Method

FoG introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of the OBAC backbone: (1) Experience Replay Decay, which assigns each transition a sampling weight w = max(τ, (1−ε)^(t−i)) so that older transitions are gradually down-weighted (bounded by a constant per Theorem 3.2), effectively 'forgetting' early data; and (2) Network Expansion, which dynamically inserts new residual blocks (two 512-dim dense layers + LayerNorm) into the critic at scheduled intervals after each reset, providing fresh untrained capacity to adapt to shifted data distributions. The critic starts at 2 blocks and expands to 4, with the optimizer reinitialized and learning rate scaled by the parameter ratio at each expansion. Combined with a replay ratio of 10, periodic resets, and a 250k-step OBAC protection period after resets, FoG scales the critic to 23M parameters—well beyond the 5M saturation point reported for prior methods—while maintaining training stability.

## Results

FoG achieves a total normalized score of 0.92 across 41 tasks spanning MuJoCo, DMControl, Meta-World, and HumanoidBench, substantially outperforming TD-MPC2 (0.70), SimBa (0.69), and BRO (0.76). On the hardest benchmarks, FoG reaches 0.99 on DMC-Hard and 0.96 on MuJoCo, compared to BRO's 0.76 and 0.85 respectively. FoG also unlocks favorable model scaling up to 23M critic parameters, exceeding the 5M saturation point reported by BRO. On HumanoidBench, FoG (0.82) matches TD-MPC2 (0.81), the strongest baseline in that domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoG outperforms all three primary baselines on the aggregate normalized score (0.92 vs. 0.76 BRO, 0.70 TD-MPC2, 0.69 SimBa). Against BRO, FoG gains the largest margins on DMC-Hard (+0.23) and HumanoidBench (+0.28), while on MuJoCo it improves from 0.85 to 0.96. Against TD-MPC2, FoG dominates on MuJoCo (0.96 vs. 0.07) and DMC-Hard (0.99 vs. 0.50), but is essentially tied on HumanoidBench (0.82 vs. 0.81). Against SimBa, FoG wins on every benchmark, with the largest gap on Meta-World (0.99 vs. 0.64). In a separate comparison against Neuroplastic Expansion (NE), FoG outperforms NE across all four tested tasks (HalfCheetah, Dog Walk, Dog Trot, Humanoid Walk) even at low update-to-data ratios, and NE's performance gains from increased model capacity are marginal compared to FoG's consistent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the OBAC off-policy actor-critic framework and BRO's modular network architecture (residual blocks with layer normalization), while directly addressing the primacy bias problem formalized by Nikishin et al. (2022b) and Qiao et al. (2023). It departs from prior reset-based mitigations by showing—both theoretically (Theorem 3.1) and empirically—that periodic resets alone leave older transitions over-sampled, and instead introduces two complementary mechanisms: Experience Replay Decay (analogous to memory disruption in infantile amnesia) and Network Expansion (analogous to neurogenesis). The network expansion is conceptually related to but simpler than plasticity injection (Nikishin et al., 2023) and Neuroplastic Expansion (Liu et al., 2024), requiring no complex parameter adjustments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FoG provides a principled, biologically-motivated solution to primacy bias that unlocks favorable model scaling up to 23M parameters (versus the ~5M saturation reported in BRO), and achieves state-of-the-art results across 41 tasks on four major benchmarks with a single set of hyperparameters. However, the authors note that the theoretical analysis is limited to bounding sampling frequencies rather than providing convergence guarantees, and the increased computational cost and longer training times may limit practicality in time-sensitive settings. A key open question is universality: FoG integrates well with SAC and OBAC but showed conflicting results when combined with BRO, suggesting the interaction between forgetting/growing mechanisms and other algorithmic components remains poorly understood.
