## Problem

SignSGD is widely used as a practical optimizer and a simplified model for understanding Adam, yet the precise mechanisms by which the sign function preconditions the optimization landscape and transforms gradient noise have remained qualitatively understood but quantitatively uncharacterized. This paper addresses the gap by deriving exact high-dimensional limiting equations that isolate and quantify four distinct effects of SignSGD: effective learning rate, noise compression, diagonal preconditioning, and gradient noise reshaping.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data covariance matrix K (assumed Gaussian with bounded spectrum), a label noise distribution ε with a C² density near zero, a continuous learning rate schedule η(t), and an initialization θ₀. It produces a limiting SDE (SIGNHSGD) whose drift involves the preconditioned matrix K̄ = D⁻¹K and a noise-compression factor φ(R) depending on the risk and noise law, and whose diffusion involves the arcsine kernel matrix K_σ. The SDE further concentrates to a deterministic ODE (SIGNODE) — a coupled system of d scalar equations indexed by eigenvalues of K̄ — that predicts the risk trajectory with error O(d^{δ-1/2}) for any δ ∈ (1/3, 1/2).

## Method

The core technique is high-dimensional homogenization: the discrete SignSGD iterates are shown to converge to a continuous SDE (SIGNHSGD) via resolvent-based concentration of measure, exploiting the fact that the sign function's non-Lipschitz compression can be Taylor-expanded around zero with errors that vanish as d → ∞. The key insight is that the sign operator's effect decomposes cleanly into four multiplicative/additive terms in the SDE: a risk-dependent effective learning rate (2/π)/√(2P), a noise-compression scalar ψ(R) = E[exp(-ε²/4R)]·√(1+E[ε²]/2R), a diagonal preconditioner D⁻¹ (where D_ii = √K_ii), and a reshaped diffusion matrix K_σ with entries arcsin(K_ij/√(K_ii K_jj)). The SDE is then concentrated to a deterministic ODE by introducing d scalar variables r_i tracking risk along eigendirections of K̄, yielding a closed coupled system whose stationary point and convergence rate can be analyzed explicitly. The analysis requires a resolvent off-diagonal decay assumption on K̄ (no low-dimensional eigenvector structure) and a C² density condition on the noise near zero to ensure Lipschitz continuity of the SDE coefficients.

## Results

The paper derives an exact high-dimensional limiting SDE (SIGNHSGD) and deterministic ODE (SIGNODE) for SignSGD risk dynamics, with Theorem 1 establishing that the risk of discrete SignSGD converges to the SDE solution at rate O(d^{δ-1/2}) and Theorem 2 extending this to the ODE. Theorem 3 gives the exact limiting risk under Gaussian noise: R_∞ = (πη/(32d))Tr(D)·(πηTr(D)/(2d) + √(π²η²Tr(D)²/(4d²) + 16v²)), showing a crossover in η-dependence. Theorem 4 establishes that SignSGD converges at rate Tr(K̄)/(d·λ_min(K)), which beats SGD's rate Tr(K)/(d·λ_min(K)) whenever the diagonal-preconditioned condition number κ̄(K̄) < κ̄(K). The ODE accurately tracks risk dynamics even at d=500 and on real non-Gaussian datasets (CIFAR-10, IMDB), demonstrating universality of the Gaussian-data assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against vanilla SGD (Collins-Woodfin & Paquette, 2023), with learning rates matched via the effective-learning-rate identification η_SGD = (2/π)·η/√(2P(Θ_t)). The paper isolates four effects and determines when SignSGD wins or loses: (1) Diagonal preconditioning — SignSGD wins when κ̄(K̄) < κ̄(K), e.g., on diagonal or block-diagonal covariances with differing scales, where it achieves a speedup analogous to conjugate gradient over gradient descent; it loses when eigenvectors are randomized (diagonal entries concentrate, nullifying the preconditioner). (2) Noise compression — SignSGD wins for heavy-tailed noise (Student's-t, Cauchy) where ψ > π/2, and catastrophically loses for noise bounded away from zero (Rademacher) where ψ → 0 exponentially, creating a risk floor. (3) For isotropic data with Gaussian noise, ψ = 1 and both methods are equivalent after optimal learning-rate tuning. (4) Gradient noise reshaping (K → K_σ) has no closed-form winner but can break alignment between large gradient-variance and large gradient-bias directions, aiding stability. On CIFAR-10 and IMDB, the SignSGD ODE matches empirical risk curves closely while the SGD baseline (VANILLAODE) does not capture the same dynamics, confirming the preconditioning and reshaping effects in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional homogenization framework of Collins-Woodfin & Paquette (2023) for vanilla SGD to SignSGD, overcoming the technical challenge of the non-smooth sign function through resolvent-based concentration estimates. It builds on the broader high-dimensional optimization program (Paquette et al., 2022) and the weak-approximation SDE literature (Li et al., 2019), while distinguishing itself from the concurrent work of Compagnoni et al. (2025) by operating in a qualitatively different asymptotic regime. The paper positions SignSGD as a tractable proxy for understanding Adam, connecting to prior empirical observations about the sign component's role in adaptive methods (Kunstner et al., 2023; Balles & Hennig, 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantitative theoretical decomposition of SignSGD's effects into four precisely characterized mechanisms (effective learning rate, noise compression, diagonal preconditioning, gradient noise reshaping), moving beyond the qualitative consensus in the adaptive-optimizer literature. The exact ODE (SIGNODE) provides a practical tool for predicting risk dynamics, validated on real non-Gaussian datasets (CIFAR-10, IMDB), and the conjectured Adam limit opens a path toward understanding the dominant optimizer in practice. Key limitations include the restriction to linear regression with Gaussian data (though universality is observed empirically), the one-pass setting, and the need for Assumption 3(iii) on eigenvector structure. Open questions include formalizing the non-Gaussian data universality, analyzing non-diagonal preconditioning effects, and rigorously establishing the Adam limit.
