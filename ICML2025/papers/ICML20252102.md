## Problem

Learning probabilistic energy-based models by maximum likelihood estimation requires computing the partition function, which is intractable in combinatorially-large discrete spaces such as sets or permutations. Existing work either relies on MCMC sampling (introducing bias and high cost) or sidesteps the partition function entirely, yielding non-probabilistic EBMs. No tractable method existed for learning *probabilistic* EBMs in these spaces without MCMC, nor for estimating the log-partition on unseen inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular feature vector x and produces either a binary label vector (multilabel classification over {0,1}^k) or a permutation (label ranking over k! outputs), depending on the task. Uniquely, it also outputs a learned log-partition function τ(x) parameterized as a separate neural network, which can be evaluated at inference time on unseen inputs to approximate the normalization constant. The energy model g and the log-partition τ are jointly optimized, with g parameterized as a linear model, MLP, or ResNet, and τ as a constant, MLP, ResNet, or input-convex neural network (ICNN).

## Method

The core idea is to introduce the log-partition as a Lagrange multiplier τ ∈ F(X) and jointly minimize over both the energy function g and τ in a min-min formulation, yielding the tractable objective L_MLE(g,τ) = E_x[τ(x) + Σ_{y'} q(y'|x)(exp(g(x,y')−τ(x))−1)] − E_{(x,y)}[g(x,y)]. This is optimized via a doubly stochastic scheme that samples (x,y) pairs from the data distribution and y′ samples from a fixed reference distribution q, requiring no MCMC. The paper proves that minimizing in the space of continuous functions exactly recovers the MLE solution, and that the approach generalizes to the full family of Fenchel-Young losses (e.g., sparsemax), providing the first tractable method for sparsemax-based EBMs in combinatorial spaces. In practice, both g and τ are parameterized as neural networks, and the learned τ generalizes to unseen inputs, as empirically validated against closed-form log-sum-exp values.

## Results

On multilabel classification, the proposed logistic min-min approach with an MLP energy model achieves f1-scores of 65.04 (Yeast), 75.35 (Scene), 46.48 (Birds), 65.33 (Emotions), and 44.91 (Cal500), consistently outperforming MCMC-based MLE and the min-max adversarial baseline. The sparsemax min-min variant reaches 46.39 on Cal500 and 75.83 on Scene, the best scores in those columns. On label ranking (Kendall's tau), the logistic min-min method with a Birkhoff polytope and MLP achieves 90.80 (Authorship), 85.43 (Glass), and 98.52 (Iris), surpassing both MCMC and min-max baselines across all six ranking datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is the generalized Fenchel-Young loss (Blondel et al., 2022), a non-probabilistic structured prediction loss. The proposed logistic min-min approach outperforms it by 3.6–3.7 f1 points on Yeast (65.04 vs. 61.31 for MLP), 4.5 points on Scene (75.35 vs. 70.84), 5.7 points on Birds (46.48 vs. 40.73), 3.5 points on Emotions (65.33 vs. 61.85), and 7.6 points on Cal500 (44.91 vs. 37.35). Against MCMC-based MLE (Metropolis-Hastings), the proposed method wins on Yeast (65.04 vs. 60.34), Birds (46.48 vs. 33.13), and Emotions (65.33 vs. 62.23), though MCMC is competitive on Cal500 (45.70 vs. 44.91). The min-max adversarial baseline (using REINFORCE gradients) is substantially worse, losing by 6–10 f1 points on most datasets (e.g., 52.80 vs. 63.41 on Yeast, 52.29 vs. 70.78 on Scene). The sparsemax min-min variant also beats the generalized Fenchel-Young baseline on all five datasets, confirming the advantage extends beyond the logistic loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., 2020a) and the generalized Fenchel-Young losses for non-probabilistic EBMs (Blondel et al., 2022) into the probabilistic setting, where the log-partition function must be handled. The key conceptual departure from prior work that treats the log-partition as an optimization variable (Wang et al., 2018; Arbel et al., 2021; Senetaire et al., 2025) is the move from unsupervised density estimation (where the log-partition is an input-independent scalar) to supervised structured prediction (where it is an input-dependent function parameterized as a neural network). The min-min formulation with a Lagrange multiplier also distinguishes this work from adversarial min-max approaches (e.g., f-GANs) and from MCMC-based MLE (contrastive divergence), offering unbiased gradients that only require sampling from a fixed reference distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tractable MCMC-free method for MLE of probabilistic EBMs in combinatorially-large discrete spaces (permutations, sets), with provable recovery of the exact MLE objective in function space. The learned log-partition network generalizes to unseen inputs, enabling a new means of estimating normalization constants at inference time. A notable empirical finding is that using fewer prior samples y' acts as an implicit regularizer, yielding better test f1-scores than exact MLE—a phenomenon that challenges the assumption that more accurate gradient estimates always improve generalization. Open questions include whether the learned log-partition can be leveraged to design better samplers from p(y|x), and whether the approach extends to continuous output spaces where the reference distribution is more complex.
