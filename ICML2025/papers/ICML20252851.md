## Problem

Existing VAE-based adversarial attacks on diffusion models introduce noise distortions but fail to completely erase identity semantics from generated images. The paper identifies that the variance of VAE latent codes is the key factor governing semantic erasure, and reveals that prior loss functions (MSE, PID's log-variance) suffer from gradient sign misalignment with the variance growth direction or progressive compression of optimization space, preventing efficient and complete semantic removal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 reference images per identity at 512×512 resolution (from CelebA-HQ, VGGFace2, or WikiArt) and produces an adversarial perturbation δ (L∞ budget η=0.05) via 30 PGD steps with step size 1/255. The perturbed images are then used as the training set for downstream personalization fine-tuning (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion, IP-Adapter). The attack targets only the VAE encoder's variance output, operating independently of the UNet/denoiser architecture and the text prompt, which is what enables cross-model transferability.

## Method

The paper decomposes the gradient ∂L/∂δ via the chain rule into ∂L/∂σ² · M_{i,j}, where M_{i,j} is the product of two Jacobian matrices mapping pixel-space perturbations through the VAE encoder to latent variance. This reveals that the loss's gradient with respect to σ² must maintain a consistent sign aligned with M_{i,j} for effective variance growth. The Laplace (LA) loss L=|σ²−μ|/b provides a constant gradient 1/b, making each PGD step locally optimal by letting the Jacobian structure alone determine the update direction. The Lagrange Entropy (LE) loss L=−Σσ²ⱼlog(σ²ⱼ)+λ(Σσ²ⱼ−c)² combines an entropy term that strongly boosts small variance components with a Lagrange penalty that preserves optimization space for larger variances, avoiding both MSE's sign flips and PID's late-stage gradient decay. Both losses achieve complete identity erasure (ISM=0, FDFR=1) in 30 steps versus PID's 900 steps, using only ~4.5 GB GPU memory.

## Results

Against DreamBooth on CelebA-HQ and VGGFace2, both LA and LE achieve ISM = 0 and FDFR = 1, completely erasing identity semantics and generating pure-noise images. LE attains Brisque = 155.804 and LPIPS = 1.021 on CelebA-HQ (154.494 / 1.028 on VGGFace2), while LA reaches Brisque = 155.845 and LPIPS = 0.959 / 1.031. The best baseline, PID, still retains ISM = 0.069 (CelebA-HQ) and 0.046 (VGGFace2) with FDFR of only 0.938 and 0.968, confirming residual identity leakage. Both proposed methods complete the attack in 30 steps (~8 s, 4.5 GB GPU) versus PID's 900 steps (~241 s, 4.6 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both LA and LE decisively outperform all eight baselines on every metric across CelebA-HQ and VGGFace2, achieving ISM = 0 and FDFR = 1 (complete identity erasure) where the strongest baseline PID still leaks ISM = 0.069 / 0.046 and FDFR = 0.938 / 0.968. The VAE-based baselines Mist (MSE loss) and PID (log-variance loss) are the closest competitors but fail to fully erase semantics: Mist retains ISM ≈ 0.25 and PID retains ISM ≈ 0.05–0.07. UNet-gradient-based methods (AdvDM, ASPL, MetaCloak, SimAC, DisDiff, SDS-) perform substantially worse, with ISM ranging from 0.14 to 0.66, confirming their poor transferability and reliance on specific prompts/architectures. In cross-model transferability tests (Tables 2–3), LA and LE maintain ISM = 0 on SD2.1 and ISM ≈ 0.17–0.29 on SDXL/SD3.5/FLUX.1-dev, while all baselines degrade sharply (e.g., PID drops to ISM = 0.48–0.73 on SD3.5/FLUX). In terms of efficiency, LA/LE require only 8 s and 4.5 GB GPU per image versus PID's 241 s and ASPL/SimAC's 125–190 s with 34–39 GB, a 30× speedup over PID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-encoder attack line initiated by Mist (MSE-based variance maximization) and PID (log-variance loss), which identified that VAE latent code statistics influence generated image quality. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical analysis showing that prior loss functions (MSE, PID's log-variance) suffer from gradient sign misalignment with the variance-growth direction or progressive compression of optimization space, and that variance alone—without attacking the mean—is sufficient for complete semantic erasure. The proposed LA (constant-gradient Laplace) and LE (entropy + Lagrange) losses are designed to maintain consistent alignment with the variance-growth direction at every PGD step, replacing PID's log-variance objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup (30 vs. 900 PGD steps) and 4.5 GB / 8-second per-image cost make VAE-based protection practical for individual users with consumer hardware, addressing a major adoption barrier of prior methods. Transferability across UNet-based (SD2.1, SDXL) and MM-DiT-based (SD3.5, FLUX.1-dev) architectures is a meaningful advantage over UNet-gradient-dependent baselines, though complete erasure (ISM=0) degrades to partial erasure (ISM≈0.22–0.28) on the most advanced architectures. Open questions include robustness against purification (DiffPure, GrIDPure reduce FDFR from 1 to ~0.24) and adaptive attacks that clip or fix latent variance, as well as whether the gradient explosion observed after 30 steps can be mitigated to allow further optimization without early stopping.
