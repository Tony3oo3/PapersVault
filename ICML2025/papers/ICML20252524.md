## Problem

Existing federated fine-tuning methods for CLIP use a single shared adapter that cannot distinguish domain-specific characteristics across heterogeneous clients, and they lack the ability to generalize to unseen out-of-domain data. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously by designing a cross-silo federated approach with multiple fine-grained domain-specific adapters and a dedicated cross-domain generalization mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224) as input, processed through CLIP's frozen ViT-B-32 image encoder and text encoder on the server side, and outputs a class label from a shared label set across all domains. For in-domain inference, the domain-specific adapter logit is used directly; for out-of-domain inference, an attention-weighted aggregation across all domain adapters produces the final prediction. The server also consumes synthetic images generated by Stable Diffusion 2.0 (10% of real data volume per domain) to enable knowledge transfer without exposing private client data.

## Method

FedAG places the full CLIP model on the server with N domain-specific attention-based adapters, while each client holds only a lightweight ViT-Tiny model trained on its private domain data. The server update employs two key modules: (1) quality-aware in-domain mutual learning, which uses bidirectional KL divergence between client model predictions and CLIP adapter predictions on synthetic data, weighted by per-sample quality scores estimated via prototype-based cosine similarity; and (2) attention-regularized cross-domain learning, which aggregates logits from all N adapters through learned attention weights and applies a margin-based regularizer to ensure the domain-specific adapter dominates. A momentum update (γ=0.9) bridges the gap between real-data-trained client models and synthetic-data-trained server models at each communication round, preventing performance disruption from the distribution shift.

## Results

FedAG achieves the best in-domain accuracy across all three domain-shift datasets: 98.62% on ImageCLEF-DA (Caltech), 88.79% on Office-Home (Real), and 84.92% on DomainNet (Real). For out-of-domain generalization, FedAG reaches 83.78% on ImageCLEF-DA (Pascal), 68.15% on Office-Home (Clipart), and 63.29% on DomainNet (Sketch), with a particularly striking 36% relative improvement over the best baseline (FedOT) on the Quickdraw domain (21.04% vs. 15.51%). FedAG also approaches or surpasses the centralized upper bound CLIP_M in several domains (e.g., 98.62% vs. 98.55% on Caltech, 98.56% vs. 95.86% on ImageNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAG outperforms all six baselines on every domain across all three datasets in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. Against the zero-shot CLIP_Z baseline, FedAG gains up to 7.7% on DomainNet Clipart (70.36% vs. 62.67%) and 1.7% on Office-Home Real (88.79% vs. 86.33%), confirming the value of federated fine-tuning over raw zero-shot inference. Compared to centralized PEFT methods (CLIP_L with LoRA and CLIP_A with a shared adapter), FedAG achieves 0.13–3.2% higher accuracy, with the largest gap on DomainNet Clipart (70.36% vs. 68.15% for LoRA), attributed to domain-specific adapters versus a single shared adapter. Among federated baselines, FedAG surpasses FedCLIP by 1.4–2.4% (e.g., 70.36% vs. 67.96% on Clipart) and FedOT by 1.2–2.7% (e.g., 70.36% vs. 67.68% on Clipart), demonstrating that multi-adapter design with cross-domain learning is more effective than single-adapter aggregation. Classical federated methods (FedAvg, FedProx) lag significantly behind, with FedAvg achieving only 51.66% on DomainNet Clipart versus FedAG's 70.36%, highlighting the importance of leveraging the foundation model. In the out-of-domain evaluation, FedAG's advantage over FedOT is most pronounced on DomainNet Quickdraw (21.04% vs. 15.51%, a 36% relative gain), underscoring the critical role of the attention-regularized cross-domain learning module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) line of work initiated by FedCLIP and FedPETuning, which deploy a single shared adapter on each client and aggregate via FedAvg. FedAG departs from this by (1) placing the full CLIP model on the server rather than each client, (2) using multiple domain-specific attention-based adapters instead of one shared adapter, and (3) introducing a bidirectional quality-aware mutual learning loss and an attention-regularized cross-domain aggregation mechanism that have no direct counterpart in prior federated fine-tuning methods. The use of Stable Diffusion-generated synthetic data for server-side training and textual inversion for implicit style transfer are practical additions not seen in the FedCLIP/FedOT baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: federated fine-tuning of vision foundation models that must generalize to unseen domains, a scenario common in real deployments where new data distributions appear after training. The finding that a federated method with only synthetic data on the server can match or slightly exceed a centralized upper bound (CLIP_M) is encouraging for privacy-preserving settings. However, the cross-silo assumption (few, mutually exclusive domain clients) limits immediate applicability to cross-device federated learning, and the reliance on Stable Diffusion for synthetic data generation introduces a dependency on the quality of text-to-image models. Open questions include scalability to many domains, applicability to other foundation model families (language, multimodal), and whether the cross-domain attention mechanism generalizes beyond image classification.
