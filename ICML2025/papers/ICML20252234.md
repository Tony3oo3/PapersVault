## Problem

RL-trained policy networks produce erratic, fluctuating actions in real-world deployment, causing actuator wear, safety risks, and performance degradation. Prior work either constrains policy smoothness via Lipschitz bounds or adds action-similarity penalties, but none explicitly identifies and decouples the two root causes—observation noise and policy non-smoothness—while the previous SOTA (LipsNet) is restricted to MLPs with piecewise-linear activations and requires Jacobian computation at inference time.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a window of N consecutive observation vectors (each of dimension D), stacked into an N×D matrix; when fewer than N observations are available, missing entries are zero-padded. After Fourier filtering, only the current-timestep filtered feature (a single D-dimensional vector) is passed to the controller layer, which outputs a single continuous control action. The trainable filter matrix H is complex-valued of shape N×(⌊D/2⌋+1), exploiting the conjugate symmetry of the real-valued observation spectrum.

## Method

LipsNet++ is a two-stage policy network that decouples noise filtering from smooth control. The Fourier filter layer applies a 2D FFT to the N×D observation window, performs a Hadamard product of the half-spectrum with a trainable complex filter matrix H, restores full conjugate symmetry, and applies IFFT to recover filtered time-domain signals; a Frobenius-norm penalty on H in the actor loss encourages the network to suppress noise frequencies while retaining informative ones. The Lipschitz controller layer is an arbitrary differentiable network whose smoothness is enforced by a Jacobian regularization term (λ_k‖∇f‖) added to the actor loss, which the paper proves (Theorem 3.2) approximates the local Lipschitz constant and converges to it as the neighborhood radius shrinks. Unlike LipsNet's MGN, this regularization is network-wise rather than layer-wise, requires no Jacobian computation during forward inference, and supports any differentiable architecture. The combined tailored loss L'' = L + λ_k‖∇f‖ + λ_h‖H‖_F is injected via a PyTorch backward hook, making LipsNet++ a drop-in replacement for the actor in standard actor-critic algorithms (TD3, DDPG, SAC, DSAC, etc.).

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) with TD3, LipsNet++ achieves the highest Total Average Return (TAR) and lowest Action Fluctuation Ratio (AFR) across all four environments (Cartpole, Reacher, Cheetah, Walker) in both noise-free and noisy settings. In the Walker environment, LipsNet++ increases TAR by 3.4% (961 vs. 929) and reduces AFR by 35.5% (0.78 vs. 1.21) compared to LipsNet-L, the previous SOTA. On the double integrator at high noise (σ=0.3), LipsNet++ achieves TAR of −59.9 and AFR of 0.05, an 8.2% TAR improvement and 75.0% AFR reduction over LipsNet-L. In real-world mini-vehicle driving at noise amplitude 20, LipsNet++ achieves a 5.9% TAR increase and 90.0% AFR reduction over a standard MLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipsNet++ outperforms all four baselines on both TAR and AFR across every tested environment. Against LipsNet (Song et al., 2023), the previous SOTA, LipsNet++ achieves a 3.4% TAR gain and 35.5% AFR reduction on DMControl Walker, and an 8.2% TAR gain with 75.0% AFR reduction on the double integrator at σ=0.3; it also eliminates LipsNet's forward-inference Jacobian computation, reducing forward time from 0.75 ms to 0.16 ms. Against MLP-SN (Takase et al., 2020), LipsNet++ dominates on DMControl Reacher (TAR 988 vs. 967 at best spectral norm, AFR 0.01 vs. 0.13) and on the double integrator at σ=0.3 (TAR −59.9 vs. −71.8, AFR 0.05 vs. 0.20), while avoiding the layer-wise tuning difficulty of spectral normalization. Against CAPS (Mysore et al., 2021) and L2C2 (Kobayashi, 2022), both evaluated only on the double integrator, LipsNet++ achieves substantially better AFR at high noise (0.05 vs. 0.37 for CAPS and 0.33 for L2C2 at σ=0.3) while also maintaining higher TAR (−59.9 vs. −85.9 and −82.3), without requiring neighboring-state sampling or sensitive penalty hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LipsNet (Song et al., ICML 2023), which constrained policy smoothness via Multi-dimensional Gradient Normalization but was limited to MLPs with piecewise linear activations and required Jacobian computation at inference. It also departs from the broader line of action-smoothing methods (CAPS, L2C2, SR²L, PIC, TAAC, MLP-SN) by identifying observation noise as a *separate* fundamental cause of action fluctuation, distinct from policy non-smoothness, and addressing both in a decoupled manner. The Fourier filter layer (FFT + trainable complex filter matrix) and the Jacobian regularization (replacing MGN with a network-wise, inference-free constraint) are the two new architectural contributions, framed through the classical control-theory analogy of filter-then-controller within a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying RL in physical systems (drones, vehicles, robots) where action jitter causes actuator wear and safety risks, and provides a drop-in PyTorch module compatible with standard actor-critic algorithms (TD3, DDPG, SAC, DSAC, PPO). The real-world mini-vehicle experiments (90% AFR reduction, successful obstacle avoidance under 10× noise where MLP crashes) strengthen the sim-to-real claim beyond typical benchmark-only validation. Limitations include ~1.6× training wall-clock overhead and slower backward propagation (1.98 ms vs. 0.45 ms for LipsNet-L), which the authors acknowledge and propose to address via zero-order gradient estimation. Open directions include an attention mechanism for input-adaptive filter matrices and extension to highway-scale vehicles and embodied AI.
