## Problem

Split Conformal Prediction guarantees only marginal FCR control (expected false coverage rate), not FCP control (actual false coverage proportion) with high probability across multiple test points simultaneously. While Gazin et al. (2024) provided valid FCP bounds via DKW-type inequalities, those bounds are conservative, yielding unnecessarily wide intervals. Additionally, existing aggregation schemes for conformal prediction (Bonferroni, CV+) require the aggregated statistic to be a valid p-value, limiting flexibility and producing overly conservative intervals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained regression model (or K models for aggregation), a calibration set of n labeled points, and m test points with observed features but unobserved outcomes. It outputs prediction intervals for each test point along with a uniform-in-α FCP upper bound that holds with probability at least 1−δ. A key practical property is that once calibration is performed, the FCP bound is valid simultaneously for all α values, allowing users to adjust the nominal level post hoc without re-running the procedure.

## Method

CoJER (Conformal-JER) achieves sharp FCP control by combining the joint distribution characterization of conformal p-values under exchangeability (Gazin et al., 2024) with Joint Error Rate (JER) calibration (Blanchard et al., 2020). The key insight is to derive the candidate threshold template directly from Monte Carlo samples of the conformal p-value distribution itself, rather than using a priori templates, which yields tighter JER control and hence sharper FCP bounds. The calibration selects the least conservative threshold family from this distribution-matched template that controls the empirical JER at level δ, and FCP control follows via a tight equivalence between FCP and the empirical CDF of conformal p-values. For aggregation, the method applies a generic function f (harmonic mean in practice) to per-model p-values before JER calibration, requiring no validity of the aggregated statistic as a p-value—only the exchangeability of the underlying non-conformity scores. This yields a fully nonparametric procedure with intervals only ~15% wider than standard SCP on average, compared to ~80% for the DKW-based alternative.

## Results

Across 17 OpenML datasets and 5 regression models (RF, MLP, SVR, KNN, Lasso), CoJER achieves valid FCP control at level δ=0.1 (i.e., FCP ≤ α in ≥90% of splits), while standard SCP fails FCP control in up to 65% of splits (FCP event coverage as low as 30–35%). In terms of interval informativeness, CoJER intervals are on average only ~15% longer than standard SCP intervals (~25% in the worst case), compared to ~80% longer for the DKW-based method of Gazin et al. (2024). In the aggregation setting, CoJER with harmonic-mean aggregation yields intervals ~75% shorter than CV+ (Barber et al., 2021) and ~20% shorter than Bonferroni intersection (Lei et al., 2018), while maintaining FCP control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Split Conformal Prediction (Lei et al., 2018), CoJER trades a modest increase in interval length (~15% on average) for a strong FCP guarantee that SCP provably lacks; SCP's FCP event coverage drops to 30–35% on some model–dataset combinations. Against the DKW-type FCP control of Gazin et al. (2024), CoJER wins decisively on interval tightness: in worst-case scenarios CoJER intervals are ~25% longer than SCP while Gazin et al. intervals are ~80% longer, making CoJER substantially less conservative. In the multi-model aggregation setting, CoJER (harmonic mean) outperforms both CV+ (arithmetic mean p-value aggregation, Barber et al., 2021) and Bonferroni intersection (Lei et al., 2018): CV+ intervals are ~75% larger and Bonferroni intervals ~20% larger on average, while all three methods nominally control FCP. Notably, CV+ and Bonferroni achieve FCP control only through excessive conservativeness (FCP event realized 100% of the time on most datasets), whereas CoJER provides a formal theoretical guarantee via JER control without requiring the aggregated statistic to be a valid p-value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads: the characterization of the joint distribution of conformal p-values under exchangeability (Gazin et al., 2024) and the Joint Error Rate (JER) control framework (Blanchard et al., 2020; Blain et al., 2023). Where Gazin et al. derive FCP bounds via a DKW-type inequality on the empirical CDF of conformal p-values, CoJER instead calibrates a threshold family directly against the JER, using a template whose shape is derived from the conformal p-value distribution itself—yielding tighter bounds. The aggregation extension departs from prior conformal aggregation methods (Lei et al., 2018; Barber et al., 2021) by not requiring the aggregated statistic to be a valid p-value, making the procedure valid for arbitrary aggregation functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCP control is the practically relevant guarantee when making simultaneous predictions (e.g., multiple patients, assets, or batches), yet standard SCP only controls the expected FCP (FCR), which can be violated in a large fraction of realizations. CoJER closes this gap at a modest interval-length cost (~15% over SCP vs. ~80% for the prior DKW-based method), making it a drop-in upgrade for practitioners already using SCP. Open questions include the impact of FCP vs. FCR control on downstream decision-making, extension to classification and other UQ frameworks (e.g., jackknife+), and whether the Monte Carlo calibration overhead becomes prohibitive at very large m.
