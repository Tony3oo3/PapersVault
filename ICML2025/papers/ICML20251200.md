## Problem

Existing unsupervised multivariate time-series anomaly detection (MTSAD) methods—whether reconstruction-based or contrastive learning-based—rely on superficial differences in data values or distributions and overlook inter-variable causal relationships. This causes normal causal-preserving variations (e.g., an air conditioner running longer during a heatwave) to be misidentified as anomalies, producing false alarms. The paper tackles this by explicitly integrating causal structure into the contrastive learning pipeline for MTSAD.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sliding windows of multivariate time-series (default window size 10 over N variables) along with a pre-trained forecasting-based causal discovery model (CUTS+) that supplies a binary causality matrix A and causal relationship functions. At inference, each test window produces a single anomaly score computed as the sum of two z-normalized components: the L2 (or cosine) distance between the window's encoder embedding and the centroid of all normal positive-sample embeddings, and the MSE forecasting error of the causal discovery model. The score is thresholded to yield an anomaly/normal label per window.

## Method

CAROTS first trains a forecasting-based causal discovery model (CUTS+) on normal data to obtain a causality matrix A and a causal forecaster F. Two augmentors then generate contrastive pairs: the Causality-Preserving Augmentor (CPA) adds Gaussian noise to randomly selected causing variables and uses F to predict the affected variables, yielding positive samples that maintain causal structure; the Causality-Disturbing Augmentor (CDA) performs a depth-first search on the causal graph to extract subgraphs and injects random biases into those variables, yielding negative samples with broken causality. A novel Similarity-filtered One-class Contrastive (SOC) loss trains an LSTM encoder by, for each anchor, restricting the positive set to samples whose cosine similarity exceeds a threshold α that is linearly scheduled from 0.5 to 0.9 over training, thereby progressively incorporating more semantically diverse normal variations while keeping causality-disturbing negatives separated. The final anomaly score ensembles the contrastive-learning embedding distance and the causal forecaster's prediction error, both z-normalized over training data.

## Results

On the SWaT dataset, CAROTS achieves AUROC 0.852, AUPRC 0.764, and F1 0.791, outperforming all eight baselines. On the synthetic Lorenz96 dataset with explicit causal structure, CAROTS reaches AUROC 0.998 on Point Global anomalies and 0.975 on Point Contextual anomalies, dramatically surpassing the next-best method (TimesNet at 0.710 and 0.594, respectively). Across all seven real-world datasets, CAROTS or its variant CAROTS† (without the causal-discovery score) achieves the top AUROC in 6 out of 7 settings, with the ensemble score providing a consistent 1–3% AUROC boost over the contrastive-learning-only variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAROTS outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of real-world dataset–metric combinations. On SWaT, CAROTS leads the strongest reconstruction-based baseline TimesNet (AUROC 0.808) by 4.4 points and the strongest contrastive baseline CTAD (0.820) by 3.2 points. On the particularly challenging WADI dataset, CAROTS† achieves AUROC 0.622 and F1 0.391, beating CTAD (0.599 / 0.374) and all reconstruction methods by wide margins. On the synthetic Lorenz96 dataset, CAROTS achieves an average AUROC of 0.909, nearly 21 points above the next-best method (TimesNet at 0.700), and reaches near-perfect detection (0.998) on Point Global anomalies where all baselines score at or near chance (≤0.710). On the VAR dataset, CAROTS is competitive but not uniformly best: USAD edges it out on average AUROC (0.810 vs. 0.805), though CAROTS leads on Point Contextual (0.648 vs. 0.610) and ties on Collective Global (0.997). The ablation in Table 4 confirms that removing the similarity-filtered one-class contrastive loss drops SWaT AUROC from 0.852 to 0.819, and removing the contrastive-learning score A_CL drops it to 0.814, validating that both the causal-aware contrastive training and the ensemble scoring contribute meaningfully over the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAROTS sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: forecasting-based causal discovery in multivariate time series (CUTS+, NGC, CUTS) and contrastive learning for anomaly detection (CTAD, CARLA, CL-TAD). It departs from prior contrastive MTSAD methods, which use generic time-series augmentations (masking, noise, reconstruction) to create positive/negative pairs, by instead leveraging a learned causal graph to generate causality-preserving and causality-disturbing samples as positives and negatives. The similarity-filtered one-class contrastive loss extends the one-class contrastive framework (Kim et al., 2023) by introducing a progressive similarity threshold that prevents premature collapse of semantically diverse normal samples into a single cluster.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly integrate causal structure into the contrastive learning loop for MTSAD, offering a principled way to distinguish normal causal variations (e.g., heatwave extending AC runtime) from true anomalies (broken AC). The dramatic 50% AUROC gap on synthetic datasets with known causal structure validates the approach, though real-world gains are more modest and the ensemble causal-discovery score (ACD) sometimes underperforms the contrastive score alone. Key open questions include handling non-stationary causal relationships, confounding variables, and exogenous factors, all of which the authors acknowledge as limitations. The assumption of time-invariant causality, while empirically supported by their quarter-wise similarity analysis, may not hold in rapidly evolving systems.
