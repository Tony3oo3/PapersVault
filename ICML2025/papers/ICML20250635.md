## Problem

Current MLLM benchmarks assume that higher aggregate scores across tasks indicate a stronger generalist closer to AGI, but this conflates task coverage with genuine multimodal generalization. The paper argues that the critical missing criterion is synergy—the ability for knowledge learned in one modality or task to transfer and enhance performance in others—which no existing evaluation framework explicitly measures or rewards.

## Input / Output

General-Bench consumes multimodal inputs in their native formats (images, video clips, audio signals, 3D point clouds, and text prompts) across 700+ tasks spanning 145 skills and 325,800 instances, with each task carrying a predefined instruction prompt applied uniformly across all models in a zero-shot setting. The output is a hierarchical level assignment (Level-2 through Level-5) where a model is classified at its highest level achieving a non-zero score, alongside per-task performance scores normalized to a 100-point scale and a 'win-over-specialist' count indicating how many tasks the model surpasses the corresponding SoTA specialist on.

## Method

General-Level defines a 5-tier taxonomy where each level imposes a stricter synergy requirement: Level-2 scores the average across all supported comprehension and generation tasks; Level-3 applies a binary mask that zeroes out any task where the model fails to beat the SoTA specialist, rewarding only synergistic gains; Level-4 computes the harmonic mean between comprehension and generation scores to enforce balance; and Level-5 multiplies the Level-4 score by a weight reflecting whether the model also surpasses SoTA NLP specialists, capturing the reverse-direction synergy from non-language to language. The key design choice is using 'outperforming a SoTA specialist on a given task' as a tractable proxy for the synergy effect, avoiding the infeasible requirement of isolating independent task distributions from an already jointly-trained model. The scoring is mathematically guaranteed to be monotonically decreasing across levels (S₅ ≤ S₄ ≤ S₃ ≤ S₂), and modality-specific scores are averaged with equal weights to prevent bias from imbalanced task counts across modalities.

## Results

On General-Bench (702 tasks, 325,800 instances across image, video, audio, 3D, and NLP), no evaluated MLLM achieves Level-5 (total synergy). Among closed-source models, GPT-4o attains the highest Level-3 score (14.51) and Level-2 score (19.67) on image comprehension, while GPT-4V scores 12.85 and 18.16 respectively. Only two models—Emu2-32B (Level-4 = 1.25) and Vitron-V1 (Level-4 = 4.59)—achieve non-zero Level-4 scores, indicating cross-comprehension-generation synergy. Critically, no MLLM surpasses SoTA NLP specialists on language tasks, and GPT-4V/GPT-4o provide zero support for image generation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12+ MLLMs (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro/Flash, Claude-3.5-Opus, Emu2-32B, Phi-3.5-Vision, Qwen2-VL-72B, SEED-LLaMA, DeepSeek-VL, InternVL2.5, Vitron-V1, NExT-GPT, AnyGPT, LaVIT) against SoTA specialists per task. GPT-4o leads closed-source models with a 41.2% win-over-specialist rate and Level-3 score of 14.51, but still scores 0.00 on Level-4 (cross-comprehension-generation synergy) and provides no image generation support. Among open-source models, InternVL2.5-8B achieves the highest task support rate (67.3%) and a competitive Level-3 score (13.09), while Qwen2-VL-72B attains the best open-source win rate (36.4%). Vitron-V1 stands out as the only model supporting >90% of image tasks (92.6%) and achieving the highest Level-4 score (4.59), though its Level-3 score (7.65) is lower than GPT-4o's. Emu2-32B achieves the highest Level-2 score (30.90) among all models but a low Level-3 (5.18), reflecting broad task coverage without strong per-task synergy. No model—regardless of scale or architecture—surpasses SoTA NLP specialists, confirming the absence of reverse-direction (non-language → language) synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing MLLM evaluation paradigm (MME, MMMU, SEED-Bench, MMT-Bench, MEGA-Bench) that reduces assessment to uniform multiple-choice QA on image comprehension, by introducing a synergy-based 5-level taxonomy (General-Level) inspired by autonomous driving level classifications. Rather than treating MLLM evaluation as a simple performance comparison across tasks, the authors reframe it around whether models preserve synergy across comprehension/generation and across modalities, positioning against the 'super agent' approach of bolting SoTA specialists onto an LLM. General-Bench extends the scope of existing benchmarks from predominantly image-only, comprehension-only, coarse-grained evaluation to a multi-modality (image, video, audio, 3D, language), multi-task (comprehension, generation, editing), and fine-grained (pixel-level) setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a structured, mathematically grounded methodology (with proven monotonicity properties) for tracking progress toward multimodal AGI, moving beyond the simplistic 'higher score = better generalist' assumption. The key open question it surfaces is whether any architecture can achieve true cross-modal synergy—particularly the reverse direction where non-language modalities enhance language intelligence—which no current model demonstrates. Limitations include the reliance on a proxy for synergy (beating SoTA specialists) rather than direct measurement of joint vs. marginal task performance, and the benchmark's zero-shot protocol may understate models' true capabilities. The 325K+ instance benchmark across 5+ modalities is likely to become a standard reference for the field.
