## Problem

Existing generative models for de novo binder design are restricted to single molecular domains (peptides, antibodies, or small molecules), failing to exploit cross-domain transferability or address diverse therapeutic needs with a unified system. The core challenge is that different molecular types have fundamentally distinct structural representations—atomic graphs for small molecules versus linear amino acid chains for peptides and CDR-defined regions for antibodies—making a single generative framework that preserves both atomic detail and hierarchical priors an open problem.

## Input / Output

The model takes a target protein binding site (residues within 10 Å of the reference binder, defined via Cβ atoms or center of mass) as a 3D block graph, along with a specified number of blocks to generate and a binary type prompt per block (1 = natural amino acid only, 0 = amino acids or molecular fragments). The binding site is encoded independently of any binder to avoid information leakage. Output is a full-atom 3D molecular binder: atom element types, 3D coordinates, intra-block chemical bonds (from vocabulary lookup), and inter-block bonds (predicted via spatial proximity within 3.5 Å), refined over an additional encode-decode cycle.

## Method

UniMoMo represents all molecules uniformly as graphs of blocks (standard amino acids or principal subgraph fragments), then applies a two-stage generative pipeline. First, an iterative full-atom VAE compresses each variable-size block into a fixed-dimensional latent point (8-dim invariant state + 3D equivariant coordinate) via an E(3)-equivariant transformer encoder, and reconstructs full-atom geometry through a two-stage decoder: block-type classification followed by 10-step flow-matching coordinate refinement with simultaneous inter-block bond prediction. Second, an E(3)-equivariant latent diffusion model (100 steps, cosine schedule) operates on the compressed latent point cloud to generate global block arrangements, conditioned on the encoded binding site. The key architectural insight is decoupling global spatial arrangement (handled by diffusion in low-dimensional latent space) from local atomic geometry (handled by the iterative autoencoder decoder), which bypasses intra-block dependencies for the diffusion model while preserving full-atom fidelity. A single set of model weights is trained jointly on peptide, antibody, and small-molecule data, demonstrating that multi-domain training improves performance across all three benchmarks.

## Results

On peptide design (LNR benchmark), UniMoMo(all) achieves the best AAR (39.45%), C-RMSD (2.19 Å), L-RMSD (1.27 Å), and pyRosetta binding energy (ΔG = −34.35), with IMP of 40.86% and the lowest clash rates (0.45% in, 0.93% out). On antibody CDR-H3 design (RAbD), UniMoMo(all) reaches 52.34% AAR, 1.04 Å RMSD, and 65.00% IMP at 100 generations, surpassing all baselines. On small molecule design (Crossdocked2020/CBGBench), UniMoMo(all) attains the highest overall weighted score of 10.38 (Rank 1), outperforming MolCRAFT (9.62) and VoxBind (9.37), with best-in-class substructure JSD (atom type 0.0280, ring type 0.1073) and lowest clash ratios (0.0040 Cα, 0.0708 CM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMoMo(all) outperforms all 19 baselines across the three molecular domains. In peptide design, it surpasses PepGLAD (the closest competitor) by 0.83% AAR, 0.55 Å C-RMSD, and 11.23 in ΔG (−34.35 vs −23.12), while achieving 2.6× lower Clash_in (0.45% vs 1.82%) and 2.3× lower JSD_bb (0.205 vs 0.474). In antibody design, UniMoMo(all) beats DiffAb (the strongest generative baseline) by 2.6% AAR (52.34% vs 49.74%), 0.42 Å RMSD (1.04 vs 1.46), and 4.66% IMP (65.00% vs 60.34%) at 100 generations, with substantially lower clash rates (0.18% vs 0.31% in, 0.03% vs 0.25% out). In small molecule design, UniMoMo(all) leads MolCRAFT by 0.76 weighted score (10.38 vs 9.62) and VoxBind by 1.01, with best substructure fidelity (atom-type JSD 0.0280 vs 0.0490 for MolCRAFT) and lowest clash ratios (0.0040 Cα vs 0.0264). A consistent finding across all domains is that multi-domain training (UniMoMo-all) improves over single-domain training (UniMoMo-single) by 0.35–1.35 weighted score points, confirming cross-domain transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMoMo extends the geometric latent diffusion paradigm from PepGLAD (Kong et al., 2024b) to a multi-domain setting, replacing the single-domain peptide focus with a unified block-level graph representation that encompasses peptides, antibodies, and small molecules. The molecular decomposition relies on the principal subgraph algorithm (Kong et al., 2022) to extract recurring fragments, while the iterative full-atom decoder borrows from flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) for coordinate refinement. The key departure from prior work is the explicit unification: rather than maintaining separate models per molecular domain (as in PepFlow, DiffAb, TargetDiff, etc.), a single E(3)-equivariant transformer-based diffusion model operates on a shared latent space, with domain-specificity controlled only by a binary prompt per block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework demonstrating that a single generative model can design binders across peptides, antibodies, and small molecules, with consistent performance gains from multi-domain training over domain-specific variants. The GPCR case study showing a small molecule mimicking arginine side-chain interactions and adopting amide-linkage scaffolds from peptides/antibodies provides compelling evidence of cross-domain knowledge transfer at the physiochemical level. Open questions include whether the approach scales to additional molecular types (e.g., nucleic acids, macrocycles), whether the self-adaptive block-size mechanism generalizes to larger binding sites, and whether in silico affinity predictions (Rosetta, Vina) translate to experimental validation. The framework also raises the question of whether unified modeling can be extended to co-design of multiple binder types simultaneously for combinatorial drug discovery.
