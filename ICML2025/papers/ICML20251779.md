## Problem

Standard gradient descent on two-layer ReLU networks provably fails to learn robust classifiers for orthonormal Gaussian mixture data, as the implicit bias aligns neurons with class averages rather than individual cluster centers. This paper asks whether a suitable architectural choice (activation function) can make vanilla gradient flow, without adversarial training, provably converge to a classifier achieving the maximum possible l2-robustness of √2/2.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a balanced dataset of KN samples drawn from K isotropic Gaussians with orthonormal unit-norm centers in R^D (N samples per cluster, split into K1 positive and K2 negative classes) and produces a two-layer pReLU network f^(p)(x;θ) = Σ v_j σ^p(⟨x,w_j⟩)/||w_j||^(p-1) that is positively homogeneous of degree 1. The trained network is robust to l2 adversarial perturbations of radius √2/2 − Õ(α0^(2/p)) with high probability, where α² is the intra-cluster variance. The analysis requires small balanced initialization (‖w_j(0)‖ = |v_j(0)| = ε) and a non-degenerate initialization shape with a Θ(1) Voronoi gap relative to the cluster centers.

## Method

The key architectural choice is the pReLU activation with weight normalization (p > 2), which differs from standard ReLU by multiplying the hidden activation by cos^(p-1)(x, w_j), discouraging large angular separation between data and neurons. This inductive bias causes gradient flow to align each neuron with its nearest individual cluster center (rather than a class average), enabling the network to internally represent the multi-cluster structure essential for robustness. The proof proceeds in two phases: an alignment phase where neuron directions converge to cluster centers (cos(μ_k, w_j) → 1 − Õ(α²)), and a convergence phase where per-cluster weight norms grow to 1 ± Õ(α²) while void-region neurons remain negligible. Theorem 1 establishes that the trained network is Õ(α0²)-close to the target classifier F^(p) = Σ σ^p(⟨x,μ_k⟩) (positive) − Σ σ^p(⟨x,μ_k⟩) (negative), and Proposition 3 then converts this proximity into a near-optimal l2-robustness guarantee.

## Results

The paper establishes that no classifier can defend against ℓ₂-adversarial attacks of radius √2/2 for orthonormal GMM data (Proposition 1), while the Bayes optimal classifier achieves robustness of √2/2 − O((α²/D)^(1/4)) (Proposition 2). The main theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that gradient flow on a two-layer pReLU network (p > 2) with small balanced initialization converges to a classifier within Õ(α₀²) of the optimal pReLU classifier F^(p), yielding ℓ₂-robustness of √2/2 − Õ(α₀^(2/p)) (Corollary 1). Empirically, on synthetic orthonormal GMM data (D=2000, K₁=12, K₂=8), only pReLU (p=3) with small initialization finds a nearly optimal robust classifier, while ReLU, depth-4 MLP, Tanh, and logistic regression all fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares pReLU (p=3) against several baselines on synthetic orthonormal GMM data. Standard ReLU (p=1) networks, deeper MLPs (depth 4), Tanh activations, and logistic regression all fail to achieve robustness beyond O(1/√K), consistent with prior theory (Frei et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025). A key finding is that regular polynomial ReLU networks (without weight normalization) span the same function space as pReLU but fail under GD, demonstrating that parametrization—not just function class—determines the implicit bias and resulting robustness. Additionally, increasing the initialization scale from N(0, 10⁻⁴) to N(0, 0.25) causes even pReLU to fail, confirming that small initialization is essential. Random initialization still converges to the robust classifier but with slower alignment than a non-degenerate (nudged) initialization, validating the theoretical non-degeneracy gap assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper proves a conjecture from Min & Vidal (2024) that gradient flow on two-layer pReLU networks with small initialization converges to a nearly optimal robust classifier for orthonormal Gaussian mixture data. The analysis extends the two-phase (alignment + convergence) framework from Boursier et al. (2022) for ReLU networks with orthogonal inputs to the more complex setting of pReLU activations with non-zero intra-cluster variance. It also builds on the negative result of Frei et al. (2023), who showed that ReLU networks trained by GD are provably non-robust on the same data, by identifying the activation function as the critical architectural choice that enables robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous proof that standard training without adversarial examples can provably achieve maximum ℓ₂-robustness for a non-trivial data distribution, resolving a key open question about whether implicit bias alone suffices for robustness. However, significant limitations remain: the non-degeneracy gap assumption (Δ=Θ(1)) is not satisfied by random initialization, the data model is restricted to orthonormal GMMs, and the analysis covers only two-layer networks with ℓ₂ loss. Open questions include generalizing to more realistic data geometries (e.g., unions of subspaces), removing the initialization constraint, and extending to deeper architectures and classification losses.
