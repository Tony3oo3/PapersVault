## Problem

While LLM weight matrices empirically exhibit low-rank structure, the underlying mechanism driving this phenomenon remains poorly understood, and prior compression methods apply uniform rank reduction across all layers despite the non-uniformity of low-rank convergence. This paper asks: why do different LLM components (Q/K vs. V projections, gate vs. up/down projections, early/late vs. middle layers) converge to different ranks, and how can this non-uniformity be exploited for both compression and efficient fine-tuning?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM checkpoint (e.g., LLaMA-2 7B/13B, Mistral-7B) and a single scalar target effective rank reduction ratio (ERR, e.g., 50%), performing one-shot SVD on each weight matrix with no calibration data. It outputs a compressed model where each layer's rank is determined by a global threshold on normalized singular values: LRCs (heavy-tail singular value distributions) are factorized into A×B products with aggressive rank reduction (sometimes >90%), while N-LRCs are preserved at full or near-full rank. For the PEFT variant, the output is a fine-tuned model where only LRCs are updated in their low-rank format, requiring ~35% of the trainable parameters of full fine-tuning on the same compressed checkpoint.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a Hessian eigenspace stabilization framework: under KL-condition and spectral-gap assumptions, the Hessian's dominant eigenspace converges during training, and gradients asymptotically align with this dominant subspace (Theorems 2.1–2.2). Components with a clear Hessian spectral gap (self-attention Q/K projections, MLP gate projections, early and late layers) stabilize rapidly, producing low-rank weights, while those without a gap (V projections, up/down projections, middle layers) remain high-rank. WeLore-COMP exploits this by applying a single global threshold k on normalized singular values across all layers, found via linear search to match the target ERR, yielding highly non-uniform per-layer ranks in a data-agnostic, one-shot manner. WeLore-PEFT then selectively fine-tunes only the LRCs in their low-rank A×B representation while freezing N-LRCs, achieving training loss trajectories that match or exceed full fine-tuning with ~3× better throughput and 40% less GPU memory, because gradients and optimizer states are inherently low-rank.

## Results

For compression, WeLore-COMP achieves 14.41 PPL on C4 at 30% effective rank reduction for LLaMa-2 7B, compared to 91.99 for uniform reduction (~6.4× better) and NaN for OWL. At 50% compression, WeLore-PEFT recovers perplexity to 11.87 (vs. 1836.62 without finetuning) using only 35% of trainable parameters with ~3× better throughput than full finetuning. On downstream tasks at 30% compression, WeLore-PEFT achieves 76.74 on CommonsenseQA, 53.33 on SVAMP, and 98.67 on CoinFlip, matching or exceeding dense full finetuning (77.05, 40.67, 75.00) while using far fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeLore-COMP decisively outperforms both Uniform and OWL rank reduction across all compression ratios: at 30% ERR on LLaMa-2 7B, WeLore-COMP achieves 14.41 PPL versus 91.99 (Uniform) and NaN (OWL), a ~6.4× improvement. Against SVD-LLM (which uses calibration data), WeLore-COMP is competitive on factoid-QA (71.89 vs. 71.95) but superior on multi-turn conversation (6.09 vs. 5.79) and summarization (6.46 vs. 6.12) without requiring any calibration set. For fine-tuning, WeLore-PEFT consistently and substantially outperforms both LoRA and GaLore: at 50% compression on CommonsenseQA, WeLore-PEFT scores 70.52 versus 35.38 (LoRA) and 35.12 (GaLore), a ~2× margin. WeLore-PEFT also matches or exceeds dense full finetuning on several tasks (e.g., CoinFlip: 98.67 vs. 75.00; SVAMP at 30%: 53.33 vs. 40.67) while using only ~35% of trainable parameters and delivering ~3× better throughput and 40% less GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior low-rank compression work (SVD-LLM, ASVD, uniform SVD) that applies the same or heuristically-derived rank reduction across all layers, by grounding non-uniform rank allocation in a Hessian spectral gap analysis of gradient subspace stabilization. It also repositions parameter-efficient fine-tuning: rather than adding auxiliary low-rank adapters (LoRA) or projecting gradients (GaLore), WeLore-PEFT directly fine-tunes the original low-rank-factorized weights of identified Low-rank Components while freezing Non-Low-rank Components. The theoretical framework draws on classical tools (Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz condition, Davis-Kahan theorem, Weyl's inequality) but applies them to the specific question of why different Transformer sub-layers (Q/K vs. V, gate vs. up/down projections) exhibit different degrees of low-rank convergence, a mechanistic explanation that prior data-manifold or optimization-dynamics accounts do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of compression and PEFT into a single one-shot, data-agnostic pipeline (WeLore) is practically attractive: it avoids the calibration-data dependency of activation-guided SVD methods and the added-parameter overhead of LoRA, while delivering 3× throughput and 40% memory reduction with performance matching or exceeding full fine-tuning. The Hessian spectral gap as a diagnostic for layer-wise compressibility offers a principled, architecture-aware alternative to uniform or outlier-weighted heuristics. Open questions include whether the theoretical assumptions (uniform spectral gap, KL condition, reversibility) hold for all architectures and training regimes, whether the LRC/N-LRC partition remains stable under continued pre-training or domain shifts, and how the approach extends to multimodal or mixture-of-experts models where the gradient dynamics may differ substantially.
