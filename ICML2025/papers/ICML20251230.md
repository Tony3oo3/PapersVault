## Problem

Computing minimum-size binary decision diagrams (BDDs) is NP-complete, and prior exact algorithms relied on inherently enumerative approaches with running times exponential in s² (where s is the target size). The witness paradigm, which proved highly effective for computing minimum-size decision trees, had an open question of whether it could be extended to BDDs. This paper resolves that question affirmatively, yielding a branch-and-bound algorithm with a running time exponential only in s rather than s².

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled tabular dataset (E, λ) with n examples in d dimensions and a size bound s, and produces a minimum-size BDD (or OBDD) as a directed acyclic graph whose internal nodes are threshold cuts on feature dimensions and whose leaves are class labels. The BDD size is the number of split vertices, and the algorithm also handles variants allowing up to t misclassifications (ERROR BSBDD) and ensembles of BDDs with majority voting. The output BDD is guaranteed to be reduced (every arc carries at least one example) and consistent with witness constraints.

## Method

WitBDD is a branch-and-bound algorithm that incrementally builds a witness BDD (WBDD), a modified DAG structure with split vertices, merge vertices, and witness-labeled arcs. At each recursive step, it selects a dirty (misclassified) example e and branches over all valid one-step-refinements—leaf-insertions, H-insertions, or Φ-insertions—that add one new split vertex with a cut separating e from the witnesses already on the classification path. The key mechanism is that e is then labeled as a witness on the new arc, mandating that e must traverse that arc in all descendant solutions, which prunes the search space dramatically. The algorithm achieves O((6s²δD)^s · sn) time, where δ is the maximum Hamming distance between any two examples and D is the maximum domain size, improving on the prior O((3δD)^(s²) · n^O(1)) bound. Correctness is established via an embedding argument showing that if a perfect BDD of size ≤ s exists, the algorithm's search tree always contains a path leading to it.

## Results

On 700 benchmark instances (35 PMLB datasets, 10% and 20% random subsamples), WitBDD solves 443 instances while the SAT-based baseline (Cabodi et al., 2024) solves 557, with all WitBDD-solved instances also solved by SAT. On the 353 instances with optimal BDD size ≤ 4, WitBDD is on average ~828× faster (median speedup 64×); on the 90 instances with optimal size > 4, the SAT encoding is on average ~37× faster (median speedup 7×). WitBDD computes provably optimal BDDs up to size 7, while larger BDDs (s > 9) remain out of reach for both methods within the 1-hour time limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the SAT-based MSOBDD solver of Cabodi et al. (2024), re-implemented in Python with CaDiCaL 1.9.5. WitBDD decisively outperforms the SAT encoding on small BDDs: on the 353 instances with optimal size ≤ 4, WitBDD is on average 828× faster with a median speedup of 64×, making it the clear winner in the regime where BDDs are most practically useful. However, the SAT encoding wins on larger instances: on the 90 instances with optimal size > 4, it is on average 37× faster (median 7×), and it solves 114 more instances overall (557 vs. 443). All 443 instances solved by WitBDD are a strict subset of those solved by the SAT approach, indicating that the SAT encoding has a broader reach but is much slower per-instance on small problems. The authors attribute WitBDD's degradation at larger sizes to the exponential growth of the search space (Refine called ~360×10⁶ times on average for size ≥ 5 vs. only 4×10⁶ for size < 4), and note that the implementation is a proof-of-concept with room for symmetry-breaking and improved lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the witness paradigm—previously successful for computing minimum-size decision trees (Komusiewicz et al., ICML 2023)—to the BDD setting, where its applicability was explicitly left open by Ordyniak et al. (AAAI 2024). It upgrades the theoretical status of BDDs from 'extendable' to 'strongly extendable' within the general framework of Ordyniak et al., and improves the running-time bound from O((3δD)^(s²)·n^O(1)) to O((6s²δD)^s·sn), reducing the exponent on the base from s² to s. The algorithm is a branch-and-bound search-tree method (WitBDD) that incrementally builds a witness BDD by identifying dirty examples and mandating their classification paths, in contrast to the purely enumerative approaches previously known for BDDs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a concrete open problem (extending the witness paradigm to BDDs) and provides the first practically competitive exact algorithm for minimum-size BDDs, outperforming the SOTA SAT-based approach for small BDD sizes (≤4). However, the proof-of-concept implementation is limited to BDDs of size up to 7 and is substantially slower than SAT for medium sizes (5–8), leaving a clear gap for larger instances. Open directions include symmetry breaking, stronger lower bounds (beyond the PairLB and packing bounds used), and whether the algorithm can be made competitive at larger sizes. The ETH-based lower bounds showing the s-dependency in the exponent is tight provide useful guidance on where further improvements are unlikely.
