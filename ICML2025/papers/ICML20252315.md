## Problem

The ε-best-arm-identification problem with linear bandit feedback on continuous arm sets has remained open for instance-dependent optimality: existing continuous-arm methods (e.g., MVR, uniform exploration) are instance-independent, while finite-armed instance-optimal algorithms cannot be naively extended due to the infinite dimensionality of the probability measure space and the non-smoothness of the optimization objective. This paper is the first to provide a tractable algorithm achieving instance-dependent (asymptotic) optimality for general continuous arm sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252315/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a compact arm set X ⊂ R^d, a Bayesian linear reward model (Gaussian prior on the parameter vector), and a given recommendation rule (e.g., greedy argmax of the posterior mean). At each round it outputs a discrete sampling distribution π_t (a Frank-Wolfe mixture of Dirac deltas mixed with a base exploration distribution) and a recommended ε-optimal arm ζ_t. The method requires only two computation oracles on X: an argmax oracle for quadratic objectives x↦xᵀVx and one for fractional quadratic objectives x↦(xᵀV₁x)/(xᵀV₂x), both solvable by standard nonlinear or convex optimization solvers.

## Method

The key insight is reparameterizing the sampling distribution π to its second-moment matrix V(π)=E[xxᵀ], reducing the infinite-dimensional optimization over P(X) to a finite-dimensional convex problem over the set V(X) of valid covariance matrices. The algorithm (PMCA) applies projected subgradient descent to a regularized version of the objective Γ*(V;ζ,f), where the subgradient is computed by solving a fractional quadratic oracle call to identify the worst-case arm ξ_t. The projection onto V(X) and the reconstruction of a concrete sampling distribution from the matrix are handled simultaneously by an adapted Frank-Wolfe algorithm (Algorithm 2), which is shown to be equivalent to the approximate Carathéodory problem. A decaying mixture with a base exploration distribution π_exp ensures positive definiteness and bounded subgradients. The main theorem establishes that the posterior misidentification probability decays at the instance-optimal rate (2τ*_X(f;ζ_∞))⁻¹, matching the lower bound from the asymptotic analysis.

## Results

The paper evaluates PMCA on synthetic 2D environments where the arm set X is a subset of the unit circle in R², with f(x) = (cos(θ_f), sin(θ_f))·x, λ = 10⁻², and ε = 10⁻². The evaluation metric is p_t = sup_{ξ∈X} P_t(f(ζ_t) ≤ f(ξ) − ε), a computable upper bound on the posterior probability of misidentification (lower is better). Across all tested problem instances (a = 0.2, b ∈ {0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0}), PMCA achieves the fastest decay of p_t, outperforming both MVR and the uniform random policy, with 95% confidence intervals over 10 repetitions shown in Figure 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PMCA against two baselines: (1) the uniform random policy, which is known to be optimal for the unit sphere arm set (Jedra & Proutiere, 2020) but sub-optimal for general continuous arm sets, and (2) MVR (Maximum Variance Reduction, Vakili et al., 2021), which is instance-independent optimal for simple regret minimization in Gaussian process bandits. PMCA outperforms both baselines across all problem instances tested, achieving a lower p_t (faster decay of the misidentification probability upper bound) at every time step. MVR consistently outperforms the uniform policy, confirming that variance-reduction-based exploration is better than uniform sampling for non-symmetric arm sets. However, MVR is sub-optimal for several problem instances because its instance-independent analysis does not adapt to the specific geometry of the arm set and reward function, whereas PMCA's instance-dependent optimization of the sampling distribution via projected subgradient descent in the covariance matrix space allows it to concentrate sampling on the most informative directions for the given instance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-armed ε-BAI literature (Track-and-Stop, Lazy Track-and-Stop) to continuous arm sets by reparametrizing the sampling distribution π → V(π) = E[xxᵀ], reducing an infinite-dimensional optimization over probability measures to a finite-dimensional matrix problem solvable via projected subgradient descent. The projection and distribution-reconstruction steps are handled through a Frank-Wolfe algorithm whose convergence is analyzed via the approximate Carathéodory framework (Combettes & Pokutta, 2023). The paper departs from prior continuous-arm approaches (uniform exploration of Jedra & Proutiere 2020, instance-independent MVR of Vakili et al. 2021, and Bhat & Amballa 2022) by achieving instance-dependent optimality, which the authors explicitly identify as an open problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tractable algorithm with instance-dependent (asymptotic) optimality for pure exploration on general continuous arm sets, closing a gap the authors identify as open. Key limitations include the purely asymptotic nature of the optimality guarantee (no finite-time bounds), the assumption that a recommendation rule is given externally, and the requirement of quadratic/fractional-quadratic optimization oracles that can be NP-hard for general arm sets. Future directions highlighted include extending to Gaussian process reward models (where exponential decay may not hold depending on kernel smoothness) and developing finite-time analyses.
