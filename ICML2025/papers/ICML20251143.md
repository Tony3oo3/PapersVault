## Problem

Despite the practical success of gradient-based optimization of neural networks, worst-case theory predicts computational hardness for learning such models. This paper identifies a fundamental limitation of distribution-free gradient descent: if a differentiable parametric model class can learn a target function class in a distribution-free manner via mini-batch SGD, then most functions in the target class must admit a simple linear combination of random features. This reveals why distributional assumptions are essential for gradient descent to succeed on tasks like parity learning, where random features are provably insufficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes a p-parameter differentiable hypothesis class, bSGD hyperparameters (T steps, gradient precision c ∈ (0,1], batch size b), a target function class F, and a prior distribution μ over F. It outputs a polynomial bound d ≤ poly(Tp/c²) on the number of random features, together with the existence of a weight vector v such that the linear combination Σvᵢφᵢ approximates f under 0/1 loss with probability 99/100 over f ~ μ. A critical constraint is that c must be bounded below (c ≥ 1/8b); without this precision restriction, bSGD can simulate PAC learning and the random feature collapse does not hold.

## Method

The proof chains together paradigm transformations: bSGD is converted to statistical query (SQ) learning via Abbe et al. (2021), the SQ query count is bounded by the SQ dimension of the target class via Blum et al. (1994), and SQ dimension is then connected to random feature approximation through communication complexity tools — specifically Sherstov's discrepancy theorem, 2-party norm bounds, and a Random Feature lemma that constructs weak approximators by sampling a random function g ~ μ and a labeled example. These weak approximators (achieving 1/2 − Ω(sq(F)⁻⁸) accuracy) are combined into a strong linear combination via Adaboost-style boosting, requiring O(sq(F)^24.01) features. The paper also introduces average probabilistic dimension complexity (adc), which replaces the universal quantification over the hypothesis class with high-probability guarantees under a prior μ, and proves an infinite separation between adc and standard dimension complexity using Zarankiewicz matrices, resolving an open question from Kamath et al. (2020) in a relaxed form.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 3.3) establishes that if a p-parameter differentiable model can be learned distribution-freely by mini-batch SGD in T steps with gradient precision c, then for any prior μ over the target class, the target function is approximated by a linear combination of at most poly(Tp/c²) random features with high probability (≥ 1−δ). Theorem 4.1 further shows that average probabilistic dimension complexity satisfies adc_{ε,δ}(μ) ≤ O(sq(F)^{24.01}), giving an explicit polynomial relationship between adc and statistical query dimension. As a corollary (Corollary 3.7), the paper proves an infinite separation between adc and standard dimension complexity: there exists a hypothesis class where adc_{ε,δ} ∈ O(1/ε) while dc ∈ Ω(N^{1−ε}), improving upon the exponential separation (O(n) vs 2^{Ω(n^{1/4})}) previously known between probabilistic and standard dimension complexity by Kamath et al. (2020).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or method comparisons. The paper positions its contribution relative to two prior theoretical frameworks: (1) the NTK/LLRF literature (Andoni et al. 2014, Daniely 2017, Du et al. 2018, Allen-Zhu et al. 2019), which uses the connection between wide-network SGD and random features to prove positive convergence guarantees, whereas this paper uses the same connection to prove a negative limitation result for distribution-free learning; and (2) Kamath et al. (2020), who introduced probabilistic dimension complexity and proved an exponential separation from standard dimension complexity, leaving open whether an infinite separation exists. The present paper resolves this open question affirmatively by introducing the relaxed notion of average probabilistic dimension complexity (adc), showing that the relaxation from universal to average-case quantification is sufficient to achieve an infinite separation, while also demonstrating (Theorem C.7) that proving a super-polynomial gap between adc and probabilistic dimension complexity would imply super-polynomial lower bounds for depth-2 threshold circuits, a major open problem in complexity theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the NTK/overparameterized-network line (Chizat et al., Du et al., Jacot et al.) that uses the gradient-descent-to-random-features connection to prove *power* results for learning wide networks. Here, the same connection is inverted: it is used to prove a *limitation* result, showing that distribution-free bSGD learnability implies random-feature approximability. The proof pipeline builds on Abbe et al. (2021) for the bSGD-to-SQ conversion, Blum et al. (1994) for SQ-dimension characterization, Sherstov (2008b) for the SQ-dimension-to-discrepancy bridge, and Adaboost (Freund & Schapire, 1997) as a constructive weak-to-strong lifting. The new complexity measure, average probabilistic dimension complexity (adc), extends Kamath et al. (2020)'s probabilistic dimension complexity by replacing universal quantification over the hypothesis class with a high-probability guarantee under a prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a general theoretical explanation for why distributional assumptions are essential for gradient-based learning to succeed, going beyond the specific parity-function examples studied in prior work. It also resolves (in a relaxed form) the open question from Kamath et al. (2020) about whether an infinite separation exists between probabilistic and standard dimension complexity, by showing such a separation for adc. A notable limitation is that the polynomial exponent (24.01) is far from tight, and the complexity-theoretic barrier (Theorem C.7) suggests that proving the relaxation from probabilistic DC to adc is *necessary* for the infinite separation would require superpolynomial threshold-circuit lower bounds. Open questions include whether the distribution-specific setting admits a matching positive characterization, and whether the loose polynomial can be tightened.
