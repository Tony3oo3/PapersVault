## Problem

RoPE-based language models fail to generalize beyond their training context length, and prior work has attributed this solely to limitations within the attention mechanism. This paper shows, via Discrete Signal Processing analysis, that the periodic extension RoPE provides is actually undermined by spectrum damage from nearly all LM components: linear layers and activation functions leak and distort frequency information, while undertrained low-frequency components within attention cannot complete full cycles during pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250977/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FoPE takes the same inputs as RoPE—M-dimensional query/key hidden states and integer token position indices—and outputs position-encoded vectors of the same dimensionality. The key structural difference is that each dimension is no longer a single sinusoid but a Fourier Series of D frequency components (D ≥ M), implemented via a fixed, non-learnable weight matrix W^F ∈ R^{D×(M−M_0)} that maps the dominant frequency plus harmonic coefficients into the final rotation. Because the matrix requires no gradients, FoPE adds negligible memory and compute overhead relative to RoPE.

## Method

FoPE modifies RoPE along two axes grounded in a DSP-based analysis showing that RoPE implicitly performs a Non-Uniform Discrete Fourier Transform (NUDFT) on hidden states. First, each attention dimension is modeled as a Fourier Series h_m(n) = H_m(n)(e^{iω_m n} + Σ_ω a_ω e^{iω n}), where the dominant frequency ω_m follows RoPE's schedule and additional harmonic frequencies ω are sampled from [0, π] with coefficients a_ω initialized from N(0, σ); this multi-frequency representation lets attention separate information that linear layers and activations have mixed across frequencies. Second, frequencies below the floor ω_l = 2π/N (which cannot complete a full cycle within the training length N) are clipped to zero-frequency, eliminating the noisy, undertrained components that introduce positional bias and break periodic extension. The theoretical contribution formalizes how spectral leakage (from linear projections) and spectral distortion (from nonlinear activations, via a Taylor-expansion lemma) destroy the one-to-one coefficient-to-frequency correspondence that RoPE's periodicity requires, and why zero-frequency is the optimal substitute for undertrained components since it carries both long- and short-range dependency without positional bias.

## Results

On Passkey Retrieval (1.2B models, trained at 512 context), FoPE maintains stable retrieval accuracy across all tested positions, while RoPE drops to 0% at 2× training length and ALiBi shows linear decline. On C4 perplexity, FoPE achieves significantly lower perplexity than RoPE at longer context windows. After fine-tuning SmolLM-1.7B (2048→4096 context), FoPE outperforms RoPE on most summarization and QA benchmarks, with the largest gains at 4-8k and 8k+ lengths (e.g., TREC: 56.00 vs 42.00 at 4-8k; SAMSum: 15.85 vs 6.37 at 4-8k). On broader downstream tasks (Table 4), FoPE achieves 43.37 average accuracy vs RoPE's 42.98 and 27.57 MMLU average vs RoPE's 26.68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoPE, FoPE achieves a decisive win on length generalization: Passkey Retrieval accuracy remains stable at all positions while RoPE collapses to 0% beyond 2× training length, and C4 perplexity stays significantly lower at extended contexts. On the fine-tuning benchmarks (Table 1), FoPE outperforms RoPE on 13 of 15 benchmark-length combinations, with the most dramatic gains at 4-8k and 8k+ (e.g., TREC 56.00 vs 42.00; SAMSum 15.85 vs 6.37 at 4-8k). The sole exception is TriviaQA at shorter contexts (0-4k, 4-8k), where FoPE is ~2-3 points lower, though it recovers and surpasses RoPE at 8k+ (33.87 vs 25.02). Against ALiBi, FoPE wins decisively on Passkey Retrieval (stable vs linearly declining accuracy) but is slightly worse on C4 perplexity, which the authors attribute to ALiBi's linear decay being well-suited to the short-distance-dependent C4/Books corpus. Against YARN as an extrapolation method, FoPE+YARN outperforms RoPE+YARN on both perplexity and Passkey accuracy, and FoPE also outperforms YARN alone when applied to RoPE-based models. On broader downstream tasks (Table 4), FoPE achieves the best average accuracy (43.37) and MMLU score (27.57) among all six position embeddings tested, marginally beating KERPLE (43.22) and RoPE (42.98).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2024), reinterpreting its per-dimension rotation as an implicit Non-Uniform Discrete Fourier Transform (NUDFT) on hidden states. While prior length-extrapolation methods (YARN, NTK-aware scaling, CLEX) diagnose RoPE's failure within the attention mechanism itself, this paper departs by identifying 'Spectrum Damage' originating from linear layers and activation functions outside attention, as well as from undertrained low-frequency components within attention. The Fourier Series representation of each dimension is a conceptual departure from RoPE's single-frequency-per-dimension design, drawing on classical DSP results about harmonic generation by nonlinearities (Lemma 3.1) and spectral leakage from time-domain truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSP-based theoretical lens provides a unifying explanation for why RoPE struggles with length generalization beyond what prior interpolation-based methods address, and the zero-frequency clipping of undertrained components offers a simple, training-free modification applicable to existing RoPE-based open-source models. However, the hyperparameters (σ, D) require per-scale tuning, and the theoretical modeling, while elegant, relies on simplifying assumptions (e.g., treating Spectrum Damage as Gaussian) that may not hold in all architectures. Open questions include whether the Fourier Series approach remains beneficial at much larger scales (70B+), whether the zero-frequency substitution is truly optimal versus learned low-frequency components, and how this interacts with other emerging position-embedding designs.
