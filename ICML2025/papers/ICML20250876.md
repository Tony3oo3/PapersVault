## Problem

Exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL) suffers from two coupled failures: semantic shift, where old-class embeddings drift in the feature space after backbone updates, and decision bias, where the classifier overfits to new-task data trained via backpropagation without access to old samples. Existing remedies either freeze the backbone (sacrificing plasticity) or rely on non-parametric NCM classifiers (vulnerable to representation degradation), leaving the stability-plasticity dilemma unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet-100/1k) in a task-wise cold-start protocol and outputs a class label over the cumulative class set. Crucially, no exemplars are stored; the only per-class memory from prior tasks is an uncentered covariance matrix (d×d) and a prototype vector (d), giving O(d²+d) storage per class. The classifier is produced in closed form via ridge regression rather than iterative optimization.

## Method

DPCR comprises three stages: (1) standard LwF-style backbone training with knowledge distillation on the current task; (2) a Dual-Projection (DP) that estimates semantic shift by composing a task-wise projection P (solved in closed form via a regularized Moore-Penrose inverse on current-task embeddings from both old and new backbones) with a category-wise row-space projector U^r U^{r⊤} obtained from the SVD of each stored class covariance, yielding a per-class calibrated projection P_{i,c}; (3) a Ridge Regression-based Classifier Reconstruction (RRCR) that propagates old covariances and prototypes through the calibrated projections and combines them with freshly computed new-task statistics to solve a single ridge-regression problem for the full classifier weight matrix, followed by a category-wise L2 normalization to counteract numerical imbalance from the non-unitary projections. The key distinction from prior analytic-continual-learning methods is that the backbone is never frozen, and the shift estimation captures both task-level and class-level geometry simultaneously rather than a single global translation or task-level linear map.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (T=10), DPCR achieves 50.24% final accuracy (A_f) and 63.21% average incremental accuracy (A_avg), surpassing the second-best method LDC by 3.64% and 1.54% respectively. On ImageNet-100 (T=20), DPCR reaches 38.35% A_f and 57.22% A_avg, outperforming LDC by 3.48% and 2.36%. DPCR also leads on CUB200 (T=5: 29.51% A_f) and ImageNet-1k (T=10: 35.49% A_f), demonstrating consistent superiority across fine-grained and large-scale benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPCR outperforms all 8 EFCIL baselines across every dataset and task-split setting. The closest competitor is LDC (ECCV 2024), which DPCR surpasses by 2.2–3.6% A_f on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, and by 1.5–2.4% A_avg. ADC (CVPR 2024) is the second-closest on CIFAR-100 T=10 (46.80% vs. 50.24% A_f) but trails more substantially on ImageNet-100 T=20 (32.21% vs. 38.35% A_f). Backbone-freezing methods (ACIL, FeCAM, DS-AL) consistently underperform, with ACIL achieving only 35.53% A_f on CIFAR-100 T=10 versus DPCR's 50.24%, confirming that freezing the backbone severely limits plasticity. Against exemplar-based methods (iCaRL, PODNet, FOSTER) with 500 stored samples, DPCR achieves the best A_f on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100; with 1000 exemplars, FOSTER edges out DPCR on ImageNet-100 T=20 A_avg (61.93% vs. 57.94%), but DPCR still leads on A_f (40.82% vs. 47.86% for FOSTER on ImageNet-100 T=20). On fine-grained CUB200 (T=5), DPCR achieves 29.51% A_f, beating LDC (28.70%) and ADC (28.84%), and on ImageNet-1k (T=10) it reaches 35.49% A_f versus LDC's 35.15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPCR sits at the intersection of three EFCIL research threads: knowledge-distillation-based representation learning (LwF), learnable drift compensation for semantic shift estimation (LDC), and analytic/ridge-regression-based classifier training (ACIL). It departs from LDC by augmenting the task-wise linear projection with a training-free category-wise row-space projection (CIP) derived from per-class covariance SVD, and from ACIL/DS-AL by allowing the backbone to update incrementally rather than freezing it after the first task. The key conceptual departure from prototype-based methods (SDC, ADC, LDC) is replacing the NCM classifier with a parametrized ridge-regression classifier reconstructed from calibrated covariance and prototype statistics, thereby decoupling classifier quality from representation drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPCR provides a clean, BP-free recipe for simultaneously correcting semantic shift and decision bias in EFCIL, which prior methods addressed only partially. The practical implication is that a practitioner can obtain a well-balanced classifier without iterative back-propagation on the classifier head, reducing training cost and eliminating the new-task bias inherent in gradient-based updates. Limitations include evaluation only with ResNet-18 in the cold-start setting, per-class covariance storage of O(d²) memory, and untested applicability to task-free or domain-incremental scenarios. Open questions include whether the dual-projection generalizes to deeper backbones, very long task sequences where cumulative shift estimation may degrade, and whether the ridge-regression formulation extends naturally to non-linear classifiers.
