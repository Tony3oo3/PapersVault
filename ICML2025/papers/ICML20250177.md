## Problem

Protein fitness optimization must navigate a vast, discrete, and sparsely populated sequence space where only a small fraction of sequences exhibit meaningful functionality. Existing gradient-based methods (GWG, GGS, discrete diffusion) operate directly in the discrete token space, suffering from non-smooth gradients and the combinatorial explosion of possible sequences. This paper addresses the gap by reframing the problem in a continuous latent space where smooth gradient-based sampling becomes tractable, even under limited-data regimes of only a few thousand mutations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of protein sequence–fitness pairs (N ≈ 2,100–3,400 sequences of length d=28 for AAV or d=237 for GFP, each over a 20-amino-acid vocabulary) and a target fitness value y (set to 1 for maximum fitness) as input. It produces decoded protein sequences by integrating a learned flow ODE in a compressed latent space (l=16 or l=32 dimensions) from a Gaussian initialization z_0, followed by top-k selection (k=128) ranked by the CNN fitness predictor. The output sequences are discrete amino acid strings evaluated in-silico by an oracle trained on the full deep mutational scanning dataset.

## Method

VLGPO combines three components in a variational framework: (1) a β-VAE that encodes discrete sequences into a continuous latent space, (2) a flow matching model trained to learn the generative prior p(z) over latent representations, and (3) a CNN fitness predictor g_φ that provides classifier guidance via the gradient ∇_z log p(y|x). The key technical contribution is a manifold-constrained sampling scheme (Algorithm 1) where, at each ODE step, the model first estimates the denoised latent ẑ_1 using the flow velocity, evaluates the fitness likelihood at the decoded x̂_1, but computes the gradient with respect to the current latent z_t — thereby constraining updates to remain on the time-dependent data manifold and preventing drift. This variational decomposition p(x|y) ∝ p(y|x)·p(x) makes the framework modular: the prior, predictor, and latent encoder can be independently swapped, and the guidance strength α_t and number of gradient steps J are tunable per task to balance fitness improvement against diversity.

## Results

VLGPO achieves state-of-the-art median normalized fitness across all four benchmark tasks. On GFP (medium/hard), VLGPO with predictor g_φ reaches 0.87/0.75 fitness, and with the smoothed predictor g_φ̃ reaches 0.84/0.78, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (gg-dWJS at 0.55/0.61 and GGS at 0.76/0.74). On AAV (medium/hard), VLGPO achieves 0.58/0.51 (g_φ) and 0.53/0.61 (g_φ̃), beating the closest competitor GGS (0.51/0.60) and gg-dWJS (0.48/0.33). Diversity and novelty scores remain competitive with baselines, with VLGPO values (e.g., 4.31 diversity on GFP medium) comparable to the 99th-percentile reference sequences (4.73 for GFP, 5.23 for AAV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLGPO outperforms all nine baselines on median normalized fitness across all four tasks. The closest competitor is GGS (Kirjner et al., 2023), which shares the same smoothed predictor g_φ̃; VLGPO still wins on GFP medium (0.84 vs. 0.76), GFP hard (0.78 vs. 0.74), AAV medium (0.53 vs. 0.51), and AAV hard (0.61 vs. 0.60). GWG (Grathwohl et al., 2021), which uses the identical unsmoothed predictor g_φ, is far behind at 0.10 (GFP medium) and 0.00 (GFP hard), highlighting the advantage of the latent flow-matching prior over discrete MCMC. gg-dWJS (Ikram et al., 2024) is competitive on GFP (0.55/0.61) but collapses on AAV hard (0.33) due to sparsity in the one-hot-encoded space for longer sequences. The remaining baselines—GFN-AL, CbAS, AdaLead, BOqei, CoMS, and PEX—all trail by at least 0.10 fitness on at least one task, with several (CoMS, PEX, GWG on GFP hard) producing near-zero fitness, indicating failure to escape the low-fitness training range.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLGPO unifies three established threads—VAE-based latent embedding of discrete sequences, flow matching as a generative prior, and classifier-guided sampling—into a single variational framework for protein fitness optimization. It departs from prior discrete-space methods (GWG, GGS, GFlowNets) by operating entirely in a continuous latent space, where the flow matching prior provides smooth gradients and the manifold-constrained likelihood gradient (borrowed from diffusion posterior sampling in image reconstruction) keeps samples on the learned manifold. The variational decomposition p(x|y) ∝ p(y|x)p(x) is the conceptual glue, making the prior and the fitness predictor interchangeable plug-and-play components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLGPO demonstrates that a continuous latent-space formulation with a learned generative prior can substantially outperform discrete-space gradient methods (GWG, GGS) on the same predictors, suggesting that the latent representation itself is a key source of robustness in data-scarce protein design. The ablation showing that a directly learned conditional posterior p(x|y) fails on the hardest task while explicit classifier guidance succeeds highlights a practical lesson: in limited-data regimes, decoupling the generative model from the fitness signal via a separate predictor is more reliable than end-to-end conditioning. Open questions include generalization beyond AAV and GFP, whether pretrained protein language model embeddings can replace the VAE, and whether in-silico gains translate to wet-lab validation.
