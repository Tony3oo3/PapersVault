## Problem

Implicit neural volume representations (e.g., NeRF, TensoRF, K-Planes) offer strong expressiveness and memory efficiency but require nonconvex optimization that is slow, sensitive to initialization, and offers no convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether volume parameterizations can be designed so that, for convex objectives, training reduces to a convex program with guaranteed global optimality while retaining competitive expressiveness and model size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252220/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For radiance field reconstruction, the model consumes color photographs from known camera poses and produces rendered novel-view images via volumetric ray integration. For 3D segmentation, it takes either 2D tomographic masks (minimizing MSE between projected ray density and ground-truth masks) or 3D occupancy labels derived via Space Carving, and outputs a continuous 3D density/occupancy field. For video segmentation, it receives segmentation masks on a subset of frames (every third frame held out) and predicts masks for unseen temporal frames, effectively performing temporal super-resolution on binary masks.

## Method

GA-Planes represents a volume as a mixture of geometric-algebra-inspired feature grids: 1D line grids, 2D plane grids, and a 3D volume grid, each at configurable spatial resolution and feature dimension. Features are extracted at query points via (bi/tri)linear interpolation and combined by elementwise multiplication (nonconvex variant, Equation 3) or concatenation (convex/semiconvex variant, Equation 2), then decoded by an MLP. The key contribution is a convex and semiconvex reformulation of the decoder: the semiconvex model replaces ReLU with a frozen random gating pattern (indicator function) while keeping grid features and MLP weights as separate trainable parameters (Burer-Monteiro factorization, separately convex in each); the fully convex model fuses the decoder weights into the feature grids to eliminate the bilinear product entirely. In 2D, the paper proves equivalence to a low-rank plus low-resolution matrix completion problem, showing that feature multiplication yields rank-k capacity (matching truncated SVD) while addition/concatenation caps rank at 2 with a linear decoder, and that an MLP decoder raises the rank ceiling to the grid resolution r₁.

## Results

On radiance field reconstruction (NeRF-Blender, 8 scenes), nonconvex GA-Planes at 0.25M parameters matches or exceeds K-Planes (0.39M) and TensoRF (0.32M) in PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, and at smaller model sizes (~0.1M) retains substantially higher quality than all baselines. On 3D segmentation with 3D Space Carving supervision, GA-Planes achieves 0.964 IOU (nonconvex), 0.957 (semiconvex), and 0.932 (convex), outperforming Tri-Planes/K-Planes (0.881 nonconvex). On video segmentation (temporal super-resolution), GA-Planes reaches 0.981 IOU (nonconvex) and 0.975 (semiconvex) versus 0.732 for Tri-Planes with addition. In the 2D image compression experiment, the low-rank + low-resolution decomposition (analogous to GA-Planes) achieves 29.60 dB PSNR versus 26.26 dB for the classic low-rank + sparse decomposition at the same 18.75% parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-Planes (Fridovich-Keil et al., CVPR 2023), GA-Planes achieves comparable radiance field quality at ~36% fewer parameters (0.25M vs 0.39M) and substantially outperforms it on video segmentation (0.981 vs 0.727 IOU nonconvex; 0.975 vs 0.727 semiconvex). Against TensoRF (Chen et al., ECCV 2022), GA-Planes matches performance at large model sizes (~10M params) but retains higher PSNR at small model sizes where TensoRF degrades sharply. Against Mip-NeRF (Barron et al., ICCV 2021), GA-Planes achieves comparable or better PSNR/SSIM at equivalent or smaller model sizes across all 8 NeRF-Blender scenes. Against Tri-Planes with addition (Chan et al., CVPR 2022), GA-Planes with concatenation dominates on all three segmentation tasks: 0.964 vs 0.941 IOU on 3D Space Carving (nonconvex), 0.981 vs 0.732 on video fitting (nonconvex), and critically, GA-Planes maintains strong performance under convex (0.932) and semiconvex (0.957) training while Tri-Planes with addition collapses to 0.642 and 0.636 respectively. The convex/semiconvex formulations of GA-Planes also demonstrate superior optimization stability: with a very small model, the convex variant achieves 0.639 ± 0.000 IOU across 10 seeds while the nonconvex variant yields only 0.567 ± 0.229, with some seeds failing entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

GA-Planes unifies a broad family of existing volume parameterizations—NeRF, Plenoxels/DVGO, TensoRF, K-Planes, Tri-Planes, and MERF—under a single geometric-algebra-inspired mixture-of-primitives framework, where line, plane, and volume feature grids are combined via addition, multiplication, or concatenation before an MLP decoder. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of convex and semiconvex training formulations, leveraging the convex neural network equivalence (Pilanci & Ergen, 2020) and Burer-Monteiro factorization (Sahiner et al., 2024) to guarantee global optimality for tasks with convex objectives. The 2D theoretical analysis further connects the model to low-rank plus low-resolution matrix completion, providing a principled lens that generalizes the classic low-rank plus sparse decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convex and semiconvex GA-Planes formulations offer the first volume parameterizations with guaranteed convergence to global optima, which is practically valuable for 3D segmentation and video fitting tasks where the objective is convex and initialization sensitivity is a real concern (demonstrated by the 0.229 std deviation of the nonconvex model vs. 0.000 for convex in Table 3). The low-rank plus low-resolution interpretation provides actionable design guidance: multiplication of features is essential for expressiveness with linear decoders, and grid resolution (not feature dimension) bounds the effective rank when using MLP decoders. Limitations include restriction to 3D (or lower) volumes, reconstruction rather than generation, and the fact that the radiance field task—where GA-Planes shows its strongest empirical results—remains inherently nonconvex, so the convexity guarantee does not apply to the paper's headline experiment.
