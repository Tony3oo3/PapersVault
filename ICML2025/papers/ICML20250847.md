## Problem

Diffusion models generate realistic images but consistently fail to capture precise conditional relationships between dependent features (e.g., sun height determining shadow length, object size dictating reflection size). Prior studies on DM rule-learning produce contradictory conclusions due to varying rule complexity and lack a unified framework with adjustable difficulty or theoretical grounding for why the denoising score matching (DSM) objective cannot recover fine-grained inter-feature rules.

## Input / Output

The paper evaluates both pre-trained text-to-image models (SD-3.5 Large, Flux.1 Dev, SDXL, DALL-E, Infinity) with text prompts and trains unconditional DDPM from Gaussian noise on 32×32 (or 64×64) RGB synthetic images. Four synthetic tasks encode specific geometric feature pairs (e.g., sun center, pole top, shadow endpoint in Task A) with predefined coarse rules (e.g., opposite sides) and fine rules (e.g., l₁h₂ = l₂h₁). Evaluation extracts key points via color-based masking and contour detection, then checks whether geometric ratios match ground-truth values, reporting R² and a composite Error metric decomposed into bias and variance components.

## Method

The paper introduces four synthetic tasks (A–D) inspired by real-world failures, each with a coarse-grained rule (e.g., sun and shadow on opposite sides) and a fine-grained rule (e.g., precise linear alignment l₁h₂ = l₂h₁), enabling controlled evaluation of rule-learning at two difficulty levels. Theoretically, on a multi-patch data model where two patches are ζu and (1−ζ)v, the paper proves that the true score function satisfies a structural identity (sum of directional coefficients equals 1), and that any two-layer network trained via DSM exhibits a non-vanishing constant lower bound on rule-conforming error, decomposable into irreducible bias and variance terms. As a mitigation, the paper trains a U-Net classifier via contrastive learning (NT-Xent + cross-entropy) on pairs that satisfy fine rules versus those that only satisfy coarse rules, then applies classifier guidance during DDPM sampling (Guided DDPM) or post-hoc filtering in pixel space (Filtered DDPM), achieving limited but consistent improvements in Error and R² across all tasks.

## Results

Across four synthetic tasks (A–D), DDPM satisfies coarse-grained rules with near-zero violations (Table 1: 0–1 violations out of ~1400 valid samples), but fails on fine-grained rules, as shown by R² values well below 1 (e.g., 0.57 on Task C, 0.79 on Task D). The proposed Guided DDPM (classifier guidance) and Filtered DDPM (pixel-space filtering) both reduce Error and improve R² over the DDPM baseline on all four tasks. The largest gains appear on Task C (Error drops from 0.41 to 0.25 with filtering; R² rises from 0.57 to 0.64), while Task B shows the most dramatic Error reduction (0.11 → 0.05 with filtering).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard unconditional DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), trained with the same U-Net architecture and DSM objective. Both Guided DDPM and Filtered DDPM consistently outperform DDPM on all four synthetic tasks for both metrics: Error is reduced and R² is improved in every case. The improvement is most pronounced on Task B (Error: 0.11 → 0.05, a 55% reduction with filtering) and Task C (Error: 0.41 → 0.25, a 39% reduction with filtering), while Task D shows the smallest relative gain (Error: 0.46 → 0.39). Filtered DDPM generally achieves the best Error across tasks, while Guided DDPM sometimes matches or slightly exceeds it on R² (e.g., Task C: 0.67 vs. 0.64). The authors note that despite these gains, the absolute R² values remain well below 1, indicating that neither strategy fully resolves the fine-grained rule learning problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on diffusion models' rule-learning capabilities by shifting focus from independent feature composition (Okawa et al., 2024; Deschenaux et al., 2024) to dependent inter-feature rules, where prior findings were contradictory due to uncontrolled rule complexity. The theoretical analysis builds directly on the multi-patch data model and two-layer score network framework from Han et al. (2024a), repurposing it to prove a constant-error lower bound for learning hidden norm constraints via DSM. The mitigation strategy repurposes the standard classifier guidance mechanism from Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), but applies it to a novel contrastive-learning objective designed to distinguish fine-grained from coarse-grained rule compliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental and provable limitation of the DSM training paradigm: diffusion models cannot learn fine-grained inter-feature rules regardless of model scale, data size, or resolution, which challenges the assumption that better joint-distribution estimation automatically yields correct conditional relationships. The finding that low FID coexists with violated physical rules (e.g., shadow direction, mirror reflections) has direct implications for evaluation methodology in generative AI. The limited success of classifier guidance highlights an open problem: how to obtain reliable reward signals for subtle, fine-grained rules in real-world settings where such rules are difficult to define or detect, a challenge distinct from standard test-time alignment tasks.
