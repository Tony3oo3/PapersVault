## Problem

Existing federated LLM fine-tuning frameworks (e.g., OpenFedLLM, FederatedScope-LLM) apply classical FedAvg directly to LoRA parameters without accounting for the unique structure of LLM supervised fine-tuning. When clients train on heterogeneous instruction datasets, naive averaging causes the global model to drift toward specific local distributions, degrading general capabilities and causing catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks: how can federated fine-tuning simultaneously preserve the base model's general knowledge while effectively absorbing diverse domain-specific instruction knowledge?

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private instruction dataset (Alpaca, MAM-Mo, CodeAlpaca, or FinGPT, ~5000 samples each) and receives the current global LoRA parameter pair (A ∈ R^{d×r}, B ∈ R^{r×k}, r ≪ min(d,k)) from the server. After local training, the client does not upload the full LoRA delta; instead it uploads a sparsely masked update where only parameters whose momentum-smoothed local change exceeds their global-model importance are transmitted. The server outputs an updated global LoRA pair that is redistributed for the next round, with the final model computed as θ' = θ + A×B on top of the frozen base LLM (Llama-2-7B or Mistral-7B).

## Method

FedICU introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, Consensus-Divergence Splitting decomposes each client's LoRA update Δθ_k into a column-wise L2-norm magnitude vector (consensus, capturing shared general capability) and a normalized direction matrix (divergence, capturing client-specific adaptation). Consensus vectors are aggregated by simple averaging (proven variance-optimal under equal-noise assumptions via Lagrange multipliers), while divergence vectors are aggregated using temperature-scaled softmax weights derived from pairwise cosine similarities across clients, aligning directions that agree and down-weighting outliers. Second, Importance-Aware Updating computes two per-parameter scores on the client side: a generalization importance I (sigmoid-normalized magnitude of the previous global LoRA weights) and a specialization importance G (sigmoid-normalized magnitude of momentum-smoothed local updates). A binary mask W[v]=1 is set only where G[v]>I[v], so the uploaded update θ_k' = W⊙(θ^{i-1}+m_k^i)+(1−W)⊙θ^{i-1} retains only parameters where local adaptation meaningfully exceeds the global model's existing knowledge, reducing communication cost to O(Σ α_k·N) and preventing overfitting to any single client's instruction distribution.

## Results

On the Llama-2-7B federated SFT benchmark, FedICU achieves the best MT-Bench scores across all three generalization metrics: MT-1 (4.83, +65.4% over base), MT-2 (3.43, +67.3%), and MT-Final (4.13, +66.5%). It also attains the top Code (HumanEval) score of 0.10 (tied with Scaffold) and a Math (GSM8K) score of 0.14. The average rank across all six benchmarks is 1.8, substantially better than the next-best baseline (Scaffold at 3.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedICU outperforms all five baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedAvgM, Scaffold, FedAdam/FedYogi) on the three MT-Bench generalization metrics, with the largest margin over FedAvg (+0.25 on MT-1, +0.40 on MT-2, +0.33 on MT-Final). On Code (HumanEval), FedICU ties Scaffold at 0.10 and edges out FedAvgM (0.09) and FedAvg (0.05). However, FedICU does not dominate every domain: FedAvgM achieves a higher Financial (MMLU) score (0.40 vs. 0.35), and FedYogi leads on Math (GSM8K) with 0.16 versus FedICU's 0.14. Despite these two narrow losses, FedICU's average rank of 1.8 is more than 1.5 points better than the next-best method (Scaffold at 3.4), confirming its overall superiority in maintaining general capabilities while adapting to heterogeneous domain-specific instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning and the FedAvg federated aggregation paradigm, but departs from prior federated LLM frameworks (OpenFedLLM, FederatedScope-LLM, Shepherd) that treat LLM SFT as a plug-in to classical FL algorithms. The key conceptual departure is decomposing LoRA update matrices into magnitude (consensus) and direction (divergence) components, inspired by weight normalization, and applying distinct aggregation strategies to each: simple averaging for consensus and cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation for divergence. The importance-aware binary masking for sparse parameter uploads draws on parameter pruning and lottery ticket ideas but applies them specifically to the federated upload decision rather than model compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consensus-divergence decomposition offers an interpretable lens into what constitutes 'general' versus 'domain-specific' knowledge in LoRA fine-tuning, which could inform future work on model merging, continual learning, and multi-task adaptation beyond federated settings. The sparse updating mechanism provides a practical communication-efficiency gain while mitigating catastrophic forgetting, addressing a real deployment concern for federated LLM systems. However, the approach is limited to LoRA (not full fine-tuning), tested only on 7B-scale models with four clients, and the binary mask threshold is a simple heuristic without adaptive tuning. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to higher-rank adapters, larger model families, or more extreme heterogeneity (e.g., different base models across clients).
