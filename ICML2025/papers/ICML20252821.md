## Problem

Constructing a complete, arbitrage-free implied volatility surface from fewer than 10 option contracts at one-minute frequency within milliseconds remains unsolved. Existing parametric models (e.g., SSVI) require iterative calibration per time slice, while deep learning approaches assume dense observations and cannot be retrained in real time. This paper tackles the practically demanding regime where only a handful of liquid contracts have reliable quotes at any given moment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-sized set of 9 option contracts, each specified by a (k, t, σ) triplet (log forward moneyness, annualized time to maturity, implied volatility), selected as the closest available contracts to {ATM, 25Δ Call, 25Δ Put} × {7 days, 1 month, 3 months}. It produces a continuous implied volatility surface σ(k,t) over k ∈ [−1.5, 1.5] and t ∈ [0.01, 2] in a single forward pass (~2 ms on GPU), with no per-instance optimization. Unlike fixed-grid baselines (VAE), the input is a permutation-invariant set with continuous coordinates, and the output is guaranteed free of static arbitrage via an auxiliary training loss.

## Method

HyperIV employs a hypernetwork architecture: a Transformer encoder (2 layers, 2 heads, 128 hidden dim, no positional embeddings, mean pooling) maps the 9-element reference set to a 337-parameter vector that parameterizes a compact 2-hidden-layer MLP (16 neurons, tanh activations, softplus output) which evaluates σ(k,t). Training is supervised (MSE on implied volatilities across historical intervals) augmented by an auxiliary arbitrage-free loss that penalizes violations of calendar-spread monotonicity (σ + 2t∂σ/∂t ≥ 0), butterfly density non-negativity (g(k,t) ≥ 0), and the integrates-to-one constraint via trapezoidal integration. The key insight is that the hypernetwork learns to 'calibrate' the surface network on-the-fly from sparse observations, eliminating per-instance optimization while the auxiliary loss (which approaches ~10⁻⁸ during training) enforces arbitrage-free properties without hard architectural constraints.

## Results

HyperIV achieves the lowest implied volatility MAE on 7 out of 8 asset-interval combinations, with particularly large margins on VIX (0.0336 vs. 0.2771 for SSVI and 0.1345 for GNO) and MXEF (0.0172 vs. 0.1040 for GNO). The only loss is on 1-minute SPX data, where GNO (0.0140) edges out HyperIV (0.0167). In terms of price MAE (Table 5), HyperIV again leads on 7/8 settings, with the largest gap on MXEF (1.76 vs. 11.70 for GNO). Computationally, HyperIV constructs a full 10,000-point surface in 2.09 ms with only 532 MiB testing memory, compared to 13.11 ms for SSVI and 7.86 ms for GNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperIV outperforms all three baselines on 7 of 8 asset-interval settings for implied volatility MAE, losing only to GNO on 1-minute SPX (0.0167 vs. 0.0140). Against SSVI (4-parameter parametric model), HyperIV wins decisively on every setting, with the gap most dramatic on VIX where SSVI's MAE of 0.2771 is over 8× worse than HyperIV's 0.0336. Against VAE (332K parameters), HyperIV wins on all 8 settings, and notably VAE's large parameter count does not translate to accuracy — SSVI with only 4 parameters actually beats VAE on MXWLD and MXEF. Against GNO (102K parameters), HyperIV wins on 7/8 settings and is the only method that does not catastrophically fail on VIX or MXEF, where GNO's MAE degrades to 0.1345 and 0.1040 respectively. In cross-asset generalization (Table 7), HyperIV trained on SPX achieves MAE of 0.0154 on NDX and 0.0124 on RUT, surpassing SSVI calibrated directly on those target datasets, while GNO shows severe directional asymmetry (0.0185 for SPX→NDX but 0.1272 for NDX→SPX).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016) with set-transformer-style permutation-invariant encoding (Lee et al., 2019) to address implied volatility surface construction from extremely sparse observations. It departs from prior calibration-acceleration work (Hernandez, 2016; Yang & Hospedales, 2023) by eliminating fixed moneyness/maturity grids and from calibration-free deep methods (Bergeron et al., 2021; Gonon et al., 2024) by explicitly targeting the sub-10-contract regime where those methods were not designed or validated. The auxiliary arbitrage-free loss (calendar spread + butterfly density) follows the soft-constraint approach common in recent deep IV models, but is applied to a 337-parameter surface network whose weights are generated on-the-fly rather than trained per interval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-millisecond surface construction from 9 quotes addresses a genuine bottleneck in real-time options trading where traditional calibration is infeasible, and the demonstrated cross-asset generalization (outperforming SSVI calibrated directly on target data for most assets) suggests the learned surface prior is robust. Key limitations include the lack of interpretability relative to parametric models, the GPU dependency for inference, and the expected VIX generalization gap. Open questions include whether temporal dynamics and historical context can be incorporated, and whether the hypernetwork approach extends to exotic option surfaces or multi-asset joint modeling.
