## Problem

Current multimodal learning frameworks optimize alignment in the original token or pixel space, which is prone to modality collapse from conflicting gradients, missing modalities, and information uncertainty in continuous domains. No generalized framework existed that applied the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to any-to-any multimodal modeling, where input and output can be arbitrary combinations of modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any combination of modalities (text, RGB image, audio, or multi-modality concatenation such as image + text question for VQA), each encoded by a frozen pretrained uni-modal encoder (LLaMA-3-8B, DINOv2-Large, LanguageBind) with 3 LoRA-finetuned layers. The MoE predictor projects the input latent vector into the output modality's embedding space, producing a cross-modal alignment score that serves as both training loss and retrieval/classification metric. For classification, labels are encoded as one-hot vectors treated as a separate modality, enabling self-supervised alignment without explicit classification heads.

## Method

M3-JEPA implements the JEPA latent predictor as a Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) with N=12 experts, top-K=4 routing, and L=2 parallel gates—one routing to the contrastive loss (in-batch cosine-similarity InfoNCE) and one to the regularization loss (L2 distance). The gating function concatenates the input embedding with a learnable modality-specific embedding, disentangling shared cross-modal information from modality-specific details. Training uses Alternating Gradient Descent (AGD), cycling through T directional tasks (e.g., image→text, text→image, audio→text) at each step to decouple conflicting gradients. Only 140M parameters are trainable (the full MoE predictor plus 3 LoRA layers per encoder), while the information-theoretic analysis shows the combined loss maximizes mutual information and minimizes conditional entropy, with an optimal loss weight α=0.5 derived from both free-energy and convergence arguments.

## Results

On vision-language retrieval, M3-JEPA (140M trainable params) achieves R@1 of 97.8 on both Flickr30K Image→Text and Text→Image, and R@1 of 87.7 / 89.7 on COCO Image→Text / Text→Image, outperforming all baselines including BLIP-2 w/ViT-g (1.2B) and BEiT-3 (1.9B). On audio-text retrieval (zero-shot), M3-JEPA reaches R@1 of 20.1 on Clotho Text→Audio and 20.4 on AudioCaps Audio→Text, surpassing LanguageBind by 4.6 and 2.6 points respectively. On ImageNet-1K classification, M3-JEPA attains 86.6% accuracy, exceeding DINOv2 (83.2%) and CLIP-ViT (82.1%). On VQA, M3-JEPA scores 82.5 on VQAv2 test-standard and 87.6 on NLVR-2 test-P, placing second behind BEiT-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3-JEPA outperforms all 23 baselines on the primary vision-language retrieval benchmark (Table 1), achieving the highest R@1 on both Flickr30K and COCO for both retrieval directions. Against the strongest dual+fusion encoder baselines, M3-JEPA surpasses BLIP-2 w/ViT-g (1.2B params) by +2.3 R@1 on COCO Text→Image (89.7 vs 68.3) and +2.3 on COCO Image→Text (87.7 vs 85.4), while using only 140M trainable parameters (~8.5× fewer). It also beats BEiT-3 (1.9B) by +2.9 R@1 on COCO Image→Text and +22.5 on COCO Text→Image. On audio-text retrieval (Table 2), M3-JEPA consistently outperforms LanguageBind, ImageBind, AVFIC, and VALOR across all four retrieval directions on both Clotho and AudioCaps in a zero-shot setting. On ImageNet-1K (Table 3), M3-JEPA leads DINOv2 by +3.4% and CLIP-ViT by +4.5% in accuracy. The one area where M3-JEPA trails is VQA (Table 4): it scores 82.5 on VQAv2 test-standard and 87.6 on NLVR-2 test-P, both second-best but below BEiT-3 (84.0 / 92.6), which the authors attribute to BEiT-3's larger pretraining corpus and M3-JEPA's simple concatenation of multi-modal inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), previously applied only to single-modality vision (I-JEPA, MC-JEPA), into a generalized any-to-any multimodal alignment framework. It departs from the dominant generative paradigm (CLIP, BLIP-2, Flamingo) that aligns in discrete token space by instead projecting inputs into the output's latent embedding space via a Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts predictor, drawing on MMoE's multi-task gating and AGD's alternating optimization. The key architectural departure from prior connector-based approaches (Q-former in BLIP-2, MLP in LLaVA) is the use of a top-K MoE with disentangled modality-specific and shared gating, combined with an energy-based loss formulation grounded in information theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M3-JEPA offers a practical and theoretically grounded alternative to LLM-backbone multimodal models, achieving SOTA retrieval with only 140M trainable parameters versus 1.2B for BLIP-2, while generalizing zero-shot to new modalities (audio) and tasks (classification). However, the framework remains non-generative, is not truly modality-agnostic (new modalities require manual encoder selection and expert addition), and underperforms BEiT-3 on VQA due to its simple concatenation strategy for multi-modal inputs. Open questions include whether JEPA can be combined with generative objectives, whether meta/hyper-networks can automate modality expansion, and whether the convexity assumption underlying the AGD convergence guarantee holds in practice for deep multimodal encoders.
