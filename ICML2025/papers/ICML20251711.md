## Problem

Neural operators learn mappings between function spaces (e.g., PDE solution operators) but provide no mechanism to quantify the uncertainty in their predictions, which is essential for safety-critical decision-making, active learning, and detecting distribution shift. Existing uncertainty quantification approaches for neural operators either do not extend to Fourier neural operators or do not produce a structured Gaussian process belief over the output function space, limiting their compatibility with downstream GP-based tools such as Bayesian optimization and probabilistic numerics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural operator (e.g., an FNO), a Gaussian weight-space belief (μ, Σ) obtained post-hoc via Laplace approximation, SWAG, or variational inference, and an input function (PDE parameters or initial conditions). It produces a function-valued Gaussian process over the output Banach space, from which one can read off the predictive mean and covariance functions at arbitrary spatial points and draw full function samples. Crucially, the output is resolution-agnostic by design: in the FNO case study, evaluating the GP at new output points requires only the cached hidden state v^(L-1) from a single forward pass, not a re-evaluation of the full network.

## Method

LUNO constructs a function-valued Gaussian process posterior over a neural operator's output in three steps: (1) uncurrying the operator F: A×W→U into a pointwise function f: (A×D_U)×W→R^{d'_U}; (2) linearizing f around the weight-space mean μ to induce a multi-output GP with mean m(a,x)=f((a,x),μ) and covariance K((a₁,x₁),(a₂,x₂))=∇_w f|_μ Σ ∇_w f|_μᵀ; and (3) probabilistically currying the resulting GP back into a U-valued GP. The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.2, which proves that function-valued GPs in separable Banach spaces are equivalent to multi-output GPs with augmented input spaces, providing a rigorous bridge between the two formulations. For FNOs restricted to last-layer Laplace, the resulting GP is parametric—its moments depend only on the hidden state v^(L-1), μ, and Σ—enabling efficient lazy sampling of entire output functions without discretizing the GP. The framework is compatible with arbitrary weight-space beliefs and extends to abstract Banach spaces (e.g., Sobolev spaces for weak PDE solutions) via a generalized probabilistic currying theorem.

## Results

On the low-data Burgers' equation task (25 training trajectories), LUNO-LA achieves the best expected marginal NLL of −2.0787, outperforming LUNO-Iso (−1.9488), Sample-Iso (−1.9341), Input Perturbations (−1.8720), and Deep Ensembles (−0.8145), while maintaining competitive RMSE (3.62×10⁻²). On the out-of-distribution Advection-Diffusion task, LUNO-LA achieves NLL of −1.126 on the Flip perturbation and 1.164 on Pos-Neg-Flip, dramatically outperforming all other weight-space methods (e.g., Sample-LA: 4.395 and 27.046; Input Perturbations: 2.573 and 494.935), though Deep Ensembles still lead on the in-distribution Base setting (−5.313 vs. −2.934). LUNO-Iso is also faster than the deep ensemble in wall-clock time for single-trajectory predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Input Perturbations (Pathak et al., 2022), LUNO-LA wins decisively on NLL in both the low-data Burgers' setting (−2.0787 vs. −1.8720) and the OOD Advection-Diffusion setting (−1.126 vs. 2.573 on Flip; 1.164 vs. 494.935 on Pos-Neg-Flip), indicating far better-calibrated uncertainty under distribution shift. Against Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), the picture is mixed: ensembles achieve slightly lower RMSE on Burgers' (3.49×10⁻² vs. 3.62×10⁻²) and the best Base OOD NLL (−5.313 vs. −2.934), but LUNO-LA substantially outperforms ensembles on the more challenging OOD perturbations (Flip: −1.126 vs. −3.825; Pos-Neg-Flip: 1.164 vs. −1.014). The paper also notes a structural advantage: ensemble covariance is fundamentally rank-deficient (bounded by ensemble size), leaving certain error directions unaccounted for, whereas LUNO-LA's covariance rank is bounded only by the number of parameters considered. LUNO-LA also avoids the 10× training cost of ensembles and is faster at inference than the ensemble in the authors' JAX implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUNO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural operators for PDE solution learning (Kovachki et al., Li et al.), (2) Bayesian deep learning via weight-space uncertainty (Laplace approximation, SWAG, variational inference), and (3) function-valued Gaussian processes previously studied in Hilbert spaces (Owhadi, 2023; Batlle et al., 2024). The key departure is formulating the theory natively in Banach spaces and introducing 'probabilistic currying'—a formal equivalence between function-valued GPs and standard multi-output GPs with augmented input spaces—which makes the construction compatible with any existing weight-space inference method. Prior UQ work on neural operators (Magnani et al., 2022 for graph neural operators; Garg & Chakraborty, 2023 for DeepONets) did not provide a function-space formulation or extend to FNOs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUNO provides a practical, post-hoc, resolution-agnostic uncertainty framework for neural operators that produces a full covariance structure over the output function space, enabling downstream tasks like active learning, Bayesian optimization, and probabilistic numerics that require structured (not just pointwise) uncertainty. The rank-deficiency argument against deep ensembles—showing that ensemble covariance cannot explain errors in its null space—is a useful practical insight for practitioners choosing UQ methods. The main limitation is the difficulty of accurately modeling weight-space covariances (especially for large networks), and the empirical validation is confined to relatively small-scale 1D/2D PDEs; scaling to high-dimensional or industrial PDE settings remains open.
