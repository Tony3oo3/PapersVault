## Problem

World models for low-dimensional control environments are typically trained from scratch on a single environment, limiting generalization to out-of-distribution transitions. The heterogeneity in sensor and actuator dimensions across environments (state dims 3–78, action dims 1–21) creates a fundamental barrier to pre-training: zero-padding imposes dimension limits, and per-environment heads prevent zero-shot transfer. This paper addresses whether a single world model can be pre-trained across heterogeneous environments and still achieve positive transfer to complex downstream tasks like transition prediction, off-policy evaluation, and model predictive control.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a trajectory flattened into a 2D matrix of shape T×M (timesteps × variates), where each variate is a single scalar from the state, action, or reward vector, discretized into B uniform bins via Gaussian histograms (input) or one-hot encoding (target). Three learned embeddings (timestep, variate identity, prediction target) are added to the projected bin representation before processing. The output is a joint categorical distribution over all state and reward variates for the next timestep, from which point predictions are obtained by taking the expectation over bin centers. For downstream OPE, the model rolls out a target policy over 2000 steps to estimate policy value; for MPC, it scores 128 candidate action sequences over a 25–50 step horizon.

## Method

TrajWorld is a Transformer that processes trajectories through interleaved temporal attention (causal, applied independently per variate across timesteps) and variate attention (bidirectional, applied across all variates at each timestep), explicitly modeling the 2D structure of trajectory data rather than flattening to 1D. The key design insight is scalarization: instead of treating state/action vectors as fixed-size inputs, each scalar dimension is processed independently, enabling the architecture to naturally accommodate varying numbers of sensors and actuators without padding or per-environment heads. Rather than conditioning on environment IDs, the model leverages in-context learning from historical transitions to infer environment dynamics, making pre-training on diverse data critical for learning 'how to learn dynamics' rather than memorizing specific environments. The model is pre-trained for 1M steps on UniTraj (1.3M trajectories, 719M steps from 80 environments spanning exploratory, expert, and replay-buffer data) with per-environment batching, then fine-tuned for up to 1.5M steps on target environment data. This pre-training yields substantial gains in transition prediction (reducing MAE across 75 train-test pairs), state-of-the-art off-policy evaluation on DOPE, and superior MPC performance, while baselines like MLP Ensemble suffer negative transfer and TDM (1D attention) lags behind.

## Results

On the DOPE off-policy evaluation benchmark (15 datasets across Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah), pre-trained TrajWorld achieves the highest average rank correlation (e.g., 0.95 on Hopper expert, 0.94 on Walker2D expert, 0.94 on HalfCheetah expert), outperforming the prior SOTA ETM (0.85, 0.54, 0.81 respectively) and all other baselines. On model predictive control with proposal policies, TrajWorld yields the highest returns: 1401±236 (Hopper), 3427±370 (Walker2D), and 5809±15 (HalfCheetah), surpassing TDM (1117, 2619, 5647) and MLP Ensemble (1091, 3465, 5692). On transition prediction across 75 train-test dataset pairs, TrajWorld with pre-training achieves the lowest aggregated MAE, while MLP Ensemble with pre-training actually suffers negative transfer (worse than its from-scratch counterpart).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajWorld (w/PT) outperforms ETM (the prior SOTA on DOPE) on rank correlation in 11 out of 15 environment-level combinations, with the largest gaps on Walker2D expert (0.94 vs. 0.54) and Hopper random (0.81 vs. 0.61), though ETM retains an edge on Hopper m-replay (0.97 vs. 0.61) and m-expert (0.95 vs. 0.87). Against TDM (Schubert et al., 2023), TrajWorld wins on most settings but TDM occasionally matches or exceeds it (e.g., Hopper m-replay: 0.89 vs. 0.61); notably, TDM exhibits negative transfer in the MPC setting despite positive transfer in prediction and OPE. MLP Ensemble with pre-training suffers clear negative transfer, dropping below its from-scratch counterpart in nearly all OPE settings (e.g., Hopper expert: 0.42 vs. 0.62 rank correlation), highlighting that naive zero-padding architectures are incompatible with heterogeneous pre-training. On MPC with proposal policies, TrajWorld (w/PT) achieves the highest return in all three environments (1401, 3427, 5809), beating TDM (w/PT) by 25%, 31%, and 3% respectively. The classical OPE baselines (FQE, DR, IS, DICE, VPM) are included in the DOPE benchmark comparison but are not the primary focus of the paper's model-based comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalist dynamics model paradigm of Schubert et al. (2023, TDM), which trained a transformer on 80 heterogeneous environments but only demonstrated positive transfer to simple cart-pole tasks. The key architectural departure is the introduction of interleaved temporal and variate attentions (inspired by axial attention in multidimensional transformers, Ho et al. 2019), replacing TDM's 1D sequence flattening with an explicit 2D structure over timesteps and variates. Conceptually, the paper also departs from prior cross-environment approaches that rely on zero-padding or per-environment heads by leveraging in-context learning from trajectory history to infer environment dynamics, and by processing data at the scalar (variate) level rather than the vector level to exploit inherent homogeneity across differently-sized state and action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that world model pre-training yields positive transfer to complex, high-dimensional control environments (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D), addressing a gap left by prior work that only validated transfer on simple tasks. The UniTraj dataset (1.3M trajectories, 80 environments, diverse policy distributions) is a significant practical resource for the community. Key limitations include the bounded discretization range (preventing extrapolation beyond training data extremes), the larger model capacity complicating uncertainty calibration for offline RL, and the restriction to low-dimensional proprioceptive data. Future work on multimodal (visual + proprioceptive) world models and extending the value range of bins for out-of-distribution transitions are natural next steps.
