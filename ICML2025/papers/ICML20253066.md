## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal LLMs suffer from an 'attention deficit disorder' where cross-modal attention is inconsistent across layers and decays exponentially with depth, causing up to 10× disparity in attention scores between visual and textual tokens. This imbalance leads to neglect of fine-grained visual details, resulting in near-random performance on high-level tasks requiring cognition and emotion understanding (e.g., ~50% accuracy on 2-class sarcasm detection).

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 alongside text prompts and multi-turn conversation history (including video keyframes and transcripts for the Godfather demo). Outputs are structured as free-form text responses for perception and cognition tasks, discrete emotion labels (6-class for TumEmo, binary for MVSA/HFM), and role-playing dialogue turns. Training uses LoRA-based tuning with token merging on 700K samples for 1 epoch, keeping the alignment computation at linear complexity in token count.

## Method

MODA splits standard multimodal attention into self-modal and cross-modal components, each governed by its own modulated attention mask. The Duplex (V/T)-Aligner maps tokens from one modality into the other's representation space using normed Gram matrices (K^{m⊤}K^{m}) as kernelized transfer functions, implementing a 'correct-after-align' strategy that decouples modality alignment from cross-layer token mixing. The Modular Masked Attention assigns per-modality masks with pseudo-attention scores (p_ij = p_base − (j−1)β) to prevent attention collapse and enforce modality-aware positional priors. Self-modal and cross-modal outputs are concatenated (rather than added) to preserve distinct feature streams, and the full mechanism is trained via LoRA on top of Llama-3-8B or Hermes2-Yi-34B backbones.

## Results

On 16 perception benchmarks, MODA-8B achieves category averages of 72.1 (General), 61.5 (Knowledge), 72.0 (OCR&Chart), and 66.0 (Vision-Centric), outperforming Cambrian-1-8B (73.1/61.3/71.3/65.0) and LLaVA-NeXT-8B (72.5/55.6/63.9/56.6) on fine-grained tasks. At 34B scale, MODA-34B reaches 69.9 on Vision-Centric and 74.7 on OCR&Chart, surpassing Cambrian-1-34B (68.5/71.9) and LLaVA-NeXT-34B (64.0/67.7). On the emotion-specialized setting, MODA-8B achieves 0.841 average across MVSA-S, MVSA-M, TumEmo, and HFM, outperforming task-specific SOTA methods SPFVTE (0.806) and MULSER (0.757). On the cognition-specialized MMRole benchmark, MODA-8B scores 0.995, matching the dedicated MMRole-9B model (0.994).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source baseline Cambrian-1, MODA-8B wins on Vision-Centric (66.0 vs. 65.0) and OCR&Chart (72.0 vs. 71.3) but trails slightly on General (72.1 vs. 73.1); at 34B, MODA-34B leads Cambrian-1-34B on all four categories (76.7/69.5/74.7/69.9 vs. 76.8/67.0/71.9/68.5). Against LLaVA-NeXT, MODA achieves a substantial margin on fine-grained tasks: +9.4 on Vision-Centric (8B) and +5.9 (34B), and +8.1 on OCR&Chart (8B). On the cognition benchmark (MMRole), MODA-8B (0.972) outperforms Cambrian-1-8B (0.895) and LLaVA-NeXT-8B (0.968), and with cognition-specific tuning reaches 0.995, matching the dedicated MMRole-9B model (0.994). On emotion benchmarks, MODA-8B (0.588) surpasses Cambrian-1-8B (0.547) and LLaVA-NeXT-8B (0.576); with emotion-specific tuning, MODA-8B (0.841) exceeds all task-specialized baselines including SPFVTE (0.806) and MULSER (0.757). Against proprietary models (GPT-4V, Gemini, Grok-1.5), MODA-34B is competitive on Knowledge (69.5 vs. GPT-4V 65.2) and Vision-Centric (69.9 vs. GPT-4V 62.4), though GPT-4V and Gemini-1.5 Pro still lead on OCR&Chart.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODA builds directly on the MLLM architecture lineage (LLaVA-NeXT, Cambrian-1, Mini-Gemini-HD) but departs from their shared reliance on standard self-attention for multimodal token mixing. The Duplex (V/T)-Aligner repurposes the Gram-matrix-based embedding space mapping from latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) as a cross-modal token transfer function, while the Modular Masked Attention draws on efficient-attention masking strategies (Longformer, StableMask) but applies them per-modality rather than per-position. Unlike Cambrian-1's spatial vision aggregator, which patches visual features post-encoding, MODA intervenes at the attention computation level itself, decomposing it into self-modal and cross-modal sub-operations with independent masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of the Deficit Disorder Attention (DDA) problem—layer-by-layer exponential decay of cross-modal links compounded by modality-biased attention—gives the community a concrete diagnostic for why MLLMs underperform on fine-grained perception, cognition, and emotion tasks. The most compelling result is that an 8B general-purpose MLLM with MODA outperforms task-specialized emotion models (SPFVTE, MULSER) on MVSA and TumEmo, suggesting that better attention geometry can substitute for task-specific architecture. Open questions include whether the Gram-matrix alignment generalizes beyond two modalities (e.g., audio-visual-textual), whether the linear-complexity claim holds at much longer sequences, and whether the modest ~1-point perception gains over Cambrian-1 persist at larger scales or with more training data.
