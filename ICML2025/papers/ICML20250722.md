## Problem

Deep learning weather forecasters struggle to simultaneously handle global and regional high-resolution scales, suffer from over-smoothing that erases extreme events and high-frequency disturbances in long-term rollouts, and lack explicit dynamic-system modeling of multi-scale node interactions. No existing framework unifies global forecasting, regional refinement, and ensemble prediction within a single architecture while preserving fine-grained atmospheric features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 69-channel meteorological state (4 surface variables + 5 variables × 13 pressure levels) on a 120×240 lat-lon grid at 1.5° resolution for global tasks, or 0.25° regional data augmented with interpolated global model forecasts as boundary forcing for regional tasks. It outputs the next 6-hour state autoregressively; multi-day forecasts are produced by recursive rollout, and ensemble forecasts average 50 members perturbed with Perlin noise. For regional forecasting, the global model's future predictions are concatenated with regional data as trainable input channels rather than fixed forcing.

## Method

OneForecast builds an Earth-specific multi-scale graph on a 5-level subdivided icosahedron mesh (12,337 nodes, 98,296 edges) with additional region-refined densification over target areas, connected to the lat-lon grid via unidirectional G2M/M2G edges. The core message-passing unit is Multi-stream Messaging (MSM), which replaces standard MLP-based updates with a dynamic multi-head gated edge module (concatenating edge, source, and destination features through a two-layer MLP producing per-head gating vectors) followed by a multi-head node attention mechanism that computes softmax-normalized attention over incoming edges. The paper proves (Theorem 2.1) that when gating weights scale proportionally to |λ−1|+ε, the MSM operator acts as a strictly high-pass filter in the graph spectral domain, explaining its ability to preserve high-frequency signals. For regional forecasting, the Neural Nested Grid (NNG) method interpolates global model forecasts (both interior and boundary) to regional resolution and concatenates them with regional data as trainable input, enabling end-to-end coupling without fixed forcing. The full model (24.76M params, 16 MSM layers) is trained with relative L2 loss over 200 epochs using cosine annealing on 1959–2017 ERA5 data.

## Results

On the WeatherBench2 benchmark (1.5° global, 69 variables), OneForecast achieves the best ensemble RMSE across all lead times: 0.4393 (7-day), 0.4699 (8-day), 0.4951 (9-day), and 0.5167 (10-day), outperforming GraphCast-ENS (0.4412–0.5331) and Pangu-ENS (0.4435–0.5205). For extreme event detection, OneForecast attains the highest CSI (0.14) and SEDI (0.31) on 10 m wind, and the highest CSI (0.21) and SEDI (0.40) on 2 m temperature. In typhoon track prediction, OneForecast reduces the mean position error to 157 km, substantially below GraphCast (197 km) and Pangu (231 km), despite using lower-resolution (1.5°) input data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneForecast is compared against Pangu, GraphCast, FuXi, Fengwu, and Graph-EFM. In deterministic global forecasting (Table 1, 1300 ICs), OneForecast achieves the lowest normalized RMSE and highest ACC among the four retrained baselines (Pangu, GraphCast, FuXi, and OneForecast under identical training settings). In ensemble forecasting (Table 2), OneForecast-ENS outperforms GraphCast-ENS by 0.017 RMSE at 10 days (0.5167 vs. 0.5331) and Pangu-ENS by 0.004 (0.5167 vs. 0.5205). For extreme events, OneForecast leads all baselines on both CSI and SEDI for 10 m wind and 2 m temperature (Table 6), with the largest margin over FuXi (SEDI 0.40 vs. 0.27 for T2M). In typhoon tracking (Table 7), OneForecast achieves 157 km mean position error, beating GraphCast (197 km) by 40 km and Pangu (231 km) by 74 km, even though baselines were trained on 0.25° data while OneForecast uses 1.5°. For regional high-resolution forecasting (Figure 6), the proposed Neural Nested Grid method outperforms both direct high-resolution training and Graph-EFM's boundary-condition approach, showing stable long-term RMSE/ACC curves where the other methods degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneForecast extends the GraphCast lineage (Lam et al., 2023) and Multi-scale MeshGraphNets (Fortunato et al., 2022) by adding a region-refined icosahedral mesh and a multi-stream messaging module with dynamic multi-head gating, drawing on multi-grid theory (He & Xu, 2019) for its multi-scale graph construction. Its most direct conceptual departure is from Graph-EFM (Oskarsson et al., 2024), which also uses hierarchical GNNs for global-regional coupling but treats global low-resolution forecasts as fixed, non-trainable forcing; OneForecast instead applies trainable local refinement and end-to-end coupling via a neural nested grid method adapted from classical nested-grid NWP (Phillips & Shukla, 1973). The ESMLP edge-update mechanism from Pfaff et al. (2020) is retained as a building block, while the dynamic gating and attention layers constitute the paper's primary architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified global-regional framework addresses a practical gap: prior DL weather models either handle global forecasts or regional downscaling separately, and the neural nested grid method offers a low-cost path to high-resolution regional forecasts by reusing a pre-trained global model. The multi-stream messaging module, backed by a (somewhat idealized) high-pass filtering theorem, provides a principled mechanism to combat over-smoothing in extreme-event and long-lead forecasts. Limitations include the use of 1.5° global data (coarser than the 0.25° baselines in some comparisons), demonstration of the regional method on only two variables, and ensemble perturbation via simple Perlin noise rather than learned uncertainty. Open questions include whether the approach scales to km-scale regional grids, whether the theoretical gating assumption is empirically validated beyond the spectral-error plots, and how the framework performs on variables beyond the standard 69-channel ERA5 set.
