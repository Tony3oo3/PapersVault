## Problem

In the cooperative rationalization framework (RNP), the generator's conditional sampling process can introduce spurious correlations between trivial patterns and labels, even in datasets that are inherently clean. Previous work implicitly treated sampled (Z,Y) pairs as approximating P(Z,Y), when they actually approximate P(Z,Y|g), meaning patterns independent of Y in the original data can become correlated with Y after the generator's selection. This paper identifies this overlooked approximation error, provides theoretical and empirical evidence for it, and proposes a practical remedy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence (or graph) paired with a classification label as input. The generator produces a binary mask selecting a sparse subset of tokens (or nodes) as the rationale candidate, with sparsity controlled to target levels (e.g., 10%, 20%, 30% of tokens). The predictor then classifies based solely on the selected rationale, and the attacker independently selects a competing subset. For graph tasks, node-level selection forms a subgraph as the rationale. The final outputs are the rationale (selected tokens/nodes) and the predicted class label.

## Method

The proposed A2I (Attack to Inspection and Instruction) method introduces a third component—an attacker—alongside the standard generator and predictor. The attacker is trained to select patterns from the input that cause the predictor to flip its prediction to the opposite class, thereby exposing any trivial patterns the predictor has erroneously learned as indicative features. To prevent the predictor from exploiting these spurious correlations, an instruction loss is added to the predictor's objective: the predictor is forced to output a uniform distribution (e.g., [0.5, 0.5] for binary classification) on the attacker's selected patterns, effectively teaching it that such patterns carry no label information. Training alternates between updating the attacker (to find exploitable patterns) and jointly updating the generator and predictor (with both the standard RNP loss and the instruction loss). The key theoretical insight is that if a trivial pattern T is independent of Y in the original data but becomes correlated under the generator's conditional sampling, an attacker with sufficient expressiveness can always construct a selection that inverts the predictor's decision on T, and the instruction loss then rectifies the sampled distribution back to P(Y), giving the generator correct feedback.

## Results

On the BeerAdvocate benchmark (the most widely used rationalization benchmark), RNP+A2I achieves F1 scores of 57.8, 66.6, and 62.0 at S≈10% on Beer-Appearance, Beer-Aroma, and Beer-Palate respectively, outperforming standard RNP by up to 9.0 F1 points. When layered on top of the stronger FR backbone, FR+A2I reaches F1 of 57.7, 66.9, and 62.6 at S≈10%, improving over FR by up to 6.9 points. The method also generalizes to graph classification (BA2Motifs, GOODMotif) and achieves comparable or better F1 than llama-3.1-8b-instruct on several settings. Attack success rate drops to ~50% (random) with A2I, confirming the predictor no longer relies on trivial patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNP+A2I consistently outperforms standard RNP across all 9 settings (3 datasets × 3 sparsity levels), with the largest gain of +9.0 F1 on Beer-Appearance at S≈10% (48.8→57.8). FR+A2I improves over FR on every setting, reaching +6.9 F1 on Beer-Appearance S≈10% (50.8→57.7) and +2.7 on Beer-Aroma S≈10% (64.2→66.9). Against the causal method Inter_RAT, FR+A2I wins on 8 of 9 settings (e.g., 57.7 vs 41.6 on Beer-Appearance S≈10%), while RNP+A2I also dominates Inter_RAT across the board. Compared to NIR (noise injection), FR+A2I achieves higher F1 on all 9 settings, with the gap ranging from +1.1 to +9.5 points. On the HotelReview benchmark (Table 2), RNP+A2I and FR+A2I similarly outperform RNP, Inter_RAT, NIR, and FR on all three hotel datasets. On graph datasets (BA2Motifs, GOODMotif), FR+A2I achieves the best F1 (42.3 and 46.1), surpassing FR (39.6 and 44.3) and RNP (36.4 and 43.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rationalizing Neural Predictions (RNP) cooperative game framework (Lei et al., 2016) and its many variants (FR, Inter_RAT, NIR, CR). It departs from prior work by identifying the root cause of the long-observed 'degeneration' phenomenon as a sampling bias: all previous methods implicitly treat the generator-sampled distribution P(Y,Z|g) as the true joint P(Y,Z), which can create spurious correlations between trivial patterns and labels even in clean datasets. While prior methods (Yu et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2022; Storek et al., 2023) applied regularization or noise injection to mitigate degeneration without fully explaining its origin, this paper provides both a theoretical account (conditional independence failure under the generator) and a practical adversarial solution (A2I) that can be layered on top of any existing rationalization method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a predictor trained on randomly selected patterns can reach ~95% accuracy undermines a core assumption in the rationalization literature: that high prediction accuracy on extracted rationales validates their semantic relevance. This has implications for any work using rationalization as a data-cleaning or interpretability tool, including LLM fine-tuning pipelines. The method is model-agnostic and composable, but its scope is limited to the generator-predictor cooperative game; whether analogous sampling-bias issues arise in other self-explaining or attention-based frameworks remains open. The comparison with llama-3.1-8b-instruct suggests small rationalization models remain competitive for rationale quality, but the LLM comparison is limited to a single model and two prompting strategies.
