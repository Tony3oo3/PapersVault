## Problem

Despite Adam's widespread practical success in deep learning, its convergence theory has lagged behind SGD because prior analyses required stronger assumptions on stochastic gradients (e.g., almost surely bounded gradients, L2-bounded gradients, or coordinate affine noise variance) than the standard L-smoothness plus ABC inequality used for SGD. This paper closes that gap by proving Adam achieves the same convergence guarantees as SGD under the same, weaker assumptions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an L-smooth, lower-bounded loss function with unbiased stochastic gradients satisfying the ABC inequality (bounding E[‖g_t‖²|F_{t-1}] by A(f(w_t)−f*)+B‖∇f(w_t)‖²+C), along with Adam hyperparameters (β₁, β_{2,t} schedule, learning rate η_t, and smoothing term μ). It produces three convergence results: a non-asymptotic sample complexity of O(ln T/√T) free of any O(1/μ) dependence, asymptotic almost sure convergence of the gradient norm to zero, and asymptotic L1 convergence (E[‖∇f(w_t)‖]→0). The hyperparameters are chosen T-independently, which is what introduces the ln T factor.

## Method

The framework introduces a new Lyapunov function Π_{Δ,t}·f̂(u_t) where Π_{Δ,t} is a product of scaling factors that absorb the extra error term arising from the difference between successive adaptive learning rates (η_{v_{t-1},i} − η_{v_{t,i}}). This is the key innovation: rather than trying to cancel this term against the descent term (which only works under stronger assumptions like coordinate affine noise variance), the authors move it to the left-hand side and encode it into the Lyapunov function's multiplicative weight. The momentum term is handled via an auxiliary variable u_t that decouples the recursive update. Bounding the p-th moment of Π_{Δ,t}^{−1} requires Burkholder's inequality applied to a discrete martingale, yielding a recursive relationship between p-th and p/2-th moments. For L1 convergence (which does not follow from almost sure convergence without bounded gradients), the authors employ a first hitting time decomposition to bound E[sup_k ‖∇f(w_k)‖].

## Results

Under the ABC inequality (a standard assumption for SGD), the paper proves that Adam achieves a non-asymptotic sample complexity of O(ln T / √T) for the average gradient norm squared, free of any O(1/μ) dependence on the smoothing parameter. Additionally, Adam is shown to converge almost surely (lim ||∇f(w_t)|| = 0 a.s.) and in the L1 sense (lim E[||∇f(w_t)||] = 0) under the same assumptions with γ > 1 and δ > 0. These are the first results to simultaneously establish all three convergence properties under assumptions no stronger than those required for SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's key advantage is achieving the weakest assumption (ABC inequality, standard for SGD) while simultaneously obtaining all three convergence properties. Compared to Wang et al. (2024a), which achieves O(1/√T) under the stronger coordinate affine noise variance condition, this work trades a logarithmic factor (O(ln T/√T)) for a strictly weaker assumption but gains both A.S. and L1 convergence. Against He et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2024), which require almost surely bounded gradients and incur an O(poly(1/μ)) penalty, this work eliminates the μ-dependence entirely while using a much milder assumption. Compared to Hong & Lin (2024) and Zou et al. (2019), which match the O(ln T/√T) rate but under stronger gradient conditions and without asymptotic convergence guarantees, this paper adds both A.S. and L1 convergence. The only prior work achieving A.S. convergence under bounded gradients (Xiao et al., 2024) does not provide L1 convergence or a sample complexity bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical SGD convergence analysis framework (approximate descent inequality) to Adam by introducing a novel Lyapunov function augmented with a product scaling factor Π_{Δ,t} that absorbs the extra error term arising from the difference between successive adaptive learning rates. Prior Adam convergence analyses (Reddi et al. 2018, Zou & Shen 2019, He et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2024a) required progressively stronger assumptions on stochastic gradients—ranging from almost surely bounded gradients to coordinate affine noise variance—while this work operates under the ABC inequality, the standard assumption used for SGD. The momentum handling follows the auxiliary variable technique of Liu et al. (2020), and the bounding of the product term's reciprocal moments leverages Burkholder's inequality and discrete martingale theory in a way not previously applied to Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that Adam achieves the same convergence guarantees as SGD under the same assumptions (L-smoothness + ABC inequality), this paper removes a significant theoretical asymmetry that has constrained the rigorous justification of Adam in non-convex optimization. The unified framework covering non-asymptotic sample complexity, almost sure convergence, and L1 convergence under a single set of assumptions provides a template for analyzing Adam variants. Limitations include the restriction to specific hyperparameter schedules (T-independent β_{2,t}), the focus on smooth non-convex objectives, and the absence of empirical validation; extending the framework to T-dependent parameters, non-smooth settings, or distributed/parallel variants remains open.
