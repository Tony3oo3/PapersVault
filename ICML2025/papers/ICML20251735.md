## Problem

Despite extensive work on Asynchronous SGD for distributed training, no fully asynchronous variant had been shown to achieve the optimal time complexity lower bound under heterogeneous worker computation times. Tyurin & Richtárik (2023) proved that all existing ASGD methods are suboptimal and introduced the semi-synchronous Rennala SGD as the first optimal method, raising the question of whether full asynchrony is fundamentally incompatible with optimality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial point x^0 ∈ ℝ^d, a constant stepsize γ, and a delay threshold R (set to max{1, ⌈σ²/ε⌉}, independent of worker speeds). Workers asynchronously compute unbiased stochastic gradients with bounded variance σ²; the server applies each arriving gradient only if its delay δ^k < R, otherwise discarding it and redirecting the worker to the current iterate. The output is an ε-stationary point satisfying E[‖∇f(x̄)‖²] ≤ ε, with the total wall-clock time matching the theoretical lower bound under both fixed and universal (arbitrarily time-varying) computation models.

## Method

Ringmaster ASGD modifies classical Asynchronous SGD with a single mechanism: a delay threshold R that causes the server to ignore any stochastic gradient computed at a point more than R iterations old. When a gradient arrives with delay δ^k ≥ R, it is discarded and the worker is immediately reassigned to compute a fresh gradient at the current iterate x^k (Algorithm 5 additionally terminates in-flight computations exceeding the threshold). This is equivalently expressed as an adaptive stepsize rule where γ_k = γ for fresh gradients and γ_k = 0 for stale ones. The key theoretical contribution is showing that choosing R = max{1, ⌈σ²/ε⌉} — a value depending only on problem parameters (σ², ε) and not on worker speeds — yields a time complexity of O(min_m [(1/m)Σ(1/τ_i)]^{-1}(LΔ/ε + σ²LΔ/(mε²))), matching the lower bound. Unlike Rennala SGD, which batches B fresh gradients before updating (semi-synchronous), Ringmaster ASGD updates immediately upon each relevant gradient arrival, making it fully asynchronous and better suited to sparse models and heterogeneous environments.

## Results

Ringmaster ASGD is the first fully asynchronous SGD method to achieve the optimal time complexity lower bound: min_{m∈[n]} [(1/m)Σ_{i=1}^m (1/τ_i)]^{-1} (LΔ/ε + σ²LΔ/(mε²)), matching the theoretical lower bound established by Tyurin & Richtárik (2023). The optimal delay threshold R = max{1, ⌈σ²/ε⌉} is independent of worker computation times, making the method robust to heterogeneous and dynamically fluctuating worker speeds. In experiments with n=6174 workers and d=1729 on a convex quadratic problem, Ringmaster ASGD converges significantly faster than both Delay-Adaptive ASGD and Rennala SGD. A 20-layer ReLU network trained on MNIST with the same heterogeneous worker setup confirms the practical advantage of Ringmaster ASGD over both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous state-of-the-art Asynchronous SGD (Koloskova et al., 2022; Mishchenko et al., 2022, referred to as Delay-Adaptive ASGD), Ringmaster ASGD strictly dominates: the classical method's time complexity uses all n workers and can be arbitrarily worse than the optimal bound when worker speeds are heterogeneous (e.g., with τ_i = √i, the gap grows as √n). Against Rennala SGD (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023), both methods achieve the same optimal time complexity, but Ringmaster ASGD is fully asynchronous (no synchronization barrier), updates the model immediately upon receiving a relevant gradient, and never discards gradients from fast workers—giving it a practical edge confirmed in experiments where it converges faster on both the convex quadratic and MNIST neural network tasks. Against the paper's own preliminary Naive Optimal ASGD, Ringmaster ASGD matches the same optimal time complexity under the fixed computation model but additionally adapts to dynamically changing worker speeds, making it robust to adversarial or chaotic computation environments where the initially fastest workers may become the slowest.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ringmaster ASGD sits at the intersection of three research threads: the classic Asynchronous SGD lineage (Tsitsiklis et al. 1986; Recht et al. 2011/Hogwild!), the suboptimal convergence analyses of Koloskova et al. (2022) and Mishchenko et al. (2022), and the optimality framework established by Tyurin & Richtárik (2023), who proved lower bounds and showed that no existing ASGD variant achieves them, introducing the semi-synchronous Rennala SGD as the optimal method. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a delay threshold R that discards overly stale gradients, which the authors show is sufficient to close the gap between fully asynchronous SGD and the theoretical lower bound. The universal computation model from Tyurin (2025) provides the generality for the final optimality guarantee under arbitrary, time-varying worker speeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a fundamental open question raised by Tyurin & Richtárik (2023): whether fully asynchronous SGD is inherently suboptimal or whether a suitable variant can match the theoretical lower bound. The answer—yes, with a simple delay-threshold mechanism—revalidates the practical appeal of lock-free asynchronous training (particularly for sparse models and large-scale datacenter deployments) while providing the first rigorous optimality guarantee. Open questions include extending the analysis to heterogeneous data distributions (federated learning), incorporating communication costs, and deriving closed-form guarantees under random computation-time distributions, all of which the authors identify as future work.
