## Problem

Training high-quality one-step generative models remains challenging: GANs suffer from unstable discriminator training, consistency models are sensitive to noise schedules and distance measures, and diffusion distillation methods require a pretrained teacher model plus expensive regularizers. This paper asks whether a simple, stable divergence-minimization framework can produce competitive one-step generators from scratch without simulating reverse diffusion trajectories or relying on a pretrained model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251063/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a random noise vector z ~ N(0, I) (optionally concatenated with a class label for conditional ImageNet generation) and produces a single 32×32 or 64×64 RGB image in one forward pass (NFE = 1). The score model and discriminator operate on noisy versions of both real and generated samples at multiple noise levels σ_t drawn from a variance-exploding schedule, with the score network additionally conditioned on a continuous α ∈ [0,1] via a Fourier embedding to distinguish which mixture component to score.

## Method

SMT minimizes a weighted sum of α-skew Jensen-Shannon divergences between the real and fake distributions at multiple noise levels. The key insight is that the generator gradient for each α-JSD reduces to the difference between the score at α=0 (pure fake score) and the score of the α-mixture distribution, both evaluated on generated samples. An amortized score network s_ψ(x_t; α, t) is trained via denoising score matching on a mixture of real and fake noisy samples, so its minimizer provably equals the true mixture score. For distillation (SMD), the pretrained diffusion model supplies the real score, and the mixture score is reparameterized as a convex combination of the real and fake scores weighted by a learned log-density-ratio discriminator, eliminating the need for a separate α-conditioned score network. A lightweight GAN regularizer based on the non-saturating α-JSD loss, built on top of the score network's bottleneck features, accelerates early convergence without destabilizing training.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 (class-conditional), SMT trained from scratch achieves FID 3.23 with 296M parameters and 1 NFE, outperforming iCT (4.02) and ECT (5.51) while matching iCT-deep (3.25) with half the parameters. In the distillation setting, SMD achieves FID 1.48, outperforming DMD2 w/GAN (1.51), SiD (1.52), and MultiStep-CD (3.20) without requiring expensive ODE simulation or extended finetuning. On CIFAR-10 (unconditional), SMT achieves FID 3.13 from scratch and SMD achieves FID 2.22 via distillation, both competitive with state-of-the-art one-step methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 64×64 from scratch, SMT (FID 3.23) outperforms consistency training CT (13.0), iCT (4.02), and ECT (5.51), and matches iCT-deep (3.25) while using only 296M vs. 592M parameters. In the distillation setting, SMD (FID 1.48) beats DMD2 w/GAN (1.51), SiD (1.52), MultiStep-CD (3.20), and CTM (1.92) without requiring ODE simulation, expensive regularizers, or extended finetuning stages. On CIFAR-10, SMT (3.13) is competitive but trails iCT-deep (2.51) which uses 112M parameters; SMD (2.22) outperforms DMD2 w/GAN (2.43) and Diff-Instruct (4.53) but falls short of SiD (1.92). The paper emphasizes that SMT/SMD achieve these results with a single 200k-step training run (batch size 40), whereas DMD2's best result (1.23) requires an additional 400k-step GAN finetuning stage and DMD's regularizer requires simulating thousands of reverse diffusion steps per minibatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMT/SMD unifies ideas from three distinct lineages: GANs (one-step generation via divergence minimization), diffusion models (stable training through multi-noise-level denoising score matching), and consistency models (training from scratch without a pretrained teacher). The key departure is replacing the ODE-trajectory simulation of consistency models and the reverse-KLD-only objective of DMD/DMD2 with a family of α-skew Jensen-Shannon divergences, whose gradients are computed by estimating the score of a real-fake mixture distribution via DSM. The gradient structure generalizes the DMD update (recovered at α=1), while the multi-α averaging and the amortized score network with α-embedding are novel architectural and objective-level contributions. The GAN regularizer is adapted from DMD2 but reparameterized through the α-JSD discriminator, and the explicit score decomposition (Proposition A.2) provides a cleaner distillation pathway that avoids the expensive ODE-simulation regularizer of DMD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a conceptually simpler and more stable recipe for one-step generation: no probability-flow ODE simulation, no mandatory pretrained teacher, and no sensitivity to noise-schedule or distance-function choices that plague consistency training. The from-scratch result (FID 3.23 on ImageNet 64×64 in 200k steps vs. 800k for iCT) suggests a meaningful compute efficiency gain, and the distillation result (FID 1.48) matches or slightly exceeds DMD2 without its 400k-step GAN finetuning stage. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (256×256, 512×512), whether few-step generation (2–4 NFE) can be supported as with consistency models, and whether the multi-α divergence framework extends to other modalities (audio, video) or conditional settings beyond class labels.
