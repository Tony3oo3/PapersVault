## Problem

Traditional imitation learning methods replicate only the specific strategies shown in expert demonstrations, producing a single policy that lacks behavioral diversity and robustness in stochastic or unseen scenarios. Combining Quality Diversity (QD) optimization with gradient-based policy optimization (e.g., PPO) is particularly challenging because gradient methods converge to high-reward local optima while ignoring behavioral diversity, and this problem is exacerbated in QD-IRL where the reward inferred from limited demonstrations is even more localized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of diverse expert demonstrations (4 episodes per environment, selected from top-500 elites to maximize behavioral spread) and the environment dynamics (MuJoCo robot locomotion: HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Humanoid, Hopper, Ant). It produces a MAP-Elites policy archive on a 50×50 grid in the behavioral measure space (e.g., proportion of time each leg contacts the ground), where each occupied cell stores the highest-fitness policy for that behavioral region. Archive quality is evaluated via QD-Score, coverage, best reward, and average reward computed with the true environment reward.

## Method

Extrinsic Behavioral Curiosity (EBC) adds an episode-level reward bonus r_EBC = q·𝕀(mⁱ ∈ A_e) to every timestep of an episode whose behavioral measure mⁱ falls in an empty cell A_e of the current QD archive, with q=2 as the weight. This bonus is integrated into three adversarial IRL backbones (GAIL, VAIL, DiffAIL) whose discriminators provide the imitation reward, while the QD-RL backbone PPGA (using VPPO for gradient approximation and xNES for evolutionary search) optimizes the combined objective. Lemma 3.1 provides a probabilistic guarantee that PPO with the indicator reward monotonically increases the likelihood of generating measures in unoccupied archive regions. The EBC bonus synergizes with PPGA's xNES emitter by making archive improvement higher when new cells are filled versus replacing existing elites, thereby steering the search distribution toward under-explored behavioral regions. Once a region is occupied, its bonus drops to zero, automatically shifting emphasis from exploration to exploitation.

## Results

EBC improves QD-Score of GAIL, VAIL, and DiffAIL by 38%, 32%, and 71% in Walker2d, and by 185%, 42%, and 150% in Humanoid, respectively. Coverage of all EBC-enhanced methods consistently reaches near 100%, substantially outperforming both their non-EBC counterparts and PPGA with true reward (which covers only 62% of cells in Humanoid). In Humanoid, VAIL-EBC and DiffAIL-EBC match or surpass the PPGA expert (true reward) QD-Score by approximately 20%, demonstrating that imitation-based QD-IRL with EBC can exceed expert performance. Statistical testing (Tukey HSD) confirms 5 of 9 EBC effects are significant at p ≤ 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPGA with true reward (the expert baseline), EBC variants trade some QD-Score in HalfCheetah and Walker2d for dramatically higher coverage (near 100% vs. 62–95%), and in Humanoid, DiffAIL-EBC (8.92×10⁶) and VAIL-EBC (7.26×10⁶) match or exceed the expert's 7.38×10⁶ QD-Score. Against their non-EBC IRL counterparts, EBC consistently wins: GAIL-EBC improves QD-Score by 30–185%, VAIL-EBC by 4–42%, and DiffAIL-EBC by 71–150% across environments, with the largest gains in the hardest Humanoid task. Against the three external IRL baselines (GIRIL, AIRL, Max-Ent IRL), all EBC variants dominate in QD-Score across all three environments; for example, in Humanoid, GAIL-EBC (5.31×10⁶) outperforms AIRL (2.31×10⁶) by 130% and Max-Ent IRL (1.82×10⁶) by 192%. The one exception is HalfCheetah QD-Score, where AIRL (3.11×10⁶) slightly exceeds GAIL-EBC (2.64×10⁶), though GAIL-EBC still achieves higher coverage (98% vs. 84%). Overall, EBC provides the most consistent and largest improvements in the high-dimensional Humanoid environment, where behavioral diversity is most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Quality Diversity (QD) optimization and Imitation Learning (IL), building directly on PPGA (Batra et al., 2023) as the QD-RL backbone and on adversarial IRL methods (GAIL, VAIL, DiffAIL) as the reward-inference front-end. The key conceptual departure from prior QD-RL work is the introduction of Extrinsic Behavioral Curiosity (EBC), which differs from intrinsic curiosity modules (e.g., ICM) by operating at the behavior level via an external QD archive rather than at the state level, and from the closely related single-step archive bonus of Yu et al. (2025) by using an episode-level bonus aligned with episode-based measure computation. The paper also positions itself against multi-domain IL and skill-discovery methods, arguing that its goal is to build a flat archive of diverse, high-performing policies rather than hierarchical or context-conditioned policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EBC provides a simple, plug-and-play mechanism that can enhance any IRL method in a QD setting, and the finding that adding EBC to the true reward function also substantially improves PPGA (coverage from ~60% to ~100%) suggests that standard reward functions are inherently suboptimal for behavioral exploration in QD-RL. Limitations include evaluation restricted to MuJoCo locomotion tasks with only three seeds, the acknowledged naive uniform propagation of the episode-level bonus to every timestep, and the fact that the 'expert' baseline is itself PPGA with true reward rather than a truly optimal policy. Open questions include whether EBC generalizes to real-world datasets (e.g., human motion capture), whether more principled credit assignment for the episode-level bonus would further improve performance, and whether the approach extends beyond locomotion to manipulation or navigation tasks.
