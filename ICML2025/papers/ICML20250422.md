## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for time series forecasting operate independently on each target series and cannot exploit dependencies among correlated time series when constructing prediction intervals. This oversight leads to unnecessarily wide intervals and missed opportunities to reduce uncertainty using spatiotemporal structure. The paper addresses this gap by introducing the first CP method that leverages graph deep learning operators to quantify uncertainty across a collection of correlated time series.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250422/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

COREL is a post-hoc method that consumes a short window of recent prediction residuals (from any pre-trained point predictor), optional exogenous covariates, and a learned sparse K-NN adjacency matrix as input. It outputs a prediction interval [x̂ + q̂^{α/2}, x̂ + q̂^{1−α/2}] for each time series at each target step, where the quantile estimates are produced by a network trained to predict 39 equally spaced quantiles of the residual distribution. The graph structure is not given a priori but is learned end-to-end from the residuals themselves, and only a short recent window (not the full history) is fed to the model at each step.

## Method

COREL implements a hybrid global-local spatiotemporal graph neural network (RelQP) that predicts quantiles of the prediction-residual distribution: a GRU encoder processes each node's recent residuals, two message-passing GNN layers propagate information across a learned sparse graph, and an MLP readout maps node representations to quantile estimates via the pinball loss. The graph topology is learned end-to-end through a probabilistic K-NN sampling module (Gumbel-TopK with straight-through gradients), so the relational structure is discovered from data rather than specified externally. Most parameters are shared globally across all time series, while per-node embeddings provide local adaptivity; at test time, only these embeddings are fine-tuned on the most recent observations to handle non-stationarity without retraining the full model. A theoretical bound (Prop. 3.1) links the coverage gap to the total variation distance between the learned and true residual distributions, and the approach achieves state-of-the-art Winkler scores on METR-LA, AQI, and CER-E benchmarks.

## Results

On the METR-LA traffic dataset with an RNN base predictor, COREL achieves a Winkler score of 23.78±0.20, outperforming the next-best method HopCPT (25.63±0.19) by ~7% and standard SCP (40.80) by ~42%. On the CER-E energy dataset, COREL reaches a Winkler score of 3.71±0.04 (vs. 3.87 for HopCPT and 5.69 for SCP), with the narrowest PI-Width of 1.83±0.03. On the AQI air-quality dataset, COREL achieves a Winkler score of 107.67±0.94, reducing the interval width to 68.13±1.30 compared to 118.06 for SCP. In the controlled GPVAR experiment, COREL with a learned graph matches the performance of using the ground-truth graph (PI-Width 1.32 vs. 1.33) while achieving near-optimal coverage (ΔCov ≈ 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COREL achieves the lowest Winkler score across all three real-world datasets with an RNN base predictor, outperforming the strongest competitor HopCPT by 7% on METR-LA (23.78 vs. 25.63), 4% on CER-E (3.71 vs. 3.87), and 19% on AQI (107.67 vs. 133.24). Against standard SCP, COREL reduces the Winkler score by 42% on METR-LA, 35% on CER-E, and 28% on AQI, primarily through substantially narrower prediction intervals (e.g., PI-Width 14.38 vs. 20.69 on METR-LA). SPCI and SeqCP, while achieving good coverage, produce much wider intervals and higher Winkler scores (e.g., 37.95 and 50.12 respectively on METR-LA vs. 23.78 for COREL). The ablation CORNN (same architecture without message-passing or node embeddings) is competitive but consistently loses to COREL on Winkler score, confirming the value of the relational components. The main exception is when the base predictor is itself an STGNN with a pre-defined graph, where the gap between COREL and SCP narrows because the base model already captures spatiotemporal dependencies, leaving less residual structure to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

COREL sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: conformal prediction for time series (SCP, NexCP, SeqCP, SPCI, HopCPT), graph deep learning for spatiotemporal forecasting (STGNNs), and quantile regression networks. The key departure is that all prior CP methods for time series construct prediction intervals independently per series, ignoring inter-series dependencies; COREL is the first to embed GDL message-passing operators within the CP calibration pipeline to condition residual quantile estimates on neighboring series. The hybrid global-local architecture (shared STGNN + per-node embeddings) adapts the Smyl-style design from point forecasting to the post-hoc uncertainty quantification setting, and the probabilistic graph structure learning module (Gumbel-TopK over K-NN graphs) is borrowed from the graph learning literature but applied here to the residual space rather than the signal space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: practitioners with collections of correlated time series (traffic, energy, air quality) have had no principled post-hoc method that exploits inter-series structure when building prediction intervals, and COREL provides one with a clean total-variation coverage bound. However, several limitations temper the impact: the method only produces marginal (per-node) PIs rather than joint distributions, the adaptation mechanism is heuristic (periodic embedding fine-tuning) without formal guarantees under non-stationarity, and the sparsity assumption on the relational graph may not hold in all domains. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous time series, providing coverage guarantees under explicit non-stationarity models, and scaling to very large collections where the O(WN+E) forward pass becomes costly.
