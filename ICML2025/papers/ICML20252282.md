## Problem

Existing RNN layers such as Mamba achieve linear complexity but their fixed-size hidden states limit expressiveness in long context: perplexity plateaus after 16k tokens while Transformers continue improving. The paper asks whether RNN hidden states can be made substantially more expressive without sacrificing linear per-token cost, by replacing handcrafted update rules with a learned compression mechanism.

## Input / Output

The TTT layer consumes a sequence of text tokens x_1,...,x_T and produces output representations z_1,...,z_T, where each z_t is the prediction of the hidden-state model f evaluated on a test-view projection θ_Q x_t. Input tokens are routed through three learnable low-rank projections: a training view θ_K x_t (corrupted input for the inner-loop self-supervised task), a label view θ_V x_t (reconstruction target), and a test view θ_Q x_t (query for the output rule). The hidden state W_t is a full neural network (square linear model or two-layer MLP with GELU) whose weights are updated at every timestep via gradient descent, making the forward pass inherently sequential in the primal form but parallelizable within mini-batches.

## Method

The core idea is to make the RNN hidden state a machine learning model f and the update rule a step of self-supervised learning: W_t = W_{t-1} − η∇ℓ(W_{t-1}; x_t), where ℓ is a reconstruction loss with learnable views. The self-supervised task is itself learned end-to-end in the outer loop (bi-level optimization), so the inner-loop model discovers what to compress from context rather than relying on handcrafted heuristics. For hardware efficiency, the paper introduces mini-batch gradient descent (b=16 tokens per step) to parallelize gradient computations, and a dual form that replaces expensive per-token outer products with batched matrix multiplications, achieving >5× wall-clock speedup on TPUs. The paper proves two theoretical equivalences: TTT-Linear with batch GD reduces to linear attention, and TTT with a Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator reduces to self-attention, positioning TTT as a unifying framework that spans both RNN and attention mechanisms.

## Results

On the Pile at 8k context, TTT-Linear (Mamba backbone) and TTT-MLP (Mamba backbone) achieve significantly lower perplexity than Mamba across all model sizes (125M–1.3B), while at 2k context all three methods are roughly comparable. On Books3 at 32k context, both TTT variants continue to reduce perplexity as context grows, whereas Mamba plateaus after 16k tokens. At the 125M scale, TTT-Linear reaches a perplexity of 11.09 (vs. 15.91 for vanilla linear attention), and on a v5e-256 TPU pod, TTT-Linear trains at 0.27s/iteration vs. 0.30s for the Transformer baseline at 2k context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba (the primary RNN baseline), TTT-Linear and TTT-MLP tie at 2k context on the Pile but clearly outperform it at 8k and 32k context, with the gap widening as context length increases; Mamba's per-token perplexity plateaus after 16k while TTT methods keep improving. Against the Transformer++ baseline (RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm), TTT methods are more FLOP-efficient: the Transformer achieves comparable or slightly better raw perplexity at every model size, but its quadratic attention cost makes its scaling curve non-competitive in FLOPs, especially beyond 8k context. Against linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020), TTT-Linear is shown to be a strict superset: starting from the proven equivalence (perplexity 15.23 at 125M), adding mini-batch GD, learnable W₀, LN+residual, learnable η, and the Mamba backbone progressively reduces perplexity to 11.09, with mini-batch GD contributing the single largest gain (−1.70). TTT-MLP, which uses a two-layer MLP as the hidden state, is slightly worse than TTT-Linear at short context under matched FLOPs but pulls ahead at 32k context, suggesting greater long-context potential at the cost of higher wall-clock latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the fast-weight-programmer lineage (linear attention, DeltaNet) by allowing the RNN hidden state to be an arbitrary neural network rather than a fixed-size matrix, with the self-supervised compression task learned end-to-end in an outer loop. Theoretically, it shows TTT-Linear with batch gradient descent is exactly linear attention, and TTT with a Nadaraya-Watson kernel recovers self-attention, placing both under a single framework. Empirically, it departs from prior RNN work (Mamba, Griffin) by demonstrating that the bottleneck for long-context RNNs is not the recurrence itself but the expressiveness of the compression heuristic, and that self-supervised learning at test time is a principled solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, practical recipe for RNN layers that match Transformer long-context perplexity trends (continuing to improve past 16k tokens where Mamba plateaus) while retaining linear complexity, which is a significant step toward making linear-time sequence modeling viable for long documents. However, wall-clock time remains a substantial limitation—TTT-MLP in particular is much slower than Mamba in practice—and the evaluation is capped at 1.3B parameters and 32k context, leaving open whether the advantage persists at the scales where quadratic attention is truly prohibitive. The unifying theoretical lens (linear attention = TTT-Linear, self-attention = TTT with kernel) is likely to shape how the community thinks about the design space of sequence modeling layers going forward.
