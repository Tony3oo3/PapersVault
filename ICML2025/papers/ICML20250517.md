## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods test robustness only against non-adaptive attackers who lack knowledge of the watermarking algorithms, relying on obscurity rather than genuine security. This paper asks what happens when an attacker knows the watermarking algorithms (KEYGEN, EMBED, VERIFY) but not the secret key, and can adaptively optimize a paraphraser against the specific watermark. The authors show that even resource-constrained attackers (no-box, offline, limited compute) can evade all surveyed watermarks at over 96% evasion rate with negligible quality degradation, challenging the robustness claims of current methods.

## Input / Output

The attacker takes as input watermarked text generated by a surrogate open-weight model (e.g., Llama2-7b) using a surrogate watermarking key derived from known algorithms and random seeds, since the attacker has no access to the provider's model or secret key. The output is paraphrased text produced by a fine-tuned open-weight paraphraser (0.5b–7b parameters) that evades watermark detection while preserving text quality. The attacker operates under strict constraints: no access to the provider's LLM (no-box), no access to the VERIFY function (offline), and no knowledge of the provider's random seeds or model parameters, requiring optimization over many random surrogate keys to handle this uncertainty.

## Method

The paper formulates watermark robustness as an objective function (Equation 2) that maximizes the expected evasion rate plus text quality over random seeds, messages, and queries, using a surrogate model to generate surrogate watermarking keys. To optimize this non-differentiable objective over discrete text, the authors curate a preference dataset via best-of-N rejection sampling (Algorithm 1): for each watermarked sample, N paraphrases are generated and partitioned into positive (evasive, high-quality) and negative (watermark-retaining) examples, forming triplets for DPO training. The paraphraser is then fine-tuned with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using LoRA (rank 32, alpha 16) for one epoch at 16-bit precision, requiring less than 7 GPU hours total. A key design choice is that the attacker optimizes against a surrogate model different from the provider's, and the resulting attacks remain Pareto-optimal even when transferred to unseen watermarking methods in the non-adaptive setting.

## Results

Against a Llama3.1-70b target model using the Exp watermark, the adaptively tuned Qwen2.5-7b paraphraser achieves a 97.3% evasion rate at 0.846 LLM-Judge quality, while the non-adaptive variant (trained on Inverse) reaches 97.6% evasion at 0.845 quality. Against a Llama2-13b target, all adaptive attacks achieve ≥96.6% evasion and non-adaptive attacks achieve ≥94.3%, with the best adaptive attack (trained on Exp) reaching 97.0% evasion at 0.901 quality. The entire attack pipeline (dataset curation + LoRA fine-tuning) requires less than 7 GPU hours and costs under $10 USD, using models up to 46× smaller than the provider's model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's adaptively tuned 7b paraphrasers dominate all surveyed non-adaptive baselines: simple perturbations (Swap, Synonym, Contraction from Piet et al. 2023; HELM from Bommasani et al. 2023) achieve less than 20% evasion, while the 11b DIPPER Seq2Seq model (Krishna et al. 2023) and vanilla Llama2-7b reach only 61.8%. ChatGPT-3.5 achieves 86.1% evasion but at lower quality (0.837) than the paper's optimized attacks (0.845–0.853). Against all four surveyed watermarks (Exp, Dist-Shift, Binary, Inverse), adaptive attacks achieve ≥96.6% evasion, and the attacks transfer effectively to unseen watermarks in the non-adaptive setting (≥94.3%). In the appendix, the method also evades three additional watermarks (SynthID, Unigram, SIR), where GPT-4o is on the Pareto front only against SIR and KGW due to its high quality but sub-90% evasion, while the paper's attacks are the sole Pareto-optimal methods against SynthID, Exp, and Unigram.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) as the core training algorithm, repurposing it from alignment to adversarial watermark evasion. It departs from prior watermark evasion work (DIPPER, handcrafted perturbations, black-box attacks by Jovanović et al. and Pang et al.) by formulating the first no-box, offline, *adaptive* attack: the attacker knows the watermarking algorithms (KEYGEN, EMBED, VERIFY) but not the secret key, and optimizes a paraphraser via preference-based fine-tuning against surrogate keys. The conceptual framing of robustness as a differentiable objective function (Eq. 2) over random seeds and surrogate models is a novel operationalization of the (ε,δ)-robustness definition, bridging the gap between the security property and a trainable loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings challenge the robustness claims of all surveyed LLM watermarking methods, showing that even resource-constrained attackers (<$10, <7 GPU hours, 46× smaller models) can achieve >96% evasion with negligible quality loss. The transferability result—that an attack trained on one watermark evades unseen watermarks—suggests a shared structural vulnerability across token-level watermarking schemes, implying that future methods must be tested against adaptive, optimizable attacks rather than only handcrafted or non-adaptive baselines. Open questions include whether adaptive defenses (e.g., adversarial training of the watermark) can close this gap, how watermarking interacts with other defenses like safety alignment, and whether results extend to non-prose domains such as code generation.
