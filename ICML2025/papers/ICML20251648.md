## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) produce adaptive, input-specific sparsity patterns but require careful tuning of nonconvex penalty hyperparameters and suffer from numerous local minima. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) offer a hyperparameter-free loss with provably fewer local minima, but their stochastic encoder forces a fixed sparsity pattern across all samples, making them unable to adapt active dimensions to varying manifold complexity. The paper asks whether a single model can combine SAE-style adaptive sparsity with VAE-style hyperparameter-free training and local-minima smoothing.

## Input / Output

The method accepts diverse inputs—RGB images (MNIST, FashionMNIST at 28×28), LLM intermediate activation vectors (d=512 from Pythia), text embeddings (d=768 from ModernBERT), and synthetic points on unions of manifolds—and produces a sparse latent code z ∈ R^κ (with κ overcomplete relative to d) plus a reconstruction. Unusually, the number of active (nonzero) latent dimensions is not fixed but adapts per sample: the encoder's variance network σ_z(x;φ) determines which dimensions carry information, and the method outputs an active-dimension count that should match the local manifold dimension. Active/inactive partitioning is determined by a variance-based threshold that minimizes the gap between mean variances above and below the split.

## Method

VAEase modifies a standard VAE by replacing the decoder input z with a gated version z̃ = (1 − σ_z(x;φ)) ⊙ z, where σ_z is the encoder's per-dimension standard deviation and ⊙ denotes the Hadamard product. For inactive dimensions (σ_z ≈ 1), the gate clamps the noisy latent value to zero, so the decoder receives a deterministic zero that cannot disrupt reconstruction even with nonzero decoder weights; for active dimensions (σ_z ≈ O(√γ)), the gate passes the informative mean through nearly unchanged. This single architectural change—requiring no additional parameters beyond a vanilla VAE—restores SAE-like adaptive sparsity while preserving the VAE's hyperparameter-free KL-regularized loss and its stochastic smoothing of suboptimal local minima. Theoretically, the paper proves that as the decoder variance γ→0, global VAEase minimizers use exactly r_i active dimensions on each manifold M_i of dimension r_i, and that in a linear-decoder setting VAEase has a unique minimum while an analogous SAE can have 2^d local minima.

## Results

On real-world datasets, VAEase achieves the best reconstruction error (RE) and average active dimensions (AD) across all four benchmarks: MNIST (RE=9.71, AD=16.2), FashionMNIST (RE=8.39, AD=12.4), LLM intermediate activations (RE=39.5, AD=22.5), and text embeddings (RE=0.187, AD=16.7). On synthetic data with known ground-truth manifold dimensions, VAEase recovers dimensions closest to truth (e.g., 5,4,4 vs. GT 4,4,4 on linear subspaces; 5,5,11,11 vs. GT 5,5,10,10 on nonlinear manifolds). On pseudo-MNIST with a known 16-dimensional manifold, VAEase estimates 14.93 active dimensions, far closer to ground truth than diffusion-based estimators (DM-NB: 105.94, DM-FLIPD: 169.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAEase outperforms all SAE variants (SAE-ℓ1, SAE-log, SAE-Tk) and the vanilla VAE on every real-world benchmark. Against SAE-Tk (the closest competitor), VAEase achieves substantially lower AD on FashionMNIST (12.4 vs. 15.0), LLM activations (22.5 vs. 30.0), and text embeddings (16.7 vs. 30.0), while also matching or beating its RE; notably SAE-Tk requires manual selection of k from a data-dependent manifold dimension estimate, whereas VAEase needs no such prior. The vanilla VAE performs particularly poorly on LLM activations (AD=87.9 vs. VAEase's 22.5) and text embeddings (AD=90.0 vs. 16.7), confirming its inability to produce adaptive sparsity. On the pseudo-MNIST manifold dimension estimation task, VAEase (14.93) is dramatically more accurate than both diffusion-based post-processing methods: DM-NB (Stanczuk et al., 2024) estimates 105.94 and DM-FLIPD (Kamkari et al., 2024) estimates 169.81, both far exceeding the true upper bound of 16. On synthetic data with known ground-truth dimensions, VAEase is the only method that correctly identifies individual manifold dimensions (e.g., 5,5,11,11 vs. GT 5,5,10,10), while all baselines overestimate by 2–30 dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAEase is positioned as a minimal modification to the standard VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2014), repurposing the encoder variance network σ_z as a Hadamard-product gating mechanism to clamp inactive latent dimensions, thereby recovering the adaptive sparsity that canonical SAEs enjoy without their hyperparameter sensitivity. The conceptual lineage draws from conditional VAEs (Zheng et al., 2022), which demonstrated that decoder conditioning can produce input-adaptive sparsity, but VAEase eliminates the need for an explicit conditioning variable by leveraging σ_z itself. The local-minima-smoothing analysis extends Wipf (2023), who proved the phenomenon only for fixed-sparsity VAEs, to the substantially harder adaptive-sparsity regime. The paper explicitly departs from the decades-old SAE recipe (deterministic encoder + nonconvex ℓ₀-approximation penalty) by arguing that the stochastic VAE chassis, properly modified, subsumes SAE advantages while eliminating their tuning burden.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given the contemporary surge in SAE-based LLM interpretability work (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024), a hyperparameter-free, adaptive-sparsity alternative with provable manifold-recovery guarantees is practically valuable: it removes the need to tune λ₁, λ₂, and penalty curvature while still producing input-adaptive sparse codes. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.5) that global optima use exactly dim[Mᵢ] active dimensions on each constituent manifold is a clean result that no prior SAE or VAE formulation provides. Limitations include reliance on Lipschitz continuity assumptions, evaluation primarily on small-scale image datasets and a single LLM (Pythia), and the pseudo-MNIST diffusion comparison resting on a single GAN-generated dataset. Open questions include whether the σ_z gating mechanism scales gracefully to very large latent dimensions (κ >> 1000) and whether the theoretical manifold-recovery guarantees hold under the non-convex loss landscapes encountered in practice with deep ReLU networks.
