## Problem

In offline-to-online RL, existing data augmentation methods like EDIS generate synthetic data from offline datasets that still exhibits a significant distribution gap with the current online policy, limiting performance gains. Prior approaches either only augment offline data (retaining its characteristics) or require complex multi-model guidance (separate energy functions, pre-trained classifiers) that introduces substantial training overhead. The paper asks whether a simpler, unified mechanism can generate higher-quality data that better aligns with the evolving online policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two streams of state-action-reward transitions: a static offline dataset collected by a behavior policy and online transitions gathered during the fine-tuning phase. These are fed into a single conditional diffusion model where offline and online data are assigned distinct categorical labels. The output is synthetic state-action-reward transitions split into two synthetic buffers (offline-synthetic and online-synthetic), which are then reweighted at an 8:2 ratio favoring online synthetic data before being concatenated with real data in training batches at a 1:1:1 (online:offline:synthetic) ratio.

## Method

CFDG treats offline and online data as two labeled categories and trains a single denoising network (built on the Elucidated Diffusion Model) to parameterize both the conditional score ε_θ(z_λ, c) and the unconditional score ε_θ(z_λ) by randomly dropping the label with probability p_uncond during training. At sampling time, classifier-free guidance (Eq. 7) linearly combines the conditional and unconditional score estimates with a guidance weight w to steer generation toward the desired data category, eliminating the need for a separate classifier. The diffusion model is periodically retrained on the current offline and online buffers during the online phase, and a reweighting step increases the sampling weight of online synthetic data (8:2 over offline synthetic) to better support exploration aligned with the current policy. This plug-and-play augmentation is integrated into existing O2O RL algorithms (IQL, PEX, APL) by simply adding synthetic samples to the replay buffers, requiring no changes to the underlying RL loss functions.

## Results

On the D4RL Locomotion benchmark (12 tasks), CFDG improves IQL from 810 to 933 total normalized score (+15%), PEX from 890 to 1024 (+15%), and APL from 972 to 1081 (+11%). On AntMaze (4 tasks), IQL improves from 250 to 266 and PEX from 264 to 284. In the model-based comparison (base IQL), CFDG outperforms both SynthER and EDIS across all 12 Locomotion tasks, with a 15% aggregate improvement over prior model-based methods. On the Adroit benchmark, CFDG achieves an average score of 43.3 versus 25.7 (base IQL) and 26.6 (EDIS), representing over 50% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFDG is integrated into three SOTA O2O RL algorithms (IQL, PEX, APL) and consistently improves them: IQL gains +15% on Locomotion (810→933), PEX gains +15% (890→1024), and APL gains +11% (972→1081). Against the model-based data augmentation baselines, CFDG outperforms EDIS (energy-guided diffusion on offline data) on all 12 Locomotion tasks and on Adroit (43.3 vs 26.6 average), and also outperforms SynthER (standard diffusion on online data), confirming that augmenting both online and offline data simultaneously with classifier-free guidance is superior to augmenting either type alone. In terms of data quality, CFDG-generated samples have lower Jensen-Shannon divergence to the online data distribution than both EDIS-generated data and raw offline data (e.g., state divergence 0.50 vs 0.56 vs 0.69), explaining the performance gains. The only exceptions where CFDG does not improve are a few already-saturated tasks (e.g., halfcheetah-me-v2 for IQL: 95→93) and hopper-r-v2 where high variance in the base makes comparison unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classifier-free diffusion guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021), originally designed for conditional image generation, as a data augmentation mechanism in offline-to-online RL. It builds on the Elucidated Diffusion Model (Karras et al., 2022) as its implementation backbone and departs from prior O2O RL data augmentation methods—EDIS, which uses energy-guided diffusion to transform offline data toward the online policy, and SynthER, which augments only online data—by treating offline and online data as two distinct class labels within a single conditional diffusion model. The key conceptual departure is eliminating the need for separate energy functions or a pre-trained classifier, replacing them with the simpler label-conditioning mechanism of classifier-free guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFDG offers a drop-in, algorithm-agnostic data augmentation module that consistently improves multiple SOTA O2O RL methods (IQL, PEX, APL) by ~15% on D4RL benchmarks, with the practical advantage of requiring no additional classifier training or complex energy function design. The finding that augmenting both data types simultaneously outperforms augmenting either alone, and that online data augmentation is inherently more effective than offline data augmentation, provides useful guidance for future O2O RL data strategies. Limitations include evaluation restricted to low-dimensional continuous control (MuJoCo, AntMaze, Adroit) with no pixel-based or high-dimensional state experiments, and the method's reliance on a fixed synthetic buffer size and update frequency that may not transfer cleanly to tasks with very different data scales.
