## Problem

Existing probabilistic time series forecasting methods, particularly diffusion-based and flow-based generative models, suffer from rapid performance degradation and increasing computational cost as the forecasting horizon extends. The inherent nonlinearity of time series makes it difficult to model state transitions and quantify uncertainty over long horizons, while iterative generation processes cause error accumulation and inefficiency. This paper addresses the gap in Long-term Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting (LPTSF) by seeking a one-step generative framework that can handle nonlinear dynamics while explicitly modeling and refining process uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series context X ∈ R^{N×T} (N variables, T context length; T=96 for most datasets, T=36 for ILI-L) and patchifies it into n=T/s non-overlapping multivariate patches that are jointly embedded to preserve cross-variable interactions. The output is a full conditional distribution P(Y|X) over the forecasting horizon (L ∈ {96,192,336,720} for most datasets, L ∈ {24,36,48,60} for ILI-L), parameterized as an isotropic Gaussian N(μ, σ) in the original observation space, from which 100 samples are drawn to estimate CRPS and NMAE.

## Method

K²VAE is a VAE-based generative model whose encoder consists of a KoopmanNet and a KalmanNet. The KoopmanNet applies a learnable MLP measurement function ψ to project patchified tokens into a measurement space, then fits a Koopman operator K = K_loc + K_glo (where K_loc is computed via one-step eDMD with Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse and K_glo is a learnable matrix) to iterate from the first token and generate a biased linear dynamical system. The KalmanNet refines this biased system by feeding the nonlinear residual through a Transformer-based Integrator as control inputs into a Kalman filter process model with learnable state-transition (A), control (B), and observation (H) matrices; it iteratively performs predict and update steps using the Kalman gain to produce refined state vectors and covariance matrices that define the variational posterior Q(Z|X) = N(Z', P). A skip connection adds the Integrator output to the KalmanNet output, and during training the reconstruction loss drives the residual toward zero so the Integrator gradually becomes unnecessary and the KalmanNet converges to the Koopman operator. The decoder uses two MLPs to map resampled latent vectors back to the original space, yielding the likelihood P(Y|Z) = N(μ, σ), and the overall objective combines the ELBO with an L2 reconstruction loss that enforces linearization of the measurement space.

## Results

On short-term probabilistic forecasting (8 datasets), K²VAE achieves a 7.3% average CRPS reduction and 14.5% NMAE reduction over the second-best baseline CSDI, with best CRPS values of 0.009 (Exchange-S), 0.039 (Solar-S), and 0.122 (ETTm2-S). On long-term forecasting at L=720 (9 datasets), K²VAE surpasses the best baseline PatchTST by 20.9% in CRPS and 19.9% in NMAE, achieving CRPS of 0.057 on Electricity-L, 0.069 on Exchange-L, and 0.142 on ILI-L. K²VAE also achieves the lowest inference time and GPU memory among all compared methods, confirming its one-step generation efficiency advantage over diffusion- and flow-based models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

K²VAE achieves the best CRPS and NMAE on all 9 long-term datasets (L=720), outperforming every baseline. Against the strongest point-forecasting baselines, K²VAE beats PatchTST by 20.9% CRPS and 19.9% NMAE on average, and Koopa (the closest Koopman-based competitor) by a similar margin; for example, on Electricity-L K²VAE reaches CRPS 0.057 vs. Koopa's 0.113 and PatchTST's 0.127. Among generative baselines, K²VAE dominates diffusion-based methods (TimeGrad, TSDiff, CSDI) and flow-based methods (GRU NVP, GRU MAF, Trans MAF) by large margins—on ETTh2-L at L=720, K²VAE achieves CRPS 0.280 while GRU MAF degrades to 0.990 and TimeGrad to 0.445. On short-term tasks, K²VAE edges out CSDI (the best generative baseline) by 7.3% CRPS and 14.5% NMAE on average, and matches or beats point-forecasting models like FITS and PatchTST on most datasets. Notably, K²VAE shows its largest relative gains on non-stationary datasets (Exchange-S/L) and at longer horizons, where diffusion- and flow-based baselines suffer severe error accumulation while K²VAE's KalmanNet-based refinement keeps uncertainty well-calibrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three classical tools—Koopman operator theory for nonlinear-to-linear system transformation, Kalman filtering for recursive state estimation with uncertainty, and the VAE framework for probabilistic generation—into a unified architecture for time series forecasting. It extends Koopa (Liu et al., 2023), which applied Koopman theory to deterministic long-term forecasting, into the probabilistic setting by coupling the KoopmanNet with a KalmanNet that models process uncertainty as the variational posterior. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based (TimeGrad, TSDiff, CSDI) and flow-based (GRU NVP, Trans MAF) generative paradigms by adopting a one-step VAE generation strategy, arguing that the linear dynamical system structure makes iterative probabilistic transition paths unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing probabilistic forecasting models degrade sharply at long horizons (L=720), sometimes performing worse than simple point-forecasting models, while diffusion/flow models become computationally prohibitive. The one-step generation paradigm with explicit uncertainty modeling via Kalman gain offers a compelling efficiency-accuracy tradeoff that could influence how probabilistic forecasting architectures are designed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly non-stationary or regime-switching dynamics beyond the tested benchmarks, and the authors themselves point toward foundation probabilistic forecasting models as a future direction. A limitation is that all experiments use standard ProbTS benchmarks with fixed context lengths, leaving open whether the Koopman linearization assumption holds under distributional shift.
