## Problem

Existing generalized α-investing (GAI) algorithms for online FDR control require independence or positive regression dependence on a subset (PRDS) among p-values, conditions that are rarely satisfied in real-world streaming data with complex temporal correlations. The e-LOND algorithm extends online FDR control to arbitrary dependence via e-values but is conservative because testing levels are derived from prespecified descending sequences rather than dynamically allocated. The paper asks whether a GAI-like dynamic allocation framework can be built on e-values to achieve provable FDR control under general dependence while recovering the power lost by e-LOND.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes an infinite (or finite) stream of non-negative e-values e_t satisfying conditional validity E[e_t | F_{t-1}] ≤ 1 under the null, along with a user-specified target FDR level α and allocation parameters (ω₁ ∈ (0,1), φ > 0, ψ > 0). At each time t, it outputs a binary rejection decision δ_t = 1{e_t ≥ 1/α_t} where the testing level α_t must be F_{t-1}-measurable (predictable). The framework also generalizes to conditionally super-uniform p-values, in which case the decision becomes δ_t = 1{p_t ≤ α_t} and the e-SAFFRON null-proportion indicator flips from 1{e_j < 1/λ} to 1{p_j > λ}.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new oracle FDP estimator FDP*_e(t) = Σ_{j∈H₀(t)} α_j/(R_{j-1}+1) whose denominator uses the (j−1)-measurable lower bound R_{j-1}+1 for the unobservable total rejections R_t, enabling FDR control under arbitrary dependence via Theorem 3.1. Unlike GAI, where rejections earn additional α-wealth, the e-GAI framework adopts a risk aversion investing (RAI) strategy: the allocation coefficient ω_t is updated by increasing it after non-rejections (encouraging further testing) and decreasing it after rejections (penalizing the risk of false discoveries), formalized as ω_{t+1} = ω_t + ω₁φ^{t-R_t}(1-δ_t) − ω₁ψ^{R_t}δ_t. Two algorithms are instantiated: e-LORD uses the conservative overestimate Σ α_j/(R_{j-1}+1) ≤ α, while e-SAFFRON adaptively weights terms by 1{e_j < 1/λ}/(1−λ) to estimate the null proportion, yielding higher power when alternatives are non-negligible. The framework further extends to decaying-memory FDR (mem-FDR) via mem-e-LORD and mem-e-SAFFRON to address α-death in long-term testing, and to conditionally super-uniform p-values (pL-RAI, pS-RAI) with identical theoretical guarantees.

## Results

On the NYC Taxi anomaly detection task (α=0.1), e-LORD achieves FDP 0.085 with 47 discoveries and e-SAFFRON achieves FDP 0.087 with 46 discoveries, both substantially outperforming e-LOND (FDP 0.061, 33 discoveries). In the Gaussian simulation (T=500, ρ=0.5, μ_c=3), e-LORD with ω₁=1/T reaches 70.0% power versus e-LOND's 30.9%, while pL-RAI and pS-RAI outperform LORD++ and SupLORD in power. On the p-value NYC Taxi comparison (α=0.2), pL-RAI and pS-RAI maintain FDP below target (0.197, 0.195) while LORD++ (0.261) and SAFFRON (0.361) exceed it, and SAFFRON inflates FDR heavily in the simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against e-LOND (Xu & Ramdas, 2024), the primary e-value baseline, e-LORD and e-SAFFRON achieve dramatically higher power: 47 and 46 discoveries versus 33 on NYC Taxi, and 70.0% versus 30.9% power in the Gaussian simulation (T=500), while all three maintain valid FDR control. Against LORD++ (Ramdas et al., 2017), pL-RAI and pS-RAI achieve higher power in simulation and, critically, LORD++ has no theoretical FDR guarantee under the complex dependence structures tested; on NYC Taxi, LORD++ exceeds the target FDP (0.261 vs α=0.2) while pL-RAI stays below (0.197). Against SAFFRON (Ramdas et al., 2018), the gap is stark: SAFFRON heavily inflates FDR (0.361 on NYC Taxi, well above α=0.2, and visibly above target in simulation), whereas pS-RAI maintains control at 0.195. Against SupLORD (Xu & Ramdas, 2022), pL-RAI and pS-RAI achieve higher power in simulation, and SupLORD slightly exceeds the target on NYC Taxi (0.217 vs α=0.2) while pL-RAI and pS-RAI remain below it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized α-investing (GAI) framework—pioneered by Foster & Stine (2008) and developed through LORD++ (Ramdas et al., 2017) and SAFFRON (Ramdas et al., 2018)—to the e-value setting, where prior work (Xu & Ramdas, 2024, e-LOND) achieved online FDR control under arbitrary dependence but relied on prespecified descent sequences that sacrifice power. The key conceptual departure is the risk aversion investing (RAI) strategy: unlike GAI where rejections generate additional α-wealth, e-GAI treats each rejection as consuming wealth, reflecting the inability to safely 'earn' budget under unknown dependence. The paper also shows e-LOND is a special case of e-LORD, unifying the two approaches within a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The e-GAI framework provides the first provable online FDR control under arbitrary dependence with dynamic (data-driven) testing level allocation, addressing a critical gap: GAI methods like LORD++ and SAFFRON have no theoretical guarantee under the complex dependence structures common in practice (e.g., stock markets, sensor networks). Open questions include how to exploit known dependence structure to tighten the FDP_e*(t) denominator, and how to better estimate null proportions in the adaptive (SAFFRON-like) variant. The practical impact is demonstrated on NYC taxi anomaly detection and Nasdaq bubble dating, where e-LORD/e-SAFFRON identify substantially more true anomalies than e-LOND while maintaining FDR control.
