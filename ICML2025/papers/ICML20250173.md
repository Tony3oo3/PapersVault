## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods target structured 1D/2D data (images, graphs) and fail on unstructured 3D point clouds due to two unique challenges: random rotations of the same object produce different coordinate representations that existing DD cannot reconcile, and point clouds have flexible resolutions unlike fixed-size image tensors. The paper theoretically proves that random rotations weaken the principal components of real data representations, causing synthetic data to degenerate and model performance to drop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes uniformly sampled scalar noise in [0,1] and a one-hot class label, producing 3D point coordinates via a SIREN-based implicit neural representation. The number of output points equals the number of noise samples, enabling resolution-agnostic generation: training can use 256 or 512 points while evaluation generates 1024 points from the same generator. For part segmentation, noise is pre-partitioned into value ranges (e.g., (0, 0.45) → fuselage) so each point carries a part label before generation.

## Method

DD3D combines two plug-and-play components optimized jointly via bi-level gradient matching. First, a point cloud rotator aligns each cloud to a canonical orientation using PCA eigenvectors, then resolves the 8-fold sign ambiguity by learning a sign-equivariant reflection matrix through sinusoidal feature encoding, average pooling, and a linear sign decoder. Second, a SIREN-based point-wise generator with FiLM-style conditional modulation (element-wise multiplication of label-conditioned MLP outputs onto each SIREN layer) maps noise to 3D coordinates at arbitrary resolution. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 3.1–3.2) shows that gradient matching reduces to variance preservation and that random rotations strictly reduce the maximum singular value of data representations, motivating the rotation-invariant alignment. Training uses low-resolution point clouds to supervise the generator, then generates high-resolution data for evaluation, reducing memory by ~4× compared to fixed-tensor DD.

## Results

On shape classification, DD3D achieves the best accuracy across all three datasets and both aligned/rotated settings. At CPC=50, DD3D reaches 61.96% on ScanObjectNN (vs. 57.52% for GM), 83.91% on ModelNet40 (vs. 83.02% for DM), and 40.61% on MVPNet100 (vs. 37.41% for GM), closing the gap to full-dataset baselines (66.96%, 88.05%, 55.63%) more effectively than any other method. On part segmentation (ShapeNet), DD3D with CPC=1 achieves 73.06% OA and 60.27% Instance IoU, outperforming GM (65.56% OA) by 7.5 points and reaching 90% of the full-dataset performance (90.04% OA) using only 1% of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DD3D consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the coreset-based methods (Herding and K-Center), DD3D leads by 4–9 points at CPC=50 on aligned data (e.g., 61.96% vs. 54.22% K-Center on ScanObjectNN) and by even larger margins on rotated data where coreset selection degrades most severely. Among distillation-based baselines, DD3D beats Gradient Matching (GM) by 2–6 points (e.g., 83.91% vs. 81.74% on ModelNet40 Aligned CPC=50; 40.61% vs. 37.41% on MVPNet100 CPC=50) and Distribution Matching (DM) by 1–4 points. Trajectory Matching (TM) is the weakest distillation baseline and runs out of memory at CPC=50 on two of three datasets, while DD3D remains stable. On part segmentation, DD3D surpasses GM by 7.5 points in OA at CPC=1 (73.06% vs. 65.56%) and by 2.1 points at CPC=10 (80.37% vs. 78.32%), demonstrating that the point-wise generator and rotator are particularly beneficial for fine-grained tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset distillation (DD) paradigm, well-established for images, videos, and graphs, to the 3D point cloud domain. It builds on SIREN (implicit neural representations with periodic activations) as the generative backbone and on gradient matching as the distillation objective, but introduces two key departures: a plug-and-play rotator that resolves PCA's sign ambiguity to enforce rotation-invariant alignment, and a point-wise INR generator that decouples training resolution from evaluation resolution. The theoretical analysis connecting gradient matching to variance preservation and showing that random rotations strictly reduce the maximum singular value of learned representations provides a principled justification for the rotator component, which prior DD methods entirely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DD3D is the first framework to address both the orientation and resolution challenges specific to 3D point cloud distillation, and its plug-and-play rotator is applicable to any rotation-sensitive point cloud model beyond the distillation setting. The ability to train at 256 points and evaluate at 1024 points with comparable accuracy offers a practical ~4× memory reduction that matters for resource-constrained and edge-computing scenarios. However, the paper acknowledges that scene-level tasks (detection, segmentation of large scenes) remain out of reach due to foreground-background imbalance and the need to learn continuous bounding box coordinates, and the synthetic data still carries bias from the distillation backbone, limiting cross-architecture transfer. A natural next step the authors identify is initializing the generator with real data rather than training from scratch.
