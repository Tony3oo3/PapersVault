## Problem

Recent state-of-the-art RL algorithms (MEME, Dreamer-v3, Agent57) require GPU clusters, TPUs, or weeks of walltime to train a single agent, making them inaccessible to smaller research labs and hobbyists. Unlike NLP or vision where foundation models can be fine-tuned, RL agents must be trained from scratch for each environment, so computational accessibility is a critical bottleneck. The paper asks whether a non-recurrent, non-distributed algorithm can match or exceed Rainbow DQN's performance while remaining trainable on a single desktop PC within a practical walltime.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 4 stacked grayscale frames at 84×84 resolution (Atari) or 140×114 (Wii games) and outputs a discrete action from the environment's action set. For Atari, 64 parallel vectorized environments feed a single gradient update with batch size 256 per step; for Wii games this drops to 4 parallel environments due to emulator overhead. The network produces a full return distribution via IQN (8 tau samples, 64 cosine embeddings) rather than a scalar Q-value, and the policy is derived as a softmax over Q-values with temperature τ=0.03 for the Munchausen update.

## Method

BTR extends Rainbow DQN with six additional components: (1) an Impala residual CNN architecture scaled to width×2, (2) a 6×6 adaptive maxpooling layer after the convolutional blocks to reduce parameters by 77% and support variable input resolutions, (3) spectral normalization on all convolutional residual layers to control Lipschitz constants, (4) Implicit Quantile Networks replacing C51 to remove the fixed range limit on Q-values, (5) Munchausen RL replacing Double DQN by adding a scaled-log policy term to the bootstrapping target (converting DQN to Soft-DQN), and (6) vectorized environments (64 parallel, batch 256, replay ratio 1/64) to reduce walltime. Key hyperparameter choices include γ=0.997 (following MuZero Reanalyse), learning rate 1×10⁻⁴, target network updates every 500 gradient steps, and combined ε-greedy + Noisy Networks exploration (ε disabled after 100M frames). The resulting 2.9M-parameter network trains 200M Atari frames in under 12 hours on an RTX 4090 desktop, achieving an IQM of 7.4 on Atari-60 (vs. Rainbow DQN's 1.9) and successfully solving three 3D Wii games (Super Mario Galaxy, Mario Kart Wii, Mortal Kombat) with minimal architectural changes.

## Results

On Atari-60, BTR achieves a human-normalized IQM of 7.4 (vs. Rainbow DQN's 1.9, DQN's 0.77, and FE-Rainbow's ~2.8), outperforming humans on 52 of 60 games. On the Atari-5 benchmark, BTR reaches an IQM of 7.74, nearly doubling FE-Rainbow (4.07) and Rainbow DQN Full (3.58). On Procgen, BTR exceeds Rainbow DQN + Impala (4x width) while using 8 hours of walltime versus 41 hours. BTR also solves three modern 3D Wii games (Super Mario Galaxy final level, Mario Kart Rainbow Road, Mortal Kombat Endurance) that Rainbow DQN fails to complete, all trained on a single desktop PC within 12 hours for 200M Atari frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTR decisively outperforms Rainbow DQN across all benchmarks: Atari-60 IQM 7.4 vs. 1.9 (3.9×), Atari-5 IQM 7.74 vs. 3.58 (2.2×), and Procgen where BTR exceeds Rainbow DQN + Impala (4x width) in 8 hours vs. 41 hours. Against the closest non-recurrent baseline FE-Rainbow (Schmidt & Schmied, 2021), BTR achieves 7.4 vs. ~2.8 IQM on Atari-60 and 7.74 vs. 4.07 on Atari-5, while also scaling to 200M frames (FE-Rainbow was optimized for 10M). BTR is competitive with compute-heavy methods like Dreamer-v3 (IQM 9.6) and MEME (IQM 9.6) but requires only 0.9 A100-GPU-days versus 7.7 for Dreamer-v3, and uses a 2.9M-parameter non-recurrent network versus 200M+ parameters. On modern 3D games, BTR consistently completes all three Wii titles while Rainbow DQN plateaus before finishing any. BTR also outperforms PQN (Gallici et al., 2024) on Atari-5 with life information (IQM 12.18 vs. 3.86 at 400M frames), though PQN trains faster (2 hrs vs. 22 hrs on A100) due to its JAX+envpool backend.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTR follows the same 'combine independently validated improvements' philosophy as Rainbow DQN (Hessel et al., 2018), but replaces two of Rainbow's six components (Double DQN → Munchausen RL, C51 → IQN) and adds four new ones (Impala architecture with 2× width scaling, 6×6 adaptive maxpooling, spectral normalization, and vectorized environments with a 1/64 replay ratio). The closest prior work is Fast and Efficient Rainbow (Schmidt & Schmied, 2021), which also optimized Rainbow for computational efficiency but targeted only 10M frames and dropped C51 entirely; BTR instead scales to 200M frames and multiple domains. The algorithmic core draws on Munchausen RL (Vieillard et al., 2020) for its update rule and IQN (Dabney et al., 2018) for distributional learning, while the network architecture is adapted from Impala (Espeholt et al., 2018) with spectral normalization (Miyato et al., 2018) applied to all convolutional residual layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is making state-of-the-art non-recurrent RL (IQM 7.4 on Atari-60) trainable on a single desktop PC in under 12 hours, dramatically lowering the barrier for smaller labs and hobbyists compared to Rainbow DQN's 35 hours or MEME/Dreamer-v3's multi-day GPU cluster runs. The first demonstration of RL agents solving modern 3D games (Super Mario Galaxy, Mario Kart Wii, Mortal Kombat) via a Wii emulator opens a new class of environments for RL research. Key limitations include the absence of an explicit exploration mechanism (struggling on hard-exploration tasks like Montezuma's Revenge) and the inability to match recurrent/world-model approaches at the very top of the performance distribution. Open questions include how to incorporate recurrent architectures without sacrificing computational accessibility, and whether the architecture's dominance in ablations (Impala contributing +142% IQM) points to underexplored network design directions in RL.
