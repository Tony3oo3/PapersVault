## Problem

SNNs suffer from accuracy degradation relative to ANNs and, critically, their inference timesteps are fixed at training time—changing the number of timesteps at deployment requires full retraining, severely limiting operational flexibility. Existing logits-based distillation methods for SNNs treat the network as an end-to-end spatial model, defining loss only on the final ensemble voting output and ignoring the per-timestep structure, so convergence is not guaranteed for implicit submodels at fewer timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252940/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event-integrated frames (48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS), fed directly into the input layer at every timestep as rate coding. The SNN backbone (ResNet-18/19/34 with LIF neurons) produces a logit vector at each of T timesteps; the final prediction is the mean (ensemble voting) across timesteps, yielding a single class label. A key deployment property is that the same trained model can be evaluated at any T_k ≤ T without retraining, producing valid class labels at reduced latency.

## Method

The core contribution is temporal-wise decoupling of the distillation objective: instead of computing cross-entropy and KL-divergence losses on the ensemble voting output z_ens, the losses are defined independently at each timestep t and averaged, yielding L_TWCE and L_TWKL. A third self-distillation term L_TWSD is added, using the ensemble logits as soft targets for each individual timestep's logits, acting as a regularization that tightens the gap between the decoupled and standard objectives without any extra computational branches. Theoretically, the paper proves via Jensen's inequality that L_TWKD upper-bounds L_SKD (Proposition 2) and, more importantly, that L_TWKD^(T) upper-bounds the implicit L_SKD^(T_k) for any T_k ≤ T (Proposition 3), guaranteeing convergence of all full-range timestep submodels from a single training run. The framework is implemented with BPTT and sigmoid surrogate gradients on SpikingJelly, adding negligible overhead over standard logits-KD since it only changes where the loss is computed, not the forward pass.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 at T=6, the proposed temporal-wise distillation achieves 82.56% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best direct-training method (RateBP, 80.83%) and the best distillation-based method (SAKD, 81.70%). On ImageNet with ResNet-34 at T=4, it reaches 71.04%, outperforming SAKD (70.04%) and RateBP (70.01%). On CIFAR10-DVS with ResNet-18 at T=4, it achieves 83.50%, exceeding SM (83.19% at T=10) and SAKD (81.50% at T=4). A key deployment advantage is that a single model trained at T=6 maintains strong accuracy across all inference timesteps T=1–6 without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 19 baselines on CIFAR-100 at T=6: it beats the strongest direct-training method RateBP (80.83%) by 1.73 points and the strongest distillation method SAKD (81.70%) by 0.86 points. On CIFAR-10 at T=6, it achieves 97.00%, surpassing SAKD (96.82%) and RateBP (96.36%). On ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), it leads SAKD (70.04%) and RateBP (70.01%) by ~1 point. On CIFAR10-DVS at T=4, it exceeds SM (83.19% at T=10) and SAKD (81.50% at T=4) by 0.31 and 2.0 points respectively. Notably, EnOF achieves 82.43% on CIFAR-100 at only T=2, but the proposed method matches or exceeds this at T=2 (81.47%) while also delivering 82.56% at T=6, demonstrating superior full-range timestep robustness that no baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hinton's logits-based knowledge distillation to the SNN setting by decoupling the distillation loss across timesteps, building directly on the per-timestep objective separation introduced by TET (Deng et al., 2022) for classification. It departs from prior SNN distillation work (KDSNN, LaSNN, SAKD, BKDSNN) that applies KD loss only on the final ensemble output, and from EnOF (Ding et al.) which uses adjacent-timestep KL between submodels, by instead using the full ensemble voting output as a self-distillation target for every individual timestep. The theoretical contribution—showing via Jensen's inequality that temporal-wise distillation forms a scaled upper bound on all implicit sub-model losses—provides a convergence guarantee absent from prior SNN training analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment flexibility (a single T=6-trained model serving all inference timesteps 1–6 without retraining) addresses a real operational constraint in neuromorphic hardware deployment, and the zero-overhead implementation makes it immediately adoptable. Limitations include the restriction to logits-based distillation (no feature alignment), modest gains from the self-distillation term (~0.2–0.3%), and validation primarily on ResNet-family architectures. Open questions include whether the temporal decoupling principle transfers to spiking transformer architectures (where ANN teacher selection is non-trivial) and whether the full-range convergence guarantee holds under more aggressive timestep reductions or different neuron models.
