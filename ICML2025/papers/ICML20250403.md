## Problem

Decision trees and random forests are workhorses of supervised learning in Euclidean space, but no principled variant exists for product manifolds—Cartesian products of hyperbolic, hyperspherical, and Euclidean components that capture heterogeneous geometric structure in real data. Naively applying standard DTs to ambient coordinates ignores manifold geometry, producing splits that violate continuity, geodesic convexity, and equidistance, which can cause misgeneralization and undermine interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes points represented as coordinates in the ambient space of each component manifold (e.g., a point in S²×E²×H² has 3+3+3 ambient coordinates), which are preprocessed into projection angles θ = tan⁻¹(x₀/x_d) for every 2D subspace {x₀, x_d} within each component. For link prediction, inputs are pairs of node embeddings concatenated with their product-manifold distance δ_P(x_i, x_j), forming a binary classification problem. Outputs are majority-vote class labels, mean regression values, or binary edge predictions, with the tree structure itself providing interpretable 2D-projection visualizations at every node.

## Method

The core contribution is an angular reformulation of DT splits that unifies all three constant-curvature geometries: each point is projected onto 2D subspaces {x₀, x_d} and represented by a single angle θ = tan⁻¹(x₀/x_d), and splits are defined as angular intervals S(x,d,θ) = I{θ(x,d) ∈ [θ, θ+π)}. Manifold-specific midpoint formulas ensure equidistance—arithmetic mean for hyperspherical, tan⁻¹(2/(u_d+v_d)) for lifted Euclidean, and a cot⁻¹-based formula involving V = sin(2θ_u−2θ_v)/(sin(θ_u+θ_v)sin(θ_v−θ_u)) for hyperbolic. For product manifolds, the algorithm iterates over all precomputed angles across all components, letting a single tree allocate splits across components by relevance. A proof sketch (building on Udriste, 1994) establishes that homogeneous hyperplane splits in any constant-curvature manifold are geodesically convex, and that the angular criterion reduces to the simplified linear classifier form sign(x₀cos(θ) − x_d sin(θ)), guaranteeing all five desiderata (continuity, convexity, equidistance, O(nd) candidates, O(1) evaluation) are preserved.

## Results

Across 57 benchmarks spanning classification, regression, and link prediction on product manifolds, Product RFs achieved top-1 performance on 29 tasks (51%) and top-2 on 41 tasks (72%). On the most challenging synthetic classification task (8-class Gaussian mixture in H⁴), Product RF reaches 69.3% accuracy versus 53.1% for Ambient RF and 28.8% for κ-GCN. On the Lymphoma VAE benchmark, Product RF achieves 84.4% accuracy, outperforming all baselines. For regression on H⁴, Product RF attains 0.015 RMSE, the best among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Scikit-learn baselines (Ambient RF operating in the ambient R^{D+1} space and Tangent RF operating in the tangent plane at the origin), Product RF wins decisively on hyperbolic-dominated manifolds: on H⁴ Gaussian classification it achieves 69.3% vs. 53.1% (Ambient) and 63.8% (Tangent), and on H²S² it reaches 41.8% vs. 37.3% and 37.6%. On purely Euclidean or spherical manifolds the gap narrows to within noise (e.g., E⁴: 34.4 vs. 34.2; S⁴: 37.7 vs. 38.4), and on a few VAE-embedded datasets (Blood, MNIST) the Ambient/Tangent RFs edge out Product RF by 2–4 points. Against κ-GCN (Bachmann et al., 2020), Product RF is consistently superior across all 18 classification benchmarks, with margins ranging from ~3 points on PolBlogs (92.8 vs. 69.9) to over 40 points on H⁴ Gaussian (69.3 vs. 28.8) and MNIST (36.0 vs. 14.0). For regression, Product RF achieves the lowest RMSE on 8 of 11 product-manifold signatures, most notably on H⁴ (0.015 vs. 0.020 for Ambient RF). On link prediction, Product RF is competitive but not dominant: it ties for best on Dolphins (94.1) and wins on Karate Club (93.9 vs. 91.8 Ambient RF), while Ambient MLP and κ-GCN lead on Football and Karate Club (95.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the angular-split formulation of hyperbolic decision trees (Chlenski et al., 2024) and hyperbolic random forests (Doorenbos et al., 2023) to all constant-curvature manifolds and, crucially, to product manifolds combining hyperbolic, hyperspherical, and Euclidean components. The key conceptual move is representing every split as an angle in a 2D subspace, which recovers classical CART thresholding in the Euclidean case (via a trivial lift), matches the hyperbolic midpoint formula, and introduces a new hyperspherical DT algorithm. This departs from prior product-manifold classifiers (linear models of Tabaghi et al., 2021; κ-GCN of Bachmann et al., 2020) by providing an interpretable, tree-based alternative that preserves geodesic convexity, continuity, and O(1) split evaluation simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Product DTs/RFs fill a clear gap in the non-Euclidean ML toolkit: they sit between simple linear classifiers and opaque neural networks, offering interpretability (per-node 2D visualizations, feature-pair attribution) while respecting manifold geometry. The main limitation is dependence on upstream embedding quality and signature selection, and the absence of a privileged basis in non-Euclidean spaces weakens the classical inductive bias of axis-aligned splits. Open directions include oblique/rotation forests to exploit non-privileged dimensions, a continuous κ-stereographic unification of all three curvatures, and extensions to simplex geometry.
