## Problem

Existing FL OOD generalization methods extract only the invariant causal features shared across all clients (their intersection), inevitably discarding client-specific causal features that are useful for generalizing to unseen target distributions. This limitation is especially severe under data heterogeneity, where the shared invariant subset becomes very small. The paper asks whether the global feature extractor should instead capture the union of all causal feature types present across clients, and how to handle the resulting irrelevant (inactive) causal features at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (Waterbirds at 224×224, PACS at 224×224) or grayscale digit images (MNIST, SVHN, USPS, etc.) and outputs a class label. The architecture is a three-stage pipeline: a comprehensive feature extractor (ResNet-18, AlexNet, or MLP depending on dataset) produces a full feature vector, a two-layer feature compressor generates a soft selection mask over that vector, and a local counterfactual generator (two conv layers + AdaIN_noise) produces augmented images used only during training. At deployment, only the feature extractor and compressor are transferred to the target client; the counterfactual generator remains on training clients.

## Method

FedUni restructures the structural causal model so that the global causal variable Z_C^g is defined as the union (not intersection) of client-specific causal variables, and introduces a new variable Z_F^U for inactive causal features that are causal locally but irrelevant to the target task. The comprehensive feature extractor is trained jointly with two auxiliary modules: (1) a causal intervention module that uses a trainable counterfactual generator (Conv + AdaIN_noise) to produce diverse augmented images, enforcing feature invariance via a contrastive loss while maximizing diversity via a normalized mutual information objective; and (2) an inactive causal feature compressor (two-layer network) that learns a soft mask to suppress Z_F^U, optimized by minimizing the conditional mutual information between the label and the compressed features while maximizing it for the retained features. The feature extractor and compressor are aggregated via FedAvg across clients, while the counterfactual generator is updated locally, allowing the global model to benefit from collaborative training without collapsing to only invariant features.

## Results

On the spurious correlation benchmarks, FedUni achieves the best OOD-worst accuracy across all three datasets: 78.05% on Waterbirds (vs. 74.59% for FedSDR), 56.41% on CMNIST (vs. 37.41% for FedSR), and 46.25% on CFMNIST (vs. 44.52% for FedAvg). On the cross-domain Digits benchmark, FedUni reaches 66.85% average accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best method FedSR (57.24%) by nearly 10 points. On PACS, FedUni achieves 76.24% average accuracy versus 69.36% for FedSDR, with the largest gain on the Sketch domain (76.04% vs. 51.64%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedUni outperforms all eight baselines on the OOD-worst metric across every dataset, which is the most challenging evaluation setting. The largest margin is on CMNIST OOD-worst, where FedUni (56.41%) surpasses the next-best MOON (39.62%) by 16.8 points and FedSR (37.41%) by 19 points, indicating that preserving the full union of causal features is critical when spurious correlations are strong. On Waterbirds, FedUni leads FedSDR (the strongest OOD baseline) by 3.5 points on OOD-worst and 2.7 points on OOD-avg. On the cross-domain Digits benchmark, FedUni's 66.85% average exceeds FedSR (57.24%) by 9.6 points, with particularly large gains on MNISTM (52.40% vs. 36.46%) and SYN (66.29% vs. 48.09%). On PACS, FedUni achieves 76.24% average vs. FedSDR's 69.36%, with the Sketch domain showing the most dramatic improvement (76.04% vs. 51.64%). The standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, MOON) are consistently outperformed on both ID and OOD metrics, confirming that the causal feature extraction and compression architecture provides benefits beyond what heterogeneity-aware aggregation alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedUni builds on two converging lines of work: (1) causal feature learning for OOD generalization in centralized settings (IRM, CIRL, DaC), which motivates extracting invariant causal features and using counterfactual interventions to eliminate spurious correlations, and (2) FL OOD generalization methods (FedSR, FedIIR, FedDG-GA, FedSDR) that extract inter-client invariant features via adversarial alignment, regularization, or gradient matching. The key departure is conceptual: prior FL methods define the global causal variable as the *intersection* of client-specific causal features, while FedUni restructures the SCM to define it as the *union*, introducing a new 'inactive causal feature' variable (Z_F^U) that must be explicitly compressed. The counterfactual generator draws from single-domain generalization literature (L2D, UDP) but is adapted to the FL client-side setting with a lightweight AdaIN-based architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that retaining only invariant causal features across FL clients systematically discards useful client-specific causal information, and that a union-plus-compression strategy can recover this lost information—reframes how the FL OOD generalization problem should be formulated. The practical impact is notable: the method achieves large OOD-worst accuracy gains (e.g., +17 points on CMNIST, +10 on Digits SVHN) over strong baselines with a simple two-layer compressor. Limitations include the per-client counterfactual generator adding computational overhead, reliance on the SCM assumptions holding in practice, and evaluation limited to image classification on relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the union-of-causal-features principle extends to non-visual modalities, tasks with more complex causal structures, or settings where the number of clients is very large.
