## Problem

When fine-tuning a pre-trained generalist policy on multiple tasks under a limited demonstration budget, it is unclear which tasks to demonstrate and in what order. Prior active data selection work addressed single-task or non-sequential settings, leaving the multi-task sequential decision-making case—where the agent must adaptively choose which task's expert trajectory to request—unexplored and lacking formal guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250086/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained policy (MLP or diffusion policy, optionally with a frozen visual encoder like R3M for RGB inputs), a known target task distribution μ_c, and a demonstration budget N. At each round it outputs a selected task c_n for which an H-step expert trajectory is collected, and after N rounds produces a fine-tuned task-conditioned policy. Notably, no access to pre-training data is required, and the task selection is fully sequential and adaptive based on previously collected demonstrations.

## Method

AMF selects each next task by maximizing the expected mutual information between the Bayesian policy posterior and a new expert trajectory, equivalently minimizing expected posterior entropy over the policy's state occupancy. For GP policies (AMF-GP), conditional entropy is computed in closed form from the GP posterior; for neural networks (AMF-NN), the policy is approximated as a linear function over loss-gradient embeddings, reducing entropy estimation to a GP kernel computation in embedding space. Occupancy expectations over unobserved tasks are handled via importance sampling with weights derived from the likelihood ratio of trajectories under different tasks. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, an Adaptive Prior mechanism retains a frozen copy of the pre-trained policy and linearly interpolates its actions with the fine-tuned policy using task-dependent weights α(c) trained with a conservative penalty toward zero. Under Lipschitz-smooth MDP and RKHS policy assumptions, the paper proves the imitator's performance gap to the expert shrinks as O(γ_(Hn))/√n, where γ_(Hn) is the maximum information gain from Hn samples.

## Results

The paper evaluates AMF across four environments (2D Integrator, Metaworld, FrankaKitchen, Robomimic) using multi-task success rate and discounted return as metrics. In the GP setting (2D Integrator), AMF-GP increases returns faster than uniform task sampling, with the performance gap growing as the pre-training distribution diverges from the evaluation distribution (e.g., when only 6/12 tasks are demonstrated during pre-training). In the neural network setting (Metaworld, FrankaKitchen), AMF-NN achieves higher average multi-task success rates than random uniform task selection, particularly under mismatched pre-training; when pre-training covers all tasks equally, AMF-NN's performance matches the uniform baseline. On Robomimic with diffusion policies, AMF-NN combined with the Adaptive Prior consistently outperforms uniform sampling, especially under distribution mismatch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is uniform i.i.d. task sampling (random task selection), which serves as the natural non-active strategy. AMF (both GP and NN variants) outperforms uniform sampling when the pre-training distribution does not match the evaluation task distribution — the more severe the mismatch (e.g., only half the tasks demonstrated during pre-training), the larger AMF's advantage. When pre-training covers all evaluation tasks equally, AMF's performance matches the uniform baseline, confirming that active selection adds no value in the well-matched regime. For uncertainty estimation in AMF-NN, loss-gradient embeddings (Ash et al., 2020) outperform both last-layer gradient embeddings and test-time Dropout, yielding higher multi-task success rates. The Adaptive Prior (a task-dependent linear combination of fine-tuned and pre-trained policy outputs) prevents catastrophic forgetting and is beneficial across all settings, with the largest gains under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transductive active learning framework of Hübotter et al. (2024) from non-sequential supervised domains to sequential decision-making (contextual MDPs), deriving the first performance guarantees for active multi-task behavioral cloning. For practical neural network policies, it adapts the loss-gradient embedding technique of Ash et al. (2020) to approximate GP-based uncertainty estimation, enabling the information-gain criterion to operate without access to pre-training data. The paper also introduces an 'Adaptive Prior' mechanism inspired by state-free priors in offline RL (Bagatella et al., 2022) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, which is orthogonal to and compatible with any data selection strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal convergence guarantees for active multi-task fine-tuning and demonstrates that information-gain-based task selection outperforms uniform sampling, particularly under distribution mismatch between pre-training and evaluation. The Adaptive Prior offers a broadly applicable, lightweight solution to catastrophic forgetting that benefits any fine-tuning pipeline. Key open questions include whether the method scales to truly large generalist policies (e.g., Octo, RT-X), whether uncertainty estimation via loss-gradient embeddings remains informative at scale, and whether the BC proxy objective can be replaced by direct RL objective estimation. The authors note that gains are modest when pre-training already covers all tasks uniformly, limiting the method's value in well-matched settings.
