## Problem

Decision-based black-box adversarial attacks suffer from high query complexity, and existing normal-vector-based methods overlook local optima caused by varying decision boundary geometries. In particular, narrow adversarial regions with high boundary curvatures—common in targeted attacks—trap perturbation optimization, degrading performance across diverse models. The paper identifies this local-optima problem and introduces a curvature-aware mechanism to detect and escape it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252624/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a correctly classified source image (pixels in [0,1], shape C×W×H) and, for targeted attacks, a target image whose class the attacker wants to induce. The attacker interacts with the target model solely through top-1 label queries (hard-label feedback), with no access to logits, gradients, or architecture. The output is an adversarial example minimizing the ℓ₂ perturbation norm under a fixed query budget (e.g., 2,000–10,000 queries).

## Method

TtBA constructs each new perturbation direction as a weighted combination of the current unit direction d̂ and its unit normal vector N̂: d_k = k·N̂ + (1−k)·d̂. A binary search identifies k_bridge—the weight at which the decision boundary along d_k equals that of the current direction—requiring only ~10 queries per iteration. The key empirical and theoretical finding is that the near-optimal weight k_local is linearly related to k_bridge, with k_local/k_bridge concentrated in [0.5, 0.7], so TtBA simply sets k = (2/3)·k_bridge without further search. When k_bridge falls below a threshold (0.1), indicating a high-curvature narrow adversarial region, TtBA increases k to 0.9·k_bridge to force the direction away from the local optimum and escape the trap. This curvature-adaptive strategy, combined with the two-third bridge rule, eliminates the need for expensive local searches while maintaining query efficiency.

## Results

TtBA achieves the best average ℓ2 perturbation on 7 out of 9 models for non-targeted attacks at 10,000 queries, and surpasses all baselines in nearly all targeted attack scenarios. On ImageNet-ResNet50 (non-targeted, 10K queries), TtBA reduces ℓ2 from 6.203 (CGBA-H) to 3.448, a 44% improvement; on ImageNet-EfficientNet, it drops from 2.688 to 2.008 (25% reduction). For targeted attacks on ImageNet-VGG19 at 10K queries, TtBA achieves ℓ2 = 12.26 (median 8.42) versus 14.32 (median 10.59) for CGBA-H. The only loss is on CIFAR100-ViT targeted at 10K queries, where CGBA-H achieves a marginally lower average ℓ2 (4.327 vs. 4.464).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TtBA is compared against four state-of-the-art decision-based attacks: HSJA (Chen et al., 2020), Tangent Attack/TA (Ma et al., 2021), CGBA, and CGBA-H (Reza et al., 2023). TtBA consistently outperforms HSJA and TA across all 9 models and both attack types, with the largest margins on ImageNet (e.g., ℓ2 = 3.448 vs. 7.840 for HSJA on ResNet50 non-targeted). Against CGBA, TtBA wins on 7 of 9 models for non-targeted attacks at 10K queries; CGBA retains a slight edge on CIFAR10-CNN (0.181 vs. 0.180) and CIFAR100-ViT (0.744 vs. 0.734) at lower query budgets (2K), but TtBA surpasses CGBA at 5K and 10K queries on both. Against CGBA-H, TtBA wins in nearly all targeted attack scenarios (e.g., ℓ2 = 27.83 vs. 34.35 on ImageNet-ResNet50, 12.26 vs. 14.32 on ImageNet-VGG19), with the sole exception being CIFAR100-ViT targeted at 10K queries where CGBA-H achieves a marginally lower average ℓ2 (4.327 vs. 4.464). On adversarially trained WideResNet models (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), TtBA also outperforms all four baselines in non-targeted settings and matches or exceeds CGBA-H in targeted settings beyond 4,000 queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

TtBA extends the normal-vector-based decision-based attack lineage initiated by HSJA (Chen et al., 2020) and refined by CGBA (Reza et al., 2023), which use boundary normal vectors to guide perturbation optimization. Its key departure is the introduction of a 'bridge direction' — a weighted combination of the current perturbation direction and its normal vector — governed by a new curvature-sensitive metric k_bridge identified via binary search. Unlike prior geometric attacks (G-TA, CGBA-H) that handle high-curvature boundaries through fixed search strategies, TtBA explicitly detects and escapes local optima in narrow adversarial regions by modulating the normal-vector weight based on k_bridge magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The k_bridge metric provides a principled, query-efficient way to estimate local decision boundary curvature without explicit gradient access, and the empirically validated 2/3 ratio between k_local and k_bridge eliminates the need for expensive inner-loop optimization, reducing query overhead to ~5.7% of budget. The local optima escape mechanism addresses a practical failure mode in targeted attacks that prior curvature-aware methods overlooked. Open questions include whether the 2/3 ratio generalizes to score-based (soft-label) settings, whether it holds for non-image modalities, and whether the approach can be integrated into multi-objective frameworks balancing imperceptibility, transferability, and query cost.
