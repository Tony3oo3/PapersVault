## Problem

Despite significant practical progress in LLM quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ, OWQ, etc.), the rate-distortion theoretic foundations of LLM compression remain largely unexplored. Existing methods inherit heuristics from the Optimal Brain Surgeon pruning algorithm and require weight fine-tuning during quantization, lacking a principled optimization formulation that directly minimizes output distortion under a bit budget constraint. This paper establishes the rate-distortion framework for LLM quantization and provides a solver that determines optimal per-group bit depths without requiring weight fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM's weight matrices, a small calibration set (128 sequences of 2048 tokens from C4), and a user-specified target average bit rate (e.g., 3.0 or 4.0 bits per weight). It produces quantized weights organized into groups (default size 512), where each group receives its own bit depth (0–8 bits, clamped), a weight scale, and a weight mean, along with corrected bias vectors that compensate for non-zero-mean quantization errors. The bit depths are fractional during optimization and rounded to integers at the end, enabling arbitrary average bit rates including sub-3-bit targets.

## Method

Radio formulates LLM quantization as a constrained least-squares problem: minimize expected output distortion (squared difference between quantized and unquantized model outputs) subject to a total bit budget constraint. Using a classic rate-distortion result that quantization error halves per additional bit, the paper derives a closed-form expression for the distortion gradient with respect to each group's bit depth, enabling a dual-ascent solver that alternately updates bit depths (primal) and a Lagrange multiplier (dual) until the marginal distortion reduction is equalized across all groups. To handle the non-uniform weight distributions typical of LLMs, the method applies a companding transform (the normalized cubic root of a Laplace CDF) before uniform quantization, achieving finer resolution in high-probability regions. Gradient variances are accumulated efficiently via PCA projection along the embedding dimension and minibatch backpropagation, and bias vectors are corrected at each quantization step to eliminate systematic output bias. The approach also supports weight grouping at row/column granularity, which yields additional bit-rate savings by exploiting per-channel variance heterogeneity.

## Results

On WikiText2 perplexity (lower is better), Radio achieves the best quantized-model accuracy across all tested OPT and Llama-2 models at both 4-bit and 3-bit average bit depths. At 4-bit, Radio reduces perplexity from 14.63 (FP16) to 14.20 on OPT-1.3B and from 3.32 (FP16) to 3.40 on Llama-2 70B, outperforming the next-best methods (AWQ/128, OmniQuant/128) by 0.01–0.75 PPL. At 3-bit, the advantage is more pronounced on smaller models: Radio achieves 30.71 PPL on OPT-125M versus 32.25 for OmniQuant/128 (a 1.54 PPL gap), while on Llama-2 70B the gap narrows to 0.02 PPL (3.72 vs. 3.74 for AWQ/128). On downstream tasks (GSM8K, ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande), Radio-quantized 3-bit Llama-2 models score 0.5–1.5 points higher than GPTQ/256 and AWQ/256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Radio outperforms all six baselines (GPTQ, QuIP, OWQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, SqueezeLLM) on WikiText2 perplexity at both 3-bit and 4-bit across every model size tested. The largest absolute gains appear on smaller models: at 3-bit, Radio reduces OPT-125M perplexity by 1.54 PPL over OmniQuant/128 (30.71 vs. 32.25) and by 2.15 over GPTQ/256 (30.71 vs. 32.86); at 4-bit, the gap over AWQ/128 on OPT-125M is 1.88 PPL (27.23 vs. 29.11). On the largest models (OPT-66B, Llama-2 70B), the advantage narrows to 0.00–0.02 PPL, suggesting that larger models are inherently more compressible and the methods converge. On downstream tasks (GSM8K, ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande), Radio-quantized 3-bit Llama-2-70B scores 49.81% on GSM8K versus 46.47% (GPTQ/256) and 48.07% (AWQ/256), and leads on 4 of 5 common-sense QA benchmarks. Notably, RTN-quantized models show similar perplexity to Radio on large models but collapse on downstream tasks (e.g., 6.14% vs. 49.81% GSM8K on Llama-2-70B), underscoring that perplexity alone is insufficient for evaluating quantization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Hessian-based lineage (OBS → GPTQ → OWQ/AWQ/QuIP) by reframing LLM weight quantization as a rate-distortion optimization problem rooted in classical signal processing theory (Gersho & Gray, 1991). Rather than sequentially removing weights and compensating via fine-tuning as GPTQ does, Radio solves a constrained least-squares problem over bit depths using dual ascent, then applies a simple integer-rounding heuristic for actual quantization. The companding (Laplace-based sigmoid) quantization step and per-group bit-depth assignment also distinguish it from the uniform round-to-nearest or limited mixed-precision choices (e.g., 4 or 16 bits) used by prior mixed-precision methods, which are constrained by the combinatorial nature of bit-depth selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rate-distortion framing provides a principled, non-heuristic foundation for LLM quantization that was previously dominated by ad hoc Hessian approximations, and it naturally extends to per-group (per-channel or per-weight-group) bit-depth assignment without the combinatorial explosion that limits current mixed-precision approaches. The method's simplicity (no weight fine-tuning during quantization) makes it applicable to activation quantization for batched inference, a setting where GPTQ-style fine-tuning would introduce unacceptable latency. Open questions include whether the framework extends to vector quantization, whether the Laplace companding assumption holds across all model architectures, and how the approach scales to models beyond 100B parameters where running time (11h for 70B) becomes a practical concern.
