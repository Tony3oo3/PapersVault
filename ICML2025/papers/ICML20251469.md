## Problem

While optimal convergence rates for decentralized optimization are well-established for doubly-stochastic and column-stochastic mixing matrices, the row-stochastic scenario—where only in-degree information is available—remained entirely unexplored. No lower bound on convergence rate existed, no algorithm had been proven to achieve linear speedup, and the impact of row-stochastic network topology on algorithmic performance was unclear. This paper bridges these gaps by establishing the first convergence lower bound and designing algorithms that attain it.

## Input / Output

Each of n nodes holds a local dataset with potentially heterogeneous distribution and accesses its loss function only through a stochastic gradient oracle with bounded variance σ². The network topology is encoded as a row-stochastic mixing matrix A (constructed from in-degree information via a_{ij}=1/(1+d_i^in)), and the algorithm requires only in-neighbor communication. The output is a stationary point of the non-convex objective f(x)=n^{-1}Σf_i(x), with convergence rate governed by the generalized spectral gap β_A and equilibrium skewness κ_A of the mixing matrix.

## Method

The paper introduces PULL-DIAG-GT, which embeds the PULL-DIAG gossip protocol (power-iteration estimation of the Perron vector followed by diagonal correction) into gradient tracking to handle the biased weighted average inherent to row-stochastic mixing. A novel analysis framework proves linear speedup by decomposing the descent lemma into consensus error and a new 'descent deviation' term, then bounding both via rolling-sum inequalities and the spectral gap. To eliminate the instability from inverting small diagonal entries of A^k (which required an extra bounded-diagonals assumption), the authors introduce MG-PULL-DIAG-GT, performing R consecutive gossip steps per iteration with R chosen as ⌈3(1+ln(κ_A)+ln(n))/(1-β_A)⌉. This multi-step gossip exponentially improves the spectral parameter from β_A to β_A^R while reducing gradient variance via R-mini-batching, ultimately matching the established lower bound up to a ln(n) gap and achieving near-optimal complexity.

## Results

The paper's experiments primarily validate theoretical convergence guarantees rather than reporting final accuracy numbers. On synthetic non-convex logistic regression (Figure 2), PULL-DIAG-GT demonstrates linear speedup: the gradient norm decreases proportionally to 1/√n across network sizes of 1, 2, 8, 16, 128, and 512 nodes on exponential graphs, and 1, 5, 10, 16 nodes on ring graphs. On MNIST (Figure 3) and CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (Figure 4), MG-PULL-DIAG-GT achieves consistently faster training loss convergence than vanilla PULL-DIAG-GT across all four tested topologies (ring, grid, geometric, nearest-neighbor) with 16 nodes, with the advantage most pronounced on sparse topologies like ring and grid graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MG-PULL-DIAG-GT against vanilla PULL-DIAG-GT (the ROW-ONLY gradient tracking algorithm from Xin et al. 2019c / FROST, and Li et al. 2019) on both MNIST and CIFAR-10. MG-PULL-DIAG-GT wins on convergence speed across all tested topologies: on MNIST, it reaches lower training loss faster at every communication round for ring, grid, geometric, and nearest-neighbor graphs (Figure 3). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the advantage is most visible on sparse topologies (ring and grid), where the multi-step gossip strategy effectively mitigates the instability caused by inverting small diagonal entries of A^k in the early iterations (Figure 4). The comparison is framed in terms of convergence rate (training loss vs. communication rounds) rather than final test accuracy, though the paper notes corresponding test accuracy results in Appendix E (Figures A2, A3). The key practical takeaway is that MG-PULL-DIAG-GT removes the need for Assumption 4 (bounded diagonals / self-loops) while matching the theoretical lower bound up to a logarithmic ln(n) gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the column-stochastic decentralized optimization framework of Liang et al. (2023) to the row-stochastic setting, demonstrating that the same generalized spectral gap and equilibrium skewness metrics characterize both network types. It builds on the PULL-DIAG gossip protocol (Mai & Abed, 2016) and gradient tracking (Nedić et al., 2017) to form PULL-DIAG-GT, the standard ROW-ONLY algorithm, and introduces a novel analysis framework to handle the descent-direction deviation unique to row-stochastic mixing. The multi-step gossip (MG) technique, which replaces single gossip steps with R consecutive steps to stabilize the Diag(A^k)^{-1} correction, is a new algorithmic ingredient not present in prior ROW-ONLY methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish a convergence lower bound and near-optimal algorithm for decentralized stochastic optimization over row-stochastic networks, closing a gap that has persisted since the column-stochastic case was resolved. The practical relevance is significant because directed topologies arise naturally when bidirectional communication is infeasible (power constraints, connection failures), and the results provide concrete guidance on how network sparsity and asymmetry affect training efficiency. A key limitation is the residual logarithmic gap ln(n) between the upper and lower bounds, and the explicit dependence on θ_A (the diagonal bound) in PULL-DIAG-GT remains uncharacterized, leaving room for tighter analyses.
