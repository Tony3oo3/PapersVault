## Problem

Provenance-based Intrusion Detection Systems (PIDSes) face a fundamental tension: frequency-database baselines cannot adapt to new benign entities (out-of-vocabulary problem) or normality shifts over time, while GNN-based approaches incur prohibitive computational and memory costs on large-scale provenance graphs and cannot support real-time streaming detection. This paper addresses how to build a learned, adaptive baseline that generalizes to unseen events while remaining lightweight enough for real-time operation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming provenance graph events as (source, relationship, destination, timestamp) triples, where source/destination are typed entities (process, file, network) and relationship is one of eight operation types. Each event is encoded into a 500-dimensional vector (or 214-dim with one-hot variant) and fed to a lightweight regression model that outputs a continuous regular score in (0,1). During online detection, these per-event scores serve as initial tags that propagate along causal edges in the provenance graph, producing path-level anomaly scores and triggering real-time alerts with interpretable attack paths when the aggregated score drops below a threshold.

## Method

The approach has two phases. In the offline phase, events from historical audit logs are encoded using FastText with a critical design choice: out-of-vocabulary words are mapped to zero vectors rather than being handled by subword decomposition or document-level inference. This zero-vector encoding is key because malicious events contain a higher proportion of OOV entities than unseen benign events, allowing the regression model to assign lower regular scores to attacks. A lightweight neural network (MLP, LSTM, or CNN) is then trained via MSE regression to predict each event's regular score (computed as the conditional frequency of the full triple over the source-relationship pair), with artificially constructed negative samples to improve generalization. In the online phase, the trained model assigns initial tags to streaming events, and a tag-propagation algorithm aggregates these scores along causal paths using a decay factor α, triggering alerts when the path-level score Γ_P = (1/α)∏(Γ_e·α) falls below a threshold. This decouples the heavy graph analysis into per-event scoring plus lightweight tag propagation, achieving F1 > 99% on anomaly path mining while operating in microsecond-scale per-event encoding time.

## Results

On the anomaly path mining task, the best configuration (FastText with zero-vector OOV handling) achieves an F1 score of 99.63% (Precision 99.27%, Recall 100.00%) on E3-CADETS. In the node-level comparison against SOTA PIDSes, the proposed method attains F1 scores of 0.4778 (E3-CADETS), 0.3200 (E3-THEIA), and 0.0229 (E3-TRACE), substantially outperforming all baselines on CADETS and THEIA. The method also achieves 100% accuracy in predicting regular scores for malicious events absent from the frequency database (Accuracy2), while maintaining ~90.6% overall prediction accuracy (Accuracy1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nodoze (frequency-database baseline), the proposed method wins on F1 across all three datasets (0.4778 vs. 0.0885 on CADETS, 0.3200 vs. 0.1791 on THEIA, 0.0229 vs. 0.0125 on TRACE), primarily by reducing false positives by an order of magnitude (69 vs. 667 on CADETS). Against Flash (GNN-based, IEEE S&P 2024), the advantage is overwhelming: Flash produces 6,996 false positives on CADETS and 60,484 on TRACE, yielding F1 scores near zero (0.0019, 0.0001), while the proposed method maintains 32 true positives with only 69 FPs on CADETS. Against Kairos (time-window GNN, arXiv 2023), the proposed method achieves 0.4778 F1 on CADETS versus Kairos's 0.0003, with Kairos generating 119,878 false positives; on THEIA the gap is 0.3200 vs. 0.0021. The key differentiator is that the learned baseline with tag-propagation avoids the massive false-positive explosion characteristic of GNN-based methods on large-scale provenance graphs, while also supporting real-time streaming detection that GNN approaches cannot practically achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of frequency-based provenance intrusion detection (Nodoze, Hassan et al. 2019), tag-propagation streaming frameworks (Hossain et al. 2017; Milajerdi et al. 2019), and learned index structures (Kraska et al. 2018; Ding et al. 2023). Rather than adopting the GNN-based approaches that dominate recent PIDS work (Flash, Kairos, ThreatTrace, ShadowWatcher), it replaces the traditional frequency database with a lightweight neural regression model trained on NLP-style event embeddings (FastText, Word2Vec, Doc2Vec), drawing an explicit analogy to learned storage. The key conceptual departure is treating provenance events as 'sentences' and their regular scores as regression targets, enabling generalization to out-of-vocabulary entities without the computational overhead of graph-level models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28× storage reduction over frequency databases and microsecond-level event encoding make real-time provenance-based detection more practical, and the OOV-as-zero-vector mechanism offers a simple, effective solution to the normality-shift problem that plagues static baselines. However, the node-level F1 scores in the SOTA comparison remain modest (0.48 on E3-CADETS), and evaluation is confined to DARPA TC datasets, leaving open questions about scalability to production-scale graphs and robustness against truly novel multi-stage APTs. The finding that a naive zero-vector OOV strategy outperforms sophisticated subword or document-level inference is practically useful but may not generalize beyond the specific event structure of provenance logs.
