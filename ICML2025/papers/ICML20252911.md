## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods focus on instance-wise alignment while neglecting feature correlation alignment (CORAL), rely on computationally expensive backpropagation that is impractical for edge deployment, and suffer from domain forgetting. The central challenge is that CORAL requires access to source-domain features to compute the source covariance, which is unavailable in TTA due to privacy and resource constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252911/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled test images and a frozen pre-trained model (encoder f and decoder g), with no access to source data. It maintains an incrementally updated pseudo-source bank of k high-certainty embeddings (selected by minimum prediction uncertainty ‖ŷ−p‖²_F) and produces both a transformed feature embedding Z′_t = (Z_t − μ_t)W + μ̂_s and a final class prediction P′_t = g(Z′_t). No model parameters are modified at any point, so the original source-domain knowledge is fully preserved.

## Method

The paper proves that the feature correlation of k high-certainty test instances closely approximates the true source correlation (Theorem 3.5), and that minimizing the correlation distance between test and pseudo-source embeddings reduces the test classification error bound (Theorem 3.6, Corollary 3.7). LinearTCA exploits this by computing the pseudo-source covariance from the k most certain test instances, then deriving a closed-form linear transformation W = U_t Λ_t^{1/2} Ũ_s^T Λ̂_s^{−1/2} via eigenvalue decomposition that maps the test covariance onto the pseudo-source covariance. The transformed embeddings are passed through the frozen decoder to yield final predictions, requiring only a single forward pass plus a small matrix computation (4% GPU memory, 0.6% computation time of the best TTA baseline). LinearTCA+ extends this as a plug-and-play module: after any existing TTA method updates the model, LinearTCA is applied to the resulting embeddings to further boost accuracy without additional backpropagation.

## Results

On domain generalization (PACS, OfficeHome, DomainNet), LinearTCA+ achieves 69.58% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline TIPI (65.77%) by 3.81%. On image corruption (CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, ImageNet-C), LinearTCA+ reaches 61.04% average accuracy versus 53.20% for the best baseline TSD. On CLIP-ViT-B/16, LinearTCA achieves 97.36% on PACS, 83.77% on OfficeHome, and 84.18% on VLCS, surpassing the best prior method (WATT-S) by 1.28%, 2.08%, and 2.85% respectively. In terms of efficiency, LinearTCA adds only +0.07s runtime and +0.00 MB peak GPU memory over the source model, compared to +332.76s and +13,044 MB for TEA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinearTCA+ (the plug-and-play variant) achieves the best average accuracy across all benchmarks: 69.58% on domain generalization (vs. TIPI 65.77%, TEA 66.98%) and 61.04% on image corruption (vs. EATA 59.29%, TSD 53.20%). Standalone LinearTCA outperforms all baselines on OfficeHome (70.12% vs. TIPI 69.57%) and DomainNet (46.16% vs. EATA 47.65% is the only exception where EATA edges it slightly), but trails BP-based methods on CIFAR-10-C (66.16% vs. TSD 73.88%) and ImageNet-C (25.60% vs. EATA 50.58%), which the authors attribute to the linear transformation being insufficient for complex nonlinear shifts. On CLIP-ViT-B/16, LinearTCA surpasses all baselines including WATT-S (96.08% on PACS) and TPT (93.08%), achieving 97.36% on PACS and 84.44% on VLCS. In efficiency, LinearTCA adds negligible overhead (+0.07s, +0.00 MB) compared to TENT (+9.22s, +3,973 MB) and TEA (+332.76s, +13,044 MB). On forgetting resistance, LinearTCA retains 87.95% source-domain accuracy (only −1.17% from source) versus TSD (71.93%, −17.19%) and TEA (85.76%, −3.36%), and even shows positive backward transfer on PACS (+0.08% over source).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CORAL (Correlation Alignment) paradigm from classical domain adaptation to the test-time adaptation (TTA) setting, where source data is inaccessible. The key conceptual departure is the construction of a 'pseudo-source' from high-certainty test instances, theoretically justified via Lipschitz continuity and Taylor approximation bounds, rather than requiring actual source features. It also departs from the dominant TTA literature (TENT, EATA, TSD, TEA, etc.) by entirely avoiding backpropagation, instead applying a closed-form linear transformation derived from eigenvalue decomposition to align second-order statistics. The theoretical framework connects Wasserstein-distance-based domain adaptation bounds with correlation alignment, bridging two previously separate lines of analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing three simultaneous limitations of current TTA: computational overhead (4% GPU memory, 0.6% time vs. best baseline), domain forgetting (no parameter updates), and the overlooked role of feature correlations. The plug-and-play LinearTCA+ module offers a low-cost way to boost any existing TTA method, making it broadly applicable. However, the reliance on a linear transformation limits effectiveness under complex nonlinear shifts, and standalone LinearTCA underperforms backprop-based methods on corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C). Open questions include whether nonlinear (MLP-based) transformations can close this gap, and the unexplained 'positive backward transfer' phenomenon where LinearTCA actually improves source-domain accuracy.
