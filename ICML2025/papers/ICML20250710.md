## Problem

Standard preference alignment assumes that all error-free preference data is beneficial, implicitly treating data difficulty as irrelevant to model capacity. This paper identifies and validates a critical gap: overly difficult preference examples—those exceeding the model's capacity—actively degrade alignment performance rather than merely failing to help, a risk overlooked by prior data-selection work focused solely on label noise and mislabeled samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250710/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference dataset of (prompt, chosen, rejected) triples and an SFT model as the starting point for alignment. It first produces a validation-loss ranking of all examples (computed via six reference models trained on random half-splits), then outputs an aligned policy model trained exclusively on the easiest τ% of examples in easy-to-difficult sequential order. The τ threshold is model- and dataset-dependent, tuned via a third-party evaluator such as AlpacaEval 2.

## Method

Selective DPO extends standard DPO with a principled data-selection pipeline built on the insight that preference examples have an inherent, model-relative difficulty measurable by validation loss. Concretely, it (1) trains six reference DPO models on random half-splits of the training set, (2) evaluates each held-out example's validation loss under its complementary reference models to rank examples by difficulty, and (3) aligns the target SFT model via DPO on only the lowest-τ% examples, processed sequentially from easy to difficult. The validation loss is validated as a computationally efficient proxy for the 'learned step' (the earliest training step at which the implicit reward model reliably separates preferred from rejected responses), with Spearman correlations of 0.92–0.93 across four model families. Experiments across 3B–14B Qwen models confirm that the optimal τ scales with model capacity (64% for 3B to 81% for 14B), establishing that difficulty is relative rather than absolute.

## Results

Selective DPO achieves 28.9% win rate (WR) on AlpacaEval 2 with Mistral-7B-SFT and 25.3% WR with Llama-3-8B-SFT, representing 9–16% absolute gains over standard DPO (12.5% and 15.5% WR respectively). On Arena-Hard, Selective DPO reaches 17.0% WR (Mistral-7B) and 24.1% WR (Llama-3-8B), surpassing all DPO variants including SimPO and WPO. On MT-Bench, Selective DPO scores 7.4 (Mistral-7B) and 8.0 (Llama-3-8B) with GPT-4 as evaluator, matching or exceeding the strongest baselines. The method also improves length-controlled win rate (LC) to 27.1% and 24.9% for the two models, outperforming WPO (24.4% and 23.1%) and SimPO (21.5% and 22.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selective DPO outperforms standard DPO by 16.4 percentage points in WR on Mistral-7B (28.9% vs. 12.5%) and 9.8 points on Llama-3-8B (25.3% vs. 15.5%), confirming that filtering overly difficult examples yields substantial alignment gains. Against the strongest DPO variants, Selective DPO beats SimPO by 8.1 WR points on Mistral-7B (28.9% vs. 20.8%) and 5.0 points on Llama-3-8B (25.3% vs. 20.3%), and surpasses WPO by 5.2 and 3.1 WR points respectively. On Arena-Hard, Selective DPO leads SimPO (17.0% vs. 16.6% Mistral; 24.1% vs. 23.4% Llama-3) and WPO (17.0% vs. 16.7%; 24.1% vs. 23.1%), indicating the gains are not merely a length artifact. Data-correction baselines (Label Flipping, Label Smoothing) provide only marginal improvements over DPO (e.g., 13.1% vs. 12.5% WR on Mistral), supporting the paper's claim that difficult examples are not simply mislabeled. Among other data-selection strategies, CHES (16.6% WR), RM filtering (13.1%), and PPL-based selection (15.4%) all underperform Selective DPO by 12–16 WR points on Mistral-7B, demonstrating that validation-loss-based difficulty ranking is more effective than reward-margin or perplexity-based criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) by introducing a principled data selection step that filters examples exceeding the model's capacity, drawing on the curriculum learning literature's use of validation loss as a difficulty proxy (Wu et al., 2021; Bengio et al., 2009). It departs from prior alignment data selection work, which has focused on removing noisy or mislabeled data (label flipping, confidence-based filtering), by arguing the core issue is not data quality but the mismatch between example difficulty and model capacity. The paper positions Selective DPO as orthogonal to algorithmic DPO variants (SimPO, WPO, IPO, KTO), which modify the loss function, whereas this work modifies the training data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that more clean preference data can actively degrade alignment challenges a widespread assumption in the field and suggests practitioners should calibrate data difficulty to model capacity rather than simply maximizing dataset size. However, the principle is validated only in the DPO setting, limiting direct applicability to RLHF or other alignment paradigms. The observed GSM8K performance drop on Mistral-7B reveals a tension: filtering out difficult examples (which often involve mathematical reasoning) can erode downstream task capabilities, raising open questions about how to balance alignment quality with general competence. The method also introduces non-trivial computational overhead (six reference model trainings) and a length bias that may require additional regularization.
