## Problem

Medical AI models can localize tumors via segmentation masks but cannot explain *why* a region is suspicious using clinically meaningful terms, nor can they point to the specific visual evidence supporting each clinical concept. Existing approaches are fragmented: segmentation models give 'where' without 'why,' LLMs give 'why' without spatial grounding, and saliency maps give 'where' without clinical semantics. The paper addresses two compounding challenges: the absence of large-scale image-mask-rationale training data, and the prohibitive cost of pixel-level concept annotations needed to ground clinical terms to image regions.

## Input / Output

The model takes 2D prostate MRI slices (T2W, ADC, or DWI) as input and simultaneously produces three outputs: (1) segmentation masks for lesion, zone, and gland structures, (2) a textual rationale composed of clinical concepts (e.g., 'circumscribed, homogenous moderate hypointense focus') retrieved by traversing the PI-RADS Decision Tree in a shared vision-language embedding space, and (3) a per-concept visual evidence heatmap generated by computing patch-level image-text similarity. At inference, subsequent tree nodes condition on prior selections (e.g., signal characteristics are only inferred after lesion location is determined), mirroring the sequential logic of radiologist reasoning.

## Method

The architecture couples a MedSAM-based image encoder and mask decoder with a BiomedCLIP text encoder, trained jointly with Dice loss for segmentation and InfoNCE contrastive loss to learn a shared vision-language embedding space. The key technical contribution is a rationale-informed optimization that adds two self-supervised constraints—eliminating the need for pixel-level concept annotations: a *disentanglement constraint* requiring that heatmaps of clinically distinct concepts be separated (L2 distance ≥ ε₁), and a *localization constraint* requiring that the sum of all concept heatmaps align with the ground-truth segmentation mask (distance ≤ ε₂). These constrained objectives are converted to an unconstrained form via KKT conditions and Lagrange multipliers for tractable gradient-based training. The training data consists of 180K image-mask-rationale triples curated from 1,500 PI-CAI prostate MRI scans, where rationales are extracted from radiologist-authored PI-RADS reports using GPT-4o guided by a structured PI-RADS Decision Tree (PDT) to fill in implicit reasoning steps, then expanded via paraphrasing. Ablations confirm both constraints are necessary: disentanglement alone yields trivial non-overlapping random heatmaps, while only the localization constraint (without disentanglement) fails to separate individual concepts.

## Results

On the in-domain Rationale Dataset, the proposed method achieves 0.552 Lesion DSC, 0.574 AP, and 0.888 AUROC, outperforming the best baselines (Swin UNETR for segmentation, SSL CSwin U-Net for detection) by 2.0% in AP and 1.2% in average DSC. In zero-shot evaluation on Prostate158, the model surpasses the best baselines by 8.8% in average segmentation DSC (0.607 vs. 0.558) and 9.7% in cancer detection (0.700 vs. 0.621 average). For visual evidence localization, the model achieves 0.403 Relevant Mass Accuracy (RMA) versus 0.010 for the unconstrained baseline, a 40× improvement in grounding clinical concepts to correct image regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U-Net, the proposed method wins on Lesion DSC (0.552 vs. 0.504), AP (0.574 vs. 0.518), and AUROC (0.888 vs. 0.855), though it trails on Gland DSC (0.843 vs. 0.882). Versus Swin UNETR, the method substantially outperforms on Lesion DSC (0.552 vs. 0.431) and detection metrics, but Swin UNETR retains a slight edge on Gland DSC (0.898 vs. 0.843). Against the strongest detection baselines (SSL CSwin U-Net at 0.545 AP, 0.888 AUROC), the proposed method improves AP by 2.9% absolute while matching AUROC. SAM-based models (MA-SAM, SAMed) are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with the proposed method achieving 12% higher average DSC. In zero-shot transfer to Prostate158 and MSD-Prostate, the method beats all baselines including BiomedParse (which collapses to 0.271 avg DSC and 0.002 AP on Prostate158). For rationale generation and visual evidence localization, OpenAI o1 performs worse than random guessing on tumor classification and PI-RADS scoring, while the proposed model achieves 0.403 RMA versus o1's inability to produce spatially grounded explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads in medical AI: segmentation models (MedSAM, Swin UNETR) that localize 'where' without explaining 'why'; concept bottleneck models (CBMs, ProtoPNet) that provide clinical concepts but fail at spatial grounding; and vision-language models (BiomedCLIP, BiomedParse) that align image-text globally but cannot disentangle fine-grained concepts like 'capsular invasion' from 'benign hyperplasia.' The method builds directly on MedSAM's image encoder and mask decoder plus BiomedCLIP's text encoder, adding a contrastive InfoNCE objective to create a shared embedding space, then introduces disentanglement and localization constraints (solved via KKT conditions) to ground concepts to pixels without manual annotation. The PI-RADS Decision Tree–guided GPT-4o pipeline for rationale extraction is a novel data curation strategy that departs from prior work requiring pixel-level concept labels (Tschandl et al., Daneshjou et al., Bannur et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical trust barrier: radiologists need to audit *why* a model flagged a region, not just *where*. The 180K image-mask-rationale triple dataset and the self-supervised optimization (avoiding prohibitively expensive pixel-level concept annotations) provide a reusable pipeline that the authors argue extends to breast, lung, and liver cancers. Key limitations include the single-cancer, single-institution scope, the modest segmentation cost of adding constraints (0.758→0.746 DSC), and the fact that RMA evaluation relies on a single metric (Relevant Mass Accuracy) that may not capture all clinically relevant localization errors. Open questions include whether the PI-RADS Decision Tree abstraction generalizes to less structured reporting systems and whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric reasoning rather than 2D slice-level concepts.
