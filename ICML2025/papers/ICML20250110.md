## Problem

Dyna-style model-based RL algorithms like MBPO and ALM are widely adopted for their sample efficiency gains in OpenAI Gym, yet their performance in the equally standard DeepMind Control Suite has been largely unexamined. This paper reveals that adding synthetic rollouts—the core mechanism of Dyna—actually degrades performance below the base off-policy algorithm (SAC or DDPG) across most DMC environments, raising the question of whether this gap is fundamental or addressable with modern techniques.

## Input / Output

The agents consume proprioceptive state observations (joint positions, velocities, and related kinematic quantities) from MuJoCo-based continuous control environments and output continuous control actions. The evaluation spans 6 OpenAI Gym tasks and 15 challenging DMC tasks (hopper, humanoid, quadruped, walker, etc.), all sharing the MuJoCo physics backend but differing in reward normalization, termination conditions, physical parameters, and numerical integration schemes. The diagnostic experiments also involve varying the synthetic-to-real data ratio, applying periodic network resets, and substituting a perfect environment model for the learned ensemble.

## Method

This is a diagnostic empirical study rather than a new algorithm. The authors build a JAX-based reimplementation of MBPO (~40× faster than the original PyTorch code) and systematically ablate Dyna-style enhancements by comparing each algorithm against its 'no-Dyna' base (SAC for MBPO, DDPG/TD3 for ALM) under identical hyperparameters. They investigate three candidate failure mechanisms: (1) predictive model error, quantified via percent model error and tested by varying the synthetic-to-real ratio and substituting a perfect model; (2) critic divergence (over/underestimation) induced by non-stationary synthetic targets, mitigated with layer normalization; and (3) plasticity loss in the off-policy base networks, addressed via periodic reinitialization. Comprehensive hyperparameter sweeps (174 configurations, 441 runs) confirm that no tuning recovers MBPO's performance in DMC, and even a perfect 1-step model fails to let MBPO consistently surpass SAC, indicating the failure is more deeply rooted than model inaccuracy alone.

## Results

The paper's central finding is a stark benchmark-dependent performance gap: MBPO consistently outperforms its base algorithm SAC across all six OpenAI Gym environments, yet fails to improve beyond a randomly initialized policy in 6 out of 15 challenging DMC environments. The same failure pattern is observed for ALM (built on DDPG), confirming the issue is not specific to one implementation. Even granting MBPO access to a perfect 1-step predictive model, it still cannot consistently surpass SAC in 4 out of 6 DMC environments tested. The authors' JAX-based reimplementation achieves a ~40× wall-clock speedup over the original PyTorch MBPO and ~4× over ALM, reducing a 104 GPU-day experiment to ~4 GPU-days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (its base off-policy algorithm), MBPO wins decisively in OpenAI Gym, matching or exceeding SAC's sample efficiency and final return across all six Gym tasks. In DMC, the relationship inverts completely: MBPO fails to improve beyond a random policy in 6 of 15 challenging environments, while SAC with identical hyperparameters trains competent policies in the same settings. Against ALM (a Dyna-style algorithm built on DDPG rather than SAC), both algorithms exhibit the identical Gym-success/DMC-failure pattern, and MBPO matches or surpasses ALM in sample efficiency in Gym. Critically, even when MBPO is given a perfect 1-step environment model (bypassing all model-learning errors), it still cannot consistently outperform SAC in DMC, losing in 4 of 6 tested environments. The paper also shows that applying the same periodic-reset mitigation to both MBPO and SAC leaves SAC significantly ahead, confirming that Dyna-style synthetic rollouts actively degrade the base algorithm's performance in DMC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a diagnostic empirical study that builds directly on the Dyna-style model-based RL lineage, specifically MBPO (Janner et al., 2019) and ALM (Ghugare et al., 2022), and their respective base off-policy algorithms SAC and DDPG. It departs from the prior work's implicit assumption that Dyna-style synthetic rollouts provide consistent sample-efficiency gains by systematically auditing these algorithms across two equally standard MuJoCo-based benchmarks (OpenAI Gym and DMC) and showing the gains vanish—or reverse—in DMC. The paper also draws on recent work addressing critic divergence (Nauman et al., 2024; Hussing et al., 2024), plasticity loss (Lyle et al., 2022; Qiao et al., 2023), and model error (Gu et al., 2016; Sikchi et al., 2022) as candidate explanations, while noting that DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2025) represents a subclass of DMBRL that does succeed in DMC, suggesting the failure is specific to a particular architectural/algorithmic family rather than Dyna in general.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a ~1k-citation algorithm (MBPO) fails to improve beyond a random policy in 6 of 15 DMC environments—and that even a perfect predictive model does not restore its Gym-level advantage over SAC—challenges the community's confidence in Dyna-style methods and highlights a benchmark-overfitting problem that went unnoticed for years. The paper leaves the root cause unresolved: model error, critic divergence, and plasticity loss each contribute but none individually or jointly close the gap, pointing to deeper, unidentified algorithmic issues. The 40× JAX speedup lowers the computational barrier for future DMBRL research, and the broader call for more rigorous cross-benchmark evaluation and self-policing of influential algorithms has implications beyond this specific method family.
