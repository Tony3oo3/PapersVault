## Problem

Prior dynamic k-center algorithms each achieve near-optimality in at most two of the three key metrics (approximation ratio, update time, recourse), but none simultaneously: Bateni et al. (2023) give near-optimal approximation and update time but Ω(k) recourse, Lacki et al. (2024) and Forster & Skarlatos (2025) give O(1) recourse but Õ(poly(n)) update time, and Bhattacharya et al. (2024a) give Õ(k) update time and Õ(1) recourse but polylogarithmic approximation. This paper asks whether O(1)-approximation, Õ(k) update time, and O(1) recourse can be achieved simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a metric space (V,d) that evolves via a stream of point insertions and deletions, along with a fixed budget k and known bounds d_min, d_max on the non-zero distances (giving aspect ratio Δ). After each update, it must output a set S ⊆ V of at most k centers minimizing max_{x∈V} d(x,S), while guaranteeing bounded expected amortized recourse (number of points added/removed from S per update) and Õ(k) amortized update time. The guarantees hold against oblivious adversaries and in expectation.

## Method

The algorithm maintains a hierarchy of nested threshold graphs G_i at radii λ_i = 2^{i-2}·d_min, where each level's solution I_i is an MIS of G_i restricted to I_{i-1} (maintained via the DynamicMIS black box of Behnezhad et al., 2019). The nesting ensures that switching between levels (when the index i* with |I_{i*}| ≤ k changes) incurs only O(1) recourse, since the output is the first k points in a fixed lexicographic ordering across all levels. To reduce update time from Õ(n) to Õ(k), the algorithm is composed with a dynamic sparsifier (a dynamized Mettu-Plaxton hierarchy) that maintains a 4-approximate subspace U ⊆ V of size O(k log(n/k)) with O(1) amortized recourse, so the k-center algorithm only operates on the sparsified space of size Õ(k). The final approximation ratio is 20 (4 from the sparsifier plus 2·8 from the k-center algorithm), with O(k log⁵(n) log Δ) amortized update time and O(1) amortized recourse.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.3) is a dynamic k-center algorithm maintaining a 20-approximation with O(k log⁵(n) log Δ) expected amortized update time and O(1) expected amortized recourse against oblivious adversaries. This is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve O(1) approximation ratio, Õ(k) update time, and O(1) recourse. The intermediate result (Theorem 1.2) achieves an 8-approximation with O(n log⁴(n) log Δ) expected worst-case update time and 4 expected worst-case recourse before sparsification is applied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's algorithm is the first to simultaneously achieve O(1) approximation, Õ(k) update time, and O(1) recourse, a combination no prior method achieves. Compared to Bateni et al. 2023, it trades a slightly worse approximation (20 vs. 2+ε) for a dramatically better recourse bound (O(1) vs. Ω(k)), while maintaining comparable Õ(k) update time. Against Lacki et al. 2024 and Forster & Skarlatos 2025, it achieves a much faster update time (Õ(k) vs. Õ(poly(n))) but at the cost of a larger constant approximation (20 vs. O(1)) and amortized rather than worst-case recourse guarantees. Versus Bhattacharya et al. 2024a, it improves the approximation from O(log n log k) to a constant 20 and tightens recourse from Õ(1) to O(1), though it handles only oblivious rather than adaptive adversaries. The key trade-off is that the 20-approximation is a larger constant than the near-optimal 2+ε, but the algorithm is significantly simpler in design, combining a variant of Bateni et al.'s threshold-graph framework with the dynamic sparsifier of Bhattacharya et al. 2023a.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two existing techniques in a novel way: the threshold-graph/MIS framework of Bateni et al. (SODA'23) for dynamic k-center, and the dynamic sparsifier of Bhattacharya et al. (NeurIPS'23) originally designed for k-median. The key conceptual departure is ensuring the MISs across threshold graph levels are nested (by computing each MIS on the subgraph induced by the previous level), which yields constant recourse when switching levels—a property absent in Bateni et al.'s original algorithm. The sparsifier is then used as a black box to reduce the effective problem size from n to Õ(k), converting the Õ(n) update time of the nested-MIS algorithm into Õ(k), while the Mettu-Plaxton AlmostCover subroutine provides the underlying static clustering guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves an explicit open question from prior work: whether one can simultaneously achieve O(1) approximation, Õ(k) update time, and O(1) recourse for dynamic k-center. The 20-approximation (vs. the optimal 2) and the oblivious-adversary restriction are notable limitations, and the authors identify improving the approximation factor, tightening recourse to 8+ε (achieved in Appendix C via BufferedSparsifier), and extending to outliers as natural next steps. For the broader dynamic algorithms community, the result demonstrates that a simple composition of a stable MIS maintenance scheme with a sparsification layer can close the gap between previously incompatible metric guarantees, potentially informing similar compositions for other dynamic clustering objectives.
