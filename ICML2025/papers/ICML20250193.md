## Problem

Real-world multimodal data is frequently unpaired, making it impossible to learn a joint distribution directly. While Minimum Entropy Coupling (MEC) provides a principled information-theoretic framework for finding the optimal joint distribution given marginals, prior work has been confined to discrete distributions, leaving the continuous case—which covers images, omics profiles, and most practical data—unaddressed. Additionally, existing unpaired matching methods (optimal transport, GAN-based translation) require geometrically comparable spaces or hand-crafted cost functions, limiting their generality.

## Input / Output

For image translation, the method takes a 256×256 source-domain image and produces a translated image in the target domain by sampling from a learned conditional diffusion model, with a guidance scale parameter controlling the trade-off between fidelity to the source and realism in the target. For single-cell multi-omics, it takes a gene expression or chromatin accessibility profile (50-dimensional embedding) and generates a coupled sample in the other modality's space, after which the nearest observed cell is identified via Euclidean distance. Unlike deterministic mapping approaches, DDMEC is fundamentally generative: it samples from a coupling distribution, so multiple valid outputs are possible for a single input.

## Method

DDMEC formulates continuous MEC as minimizing conditional entropy (equivalently, negative log-likelihood) under soft marginal constraints, and instantiates this with two specular conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) that cooperate in an alternating optimization loop. Each model is first pretrained unconditionally on its marginal distribution, then fine-tuned as a conditional model using PPO-style reinforcement learning where the reward signal is the log-likelihood assigned by the *other* model to the generated sample pair, while KL-regularization to the pretrained weights enforces the marginal constraints. The joint consistency constraint (p_{X,Y}^θ ≈ p_{X,Y}^φ) is enforced by an additional denoising-matching loss that aligns the two models' joint distributions. Classifier-free guidance is used at inference to control conditioning strength, and DDIM sampling with 50–100 steps is employed for efficient generation. This cooperative, information-theoretic formulation eliminates the need for geometric cost functions or comparable feature spaces, making the method applicable to fundamentally different modalities.

## Results

On the single-cell alignment task, DDMEC achieves 66.3% cell-type and 46.0% sub-cell-type accuracy on PBMC, outperforming the best baseline scTopoGAN (61.7% / 41.3%), and 77.3% / 44.2% on BM, setting the best sub-cell-type score. On unpaired image translation, DDMEC attains the best FID of 57.89 on CAT→DOG (100 steps, guidance=7) and 34.86 on MALE→FEMALE (100 steps, guidance=2), while achieving the highest SSIM of 0.360 on WILD→DOG (50 steps, guidance=9). On the SNAREseq dataset, DDMEC achieves the best FOSCTTM of 0.147 and 98.6% label-transfer accuracy, on par with InfoOT (98.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among diffusion-based baselines, DDMEC outperforms SDDM (FID 62.29→57.89 on CAT→DOG; 44.37→34.86 on MALE→FEMALE), EGSDE (65.82→57.89; 41.93→34.86), ILVR (74.37→57.89; 46.12→34.86), and SDEdit (74.17→57.89; 49.43→34.86) on FID across all three image-translation tasks. On SSIM, DDMEC trails CUT (0.601 vs 0.397 on CAT→DOG) and SDEdit (0.572 vs 0.549 on MALE→FEMALE), but surpasses SDDM, EGSDE, and ILVR on WILD→DOG SSIM (0.346–0.360 vs 0.328–0.343). StarGANv2 achieves a slightly lower FID on CAT→DOG (54.88 vs 57.89) but with substantially worse SSIM (0.27 vs 0.397), indicating poor structural fidelity. In single-cell alignment, DDMEC dominates on PBMC (66.3% vs scTopoGAN's 61.7% cell-type; 46.0% vs 41.3% sub-cell-type) and achieves the best sub-cell-type accuracy on BM (44.2% vs SCOT's 31.6%), though SCOT leads on BM cell-type (90.5% vs 77.3%). On SNAREseq, DDMEC achieves the best FOSCTTM (0.147 vs InfoOT's 0.156) with near-identical accuracy (98.6% vs 98.8%), confirming competitive performance against the information-maximizing OT baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDMEC extends the Minimum Entropy Coupling (MEC) problem from its traditional discrete-distribution setting to arbitrary continuous distributions, using denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) as the parametric class for joint distributions. The training procedure builds directly on the RL fine-tuning framework of DPOK (Fan et al., 2023) with KL-regularization, the conditional control mechanism of ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023), and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant OT/Gromov-Wasserstein framing of the coupling problem by replacing geometric transport costs with an information-theoretic objective (joint entropy minimization), thereby eliminating the requirement that the two modalities live in geometrically comparable spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a general, assumption-light framework for coupling unpaired data that works across fundamentally different domains (single-cell multi-omics and image translation) without requiring domain-specific distance metrics or geometric comparability. The guidance scale provides an interpretable knob trading off marginal fidelity (SSIM) against generation quality (FID), which maps naturally to the MEC trade-off between entropy minimization and marginal constraint adherence. Open questions include scalability beyond two modalities, theoretical guarantees for the approximate cooperative optimization, and whether the RL fine-tuning overhead can be reduced. The method's reliance on pretrained unconditional diffusion models also limits its applicability to domains where such models are available or can be trained.
