## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a ~70% grounding error rate on geometric entities in mathematical diagrams, and correcting these visual perception errors yields a 12% improvement in reasoning accuracy. Existing efforts to improve mathematical MLLMs have focused on scaling instruction datasets and strengthening LLM backbones, leaving the root cause—failure to perceive fine-grained visual primitives such as lines, circles, angles, boundaries, and junctions—unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252995/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a mathematical diagram image (resized to 448×448 for the CLIP ViT-L encoder and 1000×1000 for the GeoGLIP Swin-Tiny encoder) paired with a text question, and produces a free-form text answer. Internally, GeoGLIP generates a five-level feature pyramid (F_geo^1 through F_geo^5) at progressively coarser resolutions, which are dynamically routed and fused channel-wise with CLIP visual tokens before projection into the LLM's embedding space, preserving the original sequence length.

## Method

PRIMITIVE introduces GeoGLIP, a geometric-grounded extension of GLIP (Faster-RCNN with Swin-Tiny backbone) trained via multi-task learning on shape grounding, boundary detection, and junction detection using synthetic Matplotlib-generated annotations and off-the-shelf detector labels. A cross-resolution mixture (MHSA between F_geo^2 and F_geo^4, upsampled and added to F_geo^1) produces an enriched high-resolution feature F_geo^1* for fine-grained boundary and junction decoding. A lightweight MLP feature router takes spatially pooled features from each GeoGLIP level plus the CLIP feature map and outputs soft routing weights, dynamically selecting which hierarchical feature maps contribute to the visual soft prompts. These weighted prompts are fused channel-wise with CLIP tokens and projected via an MLP into the LLM embedding space. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: multi-task visual perception for GeoGLIP, frozen-encoder visual-language alignment, and joint projector+LLM instruction tuning on Geo170K and MathV360K.

## Results

On MathVista (testmini), PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B achieves 50.4% top-1 accuracy, matching GPT-4V (49.9%) while using only 7B parameters. On MathVerse (testmini), PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B reaches 28.5% overall accuracy, outperforming all other 7B-parameter models by 12% over G-LLaVA (16.6%) and surpassing the larger LLaVA-NeXT-8B (19.3%). On GeoQA, PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B achieves 79.6% top-1 accuracy, and even the base PRIMITIVE-7B (LLaMA2-7B) reaches 67.0%, exceeding G-LLaVA (64.2%) and MAVIS-7B (66.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against G-LLaVA (same LLaMA2-7B backbone and instruction dataset), PRIMITIVE-7B improves by +7.7% on MathVerse (21.2% vs. 16.6%), +12.3% on MathVista (37.4% vs. 25.1%), and +2.8% on GeoQA (67.0% vs. 64.2%), confirming the benefit of geometric soft visual prompts over CLIP-only features. PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B (50.4%) matches GPT-4V (49.9%) on MathVista overall accuracy while using a 7B open-source model, and surpasses the 13B SPHINX-Plus (36.7%) by 13.7 points. On MathVerse, PRIMITIVE-7B (21.2%) exceeds LLaVA-NeXT-8B (19.3%) despite being smaller, and PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B (28.5%) leads all open-source 7B models by a wide margin over Math-LLaVA-13B (19.0%). On GeoQA, PRIMITIVE-7B (67.0%) edges out MAVIS-7B (66.7%), which was trained on an 8× larger mathematical instruction dataset (588K+834K vs. 40K+60K+110K), demonstrating that targeted visual perception training is more efficient than scaling instruction data. On the FunctionQA subset of MathVista, PRIMITIVE-Qwen2.5-7B (53.3%) substantially outperforms MAVIS-7B (40.3%) and GPT-4V (69.5% is the only model above it among those listed). The paper also shows that removing the GeoGLIP soft prompts (PRIMITIVE(-)) consistently degrades performance across all three LLM backbones (e.g., 48.7%→42.3% for DeepSeek, 50.4%→44.0% for Qwen2.5 on MathVista), isolating the contribution of the geometric visual encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GLIP (a Faster-RCNN-based open-set detector) into GeoGLIP, a geometric multi-task detector that simultaneously performs shape grounding, boundary detection, and junction detection on mathematical diagrams. The overall MLLM pipeline builds on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture, but the key conceptual departure is from the dominant paradigm in mathematical MLLMs (MAVIS, Math-LLaVA, G-LLaVA, GeoGPT4V) that focuses on scaling large-scale mathematical visual instruction datasets. Instead, the authors argue the bottleneck is fine-grained visual perception of geometric primitives and address it with a dedicated geometric vision encoder plus a dynamic feature router that generates visual soft prompts, drawing on junction/boundary detection literature and cross-resolution feature fusion techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GPT-4o misperceives geometric entities in ~70% of cases, and that correcting these errors yields a 12% accuracy gain, reframes the mathematical MLLM problem from one of data scale to one of visual perception quality. The practical implication is significant: a small synthetic dataset (40K images generated via Matplotlib) for visual encoder training outperforms approaches requiring 588K+834K instruction samples, offering a more scalable and cost-effective path. However, limitations remain: junction ground truth relies on an out-of-domain off-the-shelf model, the approach is sensitive to Gaussian noise, and generalization beyond geometric diagrams to other diagram types (charts, circuit diagrams, etc.) is untested. Open questions include whether the feature router's decisions can be made interpretable and whether real-world pixel-level annotations would further close the gap.
