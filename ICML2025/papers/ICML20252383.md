## Problem

Existing stochastic variance-reduction methods for variational inequalities and generalized equations either require monotonicity assumptions, certify convergence via gap functions that are inapplicable to nonmonotone settings, or directly approximate the operator G without exploiting the structure of consecutive iterates. No single-loop method achieves the state-of-the-art O(n + n^{2/3} ε^{-2}) oracle complexity for nonmonotone problems using the operator residual norm as the certification metric.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum operator G = (1/n)Σᵢ Gᵢ (with n ≫ 1 components) and a maximally monotone multivalued mapping T, starting from an initial point x⁰. It produces an ε-solution certified by the operator residual norm E[‖Gxᵏ + vᵏ‖²] ≤ ε² (or the FBS residual for the constrained case), with oracle complexity O(n + n^{2/3} ε^{-2}) evaluations of individual Gᵢ. Unlike gap-function-based methods, this residual metric is directly meaningful in nonmonotone settings and cannot be converted to a duality gap under the weak-Minty solution assumption.

## Method

The key insight is to construct a forward-reflected quantity S_γᵏ = Gxᵏ − γGx^{k−1} (with γ ∈ (1/2,1)) from two consecutive iterates, and to build unbiased variance-reduced estimators specifically for S_γᵏ rather than for Gxᵏ directly. Two estimators are designed: a loopless-SVRG variant using a random snapshot point wᵏ updated with probability p, and a SAGA variant maintaining a table of per-component evaluations. The VFR method solves the nonlinear equation (NE) via the simple update x^{k+1} = xᵏ − ηS̃_γᵏ, while the VFRBS method solves the generalized inclusion (NI) by adding a backward resolvent step J_{γηT} and a correction term γ⁻¹(2γ−1)(yᵏ − xᵏ) that distinguishes it from all existing deterministic and stochastic FRBS schemes. The choice γ ∈ (1/2,1) excludes classical optimistic-gradient and FRBS methods as special cases, and the weak-Minty solution condition (Assumption 1.4) permits nonmonotone operators while enabling the O(1/K) residual convergence rate.

## Results

Both proposed algorithms (VFR for nonlinear equations and VFRBS for generalized inclusions) achieve an oracle complexity of O(n + n^{2/3} ε^{-2}) evaluations of G_i to obtain an ε-solution in operator residual norm, matching the best-known complexity of SVRG and SAGA in stochastic nonconvex optimization. In numerical experiments on nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems (p=100, n=5000 and p=200, n=10000) and logistic regression with ambiguous features (a9a: 134 features/3561 samples; w8a: 311 features/45546 samples), the loopless SVRG variant (LVFR-svrg) consistently achieves the fastest convergence in relative residual norm, significantly outperforming all three baselines across all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: the deterministic optimistic gradient method (OG, Daskalakis et al. 2018), the variance-reduced FRBS scheme (VFRBS, Alacaoglu et al. 2023), and the variance-reduced extragradient (VEG, Alacaoglu & Malitsky 2022). In the unconstrained nonconvex-nonconcave minimax experiments, all three SVRG/SAGA variants of the proposed VFR significantly outperform OG, VFRBS, and VEG in relative residual norm; notably, VFRBS and VEG fail to beat the deterministic OG baseline. In the constrained (simplex) minimax case, the proposed SVRG variants again dominate, with VFR-saga performing comparably to VEG and VFRBS matching OG. On the logistic regression with ambiguous features task (a9a and w8a), the three proposed variants (VFR-svrg, LVFR-svrg, VFR-saga) all outperform the three baselines, with OG being the worst performer overall and VEG trailing behind VFRBS. The key theoretical advantage is that the proposed methods operate under the weaker weak-Minty solution assumption (covering nonmonotone operators) rather than the co-coercive or monotone assumptions required by all three baselines, while achieving the same O(n + n^{2/3} ε^{-2}) complexity as SVRG/SAGA in nonconvex optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard approach in stochastic variance-reduction methods for variational inequalities and root-finding, which directly approximate the operator G. Instead, it constructs unbiased variance-reduced estimators for the intermediate forward-reflected quantity S_γ^k = Gx^k − γGx^{k−1}, a linear combination of two consecutive operator evaluations. This reparameterization, combined with the forward-reflected-backward splitting framework (Malitsky & Tam, 2020) and loopless SVRG/SAGA estimators (Kovalev et al., 2020; Defazio et al., 2014), yields single-loop algorithms that work under the weaker weak-Minty solution condition rather than the monotonicity or co-coercivity assumptions required by prior methods (Alacaoglu & Malitsky, 2022; Davis, 2022; Cai et al., 2024). The choice γ ∈ (1/2, 1) deliberately excludes classical optimistic gradient and FRBS schemes as special cases, making the resulting algorithms genuinely distinct from existing deterministic and stochastic counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The methods achieve the state-of-the-art O(n + n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) oracle complexity for nonmonotone generalized equations, matching the best known bounds for SVRG/SAGA in nonconvex optimization, while operating under a strictly weaker assumption (weak-Minty) than prior variance-reduced methods. The VFRBS algorithm for nonlinear inclusions is entirely novel, featuring a correction term γ^{-1}(2γ−1)(y^k − x^k) absent from all existing deterministic and stochastic splitting methods. Key limitations include large constant factors (Γ ≈ 731–2816) and the inability to leverage biased estimators (SARAH, STORM) due to the absence of an objective function for controlling bias terms. Open questions include whether nested or multi-loop enhancements can further improve the ε-dependence, and whether the S_γ^k estimation framework extends naturally to other operator structures beyond finite sums.
