## Problem

Self-evolving training—where models iteratively learn from their own outputs—has shown promise for text-only reasoning but remains underexplored for multimodal reasoning, where the interplay of visual and textual modalities introduces additional complexity. No unified framework exists for multimodal self-evolving training, the critical design factors (training method, reward model, prompt variation) have not been systematically studied, and a central challenge of performance saturation due to diminishing exploration potential has gone unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (encoded via SigLIP SoViT-400m/14, sliced into up to 9 segments each compressed to 96 queries via a perceiver resampler) paired with text queries as input, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace with intermediate steps followed by a final answer. Training data comes from MathV360K (180K labeled + 180K unlabeled examples), and during rollout the model samples 16 responses per query at a dynamically adjusted temperature. The PRM scores each intermediate reasoning step, and the final output is structured as step-by-step reasoning concluding with an answer that is matched against ground truth for reward computation.

## Method

M-STAR reframes self-evolving training through an RL lens and combines three optimized static design choices with a dynamic exploration mechanism. For training method, it introduces Continuous Self-Evolving, which inherits optimizer states and learning-rate schedulers across iterations (unlike ReST^EM or iterative RFT that reset them), with a 25% iteration interval to balance online and offline learning. For reward, it trains the first multimodal Process Reward Model via Monte Carlo rollouts (16 completions per step, 8 repetitions) and uses it as a Top-2 reranker over ground-truth-filtered correct responses rather than as a verifier, since it underperforms at Best-of-N verification but effectively identifies responses with fewer irrelevant steps. For prompt variation, it finds that unlabeled prompts only help with oracle reward signals and hurt performance with the PRM, so training proceeds on labeled data only. Orthogonally, it introduces Reward-Pass@2 (the fraction of validation samples where correct responses appear in the PRM's top-2) as a monitoring metric and automatically adjusts the sampling temperature every 2 iterations (searching 0.3–1.6 in steps of 0.1) to sustain exploration and counteract the observed Pass@K decay, preventing the performance saturation that plagues fixed-temperature self-evolving training.

## Results

On MathVista, M-STAR achieves 59.5% accuracy with MiniCPM-V-2.5 (8B), 54.5% with Phi-3.5-Vision (4B), and 50.3% with InternVL2-2B (2B), outperforming the best iterative baselines (Iterative RFT and ReST^EM) by 3.8–4.4 points across all three model sizes. Across five diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, M3CoT, MMStar-R, MMBench-R, AI2D), M-STAR yields average improvements of +6.6, +8.8, and +27.1 points over the respective warmup-only baselines for the 8B, 4B, and 2B models. The largest single-benchmark gain is on M3CoT for InternVL2-2B (+28.9 points), while the smallest is on MMStar-R for InternVL2-2B (+0.2 points), indicating that smaller models struggle to generalize beyond their training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-STAR consistently outperforms both named baselines across all three model sizes on MathVista. Against ReST^EM (Singh et al., 2023), M-STAR gains +4.4 points on MiniCPM-V-2.5 (59.5 vs. 55.1), +4.0 on Phi-3.5-Vision (54.5 vs. 50.5), and +2.4 on InternVL2-2B (50.3 vs. 47.9). Against Iterative RFT (Yuan et al., 2023), the margins are +3.8, +4.3, and +2.8 points respectively. The gap is largest for the mid-size Phi-3.5-Vision model, suggesting that the continuous self-evolving + PRM reranking + adaptive temperature strategy benefits most from models with sufficient capacity to exploit the richer training signal. Notably, even the intermediate step of adding PRM re-ranking to continuous self-evolving already surpasses both baselines, indicating that the process reward model contributes the single largest incremental gain over the iterative training loop alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-evolving training paradigm (STaR, ReST^EM, RFT) from text-only reasoning to the multimodal setting, reframing the iterative generate-improve loop through an RL lens to systematically identify three design axes: training method, reward model, and prompt variation. It builds directly on the Process Reward Model literature (Lightman et al., Math-Shepherd) by training the first multimodal PRM via Monte Carlo rollouts, and on the continuous-optimization insight from online RL to propose 'continuous self-evolving' as a smoother interpolation between offline iterative training and fully online policy optimization. The adaptive temperature mechanism draws on the exploration-exploitation trade-off observed in text-only self-improvement (Wu et al., 2024; B-STaR), but applies it to the multimodal context where exploration loss was previously uncharacterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic design guidelines for multimodal self-evolving training, which is practically important as high-quality multimodal CoT annotations remain scarce. The finding that the multimodal PRM functions as a reranker rather than a verifier (underperforming on BoN and weighted voting) challenges the assumption that step-level reward models must be strong verifiers to be useful, and opens the question of whether better step-level annotation strategies (e.g., from stronger teacher models) could unlock verifier-level performance. Key limitations include the PRM's poor generalization to unlabeled data (making prompt variation ineffective), the modest gains from adaptive temperature (≈0.3 points over static), and the observation that smaller models (InternVL2-2B) struggle to generalize self-evolved reasoning across domains—suggesting a capacity floor for this training paradigm.
