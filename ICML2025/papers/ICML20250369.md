## Problem

As context length scales to 128K tokens, self-attention incurs quadratic computational cost and the context becomes dominated by redundant information that dilutes attention on truly relevant tokens. Existing efficient attention methods (StreamingLLM, LM-Infinite, MInference) rely on fixed sparsity patterns that discard intermediate tokens, risking loss of critical information needed for comprehensive long-context understanding. The open question is how to reduce context redundancy while guaranteeing that every token retains access to all preceding tokens.

## Input / Output

The method takes a token sequence X ∈ R^{L×d} (up to 128K tokens) and produces per-token attention output representations of the same dimensionality, making it a drop-in replacement for standard self-attention in pretrained LLMs (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B). Two hyperparameters control the trade-off: group size g (default 16) determines compression granularity, and local window size s (default 1024) sets the neighborhood range for fine-grained attention. Both are adjustable at inference time without retraining, enabling dynamic latency-accuracy trade-offs for variable traffic.

## Method

CCA-Attention comprises two complementary modules fused via a single softmax over concatenated key-value matrices. The globality-aware pooling module partitions the sequence into m = ⌊L/g⌋ groups and, for each group, computes a weighted sum of its tokens using the last token's query against the group's keys (Eq. 2), yielding one 'core token' per group that dynamically encodes the most semantically important information. Attention is then computed between all L queries and only m core-token keys/values, reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(Lm + Ls). The locality-preserving module ensures each query also attends to its nearest s tokens (with a group-alignment offset) to capture fine-grained local dependencies. A theoretical reachability proof (Proposition 1) shows that every token's output is a positive-weighted combination of all preceding tokens' values, guaranteeing full information exchange. The method requires only minimal fine-tuning (1000 steps on SlimPajama) to integrate into pretrained LLMs, and reduces KV cache from O(L) to O(L/g + s), achieving 7.9× speedup at 128K context.

## Results

On LongBench-E with LLaMA2-7B-32K, CCA-LLM achieves an average score of 21.86, outperforming all efficient attention baselines (StreamingLLM: 14.95, LM-Infinite: 18.76, MInference: 21.14) while remaining close to vanilla self-attention (22.11). At 128K context length, CCA-LLM achieves a 7.9× first-token latency speedup (15.89s vs. 124.85s) and 46% KV-cache memory reduction (19.12GB vs. 35.58GB) compared to full self-attention. On the multi-document EM Score benchmark at 128K, CCA-LLM scores 32.0, surpassing vanilla self-attention (30.3), MInference (28.9), and StreamingLLM (25.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCA-LLM outperforms all three efficient attention baselines on LongBench-E average score: 21.86 vs. StreamingLLM (14.95), LM-Infinite (18.76), and MInference (21.14) on LLaMA2-7B-32K, with a similar margin on LLaMA2-7B-80K (22.24 vs. 14.94, 21.20, 22.08). In terms of efficiency, CCA-LLM achieves the largest speedup (3.5×/5.7×) and the greatest KV-cache reduction (46%/44%) among all methods, whereas MInference only reduces memory by 6–10% and does not reduce KV cache during decoding. Compared to vanilla self-attention, CCA-LLM trades a small accuracy drop (21.86 vs. 22.11 on 32K; 22.24 vs. 22.42 on 80K) for a 3.5–5.7× latency reduction and ~45% memory savings. On the 128K EM Score benchmark, CCA-LLM (32.0) actually surpasses vanilla self-attention (30.3), suggesting that redundancy reduction improves long-context retrieval. The method also generalizes to newer architectures, matching or exceeding MInference on LLaMA3.1-8B-128K (37.81 vs. 37.74) and Qwen2.5-7B-128K (38.08 vs. 36.72) while delivering 3.1×/3.9× speedups and 49%/45% memory reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCA-Attention carves new ground at the intersection of sparse attention and context compression. It departs from fixed-pattern sparse attention (Longformer, BigBird, MInference) and token-discarding approaches (StreamingLLM, LM-Infinite) by introducing a dynamic, content-aware mechanism: groups of tokens are compressed into 'core tokens' via a learned weighted pooling that uses the last token in each group as a query, while a locality-preserving sliding window retains fine-grained neighborhood context. Unlike linear attention reformulations (RetNet, Linear Transformer) that require training from scratch, or context compression methods (Gisting, Compressive Transformers) that rely on auxiliary networks or special tokens, CCA-Attention is a drop-in replacement for standard self-attention that preserves input/output dimensions and can be integrated into pretrained LLMs with only 2–5B tokens of fine-tuning. The theoretical reachability guarantee (Proposition 1) distinguishes it from prior sparse methods by formally ensuring every token retains a communication path to all preceding tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.9× prefilling speedup at 128K context combined with 46–49% KV-cache reduction addresses two of the most acute bottlenecks in deploying long-context LLMs in production, and the inference-time tunability of group size g and window size s offers a practical lever for serving under variable traffic. However, the accuracy gains over the strongest baseline (MInference) are modest on LongBench-E and RULER, and the method still requires non-trivial fine-tuning (1000 steps on 8×A800s) even in its 'partial' variant. Open questions include whether the compression quality degrades at scales beyond 128K, how the method interacts with multi-head or multi-query attention variants, and whether the surprising MMLU improvements (up to ~20 points over full self-attention in supplementary Table 6) generalize or are an artifact of the specific RoPE modification used.
