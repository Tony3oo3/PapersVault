## Problem

Existing online learning theory for bilateral trade and brokerage is almost entirely non-contextual, despite the fact that real-world brokers in over-the-counter markets routinely observe rich contextual information (asset features, market conditions) before proposing a trading price. This paper fills that gap by developing the first parametric (linear) contextual online learning theory for brokerage, providing tight regret guarantees that were previously unavailable for this setting.

## Input / Output

At each round the broker observes a d-dimensional context vector c_t ∈ [0,1]^d and must output a single scalar brokerage price P_t ∈ [0,1]. The feedback is unusually limited: in the 2-bit setting, the broker only receives two indicators (whether P_t ≤ V_t and whether P_t ≤ W_t), which the paper notes is not even sufficient to reconstruct standard bandit feedback since the posted price directly determines what information is revealed. In the full-feedback variant, the actual valuations V_t, W_t ∈ [0,1] are revealed. Valuations are modeled as zero-mean perturbations of a hidden linear market value m_t = c_t^T φ with unknown φ ∈ [0,1]^d.

## Method

The key structural insight (Lemma 2.1) is that posting the unknown market value maximizes expected gain from trade, and the regret of any suboptimal price is at most quadratic in its distance from the market value—this holds even when the two traders' noise distributions differ. Algorithm 1 exploits this via an adaptive explore/exploit rule: a deterministic threshold on the context norm in the inverse of the accumulated design matrix decides whether to explore (post a uniform random price, yielding an unbiased estimate of m_t) or exploit (post c_t^T φ̂ from a ridge-regression estimate of φ). This yields a Õ(√(LdT)) regret upper bound with a matching Ω(√(LdT)) lower bound under 2-bit feedback, and an O(Ld ln T) bound (also tight) under full feedback. The paper further proves that without the bounded-density assumption on valuations, the problem becomes unlearnable even with full feedback, establishing the necessity of this regularity condition.

## Results

The paper establishes tight regret bounds for contextual online brokerage. Under 2-bit feedback with bounded density (L), Algorithm 1 achieves R_T ≤ 1 + 6√(LdT ln T) (Theorem 3.1), matching a worst-case lower bound of Ω(√(LdT)) (Theorem 3.2) up to a √(ln T) factor. Under full feedback with bounded density, Algorithm 2 achieves the exponentially faster rate R_T ≤ 1 + 4Ld ln T (Theorem 4.1), which is also tight (Theorem 4.2). Without the bounded density assumption, the problem is unlearnable even with full feedback: R_T ≥ T/32 (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The comparisons are against prior theoretical results. The non-contextual brokerage problem of Bolić et al. (2024) achieves M ln T and √(MT) rates for full and 2-bit feedback respectively, but requires traders to share the same valuation distribution; this paper extends to the contextual setting with arbitrary (possibly different) distributions while maintaining the same parametric dependence. Gaucher et al. (2025) study a noisy linear contextual bilateral trade problem and obtain a T^{2/3} rate under 2-bit feedback, but their algorithm cannot be directly adapted here because the reduction to seller/buyer roles breaks the independence assumption. The key distinction of this paper is achieving tight dependence on all relevant parameters (L, d, T) simultaneously, whereas most prior bilateral trade works obtain at best tight dependence on the time horizon T only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-contextual online brokerage problem of Bolić et al. (2024) to the contextual setting, where the broker observes a d-dimensional context before proposing a price. It also generalizes the structural result of Bolić et al. by removing the restrictive same-distribution assumption on traders' valuations, showing the market value remains optimal even when traders have arbitrarily different (but zero-mean) noise distributions. The paper departs from Gaucher et al. (2025), who study contextual bilateral trade with fixed seller/buyer roles, by handling the brokerage setting where roles are flexible, and from Bachoc et al. (2025), which addresses a non-parametric contextual brokerage variant, by providing a parametric (linear) model with tight guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a complete theoretical picture with matching upper and lower bounds for contextual brokerage under both 2-bit and full feedback, with tight dependence on all parameters (dimension, density bound, horizon). The unlearnability result (Theorem 5.2) is a sharp negative: without bounded densities, the problem is unlearnable even with full feedback and prior knowledge of all market values, highlighting that the bounded-density assumption is not merely technical but fundamental. Open questions include the 'inherent value' variant where the market value is not the average of traders' valuations, and extending these results to other contextual market problems (market making, auctions, taxation) that remain open.
