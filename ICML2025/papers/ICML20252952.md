## Problem

Existing verify-and-improve methods for LLM reasoning suffer from two key limitations: a restricted feedback space (e.g., fixed compiler messages or tool outputs) and the absence of joint training between the LLM agent and its feedback provider. This results in suboptimal coordination during iterative refinement, where the critic's feedback is not co-optimized with the actor's ability to act on it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252952/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a math problem x as input and produces a final answer via majority voting over an initial response plus L refinement turns (typically L=4 at inference, giving 5 total answers). At each turn, the state contains only the problem and the most recent answer (Markovian assumption), not the full conversation history, which enables the trained policy to generalize to arbitrary test-time horizons beyond the single refinement step used during training. Training data is self-generated by sampling n=8 candidate responses per state from the reference policy on OpenMathInstruct-2 problems.

## Method

DPSDP formulates the actor-critic refinement loop as a 3-step MDP (initial answer → critic feedback → refined answer) and applies a KL-regularized policy optimization objective solved via DPO loss on self-generated pairwise preference data. The key design choices are: (1) a reduced-context state definition where each agent sees only the latest answer, enabling horizon generalization from 1 train-time refinement to 5+ test-time refinements; (2) Q-value estimation by rolling out the reference policy, which eliminates loop-carried dependencies across MDP steps and consolidates all pairwise data into a single DPO training step; and (3) amplification of binary Q-values (0 or 1) to large values so the cross-entropy loss reduces exactly to standard DPO. The actor and critic are trained jointly via separate DPO objectives on their respective pairwise datasets, with a preliminary SFT phase on a capable model's feedback to bootstrap reflection capabilities.

## Results

On MATH 500, DPSDP with Ministral-8B improves majority-voting accuracy over five refinement turns (m1@t5) from 57.2% (SFT baseline) to 63.2%, and first-turn accuracy (p1@t1) from 55.4% to 58.2%. On GSM8K, m1@t5 rises from 82.3% to 89.1%. Similar gains are observed with Llama-3.1-8B (MATH 500 m1@t5: 53.4% → 58.4%) and Qwen2.5-3B (MATH 500 m1@t5: 60.4% → 62.0%). On out-of-distribution Olympiad Bench, Ministral-based DPSDP improves m1@t5 from 23.4% (SFT) to 27.0%, demonstrating generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSDP substantially outperforms STaR on MATH 500 m1@t5 across all three base models (e.g., 63.2% vs. 55.0% for Ministral), confirming that incorporating both correct and incorrect trajectories during training is critical for iterative refinement. Against STaR-DPO, DPSDP achieves a 4.4-point gain on MATH 500 m1@t5 (63.2% vs. 58.8% for Ministral), attributed to the restart-style data collection that enables broader action-space exploration; on GSM8K the two methods are nearly tied (89.1% vs. 89.5%). Compared to Oracle-RISE, which has access to ground-truth correctness feedback at each turn, DPSDP achieves a slightly lower m1@t5 on MATH 500 (63.2% vs. 65.4%) but notably surpasses it on p1@t5 (70.0% vs. 65.8%), indicating that the generative critic's feedback drives more active solution-space exploration than binary oracle signals. On the hardest OOD benchmark (Olympiad Bench), DPSDP (27.0% m1@t5) still trails Oracle-RISE (30.6%), but the gap narrows considerably relative to the SFT baseline, suggesting the multi-agent reflection process partially compensates for the absence of oracle feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Policy Search by Dynamic Programming (PSDP) framework from Bagnell et al. (2003) to the multi-turn LLM refinement setting, replacing the intractable argmax over an open-ended action space with a KL-regularized objective solvable via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). It builds directly on the multi-turn MDP formulation for iterative self-improvement introduced by Qu et al. (2024, RISE) and Kumar et al. (2024), but departs from them in two key ways: (1) it introduces a separate critic agent jointly trained with the actor, expanding the feedback space beyond fixed oracles or binary correctness signals, and (2) it defines Markovian states containing only the most recent answer, enabling horizon generalization at test time. Compared to STaR and STaR-DPO, DPSDP adds a restart-style data collection mechanism and multi-agent coordination that the paper shows yields meaningfully better exploration of the solution space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical performance guarantee (Theorem 1) is a rare contribution in the LLM refinement literature, providing a concrete bound on how far the learned policy can be from optimal under standard concentrability assumptions. The finding that non-Markovian (full-history) states degrade performance due to distribution shift challenges the intuitive assumption that more context is always beneficial, and has practical implications for how multi-turn agent systems should be architected. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains (code, science), whether online/iterative variants can further close the gap to oracle feedback, and whether the single-agent positive transfer observed on easier benchmarks can be harnessed for harder tasks.
