## Problem

Post-training quantization methods designed for Transformer LLMs degrade severely when applied to RWKV models: smooth- and rotation-based scalar quantization (e.g., QuaRot) introduces >99% FLOP overhead because RWKV's non-linear operators (token-shift, Sigmoid, exponential) block parameter fusion, while vector quantization (e.g., VPTQ) suffers >16% accuracy loss because RWKV weights are more uniformly distributed than LLaMA weights, making clustering ineffective. No comprehensive PTQ framework existed for the RWKV family.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained RWKV model weights (RWKV-6, RWKV-7, or VRWKV variants) and 128 calibration samples from the corresponding test dataset. It produces quantized weights at an effective 3.275 bits per weight, achieved by applying 3.25-bpw scalar quantization (GPTQ) to approximately 90% of layers and 3.5-bpw vector quantization (GPTVQ) to the remaining 10%, with the per-layer assignment determined automatically by the proxy. For VQ-assigned layers, codebooks are optimized specifically for RWKV's element-wise multiplication operator rather than standard matrix multiplication.

## Method

RWKVQuant introduces a coarse-to-fine proxy that assigns each weight tensor to either SQ or VQ in O(M) time (vs. O(2^M) exhaustive search). The coarse-grained proxy computes the Information Entropy of normalized inter-value gaps from sorted weights to measure overall uniformity; the fine-grained proxy applies a Taylor expansion of this entropy to extract weighted high-order central moments (variance, skewness, kurtosis) that detect local outliers invisible to the coarse measure. Only when both proxies fall below their thresholds is SQ applied; otherwise VQ is used. Additionally, the paper proposes a codebook optimization for RWKV's element-wise multiplication modules: the KMeans clustering is weighted by squared calibration activations (reflecting each position's contribution to output error), and a percentile-based clipping operation is applied to activation batches before averaging to prevent outliers from distorting the representative features used for codebook generalization.

## Results

RWKVQuant quantizes RWKV models to 3.275 bpw (hybrid of 3.25-bit SQ and 3.5-bit VQ) with minimal accuracy loss. On RWKV6-14B, it achieves 62.69% average zero-shot accuracy and 2.89 LAMBADA PPL, compared to 63.65% and 3.02 for full precision (less than 1% accuracy drop). On RWKV6-7B, it reaches 60.19% accuracy and 3.23 PPL vs. 61.69% and 3.21 for FP. The method also delivers 2.14× inference speedup and 2.83× memory saving on RWKV6-14B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RWKVQuant outperforms all seven baselines (RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, QuaRot, KMeans, GPTVQ, VPTQ) on nearly every model and metric. Among SQ methods, GPTQ is the strongest baseline but still trails RWKVQuant by 2.5% accuracy on RWKV6-14B (60.18 vs. 62.69) and 0.9% on RWKV6-7B (59.28 vs. 60.19); AWQ and QuaRot suffer severe degradation on RWKV (e.g., AWQ drops to 49.35% on RWKV6-14B vs. 63.65% FP), confirming the paper's claim that non-linear operators block parameter fusion. Among VQ methods, GPTVQ is the closest competitor on PPL for large models (3.15 vs. 2.89 on RWKV6-14B) but loses on accuracy (59.63 vs. 62.69); KMeans achieves competitive PPL on RWKV6-14B (4.61) but lags significantly on accuracy (61.40). On the smallest model (RWKV7-0.1B), the gap is most dramatic: RWKVQuant achieves 18.41 PPL while the best baseline (GPTVQ) reaches only 23.75, and QuaRot catastrophically fails at 243.99 PPL. On vision tasks (Table 3), RWKVQuant also leads on classification and segmentation for both RWKV-T and RWKV-S, with detection performance close to the best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) literature for LLMs—encompassing scalar quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QuaRot, SpinQuant) and vector quantization methods (GPTVQ, VPTQ, AQLM)—to the RWKV architecture family, for which no comprehensive PTQ framework previously existed. It departs from prior SQ work by showing that smooth- and rotation-based parameter fusion is blocked by RWKV's non-linear operators (token-shift, Sigmoid, exponential), and from prior VQ work by demonstrating that RWKV's more uniformly distributed weights (≈60% of layers vs. ≈10% in LLaMA) degrade K-Means clustering. The proposed hybrid strategy, guided by an Information Entropy-based coarse proxy and a Taylor-expansion fine proxy, is a novel combination that selectively applies GPTQ-style SQ and GPTVQ-style VQ per layer, with a codebook optimization tailored to RWKV's element-wise multiplication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RWKVQuant is the first comprehensive PTQ framework for the RWKV family, enabling practical deployment on resource-constrained devices (2.14× speedup, 2.83× memory saving at ~3-bit for RWKV-6-14B with <1% accuracy loss). The empirical finding that RWKV weights are substantially more uniform than LLaMA's provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why standard VQ methods underperform on this architecture. Open questions remain: the thresholds τc and τf are set empirically via percentile heuristics rather than learned, the bpw is fixed at 3.275, and the proxy operates at layer granularity—finer-grained (channel- or block-level) decisions and adaptive bit allocation are left as future work. The framework's applicability to other RNN-like architectures with similar non-linear fusion paths is unexplored.
