## Problem

While foundation models have transformed language and vision, no unified model has successfully addressed the three core challenges of scientific PDE solving simultaneously: multi-scale (handling 1D/2D/3D data with varying grid resolutions), multi-physics (modeling coupled physical quantities like velocity, pressure, and density), and physical alignment (incorporating governing equations as constraints). Existing neural operator approaches are restricted to fixed grids, single dimensions, or single-time-step windows, limiting their transferability across diverse physical systems.

## Input / Output

The model consumes spatial-temporal physical field data at arbitrary grid resolutions (e.g., 64×64 to 512×512 for 2D, 64³ to 128³ for 3D) and PDE equation text in LaTeX form. The Fourier encoder with TopK mode truncation maps all grid sizes to a fixed-length frequency representation, enabling unified processing without retraining. Output is the predicted physical field at the next timestep (autoregressive rollout), and for inverse problems, estimated physical parameters such as forcing or buoyancy coefficients.

## Method

OmniArch uses a Fourier Encoder-decoder (FFT with TopK mode selection, IFFT with zero-padding for decoding) to convert spatial fields into fixed-length frequency-domain tokens, naturally unifying 1D/2D/3D inputs of different resolutions. A shared LLaMA-based Transformer backbone processes these tokens with a novel Temporal Mask that enables full intra-timestep attention among all coupled physical quantities while enforcing causal inter-timestep dependencies, mirroring multi-step PDE solvers. For physics-informed fine-tuning, the PDE-Aligner employs contrastive learning in the frequency domain, aligning phase-difference and magnitude-ratio fingerprints of field evolution with BERT-encoded PDE equation captions, augmented by an energy conservation term enforcing Parseval's theorem. Pre-training uses nRMSE loss across 11 PDE types from PDEBench/PDEArena, and fine-tuning subtracts the alignment loss from the simulation loss to steer predictions toward the correct physical regime.

## Results

OmniArch-L with PDE-Aligner fine-tuning achieves state-of-the-art nRMSE across 11 PDE types from PDEBench and PDEArena. Headline results include 0.0200 on 1D CFD (98.7% improvement over FNO's 1.4100), 0.0041 on 1D Advection (beating PDEformer-1's 0.0043), 0.0012 on 2D Shallow Water (vs. MPP-L's 0.0022), and 0.1671 on 3D Maxwell (vs. FNO's 0.1906). The PDE-Aligner fine-tuning step adds a consistent 21–24% improvement over pre-training alone across all dimensions. On 3D CFD, FNO (0.3052) still outperforms OmniArch-L (0.3723), remaining the one task where the foundation model does not lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against task-specific experts, OmniArch-L (FT) dominates: it reduces nRMSE by 98.7% over FNO on 1D CFD (0.0200 vs. 1.4100), by 81.6% on 2D Incompressible NS (0.0827 vs. 0.2574), and by 12.3% on 3D Maxwell (0.1671 vs. 0.1906). Among unified pre-training models, OmniArch-L (FT) outperforms MPP-L on all shared 2D tasks (e.g., 0.0125 vs. 0.0178 on 2D CFD; 0.0012 vs. 0.0022 on SWE) and beats DPOT-L on 2D Reac. (0.0084 vs. 0.0263) and 3D CFD (0.3723 vs. 0.4321). The sole exception is 3D CFD, where FNO (0.3052) still leads OmniArch-L (0.3723), and 1D Advection, where PDEformer-1 (0.0043) narrowly edges OmniArch-L (0.0041) before PDE-Aligner fine-tuning closes the gap. In zero-shot transfer to unseen 2D PDEs (Shock, KH, OTVortex), OmniArch-L achieves 4–7× lower nRMSE than MPP-L (e.g., 0.2126 vs. 0.3243 on Shock; 0.2763 vs. 1.3261 on KH), confirming that the Fourier-encoder-based unified training captures transferable physical operators rather than dataset-specific patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniArch extends the pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm established for FNO-based foundation models (Subramanian et al., 2023) and the contrastive learning framework of SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) into a unified multi-scale PDE setting. It departs from prior multi-physics pre-training efforts (MPP, Poseidon, DPOT) that were restricted to 2D data and fixed grids by introducing Fourier Encoder-decoders for resolution-agnostic inputs and a Temporal Mask that enables simultaneous attention across coupled physical quantities within each timestep. The PDE-Aligner introduces a novel frequency-domain contrastive loss that aligns physical field dynamics with textual PDE descriptions, a mechanism absent from prior physics-informed fine-tuning approaches that relied on residual-based losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first model to conduct unified 1D-2D-3D PDE pre-training, OmniArch establishes a template for scientific foundation models that could accelerate simulation across fluid dynamics, weather forecasting, and materials science. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen PDE systems (shock waves, Kelvin-Helmholtz instability) and in-context learning of neural operators from short time-series prompts suggest emergent capabilities analogous to those in LLMs, though the evidence base remains limited to a small number of test cases. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to genuinely complex 3D turbulent systems (where performance still lags), how interpretability can be improved beyond the PDE-Aligner's classification accuracy, and whether the frequency-domain representation generalizes to non-periodic or irregular geometries.
