## Problem

Current text-to-video (T2V) evaluation benchmarks focus on video quality metrics (motion smoothness, background consistency) or spatial relationships, but fail to assess whether generated videos adhere to fundamental physical laws. No existing benchmark or evaluation metric is specifically designed to measure physical commonsense correctness in T2V outputs, leaving a critical gap in understanding how far these models are from functioning as world simulators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework consumes a generated video (varying in duration from 3–24s and resolution from 512×320 to 1280×768 depending on the T2V model), the original text prompt, the annotated physical law, and GPT-4o-generated customized physics questions (retrieval prompts and scoring questions) specific to each evaluation stage. It outputs two 4-point scale scores: a Semantic Alignment (SA) score measuring prompt-video correspondence, and a Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) score computed as the floor-rounded average of three sub-scores (key phenomena detection, physics order verification, and overall naturalness), each discretized to 0–3.

## Method

PhyGenEval decomposes physical correctness assessment into a three-tier hierarchical strategy that progresses from single-image to full-video analysis, addressing the failure of directly querying VLMs about embedded physical laws. Stage 1 (Key Physical Phenomena Detection) uses CLIPScore to locate keyframes via retrieval prompts, then VQAScore to verify whether expected physical phenomena (e.g., egg cracking, stone sinking) are present in ±2 adjacent frames. Stage 2 (Physics Order Verification) employs multi-image VLMs (GPT-4o or LLaVA-Interleave) to check causal sequence correctness across three frame-pair questions (before, after, and overall). Stage 3 (Overall Naturalness Evaluation) uses video-based VLMs (InternVideo2 or GPT-4o) to select from four GPT-4o-generated naturalness levels (Completely Fantastical to Almost Realistic). GPT-4o pre-processes each prompt to extract the underlying physical law and generate stage-specific questions, while ensemble averaging across open-source and closed-source VLMs at each stage reduces single-model bias. The companion benchmark PhyGenBench provides 160 prompts across 27 physical laws in four domains (mechanics, optics, thermal, material properties), each ensuring a one-to-one correspondence between prompt and physical phenomenon to avoid evaluation ambiguity.

## Results

PhyGenEval achieves an overall Kendall's τ of 0.78 and Spearman's ρ of 0.81 correlation with human judgments on physical commonsense alignment (PCA), substantially outperforming all existing metrics. On the PhyGenBench benchmark, even the best-performing T2V model (Sora) attains only a 0.55 average PCA score, while Gen-3 scores 0.51, indicating that current models remain far from world-simulator-level physical understanding. Across all four physical domains (Mechanics, Optics, Thermal, Material), models consistently score below 0.70, with Optics being the strongest domain (up to 0.67 for Hailuo) and Mechanics the weakest (0.30–0.50 range).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyGenEval decisively outperforms all three competing evaluation metrics on physical commonsense alignment (PCA) correlation with human judgments. VideoPhy (Bansal et al., 2024) achieves near-zero correlation (τ=0.03, ρ=0.04 overall), indicating it is essentially uninformative for physical correctness; it even shows negative correlation in Optics (τ=−0.15). VideoScore (He et al., 2024b) and DEVIL (Liao et al., 2024) both reach only τ≈0.17 and ρ≈0.18–0.19 overall, confirming that general-purpose video quality metrics cannot detect physical law violations. PhyGenEval's hierarchical three-stage design (key phenomena detection → order verification → overall naturalness) yields a 4–25× improvement in correlation over these baselines across every physical domain. For semantic alignment (SA), PhyGenEval also outperforms the Grid-LLaVA baseline from T2V-CompBench (Sun et al., 2024), achieving τ=0.53/ρ=0.56 versus τ=0.35/ρ=0.39, validating the two-stage object-then-action decomposition strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of T2V evaluation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, T2V-CompBench) and the nascent line of physical commonsense evaluation for video generation, most directly extending VideoPhy (Bansal et al., 2024). It departs from prior T2V benchmarks by organizing evaluation around 27 explicit physical laws across four domains rather than general video quality or spatial relationships, and from VideoPhy by annotating each prompt with its underlying physical law and designing a hierarchical three-stage evaluation (key phenomena detection → order verification → overall naturalness) that uses GPT-4o-generated physics-informed questions rather than asking VLMs to directly judge physical correctness. The evaluation methodology draws on VQAScore, CLIPScore, and multi-image VLMs (LLaVA-Interleave, InternVideo2) but restructures them into a progressive pipeline specifically calibrated for physical law verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, law-annotated tool for measuring whether T2V models understand intuitive physics, and the finding that even Sora scores only 0.55 (out of 1.0) on physical commonsense while achieving 0.84+ on semantic alignment sharply quantifies the gap between visual plausibility and physical correctness. The negative results—that prompt rewriting with expected physical outcomes yields only marginal gains (Kling: 0.49→0.56) and that VEnhancer's quality improvements leave physical scores essentially unchanged—challenge the assumption that better prompts or higher visual fidelity will naturally produce physically correct videos. Open questions include how to explicitly inject physical law knowledge into T2V training (e.g., via synthetic physics data as in PhysGen), whether the 160-prompt benchmark is sufficient to capture the full breadth of intuitive physics, and whether the heavy reliance on GPT-4o for question generation and scoring introduces a circularity that limits generalizability to future model architectures.
