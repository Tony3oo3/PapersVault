## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs either treat all hidden features equally by minimizing layer-wise output error, or incorporate end-loss gradients but approximate the Fisher information matrix as diagonal, discarding critical cross-weight interactions within output channels. No prior approach both leverages end-loss gradient information and preserves these within-channel dependencies in a way that is computationally and memory-feasible at the scale of modern LLMs (7B–70B parameters).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM's weight matrices, a small calibration dataset (e.g., 1024 sentences × 4096 tokens from RedPajama), and a single backpropagation step to obtain end-loss gradients with respect to each layer's output features. It produces quantized weights in multiple formats—non-uniform scalar (with per-channel codebooks and assignment matrices), vector (trellis-based), or weight-and-activation (uniform scalar)—at target bit-widths of 2, 3, or 4 bits. The key intermediate artifact is a set of group-averaged Hessian matrices (g per layer, where g ≪ d_out) that can be cached to disk and reused across different bit-widths and quantization configurations.

## Method

GuidedQuant replaces the standard layer-wise output-error Hessian H = XᵀX with a block-diagonal Fisher approximation that preserves cross-weight interactions within each output channel, then makes this tractable by partitioning output channels into g groups and averaging the per-channel Hessian blocks within each group. This yields a layer-wise objective (Eq. 7) that is a refined quadratic approximation of the end-loss change, and it plugs directly into any existing layer-wise output-based quantizer (GPTQ, QTIP, SpinQuant, LNQ) by simply substituting the Hessian. For non-uniform scalar quantization, the paper introduces LNQ, an alternating minimization algorithm that optimizes the codebook in closed form (least-squares solution) and the assignments via cyclic coordinate descent, guaranteeing monotonic decrease of the quantization objective. The full pipeline requires only one backpropagation pass for gradient computation, with total memory cost O(Lg(d_in² + n)) and time O(Lg(nd_in² + T_Q)), making it scalable to 70B-parameter models.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B at 2-bit weight-only scalar quantization, LNQ+GuidedQuant achieves 8.83 WikiText2 perplexity, dramatically outperforming SqueezeLLM (39.58) and GPTVQ 1D (51.87), and approaching the 16-bit original (5.12). At 3-bit, LNQ+GuidedQuant reaches 5.57 WikiText2 perplexity, beating SqueezeLLM (5.74) and GPTQ (8.06). For weight-only vector quantization, QTIP+GuidedQuant achieves 6.11 WikiText2 perplexity at 2-bit on Llama-2-7B, surpassing QTIP (6.82), AQLM (6.59), and QuIP# (8.22). For weight-and-activation quantization (W4A4KV4), SpinQuant+GuidedQuant reduces WikiText2 perplexity from 5.95 to 5.89 on Llama-2-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LNQ+GuidedQuant consistently outperforms all scalar quantization baselines across 2-, 3-, and 4-bit settings on Llama-2-7B/13B/70B. At 2-bit, the improvement over SqueezeLLM is dramatic (8.83 vs. 39.58 Wiki2 perplexity on 7B), and LNQ alone (23.31) already surpasses GPTVQ 1D (51.87), showing that the closed-form codebook + CD optimization in LNQ is a major fix over GPTVQ 1D's suboptimal algorithm. At 3-bit, LNQ+GuidedQuant beats SqueezeLLM (5.57 vs. 5.74) and GPTQ (5.57 vs. 8.06) on 7B. For vector quantization, QTIP+GuidedQuant beats QTIP (6.11 vs. 6.82 Wiki2 at 2-bit on 7B), AQLM (6.11 vs. 6.59), and QuIP# (6.11 vs. 8.22). For weight-and-activation quantization, SpinQuant+GuidedQuant marginally improves over SpinQuant (5.89 vs. 5.95 Wiki2 at W4A4KV4 on 7B) and QuaRot (5.89 vs. 6.08), with the gap widening on 13B (5.19 vs. 5.24 and 5.34 respectively). QuIP is only reported for 13B/70B in the paper and is beaten by LNQ+GuidedQuant at 2-bit (7.26 vs. 13.48 on 13B) and 3-bit (4.91 vs. 5.12 on 13B).

## Relation to Prior Work

GuidedQuant sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) layer-wise output-based PTQ methods (GPTQ, OBQ, QTIP, SpinQuant) that minimize per-layer reconstruction error, and (2) second-order compression methods (OBS, WoodFisher, Optimal BERT Surgeon) that approximate the Hessian via block-diagonal Fisher information matrices. The key departure is making the block-diagonal Fisher approximation—previously used for pruning CNNs and BERT—computationally feasible for LLM-scale quantization by averaging Hessian blocks across groups of output channels. For non-uniform scalar quantization, LNQ replaces GPTVQ 1D's gradient-descent codebook updates with closed-form least-squares solutions and its GPTQ-based assignment optimization with cyclic coordinate descent, drawing on the CD quantization literature (QuantEase, CDQuant, QuIP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GuidedQuant provides a principled, drop-in mechanism to incorporate end-loss gradient information into any existing layer-wise PTQ method, consistently improving SOTA across weight-only scalar, vector, and weight-and-activation quantization without retraining. The 2-bit results are particularly striking (e.g., 8.83 vs. 39.58 perplexity on Llama-2-7B WikiText2), suggesting that the diagonal Fisher approximation used by SqueezeLLM is a severe bottleneck at extreme compression. Open questions include whether more sophisticated grouping strategies (beyond consecutive channels) yield further gains, whether the approach extends to cross-layer interactions, and how the method behaves under fine-tuning or for architectures with different layer structures. The paper also notes that compression can adversely affect fairness in language models, an area where mitigation strategies for quantized models remain underexplored.
