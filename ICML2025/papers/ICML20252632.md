## Problem

While heuristic methods that use a reference model's RHO loss (target loss minus reference loss) for data selection and weighting have shown empirical success in accelerating CLIP and LLM training, no theoretical framework explains why this approach improves generalization or provides principled guidance for optimal data weighting. This paper addresses this gap by deriving the first generalization bounds for learning with a reference model, showing that the reference model reduces the variance term in the excess risk bound and dramatically lowers sample complexity from O(m) to O(√m).

## Input / Output

The method takes image-text pairs (e.g., from DFN-192M or CC12M) as training data, plus a pre-trained reference CLIP model whose image and text embeddings are computed offline and stored on disk. During training, the reference model's pre-computed features are loaded per mini-batch to compute RHO losses for each negative sample, which reweight the contrastive objective without requiring the reference model to be in the forward pass. The trained target model (ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32) produces image-text similarity scores used for zero-shot classification and retrieval on benchmarks like ImageNet-1K and the 38-task Datacomp suite.

## Method

The core contribution is DRRho risk minimization, which applies Distributionally Robust Optimization to the RHO loss ℓ(θ,z) − ℓ(θ_ref,z) across training data, yielding a worst-case risk that adaptively upweights samples where the target model is weak relative to the reference model. For CLIP, the KL-regularized DRO formulation produces a temperature-parameterized contrastive loss where each negative sample's contribution is weighted by exp(RHO_loss/τ), effectively performing per-anchor data re-weighting of negatives rather than anchor-level selection. The generalization bound shows the variance term becomes Var(ℓ(θ*,·) − ℓ(θ_ref,·)) instead of Var(ℓ(θ*,·)), which is empirically smaller, and Corollary 4.3 shows DRRho needs only O(√m) samples to match a reference model trained on m samples. Optimization uses the SogCLR algorithm with running estimators for the log-sum-exp terms, enabling small-batch training without the performance loss typical of mini-batch contrastive methods.

## Results

On DFN-192M (1.28B samples seen, ViT-B/16 target), DRRho-CLIP achieves 68.84% ImageNet-1K Top-1 accuracy and 55.20 Datacomp average with the weakest reference (ViT-B/32 WIT-400M), surpassing the reference model's own 63.32% and OpenCLIP's 66.94%. With stronger references (ViT-B/16 DFN-2B, ViT-L/14 DFN-2B), DRRho-CLIP(w/Distillation) reaches 69.50% and 69.25% ImageNet Top-1 respectively. In data-efficiency experiments, DRRho-CLIP with only 50% of training data matches FastCLIP trained on 100% of the data, and the RHO loss variance is reduced by ~38% (from 7.26 to 4.49 ×10⁻³ for image loss) compared to the standard loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRRho-CLIP decisively outperforms the heuristic RHO-based methods JEST and JEST (Top-k) by a large margin: 68.84% vs. 56.40–57.75% ImageNet Top-1 (a gap of over 11 points), demonstrating that the theory-driven DRO formulation is substantially better than ad-hoc mini-batch data selection. Against OpenCLIP (66.94%) and FastCLIP (67.37%), which do not use a reference model, DRRho-CLIP gains +1.47 and +1.47 points on ImageNet Top-1 respectively, even with the weakest reference model. In the distillation setting with strong references (ViT-B/16 DFN-2B, ViT-L/14 DFN-2B), DRRho-CLIP(w/Distillation) achieves the best results (69.50% and 69.25%), beating FastCLIP(w/Distillation) by 0.35 and 0.40 points and MobileCLIP by 0.83 and 0.78 points. Notably, with the weak ViT-B/32 (WIT-400M) reference, DRRho-CLIP (68.84%) surpasses the reference model itself (63.32%), confirming the weak-to-strong generalization property. On Datacomp, DRRho-CLIP(w/Distillation) leads all methods with strong references (57.17 vs. 55.60 for FastCLIP w/Dist. with ViT-B/16 ref), while with the weak reference it matches or slightly exceeds non-distillation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes 'model steering'—using a trained reference model to guide training via the RHO loss (ℓ(θ,z) − ℓ(θ_ref,z))—into a principled DRO-based framework, providing the first generalization theory for this paradigm. It builds directly on Duchi & Namkoong's (2016) DRO generalization bounds, the contrastive-loss-to-DRO connection from Qiu et al. (2023), and the SogCLR optimization algorithm from Yuan et al. (2022), while extending the heuristic RHO-loss data selection of Mindermann et al. (2022) into a theoretically grounded objective. The paper positions model steering as complementary to knowledge distillation (the reference need not be stronger) and as a unifying lens over existing ad-hoc methods like JEST and ActiveCLIP, showing they are suboptimal approximations to the proposed DRRho risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first generalization bounds for learning with a reference model explain *why* model steering improves data efficiency (reduced variance in the excess risk term) and predict a sample complexity of O(√m) versus O(m) for ERM, which is empirically validated with ~50% data reduction. The better scaling law (smaller β) over OpenCLIP has direct practical implications for large-scale CLIP training budgets. Open questions include whether the variance-reduction assumption (that ℓ(θ_ref,·) approximates ℓ(θ*,·)) holds for very weak or mismatched reference models, whether the framework extends cleanly to non-contrastive objectives (e.g., autoregressive LLMs), and whether the theoretical O(√m) sample complexity gap is tight in practice.
