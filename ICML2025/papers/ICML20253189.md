## Problem

Existing parallel sampling methods for log-concave distributions and score-based diffusion models achieve O~(log²(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity, which may not be optimal relative to known lower bounds. This paper asks whether logarithmic adaptive complexity O~(log(d/ε²)) is achievable for both problems and provides an affirmative answer by introducing a new parallelization strategy across time slices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253189/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For log-concave sampling, the method requires a potential f that is α-strongly convex and β-smooth (condition number κ = β/α), an initial distribution μ₀, and a pointwise δ-accurate score estimate s ≈ ∇f, producing a sample with KL divergence ≤ ε² from π ∝ exp(−f). For diffusion models, it takes a learned NN-based score s_t^θ that is L²-accurate, bounded, and Lipschitz, producing a sample from the data distribution q₀. The space complexity is O~(d²/ε²) in both settings; the method operates on overdamped Langevin diffusion (or SDE implementations for diffusion models), which have less smooth trajectories than underdamped alternatives, trading higher storage for the improved adaptive complexity.

## Method

The core contribution is a diagonal-style parallel Picard iteration that updates time slices in a staggered pattern rather than all simultaneously or sequentially. The time horizon T = O~(log(d/ε²)) is split into N = O~(log(d/ε²)) slices of O(1) length, each with M = O~(d/ε²) grid points; for the j-th update at the n-th slice, the algorithm uses the right boundary of the previous slice (updated j times) and the current slice's points (updated j−1 times) as inputs, then performs P = O(log κ) Picard iterations to prevent exponential accumulation of score estimation errors. The total Picard depth is J = O(N + log d), and the key technical insight is that this diagonal structure ensures the truncation error converges along both the time direction (via gradient contraction with factor 1 − O(1/κ)) and the Picard direction (via exponential convergence with factor O((β²h²)^P)), yielding O~(log(d/ε²)) total adaptive complexity. For diffusion models, the method additionally employs a shrinking step-size discretization toward the data end, early stopping, and an exponential integrator within Picard iterations, while the Lipschitz score eliminates the need for repeated inner iterations (P = 1).

## Results

For strongly log-concave sampling via overdamped Langevin diffusion, the proposed parallel Picard method achieves Õ(log(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity, improving over the previous best of Õ(log²(d/ε²)), with space complexity Õ(d²/ε²) and Õ(κ²d/ε² · log(d/ε²)) gradient queries per iteration. For SDE implementations of score-based diffusion models, the method similarly achieves Õ(log(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity with Õ(d²/ε²) space complexity, surpassing all existing parallel methods that require Õ(log²(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity. The improvement comes from a diagonal-style parallel update across time slices combined with P = Θ(log κ) inner Picard iterations per update to control score estimation error accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method strictly improves adaptive complexity over all five baselines, reducing it from Õ(log²(d/ε²)) to Õ(log(d/ε²)) for both log-concave sampling and diffusion model sampling. Against Anari et al. (2024) for overdamped Langevin diffusion, the improvement is from log² to log in adaptive complexity while maintaining the same Õ(d²/ε²) space complexity. Against underdamped Langevin baselines (Shen & Lee 2019, Yu & Dalalyan 2024, Anari et al. 2024), the method trades a slightly worse space complexity (d²/ε² vs. d^{3/2}/ε) for the improved log vs. log² adaptive complexity, which the authors attribute to the less smooth trajectory of overdamped dynamics. For diffusion models, the method outperforms Chen et al. (2024) and Gupta et al. (2025) on adaptive complexity (log vs. log²) while matching their Õ(d²/ε²) space complexity for SDE implementations. The trade-off is that the method requires slightly more processors and gradient evaluations (a log factor increase) compared to prior parallel approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of parallel sampling methods (Shen & Lee, 2019; Anari et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Yu & Dalalyan, 2024; Gupta et al., 2025) that use Picard iterations or randomized midpoints to parallelize within individual time slices, achieving Õ(log²(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity. The key departure is a diagonal-style update scheme that parallelizes *across* time slices rather than only within them, drawing on waveform relaxation ideas from scientific computing (Gear, 1991; Gander, 2015; Wang, 2023). The analysis builds on the interpolation method and Girsanov's theorem framework from Anari et al. (2024) and the Langevin dynamics convergence theory of Chewi (2023), while the diffusion model component adapts the SDE/Picard framework of Chen et al. (2024) with an exponential integrator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from Õ(log²(d/ε²)) to Õ(log(d/ε²)) adaptive complexity is a clean theoretical improvement for both log-concave sampling and diffusion model inference, potentially reducing the number of sequential score evaluations needed for high-dimensional sampling. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the tradeoff is increased space complexity and processor requirements. Open questions include whether the condition number dependence can be improved via parallelization, whether the method extends to underdamped Langevin diffusion with comparable space complexity, and whether the uniformly Lipschitz score assumption (which may be too strong near singularities) can be relaxed. The practical impact hinges on whether the theoretical log-factor gain translates to meaningful speedups in real diffusion model pipelines where score evaluations are the bottleneck.
