## Problem

Patch-based image tokenization divides images into fixed-size square patches, ignoring the morphological structure of visual content. This creates a fundamental trade-off: large patches fuse multiple semantics into a single token (polysemanticity), while small patches produce excessive token counts (redundancy). The paper asks whether adaptive, subobject-level token segmentation—drawing an analogy to subword tokenization in NLP—can resolve this conflict and improve downstream vision-language model learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (H×W×3, typically 1024×1024) and produces a token segmentation map M ∈ {0,…,N−1}^{H×W} assigning each pixel to a token, along with visual tokens for a VLM. Each visual token is constructed by pooling upsampled encoder features over the segment (content) and encoding a bounding box plus a cropped binary mask (position), then fusing via a small MLP. The number of tokens N is data-dependent and controlled by a single scalar threshold t, unlike the fixed grid of patch tokenization.

## Method

EPOC (Efficient and PanOptiC) replaces SAM's prompt-conditioned mask decoding with a two-stage pipeline: (1) a compact SegFormer-b0 (3.7M parameters) trained on SA-1B predicts a pixelwise boundary probability map P ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} in a single forward pass, and (2) a non-parametric watershed algorithm treats P as a topographical surface, flooding basins below a threshold t until the entire image is submerged, with watersheds forming at ridges to separate segments. This guarantees complete pixel coverage (panoptic) while making inference cost O(1) independent of the number of tokens. The threshold t provides a single-knob control over segmentation granularity. For VLM integration, the paper designs a token embedding that handles arbitrary-shaped, dynamically arranged tokens via box-mask decomposition, and trains Llama-style VLMs with next-token prediction on text tokens only, freezing the visual feature encoder.

## Results

EPOC (3.7M params) achieves up to 17.1 FPS with under 10% GPU utilization, outperforming SAM ViT-B (93.7M params, 0.6 FPS, 96.5% GPU) by ~28x in throughput while matching or exceeding its boundary precision-recall on zero-shot datasets (PPP, PIN++). In intrinsic evaluation, subobject-level methods (EPOC, SLIC) reach >90% token monosemanticity versus <60% for object-level panoptic segmentation. In extrinsic VLM evaluation, subobject-level tokenization (EPOC, SLIC) achieves lower validation perplexity than patch baselines with fewer visual tokens across ImageNet-1K, ShareGPT4V, Pixmo-cap, and CLEVR-cap, and remains effective even with weaker VAE embeddings where patch-based models fail to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against patch-based tokenization (ViT), EPOC and other adaptive methods clearly win on morphology alignment (boundary precision/recall) and monosemanticity, and in extrinsic VLM evaluation achieve lower perplexity with fewer tokens; patch-based models also fail to converge with weaker VAE embeddings while adaptive tokenizers remain effective. Against object-level panoptic segmentation (Mask2Former, OneFormer), EPOC wins decisively on zero-shot generalization (PPP, PIN++) and monosemanticity (>90% vs <60%), though Mask2Former-tiny is faster in raw FPS (53.1 vs 17.1) due to its simpler architecture. Against SAM ViT-B, EPOC matches or exceeds boundary precision-recall on zero-shot benchmarks while being ~25x smaller (3.7M vs 93.7M params) and ~28x faster (17.1 vs 0.6 FPS). Against FastSAM (72.2M params, 12.2 FPS) and MobileSAMv2 (711M params, 1.2 FPS), EPOC matches segmentation quality while offering substantially better efficiency. Against SLIC superpixels, EPOC wins on morphology alignment (learned semantic boundaries vs bottom-up color clustering) but SLIC is marginally faster at lower k values; both achieve comparable monosemanticity in extrinsic evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct structural analogy between NLP subword tokenization (BPE, SentencePiece) and image tokenization, arguing that patch-based ViT tokenization is the visual equivalent of character-level tokenization—suboptimal and rarely adopted in NLP. It builds on SAM's 'segment everything' paradigm for subobject-level segmentation but departs from SAM's prompt-conditioned mask decoding by replacing it with a lightweight boundary-detection task (SegFormer-b0, 3.7M params) followed by a non-parametric watershed algorithm, achieving O(1) inference complexity versus SAM's O(N). The object-level baselines (Mask2Former, OneFormer) and superpixel methods (SLIC) are positioned as intermediate alternatives, while the token embedding design for arbitrary-shaped segments extends standard VLM architectures (LLaVA-style) to handle dynamic spatial arrangements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 170× parameter reduction (3.7M vs. SAM's 641M) while maintaining comparable segmentation quality is a practically significant efficiency win for pipelines that need to tokenize large volumes of images. The finding that subobject-level tokenization enables faster convergence and better generalization with fewer visual tokens—particularly the failure of patch-based models to converge with weaker VAE embeddings—suggests adaptive segmentation can simplify the learning problem itself, not just reduce token count. Key open questions include whether the benefits persist at larger LLM scales (tested only up to 1.7B), whether task-conditioned segmentation can recover the intra-token spatial structure that is inevitably discarded, and how the approach extends to video where token explosion is a persistent bottleneck.
