## Problem

Mainstream text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, SDXL) support only English and a few Latin-based languages, limiting their global applicability. Existing multilingual T2I solutions either rely on translation pipelines (introducing latency and cultural nuance loss) or require extensive curated multilingual image-text datasets and heavy compute (e.g., AltDiffusion needs ≥19×64 GPU-days). The paper asks whether a frozen multilingual text encoder pre-trained on noisy web-scale image-text pairs can be cheaply adapted to enable native multilingual generation without retraining the diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252754/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MuLan accepts free-form text prompts in any of 110+ languages, including mixed-language prompts (e.g., 'Stunning botanical水彩风格 白色背景') and emoji-only prompts, and outputs a single RGB image. Resolution depends on the frozen backbone: 512×512 for SD 1.5 and PixArt-α, 768×768 for SD 2.1, and up to 1024×1024 for SDXL. Because the adapter is plug-and-play and the diffusion weights remain untouched, the generated images are fully compatible with downstream community tools (LoRA, ControlNet, LCM, IP-Adapter) without any additional fine-tuning.

## Method

MuLan introduces a lightweight language adapter (<20M parameters) that re-projects embeddings from a frozen multilingual text encoder (InternVL-LLaMA) into the conditioning space of a frozen diffusion model, trained solely on ~17M English image-text pairs from LAION-EN. The key architectural choice is the adapter design: a one-layer encoder-decoder transformer with learnable queries for SD/SDXL backbones, and a simple MLP for PixArt-α. The critical empirical finding is that image-centered alignment (contrastive learning on multilingual image-text pairs, as in InternVL-LLaMA) substantially outperforms language-centered alignment (distillation on parallel text corpora) for downstream T2I quality, because the image acts as a semantic bridge that pre-aligns multilingual embeddings before the adapter maps them into the visual feature space. Training requires only ~12 hours on 8 A100 GPUs for SD 1.5, and the adapter achieves CLIP similarity scores nearly matching English (39.57 English vs. 39.61 average across other languages) while surpassing specialized single-language models like Taiyi (Chinese) and Japanese SDXL.

## Results

On the XM12 benchmark (12 mainstream languages), InternVL-MuLan-PixArt achieves an average CLIP similarity (SIM) of 39.5 and FID of 11.8, while InternVL-MuLan-SD15 achieves SIM 37.7 and FID 14.4. The headline claim is that generation quality in non-English languages nearly matches English: CLIP similarity scores of 39.57 (English) vs. 39.61 (average of other languages) on COCO2014 across 85 languages. Training requires only ~12 hours on 8 A100 GPUs with 17M English image-text pairs, and the adapter has fewer than 20M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AltDiffusion (the strongest native multilingual T2I baseline), MuLan-PixArt wins on average CLIP similarity (39.5 vs. 38.8) and substantially outperforms it on low-resource languages in the 85-language COCO2014 evaluation (e.g., 38.85 vs. 40.30 for Chinese, 38.05 vs. 39.43 for Japanese), though AltDiffusion retains a FID advantage (9.1 vs. 11.8 on XM12). Against GlueGen, MuLan-PixArt dominates on both SIM (39.5 vs. 35.6) and FID (11.8 vs. 17.4), and GlueGen does not support Hindi, German, Korean, Russian, Arabic, or Polish at all. Compared to translation-based baselines, MuLan-PixArt (SIM 39.5) slightly trails PixArt+Google Translate (39.7) but clearly beats PixArt+NLLB (38.3), and MuLan-SD15 (37.7) beats SD15+Google (36.9) and SD15+NLLB (36.5). Against language-specialized models, InternVL-MuLan-SD15 surpasses Taiyi-SDXL-3.5B on Chinese (37.84 vs. 35.44) and matches JapaneseSDXL on Japanese (38.01 vs. 38.00), all while supporting 110+ languages with a single adapter. On fine-grained VQAScore (GPT-4o evaluator), MuLan-PixArt achieves 0.74 average, matching PixArt+Google (0.75) and far exceeding AltDiffusion (0.58) and GlueGen (0.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

MuLan builds directly on InternVL-LLaMA's image-centered multilingual alignment (contrastive learning on noisy LAION-5B image-text pairs) and repurposes it as a frozen text encoder for diffusion-based T2I generation. It departs from prior multilingual T2I approaches—AltDiffusion (retrains encoder + UNet on billions of multilingual pairs), GlueGen (language-centered distillation to CLIP space), and translation-based pipelines—by showing that a pre-aligned multilingual encoder plus a <20M-parameter adapter trained solely on English data suffices. The paper also introduces a conceptual distinction between language-centered alignment (parallel-text distillation) and image-centered alignment (contrastive image-text learning), demonstrating the latter's clear superiority for downstream generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a plug-and-play adapter that adds 110+ language support to the entire Stable Diffusion ecosystem (SD 1.5/2.1, SDXL, PixArt-Alpha) in ~12 GPU-hours, while remaining fully compatible with community tools like LoRA, ControlNet, LCM, and IP-Adapter without any retraining. The counterintuitive finding that noisy web-scale image-text data outperforms curated multilingual corpora for alignment challenges the assumption that high-quality data is always preferable. Open questions include whether the method can avoid paired-data bias and distribution shift, and whether it can be extended to improve multilingual prompt understanding and compositional following rather than just subject-level alignment.
