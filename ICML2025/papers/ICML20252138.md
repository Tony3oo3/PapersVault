## Problem

Text encoders in T2I diffusion models account for over 70% of total model parameters yet contribute less than 0.5% of FLOPs, creating a severe memory bottleneck that hinders deployment on resource-constrained hardware. Existing blockwise pruning methods (ShortGPT, LaCo, FinerCut) are designed for autoregressive LLMs and perform poorly when applied to T2I text encoders, particularly at high sparsity levels (>40%), where they fail to preserve text-image alignment. No prior work has specifically addressed lightweight text encoder construction for T2I diffusion models.

## Input / Output

Skrr is a post-hoc compression method applied to the T5-XXL text encoder within existing T2I diffusion pipelines (PixArt-Σ, SD3, FLUX.1-dev). It takes a text prompt (typically 150–250 tokens) as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image via the standard diffusion sampling process (20 NFE, Bfloat16 precision). The method requires a 1k-prompt calibration set from CC12M to determine which sub-blocks to skip and reuse, but at inference time the compressed encoder simply replaces the original T5-XXL with no changes to the denoising module or VAE.

## Method

Skrr operates in two phases. The Skip phase uses a beam search algorithm (beam size k=3) to sequentially select which MHA and FFN sub-blocks to prune, evaluating each candidate via a T2I-tailored discrepancy metric: the MSE between the projected text embeddings (after the denoising module's projection layer) of the dense and pruned models, computed over both conditional prompts and the null condition f_∅. This dual-metric design captures both semantic alignment and the norm inflation of the null embedding that causes abnormal images under classifier-free guidance. The Re-use phase then, for each skipped sub-block, tests whether feeding the hidden state back into an adjacent unskipped sub-block of the same type (MHA→MHA, FFN→FFN) reduces the discrepancy further, and retains the reuse only if it strictly improves the score. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) proves that under the condition ‖θ_i − θ_i*‖ < ‖θ_i‖, the Re-use error bound is strictly tighter than Skip alone, providing a formal guarantee that reuse can only help.

## Results

On PixArt-Σ at ~42% sparsity, Skrr achieves FID 19.93 (surpassing the dense model's 22.89), CLIP 0.312, DreamSim 0.741, and GenEval Overall 0.442. ShortGPT collapses catastrophically at 40.5% sparsity (FID 55.26, GenEval Overall 0.087), while LaCo (FID 21.60, GenEval 0.275) and FinerCut (FID 20.36, GenEval 0.367) degrade more gradually but still fall well behind Skrr. Skrr reduces memory from 10.18 GB to 6.46 GB (36.5% reduction) with negligible FLOPs increase (<0.05% of pipeline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skrr outperforms all three LLM-targeted pruning baselines across every metric at comparable sparsity levels (~42%). Against ShortGPT, the gap is dramatic: Skrr's GenEval Overall is 0.442 vs. 0.087 (5× improvement), and FID is 19.93 vs. 55.26, indicating ShortGPT's greedy block-removal strategy fails catastrophically for T2I text encoders. Against LaCo, Skrr improves GenEval Overall by 61% (0.442 vs. 0.275) and FID by 7.7% (19.93 vs. 21.60), with a notable advantage in counting (0.450 vs. 0.150). Against the closest competitor FinerCut, Skrr leads on FID (19.93 vs. 20.36), CLIP (0.312 vs. 0.308), DreamSim (0.741 vs. 0.731), and GenEval Overall (0.442 vs. 0.367, a 20.4% relative improvement), with the largest margin in counting (0.450 vs. 0.306). The paper also demonstrates that Re-use is plug-and-play: applying it to ShortGPT and FinerCut qualitatively restores fidelity and text alignment, confirming the mechanism's generality beyond Skrr's own Skip phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts blockwise pruning techniques originally developed for autoregressive LLMs (ShortGPT, LaCo, FinerCut) to the text encoder of T2I diffusion models, a component that prior diffusion-model compression work (targeting the denoising module via KD, quantization, or weight pruning) has largely overlooked. The key methodological departure is the use of a T2I-specific discrepancy metric that routes text encoder outputs through the denoising module's projection layer and accounts for the null condition used in classifier-free guidance, combined with a beam search strategy (borrowed from NMT) to handle inter-block interactions during pruning. The Re-use mechanism, which feeds hidden states from adjacent retained blocks back into skipped positions, is novel and supported by a Lipschitz-based error bound theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that text encoders consume over 70% of total parameters while contributing less than 0.5% of FLOPs identifies a critical and previously underexplored bottleneck for deploying T2I models on memory-constrained hardware. Skrr's plug-and-play nature (no retraining required) makes it immediately applicable to existing pipelines, and the finding that pruning can paradoxically improve FID—attributed to implicit perturbation of the null condition—opens an interesting question about whether structured noise in the unconditional branch is a useful regularizer. Limitations include the theoretical bounds being loose (product of Lipschitz constants), the Re-use mechanism adding a small FLOPs overhead, and evaluation primarily on T5-XXL-based models, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to fundamentally different encoder architectures.
