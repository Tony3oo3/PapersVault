## Problem

Standard diffusion models rely on isotropic Gaussian noise in the forward process, which inherently biases the generative process toward Euclidean geometry and fails to preserve the angular class structure of hyperspherical data such as face embeddings. When data reside on a hypersphere S^{d-1} with class-specific hypercone geometry, Gaussian noise distorts angular relationships and cannot model directional uncertainty, leading to suboptimal generation that clusters samples near class means (simplicity bias) and loses inter-class angular separation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label as conditioning input and operates on normalized hyperspherical embeddings (unit vectors on S^{d-1}) extracted via ArcFace/iResNet50 for face datasets or a CNN for object datasets, with a latent space of 32×32×3. The output is a generated RGB image decoded from the denoised hyperspherical embedding, along with the embedding itself which preserves class-specific angular geometry. For face generation, a hybrid formulation additionally models magnitude (norm) uncertainty via Gaussian noise alongside directional uncertainty via vMF noise.

## Method

HyperSphereDiff replaces Gaussian noise with von Mises-Fisher (vMF) noise in both forward and reverse diffusion processes. The forward step uses angular interpolation z_t = cos(θ_t)z_{t-1} + sin(θ_t)v (equivalent to vMF with concentration κ_t = cot(θ_t)), progressively transitioning from class-structured perturbations to uniform hypersphere noise. The reverse step combines a learned score function with vMF sampling: z_{t-1} ~ vMF(Π(z_t + η_t ∇log f(z_t; μ_c, κ_t)), κ_t), where the score network is trained with MSE or angular losses (cosine/geodesic). The framework introduces a class-specific hypercone formalism with adaptive concentration κ_θ(z_t, y) = κ_max · σ(β[θ_y − ∠(z_t, m_t)]) that dynamically locks samples into learned truncated regions, and two new evaluation metrics—Hypercone Coverage Ratio (HCR) and Hypercone Difficulty Skew (HDS)—to quantify geometry preservation and sample diversity.

## Results

HyperSphereDiff (vMF-based) consistently outperforms Gaussian diffusion on the two proposed geometry metrics across all six datasets: Hypercone Coverage Ratio (HCR) drops from 0.17–0.46 (Gaussian) to 0.13–0.22 (vMF), and Hypercone Difficulty Skew (HDS) drops from 0.72–0.91 to 0.41–0.62, indicating better class-structure preservation and harder sample generation. On FID, vMF achieves lower (better) scores on four of six datasets (MNIST 1.86 vs 1.95, Cars-196 7.87 vs 9.09, CelebA 9.29 vs 9.31, D-LORD 9.27 vs 11.38), while Gaussian retains a slight edge on CIFAR-10 (3.45 vs 3.52) and CUB-200 (8.47 vs 9.11). The largest relative gains appear on face datasets, where HCR nearly halves (D-LORD: 0.46→0.21; CelebA: 0.42→0.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Gaussian (variance-preserving) diffusion (DDPM). HyperSphereDiff (vMF) wins decisively on both proposed geometry metrics—HCR and HDS—across all six datasets, with the most pronounced improvements on face data (D-LORD HCR drops from 0.46 to 0.21; CelebA HCR from 0.42 to 0.22) and fine-grained objects (CUB-200 HDS from 0.81 to 0.41). On FID, vMF achieves lower scores on four of six datasets (MNIST, Cars-196, CelebA, D-LORD), with the largest FID gain on D-LORD (9.27 vs 11.38, a ~19% relative reduction). Gaussian diffusion retains a marginal FID advantage on CIFAR-10 (3.45 vs 3.52) and CUB-200 (8.47 vs 9.11), suggesting that for simpler or more uniformly distributed object classes, the Euclidean noise assumption is less detrimental. Overall, the vMF approach trades a small FID cost on two datasets for substantially better angular-geometry preservation and harder-sample diversity on all six.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard DDPM/score-based diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) by replacing isotropic Gaussian noise with von Mises-Fisher (vMF) noise, motivated by the observation that many real-world embeddings (face features, fine-grained object representations) naturally reside on hyperspheres. It builds on the geometry-aware embedding work of Falorsi et al. (2018) and the broader exploration of non-Gaussian noise in diffusion (Xu et al., 2023), but departs by providing a systematic hypercone formalism and proving the equivalence between angular interpolation and vMF transitions (Lemma D.1). The adaptive class-dependent concentration mechanism and truncated vMF reverse process represent a further departure from prior manifold-aware diffusion attempts (Rezende et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022), which the authors note remain limited in handling anisotropic angular uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a principled way to preserve angular class structure during generative modeling on hyperspheres, with practical validation in face recognition (5% accuracy gain on D-LORD) and fine-grained object generation. However, the FID results are mixed—vMF diffusion is sometimes worse than Gaussian (e.g., CIFAR-10: 3.52 vs. 3.45; CUB-200: 9.11 vs. 8.47)—and the best results come from a hybrid Gaussian+spherical approach, suggesting pure angular modeling is insufficient for pixel-space quality. Open questions include whether the framework scales to high-resolution image generation, how to learn adaptive κ schedulers end-to-end, and whether hierarchical hypercone partitioning can capture finer intra-class variations. The HCR/HDS metrics, while useful for this specific geometry, may not transfer to other non-Euclidean settings.
