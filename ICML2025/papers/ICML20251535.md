## Problem

Chain-of-thought prompting is widely deployed as a default inference-time reasoning strategy, yet the literature lacks a systematic account of when CoT actually reduces model performance. This paper addresses that gap by drawing on cognitive psychology: if verbal thinking impairs human performance on certain task types, do analogous CoT failures emerge in LLMs and LMMs?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251535/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the six tasks is massively scaled up from its original psychology paradigm: artificial grammar learning uses 15 in-context training strings plus one test string per question (4,400 questions across 100 finite-state grammars); face recognition presents one target face image and five candidate images (500 problems, 2,500 unique faces generated via stable-image-ultra); vehicle classification is a multi-turn task where the model receives one vehicle's feature description at a time with feedback, iterating up to 15 passes over 10 vehicles (240 lists); logical inconsistency uses 3,216 statement pairs drawn from SNLI, MNLI, and synthetic data; spatial intuition presents code-drawn cup images with four lettered height options (100 problems); and apartment selection provides 320 feature statements per choice set (300 sets). Outputs are binary classification labels, multiple-choice selections, or the number of rounds needed to achieve perfect classification.

## Method

The authors identify six task archetypes from the cognitive psychology literature where verbal deliberation impairs human performance, select a representative study for each, and adapt it into a scalable LLM/LMM evaluation. They then compare zero-shot prompting against CoT prompting (and, in some cases, tree-of-thought and steering prompts) across a suite of state-of-the-art models. Three archetypes—implicit statistical learning, verbal overshadowing (face recognition), and classification with exceptions—show large, consistent CoT-induced performance drops (up to 36.3% absolute accuracy loss for o1-preview vs. GPT-4o, and up to 331% more learning rounds), while the other three (logical inconsistency, spatial intuition, preference aggregation) show mixed or positive CoT effects, which the authors attribute to model-specific advantages such as access to logical priors or long context windows that humans lack. To validate that their psychology-based heuristic is more effective than random task selection at isolating CoT failures, they run a bootstrap test resampling 50 comparisons from their results against 378 zero-shot-vs-CoT comparisons from the Sprague et al. (2025) meta-study, finding that none of 100,000 resamples exceed their observed average performance decrease.

## Results

On the artificial grammar learning task (implicit statistical learning), CoT causes a 36.3% absolute accuracy drop for o1-preview (57.7% vs. GPT-4o zero-shot at 94.0%) and a 23.1% drop for GPT-4o (64.4% vs. 87.5%), with consistent decreases across all nine models tested. On facial recognition, all six LMMs show CoT-induced drops (ranging from 2.0% to 14.4% absolute). On the data-with-exceptions task, CoT increases the number of learning rounds by up to 331% (GPT-4o: 12.5 vs. 2.9 rounds). A bootstrap analysis against 378 comparisons from Sprague et al. (2025) confirms the psychology-based heuristic identifies CoT failures significantly more efficiently than random sampling (p < 0.00011).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is zero-shot prompting vs. CoT prompting (following Wei et al., 2022), and the key finding is that CoT *reduces* performance on three of six task archetypes, directly contradicting the default assumption that CoT helps. On the implicit statistical learning task, zero-shot GPT-4o achieves 87.5% accuracy while CoT drops it to 64.4% (−23.1%), and o1-preview's built-in reasoning yields only 57.7% versus GPT-4o's 94.0% zero-shot. Tree-of-thought (Yao et al., 2023) was tested as an alternative inference-time reasoning method and improved CoT accuracy marginally (64.55% vs. 62.52%) but remained far below zero-shot (94.00%), indicating the failure is not specific to standard CoT. The bootstrap validation against Sprague et al. (2025)'s meta-study of 378 zero-shot vs. CoT comparisons shows that none of 100,000 resamples matched the magnitude of CoT-induced decreases found in this paper's 50 comparisons, confirming the psychology-based heuristic is significantly more effective at isolating CoT failures than random task selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two literatures: the growing body of work evaluating when chain-of-thought prompting helps or hurts LLMs (Wei et al., 2022; Sprague et al., 2025), and decades of cognitive psychology research on verbal overshadowing, implicit statistical learning, and the costs of deliberation (Schooler & Engstler-Schooler, 1990; Dijksterhuis, 2004; Williams et al., 2013). Rather than building on a specific prior ML method, it repurposes well-controlled psychological paradigms as scaled-up evaluation tasks for LLMs and LMMs, using the psychology literature as a heuristic to predict where CoT will fail. The closest prior ML work is the meta-study by Sprague et al. (2025), which identifies broad task categories where CoT helps (math, symbolic reasoning) but does not offer a principled mechanism for predicting failure cases; this paper provides that mechanism by grounding predictions in known human cognitive limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: as inference-time reasoning becomes the default in frontier models (o-series, Claude thinking), practitioners need to know when CoT actively harms performance rather than merely failing to help. The released benchmark of six scaled-up tasks gives ML practitioners concrete evaluation suites for stress-testing reasoning pipelines. Key limitations and open questions include whether the heuristic covers all CoT failure modes (it is explicitly not exhaustive), whether alternative task implementations (e.g., memory-constrained agents) might produce different human-model parallels, and whether findings generalize beyond the specific prompting strategies tested. The bootstrap validation showing the heuristic outperforms random sampling at identifying CoT failures is encouraging but relies on a single meta-study as the comparison population.
