## Problem

Unsupervised DNA pre-training on raw sequences alone cannot capture the cell-type-specific regulatory context that governs DNA function, since genomic profiles like chromatin accessibility and histone modifications are essential for understanding sequence function. Meanwhile, existing supervised genomic profile prediction models (e.g., Enformer) use a shared encoder with independent prediction heads, failing to model species-specific regulatory differences and the interdependencies between different genomic profiles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252867/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 131,072 bp DNA sequences from human and mouse genomes, first compressed to 128 bp resolution via 1D-CNNs, and predicts 5,313 (human) or 1,643 (mouse) genomic profile tracks spanning four assay types: chromatin accessibility (DNase/ATAC-seq), transcription factor binding (TF ChIP-seq), histone modifications (Histone ChIP-seq), and transcriptional activity (CAGE). The learned intermediate representations serve as transferable DNA embeddings for downstream classification tasks (regulatory elements, splice sites, epigenetic marks) via either full fine-tuning or frozen-embedding evaluation.

## Method

SPACE is a three-stage architecture: (1) a CNN-based local context aggregation module (following Enformer) compresses nucleotides to 128 bp resolution; (2) a species-aware Transformer encoder uses sparse cross-species Mixture-of-Experts with N shared experts and M species-specific gating networks, augmented by species-specific embedding tokens and a mutual information loss that encourages species-specific expert specialization; (3) a profile-grouped enhancement decoder categorizes base predictions into Q biological profile types and applies K cross-profile shared experts via a dual-gated mechanism—group-level gating (based on species embedding and pooled sequence context) captures evolutionary conservation, while expert-level gating (based on prediction patterns) captures functional interdependencies between profiles. The final prediction is a residual sum of base and enhanced predictions. Training uses Poisson negative log-likelihood plus mutual information regularization (α=0.01) over 50K steps on 8 A40 GPUs, with a 588M-parameter sparse-activated model.

## Results

On the 18-task Nucleotide Transformer downstream benchmark (MCC), SPACE achieves state-of-the-art performance on 11 out of 18 tasks, outperforming both the supervised Enformer baseline and the 2.5B-parameter NT-Multispecies model. Notable wins include H3K4me3 (0.661 vs. Enformer 0.635), Enhancers (0.631 vs. 0.614), and H3K9ac (0.635 vs. 0.593). On the GUE cross-species benchmark (yeast epigenetic marks, MCC), SPACE improves over Enformer by 8.9–27.3 points (e.g., H3K4me3: 49.47 vs. 22.19), and on Genomic Benchmarks, SPACE reaches 0.905 MCC on mouse enhancers and 0.721 on Drosophila enhancers vs. Enformer's 0.835 and 0.613.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPACE (588M sparse) outperforms Enformer (the primary supervised baseline) on 11/18 NT downstream tasks, with the largest margins on H3K9ac (+4.2), H3K4me3 (+2.6), and Enhancers (+1.7) MCC; it loses on splicing tasks (Acceptors: 0.902 vs. 0.739 for Enformer, but NT-Multispecies reaches 0.964). Against the unsupervised NT-Multispecies(2.5B) model, SPACE wins on all chromatin and regulatory tasks but trails on splicing (0.906 vs. 0.970 All), suggesting MLM pre-training retains an edge for short-motif tasks. On the GUE cross-species benchmark, SPACE dramatically surpasses Enformer on yeast epigenetic marks (e.g., H3K4me3: 49.47 vs. 22.19 MCC) and viral classification (F1 70.26 vs. 61.33), while also exceeding DNABERT-2 and all NT variants. On Genomic Benchmarks, SPACE beats Caduceus-PS on mouse enhancers (0.905 vs. 0.793) and Drosophila enhancers (0.721 vs. not reported by Caduceus), and outperforms Enformer on all five evaluated tasks. On the BEND frozen-embedding benchmark, SPACE achieves SOTA on chromatin accessibility, histone modification, and CpG methylation, surpassing the second-best by 0.04 on chromatin accessibility, though it underperforms on variant-effect tasks where MLM-based models (e.g., GPN-MSA) excel.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACE builds directly on Enformer's architecture (CNN-based local context aggregation, Poisson loss, same pre-training dataset) but departs from it in two key ways: replacing the shared encoder with a species-aware sparse MoE encoder and replacing independent prediction heads with a profile-grouped dual-gated expert decoder. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant unsupervised DNA pre-training paradigm (DNABERT, Nucleotide Transformer, HyenaDNA) by arguing that supervised genomic profile prediction encodes richer biological context than pure sequence modeling (MLM/NTP). The MoE mechanism is adapted from LLM literature (Switch Transformers, DeepSeek-V2) and repurposed for cross-species and cross-profile knowledge sharing in genomics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption in DNA foundation models that unsupervised sequence pre-training is the optimal paradigm, offering empirical evidence that supervised genomic profile prediction yields stronger representations for downstream tasks. However, the comparison is confounded by model scale (588M sparse vs. NT's 2.5B dense), and the BEND benchmark reveals that SPACE still struggles with variant effect prediction, suggesting supervised pre-training alone may not capture all aspects of sequence semantics. Key open questions include whether scaling to more species and larger model sizes would close remaining gaps, and whether hybrid objectives combining supervised profile prediction with unsupervised sequence modeling could yield the best of both paradigms.
