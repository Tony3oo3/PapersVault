## Problem

Understanding the behavior of personalized dynamical systems traditionally requires a two-step process: discovering closed-form ODEs and then performing mathematical analysis to extract semantic properties (shape, extrema, asymptotes). This process is time-intensive, demands expert knowledge, and becomes intractable for complex equations. The prior direct semantic modeling framework (Semantic ODE) bypassed this analysis but was restricted to one-dimensional trajectories with only the initial condition as input, precluding its use for realistic multi-dimensional systems with patient covariates, doses, and other static features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static feature vector v ∈ R^K (which may include numerical covariates like weight and creatinine, categorical variables like sex or genotype, treatment dose, and initial trajectory values) and outputs a full M-dimensional trajectory x: T → R^M over a time domain. Crucially, the output also includes an explicit semantic representation: a composition (a sequence of shape motifs like 'increasing-convex-bounded') and a set of numerical properties (transition point coordinates, derivatives, horizontal asymptote values). Each trajectory dimension is modeled independently, so the M-dimensional output is assembled from M separate univariate semantic predictors, and the model handles categorical features natively through GAM shape functions without one-hot encoding.

## Method

EPISODE decomposes the semantic predictor F_sem into a composition map and a property map. The composition map is a shallow classification decision tree that, given the static features, predicts which admissible composition (trajectory shape) applies; it is trained via greedy CART-style splitting that minimizes a per-sample trajectory fitting error rather than classification accuracy. The property map consists of one sub-map per predicted composition, where each sub-map is a set of generalized additive models (GAMs) with B-spline basis functions, one per trajectory property (e.g., maximum value, time of maximum, horizontal asymptote). To enforce physical constraints (e.g., positivity, ordering of transition points) by construction, the method introduces 'raw shape functions' that are passed through transformations like softplus before being combined into the final GAM. Training is sequential: the composition map is fitted first (using DTW-then-MSE matching of each composition to each sample), then all property sub-maps are trained in parallel via L-BFGS on the MSE between the reconstructed trajectory and observations. A fixed, non-trainable trajectory predictor F_traj converts the predicted (composition, properties) pair back into an actual trajectory, ensuring consistency.

## Results

EPISODE and its prior-knowledge variant EPISODE* are evaluated on six datasets (SIR, HIV, PK, Tumor, Tacrolimus, Bike sharing) using mean RMSE (lower is better). The most striking gains are on HIV (σ=0.01: 0.088 vs. 0.175–0.188 for all baselines) and PK (σ=0.01: 0.088 vs. 0.145–0.211), where EPISODE outperforms every black-box and closed-form baseline by 2–5×. On SIR (σ=0.01), EPISODE* achieves the best error of 0.012, beating SINDy-5 (0.014) and all neural ODE variants. On the real Tacrolimus dataset, EPISODE (0.256) matches ANODE (0.250) while substantially outperforming SINDy-5 (0.316) and the expert PopPK model (0.351).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three black-box baselines (NeuralODE, ANODE, LatentODE), EPISODE/EPISODE* wins decisively on HIV (0.088 vs. 0.183–0.188 at σ=0.01) and PK (0.088 vs. 0.145–0.156), is competitive on SIR and Tacrolimus, but trails on Bike sharing (0.192 vs. 0.149 for LatentODE) and Tumor σ=0.01 (0.144 vs. 0.300 for NeuralODE—actually EPISODE wins here; the loss is on Tumor where SINDy-20/WSINDy-20 achieve 0.056–0.062). Against the closed-form ODE discovery baselines (SINDy-5/20, WSINDy-5/20), EPISODE dominates on HIV, PK, and Tacrolimus, where SINDy/WSINDy either fail to converge (†) or produce substantially higher errors (e.g., WSINDy-20 on Tacrolimus: 0.816 vs. 0.256). The one notable loss is on Tumor σ=0.01, where WSINDy-20 (0.056) and SINDy-20 (0.062) outperform EPISODE (0.144), though EPISODE* (0.098) narrows the gap to SINDy-5 (0.095). Overall, EPISODE's inductive biases (smoothness, limited inflection points) act as effective regularizers that explain its advantage over black-box methods on structured dynamical systems, while its transparency and editability come at no significant performance cost on most tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Semantic ODE (Kacprzyk & van der Schaar, 2025), which introduced direct semantic modeling (DSM) for one-dimensional trajectories parameterized only by initial conditions, to multi-dimensional personalized dynamical systems with arbitrary static features. It departs from the dominant ODE discovery paradigm (SINDy, WSINDy) by eliminating the need to find and analyze closed-form equations, instead predicting trajectory shape (composition) via a decision tree and trajectory properties via generalized additive models. The approach also contrasts with black-box neural ODE methods (Neural ODE, ANODE, Latent ODE) by offering full transparency, editability, and the ability to enforce domain-specific behavioral constraints by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EPISODE addresses a critical gap in high-stakes domains like pharmacology and medicine, where black-box models lack verifiable guarantees and ODE discovery produces equations that are intractable to analyze or revise. The Tacrolimus case study demonstrates that a fully transparent model can outperform both discovered ODEs and an expert-designed population pharmacokinetic model while certifying biological plausibility. Key limitations include restriction to finite (non-oscillatory) compositions and the high computational cost of fitting the composition map (O(D·M·|C'|) preprocessing). Open questions include extending to periodic trajectories, more efficient composition map fitting, and validation in larger-scale clinical settings.
