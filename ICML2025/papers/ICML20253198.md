## Problem

No publicly available dataset has been explicitly designed for 3D trajectory prediction, and the task is substantially more complex than its 2D counterpart: predicting each 3D point requires optimizing a 3D Gaussian with 9 free parameters versus 5 for 2D, roughly doubling per-point complexity and scaling linearly with the number of agents. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the 3DMoTraj dataset (UUV trajectories in oceanic environments) and a method that explicitly reduces 3D prediction complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 frames (4 seconds at 2 fps) of observed 3D trajectory per agent, from which position, velocity, and acceleration are extracted along each axis and encoded via MLPs. It produces 12 frames (6 seconds) of predicted 3D future trajectory, with intermediate key points at frames 3, 6, 9, and 12. Evaluation uses a leave-one-out cross-validation protocol across 8 oceanic scenarios, and the dataset additionally provides frame-wise motion intentions (speed up/down/constant) and static intentions (endpoint octant in a Frenet-Serret local frame).

## Method

The method builds on LBEBM's encoder and key-point-conditioned framework but replaces its single decoder with two stages. First, decoupled trajectory prediction uses three independent MLP decoders to predict future trajectories along the x-, y-, and z-axes separately, reducing each 3D point from 9 to 6 free parameters (three 1D Gaussians) and producing a coarse prediction. Second, correlated trajectory refinement employs a novel SCA-LSTM (State-Correlation and Aggregation LSTM) with a center LSTM and three node LSTMs; a State-Correlation module uses gated message passing from center to node hidden states to refine node cell states, while a State-Aggregation module uses axis-wise attention and aggregation gates to update the center cell state from node hidden states. After iterative SC/SA refinement (3 iterations, 1 layer optimal), the LSTMs predict per-axis offsets that correct the coarse predictions. Training uses a composite L2 loss over key points, future trajectories, and refinement offsets, with the Adam optimizer at lr=0.0005 for 100 epochs.

## Results

On the 3DMoTraj dataset (best-of-20 evaluation), the proposed method achieves a mean ADE of 0.58 m and FDE of 1.02 m, outperforming the second-best method S-Implicit (ADE 0.68, FDE 1.22) by 14.6% and 16.3% respectively. The method also surpasses NPSN (ADE 0.75, FDE 1.04) by 22.7% in ADE. On 2D generalization tests, the decoupled prediction strategy improves over the LBEBM baseline by 4.5%/5.0% (ADE/FDE) on ETH/UCY and 2.4%/3.3% on SDD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on both ADE and FDE across the 3DMoTraj dataset. Against the closest competitor S-Implicit (Mohamed et al., 2022), it reduces ADE from 0.68 to 0.58 (−14.6%) and FDE from 1.22 to 1.02 (−16.3%). NPSN (Bae et al., 2022) achieves a competitive FDE of 1.04 but lags significantly in ADE (0.75 vs. 0.58, a 22.7% gap). MRGTraj (Peng et al., 2023) and MS-TIP (Nath et al., 2024) trail by 15.5% and 17.2% in ADE respectively. The gap widens substantially against RNN-based and one-shot methods: LBEBM (the baseline encoder) is outperformed by 31% in ADE, while SSTGCNN (Mohamed et al., 2020) and MSRL (Wu et al., 2023) are surpassed by over 70% and 61% in ADE respectively, confirming that 2D-designed methods struggle with the added z-axis complexity. Visual comparisons (Figure 5) further show the advantage is most pronounced along the z-axis and on fluctuating trajectories caused by ocean currents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LBEBM (Pang et al., 2021) as its encoder and key-point prediction backbone, but departs from the 2D trajectory prediction paradigm by introducing a decoupled-then-refine strategy motivated by the mathematical decomposition of 3D Gaussian distributions into independent 1D marginals plus a correlation correction term. The SCA-LSTM refinement module is a novel architecture that has no direct predecessor in the trajectory prediction literature; it draws on gated message-passing ideas from graph neural networks but applies them to inter-axis state correlation within a single agent. The paper positions itself against the explicit gap that no publicly available 3D trajectory prediction dataset existed, distinguishing its UUV-based 3DMoTraj from 2D pedestrian/vehicle datasets (ETH/UCY, SDD, Argoverse, Waymo) and from intention-annotated datasets (PIE, LOKI) that remain 2D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3DMoTraj dataset fills a genuine gap as the first benchmark explicitly designed for 3D trajectory prediction, and its frame-wise motion and static intention annotations (using the Frenet-Serret frame for octant labeling) provide a structured foundation for future intention-conditioned 3D prediction research. However, the dataset is limited to three UUVs per scenario with relatively simple formation-based interactions, and the method's failure case analysis reveals that it struggles with multiple sharp bends, suggesting that richer interaction modeling and 3D environmental representations (e.g., point cloud maps) are needed for truly complex 3D scenarios. Open questions include whether the decoupled-then-refine strategy generalizes to larger multi-agent 3D scenes, to UAV trajectories in air, and to settings with occlusion or sensor noise beyond ocean-current fluctuations.
