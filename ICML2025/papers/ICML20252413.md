## Problem

Instance-wise feature selection for images typically sparsifies at the pixel level, which admits trivial, uninformative solutions (e.g., evenly-spaced masks that remove 80% of pixels without degrading accuracy) and fails to produce human-understandable explanations. Additionally, existing methods fix a single sparsity level across all instances, ignoring that the information required for a confident prediction varies per sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252413/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, partitions it into ~100 SLIC superpixels, and produces a binary mask over those regions (not pixels), a class prediction from a ViT-Tiny classifier, and 3-dimensional part-relationship embeddings per region. At inference, the sparsity threshold τ is determined dynamically per instance by increasing it until the classifier's maximum class probability reaches a user-specified certainty δ (0.8 for ImageNet, 0.99 otherwise), so the number of active regions adapts to prediction difficulty.

## Method

P2P learns a binary mask over instance-specific SLIC superpixels rather than individual pixels, enforcing that each selectable feature is a perceptually meaningful atomic region. A frozen MobileNetV3 selector predicts selection parameters at pixel-level, which are aggregated within each region to yield region-wise selection probabilities parameterized as a non-diagonal logit-normal distribution, capturing inter-part relationships through learnable region embeddings whose dot products form the covariance matrix. A Gumbel-Softmax trick enables differentiable binary mask sampling, and the masked input is fed to a ViT-Tiny classifier. The training loss combines cross-entropy, a thresholded sparsity penalty (active only when the expected fraction of active pixels exceeds τ), and an L1 regularizer on the covariance norm. During training, τ is randomly sampled from U[0.05, 1] per instance to make the model adaptable; at inference, τ is found by stepwise increase until the classifier's certainty meets δ, effectively letting the model query for more information only when needed.

## Results

P2P achieves near-blackbox accuracy while masking 20–50% of image content: 94.45% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 95.79% blackbox), 89.53% on COCO-10 (vs. 89.36% blackbox), 68.70% on ImageNet (vs. 71.22% blackbox), and 94.42% on ImageNet-9 (vs. 94.45% blackbox). On localization, P2P substantially outperforms all baselines, reaching 47.01% on COCO-10 and 69.25% on ImageNet-9, compared to the next-best COMET at 36.43% and 63.73% respectively. On the semi-synthetic BAM datasets, P2P matches COMET in accuracy (88.92% vs. 89.33% on BAM Object) while achieving the best localization (83.02% vs. 82.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2P outperforms COMET (Zhang et al., 2025) on localization by 10.6 points on COCO-10 (47.01% vs. 36.43%) and 5.5 points on ImageNet-9 (69.25% vs. 63.73%), while matching or exceeding its accuracy on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., 89.53% vs. 88.43% on COCO-10); however, COMET retains a 2.2-point accuracy edge on ImageNet (70.90% vs. 68.70%). The paper further shows COMET's continuous-valued mask is unfaithful: inverting the mask (COMET⁻¹) yields nearly identical accuracy, indicating predictions do not depend on the highlighted regions. Against B-cos (Bohle et al., 2022), P2P wins on both accuracy (e.g., 94.45% vs. 93.80% on CIFAR-10, 89.53% vs. 84.54% on COCO-10) and localization (47.01% vs. 34.57% on COCO-10). P2P decisively outperforms DiET (Bhalla et al., 2024) and RB-AEM (Ganjdanesh et al., 2022) across all datasets, with DiET collapsing to 5.08% on ImageNet and RB-AEM reaching only 57.76%. REAL-X (Jethani et al., 2021) is the closest pixel-level baseline on accuracy (69.08% on ImageNet) but trails P2P on localization (56.13% vs. 69.25% on ImageNet-9). On the controlled BAM datasets, P2P matches COMET in accuracy while achieving the best localization, confirming that the region-based formulation captures the true predictive signal without spurious shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-wise feature selection framework introduced by Chen et al. (2018) from tabular data to images, but departs fundamentally from prior image-based methods (REAL-X, DiET, RB-AEM, COMET) by shifting the unit of selection from individual pixels to semantically meaningful regions (superpixels), drawing an explicit analogy to Group Lasso. It also introduces two additional departures: binary (rather than continuous-valued) masks to ensure true information removal, and a dynamic thresholding mechanism that adapts sparsity per-instance based on classifier certainty rather than relying on a fixed regularization hyperparameter. The logit-normal distribution for modeling inter-region relationships is borrowed from segmentation and concept-based model literature but applied here for the first time in the feature-selection context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental misspecification in pixel-level instance-wise feature selection: random evenly-spaced masks achieve near-optimal accuracy, meaning the optimization landscape admits trivial, uninformative solutions that provide no interpretability. The COMET⁻¹ result (inverting the mask yields comparable performance) raises serious questions about the faithfulness of continuous-valued masking approaches, a concern that extends to any method where 'dimmed' pixels retain numerical influence. Open questions include whether region-based selection generalizes beyond superpixel-based partitioning (e.g., to learned or object-aware segments), how the approach scales to architectures that natively handle variable region counts, and whether the dynamic thresholding mechanism transfers to multi-label or open-vocabulary settings.
