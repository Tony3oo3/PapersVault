## Problem

Standard flow matching for molecular structure generation only minimizes the energy of trajectory samples during training, leaving the energy landscape unshaped for the contrastive samples the model itself produces. This paper makes the connection between flow matching and energy-based models explicit, showing that the x1-parameterized CFM loss is already an energy minimization, and proposes a simple modification that additionally shapes the landscape using the model's own predictions to drive the flow map toward idempotency.

## Input / Output

For molecular docking, the model takes protein receptor Cartesian coordinates (R^{3×n_p}) and a random ligand configuration sampled from a harmonic prior, producing ligand atom 3D positions (R^{3×n_l}) as the binding pose. For protein backbone generation, the input is a random composition of uniform SO(3) rotations and Gaussian translations (the prior on SE(3)^N), and the output is a full protein backbone as N SE(3) frames. At inference, the predictor-refiner sampler performs two network evaluations per ODE step (one prediction, one refinement), so 10 steps yield 20 NFEs, matching the computational budget of the HarmonicFlow baseline at 20 steps.

## Method

IDFlow augments standard conditional flow matching with an idempotent refinement loss: during 50% of training, the network predicts x̂₁ from the interpolant x_t, detaches the prediction, feeds it back through itself (up to K_max iterations), and minimizes the L2 distance between the refined output and the ground-truth x₁. This makes the flow map approximately idempotent—f(f(x)) ≈ f(x)—so the network simultaneously acts as a neural sampler and a structure refiner, analogous to AlphaFold's recycling mechanism. The energy function is the reconstruction error ||G(x̂₁) − x̂₁||², which under the Boltzmann distribution defines a density whose gradient flow converges to the data manifold. At inference, the predictor-refiner sampler integrates the ODE with one refinement per step, and the idempotency guarantees that additional function evaluations do not drastically alter the output, providing stability. The framework is orthogonal to the base architecture and applies identically in Euclidean space (Tensor Field Networks for docking) and on the Riemannian manifold SE(3)^N (IPA-based structure transformer for protein backbones) using geodesic interpolants via the exponential and logarithmic maps.

## Results

On single-ligand docking (PDBBind, time split, radius pocket), IDFlow achieves 34.7% RMSD < 2 Å and 3.1 Å median, outperforming HarmonicFlow (28.3%, 3.2 Å) and Product Space Diffusion (15.2%, 4.3 Å) at the same 20 NFE budget. On multi-ligand docking (Binding MOAD), IDFlow reaches 83.1% RMSD < 5 Å versus 75.0% for HarmonicFlow. For protein backbone generation on PDB (200 NFEs), IDFlow achieves 0.927 designability, surpassing FrameFlow (0.824) by 10.3% and approaching FoldFlow2 (0.94) while maintaining balanced helix/strand content (0.61/0.16 vs. FoldFlow2's 0.87/0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDFlow consistently outperforms its direct flow-matching baselines: on single-ligand docking it beats HarmonicFlow by 5.5–6.4 percentage points in RMSD < 2 Å across all PDBBind splits, and on protein backbone generation it surpasses FrameFlow by 5.5–10.3% in designability at matched NFEs. Against diffusion baselines, IDFlow outperforms Product Space Diffusion (DiffDock) by 19.5 points on time-split radius-pocket docking (34.7% vs. 15.2% RMSD < 2 Å) and matches or exceeds EigenFold Diffusion on multi-ligand docking (43.8% vs. 39.7% RMSD < 2 Å, 83.1% vs. 73.5% RMSD < 5 Å). On protein backbone generation, IDFlow (200 NFEs) approaches FoldFlow2 (0.927 vs. 0.94 designability) without sequence conditioning or a more advanced architecture, while maintaining balanced secondary structure (0.61 helix / 0.16 strand) unlike FoldFlow2's helix collapse (0.87/0.01). IDFlow also outperforms GAFL (0.927 vs. 0.866 designability) and RFdiffusion (0.927 vs. 0.89) on PDB, though RFdiffusion was trained on a larger dataset. The method ties HarmonicFlow on multi-ligand RMSD < 2 Å (43.8% vs. 44.4%) but clearly wins on RMSD < 5 Å (83.1% vs. 75.0%), indicating better mode coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) through an energy-based model lens, specifically adopting LeCun's (2022) contrastive EBM principle of mapping off-manifold points to on-manifold points. It builds directly on HarmonicFlow (Stark et al., 2023) for docking and FrameFlow (Yim et al., 2023a) for protein backbone generation, adding an idempotent refinement loss that makes the flow map a predictor-refiner. The conceptual lineage connects to AlphaFold's iterative structure refinement, predictor-corrector samplers in diffusion models, and consistency models, but the paper's specific contribution is showing that the x1-parameterized CFM loss naturally induces an energy landscape where the reconstruction error serves as the energy function, and that training for idempotency (f(f(x)) = f(x)) is the principled way to shape this landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The idempotent flow map provides a theoretically grounded justification for iterative refinement in structure generation, bridging the gap between flow matching training and the empirically successful refinement strategies in AlphaFold. The method introduces only one hyperparameter (K_max) and requires minimal architectural changes, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing flow matching pipelines. Key limitations include increased training cost from multiple forward passes and discretization errors that limit the benefit of additional refinement steps. Open questions include whether combining the EBM framework with biophysics-informed energy functions (e.g., Amber) can produce chemically more plausible structures, and whether the idempotent objective generalizes to other generative tasks beyond molecular structure.
