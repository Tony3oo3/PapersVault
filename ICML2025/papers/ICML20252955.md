## Problem

Existing ICRL methods either assume access to a known or generative transition model, or rely on entropy-based exploration without theoretical guarantees on sample efficiency. No prior work provides a principled exploration strategy that provably recovers feasible constraints from interactive, model-free environments with tractable sample complexity bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252955/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert policy observations (action preferences at visited states) and environment transition tuples (s, a, s') collected online through exploration, alongside a known reward function and initial state distribution. It produces a feasible cost set — the minimal collection of cost functions c ∈ [0, C_max]^{S×A} under which the expert policy remains optimal — parameterized by a penalty vector ζ and a cost-shaping value function V^c. At each iteration it also outputs an exploration policy, and convergence is certified by a PAC optimality criterion (Definition 4.9) ensuring Q-function divergence between true and estimated feasible sets stays below ε with probability ≥ 1−δ.

## Method

The paper derives an explicit parametric form for the feasible cost set (Lemma 4.5: c = A_M^{r,π^E} ζ + (E − γP_T)V^c) and propagates estimation errors in transition dynamics and expert policy into bounded cost-function errors via Hoeffding confidence intervals (Lemma 4.8). Two exploration algorithms are then designed around this error bound: BEAR solves an RL subproblem at each iteration with the bounded error C_k(s,a) as the reward, directly minimizing aggregate estimation error; PCSE further restricts the exploration policy to a set Π_k of plausibly optimal policies (constrained by both cost budget and reward lower bound) and solves the resulting constrained optimization via a two-timescale stochastic approximation on the Lagrangian dual. Both algorithms are shown to satisfy the PAC optimality criterion with sample complexity Õ(σ²(2C_max − ε_K(1−γ))² / ((1−γ)²ε_K²C_max²)) for BEAR, and PCSE achieves a tighter bound that additionally depends on the minimum cost advantage gap, yielding faster convergence when the constraint-violating region is well-separated.

## Results

The paper evaluates BEAR and PCSE across four discrete Gridworld environments and one continuous Point Maze environment, measuring discounted cumulative rewards, discounted cumulative costs, and WGIoU (similarity between inferred and ground-truth cost functions). Results are reported as training curves rather than a summary table. PCSE converges the fastest among all six exploration strategies in all four Gridworlds, and in Gridworld-2 and Gridworld-4, WGIoU converges to a value less than 1 because some ground-truth constraints lie in non-critical regions that ICRL does not need to recover. In the continuous Point Maze, PCSE successfully infers spatially localized constraints (shown as blue dots) while achieving near-expert reward and cost performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BEAR and PCSE against four exploration baselines: random exploration, ε-greedy exploration, maximum-entropy exploration, and upper confidence bound (UCB) exploration. PCSE consistently outperforms all four baselines in convergence speed across all four Gridworld environments on all three metrics (rewards, costs, and WGIoU). BEAR also outperforms the four baselines but converges more slowly than PCSE, consistent with the theoretical result that PCSE's sample complexity is a tighter (smaller) upper bound. Maximum-entropy exploration, which is the most common strategy in prior ICRL work, produces overly conservative constraints and converges more slowly than both proposed methods. The paper does not report specific numerical gaps between methods, as results are presented as training curves in Figures 3 and 4 rather than a final-metric comparison table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ICRL problem formulation of Malik et al. (2021) and the maximum causal entropy constraint inference framework of Scobee & Sastry (2020), but departs from both by removing the assumption of a known or generative transition model. Prior ICRL exploration strategies (entropy maximization, random, ε-greedy) lack theoretical grounding in estimation error, and prior IRL exploration work (Metelli et al., 2021; Lindner et al., 2022) addresses reward recovery rather than constraint recovery. The paper's key conceptual departure is framing exploration as minimizing a quantifiable bounded error on the feasible cost set, yielding two algorithms (BEAR and PCSE) with the first tractable sample complexity guarantees for ICRL in an unknown environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework connecting exploration strategy to constraint estimation accuracy in ICRL, providing practitioners with a principled alternative to heuristic exploration (e.g., entropy maximization) that provably converges. The PCSE result—that restricting exploration to plausibly optimal policies yields tighter sample complexity than uniform error reduction—is a useful design principle for constrained learning more broadly. However, the discrete state-space assumption and direct dependence on |S|×|A| in sample complexity severely limit practical applicability; scaling to continuous or high-dimensional environments remains an open challenge. Future work on finite-horizon lower bounds, transferability of feasible constraints, and relaxing the expert-optimality assumption would strengthen the framework's generality.
