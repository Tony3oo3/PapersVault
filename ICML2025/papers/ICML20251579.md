## Problem

Existing ANN-to-SNN conversion methods suffer from three distinct error sources—clipping, quantization, and uneven spike arrival—that cause severe accuracy degradation at low latency, typically requiring tens to hundreds of time steps to recover performance comparable to the source ANN. This paper addresses the gap of achieving near-lossless conversion at ultra-low latency (as few as 2 time steps) by jointly mitigating all three error types rather than addressing them individually.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251579/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label, identical to the source ANN. The key operational difference is that inference is event-driven: the converted SNN processes each image over T discrete time steps (T=2 to T=64), where neurons accumulate membrane potential and emit binary or dual-polarity spikes, with the final classification determined by the maximum average postsynaptic potential in the output layer. The conversion itself is parameter-free at inference time—ANN weights and learned thresholds are directly mapped to SNN synaptic weights and firing thresholds without additional calibration.

## Method

The framework has three components that jointly eliminate the three conversion errors. First, the source ANN is trained with a quantization clip-floor-shift (QCFS) activation function featuring a learnable threshold λ per layer, which is then directly mapped to the SNN spike threshold θ, eliminating clipping error by construction. Second, a dual-threshold neuron is introduced that can emit negative spikes (when membrane potential drops below a small negative threshold θ′ and at least one positive spike has already fired), with a modified reset rule that adds back the positive threshold to restore erroneously subtracted potential—this corrects the over-firing caused by non-uniform spike arrival and reduces quantization error. Third, the initial membrane potential of each layer is analytically set to θ/2, which minimizes the expected squared conversion error between ANN activations and SNN firing rates. Together, these techniques allow the converted SNN to match or exceed source ANN accuracy at T=2 time steps (e.g., 94.75% on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18) while consuming orders of magnitude less energy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed method achieves 94.75% top-1 accuracy at T=2 and 95.99% at T=4, matching or exceeding the source ANN accuracy (96.39%) with only 2–4 time steps. On ImageNet, the method reaches 74.15% (VGG-16) and 72.37% (ResNet-34) at T=16, substantially outperforming the closest baseline QCFS (50.97% and 59.35% respectively). Energy consumption drops from 4.170 mJ (ANN) to 0.008 mJ at T=4 while maintaining 76.50% accuracy on CIFAR-100 with VGG-16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every architecture and time step where comparisons are available. The most dramatic gains appear at ultra-low latency: on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 at T=2, the method achieves 94.75% versus 75.44% for both QCFS and Opt (a +19.31 pp gap), and on ResNet-20 at T=2 it reaches 84.52% versus 73.20% for QCFS (+11.32 pp). At T=4, the advantage narrows but remains clear: 95.99% vs. 90.43% (QCFS) on ResNet-18 and 90.55% vs. 83.75% (QCFS) on ResNet-20. Older conversion methods (RMP, TSC, RTS, RNL) only report results at T≥32, where the proposed method still leads by 3–5 pp (e.g., 95.43% vs. 92.79% TSC on VGG-16 at T=64). On ImageNet at T=16, the method achieves 74.15% (VGG-16) and 72.37% (ResNet-34), exceeding QCFS by 23.18 pp and 13.02 pp respectively, and surpassing SNNC-AP which requires T=256 to reach comparable accuracy. The dual-threshold neuron ablation (Table 3) confirms that the largest single contribution comes from this component, adding up to +19.31 pp on ResNet-18 at T=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the ANN-to-SNN conversion lineage that began with Cao et al. (2015) and progressed through weight/threshold normalization (Diehl et al., 2015; Rueckauer et al., 2017), residual membrane potential (Han et al., 2020), and threshold offset (Deng & Gu, 2021). Its most direct predecessor is Bu et al. (2023), whose quantization clip-floor-shift (QCFS) activation function is adopted here as the learnable clipping mechanism, and Bu et al. (2022), whose membrane potential initialization idea is extended with a derived optimal value (θ/2). The key departure is the dual-threshold neuron, which introduces a negative-spike correction mechanism absent from all prior conversion methods, targeting the unevenness error that previous work could only mitigate by increasing time steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling SNNs to operate at T=2 with near-ANN accuracy (94.75% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18) and ~1000× lower energy per inference (0.004 mJ vs. 4.17 mJ), which is critical for real-time deployment on neuromorphic hardware where long time steps are infeasible. Limitations include the empirical choice of the negative threshold (−1e-3), the requirement to train the source ANN with a modified activation rather than converting an off-the-shelf ReLU network, and evaluation restricted to image classification on standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether the dual-threshold mechanism translates to actual spiking hardware (where negative spikes may not be physically realizable), whether it generalizes to non-vision tasks, and whether the approach scales to transformer-based or generative architectures.
