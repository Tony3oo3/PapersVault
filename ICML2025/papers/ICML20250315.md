## Problem

Existing LLM guardrails filter harmful text inputs and outputs but cannot safeguard LLM-based agents as sequential decision-making systems, where unsafe behaviors emerge over multi-step interactions with dynamic environments. Additionally, safety policies governing agents are encoded in lengthy, complex regulatory documents (e.g., EU AI Act, corporate handbooks), making it difficult to systematically extract, verify, and enforce rules across different platforms and action types.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, SHIELDAGENT receives the protected agent's invoked action a_i, the current environment observation o_i (which for web agents includes HTML accessibility trees, visual screenshots, and terminal stdout), the full interaction history H_<i, and the pre-constructed safety policy model G_ASPM. It outputs a binary safety label l_s, a list of per-rule violation flags V_s identifying which specific LTL rules are violated, and a natural-language explanation T_s justifying the decision with remediation suggestions. The verification is action-scoped: only the rule circuit corresponding to the invoked action predicate is checked, rather than the entire policy.

## Method

SHIELDAGENT constructs an Action-based Safety Policy Model (ASPM) by extracting LTL rules from policy documents via GPT-4o, then iteratively refining them through Verifiability Refinement (decomposing compound rules into atomic, observable predicates) and Redundancy Pruning (merging semantically similar predicates via embedding-based clustering). Rules are organized into action-based probabilistic circuits using spectral clustering over state predicates, so that only the circuit relevant to the invoked action is verified at inference. During shielding, the agent generates a step-by-step plan using a tool library (search, binary-check, content moderation APIs, formal model-checking via StormPy) to assign boolean truth values to all state predicates, then runs formal verification on each rule. Safety is determined by a Markov Logic Network–based relative condition inspired by control barrier functions: the action is safe iff P(action=1) − P(action=0) ≥ ε, where rule weights θ are trained via a guardrail hinge loss on labeled trajectories. A hybrid memory module (short-term KV-cache of interaction history plus long-term storage of successful shielding workflows) further reduces redundant computation.

## Results

On SHIELDAGENT-BENCH, SHIELDAGENT achieves 90.4% guardrail accuracy (ACC@G) under agent-based perturbations and 91.7% under environment-based perturbations, with the lowest false positive rate (4.8% average) and a high rule recall rate (ARR@R) of 90.1%. Across three existing benchmarks (ST-WebAgentBench, VWA-Adv, AgentHarm), SHIELDAGENT achieves 91.1%, 94.1%, and 86.9% accuracy respectively, outperforming the best baseline by 7.4% on average. In terms of efficiency, SHIELDAGENT reduces closed-source API queries by 64.7% and inference time by 58.2% compared to the most expensive baseline (Rule Traverse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHIELDAGENT is compared against three baselines: Direct prompting (GPT-4o given the full policy), Rule Traverse (iteratively checking each rule), and GuardAgent (Xiang et al., 2024, a prior LLM-agent-based guardrail). On SHIELDAGENT-BENCH, SHIELDAGENT outperforms the strongest baseline (Rule Traverse) by 10.2% in average accuracy (90.4% vs. 82.0% agent-based; 91.7% vs. 81.6% environment-based) while simultaneously achieving a substantially lower false positive rate (5.6% vs. 18.1% agent-based). Against GuardAgent, SHIELDAGENT gains 9.0% accuracy under agent-based perturbations and 6.9% under environment-based perturbations, with a 5.1–6.7 percentage point reduction in FPR. On the three existing benchmarks, SHIELDAGENT leads by 7.4% average accuracy over the best baseline, with the largest margin on ST-WebAgentBench (91.1% vs. 84.0% for GuardAgent). Notably, SHIELDAGENT also achieves the best efficiency: it requires only 9.5–11.2 API queries per sample versus 27.1–31.5 for Rule Traverse, and runs in 31–34 seconds versus 75–80 seconds, a 64.7% reduction in queries and 58.2% reduction in inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic rule circuit framework from R²-Guard (Kang & Li, 2024) and Markov Logic Networks (Richardson & Domingos, 2006) from model-level LLM guardrails to agent-level action trajectory verification. It departs from GuardAgent (Xiang et al., 2024), which operates solely in textual space and relies on the model's internal knowledge, by introducing explicit policy compliance through LTL rule extraction, formal model-checking verification, and action-based probabilistic circuits (Kisa et al., 2014) that scope verification to only the relevant action. The key conceptual shift is treating safety as a structured logical reasoning problem over a policy knowledge graph rather than a content-moderation or free-form LLM judgment task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM agents are deployed in high-stakes domains (trading, shopping, data management), the gap between model-level guardrails and agent-level safety enforcement is a critical unsolved problem. SHIELDAGENT-BENCH (3K samples, 7 risk categories, 6 environments, two attack types) fills a clear benchmarking void, and the 64.7% API-query reduction alongside 90.1% rule recall suggests that structured formal verification can be both more accurate and more efficient than iterative LLM prompting. Open questions include scalability to non-web agents (embodied, code), the reliance on GPT-4o for rule extraction and planning (limiting open-source deployment), and whether the probabilistic circuit weights generalize across policy domains without retraining.
