## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models with classifier-free guidance produce low-diversity outputs when re-prompted with the same text, typically generating slight variations of a single image rather than exploring the full distribution. Additionally, these models can memorize and reproduce training-set images, raising reliability and intellectual-property concerns. Existing diversity-inducing methods perturb trajectories at every timestep with dense, never-zero kernels, offering no hard guarantee that outputs stay outside a protected region.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free post-hoc intervention applied to any pretrained diffusion model (class-conditional or text-conditional, in RGB or VAE latent space). At each reverse-diffusion timestep, it consumes the current latent x_t, the pretrained denoiser's prediction of the expected clean image x̂_0, and a set of shielded reference images (which may be static protected images, previously generated batches, or concurrent batch members). It outputs a corrected x̂_0 that is pushed to the boundary of any violated shield before the diffusion scheduler steps to t−1. The only tunable hyperparameter is the shield radius r, whose scale depends on the latent space dimensionality (e.g., r=20 for VAE latents, r=50 for RGB Simple Diffusion, r=200 for SD3).

## Method

SPELL (Sparse Repellency) adds a guidance term to the reverse diffusion SDE that is derived via Bayes' rule as an approximation to Doob's h-transform for the constraint x_0 ∉ S, where S is a union of balls of radius r around shielded images. At each timestep, the pretrained denoiser predicts x̂_0 = D_θ(t, x_t); for every shielded image z_k within distance r of x̂_0, a correction δ_k = ((x̂_0 − z_k)·r/‖x̂_0 − z_k‖) − (x̂_0 − z_k) is accumulated, which exactly places the expected output on the shield boundary. The ReLU weighting σ_relu(r/‖x̂_0 − z_k‖ − 1) ensures the correction is identically zero whenever the trajectory is already heading outside all shields, making interventions sparse in both time (mostly early timesteps) and space (typically one active shield). For intra-batch diversity, the shielded set is dynamically updated at each step to include the expected outputs of all other trajectories in the batch; for inter-batch diversity, previously generated images are added. At scale (1.2M ImageNet images), approximate nearest-neighbor search via Faiss Voronoi cells retrieves only nearby shields, keeping the per-step overhead negligible.

## Results

SPELL consistently increases diversity across all tested diffusion models: on Latent Diffusion (CC12M), Recall rises from 0.236 to 0.289 and Vendi Score from 2.527 to 2.695, while FID remains nearly unchanged (9.501 vs. 9.554). On SD3-Medium, Recall improves from 0.379 to 0.483 and Vendi from 3.749 to 4.711. In the shielded generation experiment, SPELL reduces the rate of generated images falling within protected shields from 7.60% to 0.16% when shielding all 1.2M ImageNet-1k training images, with precision remaining largely unaffected (0.792 → 0.768).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPELL is compared against three training-free diversity methods: Particle Guidance (PG, Corso et al. 2024), Interval Guidance (IG, Kynkäanniemi et al. 2024), and CADS (Sadat et al. 2024). At comparable diversity levels (Recall ≈ 0.30–0.35), SPELL maintains substantially higher precision (0.531–0.542) than PG at strength 256 (0.523) or CADS at mixture 0.001 (0.531), while achieving a lower FD_DINOv2 (98.7–106.8 vs. 154.9 for PG-256 and 105.1 for CADS). IG achieves the highest recall (up to 0.673) but at a severe cost: precision drops to 0.385–0.451 and CLIP score falls to 25.9–26.8, indicating degraded prompt adherence. PG degrades rapidly with strength: at strength 512, FID jumps to 23.1 and FD_DINOv2 to 286, whereas SPELL at r=40 keeps FID at 13.1. Overall, SPELL achieves the most favorable diversity–precision Pareto front, with the key advantage that its ReLU-gated interventions are sparse—trajectories already heading to diverse outputs are left untouched, preserving image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPELL is positioned as a sparse, hard-guarantee alternative to two closely related guidance mechanisms: Particle Guidance (Corso et al., 2024), which applies dense repulsion between all batch particles at every timestep, and Diffusion Posterior Sampling (Chung et al., 2023), which provides soft Gaussian-based corrections that never fully vanish. The paper derives SPELL from the Doob h-transform conditioning framework but replaces the intractable likelihood ratio with a simple geometric projection (pushing the expected denoised output to the boundary of a shield ball), yielding a ReLU-gated correction that is exactly zero outside the shield. This sparsity is the key conceptual departure: unlike prior diversity methods that perturb every trajectory at every step, SPELL intervenes only when and where needed, enabling scaling to reference sets of millions of images via approximate nearest-neighbor search (Faiss).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPELL offers a training-free, architecture-agnostic post-hoc method that practitioners can drop into any pretrained diffusion pipeline to address both diversity and memorization concerns without retraining or fine-tuning. The demonstrated scaling to 1.2M ImageNet images with a 0.16% shield-violation rate opens a practical path for model providers to guarantee non-reproduction of training data. Key limitations remain: the hard guarantee only holds for disjoint shields, the L2 distance is computed in VAE latent space rather than a semantic embedding space, and the method relies on the expected denoised output rather than samples from the true posterior, which may behave differently under ODE samplers. The unexpected increase in copyright-watermark frequency under SPELL also raises questions about how repellency interacts with spurious modes learned from training data.
