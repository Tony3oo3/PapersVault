## Problem

Existing RLHF regularization techniques for mitigating reward hacking (KL divergence, length penalties) impose constraints on the output space without addressing the underlying internal mechanisms of reward hacking within LLMs. This paper identifies that reward hacking manifests internally as an excessive increase in energy loss (L1-norm difference between input and output hidden states) in the LLM's final layer during RL training, and provides both theoretical and empirical evidence linking this phenomenon to reduced contextual relevance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text prompts and instructions, producing text responses as in typical RLHF. Unusually, during reward calculation it performs forward passes to compute the L1-norm energy loss in the final layer for both the SFT reference model (precomputed and stored per prompt) and the current RLHF policy model, then uses the absolute difference between these two values as a penalty subtracted from the reward model score. The energy loss is averaged across all tokens in the response, since each token requires a separate forward pass.

## Method

The paper defines energy loss in layer ℓ as ‖h_ℓ^in‖₁ − ‖h_ℓ^out‖₁ and formally identifies the 'energy loss phenomenon' where final-layer energy loss gradually increases during RL, with excessive increase characterizing reward hacking. Theoretically, Theorem 3 proves that under mild conditions, increased energy loss reduces the upper bound of contextual mutual information I(X;Y), linking energy loss to suppressed contextual relevance—a critical aspect of reward hacking. The proposed EPPO algorithm modifies the standard PPO reward by subtracting η|ΔE_SFT − ΔE_RLHF|, penalizing the increase in final-layer energy loss relative to the SFT baseline. Unlike output-space constraints (KL, length), this internal-representation penalty preserves a broader policy optimization landscape, and Corollary 5 shows EPPO can be interpreted as an entropy-regularized RL algorithm, explaining its benefits for training stability and exploration.

## Results

On Llama3-8B under GPT-4 evaluation, EPPO achieves win rates of 76% (Anthropic-Helpful), 78% (Anthropic-Harmless), 68% (AlpacaFarm), and 66% (TL;DR Summary) against standard PPO. Against the strongest RL regularization baselines, EPPO wins 59% vs PPO w/ KL on Anthropic-Helpful and 58% vs PPO w/ LP on AlpacaFarm. Against reward modeling baselines, EPPO wins 64% vs ERM-Mean on Anthropic-Helpful and 66% vs ERM-Mean on Anthropic-Harmless. EPPO also outperforms SFT by 72–81% win rate across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPPO consistently outperforms both RL regularization baselines: it wins 76–78% against PPO on Anthropic-Helpful/Harmless, and beats PPO w/ KL (59% on Helpful, 51% on AlpacaFarm) and PPO w/ LP (69% on Helpful, 58% on AlpacaFarm) across all four datasets, with the largest margins on Anthropic-Helpful where PPO w/ KL drops to only 46% on Harmless. Against reward modeling baselines, EPPO wins 64–66% vs ERM-Mean, 63–66% vs ERM-WCO, 59–64% vs ERM-UWO, and 53–57% vs WARM, though margins narrow on TL;DR Summary (40–56%). Against the advanced RM methods ODIN and InfoRM (Table 2), EPPO alone is competitive (61% vs ODIN on Helpful, 40% vs InfoRM on Helpful), and combining EPPO with these RMs (EPPO+ODIN, EPPO+InfoRM) further improves win rates to 67% and 46% respectively on Anthropic-Helpful. The paper attributes EPPO's advantage to constraining only the final-layer energy loss rather than the output space, preserving a broader policy optimization landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF framework (Ouyang et al., 2022) and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) by introducing a regularization that operates on internal LLM representations rather than the output space. It departs from prior reward hacking mitigation strategies—KL divergence penalties, length penalties, and reward model ensembles (ERM, WARM, ODIN, InfoRM)—by identifying a specific internal mechanism (energy loss in the final layer) and penalizing its increase directly in the reward signal. The theoretical analysis draws on variational mutual information bounds (Poole et al., 2019) and connects the energy loss penalty to entropy-regularized RL (Haarnoja et al., 2017), providing a principled interpretation that prior output-space constraints lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of energy loss as a measurable, layer-specific signature of reward hacking offers a new diagnostic tool for practitioners monitoring RLHF training, and the EPPO penalty is simple to implement (a single scalar subtraction from the reward). However, the theoretical bounds rely on a Gaussian variational assumption over L1 norms that is quite loose, and the threshold in Corollary 4 beyond which the negative correlation breaks is not precisely characterized. Open questions include whether the energy loss signal generalizes to non-transformer architectures, whether it captures all forms of reward hacking (e.g., style-based exploitation), and whether the entropy-regularization interpretation holds under different norm choices or layer selections.
