## Problem

Bivariate causal discovery from observational data relies on the algorithmic Markov condition, which states that the true causal direction yields a more succinct codelength for the joint distribution. Previous compression-based (MDL) methods approximate these codelengths using simple regression functions or Gaussian processes, creating a trade-off: simple functions compromise model fitness when ground-truth conditionals are complex, while GPs introduce prohibitive computational cost. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a more flexible and scalable model class for the conditionals without sacrificing the computability of model complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes univariate continuous cause-effect pairs (X, Y) from observational data, with both variables standardized to zero mean and unit variance to avoid scale-based bias. For each assumed direction, a Bayesian neural network models the conditional as a Gaussian with predicted mean and standard deviation, while the marginal of the assumed cause is encoded with a fixed standard Gaussian. The output is a signed causal indicator score (difference of two-direction codelengths) whose sign determines the inferred direction and whose magnitude reflects confidence.

## Method

COMIC models each conditional distribution p(Y|X) and p(X|Y) with a Bayesian neural network (one hidden layer of 50 tanh units, two output nodes for mean and log-standard-deviation) under Gaussian priors and mean-field variational posteriors. The codelength for each direction is computed via the variational Bayesian code (bits-back coding), which decomposes into an expected log-likelihood (data fitness) plus a KL divergence between the variational posterior and the prior (model complexity), equivalent to minimizing the negative ELBO. The causal direction is inferred by comparing the sum of the marginal codelength (standard Gaussian) and the variational conditional codelength in both directions. A key theoretical contribution is proving non-separable-compatibility of the resulting Bayesian causal models, which guarantees identifiability via marginal likelihoods in the large-sample limit. Hyperparameters of the priors are selected via Laplace approximation around the MAP solution, and training uses a warm-up schedule that anneals the KL weight from 0 to 1 over the first 250 epochs.

## Results

COMIC achieves an overall accuracy of 0.804±0.200 and overall Bi-AUROC of 0.843±0.186 across 13 benchmarks, ranking second overall behind GPLVM (0.909±0.105 accuracy, 0.951±0.069 Bi-AUROC). COMIC achieves perfect 1.00 accuracy and Bi-AUROC on all five Tagasovska et al. synthetic benchmarks (AN, AN-s, LS, LS-s, MN-U), matching QCCD, GPLVM, and LOCI. On the real-world Tübingen benchmark, COMIC reaches 0.67 accuracy and 0.75 Bi-AUROC, outperforming LOCI (0.56/0.65) and CAM (0.52/—) while remaining competitive with SLOPPY-AIC (0.71/—) and QCCD (0.77/—). Notably, COMIC runs on CPU, whereas GPLVM requires GPU execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMIC outperforms all maximum-likelihood-based baselines: it beats LOCI (0.773 overall accuracy) and CAM (0.725) by 3.1 and 7.9 percentage points respectively, confirming that the marginal-likelihood (Bayesian) objective provides a meaningful advantage over pure model-fitness scoring. Among complexity-based methods, COMIC surpasses SLOPPY-AIC (0.544), SLOPER (0.609), SLOPE (0.429), and IGCI-Uniform (0.464) by large margins, while remaining competitive with SLOPPY-BIC (0.749) and QCCD (0.796), which are the strongest compression-based baselines. On the challenging SIM and SIM-c datasets, COMIC (0.57/0.54 accuracy) matches SLOPPY-AIC and SLOPER but trails GPLVM (0.83/0.79) by a wide gap. On the real-world Tübingen benchmark, COMIC (0.67 accuracy) is slightly below SLOPPY-AIC (0.71) and QCCD (0.77) but clearly above LOCI (0.56) and CAM (0.52). GPLVM dominates overall (0.909 accuracy, 0.951 Bi-AUROC) but at the cost of requiring GPU computation and extensive random restarts, whereas COMIC runs on CPU with a single variational optimization pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMIC extends the line of MDL/compression-based causal discovery methods (SLOPE, SLOPER, SLOPPY, QCCD) that approximate Kolmogorov complexity via two-part codelengths, but replaces their simple regression models (polynomials, splines, quantiles) with Bayesian neural networks whose complexity is estimated through variational Bayesian (bits-back) coding. The identifiability analysis directly adapts the separable-compatibility framework from Dhir et al. (2024a), who used Gaussian process latent variable models, to the neural network setting. The variational inference machinery (mean-field posteriors, ELBO objective, warm-up schedule) is drawn from the Bayesian deep learning literature (Louizos et al., 2017; Honkela & Valpola, 2004), repurposed here as a codelength estimator rather than a predictive tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COMIC offers a practical middle ground between inflexible regression-based compression methods and computationally expensive GP-based approaches, running on CPUs while achieving competitive accuracy across 13 benchmarks. The non-separable-compatibility proof (Prop. 4.2) provides a theoretical guarantee that neural network-based Bayesian causal models are identifiable in the large-sample limit, which is a clean theoretical contribution. Key open questions include the non-convexity of the variational objective (convergence/consistency guarantees are lacking), the bias introduced by the standard Gaussian marginal assumption toward 'more Gaussian' causes, and the natural extension to multivariate settings. The method's reliance on a single hidden layer with 50 units also raises questions about whether deeper or wider architectures would improve performance on the harder benchmarks (SIM, CE-Cha) where it still trails GPLVM.
