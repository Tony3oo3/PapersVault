## Problem

As Moore's law tapers off, discovering novel analog IC circuit topologies becomes critical for sustaining performance, yet existing graph-based generative methods are limited to circuits of ~63 devices due to O(n²) device-pin graph complexity and non-optimal sequence encoding that causes error propagation. This paper addresses the scalability and precision gap by proposing a lightweight graph modeling framework that reduces representation complexity while maintaining a unique mapping between graph and circuit topology.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a circuit type specification (e.g., Op-Amp, LDO, PLL) and optional performance metric targets, and produces a token sequence of up to 1024 tokens drawn from a 1029-token vocabulary that encodes the optimal Eulerian circuit of the topology graph. The tokenizer spans three tiers: basic device pins (indices 0–549), mined subcircuit pins (550–890), and circuit-level pins like VDD/VSS (891–1027), allowing the model to generate at either the fundamental device-pin level or the hierarchical subcircuit level. The output sequence is decoded back into a circuit topology graph that can be simulated in SPICE for validity checking and sized via genetic algorithms for performance evaluation.

## Method

AnalogGenie-Lite is a decoder-only transformer (6 layers, 6 heads, 11.825M parameters) whose key innovation is a three-level lightweight graph modeling pipeline. At the graph level, it prunes redundant device self-nodes and replaces O(n²) fully-connected shared-net edges with O(n) star-topology edges by selecting a hub node that avoids overlapping device-pin cycles. At the subgraph level, it applies the gSpan algorithm to mine frequent subcircuits from a 3,350-topology database, prunes isolated nodes within each subgraph, and restructures remaining non-isolated nodes into a compact cycle with dedicated subcircuit-pin tokens. At the sequence level, it solves the Chinese Postman Problem using the Blossom algorithm for minimum-weight perfect matching on odd-degree vertices followed by Dijkstra shortest-path edge duplication, yielding the shortest closed path visiting every edge at least once—reducing sequence length by 5.15×–71.11× over prior non-optimal Eulerian encodings. Training proceeds via self-supervised pretraining on augmented Eulerian circuit sequences, followed by RLHF fine-tuning targeting specific circuit types and performance metrics.

## Results

AnalogGenie-Lite (Graph+Subgraph+Sequence) achieves 97% validity, 324-device scalability, 99.8% novelty, and an Op-Amp FoM of 15017.7, outperforming all prior methods. Compared to the best prior art (AnalogGenie), it delivers a 5.15× to 71.11× reduction in average sequence length and a 23.5% to 33.6% improvement in validity. The model also achieves a power converter FoM of 4.02, surpassing AnalogGenie (3.3) and LaMAGIC (2.7). Cross-domain experiments on protein, ego, community, molecule, and 3D point cloud graphs show compression ratios of 9.33× to 572.56× over adjacency matrices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior method AnalogGenie (Gao et al., 2025), AnalogGenie-Lite wins on every metric: validity improves from 73.5% to 97%, scalability from 63 to 324 devices, MMD drops from 0.0532 to 0.0408, and Op-Amp FoM rises from 13744.8 to 15017.7. Versus text-based methods, AnalogGenie-Lite dominates: AnalogCoder (63.4% validity, 10 devices, FoM 233.4) and Artisan (82% validity, 18 devices, 0% novelty) are far outperformed on both validity and scalability, while AnalogGenie-Lite also generates 99.8% novel topologies. Against graph-based baselines, CktGNN (66.3% validity, 22 devices, MMD 0.313) and LaMAGIC (72% validity, 4 devices) are limited to single circuit types and small circuits, whereas AnalogGenie-Lite handles all 11 types at 324 devices. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is Op-Amp FoM, where Artisan (12769.5) is close but still below AnalogGenie-Lite (15017.7), and LaMAGIC's MMD of 0.001 is artificially low due to its extremely restricted design space (3% novelty).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of AnalogGenie (Gao et al., 2025), which first introduced device-pin graph representation and non-optimal Eulerian circuit encoding for analog circuit topology generation. It departs from that predecessor in three specific ways: pruning redundant device-self nodes and multi-pin shared edges (reducing O(n²) to O(n)), mining frequent subcircuits via gSpan to create compact hierarchical tokens, and replacing the non-optimal edge-duplication strategy with an optimal Chinese Postman solution. It also positions against text-based generators (AnalogCoder, Artisan) and adjacency-matrix-based graph generators (CktGNN, LaMAGIC), arguing that all suffer from either multi-token error propagation or quadratic space complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.15×–71.11× scalability gain (from 63 to 324 devices) and 23.5%–33.6% validity improvement make large-circuit topology discovery practically feasible for the first time with a sequence-based generative model, addressing a long-standing bottleneck in analog IC automation. The cross-domain case study (protein, ego, community, molecule, 3D point cloud graphs) suggests the Chinese Postman sequence encoding is a broadly applicable graph-to-sequence technique beyond circuits. Open questions remain around efficiently mining representative subgraphs from diverse or growing datasets and further compressing sequences while preserving the features the transformer needs for effective learning.
