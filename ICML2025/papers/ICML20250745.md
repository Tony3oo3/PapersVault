## Problem

Solving entropic optimal transport problems is computationally expensive, and existing neural OT solvers (e.g., Meta OT) are inherently limited to fixed-dimension measures from a single training dataset, breaking down on out-of-distribution data. No prior method can accurately predict OT distances and plans across variable-resolution measures from arbitrary datasets while preserving the geometry of Wasserstein space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UNOT takes a pair of discrete probability measures (μ, ν) of variable resolution (10×10 to 64×64 grid) and a fixed cost function (squared Euclidean, Euclidean, or spherical distance) as input, and outputs the n-dimensional dual potential g. From g, the transport plan is recovered via u = μ ./ K·exp(g/ε) and Π = diag(u)·K·diag(v), and the potential directly serves as a Sinkhorn initialization v⁰ = exp(g/ε). The FNO architecture is discretization-invariant, so the same trained model handles any grid size within the training range without retraining.

## Method

UNOT uses a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) as the predictor S_φ, justified by a convergence result (Proposition 2) showing that discrete dual potentials converge uniformly to continuous ones as resolution increases, making discretization-invariant operators a natural fit. Training is self-supervised and adversarial: a generator G_θ (a 5-layer MLP with a residual skip connection, proven universal by Theorem 3 to produce any discrete distribution) creates training pairs, while S_φ is trained with a bootstrapping loss that runs only k=5 Sinkhorn iterations from its own prediction as initialization and uses the result as the target (Proposition 5 proves this minimizes the true L₂ loss against ground-truth potentials). The generator is trained to maximize this loss, mirroring GAN dynamics. For the spherical domain, the FNO is replaced by a Spherical FNO to respect the geometry of S².

## Results

UNOT reduces the mean number of Sinkhorn iterations needed to reach 0.01 relative error on the OT distance from 80±22 (default Ones initialization) to 3±6 on CIFAR-10, and from 52±19 (Gaussian initialization) to 3±6, yielding a 7.4× speedup. Across all six evaluation datasets (MNIST, CIFAR, CIFAR-MNIST, LFW, BEAR, LFW-BEAR), UNOT requires between 3 and 7 iterations on average, compared to 16–80 for the default and 10–52 for the Gaussian baseline. The paper reports that UNOT predicts OT distances with 1–3% relative error and achieves an average speedup of 3.57× on 28×28 datasets and 4.4× on 64×64 datasets over the default initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the default Ones initialization, UNOT wins decisively on every dataset, reducing iterations by 5–27× (e.g., 3 vs. 80 on CIFAR, 7 vs. 78 on LFW). Against the Gaussian initialization of Thornton & Cuturi (2022), UNOT also wins on all six datasets, with the largest gap on CIFAR (3 vs. 52 iterations) and the smallest on MNIST (3 vs. 10). Compared to Meta OT (Amos et al., 2023), UNOT significantly outperforms on all out-of-distribution datasets (CIFAR, LFW, BEAR, cross-dataset pairs) while nearly matching it on MNIST—the only dataset Meta OT was explicitly trained on—despite UNOT never seeing MNIST during training. On Wasserstein geodesic approximation, UNOT outperforms GeONet (Gracyk & Chen, 2024) even though GeONet was trained specifically on MNIST geodesics while UNOT was not trained on geodesics at all, with both UNOT methods (OT-plan-based and barycenter-based) producing interpolations significantly closer to ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNOT builds on Fourier Neural Operators (Kovachki et al., 2024) as its core architecture, leveraging their discretization-invariance to process measures of variable resolution, and extends this to the spherical domain via Spherical FNOs (Bonev et al., 2023). It departs from prior neural OT solvers (Korotin et al., 2023; Bunne et al., 2023a; Amos et al., 2023) that solve individual OT instances or are locked to a fixed dataset and input dimension, by introducing a universal solver trained adversarially with a provably universal generator. The self-supervised bootstrapping loss, which provably minimizes error against ground-truth dual potentials without requiring full Sinkhorn convergence, is a new training signal distinct from the dual-objective loss used in Meta OT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNOT provides the first neural OT solver that generalizes across datasets, input dimensions, and resolutions for a fixed cost function, achieving up to 7.4× Sinkhorn speedups and accurately capturing Wasserstein geometry (barycenters, geodesics) without being explicitly trained on those tasks. Key limitations include inability to extrapolate to resolutions significantly above training range and lack of generalization across cost functions, restricting practical deployment. Open directions include scaling to higher resolutions, non-uniform grids, and other data modalities, as well as extending the framework to handle variable regularization parameters.
