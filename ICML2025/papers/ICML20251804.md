## Problem

Latent Space Bayesian Optimisation (LSBO) tightly couples a VAE's latent space with a GP surrogate, leading to two fundamental pathologies: (i) the GP surrogate is poorly aligned with the objective because the VAE's unsupervised training distorts local neighbourhoods and its stochastic decoder introduces irreducible noise, and (ii) the standard practice of restricting search to a fixed box in latent space is geometrically wrong, since the VAE prior's probability mass concentrates in a thin high-dimensional annulus rather than a central region. The paper asks whether one can decouple the generative and predictive roles entirely, using each model for what it does best.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained VAE (e.g., a 256-dim SELFIES VAE or 128-dim fully-connected VAE) that is never fine-tuned during optimisation, count-based molecular fingerprints for the Tanimoto kernel, and sequential objective function evaluations on decoded molecules. It outputs candidate molecular structures (as SELFIES or SMILES strings) that the GP predicts will exceed the current best observed value. A key operational detail is that during optimisation the VAE's stochastic decoder is replaced by a deterministic most-likely decoding (argmax over the decoder output), so each latent code maps to exactly one molecule, and the GP is fit on structure-space data pairs (x_i, y_i) rather than latent-space pairs (z_i, y_i).

## Method

COWBOYS decouples the VAE and GP by never fitting the surrogate in latent space and never using the latent space as a search space. Instead, at each optimisation step it samples a new molecule from the GP-conditioned VAE distribution p_θ(x | f_x > f*, D), where f_x is the GP posterior predictive at x and f* is the best value observed so far. To make this tractable, the VAE decoder is approximated as a deterministic mapping h_θ(z), reducing the problem to sampling a latent code z from the posterior p(z | g_θ,z > f*, D) ∝ p(g_θ,z > f* | D) × p(z), where the likelihood is the closed-form Probability of Improvement under the Tanimoto-kernel GP evaluated at the decoded structure. This posterior is sampled using a modified Preconditioned Crank-Nicolson MCMC algorithm that naturally concentrates on the high-probability annulus of the Gaussian prior, eliminating the need for any box-shaped search region. The VAE is used purely as a generative model (initial design samples from p_θ(x) directly), while the GP with its structure-space kernel handles all predictive modelling, and the two are linked only through the Bayesian conditioning step.

## Results

On the 25-task discrete sequence optimization benchmark (Table 1, 300 evaluations, batch size 1), COWBOYS achieves the best or near-best score on the majority of tasks, with particularly large margins on drd2_docking (0.342 vs. 0.097 for HillClimbing, 0.087 for CMAES), gsk3_beta (0.368 vs. 0.252 for HillClimbing), and isomer_c7h8n2o2 (1.000 vs. 0.788–0.880 for all baselines). On the six GUACAMOL lead-optimization tasks (Figure 3, 100 steps, batch size 5, 256-dim SELFIES VAE), COWBOYS substantially outperforms all LSBO baselines including those that fine-tune the VAE, with only InvBO occasionally matching its performance. In the large-budget comparison (Figure 4, up to 4,000 steps), COWBOYS maintains its advantage even when non-fine-tuning baselines are given two orders of magnitude more evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COWBOYS decisively outperforms the discrete-space baselines Bounce (Papenmeier et al., 2023) and ProbRep (Daulton et al., 2022) across nearly all 25 tasks in Table 1, with margins often exceeding 0.2–0.3 in objective value (e.g., 0.342 vs. 0.01 on drd2_docking). Against the high-dimensional latent-space BO methods TURBO (Eriksson et al., 2019) and RandomLineBO (Kirschner et al., 2019), COWBOYS wins on most tasks but is outperformed by RandomLineBO on rdkit_logp (10.68 vs. 19.48) and by HillClimbing on albuterol_similarity (0.472 vs. 0.487). In the GUACAMOL lead-optimization setting (Figure 3), COWBOYS surpasses all VAE-fine-tuning LSBO methods—LOL-BO (Maus et al., 2022a), W-LBO (Tripp et al., 2020), CoBO (Lee et al., 2024), PG-LBO (Chen et al., 2024), and InvBO (Chu et al., 2024)—as well as standard LS-BO (Gomez-Bombarelli et al., 2018) and TURBO-L, with InvBO being the closest competitor. In the large-budget comparison (Figure 4), COWBOYS maintains a clear advantage over EULBO (Maus et al., 2024), Vanilla BO (Hvarfner et al., 2024), and IPA (Moss et al., 2023) even when those methods are granted 4,000 evaluation steps versus COWBOYS's 100. The Graph-GA baseline (Jensen, 2019) is consistently outperformed by COWBOYS on all six GUACAMOL tasks in Figure 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

COWBOYS departs fundamentally from the Latent Space BO (LSBO) paradigm initiated by Gomez-Bombarelli et al. (2018), which fits Gaussian Process surrogates directly in a VAE's latent space. Rather than following the dominant line of work that fine-tunes VAEs during optimisation to improve latent-space alignment (Tripp et al. 2020, Maus et al. 2022a, Lee et al. 2024, Chu et al. 2024), COWBOYS decouples the generative model from the surrogate: the GP is trained in the original structured space using a Tanimoto kernel, while the VAE serves purely as a generator whose sampling is conditioned on the GP via a Bayesian update. The practical sampling mechanism draws on Preconditioned Crank-Nicolson MCMC (Cotter et al. 2013), adapted to the high-dimensional Gaussian prior of the VAE, and the search-space critique leverages the Gaussian Annulus Theorem from high-dimensional probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COWBOYS offers a principled alternative to the increasingly complex fine-tuning heuristics in LSBO, showing that a simple decoupled architecture can outperform all fine-tuning methods in the low-budget regime that mirrors realistic wet-lab molecular design. The Gaussian Annulus Theorem argument—that box-shaped latent search spaces are fundamentally misaligned with where VAE probability mass actually resides—provides a clear conceptual diagnosis of a long-standing LSBO weakness. Key limitations include the current inability to scale to large evaluation budgets (tens of thousands) due to the lack of scalable GPs for discrete data, the deterministic decoder approximation that may reduce candidate diversity, and the reliance on a PI-like conditioning that is known to be suboptimal. Extending COWBOYS to large-budget settings, integrating it with VAE adaptation, and incorporating more sophisticated acquisition functions are the main open directions.
