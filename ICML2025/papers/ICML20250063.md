## Problem

While instance-specific lower bounds on the expected number of misclassified nodes in the Labeled Stochastic Block Model (LSBM) were established by Yun & Proutiere (2016), no algorithm was known to match these bounds in expectation for the general LSBM with heterogeneous edge-label probabilities. Prior algorithms either provided only minimax guarantees (Gao et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2020) or only high-probability guarantees without quantified failure probabilities (Yun & Proutiere, 2016), leaving a gap between the theoretical lower bound and achievable performance in expectation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the collection of labeled adjacency matrices (A^ℓ)_{ℓ∈[L]}, where A^ℓ_uv = 1 if label ℓ is observed on the undirected edge (u,v), and produces cluster assignments (Ĉ_k)_{k=1,...,K̂} as output. Notably, the number of clusters K and all model parameters (α, p) are unknown and must be estimated from data; the algorithm requires no prior knowledge of K. The overall computational complexity is O(n(log n)^3) floating-point operations, achieved by performing spectral clustering only once rather than n times as in prior work.

## Method

IAC (Instance-Adaptive Clustering) is a two-phase algorithm. Phase 1 (spectral initialization) trims high-degree nodes, concatenates the per-label adjacency matrices into Ā = [A^1_Γ, ..., A^L_Γ], applies an iterative power method with singular value thresholding to estimate the rank K, and runs k-means with an expanded set of (log n)^2 random centroid candidates. Phase 2 (likelihood-based improvement) estimates the label probabilities p̂(i,j,ℓ) from the initial clusters and then iteratively reassigns each node to the cluster maximizing its log-likelihood under the estimated parameters, for log n iterations. The key design choices that enable instance-optimal guarantees in expectation are: (i) using the concatenated matrix rather than a randomly weighted combination (which prevents tight failure-probability control), and (ii) the likelihood step operating along the asymmetric, nonlinear decision boundary encoded in the divergence D(α,p), which accounts for the information geometry of the general LSBM.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.2) establishes that IAC misclassifies at most s nodes both in expectation and with high probability whenever nD(α,p)/log(n/s) ≥ 1, matching the instance-specific lower bound of Yun & Proutiere (2016) exactly. This is the first algorithm to achieve instance-optimal cluster recovery in the LSBM in both expectation and high probability. The overall computational complexity is O(n(log n)³) floating-point operations, achieved by performing spectral clustering only once (via iterative power method with singular value thresholding) followed by O(log n) iterations of likelihood-based local improvement. The spectral initialization phase (Theorem 4.1) guarantees O(1/p̄) misclassified nodes with probability at least 1 − exp(−Cnp̄), and the improvement phase reduces this to zero for all well-behaved nodes after O(log n) iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Gao et al. (2017), which achieves the minimax optimal recovery rate n·exp(−(1+o(1))·nI*(n,a,b)/K) for the standard SBM. IAC improves upon this in three key ways: (1) it provides an instance-specific guarantee governed by the divergence D(α,p) rather than a minimax bound, meaning it adapts to the actual hardness of each LSBM instance; (2) it runs spectral clustering only once with O(n(log n)³) total complexity, whereas Gao et al. requires n separate spectral clustering runs (one per node) for their guaranteed algorithm; and (3) the paper reports that IAC empirically exhibits better classification accuracy than the penalized local MLE of Gao et al. in several simple scenarios (Appendix F). However, Gao et al. retains an advantage in generality: their high-probability guarantee extends to dense regimes p̄ = ω(log n/n) and allows K to grow with n, whereas IAC assumes fixed K and p̄ = O(log n/n). The paper also contrasts with Xu et al. (2020), which provides instance-specific analysis but only for the homogeneous LSBM and also requires n spectral clustering runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Yun & Proutiere (2016), which established an instance-specific lower bound on misclassification in the Labeled SBM and provided a two-phase spectral-plus-improvement algorithm with only high-probability guarantees. The key departure is a redesigned spectral initialization (concatenated label matrices with iterative power method and singular value thresholding, replacing the randomly weighted matrix) and a refined likelihood-based improvement step that together yield matching guarantees in expectation as well as high probability. The work also generalizes beyond the minimax framework of Gao et al. (2017) and Zhang & Zhou (2016), providing instance-specific (rather than worst-case) error bounds for heterogeneous LSBMs where edge label probabilities vary by cluster pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between upper and lower bounds for LSBM cluster recovery in both expectation and high probability, establishing a clean necessary-and-sufficient condition (nD(α,p)/log(n/s) ≥ 1) that is instance-specific and does not require knowledge of model parameters. The O(n polylog(n)) computational complexity is a significant practical improvement over prior methods requiring O(n²) or n separate spectral decompositions. Open questions include extending the analysis to Multiplex SBMs and node-attributed SBMs, and understanding robustness to model misspecification or adversarial noise—directions the authors flag but do not address.
