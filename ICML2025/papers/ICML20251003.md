## Problem

In GCNs, the message-passing mechanism propagates label information to unlabeled neighbors, but this additional information does not always contribute positively to predictions—some unlabeled nodes (termed NELU nodes) actually suffer from inconsistent label propagation. Existing graph structure learning methods rely on heuristic rules or downstream task supervision without guaranteeing that the learned structure enables effective label utilization for unlabeled nodes, and they fail to exploit both the consistency and mutually exclusive information between the original and learned graph structures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, and a partially labeled matrix Y ∈ R^{n×c} (only m labeled nodes out of n total) as input. It produces two outputs: (1) a learned ELU graph S* (a sparse n×n adjacency matrix constructed iteratively via LPA and a pre-trained MLP, with elements below a threshold zeroed out), and (2) final node class predictions obtained by fusing GCN representations from both the original graph and the ELU graph via a weighted combination (1−η)H̄ + ηH̃ followed by softmax. The initial label set is expanded using pseudo-labels of ELU nodes to mitigate label scarcity.

## Method

ELU-GCN is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, it constructs the ELU graph by iteratively alternating between updating LPA label estimates Q^(i) = S^(i−1)Q^(i−1) (with labeled rows fixed to ground truth) and solving for S^(i) via a closed-form ridge regression S^(i) = Q^(i)H^T(HH^T + βI)^{−1}, where H = MLP(X) is pre-trained; the Woodbury identity reduces per-iteration complexity from O(n³) to O(nc²+c³). In Stage 2, a novel contrastive loss L_con pulls representations of ELU nodes (where LPA and GCN agree) closer across the two graph views while pushing NELU node representations apart, combined with an entropy regularization term to place decision boundaries in low-density regions. The final training objective is CE(Ŷ, Y) + λL_con. Theoretically, the paper proves that minimizing the ELU graph objective is equivalent to approximately minimizing ‖AY − Y_true‖²_F, which by Theorem 3.3 guarantees optimal generalization upper bounds for the GCN.

## Results

ELU-GCN achieves the best node classification accuracy on all seven primary datasets, with headline results of 84.29% on Cora, 74.23% on Citeseer, 80.51% on Pubmed, 83.73% on Computers, 90.81% on Photo, 70.90% on Chameleon, and 56.91% on Squirrel. On average, ELU-GCN improves by 4.05% over vanilla GCN and by 2.21% over the strongest baseline (GSR) across all seven datasets. The gains are most pronounced on heterophilic graphs (Chameleon and Squirrel), where ELU-GCN outperforms GCN by an average of 9.5%, confirming the theoretical prediction that the original graph structure is particularly unsuitable for GCN generalization in heterophilic settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELU-GCN outperforms all 12 baselines on all seven primary datasets. Against traditional GNNs (GCN, GAT, APPNP), ELU-GCN gains 4.05% on average over GCN, with the largest margin on Squirrel (+13.48%) and Chameleon (+10.08%), where heterophily severely limits message-passing effectiveness. Among advanced GNNs, PCNet is the strongest (59.74% on Chameleon, 48.53% on Squirrel), yet ELU-GCN still leads by 3.26% on average. Among structure-improvement baselines, GSR is the closest competitor (62.28% on Chameleon, 50.53% on Squirrel), but ELU-GCN surpasses it by 2.21% on average, with the largest gap on Chameleon (+8.62%) and Squirrel (+6.38%). Notably, CoGSL fails (OOM) on Pubmed and Computers, and NodeFormer degrades substantially on heterophilic graphs (34.71% on Chameleon), while ELU-GCN maintains strong performance across all graph types. On the four social network datasets (Caltech, UF, Hamilton, Tulane), ELU-GCN also achieves the best accuracy, improving by 1.27% on average over GSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label-utilization-in-GCNs (LU-GCN) line of work, which includes self-training, label-as-feature, and graph-structure-learning approaches. It builds directly on the GCN message-passing framework (Kipf & Welling, 2017) and the Label Propagation Algorithm (Zhu, 2005), but departs from prior graph learning methods (NeuralSparse, PTDNet, GSR, BAGCN) by introducing a quantitative criterion—LPA-GCN prediction consistency (Definition 3.2)—to directly optimize the graph structure so that propagated label information positively influences unlabeled node predictions. The contrastive loss design draws on multi-view consistency/exclusivity principles (Wang et al., 2017), and the Woodbury identity is repurposed to reduce the ELU graph computation from O(n³) to O(nc²+c³).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, parameter-free mechanism for constructing a graph structure that provably improves GCN generalization (Theorems 3.3–3.4), which is valuable for semi-supervised node classification where label scarcity is the core challenge. The particularly large gains on heterophilic graphs (~9.5% on Chameleon/Squirrel) suggest the approach addresses a real structural limitation of original graphs in non-homophilic settings. Limitations include the need for a pre-trained MLP (H) that is frozen during ELU graph construction, the one-shot (non-end-to-end) nature of the graph learning, and the additional contrastive loss hyperparameters (λ, τ, η). Open questions include whether the ELU graph can be jointly optimized with the GCN in an end-to-end fashion, how the method scales to graphs with millions of nodes, and whether the LPA-GCN consistency criterion generalizes to GNN architectures beyond standard GCN.
