## Problem

Causal abstraction (CA) learning from data has been limited by restrictive assumptions: full SCM specification, known causal DAGs, interventional data, linearity of structural functions, or aligned samples. This paper tackles CA learning in a realistic setting where SCMs are inaccessible, only observational data is available, and samples are misaligned, requiring only partial prior knowledge of the CA's structural mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two covariance matrices estimated from observational data (Σ^ℓ ∈ S_{++}^ℓ for the low-level model and Σ^h ∈ S_{++}^h for the high-level model) plus a binary prior structure matrix B ∈ {0,1}^{ℓ×h} encoding which low-level variables map to which high-level variables; B may be partial, with some rows containing multiple ones to express uncertainty. The output is a CA matrix V^⊤ ∈ R^{h×ℓ} whose columns V must be orthonormal (V ∈ St(ℓ,h)), and in the smooth formulation (CLinSEPAL) an additional support matrix S ∈ [0,1]^{ℓ×h} is jointly learned so that B⊙S is a valid constructive mapping.

## Method

The core insight is the Semantic Embedding Principle (SEP): a valid CA must allow perfect reconstruction of high-level causal knowledge when embedded into the low-level and abstracted back. For linear CA, SEP forces V onto the Stiefel manifold St(ℓ,h), turning CA learning into a Riemannian optimization problem. With Gaussian measures and KL divergence, the objective becomes D_V^KL = Tr{(V^⊤Σ^ℓV)^{-1}Σ^h} + log det{V^⊤Σ^ℓV}, which is smooth but nonconvex on the manifold. The paper formulates two variants: a nonsmooth problem (penalizing entries of V where B has zeros) solved via manifold ADMM (LinSEPAL-ADMM) or manifold proximal gradient (LinSEPAL-PG), and a smooth problem (integrating B into the objective via a jointly optimized support S with Stiefel, sphere, and surjectivity constraints) solved by combining splitting of orthogonality constraints, ADMM, and successive convex approximation (CLinSEPAL), which guarantees a constructive CA.

## Results

On synthetic full-prior (fp) data across settings (ℓ,h) ∈ {(12,2), (12,4), (12,6)}, all three methods (LinSEPAL-ADMM, LinSEPAL-PG, CLinSEPAL) produce constructive CAs and achieve low KL divergence D_V^KL, with CLinSEPAL and LinSEPAL-ADMM showing slightly lower normalized Frobenius distance from the ground truth V* than LinSEPAL-PG, particularly at (ℓ,h)=(12,4). F1 scores confirm that all methods recover the correct structural support of the CA in the fp setting. In the partial-prior (pp) setting (ℓ,h)=(4,2) with 25%–75% known rows of B, only CLinSEPAL consistently guarantees a constructive abstraction; LinSEPAL-PG fails the 90% constructiveness threshold at 50% prior drop, and at 75% drop only CLinSEPAL succeeds in both constructiveness and minimizing D_V^KL. On real rs-fMRI data from the Human Connectome Project (ℓ=45 ROIs, h=14 macro ROIs), CLinSEPAL recovers the ground-truth V* in the full-prior scenario, and in the partial-prior scenario (h=8 functional networks) it correctly retrieves the CA structure under low uncertainty while still providing useful alignment under medium/high uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external CA-learning baselines; instead it compares its three proposed methods—LinSEPAL-ADMM, LinSEPAL-PG, and CLinSEPAL—under identical synthetic and real-data protocols. In the full-prior synthetic setting, all three methods are competitive, with CLinSEPAL and LinSEPAL-ADMM marginally outperforming LinSEPAL-PG on normalized Frobenius distance (especially at (ℓ,h)=(12,4)) while all achieve comparable D_V^KL and F1 scores. In the partial-prior setting, CLinSEPAL is the only method that consistently returns a constructive CA across all levels of prior-knowledge drop (25%, 50%, 75%), whereas LinSEPAL-PG fails the constructiveness threshold at 50% and LinSEPAL-ADMM shows a reduced fraction of constructive solutions. On the Human Connectome Project brain data, CLinSEPAL is the sole method applied (as it is the only one guaranteeing constructiveness), and it successfully recovers the ground-truth abstraction in the full-prior scenario and provides structurally meaningful results under partial prior knowledge. The paper positions its contribution as the first to operate without assumptions (NA1)–(NA5) that limit prior methods (Zennaro et al. 2023, Felekis et al. 2024, Dyer et al. 2024, Kekić et al. 2024, Massidda et al. 2024), rather than as a direct numerical comparison against them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the α-abstraction category-theoretic framework of Rischel (2020) by proposing an alternative formalization that maps a dyadic index category onto exogenous and endogenous probability measures, rather than the standard functorial semantics from graphical structure to discrete distributions. It departs from prior CA learning methods (Zennaro et al. 2023; Felekis et al. 2024; Dyer et al. 2024; Kekić et al. 2024; Massidda et al. 2024) by systematically removing five restrictive assumptions (NA1–NA5) and replacing them with a single mild assumption of partial structural prior knowledge (A1). The optimization machinery draws on Riemannian optimization paradigms—MADMM (Kovnatsky et al., 2016), ManPG (Chen et al., 2020), SOC (Lai & Osher, 2014), and SCA (Nedić et al., 2018)—but their combination into the CLinSEPAL algorithm for jointly optimizing the Stiefel-constrained product (B⊙S⊙V) is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Semantic Embedding Principle provides a principled, one-directional information-preservation criterion that naturally links linear CA to Stiefel manifold geometry, turning an otherwise ill-posed learning problem into a structured Riemannian optimization task. The spectral interlacing theorem (Thm 4.3) gives necessary conditions for the existence of a linear CA between Gaussian measures, which is a useful diagnostic tool. Key limitations include the restriction to linear CA and Gaussian measures, the nonconvexity of the objective (no global optimality guarantees for LinSEPAL-ADMM), and the fact that the optimal solution is not unique (both V and −V are valid). Open questions include extending to nonlinear CA maps, non-Gaussian distributions, and establishing identifiability results that could yield interventional consistency without interventional data.
