## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning frameworks face a fundamental cost-quality trade-off: single-query methods (CoT, AoT) are cheap but require intricate prompt engineering and fail on sequential decision-making tasks, while multi-query tree-search methods (ToT, GoT, LATS) achieve high quality at prohibitive cost with no control over search tree growth. No prior method effectively balances exploration and exploitation to deliver strong quality at a fraction of the cost of state-of-the-art tree-search approaches.

## Input / Output

FOA takes a task-specific problem statement (e.g., four numbers for Game of 24, 5×5 crossword clues, a web shopping instruction, or a multi-step scientific question) and produces a final solution (arithmetic expression, filled crossword grid, purchased product, or numerical answer) along with the reasoning trace. Rather than crafting custom prompts, FOA reuses the exact prompts from the baselines it compares against (ToT, LATS, ReST-MCTS*), treating itself as a runtime that orchestrates any existing agent's step and evaluate calls. The fleet size n, mutation interval k, and discount factor γ are tunable hyperparameters that directly control the cost budget and search depth.

## Method

FOA implements a genetic-type particle filter over a dynamic search tree: n agents independently mutate their states for k steps (mutation phase), then the fleet is resampled with replacement using a heuristic value function (selection phase), allocating more agents to high-value states while preserving diversity when no single state dominates. A backtracking mechanism with discount factor γ allows agents to recover from localized mistakes by resampling from previously visited states rather than only current ones. A 'sudden death' rule prunes agents that enter invalid states, replacing them with random duplicates to maintain fleet size without extra value-function calls. The resampling dynamically adjusts the effective branching factor between 1 (all agents retained, maximizing exploration) and n (one agent duplicated, maximizing exploitation), in contrast to fixed-branching tree-search methods. As a runtime, FOA wraps any existing agent unchanged, requiring no modification to the agent's internal policy or prompting strategy.

## Results

Across four benchmark tasks (Game of 24, Mini-Crosswords, WebShop, SciBench) and four LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, LLaMA3.2-11B, LLaMA3.2-90B), FOA achieves an average ~5% absolute quality improvement over the best prior method while requiring only ~35% of its cost. On Game of 24 (GPT-4), FOA reaches 76% success rate at $62.93, surpassing ToT (74% at $75.02). On WebShop (GPT-3.5), FOA scores 75.6 average at $1.68, dramatically outperforming LATS (66.1 at $232.27). Notably, FOA + LLaMA3.2-11B surpasses the larger LLaMA3.2-90B model on all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOA outperforms all 13 baselines across all four tasks. On Game of 24, FOA (76%) beats ToT (74%) by 2% while costing 25% less, and dominates GoT (63%), AoT (49%), and ReST-MCTS* (38%) by 13–38 percentage points. On Mini Crosswords, FOA (46% overlap) surpasses GoT (41.2%) by 4.8% at 57% lower cost. On WebShop, FOA (75.6) exceeds LATS (66.1) by 9.5 points at only 0.7% of LATS's cost ($1.68 vs. $232.27), and also beats all supervised fine-tuned models (WebGUM 67.5, IL+RL 62.4). On SciBench, FOA (11.0% accuracy) edges out ReST-MCTS* (9.3%) by 1.7% at 84% lower cost. Single-query methods (IO, CoT, CoT-SC) are consistently the cheapest but achieve the lowest quality, confirming that FOA's multi-agent resampling strategy provides the best cost-quality trade-off at every price point.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOA repurposes genetic particle filtering (Holland, 1992) — a well-studied optimization technique from vehicle routing, robotics, and time-series forecasting — as a coordination mechanism for LLM-based reasoning agents. It departs from the dominant tree-search paradigm (ToT, GoT, LATS, ReST-MCTS*) by replacing fixed-branching beam search with dynamic resampling: agents explore autonomously for k steps, then a value-function-guided importance sampling step either duplicates high-value agents (exploitation) or retains diverse agents (exploration). Crucially, FOA is framed as a model-agnostic runtime that wraps any existing agent or prompting strategy unchanged, rather than introducing a new prompt template, distinguishing it from both single-query methods (CoT, AoT) and refinement-based approaches (Reflexion, RAFA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent cost-quality dominance across four heterogeneous tasks (math puzzles, crosswords, web navigation, scientific reasoning) and four base models provides practical evidence that multi-agent LLM reasoning need not be prohibitively expensive, addressing a key barrier to real-world deployment. The finding that LLaMA3.2-11B + FOA surpasses the 8× larger LLaMA3.2-90B model suggests that structured search orchestration can partially substitute for raw model scale. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks without a clear value function, whether adaptive fleet sizes or hierarchical agent spawning (nested particle filters) yield further gains, and whether the modest absolute quality ceilings (e.g., 11% on SciBench) reflect task difficulty or framework limitations.
