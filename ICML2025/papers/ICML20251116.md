## Problem

The ℓ₂ mechanism, a K-norm mechanism with density proportional to exp(-‖y-T(X)‖₂/σ), is known to satisfy pure DP but its approximate (ε,δ)-DP guarantees were not well understood. This paper provides the first algorithm to calibrate the ℓ₂ mechanism for (ε,δ)-DP, revealing that it empirically achieves lower mean squared ℓ₂ error than both the Laplace and analytic Gaussian mechanisms across a range of dimensions and privacy parameters.

## Input / Output

Given a d-dimensional statistic T with ℓ₂ sensitivity Δ₂ (normalized to 1), privacy parameters (ε, δ), and dimension d, the algorithm binary searches over σ using Riemann sum approximations over 1000 radii to find the minimum σ satisfying (ε,δ)-DP. The resulting mechanism samples a noisy output y = T(X) + rz where r ~ Gamma(d+1, σ) and z is uniform on the unit ℓ₂ ball. A parallel sampler decomposes this into per-coordinate operations: each of d workers samples a log-uniform and a Gaussian, and a central manager combines them to produce the full d-dimensional output.

## Method

The privacy analysis leverages the privacy loss random variable framework of Balle & Wang (2018), reducing (ε,δ)-DP verification to upper-bounding P[ℓ ≥ ε] and lower-bounding P[ℓ ≤ -ε]. The key geometric insight is that the high privacy loss region V = {y : (‖y-e₁‖₂ - ‖y‖₂)/σ ≥ ε} intersects each spherical level set S_{r,0} in a spherical cap of height h(r) = min(r(1-εσ) + (1-(εσ)²)/2, 2r). Cap fractions are computed via the regularized incomplete beta function, and the overall probability bounds are constructed as Riemann sums over a grid of radii, yielding provably conservative (upper/lower) bounds. Binary search over σ then finds the minimum noise scale satisfying the (ε,δ)-DP inequality. Experiments confirm the bounds are tight for n_r = n_R = 1000 and d ≤ 100, and the ℓ₂ mechanism's error gap over the best of Laplace/Gaussian peaks at 50% at d=7 before shrinking below 1% at d=500.

## Results

Under (1, 10⁻⁵)-DP, the ℓ₂ mechanism achieves lower mean squared ℓ₂ error than both the Laplace and analytic Gaussian mechanisms across all dimensions d ≥ 1, with the analytic Gaussian normalized to error 1. The relative advantage over the better of the two baselines peaks at 50% at d = 7, shrinks to 5% at d = 100, and falls below 1% at d = 500. At d = 1 the ℓ₂ mechanism is identical to the Laplace mechanism (zero gap), and as d → ∞ it converges to the analytic Gaussian mechanism. Additionally, unlike the Gaussian mechanism, the ℓ₂ mechanism always satisfies a pure DP guarantee for any (ε, δ) setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the Laplace mechanism (Dwork et al., 2006), the ℓ₂ mechanism is identical at d = 1 but strictly dominates for all d > 1 in mean squared ℓ₂ error; the Laplace mechanism's normalized error relative to the analytic Gaussian grows monotonically with d (truncated at d = 8 in the paper's plot), while the ℓ₂ mechanism remains below both baselines. Compared to the analytic Gaussian mechanism (Balle & Wang, 2018), the ℓ₂ mechanism achieves lower error at every finite dimension, with the gap peaking at 50% (d = 7) and shrinking to <1% by d = 500. A qualitative advantage over the Gaussian mechanism is that the ℓ₂ mechanism always satisfies a pure DP guarantee (right plot of Figure 1), whereas the analytic Gaussian mechanism does not. The paper also validates its privacy analysis by showing that the algorithmic σ estimates closely track empirical Monte Carlo estimates across d = 1 to 100 (Figure 4), confirming the analysis is both sound and tight for n_r = n_R = 1000.

## Relation to Prior Work

The ℓ₂ mechanism is an instance of the K-norm mechanism (Hardt & Talwar, 2010), which immediately yields a pure DP guarantee, but its approximate DP properties were previously uncharacterized. The paper builds on the privacy loss random variable framework of Balle & Wang (2018) to derive the first (ε,δ)-DP calibration for this mechanism, using spherical cap geometry to bound the relevant tail probabilities. This departs from Ganesh & Zhao (2021), who analyzed related 'generalized Gaussian' mechanisms via spherical caps but only for ℓ∞ sensitivity and with a looser sufficient condition, and from prior objective-perturbation work (Chaudhuri et al., 2011; Kifer et al., 2012) that applied the ℓ₂ mechanism exclusively under pure DP. The paper also explicitly rules out the concentrated-DP route via the exponential mechanism, showing it recovers only the Laplace mechanism's guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a mechanism that strictly dominates both the Laplace and analytic Gaussian mechanisms in mean squared ℓ₂ error across all dimensions while retaining a pure DP guarantee, which is practically useful for applications like private SGD where ℓ₂ sensitivity is the natural constraint. The parallel sampler makes the mechanism deployable in distributed settings. Limitations include the ~100× slower σ computation (mitigated by being a one-time cost), the reliance on numerical Riemann-sum approximations for the privacy bound (tight in practice but not closed-form), and the fact that the advantage over the Gaussian mechanism shrinks to <1% by d=500, limiting its practical edge in very high dimensions. Open questions include whether the spherical-cap analysis extends to other K-norm mechanisms (e.g., ℓ₁, ℓ∞) under approximate DP, and whether the gap can be closed analytically.
