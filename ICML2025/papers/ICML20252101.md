## Problem

Existing intrinsic motivation approaches for RL exploration (novelty, information gain, prediction error) tend to uncover only low-level interactions rather than semantically meaningful, goal-directed behaviors. Prior work that injects human priors via foundation models relies on unrealistic assumptions such as language-embedded environments, exhaustive offline datasets, or access to high-level actions, and none learns an internal model of interestingness. SENSEI addresses this gap by enabling model-based RL agents with image observations and low-level actions to explore using VLM-distilled semantic rewards.

## Input / Output

The method takes pixel-based observations (64×64 for the world model; higher resolution up to 1202×1080 for VLM annotation), low-level actions (discrete or continuous), a short environment description (human-written or VLM-generated), and an initial dataset D_init collected via Plan2Explore self-supervised exploration. It produces a distilled reward function R_ψ (a CNN+MLP mapping images to scalar interestingness scores), a learned RSSM world model that internally predicts both semantic rewards and ensemble disagreement, and an exploration policy. Notably, the VLM is queried only offline during annotation; during exploration the agent relies entirely on its world model's internal prediction of interestingness.

## Method

SENSEI operates in three stages. First, VLM-MOTIF distills a semantic reward: GPT-4 is prompted to compare pairs of observations for interestingness, and the resulting preference dataset is used to train a reward function R_ψ via cross-entropy loss. Second, a DreamerV3-style RSSM world model is trained end-to-end to predict observations, episode continuations, extrinsic rewards, and—critically—the semantic exploration reward r_t^sem, giving the agent an internal model of interestingness usable during imagination-based policy training. Third, the exploration policy uses a Go-Explore-inspired adaptive switching mechanism: when the predicted semantic reward falls below a moving quantile threshold Q_k, the agent primarily maximizes interestingness (go mode); above the threshold, it shifts to maximizing epistemic uncertainty via ensemble disagreement (explore mode), implemented through adaptive weighting of the two intrinsic reward signals. This combination of semantic guidance and novelty-seeking prevents the agent from getting stuck in local optima while still discovering diverse, meaningful behaviors.

## Results

In MiniHack KeyRoom-S15, SENSEI outperforms PPO by roughly two orders of magnitude in sample efficiency (PPO requires >20M steps to consistently solve the task). In Pokemon Red, only SENSEI reaches the first Gym (segment 9) out of 10 required segments, and from episode 390 onward SENSEI achieves twice as many Pokémon level-ups per episode as Plan2Explore. In Robodesk, SENSEI interacts more with most available objects than both Plan2Explore and RND over 1M exploration steps, and the zero-knowledge variant (SENSEI GENERAL) performs roughly on par with the expert-described version. For downstream task learning, world models pre-trained with SENSEI enable faster policy convergence than Plan2Explore world models or training from scratch with DreamerV3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Plan2Explore (Sekar et al., 2020), the primary model-based exploration baseline, SENSEI consistently wins: it produces more task-relevant interactions in MiniHack (key pickups, door openings, chest finds), more diverse object interactions in Robodesk (vs. Plan2Explore's tendency to perform arm stretches), greater map coverage in Pokemon Red, and faster downstream task learning. Against RND (Burda et al., 2019), SENSEI also wins on Robodesk object interactions, as RND predominantly moves the arm in the screen center hitting buttons. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) trained from scratch, SENSEI achieves roughly two orders of magnitude better sample efficiency on MiniHack KeyRoom-S15. Against DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023) without intrinsic exploration, SENSEI wins on Pokemon Red (Dreamer fails to engage the battle system and never levels up its Pokémon) and on MiniHack downstream tasks where SENSEI's pre-trained world model converges faster than DreamerV3 trained from scratch. The zero-knowledge SENSEI GENERAL variant (no human environment description) matches the expert-described SENSEI on Robodesk, confirming the approach does not require manual annotation of environment context.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENSEI sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) model-based RL with RSSM world models (DreamerV3), (2) foundation-model-guided intrinsic motivation via preference distillation (MOTIF), and (3) ensemble-disagreement exploration (Plan2Explore). The key departure from prior foundation-model-guided exploration work (MOTIF, ELLM, OMNI) is that SENSEI operates purely from image observations and low-level actions without assuming language-grounded environments, pre-existing offline datasets, or high-level action spaces. Crucially, rather than continuously querying the VLM during exploration, SENSEI distills the VLM's notion of interestingness into a reward function that the world model learns to predict internally, enabling the agent to evaluate semantic interestingness during imagined rollouts without external API calls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SENSEI provides a general, modular framework for incorporating foundation model feedback into model-based RL exploration, a direction that will grow in importance as VLMs improve. The learned internal model of interestingness enables significantly faster downstream task learning (roughly two orders of magnitude over PPO in KeyRoom), and the iterative annotation refinement in Pokemon Red—where a second round of VLM annotations on newly collected data allows the agent to defeat the first Gym—suggests a path toward progressively unlocking more complex behaviors. Key limitations include reliance on fully-observable observations (occlusions degrade VLM annotations), out-of-distribution degradation as the agent explores beyond the initial annotation set, and the computational cost of VLM annotation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to photorealistic or real-world settings and how many iterative refinement rounds are needed for truly open-ended progress.
