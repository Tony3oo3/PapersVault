## Problem

Training large transformers is bottlenecked by the memory wall, where intermediate activations must be stored for back-propagation. Existing reversible DNNs alleviate this by recomputing activations on-the-fly, but they all require non-standard or heavily modified architectures. The open question is whether exact bit-level reversibility can be achieved in transformers while preserving the standard architecture for inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253022/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text sequences (for NLG, translation, and text generation) and RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) as input, producing generated text, translated text, or class labels respectively. A key operational detail is that during training, 1-bit-per-activation-value side information must be stored per transformer block to recover binary quantization loss, though this is far lighter than storing full activations. At inference, the architecture is identical to a standard transformer up to a fixed-point quantization at precision 2^{-l} (l=9 in experiments).

## Method

BDIA-transformer treats each transformer block as an Euler integration step of a neural ODE and applies the bidirectional integration approximation (BDIA) with a random hyperparameter γ_k ∈ {−0.5, 0.5} drawn independently per block per training sample, effectively averaging two consecutive integration approximations. This randomization trains an ensemble of 2^{K−1} ODE solvers, regularizing the model and improving validation accuracy. Exact bit-level reversibility is achieved by quantizing all activations to fixed-point precision 2^{−l} and storing a lightweight 1-bit side vector per block that captures the parity of each quantized value, enabling lossless reconstruction of intermediate states during online back-propagation. The specific choice γ ∈ {±0.5} is critical: as a power of two, multiplication by γ shifts the mantissa by exactly one bit, guaranteeing at most one bit of loss that the side information can recover. At inference, E[γ]=0 collapses the update to the standard residual transformer expression, so no architectural change is needed for deployment.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BDIA-ViT achieves 89.10% validation accuracy, outperforming ViT (88.15%) and RevViT (86.22%), while using only 693.4 MB peak memory versus 1570.6 MB for ViT. On CIFAR-100, the gap widens substantially: BDIA-ViT reaches 66.09% versus 61.86% for ViT and 61.89% for RevViT. For fine-tuning GPT-2 medium on the E2E NLG Challenge, FT-BDIA achieves BLEU 68.6, NIST 8.65, and ROUGE-L 71.3, consistently outperforming direct fine-tuning (BLEU 66.8, NIST 8.52, ROUGE-L 70.3) while approaching LoRA (BLEU 69.0, NIST 8.70). Training time per epoch increases modestly (47.0s vs. 30.6s for ViT) due to online back-propagation and side-information handling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT, BDIA-ViT wins on validation accuracy for both CIFAR-10 (+0.95 pp) and CIFAR-100 (+4.23 pp) while reducing peak memory by 56% (693.4 MB vs. 1570.6 MB), at the cost of ~54% more training time per epoch (47.0s vs. 30.6s) due to online back-propagation. Against RevViT, BDIA-ViT achieves substantially higher accuracy on both datasets (89.10% vs. 86.22% on CIFAR-10; 66.09% vs. 61.89% on CIFAR-100) while using only 21% more memory (693.4 MB vs. 572.7 MB) and 13% more time per epoch. For GPT-2 medium fine-tuning on E2E, FT-BDIA outperforms direct fine-tuning (FT) on all five metrics (e.g., BLEU 68.6 vs. 66.8) but remains slightly below LoRA (BLEU 69.0, NIST 8.70), which trains far fewer parameters (0.35M vs. 354.92M). The paper also notes that RevViT does not consistently improve over standard ViT, suggesting that architectural modifications for reversibility can impose uncontrolled regularization, whereas BDIA's ensemble-of-ODE-solvers approach yields consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the bidirectional integration approximation (BDIA) technique, originally designed for exact diffusion inversion (Zhang et al., 2023a), into the transformer training loop by leveraging the neural ODE interpretation of residual blocks (Chen et al., 2018). It departs from prior reversible DNN work (RevNet, RevViT, i-ResNet) in a key architectural respect: by randomizing γ ∈ {−0.5, 0.5} per block per sample during training and taking E[γ]=0 at inference, the standard transformer architecture is preserved exactly, unlike prior reversible models that require structural modifications. The training procedure is conceptually analogous to dropout (training an ensemble), but operates on the choice of ODE integration scheme rather than on network sub-structure, and activation quantization with 1-bit side information is introduced to achieve exact bit-level reversibility for online back-propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-preserving property makes BDIA a drop-in training technique for fine-tuning existing transformer-based LLMs without any inference-time changes, which is practically valuable given the prevalence of fine-tuning in production. The consistent validation improvements across NLG, image classification, and translation (e.g., +4.2 pp on CIFAR-100) suggest the ensemble-of-ODE-solvers regularization is robust, though all experiments use relatively small models (6-block ViT, GPT-2 medium) and a single GPU, leaving open whether the effect persists at LLM scale. The finding that RevViT does not consistently outperform ViT is a useful negative result for the reversible-architecture community. Open questions include interaction with other regularization at scale, optimal quantization precision for very deep models, and whether the 1-bit-per-activation side information overhead becomes non-negligible for billion-parameter models.
