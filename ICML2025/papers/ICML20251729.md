## Problem

Existing diffusion models on Riemannian manifolds suffer from a significant runtime gap compared to their Euclidean counterparts: training objectives require either exponential-in-d heat kernel expansions or O(d) gradient evaluations per iteration, and sampling guarantees are not polynomial in dimension. This paper closes that gap for symmetric-space manifolds (torus, sphere, SO(n), U(n)) by eliminating the need for manifold heat kernels and reducing per-iteration cost to near-linear in d with O(1) gradient evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset D on a symmetric manifold M, a projection map φ: R^d → M (e.g., SVD-based for SO(n)/U(n), normalization for the sphere, mod-2π for the torus), and its restricted inverse ψ: M → R^d. Training produces two neural networks: a drift model f(x,t) mapping to the tangent space T_xM and a covariance model g(x,t) that, despite being a d×d matrix, is parameterized by only d=n² scalars for SO(n)/U(n) due to group symmetries. Sampling starts from φ(N(0,I_d)) and integrates the learned reverse SDE via the exponential map, producing a point on M.

## Method

The core idea is to define the forward diffusion as the projection φ of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in R^d onto M, rather than standard Brownian motion on M. This introduces a spatially-varying covariance term that accounts for curvature, and crucially allows the forward process to be sampled by drawing a Gaussian and projecting—no SDE/ODE solver needed. Training uses Itô's Lemma to project the Euclidean reverse diffusion SDE onto M, yielding an objective (equations 4–5) that depends only on the closed-form Euclidean heat kernel and derivatives of φ, bypassing the intractable manifold heat kernel. For SO(n) and U(n), the projection is computed via a single SVD in O(n^ω)=O(d^{ω/2}) operations, and the covariance's symmetry structure reduces its computation from O(d²) entries to O(d^{ω/2}). For sampling guarantees, the authors develop an optimal-transport-based coupling argument (replacing Girsanov's theorem, which fails due to the spatially varying covariance) combined with an average-case Lipschitz condition verified via random matrix theory, yielding polynomial-in-d total variation bounds.

## Results

On the torus, the proposed method achieves NLL of 0.97 at d=1000, substantially outperforming RSGM (2.51) and the Euclidean baseline (8.7), with the gap widening as dimension increases. On U(n) quantum oscillator data, the method achieves the best C2ST score (0.90) at n=15, compared to 1.00 for Euclidean, RSGM, and TDM, with the advantage emerging at n≥9. For per-iteration training runtime on U(n), the method stays within a factor of 3 of the Euclidean model (0.60s vs 0.21s at n=50), while RSGM and TDM are 57× and 45× slower respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RSGM (De Bortoli et al., 2022), the proposed method wins on all empirical metrics: lower NLL on the torus for d≥10 (e.g., 0.97 vs 2.51 at d=1000), lower C2ST on U(n) for n≥9 (0.90 vs 1.00 at n=15), and faster per-iteration runtime at every dimension tested (0.60s vs 11.55s at n=50). Against TDM (Zhu et al., 2025), the method similarly achieves lower C2ST on U(n) for n≥9 and is 16× faster per iteration at n=50 (0.60s vs 9.43s). RDM (Huang et al., 2022) and SCRD (Lou et al., 2024) are compared only in the theoretical complexity table (Table 1), where the proposed method requires O(1) gradient evaluations versus O(d) for RDM and avoids the exponential-in-d heat kernel expansions needed by SCRD; neither is included in empirical comparisons due to implementation limitations (SCRD is restricted to n=3, RDM lacks experiments for n>3). The Euclidean baseline with projection preprocessing/postprocessing is consistently outperformed on both sample quality and, at higher dimensions, on the rate of degradation with increasing d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and Anderson's (1982) reverse-time SDE, but departs fundamentally from prior manifold diffusion methods (RSGM, RDM, TDM, SCRD) by replacing heat-kernel-based objectives with a projection of Euclidean Ornstein-Uhlenbeck dynamics onto the manifold via Itô's Lemma. The key conceptual departure is introducing a spatially varying covariance term that accounts for nonzero curvature, enabling the forward diffusion to be computed as a simple projection (e.g., SVD for SO(n)/U(n)) rather than requiring intractable manifold heat kernels or O(d) gradient evaluations for Riemannian divergence. For sampling guarantees, the paper replaces the Girsanov-based arguments used in prior Euclidean and manifold diffusion analyses with an optimal transport coupling approach, necessitated by the non-constant covariance structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant computational gap: per-iteration training on SO(n)/U(n) drops from exponential-in-d (heat kernel expansions) or O(d) gradient evaluations (ISM) to O(d^1.19) arithmetic with O(1) gradient evaluations, staying within a factor of 3 of Euclidean diffusion even at d=1225. The first polynomial-in-d sampling accuracy guarantees (vs. non-polynomial bounds in RSGM) are a meaningful theoretical advance for the field. Open questions include extending guarantees to non-symmetric manifolds (e.g., positive semi-definite matrices), tightening the dimension dependence in the TV bound (currently d^9 for SO(n)/U(n)), and validating on real-world structured data beyond synthetic wrapped Gaussians and quantum oscillators.
