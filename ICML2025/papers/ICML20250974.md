## Problem

DPO-based fine-tuning of diffusion models is limited by visual inconsistency: well-aligned and poorly-aligned images differ in unrelated factors (background, structure, style), preventing the model from isolating which factors drive alignment. Existing pixel-level editing methods can produce visually consistent pairs but destroy the timestep-by-timestep denoising trajectories that RL training requires, creating a fundamental gap between alignment correction and RL-trainable data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250974/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt c, a well-aligned reference image I^r, a poorly-aligned base image I^b (both generated from the same prompt), and the shared random noise x_T of the base image, D-Fusion produces a target image I^a at 64×64 latent resolution that is as well-aligned as I^r yet visually consistent with I^b, along with its complete denoising trajectory {x_T^a, …, x_0^a}. The cross-attention mask is extracted at 16×16 resolution from the first up-sampling layer of the U-Net, and an adoption threshold check (using CLIPScore) filters out failed fusion cases before training.

## Method

D-Fusion operates in two steps during a forward denoising pass. First, cross-attention mask extraction: at each timestep t, the cross-attention maps from the reference image's denoising process are averaged across heads and blocks in the first up-sampling layer, binarized per item-related token using a threshold, and merged via XOR to yield a binary mask M_t marking alignment-relevant regions. Second, self-attention fusion: starting from the base image's noise, the model denoises step-by-step while, at each timestep, replacing the keys and values in the self-attention module within the masked region with those from the reference image (via Hadamard product with the flattened mask), leaving queries and unmasked keys/values from the base image intact. Because fusion is applied progressively along the denoising process, the intermediate states naturally form a valid denoising trajectory. The resulting (I^b, I^a) pairs are then used for DPO, DDPO, or DPOK training with LoRA fine-tuning, where the model learns to prefer the well-aligned target over the poorly-aligned base while the visual consistency of the pair provides a dense, focused learning signal.

## Results

On the generalization test (unseen prompts), DPO+D-Fusion achieves CLIPScore of 0.3758, 0.3446, and 0.3276 on Templates 1, 2, and 3 respectively, consistently outperforming both the original Stable Diffusion (0.3725, 0.3396, 0.3213) and naive DPO (0.3733, 0.3407, 0.3230). The alignment curves (Figure 5) show that D-Fusion yields higher CLIPScore at every training data volume across all three templates. Human evaluation with 22 raters confirms that images from DPO+D-Fusion are preferred over both SD and naive DPO outputs on all templates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPO+D-Fusion outperforms the original Stable Diffusion baseline on all three templates by 0.0033, 0.0050, and 0.0063 CLIPScore respectively, with the largest gain on Template 3 (positional relationships). Against naive DPO, D-Fusion consistently improves alignment by 0.0025, 0.0039, and 0.0046 CLIPScore, demonstrating that visually consistent training pairs provide a meaningful advantage over raw preference pairs. In the ablation study (Figure 7), D-Fusion also enhances DDPO and DPOK beyond their vanilla versions, confirming compatibility across RL algorithms. Notably, DDIM inversion as an alternative trajectory construction method fails to produce any noticeable alignment improvement, validating that D-Fusion's step-by-step self-attention fusion is essential for generating usable denoising trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO paradigm for diffusion model alignment (Wallace et al., 2023) and the attention-control literature for diffusion models (MasaCtrl, Prompt-to-Prompt, PnP), but departs from both in a key way: it identifies visual inconsistency between preference pairs as the fundamental bottleneck limiting DPO's effectiveness, drawing an analogy to fine-grained token-level corrections in LLM alignment (SDPO, RLHF-V). Unlike existing image editing methods (Imagen Editor, Imagic) that operate at the pixel level and destroy denoising trajectories, D-Fusion performs self-attention fusion step-by-step during the denoising process, preserving the timestep-by-timestep decision sequences required for RL training. The method repurposes cross-attention maps (from Prompt-to-Prompt) for mask extraction and self-attention key/value manipulation (from MasaCtrl) in a novel combination specifically designed to maintain trajectory integrity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and compatible technique for improving RL fine-tuning of diffusion models across multiple algorithms (DPO, DDPO, DPOK), addressing a real limitation that practitioners encounter. However, the improvements are modest (e.g., CLIPScore 0.3725→0.3758 on Template 1), the method is validated only on Stable Diffusion 2.1 with U-Net architecture, and requires per-prompt mask threshold tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to DiT-based models, whether mask thresholds can be fully automated, and whether the visual consistency benefit persists at larger model scales or with more diverse prompt distributions.
