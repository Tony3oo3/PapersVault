## Problem

VLM-based VLA models inherit strong semantic reasoning from vision-language pre-training but systematically neglect low-level visual and spatial details (distance, size, physical dynamics) that are critical for precise robotic control. Existing pre-training paradigms prioritize multi-modal understanding tasks like VQA, leaving a gap in capturing the fine-grained visual patterns needed for embodied decision-making. The paper asks whether a unified training pipeline can retain semantic generalization while simultaneously emphasizing low-level features essential for control.

## Input / Output

The model takes a current RGB observation (512×512) and a language instruction as input, optionally augmented with a self-generated scene description produced by the model's own MMU head. It produces three outputs in a single autoregressive pass: a predicted future image (decoded from discrete VQ-GAN/MagVIT tokens), a sequence of robot actions (relative end-effector position via MSE and binary gripper state via BCE, processed through a MAP attention module and linear layer), and a natural-language scene description. Two separate vision encoders handle the two image modalities: a continuous CLIP-ViT encoder for understanding tasks and a discrete VQ-GAN encoder for prediction tasks.

## Method

UP-VLA builds on the Show-o unified VLM backbone (Phi-1.5 LLM) and co-trains three objectives—multi-modal understanding, future image prediction, and action learning—within a single autoregressive framework using task-specific attention masks. For understanding, image tokens are placed before text and attend to each other; for prediction, image tokens are placed after language tokens so they attend to all prior context and predict future discrete image tokens at the same positions; for action learning, both understanding and prediction tokens are concatenated so actions attend to both high-level semantics and low-level visual dynamics. The action prompt is further augmented with a self-generated scene description (via the MMU head) to inject semantic context into the prediction pathway. Training proceeds in two stages: a pre-training phase mixing Bridge robot data (25k demos) for future prediction with LLaVA-Tuning-665K for understanding, followed by an action-tuning phase on downstream embodied tasks while continuing to co-train with image-text pairs to preserve semantic capability.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC→D zero-shot long-horizon benchmark, UP-VLA achieves an average completion length of 4.08, a 33% improvement over the previous best prediction-based method GR-1 (3.06) and a 22% improvement over the best VLA-based method 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35). On the in-distribution ABCD→D setting, UP-VLA reaches 4.42, outperforming GR-1 (4.21) and Robo-Flamingo (4.09). In real-world Franka-Emika Panda manipulation, UP-VLA achieves 80% success on seen tasks and 58% on unseen objects, surpassing all baselines on precise-operation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UP-VLA outperforms all nine baselines on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark. Against VLM-based VLA methods, UP-VLA (4.08) substantially exceeds Robo-Flamingo (2.47), 3D-VLA (0.71), and the same-backbone RT-2 reimplementation UP-VLA-RT-2 (1.44), demonstrating that pure semantic pre-training is insufficient for visual generalization. Against prediction-based methods, UP-VLA surpasses GR-1 (3.06) by 33%, SuSIE (2.69), and Uni-Pi (0.92), showing that combining prediction with multi-modal understanding yields stronger generalization than prediction alone. The closest competitor is 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35), which leverages depth information; UP-VLA still leads by 0.73 in average completion length. On the in-distribution ABCD→D setting, UP-VLA (4.42) narrowly beats GR-1 (4.21) and Robo-Flamingo (4.09), confirming consistent gains. In real-world experiments, UP-VLA achieves the highest success rates on precise-operation tasks (e.g., routing cables, grasping small unseen objects), where prediction-based methods like UP-VLA-phi-w/o-mmu outperform VLA-only methods but still fall short of the full UP-VLA model.

## Relation to Prior Work

UP-VLA builds directly on Show-o's unified autoregressive framework for multimodal understanding and generation, extending it to the embodied robotics domain by adding an action prediction head. It departs from two parallel lines of VLA research: (1) VLM-based approaches like RT-2 and Robo-Flamingo that inherit only high-level semantic knowledge from vision-language pretraining, and (2) prediction-based approaches like GR-1 and SuSIE that learn physical dynamics through future frame generation but lack explicit semantic grounding. The key conceptual departure is the joint co-training of both objectives within a single autoregressive model using a flexible attention mask, rather than treating understanding and prediction as separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean empirical decomposition showing that VLM-based VLAs are weak on spatial/precise control tasks while prediction-based methods are weak on semantic generalization, and that a unified training paradigm can address both gaps simultaneously. The 33% improvement on Calvin ABC→D and the real-world results on Franka Panda validate the approach, though the 1.3B backbone and limited dataset scale (25k Bridge demos, 2k real-world demos) leave open whether the gains persist at larger scale. A notable limitation is that the predicted future images exhibit background color drift, suggesting the visual generation component is undertrained relative to the understanding component. Future work should explore whether this unified paradigm scales to larger VLMs and more diverse embodiments, and whether the specific attention mask design is optimal or if alternative fusion strategies could further improve performance.
