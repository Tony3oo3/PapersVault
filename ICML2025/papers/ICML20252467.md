## Problem

When adapting VLMs like CLIP to downstream classification via pseudolabels, the generated labels are systematically imbalanced across classes, causing confirmation bias and degraded performance. Existing methods apply post-hoc balancing heuristics (e.g., top-k selection, candidate sets) without investigating why certain classes receive disproportionately few or incorrect pseudolabels. This paper identifies two root causes—concept mismatch (text features misaligned with visual concepts) and concept confusion (text features failing to capture distinguishing features between similar classes)—and proposes targeted mechanisms to address each.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images and a set of class name strings (optionally augmented with 2 labeled images per class in SSL or a 62-38 seen/unseen split in TRZSL). During initialization, an LLM (ChatGPT 4o-mini) is queried to generate enhanced textual descriptions for detected mismatched classes, and K-means clustering is applied to CLIP image features. The output is a per-image class label; during fine-tuning, the model dynamically assigns pseudolabels to unlabeled samples via a confidence threshold (τ=0.85 or 0.5 depending on dataset), and all adapters are disabled at inference.

## Method

CAP operates in three stages. First, concept alignment uses an iterative K-means-based mismatch detection algorithm that progressively removes well-matched text-image pairs, isolating concept-mismatched classes; for these, LLM-generated enhanced descriptions are selected by maximum similarity to remaining image-feature centroids, and top-k images are assigned as pseudolabeled samples. Second, a confusion-aware calibrated margin modifies the cross-entropy loss by adding a per-pair margin M = S ⊙ m, where S is the max of visual-prototype and text-prototype cosine similarities between class pairs, and m scales each class's margin by its prediction tendency δ_c (inverse of its confident-sample count relative to the most-predicted class) and overall imbalance Δ; this margin is recomputed each epoch to progressively sharpen predictions between confused classes. Third, the fine-tuning stage deploys MaPLe-style multi-modal prompts with two independent visual adapters—a main adapter trained only on the high-accuracy initialization pseudolabels and a pseudo adapter trained on dynamically generated pseudolabels from unlabeled data via FixMatch-style thresholding—so that errors from less reliable labels do not contaminate the main adapter's representations.

## Results

Under the unsupervised learning (UL) setting, CAP achieves test accuracy of 76.80% on Flowers102, 81.48% on RESISC45, 55.29% on DTD, 75.01% on EuroSAT, 55.76% on CUB, and 18.42% on FGVC-Aircraft, surpassing the SoTA baseline CPL by 3.90%, 4.09%, 3.38%, 7.75%, 2.29%, and 0.07% respectively. Under semi-supervised learning (SSL), CAP reaches 92.78% on EuroSAT (vs. 77.51% for CPL) and 83.32% on RESISC45 (vs. 80.98%). Under transductive zero-shot learning (TRZSL), CAP achieves 89.53% on Flowers102 and 88.82% on RESISC45, outperforming CPL by 2.18% and 2.97%. Overall, the paper reports a 6.29% relative improvement over the SoTA method (CPL) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAP is compared against zero-shot CLIP, FPL, GRIP, and CPL (the SoTA). Under UL, CAP outperforms CPL on all six datasets, with the largest gains on EuroSAT (+7.75%), RESISC45 (+4.09%), and Flowers102 (+3.90%). Under SSL, CAP wins on four of six datasets (notably EuroSAT +15.27% and RESISC45 +2.34%) but trails CPL on CUB (−0.49%) and FGVC-Aircraft (−0.69%). Under TRZSL, CAP again leads on Flowers102, RESISC45, DTD, and EuroSAT (by 1.55–2.97%) but falls behind CPL on CUB (−4.85%) and FGVC-Aircraft (−1.83%), suggesting the method is less effective on fine-grained datasets with very high intra-class similarity. Against the older baselines FPL and GRIP, CAP achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., +10.13% over GRIP on EuroSAT UL), confirming that the concept alignment and calibrated margin mechanisms address failure modes that iterative top-k selection alone cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudolabel-based VLM adaptation line (UPL, FPL, GRIP, CPL) by diagnosing the root causes of pseudolabel imbalance—concept mismatch and concept confusion—rather than applying post-hoc balancing heuristics. It builds on MaPLe's multi-modal prompt architecture for fine-tuning and borrows the confidence-thresholding mechanism from FixMatch for dynamic pseudolabel generation during training. The key departure is a detect-then-enhance pipeline that uses iterative K-means clustering to identify semantically misaligned classes and LLM-generated descriptions to repair them, combined with a logit-adjustment-inspired margin that adaptively penalizes confusion between similar classes based on inter-class similarity and prediction tendency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual decomposition of pseudolabel imbalance into mismatch (text-image semantic gap) versus confusion (insufficient discrimination between similar classes) provides a principled diagnostic that prior work lacked, and the LLM-enhanced text descriptions offer a practical, plug-in technique for improving CLIP's zero-shot alignment on underperforming classes. However, the method's reliance on an external LLM for text augmentation introduces cost and availability constraints, and the independent adapter component—motivated as critical for preventing error accumulation—shows only marginal gains over shared adapters in ablation. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLMs, whether it transfers to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), and how it scales to very large class spaces where LLM-based description generation becomes impractical.
