## Problem

Robotic geometric assembly—reassembling broken fragments into their original shape with bimanual manipulation—remains largely unexplored despite broad applications (repair, archaeology, surgery). Prior work either synthesizes broken parts in simulation or predicts assembled poses in the vision domain, but neither addresses the actual step-by-step robotic manipulation process, where the observation space (arbitrary fragment geometries) and action space (long-horizon, contact-rich, collision-avoiding bimanual coordination) are exceptionally large.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial 3D point cloud O (from a single front-facing camera) and an imaginary assembled shape S in an arbitrary pose (assumed available from prior pose-estimation methods) as input. It produces a three-phase action plan: per-point affordance maps and SE(3) gripper poses for pick-up, alignment, and assembly steps, along with a predicted disassembly direction and an SE(3) transformation matrix that maps the imaginary assembled shape to collision-free alignment poses. The affordance maps are structured as two conditional submodules (first gripper unconditional, second conditioned on the first), enabling sequential bimanual coordination.

## Method

BiAssemble decomposes the long-horizon assembly into three steps (pick-up, alignment, assembly) and learns each with dedicated predictors. A Disassembly Predictor uses SO(3)-equivariant VN-DGCNN to encode the assembled shape and a cVAE to predict the distribution of collision-free disassembly directions. A Transformation Predictor (PointNet++ + cVAE) then maps the assembled shape and disassembly direction into an SE(3) transformation that yields alignment poses reachable without collisions from the initial fragment poses. The core BiAffordance Predictor extends DualAfford's two-submodule architecture with Affordance (MLP), Actor (cVAE), and Critic (MLP) networks per gripper; it is trained in a two-stage pipeline where the second gripper module M2 is trained first, then M1 is supervised by M2's critic scores, ensuring the first gripper selects grasp points that are easily collaborable. The key insight is leveraging point-level affordance for geometric generalization while conditioning it on downstream alignment and assembly feasibility, rather than only short-term graspability.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad Dataset simulation benchmark, BiAssemble achieves a 24.10% assembly success rate on novel instances within training categories and 17.4% on unseen categories, substantially outperforming all baselines. The metric is binary assembly success (relative distance and rotation angle within threshold at the end of the three-step pick-up/alignment/assembly process). On the real-world benchmark with Franka Panda arms and Azure Kinect input, the method demonstrates successful bimanual assembly of mugs and other objects, confirming sim-to-real transfer. Ablations show that removing the SE(3)-equivariant encoder drops performance to 21.20%/14.4%, and providing ground-truth disassembly direction and transformation (w/ GT Target) yields 22.40%/19%, indicating the learned predictors are competitive with oracle inputs on training categories but slightly inferior on unseen ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiAssemble outperforms all four baselines across both evaluation settings. Against DualAfford (the closest learned baseline, designed for short-term bimanual tasks), BiAssemble improves success rate by 15.7 percentage points on novel instances (24.10% vs. 8.40%) and by 10.2 points on unseen categories (17.4% vs. 7.2%), demonstrating that incorporating long-horizon alignment/assembly awareness into affordance prediction is critical. Against SE(3)-Equiv (a pose-prediction method adapted with heuristic grasping), the gap is even larger at 21.1 and 15.8 points, confirming that ignoring robotic execution constraints severely limits assembly success. The ACT imitation-learning baseline (0.30%/0.4%) and the hand-engineered Heuristic baseline (4.20%/4.4%) both fail to generalize, with ACT struggling particularly in the alignment phase due to the enormous observation and action spaces. Notably, the w/ GT Target ablation (22.40%/19%) slightly exceeds BiAssemble on unseen categories, suggesting that the learned Disassembly and Transformation Predictors could benefit from more diverse training data for out-of-distribution geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DualAfford (Zhao et al., 2022), which learns collaborative point-level affordance for short-term bimanual tasks (pushing, rotating), by incorporating awareness of subsequent long-horizon alignment and assembly steps into the affordance signal. It also builds on the SE(3)-equivariant shape assembly line (Wu et al., 2023c; Scarpellini et al., 2024), which predicts assembled part poses from fracture geometry but ignores robotic execution, by adding a full manipulation pipeline (disassembly direction prediction, transformation prediction, and affordance-guided grasping). The key conceptual departure is bridging the gap between vision-based pose estimation for broken parts and actual bimanual robotic execution, a gap the authors identify as unaddressed by both the graphics/graphics-CV community and the bimanual manipulation community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geometric assembly of broken objects has clear real-world applications (repair, archaeology, surgery, paleontology) yet remains under-explored in robotics; this paper provides both a method and a reproducible real-world benchmark to advance the field. However, success rates remain modest (24–25% on training categories, 17–18% on unseen categories), and the method relies on a strong assumption of access to a correct 'imaginary assembled shape,' which in practice must come from a separate pose-estimation pipeline. Open questions include scaling beyond two-fragment assembly (the multi-fragment extension achieves only ~17% average), handling genuinely imperfect shape predictions without fine-tuning, and closing the sim-to-real gap for contact-rich manipulation.
