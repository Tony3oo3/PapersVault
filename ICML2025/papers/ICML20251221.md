## Problem

Data contamination during pre-training can invalidate evaluation benchmarks, yet prior studies have been limited to small model scales (≤774M parameters), treated contamination as a fine-tuning or extended pre-training problem rather than a direct pre-training phenomenon, and lacked rigorous decontamination of training data. This paper asks: how does contamination systematically inflate machine translation performance metrics, and how does this inflation interact with model scale, temporal distribution of contaminated samples, contamination frequency, and language resource availability?

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a 325B-token multilingual pre-training mixture (60% Dolma, 35% Madlad, 5% WMT'23 parallel data) and systematically injects MT test examples as contamination, configured along three axes: mode (source-only, target-only, or full prompted source-target pairs), temporal distribution (early at 30%, middle at 60%, late at 90%, or uniform across 30–90% of training), and frequency (1, 10, or 100 copies). Contamination replaces less than 5% of examples per batch to preserve the parallel-data ratio. The output is BLEU and MetricX scores for contaminated versus uncontaminated models evaluated on both contaminated (WMT'23, 10 language pairs) and non-contaminated (WMT'24, 5 overlapping pairs; FLORES, 3 near-zero-resource languages) test sets, yielding absolute and relative performance inflation measurements across 42 conditions per model scale.

## Method

The core contribution is a controlled, large-scale contamination study at 1B and 8B parameter scales using a checkpoint-branching strategy: after decontaminating the train-test split via 8-gram subword search (70% overlap threshold, removing ~10% of test examples) and training a baseline decoder-only transformer (256K SentencePiece vocabulary, 4096 context, 155K steps), each contamination experiment branches from the baseline checkpoint and continues pre-training on a modified data mixture where only the contamination instances differ. This branching reduces total compute by 53.6% while fixing initialization and training history to isolate contamination's effect. The study covers 13 language pairs across 8 languages with varying resource levels, and critically evaluates models on both contaminated (WMT'23) and non-contaminated (WMT'24) test sets to distinguish genuine capability gains from evaluation inflation. Key findings include that full source-target contamination inflates BLEU by up to 30 points for 8B models (2.5× the 1B effect), uniform temporal distribution produces the most persistent inflation, and contamination requires sufficient language representation in the pre-training data to have any measurable impact.

## Results

Full (source+target) contamination inflates BLEU scores by up to 30 points for 8B-parameter models, with the 8B model's average inflation being 2.5× larger than the 1B model's (which peaks around 9 BLEU points). Source-only and target-only contamination produce smaller and less consistent over-estimations that do not generalize to non-contaminated WMT'24 test sets, whereas Full contamination yields up to 26 BLEU points larger improvements on contaminated vs. non-contaminated datasets. Uniform contamination throughout training produces the most persistent performance inflation, while early concentrated contamination causes large initial spikes (~70 BLEU) that decay to ~40 points after ~100K steps. For near-zero-resource languages (Achenese, Wolof, Yoruba), contamination produces no measurable inflation (≤3 BLEU points), confirming that sufficient language representation in pre-training data is a prerequisite for contamination effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between uncontaminated baseline models and contaminated variants under 42 different conditions (mode × temporal distribution × frequency) at two scales (1B and 8B). Full source-target contamination consistently outperforms the baseline across nearly all language pairs, with the 8B model showing substantially larger inflation (up to 30 BLEU) than the 1B model (up to ~9 BLEU), a 2.5× gap. Partial contamination (source-only or target-only) yields negligible and inconsistent improvements that do not transfer to the non-contaminated WMT'24 test sets, confirming that the Full-contamination gains are genuine over-estimations rather than capability improvements. Increasing contamination frequency from 1 to 100 copies monotonically increases inflation for Full contamination (e.g., 8B He X→EN goes from 40.99 at 1 copy to 59.93 at 100 copies, a +21.3 BLEU gain over baseline), while partial contamination shows no such frequency dependence. The En→X direction consistently shows larger percentage improvements than X→En, and contamination has no measurable effect on languages with no representation in the pre-training data (Achenese, Wolof, Yoruba), establishing a necessary threshold of language coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of contamination studies by Magar & Schwartz (2022) and Jiang et al. (2024), which examined contamination during pre-training but at much smaller scales (100M–774M parameters), to 1B and 8B models trained on 325B tokens. It departs from Zhou et al. (2023) and Yang et al. (2023), which studied contamination via extended pre-training or fine-tuning, by isolating contamination within the actual pre-training process using a checkpoint-branching strategy. The paper also carves new ground by being the first to study contamination in a multilingual machine translation setting with rigorous 8-gram decontamination, systematically varying contamination mode, temporal distribution, and frequency across 42 conditions per model scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As pre-training corpora grow and data collection accelerates, contamination is no longer a theoretical concern but a practical threat to evaluation validity; this paper quantifies that threat at scales relevant to current LLM development. The finding that contamination sensitivity scales super-linearly with model size (2.5× for 8B vs. 1B) and that uniform contamination—the most realistic scenario—produces the most persistent inflation has direct implications for how practitioners should interpret benchmark scores and design decontamination pipelines. Open questions include whether the scaling trend holds beyond 8B parameters, whether findings generalize to encoder-decoder or other architectures, and how to reliably detect and correct for contamination in production models where training data provenance is opaque.
