## Problem

Existing Koopman spectral estimation methods like ResDMD use spectral residuals only as a post-hoc filter on precomputed eigenpairs, which cannot discover eigenpairs that were never computed in the first place — a limitation termed the 'spectral inclusion' problem. This means critical portions of the true spectrum, particularly continuous spectra essential for characterizing chaotic and turbulent dynamics, can be entirely missed. The paper asks whether spectral residual minimization can be elevated from a filtering criterion to a primary optimization objective that actively searches for a more complete and accurate spectrum.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired time-series snapshots (X, Y) with Y = F(X), a target number of observables N_K, and neural network hyperparameters (hidden layer sizes, learning rate, regularization σ). For high-dimensional inputs (e.g., 295,122 spatial points in turbulence or 7,000+ neurons in neural recordings), truncated SVD reduces dimensionality before dictionary evaluation. Outputs include Koopman eigenvalues/eigenfunctions ordered by ascending spectral residual, spatial Koopman modes (mapped back to original dimensions via SVD right-singular vectors), an ε-pseudospectrum computed by scanning grid points in the complex plane, and the learned dictionary functions themselves. Non-trainable basis functions (constant ones and state coordinates) are appended to the neural network output to prevent trivial zero-residual solutions.

## Method

ResKoopNet parameterizes the Koopman dictionary Ψ(x; θ) with a feedforward neural network (3 hidden layers, tanh activation) and minimizes the total spectral residual J(θ) = (1/m)‖(Ψ_Y(θ) − Ψ_X(θ)K(θ))V(θ)‖_F² as its loss, where V(θ) contains the eigenvectors of the Koopman matrix. Training alternates between a closed-form Koopman matrix update K̃(θ) = (G(θ) + σI)⁻¹A(θ) and a stochastic gradient descent step on θ. Although this matrix expression resembles EDMD's, it is derived from minimizing the spectral residual (which explicitly incorporates the K*K Gram matrix) rather than the prediction error, making the loss an eigenspace-projected residual that weights errors along spectral directions. This distinction, combined with the neural network's ability to explore the dictionary space adaptively, allows ResKoopNet to discover eigenpairs that fixed-dictionary or filter-only methods miss, yielding both discrete eigenvalues and continuous-spectrum pseudospectral regions with theoretical convergence guarantees grounded in Barron space approximation theory.

## Results

On the pendulum system, ResKoopNet recovers the full Koopman spectrum (unit circle) with only N_K=300 observables, compared to ~460–964 basis functions required by ResDMD. On the turbulent airfoil pressure field (295,122 spatial points × 798 time steps), ResKoopNet's leading Koopman mode (smallest spectral residual) accurately reconstructs the global pressure field structure, a feature not captured by Kernel-ResDMD or Hankel-DMD. On the Sensorium 2023 neural dynamics dataset (5 mice, 6 video stimuli, >7,000 neurons each), ResKoopNet achieves the lowest Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI) across all five mice, indicating the most compact and well-separated clustering of neural states corresponding to different visual stimuli, outperforming Hankel-DMD, EDMD+RBF, and Kernel-ResDMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResKoopNet outperforms all five baselines across the three experiments. Against EDMD (Williams et al., 2015) and EDMD-DL (Li et al., 2017), ResKoopNet avoids spectral pollution and captures the continuous spectrum on the pendulum, whereas both EDMD variants produce spurious eigenvalues unrelated to the true unit-circle spectrum. Against Hankel-DMD (Arbabi & Mezić, 2017), ResKoopNet recovers the full spectral structure (discrete + continuous) in the pendulum and the global pressure field in turbulence, while Hankel-DMD captures only the point spectrum and fails to separate neural states in the high-dimensional Sensorium dataset. Against ResDMD (Colbrook & Townsend, 2024), ResKoopNet achieves comparable spectral accuracy with significantly fewer observables (300 vs. ~460–964) and, critically, actively discovers spectral components rather than merely filtering precomputed polluted spectra, thereby resolving the spectral inclusion problem. Against Kernel-ResDMD (Colbrook et al., 2023), ResKoopNet uniquely recovers the fundamental pressure field structure in the turbulence experiment and achieves substantially lower Davies-Bouldin Indices in the neural dynamics clustering task, where Kernel-ResDMD with 299 eigenfunctions shows no clear state separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ResDMD framework (Colbrook & Townsend, 2024), which introduced the spectral residual as a post-hoc filter for spurious eigenvalues in EDMD-based Koopman approximations. The key departure is repurposing the spectral residual from a filtering criterion into a differentiable training loss, enabling a neural network to actively learn dictionary functions that minimize spectral error rather than merely selecting from a precomputed set. This addresses the 'spectral inclusion' problem identified in ResDMD, where the computed spectrum is constrained to a subset of the true spectrum. The approach also builds on EDMD-DL (Li et al., 2017) for neural dictionary learning but fundamentally changes the optimization objective from prediction error to eigenspace-projected spectral residual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Resolving the spectral inclusion problem is important because it means methods can now recover the complete Koopman spectrum (both discrete and continuous) rather than a potentially incomplete subset, which is critical for characterizing chaotic and mixing dynamics. The practical impact is demonstrated by recovering the global pressure field structure in turbulence (missed by Kernel-ResDMD and Hankel-DMD) and achieving clear state separation in neural dynamics where all baselines fail. Key limitations include higher computational cost than classical methods, no handling of stochastic dynamics, and dependence on neural architecture choices. Open questions include whether physics-informed constraints can reduce the dictionary size further and whether the framework extends to stochastic systems.
