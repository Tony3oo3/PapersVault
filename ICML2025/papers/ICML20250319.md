## Problem

Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) achieves Best-of-Both-Worlds regret guarantees without solving an optimization problem, but its standard Geometric Resampling (GR) loss estimator incurs O(K²) per-round average complexity—worse than FTRL-based policies like Tsallis-INF that run in O(K). This paper asks whether the resampling procedure can be made nearly linear in K while preserving the BOBW guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250319/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives the chosen arm I_t, the cumulative estimated loss vector L̂_t ∈ ℝ^K, the learning rate η_t, and the perturbation distribution D. It outputs a scalar estimator ŵ_{t,I_t}^{-1} = M_t · σ_{I_t} / (1 − F^{σ_{I_t}}(η_t L̂_{t,I_t})) (or the simpler M_t · σ_{I_t} for CGR I), which is multiplied by the observed loss to form the one-hot loss estimate. CGR I requires only the symmetry of D, while CGR II additionally requires explicit evaluation of the CDF F(x) and its inverse F^{-1}(x) for inverse-transform sampling from truncated distributions.

## Method

The key insight is that original GR wastes resampling iterations on perturbation vectors that provably cannot satisfy the termination condition (the chosen arm being the argmin of perturbed losses). CGR conditions the resampling on a necessary condition A_t for termination—specifically, that the perturbation of the chosen arm is the maximum among arms with no worse cumulative loss (and, in CGR II, exceeds a threshold η_t L̂_{t,I_t}). By Lemma 1, the expected number of resampling drops from 1/w_{t,I_t} to P(A_t|L̂_t, I_t)/w_{t,I_t}, yielding O(log K) in expectation. CGR I implements this conditioning via a simple value-swap (no CDF needed), while CGR II uses truncated-distribution sampling via inverse transform, achieving O(K) worst-case expected resampling under Fréchet-2 perturbations. CGR II-biased further caps resampling at G_t = ⌈(K∨4) log t⌉, introducing only O(log T) additional regret while bounding worst-case per-round cost.

## Results

CGR reduces the per-round average complexity of FTPL from O(K²) (original GR) to O(K log K), while preserving the BOBW regret guarantee of O(√(KT)) in adversarial bandits and O(Σᵢ log T/Δᵢ) in stochastic bandits. CGR II-biased further controls the worst-case resampling count to ⌈(K∨4)log t⌉ under the Fréchet-2 perturbation, and improves the dominant adversarial regret constant from 17.8√(KT) (GR) to 17.37√(KT). Empirically, the median number of resampling for CGR II is 1 (terminating in a single trial for over 50% of rounds), and FTPL CGR achieves lower regret than FTPL GR due to reduced estimator variance, while running significantly faster than both FTPL GR and Tsallis-INF as K grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FTPL GR (Honda et al., 2023), CGR wins decisively on computational efficiency: the per-round average cost drops from O(K²) to O(K log K), and the runtime gap widens sharply as K increases (e.g., at K=128, FTPL GR runtime grows sharply while FTPL CGR stays near-linear). CGR also achieves slightly better empirical regret than FTPL GR in both adversarial and stochastic settings, attributed to the lower variance of the conditional resampling estimator. Against Tsallis-INF (Zimmert & Seldin, 2021), FTPL CGR is faster in runtime (optimization-free vs. Newton's method iterations) and its regret (CGR II) is close to T-INF IW, though it remains worse than T-INF RV. Against TS-Prod (Zimmert & Marinov, 2024), FTPL CGR achieves clearly better regret (TS-Prod is the worst among all compared algorithms), but TS-Prod is slightly faster for large K due to its simpler O(K) update rule without the log K factor in CGR's complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FTPL framework for multi-armed bandits, building directly on Neu & Bartók's (2016) Geometric Resampling (GR) technique and the subsequent BOBW analyses by Honda et al. (2023) and Lee et al. (2024) for Fréchet-type perturbations. The key departure is the introduction of Conditional Geometric Resampling (CGR), which conditions the resampling distribution on a necessary condition for termination (e.g., the perturbation of the pulled arm being the maximum among arms with no worse cumulative loss), reducing average complexity from O(K²) to O(K log K). The paper also positions itself against FTRL-based policies like Tsallis-INF and TS-Prod, showing that FTPL with CGR can match or exceed their computational efficiency while retaining the optimization-free nature of FTPL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CGR is the first technique to make FTPL competitive with FTRL in computational cost for standard MAB problems while preserving the BOBW guarantee, removing a key practical barrier to adopting FTPL in settings where explicit optimization is undesirable. The biased variant (CGR II-biased) additionally provides a worst-case complexity bound of O(K log t) per round, which is significantly tighter than the O(√(KT)) bound from Neu & Bartók (2016). Open questions include extending the biased analysis to more general Fréchet-type distributions (currently limited to shape-2 Fréchet) and developing a reduced-variance loss estimator for FTPL analogous to the RV estimator used in Tsallis-INF, which would close the remaining empirical regret gap.
