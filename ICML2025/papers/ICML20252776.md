## Problem

Existing sentence embeddings are designed for similarity retrieval and fundamentally cannot capture contradiction relationships because cosine similarity is transitive: if A contradicts C and B contradicts C, cosine forces A and B to also appear similar. Cross-encoders can detect contradictions but require at least 200× more computation than vector operations, making them impractical for large-scale corpus search. No efficient, embedding-based method existed for non-similarity-based retrieval tasks such as contradiction detection.

## Input / Output

Given a query passage and a document corpus, the method computes a per-document score F(q,p) = cos(E(q),E(p)) + α·Hoyer(E_s(q),E_s(p)), where E is a standard sentence encoder and E_s is the SPARSECL-trained sparse-aware encoder. The scalar α is tuned on a validation set to adapt to different notions of contradiction across domains. The output is the top-10 passages ranked by this combined score, with the explicit assumption that similar supporting passages may coexist in the corpus alongside the true contradicting passage.

## Method

SPARSECL fine-tunes a pretrained sentence embedding model via contrastive learning with the roles of positive and negative examples reversed relative to standard CL: contradictions serve as positives and paraphrases as hard negatives, with the Hoyer sparsity of the embedding difference vector as the similarity measure in the InfoNCE loss. The key insight is that in a semantic basis, contradictions should differ in only a few coordinates while remaining similar in most others, making the difference vector sparse. The Hoyer sparsity function (scaled ℓ₁/ℓ₂ ratio) is provably non-transitive, unlike cosine, which theoretically enables distinguishing the case where A and B both contradict C but not each other. At retrieval time, the final score combines standard cosine similarity from any off-the-shelf encoder with the Hoyer sparsity score from the SPARSECL model, weighted by α, reducing contradiction detection to simple vector arithmetic that is orders of magnitude faster than cross-encoder inference.

## Results

On the Arguana counter-argument retrieval task, the best configuration (GTE CL(Cosine)+SPARSECL(Hoyer)) achieves NDCG@10 of 81.3, compared to 77.8 for standard CL(Cosine) and 72.5 for zeroshot cosine. On the synthetic MSMARCO and HotpotQA contradiction retrieval datasets, SPARSECL yields NDCG@10 scores of 95.2 and 97.9 respectively, representing over 14.6 percentage-point gains over the best cosine-only baselines. Across all three models (BGE, UAE, GTE), incorporating SPARSECL produces an average improvement of 11.0% over the corresponding cosine-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the external baseline of Shi et al. (2023) Bipolar-encoder, SPARSECL (GTE CL+SparseCL) achieves 62.9 Recall@1 on Arguana versus 49.0 for Bipolar-encoder, a 13.9-point improvement. Internally, SPARSECL consistently outperforms both the standard CL(Cosine) and Zeroshot(Cosine) baselines across all three datasets and all three model sizes. The gains are most dramatic on MSMARCO and HotpotQA (14.6+ points), where paraphrases in the corpus act as strong confounders for pure similarity search; on Arguana the improvement is more modest (~3.6% average) because counter-arguments are already the most similar passages to the query. Notably, the Prompt+CL(Cosine) baseline, which appends 'Not true:' to the query, performs well on the GPT-4-generated datasets (e.g., 88.1 on MSMARCO) but underperforms on Arguana (71.1 vs. 77.8 for CL), suggesting the prompt exploits the surface-level negation pattern in synthetic contradictions rather than capturing genuine argumentative opposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the contrastive learning framework for sentence embeddings (SimCSE, DPR) by replacing the cosine similarity objective with the Hoyer sparsity measure borrowed from signal processing (Hurley & Rickard, 2009), training embeddings so that contradicting pairs have sparse difference vectors. It departs from prior counter-argument retrieval work (Wachsmuth et al., 2018; Shi et al., 2023) by proposing a simpler scoring function (cosine + Hoyer) that avoids debate-specific structural assumptions, and from cross-encoder NLI models by achieving 200× faster inference through precomputed embeddings. The theoretical analysis showing Hoyer sparsity is non-transitive while cosine is transitive provides a principled justification for why similarity-based retrieval fundamentally cannot capture the 'A contradicts C, B contradicts C, but A does not contradict B' pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens up non-similarity-based retrieval as a distinct research direction, with immediate applications to fact-checking, LLM hallucination detection, and corpus cleaning. The practical efficiency gain (200× faster than cross-encoders) makes contradiction detection feasible at corpus scale. However, the evaluation relies on only one real-world dataset (Arguana) and two GPT-4-generated synthetic datasets, raising questions about generalization to more nuanced or domain-specific contradictions. Open questions include whether the method works without fine-tuning, how it handles multi-sentence or implicit contradictions, and whether the Hoyer sparsity objective can be extended to other non-similarity retrieval tasks beyond contradiction.
