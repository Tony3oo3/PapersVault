## Problem

High-order score estimation (e.g., the Hessian of the log-density) provides richer local information about data distributions than first-order scores, enabling faster sampling and causal discovery. However, existing methods require complete data, while real-world tabular datasets in healthcare and finance frequently contain missing values. Prior approaches either impute missing entries (introducing bias) or handle only first-order scores under missingness, leaving high-order score estimation from incomplete data unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete tabular data (continuous and discrete variables) along with a binary missing mask indicating which entries are absent, plus a pre-specified noise level σ. Missing continuous values are replaced with 0 and missing discrete values with a new category before one-hot encoding; the mask then zeros out these positions in the loss. The model outputs both the first-order score (gradient of log-density) and second-order score (Hessian of log-density) of the noise-perturbed distribution, which are subsequently used for Langevin or Ozaki sampling to generate synthetic data, or for topological ordering in causal discovery.

## Method

MissScore extends Denoising Score Matching to high-order scores under M(C)AR missing mechanisms by introducing element-wise weighted objectives: under MCAR the weight is simply the observation mask (1−m), while under MAR it uses inverse probability weighting with observation probabilities estimated via logistic regression. Building on Tweedie's formula, the second-order DSM objective regresses the n-fold tensor of the clean data onto a polynomial of the perturbed data and all lower-order scores, with the Hessian appearing as the target. First- and second-order scores are trained jointly via a multi-task loss L_DSM + ω·L_D2SM. To handle the high variance that arises as σ→0, the paper introduces tailored variance reduction: a control-variate correction for the first-order loss and antithetic sampling (evaluating the loss at x+σz and x−σz) for the second-order loss, both adapted to incorporate the missing-data weights. The key theoretical result proves that the global optimum of these weighted objectives exactly recovers the oracle high-order score under M(C)AR.

## Results

On the Census dataset (missing ratio 0.3, MCAR), MissScore with Ozaki sampling achieves 88% fidelity and 81% ML efficiency, outperforming STaSy-mean (82% fidelity, 77% ML efficiency) and DSM-delete (73% fidelity, 70% ML efficiency). For score estimation on 100-dimensional synthetic Gaussians, variance-reduced MissScore achieves MSE of 0.07±0.00 for s₁ and 0.05±0.00 for s₂ at σ=0.01 with no missing data, while without variance reduction the s₂ MSE explodes to 15.42±0.47. In causal discovery (MCAR, 10% missing, d=10–50), MissScore achieves SHD comparable to MissDAG and MissOTM while reducing runtime substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Census dataset, MissScore (Ozaki) beats STaSy-mean by 6 points in fidelity (88% vs. 82%) and 4 points in ML efficiency (81% vs. 77%), and outperforms DSM-delete by 15 and 11 points respectively, confirming that impute-then-generate introduces bias. On the Bayesian Network dataset (Figure 3), MissScore's advantage over both baselines grows with increasing missing ratio, reaching the largest gap at α=0.9. In causal discovery (Figure 4), MissScore achieves SHD comparable to MissDAG and MissOTM across dimensions d=10, 20, 50, though it is slightly worse at d=50; however, it runs significantly faster than both. MissScore clearly outperforms MissDiffAN (DiffAN + MissForest imputation), demonstrating that direct score learning from incomplete data avoids the bias introduced by imputation. Compared to MissForest+DAGMA, MissScore performs similarly, likely because DAGMA enforces additional structural constraints during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Denoising Score Matching (DSM) along two axes simultaneously: from first-order to high-order scores (building on Meng et al. 2021's Tweedie-based framework) and from complete to incomplete data (building on Ouyang et al. 2023's MissDiff for first-order scores with missing values). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of inverse probability weighting into the high-order DSM objective, yielding oracle estimators under M(C)AR mechanisms that prior work had not established for second-order or higher scores. Unlike imputation-then-train pipelines or GAN/VAE-based approaches for missing data, MissScore directly embeds the missingness structure into the score-matching loss, avoiding the bias and model inconsistency of two-stage methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: high-order scores (Hessians) enable faster sampling via Ozaki discretization and are essential for score-based causal discovery, yet both applications have been limited to complete data. By providing theoretically grounded estimators under M(C)AR and demonstrating that direct learning from missing data outperforms imputation-then-generate, the work opens a path for score-based methods in domains like healthcare and finance where incomplete records are the norm. Key limitations include the restriction to M(C)AR (MNAR is acknowledged but not fully solved), performance degradation at high dimensionality, and the absence of ground-truth validation on real-world data. Future directions include extending to image/time-series modalities and incorporating structural constraints for causal discovery.
