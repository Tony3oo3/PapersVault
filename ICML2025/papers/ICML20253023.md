## Problem

Traditional conformal prediction requires exchangeability and full-dataset access, making it unsuitable for streaming data with concept drift. Existing online conformal prediction methods lack built-in privacy mechanisms, while prior DP-conformal approaches (e.g., Angelopoulos et al., 2022) are offline and require the entire calibration set. This paper addresses the simultaneous challenge of constructing valid, adaptive prediction sets in a one-pass online setting while providing formal differential privacy guarantees at the individual level.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm consumes the newly observed covariates X_t, a pre-trained prediction model f_hat (or two quantile regression models for CQR), a per-step privacy budget (epsilon_t, delta_t, or mu_t), and a target miscoverage level alpha in (0, 1/2). It outputs a private prediction set C_hat_t(X_t) = {y : S_f(X_t, y) <= q_hat_t^{1-alpha}} (or an asymmetric interval [q_lo(X_t) - q_hat, q_hi(X_t) + q_hat] for CQR). The algorithm is strictly one-pass: it maintains only the scalar state (W_{t-1}, lambda_t, q_hat_t) and never re-accesses historical data, keeping space complexity O(1) per step.

## Method

The core mechanism estimates the (1-alpha)-quantile of non-conformity scores via a coin-betting formulation of online convex optimization: the pinball loss subgradient g_t is privatized by adding DP noise (Laplace, Gaussian, or GDP), and the quantile is tracked as the signed bet w_t = lambda_t * W_{t-1}, where the wealth W_t and betting fraction lambda_t follow the Krichevsky-Trofimov adaptive rule, eliminating manual learning-rate tuning. A truncation constant c enforces a lower bound on W_t to prevent noise-induced instability during early iterations. Privacy is guaranteed at max_j epsilon_j-DP (or max_j mu_j-GDP) via parallel composition across disjoint per-step data points, and a long-run coverage guarantee of 1 - alpha is established under bounded non-conformity scores. The CQR extension replaces the single non-conformity score with S_t = max{q_lo(X_t) - Y_t, Y_t - q_hi(X_t)}, enabling heteroscedastic-adaptive intervals while preserving the same iterative quantile-update structure.

## Results

On the Setting 2 simulation (T=10,000, target coverage 0.9), the ODPCQR variant maintains long-run coverage of 0.895–0.898 across all privacy levels (μ=2, 1, 0.5), closely matching the non-private Original (0.890–0.893). In contrast, ODPCP coverage degrades from 0.886 (μ=2) to 0.834–0.850 (μ=0.5), while interval width grows from ~5.2–7.5 to ~7.7–11.5. On the PAMAP2 heart-rate dataset, the Original algorithm suffers four sharp coverage drops (to ~0.3 and ~0.7) at data shifts, whereas ODPCP stabilizes coverage around the 0.9 target using the truncation parameter c, at the cost of wider intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-private Original baseline (Podkopaev et al., 2024), ODPCP at weak privacy (μ=2) achieves nearly identical coverage (0.886 vs. 0.889) and only marginally wider intervals (e.g., 5.23 vs. 5.18 in Case 1), but at strong privacy (μ=0.5) CP coverage drops to 0.834–0.850 and width nearly doubles (e.g., 11.48 vs. 7.45 in Case 4). The ODPCQR extension is substantially more robust: it maintains coverage of 0.895–0.898 even at μ=0.5, and its width increase is modest (e.g., 8.28 vs. 7.40 in Case 4), outperforming ODPCP by 4–6 percentage points in coverage under strong privacy. Against the offline private baseline DPCP (Angelopoulos et al., 2022), ODPCP achieves comparable long-run coverage but produces significantly narrower prediction sets, demonstrating a favorable privacy–efficiency trade-off in the online setting. On the PAMAP2 dataset, the Original algorithm's coverage drops to ~0.3 at abrupt data shifts, while ODPCP (with truncation parameter c) stabilizes near the 0.9 target, though at the cost of wider intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coin-betting conformal prediction framework of Podkopaev et al. (2024) by injecting differential privacy noise into the subgradient updates, leveraging the parameter-free wealth process and Krichevsky-Trofimov estimator from Orabona & Pal (2016) to avoid manual learning-rate tuning under noisy gradients. It departs from prior DP-conformal prediction work (Angelopoulos et al. 2022; Humbert et al. 2023; Plassier et al. 2023), which relied on offline batch quantile estimation via exponential or Gaussian mechanisms, by formulating the problem as a one-pass online procedure that never re-accesses historical data. The CQR extension follows Romano et al. (2019) but is the first to combine it with both online adaptation and per-step local differential privacy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously provide online (one-pass, streaming) conformal prediction with formal differential privacy guarantees and long-run coverage, filling a gap where prior DP-CP methods required full dataset access and prior online-CP methods lacked privacy. The practical relevance is clear for streaming, privacy-sensitive domains (healthcare, finance), though the inherent privacy-accuracy trade-off manifests as wider prediction intervals under strong privacy (μ=0.5), and the bounded-score assumption (S_t ∈ [0,D]) may be restrictive for unbounded regression targets. Open questions include whether the interval-width penalty can be reduced without sacrificing privacy, how the method behaves in high-dimensional or non-parametric settings, and whether the per-step LDP formulation can be tightened to a global (ε,δ)-DP guarantee with better composition.
