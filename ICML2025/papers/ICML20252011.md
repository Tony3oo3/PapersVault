## Problem

Given a current model and dataset, deciding whether a new sample needs annotation is a fundamental but unsolved question in efficient dataset construction. Conventional approaches retain all data (wasting annotation budget), active learning introduces pipeline complexity and distribution bias through repeated retraining, and coreset selection methods cannot leverage model-specific information without a fully trained model. The paper seeks an online, bias-free selective annotation framework that integrates model-specific estimation with distribution awareness at minimal computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB image samples (from online streams or web-sourced datasets like LAION-400M), extracts features via a pretrained backbone (e.g., MAE-pretrained ViT-Large), and operates against an existing annotated dataset stored in an online HNSW index. It outputs a prioritized annotation queue with per-sample information gain scores (both qualitative and quantitative), enabling automatic stopping when marginal gains plateau. The batch selection process completes within 1 minute on million-scale datasets, and the entire selection loop requires only ~10 A100 GPU hours of inference overhead across all rounds.

## Method

Info-Coevolution estimates each sample's information gain by fusing two prediction views: a model-based prediction (from the current network) and a data-based KNN prediction (from nearest neighbors in feature space), combined via Bayesian Prediction Fusion that accounts for per-predictor confidence. The key theoretical foundation is Theorem 3.1, which establishes that near-neighbor samples in feature space yield similar model predictions (Lipschitz continuity of the head), justifying local interpolation for entropy estimation. A dynamic rechecking mechanism updates gain estimates for all neighbors within an ε-distance whenever a new annotation arrives, automatically balancing class distribution and sample density without explicit rebalancing. The framework is online and model-update-optional: it requires only 4 rounds of continual supervised training (summing to <100% full training cost) on ImageNet-1K, achieving lossless 85.6% accuracy with 68% annotation and 85.5% with 50% annotation under semi-supervised training. The algorithm uses H(α)=α as a computationally cheap entropy proxy, requiring only the sign and relative magnitude of gains for sample ranking.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Info-Coevolution achieves lossless performance (85.6% top-1 accuracy) with only 68% of the full annotation budget, surpassing Semi-ViT's 85.5% (which requires 80% labeled + 20% unlabeled data). In the 10% supervised setting, it reaches 80.5% accuracy versus 79.2% for random selection (+1.3%). With semi-supervised training, the annotation ratio can be further reduced to 50% while maintaining near-lossless performance (85.5%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, it improves mIoU by 1.50 points (48.39 vs. 46.89) at the same 10% data budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Semi-ViT (Cai et al., 2022), Info-Coevolution achieves 85.6% top-1 accuracy with 68% of ImageNet-1K annotations, outperforming Semi-ViT's 85.5% which requires 80% labeled data plus 20% unlabeled data — a 12-percentage-point reduction in annotation cost at equal or better accuracy. Against Dataset Quantization (Zhou et al., 2023), the prior SOTA coreset selection method, DQ reaches 85.5% with 80% labeled data, while Info-Coevolution matches or exceeds this at 68% annotation, and additionally supports online/continual annotation without requiring full dataset access upfront. On CIFAR-10 with FixMatch, Info-Coevolution reaches the full-data accuracy of 95.85% with only 1,000 labeled samples, whereas random selection requires 4,000 (a 4× reduction). The method also generalizes to semantic segmentation on ADE20K, where it outperforms random selection by +1.50 mIoU and +2.09 mAcc at 10% data, confirming task-agnostic applicability beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a middle ground between active learning (which iteratively selects, annotates, and retrains the model) and coreset selection (which uses only data distribution without model information). It repurposes the information gain concept from decision trees into a generalized, locality-aware estimation for classification, and introduces Bayesian prediction fusion to merge model-based and KNN-based (data-based) confidence estimates. The online ANN-based dynamic rechecking mechanism departs from both paradigms by enabling selective annotation without frequent model updates, while the automatic stopping criterion addresses a practical gap neither prior approach handles. The closest experimental comparison is Dataset Quantization (Zhou et al., 2023), a SOTA coreset method, which the paper also outperforms in the selection-only setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32% annotation cost reduction on ImageNet-1K with lossless performance, combined with compatibility with semi-supervised learning (50% annotation ratio), offers a practical path toward democratizing large-scale dataset construction where annotation costs dominate training costs. The online, extendable design with logarithmic scaling via HNSW makes it applicable to continuously growing real-world data streams, a setting where most prior methods are ill-suited. Key limitations include the assumption that training and target distributions match, the preliminary treatment of weakly-supervised and unlabeled data, and validation primarily on classification tasks (with only a single segmentation experiment on ADE20k). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different data structures (e.g., language, video) and how it behaves under significant distribution shift.
