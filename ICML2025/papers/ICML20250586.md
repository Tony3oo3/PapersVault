## Problem

Existing 4-bit quantization formats for LLMs (int4, fp4, nf4) rely on fixed, a priori numeric distributions that mismatch the actual per-row weight distributions, leaving accuracy on the table. The paper asks whether the optimal 4-bit codebook can be learned directly from the weights themselves, without requiring any offline or online preprocessing of weights or activations (unlike GPTQ, AWQ, or QuIP).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model's weight matrices and a single hand-curated 88-word calibration prompt (covering fiction, news, code, math, and facts) to compute per-channel mean absolute activations. It produces, for each weight matrix row, a 16-entry bfloat16 dequantization lookup table (LUT) plus group-wise scale (α) and offset (β) factors at a default group size of 128, yielding a total storage cost of 4.3125 bits per weight entry (4-bit code + 0.0625-bit LUT overhead + 0.25-bit group metadata). No outlier or sensitive values are preserved in higher precision.

## Method

any4 applies group-wise scaling to each row of a weight matrix, then runs a modified K-means per row to learn a 16-entry dequantization LUT. The key distinction from vanilla K-means is the objective: instead of minimizing weight reconstruction error, it minimizes the expected output-activation error, which introduces a per-element weight of α_{i,j}·E|x_j| (group scale × mean absolute activation) into the centroid update (M-step). The E-step assigns each scaled weight to its nearest LUT entry, while the M-step sets each LUT value to the activation- and scale-weighted mean of the scaled weights in that cluster. Calibration uses only a single diverse 88-word prompt to estimate E|x_j|, outperforming standard 128-sample C4/WikiText-2 calibration. For inference, the companion tinygemm library dequantizes on the fly via a register-resident LUT addressed through GPU warp-shuffle, achieving ~2× speedup over PyTorch bfloat16 GEMM at small batch sizes on A100.

## Results

On Llama3 8B, ANY4 achieves 6.51 WikiText-2 perplexity, outperforming INT4 (6.87), FP4 (7.10), and NF4 (6.63), while FP16 baseline is 6.14. On Llama3 70B, ANY4 reaches 3.20 WikiText-2 perplexity versus 3.63 (INT4), 3.94 (FP4), and 3.43 (NF4), with FP16 at 2.86. Critically, ANY4 with simple round-to-nearest (RTN) matches or beats GPTQ+INT4 (6.5 vs 6.5 on 8B; 3.2 vs 3.3 on 70B), AWQ+INT4 (6.5 vs 6.6; 3.2 vs 3.3), and QuIP+INT4 (6.5 vs 6.5; 3.2 vs 3.4) without any weight or activation preprocessing. The per-row LUT overhead is only 0.0625 bits per entry, yielding a total of 4.3125 bits/entry with group size 128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NF4 (from QLoRA, Dettmers et al. 2023), ANY4 consistently achieves lower perplexity across all model sizes and families: on Llama3 8B, ANY4 scores 6.51 vs NF4's 6.63 on WikiText-2, and on Llama3 70B, 3.20 vs 3.43. Against GPTQ (Frantar et al. 2023), ANY4 with plain RTN ties at 4-bit on Llama3 8B (both 6.5) and beats it on Llama3 70B (3.2 vs 3.3), while also outperforming GPTQ at 3-bit (8.0 vs 8.2 on 8B; 4.6 vs 5.2 on 70B). Against AWQ (Lin et al. 2024), ANY4 wins at 4-bit on both 8B (6.5 vs 6.6) and 70B (3.2 vs 3.3), and at 3-bit on 70B (4.6 vs 4.8). Against QuIP (Chee et al. 2023), ANY4 ties at 4-bit on 8B (6.5 vs 6.5) and wins on 70B (3.2 vs 3.4), but QuIP remains superior at 2-bit (85.1 vs 1.0E3 on 8B; 13.0 vs 253.8 on 70B). Notably, ANY4 achieves these results without any weight or activation preprocessing, whereas GPTQ, AWQ, and QuIP all require calibration data and iterative or transformation-based preprocessing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical signal-processing framework of optimal non-uniform quantization (Lloyd, 1982; Max, 1960) to LLM weight compression, replacing the fixed a-priori Gaussian assumption of nf4/af4 with a per-row learned codebook via weighted k-means clustering. It positions any4 as a numeric representation choice that is orthogonal to preprocessing algorithms like GPTQ, AWQ, and QuIP, which transform weights or activations before rounding. The accompanying tinygemm library extends the int4 GEMM kernels already integrated into PyTorch (since late 2023) to support arbitrary LUT-based dequantization using GPU warp-shuffle lookups, enabling efficient inference for any4, nf4, and int4 in a unified kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

any4 offers a drop-in, preprocessing-free quantization method that consistently outperforms nf4 and int4 across model families and sizes, with the practical advantage that a single 88-word hand-curated calibration prompt matches or beats 128-sample calibration from standard datasets. The open-sourced tinygemm library (already in PyTorch core) makes the technique immediately deployable for low-latency inference. Open questions include whether any4 can be combined with orthogonal preprocessing (GPTQ, AWQ, Hadamard rotations) for further gains, how the calibration prompt affects model bias, and whether the per-row LUT overhead becomes prohibitive at very small group sizes or on hardware without efficient shuffle instructions.
