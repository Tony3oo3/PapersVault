## Problem

Existing hierarchical GNN methods for mesh-based physical simulation rely on manually designed, fixed graph hierarchies that are data-independent and static across time. These predefined structures cannot adapt to the evolving spatial correlations and diverse physical contexts that arise in complex dynamic systems, limiting the model's ability to capture long-range, time-varying node interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250509/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a time-varying mesh graph with node features (velocity, position, density, node type) and edge features (mesh-space and world-space spatial offsets), and predicts one-step temporal changes in physical quantities (velocity change, density change, pressure, or position change depending on the dataset). The mesh topology itself can change over time (as in the FoldingPaper dataset with ARCSim adaptive remeshing), and the model handles this by constructing hierarchies dynamically at each timestep rather than relying on precomputed static structures. All physical quantities are normalized to zero-mean unit variance, and Gaussian noise is added to node inputs for robustness.

## Method

EvoMesh introduces an Anisotropic Message Passing (AMP) layer that assigns learnable, direction-specific importance weights to edge features via a softmax-normalized MLP, replacing the standard isotropic summation aggregation. For hierarchical construction, the node update module outputs a 2-dimensional probability vector per node (discard vs. retain), which is sampled via Gumbel-Softmax with temperature annealing to produce differentiable, context-aware downsampling decisions (DiffSELECT). The same AMP weights are reused for inter-level REDUCE and EXPAND operations, enabling learnable, data-specific feature propagation across hierarchy levels. K-hop edge enhancement (K=2) is applied to maintain connectivity in coarser graphs, and a FeatureMixing step with skip connections refines upsampled features. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with one-step L2 supervision, requiring no precomputed hierarchies.

## Results

EvoMesh achieves the lowest RMSE across all four standard benchmarks (CylinderFlow, Airfoil, FlyingFlag, DeformingPlate) for both one-step (RMSE-1) and long-term rollout (RMSE-All) predictions. The most dramatic gains appear in long-term rollouts: EvoMesh reduces RMSE-All by 61.3% on CylinderFlow (6.571 vs. 16.98 for BSMS-GNN) and by 28.5% on DeformingPlate (1.296 vs. 1.811). On one-step predictions, improvements range from 3.1% (Plate) to 14.8% (Airfoil) over the second-best model. On the time-varying FoldingPaper dataset, EvoMesh reduces RMSE-All by 69.2% over MGN (7.412 vs. 24.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoMesh outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four standard benchmarks. Against the strongest single-level baseline MGN (Pfaff et al., 2021), EvoMesh reduces CylinderFlow RMSE-All from 59.78 to 6.571 (a 9× improvement) and Airfoil RMSE-1 from 77.38 to 41.41. Against the closest hierarchical competitor BSMS-GNN (Cao et al., 2023), EvoMesh achieves 61.3% lower RMSE-All on CylinderFlow and 28.5% lower on DeformingPlate, while also winning on all one-step metrics. HCMT (Yu et al., 2024), which uses attention-based directed propagation but still relies on static Delaunay-triangulated hierarchies, is outperformed by EvoMesh on all eight metrics, with the largest gap on Airfoil RMSE-All (2002 vs. 3238). Eagle (Janny et al., 2023) and Lino et al. (2022), both using precomputed geometric hierarchies, are consistently dominated. On the FoldingPaper dataset with time-varying meshes, EvoMesh reduces RMSE-All by 69.2% over MGN (7.412 vs. 24.08), a setting where the other four baselines cannot even be evaluated due to their reliance on precomputed static hierarchies.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoMesh extends the hierarchical GNN framework for mesh-based physical simulation established by MGN, Lino et al., BSMS-GNN, Eagle, and HCMT, all of which rely on static, precomputed graph hierarchies (via bi-stride pooling, geometric clustering, or Delaunay triangulation) that are fixed across the entire input sequence. The key departure is making the hierarchy itself a learned, time-varying output: DiffSELECT uses Gumbel-Softmax to differentiably select which nodes survive at each coarser level based on current physical context, while anisotropic message passing (AMP) replaces the isotropic summation aggregation with direction-specific, learnable importance weights that are reused for both intra-level updates and inter-level REDUCE/EXPAND operations. The implementation is built directly on the BSMS-GNN codebase, inheriting its K-hop edge enhancement and overall U-Net-style multi-scale pipeline, but replaces the fixed pooling with end-to-end differentiable node selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that spatial correlations in physical systems evolve over time and therefore the graph hierarchy should too—is well-motivated and the visualizations convincingly show learned hierarchies tracking high-intensity regions in the data. The 61.3% long-term RMSE improvement on CylinderFlow and consistent gains across five diverse benchmarks (Eulerian fluids, Lagrangian deformables, time-varying meshes) demonstrate that adaptive hierarchies are not merely a theoretical nicety but yield substantial practical accuracy gains. However, the paper acknowledges that full resolution-free generalization remains open (EvoMesh does not beat MGN on all OOD-resolution settings), and the interpretability of the learned hierarchies is limited. The differentiable graph construction paradigm could be extended to other mesh-based tasks (inverse design, control) and to incorporating physical priors for improved robustness.
