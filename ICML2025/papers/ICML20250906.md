## Problem

Next-token prediction loss is a poor proxy for the complexity of in-context computation a neural sequence model performs: trivially predictable sequences yield low loss, while random noise yields high loss despite requiring no meaningful computation. There is no general, architecture-agnostic metric that distinguishes genuinely interesting in-context reasoning (e.g., inferring an unknown grammar) from memorized retrieval or irreducible randomness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250906/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on any autoregressive sequence model (decoder-only transformer or LSTM) processing token sequences such as text, code, math solutions, or examples from probabilistic finite automata. It produces a per-token PHi loss (the KL divergence between the posterior and the learned autoregressive prior over latent codes) and a sequence-level mean PHi loss that quantifies the description length of the in-context program. The PHi layer can be trained jointly with the model from scratch or inserted post-hoc into a frozen pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama 3B), in which case only the PHi layer weights are optimized.

## Method

The PHi layer is a VAE-inspired information bottleneck sandwiched between the bottom and top layers of an autoregressive model. It comprises a posterior encoder q_ψ(z_t|h_t) that maps each hidden state to a Gaussian latent code, a learned autoregressive prior p_χ(z_t|z_{<t}) implemented as a causal transformer or RNN that predicts the current latent from all past latents, and a small decoder a_ξ that reconstructs the hidden state from the latent. The PHi loss at each timestep is the KL divergence D_KL(q_ψ‖p_χ), measuring the nats of novel information not predictable from history; the total training objective is the sum of standard next-token NLL and this PHi loss, with the reparameterization trick enabling end-to-end gradient flow. The key design choice over standard VAEs is the autoregressive learned prior with access to the full latent history, which turns the bottleneck into a predictor of future hidden states and makes the KL term a direct measure of in-context computation complexity rather than a fixed regularization.

## Results

On in-context language learning with probabilistic finite automata (PFA), PHi loss shows a significant partial correlation with PFA description length C(A) of r=0.37 (95% CI [0.32, 0.43]) after controlling for next-token loss, confirming it tracks in-context computation complexity beyond token-level uncertainty. On the MATH dataset, PHi loss on step-by-step solutions correlates with problem difficulty (Levels 1–5) with a partial correlation of r=0.079 (95% CI [0.07, 0.09]). On GSM-8k, selecting the rationale with higher PHi loss between a correct and an incorrect answer yields a significantly above-chance (>50%) probability of picking the correct one, while selecting by lower next-token loss achieves 71% accuracy—indicating the two metrics capture complementary aspects of solution quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison throughout the paper is between PHi loss and standard next-token prediction (NLL) loss as indicators of in-context computation complexity. Next-token loss fails to distinguish 'interesting' from 'boring' tasks: random sequences and in-context language learning both yield intermediate-to-high NLL, while memorized tasks yield low NLL, providing no meaningful signal about computational complexity. PHi loss, by contrast, is high only for tasks requiring genuine in-context program synthesis (in-context language learning, unknown literature, unknown code) and low for memorized or random tasks. On GSM-8k, next-token loss achieves 71% accuracy when used to select between correct and incorrect rationales, but PHi loss remains significantly predictive even after controlling for NLL (partial correlation), and in the 'counterintuitive' subset (where the lower-NLL answer is wrong), PHi loss still identifies the correct answer above chance. On the MATH dataset, next-token loss is nearly uninformative (~52% accuracy), while PHi loss shows a strong positive correlation with correctness, especially for difficult counterintuitive problems. The paper does not compare against other interpretability metrics (e.g., prequential coding, activation norms), positioning PHi loss as a novel, architecture-agnostic measure rather than a competitor to existing baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

The PHi layer is a direct descendant of Schmidhuber's (1992a) history-compression principle and the predictable-classification framework of Schmidhuber & Prelinger (1993), which first proposed incentivizing internal representations to be predictable from past inputs. The architecture borrows the VAE encoder-decoder structure and reparameterization trick from Kingma & Welling (2014), but replaces the fixed isotropic prior with a learned autoregressive prior over latent codes, turning the KL divergence into a per-step information-gain signal. This positions the work at the intersection of variational inference, information-bottleneck interpretability (Jiang et al., 2020; Bricken et al., 2023), and algorithmic-information-theoretic notions of sophistication and logical depth, which had previously remained uncomputable for neural models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first architecture-agnostic, post-hoc-insertable metric that cleanly separates 'interesting' in-context computation from trivial memorization or irreducible noise—something next-token loss provably cannot do. The finding that PHi loss predicts the correctness of self-generated chain-of-thought rationales (especially on counterintuitive problems) opens a path toward using hidden-state unpredictability as an intrinsic reward signal for open-ended exploration and self-supervised reasoning. Key open questions include the precise relationship to formal sophistication, the handling of copying/redundancy (which inflates PHi loss without adding true interestingness), optimal bottleneck placement in deep networks, and whether the signal persists when models are trained from scratch with the bottleneck integrated at scale.
