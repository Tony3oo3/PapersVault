## Problem

MIMO radar angular resolution is fundamentally limited by the number and geometry of physical antennas, and sparse array configurations that reduce hardware cost introduce missing virtual elements and high sidelobes that degrade DOA estimation. Existing approaches—supervised learning models require large training datasets with poor generalization to unseen environments, while convex matrix completion methods impose separation conditions that limit resolution. The paper addresses the gap of achieving super-resolution angular estimation from a single sparse snapshot without any offline training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of complex-valued antenna measurements (e.g., 8×8 out of a 20×20 virtual array) at known 2D virtual element coordinates, with no offline training data. It outputs a continuous complex-valued response field over the full 2D virtual array domain, parameterized as an MLP mapping 2D coordinates to a 2-vector (real and imaginary parts, rather than amplitude and phase, since phase is modulo 2π and non-differentiable). Downstream, the recovered full array response enables beamforming-based DOA estimation of azimuth and elevation angles for multiple targets.

## Method

NEAR uses a 4-layer MLP with NeRF-style sinusoidal positional encoding (T=10, hidden dim 256, ReLU) as an untrained implicit neural representation, optimized per-scene from sparse measurements. The key innovation is a physics-informed implicit regularizer that exploits the harmonic structure of planar wave propagation: the Block Hankel matrix of the true response has rank K (number of targets), so the last column must be a linear combination of the first K columns. This constraint is formulated as a least-squares term with parameterized coefficient vectors learned jointly with the INR weights, avoiding expensive SVD-based nuclear norm surrogates. The total loss combines data fitting at observed sparse locations with this Hankel-structure regularizer. Theoretically, the paper proves an exact characterization (Theorem 4.1) of the integer harmonic set representable by INRs with sinusoidal positional encoding and polynomial activations, showing the radar array response belongs to this class and that the INR can approximate the desired periodic extension as depth increases.

## Results

NEAR achieves NRMSE of 0.0495 at 20 dB SNR with 8×8 sparse sampling, outperforming the full 20×20 virtual array benchmark (0.0825) and EMaC (0.1889) by 40% and 74% respectively. In real-world localization experiments, NEAR achieves mean absolute error of 0.0718–0.0770 across 1–4 targets, beating the full-array benchmark (0.0827–0.0965) and EMaC (0.1062–0.1170). For angular resolution, NEAR matches the full-array benchmark exactly (5.72°–6.99° across 2–4.5 m range), while EMaC degrades to 8.58°–10.16° and NeRF² to 8.58°–8.89°. NEAR also runs ~2.2× faster than EMaC and NeRF² (551 s vs. 1226 s and 1278 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEAR decisively outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation metric. Against EMaC (structured matrix completion), NEAR reduces NRMSE by 74% at 20 dB (0.0495 vs. 0.1889) and achieves 30–45% better angular resolution (e.g., 5.72° vs. 8.58° at 2 m), with the gap widening at longer ranges where SNR drops. Against SIREN (vanilla INR with periodic activation), NEAR is dramatically superior—SIREN's NRMSE remains near 1.0 across all settings, indicating it fails to learn the response field without physics-informed regularization. Against NeRF² (NeRF-based RF field reconstruction), NEAR achieves 6–7× lower localization error in real-world experiments (0.0718–0.0770 vs. 0.3898–0.5096) and matches the full-array angular resolution benchmark, whereas NeRF² degrades to 8.58°–8.89°. Notably, NEAR also outperforms the noisy full 20×20 virtual array benchmark in both NRMSE and localization accuracy, demonstrating that its physics-informed regularizer provides an inherent denoising effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEAR adapts the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm from NeRF and SIREN to the radar sensing domain, mapping 2D virtual array coordinates to complex-valued antenna responses. Theoretically, it refines the expressivity analyses of Yüce et al. (2022) and Roddenberry et al. (2023) by deriving the exact set of integer harmonics representable by an INR rather than a superset. Methodologically, it departs from prior sparse-array approaches (EMaC's nuclear-norm matrix completion, ADC-SR's supervised extrapolation) and from NeRF²/NeWRF's ray-tracing-based EM field modeling by instead exploiting the harmonic structure of planar wave propagation through a novel Block Hankel least-squares regularizer integrated into the INR loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEAR demonstrates that a single-snapshot, training-free INR with a physics-informed regularizer can match or exceed a full 20×20 virtual array in angular resolution and localization accuracy on a commercial radar, suggesting a path toward super-resolution sensing without hardware upgrades. The denoising effect—where the regularized INR outperforms the noisy full-array benchmark—is a practical advantage for low-SNR scenarios. Open questions include reducing the ~9-minute optimization to real-time inference for embedded platforms, extending beyond the far-field assumption, and whether the Block Hankel regularizer generalizes to near-field or multi-static geometries. The exact expressivity characterization of INRs also provides a reusable theoretical tool for other structured-signal reconstruction problems.
