## Problem

Existing LLM compression benchmarks evaluate only static language modeling (perplexity) and NLU accuracy (GLUE), leaving a critical gap: whether compressed models retain the multi-step planning, tool integration, long-context coherence, and environmental interaction required for real agentic deployment. This paper asks whether post-training compression (quantization and pruning) truly preserves the capabilities that make LLMs functional as agents, rather than merely as text predictors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250543/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 15 open-source LLMs (ranging from Gemma-2B to Qwen2.5-32B, plus DeepSeek-R1-Distilled variants) after applying GPTQ/AWQ/SmoothQuant quantization or SparseGPT/Wanda/Magnitude pruning, and feeds them agentic task prompts, tool/API specifications, long-context documents up to 40K tokens, and interactive environment states (ScienceWorld, Jericho, PDDL). Beyond standard task scores (F1, accuracy, progress rate), the evaluation produces three diagnostic metrics per model: ERank of the logit matrix, Jaccard-based Top-K ranking consistency between compressed and original token rankings, and an energy-score difference ΔE inspired by OOD detection, enabling fine-grained attribution of where compression degrades decision-making.

## Method

ACBench organizes 12 tasks into four agentic capability axes—Action Execution (T-Eval, ToolBench, ToolAlpaca), Workflow Generation (WorfBench with function-call, embodied, problem-solving, and open-grounded subtasks), Long-Context Understanding (LongBench, LongGenBench, Needle-in-the-Haystack at 40K), and Real-World Application (AgentBoard with ScienceWorld, Jericho, PDDL, Tool-Query/Operation)—and systematically evaluates each under matched quantization (W4, W8, W16) and pruning (unstructured, 2:4 semi-structured) configurations. Three novel analytical tools are introduced to diagnose compression effects: ERank captures the effective dimensionality of logit distributions, Top-K Ranking Correlation (via Jaccard similarity of top-k token sets) measures how well the compressed model preserves the original's confidence ordering, and Energy-based Analysis quantifies distributional shifts in logit energies between original and compressed models. Key empirical findings include that 4-bit quantization preserves workflow generation and tool use with only 1–3% degradation but drops real-world application accuracy by 10–15%, that distilled reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) paradoxically underperform their undistilled counterparts on agentic tasks, and that model size modulates compression sensitivity—smaller models (<3B) suffer up to 50% degradation on specialized tasks while 32B models remain robust.

## Results

Across 15 models and 12 agentic tasks, 4-bit quantization (GPTQ/AWQ) preserves workflow generation and tool use with only 1–3% degradation, but degrades real-world application accuracy by 10–15%. AWQ consistently outperforms GPTQ, SmoothQuant, SparseGPT, and Wanda on LongGenBench and AgentBoard tasks, while Wanda with 2:4 semi-structured sparsity is the best pruning alternative. Distilled reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) suffer severe agentic degradation: T-Eval accuracy drops from 70% to 43.6%, WorfBench F1 from 44% to 20%, and PDDL progress from 33% to 1%. Smaller models (<7B) are markedly more compression-sensitive, with Qwen2.5-3B losing up to 50% on embodied tasks under GPTQ/AWQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among quantization methods, AWQ is the clear winner: it maintains baseline-equivalent performance on InternLM2.5-7B for LongGenBench where GPTQ and SmoothQuant degrade, and it is the only method preserving acceptable real-world task performance on AgentBoard alongside Wanda. GPTQ at 4-bit is competitive with AWQ on tool use (T-Eval) and workflow generation (WorfBench), with both showing only 1–3% drops, but GPTQ lags on long-context generation. SmoothQuant (W8A8) appears primarily in the same-bit-budget comparison (Figure 7) and does not clearly surpass AWQ or GPTQ in any agentic capability. Among pruning methods, Wanda with 2:4 semi-structured sparsity outperforms SparseGPT and magnitude pruning across all four agentic categories; SparseGPT and magnitude pruning cause the most severe degradation, particularly on structured output (JSON) generation and long-context retrieval. Overall, quantization (AWQ > GPTQ > SmoothQuant) dominates pruning (Wanda > SparseGPT > Magnitude) for preserving agentic capabilities, with the gap widening on real-world application tasks where pruning methods show marked deterioration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-studied but previously disconnected areas: LLM compression evaluation (LLMC, LLMCBench) and agentic capability benchmarking (T-Eval, WorfBench, AgentBoard). Prior compression benchmarks evaluate only perplexity and NLU accuracy, while agentic benchmarks assume full-precision models; ACBench is the first to systematically evaluate how quantization (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant) and pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda) affect multi-step planning, tool use, long-context retrieval, and real-world task execution. The analytical toolkit (ERank, Top-K Ranking Correlation, Energy) repurposes existing concepts from effective-rank theory and OOD detection to diagnose compression-induced representational shifts in a compression-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACBench provides the first actionable evidence that compression tradeoffs are capability-dependent: 4-bit quantization barely affects workflow generation and tool use (1–3% drop) but degrades real-world application accuracy by 10–15%, meaning a single 'compression is safe' conclusion is insufficient for agent deployment. The finding that DeepSeek-R1-Distilled models consistently underperform their undistilled counterparts across T-Eval, WorfBench, and AgentBoard challenges the assumption that reasoning distillation is universally beneficial and highlights an open problem in preserving agentic skills during distillation. Limitations include post-training-only compression (no QAT), default hyperparameters, and vLLM-compatible methods only, leaving open whether quantization-aware training or tuned group sizes could close the agentic performance gap.
