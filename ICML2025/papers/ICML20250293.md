## Problem

For size-constrained non-monotone submodular maximization in parallel settings, the state-of-the-art 1/e approximation ratio was achieved only by a continuous algorithm (Ene & Nguyen, 2020) that queries a continuous oracle, while the best purely combinatorial parallel algorithm achieved only a ~1/4 ratio. This paper closes that gap by presenting the first combinatorial parallel algorithm matching the 1/e bound with sublinear adaptivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250293/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a non-monotone submodular function oracle f: 2^U → R≥0 (accessed only through point evaluations), a cardinality constraint k, and an error parameter ε. They output a subset S with |S| ≤ k that is an approximate maximizer of f(S). Performance is measured in two parallel-computation metrics: query complexity (total oracle calls) and adaptivity (number of sequential rounds, where queries within a round are fully parallelized). The algorithms are evaluated on graph-based submodular objectives (Maximum Cut, Revenue Maximization) over social network datasets.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a 'blended marginal gains' analysis strategy that eliminates the probabilistic guessing step in INTERPOLATEDGREEDY, improving its success probability from (ℓ+1)^(-ℓ) to 1 by tracking combined marginal gains across interlaced solutions rather than branching on where the optimal element appears. Building on this, the paper introduces a unified parallel subroutine PIG that combines threshold sampling with interlaced solution construction: DISTRIBUTE creates pairwise disjoint candidate pools, PREFIX-SELECTION identifies blocks of high-marginal-gain elements, and a three-pass selection mechanism ensures uniform block sizes across all ℓ parallel solutions while preserving the alternating-addition property. A single PIG call yields a (1/4−ε) approximation with high probability (≥1−1/n); repeated PIG calls (PITG) boost the ratio to an expected (1/e−ε). Both algorithms achieve O(log(n)log(k)) adaptivity and O(n log(n) log(k)) query complexity, matching the continuous oracle's approximation ratio using only combinatorial set-function queries.

## Results

On the er dataset (n=99,997) for maxcut, PIG and PITG achieve the highest normalized objective values among all compared algorithms, followed by ATG, FASTRANDOMGREEDY, and PARSSP. On twitch-gamers (n=168,114) for revmax, PIG and PITG demonstrate greater robustness, particularly for larger values of k. In terms of query efficiency, PIG achieves second-best performance across both datasets with O(n log(n) log(k)) queries, while adaptive rounds for PIG and PITG are O(log(n) log(k)), slightly worse than ANM and AST which achieve O(log(n)). Theoretically, PITG matches the state-of-the-art 1/e−ε approximation ratio of the continuous Ene & Nguyen algorithm using a purely combinatorial approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIG and PITG outperform ATG, FASTRANDOMGREEDY, and PARSSP on objective value for both maxcut (er) and revmax (twitch-gamers), with PIG and PITG achieving the highest normalized values on er. On query efficiency, PIG ranks second-best (after ATG and AST at O(n log(k))), while PARSSP has the highest query complexity at O(n log²(n) log(k)). In adaptive rounds, ANM and AST achieve the best O(log(n)) adaptivity, outperforming PIG/PITG's O(log(n) log(k)); however, PIG and PITG still significantly outperform FASTRANDOMGREEDY (k rounds) and PARSSP (O(log²(n)) rounds). PITG's 1/e−ε ratio matches the continuous Ene & Nguyen algorithm but with a combinatorial oracle and O(n log(n) log(k)) queries versus O(k²n log²(n)) continuous oracle queries. PARSSP achieves a similar 0.25−ε ratio to PIG but at the cost of higher query complexity and worse adaptivity, while ANM achieves the best adaptivity at O(log(n)) but with a much weaker 0.039−ε ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the interlaced greedy family: INTERLACEGREEDY (Kuhnle, 2019) for 1/4-approximation and INTERPOLATEDGREEDY (Chen & Kuhnle, 2023) for 1/e-approximation, both of which required a probabilistic guessing step that limited success probability to (ℓ+1)^(-ℓ). The key departure is a novel 'blended marginal gains' analysis that eliminates this guessing step entirely, enabling a single solution family to achieve the same guarantees. On the parallel side, the paper positions itself against Ene & Nguyen (2020), which achieved 1/e with sublinear adaptivity but required a continuous oracle; here, the first purely combinatorial parallel algorithm matches that bound, closing a gap that prior combinatorial parallel methods (PARSSP, ATG, AST) left at ratios of 0.25 or below.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant for the submodular optimization community because it removes the last theoretical barrier between continuous and combinatorial parallel algorithms for non-monotone submodular maximization, providing a practical, implementable path to 1/e approximation with O(log(n)log(k)) adaptivity. The blended marginal gains technique is a reusable analytical tool that may extend to other interlaced or threshold-sampling frameworks. Limitations include the O(log(n)log(k)) adaptivity (vs. O(log(n)) for some prior methods like ANM and AST), the 1/e guarantee for PITG being in expectation rather than high probability, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like ATG being modest. Open questions include whether the adaptivity can be tightened to O(log(n)) while preserving the 1/e ratio, and whether the blended analysis extends to other constraint structures (knapsack, matroid).
