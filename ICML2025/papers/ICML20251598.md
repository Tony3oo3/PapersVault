## Problem

Linear Q-learning with function approximation has long been believed to diverge, and while Meyn (2024) recently proved almost sure boundedness under an ε-softmax policy with adaptive temperature, no nonasymptotic L² convergence rate was known. This paper fills that gap by establishing the first L² convergence rate for unmodified linear Q-learning—without target networks, weight projection, regularization, Bellman completeness, or near-optimality assumptions on the behavior policy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes online MDP transitions (S_t, A_t, R_{t+1}, S_{t+1}) generated by an ε-softmax behavior policy whose temperature κ_w = κ₀/||w_t||₂ adapts to the current weight norm, along with a fixed feature map x: S×A → R^d. It produces a weight vector w_t whose expected squared norm E[||w_t||₂²] converges to a bounded set at a polynomial or sub-exponential rate depending on the learning rate schedule. For the tabular variant, the output is a Q-value table q_t converging to the optimal q* in L²(∞-norm) with an additional logarithmic bias term.

## Method

The core contribution is a general stochastic approximation theorem (Theorem 3) for single-timescale settings where the Markovian noise transition matrix P_{w_t} evolves as fast as the weights themselves, without any projection operator. The key technical device is a stronger Lipschitz condition on P_w with a 1/(1+||w₁||+||w₂||) multiplier, which—combined with an auxiliary Markov chain construction and uniform geometric mixing—allows bounding the time-inhomogeneous noise at O(α_t · α_{t-τ,t-1} · ln t) without the two-timescale separation used in prior work. For linear Q-learning, the ε-softmax policy with adaptive temperature ensures the expected update h(w) = A(w)w + b(w) satisfies a dissipativity inequality ⟨w, h(w)⟩ ≤ −β||w||₂² + C||w||₂, yielding the L² convergence rate. For tabular Q-learning, the paper identifies a novel pseudo-contraction property of the weighted Bellman optimality operator T' = D_{μ_q}(Tq − q) + q, which drives convergence to q* via a Moreau-enveloped Lyapunov function.

## Results

The paper establishes the first L² convergence rate for unmodified linear Q-learning: E[‖w_t‖²₂] ≤ B_{l,1}/(t+t₀)^{B_{l,2}α} · ‖w₀‖²₂ + B_{l,3} (for ε_α=1), showing convergence to a bounded set without any algorithmic modifications (no target network, no projection, no regularization, no experience replay). For tabular Q-learning, the rate is E[‖q_t − q_*‖²_∞] ≤ B_{2,1}/(t+t₀)^{B_{2,2}α} · ‖q₀ − q_*‖²_∞ + B_{2,3}ln(t+t₀)/(t+t₀)^{min(1,B_{2,2}α)}, converging to the optimal Q-function. Empirically, on Baird's counterexample (7 states, 2 actions, γ=0.99, α=0.1, κ₀=100, ε=0.1), the squared L² norm of weights remains stably bounded over 1500 iterations across 10 runs, confirming the theoretical boundedness guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are framed in terms of which algorithmic modifications and assumptions are required. Compared to Meyn (2024), which establishes almost sure boundedness of linear Q-learning iterates under the same ε-softmax policy with adaptive temperature, this paper provides the stronger L² convergence rate with explicit dependence on t. Compared to Zhang et al. (2022), which handles time-inhomogeneous Markovian noise only in a two-timescale framework (where the transition matrix evolves much slower than the weights), this paper achieves the result in a single-timescale setting where the transition matrix evolves at the same rate as the weights (θ_t ≡ w_t). Compared to Zou et al. (2019) and Zhang et al. (2023a) on linear SARSA, which rely on a projection operator to ensure boundedness, this paper handles the unprojected case using a stronger Lipschitz condition (Assumption A3) with the 1/(1+‖w₁‖+‖w₂‖) factor. For tabular Q-learning (Theorem 2), this is the first L² convergence rate without count-based learning rates, using an ε-softmax policy rather than a fixed policy as required by Chen et al. (2021), Li et al. (2024), and Zhang & Xie (2024). The key technical novelty enabling these improvements is the identification of a pseudo-contraction property of the weighted Bellman optimality operator T′ = D_{μ_q}(Tq − q) + q, which is the first such property identified for this highly nonlinear operator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Meyn (2024), which established almost sure boundedness of unmodified linear Q-learning under an ε-softmax behavior policy with adaptive temperature, by providing the first L² convergence rate to a bounded set. The key technical engine is a general stochastic approximation theorem (Theorem 3) for time-inhomogeneous Markovian noise where the transition function evolves at the same timescale as the weights, generalizing the two-timescale framework of Zhang et al. (2022) and the auxiliary-chain construction of Zou et al. (2019). For tabular Q-learning, the paper introduces a novel pseudo-contraction property of the weighted Bellman optimality operator, combined with the Moreau envelope technique from Chen et al. (2021), to handle the non-smooth infinity norm. Unlike all prior analyses of linear Q-learning, the paper requires no algorithmic modifications (no target network, projection, regularization, or experience replay) and no strong assumptions (no Bellman completeness, no near-optimality of the behavior policy).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first nonasymptotic L² convergence guarantee for the vanilla linear Q-learning algorithm, resolving a long-standing theoretical gap: practitioners have long worried about divergence (the 'deadly triad'), and while Meyn (2024) showed almost sure boundedness, the rate at which the iterates approach a bounded set was unknown. The single-timescale stochastic approximation result is a reusable tool that could apply to other off-policy RL algorithms with self-referential behavior policies. Open questions include extending to almost sure convergence rates, high-probability concentration, L^p rates, and whether the ε-softmax adaptive temperature requirement can be relaxed to the more standard ε-greedy policy (which is discontinuous and breaks the Lipschitz arguments). The practical impact is somewhat limited since the required ε-softmax adaptive temperature policy is not what practitioners typically deploy.
