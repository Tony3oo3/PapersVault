## Problem

Most state-of-the-art DiD methods produce only average or group-average causal effects, leaving heterogeneous treatment effects in panel data largely unexplored. While CATE estimation under conditional exogeneity is well-studied, the CATT in DiD settings is fundamentally different when conditioning on a subset X of covariates W: prior CATE meta-learners (T-Learner, DR-learner) yield biased estimates because the parallel trends assumption does not restrict trends under treatment. No doubly robust meta-learner existed for the CATT in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a balanced panel with n units over T periods (T=2 in the main analysis, extended to staggered multi-period settings), observing outcomes Y_it, binary treatment indicators D_it, and time-invariant covariates W. The key structural detail is that the CATT is estimated conditioning on a user-specified subset X ⊂ W, while the nuisance outcome regression g₀(W) is learned on the full W using only control-group samples (D=0). The output is the CATT function θ(X) = E[Y₁(1)−Y₁(0) | D=1, X], estimated via a convex loss whose minimizer is the best L₂-projection of the true CATT over the treated population. The framework also produces CLATE estimates in the IV-DID extension and general conditional functionals under covariate shift.

## Method

The core contribution is a doubly robust meta-learner that exploits the asymmetry of the CATT to avoid estimating the treated-group outcome regression, which is infeasible when treated units are scarce. Under conditional parallel trends, the CATT is identified via E[ΔY − g₀(W) | D=1, X], and the authors construct a Neyman-orthogonal conditional moment involving the propensity π₀(W) and the control-group outcome regression g₀(W) = E[ΔY | D=0, W]. This moment is shown to be equivalent to minimizing a convex incomplete squared loss L(θ; π, g) = E[D·θ(X)² − 2·Ŷ·θ(X)], where Ŷ is a doubly robust pseudo-outcome, making the problem solvable by any standard convex optimizer (XGBoost, neural nets, linear models). The resulting estimator achieves fast rates when the product of nuisance estimation errors decays sufficiently, and the framework is generalized to a unifying treatment of conditional functionals under covariate shift via conditional Riesz representers, with further extensions to instrumented DiD with two-sided non-compliance and staggered multi-period rollouts.

## Results

On fully synthetic data (Table 1), the proposed XGBoost DR-learner achieves the lowest MSE of 0.04±0.01, roughly halving the error of the XGBoost OR baseline (0.09±0.02). On the more challenging imbalanced dataset (Table 2), XGBoost(DR) achieves MSE 0.12±0.03, outperforming both the CATE DR-learner (0.15±0.04) and the CATE OR-learner (0.27±0.08). On semi-synthetic Minimum Wage data (Table 3), XGBoost(DR) achieves MSE 1.8±0.09 versus 2.66±0.07 for the CATE DR-learner and 2.69±0.07 for the CATE OR-learner, a roughly 30–33% reduction in error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its CATT DR-learner against two families of baselines: the CATE DR-learner (Kennedy, 2023) and the CATE OR-learner / T-Learner (Kunzel et al., 2019). The proposed CATT DR-learner consistently outperforms both. Against the CATE DR-learner, XGBoost(DR) achieves MSE 0.12±0.03 versus 0.15±0.04 on the imbalanced synthetic data (Table 2) and 1.8±0.09 versus 2.66±0.07 on the semi-synthetic Minimum Wage data (Table 3), a 20–33% improvement. Against the CATE OR-learner, the gap is even larger: 0.12±0.03 versus 0.27±0.08 (Table 2) and 1.8±0.09 versus 2.69±0.07 (Table 3), roughly a 2.3× reduction in MSE. The advantage is most pronounced under covariate imbalance (Table 2), where the CATT asymmetry avoids estimating the treated-group outcome regression, and on the Minimum Wage data where the CATE baselines suffer from distributional mismatch between treated and control counties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two lines of work: the DiD doubly robust estimation literature (Sant'Anna & Zhao, 2020; Callaway & Sant'Anna, 2021) and the Neyman-orthogonal meta-learner framework for CATE (Kennedy, 2023; Nie & Wager, 2021; Foster & Syrgkanis, 2023). The key departure is identifying that when heterogeneity is projected onto a subset X ⊂ W of covariates, the CATT estimation problem is structurally distinct from CATE estimation—standard CATE DR-learners produce biased estimates in this setting because the parallel trends assumption does not restrict trends under treatment. The paper further generalizes its main result to a unifying framework for conditional functionals under covariate shift, extending Chernozhukov et al. (2023) from unconditional to conditional functionals, and to an instrumented DiD setting building on Syrgkanis et al. (2019) and Miyaji (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a concrete gap: no doubly robust estimator existed for heterogeneous CATT in DiD, and the paper shows that naively applying CATE methods yields biased results when conditioning on a covariate subset. The minimum wage case study demonstrates practical value by uncovering that county population significantly moderates the treatment effect (large negative for small counties, near zero for large counties), a pattern not highlighted in prior DiD analyses. Open questions include the validation methodology (calibration-based, which is heuristic), the behavior under parallel trends violations (only briefly addressed in appendices), and whether the general conditional functionals framework can handle concept drift or time-varying covariate shift. The staggered rollout extension is stated but not fully developed with rate guarantees.
