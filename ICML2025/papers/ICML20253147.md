## Problem

Existing DM-based CSGM-type solvers such as DMPlug suffer from two critical limitations: (1) they must retain the full computational graph across all N sampling steps, causing memory usage to grow linearly with N and quickly exceeding GPU capacity, and (2) they are susceptible to suboptimal convergence because the single-shot optimization over the initial latent vector can get trapped at local minima. This paper addresses both issues simultaneously while also expanding the effective range of the diffusion model to capture signals outside its learned distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy observation y ∈ R^m and a forward operator A (a linear measurement matrix for tasks like 4× downsampling or 70% random masking, or a convolution kernel for deblurring) as input, and outputs a reconstructed 256×256×3 RGB image. All experiments use a pretrained unconditional DDIM diffusion model (2.75 GB) with additive Gaussian noise at σ=0.01. The method is entirely training-free: no task-specific fine-tuning is required, and the same pretrained DM is applied across all linear and nonlinear inverse problem settings.

## Method

DMILO decomposes the DM's N-step sampling process into individual step functions g_i and performs Intermediate Layer Optimization: at each outer iteration, it optimizes the input to each g_i sequentially (from the final step backward), requiring gradient information for only one step at a time and thus reducing memory from O(N) to O(1) per step. Sparse deviation vectors ν are introduced at every intermediate layer with an ℓ₁ penalty, expanding the generator's effective range to capture underlying signals that lie outside the DM's learned distribution. DMILO-PGD further wraps this in a Projected Gradient Descent loop: a naive gradient descent step on the measurement loss ‖y − A(x)‖² is alternated with a DMILO-based projection step that minimizes ‖A(G(x)) − A(x̂)‖² (rather than the conventional ‖G(x) − x̂‖²), leveraging the forward operator to guide the projection. Theorem 4.4 provides a theoretical guarantee under S-REC that this measurement-guided projection recovers the true optimum up to a constant factor when m = Ω(k₂ log(L₁n/δ) + k² log(3n)).

## Results

On inpainting (CelebA, 70% random mask), DMILO-PGD achieves LPIPS 0.023, PSNR 36.42 dB, and SSIM 0.952, outperforming DMPlug (0.066, 35.51, 0.935) and all other baselines. On 4× super-resolution (CelebA), DMILO-PGD reaches LPIPS 0.056 and PSNR 33.58 dB, surpassing the best competitor by 0.047 LPIPS and 1.10 dB PSNR. On motion deblurring (CelebA), DMILO achieves FID 31.08, LPIPS 0.044, PSNR 34.15 dB, and SSIM 0.908, improving over DMPlug by 47.49 FID, 0.120 LPIPS, 3.90 dB PSNR, and 0.084 SSIM. Memory usage remains constant at ~10.53 GB regardless of sampling steps, whereas DMPlug exceeds available memory beyond 3 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMILO-PGD dominates on inpainting, beating DMPlug by 0.043 LPIPS and 0.91 dB PSNR on CelebA, and outperforming DAPS (the second-best) by 0.028 LPIPS. On super-resolution (CelebA), DMILO-PGD achieves the best LPIPS (0.056) and PSNR (33.58 dB), surpassing ΠGDM and RED-diff which lead on other metrics. DMILO (without PGD) delivers the strongest results on motion deblurring, achieving FID 31.08 on CelebA versus 78.57 for DMPlug and 78.03 for DiffPIR, with PSNR gains of 3–3.5 dB over all baselines across CelebA, FFHQ, and ImageNet. On nonlinear deblurring, both DMILO and DMILO-PGD outperform DMPlug by 2.4–2.7 dB PSNR and 0.02–0.05 LPIPS. However, DMILO-PGD underperforms notably on blind image deblurring (BID), where the naive gradient update is ill-suited for kernel estimation; DMILO alone remains the best method, beating DMPlug by 1.62 dB PSNR (Gaussian kernel, CelebA) and 0.92 dB (motion kernel, CelebA). Overall, DMILO consistently wins or ties on deblurring and BID tasks, while DMILO-PGD excels on inpainting and super-resolution where the PGD projection step provides the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DMPlug (Wang et al., 2024), which treats the entire diffusion sampling process as a single black-box generator and optimizes the initial latent vector end-to-end, creating a computational graph that grows linearly with the number of sampling steps. The key conceptual move is to exploit the natural composition structure of DDIM sampling (G = g₁ ∘ g₂ ∘ … ∘ g_N) and apply Intermediate Layer Optimization (ILO, originally from Daras et al., 2021 for GANs) at each per-step function, reducing memory from O(N) to O(1) per step. Sparse deviations (Dhar et al., 2018) and Projected Gradient Descent (Shah & Hegde, 2018) are then integrated to expand the effective range of the generator and mitigate suboptimal convergence, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DMILO maintains a constant ~10.5 GB memory footprint regardless of sampling steps (whereas DMPlug exceeds available memory at just 4 steps) and runs ~6× faster (150s vs. 925s), making CSGM-type diffusion solvers feasible at higher sampling budgets. The S-REC-based theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.4) provides the first analysis justifying measurement-guided projection in the diffusion setting. A notable limitation is that DMILO-PGD's naive gradient update fails on blind deblurring (kernel estimation), suggesting the PGD component needs task-specific adaptation. Open questions include better initialization strategies for last-timestep-only optimization and extending the theoretical analysis beyond the two-function composition assumption.
