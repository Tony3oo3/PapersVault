## Problem

Existing federated learning label noise correction methods (FedCorr, FedNoRo, FedELC) all rely on the assumption that at least some clients have entirely clean labels, and many further assume specific noise distributions. This assumption breaks down in realistic settings such as federated crowdsourcing or when all clients are compromised, causing these methods to fail catastrophically when the noisy-client ratio reaches 100%. FedClean addresses this gap by correcting label noise without requiring any clean clients or assuming a particular noise distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251092/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client contributes a local dataset of RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Clothing1M at variable resolution) paired with potentially noisy annotation labels. The method internally augments each sample with an inferred label from a locally trained CNLL model, so the effective per-sample representation becomes (image, annotation label, inferred label). The final output is a globally aggregated classification model (ResNet-18/34/50) whose training has been guided by corrected labels, along with the corrected label assignments for each client's samples.

## Method

FedClean operates in two stages. In the preprocessing stage, each client runs a local CNLL (Co-teaching or Joint Optim) to generate inferred labels, then selects clean samples where annotation and inferred labels agree; the global model is trained on these clean samples using ASSA, which weights each client's contribution by its clean-sample count rather than total dataset size. In the label correction stage, a two-sub-stage scheme corrects remaining noisy labels: Sub-stage I uses a collaborative per-sample loss (annotation loss minus inferred-label loss) computed only where the global model's prediction matches the inferred label, partitions samples via GMM, and relabels the top-confidence disputed samples with inferred labels; Sub-stage II then uses the improved global model's predictions to correct the remaining high-loss samples, gated by a confidence threshold. Mixup is applied throughout to smooth label noise, and the entire pipeline never distinguishes 'clean' from 'noisy' clients, preserving privacy while remaining robust even when all clients are noisy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with all clients noisy (ρ=1, τ=0.5), FedClean² achieves 83.75% (IID) and 80.55% (non-IID) accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best method FedFixer (62.87% IID, 59.01% non-IID) and FedCorr (47.49% IID, 39.40% non-IID). On the real-world Clothing1M dataset (non-IID, all clients inherently noisy), FedClean² reaches 72.39% accuracy, the highest among all nine compared methods. When no clients are noisy (ρ=0), all methods perform comparably (~91% on CIFAR-10 IID), confirming that FedClean's advantage is specifically in high-noise regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedClean decisively outperforms all baselines in the critical all-clients-noisy setting (ρ=1): FedClean² achieves 83.75% on CIFAR-10 IID vs. FedFixer's 62.87% (+20.9 pp), FedCorr's 47.49% (+36.3 pp), and FedAvg's 38.36% (+45.4 pp). In the moderate-noise setting (ρ=0.5), FedCorr leads (91.12% IID) while FedClean² trails slightly (88.41%), but the gap narrows to ~2.7 pp on non-IID. On CIFAR-100 (ρ=1), FedClean² reaches 66.54% vs. FedCorr's 40.92% and FedAvg's 31.34%. On the real-world Clothing1M dataset, FedClean² (72.39%) beats FedFixer (70.52%), FedELC (69.24%), and FedAvg (68.63%). The ablation confirms CNLL is the most critical component: removing it drops accuracy from 83.75% to 47.74% (ρ=1, IID), while removing either correction sub-stage costs 1.2–3.9 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedClean builds on the standard FedAvg federated training loop and integrates two representative centralized noisy label learning (CNLL) methods—Co-teaching and Joint Optim—as local preprocessing components, while drawing on zkCor for a privacy-preserving aggregation extension. It departs from prior FL label-noise methods (FedCorr, FedNoRo, FedELC, FedFixer) by eliminating the assumption that any clean clients exist, instead relying on per-sample inferred labels and a two-stage correction scheme (inferred-label-dominated, then global-model-dominated) to identify and fix noisy labels across all clients. The novel collaborative per-sample loss and adaptive sample size-weighted aggregation (ASSA) are new contributions that distinguish FedClean from both the CNLL literature it borrows from and the FL noise-correction literature it extends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the clean-client assumption addresses a realistic gap: in federated crowdsourcing, compromised-device, or adversarial settings, no client may be fully clean, yet prior methods degrade sharply under this condition. FedClean's demonstrated robustness at ρ=1 (all clients noisy) on both synthetic and real-world (Clothing1M) data makes it applicable to scenarios where existing methods simply fail. Limitations include reliance on relatively basic CNLL methods (Co-teaching, Joint Optim) whose local training cost may be prohibitive for resource-constrained clients, and the GMM-based sample partitioning introduces hyperparameters (σ₁, σ₂, ε) that require tuning. Open questions include whether more sophisticated CNLL backends (e.g., DivideMix, SELFIE) would further improve correction quality, and how the method scales to very large client populations or highly non-IID class distributions.
