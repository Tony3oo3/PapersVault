## Problem

Prior stochastic bandit literature has studied algorithms leveraging either absolute (reward) or relative (dueling) feedback in isolation, yet in practical settings like LLM training and recommendation systems, both feedback types are available simultaneously in each decision round. No prior work addresses how to fuse these two heterogeneous feedback types to reduce cumulative regret, nor how the relative cost weighting between them should influence the fusion strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252599/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In each round, the learner simultaneously selects one arm for reward feedback (observing a Bernoulli sample) and one arm pair for dueling feedback (observing a binary winner), with both observations arriving every round. The regret objective is a weighted combination R_T = α·R_T^(R) + (1−α)·R_T^(D), where α ∈ [0,1] encodes the relative cost of each feedback type. The output is a selection policy (arm + arm pair per round) with a provable regret bound that, under the assumption the optimal arm is the most effective dueling competitor, matches the information-theoretic lower bound up to a constant.

## Method

The paper proposes two fusion algorithms. ELIMFUSION maintains a single shared candidate arm set where arms are eliminated by whichever feedback type (reward UCB or dueling probability) first triggers the elimination criterion, yielding a regret bound with a suboptimal K factor inherited from dueling elimination. DECOFUSION instead decomposes suboptimal arms into two sets—those better explored via reward feedback and those via dueling feedback—by conservatively constructing these sets from empirical log-likelihood information measures rather than unknown gaps. To resolve the mismatch between the constructed and ground-truth decompositions, DECOFUSION employs a randomized decision-making strategy with threshold α²/(α²+(1−α)²) that assigns one feedback type to exploration and the other to exploitation in each round, explicitly separating the two roles. This design achieves a regret bound O(Σ_k log T / max{Δ_k^(R)/α, Δ_k^(D)/(1−α)}) matching the simplified lower bound, and yields T-independent constant regret when α=0 or α=1 (free exploration property).

## Results

In the main experimental setting (K=16 arms, α=0.5), DECOFUSION achieves an aggregated regret approximately 41× lower than ELIMFUSION and 47.5% lower than MEDNOFUSION. ELIMFUSION itself outperforms the no-fusion elimination baseline (ELIMNOFUSION) by 81.3%. A key qualitative result is that DECOFUSION achieves a T-independent constant regret when α=0 or α=1 (free exploration property), while ELIMFUSION always incurs O(log T) regret regardless of α.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two no-fusion baselines: ELIMNOFUSION (separate elimination for reward and dueling) and MEDNOFUSION (optimal MED/RMED algorithms applied independently to each feedback type). DECOFUSION outperforms both baselines across all tested settings: it beats MEDNOFUSION by 47.5% in aggregated regret and ELIMNOFUSION by a wider margin. ELIMFUSION also beats ELIMNOFUSION by 81.3%, confirming that even simple fusion via a shared candidate set is beneficial. However, ELIMFUSION is outperformed by DECOFUSION by a factor of ~41×, due to ELIMFUSION's suboptimal multiplicative K factor inherited from dueling elimination. Both fusion algorithms also outperform the no-fusion baselines on the decomposed reward-based and dueling-based regret components separately (Figure 3), demonstrating that fusion reduces regret costs on both feedback dimensions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the DR-MAB problem, where both absolute (reward) and relative (dueling) feedback are available simultaneously in each round — a setting the authors explicitly note has no prior literature. Methodologically, ELIMFUSION builds on the elimination frameworks of Auer & Ortner (2010) for stochastic bandits and Saha & Gaillard (2022) for dueling bandits, fusing them via a shared candidate arm set. DECOFUSION is more deeply rooted in the information-theoretic MED framework of Honda & Takemura (2010) and the RMED1 algorithm of Komiyama et al. (2015), but must overcome a novel 'decomposition deadlock' where the standard empirical log-likelihood conditions become inapplicable under the decomposed exploration policy the lower bound suggests. The paper departs from all prior bandit work by treating the two feedback types as complementary rather than alternative, and by introducing a weighted regret objective that captures their relative costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fusion framework is directly motivated by practical settings like LLM training (where annotators provide both absolute scores and pairwise comparisons) and recommendation systems, and the paper speculates on parallelizing SFT and RLHF/DPO steps. A notable limitation is that DECOFUSION's near-optimal bound requires the assumption that the optimal arm is the most effective dueling competitor for all suboptimal arms; without this, the upper bound is worse than the general lower bound. The experimental validation is limited to a single K=16 setting, leaving open questions about scalability and robustness. The connection to LLM training remains speculative and would require significant adaptation beyond the bandit setting.
