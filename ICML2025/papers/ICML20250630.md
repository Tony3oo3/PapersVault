## Problem

It is unclear what signals in data diffusion models exploit to learn compositional rules, how many training examples are required, and in what order different levels of composition are acquired. Prior work on sample complexity of diffusion models either assumes low-dimensional manifolds or does not address hierarchical compositional structure, leaving open whether and how generative models can beat the curse of dimensionality on data with tree-like latent organization.

## Input / Output

For the synthetic Random Hierarchy Model, the input is a noisy one-hot-encoded token sequence of length d = s^L (each token drawn from a vocabulary of size v), and the output is a clean token sequence satisfying the grammar's production rules at all L hierarchical levels. For natural data, the language model (MD4 with a 12-layer transformer, ~165M params) consumes masked token sequences from OpenWebText and generates text, while the vision model (U-Net with multi-head attention, ~120M params) takes noisy 64×64 RGB images from ImageNet and produces clean images. In all settings the network predicts the conditional expectation E[x(0)|x(t)], which parameterizes the reverse diffusion step.

## Method

The paper unifies symbolic (PCFG) and statistical (word2vec-style) viewpoints by showing that diffusion models learn the Random Hierarchy Model's production rules through iterative hierarchical clustering: at each level ℓ, the model detects statistical correlations between a visible token and tuples of latents at level ℓ−2, enabling reconstruction of level-ℓ−1 latents and collapsing synonymic s-tuples into shared representations. The key theoretical result is a sample complexity P_ℓ ∝ m^(ℓ+1) for learning rules at level ℓ, which is polynomial (not exponential) in the data dimension d = s^L, since m^(L+1) = m·d^(log m / log s). This is validated by showing that a simple k-means clustering algorithm on empirical token-tuple correlations recovers the same latents with matching sample complexity, and that one step of gradient descent on a linear model aligns weights with these correlations. Empirically, the authors train D3PM U-Nets on the RHM, an MD4 transformer on OpenWebText, and a DDPM U-Net on ImageNet 64×64, confirming that generated data exhibit progressively longer coherence lengths (measured via token-token correlations, perplexity conditioned on context length, and MMD at increasing ResNet depths) as training time or dataset size grows.

## Results

The paper derives that the sample complexity for diffusion models to learn compositional rules at hierarchy level ℓ scales as P_ℓ ∼ m^(ℓ+1), which is polynomial (not exponential) in the data dimension d = s^L. Empirically, on the Random Hierarchy Model with L=2, the measured sample complexity P* matches the predicted m^3 scaling, and the U-Net's sample complexity closely tracks that of a simple k-means clustering algorithm on token-context correlations. On OpenWebText (MD4, ~165M params), generated text shows no correlations beyond sampling noise up to ~7×10^7 training tokens, then progressively longer-range token correlations emerge; at ~10^9 tokens, words are locally coherent but sentences are not. On ImageNet 64×64 (DDPM, ~120M params), MMD measured at early ResNet-18 layers converges first, with deeper (more abstract) layers converging sequentially as training steps increase, confirming hierarchical learning in natural images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing generative models; instead, it validates a theoretical sample-complexity prediction against both the trained diffusion model and a simple correlation-based clustering algorithm. The key comparison (Figure 2, L=2) shows that the U-Net diffusion model's sample complexity P* for learning level-ℓ rules closely matches that of k-means clustering on empirical token-tuple correlations, with the diffusion model's prefactor falling between the single-token and full-tuple clustering variants. This suggests the diffusion model effectively performs hierarchical word2vec-style clustering internally. The theoretical prediction P_ℓ ∼ m^(ℓ+1) is confirmed across depths (L=2 in the main text, L=3 in Appendix D.2) and across both offline and online training settings. On natural data, the same staged-learning phenomenology (local coherence before global coherence) is observed in both text (MD4 on OpenWebText) and images (DDPM on ImageNet), supporting the generality of the mechanism beyond the synthetic RHM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical learning theory of Cagnetta & Wyart (2024) and Cagnetta et al. (2024) from next-token prediction on PCFGs to diffusion models that generate complete sequences, using the Random Hierarchy Model as a tractable testbed. The iterative synonym-clustering mechanism from Malach & Shalev-Shwartz (2018) is identified as the underlying learning algorithm, analogous to word2vec, and the sample complexity analysis is carried out for the diffusion score function rather than a supervised loss. The paper departs from prior diffusion sample-complexity work (which typically assumes i.i.d. or low-dimensional data) by showing that compositional structure changes the scaling from exponential to polynomial in the data dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that diffusion models learn compositional rules with polynomial (m^{L+1}) rather than exponential sample complexity provides a principled explanation for why these models can handle high-dimensional structured data, and the prediction that intermediate training yields locally coherent but globally incoherent outputs is confirmed on both OpenWebText and ImageNet. The connection to the renormalization group suggests a broader framework for understanding hierarchical coarse-graining in generative models, though the RHM's uniform random rules and tree structure are significant simplifications relative to natural data. Open questions include whether the polynomial scaling holds for non-tree compositional structures, for more complex grammars with shared substructures, and whether the hierarchical clustering mechanism is truly the bottleneck or merely a sufficient description of what gradient descent does.
