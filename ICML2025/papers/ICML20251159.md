## Problem

In offline goal-conditioned RL, cross-trajectory state-goal pairs are essential for goal-stitching tasks, but directly sampling them causes value overestimation on unconnected pairs (where no valid transition sequence exists in the dataset). This overestimation propagates to neighboring connected states through TD bootstrapping, misleading the policy toward suboptimal actions. The paper identifies and addresses this gap by distinguishing connected from unconnected cross-trajectory pairs and providing principled value estimation for each.

## Input / Output

The value function takes a state vector and a goal vector (both drawn from the offline dataset) and outputs a scalar value; the policy maps (state, goal) to an action. Goals are sampled from a mixture distribution p_m^α(g|s) = α·p_traj(g|s) + (1−α)·p_rand^D(g), where α controls the balance between in-trajectory (HER-style) and random dataset goals. In the hierarchical variant, the high-level policy outputs a sub-goal state s_{t+k} given (s_t, g), and the low-level policy outputs an action given (s_t, s_{t+k}), enabling multi-step planning over long horizons.

## Method

CGCIVL introduces a penalty term η·E[V(s,g)] over uniformly sampled goals into the expectile-regression value loss, provably underestimating unconnected state-goal pairs while preserving accuracy on in-trajectory pairs (Propositions 4.2–4.3). To avoid underestimating connected cross-trajectory pairs, a dual-network quasimetric distillation is used: an unconstrained network θ_v performs standard TD updates, while a quasimetric-constrained network θ_d (implemented via Interval Quasimetric Embeddings) distills from θ_v with the penalty term, leveraging the triangle inequality to bound values of connected pairs through intermediate states (Proposition 4.4). A supervised alignment term pulls θ_v toward θ_d's conservative estimates on random goals, decoupling value iteration from quasimetric constraints. A hierarchical policy framework (high-level sub-goal selection + low-level action) addresses long-horizon signal-to-noise challenges, and theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1, Proposition 4.5) guarantees a clear value separation between connected and unconnected pairs.

## Results

On OGBench, CGCIVL achieves the highest average success rate across most environments: 86.33% on PointMaze-navigate, 89.33% on PointMaze-stitch, 86.33% on AntMaze-navigate, 68.67% on AntMaze-stitch, 59.33% on HumanoidMaze-navigate, 52.00% on HumanoidMaze-stitch, 33.75% on Cube, and 77% on Scene. The most dramatic gains appear in goal-stitching tasks, where CGCIVL reaches 98% on PointMaze-large-stitch (vs. 84% for QRL and 13% for HIQL) and 81% on PointMaze-giant-stitch (vs. 50% for QRL and 0% for all other baselines). On manipulation tasks, CGCIVL leads on Cube-single (84% vs. 68% for GCIQL) and Scene (77% vs. 51% for GCIQL), though GCIQL retains an advantage on Puzzle-3x3 (95% vs. 13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGCIVL outperforms all five baselines on the majority of tasks, with the largest margins in goal-stitching scenarios. Against GCIVL (Park et al., 2023), CGCIVL improves average PointMaze-stitch success from 27.33% to 89.33% and AntMaze-stitch from 20.67% to 68.67%, confirming that the conservative penalty and quasimetric distillation directly address GCIVL's overestimation on unconnected pairs. Against HIQL (Park et al., 2023), the strongest baseline on locomotion tasks, CGCIVL leads on AntMaze-stitch (68.67% vs. 62.00%) and HumanoidMaze-stitch (52.00% vs. 40.67%), while matching HIQL on AntMaze-navigate-medium (95% vs. 96%). Against QRL (Wang et al., 2023), which is competitive on PointMaze-navigate (78.67% avg), CGCIVL pulls ahead to 86.33% and dominates on PointMaze-stitch (89.33% vs. 71.33%). GCIQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021) is the only baseline that beats CGCIVL on a specific task (Puzzle-3x3: 95% vs. 13%), but CGCIVL surpasses it on Cube-single (84% vs. 68%) and Scene (77% vs. 51%). GCBC (Lynch et al., 2020) and CRL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) are consistently the weakest baselines, with CRL scoring 0% on all giant-stitch tasks and GCBC averaging below 17% across all locomotion environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GCIVL (Park et al., 2023), an offline goal-conditioned variant of IQL, by identifying and addressing a specific failure mode: value overestimation on unconnected cross-trajectory state-goal pairs that arises when extending HER-style sampling to include cross-trajectory goals. The conservative penalty term draws on the CQL lineage of penalizing out-of-distribution values, while the quasimetric constraint leverages the triangle-inequality structure of optimal goal-conditioned value functions established by Pitis et al. (2020) and Wang & Isola (2022a). The key architectural departure is the dual-network design that decouples unconstrained TD learning from quasimetric-constrained distillation, avoiding the known instability of directly enforcing triangle-inequality constraints during value iteration (Wang et al., 2020, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical characterization (Theorem 3.3) of how overestimation on unconnected pairs propagates through TD bootstrapping to corrupt values of connected pairs, explaining why naive cross-trajectory sampling actively degrades rather than merely fails to improve performance. The practical dual-network quasimetric distillation technique is a reusable component for any goal-conditioned value learning setting. However, performance on combinatorial tasks (Puzzle: 17.25% average) remains weak, and the method requires careful per-task tuning of the cross-trajectory sampling ratio α and penalty coefficient η. Open questions include whether the conservative penalty can be made adaptive rather than fixed, and whether the approach scales to continuous goal spaces beyond the discrete state-as-goal formulation used here.
