## Problem

Vision tasks on Implicit Neural Representation (INR) data face a fundamental trade-off: raster-based methods lose the structural information encoded in the INR during conversion to pixel/voxel form, while purely INR-based methods are confined to simplistic architectures and basic operations like interpolation and filtering. No existing approach effectively combines the rich structural features of INR derivatives with the sophistication of modern raster-based vision networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252856/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an INR (typically SIREN-based) representing an image, 3D volume, or video as input, along with a pre-existing raster-based vision network for the target task. The INR is first evaluated at all grid coordinates to produce a raster tensor, while simultaneously a high-order derivative map (1st through Pth order partial derivatives at every point, normalized per-order) is computed. The output is the task result (super-resolved image, denoised image, segmentation mask, deblurred video, or optical flow field) produced by the augmented network, with no architectural restrictions on the pre-existing network.

## Method

DVI introduces a progressive INR feature extraction and fusion pipeline that augments any pre-existing raster-based vision network. It computes a high-order derivative map of the INR using a recursive polynomial expansion (HOPE) for speed, then feeds it through K sequential INR feature extractors (each comprising Swin Transformer layers and convolutions with residual connections) to produce K levels of structural features. These features are fused into K corresponding intermediate layers of the pre-existing network via Swin Transformer Cross-attention layers operating at three window sizes (2, 4, 8) for multi-scale fusion. Crucially, each level's fused feature is fed back as an additional input to the next feature extractor, creating a progressive alignment between INR structural information and the target network's learned representations at multiple depths. The entire system is trained end-to-end with the pre-existing network's original training configuration.

## Results

DVI consistently outperforms both raster-based and INR-based baselines across all five vision tasks. On image super-resolution, DVI(SwinIR) achieves 31.96/31.19/27.53/29.18 PSNR on Set5/BSD100/Urban100/Manga109, surpassing SwinIR (30.00/27.25/25.66/27.02) by up to 3.9 dB. On 3D volume segmentation, DVI(VNET) reaches 83.60 mean DSC versus VNET's 72.62 (+11.0). On video deblurring, DVI/VD-Diff attains 0.9006 SSIM vs. VD-Diff's 0.8691, and on optical flow estimation, DVI/VD-Diff reduces EPE from 4.94 to 3.92 on Sintel (all). The INR-only baseline INSP lags significantly on all tasks except 3D segmentation, where it scores 45.97 DSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVI(SwinIR) outperforms SwinIR by 1.96 dB on Set5 and 3.94 dB on BSD100 for super-resolution, and by 1.42 dB on Kodak24 for denoising. DVI(EDSR) improves over EDSR by 0.84–1.22 dB across SR test sets, while DVI(StableSR) gains 1.49–1.79 dB over StableSR. For denoising, DVI(DnCNN) surpasses DnCNN by 2.84 dB on Kodak24, and DVI(DiffBIR) edges out DiffBIR by 1.19 dB on Kodak24. In 3D segmentation, DVI(VNET) achieves 83.60 mean DSC vs. VNET's 72.62 (+11.0), DVI(UNet3D) reaches 80.49 vs. UNet3D's 68.46 (+12.0), while DVI(MedSegDiff-V2) ties MedSegDiff-V2 on mean (75.79) but gains substantially on individual organs (e.g., Pancreas: 89.58 vs. 60.67). For video deblurring, DVI/VD-Diff matches VD-Diff on PSNR (28.23) but improves SSIM from 0.8691 to 0.9006, and DVI/VDTR beats VDTR by 1.17 dB PSNR. On optical flow, DVI/VD-Diff reduces EPE from 4.94 to 3.92 (all) and from 4.17 to 3.31 (matched) versus VD-Diff. The INR-only method INSP is substantially outperformed on all tasks (e.g., 19.37 vs. 31.96 PSNR on Set5 SR), confirming that purely INR-based approaches remain limited for complex pixel-wise vision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVI bridges two previously separate lines of work: purely INR-based vision methods (e.g., INSP, which applies signal processing directly to INR derivatives) and raster-based vision networks (e.g., SwinIR, EDSR, VNET) that lose structural information when converting INR back to pixel/voxel form. It draws on INSP's insight that high-order INR derivatives encode task-relevant structural features, and on HOPE's recursive formula for efficient derivative computation, but departs from both by fusing these derivative features into pre-existing raster-based architectures via a progressive Swin Transformer cross-attention strategy rather than operating solely in the INR domain. This positions DVI as a hybrid framework that preserves the expressiveness of established vision networks while recovering the structural information that rasterization discards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DVI provides a general, plug-and-play mechanism for enhancing any differentiable raster-based vision network when the input is available as an INR, demonstrated consistently across five tasks and three modalities. The finding that INR derivatives (but not numerical derivatives of the raw signal) carry the useful structural information highlights a fundamental property of the fitting process that could inform future INR-based pipelines. Open questions remain around optimal derivative orders per task, computational overhead at inference, and whether the approach extends to non-differentiable or discrete INR formulations. The method's reliance on converting INR to raster form also means it does not fully realize the memory and bandwidth savings that motivated INR adoption in the first place.
