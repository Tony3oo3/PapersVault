## Problem

Multiphysics PDE simulation requires integrating multiple specialized solvers into coupled programs, introducing significant development overhead and computational cost, while multi-component simulation of large structures (e.g., 64 fuel elements) faces convergence difficulties as degrees of freedom grow. Existing ML surrogate models still depend on coupled or full-structure training data, which necessitates prior development of the very coupled numerical programs they aim to replace. The paper asks whether models trained on decoupled (single-process) or small-structure data can generate accurate coupled or large-structure solutions without ever seeing such data during training.

## Input / Output

For multiphysics, each conditional diffusion model takes the current noisy field plus the estimated solutions of all other physical processes (e.g., neutron flux, solid temperature, fluid velocity/pressure/temperature) as conditioning, and outputs the denoised field; the full system produces all coupled fields simultaneously. For multi-component, a single model takes the solutions of a component's three neighbors plus its heat flux and boundary-condition type as input, and outputs temperature and strain at 804 mesh points per component. Training data is generated from decoupled single-process solves (using Gaussian random fields or pre-iteration for assumed fields) or from a 16-component medium structure, while test data comes from fully coupled solves or a 64-component large structure. Network backbones are FNO/U-Net on regular meshes and Geo-FNO/Transolver on finite-element meshes, with 250 diffusion steps (reducible to 25–50 via DDIM).

## Method

M2PDE reframes multiphysics and multi-component PDE simulation as compositional generative probabilistic modeling. For multiphysics, it derives that the gradient of the joint energy with respect to any field equals the gradient of the conditional energy (∇_{z_i} E(z) = ∇_{z_i} E(z_i|z_{≠i})), so separate diffusion models trained on decoupled data (each process solved with others held fixed) can be composed at inference: an EM-like loop iteratively denoises each field conditioned on current estimates of the others, with an external loop (K=2–3) and a linearly increasing weight λ blending current and previous estimates. For multi-component, it exploits the local Markov property of a Markov random field over the component graph, so a single conditional diffusion model trained on a 16-component structure generalizes to 64 components by iteratively updating all components in parallel at each diffusion step. The denoising network ε_θ implicitly learns the energy gradient via the standard DDPM MSE objective, and DDIM sampling provides up to 29× (multiphysics) and 41× (multi-component) speedup over the full 250-step schedule.

## Results

On the nuclear thermal coupling task (Table 2), M2PDE+U-Net achieves relative L2 errors of 1.38×10⁻² (neutron), 2.75×10⁻² (solid), and 3.86×10⁻² (fluid) on coupled test data, while surrogate+U-Net fails with errors of 4.45×10⁻², 18.2×10⁻², and 8.03×10⁻² respectively. On the 64-component prismatic fuel element task (Table 3), M2PDE+Transolver achieves T=0.793×10⁻² and ε=2.07×10⁻², outperforming surrogate+Transolver (T=1.25, ε=3.31) and MeshGraphNet (T=1.28, ε=2.40), while surrogate+Geo-FNO diverges entirely. M2PDE also achieves up to 29× and 41× speedup over numerical programs in experiments 2 and 3 respectively via DDIM-accelerated sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against surrogate models (iterative composition of per-field/per-component predictors), M2PDE wins decisively in the challenging coupled and large-structure settings: on nuclear thermal coupling, surrogate+U-Net produces non-physical solid and fluid predictions (errors 18.2× and 8.03×10⁻²) while M2PDE+U-Net stays at 2.75× and 3.86×10⁻²; on the 64-component prismatic fuel element, surrogate+Geo-FNO diverges entirely while M2PDE+Transolver achieves the best T error (0.793 vs. 1.25 for surrogate+Transolver). Against graph-based methods, M2PDE+Transolver outperforms MeshGraphNet on the 64-component task (T: 0.793 vs. 1.28; ε: 2.07 vs. 2.40) and dramatically outperforms GIN (T: 0.793 vs. 4.63) and SAN (which completely fails with errors of 100 and 11800×10⁻²). On single-component and 16-component predictions, surrogate models retain a slight edge (e.g., surrogate+Transolver T=0.0764 vs. M2PDE+Transolver T=0.144 on single component), but M2PDE's advantage grows with structural complexity and coupling strength, confirming its design motivation of generalizing from small/decoupled training to large/coupled inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

M2PDE extends the compositional generative framework of energy-based diffusion models (Du et al., 2023) from single-object multi-factor settings (e.g., generating one image satisfying multiple constraints) to multi-object interaction settings where multiple physical processes or structural components must be jointly sampled. It builds on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) for the underlying generative mechanism and DDIM (Song et al., 2021) for accelerated inference, but departs from prior multiphysics ML work that trains surrogate models on coupled data by instead training conditional models on decoupled data and composing them via the energy gradient identity (Eq. 6). For multi-component simulation, it leverages the local Markov property of Markov random fields to condition each component on its neighbors, distinguishing it from domain-decomposition surrogates (CoAE-MLSim) and graph-based approaches (GIN, SAN, MeshGraphNet) that learn global or local graph relationships without a generative probabilistic formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: developing coupled numerical solvers for multiphysics and large multi-component structures is a major engineering bottleneck, and M2PDE shows that decoupled (cheaper to generate) training data can yield coupled predictions that outperform iterative surrogate composition for strongly coupled problems. However, a ~1-order-of-magnitude accuracy gap remains versus models trained directly on coupled data, and all experiments use simplified geometries and linearized couplings, leaving open whether the approach scales to fully nonlinear, 3D, multi-scale real-world problems. The open-sourced benchmark datasets for multiphysics and multi-component PDE simulation fill a gap in the scientific ML literature, and the 29–41× speedup over numerical programs for complex cases suggests meaningful practical value if the accuracy gap can be closed.
