## Problem

Existing image-to-audio and text-to-audio generation methods produce holistic soundscapes that fail to isolate the distinct sounds of specific objects in complex scenes, either forgetting subtle events or conflating co-occurring sources. There is no mechanism for users to interactively select which visual objects should produce sound, making fine-grained control over generated audio impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB image and a user-provided segmentation mask (obtained via SAM point clicks or text prompts) as input, producing a 10-second, 16 kHz audio waveform. The image is encoded into CLIP ViT-B/32 patch embeddings, and the SAM mask is rescaled to match the mean and variance of the trained attention weights before being injected into the diffusion U-Net. Notably, no text prompt is required at inference—the mask alone drives object-specific sound generation, and selecting multiple objects blends their sounds into a coherent scene.

## Method

The model fine-tunes a pretrained AudioLDM latent diffusion backbone by inserting a scaled dot-product attention layer that fuses CLAP text embeddings (as queries) with CLIP image patch embeddings (as keys and values), augmented with learnable positional encodings for spatial grounding. This attention is trained via self-supervision on 748 hours of audio-visual matched AudioSet clips, learning to assign high weights to image patches whose sounds match the text description. The key insight, formalized in Theorem 3.1, is that the InfoNCE contrastive loss underlying CLAP/CLIP pretraining is mathematically equivalent to the MLE of the softmax attention weights, so the learned attention functionally approximates a segmentation mask—enabling direct substitution of SAM masks at test time without retraining. The theoretical error bound decomposes into SAM segmentation error, contrastive loss gap, and model approximation error, all of which are minimized by the respective pre-trained components. Classifier-free guidance (λ=2.0) and DDIM sampling (200 steps) are used for generation, with a VAE decoder and HiFi-GAN vocoder reconstructing the final waveform.

## Results

On AudioCaps, the proposed method achieves the highest Sound Event Accuracy (ACC) of 0.859, the lowest KL divergence (0.517), and the highest Inception Score (2.102) among all baselines. It also attains the best subjective scores across all four criteria (OVL 3.31, RET 3.62, REI 3.48, REO 3.74), with the largest margin on Relevance to Selected Object (REO), confirming superior object-level sound alignment. On the VGG-Sound Sync dataset, the method extends its lead with ACC 0.761 and FAD 1.112, outperforming the next-best Diff-Foley (ACC 0.607, FAD 1.834).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is SSV2A (Guo et al., 2024), which also uses object-level cues via external bounding boxes; the proposed method surpasses it on ACC (0.859 vs. 0.806), KL (0.517 vs. 0.525), and all four subjective scores, while SSV2A edges ahead by a negligible margin on FAD (1.265 vs. 1.271) and AVC (0.893 vs. 0.891). Against the strongest video-to-audio baselines (FoleyCrafter, Diff-Foley, Seeing&Hearing), the method leads by 0.12–0.18 in ACC and by 0.4–0.6 in FAD, indicating substantially better sound-object alignment. Text-based models (AudioLDM1/2, Make-an-Audio) and the cascade Captioning approach lag significantly, with ACC values of 0.31–0.59 and FAD above 2.7, reflecting their inability to disentangle multi-source scenes. The two-stage Retrieve&Separate baseline performs worst overall (ACC 0.276, FAD 4.051), struggling with retrieval in multi-source settings. On the cross-dataset VGG-Sound Sync evaluation, the method maintains its lead (ACC 0.761 vs. Diff-Foley's 0.607), and on the single-object ImageHear benchmark it still achieves the highest clip-score (47.37) and ACC (0.88), confirming generalizability beyond multi-object scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on AudioLDM's conditional latent diffusion framework for audio generation and introduces object-centric learning (Greff et al., 2019) into the audio-visual pipeline via multi-modal dot-product attention between CLIP image patches and CLAP text embeddings. The key conceptual departure from prior image-to-audio work (Im2Wav, Diff-Foley, FoleyCrafter) is the substitution of learned attention weights with SAM segmentation masks at test time, grounded in a theoretical equivalence between InfoNCE contrastive loss and softmax attention. The closest concurrent work, SSV2A, also targets multi-source audio generation but relies on external bounding-box detectors rather than interactive user-selected masks, and lacks the theoretical justification for the attention-to-mask substitution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical path toward interactive, object-level audio generation from static images, which is directly useful for content creation (filmmaking, game design) where users need fine-grained control over which objects produce which sounds. The theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) formalizing when attention weights can be replaced by segmentation masks offers a general principle for test-time intervention in contrastively-pretrained models. Key limitations remain: static images cannot capture non-stationary or impact sounds, similar objects (e.g., a car producing siren vs. engine noise) introduce ambiguity, and the method's reliance on SAM quality means segmentation errors propagate directly into audio errors. Open questions include extending to dynamic video inputs, handling occluded or partially visible objects, and whether the attention-mask equivalence generalizes beyond the CLIP/CLAP embedding space.
