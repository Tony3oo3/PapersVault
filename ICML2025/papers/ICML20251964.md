## Problem

Discrete generative modeling via diffusion-based methods lacks a principled, theoretically grounded framework: existing approaches rely on complex forward kernels, unstable ratio-based estimators for backward transitions, and strong assumptions on score functions or marginal densities, yielding limited convergence guarantees and poor scalability in high dimensions. No prior method provides sharp, non-asymptotic error bounds that grow only linearly (rather than exponentially) with dimension under minimal data assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a random binary vector drawn from the uniform distribution over {0,1}^d as its initial state and produces a binary vector sampled from the learned data distribution after running the backward CTMC for a fixed time horizon T_f. The forward noising process is a homogeneous CTMC where each of the d bits flips independently via a Poisson clock with rate λ, so the transition kernel factorizes across dimensions. Experiments cover low-dimensional Bernoulli data (d = 4–16) and binarized MNIST at 32×32 resolution (d = 1024).

## Method

DMPM defines the forward noising process as a homogeneous CTMC on the hypercube {0,1}^d with a symmetric rate matrix (each bit flips at rate λ), whose time-reversal is also a tractable CTMC whose backward rates are governed by a discrete score function s_t(x). The key theoretical insight is that s_t is a conditional expectation of an explicit function of the forward process, reducing score estimation to a stable L² regression problem rather than the high-variance density-ratio estimation used in prior work. The score is further reparameterized through a discrete denoiser d_t^ℓ(x) = P(X_0^ℓ ≠ x^ℓ | X_{T_f−t} = x), moving the learning target from a space of ratios into probability space for improved training stability. Training minimizes a weighted combination of an L² denoising loss, an entropic loss, and a cross-entropy loss (optionally scaled by 1/w_t to balance timesteps). The paper proves KL-divergence convergence bounds under only two minimal assumptions (a bounded entropic approximation error and full support of the data distribution), with sampling error growing linearly in d rather than exponentially, and introduces flip-schedule and denoise-renoise samplers for efficient generation.

## Results

On binarized MNIST (d=1024), DMPM with the denoise-renoise sampler achieves a best FID of 2.89 and F1_dc of 1.00 at K=200 reverse steps, outperforming MD4 (FID 4.48, F1_dc 0.83) and DFM (FID 16.26, F1_dc 0.41) at the same step count. With only 25 network calls, DMPM (flip-schedule) already reaches FID 9.98 and F1_dc 0.92, while DFM requires 500 steps to reach FID 7.34. On the low-dimensional sawtooth dataset (d=4–16), DMPM achieves SWD values of 2.3–3.3 (×10⁻³), consistently halving the error of both MD4 and DFM across all dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMPM is compared against two state-of-the-art discrete generative methods: MD4 (masked diffusion, Shi et al. 2024) and DFM (discrete flow matching, Gat et al. 2024). On binarized MNIST, DMPM (denoise-renoise) decisively outperforms both baselines at K=200, achieving FID 2.89 vs. MD4's 4.48 and DFM's 16.26, while also reaching a perfect F1_dc of 1.00 (vs. 0.83 and 0.41). The efficiency advantage is striking: DMPM with the flip-schedule reaches FID < 10 at just 25 network calls, whereas DFM needs 500 steps to reach FID 7.34. On the sawtooth dataset, DMPM wins across all four dimensions (d=4, 8, 12, 16), with SWD roughly 2–3× lower than the best baseline at each dimension. MD4 is the stronger of the two baselines overall, particularly at higher step counts, but DMPM still leads at every evaluated K value on MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMPM extends the score-based generative modeling framework of Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) and the discrete masked diffusion of Austin et al. (2021) by specializing the forward noising process to a simple independent bit-flip CTMC on the hypercube, then deriving the backward process via the time-reversal theorem of Conforti & Léonard (2022). Unlike Campbell et al. (2022), which handles generic CTMCs and learns unstable density ratios, and Ren et al. (2024), which imposes L∞ bounds on the score, DMPM exploits the factorized structure of the bit-flip kernel to obtain a score function with a clean conditional-expectation form and an L² regression objective. The discrete denoiser reparameterization (analogous to Tweedie's formula in continuous diffusion) departs from Lou et al. (2024), who parameterize the score directly as a density ratio without the denoiser decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that DMPM achieves competitive FID on binarized MNIST with roughly 2.5× fewer network calls than Discrete Flow Matching, while providing the first convergence guarantees for a discrete score-based model that scale linearly (rather than exponentially) with dimension under minimal assumptions. The entropic Girsanov-type condition (Assumption 2.1) replacing the L² score-approximation condition of continuous SGMs is a genuinely new theoretical ingredient for jump processes. Open questions include extending the framework to non-binary categorical data, time-dependent rates, and higher-dimensional discrete structures (e.g., protein sequences, language tokens), where the current hypercube-specific factorization no longer applies directly.
