## Problem

Existing sequential recommendation systems either rely solely on item IDs or combine multimodal features (images, text, price) without respecting the temporal alignment of modalities within a user's browsing sequence. Early fusion dilutes modality-specific representations, while late fusion delays cross-modal interaction until after independent processing, missing the opportunity to fuse features of the same product at the same time step. This paper addresses how to efficiently integrate diverse modalities while preserving both their distinct contributions and their chronological consistency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user browsing history sequence (max length 20, zero-padded) where each item is represented by four modalities: a learnable product ID embedding, a 512-dim style embedding derived from VGG-19 Gram matrices, a 4096-dim Llama 3.1 text embedding plus five LLM-generated prompt embeddings (gated and projected), and a SCANE-normalized price embedding. The output is a multi-label next-item ranking: the model computes cosine similarities between a concatenated CLOZE-token representation and candidate items (ground truth purchases plus 100 sampled negatives), treating each session's final purchase order as a set of correct answers.

## Method

MTSTRec employs a mid-fusion architecture with two stages. First, modality-specific self-attention encoders (2-layer Transformer blocks) independently process each of the five feature streams. Second, the Time-aligned Shared Token (TST) module introduces exactly one shared token per time step; at each fusion layer, modality-specific tokens attend to their corresponding shared token via self-attention, and the shared token is updated by averaging the attention outputs across all five modalities at that time step. This enforces a strong prior that embeddings from different modalities at the same position belong to the same product, enabling precise cross-modal information exchange without the dilution of early fusion or the delayed interaction of late fusion. The final prediction concatenates the CLOZE tokens from all five modalities and is trained with binary cross-entropy on cosine similarities against positive and negative samples.

## Results

MTSTRec achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three e-commerce datasets. On Food E-commerce, it reaches NDCG@5 of 0.8800 and MRR@5 of 0.8086; on House-Hold E-commerce, NDCG@5 of 0.8942 and MRR@5 of 0.8568; and on H&M (Trousers), NDCG@5 of 0.2307 and MRR@5 of 0.1871. The largest relative gains appear on H&M (Trousers), where MTSTRec improves NDCG@5 by approximately 31.5%–43.7% over competing models, demonstrating the advantage of time-aligned cross-modal fusion in complex, high-cardinality product spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTSTRec outperforms all baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the ID-only sequential models, SASRec (NDCG@5: 0.8015/0.7563/0.1520) and BERT4Rec (0.8076/0.7596/0.1468 on Food/House-Hold/H&M), MTSTRec gains 7.2–9.7 percentage points on NDCG@5 for the proprietary datasets and 8.4–8.5 points on H&M. Against the multimodal late-fusion baseline MMMLP (NDCG@5: 0.8276/0.8335/0.1754), MTSTRec improves by 5.2–5.9 points on the proprietary datasets and 5.5 points on H&M. The enhanced early-fusion variants SASRec+ and BERT4Rec+ (which concatenate image and text features upfront) narrow the gap but still trail MTSTRec by 2.9–3.4 points on NDCG@5 for Food and 5.4–9.7 points for House-Hold, confirming that the TST mid-fusion strategy captures cross-modal interactions more effectively than simple feature concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTSTRec extends the Transformer-based sequential recommendation lineage (SASRec, BERT4Rec) by introducing a mid-fusion strategy for multimodal inputs, departing from both early fusion (concatenating all features before encoding, as in SASRec+/BERT4Rec+) and late fusion (processing modalities independently and concatenating at the end, as in MMMLP). The core architectural novelty—the Time-aligned Shared Token (TST) module—draws conceptual inspiration from the attention bottleneck mechanism (Nagrani et al., 2021) but re-purposes it to enforce per-time-step alignment across modalities rather than global sequence-level fusion. The feature extraction pipeline repurposes established components: VGG-19 Gram matrices for style (from Neural Style Transfer), Llama 3.1 for text embeddings and prompt-based enrichment (following LLM-Rec), and SCANE for price encoding, while the CLOZE prediction token is inherited directly from BERT4Rec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, interpretable mid-fusion mechanism (one shared token per time step) that consistently outperforms early and late fusion across three e-commerce datasets, and it releases two proprietary datasets (Food and House-Hold E-commerce) that are valuable for the multimodal sequential recommendation community. However, the evidence base is limited: two of three datasets are proprietary with small product catalogs (770 and 2,464 items), and the H&M (Trousers) results show very low absolute performance (NDCG@5 ≈ 0.23), raising questions about generalizability to larger, more diverse catalogs. Open questions include whether the TST mechanism scales to more than five modalities, whether the 1:1 shared-token ratio remains optimal in longer sequences, and whether the finding that price contributes negligibly holds in price-sensitive domains. The model's longer training time (83 min vs. 33–66 min for baselines) and higher inference time (12.43 s) are practical concerns for deployment.
