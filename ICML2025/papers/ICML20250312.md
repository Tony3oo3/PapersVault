## Problem

Urban region embeddings learned independently for different cities occupy incomparable latent spaces, preventing direct reuse of downstream predictors (e.g., GDP or population models) trained in a label-rich city for a label-scarce one. Existing two-stage translation methods suffer from frozen, rigid embeddings that cannot adapt to non-linear manifold structures, and they rely on hand-crafted cross-city correspondence rules (e.g., rank-based or hierarchical matching) that are incomplete and biased. The paper asks whether region embedding learning and cross-city latent space alignment can be unified into a single, label-free, rule-free optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes one month of taxi trip records (origin–destination region pairs) and road-network-derived region boundary polygons for each of two cities, which may have different numbers of regions (e.g., 1306 vs. 1056). Trips are aggregated into a normalized directed mobility graph per city. The output is a pair of aligned 128-dimensional region embedding matrices in a shared latent space; a ridge-regression predictor trained on source-city embeddings and socioeconomic labels (GDP, population, carbon emissions) is then applied directly to target-city embeddings without any target labels or explicit region-to-region mapping.

## Method

CoRE is a one-stage framework that jointly optimizes three losses. First, a two-layer GAT encoder maps each city's mobility graph to region embeddings, trained with a self-supervised destination-prediction loss (intra-city RE loss). Second, a latent manifold alignment (LMA) module places N_a randomly generated virtual parallel anchors in both spaces, computes first-order relative representations of each anchor against all region embeddings (cosine similarity), then computes second-order relative representations of those vectors against the anchor set itself; the L2 distance between the two resulting N_a×N_a matrices is minimized, aligning global manifold geometry without any cross-city pairing. Third, a latent individual alignment (LIA) module uses shared query/key projections in a cross-attention mechanism to produce cross-city pairwise correlation matrices, then enforces cyclical consistency so that Cor^{X→X} ≈ Attn^{X→Y}·Attn^{Y→X} (and symmetrically), preserving fine-grained inter-region relationships. All three losses are summed with hyperparameters α and β and optimized end-to-end with Adam, allowing the GAT embeddings to adapt to alignment pressure rather than being frozen as in prior two-stage pipelines.

## Results

On the XA→CD transfer, CoRE achieves MAE of 178.93 (GDP), 620.15 (Population), and 127.45 (Carbon), outperforming the second-best baselines by 2.59%, 3.43%, and 4.76% respectively. On the reverse CD→XA transfer, CoRE achieves MAE of 170.25 (GDP), 580.98 (Population), and 136.56 (Carbon), with improvements of 6.72%, 5.43%, and 3.19% over the next-best methods. Across all six task-direction combinations, CoRE consistently attains the lowest MAE and MAPE, with the largest gains (up to 10.15%) observed on the Carbon MAPE metric for CD→XA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRE outperforms all 11 baselines across every task and city-pair direction. Among the six unsupervised translation methods (RP, RA, HSP, HSA, HBP, HBA from Yabe et al. 2020), HSA and HBA are the strongest, with HSA achieving 183.68 MAE on XA→CD GDP versus CoRE's 178.93, and HBA reaching 142.91 on CD→XA Carbon versus CoRE's 136.56. The domain adaptation baselines (MMD, Adv, CrossTReS) perform notably worse, with MMD scoring 205.48 MAE on XA→CD GDP and Adv at 204.92, indicating that distribution-level alignment alone is insufficient for capturing inter-region relational structure. CARPG, the knowledge-transfer baseline, is competitive on Carbon (133.82 MAE on XA→CD) but trails CoRE (127.45) on all other tasks. The Individual baseline (no cross-city alignment) is the worst performer by a large margin (e.g., 475.84 vs. 178.93 MAE on XA→CD GDP), confirming that latent space alignment is essential for predictor reuse. Paired t-tests confirm CoRE's improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the urban region embedding literature (MVURE, HDGE, ZE-Mob) by integrating cross-city latent space alignment directly into the embedding learning process, departing from the two-stage paradigm of City2City (Yabe et al., 2020) where frozen embeddings are translated via hand-crafted correspondences. The core technical novelty borrows the relative representations framework from Moschella et al. (ICLR 2023) for zero-shot latent space communication, repurposing it to align urban region manifolds through virtual parallel anchors rather than explicit region-to-region mappings. A cross-attention cyclical consistency mechanism (analogous to back-translation in NMT) is added for individual-level alignment, complementing the global manifold alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for hand-crafted inter-city correspondences and enabling one-stage joint learning, CoRE makes cross-city urban analytics more practical for cities lacking ground truth labels—a common real-world constraint. However, the evaluation is limited to three Chinese cities with a single data modality (taxi mobility) and three downstream tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to cities with very different urban structures, other sensory data (POIs, satellite imagery), or larger-scale multi-city settings. The modest performance gains (2–10% over the best baseline) suggest the alignment problem may be partially addressed by simpler methods, and the field would benefit from testing on more diverse urban typologies.
