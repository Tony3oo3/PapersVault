## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods (Medusa, Hydra, Eagle, Eagle-2) treat all positions in a draft sequence as equally important, employing uniform head architectures and a single generation paradigm (either fully serial or fully parallel). This paper identifies and formally proves that early tokens in the draft sequence are disproportionately critical—since the first mismatch discards all subsequent tokens—yet no prior method explicitly allocates more computational resources to those positions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251443/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The draft model receives the concatenation of the target LLM's final-layer hidden state h_t and the current token embedding e(y_t), both of dimension d, projected via a fully connected layer to a single d-dimensional vector. It outputs a 7-token draft sequence: the first two tokens are generated autoregressively by the serial Transformer, and the remaining five are produced simultaneously by parallel MLP heads. These drafts are then verified by the target LLM via rejection sampling, yielding a variable-length accepted token sequence per draft round.

## Method

Gumiho combines a two-layer Transformer (serial, autoregressive) for the first two draft tokens with five lightweight two-layer MLPs (parallel) for the remaining five, motivated by a formal theorem proving that shifting acceptance probability mass toward earlier positions increases mean accepted tokens τ under a fixed total budget. The serial Transformer reuses a single model with shared KV cache across its two autoregressive steps, while the parallel MLPs all share the concatenated output of the serial heads as input. A Full Tree Attention (FTA) mechanism extends Eagle-2's dynamic tree by supplementing shorter candidate paths with tokens from longer paths at corresponding positions, increasing acceptance length at zero additional compute since those tokens' Q/K/V are already computed. Training uses a weighted combination of regression and classification losses on ShareGPT data, with top-k=10 tokens passed between serial heads and s=35 tokens extracted per MLP head for tree construction.

## Results

Across seven target LLMs (Vicuna-7B/13B, LLaMA2-7B/13B/70B, LLaMA3-8B/70B) and six benchmarks, Gumiho achieves mean accepted tokens (τ) of 4.89 on Vicuna-7B and 4.54 on LLaMA2-70B at temperature 0, with corresponding speedup ratios of 2.97× and 2.76×. Gumiho surpasses the prior SOTA (Eagle-2) by 4.5%–15.8% in speedup, with the largest gains on 70B models (11.7% on LLaMA2-70B, 15.8% on LLaMA3-70B) due to the parallel MLP heads reducing draft time on high-dimensional hidden states. Draft time is consistently shorter than Eagle-2 across all datasets and temperatures, confirming the efficiency benefit of the hybrid serial-parallel design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gumiho consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model size, dataset, and temperature setting. Against Medusa (parallel MLP heads only), Gumiho achieves roughly 1.5× higher speedup (e.g., 2.97× vs. 1.89× on Vicuna-7B) and ~2× higher τ (4.89 vs. 2.39), demonstrating the benefit of the serial Transformer for early tokens. Against Hydra (serial MLP heads), Gumiho improves speedup by ~27% (2.97× vs. 2.33× on Vicuna-7B) thanks to the parallel MLP component reducing draft time. Against Eagle (serial single-layer Transformer), Gumiho gains ~18% speedup (2.97× vs. 2.52×) and 32% higher τ (4.89 vs. 3.69) on Vicuna-7B. Against the strongest baseline Eagle-2 (serial Transformer + dynamic tree), Gumiho achieves 4.5%–15.8% higher speedup, with the gap widening for larger models: 11.7% on LLaMA2-70B (2.76× vs. 2.47×) and 15.8% on LLaMA3-70B (2.98× vs. 2.34×), attributed to both higher τ and shorter draft time from the parallel MLP heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gumiho sits at the intersection of two speculative decoding paradigms: Medusa's parallel MLP heads and Eagle/Eagle-2's serial Transformer heads. Its key conceptual departure is rejecting the implicit assumption in all prior self-drafting methods that every token in a draft sequence warrants equal architectural investment. By proving (Theorem 3.1) that concentrating accuracy on early positions increases mean accepted tokens, the paper justifies a hybrid serial-parallel design: a two-layer Transformer for the first two tokens and five parallel MLPs for the remaining five. The Full Tree Attention mechanism further extends Eagle-2's dynamic tree by exploiting the independence of parallel-generated tokens to augment shorter candidate paths at zero additional compute cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result provides a principled design criterion for speculative decoding architectures that the field previously lacked, moving beyond heuristic choices of head count and structure. The consistent 4.5–15.8% speedup over Eagle-2 across seven LLMs and six benchmarks, with particularly large gains on 70B models where parallelism amplifies, makes this a practically relevant improvement for production inference. However, the draft model is more parameter-heavy than Eagle or Medusa, increasing training memory, and the optimal serial/parallel split (2+5) was determined empirically rather than derived from the theorem. Open questions include whether the monotone-decreasing acceptance probability assumption (Eq. 5) always holds in practice and whether the hybrid principle generalizes to non-Transformer draft architectures.
