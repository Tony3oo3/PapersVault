## Problem

Value-based RL methods for continuous control typically estimate Q-values independently for each action dimension, neglecting interdependencies between dimensions. This independence assumption biases action selection toward the most frequent (often suboptimal) behaviors in the training data, making it difficult to identify truly optimal actions when learning from non-expert demonstrations or suboptimal online-collected trajectories.

## Input / Output

In D4RL, the input is a low-dimensional state vector; in RLBench, it is 84×84×3 RGB images from four camera angles (stacked over 8 frames) concatenated with proprioceptive joint states. The output is a continuous action vector (joint torques or delta joint angles) generated auto-regressively: the agent first selects a coarse-to-fine level (2 levels × 7 bins for D4RL, 3 levels × 5 bins for RLBench), then within each level selects a discrete bin for each action dimension sequentially, conditioned on all previously selected dimensions. A pre-collected offline demonstration dataset is also consumed during training via a behavior cloning objective.

## Method

ARSQ extends soft Q-learning by decomposing the Q-function into a soft value plus a sum of dimensional soft advantages, where each dimensional advantage is conditioned on previously generated action dimensions (Theorem 4.3). The policy generates actions auto-regressively along two axes: coarse-to-fine conditioning (outer) across discretization levels and dimensional conditioning (inner) across action dimensions, with a shared MLP backbone and per-dimension output heads. A log-sum-exp normalization enforces the constraint that exp(A^d/α) forms a valid probability distribution per dimension. Training uses double Q-learning (two value networks with EMA targets, taking the minimum for the target) plus a margin-based behavior cloning loss on offline demonstrations, all optimized jointly with the standard soft Bellman residual.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, ARSQ achieves a 1.62× average performance improvement over the SOTA value-based baseline CQN when trained with suboptimal (non-expert) demonstrations, and approximately 2.0× improvement when only the bottom 30% of trajectories are used as offline data. In the fully offline setting across 9 D4RL datasets, ARSQ attains a total normalized return of 741.1, surpassing the best offline RL baseline (CQL, 698.5) and the best offline IL baseline (DWBC, 653.2). On RLBench robot manipulation tasks with sparse rewards, ARSQ outperforms all baselines including DrQ-v2+, ACT, and CQN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQN (the primary value-based baseline), ARSQ wins on all 9 D4RL datasets in both the online-with-offline-data setting (1.62× average improvement) and the fully offline setting, with the largest gap on hopper-medium (99.2 vs. CQN's lower score) where suboptimal data dominates. On RLBench, ARSQ surpasses CQN, DrQ-v2+, DrQ-v2, ACT, and BC across all six evaluated tasks, confirming that the auto-regressive advantage structure outperforms both actor-critic and independent-dimension value-based approaches. In the fully offline comparison (Table 1), ARSQ's total score of 741.1 exceeds CQL (698.5), IQL (692.4), TD3+BC (677.4), Onestep RL (684.6), RvS-R (645.5), Filt. BC (666.2), DT (672.6), and DWBC (653.2), with the most dramatic win on hopper-medium (99.2 vs. next-best DT at 67.6). The only near-tie is on walker2d-medium-expert (107.9 vs. Onestep RL's 113.0), where expert data is abundant and the suboptimality advantage is minimal. In the fully online setting (Appendix F), ARSQ also outperforms PPO and CQN in sample efficiency, though it requires more environment steps than its offline-initialized variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARSQ extends the Soft Q-learning framework (Haarnoja et al., 2018) into a value-based continuous control setting by introducing a dimensional soft advantage decomposition, and adopts the coarse-to-fine action discretization strategy from CQN (Seo et al., 2024). The key departure from prior value-based continuous control methods (DecQN, CQN) is the replacement of independent per-dimension Q estimation with an auto-regressive structure that conditions each dimension's advantage on previously selected dimensions, thereby capturing cross-dimensional dependencies. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.3) establishes that the soft advantage decomposes additively into dimensional soft advantages under a normalization constraint, providing a principled bridge between the auto-regressive policy and the Q-learning update rule. The offline-to-online training paradigm with behavior cloning objectives follows the line of work by Ball et al. (2023) and Hester et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that auto-regressive value-based RL can substantially outperform independent-dimension baselines when training data is suboptimal, with a 1.62× average improvement over CQN on D4RL and consistent gains on RLBench. A notable finding is that CQN can perform worse than pure behavior cloning when demonstrations are very low quality, suggesting that independent decomposition actively biases toward frequent suboptimal modes. Practical limitations include higher inference latency (32.1 ms vs 6.9 ms on RLBench) due to sequential dimension generation, and the method's reliance on a fixed number of coarse-to-fine levels and bins. Open questions include adaptive discretization, grouping unrelated action dimensions to shorten the conditioning chain, and whether the auto-regressive structure scales to very high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., dexterous manipulation with 20+ joints).
