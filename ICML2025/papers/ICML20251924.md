## Problem

Spatiotemporal learning in scientific domains is hampered by irregular sensor distributions, noise, and massive data volumes, yet existing Implicit Neural Representation (INR) and Conditioned Neural Field (CNF) approaches lack both scalability to large models/datasets and generalizability across variable input configurations. No prior framework jointly performs reconstruction, interpolation, and forecasting from sparse, irregularly sampled observations while maintaining linear scaling with data size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251924/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SCENT consumes N_i sparse observations u_x^t_i at arbitrary spatial coordinates (grid or irregular mesh) at input time t_i, along with the target time t_o, and produces continuous field values at any number N_o of query coordinates at t_o. A key flexibility is that N_i and N_o can differ between input and output, and the model handles dynamically shifting sensor locations. During training, Δt = t_o − t_i is sampled from [0, t_h], so the same model handles reconstruction (Δt=0), interpolation, and forecasting without architectural changes.

## Method

SCENT is a Transformer-based encoder-processor-decoder (Perceiver IO-style) architecture with three key innovations. The Time-Targeted Spatial Encoder compresses N_i input tokens into M learnable query tokens via a Context Embedding Network (CEN) of sparse self-attention layers (each token attends to only S ≪ N_i tokens) followed by cross-attention, with Fourier features encoding both spatial coordinates and both input/target times. The Time Warp Processor learns continuous temporal dynamics and enables Warp-Unrolling Forecasting (WUF), which advances directly to the time horizon t_h rather than unrolling step-by-step, reducing error accumulation in long-horizon predictions. The Time-Conditioned Decoder reconstructs the field at arbitrary query coordinates via cross-attention against the M latent tokens, followed by a Calibration Network (CN) of sparse self-attention layers. Sparse attention throughout (in both CEN and CN) gives linear scaling with input size, and the learnable queries provide generalization without meta-learning or per-instance optimization.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes benchmarks (Table 2), SCENT achieves the best long-term forecasting MSE of 7.78×10⁻⁵ on NS-3 (vs. AROMA's 1.32×10⁻⁴), 1.03×10⁻¹ Rel-MSE on NS-4 (vs. AROMA's 1.05×10⁻¹), and 1.17×10⁻¹ on NS-5 (vs. AROMA's 1.24×10⁻¹). On the simulated challenging environments (Table 1), SCENT achieves the lowest Rel-MSE across all five scenarios (S1–S5), with the largest margin on S1 (2.51×10⁻² vs. FNO's 4.27×10⁻²) and S5 (3.26×10⁻¹ vs. CORAL's 9.06×10⁻¹). On the real AirDelhi datasets, SCENT achieves the best RMSE on AD-T (53.24) and AD-F (45.35), while ranking second on AD-B (44.20 vs. AROMA's 40.78). SCENT also outperforms RainNet across all lead times on rainforest nowcasting and maintains stable performance on Kuroshio path prediction beyond 50-day lead times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENT outperforms all seven baselines (FNO, DINO, CORAL, OFormer, GNOT, AROMA, RainNet) on the Navier-Stokes benchmarks, with the most dramatic gain on NS-3 where its warp-unrolling forecasting (WUF) strategy reduces error accumulation (7.78×10⁻⁵ vs. AROMA's 1.32×10⁻⁴, a 41% improvement). On the simulated challenging environments, SCENT dominates all baselines across all five data-corruption scenarios, with particularly large margins over CORAL (e.g., 3.26×10⁻¹ vs. 9.06×10⁻¹ on S5) and OFormer (3.26×10⁻¹ vs. 6.17×10⁻¹ on S5). The one notable exception is AirDelhi Benchmark (AD-B), where AROMA's diffusion backbone achieves a lower RMSE (40.78 vs. 44.20), likely due to its noise-filtering properties; however, SCENT wins on the larger and finer AirDelhi variants (AD-T: 53.24 vs. 63.06 for AROMA; AD-F: 45.35 vs. 47.49). On rainforest nowcasting, SCENT consistently beats RainNet at all lead times with relative gains increasing for longer horizons. On Kuroshio path prediction, SCENT achieves second-best performance beyond 50-day lead times, trailing only CEEMDAN-based feature engineering methods, while maintaining stable accuracy where other baselines degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCENT extends the Perceiver IO encoder-processor-decoder architecture (Jaegle et al., 2022) and the conditioned neural field (CNF) line of work (CORAL, AROMA, OFormer) from regular-grid PDE solving to irregular, sparse, and dynamically moving sensor data common in scientific domains. Key departures include adding target-time conditioning to both encoder and decoder for continuous temporal representations, introducing sparse-attention-based Context Embedding and Calibration Networks, and proposing Warp-Unrolling Forecasting (WUF) to mitigate error accumulation in long-horizon prediction. The paper positions itself as bridging the generalizability gap of standard INRs (which require per-instance retraining) and the regular-grid assumptions of prior CNF methods, unifying reconstruction, interpolation, and forecasting in a single supervised model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCENT addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific ML: existing neural field and operator learning methods degrade or fail on the irregular, noisy, and sparsely sampled data produced by real sensor networks (e.g., moving PM2.5 sensors, malfunctioning ocean buoys). The first systematic scalability study for continuity-informed architectures and the introduction of five simulated challenge datasets (S1–S5) provide useful benchmarks for the community. Open questions remain around high-frequency phenomena, multi-scale training, and whether the scalability advantage persists at extreme data volumes; the paper also acknowledges that feature-engineering baselines remain competitive on smaller datasets like Kuroshio, and AROMA still leads on the AirDelhi benchmark.
