## Problem

Standard RL agents in high-risk predator-prey environments exhibit a striking lack of self-preservation instinct, choosing marginally more efficient paths that bring them dangerously close to threats, while biological mice display sophisticated risk-assessment, waiting, and thigmotactic (wall-following) behaviors. This paper asks whether and how RL training objectives can be modified to induce biologically plausible caution and risk-avoidance without sacrificing task performance.

## Input / Output

The RL agent consumes a 10-dimensional state vector encoding its own (x, y) position and facing direction, the predator's position (only when within the agent's field of view, otherwise zeroed), distance to goal, and a binary 'puffed' flag. The agent outputs a continuous 3D action: target (x, y) coordinates for movement plus a wait/move indicator (values > 0.5 freeze the agent in place). The LLM agent variant additionally receives an RGB rendering of the arena and a textual task description, responding with a single JSON-formatted move per timestep under the same partial-observability constraint.

## Method

Building on TDMPC-2, the paper introduces two mechanisms to induce mouse-like caution. First, the Trauma-Inspired Safety Buffer (TISB) modifies experience replay by enforcing 50% negative transitions in each training batch and amplifying their reward magnitudes by a factor of 200, mimicking the heightened emotional encoding of traumatic events in biological systems. Second, a variance-penalized TD target replaces the standard Bellman backup with TD_target = r_t + γ(Q(s_{t+1}, π(s_{t+1})) − α·Var_{a∈A}(Q(s_{t+1}, a))), where the variance is estimated by uniformly sampling 400 actions and querying an ensemble of 5 Q-networks. Unlike SMiRL, which uniformly penalizes all surprising state transitions, this penalty is context-sensitive: Q-value variance spikes sharply when the predator enters view or approaches, selectively driving the agent to wait and gather information in high-uncertainty states while leaving safe-state transitions unpenalized. Together, these mechanisms increase visitation-pattern overlap with mice from 20.9% to 86.1% and reduce initial-entry distance traveled by ~45%, producing waiting, thigmotactic, and radial-exploration behaviors closely matching biological observations.

## Results

The headline metric is visitation pattern overlap with real mice: standard TDMPC-2 achieves only 20.9%, while the proposed VP-TDMPC-2 (TDMPC-2 + Trauma-Inspired Safety Buffer + Variance-Penalized TD learning) reaches 86.1%. VP-TDMPC-2 also matches mice on waiting behavior (32.4% vs. 27.7% incidence, vs. 0% for baseline TDMPC-2) and episode length (13.89 steps vs. mice's 14.04, vs. 7.09 for baseline). In terms of initial movement, VP-TDMPC-2 travels approximately 45% less distance during environment entry, closely mirroring the cautious hesitation observed in real mice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard TDMPC-2 baseline, VP-TDMPC-2 wins decisively on every behavioral fidelity metric: visitation overlap jumps from 20.9% to 86.1%, waiting behavior goes from 0% to 32.4%, and episode length shifts from 7.09 to 13.89 steps (mice: 14.04). Against SMiRL (Berseth et al., 2019), the paper's variance-penalized TD target is preferred because SMiRL's uniform surprise penalty failed to induce entrance waiting and actually counteracted TISB's path-finding benefits, as shown in Figure 9 density plots. Against the biological mice baseline, VP-TDMPC-2 closely matches on waiting incidence and episode length but still falls short on extreme thigmotaxis (wall-following), which the authors attribute to innate rodent behavioral priors. The ChatGPT-4 LLM agent, tested as an additional comparison, exhibited reckless, goal-directed behavior indistinguishable from standard RL agents rather than mice-like caution, suggesting biological misalignment is not specific to RL training. For offline RL on mouse data, IQL outperformed CQL and COMBO at capturing nuanced waiting behavior, though all offline methods were limited by data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TDMPC-2 (Hansen et al., 2023) as its base model-based RL framework, modifying its replay buffer and TD target to introduce biologically-inspired risk-avoidance mechanisms. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of RL and computational neuroscience/ethology, drawing on Prioritized Experience Replay (Schaul et al., 2015), SMiRL (Berseth et al., 2019) for surprise minimization, and distributional RL (Muller et al., 2024) for Q-value variance as an uncertainty signal. It departs from the standard RL paradigm of pure reward maximization by explicitly targeting behavioral fidelity to biological agents, using the cellworld predator-prey arena (Lai et al., 2024) as a shared physical/simulated testbed. The offline RL analysis (IQL, CQL, COMBO) serves as a diagnostic tool rather than a primary method, quantifying the policy gap via KL divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a fundamental architectural gap: standard RL lacks an explicit notion of mortality, causing agents to underweight rare but fatal risks in ways that biological organisms do not. The finding that ChatGPT-4, despite rich world knowledge, exhibits similarly reckless risk-taking suggests that biological alignment in decision-making is not automatically conferred by scale or pretraining. However, the proposed mechanisms (TISB, variance-penalized TD) are acknowledged as proof-of-concept, domain-specific to predator-prey navigation, with sensitivity to hyperparameters and training instability. Open questions include whether these biologically-grounded modifications generalize beyond this single environment, how to robustly encode irreversibility of outcomes in RL, and whether the residual gap (e.g., extreme thigmotaxis, 'baiting' behavior) requires fundamentally different architectures.
