## Problem

While scaling laws for model parameters and data are well established, the role of tokenization vocabulary size in model scaling remains underexplored, particularly the asymmetric effects of enlarging input versus output vocabularies. This paper decouples the two to show that input vocabulary scaling consistently improves performance at any model size with negligible cost, while output vocabulary scaling can be harmful for smaller models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard BPE token IDs (base vocabulary ~50K–100K) and internally expands them into hierarchical n-gram indices (up to 12.8M effective vocabulary) via modular hashing, producing a summed multi-gram embedding as the model input. Output remains standard next-token prediction, though the Over-Tokenized variant adds a sequential multi-token prediction head (MTP-DS) that conditionally predicts the next token given the previous prediction, without increasing inference cost since only the first predicted token is retained at generation time.

## Method

Over-Encoding (OE) replaces the standard single embedding lookup with a sum of 1-gram, 2-gram, and 3-gram embeddings, where each n-gram index is computed as a base-V positional encoding of the token window and mapped through a tiled embedding table of configurable size m (up to 12.8M) using modular hashing. Each n-gram embedder is further decomposed into k low-rank slices with projection matrices to model dimension, increasing combinatorial capacity without extra parameters. The paper demonstrates a log-linear scaling law: for every 4× increase in m, training loss decreases by ~0.015. Combined with Over-Decoding (OD) via DeepSeek-style sequential MTP, the full Over-Tokenized Transformer (OT) achieves complementary gains, with a 400M-parameter OE-12.8M model matching a 1B baseline at no additional training cost.

## Results

On OLMoE-1.3B (500B tokens), OE-12.8M reduces training loss from 2.554 to 2.472 (−0.082) and improves average downstream score from 0.510 to 0.524 (+0.014). On OLMoE-7B, the same configuration reduces loss from 2.305 to 2.229 (−0.076) and improves downstream from 0.601 to 0.608. The headline scaling result is that OE-12.8M with 400M parameters matches the training loss of a 1B OLMo2 baseline (2.5× larger) with no additional training cost, and a log-linear relationship is established: L = 2.6754 − 0.0256 × log₁₀(m). Combining OE with MTP-DS (OT-12.8M) further boosts downstream to 0.537 (+1.3% over OE alone) on OLMoE-1.3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OLMo2 dense baselines, OE-12.8M with 400M parameters matches the 1B baseline's training loss, effectively gaining 2.5× model capacity for free, and achieves 5.7× convergence speedup on loss and 3.0–3.9× speedups on downstream tasks (MMLU-Var, HellaSwag, ARC, PIQA). Against OLMoE MoE baselines, OE-12.8M consistently reduces loss by ~0.08 and improves downstream by 0.7–1.4% at both 1.3B and 7B scales, though the downstream gain diminishes at 7B (possibly due to overlap with MoE's sparse parameters). Against MTP (Gloeckle et al., 2024), which shows no improvement on OLMoE-1.3B (loss 2.556 vs. 2.554 baseline; downstream 0.508 vs. 0.510), OE-12.8M clearly dominates with loss 2.472 and downstream 0.524. Against MTP-DS (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2024), which yields only marginal gains (loss 2.555, downstream 0.511), OE-12.8M again wins decisively; however, combining both into OT-12.8M produces the best downstream score (0.537), demonstrating that OE and MTP-DS gains are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the n-gram embedding lineage (Huang et al., 2021; Roy et al., 2022) and the vocabulary scaling observations of Tao et al. (2024) by making a critical conceptual separation: input (embedding) and output (unembedding) vocabularies are decoupled and shown to follow distinct scaling behaviors. It reframes Multi-Token Prediction (Gloeckle et al., 2024; DeepSeek-AI, 2024) as an approximation of 'over-decoding,' unifying it under a single framework with 'over-encoding.' The hierarchical multi-gram embedding with low-rank decomposition and the tensor-parallelism engineering solution for sparse embedding lookup are novel contributions not present in prior n-gram or vocabulary-scaling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a log-linear relationship between input vocabulary size and training loss introduces vocabulary as a new, independently scalable dimension in LLM scaling laws, alongside parameters and data. Practically, the <5% training overhead for a 128× vocabulary expansion that matches a 2.5× larger model makes this an immediately adoptable technique for cost-efficient pretraining. Open questions include whether the log-linear trend persists at even larger vocabulary sizes (beyond 12.8M), whether the benefits compound or saturate when combined with other scaling axes (data, parameters), and whether the MoE-specific observation that OE gains diminish (possibly due to overlap with sparse FFN parameters) generalizes to other sparsity mechanisms.
