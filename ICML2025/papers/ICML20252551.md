## Problem

State-of-the-art flow matching and diffusion generative models rely on a two-stage training recipe: first a domain-specific compressor (e.g., ConvNet VAE for images, PointNet for point clouds, Evoformer for proteins) is trained, then a generative model is trained in the compressor's latent space. This dependence on hand-crafted, modality-specific architectures prevents the development of truly domain-agnostic generative models that can be trivially applied across data domains with a single unified training stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252551/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

INRFlow operates on sets of coordinate-value pairs rather than fixed-size tensors: for images, 2D pixel coordinates mapped to RGB values; for 3D point clouds, 3D base coordinates mapped to 3D deformation values; for proteins, per-atom feature vectors (element type, charge, valence, ESM embedding, etc.) mapped to 3D atomic positions. At inference, the user can query any number of coordinate-value pairs, enabling resolution-agnostic generation (e.g., training at 256×256 but sampling at 2048×2048) without cascading or multi-resolution training. Conditional information (class labels, reference images via DINOv2 features, amino-acid sequences) is injected through cross-attention or concatenation.

## Method

INRFlow introduces a conditionally independent point-wise flow matching (CICFM) loss that decomposes the target velocity field across individual coordinate-value pairs, so the network predicts a velocity for each (x, y) pair independently given a shared contextual latent z_{f_t}. The architecture is a modified PerceiverIO: an encoder cross-attends from coordinate-value pairs to spatially-aware latents (each latent assigned a pseudo-coordinate and attending only to its spatial neighbors), followed by self-attention blocks to update the latents; a decoder then cross-attends from query coordinate-value pairs to the updated latents to produce point-wise velocity predictions. The spatially-aware latents are the critical design choice—ablations show vanilla PerceiverIO without them yields FID 65.09 versus 7.03 with them on LSUN-Church-256. The model uses rectified flow (linear interpolation f_t = (1-t)f_0 + t·ε) and is trained end-to-end in a single stage directly in ambient space, dispensing with any pre-trained compressor.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, INRFlow-XL (733M params) achieves FID 3.74, IS 228.8, Precision 0.82, and Recall 0.52, outperforming all ambient-space baselines including ADM (FID 10.94), CDM (4.88), RIN (3.42), and HDiT (3.21), though trailing latent-space models DiT-XL (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL (2.06) that rely on VAEs pretrained on 9.23M images. On FFHQ-256 and LSUN-Church-256, INRFlow-L achieves FID-CLIP of 2.18 and 5.51 respectively, surpassing all function-space baselines (∞-Diff: 3.87/10.36, GASP: 24.37/37.46). On Objaverse image-to-3D, INRFlow achieves ULIP-I 0.2976 and P-FID 0.3638, substantially outperforming CLAY (0.2066/0.9946). On ShapeNet, INRFlow-B (108M) outperforms LION (110M) on MMD across Airplane, Chair, Car, and all-55-category settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against function-space baselines, INRFlow clearly wins: on FFHQ-256 it achieves FID-CLIP 2.18 (INRFlow-L) vs. ∞-Diff (3.87), GASP (24.37), and GEM (35.62); on Church-256 it scores 5.51 vs. ∞-Diff (10.36) and GASP (37.46). On ImageNet-256, INRFlow-XL (FID 3.74) beats all ambient-space CNN/transformer baselines trained on the same 1.28M ImageNet data—ADM (10.94), CDM (4.88), Simple Diffusion U-Net (3.76), RIN (3.42), and HDiT (3.21)—but trails the 2B-parameter U-ViT hybrids (Simple Diff. 2.77, VDM++ 2.12) and latent-space DiT-XL/SiT-XL (2.27/2.06) which benefit from VAEs pretrained on 9.23M images. On ImageNet-128, INRFlow-XL (FID 2.73) slightly edges out RIN (2.75) and ADM (2.97) while matching PolyINR (2.08) on FID but surpassing it on IS (187.6 vs. 179.0) and Recall (0.58 vs. 0.45). For image-to-3D on Objaverse, INRFlow substantially outperforms CLAY on both ULIP-I (0.2976 vs. 0.2066) and P-FID (0.3638 vs. 0.9946). On ShapeNet, INRFlow-B (108M) beats LION (110M) on MMD for Airplane (0.2861 vs. 0.3564), Chair (3.6310 vs. 3.8458), Car (0.9923 vs. 1.0635), and all-55 (3.2586 vs. 3.4336), while matching or slightly trailing on COV and 1-NNA. For protein folding, INRFlow (Cα-LDDT 0.722, TM-Score 0.664) trails the domain-specific Boltz-1/AlphaFold3 replication (0.923, 0.812) but demonstrates that a fully domain-agnostic architecture can produce reasonable 3D structures without any protein-specific inductive biases.

## Relation to Prior Work

INRFlow builds on the flow matching / stochastic interpolant framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024) and the PerceiverIO architecture (Jaegle et al., 2022), but departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (domain-specific compressor + latent-space generative model) by operating directly in ambient space. Drawing on the implicit neural representation (INR) literature, the paper formulates a conditionally independent point-wise velocity matching objective (CICFM loss) that decomposes the target velocity field across coordinate-value pairs, enabling a single transformer to handle images, 3D point clouds, and protein structures without hand-crafted inductive biases. The key architectural modification over vanilla PerceiverIO—spatial-aware latents with pseudo-coordinates—addresses the bottleneck that arises when encoding large ambient-space coordinate sets, and the rectified flow path (Liu et al., 2023) is adopted for training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for domain-specific compressors and their associated hyperparameter tuning, INRFlow offers a genuinely unified single-stage training recipe that can be trivially adapted to new data modalities, which is a practical simplification for the generative modeling community. The resolution-agnostic generation property (training at 256×256, sampling at 2048×2048 without cascading or multi-resolution training) is a qualitative capability absent from standard diffusion/flow pipelines and suggests the model learns a continuous probability density field rather than overfitting to fixed-resolution coordinate sets. However, on ImageNet-256 the FID gap versus latent-space models (3.74 vs. 2.06–2.27 for SiT/DiT) remains meaningful, and the protein folding results, while encouraging, are preliminary and far from SOTA. Open questions include whether multi-domain co-training can yield true multi-modality generation, and whether training-efficiency tricks (e.g., Sehwag et al., 2024) can close the compute gap with latent-space approaches.
