## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods are designed for Transformer-based architectures and fail to exploit the rich topological relationships among nodes and edges in Vision GNN (ViG) models, leading to suboptimal transfer performance compared to full fine-tuning. The paper identifies that semantically connected nodes in vision graphs share dominant PCA components, revealing a low-rank structure in the latent feature space that prior prompting strategies do not leverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250033/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, RGB images are resized to 256×256 and center-cropped to 224×224, then divided into patches and dynamically connected via K-nearest-neighbor edges to form a graph. The method also generalizes to molecular graphs (MoleculeNet) and protein ego networks (PPI), where nodes represent atoms or proteins and edges encode structural or interaction relationships. In all cases, the output is a single class label per input, and only the prompt parameters (~2.6M) and a shallow classification head are trained while the ViG-M or GIN backbone remains frozen.

## Method

VGP introduces three complementary prompt components, each incorporating a semantic low-rank decomposition (rank r=32 vs. feature dimension d=768). The SeLo-Graph Prompt appends M trainable virtual nodes (factorized as an M×r matrix times an r×d prompt matrix) that dynamically form edges with existing nodes via cosine-similarity KNN, enriching global connectivity. The SeLo-Edge Prompt projects each neighbor's feature into a low-rank semantic space via a shared MLP, then propagates the result through an r×d edge prompt matrix to the center node with a blending factor β, filtering high-frequency local noise. The SeLo-Node Prompt decomposes each node feature into intrinsic and semantic components, amplifying the low-rank semantic part through an r×d node prompt matrix with blending factor α. Together, these prompts modify ViG's aggregation and update operations (Equations 11–12) without altering the backbone, reducing trainable parameters by 94.6% while adding only 3.1% FLOPs.

## Results

On ten vision classification datasets using a ViG-M backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21k, VGP achieves an average accuracy of 89.6%, surpassing full fine-tuning (89.0%) while using only 2.61M trainable parameters (94.6% fewer than the 48.68M in full fine-tuning). On nine chemistry/biology graph classification tasks with a GIN backbone, VGP reaches 76.39% average accuracy, exceeding full fine-tuning (72.72%) by 3.67%. The method introduces only 3.1% additional FLOPs over the frozen backbone, confirming its computational efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGP decisively outperforms all visual prompting baselines adapted to ViG-M: it beats InsVP (84.6%) by 5.0%, DAM-VP (83.7%) by 5.9%, VFPT (83.6%) by 6.0%, VP (81.1%) by 8.5%, and VPT (80.7%) by 8.9% in average accuracy. Among graph prompting methods, VGP surpasses GPF-Plus (83.6%) by 6.0% and GraphPrompt (80.6%) by 9.0% on vision tasks, and on the GIN-based graph tasks it exceeds GPF-Plus (74.54%) by 1.85%, GPF (74.51%) by 1.88%, GPPT (61.80%) by 14.59%, and GraphPrompt (61.20%) by 15.19%. Notably, VGP also surpasses full fine-tuning on both vision (89.6% vs. 89.0%) and graph tasks (76.39% vs. 72.72%), suggesting that the low-rank semantic decomposition acts as a regularizer that prevents overfitting on smaller datasets like Food and SVHN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Vision GNN (ViG) by Han et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which represents images as dynamically constructed KNN graphs, and introduces the first parameter-efficient prompting framework tailored to this architecture. It departs from two existing lines of work: visual prompting methods (VPT, DAM-VP, InsVP) designed for Transformer attention mechanisms, and graph prompting methods (GraphPrompt, GPF-Plus, GPPT) developed for social networks and molecular graphs. The key conceptual departure is the observation that semantically connected nodes in vision graphs share dominant PCA components, motivating a low-rank decomposition design that is neither purely data-space (like VP) nor purely attention-space (like VPT), but operates on the graph topology itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: ViG-based backbones previously had no dedicated PEFT strategy, forcing practitioners to rely on full fine-tuning or ill-fitting Transformer-centric prompts. The 94.6% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning accuracy makes ViG practically viable for multi-task deployment. Open questions include whether the low-rank semantic property generalizes to other graph-based vision architectures (ViHGNN, MobileViG, GreedyViG), whether the approach extends to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the rank dimension r can be adapted per-dataset or per-layer rather than fixed globally.
