## Problem

High-dimensional tensor regression is ill-posed when the number of parameters far exceeds the number of observations, and while convex regularizers (Lasso, nuclear norm) provide theoretical guarantees, nonconvex penalties such as SCAD and MCP are known to reduce estimation bias in other high-dimensional settings. However, whether nonconvex regularizers can deliver oracle statistical rates for tensor-on-tensor regression remained an open question, with no unified framework connecting the penalty design to convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n paired observations of an M-order predictor tensor and an (N−M)-order response tensor (e.g., 3rd-order tensors of size 16×16×16 in synthetic experiments, or 64×64×3 RGB image patches on ImageNet 2012 with n=4000). It produces an estimated N-order coefficient tensor whose entries or mode-unfolding singular values are structured by the chosen regularizer, from which predicted response tensors are obtained via tensor contraction. The key constraint is that the coefficient tensor lives in a space whose dimensionality (d₁×⋯×d_N) vastly exceeds n, necessitating sparsity or low-rank structure for identifiability.

## Method

The paper formulates a regularized least-squares objective where the structural penalty R_λ(A) is built from a decomposable nonconvex univariate penalty p_λ(t) = λ|t| + q_λ(t) (with q_λ concave), applied either entry-wise to induce sparsity or to the singular values of mode-(k) unfoldings to enforce low-rankness. This decomposition into a convex norm plus a concave correction enables an accelerated proximal gradient algorithm (adapted from Yao et al., 2017) that alternates a Nesterov-extrapolated gradient step on the loss with a proximal step on the convex norm, using a nonmonotone acceptance criterion to handle the nonconvexity. Theoretically, under restricted strong convexity and smoothness of the empirical loss plus a well-conditioned Gaussian design, the estimator attains the oracle rate √(|S|/n) for sparsity and √(|S₂|)/n for low-rankness, matching the rate of an estimator that knows the true support or rank a priori—strictly faster than the corresponding convex (ℓ₁ or nuclear norm) bounds from Raskutti et al. (2019).

## Results

On synthetic 16×16×16 tensor data, the proposed nonconvex regularizer achieves MSFE of 0.4042 (entry-wise sparsity, |S|=2048, η=0.1) and 0.5482 (mode-wise low-rankness, rank=5, η=1), substantially outperforming the convex baseline (0.6938 and 1.7411 respectively). On the real-world ImageNet 2012 denoising task (64×64×3 tensor, n=4000), the nonconvex method yields MPRE of 7.6072 (sparsity) and 1.0330 (low-rank), compared to 7.7498 and 1.1027 for the convex method. The mode-wise low-rankness setting shows the largest relative improvement, with the nonconvex penalty reducing MSFE by approximately 69% over the convex nuclear-norm baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the convex regularization framework of Raskutti et al. (2019), which uses ℓ₁ (entry-wise sparsity) and nuclear norm (mode-wise low-rankness) penalties. The proposed nonconvex SCAD/MCP regularizer wins on every reported metric in both structural settings. For entry-wise sparsity, the nonconvex method reduces synthetic MSFE from 0.6938 to 0.4042 (≈42% reduction) and real-world MPRE from 7.7498 to 7.6072. For mode-wise low-rankness, the improvement is more dramatic: synthetic MSFE drops from 1.7411 to 0.5482 (≈69% reduction) and real-world MPRE from 1.1027 to 1.0330. The RMSE gap is smaller (e.g., 0.0992 vs. 0.1004 for sparsity), suggesting the nonconvex penalty primarily improves coefficient estimation accuracy rather than prediction error. The paper attributes this advantage to the unbiasedness property of nonconvex penalties, which avoids the shrinkage bias inherent in convex ℓ₁ and nuclear-norm regularizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convex tensor regression framework of Raskutti et al. (2019), which established risk bounds for L1 and nuclear norm regularizers, by replacing these convex penalties with nonconvex SCAD and MCP penalties drawn from the classical nonconvex penalized regression literature (Fan & Li, 2001; Zhang, 2010). The optimization algorithm is adapted from Yao et al. (2017)'s inexact proximal gradient method for nonconvex problems, incorporating a Nesterov-style extrapolation step. The theoretical analysis follows the restricted strong convexity/smoothness paradigm common in high-dimensional statistics (Wang et al., 2014; Gui et al., 2016), but applies it to the tensor-structured setting with mode-wise unfolding and fiber/slice decompositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish oracle statistical rates for tensor regression with nonconvex regularizers, showing that the bias inherent in convex L1/nuclear norm penalties can be eliminated while retaining fast convergence. The practical impact is demonstrated on ImageNet denoising, where the nonconvex method shows substantially lower error than its convex counterpart, particularly in the low-rankness setting. However, the analysis is currently limited to entry-wise sparsity and mode-wise low-rankness; the authors explicitly note that extending oracle guarantees to tensor decomposition-based estimators (CP, Tucker) remains open, which would be a significant step toward more flexible structural modeling.
