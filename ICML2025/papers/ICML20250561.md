## Problem

Observed tandem mass spectra in de novo peptide sequencing frequently lack informative b- and y-ion peaks due to incomplete fragmentation and instrumental constraints, leaving existing encoder-decoder models with insufficient information to reconstruct peptide sequences. No prior method explicitly compensates for this missing fragmentation before the final prediction step, leading to significant performance degradation as the missing ratio increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observed mass spectrum as a set of up to 150 (m/z, intensity) peak pairs, a precursor descriptor (mass and charge state 1–10), and a complementary spectrum as auxiliary input. It autoregressively outputs an amino acid sequence drawn from a vocabulary of 20 canonical residues plus 3 PTM classes (oxidation of Met, deamidation of Asn/Gln) and a stop token, with a maximum peptide length of 100. During training, a theoretical spectrum derived from the ground-truth peptide (all b- and y-ions at unit charge, max intensity) is also encoded to serve as the imputation target, but this is discarded at inference.

## Method

LIPNovo extends the CasaNovo transformer encoder-decoder with a latent-space imputation module that predicts representations of ideal fragmentation peaks before peptide decoding. A 3-layer transformer decoder with M=100 learnable peak queries takes the encoded observed spectrum as memory and produces M candidate latent vectors; two parallel FFN heads output the imputed representations and their validity probabilities. Training aligns predictions to the encoded theoretical spectrum via optimal bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) with a composite loss combining pairwise MSE and a binary classification term for valid/invalid predictions. At inference, only imputed peaks exceeding a probability threshold τ=0.8 are concatenated with the original spectrum embeddings and fed to the peptide decoder. The total training objective sums three terms: cross-entropy on the combined (imputed + observed) representations, cross-entropy on the theoretical spectrum representations (to keep the encoder informative), and the imputation loss itself.

## Results

On the Nine-species dataset, LIPNovo achieves 0.797 amino acid precision and recall, and 0.582 peptide precision (AUC 0.547), outperforming the closest competitor π-HelixNovo by +3.2% in amino acid precision. On Seven-species and HC-PT, LIPNovo reaches 0.557/0.560 and 0.637/0.643 amino acid precision/recall respectively, with peptide precisions of 0.327 and 0.458. Overall, LIPNovo surpasses the CasaNovo baseline by +5.6%, +20.0%, and +11.2% in amino acid precision on Nine-species, Seven-species, and HC-PT, and improves peptide precision by +13.9% over AdaNovo and +11.3% over π-HelixNovo on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIPNovo outperforms all eight compared baselines across all three datasets and all metric levels. Against the direct CasaNovo baseline (retrained with identical configurations), LIPNovo gains +5.6% amino acid precision on Nine-species (0.797 vs 0.741), +20.0% on Seven-species (0.557 vs 0.357), and +11.2% on HC-PT (0.637 vs 0.525). Against the strongest deep learning competitor π-HelixNovo, LIPNovo leads by +3.2% amino acid precision on Nine-species (0.797 vs 0.765) and by +11.3% mean peptide precision across datasets. Against AdaNovo, LIPNovo improves peptide precision by +13.9% on average and PTM precision by +8.7% to +17.4% depending on the dataset. Against the classical tool PEAKS, LIPNovo exceeds its amino acid precision by +5.1% on Nine-species (0.797 vs 0.748). Against GraphNovo (compared on its own dataset), LIPNovo achieves +1.7% peptide recall (0.729 vs 0.712) with fewer parameters (68.4M vs 88.2M) and an end-to-end architecture rather than a two-stage pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIPNovo builds directly on the CasaNovo transformer encoder-decoder architecture for de novo peptide sequencing, inserting a latent-space imputation module between spectrum encoding and peptide decoding. The imputation step is formulated as a set-prediction problem, borrowing the bipartite matching and Hungarian algorithm machinery from DETR (Carion et al., 2020) to align predicted peak representations with ground-truth theoretical peaks. This departs from prior approaches to missing fragmentation—GraphNovo's two-stage graph search, π-HelixNovo's complementary spectrum generation, and AdaNovo's adaptive training—by explicitly reconstructing missing peaks in latent space rather than working around the deficiency at the input or training level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing fragmentation is a pervasive practical limitation in tandem MS proteomics, and LIPNovo's end-to-end imputation-before-prediction paradigm offers a clean, single-model solution that outperforms more complex two-stage alternatives like GraphNovo while using fewer parameters. The upper-bound analysis (0.939 amino acid precision with oracle imputation vs. 0.797 achieved) reveals substantial headroom, suggesting that improving imputation quality is a promising direction for future work. However, gains diminish at very high missing ratios (≥0.7), indicating that the current imputation mechanism struggles when most signal peaks are absent—a gap that may require cross-sample correlations or more sophisticated generative mechanisms to close.
