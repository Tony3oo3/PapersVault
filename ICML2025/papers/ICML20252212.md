## Problem

Non-distillation Consistency Training (CT) suffers from high gradient variance and training instability because the fixed, independent noise-data pairing creates sharp boundaries in the ODE flow mapping, making the training objective highly singular. The paper asks whether learning a data-dependent noise coupling can reshape the underlying ODE trajectories to yield a smoother, lower-variance training objective without requiring a pretrained teacher model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252212/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

At sampling, the model takes a single Gaussian noise vector (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a synthetic image in 1 or 2 network evaluations. During training, the forward process uses either a variance-exploding kernel (a_t=1, b_t=t) or a linear interpolation kernel (a_t=1−t/σ_max, b_t=t/σ_max) with σ_min=0.002 and σ_max=80. The encoder is a smaller network (e.g., 1.5–6M params vs. 55–280M for the generator) that outputs per-pixel mean and scale parameters for the coupled noise distribution, preserving the input dimensionality.

## Method

VCT introduces a variational coupling mechanism where a lightweight encoder g_φ(x_0) parameterizes a data-dependent Gaussian q_φ(x_1|x_0)=N(g_φ^μ(x_0), g_φ^σ(x_0)²I) from which noise is sampled during training, replacing the standard independent N(0,I) sampling. The total loss is the standard discretized consistency loss plus a β-weighted KL divergence between q_φ and the standard normal prior, yielding an objective structurally analogous to the VAE ELBO. The encoder and consistency network are trained end-to-end: the consistency network minimizes its total derivative along trajectories while the encoder implicitly moves trajectories toward the model's space of constancy. For iCT's adaptive discretization, the KL weight is scaled as λ_KL=β/Δt_N to maintain consistent regularization strength across training stages. The method is a drop-in replacement for the independent coupling in both iCT and ECM, compatible with both VE and LI kernels, and requires only a small additional encoder (adding ~10% training time) while keeping 1-step sampling cost identical to vanilla CMs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best VCT model (ECM-VE-VC) achieves 3.26 FID (1-step) and 2.02 FID (2-step), outperforming the ECM-VE baseline (3.68/2.14) and the OT-coupling variant (3.46/2.13). On ImageNet 64×64, ECM-LI-VC reaches 4.93 FID (1-step) and 3.07 FID (2-step), the best among all re-implemented baselines, while on FFHQ 64×64 the best VCT model (ECM-VE-VC) achieves 5.47/4.16 FID. The 2-step CIFAR-10 result (2.02) is competitive with recent SoTA non-distillation CT methods such as TCM (2.05) and sCT (2.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCT consistently outperforms both the independent-coupling baselines (ECM-VE, ECM-LI) and the minibatch OT-coupling variants (ECM-VE-OT, ECM-LI-OT) across all four datasets and both transition kernels. On CIFAR-10, ECM-VE-VC improves 1-step FID from 3.68 to 3.26 (−0.42) and 2-step FID from 2.14 to 2.02 (−0.12) over ECM-VE; the OT coupling provides only a marginal gain (3.46/2.13) and degrades significantly on ImageNet (6.02/4.27 vs. 5.26/3.22 for independent coupling). On ImageNet 64×64, ECM-LI-VC achieves the best 1-step FID (4.93) among all re-implemented models, beating ECM-LI by 0.20 and ECM-LI-OT by 0.70. The 2-step CIFAR-10 FID of 2.02 (ECM-VE-VC) is on par with TCM (2.05) and sCT (2.06) from concurrent work, while the original iCT 1-step result (2.83) remains slightly ahead of the re-implemented iCT-VE-VC (2.86), though the authors note no open-source reproduction of that number exists. The learned variational coupling also reduces gradient variance during training (Figure 3), providing a mechanistic explanation for the FID improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Consistency Training (CT) by replacing the fixed independent noise-data pairing with a learned, data-dependent variational coupling, drawing a direct structural analogy to VAEs where an encoder outputs a conditional noise distribution regularized by a KL term. The formulation is grounded in the Flow Matching perspective (Lipman et al., 2023), which naturally accommodates probabilistic couplings between data and noise, and builds on the iCT (Song & Dhariwal, 2024) and ECM (Geng et al., 2025) training frameworks as drop-in baselines. It departs from prior coupling strategies in CT—minibatch OT coupling (Pooladian et al., 2023) and generator-induced coupling (Issenhuth et al., 2025)—by learning the coupling end-to-end with a small encoder, which the authors show scales better to high-dimensional data than OT-based alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-step image generation via consistency models is a practically important direction, and VCT offers a simple, well-motivated improvement that consistently reduces training variance and improves FID across all tested datasets without architectural changes to the generator. The VAE/ELBO connection provides a theoretical grounding (Proposition 6.1) that links the consistency loss to a lower bound on model evidence, which is a nice unification but does not yet yield new algorithmic consequences. Open questions include whether more expressive posterior distributions (beyond diagonal Gaussians) would further improve coupling quality, how the method extends to the continuous-time CT formulation, and whether the β hyperparameter can be made adaptive or eliminated. The requirement for a small additional encoder and dataset-dependent β tuning are practical costs that limit the 'drop-in' appeal somewhat.
