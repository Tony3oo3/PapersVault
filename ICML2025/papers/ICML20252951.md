## Problem

Existing feature selection methods score features based on all training instances or, at best, a few outliers, but they fail to handle the challenging case where intra-class instance distributions are asymmetric or contain multiple high-density clusters (ADMHC). In such settings, the class body is difficult to identify, and stray outliers that intrude into other class bodies are the primary source of misclassification, yet no prior method explicitly targets these Stray Intrusive Outliers (SIOs) for feature scoring.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled high-dimensional dataset (n instances, d features, c classes) and a single hyperparameter α ∈ (0,1] controlling the density threshold for the RDM center. It produces a per-feature score vector (s_1, …, s_d) computed via ℓ1-norm-based intrusion degrees, from which the top m features are selected in ascending score order. For deep-feature datasets (UCM, AID, ModelNet), input is 2048-dim embeddings from ResNet-152 or CurveNet; for Caltech101, it is 5243-dim Fisher Vector features obtained by systematic sampling from the original 262144-dim representation.

## Method

SIOFS is a supervised filter method that scores features by the intrusion degree of Stray Intrusive Outliers. It first computes a Refined Density-Mean (RDM) center per class by averaging the top ⌈α·n_k⌉ highest-density instances (density defined as the reciprocal of the median ℓ1 distance to all class-mates), which robustly represents the class body under asymmetric or multi-peak distributions. A modified skewness coefficient ŝ^(l) is fused into the 3σ threshold to yield Θ^(l) = u^(l) + (2 − ŝ^(l)/3)σ̂^(l), adaptively shrinking or expanding the class-body boundary depending on distribution asymmetry. SIOs are then identified as instances of class k whose ℓ1 distance to the RDM center of another class l₀ falls below Θ^(l₀) and whose own-class distance plus the oriented radius of l₀ toward k exceeds the inter-center distance (a two-hypersphere intersection test). Each feature f is scored by S_if^(kl₀) = |x_if^(k) − u_f^(k)| + D_f^(l₀)k − |u_f^(k) − u_f^(l₀)|, and the final feature score averages these values over the half of all SIO class pairs with the lowest total intrusion degree, so that features with the most negative (i.e., most discriminative) scores are ranked highest.

## Results

On 12 small-sized high-dimensional datasets, SIOFS achieves the best or second-best mean accuracy (ACC) across all 12 datasets, with notable margins such as +2.1% over ReliefF on CLL (71.32% vs. 69.22%) and +4.93% over IOFS on GISETTE (73.08% vs. 68.15%). On deep-feature datasets (UCM, AID, ModelNet), SIOFS attains the best global ACC (94.95%, 85.83%, 93.03% respectively). On the large-class-number Caltech101 dataset, SIOFS outperforms all 12 baselines at every feature percentage tested (60%–90%), reaching 54.13% ACC at 90% features versus 53.83% for the next-best IOFS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIOFS outperforms all 12 supervised baselines on 10 of 12 small-sized datasets, with the largest margins on CLL (+2.1% over ReliefF), GISETTE (+4.93% over IOFS), and Lung_dis (+0.7% over IOFS). It is narrowly second-best only on Nci9 (54.17% vs. IOFS 54.72%) and Yale (71.21% vs. IOFS 71.31%), where the differences are within one standard deviation. Among the outlier-based methods (FSDOC, IOFS), SIOFS consistently surpasses both, confirming that targeting SIOs specifically (rather than general directional or intrusive outliers) yields better feature discrimination. On deep-feature benchmarks, SIOFS achieves the best ACC on UCM (94.95%) and ties for best on AID (85.83%, tied with IOFS) and ModelNet (93.03%, tied with ILFS and MRMSR). On Caltech101, SIOFS dominates all baselines at every feature percentage, with a 0.3% gap over IOFS at 90% features. Classical filter methods (Fisher, QMI, ReliefF, TRC) are consistently outperformed by 5–15% on the most challenging datasets (CLL, Nci9, GISETTE), while embedded methods (S²DFS, ReOLSR) suffer from overfitting on small samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIOFS extends the outlier-based feature selection lineage established by the same first author's FSDOC (2022) and IOFS (2024), which scored features using directional or intrusive outliers but did not handle intra-class asymmetric distributions or multiple high-density clusters. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Stray Intrusive Outliers (SIOs) — instances that are simultaneously far from their own class body and intruding into another class body — combined with a Refined Density-Mean (RDM) center and a modified skewness coefficient fused into the 3σ principle to robustly identify class bodies under non-symmetric, multi-peak distance distributions. This positions SIOFS as a supervised filter method that departs from the standard practice of scoring features based on all training instances equally, instead focusing exclusively on the intrusion degree caused by SIOs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important and underexplored setting: small-sized high-dimensional datasets (common in genomics and medical imaging) where intra-class distributions are asymmetric or contain multiple high-density clusters, making standard class-center and body-identification approaches unreliable. The RDM center and modified SC provide a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for class body identification that generalizes across distribution types, and the SIO-based scoring offers an interpretable criterion for feature ranking. Limitations include the O(n²) cost of the RDM center computation, a single hyperparameter α that requires tuning (though the paper shows reasonable robustness), and the filter-method paradigm that does not interact with the downstream classifier. Open questions include whether the SIO concept and RDM center can be extended to deep feature selection in an end-to-end manner and whether more powerful class centers (beyond density-mean averaging) could further improve performance on highly scattered distributions.
