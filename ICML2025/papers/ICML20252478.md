## Problem

Diffusion-based I2V models use a noise-to-data generation process that is inherently mismatched with the frame-to-frames nature of image animation. The uninformative Gaussian prior forces the model to regenerate all video content from scratch rather than leveraging the rich appearance information already present in the given image, leading to suboptimal preservation of visual details and temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 256×256 RGB image (the initial frame) and a text prompt or class label as input, and produces a 16-frame 256×256 video clip. The input image is encoded via a pre-trained VAE into a latent representation, which is then replicated L times along the temporal axis to form the deterministic prior (boundary distribution) of the bridge process, rather than serving merely as a conditioning signal as in diffusion-based I2V models.

## Method

FrameBridge replaces the Gaussian prior of diffusion models with a Dirac prior (the replicated image latent), establishing a data-to-data bridge process where the forward SDE connects the video target to the image prior rather than to noise. The training objective is a denoising loss over the bridge perturbation kernel, which preserves data information in intermediate representations. Two key techniques enhance the framework: SNR-Aligned Fine-tuning (SAF) reparameterizes the bridge process so that noisy latents align with those of pre-trained T2V diffusion models (matching SNR ratios via a timestep remapping), enabling efficient transfer from existing video diffusion checkpoints; and a neural prior, a one-step regression network trained to predict the mean of subsequent frames, which narrows the gap between prior and target further than simple image replication. The bridge process is parameterized to predict the same ε-target as diffusion models, making the architecture directly compatible with pre-trained UNet or DiT backbones.

## Results

On zero-shot I2V generation (fine-tuned on WebVid-2M), FrameBridge-VideoCrafter with SAF achieves FVD 95 on MSR-VTT and FVD 258 on UCF-101, while FrameBridge-CogVideoX with SAF achieves FVD 96 on MSR-VTT and FVD 235 on UCF-101. On non-zero-shot I2V (trained from scratch on UCF-101), FrameBridge-NP achieves FVD 122, IS 63.60, and PIC 0.7662, outperforming its diffusion counterpart VDT-I2V (FVD 171). The direct comparison with DynamiCrafter (same architecture, base model, and 6.4M training budget) shows FrameBridge reduces zero-shot FVD from 234 to 95 on MSR-VTT and from 429 to 258 on UCF-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DynamiCrafter (the direct diffusion counterpart sharing the same VideoCrafter1 backbone and 6.4M training budget), FrameBridge-VideoCrafter reduces zero-shot FVD from 234 to 95 on MSR-VTT and from 429 to 258 on UCF-101, a 59% and 40% improvement respectively. FrameBridge also outperforms SEINE (FVD 245→95 on MSR-VTT), I2VGen-XL (289→95), and SparseCtrl (311→95) on MSR-VTT, and beats all of them on UCF-101 as well. On UCF-101, ConsistI2V (FVD 202) and SVD (FVD 236) achieve lower FVD than FrameBridge-VideoCrafter (258), but ConsistI2V used 32.64M iterated videos (5× more) and SVD is a training-free method; FrameBridge-CogVideoX (FVD 235) matches or beats both. In the non-zero-shot setting on UCF-101, FrameBridge (FVD 154) and FrameBridge-NP (FVD 122) both outperform VDT-I2V (FVD 171), their direct architectural counterpart, and substantially outperform ExtDM (FVD 649). The SAF ablation confirms that without SNR alignment, fine-tuning a bridge model from a diffusion checkpoint yields no improvement over the diffusion baseline (FVD 176 vs 176 at 10k steps), while with SAF the same budget drops FVD to 141 at 5k steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrameBridge extends the data-to-data bridge model framework (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024; Liu et al., ICML 2023) from image-to-image translation and speech synthesis to the image-to-video domain, replacing the noise-to-data diffusion process with a frame-to-frames generative process. It builds on the Bridge-gmax schedule and parameterization from Chen et al. (2023c) for Schrödinger bridge TTS, and introduces two techniques—SNR-Aligned Fine-tuning (SAF) and neural prior—that are specific to the I2V setting. The paper explicitly departs from the dominant diffusion-based I2V paradigm (DynamiCrafter, SEINE, SVD) by arguing that the Gaussian noise prior is fundamentally mismatched with the task of animating a given image, and demonstrates that a deterministic image-based prior better preserves appearance details and temporal coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAF technique is practically significant because it enables efficient fine-tuning of existing pre-trained T2V diffusion models (VideoCrafter, CogVideoX) into bridge-based I2V models without retraining from scratch, lowering the barrier to adopting the bridge framework. The neural prior, despite being a simple one-step regression, demonstrates that narrowing the prior-to-target gap is a general and effective principle for bridge-based generation. Limitations include evaluation only at 256×256 resolution with 16 frames, and the neural prior remains a coarse estimation; open questions include whether the approach scales to longer/higher-resolution videos, whether more sophisticated priors (e.g., multi-step or learned motion fields) yield further gains, and how the method interacts with the known dynamic-degree-vs-consistency trade-off in I2V.
