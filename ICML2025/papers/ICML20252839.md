## Problem

Prior work attributes shortcut learning to biases in superficial features (individual words or pixels), but this paper demonstrates that imbalances in the semantic distribution of sample embeddings within pretrained model latent spaces also induce spurious correlations that degrade out-of-distribution generalization. Specifically, when semantically similar samples form clusters with skewed label distributions, classifiers learn to exploit cluster membership as a shortcut rather than relying on truly discriminative features.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard text (sentences/reviews) or RGB images (X-ray scans, handwritten digits) and produces a binary or multi-class label. The key architectural detail is that raw inputs are first encoded by a frozen pretrained model (BERT, RoBERTa, LLaMA 3.2 for text; ViT, Swin, DINOv2 for images) into fixed-dimensional embeddings, and the SCISSOR debiasing module—a single Transformer block with 8 attention heads and 768 hidden units—operates exclusively on these embeddings before a linear classification head produces the final label. No data augmentation, rewriting, or modification of the pretrained backbone is required.

## Method

SCISSOR first applies Markov Clustering (MCL) to the pretrained model's embeddings to identify clusters with imbalanced label distributions, then constructs contrastive quadruplets (anchor, positive, intermediate, negative) where the intermediate sample shares both the label and cluster of the anchor. A lightweight shared-parameter Siamese network is trained with a modified triplet loss that enforces both inter-cluster contrast (separating different labels) and intra-cluster contrast (separating same-label samples within the same cluster via the intermediate sample), thereby disrupting shortcut-inducing cluster geometry. The debiasing module is inserted at the output of the frozen pretrained model and trained jointly with a linear classification head using an alternating strategy: clustering is re-run periodically as embeddings shift, with convergence monitored via the Hopkins Statistic stabilizing near 0.5. This plug-and-play design preserves the time complexity of the base model and requires no data augmentation or LLM-based rewriting.

## Results

SCISSOR achieves +5.3 absolute F1 points on GYAFC (BERT: 0.70→0.78), +7.3 on Yelp (BERT: 0.81→0.91), +7.7 on Chest-XRay (ViT: 0.72→0.84), and +1 on Not-MNIST (ViT: 0.89→0.91) over the respective baselines. The largest single-model gain is ViT on Chest-XRay, where accuracy jumps from 72.38% to 83.92% (+11.5 points). On NLP, BERT with SCISSOR reaches 90.65% accuracy on Yelp, surpassing the next-best method (IRM at 87.29%) by 3.4 points. Across all six models and four benchmarks, SCISSOR consistently achieves the highest accuracy and F1 score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCISSOR outperforms all three baselines (IRM, RAZOR, LC) across every model and dataset in both NLP and CV. Against IRM, SCISSOR gains +0.4 to +4.1 points in accuracy on NLP (e.g., BERT Yelp: 90.65 vs. 87.29) and +3.5 to +11.7 points on CV (e.g., ViT Chest-XRay: 83.92 vs. 80.47); notably, IRM actually degrades performance below the unmodified baseline on Swin/Chest-XRay (76.92 vs. 84.54) and LLaMA/GYAFC (83.65 vs. 89.40). Against RAZOR (NLP only), SCISSOR leads by +5.4 to +8.5 points in accuracy (e.g., RoBERTa Yelp: 87.79 vs. 76.36), while RAZOR slightly hurts LLaMA on GYAFC (87.00 vs. 89.40 baseline). Against LC (CV only), SCISSOR wins by +1.2 to +8.9 points (e.g., Swin Chest-XRay: 88.65 vs. 79.75), with LC also underperforming the raw baseline on DINOv2/Not-MNIST (83.63 vs. 85.40). The gains are most pronounced for smaller models (BERT, ViT), consistent with the paper's finding that larger models like LLaMA are naturally more resistant to semantic shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCISSOR extends the Siamese network and triplet loss framework (Bromley et al., 1993; Schroff et al., 2015) by introducing cluster-aware intermediate samples that disrupt semantic shortcuts, and leverages Markov Clustering (Van Dongen, 2008) to identify imbalanced regions in the embedding space. It departs from prior debiasing work (RAZOR, LC, IRM, counterfactual generation) that targets superficial features or manipulates training data, instead remapping the latent semantic space directly via a lightweight plug-and-play module on a frozen pretrained model. The key conceptual departure is treating imbalanced semantic cluster distributions—as opposed to surface-level word or pixel biases—as the root cause of shortcut learning, a mechanism the authors argue has been overlooked in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying semantic cluster imbalance as a distinct source of shortcut learning, the paper opens a new axis for robustness research beyond data augmentation and logit correction, offering a practical plug-and-play solution that avoids LLM-dependent rewriting. However, the evaluation is limited to four binary-classification datasets of modest size, and the Hopkins statistics reported (often 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁴) raise questions about whether the detected clusters are semantically meaningful or near-noise. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-class settings, larger-scale benchmarks, and whether the alternating clustering-remapping loop converges reliably in practice.
