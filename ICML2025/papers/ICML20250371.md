## Problem

Quantization of LLMs is known to degrade safety, but prior work has only examined a handful of calibration dataset-free methods, leaving open the question of how different quantization techniques (PTQ vs. QAT) and calibration datasets (benign vs. harmful) differentially impact safety. The paper asks: to what extent do these factors degrade safety, and how can the degradation be mitigated without sacrificing the utility gains that motivate quantization in the first place?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250371/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a quantized LLM (produced by AWQ, AQLM, LLM-QAT, or QLoRA at INT4 or INT8), the corresponding full-precision pre-quantization LLM, and a calibration dataset of prompts. It outputs a safety-patched quantized LLM where the weight update is constrained to a low-rank LoRA structure (rank r=2048) applied only to a sparse subset of safety-critical weights, keeping the remaining quantized weights bit-identical to the input. The safety-patching dataset is constructed on-the-fly by generating responses from both LLMs on calibration prompts, forming preference triplets (x, y_w, y_l) without any manual annotation.

## Method

Q-resafe restores safety through three coupled mechanisms. First, it constructs a safety-patching dataset by feeding calibration prompts to both the pre-quantization LLM (winner) and the quantized LLM (loser), framing safety restoration as knowledge distillation from the aligned full-precision model. Second, it optimizes a DPO loss that re-aligns the quantized model's response distribution toward the pre-quantization model's, with the DPO reference model being the original quantized weights to prevent utility drift. Third, it restricts all parameter updates to a sparse subset of safety-critical weights identified via SNIP importance scores (top-τ percentile), parameterized through a masked LoRA pair (A, B) so that the update takes the form Q = Q^0 + Quant(M_Q ⊙ AB). The safety-critical mask is periodically re-evaluated every K iterations to track shifting critical weights, and the entire procedure requires only ~1.2 GPU-hours versus 8–10 hours for full DPO fine-tuning.

## Results

Q-resafe restores safety of quantized LLMs to near pre-quantization levels across all tested scenarios. On benign datasets (Risk-I), Q-resafe increases ASR by only 1.5% (Llama-2-7B-Chat) and 0.9% (Gemma-7B-Instruct), compared to 16.6% and 11.5% for baseline quantization methods. On directly harmful datasets (Risk-III), Q-resafe limits ASR increase to 13.6% (Llama) and 1.8% (Gemma), versus up to 92.3% and 66.7% for baselines. In terms of efficiency, Q-resafe requires only 1.2 GPU hours versus 8.4 hours for SFT and 9.6 hours for DPO while achieving comparable or better safety (1.6% ASR vs. 12.4% for SFT and 1.5% for DPO on LLM-QAT 4-bit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-resafe dramatically outperforms SFT-based safety patching: on QLoRA 4-bit, Q-resafe achieves 2.4% ASR versus 26.9% for SFT (an 11x reduction), and on LLM-QAT 4-bit, 1.6% versus 12.4% for SFT. Against DPO, Q-resafe matches safety performance (1.6% vs. 1.5% ASR on LLM-QAT; 2.4% vs. 2.4% on QLoRA) while being 8x faster (1.2 GPU hours vs. 9.6 hours for LLM-QAT+DPO) and 3x faster than QLoRA+DPO (1.2h vs. 3.8h). Compared to the underlying quantization baselines, Q-resafe reduces ASR from 42.3% (QLoRA 4-bit, benign) to 2.4%, from 16.9% (LLM-QAT 4-bit, benign) to 1.6%, and from 18.5% (AQLM 4-bit, benign) to 1.8%. For the fine-tuning-free AWQ setting, Q-resafe (which keeps safety-critical weights at 16-bit) reduces ASR from 42.4% to 30.6% (Llama 4-bit) under decoding attack, versus the FP16 baseline of 29.8%. Across bit-widths (Table 7), Q-resafe maintains the lowest ASR at every precision level: 1.6% (8-bit), 1.8% (4-bit), 5.9% (3-bit), and 12.4% (2-bit), outperforming AQLM, LLM-QAT, QLoRA, and AWQ at all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established techniques—DPO for preference-based alignment, LoRA for low-rank parameterization, and SNIP for weight importance scoring—into a unified safety-patching framework for quantized LLMs. It extends the nascent line of safety evaluation for quantized models (Hong et al. 2024, Belkhiter et al. 2024, Egashira et al. 2024), which focused almost exclusively on calibration-dataset-free PTQ methods, to a systematic assessment covering both PTQ and QAT with three risk levels of calibration data. The key conceptual departure from prior safety-alignment work is the constraint that updates must respect the low-precision quantized weight format while touching only a small subset of safety-critical weights, rather than performing full-parameter fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As quantized LLMs become the default for edge and resource-constrained deployment, the finding that even benign calibration datasets cause substantial safety degradation (ASR rising from ~0.3% to 18–42%) is a practical warning for practitioners who assume utility-focused fine-tuning is safety-neutral. Q-resafe's efficiency (1.2 GPU-hours vs. 3.4–9.6 for SFT/DPO baselines) makes safety restoration feasible in the same resource-constrained settings that motivate quantization in the first place. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B models and a small set of quantization methods; the authors themselves identify safety-in-mind QAT (integrating safety objectives during quantization rather than post-hoc patching) as the more fundamental but open direction.
