## Problem

The only known adaptive experimental design with sublinear Neyman regret in the finite-population setting (ClipOGD) achieves only Õ(√T) regret, and it is unclear whether better rates are attainable. Additionally, no existing adaptive design leverages pre-treatment covariates to provide simultaneous efficiency guarantees across multiple predefined subgroups of experimental units.

## Input / Output

The method processes experimental units sequentially: at each round it receives a realized outcome (and optionally a pre-treatment covariate vector) and outputs a treatment probability p_t ∈ (0,1) used to flip a Bernoulli coin for assignment. The resulting adaptive IPW estimator is unbiased for the ATE, and the design's quality is measured by its Neyman regret—the excess variance over the best fixed-probability nonadaptive design. In the contextual setting, the method additionally takes a finite family of (possibly overlapping) covariate-defined groups and guarantees sublinear regret simultaneously on every group's subsequence.

## Method

The non-contextual design ClipOGD^SC modifies ClipOGD by switching from a constant to a decreasing learning rate η_t = 1/(2c²t), which exploits the strong convexity of the per-round Neyman objective f_t(p) = y_t(1)²/p + y_t(0)²/(1−p) under a lower-bound assumption on outcomes, yielding an anytime Õ(log T) regret bound via Hazan-style strongly convex OGD analysis. The contextual design MGATE wraps |G| copies of ClipOGD^SC (one per group) inside a scale-free sleeping experts aggregator built from SOLO FTRL and Orabona's black-box SE-to-OLO reduction; each round it computes an effective treatment probability as a convex combination of active groups' propensities weighted by the aggregator, then updates group propensities with unbiased first-order gradient estimators and updates aggregator weights with scale-free surrogate losses. This decomposition bounds the multigroup regret as the sum of the sleeping experts regret (Õ(√T)·√|G|) and the per-group ClipOGD^SC regret, giving Õ(√T) multigroup Neyman regret without requiring advance knowledge of the horizon.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that ClipOGD^SC achieves an anytime-valid Õ(log T) Neyman regret bound (Theorem 3.2), improving upon the prior Õ(√T) bound of ClipOGD^0 (Dai et al., 2023). In the contextual setting, MGATE achieves Õ(√T) multigroup Neyman regret simultaneously across all predefined groups (Theorem 4.2). Empirically, on synthetic Gaussian data (T=50,000), ClipOGD^SC's regret drops to near zero for σ=0.1 while ClipOGD^0's regret keeps increasing; on the Egypt microfinance dataset (T≈15,000), ClipOGD^SC converges to the Neyman-optimal probability with vanishing regret, whereas ClipOGD^0 exhibits higher variance and non-converging regret. In the multigroup experiment, MGATE achieves the lowest group-conditional Neyman regret across all three overlapping groups, dominating both ClipOGD variants including on the full-population group G₀.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ClipOGD^0 from Dai et al. (2023), the first adaptive design with sublinear Neyman regret. ClipOGD^SC decisively outperforms ClipOGD^0 on every metric examined: it achieves a logarithmic (vs. square-root) regret rate, is anytime-valid (no need to know T upfront), and its treatment probabilities converge in L₂ to the Neyman-optimal probability, while ClipOGD^0's propensities oscillate and only converge in the special case where the optimal probability equals the initialization (0.5). On the microfinance data, ClipOGD^SC's regret vanishes over time while ClipOGD^0's regret plateaus at a positive level. In the contextual multigroup setting, MGATE (which uses ClipOGD^SC as its per-group base learner) achieves the lowest group-conditional regret on every group simultaneously, outperforming both ClipOGD^0 and ClipOGD^SC applied non-contextually, validating the theoretical Õ(√T) multigroup guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ClipOGD (Dai et al., 2023), the first adaptive design achieving sublinear Neyman regret in the finite-population setting, by exploiting the strong convexity of the Neyman objective under a stronger lower-bound assumption on outcomes to replace the O~(√T) regret with O~(log T). For the contextual setting, it introduces a multigroup formulation and builds on the sleeping experts framework (Blum & Lykouris, 2020) combined with scale-free online learning (Orabona & Pál, 2018) and a recent OLO-to-SE reduction (Orabona, 2024) to handle overlapping groups with stochastic feedback and unbounded gradient magnitudes. The work also connects to the broader GATE estimation literature (Chernozhukov et al., 2017) and multiaccuracy (Kern et al., 2024), though it differs in targeting simultaneous efficiency guarantees for arbitrary predefined overlapping groups rather than learning data-driven disjoint partitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O~(log T) regret bound challenges the conjecture by Dai et al. (2023) that O~(√T) is the minimax Neyman rate, showing that under natural boundedness assumptions the gap to the optimal non-adaptive design shrinks near-logarithmically. The multigroup formulation is practically relevant for subgroup analysis in RCTs (e.g., ensuring efficiency for demographic subgroups simultaneously), but the O~(√T) multigroup rate leaves open whether the logarithmic improvement can be extended to the contextual setting. Key limitations include the need for a lower bound on outcomes (excluding near-zero outcomes), the requirement that groups be predefined, and the restriction to the sequential finite-population setting; future work could explore relaxing these assumptions, improving the multigroup rate, and extending to batched or non-sequential designs.
