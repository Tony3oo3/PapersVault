## Problem

Existing QA and reasoning benchmarks are static, making them vulnerable to data contamination and memorization, and they conflate reasoning ability with factual recall. No prior framework generates fresh, unique evaluation instances on demand while independently controlling reasoning difficulty (number of logical steps) and retrieval difficulty (corpus size), which is essential for disentangling these two capabilities in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a corpus of templated Wikipedia-like articles (~160 tokens each) about fictional characters plus a single CFG-generated question; the Prolog database and underlying graphs are held out. The model must output a comma-separated list of names or attribute values as the answer, scored with answer-level F1. Corpus size ranges from 50 to 10K+ characters (exceeding all current context windows at the upper end), and question difficulty is controlled by CFG recursion depth (up to 20) and the complexity of sampled relations (e.g., 'nephew' requires 2 reasoning steps, 'second cousin' requires 5).

## Method

PhantomWiki is a fully algorithmic, on-demand pipeline with two stages. Stage 1 generates a random fictional universe: family trees (following Hohenecker & Lukasiewicz), an Erdős-Rényi friendship graph, and per-character personal facts (name, date of birth, job from 300 options, hobby from 600 options), all rendered into short templated articles. Stage 2 generates questions via a context-free grammar whose recursion depth d yields Θ(d) nested templates (e.g., 'Who is the nephew of the friend of the brother of David?'), and computes exhaustive ground-truth answers by translating each question into a Prolog query over the universe's fact and rule base. The universe space scales as Θ(2^(n²)·c^n), making memorization infeasible. Evaluation spans three prompting regimes—full in-context (zero-shot and chain-of-thought), BM25-based RAG (top-4 documents), and agentic ReAct (interleaved RetrieveArticle/Search tool calls up to 50 steps)—allowing independent stress-testing of reasoning and retrieval as universe size and question complexity vary.

## Results

On the small-universe setting (n=50, ~16K tokens), the best F1 score is 52.42±2.64% (DeepSeek-R1-32B with COT in-context prompting), followed by GPT-4o COT at 50.66%. At medium scale (n=500), GPT-4o COT leads at 41.02%, while at large scale (n=5000, exceeding all context windows), agentic REACT with GPT-4o achieves the best F1 of 36.85%. Across all settings, F1 scores drop sharply as the number of reasoning steps increases, with near-zero performance on questions requiring 5+ reasoning steps under RAG prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is across prompting paradigms rather than competing methods. At small scale (n=50), in-context COT dominates: DeepSeek-R1-32B COT (52.42%) and GPT-4o COT (50.66%) far outperform RAG variants (~20–28%) and agentic REACT (5–39%). At large scale (n=5000), where in-context prompting is infeasible, agentic REACT clearly wins—GPT-4o REACT reaches 36.85% F1 versus 18.13% for ZERO SHOT-RAG and 14.25% for COT-RAG. In the multi-hop RAG ablation (Table 4, Appendix D.1), IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023) achieves 23.93% F1 at n=50, outperforming COT-RAG (20.01%), ZERO SHOT-RAG (17.55%), and Self-Ask (Press et al., 2023) at 15.77%. However, even IRCoT struggles on questions requiring many reasoning steps, confirming that static or semi-dynamic retrieval remains insufficient for complex multi-hop QA. The paper concludes that agentic workflows with dynamic retrieval are the most promising approach at scale, though all methods degrade sharply with increasing reasoning depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhantomWiki extends the CLUTRR paradigm of reasoning over relational graphs embedded in text, but replaces crowd-sourced stories with a fully algorithmic, on-demand generation pipeline using context-free grammars for question synthesis and Prolog for exhaustive answer verification. It departs from static multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA) by generating a fresh, unique fictional universe per evaluation, thereby eliminating data contamination without requiring manual curation. The family-tree generation follows Hohenecker & Lukasiewicz (2020), and the agentic evaluation leverages ReAct-style tool use, but the core contribution is the combination of CFG-based question templating with logic-programming-based ground-truth generation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhantomWiki addresses a growing and fundamental problem in LLM evaluation: static benchmarks become unreliable as models memorize their contents during pre-training. By generating unique instances on demand with zero LLM cost, it provides a scalable, contamination-resistant stress test that cleanly disentangles reasoning difficulty (via CFG recursion depth) from retrieval difficulty (via universe size). Key open questions include whether the minimalistic templated articles understate real-world retrieval challenges, whether the benchmark can be extended to temporal or multimodal settings, and whether consistency-preserving LLM rephrasing can increase realism without introducing hallucinations. The finding that even frontier models with full in-context access struggle sharply beyond ~5 reasoning steps suggests a genuine reasoning bottleneck that current scaling has not resolved.
