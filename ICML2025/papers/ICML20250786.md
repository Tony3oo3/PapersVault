## Problem

Iterative parallel decoding methods like MaskGIT use the same-sized transformer for every decoding step, incurring redundant computation since early iterations only need to capture coarse global structure while later iterations require fine-grained refinement. Additionally, unlike autoregressive models that cache intermediate computations, parallel decoding recomputes all tokens at every step. The paper asks whether compute can be allocated non-uniformly across decoding iterations to achieve significant efficiency gains without sacrificing generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250786/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the method takes a class label and produces a 256×256 or 128×128 RGB image, operating on 16×16 discrete VQ tokens (codebook size 1024) from pretrained MaskGIT tokenizers. For video generation on UCF101, it takes a class label and produces 16-frame 128×128 videos from 4×16×16 tokens via MAGVIT tokenizers. For frame prediction on Kinetics600, it takes 5 conditioning frames (512 tokens) and predicts the remaining 5 frames (512 tokens), with conditioned tokens always cached and excluded from processing.

## Method

MaGNeTS combines three mechanisms: (1) decode-time model scheduling using nested transformers extracted via MatFormer-style partial computation in both self-attention (Q, K, V projections) and MLP layers, with downscaling factors p∈{1,2,4,8} yielding four models that share all parameters; (2) KV caching of unmasked tokens across iterations so that only newly masked tokens are processed each step, with cache refresh at model-size transitions to handle query/KV dimension mismatches; (3) progressive distillation training where each smaller nested model is trained with a linearly annealed combination of ground-truth loss and distillation loss from the next larger model. The scheduling starts with the smallest model (p=8) for early iterations handling background/coarse structure and progressively upscales to the full model (p=1) for final iterations refining fine details, achieving ~3× compute reduction with competitive FID.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MaGNeTS achieves FID 3.1 (12 steps) and FID 2.9 (16 steps) with 490 and 608 GFLOPs respectively, compared to MaskGIT++'s FID 2.5 and 2.3 at 1.3k and 1.8k GFLOPs — a ~2.65–3× compute reduction with a modest FID increase. On UCF101 video generation, MaGNeTS reaches FVD 96.4 with ~1.7k GFLOPs versus MAGVIT-L's FVD 74.4 at ~4.3k GFLOPs (~2.5× savings). On Kinetics600 frame prediction, MaGNeTS achieves FVD 10.8 with ~1.2k GFLOPs versus MAGVIT-L's FVD 7.2 at ~4.3k GFLOPs (~3.7× savings). Real-time inference on a single TPUv5 shows 56.3 images/sec (285 ms latency) versus 22.5 images/sec (712 ms) for MaskGIT++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baseline MaskGIT++, MaGNeTS trades a small FID increase (3.1 vs 2.5 at 12 steps; 2.9 vs 2.3 at 16 steps) for a 2.65–3× reduction in inference GFLOPs (490 vs 1.3k; 608 vs 1.8k), while matching or exceeding precision (85 vs 83 at 12 steps). Compared to the stronger parallel-decoding method MaskBit (FID 1.7, 10.3k GFLOPs, 64 steps), MaGNeTS uses ~21× less compute but at the cost of ~0.6 FID. Against diffusion-based SOTA like DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.3, 59.5k GFLOPs, 250 steps) and LDM-4 (FID 3.6, 51.5k GFLOPs), MaGNeTS achieves competitive FID with 80–120× less compute. On UCF101 video generation, MaGNeTS (FVD 96.4, ~1.7k GFLOPs) uses ~2.5× less compute than MAGVIT-L (FVD 74.4, ~4.3k GFLOPs) with a moderate FVD gap, and on Kinetics600 frame prediction achieves FVD 10.8 with ~3.7× less compute than MAGVIT-L (FVD 7.2). The paper also shows preliminary diffusion experiments (U-ViT on ImageNet 64×64) where model scheduling alone yields ~1.53× compute gains, confirming generality beyond discrete-token parallel decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate ideas: MatFormer's nested transformer architecture (which extracts multiple model sizes from a single parameter set via partial computation) and MaskGIT/MAGVIT's iterative parallel decoding for discrete token generation. The key departure is applying nested models not for elastic inference at a fixed step, but as a decode-time schedule where model size progressively increases across decoding iterations, motivated by the observation that early iterations resolve coarse background structure while later iterations refine fine details. Additionally, the paper introduces KV-caching into parallel decoding (previously a technique exclusive to autoregressive models) and extends MatFormer's nesting from MLP layers to self-attention Q/K/V projections, enabling near-linear compute reduction with the downscaling factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~3× compute reduction with only a modest FID degradation (2.5→3.1 on ImageNet 256×256) is practically significant for anyone deploying iterative generative models, and the technique is orthogonal to tokenizer choice and sampling scheme, making it broadly applicable. The KV-caching mechanism for parallel decoding is a genuinely new technique that could be adopted by other masked/parallel generation frameworks. Open questions include whether token-dependent (rather than iteration-dependent) model schedules can yield further gains, how to eliminate the cache-refresh overhead without quality loss, and whether the approach extends cleanly to continuous-space diffusion models (preliminary U-ViT experiments show ~1.5× gains without nesting training). The method's reliance on discrete tokens for effective caching limits its immediate applicability to pixel-space or continuous-latent diffusion models.
