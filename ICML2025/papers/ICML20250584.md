## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models are increasingly used as priors for Bayesian inverse problems, but existing posterior sampling methods (DDRM, DAPS, DCPS) rely on various approximations and lack asymptotic exactness guarantees, making it unclear how well they approximate the true posterior. While Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) offers a principled framework with consistency guarantees, it had not been combined with the recently proposed 'decoupled diffusion' technique, which enables larger per-step updates by reconstructing x_0 and re-noising rather than taking small backward steps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion score network s_θ (no retraining required), a linear-Gaussian measurement model y = Ax_0 + σ_y ε, and optionally a reconstruction function f_θ (either Tweedie's formula or a PF-ODE solver). The linear operator A can be non-diagonal; in that case the algorithm operates in the SVD basis (A = USV^T) to reduce to the diagonal case. Output is either a single posterior sample x_0 or the full set of N weighted particles, with the number of particles N and diffusion steps T as the main computational knobs.

## Method

DDSMC redefines the diffusion prior over trajectories using a 'decoupled' backward kernel: at each step it reconstructs x_0 from the current state x_{t+1} (via Tweedie's formula or PF-ODE) and then samples x_t from the forward kernel q(x_t | x_0), rather than the standard one-step backward transition. A hyperparameter η smoothly interpolates between full decoupling (η=0, DAPS prior) and the standard diffusion prior (η=1). The SMC target at time t uses a Gaussian-approximated likelihood p̃(y|x_t) = N(y | Af_θ(x_t), σ_y²I + ρ_t²AA^T), while the proposal incorporates the measurement by computing a closed-form approximate posterior over x_0 given x_{t+1} and y, then pushing forward through the (decoupled) forward kernel. Because the final target at t=0 uses the exact likelihood p(y|x_0), the algorithm is asymptotically exact regardless of the approximations used at intermediate steps. A discrete extension (D3SMC) is also derived for D3PM-based models with categorical transition-matrix measurements.

## Results

On the Gaussian mixture model (GMM) benchmark, DDSMC achieves the lowest sliced Wasserstein distance across all dimensionality and observation settings, e.g., 0.88±0.33 (d_x=8, d_y=1) and 0.16±0.10 (d_x=800, d_y=4), substantially outperforming the next-best method MCGDiff (1.79 and 1.21 respectively). On FFHQ image restoration (LPIPS, lower is better), DDSMC with PF-ODE reconstruction and η=0.5 achieves 0.05/0.23/0.15 on inpainting/outpainting/super-resolution, competitive with DCPS (0.03/0.20/0.10) and DDRM (0.04/0.25/0.20). On protein structure completion (ATAD2), DDSMC matches or exceeds ADP-3D in RMSD, particularly at high noise levels (σ=0.5), with clear gains from increasing particle count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSMC decisively outperforms all five baselines on the GMM posterior approximation task (sliced Wasserstein distance), with margins of 2–10× over MCGDiff, DCPS, DDRM, and DAPS, and TDS is both worse and unstable (crashes on some seeds). The gap is largest in high dimensions (d_x=800), where DDSMC achieves 0.16±0.10 versus 1.21±0.94 for MCGDiff and 9.21±0.83 for DAPS. On FFHQ image restoration (LPIPS), DDSMC is competitive but not uniformly best: DCPS edges it out on inpainting (0.03 vs 0.05) and super-resolution (0.10 vs 0.15), while DDRM is comparable on inpainting (0.04 vs 0.05); MCGDiff, the other SMC method with the same asymptotic guarantees, is consistently worse (0.10/0.34/0.15). On protein structure completion, DDSMC matches or slightly beats ADP-3D in RMSD, with the advantage growing at higher noise levels (σ=0.5) and with more particles. The authors note that methods excelling on image LPIPS (e.g., DCPS) perform poorly on the GMM task, highlighting a gap between perceptual image quality and true posterior approximation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate research threads: the decoupled diffusion (DAPS) approach of Zhang et al. (2024), which enables larger sample updates by reconstructing x₀ and re-noising rather than stepping backward one diffusion step, and the Sequential Monte Carlo framework (Naesseth et al., 2019) for asymptotically exact posterior sampling. Unlike prior SMC-based diffusion methods (TDS, MCGDiff, SMCDiff, FPS), DDSMC uses the DAPS decoupling as its prior over trajectories and introduces a generalized inverse-temperature parameter η that smoothly interpolates between full decoupling (η=0) and standard diffusion (η=1). The Gaussian likelihood approximation from Π-GDM (Song et al., 2023) is repurposed to construct both the intermediate targets and the proposal, yielding closed-form Gaussian proposals in the linear-Gaussian setting without requiring gradient computation through the reconstruction network (unlike TDS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDSMC provides one of the first diffusion-prior posterior sampling methods with rigorous asymptotic exactness guarantees while also leveraging the decoupling mechanism to make larger, more global corrections—addressing a known weakness of standard diffusion-based samplers in high dimensions. A notable finding is the gap between perceptual image quality (LPIPS) and true posterior approximation quality (sliced Wasserstein distance): methods like DCPS that excel on LPIPS perform poorly on the GMM benchmark where the posterior is known in closed form, suggesting that common image metrics may not reflect posterior fidelity. Open questions include extending the method to non-linear-Gaussian likelihoods, understanding the optimal interplay between η, reconstruction strategy (Tweedie vs. PF-ODE), and data dimensionality, and whether the SMC particle overhead can be reduced while maintaining the asymptotic guarantees.
