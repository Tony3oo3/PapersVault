## Problem

Multi-kernel clustering (MKC) methods, widely deployed in disease diagnosis and hyperspectral image classification, have never been systematically evaluated against adversarial perturbations in a black-box setting. Two key obstacles make this difficult: clustering is unsupervised and inherently unstable (due to K-Means random initialization), complicating perturbation optimization, and each clustering evaluation is computationally expensive, making iterative attack search infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view dataset (N samples across N_d views with heterogeneous feature dimensions, e.g., 24–3703 per view) and treats the victim MKC method as a black box. The attacker perturbs a subset of samples (default 10%) in a subset of views (default 50%), with each perturbation constrained to an ℓ2 norm of ε = 0.1√d per feature vector. The output is a per-view, per-sample perturbation matrix that, when injected into the original data, degrades clustering metrics (NMI, ARI, ACC, PR) while remaining imperceptible.

## Method

AdvMKC formulates adversarial perturbation generation as a sequential decision-making problem solved with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), where the advantage function mitigates clustering instability by estimating reward relative to a learned baseline rather than relying on absolute clustering scores. The key architectural innovation is a generator-clusterer framework: an encoder-decoder generator (with per-view encoders and per-perturbed-view decoders) produces perturbations, while a separate learned clusterer approximates the victim MKC's latent representations and clustering outcomes, providing dense reward signals without repeatedly invoking the expensive black-box MKC. The reward at each step is the average cosine similarity of samples co-assigned to the same cluster, and the overall loss combines the PPO clipped surrogate objective, a clusterer regression loss, an entropy bonus for exploration, and a magnitude regularization term. Theoretically, the paper derives a bound showing that the deviation in MKKM kernel weights after T reduced-gradient iterations is proportional to the maximum spectral norm of injected kernel noise, linking perturbation magnitude and sample proportion directly to clustering degradation.

## Results

Across seven datasets and eleven MKC methods, AdvMKC consistently degrades clustering performance more than both baselines (RAMKC and EAMKC). On MSRCv1, attacking EEOMVC reduces NMI from 65.76% (no-attack) to 53.18% with AdvMKC, compared to 58.92% (RAMKC) and 56.65% (EAMKC). On ProteinFold, AdvMKC drops SMKKM's NMI from 39.97% to 28.68%, outperforming RAMKC (31.47%) and EAMKC (29.42%). Against robust MKC methods, AdvMKC reduces JMKSC's NMI on MSRCv1 from 4.87% to 1.63%, and MKCDNM's NMI from 89.77% to 85.70%, demonstrating that even noise-robust methods remain vulnerable to targeted adversarial perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three attack strategies—AdvMKC (proposed), RAMKC (random Gaussian noise), and EAMKC (evolutionary-optimized noise)—against eleven MKC victim methods. AdvMKC achieves the lowest clustering metrics (i.e., greatest attack success) in the majority of settings: on MSRCv1 it reduces EEOMVC's NMI by 12.58 points vs. 6.84 (RAMKC) and 9.11 (EAMKC), and on Citeseer it drops LSMKC's NMI from 19.33% to 14.27%, far exceeding RAMKC's 16.07% and EAMKC's 23.32%. Against robust MKC methods (JMKSC, ONMSC, MKCDNM, MKSSC-ERC), AdvMKC still degrades performance meaningfully—for example, reducing MKCDNM's NMI on MSRCv1 from 89.77% to 85.70%—while RAMKC often has negligible or even slightly positive effects (e.g., MKCDNM NMI rises to 91.74% under RAMKC). Transferability experiments show that perturbations generated against a surrogate MKC method (e.g., JMKSC) still reduce the victim SMKKM's NMI by ~5 points on MSRCv1, confirming cross-method generalization. Overall, AdvMKC dominates both baselines on most dataset–method pairs, with the largest margins on structured datasets like ProteinFold and Citeseer where cluster boundaries are well-defined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on clustering (which has focused on K-Means, DBSCAN, and hierarchical methods in feature space) to the kernel-based multi-kernel clustering paradigm, where the attack target is the kernel matrix rather than raw feature vectors. It departs from the only prior adversarial attack on multi-view clustering (Huang et al., 2024), which used GANs for deep multi-view methods and is inapplicable to MKC due to the absence of differentiable feedback. The RL formulation builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and the advantage function, while the theoretical analysis leverages the reduced gradient method from SimpleMKL (Rakotomamonjy et al., 2008) to bound perturbation impact on kernel weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of MKC adversarial robustness, revealing that methods explicitly designed to handle noisy data (e.g., JMKSC, ONMSC) remain vulnerable to targeted adversarial perturbations because they assume Gaussian noise distributions. The transferability finding—that perturbations optimized against one MKC method degrade others—has practical security implications for deployments where the specific clustering algorithm is unknown. Open questions include whether effective defenses exist for MKC, whether the theoretical bounds are tight in practice, and how the approach scales to very large datasets where even the surrogate clusterer becomes expensive.
