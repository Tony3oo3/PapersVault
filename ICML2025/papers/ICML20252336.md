## Problem

In continual reinforcement learning, agents suffer from plasticity loss — a gradual inability to adapt to new tasks — but the causal mechanism behind this phenomenon remained unclear. While NTK rank decrease has been identified as a correlated indicator, no prior work established a direct causal link between a concrete training artifact and the pathological learning dynamics. This paper identifies churn (unintended network output changes on out-of-batch data) as the mechanism that, through a vicious cycle with NTK rank collapse, drives plasticity loss.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RL inputs: state vectors (Gym Control, DMC) or 64×64 RGB frames (ProcGen, MinAtar) paired with a sequential task stream, and outputs actions via the base policy. A key practical detail is that C-CHAIN requires no cross-task buffer and no awareness of task boundaries — both the training batch B_train and the reference batch B_ref are sampled from the same online interaction data collected in the current iteration, making it a drop-in regularization layer on top of any off-the-shelf RL algorithm (PPO, DoubleDQN).

## Method

C-CHAIN adds a churn reduction loss L_cr(θ) = ½ E[C_f(x̄, θ, Δθ)²] over a reference batch B_ref (disjoint from B_train) alongside the regular RL objective, where C_f is the first-order approximation of the network output change on reference data induced by the parameter update Δθ. The gradient of this loss decomposes into two mechanistically distinct terms: a gradient decorrelation term that suppresses off-diagonal NTK entries (preventing rank collapse and maintaining plasticity across task switches), and a step-size adjustment term that projects the regular gradient onto the reference-data gradient direction, adaptively scaling the effective update. The regularization coefficient λ is auto-adjusted via a running-mean ratio to maintain a fixed relative scale β between the churn reduction term and the base RL loss, eliminating a manual hyperparameter. Empirically, the orthogonal (decorrelation) component contributes more than the projective (step-size) component, confirming that NTK off-diagonal suppression is the critical mechanism.

## Results

On continual ProcGen (16 environments, 5 tasks each, 2M steps per task), C-CHAIN achieves an aggregate score of 101.79, substantially outperforming the best baseline TRAC (77.29), LayerNorm (75.15), L2 Init (72.96), and the Oracle re-initialization baseline (70.79). On continual Gym Control (4 environments, 10 tasks each), C-CHAIN achieves an aggregate score of −221.22, beating L2 Init (−243.99), Weight Clipping (−290.99), and TRAC (−407.26). C-CHAIN also outperforms Vanilla PPO by large margins in all settings (e.g., 101.79 vs. 55.05 on ProcGen; −221.22 vs. −1156.96 on Gym Control), and in continual DMC continuous control it surpasses both Vanilla PPO and Oracle PPO across Walker, Quadruped, and Dog tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-CHAIN achieves the best aggregate score on both ProcGen (101.79) and Gym Control (−221.22), winning on 14 of 16 ProcGen environments. It most decisively outperforms TRAC (77.29) and L2 Init (72.96) on ProcGen, with particularly large gaps on Miner (+3.77 over L2 Init), Bigfish (+4.11 over TRAC), and Plunder (+0.62 over TRAC). On Gym Control, C-CHAIN trails L2 Init on CartPole (160.40 vs. 266.11) and LunarLander (−16.66 vs. −6.67), but dominates on MountainCar (−245.75 vs. −369.40 for L2 Init) where TRAC essentially fails (−399.99). Weight Clipping and ReDo perform near or below Vanilla on ProcGen, confirming that simple parameter-space constraints are insufficient for the procedural diversity of ProcGen. C-CHAIN also outperforms the Oracle (full re-initialization per task) baseline on ProcGen (101.79 vs. 70.79), demonstrating that churn reduction enables cross-task transfer that naive resetting cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the churn reduction framework of Tang & Berseth (NeurIPS 2024) from single-MDP RL to the continual RL setting, introducing C-CHAIN as a continual analogue. It uses the empirical Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) matrix as a formal bridge connecting two previously studied but separately treated phenomena: churn (output variability for out-of-batch data) and plasticity loss (NTK rank collapse, as characterized by Lyle et al., 2024). The method is positioned among a growing family of plasticity-loss remedies—dormant neuron recycling, weight clipping, L2-to-initialization regularization, and parameter-free optimizers like TRAC—but distinguishes itself by providing a mechanistic, NTK-grounded explanation for *why* churn reduction helps, identifying two distinct effects (gradient decorrelation and adaptive step-size adjustment) rather than treating it as a black-box regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean mechanistic account of the plasticity-loss vicious cycle (NTK rank decrease → gradient correlation → churn exacerbation → further rank decrease), which should guide future work on continual RL beyond ad-hoc regularizations. C-CHAIN is a simple, plug-and-play regularization that transfers across base algorithms (PPO, DoubleDQN) and environment types (discrete, continuous, image-based), making it practically accessible. However, its efficacy is notably weaker in continual supervised learning (MNIST), suggesting the churn-accumulation mechanism is specific to the sequential, non-stationary nature of RL; the authors also note that the Hessian-based term (Eq. 11) is not directly implemented, leaving room for more direct churn-reduction formulations. Open questions include applicability to robotics, LLM fine-tuning, and RLHF settings where plasticity loss is increasingly relevant.
