## Problem

Few-shot image classification suffers from limited labeled examples, and augmenting with synthetic data often degrades performance due to the distribution gap between real and synthetic samples. Existing approaches to closing this gap rely on heuristics (e.g., pixel-space distribution matching) without theoretical guarantees on how the discrepancy affects generalization. This paper derives a provable generalization bound that explicitly quantifies the roles of synthetic-data quality, distribution mismatch, and local model robustness, then uses it to guide both data generation and model training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of real labeled RGB images (16 shots per class) together with synthetic images generated by a LoRA-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 model (500 per class in the full version, 64 in the lightweight version), conditioned on the labels of the real samples. The CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder is fine-tuned via LoRA and produces a class-label prediction for each input image. A K-means partition of the combined real+synthetic feature space (typically 2× the number of classes) is computed once before training and used to define local regions for the regularization terms.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalization bound (Theorem 3.3) that upper-bounds the population loss of a predictor trained on real and synthetic data by three interpretable quantities: a model-dependent discrepancy between real and synthetic predictions, local robustness of the model around both data types, and a standard concentration term. Minimizing this bound yields a composite loss with (i) cross-entropy on real and synthetic data, (ii) an L2 discrepancy term penalizing prediction mismatch between real and synthetic samples within each K-means cluster, and (iii) an L2 robustness term penalizing prediction variance among synthetic samples within each cluster. The algorithm proceeds in two phases: first, K-means clustering (via FAISS) partitions the combined feature space to optimize the robustness term; second, the CLIP encoder is fine-tuned with the composite loss via AdamW. In the full version, the Stable Diffusion generator is first fine-tuned with LoRA following DataDream's procedure to reduce the real-synthetic gap before generating training images.

## Results

On 10 few-shot image classification benchmarks (16 real shots per class), the full version of the proposed method achieves 87.0% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline DataDream_dset (86.3%) by 0.7%. The lightweight variant (64 synthetic images per class, no generator fine-tuning) reaches 86.4%, matching DataDream_dset while using roughly 8× fewer synthetic samples. The largest gains appear on Food101 (+2.8% over DataDream_dset) and FGVC Aircraft (+2.0%), where distribution mismatch is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline DataDream_dset (86.3% avg), the full method wins on 7 of 10 datasets with margins ranging from +0.2% (SUN397) to +2.8% (Food101), while trailing by only 0.1–0.3% on the remaining three (ImageNet, Oxford Pets, Flowers102). Compared to DataDream_cls (85.7%), the gap widens to +1.3% on average. Against the earlier synthetic-data methods IsSynth (84.8%) and DISEF (84.7%), the improvement is +2.2–2.3% on average, with the largest single-dataset gain on FGVC Aircraft (+2.0% over DataDream_dset, +9.5% over IsSynth). The lightweight variant (no generator fine-tuning, 64 synthetic images/class) matches DataDream_dset at 86.4% while using ~8× fewer synthetic samples, demonstrating that the theoretical loss design—not the volume of generated data—drives the gains. On CLIP-ResNet50 (Table 3), the method also leads on 3 of 4 tested datasets, confirming architecture-agnostic benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot synthetic data augmentation line (DataDream, DISEF, IsSynth) by grounding the training procedure in a novel generalization bound that explicitly decomposes test error into distribution discrepancy, local robustness, and empirical loss terms. While DataDream and RealFake focus on pixel-space or feature-space distribution matching heuristically, and prior theoretical work (Zheng et al. 2023, Ildiz et al. 2025) addresses only linear models or full-dataset settings, this paper provides the first bound tailored to the few-shot regime with both real and synthetic samples, and derives a concrete K-means-partitioned training algorithm from it. The generator fine-tuning step directly reuses DataDream's LoRA procedure, positioning the contribution as a principled re-optimization of the training objective rather than a new generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework offers a principled answer to why naive synthetic data augmentation can hurt few-shot performance and what properties (proximity to real samples AND local robustness) a good synthetic set must satisfy. The robustness regularization term, which prior heuristic methods entirely omit, is shown empirically to be critical for generalization, suggesting a broader lesson for synthetic-data training beyond few-shot classification. However, the method underperforms in extreme 1-shot settings where the regularization terms become vacuous, and the O(√K) dependence on partition count limits scalability. Open questions include whether the bound can be directly used to optimize or filter synthetic samples at generation time, and whether the framework extends to adversarial training or domain adaptation as the authors speculate.
