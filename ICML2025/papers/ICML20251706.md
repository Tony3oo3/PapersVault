## Problem

Existing offline in-context RL methods train a learned encoder to perform approximate Bayesian inference of the task from context, but this posterior depends on the joint distribution of task and context-collection policy, which shifts between training and deployment. The paper shows that under such shifts, encoders capture spurious behavioral correlations rather than true task dynamics, causing performance to degrade or even worsen over training. The paper further proves (Theorem 3.1) that the CSRO objective of maximizing I(Z;M) while minimizing I(Z;X^b) is fundamentally infeasible when the collection policy is highly correlated with the task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context trajectory of C transition tuples (s_i, a_i, r_i, s'_i) collected by an unknown policy, which is internally decomposed into behavioral pairs X^b = {(s_i, a_i)} and dynamics-relevant pairs X^t = {(r_i, s'_i)}. Rather than passing the full context through a forward encoder, BATI searches over a finite table of N=40 sampled task latents (drawn from per-task Gaussian distributions with learnable mean and fixed log-variance −4) to find the Z* that best reconstructs X^t from X^b. The resulting Z* conditions an IQL-based in-context policy π_θ(s, Z*) that outputs actions for the test episode.

## Method

The key insight comes from the graphical model: conditioning on the behavioral component X^b blocks all paths from the context-collection policy μ to the dynamics component X^t, so the task M can be inferred from X^t alone via the maximum-likelihood objective argmax_M log p(X^t | X^b, M), which is invariant to shifts in μ. Concretely, BATI replaces the learned encoder with a table of task embedding distributions {Z_φ^M} and a dynamics estimator (g_ψ, h_ψ) that predicts X^t as a Gaussian conditioned on (X^b, Z), trained with a negative-log-likelihood reconstruction loss. The in-context policy is trained with IQL using detached task embeddings, so gradients from the RL objective do not corrupt the dynamics-supervised embeddings. At test time, N candidates are sampled from the embedding table and the one minimizing the reconstruction loss is selected, making inference a discrete search rather than a single encoder forward pass.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (online episodic return on p_test(M,μ) with adversarial context shifts), BATI achieves 46.4±2.9 on AntDir, -122.8±1.6 on HalfCheetahVel, 286.1±13.8 on HalfCheetahDir, 285.9±5.5 on HopperParam, and 565.2±7.9 on WalkerParam, outperforming all baselines in every environment. The margins are particularly dramatic in HalfCheetahDir (286.1 vs. −340.0 for CSRO) and HopperParam (285.9 vs. 144.4 for CSRO). Notably, BATI even exceeds the oracle (pre-IQL) return on WalkerParam (565.2 vs. 549.2), suggesting IQL's additional optimization on top of the inferred task latent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BATI outperforms all four baselines across all five MuJoCo environments under the adversarial context-shift evaluation. Against CSRO (Gao et al., 2024), the gap is most extreme in HalfCheetahDir (286.1 vs. −340.0) and HopperParam (285.9 vs. 144.4), where CSRO's competing objectives (maximizing I(Z;M) while minimizing I(Z;X^b)) become infeasible when the context policy is highly correlated with the task. Against UNICORN (Li et al., 2024), BATI leads by 78.4 points on AntDir (46.4 vs. −32.0) and 649.0 points on HalfCheetahDir (286.1 vs. −362.9). Against FOCAL (Li et al., 2021b), BATI wins in every environment, with the largest margin on HalfCheetahDir (286.1 vs. −336.4). Against Recon/GENTLE (Zhou et al., 2024), which shares BATI's reconstruction loss but uses a learned encoder for inference, BATI consistently outperforms, confirming that the maximum-likelihood search over task embeddings is the critical differentiator rather than the dynamics model itself. In the additional KuhnPoker multi-agent experiment, BATI (−0.049±0.025) also outperforms all baselines (CSRO −0.180, FOCAL −0.191, Recon −0.185, UNICORN −0.243), though all methods fall short of the oracle (0.0734).

## Relation to Prior Work

BATI departs from the dominant paradigm in offline ICRL task inference—using a learned encoder to approximate the Bayesian posterior p(M|X)—by reframing inference as a maximum-likelihood search over task embeddings conditioned on state-action pairs (X^b), leveraging a dynamics estimator to score candidate tasks via p(X^t|X^b, M). This is motivated by a graphical-model argument showing that conditioning on X^b blocks all paths from the context-collection policy μ to the outcome variables X^t, thereby insulating inference from distribution shifts in μ. The paper positions itself against UNICORN (information-theoretic mutual information objective), CSRO (mutual information decorrelation), and FOCAL (contrastive/distance-metric learning), arguing that all share the vulnerability of fitting a posterior under the training distribution p(M,μ) that shifts at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a concrete failure mode—spurious correlation capture by learned encoders under context shift—that explains why offline ICRL methods degrade at deployment, and offers a clean, implementable fix (dynamics-only scoring with embedding search) that requires no encoder at test time. Limitations include restriction to state-based MuJoCo locomotion tasks with small discrete task sets (20 train / 20 test), reliance on an approximation that ignores the integral over μ, and preliminary (single-environment) multi-agent results. Open questions include whether the approach scales to vision-based embodied settings, continuous or large task spaces, and whether counterfactual estimation of p(X^b|M,μ) can tighten the approximation.
