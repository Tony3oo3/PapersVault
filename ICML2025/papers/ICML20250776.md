## Problem

No post-training quantization (PTQ) method existed for Discrete Graph Diffusion Models (DGDMs). Existing quantization approaches for Image Diffusion Models rely on Gaussian denoising calibration that is incompatible with the discrete categorical sampling in DGDMs, while LLM-oriented methods target memory-bound weight loading, whereas DGDMs are computation-bound due to their small parameter counts (0.07B) and high-computation operations like FiLM. Additionally, DGDMs exhibit significant outliers in both weights and activations simultaneously, rendering smoothing-based outlier mitigation ineffective.

## Input / Output

The quantization framework takes a pre-trained DGDM (e.g., Digress with Graph Transformer backbone, or GRADE-IF with Equivariant GNN) and produces a W4A4-quantized version without retraining. For threshold calibration, it uses data-free sampling: 32 random graphs drawn from uniform categorical priors over node and edge types are passed through the full-precision model to estimate activation percentiles, avoiding any need for raw training data. At inference, the quantized model accepts a randomly sampled noisy graph (or a 3D protein structure for inverse folding) and iteratively denoises it over T diffusion steps to produce a molecular graph, non-molecular graph, or amino acid sequence.

## Method

Bit-DGDM decomposes the matrix product XW into a low-bit dense term and sparse-dense terms: XW ≈ D̂_X D̂_W + S_X LR^T + XS_W. For activations, a sparse-dense decomposition separates outliers (exceeding data-free adaptive thresholds τ_max/τ_min) into a sparse matrix S_X while the dense remainder D_X is quantized to 4-bit. For weights, an ill-conditioned low-rank decomposition (inspired by Robust PCA) factorizes W into a rank-r component LR^T plus an α-sparse outlier matrix S_W, optimized via scaled gradient descent with (R^T R)^{-1} and (L^T L)^{-1} preconditioning, with a guaranteed linear convergence rate of (1−0.6η)^k under μ-incoherence and α-sparsity assumptions. Two custom CUDA kernels handle the sparse-dense multiplications: an equidistributed kernel for XS_W (exploiting α-sparsity for uniform thread load) and an adaptive kernel for S_X LR^T (handling irregular activation sparsity via warp-level segmented reduction). The framework achieves 2.5× speedup and 2.8× memory reduction at W4A4 precision while maintaining generation quality close to BF16.

## Results

On QM9 (molecule generation), Bit-DGDM at W4A4 achieves 98.2% Validity and 95.5% Uniqueness with 2.5× speedup and 2.3 GB peak memory, closely matching the FP32 baseline (99.0% Valid, 96.2% Unique) while reducing memory by 2.8×. On CATH (protein inverse folding), Bit-DGDM achieves a Perplexity of 4.5 and 51.6% Recovery at W4A4, nearly matching FP32 (4.4 Perplexity, 52.2% Recovery) with 2.5× speedup and 2.6× memory reduction. Across all datasets, Bit-DGDM consistently outperforms every quantization baseline in generation quality while also delivering the highest or near-highest speedup among W4A4 methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bit-DGDM decisively outperforms RTN and GPTQ, which suffer catastrophic quality degradation on DGDMs (e.g., 57.6% and 72.4% Validity on QM9 vs. Bit-DGDM's 98.2%), confirming that naive round-to-nearest and Hessian-based calibration are ill-suited for discrete graph diffusion. Against the two strongest outlier-aware baselines, SVDQuant (smoothing + SVD) and DuQuant (block-wise rotation), Bit-DGDM achieves 2.6–1.7% higher Validity on QM9 and 1.8–1.2% higher Recovery on CATH, while also delivering 2.5× speedup versus their 2.1–2.2×. SqueezeLLM, which stores weights in low-bit but still computes in BF16, achieves comparable quality to Bit-DGDM (95.9% vs. 98.2% Valid on QM9) but provides only 1.1× speedup, confirming that DGDM inference is computation-bound rather than memory-bound. On CATH protein folding, Bit-DGDM achieves 4.5 Perplexity vs. 6.3 (SVDQuant), 5.6 (DuQuant), and 4.7 (SqueezeLLM), with 2.5× speedup versus 1.0–2.1× for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bit-DGDM repurposes the robust principal component analysis (RPCA) framework—decomposing a matrix into a low-rank plus sparse component—and the scaled gradient descent optimization from Tong et al. (2021) and Cai et al. (2021) for weight quantization, adapting them to handle the ill-conditioned optimization landscape created by weight outliers. It departs from LLM-oriented quantization (GPTQ, SqueezeLLM, SVDQuant, DuQuant) by recognizing that DGDMs are computation-bound rather than memory-bound, and from IDM-oriented quantization (PTQD, Q-Diffusion) by accounting for the fundamentally different categorical-sampling diffusion process. The data-free sparse-dense activation quantization is a novel contribution that avoids the calibration-data dependency of prior PTQ methods while addressing the dual-outlier problem (outliers in both weights and activations) that renders smoothing-based approaches ineffective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first post-training quantization framework specifically designed for discrete graph diffusion models, filling a gap that prevents practitioners from deploying DGDMs (used in drug design, protein folding, code generation) at reduced precision. The finding that DGDMs are computation-bound—evidenced by SqueezeLLM's negligible 1.1× speedup versus Bit-DGDM's 2.5×—refines the broader understanding of where quantization gains originate across model families. Limitations include evaluation on small models (~0.07B parameters) and two specific architectures (Digress, GRADE-IF); whether the α-sparsity assumption and data-free threshold selection generalize to larger or architecturally different DGDMs remains open. The framework's reliance on iterative weight decomposition also introduces a one-time quantization cost (~14 min) that may matter for very large model families.
