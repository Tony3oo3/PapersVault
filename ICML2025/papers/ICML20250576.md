## Problem

Generative models for de novo drug design typically use molecular docking as an oracle, but docking scores do not reliably predict real-world binding activity. More accurate methods like absolute binding free energy (ABFE) calculations are too computationally expensive (hours to days per compound) to guide generation directly. Existing multi-fidelity approaches that combine cheap and expensive oracles keep the generative model and surrogate model separate, failing to account for the fact that optimal query compounds differ across fidelity levels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein structure (PDB) for oracle evaluation and an initial set of random ZINC250k compounds as seed data, with molecules internally represented as one-hot encoded SELFIES strings fed to fully-connected networks. It produces generated molecules in SELFIES format, filtered for drug-likeness (QED > 0.4, SAscore < 4, no rings ≥ 7 atoms), which are then scored by ABFE. The multi-fidelity environment uses four oracles of increasing cost and accuracy: linear regression (~0.1s), AutoDock4 (~4s), Ensembled AutoDock4 (~44–68s), and ABFE (~9.3 hours).

## Method

MF-LAL encodes molecules into a hierarchy of K latent spaces (one per fidelity level) using a single probabilistic encoder at the lowest fidelity and probabilistic transition networks h_ξ that map between successive latent spaces, with a dedicated decoder at each level. At each fidelity, a stochastic variational Gaussian process (SVGP) surrogate predicts the oracle output from the latent vector, and all components are jointly trained by minimizing the ELBO of the latent encodings plus the GP marginal log-likelihood, backpropagating through lower fidelities. Active learning proceeds stepwise: the model starts querying the cheapest oracle and escalates to higher fidelities only when the GP posterior variance drops below a threshold, using an upper-confidence-bound acquisition function with L2 regularization. A key innovation is a likelihood-based generation term: when optimizing in latent space k, the objective includes the probability density of the candidate point under a sum-of-Gaussians distribution formed by mapping top-scoring points from fidelity k−1 through h_ξ, ensuring high-fidelity queries are restricted to compounds already validated at lower fidelities.

## Results

On BRD4(2), MF-LAL achieves a mean ABFE of −6.2 ± 3.9 kcal/mol with 6 active scaffolds among 15 generated compounds, and top-3 scores of −12.0, −10.2, and −9.8 kcal/mol. On c-MET, MF-LAL achieves a mean ABFE of −6.7 ± 3.1 kcal/mol with 4 active scaffolds and top-3 scores of −12.9, −7.9, and −7.7 kcal/mol. These represent approximately 50% improvement in mean binding free energy over the best baseline, with statistically significant margins (−1.6 kcal/mol for BRD4(2), p = 0.03; −2.8 kcal/mol for c-MET, p < 0.001). In the 40-compound robustness evaluation (Table 2), MF-LAL's advantage remains significant (p < 0.05) for both targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF-LAL outperforms all 13 baseline configurations on both targets in mean ABFE. On BRD4(2), the closest competitor is Pocket2Mol (−4.3 ± 3.8 kcal/mol), which MF-LAL beats by 1.9 kcal/mol in mean and by 2.2 kcal/mol on the best compound (−12.0 vs. −9.8); on c-MET, the closest is MF-AL-PPO (−4.2 ± 2.8), beaten by 2.5 kcal/mol in mean. Notably, the multi-fidelity baselines (MF-AL-GFN, MF-AL-PPO, VAE+MF-GP, MF-GP+ZINC250k) perform only marginally better than or comparably to single-fidelity methods, suggesting that simply combining a multi-fidelity GP with a separate generative model is insufficient. The 3D structure-based methods (Pocket2Mol, DecompDiff, TAGMol) that do not use a binding affinity oracle during generation also underperform MF-LAL, with Pocket2Mol being the strongest among them on BRD4(2) but weak on c-MET. In the 40-compound robustness test, MF-LAL maintains a statistically significant lead (p < 0.05) over the top baseline on both targets, and generates significantly more active scaffolds (8 vs. 2 on BRD4(2); 6 vs. 1 on c-MET).

## Relation to Prior Work

MF-LAL integrates three established threads: VAE-based latent space generation with gradient-based reverse optimization (Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018), stochastic variational Gaussian process surrogates (Hensman et al., 2015), and stepwise fidelity escalation from multi-fidelity bandits (Kandasamy et al., 2016). The key departure from prior multi-fidelity drug discovery work (Hernandez-Garcia et al., 2023) is the unification of the generative model and surrogate model into a single framework with a hierarchy of fidelity-specific latent spaces, rather than training a separate generative model to optimize an acquisition function computed by an independent multi-fidelity surrogate. A novel likelihood-based generation term further restricts high-fidelity queries to compounds that already scored well at lower fidelities, a constraint absent from prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck in generative drug design: the gap between fast-but-inaccurate docking and accurate-but-prohibitively-expensive binding free energy calculations. The ~50% improvement in mean ABFE over the best baseline, combined with the finding that other multi-fidelity methods perform no better than single-fidelity ones, suggests that architectural integration (shared hierarchical latent spaces with fidelity-specific decoders) is what unlocks the benefit of multiple oracles. Limitations include evaluation on only two protein targets with small compound counts (15–40), potential synthesizability issues, SVGP variance overestimation far from inducing points, and the inherent tradeoff of potentially missing high-fidelity scaffolds that score poorly at lower fidelities. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional targets, whether reaction-aware generation can improve synthesizability, and how the method scales to more fidelity levels or larger chemical spaces.
