## Problem

Prior theoretical work on generation in the limit (Kleinberg & Mullainathan, 2024; Li et al., 2024) assumes a noiseless stream where every revealed example belongs to the adversary's chosen hypothesis. This paper asks what happens when the adversary inserts a finite number of negative examples into the stream, and provides necessary and sufficient conditions for when a binary hypothesis class remains generatable under such noise.

## Input / Output

The generator receives a finite prefix of a noisy stream over a countable instance space X, where the stream is an enumeration of the support of some hypothesis h ∈ H with at most n* negative examples inserted at arbitrary positions. The generator is a deterministic map G: X* → X that must output a single new example in supp(h) \ {x_1,...,x_s} without knowing the noise level n* or the locations of the negatives. The hypothesis class is assumed to satisfy the Uniformly Unbounded Support (UUS) property, i.e., every h ∈ H has infinite support, preventing the adversary from exhausting all positive examples.

## Method

The paper introduces the Noisy Closure dimension NC_n(H), a scale-sensitive combinatorial parameter defined at each noise level n, which generalizes the Closure dimension from Li et al. (2024). The main characterization shows that H is uniformly noise-dependent generatable if and only if NC_n(H) < ∞ for every n ∈ N, with the generator computing the largest noise level n_t for which NC_{n_t}(H) < d_t (the number of distinct examples seen so far) and then playing from the noisy closure at that level. A key separation result constructs a countable class with Closure dimension 0 (trivially uniformly generatable) but NC_1(H) = ∞, showing that even a single noisy example can destroy uniform generatability. For non-uniform and limit settings, the paper provides matching sufficiency/necessity conditions via decompositions into nested subclasses, and proves that all countable classes remain noisily generatable in the limit.

## Results

The paper provides complete characterizations of noisy generatability via a new combinatorial parameter, the Noisy Closure dimension NC_n(H). The headline result (Theorem 3.3) shows that a class H is uniformly noise-dependent generatable if and only if NC_n(H) < ∞ for every noise-level n ∈ ℕ, with sample complexity Θ(NC_n(H)) at each level. A key separation (Lemma 3.5) constructs a countable class with Closure dimension C(H) = 0 (trivially uniformly generatable in the noiseless setting) yet NC_1(H) = ∞, proving that even a single noisy example can make uniform generation impossible. All finite classes remain uniformly noise-dependent generatable (Corollary 3.4), and all countable classes are non-uniformly noise-dependent generatable and hence noisily generatable in the limit (Corollary 3.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between different notions of generatability. The central comparison is between noiseless and noisy settings: Li et al. (2024) characterize noiseless uniform generatability via the Closure dimension C(H), while this paper shows that the analogous noisy characterization requires the scale-sensitive Noisy Closure dimension NC_n(H) at every noise-level n. The separation in Lemma 3.5 demonstrates that uniform noise-dependent generatability is strictly harder than noiseless uniform generatability — a class can be trivially uniformly generatable (C(H) = 0) yet fail to be uniformly noise-dependent generatable even with just one noisy example (NC_1(H) = ∞). In contrast, for non-uniform and in-the-limit settings, the paper recovers the same positive results as the noiseless case: all countable classes remain generatable, mirroring the noiseless results of Kleinberg & Mullainathan (2024) and Li et al. (2024). The proof techniques differ from Li et al. (2024) because the Noisy Closure dimension is scale-sensitive, requiring the uniform generator to combine different sub-generators for each noise-level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'Language Generation in the Limit' framework of Kleinberg & Mullainathan (2024) and the learning-theoretic reformulation by Li et al. (2024) to account for finite noisy example streams, drawing on the extensive 'noisy texts' literature from language identification in the limit (Schäfer 1985; Fulk & Jain 1989; Stephan 1997). The key technical departure from Li et al. (2024) is the introduction of the Noisy Closure dimension, a scale-sensitive combinatorial parameter defined per noise-level, which requires fundamentally different proof techniques than the noiseless Closure dimension—most notably, the uniform generator must combine sub-generators for each noise-level rather than using a single closure-based strategy. The paper positions itself as a natural robustness extension, motivated by practical concerns like LLM self-training on hallucinatory outputs and data poisoning attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete characterization of uniform noise-dependent generatability via the Noisy Closure dimension and proves that all countable classes remain noisily generatable in the limit, offering theoretical reassurance that finite data corruption does not fundamentally break generation. However, the complete characterization of non-uniform noise-dependent generatability remains open, with the sufficiency and necessity conditions not matching. A key open question is whether a computable algorithm exists for noisy generation in the limit given a membership oracle, which would bridge the gap between the information-theoretic results here and practical deployment. The scale-sensitivity of the Noisy Closure dimension suggests that robust generation may require fundamentally different sample complexity guarantees at different noise levels, a distinction absent in the noiseless theory.
