## Problem

Distributed cross-attention in MLLMs with large visual inputs (e.g., long videos) is a critical bottleneck: existing sequence-parallelism methods like Ring Attention transmit large key-value blocks across workers, making the operation communication-bound and accounting for up to 88% of iteration time despite comprising only 3% of parameters. Head-parallelism methods are further limited by the number of attention heads, capping maximum sequence length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on cross-attention layers within MLLMs where queries are derived from text prompts (S_Q typically 1K–64K tokens) and keys/values are derived from visual tokens produced by a vision encoder (S_KV ranging from 182K to 15M+ tokens for long videos). The key structural constraint is S_KV >> S_Q, which is what enables the communication reduction. The output is the exact cross-attention output O ∈ R^{S_Q × d} that fuses visual information into the LLM's intermediate representations, with no approximation.

## Method

LV-XAttn inverts Ring Attention's communication pattern: instead of rotating large key-value blocks around a ring of workers, each worker keeps its KV partition local and only exchanges smaller query blocks, partial attention outputs, and softmax statistics in a ring-style fashion. This reduces per-round communication from O(S_KV·d/n) to O(S_Q·d/n), cutting communication volume to 0.04% of Ring Attention's on Video-MME, and the residual communication is fully hidden by computation. A second contribution is an MLLM-specific activation recomputation scheme: since visual tokens y are identical across all cross-attention layers, a single copy is retained and KV blocks are recomputed during backward pass, saving memory and enabling 1.6× longer visual inputs with under 8% runtime overhead. The paper provides a theoretical runtime analysis showing LV-XAttn is compute-bound while Ring Attention is communication-bound in the S_KV >> S_Q regime, yielding speedups proportional to GPU FLOPS / network bandwidth.

## Results

On 16 A100 80GB GPUs, LV-XAttn achieves up to 27.59× speedup on the cross-attention operation and up to 10.62× end-to-end iteration speedup over Ring Attention (Llama 3-V-11b, 2K text, 384 frames). On 8 A30 24GB GPUs with slower interconnect, cross-attention speedups increase to 22.43–45.85×, though total iteration speedups are 1.65–3.45× due to slower self-attention in LM blocks. The activation recomputation technique enables processing 1.5–1.6× more frames with less than 8% runtime overhead, and the distributed cross-attention incurs less than 0.42% overhead compared to a theoretical no-communication baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ring Attention (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), LV-XAttn wins on every model and cluster configuration tested. Cross-attention speedups range from 1.47× (OpenFlamingo-3b, 64K text on A100) to 45.85× (mPLUG-Owl3-1b, 512 text on A30), with the largest gains occurring when S_KV ≫ S_Q and interconnect bandwidth is low. Total iteration speedups reach 10.62× on A100 and 3.45× on A30. Against DeepSpeed-Ulysses (Jacobs et al., PODC 2024), LV-XAttn achieves 1.34–1.55× speedup on mPLUG-Owl3-2b across 3–12 GPU configurations. More critically, on OpenFlamingo-3b with A30 24GB GPUs, DeepSpeed-Ulysses hits OOM for all tested input sizes above 256 text/256 frames on 4 GPUs, while LV-XAttn successfully processes 1K text/1K frames, enabling over 4× longer visual inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LV-XAttn inverts the communication pattern of Ring Attention (Liu et al., 2024a): rather than rotating large key-value blocks in a ring, it keeps KV blocks local and rotates the much smaller query, output, and softmax-statistics blocks. This exploits the structural asymmetry in MLLM cross-attention where S_KV ≫ S_Q, a property that head-parallelism methods (DeepSpeed-Ulysses, Megatron-LM) cannot exploit due to their head-count ceiling. The activation recomputation technique extends standard activation checkpointing (Korthikanti et al., 2023b) with the MLLM-specific observation that visual tokens are shared across all cross-attention layers, allowing a single copy to serve all layers during backward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.62× end-to-end speedup and the ability to process 1.6× longer visual inputs with <8% overhead make long-video understanding in cross-attention-based MLLMs practically feasible on existing hardware, removing what was previously a hard memory and communication bottleneck. A key limitation is that the method only applies to cross-attention-based MLLMs (Flamingo, Llama 3-V, mPLUG-Owl3, etc.) and not to concatenation-based architectures (LLaVA, InternVL) where self-attention dominates. Open questions include how the approach performs in hybrid architectures combining both paradigms, and whether the speedup degrades as S_Q grows (e.g., in OpenFlamingo where S_Q is large relative to S_KV, the cross-attention speedup drops to ~1.5–2×).
