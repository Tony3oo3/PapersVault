## Problem

Diffusion Bridge Models (DBMs) achieve strong image-to-image translation quality but require 500–1000 function evaluations for inference, making them impractical. Existing distillation methods (CDBM, DBIM) are restricted to conditional DBMs and cannot produce one-step generators due to theoretical singularities at the terminal point. No universal distillation framework existed that could accelerate both conditional and unconditional bridge matching models into few-step or single-step generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled generator G_θ takes a corrupted/degraded image x_T (e.g., low-resolution, JPEG-compressed, edge map, or normal map) and a Gaussian noise vector z as input, and directly outputs a clean image x_0 in a single forward pass (or 2–4 passes for multi-step mode). Crucially, training requires only samples from the corrupted data distribution p(x_T) — no paired clean targets are needed. Experiments span 64×64 (Edges→Handbags) and 256×256 (ImageNet, DIODE-Outdoor) resolutions across super-resolution, JPEG restoration, inpainting, and sketch-to-image tasks.

## Method

IBMD formulates distillation as an inverse Bridge Matching problem: find a coupling p_θ(x_0, x_T) (parameterized by a generator G_θ(x_T, z)) such that Bridge Matching on this coupling yields the same drift as the frozen teacher. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.2) converts this constrained problem into an unconstrained, gradient-tractable objective: the teacher's x_0-reconstruction loss minus an auxiliary bridge network's x_0-reconstruction loss, both evaluated on samples drawn from the generator-defined coupling. In practice, the algorithm alternates between (1) training the auxiliary bridge network φ to fit the generator's induced distribution and (2) updating the generator θ to minimize the gap between teacher and auxiliary losses. This design naturally enforces diversity (the negative auxiliary term penalizes generator collapse) and requires no clean target data. The same formulation extends to conditional DBMs by adding x_T as an additional conditioning input, and a multi-step variant (inspired by DMD) handles harder tasks like inpainting by training the generator at intermediate timesteps.

## Results

On 4× super-resolution (bicubic, ImageNet 256×256), IBMD-I2SB achieves FID 2.6 and CA 72.3 with just 1 NFE, outperforming the I2SB teacher (FID 2.8, CA 70.7) that requires 1000 NFE — a 1000× speedup with better quality. On JPEG restoration (QF=10), the 1-step model reaches FID 3.7 vs. the teacher's 3.6 at 1000 NFE. For conditional DDBM distillation, IBMD-DDBM achieves FID 0.67 at 2 NFE on Edges→Handbags (vs. CBT's 0.80 at 2 NFE) and FID 4.03 at 4 NFE on inpainting (vs. CBT's 4.77 at 4 NFE), while maintaining strong 1-step performance (FID 1.26 and 5.87 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBMD-I2SB (1 NFE) outperforms the I2SB teacher (1000 NFE) on both super-resolution tasks: FID 2.6 vs. 2.8 (bicubic) and 2.5 vs. 2.7 (pool), while matching or exceeding the teacher on JPEG restoration (FID 3.7 vs. 3.6 at QF=10). Against the leading conditional distillation method CDBM, IBMD-DDBM wins on Edges→Handbags at 2 NFE (FID 0.67 vs. CBT's 0.80) and on inpainting at 4 NFE (FID 4.03 vs. CBT's 4.77, CA 72.2 vs. 70.3), but CBT retains a slight edge on DIODE-Outdoor at 2 NFE (FID 2.93 vs. 3.12). Compared to DBIM (50–100 NFE), IBMD achieves comparable or better FID with 1–2 NFE, yielding a 25–100× speedup. Against non-bridge baselines (DDRM, DDNM, IIGDM, ADM, CDSB, Palette), IBMD at 1 NFE substantially outperforms all of them on super-resolution and JPEG restoration, e.g., FID 2.6 vs. IIGDM's 3.6 at 100 NFE. The key differentiator is that IBMD is the first method applicable to both unconditional (I2SB) and conditional (DDBM) bridge models, and the first to produce a true one-step generator for bridge matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBMD inverts the Bridge Matching problem (Shi et al., 2023; Peluchetti, 2023) by learning a generator that defines a data coupling whose Bridge Matching solution reproduces a given teacher drift, rather than directly distilling the teacher's sampling trajectory. This is conceptually analogous to DMD (Yin et al., 2024), which simultaneously trains a generator and an auxiliary 'fake score' model, but IBMD reformulates the objective as a KL divergence between path measures of reverse-time SDEs, which the authors argue is necessary because the forward drift in unconditional bridge matching is not fixed (unlike standard diffusion models where Fisher divergence suffices). The multi-step variant borrows the iterative inference-during-training strategy from DMD, and the conditional case extends the same framework by adding x_T conditioning, making it the first distillation method applicable to both conditional (DDBM) and unconditional (I²SB) bridge matching models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DBMs are increasingly used for image-to-image translation but require 500–1000 NFEs for inference, limiting practical deployment; IBMD closes this gap by enabling 1–4 step generation with 4×–100× speedup while sometimes matching or slightly exceeding teacher quality. The universality across conditional and unconditional formulations is a genuine gap-filler, since prior acceleration methods (DBIM, CDBM) are restricted to conditional DBMs and CDBM provably cannot produce one-step generators. Key limitations include the 3× memory overhead from backpropagating through teacher, auxiliary bridge, and generator simultaneously, and the alternating optimization schedule that makes training computationally expensive. Open questions include whether the KL-of-path-measures objective generalizes to non-Gaussian bridges or non-linear drift priors, and whether the small quality gains over the teacher (e.g., FID 2.5 vs 2.7 in pool SR) are consistent across architectures and scales.
