## Problem

Existing continual learning methods address the stability-plasticity dilemma at the parameter level (via regularization, replay, or parameter allocation), but overlook an inherent conflict at the architectural level: under a fixed parameter budget, deeper networks exhibit better plasticity while wider networks exhibit better stability. This architectural-level trade-off remains unexplored and may cap CL performance even when parameters are finely optimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251645/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates in the class-incremental learning setting, where RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) are presented in sequential tasks with non-overlapping classes, and the task identity is unknown at inference. During training, both the plastic and stable learners consume the same input batch (plus replay buffer for replay-based methods), but at inference only the stable learner is used to produce a class label over all classes seen so far, keeping test-time cost comparable to a single ResNet-18.

## Method

Dual-Arch employs two independent, lightweight networks with complementary architectures: a wide-and-shallow Sta-Net (ResNet-18 with half the residual blocks and a 2×2 penultimate feature map) dedicated to stability, and a deep-and-thin Pla-Net (ResNet-18 depth with width reduced from 64 to 42) dedicated to plasticity. For each new task, the plastic learner is first trained to convergence with standard cross-entropy loss and then frozen as a teacher; the stable learner is subsequently trained with a composite objective α·L_CE + (1−α)·L_KD + L_CL, where L_KD is a KL-divergence distillation loss from the frozen plastic learner and L_CL is the loss from any existing CL method (iCaRL, DER, MEMO, etc.). Both networks are designed with reduced parameter counts so that their combined size is smaller than a single ResNet-18, and the framework is plug-and-play: any CL method applied to the stable learner mirrors the traditional single-learner training loop. The key insight is that knowledge distillation between architecturally different networks effectively transfers new knowledge from the plastic to the stable learner without destabilizing previously learned representations.

## Results

Across five CL methods (iCaRL, WA, DER, Foster, MEMO) on four benchmarks (CIFAR100/10, CIFAR100/20, ImageNet100/10, ImageNet100/20), Dual-Arch consistently improves LA and AIA while reducing parameters by 33–52%. The largest gains are +10.29% LA and +7.62% AIA (MEMO and Foster on CIFAR100/20, respectively), with FAF reductions up to 11.62%. On ImageNet100/10, Dual-Arch with DER achieves 68.52% LA and 77.49% AIA (vs. 67.40/75.93 for ResNet-18), and with MEMO reaches 65.40% LA and 75.54% AIA (vs. 61.64/73.31). Parameter counts drop from 22.4M to 15.1M (iCaRL/WA/Foster) and from 224.4M to 106.9M (DER), while inference FLOPs are halved (255M vs. 558M for ResNet-18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-Arch outperforms all five primary CL baselines (iCaRL, WA, DER, Foster, MEMO) on every benchmark and metric. The most substantial wins are with MEMO on CIFAR100/20 (+10.29% LA, +5.09% AIA, −11.62% FAF) and Foster on CIFAR100/20 (+7.70% LA, +7.62% AIA, −11.28% FAF); even on the strongest baseline DER on ImageNet100/10, Dual-Arch still gains +1.12% LA and +1.56% AIA while cutting parameters by 52%. Against ArchCraft (the single-architecture baseline), Dual-Arch wins in most settings, e.g., 65.35 vs. 61.93 LA for MEMO on CIFAR100/10 and 68.52 vs. 68.34 LA for DER on ImageNet100/10, confirming that dual complementary architectures surpass a single CL-friendly design. On the Generalized Class-IL benchmark (GCIL-CIFAR-100), Dual-Arch improves ER from 20.30% to 27.57% LA (buffer 500) and DER++ from 25.82% to 30.34% LA (buffer 500), demonstrating generality beyond standard Class-IL. The only reported trade-off is a 1.39×–1.77× increase in training time due to sequential training of the two learners, though inference FLOPs are actually lower than the single ResNet-18 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-model CL paradigm (Kim et al. 2023; Hare & Tortoise; MKD) by arguing that the two learners should not share a uniform architecture but instead adopt complementary designs: a wide-and-shallow network for stability and a deep-and-thin network for plasticity. It builds on ArchCraft (Lu et al., 2024), which established that architecture choice matters for CL, and on prior empirical observations that wider networks forget less (Mirzadeh et al., 2022a), but departs by explicitly framing the stability-plasticity trade-off as an architectural-level conflict rather than solely a parameter-level one. The knowledge distillation mechanism connecting the two learners follows standard practice (Hinton et al., 2015), and the framework is designed as a plug-in for existing CL methods (iCaRL, DER, MEMO, etc.) rather than a standalone algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of Dual-Arch makes it broadly applicable to any existing CL pipeline, and the consistent improvements across five methods and four benchmarks (with up to 87% parameter reduction) offer a practical efficiency gain for memory-constrained deployment. However, the 1.39×–1.77× training time overhead from sequential two-model training is a non-trivial cost that limits adoption in compute-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the depth-width complementarity holds for other architecture families (e.g., Mamba, state-space models), whether the fixed 50/50 distillation weighting is optimal, and whether the approach extends naturally to online or domain-incremental settings where the plastic learner's role may differ.
