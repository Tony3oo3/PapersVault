## Problem

Event-based object detection with spiking neural networks has shown promise for low-power inference, but existing SNN detectors (e.g., SpikSSD, EAS-SNN) underperform ANN-based Transformer methods on benchmarks like GEN1. Additionally, no publicly available event-based fall detection dataset exists that preserves subject privacy, limiting research in healthcare-oriented event vision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252762/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes asynchronous event streams {(x,y,p,t)} accumulated over dataset-specific time windows (50 ms for GEN1, 200 ms for Fall, 10 ms for Aircraft), stored in HDF5 format. Because event cameras emit only contrast-change pixels, the input inherently lacks grayscale and texture information, which the authors exploit as a privacy-preserving property for fall detection. Output follows the GEN1 numpy schema: per-object bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) with class_id, class_confidence, and track_id, produced by a YOLOX-style detection head.

## Method

HsVT is a four-block hybrid backbone where each block pairs a spatial module (MaxViT's Block-SA for local attention + Grid-SA for global attention, followed by a SpikingMLP with LIF neurons) and a temporal module (LSTM in blocks 1–3, a novel Spiking Temporal Feature Extraction (STFE) unit in block 4). The STFE unit chains a convolutional layer, batch normalization, LIF spiking neurons, and an LSTM-like gating structure to extract temporal features in a spike-driven manner. Information flows both vertically across stages and horizontally across adjacent time steps, enabling the network to accumulate temporal context. Ablations show that placing STFE exclusively in the final block (rather than earlier) maximizes mAP, LIF neurons outperform IF neurons, and the ATan surrogate gradient is preferred for the 200 ms fall-detection interval. The full detector is trained end-to-end with a YOLOX head using ADAM, OneCycle LR scheduling, and mixed-precision training on dual RTX 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On the GEN1 dataset, HsVT-B (17.2M params) achieves mAP50:95 of 0.478, outperforming all prior SNN-based methods (SpikSSD: 0.408, EAS-SNN: 0.409, SpikingViT: 0.394) and the RVT hybrid baseline (0.472) while using fewer parameters than RVT (18.5M). On the Fall DVS Detection Dataset, HsVT consistently surpasses RVT across all model sizes (e.g., HsVT-small: 0.492 vs. RVT-small: 0.466 mAP50:95). On the Aircraft Detection Dataset, HsVT-tiny achieves 0.641 mAP50:95 versus RVT-tiny's 0.613. The model also demonstrates strong parameter efficiency, with HsVT-T (4.1M params) reaching 0.449 mAP50:95 on GEN1, competitive with much larger SNN detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HsVT-B (0.478) surpasses RVT (0.472) on GEN1 with fewer parameters (17.2M vs. 18.5M), and outperforms all SNN-based detectors by a wide margin: SpikSSD (0.408), EAS-SNN (0.409), SpikingViT (0.394), and SFOD (0.321). However, HsVT-B still trails the top pure-Transformer methods ERGO-12 (0.504) and STAT (0.499) by 2.6 and 2.1 mAP points respectively. On the Fall DVS Detection Dataset, HsVT outperforms RVT at every model size (HsVT-small: 0.492 vs. RVT-small: 0.466; HsVT-tiny: 0.491 vs. RVT-tiny: 0.487), with the gap widening at the small scale. On the Aircraft Detection Dataset, HsVT-tiny leads RVT-tiny by 2.8 mAP points (0.641 vs. 0.613). Notably, increasing model size does not monotonically improve performance on the FALL and AIR datasets, suggesting overfitting due to limited dataset size and diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HsVT is architecturally inspired by RVT (Gehrig & Scaramuzza, CVPR 2023), adopting its multi-stage backbone with spatial attention and temporal recurrence, but replaces the pure-ANN attention with a hybrid that interleaves MaxViT's dual-axis self-attention (Block-SA and Grid-SA) with SpikingMLP layers using LIF neurons. The temporal pathway similarly departs from RVT's LSTM-only design by introducing a spiking temporal feature extraction (STFE) module in the final block, combining convolutional, batch-normalization, and spiking-neuron components. The paper also positions itself against the growing family of pure-SNN detectors (SpikSSD, SpikingViT, EAS-SNN) by arguing that a hybrid ANN-SNN split—ANN for spatial, SNN for temporal—captures complementary strengths that neither paradigm achieves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of the Fall DVS Detection Dataset is a practical contribution: it provides a privacy-preserving, event-native benchmark for fall detection that avoids the facial-privacy and storage barriers of frame-based fall datasets, and it is generated via the ESIM simulator from the Le2i video dataset. However, the energy analysis (Appendix A) reveals that the hybrid architecture's total energy (134.5 mJ for HsVT-B) is substantially higher than pure-SNN baselines (7.26 mJ for SFOD, 28.10 mJ for EAS-SNN-M), so the efficiency argument rests primarily on parameter count rather than actual energy savings. Open questions include whether the STFE-in-last-block placement finding generalizes beyond the Aircraft dataset, whether the hybrid approach scales to larger event datasets like DSEC, and whether the observed non-monotonic model-size effect on FALL/AIR is truly a dataset-size artifact or reflects a fundamental limitation of the architecture on smaller corpora.
