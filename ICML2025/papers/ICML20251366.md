## Problem

Existing time-series generation methods are largely limited to unconditional, single-domain settings, leaving a gap for cross-domain, instance-level controlled generation that real-world applications (e.g., patient-specific ECG synthesis, domain-tailored financial data) demand. Two key obstacles remain: the scarcity of high-quality text-to-time-series paired data with instance-level detail, and the inherent modality mismatch between discrete textual tokens and continuous temporal signals that hinders precise text-driven control.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input (i) a textual description specifying trends, statistical properties, peak/dip locations, and domain context, and (ii) a small set of time series samples from the target domain (5 or 10 shots for unseen domains) used to extract prototype weights. The text is encoded by LLaMA3-8B, while the time series samples feed a feature extractor that produces a 16-dimensional prototype weight vector. The output is a synthetic univariate time series of length 168 (non-overlapping slices), generated via a denoising diffusion process conditioned on the fused text-prototype prompt.

## Method

BRIDGE is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (data preparation) uses a role-based multi-agent system—comprising a manager, two independent teams each with planner/scientist/engineer/observer agents—that iteratively refines general-purpose text templates via automatic evaluation (zero-shot TSF with LST-Prompt and LLMTime as proxy metrics) and inter-group consensus, producing a dataset-agnostic template library applied offline to populate instance-specific descriptions. Stage 2 (generation) employs a diffusion model with a hybrid conditioning mechanism: 16 randomly orthogonal semantic prototypes serve as a shared cross-domain 'dictionary,' and a Prototype Assignment Module computes per-instance weights from target-domain samples, which are injected as an attention mask into cross-attention layers alongside the text embedding. The denoising objective uses ε-parameterization, with the fused text-prototype prompt conditioning intermediate transformer blocks, enabling the model to capture both coarse domain structure (via prototypes) and fine-grained instance semantics (via text) simultaneously.

## Results

On 12 in-domain univariate datasets, BRIDGE achieves the best Marginal Distribution Distance (MDD) on 10 of 12 datasets and the best K-L divergence on 10 of 12, substantially outperforming all baselines. For example, on Electricity, BRIDGE attains MDD 0.220 and K-L 0.011, versus TimeVQVAE (1.763 / 0.185), TimeGAN (2.443 / 0.395), GT-GAN (2.026 / 0.415), and TimeVAE (3.306 / 0.580). In unseen-domain few-shot settings (5-shot and 10-shot on Stock and Web), BRIDGE also achieves the lowest MDD and K-L across all configurations, e.g., 3.112 MDD and 2.827 K-L on Stock at 10-shot versus the next-best TimeVAE at 3.338 / 4.479. Ablations show that removing text input causes the largest controllability drop (up to 12% MSE, 6% MAE), while removing prototypes causes moderate degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRIDGE outperforms all four baselines on the vast majority of datasets. Against TimeVQVAE, BRIDGE wins on 11/12 MDD scores (losing only on Air, where TimeVQVAE achieves 0.338 vs. BRIDGE's 0.447) and 11/12 K-L scores. Against TimeGAN, BRIDGE dominates on all 12 datasets for both MDD and K-L, with gaps as large as 7.6× on Electricity MDD (0.220 vs. 2.443). GT-GAN is similarly outperformed across all 12 datasets, with BRIDGE achieving roughly 2–8× lower MDD. TimeVAE is the closest competitor, winning on Solar MDD (365.9 vs. 375.5) and Air MDD (1.369 vs. 0.447 is actually BRIDGE winning; TimeVAE wins only on Solar), but BRIDGE still leads on 10/12 MDD and 10/12 K-L overall. In unseen-domain few-shot evaluation, BRIDGE's advantage over all baselines widens, confirming that the hybrid text + prototype conditioning generalizes better than fixed-architecture generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRIDGE builds on two foundational threads: the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (Ho et al., 2020) as the generative backbone, and TimeDP (Huang et al., 2025) for the semantic prototype mechanism that encodes cross-domain time-series features as a shared 'dictionary' of basis vectors. It departs from prior cross-domain TSG work (Huang et al., 2024; Kollovieh et al., 2024) that relied on explicit domain labels, and from earlier text-guided TSG efforts (GenG, Zhou et al., 2024; Time-MMD, Liu et al., 2024a) that operated only at the domain level, by introducing instance-level textual control combined with prototype-based domain conditioning. The multi-agent text refinement pipeline is itself inspired by LLM prompt-optimization literature (Zhou et al., 2023b; Guo et al., 2024) and the ReAct agent paradigm (Yao et al., 2023), repurposing these ideas to iteratively generate and evaluate time-series descriptions rather than task prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in TSG: real-world applications (medical ECG synthesis, power-grid stress testing) require generation that respects both domain characteristics and instance-specific constraints, yet high-quality text-TS paired data is scarce. The multi-agent framework for bootstrapping such data and the hybrid prototype-text conditioning offer a reusable pipeline, though the text remains relatively coarse relative to the continuous structure of time series, and the prototype vectors are randomly initialized rather than learned. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to multivariate or long-horizon generation, whether the LLM-based zero-shot forecasting proxy for text quality generalizes beyond the two evaluation datasets, and how much finer-grained control (e.g., specifying exact peak locations or amplitude) is achievable with current text encoders.
