## Problem

Existing contrastive learning approaches for short-text classification face three key limitations: data augmentation (e.g., removing graph components) can distort semantic meaning, external auxiliary information (entities, POS tags, topics) introduces noise into already sparse texts, and standard graph models are mathematically restricted to pairwise interactions, unable to capture higher-order group-wise patterns such as phrase-level repetition. This paper addresses all three by replacing graph-based augmentation with a structural-sequential contrastive framework built on simplicial complexes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251473/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text document, tokenizes it into unique words and punctuation as 0-simplexes (initialized with GloVe embeddings), constructs directed 1-simplexes (forward, backward, self-loop) following token order, and forms 2-simplexes as filled triangles among co-occurring words. The output is a class label prediction via a softmax classifier, with the document representation produced by a self-attention readout over 0- and 1-simplex features. The model operates inductively on unseen documents and is evaluated in a few-shot setting with only 40–400 labelled training samples per dataset.

## Method

C-SCN constructs a document simplicial complex per text and performs inductive message passing where each simplex aggregates from same-dimension neighbours, boundary (lower-dimension) faces, and co-boundary (higher-dimension) cofaces using MLP message functions and GRU update functions, with a global self-attention readout pooling 0- and 1-simplex features into a document vector. The key architectural insight is a dual-encoder contrastive framework: BERT produces a sequential representation Z_t while SCN produces a structural representation Z_s, and a cross-entropy contrastive loss pulls these two views together without requiring data augmentation or external knowledge. The final classification prediction is a linear interpolation of the softmax outputs from both encoders, trained jointly with a weighted sum of the classification loss and the contrastive loss (controlled by hyperparameter η). The 2-simplex types (nine variants determined by the flow directions of constituent 1-simplexes) encode group-wise phrase interactions that pairwise graph models cannot represent, and the inductive simplicial construction avoids the noise introduced by entity/POS enrichment or graph perturbation.

## Results

C-SCN achieves the best test F1 and accuracy across all four short-text classification benchmarks in a few-shot setting (20 samples per class for training). Headline results: Twitter F1 75.61% / Acc 76.09%, MR F1 69.46% / Acc 69.87%, Snippets F1 84.97% / Acc 85.56%, and StackOverflow F1 84.15% / Acc 83.87%. The closest competitor, GIFT (graph contrastive learning with SVD and k-means), trails by 2.45 F1 points on Twitter, 4.25 on MR, 1.24 on Snippets, and 1.08 on StackOverflow. Ablation studies confirm that removing either the 2-simplex component or the contrastive loss degrades performance on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-SCN outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset and metric. The strongest competitor is GIFT (graph contrastive learning with SVD-based augmentation and k-means weak labels), which C-SCN surpasses by 2.45 F1 points on Twitter (75.61 vs. 73.16), 4.25 on MR (69.46 vs. 65.21), 1.24 on Snippets (84.97 vs. 83.73), and 1.08 on StackOverflow (84.15 vs. 83.07). SHINE, which relies on hierarchical heterogeneous graphs with entity and POS tagging, is the second-closest on Twitter (72.54 F1) and Snippets (82.39 F1) but falls 3+ points behind C-SCN on MR. Notably, large pre-trained language models BERT and RoBERTa underperform in the few-shot regime (e.g., BERT achieves only 51.16% accuracy on Twitter and 50.65% on MR), while graph-based models with external auxiliary knowledge (HGAT, SHINE, NC-HGAT) achieve competitive but inferior results, suggesting that the noise introduced by entity/POS enrichment and graph perturbation-based contrastive augmentation limits their effectiveness. The ablation showing SCN (without contrastive learning) at 66.13 F1 on Twitter versus C-SCN's 75.61 confirms that the dual-encoder contrastive framework contributes roughly 9 F1 points beyond the structural simplicial representation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simplicial convolutional network (SCN) framework from Bodnar et al. (2021) — originally designed for general topological data — to the text domain by constructing document simplicial complexes from token sequences, a first in the literature. It departs from prior contrastive learning approaches for short-text classification (NC-HGAT, GIFT) that rely on data augmentation or external knowledge (entity/POS tagging) to generate positive/negative views, instead using the structural representation from SCN as a natural 'augmented view' of the sequential BERT representation in a dual-encoder setup. The message-passing mechanism on 0-, 1-, and 2-simplexes generalizes the pairwise neighborhood aggregation of standard GNNs (TextGCN, TextING, HyperGAT) to capture group-wise phrase interactions that graphs cannot express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that higher-order topological structures (simplicial complexes) can meaningfully enrich short-text representations beyond what pairwise graph models offer, while the contrastive dual-encoder design sidesteps the noise-introduction problem of graph perturbation-based augmentation. A notable open question is the interpretability of 2-simplex features and whether the approach scales to longer documents where the number of higher-order objects grows combinatorially. The finding that fixed GloVe embeddings outperform contextual BERT embeddings for the SCN branch (because contextual embeddings prevent 2-simplex formation) raises an interesting tension between contextualization and structural expressiveness that remains unresolved.
