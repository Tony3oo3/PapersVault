## Problem

Existing diffusion bridge models that use Doob's h-transform to connect two distributions (e.g., DDBMs, GOUB) frequently produce blurred or oversmoothed outputs with degraded high-frequency details, yet lack a theoretical explanation for this failure mode. The paper identifies that the h-transform implicitly enforces exact endpoint matching (equivalent to an infinite terminal penalty), which forces the SDE trajectory to sacrifice natural smoothness for pixel-perfect terminal alignment, and provides no mechanism to trade off these competing objectives.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired (high-quality x_0, low-quality x_T) image pairs at 256×256 resolution (CelebA-HQ inpainting, Rain100H deraining) or with 4× bicubic-downscaled LR inputs (DIV2K super-resolution). At inference, only the low-quality image x_T is provided, and the model iteratively denoises via a reverse SDE or deterministic Mean-ODE to recover the high-quality image x_0. A key hyperparameter γ (typically 10⁵–10⁸) controls how strictly the forward trajectory must reach x_T, and the method requires only a few lines of code change relative to existing GOUB/DDBMs implementations.

## Method

UniDB formulates the diffusion bridge as a Linear Quadratic Stochastic Optimal Control problem where the forward SDE has a general linear drift f_t·x_t + h_t·m and the cost function balances instantaneous control effort against a terminal penalty (γ/2)‖x_T^u − x_T‖². Applying Pontryagin's Maximum Principle yields a closed-form optimal controller u*_{t,γ} that depends on γ, and the paper proves that Doob's h-transform is recovered exactly when γ→∞, while finite γ produces a controller that passes near (not through) the terminal point, preserving trajectory smoothness. Concretely, for the GOU-based variant (UniDB-GOU), the only modification to GOUB is replacing σ²_{t:T} with γ⁻¹+σ²_{t:T} in both the h-function and the forward-transition mean coefficient. Training uses an L1 loss on the predicted mean μ_{t−1,θ} versus the analytic target μ_{t−1,γ} derived from the SOC-optimal forward transition, and sampling proceeds via Euler integration of either the reverse SDE or the deterministic Mean-ODE.

## Results

On DIV2K (4× super-resolution), UniDB-GOU with γ=1×10⁷ achieves PSNR 25.46, SSIM 0.6856, LPIPS 0.179, and FID 16.21, outperforming the GOUB equivalent (γ→∞) which scores PSNR 26.89 but with worse perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.220, FID 20.85). On CelebA-HQ (inpainting), the best configuration (γ=1×10⁷) reaches PSNR 29.20, SSIM 0.9077, LPIPS 0.036, FID 4.08. On Rain100H (deraining), γ=1×10⁸ yields PSNR 32.05, SSIM 0.9036, LPIPS 0.045, FID 17.65. The paper reports state-of-the-art results across all three tasks, with the most notable gains in perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID) over the Doob's h-transform baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against GOUB (SDE) and GOUB (ODE) [Yue et al., 2023], which UniDB-GOU directly subsumes as the γ→∞ special case. UniDB-GOU consistently outperforms GOUB on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID) across all three tasks—for example, on DIV2K super-resolution, FID drops from 20.85 (GOUB) to 16.21 (UniDB, γ=1×10⁷) and LPIPS from 0.220 to 0.179—while PSNR/SSIM trade-offs are task-dependent. Against DDRM [Kawar et al., 2022] and IR-SDE [Luo et al., 2023], the paper reports state-of-the-art results on all metrics for super-resolution, inpainting, and deraining, with visual comparisons (Figures 2–3) showing UniDB preserves sharper edges and finer textures. For deraining, UniDB also surpasses non-diffusion baselines MAXIM [Tu et al., 2022] and MHNet [Gao et al., 2025], and for inpainting it outperforms PromptIR [Potlapalli et al., 2023]. The key finding is that the finite-γ UniDB formulation produces more realistic high-frequency details than the exact-endpoint-matching Doob's h-transform approach, at the cost of slightly lower PSNR/SSIM in some settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion bridge models (DDBMs, GOUB) that rely on Doob's h-transform through the lens of Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC), showing that the h-transform corresponds to the degenerate case where the terminal penalty coefficient γ→∞. It builds directly on GOUB's mean-reverting GOU process and DDBMs' linear SDE formulations, unifying them under a single SOC problem with a tunable terminal penalty. The connection to prior SOC-in-diffusion work (Berner et al., 2022; Rout et al., 2024) and the infinite-dimensional SOC bridge of Park et al. (2024) is acknowledged, but UniDB's distinction is that it identifies the h-transform as a *suboptimal* special case rather than merely extending it to new function spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight—that forcing exact endpoint matching (γ→∞) is provably suboptimal and explains the blurred/oversmoothed artifacts in existing diffusion bridge outputs—provides a principled theoretical explanation for a long-observed practical limitation. The fix is remarkably minimal (replacing σ²_{t:T} with γ⁻¹+σ²_{t:T} in the h-function), making it a drop-in improvement for existing codebases. However, the practical gains are moderate (most notable in super-resolution FID, ~22% improvement; smaller in deraining and inpainting), and the paper acknowledges that sampling remains computationally intensive, leaving acceleration for future work. An open question is whether the optimal γ is task-dependent in a predictable way or requires per-task tuning.
