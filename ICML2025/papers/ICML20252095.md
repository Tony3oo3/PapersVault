## Problem

Convex optimization with noisy pairwise comparison (dueling) feedback has only been studied for restricted transfer functions—polynomial forms of degree p or the sign function—leaving common nonlinear transfers like sigmoid, tanh, and erf entirely uncovered. This paper asks whether one can design efficient optimization algorithms for a general class of transfer functions (any function admitting a series expansion about the origin) without requiring the algorithm to know the transfer, and what properties of the transfer dictate achievable convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a convex objective f only through a noisy comparison oracle: each query takes two points (w, w') and returns a single bit o ∈ {±1} whose expectation equals ρ(f(w) − f(w')), where ρ is an unknown fixed transfer function. The algorithm is fully oblivious to ρ and its parameters. It outputs an ε-optimal point w̄ ∈ D with high probability, requiring O(ε^{−4p}) queries for smooth convex objectives and Õ(ε^{−2p}) for smooth strongly convex ones, where p is the minimal nonzero degree in ρ's series expansion at zero.

## Method

The core algorithm, Projected Dueling Descent (PDD), estimates a 'relative gradient'—a p-th-order proxy of ∇f—by querying the dueling oracle on a symmetric pair (w + γu, w − γu) along a random unit direction u and using the 1-bit response o to form g = ou, then taking a projected step w ← Proj_D(w − ηg). Because the nonlinear transfer distorts gradient magnitude (introducing a ‖∇f‖^{p−1} factor) and biases estimates near the optimum, standard subgradient descent analysis fails; instead, the proof uses a stopping-time supermartingale argument to bound the first time the trajectory reaches ε-optimality. For strongly convex objectives, an epoch-based warm-start wrapper (Epoch-PDD) contracts the distance to w* by a constant factor per epoch via a separate concentration lemma (Lemma 6) that controls the biased gradient estimates near the optimum, yielding the optimal Õ(ε^{−2p}) rate.

## Results

The paper establishes two main convergence guarantees for the Projected Dueling Descent (PDD) algorithm. For smooth convex objectives, PDD requires O(ε^{-4p}) pairwise comparison queries to find an ε-optimal point with high probability (Theorem 2), where p is the minimal non-zero degree in the transfer function's series expansion about the origin. For smooth and strongly convex objectives, the epoch-based variant (Epoch-PDD) achieves Õ(ε^{-2p}) queries (Theorem 4), which is shown to be information-theoretically optimal, matching lower bounds from Jamieson et al. (2015). The algorithm is agnostic to the specific transfer function ρ, requiring only that it be differentiable, monotonically non-decreasing, and satisfy a local growth condition ρ'(x) ≥ c_ρ·p·|x|^{p-1} near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets; comparisons are made at the level of problem coverage and convergence rates. Compared to Jamieson et al. (2012), which handles polynomial transfer functions ρ(x) = c·sign(x)·|x|^p and provides tight bounds only for strongly convex + smooth objectives, this paper extends to a much broader class of transfer functions (any function admitting a series expansion about the origin, including sigmoid, arctan, tanh, erf) and additionally covers the general convex (non-strongly-convex) case with an O(ε^{-4p}) rate. Compared to Saha et al. (2021b), which is restricted to the sign transfer ρ(x) = sign(x) (corresponding to p = 0), this paper subsumes that result as a special case while handling arbitrary p ≥ 1. The strongly convex rate Õ(ε^{-2p}) matches the optimal rate of Jamieson et al. (2012) for their restricted transfer class, confirming that the generality of the transfer function does not degrade the achievable rate in the strongly convex regime. A key technical distinction is that the paper's analysis does not rely on the global structure of ρ (as Jamieson et al. do) but only on its local behavior near the origin, enabling the broader function class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dueling convex optimization framework of Jamieson et al. (2012) and Saha et al. (2021b) by replacing their restricted transfer function classes (polynomial ρ(x)=c·sign(x)|x|^p and sign(x) respectively) with a general class defined by local series-expansion behavior around the origin. The gradient estimation technique follows the zeroth-order/bandit lineage of Flaxman et al. (2005) and Saha and Tewari (2011), but the analysis must account for the nonlinear distortion of gradient magnitude by the transfer function, which is absent in those settings. The epoch-based warm-starting for the strongly convex case departs from Jamieson et al.'s line-search coordinate descent, which fails without strong convexity, in favor of a black-box reuse of the convex-case algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap: even the sigmoid transfer function, ubiquitous in preference modeling, was not covered by prior dueling optimization theory. The O(ε^{-4p}) convex rate and the matching O(ε^{-2p}) strongly convex rate (tight per Jamieson et al.'s lower bounds) give practitioners a clear picture of how transfer nonlinearity degrades optimization. Open questions include the exact lower bound for the smooth-but-not-strongly-convex case, extension beyond smoothness, regret-minimization formulations, and generalization to subset preferences or contextual/adversarial settings.
