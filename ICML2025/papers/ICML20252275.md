## Problem

Existing dynamic graph link prediction methods embed all local structures into a single geometric space (Euclidean or hyperbolic), ignoring the geometric heterogeneity across different substructures within the same graph. Additionally, they focus on macro-level temporal changes while overlooking fine-grained micro-level link variations (appearance and disappearance) between consecutive snapshots, leading to suboptimal predictive performance on evolving graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252275/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of discrete-time graph snapshots G_t = (V, E_t) with node feature vectors, extracting 4-hop ego-graphs centered on each node at every timestamp to compute per-node Gromov hyperbolicity. It outputs a probability distribution over both edge existence and edge absence for all potential node pairs at the next timestamp, computed via a Fermi-Dirac decoder parameterized by hyper-parameters r and s. The geometric space assignment (hyperbolic vs. Euclidean) is determined per-node per-timestamp based on the computed hyperbolicity of each ego-graph, rather than being fixed globally.

## Method

The framework comprises three key modules operating at each timestamp. The Dynamic Geometric Modeling (DGM) module computes the Gromov hyperbolicity δ of each node's 4-hop ego-graph; nodes with δ≈0 (tree-like hierarchical structures) are mapped into the Poincaré disk via the exponential map and processed by HGCN, while remaining nodes use standard GCN in Euclidean space, with both branches fused through a cross-geometric MLP with curvature-aware non-linear activation. The Temporal State Aggregator (TSA) maps historical representations into a higher-dimensional space, computes softmax attention scores over all past timestamps, and produces a weighted aggregate as the current hidden state. A novel Link Evolution Loss (LEL) explicitly models micro-level dynamics by minimizing cosine-similarity margins for newly appeared edges and maximizing them for disappeared edges between consecutive snapshots. The DGM output and TSA hidden state are jointly fed into a GRU cell, and the model is trained by minimizing the sum of a standard Fermi-Dirac link prediction loss and the LEL, enabling simultaneous optimization of prediction accuracy and fine-grained structural evolution.

## Results

On temporal link prediction (Table 1), the proposed method achieves AUC scores of 94.13% (DBLP), 94.17% (Ia-Enron), 93.31% (LFB), 97.19% (HepPh), and 61.68% (UNVote), outperforming all baselines by an average of 3.66% AUC and 2.04% AP over the suboptimal method. On temporal new link prediction (Table 2), improvements are substantially larger, with an average gain of 8.13% AUC and 8.94% AP, reaching 97.18% AUC on HepPh and 93.95% AUC on DBLP. The method also demonstrates superior efficiency, achieving the fastest training and inference times among all compared dynamic graph methods on HepPh and LFB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and both metrics (AUC and AP) for temporal link prediction. The strongest competitors are the hyperbolic dynamic methods HGWaveNet (Bai et al., 2023) and HTGN-B/L (Yang et al., 2022), which are the closest baselines on most datasets; the proposed method surpasses HGWaveNet by 4.17% AUC on DBLP, 5.25% on Ia-Enron, 3.80% on LFB, and 4.82% on HepPh. Euclidean dynamic methods (GRUGCN, EvolveGCN, DySAT) trail the hyperbolic dynamic methods by 3–10% AUC on most datasets, confirming the benefit of hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical structures. Static methods (GAE, HGCN, HAT) and the simple EdgeBank baseline perform substantially worse, with EdgeBank scoring below 25% AUC on most datasets. On the harder temporal new link prediction task (Table 2), the advantage widens dramatically: the method gains 20.49% AUC over the best baseline on Ia-Enron and 10.64% AP on UNVote, validating the Link Evolution Loss component's contribution to capturing fine-grained edge appearance/disappearance dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) geometric graph learning, where HGCN (Chami et al., 2019) and GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017) provide the hyperbolic and Euclidean embedding primitives respectively, and (2) dynamic/temporal graph neural networks such as EvolveGCN, DySAT, HTGN, and HGWaveNet that model link evolution over time. The key departure is that all prior dynamic methods commit to a single geometric space globally, whereas this paper introduces a per-node, per-timestamp geometry selection mechanism (Dynamic Geometric Modeling) driven by local Gromov hyperbolicity, combined with a novel Link Evolution Loss that explicitly penalizes embedding misalignment for newly appeared and disappeared edges. The Temporal State Aggregator is a lightweight attention-based alternative to the RNN-based temporal modules in EvolveGCN and VGRNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-geometric dynamic framework addresses a genuine gap: real-world dynamic graphs contain coexisting hierarchical and non-hierarchical local structures that shift over time, and no prior method adapts the embedding geometry at the local-structure level across timestamps. The Link Evolution Loss provides a principled way to capture micro-level link dynamics beyond macro-level snapshot prediction. Open questions include whether the binary hyperbolicity threshold (δ≈0 vs. δ≠0) is too coarse for structures with intermediate curvature, whether the O(k⁴·N) hyperbolicity computation scales to very large graphs, and whether the approach extends naturally to continuous-time dynamic graphs rather than discrete snapshots. The modest gains on larger datasets (3–5% AUC) versus large gains on smaller ones (20% on Ia-Enron) suggest the method's advantage may be most pronounced when structural heterogeneity is high relative to graph size.
