## Problem

Existing cluster-based graph pooling methods rely on soft node assignment and uniform (summation-based) aggregation of both node features within clusters and edge connectivity strengths between clusters, ignoring the varying importance of individual nodes and edges. Additionally, conventional MPNNs suffer from over-squashing, and existing rewiring strategies either treat all equidistant nodes equally or discard original graph topology, making them poorly suited for exploiting the edge connectivity strengths produced by cluster-based pooling.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input graph with a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} whose entries encode edge connectivity strengths (number of paths between node pairs). Through successive pooling layers, the graph is progressively coarsened by a configurable pooling ratio (e.g., 0.125, 0.25, 0.5), reducing node count while preserving weighted structural information. The final coarsened graph representation is passed through a readout function to produce a single graph-level class label.

## Method

ENAHPool performs three steps per pooling layer: (1) hard node assignment via argmax over an MD-MPNN-computed probability matrix (with straight-through estimator for backpropagation), ensuring each node belongs to exactly one cluster; (2) node-based attention that computes per-node importance scores via a shared MLP + LeakyReLU + self-attention layer, softmax-normalizes within each cluster, and forms cluster representations as weighted sums; (3) edge-based attention that computes pairwise attention coefficients between node embeddings, softmax-normalizes within each cluster pair, and aggregates edge connectivity strengths as a weighted sum to form the coarsened adjacency matrix. The companion MD-MPNN architecture raises the adjacency matrix to powers A^h, masks with binary matrices U_h to isolate edges at exactly shortest distance h, and concatenates MPNN outputs across all distances 0 to H, enabling direct multi-distance message propagation while preserving edge connectivity strength information. The backbone MPNN is GCN, and edge attention is intentionally omitted in the first pooling layer to prevent all connectivity strengths from collapsing to 1.

## Results

The proposed ENAHPool with MD-MPNN achieves the best classification accuracy on 7 out of 8 benchmark datasets. Headline results include 79.91% on D&D, 77.12% on PROTEINS, 79.34% on NCI1, 67.68% on FRANKENSTEIN, 74.54% on IMDB-B, 51.74% on IMDB-M, 81.09% on COLLAB, and 88.56% on REDDIT-B. The only dataset where it does not achieve the top score is COLLAB, where SEP-G edges it out by 0.19 points (81.28% vs. 81.09%). Even without the MD-MPNN architecture (ENAHPool(-MD)), the pooling operation alone outperforms most baselines, confirming the contribution of the attention-based aggregation design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENAHPool outperforms all 12 baselines on 7 of 8 datasets, with the sole exception being COLLAB where SEP-G achieves 81.28% versus ENAHPool's 81.09% (a 0.19-point margin). The largest margins of improvement are on REDDIT-B (88.56% vs. TopKPool's 85.12%, a +3.44-point gain) and FRANKENSTEIN (67.68% vs. ASAP's 66.26%, +1.42 points). Among cluster-based hierarchical pooling methods, ENAHPool surpasses DiffPool by up to 17.02 points (NCI1: 79.34% vs. 62.32%), MinCutPool by up to 5.09 points (REDDIT-B: 88.56% vs. 83.47% implied from missing entries, but on D&D: 79.91% vs. 78.22%), and StructPool by up to 0.70 points (NCI1: 79.34% vs. 78.64%). Against global pooling methods, ENAHPool leads GMT by 1.19 points on D&D and SortPool by 4.33 points on REDDIT-B. The ablation variant ENAHPool(-MD), which replaces MD-MPNN with conventional MPNNs, still beats most baselines, confirming that the attention-based pooling design itself contributes substantially independent of the multi-distance message passing architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cluster-based hierarchical pooling lineage (DiffPool, StructPool, MinCutPool, SEP-G) by replacing soft node assignment with hard assignment and introducing dual attention mechanisms over both intra-cluster nodes and inter-cluster edges, addressing the uniform aggregation limitation that prior methods share. The MD-MPNN architecture adapts the adjacency-matrix-power idea from MixHop but adds a shortest-path masking mechanism (via binary reachability matrices R_h and U_h) to isolate messages at exact distances, and weights them by edge connectivity strength rather than treating all equidistant nodes uniformly as in SPN or GRED. The straight-through estimator for the non-differentiable hard assignment follows the standard quantization trick from Yin et al. (2019), and the overall framework is positioned as a co-design of pooling and message-passing rather than a standalone pooling module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-design of a pooling operation that preserves edge connectivity strengths with an MPNN backbone that explicitly exploits those strengths is a clean conceptual contribution that could inform future hierarchical GNN architectures. However, the O(N³) complexity from adjacency matrix powers limits scalability to large graphs, and evaluation is restricted to small-to-medium benchmark datasets (up to ~4300 graphs with ~430 average nodes). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other MPNN backbones beyond GCN/GIN, whether the edge-attention mechanism remains beneficial at larger scales where matrix powers become prohibitive, and whether the hard-assignment + STE combination is stable in deeper pooling hierarchies.
