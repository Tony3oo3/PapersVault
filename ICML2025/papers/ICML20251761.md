## Problem

Residual connections in deep networks use a fixed identity mapping to combine the non-linear block output with the input, offering no learned mechanism to adaptively weight or augment the residual stream. While naively adding layers or parameters can improve quality, it incurs substantial increases in model size, latency, and memory. The paper asks whether a lightweight, learned augmentation of the residual connection itself can match or exceed the quality gains of naive scaling while adding negligible parameters and overhead.

## Input / Output

LAUREL is an internal architectural modification that replaces the standard residual connection within any block-based architecture (ResNet convolutional blocks or transformer encoder/decoder blocks), so it does not alter the model's external input or output format. For vision experiments, the model still takes RGB images and outputs ImageNet-1K class labels; for language experiments, it still takes text or multimodal token sequences and produces generated text or benchmark scores. The only change is that each residual connection now involves a small number of additional learned parameters (scalars or low-rank matrices) that operate on the hidden representations between blocks.

## Method

LAUREL reformulates the canonical residual connection x_{i+1} = f(x_i) + x_i as x_{i+1} = α·f(x_i) + g(x_i, x_{i-1}, …, x_0), where α is a learned scalar and g is a learned linear function over current and past residual-stream activations. Three composable variants are proposed: LAUREL-RW introduces two learned scalars (α, β) to weight the non-linear output and residual input; LAUREL-LR adds a low-rank matrix product BA (rank r ≪ D) to the residual path, contributing 2rD parameters per layer; LAUREL-PA extends this by incorporating k previous block activations with learned scalar weights and low-rank linear projections. The key insight is that by allocating a small learned linear capacity to the residual stream, the main non-linear function f is freed to focus on learning non-linear transformations, improving quality with only 0.003%–0.1% additional parameters and 1–2% latency overhead.

## Results

On ResNet-50/ImageNet-1K, LAUREL-RW+LR achieves 75.20% top-1 accuracy with only 1.68% extra parameters, matching the naive-scaling baseline (75.20% with 4.37% extra params) while using 2.6× fewer parameters; LAUREL-RW+LR+PA further improves to 75.25% with 2.40% extra params. On the 1B-parameter LLM, LAUREL-RW+LR (r=4, adding only 0.012% parameters) improves BoolQ by +13.07%, GSM8K-CoT by +5.39%, and MATH by +4.51% over the baseline. On the 4B-parameter LLM, LAUREL-RW+LR (r=64, ~0.1% extra params) yields gains of +20.05% on BookQA, +15.45% on MGSM, +12.75% on MMMU, and +2.54% on MMLU. In the direct LAUREL-40 vs. BASELINE-41 comparison, LAUREL-40 outperforms the 41-layer naive-scaling model on 8 of 10 tasks while adding ~36× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against naive model scaling (adding an extra layer). On ResNet-50/ImageNet-1K, LAUREL-RW+LR matches the 41-layer baseline's 75.20% accuracy while adding 1.68% vs. 4.37% parameters (2.6× fewer), and LAUREL-RW+LR+PA exceeds it at 75.25% with 2.40% extra params (1.82× fewer). On the 4B LLM, LAUREL-40 (4.404B params, +0.1%) beats BASELINE-41 (4.56B params, +3.63%) on 8 of 10 evaluation tasks, including large margins on BookQA (+21.46% vs. baseline-40) and MGSM (+13.94%), while tying on WMT23 and DocVQA. LAUREL-40 also incurs a slightly higher step time (+2.42%) than BASELINE-41 (+1.81%), but the parameter overhead is ~36× smaller. On the 1B LLM, LAUREL outperforms the baseline on all tasks except MBPP (neutral), with the largest gains on BoolQ (+13.07%) and GSM8K-PAL (+10.28%), all at a cost of only 0.012% extra parameters and negligible latency increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAUREL is a direct generalization of the canonical residual connection (He et al., 2016), reformulating x_{i+1} = f(x_i) + x_i into a learned linear combination over the current and previous residual-stream activations. It is conceptually related to Highway Nets and Residual Gates, which also learn gating weights on the residual path but at O(D²) parameter cost per layer, whereas LAUREL-RW uses only 1–2 scalars. The LAUREL-PA variant draws on DenseNet's idea of accessing prior activations but restricts access to k recent layers with learned scalar weights and low-rank projections to keep memory overhead at Θ(kD). The low-rank factorization in LAUREL-LR borrows the A·B decomposition from LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), but applies it at the residual-connection level during pretraining rather than as a fine-tuning adapter on weight matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAUREL offers a near-zero-cost drop-in replacement for the identity residual that consistently improves quality across both CNN and transformer architectures, and it demonstrably outperforms naive layer-adding on a parameter-efficiency basis (2.6× fewer parameters for equivalent ImageNet gains; ~36× fewer parameters than an extra layer for the 4B LLM). The main open questions are whether LAUREL-PA's benefits transfer to large-scale LLM pretraining (not tested due to compute cost), how initialization of the low-rank matrices affects convergence, and whether the gains persist at 70B+ parameter scales where the residual stream's role may shift. For practitioners, the practical recommendation is clear: try LAUREL-RW first (2 parameters), then add LR if a slightly larger budget is acceptable.
