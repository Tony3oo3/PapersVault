## Problem

Standard Reservoir Computing models time series through recursive updates between adjacent states only, making it unable to capture long-term dependencies and multi-scale cyclical patterns. While spectral methods like TimesNet address periodicity, they rely on gradient-based optimization and are resource-intensive. The paper asks whether spectral insights can be integrated into the lightweight, gradient-free RC paradigm to simultaneously model sequential and cyclical dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of variable length (15–2844 steps, 2–60 classes) as input. It first detrends the series with a Hodrick-Prescott filter, then decomposes the residual via wavelet decomposition. The output is a concatenated dynamic feature vector formed by flattening the ridge-regression readout matrices (W_out) from all FreqRes modules, which is fed to a simple classifier (default: Ridge) to produce a class label. No gradient backpropagation is involved at any stage.

## Method

SARC operates in two stages. First, it extracts multi-scale prominent frequencies: after HP-filter detrending and wavelet decomposition into J+1 cyclical components, each component undergoes FFT to select its peak frequency, which is then extended to sub-harmonics (f_j/k) bounded by a threshold κ, yielding a set F of multi-scale frequencies. Second, for each frequency f ∈ F, a Frequency-informed Reservoir Network (FreqRes) iterates hidden states in a 2D grid pattern using two independent reservoirs: W_s captures sequential dynamics (x(n−1)) and W_c captures cyclical dynamics (x(n−p), where p = ⌊L/f⌋). The readout W_out is solved in closed form via ridge regression on an autoregressive next-step prediction task, and the resulting W_out serves as the dynamic feature for that frequency. All reservoir weights are randomly initialized and fixed; only the linear readout is computed, eliminating backpropagation entirely.

## Results

On the full UCR Time Series Archive (128 datasets), SARC achieves the highest average accuracy of 0.8508 and average F1-score of 0.8449, with the best average rank (3.34) across all methods. SARC is statistically significantly better than all baselines except COTE (p=0.403) and Rocket (p=0.221), and wins in pairwise comparisons on 61–119 out of 128 datasets depending on the baseline. In terms of efficiency, SARC's training is tens to hundreds of times faster than gradient-based methods (ConvMESN, InceptionTime) and several times faster than lightweight transforms (MiniRocket, Rocket), with a complexity of O(κM(S²L+S³)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARC outperforms all eight baselines in average accuracy and F1-score on the 128-dataset UCR Archive. Against the closest competitors, COTE (0.8484) and Rocket (0.8450), SARC's advantage is small (+0.24% and +0.58% accuracy) and not statistically significant (p > 0.05), though SARC still wins more individual datasets (61/128 and 60/128 respectively). The gap widens substantially against deep learning baselines: SARC surpasses InceptionTime (0.8352) by 1.56% and TimesNet (0.6867) by 16.41% in accuracy, both with p < 0.001. Among RC-based methods, SARC improves over ConvMESN (0.8108) by 4.00% and over rmESN (0.7689) by 8.19%, confirming the benefit of spectral awareness. In runtime, SARC is tens to hundreds of times faster than gradient-based methods (ConvMESN, InceptionTime) and several times faster than MiniRocket and Rocket, while only rmESN is marginally faster but at a large accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARC extends the Reservoir Computing lineage (ESN, DeepESN, ConvMESN, rmESN) by introducing a spectral-aware mechanism that structures reservoir connectivity around data-driven cyclical periods, departing from the standard adjacent-state-only recursion. Conceptually, it is closest to TimesNet, which also leverages Fourier-derived periodicity, but whereas TimesNet uses periods to reshape inputs into 2D tensors for vision backbones, SARC uses them to define explicit cycle-skip connections within the reservoir itself. The framework is deliberately modular: the FreqRes component can be instantiated atop any existing RC variant (ESN, LeakyESN, BiESN, DeepESN), positioning SARC as an architectural add-on rather than a standalone model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SARC offers a practical efficiency-accuracy trade-off for TSC: it matches or exceeds gradient-based SOTA (COTE, InceptionTime) while being tens to hundreds of times faster in training, making it attractive for resource-constrained or real-time settings. The ablation confirming that naive gating (LSTM/GRU) degrades RC performance without adequate training is a useful caution for the RC community. Open questions include how spectral information mechanistically drives classification gains, whether the multivariate extension (currently using mean-series frequency extraction) can capture inter-variable interactions, and applicability to tasks beyond classification such as anomaly detection.
