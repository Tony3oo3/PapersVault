## Problem

Concept-based explanation methods like TCAV require practitioners to manually guess and collect candidate concept image sets, making the process labor-intensive and prone to overlooking concepts the network actually relies on. Retrieval-based alternatives (segmentation, factor analysis) are constrained to concepts already visible in test images, missing higher-level abstractions like 'stripes' for zebra classification. This paper reframes concept set creation as an image generation problem to automatically discover concepts that are genuinely relevant to a network's internal representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained classifier (e.g., GoogleNet, ViT), a set of test images for a target class, and 20 seed prompts auto-extracted by posing VQA questions (pattern, color, texture, shape) to BLIP on image patches, then filtering via CLIP cross-similarity and VLM relevance scoring. It produces generated concept images from Stable Diffusion v1-5, per-concept TCAV scores indicating importance, pixel-level concept heatmaps via CLIPSeg, and c-deletion reliability curves that validate concept ordering. The LoRA-adapted SD model (rank 8, targeting U-Net transformer modules) is the persistent artifact that encodes the learned concept generation policy.

## Method

RLPO couples a DQN agent that selects among 20 discrete seed prompts with a preference-optimization loop that fine-tunes Stable Diffusion's LoRA weights. At each step, the chosen prompt generates 2Z images split into two groups; TCAV scores are computed for both, and if the maximum score is below a threshold η (typically 0.7), the Diffusion-DPO loss (Wallace et al., 2024) updates SD weights to favor the higher-scoring group. The DQN reward is the TCAV score scaled by an adaptive factor ξ that ramps from 0.1 to 1 as the threshold is approached, encouraging the agent to keep optimizing until concepts reach an 'explainable state.' The key design choice is using TCAV as the preference signal rather than human or LLM feedback, which makes the optimization model-specific, requires <1 GB GPU memory, and runs in ~56 s per step versus 180 s for human feedback. This dual-loop architecture—discrete action selection via DQN plus continuous weight refinement via preference optimization—allows the system to navigate the vast prompt space while incrementally steering the generator toward concepts that activate the target network.

## Results

RLPO achieves a TCAV score of 1.0 (matching all baselines) while generating concepts that are substantially more novel and abstract than retrieval-based methods. Specifically, RLPO concepts have a mean Cosine Similarity of 0.49–0.52 to test images (vs. 0.69–0.77 for baselines) and a ResNet50 Cosine Similarity of 0.02–0.04 (vs. 0.42–0.67), confirming that generated concepts are genuinely different from the test class rather than being patches of it. In a human survey with 500 participants, RLPO's Exploration Gap reached 91.54% for laymen and 65.45% for experts, meaning the vast majority of humans cannot independently identify these concepts as important to the network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLPO is compared against three retrieval-based concept extraction methods: EAC (Sun et al., 2024), Lens (Fel et al., 2024), and CRAFT (Fel et al., 2023). All methods achieve a TCAV score of 1.0, so the distinguishing factor is concept novelty. RLPO decisively outperforms all three baselines on every novelty metric: its concepts have ~30% lower CLIP Cosine Similarity to test images (0.49–0.52 vs. 0.69–0.77), ~30% higher Euclidean Distance (10.48–10.74 vs. 7.17–8.45), and dramatically lower ResNet50 Cosine Similarity (0.02–0.04 vs. 0.42–0.67), indicating that RLPO generates genuinely abstract concepts (e.g., 'stripes' for zebra) rather than pixel-level patches of the test class. The human survey further confirms this advantage: retrieval-based methods have an Exploration Gap of only 6.54% (laymen) and 10.45% (experts), meaning humans can easily recognize those concepts, whereas RLPO's 91.54% and 65.45% gaps show that the generated concepts are largely beyond human intuition. No baseline matches RLPO on any of the four novelty metrics, and the margin is largest on ResNet50-based similarity (RCS), where RLPO is roughly 10–20× lower than baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions concept-based explanation (TCAV, Kim et al. 2018) by replacing the manual curation or segmentation-based retrieval of concept image sets (ACE, EAC, CRAFT, Lens) with an RL-guided generative process. It repurposes Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) by substituting human preference labels with TCAV scores as the preference signal, and wraps the entire loop in a DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) that selects among seed prompts extracted via a VQA model (BLIP). The key conceptual departure is from retrieval-based methods, which are constrained to pixel-level patches of test images, to a generative paradigm that can produce abstract, high-level concepts (e.g., 'stripes' rather than 'a patch of zebra') that the network has internally learned but that do not visually resemble the test class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in concept-based XAI: practitioners must guess which concepts to test and manually collect image sets, a process that is labor-intensive and systematically misses concepts the network has learned without human intervention. The exploration-gap survey (91.5% of laymen, 65% of experts missing generated concepts) quantifies this gap and motivates automated generation. Open questions include how the method scales to domains where the generative model's pretraining biases are problematic (e.g., medical imaging), how to automatically select the abstraction threshold η, and whether the approach generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale classification to more complex or multi-label settings. The modular design (swappable SD backbone, replaceable scoring function) makes it a practical tool, but the dependence on seed prompt quality and the single-survey evidence for the exploration gap leave room for stronger validation.
