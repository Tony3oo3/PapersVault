## Problem

Transformer neural processes (TNPs) offer a flexible probabilistic framework for spatio-temporal state estimation but cannot scale to large datasets due to the quadratic cost of full attention. Efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., Swin Transformer, ViT) that enable scaling to large gridded data are inapplicable to the unstructured, multi-modal observations typical of real-world spatio-temporal settings. The paper bridges this gap by projecting unstructured data onto a structured grid of pseudo-tokens, unlocking efficient transformer architectures for TNP-based state estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context set of unstructured input-output pairs at arbitrary spatio-temporal locations, potentially spanning multiple modalities recorded at distinct locations (e.g., weather station observations alongside gridded reanalysis fields), along with a set of target locations at which to evaluate predictions. It outputs a factorised Gaussian predictive distribution (mean and variance) at each target location, enabling evaluation at arbitrary points rather than being restricted to the input grid. The framework supports context sets exceeding 100,000 datapoints and handles cylindrical geometry (e.g., longitudinal wrapping) for Earth-based data.

## Method

The gridded TNP decomposes the processor into three stages: (1) a pseudo-token grid encoder that cross-attends from k-nearest-neighbour unstructured tokens to location-specific pseudo-tokens on a regular grid, (2) a grid processor using an efficient attention transformer (Swin or ViT) operating on the structured pseudo-token grid, and (3) a nearest-neighbour cross-attention grid decoder that retrieves predictions at arbitrary target locations from the k closest grid pseudo-tokens. The key design choice is using distinct initial pseudo-token values per grid location (rather than shared values as in VSA), enabling the model to capture fixed topographical information. Translation equivariance is incorporated efficiently by replacing the expensive pairwise-distance MLP with fixed learnable relative positional encodings in the Swin processor (exploiting the fixed grid structure), while the encoder and decoder use TE-MHCA that scales linearly due to the nearest-neighbour restriction. The model is trained via meta-learning by maximising the expected predictive log-likelihood over sampled context-target splits.

## Results

On the t2m station prediction task (ERA5, ~10K context points), the best Swin-TNP with pseudo-token grid encoder (64×128 grid) achieves a test log-likelihood of 1.819, outperforming ConvCNP (1.535) and PT-TNP (1.344) while maintaining a competitive forward pass time of 127 ms. On the more challenging multi-modal wind speed task (543K observations, 6 variables at 3 pressure levels), Swin-TNP with multi pseudo-token grid encoder (24×60) reaches 8.603 log-likelihood versus 6.143 for ConvCNP and 3.988 for PT-TNP. With approximate translation equivariance (Swin-TNP(T̃)), the model further improves to 10.429 on an unseen European test region, whereas the non-equivariant Swin-TNP collapses to −13.265, demonstrating strong spatial generalisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares gridded TNPs against three baselines: CNP, PT-TNP (induced set transformer), and ConvCNP. On the t2m station task, Swin-TNP (PT-GE, 64×128) achieves 1.819 log-likelihood, beating ConvCNP (1.535) by 0.284 and PT-TNP (1.344) by 0.475, while running faster than PT-TNP (127 ms vs. 230 ms). On the multi-modal wind speed task, the gap widens substantially: Swin-TNP (m-PT-GE, 24×60) reaches 8.603 versus ConvCNP's 6.143 (+2.46) and PT-TNP's 3.988 (+4.62), with the larger ConvCNP (48×120) still trailing at 7.841. The pseudo-token grid encoder consistently outperforms kernel-interpolation encoding (e.g., 8.603 vs. 7.603 for Swin-TNP on wind speed), and the multi-grid-encoder variant outperforms the single-grid-encoder variant (8.603 vs. 8.073). With translation equivariance, Swin-TNP(T̃) further improves over the non-equivariant Swin-TNP (1.926 vs. 1.819 on t2m; 10.086 vs. 8.603 on wind speed US) and, critically, generalises to an unseen European region (10.429) where the non-equivariant model fails catastrophically (−13.265).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer Neural Process (TNP) family by introducing a gridded architecture that bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: large-scale weather models (GraphCast, Aurora, Pangu) that exploit efficient attention on structured grids but cannot handle unstructured observations, and NPs (CNP, ConvCNP, TNP) that handle arbitrary input locations but are limited by quadratic attention complexity. The pseudo-token grid encoder draws on the Perceiver/ISTNP pseudo-token bottleneck and the Voxel Set Transformer's local cross-attention, while the grid processor directly adopts ViT and Swin Transformer architectures. The translation equivariance component adapts the TE-TNP and approximately equivariant NP frameworks of Ashman et al. (2024a,b) to the gridded setting, where fixed relative positional encodings replace the expensive per-pair MLP computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gridded TNPs remove the primary scalability barrier for NPs on large spatio-temporal data (100K+ context points), making them viable for real-world applications like weather data assimilation, multi-modal sensor fusion, and PDE modelling on irregular meshes. The spatial generalisation result—where the equivariant Swin-TNP(T) transfers from US to European wind data while the non-equivariant version fails catastrophically—is a strong demonstration of the practical value of equivariance inductive biases. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly massive datasets (millions of points, as in full-resolution global weather), how it compares to dedicated foundation models (Aurora, Prithvi WxC) on pure forecasting tasks, and whether the grid resolution and neighbourhood size choices transfer across domains without re-tuning.
