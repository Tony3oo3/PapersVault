## Problem

Existing quantum methods for differential equations (amplitude estimation, phase estimation, variational quantum algorithms) are inherently tied to specific equations and cannot learn nonlinear operators or generalize across varying conditions. The few quantum implementations of neural operators (QFNO, quantum DeepONet) either fail to outperform their classical counterparts or rely on hybrid quantum-classical architectures that introduce communication overhead. This paper addresses the absence of a pure-quantum, hardware-efficient neural operator capable of learning general differential equation operators in the NISQ era.

## Input / Output

The method takes the input function u evaluated at 100 fixed sensor points and a query output location y, both scaled by a hyperparameter coefficient λ to fit within [0, 2π] for quantum gate encoding. The output is the scalar operator value G(u)(y), computed as the expectation value ⟨ψ_θ(y,u)|H|ψ_θ(y,u)⟩ of a chosen Hamiltonian (typically H = Σσ_z^(i)) over a 5-qubit state. Input functions are sampled from mean-zero Gaussian random fields with RBF kernels, and the model is trained on pairs generated by solving ODEs (Runge-Kutta) or PDEs (finite difference) with zero initial/boundary conditions.

## Method

QuanONet mirrors DeepONet's Branch-Trunk architecture but implements both branches as pure quantum circuits using a hardware-efficient ansatz (HEA) on 5 qubits, with the Branch encoding input function values and the Trunk encoding the output location in reverse order to realize an inner-product form. The paper proves a quantum universal approximation theorem for operators (Theorem 3.2) showing that the operator can be approximated by the inner product of two quantum state functions, grounding the architecture theoretically. The key practical challenge is that the encoding coefficient λ controls the accessible frequency spectrum: too small a λ requires prohibitively deep circuits for Fourier completeness. TF-QuanONet resolves this by making the encoding coefficients and biases trainable (λ⊙[y,u]+b), allowing the circuit to adaptively match the dominant frequency of the target operator without manual hyperparameter tuning, and empirically achieves the best quantum-method performance across all four benchmark problems at matched parameter counts (~1200 for ODE, ~2400 for PDE).

## Results

On the four differential equation benchmarks (anti-derivative, homogeneous operator, nonlinear ODE, and diffusion-reaction system), TF-QuanONet achieves the lowest test MSE among all quantum methods across all λ initializations, with errors of 0.000113, 0.000192, 0.039, and 0.000055 respectively at λ=0.001. TF-QuanONet outperforms DeepONet (0.000596, 0.000665, 0.027, 0.000088) on three of four problems, with the largest margin on the anti-derivative operator (~5× lower error). On the nonlinear ODE, where the frequency spectrum is more spread out, classical DeepONet (0.027) still edges out TF-QuanONet (0.039), though TF-QuanONet remains the best quantum method. A 2-qubit TF-QuanONet deployed on IBM Brisbane Q57/Q58 achieves MSE = 1.57e-3 with zero-noise extrapolation, versus 1.5e-5 in noiseless simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-QuanONet decisively outperforms both quantum baselines (HEA and TF-HEA) on all four problems: e.g., on the anti-derivative operator it achieves 0.000113 vs. 0.030112 (HEA) and 0.002484 (TF-HEA), a 27× and 22× improvement respectively. Against the classical DeepONet, TF-QuanONet wins on three of four problems (anti-derivative: 0.000113 vs. 0.000596; homogeneous: 0.000192 vs. 0.000665; D-R system: 0.000055 vs. 0.000088) but loses on the nonlinear ODE (0.039 vs. 0.027), where the broader frequency spectrum demands deeper circuits. FNN is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing DeepONet on every problem. The key differentiator is robustness: TF-QuanONet maintains near-identical performance across λ ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}, whereas QuanONet (fixed-frequency) degrades by 10–100× when λ is poorly chosen, and HEA is highly sensitive to λ. In the appendix comparison with FNO (Li et al., 2020), TF-QuanONet also outperforms FNO on all three ODE problems at matched iteration budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical DeepONet branch-trunk architecture and the Chen-Chen universal approximation theorem for operators into the quantum domain, proving a quantum universal approximation theorem for operators (Theorem 3.2) that guarantees a pure-quantum circuit can approximate any continuous nonlinear operator. It departs from prior quantum neural operator attempts—quantum DeepONet (Xiao et al., 2024a) and QFNO (Jain et al., 2024)—by eliminating hybrid classical-quantum architectures and relying entirely on quantum circuits with hardware-efficient ansatz layers. The trainable-frequency extension (TF-QuanONet) draws on Jaderberg et al. (2024) and the quantum frequency principle (Xu & Zhang, 2024) to address the sensitivity of fixed-frequency QNNs to the encoding coefficient λ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pure-quantum neural operator grounded in a formal quantum operator approximation theorem, providing a theoretical foundation that prior hybrid approaches lacked. However, the empirical validation is limited to 5-qubit circuits on simple ODEs and one PDE, and the real-device test uses only 2 qubits with a 100× error gap versus simulation, leaving open whether quantum advantage materializes at scale. Key open questions include: whether the quantum Hilbert space provides genuine expressivity gains over classical operators for smooth, spectrally simple problems (as Yu et al., 2024 suggest), how the architecture scales beyond NISQ hardware, and whether the frequency-sensitivity issue is fundamentally resolved or merely mitigated by TF-QuanONet.
