## Problem

Standard transformer architectures are empirically designed with multiple interacting components (attention, MLP, LayerNorm, skip connections) that lack mathematical justification, and empirical studies suggest some components may be redundant. This paper asks whether a minimalistic transformer consisting solely of self-attention layers with skip connections—no MLP blocks—can achieve performance comparable to standard transformers, and provides a theoretical explanation for why self-attention alone suffices as a denoising mechanism.

## Input / Output

The architecture takes a sequence of N tokens (image patches of fixed size for vision, subword tokens for language, or input-output pairs for in-context learning) and first maps them to initial d-dimensional representations Z^(0) via positional encoding and token embedding. Each layer then iteratively refines these representations through the MSSA/MHSA operator plus a residual connection, ultimately producing task-specific outputs: a class label via a classification head, the next-token distribution via a causal language modeling head, or a scalar regression prediction for ICL. The key structural constraint is that all layers share the same form—attention plus skip connection—with no feedforward sublayer.

## Method

The paper models token representations as a mixture of noisy low-rank Gaussians, where each token's signal lies in a p-dimensional subspace U_k and noise is spread across other subspaces. Under this model, multi-head subspace self-attention (MSSA) is shown to act as a denoising operator: each head projects tokens onto its subspace, computes softmax-weighted similarities among projected representations, and pulls tokens toward their correct subspace. By unrolling this iterative denoising step as layers of a deep network (each layer = MSSA + skip connection), the authors construct an attention-only transformer with no MLP layers. Theorem 3.1 proves that under the mixture-of-Gaussians model, each layer multiplies the signal-to-noise ratio by (1 + ητ), yielding linear SNR improvement with depth. In practice, the subspace bases U_k are learned end-to-end via backpropagation, and the MSSA operator reduces to standard multi-head self-attention with shared Q=K=V projections, making the architecture a strict simplification of GPT-2 or ViT with MLP blocks removed.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, AoT-MSSA-V achieves 71.7% top-1 accuracy with 22M parameters (vs. CRATE's 79.5% with 39M), and AoT-MHSA-V achieves 69.5% with 15M parameters (vs. ViT's 72.4% with 22M). On language modeling, AoT-MHSA-L Base (122M) achieves a WikiText validation loss of 4.19 and LAMBADA validation loss of 4.42, closely matching GPT-2 Base (124M) at 4.13 and 4.32 respectively. In in-context learning on linear and sparse linear regression, AoT models achieve normalized squared error curves close to GPT-2, confirming that attention-only layers suffice for in-context function learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRATE (Yu et al., 2024) on ImageNet-1K, AoT-MSSA-V trails by 7.8 percentage points in top-1 accuracy (71.7% vs. 79.5%) but uses only 22M parameters versus CRATE's 39M, demonstrating that the attention-only design retains most of the representational power with roughly 44% fewer parameters. Against ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), AoT-MHSA-V achieves 69.5% top-1 accuracy with 15M parameters compared to ViT's 72.4% with 22M, a 2.9-point gap at 32% fewer parameters. Against GPT-2 Base (Radford et al., 2019) on language tasks, AoT-MHSA-L Base (122M, same parameter budget) is within 0.10 on WikiText val loss (4.19 vs. 4.13) and 0.10 on LAMBADA val loss (4.42 vs. 4.32), while actually outperforming GPT-2 on PTB (5.52 vs. 5.75); zero-shot accuracies are within 1–2 points across LAMBADA, CBT-CN, and CBT-NE. The paper also shows that adding MLP layers to AoT accelerates training convergence but does not improve final zero-shot performance, suggesting MLPs contribute primarily to optimization speed rather than representational capacity in this regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the MSSA operator introduced in Yu et al. (2024; 2023) for white-box transformers via sparse rate reduction, but reframes it as a subspace denoising step within an unrolled optimization framework. It departs from prior pure-attention transformer analyses (Dong et al. 2021, Alcalde et al. 2024, Noci et al. 2024) that studied rank collapse or clustering dynamics in isolation, by providing a principled generative model (mixture of low-rank Gaussians) under which each attention layer has a clear denoising interpretation and a provable linear SNR improvement guarantee. The unrolled-optimization lineage (Chan et al. 2022, Monga et al. 2021) supplies the architectural template, while the linear representation and superposition hypotheses motivate the subspace structure assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative theoretical guarantee (linear SNR improvement per layer) for why attention-only transformers work, grounding the empirical observation that MLP layers are largely redundant in a mathematical framework. This opens the door to more parameter-efficient and interpretable transformer designs, and the attention-head visualizations on ImageNet suggest individual heads acquire distinct semantic roles. Key limitations include the strong assumption of orthogonal subspaces with known bases, the gap between the idealized MSSA theory and the practical MHSA implementation, and the consistent (if small) performance deficit versus full transformers. Open questions include whether the low-rank Gaussian model holds for real pretrained representations, how the theory extends to non-orthogonal or overlapping subspaces, and whether the linear SNR rate persists at scale.
