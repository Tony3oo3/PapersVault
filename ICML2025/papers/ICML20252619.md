## Problem

Current text-to-motion generation models are constrained by small-scale datasets (at most ~320K sequences) and limited codebook sizes, preventing them from generalizing to unseen motions. It remains unexplored whether scaling both data volume and model parameters yields consistent performance gains in motion generation, and how to design motion representations that can effectively leverage million-scale training data without suffering from codebook collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252619/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes natural language motion descriptions formatted as instruction-following prompts (e.g., 'Show me a demonstration of <caption> through movement') and produces a 3D human motion sequence encoded in SMPL-D135 format (135 dimensions per frame: 9D root parameters + 126D joint 6D rotations). Internally, the motion is tokenized into discrete tokens from a 16,384-entry codebook via 2D-LFQ, which the LLM backbone generates autoregressively between <mot> and </mot> special tokens. The hierarchical text annotations include both body-level summaries (1-3 sentences) and part-level descriptions (one sentence per body part).

## Method

Being-M0 builds on a decoder-only LLM backbone (LLaMA-2/3 or GPT-2) with motion tokens added as extended vocabulary, trained in two stages: pretraining on the full 1.2M MotionLib dataset followed by instruction tuning on 900K curated prompts. The key encoding innovation is MotionBook, which combines a compact lossless SMPL-D135 feature (preserving full rotation data without IK recovery) with a 2D Lookup-Free Quantization (2D-LFQ) tokenizer that reformulates each motion sequence as a single-channel 2D image (T×D×1), enabling part-level tokenization and a binary codebook of size 2^16 that avoids lookup operations and codebook collapse. The 2D-LFQ quantizer maps each feature dimension to {-1, +1} via sign-based rounding, computing token indices through binary encoding, and is trained with reconstruction, perceptual, commitment, and entropy losses (without GAN loss). The paper demonstrates that both data scale (0.02M→1.2M) and model size (355M→13B) independently improve R-Precision and FID, with the largest gains on out-of-domain evaluation.

## Results

On the HumanML3D benchmark, Being-M0-LFQ (LLaMA2-13B) achieves the best R-Precision among all compared methods: R@1=0.528, R@3=0.820, and MMDist=2.875, outperforming the strongest specialist model LMM (R@1=0.525, R@3=0.811) and the prior best generalist MotionGPT-v2 (R@1=0.496, R@3=0.782). On the larger MotionLib evaluation set, scaling from 0.08M to 1.2M training samples with LLaMA2-13B improves R@1 from 0.124 to 0.185 and FID from 21.0 to 6.2, demonstrating clear scaling laws for both data and model size. On the out-of-distribution UNSEEN-90K test set, the MotionLib-trained model (R@1=0.098) substantially outperforms HumanML3D-trained (R@1=0.034) and Motion-X-trained (R@1=0.051) baselines, confirming superior generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Being-M0-LFQ achieves the highest R@1 (0.528) and R@3 (0.820) on HumanML3D, surpassing the best specialist diffusion model LMM (0.525/0.811) and MoMask (0.521/0.807), as well as all LLM-based generalist baselines including MotionGPT-v2 (0.496/0.782), MotionLLM (0.482/0.770), AvatarGPT (0.389/0.623), and MotionGPT2 (0.411/0.696). On MMDist, Being-M0-LFQ (2.875) also leads all baselines, beating LMM (2.943) and Fg-T2M++ (2.925). However, on FID, specialist diffusion models retain an advantage: LMM (0.040), MoMask (0.045), and DiverseMotion (0.072) all outperform Being-M0-LFQ (0.141), though Being-M0-LFQ still beats all LLM-based generalists (MotionGPT-v2: 0.191, MotionLLM: 0.491, AvatarGPT: 0.567). The 2D-LFQ tokenizer variant improves over the VQ variant by +0.009 R@1 and −0.025 FID, and on the larger MotionLib reconstruction benchmark, 2D-LFQ (FID=2.315, MPJPE=64.1) substantially outperforms VQ-VAE (FID=5.324, MPJPE=123.6) and RQ-VAE (FID=4.026, MPJPE=78.2), with the gap widening on out-of-domain data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the LLM-based multimodal paradigm (treating a new modality as a 'foreign language' with tokens appended to the vocabulary, as in MotionGPT and Visual Instruction Tuning) to human motion generation, but pushes it to a fundamentally different scale. The 2D-LFQ tokenizer draws directly from finite scalar quantization (Mentzer et al., 2023) and lookup-free codebooks (Yu et al., 2023), repurposing ideas from image/video compression to the motion domain by reformulating 1D motion sequences as 2D single-channel images. The two-stage training (pretraining on full data + instruction tuning) mirrors the LLaVA recipe, and the SMPL-D135 feature is a deliberate departure from the H3D-Format used by most prior T2M work, motivated by lossless rotation preservation and real-time applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionLib (1.2M sequences, 15× larger than prior datasets) addresses the core data bottleneck that has kept motion generation models narrow and domain-specific, and the first systematic scaling study for both data volume and model parameters provides a roadmap for the field. The 2D-LFQ tokenizer's ability to scale codebook size without collapse is a practical technique likely to be adopted by future motion and other sequential-signal tokenizers. Key open questions remain: the paper itself acknowledges that current evaluation metrics (small autoencoder-based FID/R-Precision) are unreliable and poorly calibrated, that the model still lags specialist diffusion models on in-domain small benchmarks, and that convergence is slow due to limited tokenizer capacity — all suggesting the field needs both better evaluation infrastructure and further tokenizer improvements before 'large motion models' truly match the generality of LLMs.
