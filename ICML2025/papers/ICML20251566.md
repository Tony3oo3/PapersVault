## Problem

Off-policy deep RL agents are forced to complete entire episodes even when they are traversing familiar, low-quality trajectories, wasting interaction budget and polluting replay buffers with uninformative experiences. This 'sunk cost fallacy'—the inability to make strategic stopping decisions—is a previously overlooked structural limitation in mainstream actor-critic algorithms that degrades sample efficiency, particularly in long-horizon tasks.

## Input / Output

At each environment step, LEAST consumes the current Q-value estimate (minimum of two critics) and the TD loss magnitude as scalar signals, comparing them against per-step adaptive thresholds derived from median statistics over a rolling window of recent episodes (typically 150–250 episodes). The output is a binary stop-and-reset signal that terminates the episode early; when not triggered, the agent proceeds normally with its standard policy action. No additional network parameters are introduced—the mechanism operates entirely on statistics already available during standard training.

## Method

LEAST constructs a dual-criteria adaptive stopping threshold at each intra-episode step i. The quality criterion uses the median Q-value across recent episodes at step i (stored in a reflection buffer B_Q), while the learning-potential criterion uses the ratio ω_i = Median(B_G[:,i]) / G_i, where B_G stores historical gradient magnitudes and G_i is the current TD loss. The agent stops when Q̂_i falls below ω_i × ε_i (or ω_i^{-1} × ε_i when ε_i < 0), so high current gradients (novel states) relax the threshold to encourage continued exploration, while low gradients on low-Q trajectories trigger early termination. Two auxiliary mechanisms stabilize the approach: an entropy-based dynamic buffer resize that adjusts the reflection window when policy instability inflates distributional uncertainty, and an episode-level exploration noise schedule that increases Gaussian noise σ when early stopping occurs frequently, helping the agent escape suboptimal basins after reset.

## Results

On MuJoCo benchmarks, LEAST consistently improves both sample efficiency and final performance across TD3, SAC, and REDQ, with particularly notable gains on Ant and Walker2d. On the DeepMind Control Suite (image-based), DrQv2+LEAST converges approximately 30% faster than vanilla DrQv2 on Finger Turn Hard and outperforms feature-based baselines CURL and A-LIX while approaching SOTA TACO. In the motivating PointMaze experiment, the vanilla SAC agent plateaus at ~60 normalized score on long-horizon mazes (horizon 24), while LEAST maintains stable performance by avoiding wasteful exploration of known low-quality transitions. REDQ+LEAST achieves the highest overall normalized scores among all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAST outperforms vanilla TD3 on Ant and Walker2d with faster convergence, though on HalfCheetah it achieves higher final scores at the cost of slower early-stage learning (vanilla TD3 prematurely converges to suboptimal strategies). Against vanilla SAC, LEAST accelerates early learning and helps converge to better policies; SAC+LEAST reaches performance comparable to DroQ (a dropout-regularized SAC variant), suggesting LEAST provides similar benefits without architectural changes. On REDQ, which already has strong baseline performance due to its 10-critic ensemble, LEAST's gains are less pronounced early but improve sample efficiency in middle and later training stages, with REDQ+LEAST achieving the highest overall scores. In the visual RL setting, DrQv2+LEAST outperforms both CURL and A-LIX (which improve feature representations) on DMC tasks, approaching the performance of TACO (the SOTA DrQv2 variant with temporal contrastive learning), demonstrating that adaptive stopping is a cost-effective alternative to heavy architectural modifications. The paper notes that LEAST may increase score variance across seeds (wider spread in box plots), suggesting room for improved training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard off-policy actor-critic algorithms (TD3, SAC, REDQ, DrQv2) and introduces a lightweight stopping mechanism that is orthogonal to existing sample-efficiency techniques like prioritized replay, small-batch regularization, and curiosity-driven exploration. Conceptually, it draws from behavioral economics (sunk cost fallacy) to reframe a structural limitation in RL—forced episode completion—as a cognitive bias, positioning early termination as a missing decision capability rather than a sampling or architecture problem. It departs from prior early-stopping work (Pardo et al., Poiani et al.) which focused on uncertain episode lengths or Monte Carlo settings, by targeting the more general case of strategic termination within fixed-horizon continuous control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEAST offers a drop-in, network-free mechanism that consistently improves sample efficiency across diverse algorithm families and modalities (state-vector and image-based), making it practically attractive for practitioners who want gains without architectural changes. However, the paper acknowledges increased score variance, the unresolved issue of agents re-entering suboptimal trajectories after reset, and sensitivity to several hyperparameters (start time, buffer size, noise schedule), all of which limit immediate plug-and-play adoption. Open questions include whether the Q-value/gradient threshold can be made more robust in high-dimensional or multi-task settings, and whether the conceptual framing of 'sunk cost' in RL generalizes beyond episode-level stopping to other forms of commitment bias (e.g., in curriculum learning or multi-agent coordination).
