## Problem

Existing metrics for evaluating preference data quality in LLM alignment rely on either the explicit reward margin (from a reward model) or the implicit reward margin (from the LLM policy), and these two metrics can produce contradictory quality assessments for the same preference pair. The paper asks whether a single, theoretically grounded metric can reconcile both perspectives to reliably identify high-quality data for alignment training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference pair (x, y_w, y_l) along with explicit reward scores r(x, y_w), r(x, y_l) from an external reward model (e.g., ArmoRM or PairRM) and the LLM's length-normalized log-probabilities log π_θ(y|x)/|y| to compute the implicit reward. It outputs a scalar M_AP score per pair, which is used to rank and select a top-k subset (e.g., top-40% or top-10k) for downstream DPO or SimPO training. A key implementation detail is that the implicit reward uses SimPO's reference-free formulation rather than DPO's, since the standard DPO implicit reward is identically zero when the policy and reference model are the same at initialization.

## Method

The core insight derives from DPO's optimal condition: at the aligned optimum, the implicit reward margin equals the explicit reward margin, so the gap between them quantifies remaining alignment potential. M_AP is defined as the target explicit reward margin minus the current implicit reward margin, with two critical adaptations: (1) unidirectional calibration, which removes the absolute value from the implicit margin to match the one-sided update direction of DPO/SimPO, and (2) reward noise regularization, which reintroduces absolute values to prevent the metric from amplifying erroneous reward model annotations where the LLM and RM strongly disagree. Theoretically, the paper proves that selecting data with the largest M_1 score (the uncalibrated gap) as an adversarial sampler achieves more than 2× faster convergence than uniform sampling under DPO with optimal learning rates. Practically, the metric is integrated into the eva self-play framework with an evolve-then-select modification, where prompts are first evolved via EvolInstruct and then the resulting dataset is filtered by M_AP, enabling iterative high-quality data generation that scales with both dataset size and training iterations.

## Results

In the evolve-then-select self-play setting (Table 1), M_AP-selected data consistently outperforms both the eva baseline and additional UltraFeedback data across Llama-3-8b-instruct and Gemma-2-9b-it under both DPO and SimPO. For example, with SimPO on Llama-3-8b, +ours-10k achieves 34.6 Arena-Hard WR and 43.76 AlpacaEval 2 LC, surpassing +eva-10k (32.6 / 41.12) and +uf-10k (28.5 / 35.75). On Gemma-2-9b with SimPO, +ours-10k reaches 68.34 AlpacaEval 2 WR, exceeding +eva-10k (66.86) by 1.5 points. In the data-selection experiments (§3.2), M_AP-selected top-40% subsets improve AlpacaEval 2 LC by 2.5–6.2 points and Arena-Hard WR by 4.7–7.1 points over the uniform baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares M_AP against four baselines: (1) the eva self-play framework (Ye et al., 2024), which selects data by explicit reward margin M_r; (2) the explicit reward margin metric M_r (Wang et al., 2024b); (3) the implicit reward margin metric M_π (Yang et al., 2024); and (4) Muldrew et al. (2024), which also uses implicit reward margins. In the self-play setting (Table 1), M_AP-selected data beats eva on every benchmark: e.g., Llama-3-8b SimPO +ours-10k scores 34.6 Arena-Hard WR vs. eva's 32.6, and Gemma-2-9b SimPO +ours-10k scores 68.34 AlpacaEval 2 WR vs. eva's 66.86. In the data-selection experiments (Figures 4–5), M_AP outperforms both M_r and −M_π across all models and benchmarks; for instance, on Llama-3-8b with SimPO, M_AP achieves 47.83 AlpacaEval 2 LC vs. 44.60 for M_r and 47.15 for −M_π. The paper also shows that M_AP's advantage persists under different reward models (ArmoRM vs. PairRM), training objectives (DPO, SimPO, IPO), and selection ratios (20%–60%), confirming robustness over all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously conflicting data quality metrics for preference learning—the explicit reward margin (Wang et al., 2024b; Ye et al., 2024) and the implicit reward margin (Yang et al., 2024; Muldrew et al., 2024)—into a single alignment potential metric M_AP that measures the gap between the target explicit margin and the current model's implicit margin. The formulation is grounded in the DPO optimal condition (Rafailov et al., 2024) and the SimPO reference-free implicit reward (Meng et al., 2024), while the self-play extension builds on the eva framework (Ye et al., 2024) and EvolInstruct (Xu et al., 2024a). The key departure from prior work is the reward noise regularization: by reintroducing absolute values around both margins, M_AP avoids amplifying erroneous reward model annotations that plague the unidirectional M_+ variant, a practical concern not addressed by existing single-margin metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, unified data selection criterion for offline preference learning, with a clean convergence-rate theorem showing adversarial sampling (selecting data with largest alignment potential) achieves >2× faster convergence than uniform sampling under DPO. Practically, the metric enables smaller, higher-quality training subsets (30k selected data outperforming 60k full UltraFeedback) and integrates into iterative self-play pipelines for continuous data augmentation. Open questions include how to automatically set the α/β mixing parameter, whether the metric generalizes beyond DPO/SimPO to other objectives (KTO, ORPO), and whether the theoretical speedup in the simplified bandit setting translates to the full LLM training regime. The paper also notes that post-training procedures of the tested instruction-tuned models are not fully disclosed, limiting reproducibility.
