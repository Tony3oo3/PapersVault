## Problem

Despite the empirical success of flow matching (FM) models, the theoretical understanding of how individual ODE sampling trajectories interact with the underlying data geometry remains largely unexplored. In particular, it was unknown whether trajectories are guaranteed to converge to the data distribution as t→1 when the data lies on a low-dimensional submanifold or discrete set, and how coarse-scale (clustering) and fine-scale (manifold vs. discrete) geometry shape the trajectory at different stages of sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252456/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a Gaussian noise sample x_0 ~ N(0,I) and a target data distribution p with a finite 2-moment (which may be supported on a submanifold, discrete set, or general bounded support) and produces a generated sample x_1 via ODE integration, along with the full trajectory characterization. The framework is parameterized by the noise-to-signal ratio σ = β_t/α_t rather than time t, making all results scheduling-independent and applicable uniformly across linear, EDM, and other common schedules. The output includes the flow map Ψ_1, convergence rates (O(σ^ζ/2) general, O(√σ) for manifolds, O(σ) for discrete data), and equivariance properties under similarity transformations.

## Method

The central insight is that the denoiser m_σ(x) = E[X|X_σ=x] is the sole data-dependent component of the FM vector field, and it guides ODE dynamics through two geometric properties: attracting (trajectories drawn toward a set when the denoiser direction forms an acute angle with the projection direction) and absorbing (trajectories confined within neighborhoods of a set). Two meta-theorems formalize these properties, which are then instantiated to characterize three distinct trajectory stages: initial mean-attraction, intermediate local-cluster absorption (via a well-separated cluster assumption), and terminal convergence to the data support. The terminal convergence (Theorem 5.3) is established under weak assumptions—positive reach of the support and a polynomial lower bound on the measure—that accommodate data on submanifolds, going beyond prior results that required full support. The analysis further yields equivariance of flow maps under scaling, rotation, and translation, and connects terminal Voronoi-cell absorption to memorization in discrete-data settings.

## Results

This is a primarily theoretical paper with no standard benchmark comparison table. The key quantitative findings are: (1) the FM ODE flow map converges at rate O(σ_t^(ζ/2)) for general data, O(√σ_t) for m-dimensional submanifolds, and O(σ_t) for discrete distributions; (2) for CIFAR-10 with ε=1.0, the mean attraction time σ₀(V_i^ε) across training images is approximately 0.17, corresponding to the final quarter of EDM's sampling steps, indicating that memorization risk is concentrated in the terminal stage; (3) the denoiser converges to the projection onto the data support as σ→0 for all points outside the medial axis. The paper establishes the first rigorous convergence guarantee for FM ODE trajectories at t=1 under assumptions that accommodate data supported on low-dimensional submanifolds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing generative methods on a benchmark; it is a theoretical analysis paper. Its contributions are positioned relative to prior theoretical work: Gao et al. (2024) established ODE existence only under restrictive assumptions excluding submanifold-supported data, while this paper (Theorem 4.1) proves well-posedness for any distribution with finite 2-moment. Gao & Li (2024) analyzed local cluster absorption but implicitly assumed ODE convergence and required bounded prior support; this paper provides a rigorous convergence proof (Theorem 5.3) and full trajectory analysis without those restrictions. Permenter & Yuan (2024) interpreted sampling as approximate projection to data; this paper goes further by characterizing the full three-stage trajectory evolution (mean attraction → cluster absorption → terminal convergence) with explicit rate guarantees. The concurrent work by Baptista et al. (2025) shares similar Voronoi-based analysis for discrete data but focuses on regularization rather than establishing the convergence foundation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023) and the denoiser-as-conditional-mean perspective from EDM (Karras et al., 2022), but shifts the analytical lens from distribution-level to per-sample trajectory dynamics. Prior works on diffusion/FM sampling evolution (Biroli et al., 2024; Li & Chen, 2024) focused on stochastic samplers with simplified data models like Gaussian mixtures, while Gao et al. (2024) established ODE existence only under restrictive full-support assumptions. The key departure is a rigorous per-sample trajectory analysis that handles data supported on low-dimensional submanifolds—a setting all prior convergence results excluded—unified through the novel 'attracting and absorbing' geometric framework applied to the denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing FM ODE trajectory convergence at terminal time for data on submanifolds, filling a gap that blocked theoretical understanding of the most common real-world data geometry. The practical implications are concrete: the convergence rate analysis suggests fewer sampling steps suffice at the terminal stage, the equivariance property under similarity transformations informs stable data augmentation and fine-tuning, and the memorization analysis (σ₀ ≈ 0.17 for CIFAR-10, matching the final quarter of EDM steps) provides a principled target for regularization. Open questions include whether the O(√σ) manifold convergence rate can be tightened to O(σ), and whether the framework extends to settings where the prior and data are coupled (e.g., rectification or minibatch OT).
