## Problem

Existing explainability methods for Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) are limited to factual explanations that identify which past events contribute to a prediction, but none provide counterfactual explanations that reveal the minimal set of events whose removal would flip the model's decision. This gap is particularly acute for continuous-time dynamic graphs (CTDGs), where complex temporal and spatial dependencies make the search space for explanatory subgraphs exponentially large and structurally non-trivial.

## Input / Output

CoDy takes as input a trained TGNN (e.g., TGN or TGAT), a CTDG represented as a sequence of timestamped events, a target future link event to explain, and a selection policy (random, temporal, spatio-temporal, or event-impact). The search space is constrained to events within a k-hop spatial neighborhood of the target link and the m_max most recent events satisfying that constraint (k is set to the TGNN's layer count, m_max=64 in experiments). The output is a counterfactual subgraph — a minimal subset of past events whose omission changes the model's binary prediction — along with a score quantifying the magnitude of the prediction shift; if no valid counterfactual is found, a fallback selects the perturbation set producing the largest prediction change.

## Method

CoDy adapts Monte Carlo Tree Search to counterfactual explanation by constructing a search tree where each node represents a unique subset of omitted past events, with child nodes extending their parent by one additional omitted event. The four MCTS steps are specialized: Selection uses a UCB-based score balancing exploration and exploitation (α=2/3); Simulation calls the TGNN on the perturbed graph to compute a normalized prediction-shift score; Expansion adds all valid child nodes; and Backpropagation recursively updates exploitation and exploration scores up to the root. Four selection policies rank candidate events for omission, with the spatio-temporal policy (prioritizing spatially close and temporally recent events) and event-impact policy (ranking by measured logit change from single-event removal) proving most effective. A key design choice is that once a node's score exceeds 1 (indicating a valid counterfactual), it is marked non-selectable to prevent expanding into higher-complexity counterfactuals, and a fallback strategy ensures an explanation is always returned even when no counterfactual is discovered.

## Results

CoDy-spatio-temporal achieves the highest AUFSC+ (necessity) scores across most settings, reaching 0.43 on UCI-Forums correct predictions and 0.50 on Wikipedia incorrect predictions. The headline result is a 16% improvement in AUFSC+ over the strongest baseline (GreeDy-spatio-temporal) across all tasks. CoDy-event-impact leads on incorrect predictions (e.g., 0.52 AUFSC+ on Wikipedia), while CoDy-spatio-temporal leads on correct predictions. Factual baselines perform poorly on necessity: T-GNNExplainer averages 75.05% lower AUFSC+ than CoDy-spatio-temporal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDy is compared against two factual baselines (PGExplainer and T-GNNExplainer) and its own greedy counterfactual baseline (GreeDy) across four selection policies. Against PGExplainer, CoDy dominates on all metrics: PGExplainer's AUFSC+ scores range from 0.02–0.11, while CoDy-spatio-temporal reaches 0.16–0.50, reflecting PGExplainer's inability to identify necessary (rather than sufficient) features. Against T-GNNExplainer, which is specifically designed for TGNNs, CoDy-spatio-temporal still outperforms by an average of 75.05% in AUFSC+, with T-GNNExplainer scoring only 0.01–0.19 versus CoDy's 0.16–0.50. Against GreeDy (the strongest counterfactual baseline), CoDy-spatio-temporal achieves a 16% average improvement in AUFSC+; the sole exception is UCI-Forums correct predictions where GreeDy-spatio-temporal (0.44) narrowly edges out CoDy-spatio-temporal (0.43). On the char metric for incorrect predictions, CoDy-event-impact achieves the best scores (0.58/0.57/0.68), surpassing GreeDy-spatio-temporal (0.54/0.46/0.54) by up to 22% on UCI-Messages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNN explainability literature from static graphs to Continuous-Time Dynamic Graphs (CTDGs), adapting Monte Carlo Tree Search (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) to the counterfactual setting. While factual explainers for TGNNs (T-GNNExplainer, TempME) and counterfactual explainers for static GNNs (CF-GNNExplainer, Tan et al.) already exist, no prior method addresses counterfactual explanation for TGNNs on CTDGs. The paper also introduces GreeDy as a greedy-search baseline, filling the absence of a dedicated counterfactual comparator in the temporal graph setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDy provides the first actionable, model-agnostic counterfactual explanations for TGNNs, which is critical for deploying these models in high-stakes domains like finance and healthcare where users need to understand why a prediction was made. Key limitations include the restriction to event removal (no addition or modification of graph elements), heavy reliance on repeated TGNN forward passes (~250–350 calls per explanation), and sensitivity to the exploration-exploitation parameter α and candidate set size m_max. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger graphs, whether user studies confirm the practical utility of the generated explanations, and how to extend the search to richer perturbation operations.
