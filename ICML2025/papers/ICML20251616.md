## Problem

Existing Riemannian geometry approaches to latent space analysis (pullback metrics from Shao et al. 2018, Park et al. 2023) are restricted to deterministic generative models where each latent point maps to a single output. For stochastic generative models—diffusion models with stochastic samplers and statistical physics systems—a single latent parameter corresponds to a distribution over outputs, making pullback-based metrics inapplicable. This paper asks: can one reconstruct the Fisher information metric on the latent space of such stochastic models, and what geometric structure (e.g., phase transitions) does it reveal?

## Input / Output

For diffusion models, the method consumes 2D slices of latent space constructed by bilinear interpolation between three random initial latents (60,000 images generated per slice via StableDiffusion 1.5 with DDIM, 50 steps, CFG=5), and produces a learned log-partition function log Z(α,β) whose Hessian gives the Fisher metric, geodesic interpolation paths, and phase boundary maps. For statistical physics validation, inputs are 128×128 spin configurations (Ising) or 16384-site 1D particle sequences (TASEP), reshaped into images for U²-Net processing. The log Z is defined only up to an affine transformation, so all comparisons (RMSE, geodesic lengths) are invariant to this ambiguity. The posterior approximation uses either CLIP embedding distances (for images) or a U²-Net trained to output a categorical distribution over a 128×128 parameter grid.

## Method

The method proceeds in two steps: (1) approximate the posterior p(t|x₁,…,x_N) over latent parameters given N generated samples, using either a U²-Net trained by maximum likelihood (for pixel-level statistical physics samples) or CLIP feature distances that approximate the KL divergence via squared feature-mean differences (for images); (2) learn log Z_θ(t) via an MLP (5 hidden layers, 512 units, ReLU) by minimizing Jensen-Shannon divergence between the approximated posterior and a normalized Bregman-divergence distribution derived from log Z_θ. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 3.1) is that as N→∞, the posterior concentrates as exp(−D_Bregman(t,t')), and Theorem 3.2 guarantees that vanishing MSE between Bregman divergences implies uniform convergence of the Hessian (Fisher metric). JSD is used instead of the theoretically natural MSE loss to avoid exponentially vanishing gradients (Lemma A.6). For non-exponential-family models (e.g., diffusion), the Bryant-Amari-Armstrong theorem justifies local Hessianizability in 2D, enabling the same framework. Geodesics are then computed by discretizing the curve and optimizing intermediate points with Adam under the learned Fisher metric, revealing approximately linear paths within phases but nonlinear deviations at fractal phase boundaries where the Lipschitz constant diverges.

## Results

On the 2D Ising model, the proposed convex log-partition reconstruction achieves F RMSE of 0.0883, dF/dT RMSE of 0.1106, and dF/dH RMSE of 0.1237, substantially outperforming both the Mean-as-Stat baseline (0.0981, 0.4766, 1.0936) and PCA-VAE (0.1669, 0.7428, 0.7988). On TASEP, the method achieves near-exact free energy reconstruction with F RMSE of 0.0112, compared to 0.0529 (Mean-as-Stat) and 0.0524 (PCA-VAE). For diffusion model interpolation (StableDiffusion 1.5), the Fisher-metric geodesic achieves CLIP path length 72.3±4.00 and mean curvature 0.367±0.691, matching linear interpolation in path length while reducing curvature by 72% relative to the Wang/Shao pullback-metric geodesic (1.33±0.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines on exactly solvable models and two on diffusion interpolation. Against Mean-as-Stat (Fearnhead & Prangle 2012; Jiang et al. 2017), the proposed method achieves 4.3× lower dF/dT RMSE (0.1106 vs 0.4766) and 8.8× lower dF/dH RMSE (0.1237 vs 1.0936) on Ising, and 4.7× lower F RMSE (0.0112 vs 0.0529) on TASEP. Against PCA-VAE (Walker et al. 2020), the advantage is even more pronounced: 6.7× lower dF/dT RMSE on Ising and 4.7× lower F RMSE on TASEP. For diffusion model geodesic interpolation (Table 2), the method is on par with the Wang/Shao pullback-metric baseline in path length (CLIP 72.3 vs 73.6) but achieves 72% lower mean curvature (0.367 vs 1.33), which the authors attribute to high-frequency noise introduced by finite-difference Jacobian estimation in the Wang/Shao approach. The method's key advantage is that it handles stochastic generative models (diffusion with η>0, statistical physics) where the deterministic pullback-metric formalism of Shao et al. and Wang et al. does not apply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the pullback-metric approach to latent space geometry (Shao et al. 2018, Arvanitidis et al. 2017, Park et al. 2023), which requires a deterministic generative mapping and a reference Euclidean metric in image/feature space. Instead, it reconstructs the Fisher metric directly from the data distribution via the log-partition function, leveraging the Bryant-Amari-Armstrong theorem to extend beyond exponential families to 2D sections of arbitrary generative models. The connection to statistical physics (Ising, TASEP) as exactly-solvable testbeds, and the use of posterior distributions to learn the partition function, draws on information geometry and Bayesian inference rather than Jacobian-based pullback constructions. The phase-transition framing extends prior work on diffusion model dynamics (Yang et al. 2023, Sclocchi et al. 2025) from the time-step axis to the latent-space parameter axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides the first theoretically grounded way to analyze latent space geometry for stochastic generative models (diffusion, statistical physics), where pullback-based approaches are inapplicable. The discovery of fractal phase boundaries and a divergent Lipschitz constant with respect to latent space (as opposed to time) offers a new structural explanation for the abrupt image changes observed during interpolation. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D latent sections (by the Bryant-Amari-Armstrong theorem), reliance on a single diffusion model for the main empirical claims, and the fact that the fractal boundary evidence is based on visual magnification rather than rigorous multiscale analysis. Open questions include whether the phase structure persists in higher-dimensional latent spaces, whether it is model-specific or universal across diffusion architectures, and whether the identified boundaries can be exploited for controlled generation.
