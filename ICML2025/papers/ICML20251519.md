## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of stochastic interpolants assume continuous-time generation with perfect solutions to the underlying SDEs, but practical implementations require discrete-time samplers with learned estimators over finite time steps. This gap leaves the convergence rate of discrete-time stochastic interpolants uncharacterized and the design of efficient time schedules unprincipled. The paper asks: what is the finite-time KL error of the Euler-Maruyama discretization, and how should step sizes be scheduled to minimize the number of steps needed for a target accuracy?

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a base distribution (typically Gaussian), a learned drift estimator b_F(t,x) trained via the stochastic interpolant loss, and a time schedule {t_k}_{k=0}^N with 0 < t_0 < t_N < 1 (early stopping to avoid the singularity at gamma=0). It produces a sample from the estimated distribution at time t_N along with a provable KL upper bound that decomposes into four components: initial distribution mismatch, drift estimation error, velocity-function discretization error (scaling with E||x_0-x_1||^4), and score-function discretization error (scaling with d^2 and gamma^{-4}). The bound explicitly depends on the latent scale gamma(t) and the step-size schedule, enabling schedule design as an optimization problem.

## Method

The paper derives the first finite-time KL error bound for the Euler-Maruyama discretization of the stochastic interpolant SDE, without requiring Lipschitz assumptions on the score or velocity functions. The key analytical tool is applying Itô's formula to model the evolution of the drift term b_F(t,x) along the forward path, then decomposing the resulting discretization error into derivatives of conditional expectations. By exploiting the Gaussian latent variable z embedded in the interpolant x_t = I(t,x_0,x_1) + gamma(t)z, these derivatives are expressed as conditional covariances, enabling explicit moment bounds. The resulting bound separates into velocity-related terms (involving E||x_0-x_1||^p) and score-related terms (involving gamma^{-4}d^2), revealing that step sizes should scale as h_k = O(h·gamma_k^2) to cancel the worst gamma-dependence. This motivates an exponentially decaying time schedule centered at t=1/2, which achieves an O(epsilon^{-2} d^2 log^2(1/delta)) complexity in the diffusion-model special case, matching the best known bounds for standard diffusion models.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time KL error bound for discrete-time stochastic interpolant samplers, showing that with an exponentially decaying schedule, the number of steps to achieve ε² KL error scales as N = O(ε⁻²[√(E‖x₀−x₁‖⁴)·d·log(1/(t₀(1−t_N))) + d²·log²(1/(t₀(1−t_N)))]). In experiments on the checkerboard→spiral 2D transformation, the exponentially decaying schedule achieves approximately 10× faster convergence in KL divergence compared to a uniform step-size schedule. On d-dimensional Gaussian mixtures, the empirical KL error grows nearly linearly in d, which is tighter than the theoretical O(d²) upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed exponentially decaying time schedule and a uniform step-size schedule. On the checkerboard→spiral 2D task, the decaying schedule converges to the target density roughly 10× faster in terms of number of steps (Figures 2a, 3a), consistent with the theoretical advantage: the uniform schedule incurs a 1/(t₀(1−t_N)) factor on the d² term while the decaying schedule only requires log²(1/(t₀(1−t_N))). In the special case where the base distribution is Gaussian (reducing to standard diffusion models), the proposed bound recovers the same Õ(ε⁻²·d²·log²(1/δ)) complexity as Chen et al. (2023a), though Benton et al. (2024) further improve this to Õ(ε⁻²·d·log²(1/δ)) using stochastic localization techniques that do not extend to the general interpolant setting. For the effect of source-target distance, experiments with two different source densities (Setting A closer to target, Setting B farther) confirm that convergence is faster when the coupling ν pairs nearby points, validating the √(E‖x₀−x₁‖⁴) term in the bound. On d-dimensional Gaussian mixtures, the empirical KL error at fixed N grows approximately linearly in d, which is better than the O(d²) theoretical guarantee, suggesting the bound may be loose for structured distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Girsanov-based convergence analysis paradigm from diffusion models (Chen et al. 2023a,d; Benton et al. 2024) to the more general stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo et al. 2023), where the random interpolation between two arbitrary distributions destroys the Markovian property that prior diffusion model analyses exploit. The key technical departure is that, beyond bounding score function discretization errors, the paper must separately analyze the velocity function v(t,x) arising from the general interpolation structure, using the embedded Gaussian latent variable to express derivatives of conditional expectations as conditional covariances. The exponentially decaying schedule design follows the approach of Benton et al. (2024) for diffusion models but is adapted to the γ(t)-dependent error structure of stochastic interpolants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time convergence guarantee for discrete-time stochastic interpolant samplers, closing a gap between the continuous-time theory and practical Euler-Maruyama implementations. The bound explicitly quantifies how source-target distribution distance (via E‖x₀−x₁‖ᵖ terms) and the latent scale γ(t) affect convergence, providing a principled basis for schedule design. Open questions include the d² dependence on dimension (versus near-linear d achieved for diffusion models via stochastic localization), whether the O(ε⁻²) rate can be improved, and the lack of analogous analysis for ODE-based (ε=0) stochastic interpolants. The empirical gap between the theoretical d² bound and observed near-linear d scaling on Gaussian mixtures suggests room for sharper analysis.
