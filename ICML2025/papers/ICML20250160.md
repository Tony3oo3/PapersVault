## Problem

Existing circuit discovery methods either treat components as independent entities via sequential causal interventions (activation patching) or require task-specific engineering of corrupted activation patterns (attribution patching), making them neither holistic nor generalizable across tasks. The paper asks whether circuit discovery can be formulated as a single end-to-end optimization that simultaneously identifies informative and minimal circuits without manually designing corrupted activations for each task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250160/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained Transformer (GPT-2 small or GPT-2 XL) and a set of task-specific input sentences (e.g., IOI sentences or Greater-Than year-prediction prompts) as input. It produces a sparse circuit specified by a set of critical attention heads (node-level) or residual-stream edges (edge-level), obtained by thresholding continuous IB weights λ at adaptive cutoffs that enforce a user-specified sparsity budget k. Notably, the original model's own outputs serve as supervision signals, eliminating the need to construct task-specific corrupted datasets.

## Method

IBCircuit reformulates circuit discovery as a variational Information Bottleneck: maximize I(Y; C) (circuit informativeness) while minimizing I(G; C) (information retained from the full model). To make this tractable, it parameterizes the circuit by injecting Gaussian noise ε_i ~ N(μ_i, σ_i²) into each component's activation, scaled by a learnable gate λ_i = Sigmoid(ω_i), so that λ_i → 1 preserves the signal and λ_i → 0 destroys it. The training objective combines a KL-divergence term between the noisy circuit's output and the original model's output (lower-bounding I(Y;C)) with a closed-form variational upper bound on I(G;C) derived from the Gaussian parameterization. All λ_i are optimized simultaneously via end-to-end gradient flow through the full computational graph, enabling holistic rather than sequential intervention. After training, the circuit is formed by discretizing the continuous weights: components with λ_i above an adaptive threshold (determined by a top-k constraint) are retained as the final circuit.

## Results

On the IOI task with GPT-2 small, IBCircuit achieves a higher Logit Difference with fewer node components than all baselines (SP, ACDC, AP), and consistently achieves higher Logit Difference and lower KL Divergence than ACDC and EAP across all edge sparsity levels. On the Greater-Than task, IBCircuit maintains Greater Probability above 50% and KL Divergence below 0.15 even with very sparse edge counts. For scalability, on GPT-2 XL (1.5B, 2,235,025 total edges), IBCircuit achieves performance comparable to EAP and a better Logit Difference than EAP when the number of edges ranges from 30k to 70k (approximately 97%–98.5% of total edges).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBCircuit is compared against four baselines: Subnetwork Probing (SP, Cao et al. 2021), ACDC (Conmy et al. 2023), Attribution Patching (AP, Nanda 2023), and EAP-IG (Hanna et al. 2024). On the IOI task, IBCircuit outperforms SP, ACDC, and AP in recovering the canonical circuit (Figure 2 ROC curves) and achieves higher Logit Difference with fewer nodes and edges than all three. On the Greater-Than task, IBCircuit underperforms ACDC when the number of nodes is low (below ~70), likely because the task lacks a single correct output token, making sparse circuit discovery harder; however, at higher node counts (>70), IBCircuit matches ACDC in both Greater Probability and KL Divergence. For edge-level selection on Greater-Than, IBCircuit outperforms both ACDC and EAP, maintaining Greater Probability >50% and KL Divergence <0.15 even at very sparse edge budgets. On the large-model scalability test (GPT-2 XL, 1.5B), IBCircuit matches EAP-IG and surpasses EAP in Logit Difference at 30k–70k edges, demonstrating that the holistic IB optimization scales to models an order of magnitude larger than the small-model experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes circuit discovery in Transformers through the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle (Tishby et al., 2000), using variational approximation (Alemi et al., 2017) to make the otherwise intractable mutual information terms computable. It departs from the dominant activation-patching and attribution-patching paradigms (Wang et al., 2022; Conmy et al., 2023; Nanda, 2023) in two key ways: (1) it replaces discrete, sequential causal interventions with continuous Gaussian noise injection controlled by learnable IB weights, enabling holistic end-to-end optimization; and (2) it eliminates the need for task-specific corrupted activation design by using the pretrained model's own outputs as supervision. The node/edge parameterization follows established protocols from ACDC and Wang et al., but the optimization objective and noise mechanism are novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IB formulation provides a principled, task-agnostic objective for circuit discovery that avoids the manual engineering of corrupted activations required by prior methods, lowering the barrier to applying circuit analysis to new tasks. However, the empirical results are mixed: IBCircuit underperforms ACDC on the Greater-Than task, and all experiments are confined to the GPT-2 family (small and XL), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other architectures or more complex behaviors. The post-hoc discretization of continuous IB weights into a binary circuit also raises questions about information loss at the threshold boundary. Future work could explore whether the IB framework extends to non-Transformer architectures, multi-task circuit discovery, or integration with sparse feature-based methods like SAEs.
