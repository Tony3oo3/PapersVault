## Problem

Existing Open World Object Detection (OWOD) methods rely on LLM-generated fine-grained attribute descriptions to identify unknown objects, making their performance highly sensitive to LLM description accuracy. During incremental learning, quantifying and selecting which attributes contribute to each new unknown class remains an open challenge. The paper asks whether a detector can learn to parse visual attributes directly from regions without any LLM guidance, using only minimal coarse object descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252718/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images, nine coarse attribute sentences (e.g., 'The object has a noticeable color') that describe whether an attribute is prominent rather than specifying its value, and known class names. During inference, it outputs bounding boxes with known class labels via standard OVD cosine-similarity matching, and bounding boxes with an unknown-object probability score computed from the product of an out-of-distribution term and a weighted blend of raw attribute similarity and VAP-parsed attribute confidence. The VAP itself is a lightweight MLP trained only on the visual embeddings, with text and visual encoders (CLIP ViT-B/32) kept frozen.

## Method

OW-VAP builds on YOLO-World and introduces a Visual Attribute Parser (VAP), a simple MLP that learns to extract attribute representations from visual embeddings by matching against coarse attribute text embeddings. Positive training samples come from known-class matching (TAL), while pseudo-labels are mined from background regions using three conditions: similarity exceeding the mean of positive samples, exceeding a threshold α, and ranking in the top-δ. To prevent optimization conflicts from noisy pseudo-labels, Probabilistic Soft Label Assignment (PSLA) models the loss distributions of positive and negative samples with Weibull distributions, applies a moving weighted average to stabilize the sparse positive distribution, and computes soft labels as the normalized positive density W(x) = Wb⁺(x)/(Wb⁺(x)+Wb⁻(x)), which weights the pseudo-label cross-entropy loss. At inference, the unknown probability for each region is the product of (1 − max known-class similarity) and a convex combination (parameter b) of raw attribute similarity and the VAP output, enabling LLM-free unknown detection that approaches or exceeds the OVD generalization upper bound.

## Results

On the MOWODB benchmark, OW-VAP achieves 58.8, 56.3, and 55.1 U-Recall in Tasks 1–3, surpassing the previous SOTA (ovow*) by +13.1, +9.3, and +8.7 U-Recall respectively. In Task 2, OW-VAP (56.3 U-Recall) exceeds the Zero-Shot (GT) upper bound of the OVD detector (56.0) by +0.3, indicating it surpasses the generalization limit of open-vocabulary detection. On the stricter U-AP metric, OW-VAP leads all baselines with 13.0, 9.3, and 7.8 U-AP across Tasks 1–3, achieving a maximum gap of +8.5 U-AP over the next best method in Task 1. On SOWODB, OW-VAP achieves 82.9, 85.6, and 85.7 U-Recall, exceeding ovow by over 10 U-Recall in every task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OW-VAP outperforms all 15 baselines on U-Recall across all three MOWODB tasks. Against the closest prior SOTA, ovow* (deduplicated), OW-VAP* leads by +13.1, +9.3, and +8.7 U-Recall in Tasks 1–3 respectively. Against SAM-based methods (KTCN, SGROD), OW-VAP achieves a +17.9 U-Recall advantage in Task 1; against OVD-distillation methods (SKDF), the gap is +20.4 U-Recall. Notably, in Task 2, OW-VAP (56.3) surpasses the Zero-Shot (GT) upper bound (56.0) by +0.3 U-Recall, meaning it exceeds what the underlying OVD detector can generalize to with all class names. On U-AP (Table 2), OW-VAP leads all methods in every task, with a maximum +8.5 U-AP gap over ovow* in Task 1 (13.0 vs 10.4). On confusion metrics, OW-VAP achieves the lowest A-OSE (996 in Task 1) among all methods including ovow* (1038), and shows the best WI (0.0185) among non-deduplicated methods, indicating superior separation between known and unknown predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

OW-VAP builds directly on the YOLO-World open-vocabulary detector as its backbone, adding a Visual Attribute Parser (VAP) and Probabilistic Soft Label Assignment (PSLA) on top. It departs from the dominant LLM-driven paradigm in OWOD (exemplified by FOMO, which uses GPT-3.5 to generate fine-grained attribute descriptions for known classes) by replacing those detailed sentences with a small set of coarse, degree-based descriptions (e.g., 'The object has a noticeable color') and learning to parse them from visual regions via a lightweight MLP. This positions the work against both SAM-based pseudo-labeling methods (KTCN, SGROD) and OVD-distillation methods (SKDF, ovow), offering an alternative that avoids external foundation-model supervision at inference time while still leveraging CLIP's text-visual alignment during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a detector trained on coarse, generic attribute descriptions can surpass the zero-shot OVD generalization upper bound (by 0.3 U-Recall in Task 2) challenges the assumption that fine-grained LLM-generated attributes are necessary for strong unknown-object recall. The +13 U-Recall and +8 U-AP margins over prior SOTA are substantial and suggest that the bottleneck in OWOD was not the quality of attribute descriptions but the lack of a learned mechanism to assess object-likeness from visual regions. Open questions include whether the fixed set of nine coarse attributes is optimal, whether the approach generalizes to domains beyond VOC/COCO, and whether the VAP's reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder limits its adaptability to truly novel attribute spaces.
