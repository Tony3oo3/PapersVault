## Problem

Standard image generation pipelines require training a dedicated generative model (diffusion, autoregressive, etc.) on top of a tokenizer. This paper asks whether a highly compressed 1D tokenizer alone—specifically TiTok with only 32 discrete tokens—already possesses sufficient generative capability in its decoder to perform image generation and editing without any additional training, by exploiting the semantic structure of its latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250151/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-guided editing, the method takes a 256×256 seed image and a natural language prompt, producing an edited image after ~300–400 gradient ascent iterations (~7 s on an A100). For text-to-image generation without a seed, tokens are initialized from a Gaussian (σ_init=0.3) rather than an image. For inpainting, a partial image with a soft-edged mask is provided, and the optimization uses L1 reconstruction on the unmasked region. For copy-paste editing, a target image and a reference image are tokenized and a single token position is swapped. All outputs are 256×256 RGB images decoded from 32 discrete tokens drawn from a 4096-entry codebook.

## Method

The core mechanism is test-time gradient-based optimization of the 32 continuous token vectors (pre-vector-quantization) using the straight-through estimator, with plug-and-play loss functions: CLIP similarity for text guidance or L1 reconstruction for inpainting. The key insight is that 1D tokenization with VQ forces each token to encode global image attributes (verified via per-token inter-class variance and single-token perturbation experiments), so even crude operations like copying one token between images transfer appearance properties, and gradient ascent over the full token sequence can reshape the decoded image to match a prompt. Ablations show that VQ is essential (continuous VAE tokens degrade FID from 15.1 to 33.2), the 1D structure is essential (2D MaskGIT-VQGAN fails at FID 34.3), and higher compression (fewer tokens, smaller codebook) consistently improves generation quality. Practical tweaks—averaging CLIP loss over 8 random crops, injecting token noise with a cosine schedule, L2 regularization (λ=0.02), and EMA (decay 0.98)—push FID-5k to 8.2 over 50k samples using only 1000 seed images.

## Results

Using the pretrained TiTok VQ-LL-32 1D tokenizer (32 discrete tokens, codebook size 4096) with test-time CLIP-guided optimization, the method achieves FID 8.2 and IS 182 on ImageNet 50k samples without training any generative model. The best configuration uses 1000 randomly selected seed images with CLIP top-1% association, 400 optimization iterations, and additional tweaks (random crops, token noise, EMA, L2 regularization). A single 300-iteration optimization run takes ~7 seconds per image on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed training-free method (FID 8.2, IS 182) loses on FID to RCG-G (FID 2.2) and RDM-IN (FID 5.9), both of which require training a dedicated generative model and (for RDM-IN) access to the full ImageNet training set. However, it substantially outperforms the other semi-parametric baselines: RCDM (FID 19.0, IS 52) and IC-GAN (FID 15.6, IS 59), while also requiring only 1000 randomly sampled seed images rather than the full training set or carefully selected images. Compared to ADDP (FID 7.6, IS 105), the proposed method trades a small FID increase (8.2 vs. 7.6) for a large IS improvement (182 vs. 105) and eliminates the need for any generative model training. The key differentiator is that the proposed method is the only one supporting arbitrary plug-and-play objective functions (e.g., CLIP, reconstruction, or custom losses) without retraining, making it uniquely flexible for inpainting, text-guided editing, and other tasks beyond standard generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TiTok 1D tokenizer (Yu et al., 2024b), which compresses images into 32 discrete tokens without spatial arrangement, and reframes the tokenizer's decoder as an implicit generative model. It departs from prior test-time optimization work like VQGAN-CLIP (Crowson et al., 2022) and StyleCLIP (Patashnik et al., 2021), which operate in 2D latent spaces or GAN spaces, by showing that the extreme compression of 1D discrete tokens makes even crude token manipulation (copy-paste, single-token replacement) produce coherent edits. The paper positions itself against semi-parametric generation methods (IC-GAN, RDM-IN, RCDM) that require training a generative model, arguing that a pretrained tokenizer alone suffices when guided by plug-and-play objectives like CLIP similarity or reconstruction loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a pretrained tokenizer at extreme compression ratios inherently possesses generative capability—without any dedicated generative model training—challenges the standard pipeline assumption that a separate generative model is necessary. This opens the question of whether scaling tokenizers to even higher compression (fewer tokens, smaller codebooks) could make them progressively more generative, potentially collapsing the tokenizer/generator distinction. Limitations include dependence on the specific TiTok architecture, the adversarial nature of CLIP-guided optimization (requiring SigLIP as a sanity check), and the need for seed images for best results; from-scratch generation from random tokens still lags behind seed-based initialization. The work is primarily an empirical study on ImageNet, and whether these findings generalize to other domains or tokenizer architectures remains open.
