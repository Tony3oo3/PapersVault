## Problem

Skip connections in U-Nets suffer from two structural limitations: they only exchange features within the same scale (no cross-scale interaction) and rely on simple concatenation or addition for fusion, which constrains information integration. While recent U-Net improvements have focused on enhancing encoder and decoder modules (Transformers, Mamba, MLPs), the skip connection mechanism itself has been largely overlooked since UNet++ and UNet3+.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250888/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 3D medical volumes (MRI for ACDC and MSD brain tumor, CT for KiTS2023) or 2D dermoscopy images (ISIC2017/2018) and outputs pixel-wise segmentation masks. A key structural detail is that the memory flow Y uses only 2N channels (N = number of target classes), drastically fewer than traditional decoders, because the memory carrier does not participate in feature extraction and only needs to carry the final output. The method is encoder-decoder agnostic, requiring only the skip connection feature maps X_i at each stage.

## Method

FuseUNet reinterprets the U-Net decoding process as solving an initial value problem (IVP) for a neural memory ODE (nmODE), where skip connections at L stages serve as discrete time nodes. The governing equation is Ẏ_i = -Y_i + f(Y_i + g(X_i)), where the memory flow Y accumulates multi-scale information and f is architecture-specific (convolution, Transformer, or Mamba). An adaptive predictor-corrector scheme applies the linear multistep method: lower-order Adams-Bashforth (explicit) steps predict Y_{i+1}, then Adams-Moulton (implicit) steps correct it, with the order increasing from 1 to 4 as more historical nodes become available. The final stage uses a 4-step explicit method since no subsequent node exists. This design enables all previous skip connections to interact with the current decoder stage, replacing the single-step (first-order) fusion of standard U-Nets with a high-order multi-scale integration while reducing decoder parameters by 54.9% (nn-UNet) to 29.8% (UltraLight VM-UNet).

## Results

On 3D tasks, FuseUNet with nn-UNet backbone achieves Dice avg of 91.57% on ACDC and 86.19% on KiTS, matching or slightly exceeding nn-UNet (91.54%, 86.04%) while reducing parameters by 54.9% (14.0M vs 31.2M) and GFLOPs by 34.3%. With UNETR backbone on MSD brain tumor, FuseUNet reaches 72.6% Dice avg, surpassing UNETR (71.1%) by 1.5% while halving GFLOPs (20.1 vs 40.3). On 2D skin lesion segmentation, FuseUNet with UltraLight VM-UNet backbone achieves 90.69% Dice on ISIC2017 and 89.77% on ISIC2018, outperforming the backbone (90.64%, 89.40%) with 29.8% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuseUNet consistently matches or slightly exceeds its backbone network's performance while drastically reducing parameters: on ACDC it edges out nn-UNet by 0.03% Dice (91.57 vs 91.54) with 54.9% fewer parameters, and on MSD it surpasses UNETR by 1.5% Dice (72.6 vs 71.1) with 50% fewer GFLOPs. Against other 3D baselines, FuseUNet outperforms CoTr (90.56), Swin-UNETR (91.29), and STU-Net-L (91.31) on ACDC, and beats nnFormer (75.86) and Swin-UNETR (81.27) by large margins on KiTS, though U-Mamba (86.23) retains a narrow 0.04% edge. On 2D tasks, FuseUNet surpasses UltraLight VM-UNet on both ISIC2017 (90.69 vs 90.64) and ISIC2018 (89.77 vs 89.40), and outperforms UNet, TransFuse, MALUNet, and VM-UNet on ISIC2018, though EGE-UNet (90.73) and VM-UNet (90.70) marginally lead on ISIC2017. The key differentiator is efficiency: FuseUNet achieves competitive accuracy with the fewest parameters among all compared methods on every dataset (14.0M on 3D CNN tasks, 0.036M on 2D), making it the most parameter-efficient approach in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets U-Net skip connections through the lens of numerical ODE solving, building directly on nmODEs (Yi, 2023) for the differential equation formulation and on classical Adams-Bashforth/Adams-Moulton linear multistep methods (Bashforth & Adams, 1883; Moulton, 1928) for the discretization scheme. It departs from prior nmODE-based segmentation work (He et al., 2023/2024; Wang et al., 2024c) that used at most second-order discretization with only adjacent-stage interaction, by introducing an adaptive predictor-corrector scheme that dynamically escalates to fourth-order multistep methods for full multi-scale interaction. Unlike UNet++/UNet3+ which added more connections but retained simple summation, and unlike Transformer/Mamba-based U-Nets that improved encoders/decoders while leaving skip connections unchanged, this method is explicitly encoder-decoder agnostic and operates solely on the skip connection pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mathematical foundation for skip connections, showing that the standard concatenation/addition in U-Nets is analogous to first-order Euler integration and that higher-order multistep methods can better exploit available multi-scale information. The practical benefit is substantial parameter reduction (up to 55% for nn-UNet) with maintained performance, making it attractive for deployment-constrained settings. However, the statistical analysis reveals no significant performance difference from the backbone on ACDC and KiTS, and the method's need to store multiple historical solutions introduces memory overhead that scales with the number of target classes — a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the ODE framing can guide principled design of new skip connection topologies beyond the fixed U-Net structure, and whether the memory cost can be mitigated for high-class-count 3D tasks.
