## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) rely heavily on architectural choices for generalization, yet no theoretically grounded methodology exists for principled architecture design. While NTK theory characterizes infinite-width INR training as kernel regression, the potential of kernel alignment—matching the INR's NTK to the optimal kernel that minimizes pointwise expected squared error—remains unexplored for INR optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes normalized 2D or 3D spatial coordinates (e.g., pixel positions in [0,1]² for images, ray parameters for NeRF) paired with partial observations (visible pixel values, Fourier magnitudes, or multi-view images) and produces a continuous function f_θ(γ(x)) that maps any coordinate to a predicted value. The input encoding γ(x) = [√a₀, √a₁·x]ᵀ is applied to unit-norm coordinates before feeding into the INR, and the KAR regularizer is evaluated on a uniform m×n grid over the input domain rather than only at training points.

## Method

PEAK combines a learnable polynomial input encoder γ(x) = [√a₀, √a₁·x]ᵀ with a Kernel Alignment Regularizer (KAR) that enforces the INR's prediction to satisfy f_θ(γ(x)) ≈ A(x,X)·f_θ(γ(X)), where A is parameterized as softmax(g_θ'(x)ᵀ g_θ'(X)) to guarantee non-negativity and row-stochasticity. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.3) proves that the NTK of an INR with such an encoder can approximate any positive semidefinite dot-product kernel, including the optimal kernel K*(x,x') = E[f*(x)f*(x')]. Training jointly optimizes the INR weights θ, encoder coefficients {a_j}, and the attention network g_θ' by minimizing a combined loss of data fidelity (MSE on observed pixels) plus λ times the discretized KAR over a uniform grid, all via standard gradient descent. The approach is plug-in: it wraps any existing INR architecture (MLP, SIREN, Hash/Instant-NGP) without modifying its depth, initialization, or activation functions.

## Results

On image reconstruction (Table 1), PEAK achieves the highest PSNR across all 18 image/missing-type combinations, with the largest gains on structured missing patterns (e.g., 36.08 dB on Baboon-Patch vs. 25.44 dB for Hash, the strongest baseline). On phase retrieval, PEAK improves over the best baseline by up to 8.69 dB in Fourier phase retrieval (FPR) and 11.68 dB in Gaussian phase retrieval (GPR) at the highest sampling ratio. On NeRF synthetic scenes, PEAK outperforms Instant-NGP by up to 1.53 dB PSNR at 25 views (e.g., 24.51 vs. 23.32 on hotdog), with the advantage growing as the number of input views decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEAK consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against vanilla MLP (ReLU), PEAK gains 3–10 dB PSNR on image reconstruction, with the largest margins on structured (patch) missing patterns where MLP struggles most (e.g., 36.08 vs. 18.82 dB on Baboon-Patch). Against Fourier feature encoding (Tancik et al., 2020), PEAK improves by 2–10 dB, again most pronounced on patch and textural missing data. Against Hash encoding / Instant-NGP (Müller et al., 2022), PEAK leads by 1–10 dB on image reconstruction and by up to 8.69 dB (FPR) and 11.68 dB (GPR) on phase retrieval, where Hash is the strongest baseline. On NeRF, PEAK surpasses Instant-NGP by 0.26–1.53 dB, with the advantage widening as the number of input views drops from 100 to 25. In the computational efficiency comparison, PEAK also trains faster and reaches higher PSNR with fewer parameters than SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020), Fourier, and Hash.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018; Chizat et al., 2019), kernel alignment methods from classical kernel learning (Bordelon et al., 2020; Cristianini et al., 2001), and the empirical input-encoding literature for INRs (Fourier features, Hash encoding, DINER). It departs from prior INR architecture design—which has been largely heuristic (activation choice, depth, encoding type)—by providing a theoretical target (the optimal kernel minimizing pointwise expected squared error) and showing that a learnable polynomial input encoder can approximate any PSD dot-product kernel, thereby converting the architecture-design problem into a kernel-alignment optimization. The NTK reverse-engineering result of Simon et al. (2022) is the closest theoretical precursor, but that work requires specialized single-layer polynomial activations; PEAK instead leverages existing deep architectures and their tuned initializations, modifying only the input mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers the first principled, theory-driven recipe for INR architecture design via kernel alignment, and its plug-in formulation (KAR + learnable encoder) can be grafted onto any existing INR pipeline without architectural changes. The identification of KAR as a Dirichlet Energy (Proposition 3.6) connects kernel alignment to a well-studied geometric regularizer, suggesting broader applicability. Key limitations include reliance on the infinite-width NTK limit (which may not tightly characterize finite-width training dynamics), the intractability of the true optimal kernel K* (requiring the learned attention network A as a surrogate), and the fact that the NeRF improvements are modest at higher view counts. Open questions include whether the kernel-alignment principle can guide other design choices (activation, depth) and how the approach scales to high-resolution or 3D reconstruction tasks.
