## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models follow explicit prompts well but lack multimodal in-context reasoning (e.g., inferring implicit patterns across interleaved images and texts). Existing multimodal diffusion adapters rely on pixel-level reconstruction finetuning, which does not align vision features with the textual feature space and requires scarce, complex reasoning-based datasets rather than readily available image-caption pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251768/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts flexible multimodal inputs: interleaved sequences of images and text prompts (e.g., two images plus three text words for a visual analogy), multiple images with a text prompt, or a single image with a text prompt. The VLM processes the full context and produces token features that condition the diffusion decoder, which outputs a single generated image (or video when paired with CogVideoX). No explicit reasoning dataset is needed at inference; the model reasons implicitly from the provided context.

## Method

ThinkDiff exploits the observation that recent diffusion models (e.g., FLUX) share a text encoder (T5) with an encoder-decoder LLM, so the LLM decoder and diffusion decoder operate in the same input feature space. Instead of directly aligning a VLM to the diffusion decoder, it uses vision-language training as a proxy task: a lightweight aligner network (two linear layers + GELU + RMSNorm) maps VLM token features into the LLM decoder's input space, supervised by cross-entropy loss on text prediction from simple image-caption pairs. Two variants are provided: ThinkDiff-LVLM uses a decoder-only LVLM (Qwen2-VL) with a random masked training strategy to prevent shortcut mapping and transfer reasoning from generated tokens, while ThinkDiff-CLIP uses a CLIP vision encoder with partial caption prediction. The RMSNorm in the aligner is initialized from the LLM encoder's final norm layer to resolve a scale-mismatch convergence issue, enabling stable training in just 5 hours on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the CoBSAT benchmark for multimodal in-context reasoning generation, ThinkDiff-LVLM achieves 46.3% average accuracy in the 4-shot setting, surpassing the previous SoTA SEED-LLaMA (19.2%) by 27 percentage points, while requiring only 5 hours of training on 4 A100 GPUs (vs. 216 hours on 64 A100 for SEED-LLaMA). In the 2-shot setting, ThinkDiff-LVLM achieves SoTA on 9 of 10 tasks, with particularly large gains on Action-I (45.9% vs. 18.2%), Color-II (51.1% vs. 28.7%), and Action-II (53.4% vs. 26.1%). For image-conditioned generation quality on COCO, ThinkDiff-LVLM attains CLIP-I 0.744, CLIP-T 0.590, and FID 65.8, outperforming SEED-LLaMA (0.695, 0.546, 71.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkDiff-LVLM decisively outperforms all three primary baselines (SEED-LLaMA, Emu, GILL) on the CoBSAT benchmark across both 2-shot and 4-shot settings. Against SEED-LLaMA (the previous SoTA), ThinkDiff-LVLM wins on 9/10 tasks in 2-shot and all 10 tasks in 4-shot, with the largest margins on Action-I (+27.7 pts), Color-II (+22.9 pts), and Action-II (+27.3 pts) in 2-shot; in 4-shot the gap widens further (e.g., Action-I: 43.4% vs. 5.3%, a 718.9% relative improvement). Emu and GILL perform near-randomly on most tasks (accuracy below 10%), highlighting the difficulty of in-context reasoning generation. Against Janus Pro, ThinkDiff-LVLM wins on most CoBSAT tasks, benefiting from direct LVLM-to-diffusion alignment rather than Janus Pro's two-step text-intermediate workaround. Flux Redux, a reconstruction-based image-conditioned model, scores near-zero on CoBSAT (e.g., 0.2% on Texture-II), confirming its lack of reasoning capability. For general generation quality, ThinkDiff-LVLM surpasses SEED-LLaMA on both GenEval (39.1 vs. 35.4) and DPG-Bench (54.8 vs. 47.3), approaching the FLUX upper bound (65.1 / 82.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of reconstruction-based diffusion finetuning (IP-Adapter, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), which aligns visual inputs to diffusion decoders via pixel-level diffusion losses, and from unified understanding-generation models (SEED-LLaMA, Emu, GILL) that train end-to-end with diffusion or CLIP-similarity losses. Its key conceptual departure is the 'proxy task' insight: because recent diffusion models (FLUX, SD3.5) adopt an encoder-decoder LLM (T5) as their prompt encoder, the LLM decoder and diffusion decoder share the same input feature space, so aligning a VLM to the LLM decoder via standard vision-language training on image-caption pairs transitively aligns it to the diffusion decoder. This repurposes the well-studied vision-language training recipe (as in CLIP, BLIP-2, LLaVA) as a bridge to diffusion, eliminating the need for expensive reasoning-specific datasets or diffusion losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 4 A100 GPUs for 5 hours replaces 64–128 A100s for 48–216 hours while improving CoBSAT accuracy from ~19% to 46%, making multimodal in-context reasoning generation accessible to small labs. The approach is modular (swappable VLMs, swappable diffusion decoders, demonstrated with CogVideoX for video), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include whether the proxy-task alignment degrades with more powerful or architecturally different diffusion decoders, whether reasoning fidelity can be improved without sacrificing generation quality, and whether the method generalizes beyond the CoBSAT-style analogy tasks to more open-ended compositional reasoning.
