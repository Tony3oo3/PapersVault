## Problem

The theoretical motivation for guidance in diffusion models—sampling from a scaled marginal distribution at the denoising endpoint—is provably invalid, as it conflicts with the inherent constraints of the reverse denoising process. Existing guidance implementations (CFG, CG, AutoG) do not actually sample from the intended scaled distribution, creating a fundamental gap between theory and practice that has been poorly understood in the literature.

## Input / Output

REG operates as a drop-in correction at each denoising step t=1,...,T, taking the standard noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, t, y) and the conditioning variable (class label or text embedding) as input, and producing a refined noise prediction ε̄^REG_{θ,t} that incorporates an element-wise Jacobian correction term. The correction requires computing ∂(1^T·ε_{θ,t})/∂x_t, the gradient of the sum of all noise prediction elements with respect to the noisy sample, which is obtained via a single backward pass. The method is parameterization-aware: for ε-prediction models (DiT, SD) it uses the DDPM analytical relation x̂_0 = (x_t - √(1-ᾱ_t)ε)/√ᾱ_t, while for x_0-prediction models (EDM2) the correction simplifies to ∂(1^T·D_θ)/∂x_t directly.

## Method

The paper first proves that scaling the marginal distribution (the standard guidance interpretation) is infeasible because the denoising chain implicitly couples rewards across time steps, making the constraints overdetermined. It then establishes the correct framework: scaling the joint distribution p_θ(x_{0:T}|y)·R_0(x_0,y), for which Theorem 4.1 derives the unique optimal noise prediction ε̄*_{θ,t} = ε_{θ,t} - √(1-ᾱ_t)∇log E_t(x_t,y), where E_t is the induced expected reward requiring full foresight into the denoising chain. Since E_t is intractable, existing guidance methods approximate it with the local reward R_t, incurring quantified error (Theorem 4.2). REG improves this approximation by recognizing that under a deterministic sampler, E_t reduces to R_0(x̂_0,y), and applying the chain rule introduces the Jacobian ∂x̂_0/∂x_t. The full D×D Jacobian-vector product is approximated by an element-wise diagonal multiplication, yielding the REG correction term ⊙(1 - √(1-ᾱ_t)·∂(1^T·ε_{θ,t})/∂x_t), which is method-agnostic and compatible with CFG, CG, AutoG, and their variants.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet generation, REG consistently reduces FID and increases IS across all tested models, resolutions, and guidance variants. The most notable improvement is on DiT-XL/2 at 256×256, where Cosine CFG + REG achieves FID 1.76 (vs. 2.30 without REG) and IS 287.48 (vs. 300.73). On 512×512 EDM2-XXL, REG reduces FID from 1.80 to 1.69 with Cosine CFG and from 1.81 to 1.69 with Linear CFG. On text-to-image (COCO-2017), REG improves FID by 0.64–6.52 points and CLIP score by 0.07–0.18 points across SD-v1-4 and SD-XL settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), REG consistently wins on both FID and IS: e.g., FID drops from 2.21 to 2.04 (DiT-XL/2, 256×256) and from 2.29 to 2.02 (EDM2-S, 512×512), while IS rises from 248.36 to 276.26 and 268.56 to 275.30 respectively. Against cosine CFG (Gao et al., 2023), REG produces the largest FID gains—reducing FID from 2.30 to 1.76 on DiT-XL/2 256×256 and from 1.80 to 1.69 on EDM2-XXL 512×512—though IS sometimes decreases slightly (300.73→287.48 on DiT). Against interval CFG (Kynkäanniemi et al., 2024), REG provides a modest FID improvement (1.95→1.86 on DiT-XL/2 256×256) and an IS boost (283.26→288.72 on EDM2-XXL 512×512), but the FID gain vanishes when interval CFG already applies guidance to only ~20% of timesteps (EDM2-S/XXL 512×512: 1.67→1.67, 1.45→1.45). Against AutoG (Karras et al., 2024a), REG yields a small but consistent FID improvement (1.044→1.035 on EDM2-S 64×64) with no change in IS. On text-to-image (COCO-2017), REG outperforms all three CFG variants on both FID and CLIP score for both SD-v1-4 and SD-XL, with the largest gain being Cosine CFG + REG on SD-XL (FID 32.14→25.62, CLIP 31.58→31.66).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the guidance lineage for diffusion models—classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), and auto-guidance (Karras et al., 2024)—but fundamentally reinterprets their theoretical foundation. While recent works (Bradley & Nakkiran, 2024; Chidambaram et al., 2024) identified that guidance implementations do not sample from the claimed scaled marginal distribution, this paper resolves the discrepancy by proving that the correct objective is joint-distribution scaling over the entire denoising chain, and that existing guidance formulas are approximations to the intractable optimal solution under a no-foresight constraint. REG is then derived as a tighter approximation via a Jacobian-based correction term, positioning it as a method-agnostic enhancement compatible with all existing guidance variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper corrects a foundational misconception in the diffusion guidance literature: the standard marginal-scaling interpretation is provably invalid, and the correct framework requires joint-distribution scaling. This provides a unified theoretical lens for understanding why guidance works and quantifies the approximation error of existing methods. However, the practical gains from REG are modest (FID improvements of ~0.1–0.5) and come with a 1.5–2× runtime/memory overhead, limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include whether modifying the denoising start distribution (as the theory strictly requires) yields further gains, and whether the diagonal-Jacobian approximation in REG can be tightened for higher-dimensional settings.
