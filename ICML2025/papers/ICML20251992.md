## Problem

Selecting high-quality few-shot exemplars for in-context learning is critical but computationally expensive, as each candidate exemplar subset must be evaluated via costly LLM inference. Existing task-level selection methods like LENS and EXPLORA require thousands of LLM calls, and the combinatorial space of k-sized exemplar subsets is exponentially large, making exhaustive or even uniform sampling infeasible. The paper addresses the gap of a principled, sample-efficient algorithm for identifying the top-m best exemplar subsets without evaluating the full exponential arm space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of n training exemplars (each a triplet of input, rationale, output), a validation set of n' examples, and a test input. Exemplar subsets of size k are constructed by sampling one exemplar from each of 5 pre-computed clusters, and the reward for each subset (arm) is its validation accuracy computed via LLM inference. The output is a set U of m high-scoring exemplar subsets; at inference time, a prompt generator (KNN or MMR) selects the most relevant subset from U for each test example, enabling prompt caching and eliminating per-instance retrieval overhead.

## Method

CASE (Challenger Arm Sampling for Exemplar Selection) formulates exemplar subset selection as top-m arm identification in a stochastic linear bandit setting, where arm features are SentenceBERT cosine similarities between exemplar inputs and validation examples. The key innovation is a selective exploration strategy that maintains a shortlist N_t of m' challenger arms instead of using the entire exponentially large complement of the current top-m set U_t as the challenger pool. Each iteration performs: (1) a swap between the lowest-scoring arm in U_t and highest-scoring arm in N_t, (2) uniform sampling of m' new arms from the unexplored set followed by re-selection of the top-m' to update N_t, (3) a gap-index-based selection rule (from the GIFA framework) to choose a single arm for LLM evaluation, and (4) incremental least-squares update of the linear reward model using the Sherman-Morrison formula. The algorithm stops when the gap index B_t(s_t, b_t) between the most ambiguous challenger and top-m arms falls below ε, yielding a PAC guarantee with sample complexity bounded by H^ε(μ)·C²_{δ,u}. This reduces LLM calls by ~7× and runtime by ~7× compared to LENS and EXPLORA while matching or exceeding their task performance.

## Results

On GPT-3.5-turbo with 5-shot exemplars, CASE achieves 79.91 on GSM8K, 54.72 on AquaRat, 83.42 on TabMWP, 59.72 on FinQA, and 84.49 on StrategyQA, outperforming EXPLORA by up to 2.63% and LENS by up to 10.59%. The hybrid variant CASE+KNN+SC reaches 87.49 on GSM8K, 64.17 on AquaRat, 86.23 on TabMWP, and 64.25 on FinQA, surpassing all baselines including KNN+SC (80.21 on GSM8K). Critically, CASE achieves these results with up to 7× fewer LLM calls (87% reduction) and 7× runtime speedup compared to EXPLORA, and 6×–10.5× fewer LLM calls compared to LENS. On GPT-4o-mini, CASE further improves to 91.13 (GSM8K), 73.23 (AquaRat), 89.73 (TabMWP), 72.89 (FinQA), and 95.92 (StrategyQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASE outperforms all instance-level dynamic selection methods (KNN, MMR, KNN+SC, MMR+SC, PromptPG) across all datasets where they overlap, with the hybrid CASE+KNN+SC variant achieving the largest margins (e.g., 87.49 vs. 80.21 on GSM8K, 64.25 vs. 54.49 on FinQA). Against the closest task-level competitor EXPLORA, CASE wins on 4 of 5 datasets (GSM8K +2.05, AquaRat +1.18, TabMWP +0.35, FinQA +0.26) while slightly losing on StrategyQA (84.49 vs. 85.71), yet requires up to 7× fewer LLM calls and 7× less runtime. CASE dominates LENS by a wide margin on all five datasets (e.g., +10.54 on GSM8K, +5.97 on FinQA), and also surpasses coreset selection methods (GraphCut, FacilityLocation) and Active-prompt. In synthetic top-m arm identification experiments, CASE reduces gap-index comparisons by ~46× vs. LinGIFA and ~47× vs. LinGapE (K=10), with 5.6× and 12× runtime reductions respectively. The ablation without exploration (CASE w/o exploration) underperforms full CASE on all datasets (e.g., 76.57 vs. 79.91 on GSM8K, 45.95 vs. 59.72 on FinQA), confirming the necessity of the bandit-based exploration mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GIFA framework for top-m arm identification in linear bandits (Reda et al., 2021) and the linear stochastic bandit formulation (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011), adapting them to the exemplar selection problem for in-context learning. It departs from prior exemplar selection methods like LENS (marginal-gain-based, expensive in LLM calls) and EXPLORA (heuristic contrastive exploration) by introducing a principled selective exploration strategy (CASE) that maintains a shortlist of challenger arms, avoiding the infeasible computation of gap-indices over the exponentially large space of all k-sized exemplar subsets. The selective exploration mechanism draws on the SETC algorithm from Lattimore & Szepesvári (2020), but the combination of challenger-arm sampling with gap-index-based top-m identification in the linear bandit setting is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× reduction in LLM calls and runtime without sacrificing task performance is practically significant for anyone constructing ICL prompts under budget constraints, and the task-level (static) exemplar selection enables prompt caching for KV-state reuse at inference time. The finding that exemplars selected by smaller open-source LLMs (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B) transfer well to larger models (GPT-3.5-turbo) suggests a cost-effective pipeline for exemplar selection. Open questions include deriving a rigorous regret bound for CASE (currently assumed via SETC), whether the linear similarity-based reward model is sufficient for more complex reasoning tasks, and extending the challenger-arm sampling idea to adaptive retrieval and re-ranking scenarios.
