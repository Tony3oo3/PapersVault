## Problem

Textless spoken language models struggle to generate coherent speech beyond tens of seconds due to the high temporal resolution of speech tokens (10+ tokens per 1–2 text tokens), quadratic memory growth in Transformers, and poor length extrapolation. No prior spoken LM had demonstrated unbounded long-form speech generation (minutes to tens of minutes) in a single decoding session without text intermediates, leaving a gap for applications like audiobooks, podcasts, and multi-minute voice assistant interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a short audio prompt (3 s for short-form, 10 s for long-form) and a 3 s speaker prompt for voice conditioning, and produces a continuous speech waveform of arbitrary length (evaluated up to 16 min). Audio is discretized into USM-v2 semantic tokens at 25 Hz with a 32k vocabulary, processed in overlapping 30 s windows to bound memory of the non-SSM tokenizer and codec, then synthesized non-autoregressively into SoundStream acoustic tokens and waveform. The autoregressive semantic LM maintains a constant-size recurrent state, enabling indefinite generation without growing memory.

## Method

SpeechSSM is a decoder-only hybrid state-space model built on the Griffin architecture, which interleaves gated linear recurrent units (LRUs) with local sliding-window multi-query attention in a fixed 2:1 pattern. It is initialized from RecurrentGemma (a Griffin-architecture text LM trained on 2T tokens) with new audio token embeddings, and all explicit position encodings are removed (NoPE) to promote length extrapolation. A two-stage design isolates speaker identity to a non-autoregressive SoundStorm synthesizer conditioned on a 3 s speaker prompt, freeing the semantic LM's capacity for temporal coherence. Windowed tokenization and decoding with boundary overlaps enable continuous processing of arbitrarily long audio while keeping non-SSM components memory-bounded. Trained on LibriLight 60k with 4-minute segments, the 2B and 9B variants achieve constant-memory autoregressive decoding and generate coherent multi-minute speech that outperforms sliding-window Transformer extensions on long-form continuation.

## Results

On the proposed LibriSpeech-Long benchmark (4-min continuations from 10-s prompts), SpeechSSM-2B achieves the best ASR PPL (3.75 vs. 4.74–5.71 for baselines), highest semantic similarity (Gecko 0.70 vs. 0.60–0.67), and highest speaker similarity (0.85 vs. 0.33–0.41) among all models. Its N-MOS-T (~4.12–4.16) closely matches ground truth (~4.09–4.12), while baselines degrade to 1.4–2.8. On short-form (7-s continuations), SpeechSSM-2B matches or exceeds baselines on PPL (5.76 vs. 6.17–7.25), SBERT (0.23 vs. 0.17–0.20), and N-MOS (3.87 vs. 2.23–3.24), with the 9B variant reaching 3.94 N-MOS (ground truth: 4.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeechSSM-2B decisively outperforms all five baselines on the long-form (4-min) benchmark: it achieves 50% win rate in LLM-judged side-by-sides versus GSLM (17.6%), TWIST-1.3B (16.2%), TWIST-7B (24.0%), and Spirit LM Expressive (24.2%), while also leading on PPL (3.75 vs. 4.74–5.71), Gecko semantic similarity (0.70 vs. 0.60–0.67), and speaker similarity (0.85 vs. 0.33–0.41). The N-MOS-T gap is especially stark: SpeechSSM maintains ~4.1 naturalness across all four minutes, whereas TWIST-1.3B degrades to 1.36 by minute 4 and Spirit LM Expressive to 2.60. On short-form (7-s) generation, SpeechSSM-2B matches or beats baselines on PPL, SBERT, and N-MOS, though TWIST-7B edges it on Win%GT (15.5% vs. 7.9%), suggesting the SSM advantage is most pronounced at longer horizons. Notably, no model—including SpeechSSM-9B—achieves any wins over ground truth in long-form side-by-sides, highlighting the difficulty of the task. The 9B variant further widens the gap, reaching 75% win rate over the 2B model and PPL of 3.57 (below ground truth's 3.61).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Griffin hybrid state-space architecture (gated linear recurrent units interleaved with local multi-query attention) to spoken language modeling for the first time, departing from the Transformer-based spoken LM lineage (GSLM → AudioLM → TWIST → VoxtLM → Spirit LM) that has dominated the field. Key design choices distinguish it from prior spoken LMs: using USM-v2's 32k-vocabulary semantic tokens (rather than smaller HuBERT vocabularies), isolating speaker identity to a separate SoundStorm acoustic stage, and removing all explicit position encodings to enable length extrapolation. On the evaluation side, the paper departs from the sWUGGY/sBLiMP/PPL/auto-BLEU metrics that prior work found noisy or saturating, introducing LLM-as-judge side-by-side comparisons and time-stratified quality measurements instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of unbounded long-form speech generation (16+ minutes) in constant memory, opening the door to audio-native voice assistants and long-form media generation without text intermediates. The LibriSpeech-Long benchmark and the proposed evaluation methodology (side-by-side LLM judging, N-MOS-over-time, semantic coherence over length) address well-known saturation and noise problems in spoken LM evaluation and will likely become standard for the field. However, all models still achieve 0% wins over ground truth in side-by-side comparisons, indicating a substantial quality gap remains; the extemporaneous variant (SpeechSSM-X) is only lightly evaluated, and generalization to multi-speaker dialogue or non-English speech is unaddressed.
