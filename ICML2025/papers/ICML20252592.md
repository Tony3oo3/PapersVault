## Problem

Existing differentially private decentralized online convex optimization (D-OCO) algorithms suffer from three critical limitations: they only guarantee (εT, 0)-DP over T rounds (degrading linearly with T), their regret bounds have large gaps in dependence on the number of learners n and the spectral gap ρ compared to non-private optimal bounds, and they require per-round projection operations that become prohibitive for complex decision sets. This paper asks whether (ε, 0)-DP over all T rounds can be achieved nearly for free while matching non-private regret up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Input / Output

Each of n local learners receives a sequence of G-Lipschitz (and optionally α-strongly convex) loss functions over a convex decision set K bounded by radius R, and communicates only with graph neighbors via a doubly stochastic weight matrix P with spectral gap ρ. The algorithm outputs a fixed local decision vector per block of L rounds (rather than per round), and the privacy guarantee is (ε, 0)-DP over the entire T-round horizon with Laplace noise calibrated to sensitivity 6√d(G+αR)(2+log₂T)/ε per coordinate. The projection-free variant PD-OCG additionally requires only linear optimization oracle access to K instead of projection, trading a worse T-dependence (T^{3/4} vs. √T for convex) for computational efficiency.

## Method

The core algorithm PD-FTGL introduces a block-decoupled accelerated gossip strategy: within each block of L rounds, learners perform L iterations of the accelerated gossip update (Liu & Morse, 2011) on block-level gradient sums, but crucially reset the gossip state at block boundaries so that the accumulated partial sum across blocks is a clean sequence amenable to tree-based private aggregation. At the end of each block, the PrivateSum procedure (Smith & Thakurta, 2013) adds Laplace noise along a binary tree path to produce a private estimate of the historical gradient sum, which drives an FTRL-style argmin update for the next block's decision. The key analytical insight is that the sensitivity of the block-decoupled update is independent of the block size L, so the noise scale does not inflate with L, yielding regret bounds of Õ(n(ρ^{-1/4}+ε^{-1}ρ^{1/4})√T) for convex and Õ(n(ρ^{-1/2}+ε^{-1})) for strongly convex functions. The projection-free variant PD-OCG replaces the per-block projection with L parallel iterations of the conditional gradient (Frank-Wolfe) method, limiting the accelerated gossip steps to L' = ⌈4ln(nT√14n)/√ρ⌉ to control approximation error while keeping the block size L sublinear in T.

## Results

PD-FTGL is the first D-OCO algorithm to achieve (ε,0)-DP over all T rounds, with regret bounds of Õ(n(ρ^{-1/4} + ε^{-1}ρ^{1/4})√T) for convex and Õ(n(ρ^{-1/2} + ε^{-1})) for strongly convex functions. The projection-free variant PD-OCG achieves Õ(n(T^{3/4} + ε^{-1}T^{1/4})) and Õ(n(T^{2/3} + ε^{-1})) regret for convex and strongly convex functions, respectively. For ε = Ω(√ρ), the PD-FTGL bounds nearly match the non-private lower bounds Ω(nρ^{-1/4}√T) and Ω(nρ^{-1/2} log T), implying (ε,0)-DP is achieved nearly for free. In experiments on letter (T=150,000) and poker (T=10,000,000) with n=9 and ε=10, both PD-FTGL and PD-OCG substantially outperform PD-OGD in average loss, while PD-OCG runs faster than PD-FTGL due to eliminating per-round projections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is PD-OGD from Li et al. (2018), the only prior private D-OCO algorithm. PD-FTGL and PD-OCG both achieve substantially lower average loss than PD-OGD on both letter and poker datasets at ε=10, because PD-OGD requires a much larger noise scale to achieve (ε,0)-DP over all T rounds (it originally only guarantees (ε,0)-DP per round, requiring T-fold composition). In terms of runtime, PD-OGD is significantly slower than PD-FTGL because it performs a projection operation every round, whereas PD-FTGL projects only once per block. PD-OCG is the fastest of the three methods, confirming the computational benefit of the projection-free (conditional gradient) design. Theoretically, PD-FTGL improves the dependence on n from n^{5/4} to n and on ρ from ρ^{-1/2} to ρ^{-1/4} (convex case) compared to Li et al. (2018), while also upgrading the privacy guarantee from (εT,0)-DP to (ε,0)-DP over all T rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: tree-based private aggregation for online learning (Dwork et al., 2010; Smith & Thakurta, 2013), accelerated gossip for decentralized consensus (Liu & Morse, 2011; Wan et al., 2024a;b), and projection-free optimization via conditional gradient (Frank & Wolfe, 1956; Jaggi, 2013). The key departure from prior private D-OCO work (Li et al., 2018; Hou et al., 2019; Lü et al., 2023) is that those methods simply add noise to per-round decisions, yielding only (εT,0)-DP that degrades linearly with T. The non-trivial technical challenge the paper addresses is that existing D-OCO algorithms have block-coupled communication, making their decisions inexpressible as partial sums—a prerequisite for the tree-based technique—so the authors introduce a block-decoupled accelerated gossip design specifically to enable this composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that (ε,0)-DP over all T rounds can be achieved with regret nearly matching non-private lower bounds for ε = Ω(√ρ)—suggests that strong privacy in decentralized online learning may come at negligible cost, which is a meaningful conceptual shift from prior work where privacy and regret were tightly coupled. The projection-free variant (PD-OCG) extends practical applicability to complex decision sets where projection is intractable. Open questions include establishing general lower bounds for D-OCO with DP (which remain absent even for n=1), whether the achieved rates are optimal across all ε regimes, and whether lazy OCO techniques for oblivious adversaries can further improve the bounds in the decentralized setting.
