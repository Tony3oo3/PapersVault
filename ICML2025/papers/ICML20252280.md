## Problem

Existing uplift modeling methods for incentive assignment in online marketing are validated only on in-distribution test data that mirrors the training distribution, yet in practice user features shift continuously due to time, geography, and platform context, causing unreliable uplift predictions on out-of-distribution users. These methods also struggle to simultaneously address selection bias inherent in observational (non-randomized) treatment assignment. The paper tackles both challenges jointly by learning domain-invariant causal features that generalize across environments while balancing treatment-group distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular user features (83–104 dimensions) and a binary treatment indicator (incentive received or not) from a source environment, and produces per-user uplift estimates (CATE) for a target environment where only features are available—no treatment labels or responses. The target environment is explicitly different from the source (e.g., different time period or user segment), and the model must predict treatment effects without access to target-environment outcomes during training. The final output drives incentive allocation decisions, with the model also producing a sparse k-hot mask identifying which features are causally invariant.

## Method

IDUM decomposes uplift generalization into three coupled components: (1) an Invariant Property Learning module that defines a PNS risk over a shared MLP backbone Φ(x), separating invariant features into sufficient (SF) and necessary (NC) terms under Exogeneity and Monotonicity assumptions, and derives a tractable upper bound R ≤ M + 2·SF that replaces the intractable counterfactual PNS computation; (2) a Gumbel-Softmax-based feature selection layer that produces an approximate k-hot mask m(x^c) to retain only the most informative invariant dimensions, reducing the combinatorial cost of searching over x^c; (3) an IPM-based Balancing Discrepancy regularizer (MMD or Wasserstein) that aligns treatment and control representations to mitigate selection bias. Training solves a min-max objective—minimizing over the sufficient-term parameters Θ, prediction heads h, mask m, and backbone Φ while maximizing over the necessary-term parameters Ψ—subject to a δ-semantic-separability constraint ensuring x^c and x̄^c remain meaningfully distinct, plus KL-divergence regularization for PAC-Bayes generalization bounds connecting source and target environment risks via β-divergence.

## Results

On the OOD Production dataset, IDUM achieves AUUC 0.2847, QINI 0.3681, and KENDALL 0.2051, outperforming the strongest baseline EUEN (AUUC 0.2755, QINI 0.3632, KENDALL 0.1995) across all three metrics. On the OOD Lazada dataset, IDUM reaches AUUC 0.0274, QINI 0.4633, and KENDALL 0.1790, surpassing CFRNet-mmd (AUUC 0.0262, QINI 0.4618, KENDALL 0.1737). On the in-distribution Production dataset, IDUM attains AUUC 0.2907, QINI 0.3700, and KENDALL 0.2091, again leading all baselines. The gains are most pronounced in OOD settings, where IDUM improves AUUC by up to 9.5% over the best baseline (EUEN on Production OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDUM consistently outperforms all six baseline families on both ID and OOD settings across all three metrics (AUUC, QINI, KENDALL). S-Learner and T-Learner (Künzel et al., 2019) are the weakest baselines, with AUUC values roughly 60% lower than IDUM on OOD Production, confirming that simple meta-learner architectures cannot handle distribution shift. TARNet and CFRNet (Shalit et al., 2017) are the strongest neural baselines on Lazada OOD (CFRNet-mmd: AUUC 0.0262 vs. IDUM 0.0274), but still trail IDUM by 4.6% in AUUC and 3.1% in KENDALL; on Production OOD, CFRNet variants fall further behind (AUUC 0.2665 vs. 0.2847). DragonNet (Shi et al., 2019) performs mid-range, with its propensity-score head providing modest gains over TARNet but no OOD robustness. EUEN (Ke et al., 2021) is the closest competitor on Production OOD (AUUC 0.2755, QINI 0.3632), yet IDUM still leads by 3.3% in AUUC and 2.8% in KENDALL. UniTE (Liu & Hou, 2023) and TEED (Sun et al., 2023a) show moderate performance but degrade noticeably under OOD conditions, with TEED dropping to the lowest AUUC (0.2237) on Production OOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDUM sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously separate research threads: neural-network-based uplift modeling (CFRNet, DragonNet, TARNet) that addresses selection bias via IPM-based representation balancing, and invariant learning for domain generalization (IRM, CaSN) that identifies environment-invariant causal features. The key departure is applying the Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (PNS) decomposition from Pearl's causal framework specifically to uplift estimation, separating invariant features into necessary and sufficient components with a Gumbel-Softmax-based feature selection to control computational cost. Unlike prior uplift methods that only validate on in-distribution test data, IDUM explicitly targets OOD generalization while retaining the treatment/control balancing objective, and unlike prior invariant learning work (e.g., CaSN) that targets classification, IDUM adapts the PNS risk to the CATE estimation setting with a min-max optimization over the necessary-feature distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap: production uplift models deployed in e-commerce and short-video platforms face continuous distribution shifts (seasonality, geography, user demographics) that degrade performance, yet prior methods have no mechanism to handle this. The theoretical guarantees (β-divergence-based OOD risk bound and PAC-learning convergence) provide the first generalization analysis for PNS-based uplift estimation. Limitations include reliance on exogeneity and monotonicity assumptions that may not hold in complex marketing settings, the OOD evaluation only covers feature shift (not treatment-policy shift), and the online experiment shows only modest real-world gains (0.012–0.028% watch time improvement). Open questions include extension to multi-treatment and continuous-treatment settings, and whether the PNS decomposition provides meaningful interpretability for marketing decisions.
