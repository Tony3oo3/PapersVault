## Problem

Designing target-specific cyclic peptides remains underexplored because existing geometric generative models for peptide design are trained on linear peptides, and cyclic peptide data is scarce. Prior approaches either impose cyclization constraints via post-filtering (yielding low acceptance rates) or hard-code specific cyclization patterns into the model architecture (lacking generalizability across different strategies). The paper asks whether complex cyclization geometry can be decomposed into simple, learnable unit constraints that generalize zero-shot to unseen combinations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein binding site represented as a fully-connected geometric graph (residue nodes with one-hot amino acid type features and full-atom 3D coordinates) together with a cyclization constraint specified as a pair: a type constraint (a set of node indices each assigned a required amino acid type) and a distance constraint (a set of node pairs each assigned a required Cα–Cα distance). The output is a full-atom cyclic peptide (sequence + 3D structure) that satisfies the imposed geometric constraints. Notably, the model is trained exclusively on linear peptide data; the specific constraint combinations corresponding to the four cyclization strategies (stapled, head-to-tail, disulfide, bicycle) are never seen during training and are imposed only at inference.

## Method

CP-Composer builds on the PepGLAD geometric latent diffusion framework (VAE + equivariant GNN denoiser in a compact latent space) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, it decomposes all four cyclization strategies into two fundamental unit constraints—node-level type constraints and edge-level distance constraints—and encodes them via geometric conditioning: type constraints are injected as one-hot vectors added to E(3)-invariant node features, while distance constraints are injected as RBF-encoded scalars on constrained edges, processed by additional dyMEAN message-passing layers. Second, it employs a modified classifier-free guidance scheme where the denoiser is jointly trained on conditional (constraint-present) and unconditional (constraint-absent) inputs sampled from linear peptide data, and at inference the guided score is computed as (w+1)·ε_conditional − w·ε_unconditional, amplifying the constraint signal without requiring an external energy function. The encoding is provably injective (Theorem 3.3), ensuring distinct constraints yield distinct control signals, and the entire pipeline preserves E(3)-equivariance. This compositional design allows arbitrary combinations of type and distance constraints—including high-order multi-cycle patterns—to be imposed at inference without retraining.

## Results

CP-Composer achieves zero-shot cyclic peptide generation success rates of 38.57% (stapled), 74.42% (head-to-tail), 82.50% (disulfide), and 84.62% (bicycle) at guidance weight w=5.0, dramatically outperforming the unguided PepGLAD baseline (22.80%, 30.23%, 0%, 0% respectively). The energy-guided baseline (w/EG) reaches 61.63% on head-to-tail but fails entirely (0%) on disulfide and bicycle peptides. Molecular dynamics simulations confirm that generated cyclic peptides exhibit lower RMSD (1.44±0.23 Å vs 2.57±0.51 Å for PDB 3RC4) and stronger binding affinity (ΔG = −10.66 vs −9.73 kcal/mol) compared to native linear binders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unguided PepGLAD backbone, CP-Composer improves success rates by 15.8–84.6 percentage points across the four cyclization strategies, with the most dramatic gains on disulfide (0%→82.50%) and bicycle (0%→84.62%) peptides where PepGLAD produces zero valid samples. The energy-guidance baseline (Bao et al., 2022) handles the simpler head-to-tail case reasonably (61.63% vs CP-Composer's 74.42% at w=5.0) but completely fails on disulfide and bicycle peptides (0% success), confirming that hand-crafted energy functions cannot compose multiple geometric constraints. Against DiffPepBuilder (Wang et al., 2024a), a method specifically designed for disulfide peptide generation, CP-Composer achieves 41.25% vs 23.07% on single disulfide and 62.00% vs 32.78% on dual disulfide (2*-S-S-) scenarios, while also being ~21× faster (1.42s vs 29.94s per peptide). The guidance weight w provides a tunable trade-off: higher w increases constraint satisfaction but inflates KL divergence (e.g., disulfide AA-KL rises from 0.1477 at w=1.0 to 1.6965 at w=10.0), allowing users to balance structural validity against distributional realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PepGLAD (Kong et al., 2024), a geometric latent diffusion model for full-atom peptide design, and extends the classifier-free guidance framework (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from class labels and scalar targets to composable geometric constraints on molecular graphs. Prior cyclic peptide design relied on post-filtering (Wang et al., 2024b) or hard-coded architecture modifications (Rettie et al., 2024), while energy-based guidance (Bao et al., 2022) was the standard approach for constraint injection in diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is decomposing cyclization into two fundamental unit constraints (type and distance) that can be freely composed, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen constraint combinations without any cyclic training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cyclic peptides are a critical drug format with enhanced stability and bioavailability, yet target-specific design has been bottlenecked by data scarcity; the zero-shot formulation (train on linear, generate cyclic) directly addresses this practical limitation. The composable constraint framework is generalizable beyond peptides to any geometric generation task requiring multiple simultaneous constraints, and the clear failure of energy-based guidance on multi-constraint scenarios (0% on disulfide/bicycle) versus CFG-style guidance provides actionable guidance for practitioners. Limitations include validation on only four cyclization strategies, MD confirmation on just two protein targets, and success rates that still fall short of 100%, leaving open questions about scalability to more complex or arbitrary geometric constraints in broader biomolecular design.
