## Problem

Distributed training with local updates (e.g., DiLoCo) is the default paradigm for scaling LLMs, but attention-based models produce heavy-tailed stochastic gradients with potentially infinite variance that destabilize standard SGD and most existing distributed optimizers. Prior work on clipping or adaptive methods either lacks convergence guarantees in the nested local-update setting, or requires maintaining and transmitting full preconditioner matrices that triple memory and add communication overhead. No efficient distributed algorithm with provable convergence under heavy-tailed noise and local updates had been established.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard minibatch stochastic gradients (which may have infinite variance) and two scalar clipping thresholds per coordinate (upper u_t and lower d_t), along with a learning rate schedule. Unlike Adam or RMSProp, it does not require storing or transmitting first- or second-moment statistics, so per-node memory matches vanilla SGD. The output is updated model parameters at each synchronization round, accompanied by in-expectation convergence bounds on the minimum gradient norm that depend explicitly on the noise α-moment (α ∈ (1,2)).

## Method

TailOPT is a nested distributed optimization framework where each node performs z local gradient steps (inner optimizer) before a global aggregation step (outer optimizer). The key mechanism is BiClip, a coordinate-wise operator that amplifies small gradient coordinates (|x_j| ≤ d) to the lower threshold d, clips large ones (|x_j| ≥ u) to the upper threshold u, and passes intermediate values through unchanged—effectively interpolating between clipped-SGD and adaptive preconditioning without any stored statistics. In the highlighted Bi²Clip instantiation, BiClip is applied at both the inner and outer optimizers, so the outer step treats the aggregated local update Δ_t as a pseudo-gradient and applies the same coordinate-wise thresholding. Convergence is proven under only a finite α-moment condition (α ∈ (1,2)) on the noise, with polynomially growing upper thresholds u_t and decaying lower thresholds d_t that eliminate clipping bias; the resulting rate for Bi²Clip is O(T^{-(α-1)/(4α-2)}), e.g., O(T^{-1/8}) when α ≥ 1.5.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa fine-tuning), Bi²Clip achieves the best average score of 84.52, outperforming DiLoCo (83.08), Adam² (82.93), and RMSProp-BiClip (82.99). On WMT machine translation (T5 fine-tuning), Bi²Clip attains 44.51 average BLEU/METEOR, surpassing Avg-L2Clip (43.92), Adam² (43.23), and Avg-SGD (42.68). Notably, Bi²Clip matches or exceeds Adam² performance while requiring only SGD-level memory and compute (no preconditioner storage or transmission). On synthetic heavy-tailed noise experiments, BiClip as the inner optimizer substantially outperforms SGD, L2-Clip, and Adam in recovering the ground-truth weight vector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi²Clip outperforms all five baseline families across both GLUE and WMT. Against Avg-SGD (McMahan et al., 2017), Bi²Clip gains +23.35 average points on GLUE (84.52 vs. 61.17) and +1.83 on WMT (44.51 vs. 42.68), with Avg-SGD failing entirely on CoLA and STS-B. Against Avg-L2Clip (Yang et al., 2022), Bi²Clip improves by +20.37 on GLUE and +0.59 on WMT, showing that coordinate-wise BiClip is substantially more effective than uniform L2-norm clipping under heavy-tailed noise. Against the adaptive federated optimizers of Reddi et al. (2021) (Adagrad-SGD, RMSProp-SGD, Adam-SGD), Bi²Clip leads by +11.8 to +11.9 points on GLUE average, confirming that the inner optimizer choice is critical. Against Adam² (Wang et al., 2021b), which transmits full-size preconditioners between inner and outer optimizers, Bi²Clip achieves +1.59 on GLUE and +1.28 on WMT while eliminating all preconditioner memory and communication overhead. Against DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2024), the strongest practical baseline, Bi²Clip gains +1.44 on GLUE average, with particularly large margins on RTE (+8.66), CoLA (+5.31), and MRPC (+1.22 Acc), while DiLoCo retains a slight edge on MNLI (+0.62) and QNLI (+0.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local-SGD/FedAvg paradigm (McMahan et al., 2017) and the recent DiLoCo algorithm (Douillard et al., 2024) by addressing a gap neither fills: provable convergence under heavy-tailed gradient noise with local updates. Prior heavy-tailed optimization work (Gorbunov et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020b; Yang et al., 2022) largely studied centralized settings or distributed settings without local updates, and relied on L2-norm clipping that uniformly downscales all coordinates. The key departure is BiClip, a coordinate-wise clipping operator from both above and below that interpolates between clipped-SGD and adaptive methods (Adam, Adagrad), achieving per-coordinate adaptivity without the memory and communication cost of preconditioner maintenance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Heavy-tailed gradient noise is a well-documented phenomenon in attention-based model training that destabilizes standard SGD, yet no prior distributed algorithm with local updates had convergence guarantees under potentially infinite variance. BiClip's simplicity (two scalar thresholds, SGD-level memory) makes it a practical drop-in replacement that could be widely adopted in large-scale training pipelines. Open questions include automatic selection of clipping thresholds u_t and d_t, whether integrating Adam on top of BiClip yields further gains, and the fact that the proven convergence rate for Bi²Clip (O(T^{-1/8}) for α≥1.5) is notably slower than empirical performance suggests, indicating the bounds are loose. The paper also acknowledges that formal convergence for the Adam outer optimizer variant remains unresolved due to the first-moment moving average.
