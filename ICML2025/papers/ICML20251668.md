## Problem

Existing offline RL methods apply a fixed global regularization coefficient uniformly across all states, creating a dilemma where weak regularization fails to suppress extrapolation errors while strong regularization collapses policy learning into behavior cloning. The optimal coefficient varies across tasks, training stages, and data densities, yet is typically determined by manual tuning, and this uniform constraint also hinders efficient offline-to-online fine-tuning by causing large discrepancies between offline and online update dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset D of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples and produces a learned policy (Gaussian for CQL-based, deterministic for TD3+BC-based) together with a lightweight coefficient network β_φ(s) that outputs a per-state regularization strength. For datasets with high quality variance, an IQL-style pre-trained Q and V network is used to filter actions at the state level (Q(s,a)−V(s)>0) rather than at the trajectory level, ensuring every state retains at least one constrained action. During online fine-tuning, the coefficient network is frozen and its outputs are linearly annealed to zero, requiring no access to the offline dataset.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the fixed regularization coefficient with a state-adaptive neural network β_φ(s) trained via a loss that encourages the policy's log-likelihood of dataset actions to exceed a state-level threshold C_n(s) derived from the policy's own mean and standard deviation. A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.1) establishes the equivalence between CQL's value regularizer and a negative log-likelihood policy constraint under a Boltzmann policy, unifying value-regularization and policy-constraint methods under a single coefficient-adjustment framework. The trust-region width n is linearly annealed from a small initial value to a larger final value during training, and coefficient updates are restricted to a selectively chosen sub-dataset D̂ of high-quality actions (by return threshold or IQL-style advantage), preventing low-quality data from over-constraining the policy. For deterministic policies (TD3+BC), the Gaussian log-likelihood is replaced by an MSE-based surrogate using the exploration noise as a proxy for standard deviation. For offline-to-online transfer, the coefficient network is frozen and its outputs are linearly decayed to zero over a fixed number of online steps, enabling stable fine-tuning without the offline dataset.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks, CQL(SA) achieves a total normalized score of 1139.1, improving over CQL (1030.4) by +10.5%, while TD3+BC(SA) reaches 1116.7 vs. TD3+BC's 1000.8 (+11.6%). On AntMaze, the gains are even more dramatic: CQL(SA) scores 406.8 vs. CQL's 294.1 (+38.3%), and TD3+BC(SA) scores 276.0 vs. TD3+BC's 131.8 (+109.4%), with the latter going from 0.0 to 49.4 on medium-play-v2. In the offline-to-online setting (250k steps), CQL(SA) achieves a locomotion total of 1278.4, outperforming IQL (1057.4), SPOT (1107.1), and FamCQL (1178.3), and an AntMaze total of 494.3 vs. IQL's 363.2 and SPOT's 458.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two backbone methods, CQL(SA) outperforms CQL on 11 of 12 MuJoCo tasks (e.g., hopper-medium-v2: 89.1 vs. 65.6) and on all 6 AntMaze tasks (total 406.8 vs. 294.1); TD3+BC(SA) outperforms TD3+BC on 10 of 12 MuJoCo tasks and all 6 AntMaze tasks (total 276.0 vs. 131.8, where TD3+BC scores 0.0 on four AntMaze tasks). In the offline-to-online setting, CQL(SA) achieves the best locomotion total (1278.4), beating IQL (1057.4) by +21%, SPOT (1107.1) by +15%, and FamCQL (1178.3) by +8.5%; on AntMaze O2O, CQL(SA) scores 494.3 vs. SPOT's 458.1 and IQL's 363.2. In the broader offline comparison (Table 3), CQL(SA) leads all methods on locomotion total (1139.1 vs. FamCQL 1064.1, SPOT 1046.6, IQL 1033.5, AWAC 1023, Cal-QL 1007.5) and AntMaze total (406.8 vs. SPOT 330.5, IQL 310.6, Cal-QL 299.1, AWAC 121.3). The method's advantage is most pronounced on lower-quality datasets (medium and medium-replay), where selective regularization on high-return sub-datasets provides the largest gains, while on expert datasets the improvements are more modest (e.g., walker2d-expert: 110.2 vs. 110.1 for CQL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two dominant offline RL paradigms—value regularization (CQL) and explicit policy constraint (TD3+BC)—by establishing a formal equivalence between their regularization terms (Proposition 3.1), which enables a unified state-adaptive coefficient mechanism across both. It departs from prior work that uses a fixed global regularization coefficient by replacing it with a learned, state-dependent coefficient network that adapts to the divergence between the policy distribution and the dataset. The selective regularization component draws on IQL's expectile regression to identify high-quality actions for heterogeneous datasets, and the O2O transition leverages the well-trained coefficient network to enable simple linear annealing, contrasting with prior approaches that require complex constraint relaxation schedules or Q-ensemble methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The state-adaptive regularization addresses a persistent practical pain point in offline RL: the need for extensive per-task hyperparameter tuning of the regularization strength, which the paper shows varies across tasks, training stages, and data densities. The ability to discard the offline dataset entirely during online fine-tuning (while maintaining competitive performance) has practical implications for privacy-sensitive deployments. However, the method still requires task-specific return thresholds for sub-dataset selection and IQL pre-training for high-variance datasets, limiting full automation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to model-based offline RL, continuous action spaces beyond MuJoCo, and whether the coefficient network's generalization holds in truly novel OOD states encountered during deployment.
