## Problem

Neural PDE solvers provide fast approximations to expensive numerical solvers but lack robust uncertainty quantification with statistical guarantees. Existing UQ methods for neural PDEs either require extensive labelled simulation data, lack coverage guarantees, or demand architectural modifications to the surrogate model. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a data-free, physics-informed conformal prediction framework that provides guaranteed coverage bounds without requiring any ground-truth data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatio-temporal field tensors (neural PDE predictions of shape [Nt, Nx, Ny]) and the governing PDE operator D, requiring no labelled targets. It produces calibrated error bounds in the PDE residual space (not the physical variable space), with marginal-CP yielding cell-wise error bars and joint-CP yielding domain-wide error bands. Unusually, the prediction sets are input-independent intervals [-q^α, q^α] in residual space, meaning the same bounds apply regardless of the specific initial condition, and the method operates as a purely post-hoc procedure on any pre-trained neural PDE solver.

## Method

The core contribution is the Physics Residual Error (PRE) as a data-free nonconformity score for conformal prediction: |D(f(X))|, where D is the PDE residual operator evaluated on the neural PDE prediction. The key insight is that for any well-posed PDE, the true solution has zero residual, eliminating the need for labelled calibration data—calibration uses only the model's own predictions. Gradients are estimated via finite-difference stencils implemented as convolutional kernels (e.g., a 3×3 Laplacian stencil), making the approach model-agnostic, memory-efficient, and compatible with standard ML libraries. Two CP formulations are provided: marginal-CP gives per-cell coverage guarantees for localizing physically inconsistent regions, while joint-CP uses the supremum of normalized residuals across the entire domain to produce global error bands enabling accept/reject decisions. The framework is validated on wave, Navier-Stokes, and MHD equations, as well as tokamak plasma modelling and Grad-Shafranov equilibrium reconstruction, demonstrating guaranteed coverage with evaluation times orders of magnitude faster than data-driven CP baselines.

## Results

Across three PDE benchmarks (Wave, Navier-Stokes, MHD), CP-PRE achieves guaranteed 95% coverage in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings (95.52%/95.39%, 95.44%/95.57%, 95.54%/95.67% respectively), while all Bayesian baselines degrade severely out-of-distribution. CP-PRE also delivers the fastest evaluation time among UQ methods: 32s (Wave), 134s (Navier-Stokes), and 482s (MHD), compared to CP-AER's 2022s, 20026s, and 40042s respectively, due to its data-free calibration requiring no simulation data generation. The method additionally provides the only physics-informed, modification-free, and sampling-free UQ with statistical guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CP-PRE is the only method that maintains guaranteed 95% coverage in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings across all three PDE benchmarks. All four Bayesian baselines (MC Dropout, Deep Ensemble, BNN, SWA-G) fail catastrophically out-of-distribution: on Navier-Stokes, MC Dropout drops to 44.05% and Deep Ensemble to 30.74% coverage; on MHD, MC Dropout falls to 16.91% and SWA-G to 14.22%. CP-AER (data-driven conformal prediction) also achieves guaranteed coverage in both settings but at a massive computational cost—63× slower on Wave (2022s vs 32s), 149× slower on Navier-Stokes (20026s vs 134s), and 83× slower on MHD (40042s vs 482s)—because it requires generating labelled simulation data for calibration. CP-PRE's data-free formulation eliminates this bottleneck entirely while additionally providing physics-informed error bounds in the PDE residual space, a property none of the other baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction (CP) framework for spatio-temporal PDE surrogates (Gopakumar et al., 2024a; Sun, 2022) by replacing data-dependent nonconformity scores (AER, STD) with a Physics Residual Error (PRE) score that requires no labelled calibration data. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the fact that the true PDE residual is identically zero, transforming CP from a data-calibrated procedure into a physics-calibrated one. The use of PDE residuals as a UQ tool builds on their established role in PINN training (Raissi et al., 2019) and physics-informed neural operators (Li et al., 2024), but repurposes them from an optimization signal to a post-hoc uncertainty metric. The finite-difference stencils implemented as convolutional kernels for scalable gradient estimation draw on the mathematical equivalence between FD approximations and discrete convolutions (Strang, 1986; Fiorentino & Serra, 1991).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free nature of CP-PRE is practically significant for domains where generating calibration data from expensive numerical solvers is prohibitive, as demonstrated by the 60×–80× reduction in evaluation time compared to CP-AER across Wave, Navier-Stokes, and MHD benchmarks. The application to tokamak plasma modelling and Grad-Shafranov equilibrium reconstruction addresses a real deployment bottleneck in fusion reactor design, where surrogate models currently lack any UQ capability. A key open question is the transformation of coverage bounds from the PDE residual space to the physical variable space, which requires set propagation through integral operations and remains unresolved. The framework's limitation to regular grids and its inability to distinguish aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty are acknowledged but not addressed.
