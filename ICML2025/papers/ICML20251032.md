## Problem

Protein Language Models (PLMs) encode rich evolutionary knowledge but cannot reliably generate proteins with precisely specified properties without massive sampling and screening. Existing control strategies—fine-tuning on property-specific data or pre-training with keyword tags—require retraining, demand large labeled datasets, or lack flexibility for new properties. Activation steering, a training-free inference-time technique well-established for LLMs, remains entirely unexplored for PLMs, where protein-specific structural and evolutionary constraints make direct transfer non-trivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference protein sequence (for AE-PLMs) or a superfamily description (for AR-PLMs), along with a positive set and a negative set of ~100 protein sequences that do and do not exhibit the target property, respectively. Steering vectors are computed per-layer as the mean activation difference between these sets (using average token activations for AE-PLMs and last-token activations for AR-PLMs). The output is a generated or optimized protein sequence with enhanced target property (thermostability, solubility, or GFP brightness), produced without any weight updates or explicit fitness predictors.

## Method

The core mechanism is activation addition at inference time: at each transformer layer l, the hidden state is modified as h̃_l = h_l + α·v_l, where v_l is the steering vector (mean activation difference between positive and negative protein sets) and α controls steering strength; the edited activation is then rescaled to preserve its original norm. For protein optimization (ASPO), the method extends this by identifying mutation sites via a relatedness score—the cosine similarity between each token's representation and the steering vector at a selected informative layer—selecting the T tokens with the lowest scores (most negatively related to the target property), masking them, and predicting new amino acids under activation steering, repeated over R rounds. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requiring no retraining, no surrogate fitness model, and no architectural modifications, and is validated across ESM2, ESM3, and ProLLaMA on thermostability, solubility, and GFP brightness tasks.

## Results

For lysozyme-like protein generation, activation steering on ESM2 achieves 82.20°C predicted thermostability (vs. 63.56 for fine-tuning and 56.48 for the original model) and 0.494 solubility (vs. 0.356 and 0.289). For protein optimization, ESM3+ASPO attains 88.42 thermostability fitness (medium difficulty) and 86.43 (hard), substantially outperforming all baselines. On GFP brightness optimization, ESM2+ASPO reaches 3.862 log-fluorescence fitness, more than double the best baseline (GWG at 1.683). Activation steering also maintains or improves diversity and novelty relative to fine-tuning across all architectures tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASPO (on ESM2/ESM3) decisively outperforms all three baselines on target-property fitness across every optimization task. On thermostability (hard difficulty), ESM3+ASPO reaches 86.43 fitness versus AdaLead (55.16), PEX (48.95), and GWG (47.73) — a 16–38 point margin. On GFP brightness (medium difficulty), ESM2+ASPO achieves 3.862 log-fluorescence, more than doubling GWG (1.683) and AdaLead (1.179). The trade-off is that ASPO produces lower sequence diversity and lower dissimilarity to the initial set compared to AdaLead and PEX, indicating a more focused, less exploratory search. GWG achieves the lowest Dissim_init (0.049–0.071), staying closest to the starting sequences, but at the cost of substantially lower fitness gains. Overall, ASPO wins on the primary metric (fitness) in all six task configurations while sacrificing some diversity, suggesting it is best suited for applications where targeted improvement matters more than broad exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the Activation Addition technique from LLM steering (Turner et al., 2023) to protein language models, a transfer that had not been previously explored. It positions itself against two categories of prior work: (1) PLM-based protein generation/optimization methods that require fine-tuning, keyword prompting, or surrogate fitness predictors (AdaLead, PEX, GWG), and (2) the broader activation steering literature in NLP (CAA, spectral editing, bi-level optimization) that has not been applied to biological sequence models. The ASPO framework further extends the basic steering idea with a mutation-site identification module based on cosine similarity to the steering vector, which is a novel component not present in the LLM steering literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a training-free, predictor-free method for controlling PLM outputs, which sidesteps the data and compute costs of fine-tuning and the dependency on explicit fitness models. However, the evaluation is confined to lysozyme-like proteins and GFP across three properties (thermostability, solubility, brightness), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or structure-dependent properties, to de novo design beyond a fixed fold, or to multi-property optimization with larger property sets (where gains are notably smaller). The sensitivity of solubility steering to over-steering (performance collapse at α=20) also suggests that careful hyperparameter tuning remains necessary in practice.
