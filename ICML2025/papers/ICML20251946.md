## Problem

Learning generative distributions over Implicit Neural Representation (INR) parameters remains challenging: MLP-based hypernetworks hit scalability bottlenecks when generating high-dimensional INR weights, and existing Transformer-based hypernetworks (e.g., Trans-INR) are deterministic, precluding their use as probabilistic decoders in latent variable models. No prior framework unifies scalable, probabilistic INR generation with latent diffusion models across diverse data modalities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes continuous signals as coordinate-value pairs (X, Y) — e.g., pixel coordinates and RGB values for images, 3D grid coordinates and occupancy labels for shapes, or spatial coordinates and temperature values for climate fields. Rather than producing a fixed-resolution output, the method generates the parameters of a SIREN-based INR, which can then be evaluated on coordinate grids of arbitrary density to yield signals at any resolution (from ×0.125 to ×4 relative to training resolution). For generation, the input is random noise sampled from a learned latent diffusion prior; for conditional tasks like inpainting, a partially masked image provides the conditioning signal.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hyper-Transformer Decoder (HD), a full Transformer architecture that maps tensor-shaped latent codes to INR parameters. A tokenizer splits the latent into patches, a Transformer Encoder processes them via self-attention into latent tokens, and a Transformer Decoder cross-attends to these tokens using learnable grouped weight tokens as queries to produce output weight groups. A key design choice is a new reconstruction operator R^(scale) = (1 + w^o) ⊙ w^b that replaces the normalization used in Trans-INR, preventing gradient vanishing when generating SIREN weights with periodic activations. The framework supports two training paradigms: (i) full training via an I-VAE (β-VAE with HD decoder) followed by a DDPM on the latent space, and (ii) hyper-transforming, which freezes a pre-trained LDM's encoder and diffusion model and trains only the HD decoder to maximize decoded log-likelihood, enabling efficient adaptation without retraining the generative backbone.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 64×64, LDMI achieves a PSNR of 24.80 dB and FID of 18.06 with only 8.06M hypernetwork parameters, outperforming VAMoH (PSNR 23.17, FID 66.27, 25.7M params) and Functa (FID 40.40). On ImageNet 256×256, LDMI attains an FID of 6.94, surpassing Spatial Functa (FID ≤8.5). On ERA5 climate data, LDMI achieves 44.6 dB PSNR, a 5.6 dB improvement over VAMoH (39.0 dB) and a 9.7 dB improvement over Functa (34.9 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAMoH (Koyuncu et al., 2023), LDMI wins decisively on CelebA-HQ 64×64 with a 1.63 dB PSNR gain (24.80 vs 23.17), a 3.7× FID improvement (18.06 vs 66.27), and uses only 8.06M hypernetwork parameters versus 25.7M. Against Functa (Dupont et al., 2022a), LDMI achieves a substantially lower FID on CelebA-HQ (18.06 vs 40.40) and a 9.7 dB PSNR advantage on ERA5 (44.6 vs 34.9), though Functa reports higher PSNR on CelebA-HQ (≤30.7) due to its test-time per-image optimization rather than amortized inference. On ShapeNet Chairs, Functa edges out LDMI (99.51% vs 97.25% accuracy), but LDMI still surpasses VAMoH (97.25% vs 96.75%). Against Spatial Functa (Bauer et al., 2023) on ImageNet 256×256, LDMI achieves a lower FID (6.94 vs ≤8.5) but a lower PSNR (20.69 vs ≤38.4), with the paper noting that Functa's PSNR advantage stems from its optimization-based test procedure. GASP (Dupont et al., 2022b) reports a lower FID (7.42) on CelebA-HQ but cannot perform reconstructions due to its adversarial design, whereas LDMI supports both generation and reconstruction at arbitrary resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., 2022), which provide the generative backbone operating in a compressed latent space; (2) hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2017) and their Transformer-based extensions (Chen & Wang, 2022; Zhmoginov et al., 2022), which generate INR parameters from latent codes; and (3) probabilistic INR generation (Koyuncu et al., 2023; Dupont et al., 2022a,b), which learns distributions over function parameters. The key departure from prior work is twofold: the Hyper-Transformer Decoder introduces a full Transformer Encoder-Decoder architecture with cross-attention (rather than the encoder-only design of Trans-INR) and a probabilistic formulation, and the 'hyper-transforming' strategy freezes a pretrained LDM and trains only the HD decoder, enabling efficient adaptation without full retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution-agnostic generation capability (evaluating INRs at arbitrary coordinate grids) is a practically significant advantage over pixel-grid-based diffusion models, particularly for super-resolution and multi-scale analysis. The hyper-transforming approach lowers the barrier to adopting INR-based generation by leveraging existing pretrained LDMs, which is attractive for practitioners who have invested in large-scale diffusion training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or high-fidelity 3D generation, how it handles text conditioning at scale, and whether the 102M-parameter HD decoder on ImageNet represents a fundamental cost of the method or an artifact of the dataset's complexity. The paper does not deeply explore failure modes or the limits of the frozen-latent assumption.
