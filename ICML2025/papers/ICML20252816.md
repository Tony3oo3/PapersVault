## Problem

Existing asynchronous federated learning (AFL) methods face two critical limitations: they require strong bounded data heterogeneity assumptions to guarantee convergence, and their learning rates depend on unknown problem-specific parameters (smoothness constants, staleness bounds, gradient variance) that are impractical to estimate in real FL deployments. This paper asks whether momentum-based optimization and adaptive learning strategies can simultaneously eliminate both the heterogeneity assumption and the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The system consists of N edge devices each holding a local dataset drawn from a potentially heterogeneous distribution D_i; the server coordinates training by randomly selecting subsets of S clients per round while clients perform K local SGD steps at their own pace under bounded delay τ_max. Experiments use CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB, ResNet-18) and FMNIST (28×28, 3-conv-layer CNN) with both i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. data splits. The output is the global model parameter vector θ ∈ R^d, with convergence measured via the average squared gradient norm over T communication rounds.

## Method

MasFL introduces a two-level momentum mechanism (server-side global momentum g^t and client-side local momentum g_i^{t,k}) combined with SCAFFOLD-style control variates adapted for asynchrony, where the server aggregates control-variate differences c_i^{t+1} − c_i^t to correct client drift while isolating all staleness terms on the client side. This design provably removes the bounded data heterogeneity assumption while achieving O(1/√(SKT)) convergence with linear speedup in both participating clients S and local steps K. AdaMasFL further replaces the fixed local learning rate with momentum-driven gradient normalization (θ ← θ − η·g/‖g‖), ensuring constant-magnitude updates that eliminate all dependencies on unknown smoothness L and delay τ_max. The server-side aggregation in AdaMasFL switches from momentum-based updates to averaging actual local model displacement directions, bounding the global step by γ and yielding a fully problem-parameter-free algorithm with all hyperparameters (η, γ, β) determined solely by S, K, and T.

## Results

The paper evaluates MasFL and AdaMasFL on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) and FMNIST (3-layer CNN) under both i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. data splits, comparing against CA²FL and FADAS. Results are presented as test-accuracy-vs-communication-round plots (Figures 1–3) rather than a single numerical table. The key empirical findings are: (1) both proposed methods converge faster and reach higher final test accuracy than CA²FL and FADAS, with the gap most pronounced on non-i.i.d. CIFAR-10; (2) AdaMasFL maintains nearly constant test accuracy as the number of concurrent clients M_c increases from 30 to 100 (i.e., as asynchrony grows), while baselines degrade significantly; and (3) AdaMasFL outperforms baselines across a wide sweep of learning rates, whereas CA²FL and FADAS are highly sensitive to the chosen rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art AFL baselines: CA²FL (Wang et al., ICLR 2023) and FADAS (Wang et al., ICML 2024). On both CIFAR-10 and FMNIST (i.i.d. and non-i.i.d.), MasFL and AdaMasFL achieve higher test accuracy at every communication round than both baselines, with the largest gap observed on non-i.i.d. CIFAR-10 where the two-level momentum most effectively counters client drift. Under increasing asynchrony (M_c from 30 to 100 on non-i.i.d. FMNIST), AdaMasFL maintains nearly flat test accuracy while CA²FL and FADAS show clear degradation, indicating that the adaptive gradient normalization absorbs delay-induced variance. In the learning-rate sweep (Figure 3), AdaMasFL consistently outperforms both baselines across the entire displayed range, whereas CA²FL and FADAS exhibit sharp performance drops outside a narrow optimal window. Theoretically, MasFL removes the bounded-data-heterogeneity (BDH) assumption required by CA²FL, DeFedAvg-nIID, and FADAS, and AdaMasFL further eliminates all problem-specific parameters (L, τ_max, G) from the stepsize, a property no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the buffered asynchronous aggregation mechanism of FedBuff (Nguyen et al., 2022) and the control variate framework of SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020b) to the asynchronous federated learning setting, adding a two-level (client- and server-side) momentum structure that prior AFL methods lacked. It departs from CA²FL and FADAS, which still require bounded data heterogeneity and bounded gradient assumptions, by showing that momentum-driven control variates can eliminate these restrictions entirely. The adaptive variant (AdaMasFL) draws on the parameter-free optimization literature (e.g., PAdaMFed for synchronous FL) but is the first to combine gradient normalization with two-level momentum in an asynchronous federated context, achieving fully problem-parameter-free convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of bounded data heterogeneity assumptions is practically significant because real-world FL deployments involve arbitrarily diverse client distributions, and prior methods' reliance on such bounds limited their applicability. The problem-parameter-free property of AdaMasFL removes a major practical barrier—tuning learning rates based on unknown smoothness constants and delay bounds—making the method more deployable in dynamic, heterogeneous edge environments. Open questions include whether the O(1/(SKT)^(1/4)) convergence rate of AdaMasFL (worse than MasFL's O(1/√(SKT))) can be improved, whether the bounded-delay assumption can be relaxed, and whether the approach scales to larger-scale tasks (e.g., ImageNet, NLP) beyond the CIFAR-10 and FMNIST experiments presented.
