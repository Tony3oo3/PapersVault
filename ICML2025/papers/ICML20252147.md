## Problem

Diffusion models are known to memorize training data, and prior mitigation strategies (corruption, differential privacy, sampling adaptations) typically sacrifice image quality. This paper asks whether high generation quality and low memorization can coexist, providing theoretical evidence that memorization is only necessary at low noise scales (where high-frequency details are produced) and not at high noise scales (where structural diversity is controlled).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes random noise and produces images, trained from scratch on limited datasets of 300, 1k, or 3k images from CIFAR-10, FFHQ, or Tiny ImageNet. For text-conditional generation, it fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2 on 10k image-text pairs from LAION, taking a text prompt and random noise to produce a generated image. The method introduces a single hyperparameter t_n (a noise level) that controls the memorization–quality trade-off; during training, the dataset is augmented with noisy copies of each training image at level t_n, which are used exclusively for the high-noise portion of the diffusion trajectory.

## Method

The core idea splits the diffusion training trajectory at a noise level t_n: for t ≤ t_n, the model is trained with the standard DDPM denoising score matching loss on clean training images to preserve high-frequency detail; for t > t_n, it is trained with the Ambient Score Matching loss on noisy versions of the training images (created by adding Gaussian noise at level t_n), so the model never sees clean data in the high-noise regime. The theoretical justification adapts Feldman's (2020) subpopulations model to diffusion: at low noise, heavy-tailed frequency distributions persist so memorizing rare examples is necessary for generalization, but at high noise, subpopulations merge and the heavy-tailed structure vanishes, removing that necessity. The noisy targets are also inherently harder to memorize because Gaussian noise is incompressible, and the paper proves a mutual-information bound showing that DDPM leaks m times more information about a training point than the ambient objective at the same noise level. The result is a model that copies fine details from training data via the low-noise DDPM component while generating diverse structures via the high-noise ambient component, pushing the Pareto frontier between FID and memorization without any architectural changes.

## Results

On FFHQ with 300 training images, the proposed method achieves FID 15.05 (vs. 16.21 for DDPM) while reducing the fraction of generated samples with DINOv2 similarity >0.9 to 29.86% (vs. 47.86% for DDPM). At 1k images, FID improves to 11.3 (vs. 12.26) and high-similarity memorization drops from 33.92% to 7.52%. On CIFAR-10 with 300 images, FID drops from 25.12 to 3.91 and S>0.925 memorization falls from 67.22% to 0.22%. The gains shrink as dataset size increases, with near-parity at 3k images (e.g., FFHQ FID 6.46 vs. 6.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), the proposed method achieves equal or better FID while substantially reducing memorization across all datasets and training sizes; for example, on FFHQ-300 the S>0.9 memorization rate drops from 47.86% to 29.86% with a 1.16 FID improvement. Compared to the Masking baseline (Daras et al., 2023c, random inpainting), the proposed method achieves both lower FID (15.05 vs. 23.40 on FFHQ-300) and lower memorization (S>0.85: 49.68% vs. 53.73%), demonstrating that retaining clean data for the low-noise regime is critical for quality. Against the Noise-only baseline (Daras et al., 2024b, training solely on noisy data), the advantage is even larger: FID 15.05 vs. 27.92 on FFHQ-300, confirming that the hybrid DDPM+Ambient strategy preserves high-frequency detail that pure noisy-data training cannot recover. In the text-conditional setting (Table 5), combining the proposed method with Wen et al. (2024) yields the best memorization (Sim 0.192, 95th-pct 0.267) and FID (20.74) among all text-mitigation strategies, outperforming Wen et al. alone (Sim 0.208, FID 21.44) and Somepalli et al. (2023) alone (Sim 0.319, FID 20.55). The paper concludes that DDPM-trained models are not Pareto-optimal for small datasets, as the proposed method dominates on both axes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct threads: the Ambient Diffusion framework (Daras et al., 2023c, 2024b) for training score functions from noisy/corrupted data without ever seeing clean images, and Feldman's (2020) subpopulations model for characterizing when memorization is theoretically necessary for generalization. The key departure is a principled split of the diffusion training trajectory: standard DDPM loss at low noise scales (where memorization is provably needed for high-frequency details) and ambient score matching at high noise scales (where subpopulations merge and memorization becomes unnecessary). This contrasts with prior memorization-mitigation approaches—sampling-time adaptations (Wen et al., 2024; Kazdan et al., 2024), data corruption (Somepalli et al., 2023; Daras et al., 2023c), and differential privacy (Dockhorn et al., 2022)—which typically degrade image quality, by operating entirely at training time and preserving the model's ability to copy fine details from the training set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DDPM is not Pareto-optimal for small datasets, offering a simple training recipe (one hyperparameter t_n) that reduces memorization by 2–3× at matched FID across CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and Tiny-ImageNet. The theoretical contribution—adapting Feldman's heavy-tailed frequency analysis to show that τ₁ (the memorization penalty) vanishes at high noise scales—provides a principled justification for why the split works. However, the method lacks formal privacy guarantees, the key conjecture (that concatenating a non-memorized high-noise trajectory with a memorized low-noise one avoids replicated outputs) remains unproven, and in the text-conditional setting the method alone provides minimal benefit without additional text-mitigation strategies. An end-to-end generalization bound for the proposed algorithm is an open problem.
