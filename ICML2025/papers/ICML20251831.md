## Problem

Partial domain adaptation (PDA) algorithms rely on heuristic weighting schemes to down-weight outlier source classes and on partial Wasserstein distance for domain alignment, yet no generalization bounds previously justified either choice. This paper derives PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for PDA that simultaneously motivate the partial Wasserstein alignment term and constructively yield per-sample source weights from the optimal transport plan, providing the first theoretically grounded treatment of negative transfer in PDA.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB source images from a domain with a superset label space and unlabeled RGB target images from a domain with a subset label space (e.g., 65-class Office-Home domains reduced to 25 target classes). It outputs a ResNet-50-based classifier producing class label predictions, along with per-source-sample weights computed from the optimal coupling matrix of a mini-batch partial transport problem at every training iteration. Unlike prior schemes that update weights on the full dataset every n iterations, WARMPOT recomputes weights per mini-batch, making them responsive to the evolving feature representation.

## Method

The paper derives two families of generalization bounds (feature-based and joint-distribution-based) that upper-bound the empirical target loss in terms of a weighted source loss, a partial Wasserstein distance between empirical feature (or feature-label) distributions, a total variation correction, and an irreducible difficulty term. The source weights p_i are defined as row sums of the optimal coupling matrix Π* solving the partial transport problem, so samples that dominate the transport plan receive high weight while outliers receive near-zero weight. The resulting algorithm WARMPOT minimizes the sum of the weighted source cross-entropy loss and the partial Wasserstein alignment term (with cost η₁‖f(x)−f(x̃)‖ + η₂ℓ(y,ŷ)), using an entropic OT solver per mini-batch with parameters α (fraction of target mass transported) and β (scaling of source mass) to control the degree of partial alignment. When combined with the ARPM algorithm's additional loss terms, the WARMPOT weights improve upon the state-of-the-art on Office-Home.

## Results

On the Office-Home dataset (12 domain-transfer tasks, 65 classes, 25 shared), WARMPOT achieves an average test accuracy of 77.6% (±0.7), competitive with MPOT (77.9%) and PWAN (77.3%), and substantially above the ResNet-50 no-adaptation baseline (61.4%). The headline result is that replacing ARPM's heuristic weights with WARMPOT's transport-derived weights yields ARPM+our-weights at 82.0% (±0.4) average accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA ARPM (81.7%) and all other listed methods. On the ImageNet→Caltech benchmark, WARMPOT reaches 84.8% (±0.1) and ARPM+our-weights reaches 85.1% (±0.9), competitive with PWAN (86.0%) and AR (85.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WARMPOT (77.6% avg) is roughly on par with MPOT (77.9%) and slightly ahead of PWAN (77.3%) on Office-Home, confirming that the theoretically motivated transport weights are competitive with existing partial-OT-based PDA methods. Against the broader PDA literature, WARMPOT trails the strongest baselines—ARPM (81.7%), MOT (80.6%), and SHOT++ (79.9%)—by 2–4 points, which the authors attribute to ARPM and MOT incorporating additional heuristic loss terms (uncertainty reduction, contrastive objectives) not present in the WARMPOT objective. The most striking comparison is ARPM+our-weights (82.0%) versus ARPM (81.7%): swapping ARPM's adversarial-reweighting weights for WARMPOT's partial-OT-derived weights yields a consistent 0.3-point average gain and improved per-task accuracy on 10 of 12 transfers, establishing a new SOTA on Office-Home. On ImageNet→Caltech, WARMPOT (84.8%) and ARPM+our-weights (85.1%) are competitive with AR (85.4%) and PWAN (86.0%), while clearly outperforming ARPM (84.1%) and BA3US (84.0%). The weighting-scheme ablation (Table 1) further shows that WARMPOT weights outperform MPOT's uniform weights (76.0% vs 77.6%) and ARPM's adversarial weights (72.9% vs 77.6%), and match BA3US (77.6% vs 77.6%), validating the theoretical derivation of the weights from the partial transport plan.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal-transport-based domain adaptation line (Courty et al., 2014; Shen et al., 2018) to the partial domain adaptation setting by replacing the standard Wasserstein distance with the partial Wasserstein distance, which naturally handles outlier classes. It builds directly on the mini-batch partial OT solver of Nguyen et al. (2022, MPOT) for the domain alignment term, but departs from all prior PDA weighting schemes (BA3US, ARPM, IWAN) by deriving source-sample weights constructively from the optimal transport plan rather than using heuristic class-level or discriminator-based criteria. A key technical departure from prior generalization bounds (Shen et al., 2018; Courty et al., 2017a) is that the partial Wasserstein term appears directly between empirical feature distributions, eliminating the concentration-of-measure step that was previously required to bridge population and empirical distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for using partial Wasserstein distance as a domain alignment term in PDA and yields a principled, non-heuristic weighting scheme that provably down-weights outlier source samples. Empirically, plugging these weights into the SOTA ARPM algorithm yields a small but consistent improvement (82.0 vs. 81.7 average on Office-Home), suggesting the theoretical grounding translates to practical gains. Limitations include the reliance on mini-batch approximations of the full transport problem, the absence of uncertainty or robustness terms present in ARPM's additional losses, and the fact that exact minimization of the derived bounds is computationally intractable. A natural open direction is incorporating the uncertainty-reduction and robustness objectives studied by Gu et al. (2024) into the PAC-Bayes framework to close the gap between the theoretical bound and the full SOTA algorithm.
