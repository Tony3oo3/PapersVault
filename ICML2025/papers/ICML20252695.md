## Problem

Existing audio-language models (e.g., CLAP) learn global audio-text alignments but cannot localize when a specific sound event occurs within a clip, while traditional sound event detection (SED) models achieve precise temporal localization only for a fixed, pre-defined set of classes. This paper addresses the gap by enabling open-vocabulary, frame-level sound event detection where any natural-language description can serve as a query.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second, 48 kHz audio clip and a text event description (up to 77 tokens) as input. The HTSAT audio encoder produces a sequence of L=32 frame-level embeddings (each ~0.3125 s) plus a global embedding obtained by temporal averaging, while the RoBERTa text encoder yields a single embedding per query. At inference, the model outputs calibrated per-frame detection probabilities via an unbiased classifier s(x,l,y) with a fixed 0.5 decision threshold independent of the event description, as well as a global audio-text alignment score for retrieval.

## Method

FLAM extends the LAION-CLAP architecture (HTSAT + RoBERTa) with a frame-level binary cross-entropy contrastive objective L_SED that classifies each (frame, event-text) pair as positive or negative, replacing the instance-level InfoNCE/SigLIP loss. To handle severe event-dependent label imbalance (e.g., 'thunder' is rarer and shorter than 'rain'), the logit function h(x,l,y) = α^t(y)·e^{a,loc}(x)_l·e^t(y) + β^t(y) uses per-text scale and bias terms: β^t(y) is trained as an auxiliary MLP classifier on the marginal positive rate of each event, while α^t(y) is updated through the SED loss itself. A memory-efficient ring-based chunked training strategy (inspired by SigLIP) distributes the B×K×L pairwise computation across GPUs without centralized embedding gathering. Training is two-stage: first a global contrastive pre-training (FLAM-Global), then fine-tuning with the combined loss L = γ_CLIP·L_CLIP + γ_SED·L_SED + γ_p·L_p on 1M synthetic 10-second mixtures (1–10 events inserted into background clips with random loudness, splitting, and repetition) plus closed-set SED data (AudioSet-Strong, DESED, UrbanSED). At inference, the unbiased classifier s(x,l,y) = p(z=1|x,l,y)/(p(z=1|x,l,y)+p(z=1|y)) converts raw cosine similarities into calibrated, event-independent detection probabilities.

## Results

On the primary open-vocabulary SED benchmark (Held-out), FLAM achieves 91.0 AUROC, substantially outperforming MGA-CLAP* (74.17) and FLAM-Global (67.76). On closed-set SED, FLAM leads on DESED (81.23 AUROC vs. 69.56 for MGA-CLAP*), MAESTRO (91.66 AUROC, 56.97 MPAUC), and UrbanSED (94.76 AUROC, 29.52 PSDS). FLAM also improves zero-shot classification (86.9% ESC-50, 75.6% US8K) while maintaining competitive retrieval (e.g., 32.1 R@1 on AudioCaps T2A), confirming that frame-level supervision does not degrade global alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MGA-CLAP* (retrained on the same data), FLAM wins on every SED metric: Held-out AUROC 91.0 vs. 74.17, DESED AUROC 81.23 vs. 69.56, MAESTRO MPAUC 56.97 vs. 52.50, and UrbanSED AUROC 94.76 vs. 79.12. FLAM also surpasses MGA-CLAP* on zero-shot classification (ESC-50: 86.9 vs. 72.6; US8K: 75.6 vs. 69.9) and is competitive on retrieval (AudioCaps T2A R@1: 32.1 vs. 36.7, a modest gap). Compared to LAION-CLAP and CompA (trained on different, larger web-scale corpora), FLAM trails on Clotho/AudioCaps retrieval but leads on ASFX retrieval (4.4 R@1 vs. 2.0/–) and VGGSound zero-shot (39.3 vs. 29.1/29.5), indicating that the curated sound-effects corpus and frame-level training are particularly effective for the SED and specialized-audio domains. The ablation removing closed-set SED data causes the largest drop (Held-out AUROC 91.0 → 86.91; UrbanSED AUROC 94.76 → 84.37), confirming that combining synthetic open-vocabulary and traditional closed-set SED data is critical for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAM extends the CLAP family of contrastive audio-language models from instance-level to frame-level alignment, adapting SigLIP's binary cross-entropy objective to operate on individual audio frames rather than whole clips. It incorporates logit adjustment techniques originally developed for long-tail multi-class classification (Menon et al., 2021) and spurious-correlation-robust training (Tsirigotis et al., 2023), repurposing them as per-text-event bias and scale terms to handle the severe label imbalance inherent in frame-level SED. The key conceptual departure from prior work like MGA-CLAP is the use of direct supervised frame-level labels (synthesized via a Scaper-style mixing pipeline) rather than unsupervised or temporal-aware contrastive objectives, and the extension of SED from a fixed closed vocabulary to arbitrary natural-language event queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLAM demonstrates that open-vocabulary sound event detection—localizing any text-described event in audio—is achievable with strong calibration, filling a gap between rigid closed-set SED systems and flexible but imprecise audio-language retrieval models. The text-dependent logit adjustment yields interpretable, near-binary detection probabilities that are practically useful for content indexing and accessibility applications. Limitations include the fixed 10-second input window, coarse 32-frame temporal resolution, and reliance on synthetic data whose distribution may not fully capture real-world acoustic complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to variable-length recordings, whether real-world frame-level annotations (when available) would further improve generalization, and how to handle overlapping or partially occluded events more gracefully.
