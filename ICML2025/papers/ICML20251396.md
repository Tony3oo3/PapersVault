## Problem

Irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) have irregular sampling intervals within variables and unaligned observations across variables, making dependency learning difficult. Existing approaches either pad samples to uniform lengths (inflating data volume and disrupting original sampling patterns) or represent observations as sets or bipartite graphs (which cannot propagate messages between variables lacking shared timestamps). No prior method captures both temporal and variable dependencies from the original sparse observations in a unified, padding-free representation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a lookback window as a sparse set of (timestamp, observed value, variable indicator) tuples without any padding, plus forecast queries defined as (timestamp, variable) pairs from the forecast window. It outputs predicted scalar values for each forecast query. Because the hypergraph operates directly on observed values, the number of nodes equals the number of actual observations (e.g., ~145 on MIMIC-III vs. ~9,216 with canonical padding), keeping computation proportional to data sparsity rather than the full time×variable grid.

## Method

HyperIMTS encodes each observation as a node in a hypergraph, connected by temporal hyperedges (same timestamp) and variable hyperedges (same variable), then performs three sequential message-passing stages: (1) node-to-hyperedge multi-head attention updates temporal and variable hyperedge embeddings; (2) hyperedge-to-hyperedge attention among variable hyperedges uses an irregularity-aware similarity that blends time-aware dot products over only the time-aligned observations with overall hyperedge dot products, weighted by a learnable threshold and the ratio of shared to total timestamps; (3) hyperedge-to-node attention propagates updated hyperedge information back to observation nodes. The key design choice is that inter-variable message passing (stage 2) is applied only in the final residual layer, so earlier layers learn intra-variable temporal dependencies while the last layer injects cross-variable correlations adaptively based on how well the variables' timestamps align.

## Results

HyperIMTS achieves the lowest MSE on 4 out of 5 IMTS datasets: MIMIC-III (0.4259), MIMIC-IV (0.2174), PhysioNet'12 (0.2996), and Human Activity (0.0421). On USHCN, GRU-D achieves a slightly lower MSE (0.1639) than HyperIMTS (0.1738), though the paper notes high variance on this dataset. Compared to the strongest competitor GraFITi, HyperIMTS delivers up to 11.4% MSE reduction (on MIMIC-IV: 0.2174 vs. 0.2454). The model also outperforms the best regular MTS baseline (Crossformer) by a significant margin across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperIMTS outperforms all 27 baselines on MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, PhysioNet'12, and Human Activity, with the closest competitor being GraFITi (bipartite graph, AAAI 2024), which it beats by 6.1% on MIMIC-III (0.4259 vs. 0.4534) and 11.4% on MIMIC-IV (0.2174 vs. 0.2454). On USHCN, GRU-D (RNN-based, 2018) achieves a marginally lower MSE (0.1639 vs. 0.1738), though the paper attributes this to high variance in that dataset. Among regular MTS models, Crossformer is the strongest baseline, yet HyperIMTS still surpasses it by 30.5% on MIMIC-III and 41.1% on MIMIC-IV. Notably, several regular MTS models (e.g., Informer, PatchTST) outperform dedicated IMTS methods like PrimeNet, SeFT, and mTAN, highlighting that the irregularity-aware design of HyperIMTS is critical. The non-padding hypergraph approach also achieves lower training time and GPU memory than padding-based methods (Warpformer, GNeuralFlow, tPatchGNN) while maintaining the best accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing hypergraph neural networks—previously applied to regular MTS (Ada-MSHyper, FourierGNN) and other domains—for the IMTS forecasting task, where prior work has relied on either padding-based representations (Warpformer, tPatchGNN, GNeuralFlow) or sparse set/bipartite-graph encodings (SeFT, GraFITi). The key conceptual departure is treating each observation as a node connected by both temporal and variable hyperedges, enabling a unified message-passing framework that avoids padding while still capturing cross-variable dependencies that bipartite graphs cannot model without shared timestamps. The irregularity-aware similarity mechanism, which blends time-aligned observation-level dot products with overall hyperedge-level similarities, is a novel design choice not present in prior IMTS or hypergraph literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified benchmark spanning 27 models across five IMTS datasets (healthcare, biomechanics, climate) fills a practical gap where prior comparisons were fragmented across different preprocessing pipelines and evaluation protocols. The efficiency analysis showing that non-padding methods are insensitive to lookback length growth provides actionable guidance for practitioners handling long irregular series. However, the model does not support multi-modal inputs (text notes, images) common in medical IMTS, and attention-based computation remains more resource-intensive than state-space alternatives, leaving open whether the hypergraph formulation can be made more scalable. The finding that several regular MTS models outperform dedicated IMTS models also raises questions about whether the irregularity-specific inductive biases in existing IMTS architectures are as critical as assumed.
