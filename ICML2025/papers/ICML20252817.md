## Problem

Existing survival analysis models that adapt density estimation techniques are tailored case-by-case, rely on strong assumptions like conditional independence or proportional hazards, and lack a strictly proper scoring rule for more than two competing risks. This paper addresses these gaps by providing a general, model-agnostic framework that works under the weaker copula-based dependency assumption, introduces a strictly proper scoring rule for arbitrary K, and extends upper/lower bounds estimation to individual (conditional) survival functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular features x, a censored event time t, and an event-type indicator δ ∈ {1,…,K} as input, with the time horizon discretized into B intervals {ζ_b}. It outputs the conditional CDF F_k(ζ_b|x) for each competing risk k at each time point, from which survival functions S_k(t|x) are derived. When no copula is available, it additionally produces upper and lower bounds on F_k(t|x) that quantify uncertainty from the unknown dependency structure, distinct from epistemic uncertainty of the predictive model.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step algorithm: Step 1 estimates the cumulative incidence function V_k(t|x) using any density estimation model (e.g., LightGBM, random forests, neural networks), and Step 2 post-processes these estimates into CDFs F_k(t|x) by solving the system r_{b|x} = g_C(F_{b|x} | F_{b-1|x}) where g_C is determined by a specified copula C. The second step is implemented by minimizing a sum-of-squares objective over all time points simultaneously using automatic differentiation, with F_k parameterized via a cumulative softmax to enforce monotonicity. The framework also introduces NLL-SC, a strictly proper scoring rule for K≥2 based on the survival copula, and SC-Net, a monotone neural network trained with NLL-SC as its loss. Theoretical analysis provides Wasserstein-distance error bounds showing convergence as B→∞ and the Step-1 estimation error ε→0.

## Results

On the Dialysis and oldmort datasets (B=32), the proposed TS-LGB model achieves comparable or superior performance to DeepHit across all seven evaluation metrics (CJD-Brier, CJD-Logarithmic, CJD-KS, NLL-SC, Cen-log, D-calibration, KM-calibration), while DeepHit itself outperforms the Cox model and Random Survival Forest (RSF). The paper notes that tree-based models (TS-LGB, RSF) require substantially shorter training times than neural network models (DeepHit, TS-Brier, TS-Log, SC-Net), making TS-LGB the most effective overall model on both datasets. Exact numerical values are reported only in bar-chart form (Figure 5); no tabulated point estimates are provided in the main text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two-step algorithm (TS) with five first-step variants (TS-LGB, TS-RF, TS-DRF, TS-Brier, TS-Log) plus SC-Net against three baselines: Cox (1972), Random Survival Forest (Ishwaran et al., 2008), and DeepHit (Lee et al., 2018). DeepHit clearly outperforms both Cox and RSF on all metrics for both datasets, confirming the advantage of flexible neural-network-based survival models over classical and tree-based baselines. The headline result is that TS-LGB matches or beats DeepHit on most metrics while training faster, since LightGBM is a tree-based model. Among the TS variants, TS-LGB is the strongest, followed by TS-RF and TS-DRF; the neural-network first-step variants (TS-Brier, TS-Log) are competitive but slower to train. SC-Net, the monotone neural network trained with the proposed NLL-SC loss, is also competitive but does not surpass TS-LGB. In Appendix F, additional comparisons against Deep Survival Machines (DSM), DeSurv, and Neural Fine-Gray for K=2 and K=3 settings are reported, further supporting the competitiveness of the TS framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the case-by-case adaptation of density estimation methods to survival analysis into a single two-step framework: first estimate cumulative incidence functions via any density estimation model, then post-process using a copula to recover CDFs. It extends the copulagraphic (CG) estimators of Zheng & Klein (1995) and Carrière (1995) from average to individual survival functions, and generalizes the monotone neural network + proper scoring rule paradigm of Rindt et al. (2022) from K=2 to K≥2 competing risks. The Censored Joint Distribution (CJD) representation provides a novel geometric framing that makes the post-processing step transparent and extensible to arbitrary K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design means practitioners can leverage the entire ecosystem of density estimation tools (gradient boosting, random forests, neural networks) for survival analysis without re-deriving survival-specific adaptations for each new model. The NLL-SC scoring rule fills a notable gap by providing the first strictly proper scoring rule for K>2, enabling principled evaluation under dependent censoring. However, the empirical validation is limited to two small tabular datasets, theoretical guarantees are established only for K=2, and the framework's practical advantage over purpose-built deep survival models (e.g., DeepHit) is demonstrated only marginally, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or non-tabular settings.
