## Problem

Existing approaches to composing and adapting multiple LLM experts either require large supervised training sets to tune learnable fusion components, impose strong structural assumptions about how experts should be combined (e.g., task arithmetic formulas), or merely route queries without producing new adapted models. There is no flexible, assumption-free, training-free method that can adapt a diverse pool of LLM experts to arbitrary objectives using as few as 200 data instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of n LLM experts (full models or LoRA adapters fine-tuned on diverse domains) and a model-to-scalar utility function f (which can be dataset accuracy, reward model score, or LLM-as-a-judge score), then produces a single global-best adapted expert plus N adapted experts in weight space. The initial pool is expanded to N particles via pairwise linear interpolation (crossover), and the utility function is evaluated on as few as 200 validation instances. For heterogeneous architectures, a token-probability variant operates on n-dimensional location matrices rather than raw weights, composing next-token distributions across experts.

## Method

Model Swarms adapts LLM experts by treating each as a particle in a weight-space search inspired by Particle Swarm Optimization. Each particle has a location (model weights) and velocity (direction in weight space); the velocity is updated as a normalized weighted sum of four terms—current velocity (inertia), pull toward personal best, pull toward global best, and repulsion from global worst—each modulated by a random factor drawn from U(0,1) to ensure non-deterministic exploration. Particles then take a step x_i ← x_i + λv_i, are re-evaluated on f, and stuck particles (no personal-best improvement for c_r iterations) are reset to their personal best with zero velocity. The step length λ decays geometrically each iteration, and the search terminates when the global best stagnates for c iterations or K iterations are reached. Critically, the entire process is training-free: no gradients, no loss function, no backpropagation—only linear arithmetic on model parameters guided by the scalar utility signal.

## Results

On 9 single-task datasets (knowledge, reasoning, safety), MODEL SWARMS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all tasks, outperforming the second-strongest baseline by 13.3% on average and up to 29.7% on GSM8k (0.459 vs. 0.354). The largest gains appear on reasoning tasks (Knowledge Crosswords, GSM8k, NLGraph) with a 21.0% average improvement. In the multi-task domain setting (8 tasks across medical, legal, science, culture), MODEL SWARMS leads by 5.7% on average, and on reward model adaptation it outperforms all 14 baselines (including PPO and DPO) by 6.7% using only 200 instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MODEL SWARMS wins on all 9 single-task datasets against all 12 baselines, with the largest margins on reasoning tasks: GSM8k (0.459 vs. EvolMerge/LoraHub at 0.354, +29.7%) and NLGraph (0.672 vs. LoraHub at 0.568, +18.3%). Among static composition baselines, DARE-TIES (Yu/Yadav) is the strongest, reaching 0.567 on MMLU and 0.622 on Hellaswag, but MODEL SWARMS surpasses it by 2.8% and 4.8% respectively; Slerp and Model Stocks trail further behind. Among dynamic composition baselines, LoraHub and EvolMerge are the closest competitors (achieving 11 of 18 second-place positions across val/test), yet MODEL SWARMS still leads them by 20.8% on average. On reward model adaptation (Table 4), MODEL SWARMS outperforms DPO (0.682 verbose / 0.599 concise test) by 8.8% and 6.0%, and PPO (0.641 / 0.527) by 12.9% and 11.2%, while also uniquely achieving strong scores on both conflicting preferences simultaneously—something no single baseline manages. In the multi-task domain setting (Table 2), MODEL SWARMS leads all baselines by 5.7% on average, with the largest gains in the legal domain (11.3% over the best baseline on hearsay classification).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Particle Swarm Optimization (Kennedy & Eberhart, 1995) to the LLM weight space, positioning itself as a training-free, assumption-free alternative to both static model arithmetic (Task Arithmetic, TIES/DARE, Slerp, Model Soups) and dynamic composition methods (Greedy Soup, Pack of LLMs, EvolMerge, LoraHub). Unlike prior model merging work that relies on hand-crafted composition rules (e.g., lion indoors = lion outdoors + dog indoors − dog outdoors) or requires supervised fine-tuning of learnable fusion modules, MODEL SWARMS treats each expert as a particle that proactively searches the weight space guided by personal/global best checkpoints and a flexible utility function. The closest prior work is EvolMerge (Akiba et al., 2024), which uses genetic algorithms for model merging, but MODEL SWARMS departs by using swarm intelligence with continuous velocity updates rather than discrete crossover/mutation operators, and by operating on LoRA adapter weights rather than full model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, low-data (200 examples) adaptation paradigm is practically valuable for settings where fine-tuning data is scarce or where rapid domain specialization is needed without retraining. The correctness emergence finding (36–53.5% of previously unsolvable problems become solvable) and the weak-to-strong transition (56.9% of best-ending particles started in the bottom half) suggest that the weight space between fine-tuned checkpoints contains latent capabilities not accessible to any single expert, opening questions about whether this generalizes beyond LoRA adapters and 7B-scale models. Key limitations include the same-architecture requirement for weight swarms, the computational cost of evaluating all N particles per iteration, and the dependence on a diverse initial expert pool whose selection criteria remain heuristic. Open questions include full token-swarm implementations for heterogeneous architectures, scaling to larger swarms, and whether the emergent capabilities are genuinely new or merely re-weighted combinations of existing knowledge.
