## Problem

Analog circuit topology synthesis faces an exponentially growing design space where valid, high-performance designs are sparse, making automated generation under strict constraints (efficiency, output voltage) extremely difficult. Existing LLM-based approaches are limited to small circuits (≤6 components) and lack iterative, performance-driven refinement, while search-based methods require hundreds of SPICE simulation queries per design. This paper targets the gap of scaling LLM-based synthesis to more complex power converter circuits while simultaneously optimizing multiple performance objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured natural-language prompt specifying a component pool (capacitors, inductors, n-type/p-type MOSFETs with fixed parameters) and one or more design constraints (component-only, minimum efficiency threshold, or target output voltage given a fixed 2V input). The output is a circuit topology netlist in 'incident encoding' format—a list of [component_name, port1, port2] tuples representing an undirected graph with five fixed external ports (IN, OUT, GND, GATEN, GATEP)—concatenated with a duty cycle value selected from {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9}. Circuits range from 4 to 10 components, with MOSFETs having four ports (drain, gate, source, body) simplified to two by fixing gate/body connections.

## Method

AUTOCIRCUIT-RL employs a two-phase training pipeline: (1) instruction tuning, where an LLM (Llama-3-8B or MPT-7B) is fine-tuned via negative log-likelihood to generate incident-encoded netlists from constraint prompts, and (2) RL refinement using RLAIF with PPO, where three RoBERTa-based reward estimators (validity classifier at 92% F1, efficiency regressor at 83% macro F1, output voltage regressor at MSE 8e-3) produce a piecewise reward in [-1,1] that penalizes invalid circuits and rewards constraint satisfaction. A KL-divergence penalty to the SFT reference model prevents distribution collapse during PPO updates. A third stage, Iterative Adaptation, samples 10,000 high-efficiency circuits (s_eff > 0.7) via nucleus sampling and re-applies RL training for 3–5 iterations, progressively sharpening the policy toward valid, high-performance topologies. This RL-driven multi-objective optimization outperforms Gumbel-Max fine-tuning by ~9% in validity/efficiency because it adaptively balances competing constraints through iterative AI feedback rather than a fixed relaxation.

## Results

On 4-component circuits, AUTOCIRCUIT-RL (Llama-3) achieves 75.1% classifier validity and 73.6% efficiency, outperforming the best fine-tuned baseline (Gumbel-Llama-3: 67.6% validity, 67.2% efficiency) by ~7.5% and ~6.4% respectively. The overall success rate reaches 80.7% (vs. 71.3% for Gumbel-Llama-3), and the duplicate generation rate drops to 1.29 (vs. 2.19 for Gumbel-Llama-3), a ~38% reduction. For 6–10 component circuits with only 1,000 few-shot examples, AC-RL achieves 58.5–65.5% overall success rate, compared to 12.8–21.5% for GraphVAE and 32.1–39.7% for prompt-tuned Flan-UL2-20B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-tuned Llama-2-13B and Flan-UL2-20B (Lester et al., 2021), AC-RL (Llama-3) improves overall success rate from 43–45% to 80.7% and reduces DGR from ~5 to 1.29, while also surpassing them in validity by ~18–21 percentage points. Compared to the Gumbel-Max multi-objective fine-tuning baseline from CircuitSynth (Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024), AC-RL gains ~7.5% in validity and ~6.4% in efficiency on 4C circuits, with the paper attributing the gap to RL's ability to iteratively balance competing objectives rather than the fixed Gumbel-softmax relaxation. Against GraphVAE (Simonovsky & Komodakis, 2018) on 6–10 component circuits (Table 2), AC-RL achieves 58.5–65.5% overall success rate versus 12.8–21.5% for GraphVAE, a 3–5× improvement, demonstrating that the LLM+RL approach scales far better to larger topologies than the graph VAE. AC-RL also outperforms vanilla fine-tuning of the same Llama-3 and MPT-7B backbones by ~8–11% in overall success rate, confirming that the RL refinement phase (not just SFT) drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-phase SFT-then-RL paradigm from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) and RLAIF (Bai et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2023) to the domain of analog circuit topology synthesis, replacing human preference labels with constraint-specific AI reward models (validity, efficiency, output voltage) built on RoBERTa. It builds directly on the authors' prior CircuitSynth work (Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024), which used Gumbel-Max multi-objective fine-tuning, by replacing that static optimization with iterative PPO-based RL refinement and adding an iterative adaptation loop. The netlist representation adopts incident encoding from Fatemi et al. (2024), and the framework is positioned against both search-based methods (Fan et al., 2021) requiring hundreds of simulation queries and prior LLM approaches (AnalogCoder, Artisan, LaMAGIC) that lack performance-driven iterative refinement or are restricted to small circuits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that RL refinement on top of instruction-tuned LLMs yields meaningful gains (~12% validity, ~14% efficiency) over vanilla fine-tuning for constrained circuit synthesis, and that the approach generalizes to 6–10 component circuits with only ~1,000 training examples, suggesting a path toward scalable AI-driven EDA. However, the current scope is limited to switching power converters with fixed component parameters and a single switching frequency, leaving open questions about extending to diverse circuit families (op-amps, filters), adaptive component sizing, and multi-objective trade-offs beyond efficiency and voltage. The ~38% reduction in duplicate generation and the finding that smaller RL-refined models outperform larger prompt-tuned models are practically useful for reducing computational cost in design-space exploration.
