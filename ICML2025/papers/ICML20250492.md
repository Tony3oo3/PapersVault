## Problem

Classical Euclidean Gaussian distributions cannot capture the intrinsic Riemannian geometry of SPD matrices, and prior non-isotropic Gaussian models on manifolds suffer from intractable normalizing constants or restrict the tangent-space distribution to be centered. This paper introduces a non-isotropic wrapped Gaussian on the SPD manifold that allows a non-centered tangent-space mean, providing both theoretical justification (a wrapped CLT) and a practical parameter estimation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250492/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes d×d SPD matrices (e.g., 3×3 for BCI, 5×5 for hyperspectral, up to 13×13 for multispectral imaging) and class labels during training. It produces class predictions, a probability density on the SPD manifold, and estimated parameters (p, μ, Σ) living on the product manifold Θ = P_d × R^{d(d+1)/2} × P_{d(d+1)/2}. A key structural detail is that the parameterization is non-identifiable: multiple parameter tuples define the same distribution, resolved via an equivalence relation with a minimal representative. The number of free parameters grows as O(d⁴), motivating a diagonal-Σ assumption in experiments to reduce the count to O(d²).

## Method

The wrapped Gaussian WG(p; μ, Σ) is defined by sampling a multivariate Gaussian t ~ N(μ, Σ) in the vectorized tangent space T_p P_d and mapping it to the manifold via the exponential map Exp_p ∘ Vect_p^{-1}. The density (Theorem 4.2) involves the Gaussian density evaluated at Vect_p(Log_p(x)) divided by the Jacobian determinant of the exponential map, which has a closed form in terms of eigenvalue differences (Proposition 4.3). The paper proves a wrapped Central Limit Theorem using the logarithmic product, establishes an equivalence relation among parameter sets (Proposition 4.9) with a computable minimal representative, and develops an MLE optimized via Riemannian conjugate gradient on the product manifold Θ. For classification, the authors show MDM, TS-LDA, and TS-QDA are special cases of maximum-likelihood classification under specific wrapped Gaussian assumptions, then propose two new classifiers: Ho-WDA (shared covariance Σ across classes) and He-WDA (per-class covariance Σ_k), both trained by joint or per-class MLE optimization.

## Results

On large-scale datasets, the proposed Ho-WDA classifier achieves the best accuracy: 71.20% on Salinas (vs. 69.87% for TS-QDA and 36.42% for MDM), 73.74% on Indiana Pines (vs. 73.38% for TS-QDA), 87.36% on Pavia University (vs. 87.16% for TS-QDA), and 54.66% on BreizhCrops (vs. 50.72% for TS-QDA). On the small-sample BCI datasets (BNCI2014004, Zhou2016), MDM and TS-LDA outperform the wrapped Gaussian classifiers, with MDM reaching 78.71% and 91.18% respectively, while Ho-WDA achieves 75.38% and 85.92%. On the AirQuality dataset (102 matrices), He-WDA achieves 97.00% accuracy, matching TS-QDA (97.05%) and outperforming MDM (94.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Ho-WDA and He-WDA classifiers against three established SPD-matrix baselines: MDM (Barachant et al., 2010), TS-LDA, and TS-QDA (Barachant et al., 2012). On the five large datasets (Salinas, Indiana Pines, Pavia Univ., Textile, BreizhCrops), Ho-WDA consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest margin on BreizhCrops (+3.94 pp over TS-QDA) and Salinas (+1.33 pp over TS-QDA, +34.78 pp over MDM). On the two BCI datasets with fewer than 1,000 samples, MDM and TS-LDA clearly dominate: on Zhou2016, MDM reaches 91.18% while Ho-WDA drops to 85.92% (−5.26 pp), and on BNCI2014004, TS-LDA leads at 78.73% versus Ho-WDA's 75.38%. On AirQuality (102 matrices), He-WDA (97.00%) essentially ties TS-QDA (97.05%) and beats MDM by ~3 pp, suggesting the wrapped Gaussian model can be effective even with very few samples when the underlying distribution is simple. No clear dominance of He-WDA over Ho-WDA is observed; in fact, Ho-WDA outperforms He-WDA on Salinas (71.20% vs. 62.39%) and Pavia Univ. (87.36% vs. 85.54%), likely because per-class covariance estimation is noisy with limited samples per class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of wrapped distributions on Riemannian manifolds (Chevallier & Guigui 2020, Chevallier et al. 2022, Galaz-Garcia et al. 2022) to the SPD manifold with the AIRM metric, departing from prior work by allowing a non-centered tangent-space Gaussian (μ≠0) and by using the exponential map directly rather than a volume-preserving push-forward. It also generalizes the isotropic Gaussian of Said et al. (2018) to a non-isotropic model and resolves the sampling and normalization difficulties of Pennec's (2006) entropy-based manifold Gaussian. The paper further unifies existing SPD classifiers (MDM, TS-LDA, TS-QDA) under a single probabilistic framework and introduces two new wrapped-Gaussian-based classifiers (Ho-WDA, He-WDA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wrapped CLT and the equivalence relation providing a minimal representative give the distribution a solid theoretical foundation that justifies its use as a generic building block for manifold-valued statistics. However, the O(d⁴) parameter growth of the full covariance matrix and the lack of a closed-form MLE for the base point p limit practical applicability to moderate dimensions or require the diagonal-covariance assumption. Open questions include extending the framework to Gaussian Mixture Models, VAEs, and Bayesian inference on SPD manifolds, as well as addressing the computational bottleneck of repeated exponential/logarithmic map evaluations at scale.
