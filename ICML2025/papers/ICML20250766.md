## Problem

While diffusion models have advanced satellite image synthesis, no prior work has explicitly generated location-specific satellite imagery conditioned on future or alternative climatic conditions. This gap limits predictive applications such as seasonal crop yield forecasting and climate change impact assessment, which require realistic visual representations of how weather and climate alter Earth's surface under scenario assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The text-to-image model consumes a structured prompt encoding land cover type, geographic location, date, cloudiness, and three continuous climate variables (monthly average temperature, total precipitation, daily solar radiation), processed through CLIP (spatial) and T5 (climate) encoders. The multi-conditional variant additionally takes a reference satellite image from a prior month as spatial guidance via ControlNet. Outputs are synthetic RGB Sentinel-2-style images at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution (~26 km² per tile), and the ControlNet pipeline can be iterated to produce monthly time-series sequences simulating landscape evolution.

## Method

The paper fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3) with a dual-encoder prompt strategy: CLIP encodes spatial metadata while T5 encodes climate variables, and a caption randomization scheme (stochastically dropping month or location) decouples climate effects from spurious temporal/geographic correlations. For multi-conditional generation, a ControlNet branch is initialized from the first 12 layers of the LoRA-adapted SD3-FT model and fine-tuned (1.1B parameters) to accept a prior-month satellite image as spatial control, preserving landforms while the climate prompt drives environmental changes. A parallel experiment modifies DiffusionSat's SatUNet backbone by adding three climate metadata layers (10 total) initialized randomly, then fine-tunes all 900M parameters. The high-resolution variant (SD3-FT-HR) forgoes LoRA and fine-tunes 70% of transformer blocks (1.5B parameters) at 1024×1024 on 1.2M images, achieving the best FID (49.48) among text-to-image models.

## Results

On the EcoMapper test set (5,500 locations, 2017–2024), the best text-to-image model SD3-FT-HR achieves an FID of 49.48, CLIP score of 0.31, SSIM of 0.36, PSNR of 12.01, and LPIPS of 0.69. The multi-conditional SD3 ControlNet model further improves to FID 48.20, SSIM 0.40, PSNR 13.63, and LPIPS 0.59. Fine-tuning yields substantial gains over both baselines: SD3's FID drops from 157.36 to 77.9 (SD3-FT) and DiffusionSat's from 115.00 to 59.8 (DiffusionSat-FT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two pretrained baselines—Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3) and DiffusionSat—against their fine-tuned variants on the EcoMapper dataset. Without fine-tuning, DiffusionSat substantially outperforms SD3 on all metrics (FID 115.00 vs. 157.36; SSIM 0.25 vs. 0.14; PSNR 10.18 vs. 8.25), confirming the advantage of satellite-specific pretraining. After fine-tuning with climate metadata, DiffusionSat-FT achieves the best FID (59.8) and LPIPS (0.68) among 512×512 models, while SD3-FT leads on CLIP (0.33 vs. 0.30), SSIM (0.39 vs. 0.33), and PSNR (14.24 vs. 12.14). The high-resolution SD3-FT-HR variant (1024×1024) attains the best FID among text-to-image models (49.48), and the multi-conditional SD3 ControlNet model achieves the overall best FID (48.20) and LPIPS (0.59) by adding spatial guidance from a reference image. In summary, fine-tuning is the dominant factor for performance gains, with satellite-specific pretraining (DiffusionSat) providing a secondary but consistent edge on distributional realism metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satellite image generation line of work (DiffusionSat, MetaEarth) by introducing climate-variable conditioning as a new input modality, using structured text prompts to encode temperature, precipitation, and solar radiation into Stable Diffusion 3's T5 encoder. The multi-conditional generation approach combines ControlNet's spatial guidance mechanism with climate text prompts, a combination not previously applied to remote sensing. The 2.9-million-image EcoMapper dataset, linking Sentinel-2 RGB imagery with NASA Power climate records across 15 land cover types, is the primary new resource that enables this conditioning, as prior climate-integrated datasets were smaller and highly specialized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical pipeline for generating synthetic satellite imagery under historical or future climate scenarios, which could support crop yield forecasting, land cover change assessment, and gap-filling in persistently cloudy regions. However, FID scores (49–59) remain notably higher than super-resolution baselines (16–40), and performance degrades substantially on underrepresented land cover classes (urban, wetlands), limiting immediate deployment. Open questions include extending to multispectral bands, incorporating higher time-resolution climate inputs, and linking generation directly with climate projection models for seasonal simulations.
