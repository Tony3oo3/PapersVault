## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction algorithms like ACI are instantiations of online gradient descent on the pinball loss, yet their worst-case coverage guarantees do not follow from standard no-regret analyses. The paper identifies precisely which forms of regret (external, swap, groupwise) do and do not imply coverage, revealing that external regret fails even in i.i.d. contextual settings while swap regret is tightly equivalent to threshold-calibrated coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252037/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a group membership vector g_t ∈ [0,1]^k (encoding which of k prediction-independent groups the current example belongs to) and, after predicting, a non-conformity score threshold τ_t ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a scalar threshold prediction τ̂_t = ⟨θ_t, g_t⟩ that defines the prediction set C_τ(x) = {y : f(x,y) ≤ τ(x)}, with the guarantee that empirical coverage within each group G_i equals the target rate q up to an O(√(Tk)/T_i) error. The prediction is constrained to be a linear combination of group indicator functions, making the problem k-dimensional rather than the 1-dimensional case handled by original ACI.

## Method

The paper establishes two complementary results. First, it proves an equivalence: swap regret with respect to the pinball loss is equivalent to threshold-calibrated coverage (and groupwise swap regret to multivalid coverage), providing new algorithms for group-conditional multivalid coverage via existing swap-regret minimizers. Second, it generalizes ACI to the multi-group setting via the FTRL framework: Theorem 4.1 shows that for any FTRL algorithm (including OGD, multiplicative weights), groupwise miscoverage for group G_i is bounded by ||∇R(θ_{T+1})||_∞ / T_i, where R is the regularizer. Instantiating with k-dimensional online gradient descent (GCACI, Algorithm 2) yields an O(√(Tk)/T_i) groupwise coverage error, with the bound driven by the magnitude of the final parameter vector rather than by regret. The analysis bypasses regret entirely, requiring no smoothness assumptions on the data distribution, and experiments show GCACI converges substantially faster to target coverage than the prior MVP algorithm on time-series, census, and airfoil datasets.

## Results

The paper establishes that GCACI (Group Conditional ACI) achieves an O(√(Tk)/T_i) groupwise coverage error bound for arbitrary intersecting groups, which is tight (Ω(√T) lower bound) for real-valued group functions. Empirically, the L_∞ norm of the parameter vector ||θ_t||_∞ remains bounded by a small constant across all three experiments (WSJ time series, UCI Airfoil, Folktables), yielding effective O(1/T) groupwise coverage rates far exceeding the worst-case O(√T) guarantee. GCACI converges substantially faster to the target coverage rate (q=0.9) than the MVP baseline, with MVP failing to fully converge for some smaller-sized groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GCACI against MVP (Bastani et al., 2022), the only other method providing non-trivial group-conditional coverage guarantees in sequential adversarial settings. On all three datasets (WSJ time series with 20 groups, UCI Airfoil with 6 groups, and Folktables with 12 groups), GCACI converges to the desired 90% groupwise coverage rate substantially faster than MVP. MVP does not fully converge for some smaller-sized groups, while GCACI achieves the target coverage across all groups. Both methods share similar O(√T) worst-case coverage guarantees, but GCACI's empirical performance is much stronger because its parameter norm ||θ_t||_∞ stays near a small constant rather than growing as O(√T), effectively achieving O(1/T) convergence. The paper also shows that GCACI's convergence speed is sensitive to the learning rate η, with η=1 giving the fastest convergence (though this trades off with regret guarantees optimized at η=1/√T).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ACI algorithm of Gibbs & Candes (2021), which performs 1D online gradient descent on the pinball loss for marginal coverage, and on the FTRL framework of Shalev-Shwartz et al. (2012) as a unifying lens for no-regret algorithms. It departs from the calibration-based approach of Gupta et al. (2022) and Bastani et al. (2022) (MVP) by showing that the entire FTRL family—including OGD and multiplicative weights—yields group-conditional coverage guarantees, rather than requiring the more complex sequential calibration machinery. The key conceptual departure is establishing a tight equivalence between swap regret and threshold-calibrated coverage (and between groupwise swap regret and multivalid coverage), while simultaneously showing that the weaker external regret guarantee does NOT imply coverage in adversarial or contextual settings, even under i.i.d. data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a gap explicitly noted by Gibbs & Candes (2021): why ACI's coverage guarantee does not follow from standard OGD regret analysis. By identifying FTRL (not just OGD) as the correct algorithmic family and swap regret (not external regret) as the correct regret notion, it provides a clean theoretical bridge between two previously disconnected literatures. The O(√T) groupwise coverage bound is proven tight for real-valued group functions, but the authors conjecture (with empirical support) that binary groups admit a much better rate—possibly O(1) or O(log T)—which remains an open problem. Practically, GCACI converges faster than MVP across three datasets, suggesting the simpler FTRL-based approach may be preferable in deployment.
