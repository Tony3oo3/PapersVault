## Problem

Optimal MAPF solvers such as Branch-and-Cut-and-Price are NP-hard and scale poorly to large agent counts, while existing QUBO formulations for multi-agent routing are either edge-based (far exceeding NISQ qubit budgets) or lack a theoretically sound stopping criterion. No prior work has combined quantum computing with an optimal, path-based MAPF framework that is both hardware-aware and guaranteed to return the global optimum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a weighted undirected environment graph, per-agent origin-destination pairs with optional starting times, and a maximum time horizon T, which together define a spatio-temporal directed graph G_T with T|V| nodes. Paths are encoded as binary decision variables, and the QUBO is decomposed via the conflict graph into independent connected components, each solvable as a smaller subproblem. The output is a conflict-free set of paths (one per agent) minimizing total weighted path length, with the optimality gap bounded by the Lagrangian dual.

## Method

The paper introduces QP and QCP, two optimal hybrid quantum-classical algorithms that iteratively expand a Restricted Master Problem by adding paths (pricing via k-shortest-path with Yen's algorithm under Lagrangian-reduced costs) and, for QCP, violated conflict constraints (separation). The RMP is solved as a QUBO using a conflict-graph formulation where the adjacency matrix of path conflicts directly encodes inequality constraints, avoiding slack variables and enabling decomposition into independent subproblems matched to limited qubit connectivity. A generalized optimality criterion (Theorem 4.1) based on Lagrangian relaxation provides a provable stopping condition that does not require optimal dual parameters, extending classical column-generation theory to the binary setting. Experiments on a D-Wave Advantage quantum annealer and simulated annealing show that the conflict-graph QUBO outperforms slack-variable and half-penalty formulations in both solution quality and feasibility, and that QP-ILP matches or beats state-of-the-art solvers (BCP, EECBS, LNS2, LaCAM*) on MovingAI benchmarks.

## Results

On the MovingAI benchmark with 100 agents (averaged over 25 scenarios), QP-ILP achieves the best total path cost on 6 of 7 maps, e.g. 4558.9 on den312d vs. 5499.4 for LNS2 and 5492.3 for LaCAM*, and 6107.1 on room-64-64-8 vs. 6301.2 for LNS2. The QUBO-based variant (QP-QUBO) also outperforms both LNS2 and LaCAM* on den312d (4559.0 vs. 5499.4/5492.3) and random-32-32-10 (2231.3 vs. 2239.0/2236.8), though it degrades on maze-like maps (6332.5 on maze-32-32-4 vs. 4534.3 for LaCAM*). The optimality gap (upper bound on suboptimality) is consistently smaller for the ILP-solved RMP than for the QUBO-solved RMP, confirming that exact RMP solving recovers the global optimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four state-of-the-art MAPF solvers, QP-ILP (optimal RMP solving) is the best or near-best on 6 of 7 maps, decisively beating LNS2 and LaCAM* on den312d (~17% lower cost) and room-64-64-8 (~2–3% lower). The QUBO-based QP-QUBO variant also surpasses LNS2 and LaCAM* on den312d, random-32-32-10, and empty-32-32, demonstrating that approximate QUBO solving is already competitive without exact ILP. Both BCP and EECBS are consistently outperformed by all variants of the proposed method across all maps and agent counts, as neither finds all optimal solutions within the 180 s time limit. The sole exception is maze-32-32-4, where LaCAM* (4534.3) beats QP-ILP (5715.9) due to poor PPP initialization in narrow corridors. The PPP baseline (used for path initialization) is the weakest method overall, confirming the value of the iterative pricing loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Branch-and-Cut-and-Price (BCP) framework for MAPF (Lam et al., IJCAI 2019) by replacing the ILP master problem with QUBO subproblems solvable on quantum hardware, and by eliminating the exponential branching steps through a Lagrangian-relaxation-based pricing criterion. The QUBO formulation draws on the conflict-graph approach from quantum annealing for air traffic deconflicting (Stollenwerk et al.), adapted here to the path-based MAPF setting, while the optimality criterion generalizes the integer column-generation stopping condition of Ronnberg & Larsson by relaxing the negativity assumption on reduced costs. Unlike prior quantum MAPF attempts that encode every spatio-temporal edge as a qubit (infeasible for NISQ devices), this work uses an iteratively growing path set with hardware-aware decomposition into independent subproblems via conflict-graph connected components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first quantum-classical algorithms for MAPF, this work establishes a viable template for integrating quantum solvers into combinatorial optimization pipelines where the quantum component handles a well-scoped subproblem rather than the full problem. The hardware-aware conflict-graph decomposition is a practical design principle for NISQ-era quantum applications more broadly. However, experiments are limited to ≤100 agents on small-to-medium maps, and the quantum hardware results (D-Wave Advantage) show feasibility issues on some maps, leaving open whether the approach scales meaningfully as qubit counts and coherence times improve. The generalized optimality criterion (Theorem 4.1) is a useful theoretical contribution for column generation on binary programs, but its practical impact depends on future quantum hardware maturity.
