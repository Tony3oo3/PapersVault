## Problem

Thompson Sampling with Gaussian priors (TS-Gaussian) is inherently differentially private, but its O(√T)-GDP guarantee is loose because it samples a fresh Gaussian mean reward model for every arm in every round, injecting excessive noise. Existing private bandit algorithms either add calibrated noise to empirical estimates (sacrificing regret) or modify TS-Gaussian with pre-pulling and variance scaling (M-TS-Gaussian), which still yields O(√T)-GDP at best. The paper asks whether one can design a private bandit algorithm that more efficiently trades off privacy and regret by exploiting the structural connection between Thompson Sampling and UCB exploration mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a trade-off parameter α ∈ [0,1], learning horizon T, and K arms with [0,1]-supported reward distributions, and computes a sampling budget φ = c₀T^{0.5(1-α)}ln^{0.5(3-α)}(T) that governs how many Gaussian samples are drawn per epoch. Each round, the agent outputs a single arm pull decision based on either a fresh Gaussian sample or a reused maximum model value. The theoretical outputs are a Õ(T^{0.25(1-α)})-GDP privacy guarantee (translatable to (ε,δ)-DP via GDP duality) and problem-dependent regret of O(Σᵢ ln(T^{0.5(3-α)}Δᵢ²)ln^α(T)/Δᵢ), with worst-case regret O(√(KT)ln^{0.5(1+α)}(T)).

## Method

DP-TS-UCB exploits the observation that an arm's Gaussian posterior only changes when that arm is pulled, so it caps the number of Gaussian mean reward models drawn per epoch at a budget φ. During the mandatory TS-Gaussian phase (first φ rounds of each epoch), the agent samples fresh models from N(μ̂ᵢ,ₙᵢ, ln^α(T)/nᵢ); during the optional UCB phase (remaining rounds), it reuses the maximum of the previously sampled φ models, which Lemma 4.1 shows is with high probability above the true mean μᵢ, making it functionally equivalent to a UCB upper confidence bound. An arm-specific epoch structure (updating empirical means using the most recent 2ʳ observations) ensures each observation is used at most once, bounding cumulative privacy loss. The parameter α controls the trade-off: α=0 yields near-optimal regret with Õ(T^{0.25})-GDP, while α=1 yields constant O(1)-GDP at the cost of an extra ln(T) factor in regret. The privacy proof composes per-round GDP guarantees over at most φ rounds per epoch using the GDP composition theorem, and the regret proof decomposes into a TS-Gaussian term (using an improved analysis of Agrawal & Goyal) and a UCB term (using the anti-concentration bound from Lemma 4.1).

## Results

DP-TS-UCB achieves Õ(T^0.25)-GDP with α=0 (vs. O(√T)-GDP for TS-Gaussian) while maintaining near-optimal problem-dependent regret O(K ln(T^1.5 Δ²)/Δ + K ln ln(T)/Δ). With α=1, it achieves constant O(1)-GDP at the cost of an extra ln(T) factor in regret. The worst-case regret bound is O(√(KT) ln^0.5(1+α)(T)), which is near-optimal. Empirically, DP-TS-UCB outperforms M-TS-Gaussian on both regret and GDP under matched theoretical regret bounds (Fig. 4), and under matched privacy at α=1 (Fig. 5b), though M-TS-Gaussian slightly edges out at α=0 (Fig. 5a).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TS-Gaussian (Agrawal & Goyal, 2017), DP-TS-UCB improves the GDP guarantee from O(√T) to Õ(T^0.25) at α=0, a significant privacy gain, at the cost of a slightly looser regret bound (ln(T^1.5) vs. ln(T) in the log factor). Against M-TS-Gaussian (Ou et al., 2024), DP-TS-UCB achieves strictly better GDP (T^0.25 vs. √T/ln^α(T)) with comparable regret; empirically under the same theoretical regret bound (Fig. 4), DP-TS-UCB incurs lower regret and lower GDP across all α values tested. Under matched privacy at α=1, DP-TS-UCB clearly outperforms M-TS-Gaussian (Fig. 5b), while at α=0 M-TS-Gaussian slightly wins on empirical regret (Fig. 5a) but the gap is small. Against the (ε,0)-DP baselines DP-SE (Sajed & Sheffet, 2019), Anytime-Lazy-UCB (Hu et al., 2021), and Lazy-DP-TS (Hu & Hegde, 2022) at ε=0.5, DP-TS-UCB outperforms all three at both α=0 and α=1 (Fig. 7), confirming that the GDP-based approach with Gaussian noise yields better regret than Laplace-noise-based (ε,0)-DP methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the recent observation by Ou et al. (2024) that Thompson Sampling with Gaussian priors (TS-Gaussian) is inherently differentially private, but departs from it by introducing a budgeted sampling mechanism that caps the number of Gaussian draws per arm-specific epoch. The key conceptual bridge is showing that the maximum of φ Gaussian samples (reused when the budget is exhausted) behaves analogously to the upper confidence bound in UCB1, thereby connecting two classical exploration paradigms. The arm-specific epoch structure from Hu et al. (2021), Azize & Basu (2022), and Hu & Hegde (2022) is retained to control observation reuse, while the anti-concentration bounds for Gaussian distributions provide the technical tool that makes the UCB-like reuse provably sufficient for exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from O(√T) to O(T^0.25) GDP (at α=0) is a meaningful tightening of the privacy guarantee for private bandit algorithms, and the continuous trade-off parameter α allows practitioners to dial in the privacy-regret balance rather than choosing between discrete algorithm variants. The TS-UCB connection via max-of-Gaussians is of independent theoretical interest and may inform future algorithm design beyond the private setting. Key limitations include the loss of the anytime property and a slightly worse worst-case regret bound (O(√KT ln T) vs. O(√KT ln K)); the authors identify problem-dependent GDP guarantees and recovering the anytime property as natural next steps.
