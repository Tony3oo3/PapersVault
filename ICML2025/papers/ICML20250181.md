## Problem

ANN-SNN conversion methods produce SNNs whose spike timing is suboptimal, causing performance degradation especially at low latencies. This paper identifies a previously undocumented phenomenon—temporal misalignment—where randomly permuting spike trains across time steps after each layer consistently improves accuracy, revealing that the default chronological ordering of spikes is actually harmful. The gap is that no existing conversion method accounts for or exploits this temporal misalignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained ANN models (ResNet-20/34, VGG-16, RegNetX-4GF) with standard or modified ReLU activations and converts them to SNNs without retraining. Inputs are RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or asynchronous event streams (128×128 for CIFAR10-DVS), and outputs are class labels. A key operational detail is that each TPP layer consumes all T timesteps of input during its accumulation phase before any spiking begins, making the two-phase regime inherently sequential across layers.

## Method

The core contribution is the Two-Phase Probabilistic (TPP) spiking neuron, which replaces standard integrate-and-fire neurons in converted SNNs. In phase 1, the neuron accumulates the full weighted input from the previous layer (T·X^(l)) without emitting any spikes; in phase 2, it fires probabilistically via a Bernoulli process where the spike probability at step t is v[t-1]/(θ·(T-t+1)), so the probability increases over time if no spike has yet been emitted. Theorem 1 proves that the expected spiking rate exactly matches the clipped ReLU activation of the corresponding ANN layer without assuming any particular distribution of activation values, and that the neuron's output adapts to previously emitted spikes. The probabilistic firing mechanism intrinsically reproduces the beneficial spike-permutation effect observed empirically, eliminating the need for an explicit permutator layer. The method is applied by replacing ReLU layers in existing conversion pipelines (QCFS, RTS, SNNC) with TPP neurons, requiring no additional training.

## Results

On ImageNet with VGG-16, TPP+QCFS achieves 74.23% top-1 accuracy at T=32, surpassing the QCFS baseline (68.47%) by 5.76% and matching the ANN accuracy (74.29%) at T=64. On ResNet-34 ImageNet, TPP+QCFS reaches 72.03% at T=16, a 12.68% improvement over QCFS (59.35%) at the same latency. On CIFAR10-DVS, TPP+SNNC achieves 83.20% at T=64, outperforming the best direct-training method Spikformer (80.90%) and the ANN-SNN conversion method AdaFire (81.25%). Energy consumption is reduced by ~7.7× (7.98 mJ vs 61.67 mJ) while maintaining comparable accuracy (68.39% vs 68.47%) on VGG-16 ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPP consistently outperforms all ANN-SNN conversion baselines at matched or lower latency. On VGG-16 ImageNet, TPP+QCFS achieves 74.23% at T=32 versus QCFS's 68.47% (+5.76%) and FTBC's 74.05% (+0.18%), while TPP+SNNC reaches 74.72% at T=32 versus SNNC's 63.64% (+11.08%). On ResNet-34 ImageNet, the improvement is most dramatic: TPP+QCFS gains 12.68% over QCFS at T=16 (72.03% vs 59.35%). On CIFAR-100 VGG-16, TPP+QCFS matches or slightly exceeds FTBC at all latencies (e.g., 76.55% vs 76.48% at T=32), while TPP+SNNC surpasses SNNC by 3.61% at T=32 (77.16% vs 73.55%). Against direct-training methods (Table 2), TPP+QCFS on VGG-16 CIFAR-100 achieves 76.03% at T=8, outperforming Dual-Phase (75.06% at T=8) and LM-H (73.11% at T=4); on CIFAR10-DVS, TPP+SNNC reaches 83.20% at T=64, beating Spikformer (80.90%) and AdaFire (81.25%). The method also demonstrates superior energy efficiency: at T=4 on VGG-16 ImageNet, TPP consumes 7.98 mJ versus 61.67 mJ for QCFS at T=32 while maintaining near-identical accuracy (68.39% vs 68.47%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-SNN conversion framework (Rueckauer et al., 2017; Cao et al., 2015) by identifying a previously undocumented phenomenon—temporal misalignment—where the spike timing produced by standard conversion is suboptimal and can be improved by random permutation. It builds directly on three conversion baselines (QCFS, RTS, SNNC) by replacing their integrate-and-fire neurons with the proposed two-phase probabilistic (TPP) neurons, which intrinsically replicate the beneficial permutation effect through a Bernoulli spiking mechanism. The TPP design draws conceptual inspiration from bursting neurons in neuroscience and the dual-phase mechanism of Liu et al. (2022), but introduces probabilistic firing and provides a theoretical characterization (Theorem 1) showing that expected spike rates track ReLU outputs without distributional assumptions on activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of temporal misalignment reframes a long-standing conversion error as a temporal-structure problem rather than purely a rate-matching problem, offering a principled explanation for why low-latency SNNs underperform their ANN counterparts. Practically, TPP neurons enable near-ANN accuracy at 4–8 timesteps (vs. 32–128 for baselines) with comparable energy consumption, which is significant for neuromorphic deployment. Open questions include whether the two-phase regime's linear latency overhead across layers can be further reduced, whether the probabilistic mechanism interacts beneficially with directly trained SNNs (acknowledged as future work), and whether the phenomenon generalizes beyond ReLU-like activations to more complex nonlinearities.
