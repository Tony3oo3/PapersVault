## Problem

Standard prediction-powered inference (PPI) constructs confidence intervals for the rectifier Δ_θ using off-the-shelf procedures that ignore prior knowledge about prediction quality. In practice, ML predictors are typically accurate but occasionally produce large errors or hallucinations, a structure that standard PPI cannot exploit. This paper asks whether encoding such inductive bias via a prior on the rectifier can yield shorter confidence intervals while preserving frequentist validity and robustness to poor predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labelled pairs (X_i, Y_i), N ≫ n unlabelled inputs X̃_i, black-box predictions f(X), and a prior π_0(Δ_θ; τ_n) on the rectifier. The prior scale is set parameter-free as τ_n = σ̂_θ (the estimated standard deviation of the rectifier estimator). The output is a point estimator θ̂^FABPP and a (1−α) confidence region C_α^FABPP, the latter formed by the Minkowski sum of a FAB confidence region for Δ_θ and a standard CLT-based interval for m_θ. In the mean-estimation special case, the estimator decomposes as 'classical estimator + PPI correction + Bayes correction,' where the last term is σ̂²ℓ′(Δ̂; σ̂, τ_n) computed via Tweedie's formula.

## Method

FAB-PPI applies the Frequentist-Assisted-by-Bayes (FAB) framework to the rectifier Δ_θ within the PPI decomposition g_θ = m_θ + Δ_θ. A horseshoe prior π_HS(Δ_θ; σ̂_θ) — a scale mixture of normals with a half-Cauchy mixing distribution — is placed on Δ_θ, yielding a marginal likelihood expressible in closed form via Kummer's confluent hypergeometric function _1F_1. The Bayes-assisted estimator is Δ̂^FABPP = Δ̂ + σ̂²ℓ′(Δ̂; σ̂, τ_n) (Tweedie's formula), and the FAB confidence region for Δ_θ is constructed by solving a Neyman-Pearson optimal test problem whose spending function w_δ(β) allocates the rejection budget asymmetrically. The horseshoe is preferred over a Gaussian prior because its power-law tails (π(y) ~ C/|y|²) ensure the FAB region reverts to the standard CLT interval when |Δ̂_θ| is large (Proposition 4.2), while its infinite spike at zero shrinks the interval when predictions are good. Theorem 4.1 establishes exact asymptotic coverage for both priors, and Proposition 4.3 shows the horseshoe-based estimator is consistent while the Gaussian-based one is not.

## Results

The paper reports average MSE, CI volume, and coverage (α=0.1) over 1000 repetitions across synthetic and real-data experiments. In the noisy predictions study (σ_Y ∈ {0.1, 1, 2}, n from 100 to 1000, N=10⁶), FAB-PPI++ achieves the smallest CI volume at every noise level and sample size, with the advantage over PPI++ most pronounced at intermediate noise (σ_Y=1). On real datasets (ALPHAFOLD, GALAXIES, FOREST), FAB-PPI++ outperforms both classical inference and PPI++ in MSE and CI volume while maintaining coverage at the nominal 0.9 level. In the biased predictions study, FAB-PPI with the horseshoe prior yields CIs smaller than PPI++ when bias γ≈0, and reverts to PPI++-sized CIs as |γ| grows, whereas the Gaussian prior's CIs expand without bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAB-PPI is compared against standard PPI and power-tuned PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a,b) across all experiments. Against PPI, FAB-PPI consistently achieves smaller CI volume and lower MSE when predictions are of good quality (small bias or noise), with the gap widening as prediction quality improves; when predictions are heavily biased (e.g., GENES quantile estimation), the horseshoe-prior FAB-PPI reverts to matching PPI performance, while the Gaussian-prior variant degrades substantially. Against PPI++, FAB-PPI++ dominates on CI volume at all noise levels in the synthetic study, retaining the power-tuning benefit while adding adaptive shrinkage via the FAB correction term. On real data (ALPHAFOLD, GALAXIES, FOREST, HEALTHCARE), FAB-PPI++ beats PPI++ on both MSE and CI volume with comparable coverage. The key differentiator is robustness: the horseshoe prior ensures FAB-PPI never performs worse than PPI in the large-bias regime (Proposition 4.2), a property the Gaussian prior lacks, as confirmed by the CENSUS linear regression experiment where the Gaussian-prior FAB-PPI underperforms for all n while the horseshoe version eventually matches PPI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously independent lines of work: prediction-powered inference (PPI/PPI++), which corrects ML predictions using labelled data to obtain valid confidence intervals, and the FAB (Frequentist Assisted by Bayes) framework, which constructs Bayes-optimal confidence regions with guaranteed frequentist coverage. The key departure from standard PPI is replacing the off-the-shelf CLT-based confidence interval for the rectifier Δ_θ with a FAB confidence region informed by a prior on prediction quality. The paper also contrasts with Bayesian PPI (Hofer et al., 2024), which sacrifices frequentist guarantees for Bayesian credible intervals, and builds on Cortinovis & Caron (2024) to show that the horseshoe prior's power-law tails ensure robustness (reversion to standard PPI) under prior-data disagreement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAB-PPI provides a principled, parameter-free way to exploit prior knowledge that ML predictions are usually good but occasionally poor, yielding shorter confidence intervals without sacrificing coverage. The demonstration that the 'natural' Gaussian prior leads to an inconsistent estimator while the horseshoe prior is consistent is a useful cautionary result for practitioners tempted to use simpler priors. Limitations include the computational cost of grid-based optimization in high dimensions and the heuristic choice of prior scale τ_n = σ̂_θ; open questions include whether rescaled horseshoe priors or other power-law-tailed priors (e.g., beta-prime mixtures) can further improve performance, and whether the approach extends cleanly to high-dimensional parameter spaces.
