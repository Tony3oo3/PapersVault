## Problem

Large sequence models, including LLMs and state space models, struggle to extrapolate beyond the context lengths seen during training, yet no formal theoretical guarantees for length generalization existed. This paper introduces Asymmetric-Regret—a metric that measures the regret of a predictor using limited context L′ against a benchmark using full context L—and asks whether provable length generalization is achievable for structured sequence prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251738/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The predictor receives an input vector u_t ∈ R^{d_in} at each timestep and the previous one or two outputs y_{t-1} (and y_{t-2} for Algorithm 2), then produces a prediction ŷ_t ∈ R^{d_out}. Crucially, the predictor is restricted to a context window of length L = T^q (a sublinear fraction of the horizon T) during both learning and prediction, while the benchmark predictor is allowed the full T-length history. The output of the theoretical analysis is an O(√T) upper bound on the Asymmetric-Regret, quantifying how well the short-context predictor matches the full-context optimum.

## Method

The paper proves length generalization for spectral filtering predictors, which express predictions as ŷ_t = y_{t-1} + Σ M_i u_{(t-1):0} φ_i where the spectral filters φ_i are eigenvectors of a Hankel matrix built from impulse-response functions. The key proof technique shows that the minimizer M_T* of the full-context loss also achieves small loss under a restricted context window, by establishing strong convexity of the loss (via a well-conditioned input assumption) and bounding the tail contribution of the LDS dynamics. Theorem 5 proves O(√T) asymmetric regret for Algorithm 1 under a spectral gap condition on A's eigenvalues, identifying a 'bad region' where generalization fails. To remove this spectral restriction, Algorithm 2 adds a second autoregressive term (2y_{t-1} − y_{t-2}) and uses modified filters from a squared impulse-response matrix N_T, achieving robust O(√T) asymmetric regret for any marginally-stable LDS whenever the context is at least T^{1/4+ε}.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5) shows that vanilla spectral filtering with context length T^q achieves asymmetric regret Õ(√T) against the best full-context spectral filtering predictor, provided the eigenvalues of A avoid the 'bad' range [1−log(T)/(8T^q), 1−1/(2T^{5/4})]. The improved algorithm with two autoregressive components (Theorem 6) achieves the same Õ(√T) bound for any q ≥ 1/4 + log(log(T))/(8 log(T)) with no spectral assumptions on A. Experiments on synthetic LDS (hidden dimension 512, T=2^14, k=24) confirm that context lengths approaching T^{7/8} match full-context performance in Region A, while Algorithm 2 achieves robust generalization even with context as small as √T in the adversarial Region B. On the induction heads task, vanilla STU models trained with shorter context lengths retain nontrivial accuracy beyond their training length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, the 'benchmark' is the best spectral filtering predictor with full context length T, and the question is whether a predictor trained with shorter context T^q can match it. Algorithm 1 (vanilla spectral filtering) successfully length generalizes from T^{7/8} to T when eigenvalues lie in Region A (hugging the bad range), but qualitatively fails when eigenvalues fall in Region B, confirming the sharpness of the theoretical eigenvalue condition. Algorithm 2 (adding a second autoregressive term y_{t−2}) eliminates this failure mode entirely, achieving robust length generalization even with all eigenvalues in the adversarial Region B and context lengths as small as √T. The STU experiments on induction heads show that the spectral filtering mechanism provides length generalization 'for free' without architectural modifications, unlike methods such as Mamba's nonlinear selection mechanism which require task-specific engineering to extrapolate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral filtering framework of Hazan et al. (2017b), which was designed to learn linear dynamical systems with arbitrarily long memory via online gradient descent, by introducing a new performance metric (Asymmetric-Regret) that explicitly measures the cost of using a shorter context window than the benchmark. While prior length generalization work in NLP (ALiBi, Universal Transformer, Neural GPU) relies on architectural modifications and lacks theoretical guarantees, this paper shows that spectral filtering's existing structure already provides provable length generalization for linear dynamical systems without any algorithmic change. The paper also connects to the broader state space model lineage (S4, Mamba, STU) by demonstrating that the STU architecture inherits this length generalization property from its spectral filtering core, positioning the theoretical results as a foundation for understanding why these architectures may extrapolate beyond training context lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable length generalization guarantees in sequence prediction, introducing Asymmetric-Regret as a principled metric that captures the context-length asymmetry between learner and benchmark. The identification of a sharp 'bad region' of eigenvalues where generalization fails (and succeeds just outside it) gives a concrete characterization of when and why length generalization works, which could guide architecture design. Key open questions include whether these guarantees extend to nonlinear or multi-layer settings, whether the STU's natural length generalization can be leveraged at scale in practical language modeling, and whether the two-autoregressive-component variant (Algorithm 2) can be integrated into existing state space model architectures without sacrificing their efficiency advantages.
