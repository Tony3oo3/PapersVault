## Problem

One-shot LLM compression methods that combine quantization and sparsity suffer compounded accuracy degradation, and existing low-rank compensation approaches either require expensive retraining or handle only quantization without sparsity. No unified framework existed that simultaneously applies hardware-friendly uniform quantization, semi-structured (2:4) sparsity, and low-rank error correction in a single retraining-free pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251807/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices and a small calibration set (used to estimate input activation statistics and weight histograms). It produces 4-bit quantized weights with 2:4 or unstructured sparsity, plus a pair of low-rank adapter matrices (L ∈ R^{d_in×r}, R ∈ R^{r×d_out}) that can optionally be 4-bit group-quantized (group size 128) and fine-tuned via STE. The compressed model achieves ~8× memory reduction and up to 4.3× layer-wise speedup on consumer GPUs.

## Method

SLIM is a three-stage one-shot pipeline. First, SLIM-Quant reformulates the non-convex uniform quantization scaling-factor search as a probabilistic integral over the weight magnitude PDF, solved via a multigrid numerical integration strategy that avoids grid-search suboptimality. Second, Wanda pruning is applied to the quantized weights to introduce 2:4 or unstructured sparsity. Third, SLIM-LoRA computes low-rank adapters in closed form by defining a saliency function F(W) = diag(x)W (where x is the mean absolute calibration input) that is both additive and invertible; this allows the compression error saliency diag(x)(E_Q + E_S) to be decomposed via SVD, and the adapters are recovered by inverting the saliency transform—eliminating any iterative optimization. An optional PEFT phase fine-tunes only the adapters (with frozen sparse quantized weights) over ~300K tokens to further close the accuracy gap.

## Results

Under 2:4 sparsity with 4-bit weight quantization, SLIM-LoRA achieves 51.15% average zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA-2-7B and 54.94% on LLaMA-2-13B, outperforming the best prior method (SparseGPT+OPTQ at 45.49% and 51.05% respectively) by 5.66% and 3.89%. Under 50% unstructured sparsity, SLIM-LoRA reaches 54.26% and 57.85% on LLaMA-2-7B/13B, surpassing Wanda+Best Method (53.62%/56.76%) and JSQ (52.25%/57.00%). Compressed models achieve up to 4.3× and 3.8× layer-wise speedup on RTX 3060 and A100 GPUs, with 0.23× end-to-end memory reduction. An optional PEFT fine-tuning phase adds up to 1.66% further accuracy (LLaMA-2-13B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLIM-LoRA consistently outperforms all baselines across both 2:4 and unstructured sparsity settings. Against SparseGPT+OPTQ (the strongest joint pruning+quantization baseline), SLIM-LoRA gains 5.66 points on LLaMA-2-7B (51.15 vs 45.49) and 3.89 points on LLaMA-2-13B (54.94 vs 51.05) under 2:4 sparsity. Against Wanda+Best Method, SLIM-LoRA leads by 6.29 points on LLaMA-2-7B (51.15 vs 44.86) under 2:4 sparsity, though the gap narrows to 0.64 points under unstructured sparsity (54.26 vs 53.62). JSQ, which jointly optimizes sparsity and quantization, is outperformed by SLIM-LoRA by 6.35 points on LLaMA-2-7B (2:4) and 0.85 points on LLaMA-2-13B (unstructured), and fails entirely on OPT-13B (30.73%). L²QER, the closest concurrent work on one-shot low-rank adapters, is outperformed by 7.38 points on LLaMA-2-7B (2:4) and encounters OOM errors on LLaMA-2-13B and OPT-13B. MaskLLM (the SOTA 2:4 pruning method) achieves 49.7% on LLaMA-2-7B without quantization, while SLIM-LoRA+FT with SLIM-Quant reaches 52.1%, confirming SLIM's advantage even when combined with the best available pruning mask.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLIM sits at the intersection of three established compression threads: one-shot pruning (Wanda, SparseGPT), post-training quantization (OPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant), and low-rank error compensation (L²QER, QERA). Its key departure is the unified pipeline design: SLIM-Quant reformulates uniform quantization as a convex probabilistic optimization (avoiding grid search and group-quantization overhead), Wanda handles semi-structured sparsity on the quantized weights, and SLIM-LoRA generalizes L²QER's closed-form adapter computation from quantization-only to joint quantization-plus-sparsity error via a novel saliency function that is both invertible and additive. The layer-wise OBS framework (Hassibi et al., 1993) underpins the entire optimization structure, while the saliency-based SVD solution for adapters is the paper's most distinctive mathematical contribution relative to prior low-rank compensation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLIM's practical value lies in delivering hardware-friendly 2:4 sparsity with 4-bit uniform quantization and closed-form low-rank adapters in a single pass, achieving 4.3× layer-wise speedup on consumer GPUs and 0.23× memory reduction without retraining. The finding that compressed models can outperform dense models of equivalent parameter size (Figure 2) shifts the Pareto frontier for deployment-constrained settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to OPT and LLaMA-2 families, the marginal gain from the activation-aware quantization variant (SLIM-Quant^O over SLIM-Quant^W), and the continued need for optional fine-tuning to close the gap to dense accuracy. Open questions include whether the saliency-based SVD approach generalizes to non-transformer architectures and whether the uniform-quantization advantage persists at 3-bit or lower precision.
