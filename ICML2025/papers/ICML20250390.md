## Problem

Detection Transformer encoders are the dominant computational bottleneck, yet existing token sparsification methods apply static, uniform retention ratios across all pyramid levels and encoder blocks. This ignores the empirically observed dynamic migration of token importance from lower to higher feature levels as encoder depth increases, resulting in suboptimal sparsification and unnecessary performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250390/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-level feature maps (4 pyramid levels) from a CNN or ViT backbone, flattened into token sequences of varying spatial resolution per level, and produces standard object detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels). Sparsification is applied selectively at three stages within the 6-block encoder, reducing the number of tokens updated per block while preserving the full detection head interface, so no architectural changes are needed downstream.

## Method

Dynamic DETR introduces a dynamic token aggregation pipeline with three components. First, it derives per-level retention ratios at each encoder stage by reordering a base ratio vector according to empirically measured importance rankings, so lower levels retain more tokens early and higher levels retain more later. Second, it applies level-specific aggregation: low-level tokens are merged within spatially proximal n×n windows (hyperparameter-free, leveraging FPN resolution relationships), while high-level tokens use a bipartite affinity matching scheme that distributes unimportant tokens into their most similar important counterparts. Third, a representational center-distance regularization loss aligns the centroids of sparsified and non-sparsified encoder outputs, enforcing distributional consistency without auxiliary modules. The approach is plug-and-play across DETR variants (Deformable DETR, DINO, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, H-DETR) and reduces FLOPs by 39–47% with only ~1% AP loss.

## Results

On COCO 2017 val-set, Dynamic DETR applied to DINO reduces FLOPs by 42.0% (244.5G → 141.7G) with only a 0.7 AP drop (50.9 → 50.2), while boosting FPS by 61.1% (14.4 → 23.2). On Deformable DETR, it achieves a 39.7% FLOPs reduction (179.0G → 107.9G) with a 1.0 AP drop and 31.9% FPS gain. Across five DETR variants (D-DETR, DINO, DAB-D-DETR, DN-D-DETR, H-D-DETR), Dynamic DETR consistently reduces computational cost by 39.6%–47.4% while incurring at most ~1.0 AP degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On D-DETR, Dynamic DETR (46.0 AP, 107.9G) ties Sparse DETR (46.0 AP) but saves 13.1G FLOPs, and outperforms Lite DETR (45.8 AP) by 0.2 AP at nearly identical FLOPs (107.9G vs. 108.0G). On DINO, Dynamic DETR (50.2 AP, 141.7G) substantially outperforms Sparse DETR (48.2 AP, 152.0G) by 2.0 AP with 10.3G fewer FLOPs, while trailing Lite DETR (50.4 AP, 151.0G) and Focus DETR (50.4 AP, 154.0G) by only 0.2 AP but achieving 9.3–12.3G lower FLOPs. On DAB-D-DETR, DN-D-DETR, and H-D-DETR, Dynamic DETR is the only sparsification method evaluated, reducing FLOPs by 44.0%, 39.6%, and 47.4% respectively with ~1.0 AP loss. On LVIS v1.0, Dynamic DINO achieves the best AP_f (33.4) and lowest FLOPs (146.6G) among all variants, while on PASCAL VOC 2007 it attains the highest mAP (63.8%) with the fewest FLOPs (135.2G), outperforming Sparse DINO (62.5%) and Focus DINO (51.4%) by 1.3 and 12.4 mAP points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Deformable DETR's multi-scale deformable attention encoder and extends the token merging paradigm from ViT efficiency literature (ToMe, EViT, CTS) into the DETR setting for the first time. It departs from prior efficient DETR methods (Sparse DETR, PnP DETR, Focus DETR) that apply static, uniform token discarding across all pyramid levels, by introducing a dynamic, level-aware retention ratio that tracks how token importance migrates from lower to higher FPN levels as encoder depth increases. The multi-level aggregation design—proximal window-based merging for low-level tokens and holistic affinity matching for high-level tokens—adapts the merging strategy to the distinct spatial and semantic characteristics of each pyramid level, rather than treating all tokens uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The encoder remains the dominant computational bottleneck in DETR-based detectors, and this paper demonstrates that 40–47% FLOPs reduction is achievable with only ~1% AP loss across five DETR variants, making it a practical drop-in optimization for resource-constrained deployment. The center-distance regularization (zero additional compute, +0.6 AP) offers a lightweight mechanism for preserving distributional consistency during sparsification that could inform other token-pruning or merging settings. Open questions include whether the dynamic ratio schedule generalizes to non-FPN backbones or single-level architectures, whether the approach extends to other transformer-based tasks (segmentation, tracking), and whether the observed importance migration pattern holds under different training regimes or data distributions.
