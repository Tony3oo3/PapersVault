## Problem

Existing optimizers for domain generalization (SGD, Adam, SAM) exhibit imbalanced parameter updates where a small subset of parameters dominates optimization, inadvertently reinforcing spurious correlations rather than learning domain-invariant features. While gradient-guidance DG methods steer toward a dominant direction, that direction is often itself driven by spurious features, and prior parameter-wise dropout approaches suppress overconfident parameters without explicitly adjusting gradient alignment or ensuring balanced contributions to generalization. The paper asks how to design an optimizer that explicitly equalizes each parameter's contribution to the One-Step Generalization Ratio (OSGR) throughout training.

## Input / Output

GENIE is a drop-in optimizer replacement that consumes standard RGB images from DG benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet) through a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet and outputs class labels, with no changes to model architecture or input format. Its distinctive operation occurs at the parameter-update level: it computes per-parameter gradient statistics (moving averages of gradients and squared gradients) to derive a GSNR-based preconditioning factor, noise scale, and dropout mask for each parameter at every optimization step. The method is domain-agnostic and integrates with existing DG algorithms (RSC, CORAL) and SDG settings without requiring multiple source domains or architectural modifications.

## Method

GENIE (Generalization-ENhancing Iterative Equalizer) is a preconditioning-based optimizer that dynamically equalizes the One-Step Generalization Ratio (OSGR) across all parameters. It computes a per-parameter GSNR r_j from moving averages of gradients and their squares, then applies a preconditioning factor p_j = (1/E[g_j²])·(r_j + 1/n) that ensures each parameter contributes equally to the overall OSGR, preventing high-gradient-magnitude but low-signal parameters from dominating updates. Two auxiliary mechanisms complement the preconditioning: variance-scaled noise injection (scaled by 1−tanh(1/σ²)) to boost exploration for low-preconditioning parameters, and a Bernoulli random mask to stabilize updates and reduce overfitting. Theoretically, GENIE achieves a strictly higher OSGR than SGD and Adam (via Jensen's inequality on the uniform-weight OSGR expression) while retaining the O(1/√T) convergence rate of SGD in non-convex settings, and a PAC-Bayes analysis shows its preconditioning simultaneously minimizes both the empirical loss term and the KL-divergence term in the generalization bound.

## Results

On the five standard DG benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet) using ResNet-50 with ERM, GENIE achieves the best average accuracy of 66.9%, outperforming the next-best optimizer FAD (65.3%) by 1.6 points. The largest gains appear on TerraIncognita (52.0% vs. 45.7% for SAM/FAD) and VLCS (80.7% vs. 78.9% for FAD), where overfitting to domain-specific features is most severe. GENIE also trains ~1.3× faster than SAM while matching or exceeding its accuracy at every iteration count tested (5K, 10K, 15K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENIE achieves the highest average accuracy (66.9%) across all five DG benchmarks, beating the closest competitor FAD (65.3%) by 1.6 points and SAM (64.1%) by 2.8 points. The most dramatic win is on TerraIncognita, where GENIE scores 52.0% versus 45.7% for both SAM and FAD—a 6.3-point gap attributable to GENIE's balanced parameter updates preventing overfitting to location-specific features. On PACS, FAD edges out GENIE by 0.4 points (88.2 vs. 87.8), and on DomainNet FAD leads by 0.3 points (44.4 vs. 44.1), but GENIE dominates on VLCS (+1.8 over FAD) and OfficeHome (+0.5 over FAD). Compared to standard optimizers, GENIE improves over Adam by 3.6 points (66.9 vs. 63.3) and over SGD by 4.0 points (66.9 vs. 62.9). In the Single-Domain Generalization setting (Table 4), GENIE as a standalone optimizer reaches 58.5% average, surpassing SAM (52.6%) and Adam (51.2%), and when plugged into RSC or CORAL it lifts their SDG accuracy by 20.6 and 7.1 points respectively, whereas Adam and SGD actually degrade those methods' performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the One-Step Generalization Ratio (OSGR), originally introduced by Liu et al. (2020) as a diagnostic metric for understanding why neural networks generalize, into a core optimization principle. It extends Michalkiewicz et al. (2023), who used GSNR-based parameter dropout to suppress over-predictive parameters in DG, by replacing the binary masking with a continuous preconditioning factor that directly equalizes per-parameter OSGR. The PAC-Bayes analysis draws on the SVAG variance adaptation factor from Balles & Hennig (2018) to show that minimizing the KL divergence term naturally yields the same preconditioning structure, positioning GENIE as simultaneously addressing sharpness (like SAM) and generalization (via tighter PAC-Bayes bounds), rather than treating them as separate objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GENIE offers a drop-in optimizer that improves DG performance without modifying model architecture or training procedure, which is practically valuable given that most DG methods are sensitive to optimizer choice. The finding that SAM exhibits inferior OSGR despite its sharpness-aware design challenges the working assumption that flatness alone drives OOD generalization, suggesting that parameter-level balance is a necessary complement. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with ResNet-50, a convergence proof that involves a somewhat informal replacement of batch size n with total steps T, and an ablation showing that the noise injection and random mask components contribute only marginally over preconditioning alone. Open questions include whether the OSGR-equalization principle transfers to non-vision tasks, meta-learning, or few-shot settings as the authors speculate.
