## Problem

Off-policy MARL methods that rely on joint Q-functions suffer from a critical yet overlooked issue: Target Estimation Error (TEE) in the TD target, driven primarily by extrapolation error over the exponentially growing joint action space. Prior work has focused on improving representational capacity (reducing Target Approximation Error), but the paper shows that TEE does not decrease over training and is the dominant bottleneck, especially as the number of agents increases. Additionally, the paper identifies that error propagation in value factorization requires a monotonicity condition (Error Propagation Consistency) for corrections to the joint Q-function to effectively reach individual utilities.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes individual partial observations and action-observation histories per agent, plus the global state for the mixing hypernetwork (CTDE paradigm). The TD target is computed from an ensemble of M independently parameterized Q-functions whose outputs are averaged before being used as the regression target, rather than taking a max or min. For Restricted Action Representation, the joint action vector is mapped through a learned categorical network (e.g., reducing 5^4 joint actions to 2^4 categorical outputs) before being fed into the target Q-function, or in QPLEX the λ_i weight is bounded via Sigmoid to [0,1].

## Method

The paper proposes three complementary techniques to mitigate TEE in off-policy MARL. First, annealed multi-step bootstrapping replaces the one-step TD target with Peng's Q(λ) multi-step returns, where λ is annealed from 1 down to a small λ* over training to exploit early error reduction while avoiding the policy bias that a fixed large λ induces. Second, averaged TD targets use an ensemble of M independently initialized Q-functions (or individual utilities in factorized methods) whose values are averaged within the target, reducing variance by a factor of 1/M without requiring conservative/pessimistic corrections. Third, Restricted Action Representation (RAR) reduces the effective joint action space in the target: for centralized critics (MADDPG) it maps joint actions to a smaller categorical code via a straight-through-trained network, and for QPLEX it clamps the action-conditioned λ_i weight with a Sigmoid to prevent outlier accumulation. All three techniques are plug-and-play and require monotonic factorization (EPC) to ensure joint-level error corrections propagate to individual agent policies.

## Results

The paper presents results as learning curves (figures) rather than a consolidated numerical table. On SMACv2 (15 maps), AEQMIX achieves consistently higher mean test win rates than QMIX across all scenarios, with the most dramatic gains on the challenging 10_vs_11 and 20_vs_23 maps where QMIX's win rate is notably low. On SMAC, AEFACMAC and AEMADDPG-RAR significantly outperform FACMAC and MADDPG respectively, with the largest improvements on the two Super Hard maps; notably, AEMADDPG-RAR enables MADDPG to learn on Super Hard maps where it typically fails entirely. On Google Research Football, AEQMIX also improves over QMIX. QPLEX-RAR stabilizes QPLEX, preventing a significant performance drop around 5M training steps caused by growing λ_i outliers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QMIX (Rashid et al., 2020b), AEQMIX wins on all 15 SMACv2 maps, with the largest relative gains on high-agent-count tasks (10_vs_11, 20_vs_23) where QMIX struggles most; the same pattern holds on SMAC and GRF. Against FACMAC (Peng et al., 2021), AEFACMAC achieves substantially higher win rates on all four SMAC maps, with the gap widening on Super Hard maps due to the larger joint action space. Against MADDPG (Lowe et al., 2017), AEMADDPG-RAR transforms a method that typically cannot learn on Super Hard maps into one that achieves competitive performance, representing the largest absolute improvement among all comparisons. Against VDN (Sunehag et al., 2018), AEVDN outperforms VDN on SMACv2, confirming that the proposed techniques generalize beyond QMIX-style monotonic mixing. Against QPLEX (Wang et al., 2021b), QPLEX-RAR does not necessarily achieve higher peak performance but critically stabilizes training by bounding λ_i via Sigmoid, preventing the catastrophic performance collapse that QPLEX exhibits around 5M steps due to error accumulation in rarely visited state-action pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the value factorization literature (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN) by arguing that prior work has overemphasized Target Approximation Error (TAE) — improving the representational capacity of the factorized Q-function — while neglecting Target Estimation Error (TEE) arising from extrapolation over the exponentially growing joint action space. The proposed techniques are adaptations of well-known single-agent RL tools: Peng's Q(λ) for multi-step bootstrapping, ensemble averaging for variance reduction, and action-space restriction for limiting extrapolation. The key conceptual departure is the Error Propagation Consistency (EPC) principle, which formally links monotonicity (already required for IGM) to the ability of the factorization to self-correct erroneous targets, providing a new theoretical justification for why monotonic methods outperform non-monotonic ones in complex settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that TEE, not TAE, is the primary bottleneck in off-policy MARL challenges the field's prevailing assumption that more expressive factorization yields better performance — a finding supported by the counterintuitive result that larger networks actually degrade performance due to increased extrapolation. The techniques are broadly applicable across value-based and policy-based methods (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN, MADDPG, FACMAC) and require minimal architectural changes, making them practical drop-in improvements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous action spaces, competitive or mixed-motive settings, and whether the heuristic λ annealing schedule can be made adaptive or theoretically optimal. The EPC framework also opens the door to designing new factorization structures that explicitly optimize for error propagation rather than just expressiveness.
