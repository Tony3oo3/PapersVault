## Problem

Jailbreak attacks on safety-tuned LLMs are evaluated under heterogeneous metrics (fluency, query efficiency, runtime, length), preventing fair cross-method comparison. LLM-based perplexity, the de facto fluency constraint, is incomparable across models, non-interpretable, susceptible to adversarial examples, and computationally expensive. The paper asks whether a principled, LLM-agnostic, and interpretable threat model can unify the evaluation of jailbreak attacks and reveal which methods genuinely succeed under realistic fluency constraints.

## Input / Output

The threat model takes a malicious query and a target LLM, and produces a jailbreak prompt whose bigram perplexity in a rolling window of 8 tokens (tokenized with the Llama2 tokenizer) must fall below a threshold of 38,276 (corresponding to 99.9% true positive rate on natural text). The N-gram LM is a lightweight bigram model built on ~1T tokens from the Dolma corpus, requiring no GPU RAM for inference. Attack success is measured by a fine-tuned Llama2-13B judge model, and computational cost is reported as total FLOPs (hardware-agnostic), capturing all forward/backward passes including auxiliary models used by attacks like PAIR and AutoDan.

## Method

The paper constructs a bigram language model on a 1T-token subset of Dolma (MegaWika, Project Gutenberg, StackExchange, arXiv, Reddit, StarCoder, Refined Web) with Laplacian smoothing, and uses its perplexity over a rolling window of 8 tokens as a binary fluency filter. Five representative attacks—GCG, PRS, PAIR, AutoDan, and BEAST—are each adapted to this constraint by modifying their candidate acceptance criteria: GCG restricts top-k token substitutions to those passing the filter, PRS restricts sampling to the 100k most-likely bigrams and requires both loss decrease and filter passage, PAIR/AutoDan/BEAST accept only filter-passing candidates during their respective generation steps. The key insight is that N-gram perplexity is inherently interpretable (each bigram's contribution equals its rarity in training data), enabling train-dataset attribution analysis that reveals, for example, that adaptive GCG on Llama2-7b oversamples Reddit bigrams while adaptive PRS stays closer to the Dolma distribution. Under this unified threat model, discrete-optimization attacks (PRS, GCG) significantly outperform LLM-based attacks (AutoDan, PAIR, BEAST), and the filter reduces ASR of non-adaptive GCG/PRS to near zero while adaptive versions retain meaningful success rates.

## Results

Under the proposed N-gram perplexity threat model (bigram LM on 1T Dolma tokens, threshold γ=38,276 for 99.9% TPR), adaptive PRS achieves the highest average ASR of 0.82 across 10 safety-tuned models, substantially outperforming adaptive GCG (0.24), AutoDan (0.19), PAIR (0.13), and BEAST (0.10). The N-gram perplexity filter reduces non-adaptive PRS ASR from 0.89 to 0.00 and GCG from 0.28 to 0.09, effectively eliminating the strongest unconstrained attacks. Against LLM-based self-perplexity filters, adaptive PRS achieves the same ASR (0.62) but with 4.5× lower median FLOPs (5.17×10¹⁶ vs. 2.34×10¹⁷).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adaptive PRS (0.82 avg ASR) dominates all other adaptive attacks in the threat model, outperforming adaptive GCG (0.24) by 3.4×, adaptive AutoDan (0.19) by 4.3×, adaptive PAIR (0.13) by 6.3×, and adaptive BEAST (0.10) by 8.2×. The N-gram perplexity filter is highly effective against non-adaptive discrete-optimization attacks: PRS drops from 0.89 to 0.00 and GCG from 0.28 to 0.09, while LLM-based attacks (AutoDan, PAIR, BEAST) are less affected since they already produce lower-perplexity outputs by design. Compared to LLM-based self-perplexity filters (Jain et al. 2023; Alon & Kamfonas 2023), the N-gram PPL filter achieves identical ASR (0.62) for adaptive PRS on Llama2-7b but requires only 5.17×10¹⁶ median FLOPs versus 2.34×10¹⁷ for self-perplexity — a 4.5× reduction in attacker compute. The paper argues that self-perplexity creates a false sense of robustness through security-by-obscurity, as the high computational cost of adaptive attacks masks the underlying vulnerability. Among target models, Llama2-7b is the most robust (adaptive PRS ASR 0.63), while Llama3.1-8b is the least robust (0.96), and Gemma-7b shows a distinctive pattern of rejecting outputs immediately after the target token.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established jailbreaking attack landscape (GCG, PRS, AutoDan, BEAST, PAIR) and the prior perplexity-based defense literature (Alon & Kamfonas 2023; Jain et al. 2023), but departs from both by replacing LLM-based self-perplexity with a nonparametric N-gram LM perplexity as the fluency constraint. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to the ℓ_p-ball threat models in computer vision, arguing that the N-gram perplexity threshold serves as a language-domain equivalent: a simple, model-agnostic, interpretable boundary that separates natural from adversarial text. The paper also builds on HarmBench as its evaluation harness and on the Dolma corpus for constructing the N-gram LM, while introducing adaptive variants of each attack that respect the perplexity constraint for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a unified, LLM-agnostic threat model with inherent interpretability, this work enables fair cross-model and cross-attack comparisons that were previously impossible due to heterogeneous evaluation metrics. The training dataset attribution analysis reveals *which* data domains (Reddit, code, etc.) effective attacks exploit, offering actionable insights for defenders. Key limitations include the English-centric N-gram filter (high rejection rates for morphologically complex languages) and the reliance on a single tokenizer (Llama2). Open questions include whether the community will converge on this threat model, whether tighter perplexity constraints can be made practical without unacceptable utility loss, and whether the observed transfer behavior to 405B models reflects genuine robustness or spurious feature exploitation.
