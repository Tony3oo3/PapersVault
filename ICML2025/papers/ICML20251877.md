## Problem

Deep active learning under class imbalance has been studied, but no prior work addresses the co-occurrence of imbalance and label noise, which is the most prevalent real-world annotation scenario. Existing imbalance-aware methods like GALAXY rely on bisection-based cut-finding that is fragile under label noise and does not support parallel annotation, while agnostic active learning theory handles noise but only for low-complexity hypothesis classes, making it inapplicable to deep networks.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an unlabeled pool of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN, variable resolution for FMoW/iWildcam/ImageNet-LT), a pretrained neural network (ResNet-18 or CLIP ViT-B32), and two budget parameters: B_train (examples collected before retraining) and B_parallel (number of parallel annotators). Each round produces a batch of selected examples for annotation split across K classes, with half the budget spent identifying optimal separation thresholds and half annotating around them. The final output is a trained classifier, with the key intermediate artifact being per-class one-dimensional sorted lists by margin score.

## Method

DIRECT reduces the K-class imbalanced active learning problem to K independent one-dimensional agnostic active learning problems by sorting examples per class by one-vs-rest margin scores and defining the optimal separation threshold as the index maximizing the class-count discrepancy on either side. The VReduce subroutine then identifies this threshold via a version-space interval [I, J] that is iteratively shrunk by sampling unlabeled examples uniformly within the interval and updating based on empirical loss estimates, inheriting robustness guarantees from the ACED disagreement-based framework. This replaces GALAXY's bisection-based all-cuts approach with a single-threshold identification that is exponentially robust to label noise and naturally supports parallel annotation through the B_parallel parameter. The second phase annotates equal numbers of examples on both sides of the estimated threshold per class, ensuring class-balanced and uncertain selections. Theoretical analysis shows GALAXY identifies at least one spurious additional cut with probability 1−exp(−b·log(1/(1−η))/2) under noise ratio η, while DIRECT's misidentification probability decays exponentially in budget.

## Results

DIRECT achieves state-of-the-art label efficiency across 12 imbalanced dataset settings, reducing annotation costs by over 60% compared to the best existing algorithm (GALAXY) and over 80% compared to random sampling, while maintaining balanced accuracy. Under 20% label noise on imbalanced CIFAR-100, existing baselines degrade below random sampling, whereas DIRECT still saves more than 60% annotation cost. The method is robust to batch size (B_parallel = 1, 5, 20) and consistently outperforms baselines on both ResNet-18 supervised fine-tuning and CLIP ViT-B32 semi-supervised fine-tuning under the LabelBench framework. On ImageNet-LT, DIRECT's gains are concentrated on rare classes (indices 301–1000), confirming its class-balanced annotation strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRECT outperforms GALAXY (Zhang et al., 2022) in nearly all settings, with the sole exception being the noiseless imbalanced CIFAR-100 (two classes) experiment where GALAXY slightly edges out in generalization accuracy; however, under 10%, 15%, and 20% label noise, DIRECT becomes substantially more label-efficient than GALAXY. Compared to SIMILAR (Kothawade et al., 2021) and Most Likely Positive (Jiang et al., 2018), which annotate a larger fraction of minority-class examples, DIRECT collects slightly fewer minority examples but achieves higher balanced accuracy, revealing that over-optimizing for class balance at the expense of informativeness hurts generalization. Against uncertainty/diversity-based methods (BADGE, BAIT, Cluster Margin, Confidence Sampling, CORESET, Margin Sampling), DIRECT consistently requires fewer annotations to reach the same balanced accuracy across all 12 dataset configurations. Under 20% label noise, all baselines including GALAXY underperform random sampling, while DIRECT maintains a >60% annotation cost advantage over random. DIRECT also matches or exceeds GALAXY's performance even when forced into synchronous labeling (B_parallel = 1), and its computational cost per batch is O(KN log N + B_train·N), lower than both BADGE (O(B_train·N·(K+D))) and GALAXY (O(KN log N + B_train·K·N)).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIRECT extends GALAXY's (Zhang et al., 2022) one-dimensional reduction of imbalanced active learning by replacing its bisection-based search for all cuts with an agnostic active learning procedure (version space reduction) that identifies only the single optimal separation threshold. This design choice draws directly from the theoretical agnostic active learning literature (Balcan et al., 2006; Katz-Samuels et al., 2021), which provides robustness guarantees under label noise that bisection-based methods lack. The paper thus bridges two previously disconnected threads: practical deep active learning under imbalance and the theoretical disagreement-based learning framework, repurposing the latter's version-space machinery for a concrete deep learning annotation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep active learning study to jointly address class imbalance and label noise, a combination the authors argue is the most prevalent in real-world annotation jobs. The practical impact is substantial: over 60% annotation cost reduction versus the prior SOTA (GALAXY) and over 80% versus random sampling, while also enabling parallel annotation that GALAXY's sequential bisection forbids. Open questions include extending the approach to asynchronous labeling, batching across classes for even larger parallel annotator pools, and understanding how the 1D margin-score ordering degrades when the underlying neural network is very poorly trained early in the active learning loop.
