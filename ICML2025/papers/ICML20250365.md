## Problem

Training physics-informed neural networks on singularly perturbed PDEs (e.g., Allen-Cahn, Burgers, Helmholtz) becomes intractable as a small parameter ε drives the solution toward sharp interfaces, shocks, or high-frequency oscillations, causing the loss landscape to develop near-singular structures that stall gradient-based optimization. While curriculum-style parameter scheduling has been proposed heuristically, no prior work provides a theoretical explanation for why small ε degrades training or a principled, convergence-guaranteed strategy for choosing the parameter path and step sizes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a PDE residual operator L_ε, boundary/initial conditions, a target parameter ε*, and a domain Ω ⊂ R^d, with collocation points sampled in the interior and on the boundary. For solution approximation it outputs a trained MLP u(x; θ); for operator learning (Burgers) it outputs a DeepONet mapping initial conditions u₀(x) to steady-state solutions u_∞(x) in an unsupervised setting. A key practical detail is that the homotopy path requires choosing a starting ε₀ (large, easy) and a sequence of intermediate ε values, with the step size Δε_k constrained by the Lipschitz constant of the homotopy vector field to ensure stability.

## Method

The core idea is to replace direct optimization at the target ε* with a two-phase procedure: (Phase I) train the network on the PDE at a large ε₀ where the loss landscape is well-conditioned, then (Phase II) track the solution along a continuous homotopy path by integrating the Davidenko ODE dθ/dε = −(H_u ∇_θ u)† H_ε from ε₀ down to ε*. Two implementation strategies are provided: a Forward-Euler update on the parameter ODE (suitable for small networks where the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse is tractable) and a Homotopy Loss L_Hom = L_H + λL_bc + αL_{H_ε} that embeds the path-tracking residual into a standard optimization loop (suitable for large networks). Theoretically, an NTK-based analysis shows that the smallest eigenvalue of the gradient-descent kernel K_ε = D_ε SS^T D_ε^T shrinks as ε decreases, provably slowing convergence, while a separate theorem establishes that the homotopy dynamics error remains bounded provided the step size satisfies (ε_k − ε_{k+1})P_{ε_k} ≤ P(ε₀−ε_n)/n, giving a principled rule for choosing Δε.

## Results

On the 1D Allen-Cahn equation, homotopy dynamics reduces L2RE from 8.17e-1 (classical PINN) to 8.08e-3 at ε=0.01, a ~100× improvement. On the 2D Allen-Cahn equation, the method achieves L2RE of 8.78e-3, outperforming the best baseline (Time Seq. at 8.95e-2) by over 10×. For the 20-dimensional Helmholtz equation at ε=1/50, homotopy dynamics yields L2RE of 5.89e-4 versus 9.98e-1 for classical training. In the Burgers operator learning task, the method achieves L2RE of 2.52e-2 at ε=0.05 with 0.2s inference time versus 2416s for the finite difference method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares homotopy dynamics against four established strategies on the 2D Allen-Cahn equation (ε=0.05). Homotopy dynamics (L2RE 8.78e-3) decisively outperforms all baselines: Original PINN (9.56e-1), Curriculum regularization from Krishnapriyan et al. 2021 (8.89e-1), Time-Sequential training from Wight & Zhao 2020 / Mattey & Ghosh 2022 (8.95e-2), and Resampling (8.25e-1). The closest competitor, Time Seq., is still over 10× worse in L2RE. On the 1D Allen-Cahn equation (Table 1), homotopy dynamics matches classical training at ε=0.1 (both 1.71e-2) but achieves 8.08e-3 vs 8.17e-1 at ε=0.01. For the 20D Helmholtz equation (Table 4), homotopy dynamics maintains L2RE below 6e-4 across all ε values while classical training degrades to 9.98e-1 at ε=1/50. In the Burgers operator learning setting, DeepONet trained via homotopy dynamics achieves comparable accuracy to the finite difference method (L2RE 2.52e-2 vs 1.01e-2 at ε=0.05) but with 12,000× faster inference (0.2s vs 2416s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the homotopy training lineage (Chen & Hao, 2019; Yang et al., 2025) from general neural network training to the specific challenge of singularly perturbed PDEs, where a small parameter ε creates near-singular loss landscapes. It departs from the Curriculum PINN Regularization of Krishnapriyan et al. (2021), which heuristically observed that PDE parameters affect PINN performance, by providing the first NTK-based theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) linking ε to training difficulty and establishing convergence guarantees for the homotopy path (Theorem 4.3). The method is built on the standard PINN loss formulation (Raissi et al., 2019) but replaces direct optimization with a Davidenko-equation-driven parameter sweep, and generalizes to operator learning via DeepONet (Lu et al., 2021a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-known practical bottleneck in scientific ML—PINNs failing on singularly perturbed problems—and provides both a theoretically grounded fix and a principled step-size selection strategy. The NTK eigenvalue analysis (showing λ_min(K_ε) collapses as ε→0) gives practitioners a diagnostic tool for anticipating training difficulty. Open questions include whether the approach extends to problems with multiple interacting singular parameters, whether the homotopy path can be automated without user-specified ε schedules, and how it scales to genuinely high-dimensional (d>50) or fully nonlinear time-dependent problems. The method's reliance on knowing which parameter to track and on a sufficiently small initial solution limits its plug-and-play applicability.
