## Problem

LVLMs frequently produce correct answers supported by counterfactual, insufficient, or logically inconsistent rationales, yet existing post-training alignment methods (e.g., DPO-based vision-text alignment) focus on image captioning correctness and overlook the rationale-answer relationship in VQA. The paper asks whether a correct answer actually stems from a reasonable, factually grounded rationale, and whether this alignment can be iteratively improved without external supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252706/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text question in standard VQA format. After rationale fine-tuning, the model autonomously produces a structured response in the format 'Thinking: (rationale) + Output: (answer)' without any explicit prompting. During the calibration stage, sentence-level beam search (beam width 5 at the first layer, 2 per subsequent layer, max 100 tokens per sentence) generates up to N=6 diverse rationale-answer pair (RAP) candidates per sample, each carrying a cumulative log-probability confidence score from the language decoder.

## Method

SRC is a four-stage iterative framework: (1) lightweight LoRA rationale fine-tuning (rank 4–32) on ~20K rationale-augmented VQA samples to induce the model to output rationales before answers without prompting; (2) pairwise candidate scoring where a tailored 1.5B LLM (R-Scorer, fine-tuned on 21K GPT-4o-scored pairs) performs bidirectional pairwise comparisons over beam-searched candidates, incorporating factual checks on visual elements; (3) confidence-weighted preference curation that rank-normalizes both the pairwise winning score and the model's beam-search confidence, then combines them via a weighting factor α to select preferred and non-preferred candidates; (4) iterative DPO-based preference fine-tuning (LoRA rank 256, β=0.1, with an RPO-style SFT loss at weight 0.02) over 3 iterations. The key insight is that the model's own output space contains a spectrum of RAPs ranging from optimal to inferior, and pairwise relative scoring (rather than absolute scoring) better captures subtle rationale quality differences, enabling self-calibration without external reward models.

## Results

On MMStar, SRC improves LLaVA-1.5-7B from 32.1 to 38.9 overall accuracy and LLaVA-Next-8B from 42.6 to 47.6, outperforming all six DPO-based baselines by a substantial margin (best baseline RLAIF-V reaches only 33.7 on LLaVA-1.5). Gains are particularly pronounced in fine-grained perception (25.2→33.2 on LLaVA-1.5), logical reasoning (24.4→32.8), and math (24.0→33.2). On specific-domain VQA, SRC also lifts ChartQA (17.8→24.4) and HallusionBench (24.7→27.3) for LLaVA-1.5, and ChartQA (68.7→71.4) and HallusionBench (32.1→37.5) for LLaVA-Next.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRC outperforms all six DPO-based post-training baselines on LLaVA-1.5-7B across nearly every MMStar sub-metric. The largest gap is in fine-grained perception, where SRC reaches 33.2 versus 29.6 (RLAIF-V) and 28.4 (SeVA), and in logical reasoning at 32.8 versus 28.0 (HA-DPO) and 24.8 (SeVA). The only metric where SRC trails a baseline is LLaVA-Bench (65.5 vs. 66.3 for SeVA), which the authors attribute to SRC's altered response format affecting the LLM-as-judge evaluation protocol. On LLaVA-Next-8B, SRC delivers a +5.0 overall gain (42.6→47.6) with the strongest improvements in math (+10.0) and S&T (+6.8), though instance reasoning slightly drops (54.8→52.4). Among the baselines, RLAIF-V and SeVA are the closest competitors on LLaVA-1.5 (33.7 and 33.3 overall), but both remain 5+ points below SRC, confirming that rationale-oriented alignment provides substantially larger gains than vision-text alignment alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRC extends the DPO-based preference alignment paradigm for LVLMs (POVID, HA-DPO, SeVA, CSR, RLAIF-V) by shifting the alignment target from vision-text modality consistency (i.e., ensuring image descriptions are factually correct) to rationale-answer consistency (i.e., ensuring the reasoning chain is logically grounded and supports the final answer). It also draws on the lightweight LoRA format-adaptation insight from Ghosh et al. (2024) and the LLM-as-judge scoring framework from Zheng et al. (2023), but departs from large-scale CoT distillation approaches (Mammoth-VL, LLaVA-O1) by using a small, iterative self-calibration loop rather than distilling from expert models on 100K–12M samples. The iterative self-improvement structure is conceptually related to self-rewarding and self-training methods (CSR, SIMA), but the pairwise rationale scoring with confidence-weighted curation is a distinct mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: LVLMs frequently produce correct answers supported by counterfactual or insufficient rationales, which undermines trust and downstream reasoning. The finding that a 1.5B R-Scorer can match or exceed 70B LLMs for pairwise rationale scoring is practically significant for deployment cost. Limitations include evaluation on only two model families (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-Next), reliance on a single primary benchmark (MMStar), and the open question of whether the iterative loop converges beyond 3 iterations or generalizes to non-VQA tasks like open-ended captioning. The approach also assumes access to ground-truth answers during candidate generation, which limits applicability to fully open-ended settings.
