## Problem

SAM-based federated learning algorithms (e.g., FedSAM) find local flat minima on individual clients but fail to produce a globally flat minimum, causing them to perform worse than plain FedAvg under highly heterogeneous (non-IID) data. Existing FL generalization analyses also do not adequately capture how data heterogeneity amplifies the generalization gap, leaving a theoretical and practical gap in understanding and mitigating this degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251554/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for Tiny ImageNet) partitioned across 100 clients with non-IID splits generated via Dirichlet distributions (α = 0.1, 0.3, 0.6). Each client holds n samples from its local distribution D_i, and the server aggregates updates from a 10% client subset per round. The output is a standard class-label prediction from the global model, evaluated on held-out test sets from the same datasets.

## Method

FedSWA replaces SAM's perturbation-based local optimization with Stochastic Weight Averaging: the local learning rate decays linearly from η_l to ρη_l over K iterations (cyclical schedule), and the server aggregates client weights via an exponential moving average (LookAhead-inspired) rather than simple averaging, enabling the global model to settle in a flatter, lower-loss region. FedMoSWA further introduces a momentum-based stochastic controlled update where each client maintains a control variable c_i and the server maintains a momentum-updated global control variable m (m ← m + γ/s Σ(c_i⁺ − m)); the local gradient step becomes θ_{i,k+1} = θ_{i,k} − η_k(g_i(θ_{i,k}) − c_i + m), which corrects client drift and aligns local updates with the global direction. Theoretically, a uniform-stability analysis shows FedMoSWA's generalization bound contains σ_g (data heterogeneity) without the amplification factor c̃ that multiplies σ_g in FedSWA and FedSAM, yielding a strictly tighter bound. Empirically, FedMoSWA achieves up to 13.7% accuracy improvement over FedAvg on CIFAR-100 under Dirichlet-0.6 and substantially outperforms FedSAM, MoFedSAM, SCAFFOLD, and FedACG across all heterogeneity levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 under high data heterogeneity (Dirichlet-0.1), FedMoSWA achieves 61.9% test accuracy, outperforming the next-best baseline FedACG (52.2%) by 9.7% and FedAvg (45.8%) by 16.1%. Notably, FedSAM degrades to 40.1% under the same setting—worse than FedAvg—confirming the paper's central claim that SAM-based methods fail under high heterogeneity. On Tiny ImageNet with ViT-Base (Dirichlet-0.1), FedMoSWA reaches 73.8% accuracy versus 71.6% for SCAFFOLD and 70.9% for FedAvg. Under moderate heterogeneity (Dirichlet-0.6) on CIFAR-100, FedMoSWA achieves 67.9%, surpassing MoFedSAM (60.1%) and FedACG (61.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMoSWA dominates all nine baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Under the most challenging setting (CIFAR-100, Dirichlet-0.1), it beats FedACG (52.2%) by 9.7%, MoFedSAM (51.5%) by 10.4%, and SCAFFOLD (44.3%) by 17.6%. The paper's key negative finding is that FedSAM (40.1%) and FedASAM (47.7%) both underperform FedAvg (45.8%) under high heterogeneity, validating the motivation for replacing SAM with SWA. FedSWA alone (50.3%) already surpasses FedAvg and all SAM variants, confirming that stochastic weight averaging is more robust to non-IID data than sharpness-aware minimization. On Tiny ImageNet with ViT-Base, the margins are smaller (FedMoSWA 73.8% vs. SCAFFOLD 71.6%, +2.2%) but consistent across all heterogeneity levels. Ablation (Table 6) shows that the momentum-based stochastic control contributes +11.7% over FedAvg while SWA contributes +5.6%, with the combined FedMoSWA reaching +13.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the SAM-based FL line (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedLESAM, FedASAM), arguing that these methods find local flat minima that fail to generalize under high data heterogeneity, and instead repurposes Stochastic Weight Averaging (Izmailov et al., 2018) as the global-minima-finding mechanism. The FedMoSWA component draws on momentum-based variance reduction (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2020) for the server control variable, deliberately differentiating from SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020) by using a momentum-weighted update that gives higher weight to recent client uploads. The overall framework is a modification of FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), replacing its constant learning rate with a cyclical decay schedule and its simple averaging with exponential moving average aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: FL generalization under high data heterogeneity, where popular SAM-based methods demonstrably underperform even FedAvg. The theoretical contribution—generalization bounds via uniform stability that explicitly track the heterogeneity parameter σ_g—provides a principled framework for understanding why and when FL methods generalize. However, the evaluation is limited to image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet, the generalization bounds assume full client participation, and the method mitigates but does not eliminate heterogeneity effects, leaving open whether the approach transfers to NLP, tabular, or other FL settings.
