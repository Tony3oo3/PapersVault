## Problem

The ℓ-smoothness framework (‖∇²f(x)‖ ≤ ℓ(‖∇f(x)‖)) was recently introduced to unify L-smoothness, (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, and (ρ,L₀,L₁)-smoothness, but existing GD analyses under this assumption yield rates depending on ℓ(‖∇f(x₀)‖)/ε and fail entirely when ℓ grows superquadratically (e.g., ρ ≥ 2). No prior work derived an optimal adaptive step size that works universally across all ℓ functions while simultaneously improving convergence guarantees in both nonconvex and convex regimes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires a gradient oracle for f, a starting point x₀, and the smoothness function ℓ (a non-decreasing positive locally Lipschitz function). The per-iteration step size γₖ is computed as the integral ∫₀¹ dv/ℓ(‖∇f(xₖ)‖(1+v)), which admits closed-form expressions for common ℓ (e.g., 1/L for L-smoothness, a log expression for (L₀,L₁)-smoothness) or can be evaluated numerically via quadrature (Simpson's rule or SciPy). The output is an explicit iteration count guaranteeing an ε-stationary point (nonconvex) or ε-solution (convex), with rates that separate the ℓ(0)R²/ε term from terms depending on the initial gradient norm.

## Method

The core contribution is a new optimal step size rule γₖ = ∫₀¹ dv/ℓ(‖∇f(xₖ)‖ + ‖∇f(xₖ)‖v), derived by minimizing a generalized quadratic upper bound f(y) ≤ f(x) + ⟨∇f(x), y−x⟩ + ∫₀^‖y−x‖ q⁻¹(τ; ‖∇f(x)‖) dτ, where q is an auxiliary integral function whose inverse generalizes the classical L‖y−x‖ Lipschitz bound. Two key lemmas (4.3 and 4.5) establish that the gradient difference and function upper bound are controlled by q⁻¹, and the step size is shown to be the unique minimizer of the upper bound via a one-dimensional optimization (Lemma D.1). This yields the per-iteration descent f(xₖ₊₁) ≤ f(xₖ) − (γₖ/4)‖∇f(xₖ)‖², from which improved rates follow: in the nonconvex (ρ,L₀,L₁) setting with 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 2, the rate improves from (L₀Δ + L₁‖∇f(x₀)‖^ρ Δ)/ε to L₀Δ/ε + L₁Δ/ε^{(2−ρ)/2}, and the ρ = 2 case (previously intractable) now gives L₀Δ/ε + L₁Δ. In the convex setting, the dominant term is refined from ℓ(‖∇f(x₀)‖)R²/ε to ℓ(0)R²/ε, and a new proof technique (Theorem 8.1) handles superquadratic ℓ growth without requiring bounded gradients.

## Results

In the nonconvex setting under (ρ,L₀,L₁)-smoothness with 0≤ρ≤2, the proposed step size rule achieves a convergence rate of max{L₀Δ/ε, L₁Δ/ε^((2−ρ)/2)}, improving upon the previous best of (L₀Δ + L₁‖∇f(x₀)‖^ρ Δ)/ε by eliminating the dependence on the initial gradient norm. Critically, for ρ=2 (quadratic Hessian growth), the paper establishes the first convergence guarantee of L₀Δ/ε + L₁Δ, where prior work provided no guarantee. In the convex setting, the dominating term is improved from ℓ(‖∇f(x₀)‖)R²/ε to ℓ(0)R²/ε, and a new rate L₀R²/ε + min{L₁Δ^{1/2}R/ε^{1/2}, L₁²R², L₁M₀R²/ε} is obtained for (L₀,L₁)-smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2024a), the paper strictly improves the nonconvex rate by removing the dependence on ‖∇f(x₀)‖^ρ and, most notably, extends convergence to the ρ=2 case where Li et al. provide no guarantee. In the convex setting, the dominating term is reduced from ℓ(‖∇f(x₀)‖)R²/ε to ℓ(0)R²/ε, which is a significant improvement when the initial gradient is large. Against Vankov et al. (2024), the paper matches the (L₀,L₁)-smoothness nonconvex rate exactly but improves the convex rate by introducing a new term L₁Δ^{1/2}R/ε^{1/2} in the min, which can dominate in practical regimes where R is large. Against Gorbunov et al. (2024), the paper similarly improves convex rates and avoids the exponential dependence on L₁ and R that appears in their accelerated method. The empirical experiments (Appendix A) confirm that the proposed step size rule converges in 75 iterations on f(x)=−log x−log(0.1−x) where Li et al.'s fixed step size requires over 20,000 iterations, and where Vankov et al.'s (L₀,L₁) step size diverges entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on gradient descent convergence under generalized smoothness, building directly on Li et al. (2024a) who introduced the ℓ-smoothness assumption and Vankov et al. (2024) who achieved the best-known rates under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness. The key departure is a new step size rule γₖ = ∫₀¹ dv/ℓ(‖∇f(xₖ)‖ + ‖∇f(xₖ)‖v), derived by minimizing a generalized quadratic upper bound (Lemma 4.5) that replaces the classical L‖y−x‖²/2 term. This rule subsumes the classical 1/L step size, the (L₀,L₁) logarithmic rule, and the clipping rule as special cases, while working for arbitrary non-decreasing locally Lipschitz ℓ functions—including superquadratic and exponential growth where prior methods (Li et al., 2024a) provided no guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a notable gap by establishing convergence for (ρ,L₀,L₁)-smoothness with ρ=2 (quadratic Hessian growth), a regime motivated by practical examples like f(x,y)=log(1+exp(−xy)) in neural network training where (L₀,L₁)-smoothness provably fails. In the convex setting, reducing the dominating term from ℓ(‖∇f(x₀)‖)R²/ε to ℓ(0)R²/ε is significant when the initial gradient is large. Open questions include extending to heavy-tailed stochastic gradients, developing acceleration schemes that avoid the exponential dependence on L₁ and R seen in prior accelerated methods, and connecting the theory to variance reduction and federated learning settings.
