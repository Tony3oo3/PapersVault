## Problem

Diffusion-based anomaly detection models suffer from two critical limitations: the 'identical shortcut' problem, where reconstruction networks over-rely on local pixel-level features and inadvertently preserve anomalies in their outputs, and the inherently slow multi-step denoising process that makes real-time industrial deployment impractical. Existing approaches address one issue or the other but lack a unified framework that simultaneously mitigates shortcut reliance and compresses inference to a single step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, extracts a 272-channel feature representation at 32×32 spatial resolution via EfficientNet-b4 (concatenating stages 1–4), and operates entirely in this feature space. At inference, a single forward pass through the one-step generator produces a reconstructed feature map; the pixel-wise anomaly score map (resized back to input resolution) is computed as the L2 distance between the original and reconstructed features, with image-level scores derived by averaging.

## Method

OmiAD combines two innovations: (1) an Adaptive Masking strategy that applies a binary mask with probability p(t) = p_min + (p_max − p_min)·(t/T)^k to the input features, dynamically increasing occlusion as noise level rises so the model must reconstruct anomalies from broader context rather than copying local pixels; and (2) Adversarial Score Distillation (ASD) that compresses the multi-step teacher (AMDM) into a single-step generator via SiD-style Fisher divergence score matching, augmented with a Diffusion-GAN adversarial loss where the encoder of the student score network is repurposed as a shared-weight discriminator, adding zero extra parameters. Training alternates between updating the student score (denoising + adversarial loss) and the generator (score-distillation + adversarial loss), while the teacher remains frozen. At test time, a single forward pass through the generator at a fixed noise level t_init yields the reconstruction, achieving ~200× speedup over multi-step diffusion baselines.

## Results

OmiAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmark datasets, with mAD scores of 85.3 (MVTec-AD), 79.3 (VisA), 78.9 (MPDD), and 76.3 (Real-IAD), surpassing the strongest baselines DiAD and HVQ-Trans on every dataset. On MVTec-AD, OmiAD reaches 98.8/99.7/98.5 (image-level AU-ROC/AP/F1_max) and 97.7/52.6/56.7/93.2 (pixel-level AU-ROC/AP/F1_max/AU-PRO). Inference speed is dramatically improved: OmiAD completes a batch of 64 in ~0.02 s, roughly 200× faster than DiAD (~15.7 s) and ~7–8× faster than Transformer-based methods like HVQ-Trans (~0.18 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmiAD outperforms all nine baselines on the aggregate mAD metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is DiAD (diffusion-based, AAAI 2024), which OmiAD surpasses by 1.3 mAD on MVTec-AD (85.3 vs 84.0), 9.2 on VisA (79.3 vs 70.1), 14.9 on MPDD (78.9 vs 64.0), and 23.7 on Real-IAD (76.3 vs 52.6). HVQ-Trans (NeurIPS 2023), the strongest Transformer-based baseline, is beaten by 1.7 mAD on MVTec-AD, 3.1 on VisA, 7.0 on MPDD, and 4.9 on Real-IAD. Among reconstruction/embedding baselines, RD4AD and UniAD trail OmiAD by 3–24 mAD depending on dataset, while SimpleNet and DeSTSeg show the largest gaps, particularly on Real-IAD where SimpleNet drops to 42.3 mAD. In terms of inference speed (Table 2), OmiAD at ~0.02 s per batch of 64 is roughly 200× faster than DiAD (~15.7 s) and DDAD (~6.0 s), and 7–8× faster than HVQ-Trans (~0.18 s) and UniAD (~0.19 s), making it the fastest method evaluated while simultaneously achieving the highest detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmiAD sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based anomaly detection (DADD, DiAD, TransFusion) and diffusion distillation for one-step generation (SiD, SiDA, ADD). It adapts the SiD/SiDA distillation framework—originally developed for text-to-image generation—to the anomaly detection setting, repurposing the student score encoder as a shared-weight discriminator following SiDA's design. The key departure from prior diffusion-based AD methods is the Adaptive Masking strategy, which dynamically modulates masking probability with the diffusion timestep to counteract the 'identical shortcut' problem, and the application of adversarial score distillation to compress the multi-step denoising process into a single forward pass for real-time inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200× inference speedup over multi-step diffusion baselines (e.g., DiAD at 15.6s vs. OmiAD at 0.025s per batch) makes diffusion-based anomaly detection practically viable for real-time industrial inspection, a setting where latency is critical. The fact that the one-step student matches or slightly exceeds the multi-step teacher (AMDM) in detection metrics validates that the distillation preserves the global contextual modeling that adaptive masking provides. Open questions include whether the single-step compression degrades performance on extremely subtle or high-frequency anomalies, how the approach scales to video or 3D industrial inspection, and whether the adaptive masking schedule (power-law with k=2) is optimal or dataset-dependent.
