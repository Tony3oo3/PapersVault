## Problem

Existing learning-based macro placement methods rely on reinforcement learning, which is sample-inefficient, requires hours of online training per new circuit, and struggles to generalize across netlists. This paper asks whether a generative model trained entirely on synthetic data can produce high-quality chip placements zero-shot on unseen real circuits, eliminating the need for per-circuit RL fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251312/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a netlist graph where each node carries a 2D vector of normalized object dimensions and each edge carries a 4D vector of source/destination pin offsets relative to their parent object centers; all coordinates are normalized to [-1,1]. The output is a set of 2D coordinates for every object, generated simultaneously (not sequentially as in RL). For real circuits containing hundreds of thousands of standard cells, the netlist is first partitioned into 512 clusters via hMetis, and the model places these clusters alongside macros.

## Method

The denoising architecture interleaves GATv2 graph convolution layers (for local neighborhood message passing) with multi-head attention layers (to capture long-range spatial relationships and mitigate GNN oversmoothing), with residual MLP blocks between each pair and sinusoidal 2D position encodings as additional input. Training uses a DDPM formulation on synthetic data generated by solving the inverse problem: first place objects randomly, then sample edges with probability p(l) ∝ exp(-l/s) so that the given placement is near-optimal, enabling large-scale data generation without commercial tools. A two-stage curriculum pre-trains on smaller circuits (v1, ~230 vertices) then fine-tunes on larger ones (v2, ~960 vertices) with broad log-uniform scale parameters. At inference, backwards universal guidance optimizes a weighted sum of legality (squared overlap penalty) and HPWL potentials, with the legality weight auto-tuned via Lagrangian ascent to enforce the no-overlap constraint while minimizing wirelength.

## Results

On the macro-only IBM benchmark, the diffusion model achieves an average HPWL of 2.49×10⁵ and RUDY congestion of 196, reducing HPWL by over 50% compared to all prior macro placers (7.33–8.72×10⁵). On the ISPD2005 benchmark, it achieves HPWL of 45.9×10⁵ and congestion of 546, again outperforming the best baseline (ChiPFormer: 116×10⁵ HPWL) by more than 2×. In the mixed-size setting on IBM, the method achieves an average HPWL of 22.7×10⁶, improving over DREAMPlace (23.6×10⁶) by 4% while running in minutes versus hours for RL-based alternatives. With guided sampling on the clustered IBM benchmark, legality reaches 0.997 and HPWL drops to 2.976×10⁷, a 20% improvement over DREAMPlace (3.724×10⁷).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The diffusion model decisively outperforms all five baselines on the primary macro-placement task. Against DREAMPlace (Lin et al., 2019), it achieves 20% lower HPWL on the clustered IBM benchmark (2.976 vs. 3.724 ×10⁷) and 4% lower HPWL in mixed-size placement (22.7 vs. 23.6 ×10⁶). Against the RL-based MaskPlace (Lai et al., 2022) and ChiPFormer (Lai et al., 2023), it reduces macro-only IBM HPWL from 8.72 and 7.33 to 2.49 ×10⁵ respectively, while also cutting congestion from 345/336 to 196. Against the black-box optimizer WireMask-BBO (Shi et al., 2023), it achieves 67% lower HPWL on IBM (2.49 vs. 7.43) and 70% lower on ISPD (45.9 vs. 154). Against EfficientPlace (Geng et al., 2024), it reduces HPWL from 8.32 to 2.49 ×10⁵ on IBM. Notably, the method runs in ~4 minutes per circuit on average, compared to 114–227 minutes for the RL and black-box baselines, and requires no per-circuit training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RL-based paradigm for chip placement (CircuitTraining, ChiPFormer, MaskPlace, EfficientPlace), which casts placement as a sequential MDP requiring online training per circuit, and instead adopts a diffusion model (DDPM) that places all objects simultaneously in a single generative pass. The denoising architecture combines GATv2 graph convolutions with interleaved attention layers, and the sampling procedure leverages backwards universal guidance (Bansal et al., 2023) with Lagrangian-based weight tuning to optimize HPWL and legality without additional reward models. A key conceptual departure is the synthetic data generation strategy: rather than solving the forward problem (netlist → optimal placement), the authors solve the inverse problem (random placement → plausible netlist via distance-dependent edge sampling), enabling large-scale training without proprietary circuit data or commercial EDA tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot transfer from purely synthetic data to real ICCAD04/ISPD2005 circuits—outperforming DREAMPlace by 20% HPWL in the clustered setting and all macro placers by >50% in the macro-only setting—demonstrates that generative models can replace the expensive per-circuit RL fine-tuning loop, reducing placement time from hours to minutes. The empirical identification of the scale parameter as the critical generalization bottleneck provides actionable guidance for synthetic data design in other EDA tasks. Limitations include the modest mixed-size gains (4% over DREAMPlace on average, with losses on several circuits), the absence of multimodal edge/size distributions in synthetic data, and the open question of whether combining diffusion sampling with RL fine-tuning (e.g., DDPO) could close the remaining gap to optimal placements.
