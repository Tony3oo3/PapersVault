## Problem

Classical PAC learning of d-dimensional Gaussians requires Õ(d²/ε²) samples in the worst case, and prior work on learning-augmented algorithms has focused almost exclusively on online settings (scheduling, ski-rental, matching). This paper is the first to bring the 'algorithms with predictions' framework to distribution learning, asking whether a potentially inaccurate advice distribution can provably reduce sample complexity while guaranteeing no degradation below the advice-free baseline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250157/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. samples from N(μ, Σ) together with an advice pair (μ̃, Σ̃) and outputs estimates (μ̂, Σ̂) satisfying d_TV ≤ ε with probability ≥ 1−δ. Crucially, the sample complexity is a continuous function of the advice quality measured by ‖μ−μ̃‖₁ (for the mean) or ‖vec(Σ̃^{-1/2}ΣΣ̃^{-1/2}−I)‖₁ (for the covariance), and the algorithm does not require the quality parameter Δ to be known in advance—it estimates it internally via property testing before choosing its learning strategy.

## Method

The TESTANDOPTIMIZE approach has two phases: (i) a tolerant property-testing subroutine (built from non-central chi-squared statistics for the mean and U-statistics for the covariance) multiplicatively approximates the ℓ₁ advice error via a partitioning scheme that blocks coordinates to reduce the √d approximation gap, and (ii) if the estimated error is small enough, the algorithm solves a constrained LASSO (mean) or SDP with an ℓ₁ ball and spectral-norm constraint (covariance) rather than the unconstrained empirical estimator. For the covariance case, a d-sample preconditioning step ensures ‖Σ^{-1}‖₂ ≤ 1 so that Frobenius-norm control translates to the relative error metric. This yields Õ(d^{1−η}/ε²) and Õ(d^{2−η}/ε²) samples respectively when the advice ℓ₁ error is O(ε·d^{(1−η)/2}) or O(ε·d^{1−η}), with matching information-theoretic lower bounds via a Gilbert-Varshamov code construction and Fano's inequality.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a polynomial improvement in sample complexity for PAC-learning multivariate Gaussians when given high-quality advice. For identity covariance, TESTANDOPTIMIZEMEAN achieves $\tilde{O}(d^{1-\eta}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples when $\|\mu - \tilde{\mu}\|_1 < \varepsilon d^{(1-5\eta)/2}$, improving over the advice-free $\tilde{\Theta}(d/\varepsilon^2)$ bound. For general covariance, TESTANDOPTIMIZECOVARIANCE achieves $\tilde{O}(d^{2-\eta}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples when $\|\text{vec}(\tilde{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\Sigma\tilde{\Sigma}^{-1/2} - I_d)\|_1 < \varepsilon d^{1-\eta}$, improving over $\tilde{\Theta}(d^2/\varepsilon^2)$. Matching information-theoretic lower bounds (Theorems 1.3–1.4) confirm these gains are tight up to log factors. Empirically (d=500), the LASSO-based optimization in TESTANDOPTIMIZE consistently reduces ℓ₂ error versus the empirical mean, with the largest gains at low sparsity (s=100) and low advice error (q=0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard empirical mean (for identity covariance) and empirical covariance (for general covariance), which require $\tilde{\Theta}(d/\varepsilon^2)$ and $\tilde{\Theta}(d^2/\varepsilon^2)$ samples respectively. The proposed TESTANDOPTIMIZE algorithms match this worst-case complexity when advice quality is poor (falling back to the empirical estimate), but achieve polynomially fewer samples when advice is accurate. In the experiments (Section 4, d=500), TESTANDOPTIMIZE (solving a constrained LASSO) outperforms the empirical mean in ℓ₂ error across all tested configurations, with the gap widening as sparsity s decreases (from s=300 to s=100) and as advice quality q decreases (from q=30 to q=0.1). The paper also notes that the lower bounds in Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 apply even when the advice quality parameter Δ is known to the algorithm, whereas the proposed algorithms do not require knowing Δ a priori, making them strictly stronger. No direct comparison against other learning-augmented distribution learning methods is provided, as this is the first work to apply the algorithms-with-predictions framework to distribution learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the learning-augmented algorithms (algorithms with predictions) framework—previously applied to online problems like ski-rental, scheduling, and matching—into the classical distribution learning setting, specifically for multivariate Gaussians. It builds on Ashtiani et al. (2020) for information-theoretic lower bound techniques (Fano's inequality, Gilbert-Varshamov codes) and Kamath et al. (2019) for preconditioning high-dimensional covariance estimation. The key methodological departure is combining tolerant property testing (to certify advice quality) with LASSO/SDP-constrained optimization, rather than simply using the empirical mean and covariance as in the advice-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that distribution learning sample complexity can be made advice-dependent, with matching upper and lower bounds showing polynomial improvements (d^{2-β} vs. d²) when advice is sufficiently accurate. The tightness of the bounds (via Fano's inequality constructions) rules out further improvement within the ℓ₁-advice-quality framework, making the characterization complete. Open questions include extending to ℓ₀ (sparsity-based) advice quality, other distribution families, and understanding whether the property-testing-then-optimize paradigm generalizes beyond Gaussians. The practical relevance is currently limited by the small-scale experiments (d=500) and the theoretical focus.
