## Problem

Existing core-set selection methods for deep learning either rely on heuristic uncertainty metrics that lack theoretical grounding or on complex bi-level optimization that is computationally prohibitive. A key unresolved challenge is adaptively balancing the losses of core-set and non-core-set samples without expensive nested optimization, while simultaneously addressing the tension between diversity and information gain in the selected subset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1k at 224×224) and a model that has undergone R epochs of standard SGD training (R=20 in experiments), during which per-sample loss sequences are recorded. It outputs a core-set of size ⌊ωn⌋ (ω ∈ {0.7, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1}) that replaces the full dataset for subsequent training. The only additional computation beyond the R-epoch warm-up is fitting a two-parameter negative exponential to each sample's loss curve and, for the regularized variant, training a single linear output-layer proxy model on the initial subset's features.

## Method

The paper reformulates the CS objective as minimizing the sum of squared losses over all samples, which decomposes into (i) maximizing total loss reduction and (ii) regularizing the balance of loss reduction across samples. Via first-order Taylor expansion, the loss reduction of each sample is expressed as a sum of gradient-inner-product contributions (loss reduction attribution) from all training samples, enabling a symmetry-based simplification: the optimization term reduces to selecting samples with the largest self-loss reduction Δl_j, computed by fitting l_j^(r) ≈ q_j · w_j^(−r) to the observed R-epoch loss sequence (the MRMC criterion). For the regularization term, a two-stage selection is used: the top ρ|C| samples by MRMC form an initial subset C′, a lightweight proxy model (output layer only) is trained on C′, and the remaining (1−ρ)|C| samples are chosen by maximizing a combined score φ(z_j) = φ_MRMC(z_j) − γ·exp(−L(z_j, θ′)), which favors samples whose gradients differ from C′, thereby enforcing diversity.

## Results

Under the early training protocol (R=20 epochs), MRMC-R achieves the best average accuracy across all three main datasets: 94.61% on CIFAR-10, 73.43% on CIFAR-100, and 56.11% on Tiny-ImageNet. On ImageNet-1K (60-epoch training), MRMC reaches 62.25% average top-1 accuracy at 50%/30%/10% selection ratios, outperforming D2pruning (62.04%) and Random (60.45%). The method requires only ~1 minute of selection time on ImageNet-1K versus ~6 minutes for D2pruning, and operates at near-linear O(N) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRMC-R outperforms all six baselines on average across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet under the early training protocol. On CIFAR-10, MRMC-R (94.61%) beats CCS (94.36%) by 0.25% and EL2N (94.19%) by 0.42%, while GLISTER (91.91%) and D2pruning (90.35%) trail by 2.7% and 4.3% respectively. On CIFAR-100, MRMC-R (73.43%) edges out D2pruning (72.91%) by 0.52% and GLISTER (71.64%) by 1.79%, with a large margin over uncertainty-based methods like EL2N (59.25%) and Dyn-Unc (63.37%). On Tiny-ImageNet, MRMC-R (56.11%) leads D2pruning (55.48%) by 0.63% and GLISTER (52.72%) by 3.39%. Notably, EL2N and Dyn-Unc, which are similarly simple observation-based methods, degrade severely on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet (dropping below 60% and 40% respectively), whereas MRMC maintains robust performance, suggesting its loss-reduction-based criterion generalizes better to complex tasks. On ImageNet-1K, MRMC (62.25%) surpasses D2pruning (62.04%) and Random (60.45%), though MRMC-R (61.85%) is slightly suboptimal, likely due to hyperparameter sensitivity at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the well-established core-set selection (CS) literature by replacing the computationally expensive bi-level optimization used by methods like GLISTER and GradMatch with a simpler first-order objective: minimizing the sum of squared loss across all samples. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'loss reduction attribution,' which repurposes the gradient inner product structure from optimization-based CS into a per-sample contribution score (MRMC), bridging the gap between principled optimization and heuristic uncertainty-based methods. The proxy-based regularization for diversity draws on the orthogonality condition from Theorem 4.2 but implements it through a lightweight linear probe rather than explicit gradient constraints, making it far cheaper than graph-based approaches like D2Pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, near-linear-time CS method that requires only a handful of training epochs (R=20) and avoids expensive pairwise computations, making it attractive for large-scale or resource-constrained settings. However, the reliance on assumptions about attribution stability and symmetry (validated only on 50 CIFAR-100 samples) and the multiple hyperparameters (R, ρ, γ) that must be tuned per dataset and selection ratio limit immediate broad adoption. Open questions include whether the squared-loss objective and attribution framework extend naturally to non-convex, multi-modal, or language-model training regimes, and whether the proxy regularization can be simplified or made adaptive to reduce the hyperparameter burden.
