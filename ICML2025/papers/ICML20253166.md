## Problem

Existing GNN-to-MLP distillation methods (e.g., GLNN, NOSMOG) treat the teacher GNN and student MLP as monolithic entities, relying primarily on soft label outputs for knowledge transfer. This overlooks the fine-grained, layer-level knowledge embedded in individual GNN operations—specifically the well-trained parameters of feature transformation (FT) and the distinct smoothing behaviors of graph propagation (GP) versus FT within each layer.

## Input / Output

The student MLP takes node feature vectors as input and produces node class probability distributions as output. In the graph-free setting, only node features are required at inference; in the graph-dependent setting (TINED+), graph structural information is additionally available. The student MLP architecture mirrors the teacher GNN's layer structure: for a T-layer GNN teacher, the student has 2T FC layers (one pair per GNN layer), with hidden dimensions matched to the teacher. Inference is ~94× faster than the 2-layer GraphSAGE teacher (1.63 ms vs. 153 ms).

## Method

TINED distills GNNs to MLPs layer-by-layer via two mechanisms. First, Teacher Injection exploits the observation that FT operations in GNNs are computationally identical to FC layers in MLPs, so the trained FT parameters (W, b) are directly injected into the corresponding student FC layer and fine-tuned with a gradient modifier η to control adaptation; the GP operation is approximated by a separate FC layer, with a theoretical bound (Theorem 4.1) showing the relative approximation error is upper-bounded by the largest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian. Second, Dirichlet Energy Distillation captures the opposing smoothing effects observed within each GNN layer—GP aggressively smooths embeddings (low DE ratio) while FT is conservative or even diversifying (high DE ratio, sometimes >1)—by computing Dirichlet energy ratios for both teacher operations and student FC layers, then minimizing the squared difference between corresponding ratios as an additional loss term. The final training objective combines cross-entropy on ground-truth labels, KL divergence on teacher soft labels, and the DED loss weighted by β.

## Results

Under the transductive setting, TINED achieves 82.63% on Cora, 74.43% on Citeseer, 77.09% on Pubmed, 85.18% on A-computer, 93.97% on A-photo, 64.44% on Arxiv, and 69.48% on Products (without graph dependency), surpassing all baselines on most datasets. With graph dependency, TINED+ further improves to 83.70% (Cora), 75.39% (Citeseer), and 78.59% (Products), exceeding the GraphSAGE teacher on all seven datasets. TINED is 94× faster than its 2-layer GraphSAGE teacher for inference (1.63 ms vs. 153.14 ms). On the large OGB Products dataset, TINED gains 9.42% over vanilla MLP and 5.77% over GLNN, while KRD and FFG2M run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TINED consistently outperforms GLNN (Zhang et al., ICLR 2022) across all seven datasets without graph dependency, with the largest margin on Citeseer (+3.21%) and Products (+5.77%). Against NOSMOG (Tian et al., ICLR 2022) with graph dependency, TINED+ wins on 6 of 7 datasets, e.g., 75.39% vs. 73.78% on Citeseer and 78.59% vs. 78.45% on Products, though NOSMOG edges ahead on Arxiv (71.65% vs. 71.52%). TINED surpasses the GraphSAGE teacher on the five smaller datasets (Cora through A-photo) without graph dependency, but falls short on the two large OGB datasets (Arxiv: 64.44% vs. 70.73%; Products: 69.48% vs. 77.17%), where TINED+ with graph dependency recovers and exceeds the teacher. KRD and FFG2M (Wu et al., 2023) are competitive on small datasets but run out of memory on Products, while TINED handles all seven datasets. In the production (inductive & transductive) setting, TINED+ achieves the best overall performance on Citeseer (73.58% prod) and Products (78.91% prod), confirming robustness under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

TINED extends the GNN-to-MLP distillation line initiated by GLNN (Zhang et al., 2022b) and NOSMOG (Tian et al., 2022), which treat teacher and student as monolithic entities and rely primarily on output-level soft labels. The key departure is a layer-wise decomposition: TINED identifies that feature transformation (FT) in GNNs is computationally identical to an FC layer in MLPs, enabling direct parameter injection, while graph propagation (GP) is approximated by a separate FC layer with a theoretical error bound tied to the graph Laplacian's largest eigenvalue. The Dirichlet Energy Distillation loss is a new mechanism that explicitly preserves the opposing smoothing behaviors of FT and GP operations, a layer-level property overlooked by prior output-level distillation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94× inference speedup over GraphSAGE makes GNN-quality node classification viable in latency-sensitive deployment, and the layer-wise distillation framework is generalizable across GNN architectures (GraphSAGE, GCN, GAT, APPNP). However, the student MLP's depth is rigidly tied to the teacher's layer count, limiting flexibility, and the theoretical bound in Theorem 4.1 is loose (scaling with λ_max of the Laplacian), so the approximation guarantee is more of an existence result than a tight characterization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to heterogeneous or dynamic graphs, whether the number of student FC layers can be decoupled from the teacher's depth, and whether the DE ratio mechanism generalizes beyond node classification to link- or graph-level tasks.
