## Problem

Transformer self-attention processes all tokens with equal computational cost despite attention weights being highly sparse, meaning only a small subset of tokens significantly contributes to predictions in long contexts. Existing sparse-attention methods (e.g., StreamingLLM, LM-Infinite) discard tokens to reduce cost but risk disrupting critical token interactions needed for tasks like multi-document QA and summarization. The paper asks how to reduce redundant attention computations while preserving essential token interactions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text token sequence (evaluated up to 32K tokens) and two hyperparameters: group size m (default 16) and importance rate γ (default 0.1, meaning top-γ fraction of tokens are treated as focal). It produces standard next-token predictions and attention outputs compatible with any decoder-only LLM. A key structural detail is that the grouped KV cache has three segments—focal tokens, aggregated non-focal groups, and complementary tokens—reducing KV cache storage from O(L) to O(r + (L−r)/m + m), where r is the number of focal tokens.

## Method

DGA reformulates sequence modeling as supervised learning to explicitly separate relevant from irrelevant tokens, then formulates attention optimization as a linear coding problem. It dynamically partitions tokens into focal (top-γ by accumulated attention score) and non-focal sets; non-focal tokens are grouped in blocks of size m and their KV pairs are aggregated via softmax-weighted sums using the last token's query in each group. Complementary KV pairs are appended to restore information lost due to autoregressive masking within groups. Theoretically, the group coding reduces noise variance by 1/m² (Theorem 2) and improves the Hessian condition number (Theorem 3), accelerating convergence. During decoding, groups are updated every m′=1.1m generated tokens, and the full mechanism is implemented via flash attention with a custom causal mask.

## Results

On LongBench-E (95% text length quantile 31K), DGA-LLM achieves an average score of 21.14, competitive with vanilla LLaMA2-7B (21.69) and MInference (21.64), while substantially outperforming StreamingLLM (16.43) and LM-Infinite (15.84). The headline efficiency gain is in inter-token latency (ITL): DGA-LLM achieves 28.80 ms at 16K context, which is 2.42× faster than vanilla self-attention (69.70 ms) and 3.55× faster than LM-Infinite (102.22 ms). On the EM score benchmark (4K–32K), DGA-LLM attains an average of 27.7, beating all sparse-attention baselines (StreamingLLM 24.8, LM-Infinite 26.1, MInference 23.7) though trailing vanilla (33.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGA-LLM is compared against three sparse-attention baselines: MInference (dynamic sparse attention patterns), StreamingLLM (attention sinks + sliding window), and LM-Infinite (local + global branch). On LongBench-E average score, DGA-LLM (21.14) is within 0.55 of vanilla LLaMA2-7B (21.69) and MInference (21.64), while clearly surpassing StreamingLLM (16.43) and LM-Infinite (15.84) by 4.7 and 5.3 points respectively. On inter-token latency at 16K context, DGA-LLM (28.79 ms) is 2.42× faster than vanilla (69.70 ms), 3.28× faster than MInference (94.34 ms), 2.72× faster than StreamingLLM (78.28 ms), and 3.55× faster than LM-Infinite (102.22 ms). On the EM score benchmark, DGA-LLM (avg 27.7) outperforms all three baselines (MInference 23.7, StreamingLLM 24.8, LM-Infinite 26.1) but remains below vanilla self-attention (33.6), indicating a modest quality trade-off for the large speedup. Notably, all three baselines are actually slower than vanilla self-attention in ITL, whereas DGA-LLM is the only method that is both faster and competitive in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of efficient-attention work (StreamingLLM, LM-Infinite, MInference) that discards or masks tokens to reduce computation, arguing that such fixed sparsity patterns risk disrupting critical token interactions. Instead, it draws on group sparsity theory from statistical learning (Huang & Zhang, 2010; Lounici et al., 2011) and reframes attention optimization as a linear coding problem, providing theoretical guarantees (noise robustness, condition number reduction) that prior sparse-attention methods lack. The supervised-learning reformulation of autoregressive sequence modeling is an analytical device rather than a new training paradigm, and the practical mechanism (dynamic grouping of non-focal tokens with complementary KV pairs) is a novel combination of ideas from LongLoRA-style grouping and H2O-style importance scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4–3.5× inter-token latency reduction at 16K context while maintaining competitive (though not clearly superior) average accuracy makes DGA practically relevant for latency-sensitive long-context deployments. However, the performance trade-off is non-trivial: DGA-LLM underperforms vanilla self-attention on several LongBench-E subtasks (e.g., Single-Doc QA drops from 6.43 to 3.61), and the method introduces additional hyperparameters (group size m, importance rate γ) whose optimal values are task-dependent. Open questions include whether the importance scoring generalizes to larger models (experiments top out at 7B), whether adaptive (m, γ) selection can close the performance gap, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or non-text sequential domains.
