## Problem

Existing Offline Opponent Modeling (OOM) algorithms assume the pre-collected multi-agent dataset is optimal, meaning self-agent trajectories are best responses to each opponent policy. In practice, datasets are often suboptimal, and under this violation, OOM algorithms deteriorate dramatically—especially in environments with multiple opponents. The paper asks how to make OOM robust to arbitrary suboptimality in the offline dataset without requiring additional online interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each test timestep, the method consumes the self-agent observation o¹, an in-context data set D_t (M randomly sampled (o⁻¹, a⁻¹) tuples from the most recent C opponent trajectories), and an opponent action estimate a⁻¹ produced by a learned Opponent Imitator. The Truncated Q network outputs two per-timestep predictions over a fixed horizon H: a binary confidence score (whether a non-zero reward is obtained) and a continuous reward value. The final output is a self-agent action selected either greedily from the truncated cumulative value (when the Refinement Condition is met) or from the original pretrained OOM policy (otherwise), yielding a single adaptive policy π_IPR.

## Method

TIPR is a plug-and-play framework with two components. First, Truncated Q Training learns a horizon-truncated in-context action-value function using a causal Transformer that encodes in-context opponent data D into a latent variable via cross-attention, then autoregressively predicts per-timestep confidence (BCE loss) and value (MSE loss) over H steps; an auxiliary Opponent Imitator is co-trained to estimate opponent actions. Second, Instant Policy Refinement (IPR) operates at test time: at each timestep it samples D_t, estimates the opponent action, computes a Refinement Condition (whether any self-agent action yields positive truncated cumulative confidence), and either generates a greedy action from the truncated cumulative value or falls back to the original OOM policy. The key design choices—truncating the estimation horizon to reduce cumulative error (especially with multiple opponents) and conditioning on in-context data to handle opponent non-stationarity—are theoretically justified via a No Maximization Bias probability analysis showing an optimal truncated horizon h* < T exists that balances fitting accuracy against natural reward-structure alignment.

## Results

TIPR consistently improves all five OOM baselines (DRON-concat, DRON-MoE, LIAM, Prompt-DT, TAO) across four environments (MS, LBF, PD, PP) and four suboptimality levels (ρ = 0.2–0.8), with the most dramatic gains in PD and PP where original baselines degrade to near-zero or negative returns. Truncated Q achieves substantially lower estimation error than Original Q: in PP, QMSE drops from ~4.1×10⁵ (Original Q, ρ=0.2) to 3.3 (Truncated Q), and QC accuracy reaches 89–99% across all settings. IPR outperforms OCL for policy improvement, with OCL sometimes degrading performance (e.g., in PD), while IPR provides stable gains regardless of ρ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four OOM baselines (DRON-concat, DRON-MoE, LIAM, Prompt-DT) plus TAO show dramatic performance degradation as dataset suboptimality increases (ρ from 0.8 to 0.2), with PD and PP being most severely affected (returns approaching zero or below −400). After applying TIPR, every baseline achieves consistent performance recovery across all ρ values, with the largest absolute gains in PD (2-opponent) and PP (3-opponent) environments. TAO, the strongest baseline, benefits most from TIPR in complex environments, while simpler baselines like DRON-concat show smaller but still meaningful improvements. The paper also compares IPR vs. OCL as improvement strategies: IPR consistently outperforms OCL, and OCL can actually degrade performance in PD, confirming that test-time refinement is superior to offline policy optimization under distribution shift. Ablations confirm that both the confidence mechanism (Q_C) and in-context data (D) are necessary: removing either leads to performance degradation relative to full Truncated Q.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Offline Opponent Modeling (OOM) paradigm introduced by Jing et al. (ICLR 2024), which assumed optimal offline datasets and used In-Context Learning with Transformer architectures to adapt to opponents. It draws conceptual inspiration from Offline Conservative Learning (OCL) in offline RL, which learns a Q-function to improve dataset-embedded policies, but deliberately departs from OCL by performing Instant Policy Refinement (IPR) during testing rather than offline policy optimization, motivated by severe distribution shifts when opponents are unseen. The theoretical contribution introduces a No Maximization Bias probability framework to justify horizon-truncated Q over full-horizon Q, connecting to VC-dimension-based generalization bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: real-world multi-agent datasets are rarely optimal, and existing OOM algorithms degrade dramatically under suboptimality, undermining the efficiency gains that motivated offline learning. The plug-and-play TIPR framework is broadly applicable across OOM algorithms, and the finding that standard OCL can actively harm OOM performance (due to test-time distribution shift) challenges a common assumption in offline RL. Open questions include how to automatically select the optimal truncated horizon H and how to handle opponents that continuously update their policies rather than switching from a fixed set.
