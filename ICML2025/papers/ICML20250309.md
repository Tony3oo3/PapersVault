## Problem

In silico antibody affinity maturation remains challenging because language-model-based mutation strategies lack antigen specificity, while structure-based diffusion methods require accurate antigen-antibody complex structures that are often unavailable. Additionally, training reliable affinity predictors is hampered by the scarcity of labeled binding energy data, with existing biophysical energy calculations being inherently noisy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-domain antibody (sdAb) heavy chain sequence and its target antigen sequence as input, linked into a single chain via a 32×GGGGS linker for AlphaFlow processing. It outputs 1–9 point mutations across CDR-H1/H2/H3 regions (single, double, and triple mutations accumulated over three alternating iterations), with top-3 sequences selected per stage by the sequence-based predictor. The system operates exclusively on Cβ coordinates (no side chains), constraining it to backbone-level structure modeling.

## Method

AffinityFlow alternates between two steps: (1) fixing the sequence and guiding AlphaFlow's flow-matching ODE sampling toward low binding energy via a structure-based ESM2-GVP predictor (predictor guidance with γ=5, applied in later t-stages), with Amber relaxation as a corrector between steps; and (2) inverse folding the generated structure with ProteinMPNN to propose CDR mutations, then selecting candidates with a sequence-based ESM-2+MLP predictor. To address label scarcity, a co-teaching module computes pairwise ΔΔG labels from Rosetta energies on GeoDock-docked complexes, where each predictor selects samples it agrees with to fine-tune the other, reciprocally filtering out noisy entries. This cyclical sample selection effectively converts noisy continuous energy values into reliable discrete training signals for both predictors.

## Results

On the SAbDab sdAb benchmark, AffinityFlow achieves the best Improvement Percentage (IMP) across all four CDR settings: 88.9% (CDR-H1), 93.3% (CDR-H2), 89.7% (CDR-H3), and 91.2% (All), outperforming the strongest baseline AbDPO (88.3%, 91.1%, 87.8%, 90.0%). It also achieves the lowest sequence similarity (Sim), indicating the best antigen specificity: 0.526, 0.528, 0.514, and 0.528 respectively. For naturalness (Nat), language-model baselines (ESM, AbLang, nanoBERT) score higher (0.347–0.361), but AffinityFlow (0.320–0.323) is the best among structure-aware and diffusion-based methods. Ablation on CDR-H3 shows that removing AlphaFlow drops IMP from 93.3% to 63.3%, and removing the co-teaching energy data drops it to 66.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffinityFlow outperforms all seven baselines on both IMP (functionality) and Sim (specificity) across all four CDR settings. On IMP, the closest competitor is AbDPO (90.0% on All), which AffinityFlow surpasses by 1.2 points; the gap widens to 2.2 points on CDR-H2 (93.3% vs. 91.1%) and 3.7 points on CDR-H3 (89.7% vs. 87.8%). On Sim (lower is better), AffinityFlow achieves 0.528 on All versus 0.540 for AbDPO and 0.562 for ESM, reflecting stronger antigen-specific design. On Nat (naturalness), AffinityFlow (0.323 on All) trails the language-model baselines ESM (0.361), AbLang (0.349), and nanoBERT (0.341), which are implicitly optimized for sequence naturalness during pre-training; however, it leads all structure-based and diffusion-based methods (DiffAb 0.316, AbDPO 0.315, GearBind 0.314, dWJS 0.317). The paper notes that when a standard supervised-only predictor is used instead of the co-teaching-refined one, IMP drops substantially for all methods (e.g., ESM from 64.0% to 22.7% on CDR-H3), underscoring that the shared predictor contributes significantly to baseline scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) flow-matching-based protein structure generation (AlphaFlow, which repurposes AlphaFold as a sequence-conditioned generative model), (2) predictor-guided sampling in flow matching (Zheng et al., 2023), and (3) co-teaching for noisy-label robustness (Han et al., 2018). It departs from prior antibody maturation work in two key ways: rather than using diffusion models (DiffAb, AbDPO) or language models (ESM, AbLang) for sequence design, it uses AlphaFlow's flow matching to generate diverse structural conformations guided by a structure-based affinity predictor, then applies inverse folding (ProteinMPNN) to translate structural changes back into sequence mutations. The alternating optimization framework—fixing sequence to optimize structure, then fixing structure to optimize sequence—is a novel compositional strategy not seen in prior antibody design pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important bottleneck in therapeutic antibody development: the scarcity of labeled binding-affinity data for training predictors. The co-teaching module, which extracts pairwise discrete labels from noisy Rosetta energies and uses reciprocal sample selection between sequence- and structure-based predictors, is a practical recipe that could transfer to other biomolecular design tasks with limited supervision. However, the method is restricted to single-domain antibodies (sdAb) operating on Cβ coordinates without side chains, and the ~10-minute-per-iteration computational cost is substantially higher than language-model baselines (seconds). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to full two-chain antibodies, whether the predictor guidance can be made more efficient (currently applied only in late sampling stages), and whether the modest SOTA margins (e.g., 91.2% vs. 90.0% IMP on all CDRs) would widen with more training data or longer mutation trajectories.
