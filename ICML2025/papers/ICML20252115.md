## Problem

Quantizing all LLM tensors (weights, activations, KV cache) to 4-bit without significant accuracy loss remains an open challenge because extreme activation outliers—up to 20× larger than typical values—dominate quantization error. Existing post-training methods either retain outlier channels in high precision (mixed-precision) or apply random rotations to suppress outliers (uniform-precision), but both leave a notable perplexity gap relative to the 16-bit baseline, and naively combining the two strategies does not yield the best results.

## Input / Output

ResQ consumes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices and a small calibration set (512 Wikitext samples) to compute PCA-based projection matrices offline on a single A100 GPU (≈35 min for an 8B model). The output is a deployable model where weights, activations, and KV cache are stored in mixed INT4/INT8 precision (1/8 of channels at 8-bit, the rest at 4-bit, yielding an effective 4.5-bit average), with most projection matrices fused into adjacent linear-layer weights so that inference requires only standard quantized GEMM kernels plus two lightweight runtime projections (a Hadamard transform for the FFN down-projection and an 8-bit quantized rotation for the attention key/query).

## Method

ResQ projects activations and weights onto an orthogonal basis U = PR, where P is a PCA-derived matrix whose columns are eigenvectors of the activation covariance XXᵀ arranged by increasing eigenvalue, and R is a block-diagonal random orthogonal matrix. The top-r eigenvectors (r = d/8) define a high-variance subspace whose coefficients are quantized to 8-bit, while the remaining d−r coefficients are quantized to 4-bit; within each subspace the random rotation further suppresses outliers and increases Gaussianity. Theorem 4.2 proves this PCA-based allocation is the mixed-precision scheme that provably minimizes the expected Frobenius-norm quantization error. Four projection types (U_A at block boundaries, U_B/U_C within attention for KV-cache quantization, U_D within the FFN) are introduced to cover all quantized tensors; U_A, U_B, and U_D are absorbed into neighboring weights at zero runtime cost, while U_C is applied explicitly at runtime in 8-bit because RoPE prevents fusion, and U_D uses a fast Hadamard transform to keep the FFN overhead minimal. Weight quantization is performed with GPTQ after projection fusion, and the entire pipeline is training-free.

## Results

On Meta-Llama-3-8B quantized to W/A/KV = 4.5-bit, ResQ achieves 7.1 Wikitext perplexity, 63.9% average 0-shot accuracy, and 57.2% MMLU accuracy, compared to the 16-bit baseline of 6.1 / 67.1% / 63.1%. On Meta-Llama-3-70B, ResQ reaches 4.1 perplexity and 73.9% MMLU, closing the gap to the 16-bit baseline (2.9 / 75.9%) to within 1.2 points on MMLU. On Qwen2.5-72B, ResQ achieves 4.6 perplexity and 81.5% MMLU versus the 16-bit baseline of 3.9 / 84.3%. ResQ also delivers 1.61×–3.03× prefill speedup and up to 4.98× end-to-end latency improvement over FP16 on batched inference of the 70B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResQ outperforms all five baselines across every model and task evaluated. Against SpinQuant (the strongest rotation-based baseline), ResQ achieves 4–34% lower Wikitext perplexity (e.g., 7.1 vs. 7.4 on Llama-3-8B; 4.1 vs. 6.2 on Llama-3-70B) and 0.1–5.4% higher average 0-shot accuracy, without requiring gradient-based optimization of the rotation matrix. Against QuaRot (uniform 4-bit rotation), ResQ improves perplexity by 9–87% depending on model (e.g., 7.1 vs. 7.8 on Llama-3-8B; 9.0 vs. 68.8 on Qwen2.5-3B where QuaRot degrades severely). Against QUIK (outlier-channel mixed precision), ResQ reduces perplexity by 42–50% (e.g., 7.1 vs. 14.2 on Llama-3-8B) and gains 5.8–12.3% on 0-shot accuracy. GPTQ and SmoothQuant+ are far behind, with perplexities 10–20× higher than ResQ on Llama-3-8B, confirming that naive or scaling-based 4-bit quantization is insufficient for activations. On generative tasks (GSM8K, LongBench), ResQ also leads SpinQuant by 3.8–5.5% on GSM8K and matches or exceeds it on summarization and code completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResQ unifies two previously separate strategies in LLM quantization: mixed-precision outlier retention (QUIK, LLM.int8(), Atom) and rotation-based outlier suppression (QuaRot, SpinQuant). Its key departure is replacing heuristic ℓ∞-norm outlier channel selection with PCA-driven subspace identification, which the authors prove (Theorem 4.2) minimizes expected quantization error under Gaussianity assumptions induced by random orthogonal rotation. The method also inherits GPTQ for weight quantization and borrows the Hadamard-transform trick from QuaRot for efficient FFN projections, but the PCA-based rank-r subspace decomposition and its optimality guarantee are the novel conceptual contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ResQ delivers near-lossless 4-bit quantization of weights, activations, and KV cache simultaneously without gradient-based optimization, making it a practical, training-free alternative to SpinQuant that achieves up to 5× inference speedup and enables 70B-parameter models to fit on a single GPU. The provable optimality of the PCA subspace choice provides a principled foundation that goes beyond the empirical tuning of prior rotation or outlier methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends gracefully to sub-4-bit regimes (Table 4 shows degradation at 2.75-bit), whether the calibration-data dependence of the PCA projection limits robustness across diverse deployment distributions, and whether the Hadamard constraint on U_D (required for efficient FFN projection) is truly necessary or merely a practical compromise.
