## Problem

Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs) like TabPFN achieve zero-shot meta-learning on tabular data, but their synthetic pre-training distribution is narrow (randomly initialized sparse MLPs produce highly correlated data distributions), and their dense final layer imposes a fixed class-size limit that breaks generalizability when inference-time datasets have more classes than seen during pre-training. This paper addresses both limitations to build a more robust and generalizable zero-shot tabular meta-learner.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives an entire tabular dataset in one forward pass: the training set (features + labels) is embedded into the transformer context, and the testing set (features only) is processed with test-masked attention so each test token attends only to training tokens. For classification, the output is a probability vector over classes produced by a scatter-sum over training labels (no fixed-size output layer); for regression, a single point prediction is produced. The model handles arbitrary numbers of classes, features, and categorical columns without any retraining or hyperparameter adjustment.

## Method

APT pre-trains a TabPFN-style transformer (12 blocks, 512 hidden dims, 4 heads) on a mixture of ordinary random sparse-MLP generators and adversarial data agents that perform gradient ascent on the model's prediction loss, continuously shifting the synthetic data distribution to produce harder datasets. To maintain gradient flow through categorical features, the agents use a soft-discretization scheme inspired by the Concrete distribution that converts continuous neuron values to near-categorical values via differentiable quantile ranking. Adversarial agents are periodically reset every N_e epochs to prevent convergence to a Nash equilibrium where predictors and responses become uncorrelated. The dense final layer is replaced by a mixture block: for each test point, two separate feedforwards produce (a) softmax attention weights over training embeddings and (b) soft-discrete binary gates sampled from a Concrete distribution that sparsify which training points contribute to the prediction; final class probabilities are obtained by scatter-summing gated weights grouped by their original training labels, eliminating any class-size constraint.

## Results

On 35 small tabular classification datasets (≤2,000 samples) from OpenML-CC18 with no filtering on class size, feature count, categorical features, or missing values, APT achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 0.921 and mean rank of 3.86, with 13 dataset wins — the best among all 12 compared methods. Its average inference time is 0.90 seconds on an NVIDIA H100 GPU, comparable to TabPFN (0.86 s) and orders of magnitude faster than GBDT baselines (~3,500 s). On the OpenML-CTR23 regression suite, adversarial pre-training reduces TabPFN's mean MSE from 0.412 to 0.344 on small datasets and increases wins from 6.4 to 28.6 across all 35 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT outperforms all 11 baselines on mean ROC-AUC (0.921) and mean rank (3.86) across the 35 small OpenML-CC18 classification datasets. Against the strongest GBDT, CatBoost (0.918 AUC, rank 4.03), APT gains +0.003 AUC and 7 more wins (13 vs. 6) while running ~3,900× faster (0.90 s vs. 3,542 s). Compared to TabPFN (0.913 AUC, rank 4.57), APT improves AUC by +0.008 and wins on 9 more datasets (13 vs. 4) at a negligible 4.6% runtime increase. XGBoost (0.914) and LightGBM (0.917) are also surpassed in both AUC and rank, though LightGBM's AUC is close (0.917 vs. 0.921). SVM (0.904) and the remaining classical baselines (LASSO, Ridge, RandomForest, KNN) are clearly outperformed. Among deep learning baselines, Tabular ResNet (0.825) and SAINT (0.759) show substantially weaker performance, confirming that the zero-shot meta-learning paradigm (APT, TabPFN) is far more effective than per-dataset supervised training for small tabular data. On regression (OpenML-CTR23), APT with adversarial pre-training reduces mean MSE from 0.412 to 0.344 on small datasets and from 0.340 to 0.306 on all datasets relative to TabPFN, with wins increasing from 6.4 to 28.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) framework, specifically TabPFN, by introducing two key modifications: adversarial synthetic data agents that perform gradient ascent on the meta-learner's prediction loss (analogous to GAN generators but without a real/fake distinction), and a scatter-sum mixture block that replaces the fixed-size dense output layer to handle arbitrary class cardinality. The adversarial pre-training departs from TabPFN's purely random sparse-MLP data generation by making the data distribution performance-driven and continuously shifting, while the mixture block draws conceptual inspiration from tree-based methods' label-agnostic classification. The soft-discretization mechanism for categorical features extends TabPFN's ranking discretization with a Gumbel-Softmax/Concrete-distribution-inspired continuous relaxation to enable end-to-end gradient flow through discretized variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the class size limitation is practically significant for zero-shot meta-learners, which must handle an unbounded number of class labels at inference time without retraining — a constraint that is trivial for per-dataset pipelines but critical for ZSMLs. The adversarial pre-training enriches the synthetic data distribution (KL divergence 0.813 vs. 0.134 between independent generator sets), addressing a key weakness of TabPFN's limited data diversity. However, APT still does not outperform GBDTs on large tabular datasets and retains quadratic transformer complexity, leaving open the question of how to scale zero-shot tabular meta-learning to larger problems. Future work could incorporate state-space models for linear-complexity attention, extend the mixture block to regression, and explore whether the accelerated convergence property generalizes beyond tabular settings.
