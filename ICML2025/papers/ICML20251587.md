## Problem

Existing variational inference (VI) for latent variable survival models uses a single variational distribution q_φ(z|x,y) that is marginalized over the censoring indicator δ, but the paper proves that the optimal variational posterior that tightly bounds the log-likelihood is fundamentally censor-dependent. This mismatch causes latent non-identifiability, posterior collapse, and degraded model expressiveness under non-informative censoring, yet no prior work had formally characterized or addressed this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The model consumes tabular patient covariates x (5–17 dimensions across benchmark datasets), a continuous log-transformed observed survival time y, and a binary event indicator δ (1 = event observed, 0 = right-censored). The encoder uniquely takes all three (x, y, δ) as inputs, and the decoder outputs a location-scale survival distribution parameterized by a neural network mean ζ(x,z) and a shared scale σ, with noise drawn from either a standard Gaussian (LogNormal AFT) or Gumbel-minimum (Weibull AFT) family. The final output is an individual survival function S(t|x) obtained by marginalizing over the latent z, enabling time-to-event prediction and ranking via C-index or Brier score.

## Method

The core contribution is Censor-Dependent Variational Inference (CDVI), which replaces the single q_φ(z|x,y) with a censor-dependent family q_{φ1,φ2}(z|x,y,δ) = q_{φ1}(z|x,y)^δ · q_{φ2}(z|x,y)^(1−δ), yielding a Censor-Dependent ELBO (ELBO-C) that assigns separate variational distributions to the log-density and log-survival terms. The paper proves that the optimal q_{φ1} and q_{φ2} are linked by a hazard-ratio constraint (q_{φ1} ∝ h_θ(y|x,z)·q_{φ2}), eliminating the φ1=φ2 degeneracy that causes posterior collapse in vanilla VI. The practical implementation, CD-CVAE, is a V-structure VAE where the encoder conditions on (x, y, δ) and the decoder uses a location-scale emission distribution. Two augmented variants—Importance Sampling (IS) and Delta Method (DVI)—further tighten the bound, with the paper proving monotonicity of the inference gap in sample size and consistency of the log-likelihood estimator as m,k→∞.

## Results

On the WHAS dataset, CD-CVAE achieves a C-index of 0.868, outperforming the best baseline (DSM at 0.811) by approximately 5 percentage points, which is the paper's headline result. Across all seven benchmark clinical datasets, CD-CVAE attains the best C-index on four of seven (SUPPORT, FLCHAIN, NWTCO, WHAS) and is competitive on the remaining three. For Brier scores, CD-CVAE achieves the lowest (best) values on FLCHAIN (0.110) and METABRIC (0.203), while RSF leads on WHAS (0.162) and PBC (0.119). On simulated datasets (SD1–SD6), CD-CVAE consistently reduces the KL divergence between the learned and true posterior compared to vanilla CVAE, with the gap widening as censoring rate increases (e.g., E-KL drops from 3.45 to 2.88 on SD4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CVAE achieves the largest margin over all baselines on WHAS, where its C-index of 0.868 exceeds DSM (0.811), RSF (0.808), and DCM (0.803) by 5–6.5 percentage points. On FLCHAIN and NWTCO, CD-CVAE edges out DSM by 1.0 and 0.2 C-index points respectively, while on SUPPORT it narrowly beats RSF (0.685 vs. 0.683). However, CD-CVAE is outperformed by DCM on METABRIC (0.681 vs. 0.689) and PBC (0.863 vs. 0.866), and ties with RSF on GBSG (both 0.706). For Brier scores, CD-CVAE leads on FLCHAIN (0.110 vs. 0.113 for DSM/DCM) and METABRIC (0.203 vs. 0.216 for DCM), but trails RSF on WHAS (0.168 vs. 0.162) and PBC (0.124 vs. 0.119). Overall, CD-CVAE is the strongest or near-strongest method on most datasets, with the paper noting that RSF and DCM remain competitive in computational efficiency and hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE/CVAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014; Kingma et al., 2014) to latent variable survival models, but its core contribution is a theoretical correction to the entire LVSM-for-survival line of work (Ranganath et al., 2016; Xiu et al., 2020; Nagpal et al., 2021; Apellaniz et al., 2024). It proves that the variational distribution used in all prior methods—q(z|x,y) shared across event and censored observations—is fundamentally suboptimal, and that the optimal posterior must be censor-dependent, i.e., q(z|x,y,δ). The augmented variants (IS, DVI) generalize importance-weighted autoencoders (Burda et al., 2015) and Nowozin's (2018) debiasing to the censored setting, while correcting a technical error in Domke & Sheldon (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that vanilla VI is provably suboptimal for survival analysis is a significant correction: it implies that all prior LVSMs for survival (DSM, Xiu et al., Apellaniz et al.) operate with an unnecessary inference gap that degrades estimation of θ. However, the practical gains are modest—the paper itself notes that CD-CVAE variants 'do not exhibit substantial improvements in time-to-event modeling performance'—suggesting the amortization bottleneck may dominate the theoretical gap in finite-data regimes. Open questions include whether the censor/event KL trade-off observed in experiments can be resolved, whether non-amortized CDVI closes the gap more effectively, and whether the theoretical optimality conditions translate to larger empirical gains on more complex or higher-dimensional survival tasks.
