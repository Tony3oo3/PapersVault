## Problem

Diffusion models can generate diverse and smooth robot trajectories but cannot enforce hard constraints like collision avoidance and kinematic feasibility, a limitation that becomes critical in Multi-Robot Motion Planning where multiple robots must coordinate in shared, cluttered spaces. Existing workarounds—gradient-based guidance and rejection sampling—either fail on the non-convex collision constraints or discard most generated trajectories, causing success rates to collapse as robot and obstacle counts increase.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the 2D start and goal positions for N_a robots (modeled as spheres of radius 0.05–0.1 units), the positions and sizes of N_o static obstacles, and kinematic parameters (maximum velocity per robot, time-step interval Δt), all defined on a 2×2 unit workspace. It outputs a set of H-step trajectories for all robots simultaneously, where each trajectory is a sequence of 2D positions that must satisfy start/goal boundary conditions, per-step velocity bounds, minimum inter-robot separation (R^a), and minimum robot-to-obstacle clearance (R^o). Unlike sequential planners, all robots' trajectories are generated in a single joint diffusion sampling pass.

## Method

SMD modifies the standard SGLD denoising step of a score-based diffusion model by inserting a projection operator P_Ω after each gradient update, so that every intermediate sample is mapped back onto the feasible set. The feasible set Ω is decomposed into a convex part Ω_c (start/goal constraints and velocity limits) and a nonconvex part Ω_n (inter-robot and obstacle collision-avoidance inequalities). To make the projection tractable despite the nonconvexity, SMD formulates it as an augmented Lagrangian subproblem: inequality collision constraints are converted to equalities via non-negative slack variables, and a dual-ascent loop alternates between minimizing the augmented Lagrangian over Ω_c (a convex inner problem) and updating the dual multipliers, with a geometrically increasing penalty parameter ζ. Theoretically, the convex constraints are satisfied to arbitrarily small violation ξ, while the nonconvex collision constraints are driven below user-specified tolerances δ_a and δ_o. This eliminates the need for post-hoc rejection sampling or gradient-based trajectory correction, and the framework extends naturally to additional constraints (e.g., acceleration bounds) by simply enlarging Ω.

## Results

SMD achieves a 96% success rate on the most challenging scenario (dense maps with 9 robots and 20 obstacles), a 3.6× improvement over the previous state-of-the-art MMD (27%). On corridor maps, SMD is the only method to achieve 100% success rate, while DM, MPD, and MMD all fail completely (0%). Across all random and practical map types, SMD maintains near-zero collision ratios and achieves shorter average path lengths per robot than the classical EECBS baseline (e.g., 1.36 vs 1.53 on dense maps with 9 robots).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMD decisively outperforms all four baselines. Against the standard diffusion model (DM), SMD wins overwhelmingly: DM drops to 5% success on basic maps and 0% on dense maps or with 6+ robots, while SMD maintains ≥96%. Against MPD (single-robot diffusion extended to MRMP), SMD wins in every non-trivial scenario: MPD achieves only 35% on empty maps with 6 robots and 0% with obstacles or 9 robots. Against MMD (the prior state-of-the-art combining diffusion with search-based MAPF), SMD achieves a 3.6× improvement on the hardest setting (96% vs 27% on dense maps, 9 robots) and is the sole method to solve corridor maps (100% vs 0%). Against the classical EECBS algorithm, SMD matches or exceeds success rates while producing ~20% shorter paths, though EECBS benefits from its grid-based discretization in open environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the projected diffusion framework of Christopher et al. (2024), which enforces constraints via projection operators during SGLD sampling, from single-robot to multi-robot motion planning. It departs from the gradient-based guidance paradigm used by MPD (Carvalho et al., 2023) and MMD (Shaoul et al., 2025) by replacing soft penalty steering with a hard constrained-optimization step solved via Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) and dual ascent. The key conceptual shift is treating the non-convex collision-avoidance constraints as a projection subproblem within each diffusion step rather than as a differentiable cost to be minimized, which sidesteps the local-minima failures of gradient guidance in multi-robot settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first comprehensive MRMP benchmark spanning random and practical maps with varying robot counts and obstacle densities, filling a gap in reproducible evaluation for multi-robot trajectory planning. The 3.6× success-rate improvement over the prior SOTA (MMD) in the hardest setting (dense maps, 9 robots) and the complete failure of all baselines in corridor maps highlight a practical gap that gradient-guided diffusion cannot close. Limitations include the 2D-only formulation, centralized coordination, and substantially higher inference times (up to ~550 s vs. ~190 s for MMD in the hardest case), leaving open questions about real-time applicability, 3D extension, and decentralized settings.
