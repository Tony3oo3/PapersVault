## Problem

Online RL fine-tuning of VLM agents suffers from inefficient exploration because the open-ended textual action space scales exponentially with vocabulary size and sequence length, and the non-end-to-end parsing step means most tokens in a generated utterance have negligible impact on the final executable action. Prior entropy-regularized RL methods assign uniform uncertainty to all tokens, wasting exploration budget on semantically redundant or format-fixed tokens that cannot change the parsed action.

## Input / Output

The agent receives an RGB screenshot of the current environment state, a natural-language task instruction, and the action history as input. It autoregressively generates a structured text utterance (typically JSON with 'thoughts' and 'action' fields) containing chain-of-thought reasoning followed by a textual action, which a deterministic parsing function then maps to an executable API call (e.g., normalized click coordinates, text input, or navigation commands). The utterance length is bounded (≤300 tokens in experiments) and the vocabulary is ~32,100 tokens, making the raw sampling space |V|^n astronomically large.

## Method

CoSo introduces a counterfactual reasoning mechanism to compute per-token causal weights that quantify each token's influence on the parsed action. A lightweight 0.01B BERT-based structural causal model (SCM) approximates the probabilistic mapping P(a|y, ε_a), and the causal weight for token y^i is the absolute likelihood difference between the original utterance and a counterfactual where y^i is replaced with a null token. These weights modulate the entropy term in the soft RL objective (Equation 7), concentrating exploration on the <10% of action-critical tokens while suppressing entropy for the >80% of low-impact tokens, effectively narrowing the exploration space by ~32,100^90-fold. The approach is plug-and-play with existing RL objectives (AWR/DigiRL or PPO/RL4VLM) and comes with theoretical guarantees for convergence and monotonic policy improvement.

## Results

CoSo achieves average success rates of 72.9% on Android-in-the-Wild (AitW), 49.3% on Gym Cards, and 26.5% on ALFWorld, outperforming the strongest RL baselines DigiRL (64.9% on AitW) and RL4VLM (45.1% on Gym Cards, 22.7% on ALFWorld) by 12.3%, 9.3%, and 16.7% respectively. On the LLM-agent extension (AlfredTWEnv), CoSo reaches 39.6% average success versus 32.8% for RL4VLM. The computational overhead is modest: only 0.01B additional parameters, 0.7 GB extra GPU memory, and 0.5 H100 GPU hours (<4% total training time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSo consistently outperforms all prompting-based baselines (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4V, AppAgent) across every task domain, with the largest gaps on AitW (72.9% vs. 16.7% for Gemini) and ALFWorld (26.5% vs. 13.5% for Gemini). Among offline learning methods, CogAgent (30.0% on AitW) and AutoUI+SFT (39.6% on AitW) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of online RL fine-tuning. The most direct comparisons are against the two RL fine-tuning baselines that CoSo builds upon: DigiRL (AWR-based) and RL4VLM (PPO-based). CoSo improves over DigiRL by 12.3% on AitW (72.9% vs. 64.9%) and over RL4VLM by 9.3% on Gym Cards (49.3% vs. 45.1%) and 16.7% on ALFWorld (26.5% vs. 22.7%). On the LLM-agent extension (AlfredTWEnv), CoSo also beats RL4VLM by 6.8% (39.6% vs. 32.8%), demonstrating that the causal-weighted entropy mechanism generalizes beyond VLMs to purely textual agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft RL / maximum entropy framework (Haarnoja et al., 2017) to the specific challenge of VLM agent fine-tuning, where the text-based action space causes an exponential explosion in exploration space. It builds directly on DigiRL (AWR-based, Bai et al., 2024) and RL4VLM (PPO-based, Zhai et al., 2024) as the RL backbones, but departs from their uniform entropy regularization by introducing a counterfactual reasoning mechanism (via a lightweight SCM) to compute per-token causal weights that selectively weight the entropy term. The key conceptual departure is borrowing structural causal model and counterfactual intervention ideas from causal inference to identify which tokens in a VLM utterance actually influence the parsed action, rather than treating all tokens as equally important for exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in online RL fine-tuning of VLM agents: the vast majority of tokens in generated utterances (formatted JSON, CoT boilerplate) do not affect the final parsed action, making naive exploration extremely wasteful. The approach is general (pluggable into AWR, PPO, or other RL objectives) and introduces minimal overhead (<4% compute, 0.01B params), making it attractive for practitioners. However, the authors note that experiments are limited to utterances under 300 tokens, and effectiveness on ultra-long chain-of-thought reasoning remains unexplored, pointing toward hierarchical extensions as future work. The finding that uniform entropy regularization provides only marginal gains over standard RL is a useful negative result that clarifies why targeted exploration is necessary in text action spaces.
