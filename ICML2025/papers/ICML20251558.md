## Problem

Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) divide reasoning responses into steps using rule-based methods such as predefined symbols or fixed token counts, producing coarse divisions that lack decision-making information at step boundaries. This limits the granularity and informativeness of step-level rewards, and makes PRMs difficult to apply in domains where natural step boundaries are hard to define (e.g., code generation). The paper asks whether an automatic, domain-agnostic criterion can identify truly informative reasoning step boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question and a set of model-generated responses (sampled with temperature-based random sampling), extracting the per-token probability of the sampled token as a confidence score during generation. A global threshold (set so that the bottom 2% of tokens by confidence become breaking points) is determined from the accumulated confidence distribution across all samples. The PRM itself takes a question plus a prefix of reasoning steps and outputs a binary reward score; in TVD mode, it takes the top-M candidate tokens at a low-confidence position and outputs the single best token to continue generation.

## Method

AdaptiveStep divides a response into reasoning steps at positions where the model's confidence in the sampled token falls below a data-driven threshold (2% of total tokens), identifying genuine decision points rather than arbitrary line breaks. For PRM training, each step is labeled via heuristic rollout: 8 continuations are generated from each step boundary, and the step receives a reward of 1 if any continuation reaches a correct answer (hard estimation). The PRM is trained with binary cross-entropy on these step-level labels. At inference, Token-level Value-guided Decoding (TVD) triggers the PRM whenever the policy model's Top-1 probability drops below the threshold, and the PRM selects the best among the top-M candidate tokens, effectively using the PRM as a value function to guide decoding without additional inference overhead beyond the PRM call.

## Results

On the GSM8k dataset, ASPRM-L (based on Llama-3.1-8B) achieves 83.47% accuracy via Token-level Value-guided Decoding (TVD) with MetaMath-Llama, improving over greedy decoding (81.80%) by 1.67 points, while ASPRM-M (Mistral-based) reaches 79.53% with MetaMath-Mistral vs. 77.10% greedy. On MATH500, ASPRM-L achieves 42.00% (vs. 38.80% greedy) and ASPRM-M reaches 28.60% (vs. 25.00% greedy). For code generation, ASPRM-D improves Pass@1 from 26.28% to 28.00% on LeetCodeDataset and from 19.21% to 19.92% on LiveCodeBench. In Best-of-N evaluation, ASPRM-L outperforms both Math-Shepherd and ER-PRM across most math benchmarks while using less than 70% of their training data construction costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ASPRM against two open-source math PRMs: Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024a) and ER-PRM (Zhang et al., 2024). In TVD evaluation, ASPRM-L consistently outperforms both baselines: on GSM8k with MetaMath-Llama, ASPRM-L scores 83.47% vs. Math-Shepherd's 81.73% and ER-PRM's 81.58%; on MATH500 with MetaMath-Llama, ASPRM-L reaches 42.00% vs. 41.00% (Math-Shepherd) and 38.60% (ER-PRM). Notably, both baselines actually degrade performance below greedy on GSM8k (75.66% and 75.13% vs. 77.10% greedy with MetaMath-Mistral), while ASPRM maintains improvement. In BoN evaluation (Figure 4), ASPRM-L achieves the highest scores across most math benchmarks despite using only a single model for data construction and less than 70% of the construction cost of either baseline. For code generation, no competitive open-source code PRM baseline exists, so the authors train a Code-ORM as a comparison; ASPRM-D shows superior robustness as N increases in BoN and outperforms greedy in TVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PRM training framework established by Lightman et al. (2023) and the heuristic rollout-based reward estimation from Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024a), but departs from the dominant rule-based step-dividing paradigm (line breaks, fixed token intervals) used by Math-Shepherd, ER-PRM, and PRM800K. The key conceptual shift is using the model's own token-level prediction confidence as a natural signal for where reasoning decisions occur, replacing externally imposed structural boundaries. The paper also extends PRM usage beyond Best-of-N selection into Token-level Value-guided Decoding (TVD), connecting to the broader guided-decoding literature (PPLMCTS, value-guided MCTS) but applying a step-level PRM at the token level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confidence-based division offers a practical, domain-agnostic alternative to rule-based step splitting that reduces data construction costs by over 30% while achieving SOTA BoN performance, making PRM training more accessible. The TVD integration provides a lightweight inference-time improvement without architectural changes. However, open questions remain: the 2% threshold is heuristic (borrowed from Kahneman's System 2 proportion) and the optimal value appears model-dependent; cross-domain transfer is inconsistent (code PRM helps MATH500 but hurts GSM8k); and the method's reliance on a single policy model for data generation limits transferability compared to multi-model approaches like Math-Shepherd.
