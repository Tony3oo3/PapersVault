## Problem

Spatio-temporal graph neural networks (STGNNs) rely on node-to-node messaging (GCN, self-attention) for feature interaction, making them highly sensitive to spatio-temporal distribution shifts and structural changes (new or removed nodes). The paper identifies that this node-to-node coupling actually hinders generalization—ablations show variants without such messaging perform better—yet no existing framework replaces it with a mechanism that is both robust to OOD shifts and capable of inductive learning on unseen nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical graph signal X ∈ R^{T×N×c} (T past time steps, N nodes, c feature channels) along with the graph adjacency matrix, plus timestamp-of-day and day-of-week embeddings as temporal priors. It produces a future prediction Ŷ ∈ R^{N×T_P×c} as the sum of a temporal component and a spatial component. Notably, the model must handle test-time graphs with 10% fewer nodes and 30% new nodes relative to training, and the ConAU mechanism (K ≪ N) enables representation of unseen nodes without retraining.

## Method

STOP replaces node-to-node messaging with a centralized interaction: K learnable Context-Aware Units (ConAU) exchange features with all nodes via a multi-head low-rank attention that decomposes into an aggregation step (KQ^T, extracting context from nodes) and a diffusion step (QK^T, distributing context back), yielding O(KNd_h) complexity instead of O(N²d_h). During training, M Generalized Perturbation Units (GenPU) stochastically mask node-to-ConAU aggregation paths, creating diverse perturbed environments; a spatio-temporal DRO objective then selects the worst-performing GenPU branch for gradient descent, forcing the model to learn invariant knowledge. Temporal dynamics are modeled by decomposing input into long/short-term patterns via moving average, processing each through separate MLPs, and mixing channels with L-layer MLPs; spatial dynamics are captured by subtracting context features from the temporal representation and passing the residual through another channel-mixing MLP. The final prediction sums the temporal and spatial prediction components.

## Results

STOP achieves the best OOD generalization across all six datasets and all prediction horizons, with up to 17.01% improvement over the strongest baseline (STONE) in MAPE. On LargeST-SD (horizon 3), STOP attains MAE 17.71, RMSE 28.45, MAPE 11.73, outperforming STONE (18.44/29.55/12.32) and CaST (21.35/33.28/16.04). On KnowAir (horizon 24), STOP reaches MAE 28.70, RMSE 42.39, MAPE 57.96, versus STONE's 30.56/45.48/75.11. For inductive learning on new nodes (Table 4), STOP achieves MAE 22.74 on SD and 24.76 on GBA, yielding up to 18.44% improvement over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STOP outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and prediction horizon. Against the closest OOD-specific baseline STONE, STOP reduces MAE by 4–12% (e.g., 17.71 vs 18.44 on SD-h3; 28.70 vs 30.56 on KnowAir-h24) and MAPE by up to 23% (57.96 vs 75.11 on KnowAir-h24). CaST, the other OOD baseline, is consistently weaker, with MAPE gaps reaching 17+ points on KnowAir. Among standard STGNNs, Transformer-based models (D²STGNN, STAEformer) are the strongest baselines but still trail STOP by 3–8% in MAE; GCN-based models (STGCN, GWNet) and STNN perform worst in OOD settings due to their sensitivity to structural shifts. In inductive learning (Table 4), STOP achieves MAE 22.74 on SD and 24.76 on GBA for new nodes, beating STONE (25.06/26.28) by ~9–10% and D²STGNN (25.85/26.36) by ~12–14%, confirming the centralized messaging mechanism's advantage for unseen nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

STOP departs from the dominant STGNN paradigm (GCN-based or self-attention-based node-to-node messaging) by replacing distributed interaction with a centralized mechanism where nodes interact only with a small set of learnable Context-Aware Units via low-rank attention. It builds on the DRO framework of Duchi & Namkoong (2019) by instantiating a spatio-temporal-specific DRO objective through Generalized Perturbation Units that mask node-to-ConAU messages, and positions itself alongside prior spatio-temporal OOD methods (STONE, CaST, CauSTG) but from a message-mechanism reformulation angle rather than a causal-decoupling angle. The temporal decomposition and channel-mixing MLP backbone draws from the iTransformer/RPMixer line of work, while the inductive learning evaluation connects to the broader graph OOD literature (Hamilton et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ablating node-to-node messaging from strong STGNNs actually improves OOD performance challenges a core design assumption in the field and suggests that the interaction mechanism itself, not just the learned weights, is a source of distributional fragility. The ~20× training-speed advantage over Transformer-based baselines like D²STGNN makes centralized interaction a practical option for large-scale deployment (8,600-node CA dataset). Open questions include whether the fixed number of ConAUs (K≪N) is sufficient for highly heterogeneous urban scenes, whether the DRO formulation generalizes to non-graph spatio-temporal data, and whether the approach extends to truly dynamic graph topologies where edges change over time rather than just node counts.
