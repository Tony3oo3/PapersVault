## Problem

RoPE (Rotary Position Encodings) has become the dominant position encoding in transformers, yet it is not the most general algorithm satisfying both separability and translational invariance. Existing generalizations either break translational invariance for multi-dimensional position vectors or incur prohibitive memory costs, making them unsuitable for robotics where efficient 2D/3D token representation is critical. The paper asks: what is the most general position encoding preserving both properties, and can it be computed efficiently enough for real-world deployment?

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB or RGB-D images (224×224 to 256×256) paired with text prompts, where each image patch is assigned a position vector of arbitrary dimensionality — 2D for standard images, 3D when depth is incorporated as a third coordinate via per-patch mean-pooling followed by a learnable linear layer. Outputs span class labels, retrieval rankings, 2D/3D bounding boxes with full SE(3) pose (a 12D vector of translation, rotation, and size), and robot action sequences for manipulation policies. A key practical property is that STRING can be initialized from standard 2D pre-trained checkpoints and fine-tuned for 3D tasks without retraining the backbone, since depth modulates attention rather than altering the input representation.

## Method

STRING defines position encodings as R(r_i) = exp(Σ_k L_k [r_i]_k), where {L_k} are learnable commuting skew-symmetric generators, and proves (Theorem 3.2) that this is the most general mapping satisfying the group-like translational invariance R(r_i)^T R(r_j) = R(r_j − r_i) with R(0) = I. A structural result (Theorem 3.4) shows any STRING reduces to P·RoPE(r)·P^T for a fixed orthogonal matrix P independent of position, enabling efficient computation without dense matrix exponentiation. Two practical instantiations follow: Cayley-STRING parameterizes P via the Cayley transform of a learnable skew-symmetric matrix (O(d³) training, O(d) inference after absorbing P into projections), and Circulant-STRING uses circulant generators computable in O(d log d) via FFT. The learnable basis change P, shared between queries and keys, is the mechanism that gives STRING its consistent empirical advantage over RoPE despite their mathematical equivalence up to a change of basis.

## Results

On ALOHA Unleashed simulation (12 dexterous manipulation tasks), Cayley-STRING achieves a 46% mean success rate (MultiTask), outperforming RoPE (42%) and the ViT baseline (37%). On ImageNet2012 classification, Circulant-STRING reaches 81.22% top-1 accuracy vs. 80.04% for standard ViT and 80.18% for RoPE. For 3D object detection (Proc4GEM), Cayley-STRING on ViTD achieves 72.67% 3D IoU, a 2% relative improvement over the best RoPE variant (71.21%). On the real KUKA robot, 3D STRING policies reach ~74% pick success rate vs. ~65% for the 2D baseline, with significantly better out-of-distribution robustness (e.g., ~50% vs. ~10% under table-height variation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRING (both Cayley and Circulant variants) consistently outperforms RoPE (Su et al., 2024) across all major benchmarks: +1.04% on ImageNet2012 (81.22% vs. 80.18%), +0.35 mean AP on COCO/LVIS detection (28.24% vs. 28.18%), +1.5% relative 3D IoU on Proc4GEM (58.95% vs. 58.09% for ViT), and +4% absolute MultiTask success on ALOHA (0.46 vs. 0.42). Against RoPE-Mixed (Heo et al., 2025), STRING wins on ImageNet2012 (81.22% vs. 80.86%), WebLI retrieval (70.48 vs. 69.85 mean recall), and 3D detection (72.67% vs. 70.90% for ViTD), though RoPE-M slightly edges out on Places365 (56.69% vs. 56.77% for Circulant-S). STRING also beats the standard ViT with absolute position encodings (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) by 1.18% on ImageNet2012 and 9% absolute on ALOHA MultiTask. The OWL-ViT baseline (Minderer et al., 2022) without any relative PE is outperformed by +1.03 mean AP on 2D detection and +9.18% 3D IoU on Proc4GEM. On the real KUKA robot, 3D STRING with depth input (~74%) substantially outperforms the 2D SigLIP-based policy (~65%), and STRING also mitigates the negative impact of noisy depth normal maps (recovering from 42% to 53% when combined with normal-map fusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

STRING generalizes Rotary Position Encodings (RoPE) through a Lie-group-theoretic framework, proving that any translationally invariant, continuously differentiable position encoding using matrix multiplication must take the STRING form (Theorem 3.2), with RoPE as a special case. This departs from prior generalizations like LiERE (Ostmeier et al., 2024), which uses dense SO(d) matrices but breaks translational invariance for multi-dimensional coordinates, and from the ad hoc 2D/3D RoPE extensions of Heo et al. (2025). The computationally efficient instantiations draw on the Cayley Transform (Diele et al., 1998) for parameterizing the learnable orthogonal basis change, and on circulant matrix structure with FFT for a lighter-weight alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, complete characterization of translationally invariant position encodings, giving practitioners a clear design space and explaining why a learnable basis change (which in principle should be absorbable by existing weights) consistently improves performance. The 3D robotics results are practically significant: STRING enables depth integration that actually helps (unlike naive normal-map fusion, which degrades performance), and 3D STRING policies show substantially better OOD robustness to table-height changes and distractors. Open questions include whether the modest 1–2% gains on standard vision benchmarks persist at larger model scales, whether the theoretical generality of STRING translates to advantages beyond the tested instantiations, and whether the robotics results generalize beyond the specific ALOHA and KUKA platforms tested.
