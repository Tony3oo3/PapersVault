## Problem

Current neural network verifiers guarantee theoretical soundness by bounding the full-precision output, but this does not imply practical soundness for deployed networks where floating-point non-associativity and stochastic operation ordering can produce outputs outside the verified bounds. The paper proves this gap formally for interval analysis and its symbolic variants, and demonstrates empirically that all state-of-the-art verifiers are vulnerable to deployment-specific backdoor attacks that remain invisible to the verifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a host neural network (a 2-convolutional-layer MNIST classifier) and a deployment environment specification (IEEE 754 precision, expression tree/operation order, hardware platform, framework) as input, and produces backdoored adversarial networks whose malicious behavior is triggered purely by environment properties rather than input perturbations. The detector neurons receive constant all-ones inputs, making the backdoor fully independent of the actual network input. Verification is performed over l∞-norm neighborhoods of radius 0.1, and the output is a per-verifier, per-attack sound/unsound verdict indicating whether the verifier correctly detected the planted backdoor.

## Method

The paper first proves that interval bound propagation (IBP), polyhedra-based methods (DeepPoly, CROWN), and zonotope-based methods (DeepZ, RefineZono) are not practically sound by constructing, for any expression tree a verifier uses, an input and a different deployment expression tree whose floating-point output falls outside the computed interval. It then designs detector neurons—linear neurons with carefully chosen weights such as ω+1−ω for precision detection or sums of ω/h terms for expression-tree detection—that output different values depending on the deployment's precision or operation order. These detectors are integrated into the host network following the Zombori et al. backdoor technique, with α/β parameters controlling whether a zero or non-zero detector output triggers an adversarial class-flip. Empirically, five adversarial networks (two precision-based, three expression-tree-based of increasing difficulty) are tested against ten verifier configurations across PyTorch and Flux on CPU and GPU, revealing that no tested verifier is practically sound against the hardest attacks (Precision and Order3).

## Results

The paper demonstrates that none of the 10 tested verifier configurations (MIPVerify, MN-BaB, β-CROWN BaB in 32-bit/64-bit CPU/GPU, GCP-CROWN, DeepPoly, RefinePoly, DeepZ, RefineZono) are practically sound: all are unsound for the Precision attack and the hardest Order3 expression-tree attack. The base MNIST model achieves 97% verified robust accuracy (l∞ radius 0.1), and unsound verifiers incorrectly certify this 97% (or 96%/94% for incomplete methods) instead of the correct 0% for backdoored networks. For the easier Order1 attack, all verifiers correctly detect the backdoor (0% robust accuracy), while for Order2, only polyhedra- and zonotope-based methods (DeepPoly, RefinePoly, DeepZ, RefineZono) succeed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the Zombori et al. (2021) numerical attack, which exploits solver-specific floating-point errors in the verification algorithm itself. In contrast, the paper's Precision and Order3 attacks exploit the gap between the full-precision model and the deployed network, and are effective against ALL verifiers regardless of implementation details. Specifically, the Zombori attack only fools verifiers that use optimization (MIPVerify, MN-BaB, RefinePoly, RefineZono), while the paper's Precision attack fools all 10 configurations and Order3 also fools all 10. For the intermediate Order2 attack, the wider intervals in polyhedra-based (DeepPoly, RefinePoly) and zonotope-based (DeepZ, RefineZono) methods accidentally cover the adversarial output, making them appear sound, whereas MILP-based (MIPVerify) and BaB-based (MN-BaB, β-CROWN, GCP-CROWN) methods fail. The paper proves theoretically (Propositions 6.2, 6.5) that these 'successful' Order2 detections are coincidental and not guaranteed, since no fixed expression tree ordering (including decreasing or decreasing-absolute-value) is universally sound (Proposition 6.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire neural network verification literature (IBP, DeepPoly, CROWN, DeepZ, MIPVerify, β-CROWN, GCP-CROWN, MN-BaB) by arguing that all existing verifiers target the wrong mathematical object: the full-precision theoretical model rather than the deployed floating-point implementation. It builds on the floating-point non-associativity literature (Schlögl et al. 2023, Villa et al. 2009) and the adversarial-attack-on-verification line (Zombori et al. 2021, Jia & Rinard 2021), but distinguishes itself by exploiting the deployment environment itself (expression trees, precision) rather than numerical errors in the verifier or precision mismatches. The parallel work of Cordeiro et al. 2025 identifies the same 'implementation gap' from a programming-language perspective, but this paper provides formal proofs of unsoundness and constructive backdoor attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that no currently known verifier is practically sound for deployed networks, which undermines the safety guarantees that the entire verification subfield has been building toward. The open question of how to achieve practical soundness is left unresolved, with the authors noting that it likely requires strong assumptions about the deployment environment and that finding optimal expression trees is probably NP-hard. For safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, medical AI), this means that existing 'certified' robustness guarantees may be vacuous in real deployments. The paper's limitation is that it identifies the problem without proposing solutions, and the backdoor constructions, while theoretically clean, use large weights and constant inputs that would be detectable in practice.
