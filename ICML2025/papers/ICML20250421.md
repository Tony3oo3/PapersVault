## Problem

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis posits that foundation models converge to a shared representation space, but representational similarity is almost always measured on a single dataset without examining whether those similarities are stable across different stimulus sets. This paper asks whether pairwise representational similarities between models transfer from one dataset to another, and which model characteristics—training objective, architecture, model size, or training data—determine this cross-dataset consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250421/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 64 pretrained vision models (CNNs and Transformers from ~12M to >1.4B parameters, trained via supervised, self-supervised, or image-text objectives) and 23 image datasets from the CLIP benchmark and Breeds, spanning natural multi-domain, natural single-domain, specialized, and structured categories. For each model-dataset pair, latent representations are extracted from model-specific layers (penultimate for supervised, average pooling for SSL, image encoder for image-text), resized to 224×224, and L2-normalized. The output is a distribution of Pearson correlation coefficients across all dataset pairs, quantifying how consistently the relative ordering of pairwise model similarities is preserved when switching evaluation datasets, broken down by model category combinations.

## Method

The core framework defines, for two model sets Θ and Φ and a dataset X, a similarity vector s_(X,Θ,Φ) containing CKA values for all model pairs, then measures cross-dataset consistency as the Pearson correlation between similarity vectors computed on two different datasets; aggregating over all dataset pairs yields R(Θ,Φ), the full distribution of consistency values. They employ CKA with a linear kernel (capturing global representational structure) and CKA with an RBF kernel at σ=0.2 (capturing local structure), plus RSA with Spearman correlation as a third metric, and demonstrate that 10,000 stratified samples suffice for stable CKA linear estimates on ImageNet-scale datasets, making the quadratic kernel computation tractable. By stratifying 64 models across four attributes (training objective, architecture, training data scale, model size) and 23 datasets across four categories, they identify training objective as the dominant factor: SSL models exhibit the most dataset-invariant relative similarities, while supervised and image-text models show weaker, more dataset-dependent consistency. The framework further links representational similarity to downstream performance gaps via linear-probe top-1 accuracy, revealing that the similarity–performance relationship is strong on single-domain and structured datasets but weak on multi-domain datasets.

## Results

Across all 23 evaluation datasets and 64 vision models, the mean Pearson correlation of pairwise representational similarities (CKA linear) between dataset pairs is 0.756 (std=0.124), indicating that the relative ordering of model similarities is largely but not perfectly preserved across datasets. Self-supervised (SSL) models exhibit the highest similarity consistency across datasets, followed by image-text models (which form a tight cluster of high absolute similarities), while supervised models show the weakest consistency. The correlation between representational similarity and downstream performance gap is strongly negative for single-domain and structured datasets but only weakly negative for multi-domain datasets like ImageNet-1k, where many high-performing model pairs remain representationally dissimilar.

**Summary of key consistency findings by model and dataset category (Pearson correlation of CKA similarities across dataset pairs).**

|---|---|---|

| SSL vs. other objectives | High | SSL models correlate more strongly with other groups than those groups correlate with themselves |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it compares representational similarity consistency across model training objectives, architectures, sizes, and dataset types. SSL models (e.g., DINOv2, SimCLR, MoCo) consistently outperform both image-text (CLIP, SigLIP, EVA-CLIP) and supervised (ResNet, ViT, ConvNeXt) models in cross-dataset similarity consistency, with the effect holding across CKA linear, CKA RBF (σ=0.2), and RSA Spearman metrics. Architecture (CNN vs. Transformer) and model size (small to xlarge) have minimal impact on consistency, as confirmed by a controlled anchor-model analysis (six models matched for architecture and size but differing in objective). For the similarity–performance relationship, single-domain datasets (Flowers, Cars, Pets) show a strong negative correlation between CKA and performance gap, whereas multi-domain datasets (ImageNet-1k, Caltech-101) show only a weak correlation, suggesting that the Platonic Representation Hypothesis's claim of performance-driven convergence is dataset-dependent rather than universal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-established toolkit of representational similarity measures (CKA, RSA, CCA) and the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024), which posits that foundation models converge to a shared representation space as a function of task performance. However, prior work has almost exclusively measured pairwise representational similarity on a single dataset, with only limited studies (Brown et al., 2024; Baek et al., 2022) touching on cross-distribution transfer. The paper carves new ground by proposing a principled framework for quantifying the *consistency* of relative pairwise similarities across multiple datasets and systematically identifying which model and dataset attributes drive that consistency, rather than simply reporting similarity values on one benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings challenge the Platonic Representation Hypothesis by showing that representational convergence is substantially dataset-dependent: the correspondence between representational similarity and task performance is strong on single-domain and structured datasets but weak on multi-domain datasets like ImageNet-1k. This has practical implications for model selection and comparison, as similarity rankings measured on one dataset may not transfer to another, particularly for supervised and image-text models. Open questions include whether the SSL consistency advantage stems from the extraction layer (average pooling vs. penultimate layer), whether findings generalize to non-vision modalities, and how to leverage consistency scores for principled model selection in deployment settings.
