## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning methods (CoT, RL) depend on parametric knowledge that is insufficient for scientific tasks where relevant information is rare in web data, while retrieval-based approaches (RAG, ToG, GraphRAG) assume access to a comprehensive knowledge base containing the correct reasoning logic. In scientific and niche domains, constructing such exhaustive knowledge bases is impractical, and the limited external information that is available often does not directly answer the query. The paper asks: can LLMs be guided to perform faithful multi-step reasoning using only sparse, indirect expert knowledge that serves as 'inspiration' rather than a direct answer?

## Input / Output

GIVE takes a natural-language query and a knowledge graph (KG) as input, where the KG can range from extremely small (135 nodes in UMLS for biomedical tasks) to very large and noisy (844k+ nodes in full ConceptNet for commonsense tasks). The KG is not expected to contain the answer directly; instead, it provides semantically related entities and relations that guide divergent reasoning. The output is a progressive, structured reasoning chain: first an answer grounded in affirmative (LLM-validated) knowledge, then refined with counterfactual (rejected) connections to suppress hallucination, and finally refined again with ground-truth KG triplets. Final answers are yes/no/maybe, multiple-choice, or free-text depending on the task.

## Method

GIVE is a training-free, graph-inspired reasoning framework that merges parametric (LLM internal) and non-parametric (KG) memories through a process called 'veracity extrapolation.' It first decomposes the query into entity and relation sets, then constructs entity groups by retrieving the p most semantically similar KG concepts for each queried entity via SentenceTransformer cosine similarity. Within each group, the LLM induces connections between the queried entity and its neighbors using internal knowledge. Across groups, GIVE identifies all potential relations (from the query and from existing KG edges between group members) and instructs the LLM to validate or reject each candidate edge, using the presence of analogous expert edges as evidence for extrapolation. Rejected edges form a counterfactual knowledge set that explicitly tells the LLM what is *not* true, mitigating hallucination. For multi-hop queries, GIVE discovers intermediate entity groups via optimal 2-hop paths. Answer generation is progressive: the LLM first answers using only affirmative knowledge, then refines with counterfactual knowledge, and finally with ground-truth KG triplets, producing a fully interpretable reasoning trace.

## Results

On biomedical QA with a sparse 135-node UMLS knowledge graph, GIVE_a+c+e with GPT3.5-turbo achieves 53.6% on PubMedQA, 88.2% on BioASQ, and 73.4% on ProcessBank, surpassing GPT4's I/O prompt scores (42.2%, 88.2%, 64.8%) and all retrieval-based baselines. On CommonsenseQA with the full ConceptNet (844k nodes), GIVE_a+c+e reaches 74.7% accuracy, outperforming RAG (71.3%) and ToG (69.8%) by 3.4 and 4.9 points respectively. On TruthfulQA (open-domain), GIVE attains the highest GPT4o win rate at 50.3%. The headline improvement is the 43.5% → 88.2% accuracy jump on BioASQ (GPT3.5-turbo, I/O prompt → GIVE_a+c+e), demonstrating that minimal external knowledge can unlock reasoning beyond the model's parametric capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIVE consistently outperforms all five baselines across biomedical, commonsense, and open-domain tasks. Against I/O prompting (Brown et al., 2020) and CoT (Wei et al., 2023), GIVE_a+c+e gains +7.4 points on PubMedQA and +44.7 points on BioASQ (GPT3.5-turbo), showing that internal-knowledge-only reasoning fails when the query requires knowledge absent from pretraining. Against text-based RAG (Lewis et al., 2021), GIVE is dramatically superior on biomedical tasks (53.6% vs 13.4% on PubMedQA) because RAG retrieves semantically similar but irrelevant passages that induce hallucination; on CommonsenseQA the gap narrows to 3.4 points (74.7% vs 71.3%). Against KG-based ToG (Sun et al., 2024), GIVE wins by 36.0 points on PubMedQA and 70.2 points on BioASQ (GPT3.5-turbo), as ToG's graph traversal prunes away the limited candidate edges available in a 135-node KG. Against GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024), GIVE outperforms on all three biomedical datasets (e.g., 88.2% vs 10.3% on BioASQ with GPT3.5-turbo), with GraphRAG excluded from larger-KG comparisons due to context-length limitations. Notably, GIVE enables GPT3.5-turbo to match or exceed GPT4 on BioASQ (88.2% vs 88.2%) and ProcessBank (73.4% vs 64.8%), demonstrating that structured external guidance can close the model-size gap in knowledge-intensive scientific reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIVE carves out a middle ground between two dominant paradigms: (1) internal-knowledge reasoning (CoT prompting, RL-based chain-of-thought) that assumes the LLM's parametric memory is sufficient, and (2) retrieval-augmented or KG-traversal methods (RAG, ToG, GraphRAG) that assume access to a comprehensive, directly-answerable knowledge base. Rather than retrieving evidence that directly resolves the query, GIVE repurposes a limited KG as a source of *analogical inspiration*—using semantically similar entities and their known relations to prompt the LLM into 'veracity extrapolation,' where it validates or rejects potential connections between the query's own concepts. This associative, counterfactual-augmented reasoning loop is conceptually distinct from both pure CoT and standard RAG/ToG traversal, and is entirely training-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that a 135-node KG can act as a 'cognitive scaffold' that lets a smaller, cheaper LLM match or exceed a much larger model on knowledge-intensive scientific QA, reducing deployment cost in domains where building exhaustive knowledge bases is infeasible. Open questions include whether the veracity-extrapolation paradigm generalizes beyond the tested biomedical/commonsense settings, how the method behaves when no KG (even a tiny one) is available, and whether the multi-call LLM overhead can be further reduced for real-time use. The paper also flags the need for RL post-training strategies that explicitly leverage external 'inspiration' rather than relying on parametric knowledge alone.
