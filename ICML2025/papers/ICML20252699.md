## Problem

Existing offline preference optimization methods face a fundamental trade-off: DPO relies on a static reference model that often fails to distinguish preferred from rejected responses, while SimPO discards the reference entirely and applies a uniform reward margin γ to all pairs, ignoring instance-wise variability in preference strength. The paper asks whether the reference model can be reparameterized to simultaneously provide a stable baseline and an adaptive, instance-specific margin.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference triplets (x, y_w, y_l) — a text prompt and a winning/losing response pair — from UltraFeedback-derived datasets, and outputs a fine-tuned language model that generates text responses. Training operates on length-normalized sequence log-likelihoods (β/|y| · log π_θ(y|x)), with the implicit reference model computed on-the-fly from the current policy and a frozen SFT reference. The stop-gradient on the implicit reference ensures it acts as a fixed target during each update step, and Z-score normalization of the margin term stabilizes training across heterogeneous pair difficulties.

## Method

AlphaDPO introduces an implicit reference model π̂_ref(y|x) ∝ U(y|x)(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x))^α that interpolates between SimPO's uniform reference (α=0) and DPO's full SFT reference (α=1), yielding an instance-adaptive reward margin M(x,y_w,y_l) = β·log[π_θ(y_w)π_ref(y_l)/(π_ref(y_w)π_θ(y_l))] that captures per-pair policy-reference divergence. The final loss is −log σ(u(x,y_w,y_l) − sg[γ + αM*(x,y_w,y_l)]), where u is the length-normalized policy log-likelihood difference, sg denotes stop-gradient, and M* is Z-score-normalized. Theoretically, Lemma 4.1 shows M approximates the difference in sequential KL divergences between π_ref and π_θ along y_w and y_l, establishing that AlphaDPO implicitly controls KL divergence at the sequence level — offering TDPO's alignment-diversity balance with greater computational efficiency and robustness to token-level noise in the reference model.

## Results

AlphaDPO achieves a 58.7% length-controlled (LC) win rate on AlpacaEval 2 and a 35.7% win rate on Arena-Hard using Llama3-Instruct v0.2 (8B), establishing it as the strongest 8B open-source model at the time. Across all four model settings (Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B, Llama3-8B v0.2, Gemma2-9B), AlphaDPO outperforms the best baseline (SimPO) by an average of ~3 percentage points on AlpacaEval 2 LC. On Arena-Hard, AlphaDPO achieves state-of-the-art or second-best results, with the largest margin over SimPO being +3.0 pp on Gemma2-9B (60.8% vs 57.8% WR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaDPO consistently outperforms all seven main baselines (DPO, IPO, CPO, KTO, ORPO, R-DPO, SimPO) on AlpacaEval 2 LC across all four model settings, with the largest margins over DPO (+6.4 pp on Llama3-8B v0.2) and ORPO (+22.2 pp on Llama3-8B v0.2). The closest competitor is SimPO, which AlphaDPO beats by 2.1–3.1 pp on AlpacaEval 2 LC and 0.7–3.0 pp on Arena-Hard WR depending on the model. On Arena-Hard, AlphaDPO achieves the best WR in three of four settings (Llama3-8B, Llama3-8B v0.2, Gemma2-9B) and is competitive on Mistral-7B. In the appendix comparison with TDPO (token-level DPO), AlphaDPO's sequence-level margin M outperforms TDPO's token-level margin δ (58.7 vs 56.9 LC on Llama3-8B v0.2), demonstrating that sequence-level approximation is more robust when the reference model is suboptimal. R-DPO, which also addresses length bias, is outperformed by AlphaDPO by 7.1 pp on AlpacaEval 2 LC (Llama3-8B v0.2) and 2.9 pp on Arena-Hard WR (Gemma2-9B).

## Relation to Prior Work

AlphaDPO unifies DPO and SimPO under a single framework by showing that SimPO is DPO with a uniform implicit reference model, then proposes an adaptive reference distribution that interpolates between the two via a smoothness parameter α. The method draws structural inspiration from the weak-to-strong preference optimization framework (Zhu et al., 2025) and establishes a theoretical equivalence between its sequence-level margin term and the token-level sequential KL divergence used in TDPO (Zeng et al., 2024), positioning AlphaDPO as a computationally efficient sequence-level alternative to token-level KL control. It departs from both DPO (which relies on a potentially miscalibrated static reference) and SimPO (which imposes a one-size-fits-all constant margin) by making the reward margin instance-adaptive through the policy-to-reference likelihood ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified reference-model perspective provides a cleaner conceptual lens for the DPO/SimPO design space, and the adaptive margin mechanism offers a principled way to handle heterogeneous preference strength across training pairs without per-instance tuning. However, the method introduces an additional hyperparameter α that requires manual tuning and whose optimal value varies across benchmarks, and it remains an offline approach that does not address online or interactive alignment. Open questions include whether α can be learned adaptively, whether the sequence-level KL approximation holds under more aggressive policy updates, and whether the approach generalizes to listwise or non-pairwise preference settings.
