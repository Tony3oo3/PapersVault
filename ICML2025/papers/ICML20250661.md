## Problem

The paper addresses what convergence rate is fundamentally achievable for last-iterate convergence of uncoupled learning in zero-sum matrix games with bandit feedback. Prior work (Cai et al., 2023; Dong et al., 2024) achieved only O(T^{-1/8}) exploitability gap bounds, and it was open whether requiring the actual policies played at each iteration to converge (rather than just the average) imposes a fundamental rate penalty. The paper proves it does, establishing a tight lower bound of Ω(T^{-1/(2+p)}) for L^p convergence, in contrast to the standard O(T^{-1/2}) rate for average-iterate convergence.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, both players independently sample actions from their current mixed policies and observe only a single stochastic loss ℓ^t ∈ [0,1] whose mean equals the true payoff; the full payoff matrix and the opponent's sampled action are never revealed, and no communication between the two algorithm instances is permitted. The output is not a single final policy but an entire learning sequence (μ^t, ν^t)_{t≥1} whose exploitability gap must converge in L^p norm at every iteration, a strictly stronger requirement than outputting one good average policy at the end of the procedure.

## Method

The paper contributes two algorithms matching the optimal rate up to logarithmic factors. The first, Simultaneous Explore or Exploit, wraps EXP3-IX in an exploration-exploitation schedule where at each step both players either advance the underlying algorithm by one iteration or play its current average output, with the exploration probability decaying as t^{-p/(2+p)}; a shared uniform seed synchronizes the two players without any communication. The second, Uncoupled Regularized EXP3, performs a two-step mirror descent: the first step regularizes the policy toward a reference distribution via KL divergence (making the pseudo-gradient operator strongly monotone), and the second step takes a standard mirror descent update using an unbiased importance-sampling loss estimate rather than the biased IX variant; the regularization parameter τ scales as T^{-1/4}, balancing the O(τ) bias of the regularized equilibrium against the O(1/(τ√T)) convergence rate. A doubling trick with a smooth exponential transition between successive regularization levels provides anytime guarantees without restarting the algorithm.

## Results

The paper establishes a lower bound of Ω(T^{-1/(2+p)}) for the L^p last-iterate convergence of uncoupled algorithms in zero-sum matrix games with bandit feedback (p ∈ (0,2]), which for p=2 gives Ω(T^{-1/4}). Two proposed algorithms match this rate up to logarithmic factors: Simultaneous Explore or Exploit achieves Õ(T^{-1/(2+p)}) for any p ∈ (0,2], and Uncoupled Regularized EXP3 achieves Õ(T^{-1/4}) for L^2. This improves upon the previous best upper bound of Õ(T^{-1/8}) (high probability) by Cai et al. (2023) and Dong et al. (2024), representing a significant rate improvement from T^{-1/8} to T^{-1/4}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior methods for last-iterate convergence in zero-sum games with bandit feedback. Cai et al. (2023) achieved Õ(T^{-1/8}) in high probability and Õ(T^{-1/6}) in L², while Dong et al. (2024) achieved Õ(T^{-1/8}) in high probability. The proposed Uncoupled Regularized EXP3 improves the L² rate from T^{-1/6} (Cai et al.) to T^{-1/4}, and the Simultaneous Explore or Exploit framework achieves T^{-1/(2+p)} for all p ∈ (0,2], which at p=2 also gives T^{-1/4}. Both proposed methods are provably optimal up to logarithmic factors, as the paper's lower bound of Ω(T^{-1/(2+p)}) matches the upper bounds. The improvement is substantial: going from T^{-1/8} to T^{-1/4} means the exploitability gap shrinks four times faster in the exponent, and the new lower bound proves this is the best possible rate for uncoupled last-iterate convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work on uncoupled learning in zero-sum games with bandit feedback initiated by Cai et al. (2023) and Dong et al. (2024), which achieved O(T^{-1/8}) exploitability gap rates. It builds on EXP3-IX (Kocák et al., 2014; Neu, 2015) as the underlying bandit algorithm and adapts the two-step mirror descent update from Munos et al. (2023) to handle non-symmetric games with bandit feedback. The key conceptual departure is a new lower bound of Ω(T^{-1/4}) showing that last-iterate convergence is fundamentally harder than average-iterate convergence in this setting, along with two algorithms (an exploration-exploitation trade-off and a regularized mirror descent) that match this bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lower bound result resolves an open question raised by Cai et al. (2023) and establishes that the T^{-1/4} rate is the best possible for uncoupled last-iterate convergence, providing a clear theoretical ceiling for future algorithm design. The regularized EXP3 algorithm is notable for avoiding the computation of average policies, which is practically important when policies are represented by neural networks. Open questions remain: whether the rate is tight for p > 2 or high-probability settings, whether synchronization between players can be eliminated, and whether optimistic mirror descent variants might achieve the optimal rate without the regularization or doubling trick machinery. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation.
