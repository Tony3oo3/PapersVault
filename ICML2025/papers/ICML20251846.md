## Problem

Existing layer-wise gradient compression schemes (e.g., L-GreCo) have shown strong empirical success but lack rigorous theoretical guarantees for handling statistical heterogeneity across layers and over training. Meanwhile, distributed variational inequality (VI) solvers such as Q-GenX rely on restrictive almost-sure boundedness assumptions and require two gradient evaluations per iteration via extra-gradient steps, limiting their applicability to monotone VIs like GAN training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251846/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of K distributed nodes receives local training data and computes stochastic dual vectors (gradients) at the current iterate. These vectors are decomposed into an L^q norm scalar, per-coordinate signs, and normalized coordinates in [0,1], which are then quantized using layer-specific sequences of levels (M types total) and encoded via a parallel coding protocol. The output is a compressed bit-string broadcast to all nodes, which is decoded back into an unbiased estimate of the dual vector for the next optimistic dual averaging update; over T iterations this yields trained model parameters (e.g., WGAN generator/discriminator or Transformer-XL weights).

## Method

The paper introduces a general layer-wise unbiased quantization framework in which M distinct sequences of quantization levels are assigned to different layer types, with levels adaptively optimized by minimizing a minimum-quantization-variance (MQV) objective over weighted conditional CDFs of normalized coordinates. A novel parallel coding protocol encodes all M types simultaneously, allowing codeword sharing across types to reduce expected code length. Built on this compression, QODA (Quantized Optimistic Dual Averaging) solves distributed monotone VIs by storing the previous iteration's compressed gradient for the extrapolation step, thereby eliminating the second gradient call required by extra-gradient methods like Q-GenX and halving per-iteration communication. Adaptive learning rates are derived from accumulated squared differences of consecutive compressed dual vectors. The paper establishes tight variance and code-length bounds for the quantization (recovering known lower bounds in the global L^2 case) and proves joint convergence rates of O(1/√T) under absolute noise and O(1/T) under relative noise, the latter without requiring co-coercivity when almost-sure boundedness holds.

## Results

For WGAN training on CIFAR-10/100 with 12 GPUs, QODA with 5-bit layer-wise quantization achieves a 2.50× speedup (127 ms vs. 318 ms per step) over the uncompressed baseline, and a 2.47× speedup at 16 GPUs. Under bandwidth-limited conditions (1 Gbps), QODA still delivers a 1.47× speedup (197 ms vs. 291 ms). For Transformer-XL on WikiText-103, layer-wise quantization (L-GreCo) achieves 1.47×–1.52× higher compression rates than global quantization at the same PowerSGD rank while matching or exceeding baseline perplexity (e.g., 23.70 vs. 23.20 at rank 16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Q-GenX (Ramezani-Kebrya et al., 2023), a distributed extra-gradient VI solver with global quantization. QODA with layer-wise quantization outperforms Q-GenX on WGAN training: Figure 4 shows that the layer-wise (L-GreCo) variant achieves lower FID scores throughout training compared to the global-quantization Q-GenX variant on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, while also reducing per-step communication by half (one gradient step instead of two). Against the uncompressed baseline, QODA delivers up to a 150% (2.5×) speedup in end-to-end training time at 12 GPUs, and the speedup grows with node count (1.28× at 4 GPUs → 2.50× at 12 GPUs), whereas the uncompressed baseline actually degrades in wall-clock time beyond 8 GPUs. For Transformer-XL on WikiText-103, layer-wise quantization consistently achieves 1.47×–1.52× higher compression rates than global quantization at the same PowerSGD rank (e.g., 40.38× vs. 27.44× at rank 16) while maintaining comparable test perplexity (23.70 vs. 23.73).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributed variational inequality (VI) solving literature by replacing the extra-gradient approach of Q-GenX (Ramezani-Kebrya et al., 2023) with optimistic dual averaging, halving the per-iteration communication cost, and by substituting global quantization with a general layer-wise quantization framework that assigns M distinct adaptive sequences of levels to different layers. The layer-wise framework provides the first tight variance and code-length bounds for adaptive per-layer quantization, formally grounding the empirically successful L-GreCo method (Markov et al., 2024) and generalizing the global quantization bounds of QSGD (Alistarh et al., 2017) and NUQSGD (Ramezani-Kebrya et al., 2021) to the multi-sequence, L^q-normalized setting. A key departure from prior VI solvers is the elimination of the almost sure boundedness assumption on stochastic dual vectors and, under relative noise, the co-coercivity assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap by providing the first rigorous theoretical framework for layer-wise quantization with tight bounds that match known lower bounds in the global L^2 case, giving practitioners a principled basis for per-layer compression decisions rather than relying on heuristics. The 150% end-to-end speedup for WGAN training on 12+ GPUs demonstrates practical relevance in bandwidth-constrained distributed settings, and the Transformer-XL ablation (embedding layer far more sensitive to quantization than attention or FF layers) offers actionable guidance for compression budgets. Open questions include extending the framework to non-monotone or Minty VIs, applying layer-wise quantization to adversarial training, and whether the optimistic dual averaging advantage persists in asynchronous or heterogeneous-node settings.
