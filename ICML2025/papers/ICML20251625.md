## Problem

Existing multi-modal clustering methods either perform shallow consensus fusion across modalities or focus on only one aspect (feature distribution or clustering assignment) without jointly exploiting both. Crucially, all prior deep information-bottleneck-based MMC methods ignore the rich latent information contained in the encoder's intermediate hidden layers, failing to capture the hierarchical interdependencies between modalities that could improve clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251625/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted multi-modal feature vectors of varying dimensions (e.g., SIFT at 1200-d, BoW at 500-d, Color Attention/SIFT/TPLBP at 1000-d each) as input, with the number of modalities M and number of clusters K specified a priori. Each modality is processed independently by its own encoder, and the final cluster assignment for each sample is determined by majority voting across modalities (Eq. 2). The method operates in a fully unsupervised setting with no ground-truth labels during training.

## Method

SDCIB employs M modality-specific variational IB encoders (4 FC layers: 1024→1024→1024→128 with BatchNorm+ReLU) and, for the first time, explicitly introduces all intermediate hidden-layer representations H_1^i, …, H_{L-1}^i into the objective function alongside the final compressed representation H_L^i. The loss has two coupled components: an information-bottleneck term that compresses each modality while retaining relevance to its clustering output, and a dual consistency term that maximizes mutual information between modalities at both the feature level (I(H_l^i; H_l^j) for every layer l) and the clustering-assignment level (I(Y^i; Y^j)). Mutual information is estimated via three complementary strategies: a variational (KL-divergence) bound for I(X^i; H_l^i), InfoNCE contrastive estimation for cross-modal feature and assignment consistency, and MINE for I(Y^i; H_l^i) to avoid dimension-mismatch issues. A linear+softmax clustering head produces soft assignments per modality, and the overall loss L_SDCIB = α·L_SIB − (1−α)·L_Con is optimized with Adam (lr=1e-4, batch=32, 40 epochs).

## Results

SDCIB achieves the best ACC and NMI across all four multi-modal datasets: 67.5% ACC / 59.2% NMI on Caltech-2V, 56.5% ACC / 36.4% NMI on Event, 52.9% ACC / 28.7% NMI on IAPR, and 61.4% ACC / 44.7% NMI on ESP. The headline improvement over the strongest baseline is 9.3% ACC on ESP (vs. MFLVC's 52.1%) and 7.4% ACC on Event (vs. CoMVC's 49.1%). NMI gains range from +0.9 (Event) to +7.8 (ESP) over the best competing method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDCIB outperforms all 14 baselines on both ACC and NMI across all four datasets. Among deep MMC methods, the closest competitor is MFLVC (CVPR'22), which SDCIB surpasses by 6.0% ACC on Caltech-2V (67.5 vs. 61.5), 8.0% on Event (56.5 vs. 48.5), 5.6% on IAPR (52.9 vs. 47.3), and 9.3% on ESP (61.4 vs. 52.1). CoMVC (CVPR'21) is the second-strongest baseline on Event (49.1% ACC) and IAPR (46.7% ACC), where SDCIB leads by 7.4% and 5.6% respectively. DIVICE (AAAI'24) achieves the best ACC on Caltech-2V among baselines (64.1%) but SDCIB still leads by 3.4%. Traditional methods (CoregMVSC, RMKMC, SwMC, ONMSC) and single/full-modal baselines (KM, Ncuts, AmKM, AmNcuts) are consistently 10–30+ points behind SDCIB, confirming the advantage of the deep information-bottleneck formulation with hidden-layer exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of information bottleneck theory, contrastive learning, and deep multi-modal clustering. It departs from prior multi-modal IB methods (Federici et al. 2020, Yan et al. 2024/2025) that only exploited the final encoder output, by explicitly incorporating all intermediate hidden-layer representations into the objective function. It also extends the consistency paradigm used in contrastive multi-view clustering (CoMVC, MFLVC, DIVICE) by enforcing alignment at both the feature-distribution and cluster-assignment levels simultaneously, rather than relying on a single consistency signal. The variational IB encoder with Gaussian latent assumptions follows the standard VAE-style parameterization, while the mutual information estimation strategy (variational bound + InfoNCE + MINE) is a pragmatic combination of existing estimators rather than a new estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is showing that intermediate encoder layers carry useful cross-modal structure that, when explicitly regularized, yields consistent 3–10% gains over strong deep baselines on four standard benchmarks. However, the evaluated datasets (Caltech-2V, Event, IAPR, ESP) are small (1.4K–11K samples) and over a decade old, limiting confidence in scalability to modern large-scale multi-modal settings. The authors themselves flag open challenges: handling incomplete/missing modalities, removing the need for a pre-specified cluster count K, and moving from batch to online/streaming learning. Whether the hidden-layer consistency signal remains beneficial at much greater network depth or with transformer-based encoders remains untested.
