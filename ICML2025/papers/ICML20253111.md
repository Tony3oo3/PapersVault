## Problem

Existing algorithms for learning IIEFGs with bandit feedback (Bai et al., 2022; Fiegel et al., 2023) only guarantee average-iterate convergence, requiring all generated policies to be averaged—a step that becomes infeasible when policies are approximated by nonlinear function approximators such as neural networks. Meanwhile, last-iterate convergence algorithms for IIEFGs (Lee et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023) rely on full-information feedback and cannot operate in the bandit setting. This paper answers the open question of whether finite-time last-iterate convergence can be achieved for IIEFGs under bandit feedback with fully uncoupled players.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires the game tree structure (the infoset-action space and its descendant relationships) as a priori knowledge, which can be extracted in O(XA) time via a single traversal. At each step, each player observes only their own infoset and the scalar reward from the single experienced action (bandit feedback), without access to the opponent's policy, the underlying state, or state transition probabilities. The output is an approximate NE policy profile (μ^k, ν^k) at every episode k, with a last-iterate convergence rate of Õ(k^{-1/8}) with high probability and Õ(k^{-1/6}) in expectation, and the two players operate entirely independently with no communication or shared randomness.

## Method

The algorithm applies OMD with a negentropy regularizer weighted by a 'virtual transition' p^x over the infoset-action space, which regularizes probability measures over infoset-action pairs rather than sequence-form policies directly. This design circumvents the coupling between infoset-action pairs on different steps inherent in the dilated negentropy regularizer, which would otherwise yield a vacuous stability term when combined with entropy regularization under bandit feedback. The virtual transition is computed via backward dynamic programming (Algorithm 2) to maximize the minimum visitation probability across all infosets, ensuring the Bregman divergence-based stability term remains bounded by O(X). The entropy regularization technique perturbs the bilinear game into a strongly convex–strongly concave surrogate, and the resulting OMD update admits an efficient closed-form solution via backward induction (Algorithm 3), avoiding the need for iterative projection onto the constrained policy set.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical convergence rate guarantees. Under bandit feedback, Algorithm 1 achieves a last-iterate NE gap of Õ((X+Y)[(XA+YB)^{1/2}+(X+Y)^{1/4}H]k^{-1/8}) with high probability for each episode k, and an improved rate of Õ((X+Y)[(X²A+Y²B)^{1/2}+(X+Y)^{1/4}H]k^{-1/6}) in expectation. A matching lower bound of Ω(√(XA+YB)·k^{-1/2}) is established for any uncoupled algorithm in this setting. Empirical evaluations on Lewis Signaling, Kuhn Poker, Leduc Poker, and Liars Dice (deferred to Appendix I) show competitive performance against baseline methods, though no specific numerical comparisons are provided in the main text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions itself against two families of prior work. For full-information last-iterate convergence, Lee et al. (2021) and Liu et al. (2023) achieve Õ(1/k) rates, but both require full-information feedback (and Lee et al. additionally assumes a unique NE; Liu et al. requires a central controller), making them inapplicable to the bandit feedback setting. For bandit feedback, Bai et al. (2022) and Fiegel et al. (2023) achieve (nearly) optimal sample complexity of Õ(H(XA+YB)/ε²) but only provide average-iterate convergence guarantees, which are infeasible when policies are parameterized by nonlinear function approximators. The proposed algorithm is the first to combine bandit feedback with last-iterate convergence, at the cost of a slower k^{-1/8} rate (vs. k^{-1} for full-info methods). The gap between the upper bound (k^{-1/8}) and the lower bound (k^{-1/2}) remains open, and the authors note that closing it would require optimistic OMD/FTRL techniques that are currently difficult to stabilize under bandit feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) OMD/FTRL algorithms with balanced transitions for IIEFGs under bandit feedback (Bai et al., 2022; Fiegel et al., 2023), which achieve near-optimal sample complexity but only with average-iterate convergence, and (2) entropy-regularized OMD achieving last-iterate convergence for IIEFGs under full-information feedback (Lee et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023). The key departure is the identification that the dilated negentropy regularizer—standard in all prior IIEFG work—produces vacuous stability bounds under bandit feedback due to coupled infoset-action pairs, and the introduction of a virtual transition-weighted negentropy that decouples these pairs while retaining a closed-form policy update. The analysis draws on the entropy regularization framework of Cen et al. (2021) and Cai et al. (2023) for Markov games, adapted to the partially observable sequential setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm providing finite-time last-iterate convergence guarantees for IIEFGs under bandit feedback, which is practically important because averaging policies is infeasible when policies are represented by nonlinear function approximators (e.g., neural networks as in DeepStack/Libratus). The algorithm is fully uncoupled, model-free, and requires no inter-player communication, making it applicable to realistic adversarial settings. However, the convergence rate of Õ(k^{-1/8}) with high probability leaves a significant gap to the Ω(k^{-1/2}) lower bound, and the authors identify optimistic OMD/FTRL as a promising but technically challenging direction for improvement. The requirement of knowing the game tree structure a priori (though extractable in O(XA) time) limits applicability to games with exponentially large trees like no-limit Texas Hold'em.
