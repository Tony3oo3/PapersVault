## Problem

LLMs used as pairwise comparators in decision-making systems frequently produce logically inconsistent judgments—violating transitivity, commutativity, and negation invariance—yet prior consistency research has focused narrowly on factual knowledge or entailment for limited statement pairs. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a universal framework to quantify logical preference consistency across arbitrary item sets and demonstrating that these properties serve as strong proxies for overall model reliability and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes a set of N items with a uniform binary relation and an LLM acting as a pairwise comparison operator F: X×X → {r_ij, r_ji}, producing three scores: s_tran(K) (fraction of K-node subgraphs that are acyclic, with K configurable and M=1000 sampled subgraphs), s_comm (fraction of pairs where judgment is invariant to item order), and s_neg (fraction of pairs where the model correctly reverses under negated prompting). For REPAIR, the input is a sparse, noisy set of pairwise preference annotations (e.g., 14.8K comparisons with ~10% synthetic label noise), and the output is a refined, self-consistent, and augmented dataset (up to 61.8K comparisons) ready for instruction-tuning.

## Method

REPAIR (Ranking Estimation and Preference Augmentation through Information Refinement) first estimates a global ranking from noisy pairwise data using the win-loss rate method, which is robust to sparse comparisons and order-invariant. It then extrapolates this ranking into a complete, conflict-free pairwise comparison set, and optionally augments it with negated-relation comparisons to improve negation invariance. The refined data is used for instruction-tuning (LoRA, r=16, α=64, lr=5e-5, 2 epochs) of Llama-3-8B-Instruct. The key insight is that establishing a coherent ranking before generating additional comparisons avoids propagating the original noise, yielding models with significantly higher transitivity and commutativity while preserving human alignment. The evaluation framework itself is grounded in relational algebra: transitivity is checked via cycle detection on directed relation graphs, commutativity via pairwise order-swap invariance, and negation invariance via explicit negated-relation prompting.

## Results

On the Summarize from Feedback dataset, REPAIR-ed data achieves s_tran(5)=95.4 and s_comm=91.2, substantially outperforming perturbed data (91.9/88.4) while maintaining human agreement at 70.1% (vs. 70.3% for perturbed). Adding negated comparisons (REPAIR-ed+Neg.) boosts negation invariance from 60.8 to 87.9. In the downstream PairS ranking task on SummEval, Phi-3-medium (s_tran(5)=96.2) achieves the best Spearman correlation of 38.9, outperforming Mistral-7B (27.7) and GPT-3.5-turbo (33.5), confirming that higher transitivity directly translates to better ranking performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the PairS ranking experiments (Table 4), Mistral-7B has the highest transitivity (s_tran(5)=95.1) among the three compared models but the lowest commutativity (59.9), yielding the worst raw PairS score (27.7) and the largest calibration gain (+3.5). GPT-3.5-turbo has the lowest transitivity (82.5) and moderate commutativity (67.5), achieving PairS of 33.5 with a +2.8 calibration gain. Llama-3-8B, with moderate transitivity (91.0) but high commutativity (76.1), reaches PairS of 37.7 and requires the least calibration (+1.2). The paper's key finding is that transitivity—not human agreement accuracy—drives PairS performance: Phi-3-mini (H.=65.6, s_tran=92.8) outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo (H.=66.3, s_tran=82.5) in PairS despite lower human alignment. REPAIR-ed Llama-3-8B on MS MARCO further demonstrates the framework's generalizability, improving PairS from 58.1 (perturbed) to 61.0 while reducing calibration need from +2.2 to +1.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM consistency literature, which has previously focused on first-order relations (2–3 statement entailment consistency in NLI, factual knowledge symmetry/transitivity in QA) to a universal multi-item preference consistency framework grounded in relational algebra. It draws on rank aggregation methods (win-loss rate, Elo, Bradley-Terry) from social choice theory and repurposes the RLHF reward-model idea—estimating a global ordering from noisy pairwise data—as a data refinement mechanism (REPAIR) for instruction tuning. The paper also connects to the positional/permutation bias literature (Wang et al. 2024b, Liusie et al. 2024a) by formalizing commutativity as a measurable property and linking it to human alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a simple, task-agnostic set of three metrics (transitivity, commutativity, negation invariance) to diagnose logical incoherence in LLM judges before deploying them in ranking or decision pipelines, and REPAIR offers a low-cost data-side fix that improves consistency without sacrificing human alignment. Limitations include evaluation of the training pipeline only on 8B-parameter models, restriction to binary preference relations, and the fact that the consistency gains, while consistent across datasets, are moderate in absolute terms. Open questions include whether the framework and REPAIR scale to larger models or non-preference relational tasks (e.g., multi-hop reasoning), and whether the observed CoT-consistency trade-off persists under stronger reasoning models.
