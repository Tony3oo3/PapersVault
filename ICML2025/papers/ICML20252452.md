## Problem

GraphODE models for coupled dynamical systems suffer from poor generalization under limited observational data due to two structural flaws: static attributes (e.g., material properties) become entangled with dynamic states during initialization, and the coupling factor learns context-specific interaction patterns rather than universal physical laws. The paper formalizes these issues as backdoor paths in a Structural Causal Model and proposes targeted interventions to sever them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252452/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M timesteps of d-dimensional node feature vectors (historical trajectories) along with a graph adjacency matrix encoding pairwise interactions among 3–5 coupled entities (springs, charged particles, or pendulums). It outputs predicted future node trajectories over a configurable prediction horizon T, evaluated under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings where OOD test sets shift at least one physical parameter (box size, velocity norm, interaction strength, or stick length/mass) outside the training range.

## Method

GREAT introduces two modules grounded in SCM-based causal analysis. The Dynamic-Static Equilibrium Decoupler (DyStaED) projects the encoded latent state onto two learnable subspaces (S_static and S_dynamic) with an orthogonality loss, separating time-invariant attributes from dynamic patterns; a Dynamic Hawkes Process Augmentation then enriches the dynamic component with exponentially decaying temporal dependencies. The Causal Mediation for Coupled Dynamics (CMCD) models the coupling factor η_i(t) as a K-dimensional categorical latent variable sampled via Gumbel-Softmax, decomposes the state into trigonometric sub-states (sin/cos modes), and modulates the ODE evolution per coupling pathway. A variational KL term regularizes the coupling posterior against a uniform context-agnostic prior, yielding an ELBO-style objective L = L_predict − L_KL + λ_o·L_o that enforces both reconstruction fidelity and disentanglement.

## Results

GREAT achieves the best RMSE and MAPE across all three coupled dynamical system datasets (SPRING, CHARGED, PENDULUM) in both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings. On SPRING, GREAT reaches RMSE 3.687 / MAPE 2.348 (ID) and RMSE 3.619 / MAPE 4.521 (OOD), outperforming the closest competitor PGODE (RMSE 3.901 ID, 4.021 OOD). The advantage is most pronounced in OOD scenarios, e.g., on PENDULUM OOD GREAT achieves RMSE 18.24 vs. LG-ODE's 19.35 and PGODE's 20.19, and on CHARGED OOD RMSE 2.690 vs. PGODE's 2.904.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GREAT outperforms all nine baselines on every metric (RMSE and MAPE) across all three datasets in both ID and OOD settings. The closest competitors are PGODE and TREAT, which are the most recent GraphODE methods; on SPRING ID, GREAT improves RMSE by ~5.5% over PGODE (3.687 vs. 3.901) and ~5.7% over TREAT (3.687 vs. 3.892). In OOD settings the gap widens: on PENDULUM OOD, GREAT achieves RMSE 18.24, beating LG-ODE (19.35) by 5.7% and PGODE (20.19) by 9.7%, while on CHARGED OOD GREAT's RMSE of 2.690 surpasses PGODE (2.904) by 7.4%. Traditional methods (VAR, LSTM) and earlier Neural ODE approaches (LatentODE, HODEN, TRS-ODEN) are substantially outperformed, with VAR showing the worst OOD degradation (e.g., RMSE 24.55 on SPRING OOD vs. GREAT's 3.619). The ablation in Figure 5 confirms that both DyStaED and CMCD contribute, with CMCD having a larger impact in OOD scenarios, validating that causal regularization of coupling dynamics is the key driver of generalization gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GraphODE lineage (Neural ODEs, Coupled Graph ODE, PGODE) by introducing a Structural Causal Model analysis to diagnose why existing architectures fail to generalize. While prior work (Huang et al., 2021; Luo et al., 2024) focused on refining the encoder for better state initialization, this paper identifies the processor's coupling factor as a second, overlooked source of distributional bias. The DHPA module repurposes the Hawkes process decay kernel (Okawa et al., 2021) for temporal augmentation of dynamic states, and the CMCD module adapts variational deconfounding (Wu et al., 2024a) to the ODE state-evolution context. The key conceptual departure is framing generalization failure as two identifiable backdoor paths in an SCM rather than as a generic distribution-shift problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCM-based diagnosis provides a principled, interpretable explanation for GraphODE generalization failures that goes beyond empirical observation, potentially guiding future architecture design beyond this specific method. However, the evaluation is limited to three small physics-based simulation datasets (3–5 entities each), leaving open whether the causal deconfounding approach scales to larger, noisier, or real-world coupled systems. The finding that CMCD's benefit is disproportionately larger in OOD settings suggests the coupling-factor regularization is the more critical component, but the moderate absolute gains over strong baselines like TREAT and PGODE (roughly 5–15% RMSE reduction) temper the practical urgency. An open question is whether the orthogonal subspace decomposition and Gumbel-Softmax coupling estimation remain effective when the number of entities or coupling pathways grows substantially.
