## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods assume all views are clean, yet real-world multi-source data is pervasively noisy, causing severe performance degradation that can even fall below single-view baselines. Prior noise-robust approaches (e.g., RMCNC, MVCAN) enhance feature robustness but lack dedicated mechanisms to explicitly identify which samples are noisy and rectify them in an unsupervised setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view dataset {x^v}_{v=1}^V with N samples across V views (ranging from 2 to 5 views, 544 to 13,000 samples), where noise is injected at ratios from 10% to 90% into non-first views. The first view is assumed clean as a reference. Output consists of K cluster assignments and per-sample soft class probability vectors, along with per-sample clean/noisy probability estimates (φ_i) derived from a two-component GMM. The architecture uses per-view autoencoder encoders/decoders with MLP projectors and classifier heads.

## Method

AIRMVC comprises three coupled components: (1) Noisy identification reformulates noise detection as anomaly detection by modeling representation distributions with a GMM whose latent variable is replaced by the network's soft predictions, then applying a two-component GMM to estimate each sample's clean probability φ_i; (2) Hybrid rectification interpolates between a view's own soft prediction and the first (clean) view's prediction, weighted by φ_i, and trains via a cross-entropy rectification loss; (3) A noise-robust contrastive mechanism uses soft-prediction agreement (threshold τ) as an additional validation criterion beyond index-based pairing to construct reliable cross-view positive/negative pairs. The overall objective combines autoencoder reconstruction loss, rectification loss, and contrastive loss (all weighted by α=β=1.0), optimized with Adam over 400 epochs after 100-epoch autoencoder pre-training. A theoretical proof (Theorem 4.1) shows the learned representations retain clean information (I(E*;y) ≥ I(x;y)−ϑ) while discarding noisy information (I(E*;y') ≤ I(x;y')−η+ϑ).

## Results

AIRMVC achieves the best performance across all six benchmark datasets (BBCSport, WebKB, Reuters, UCI-digit, Caltech101, STL10) at every noise level tested (10%–90%). At 10% noise, AIRMVC reaches 93.95% ACC / 89.08% NMI on UCI-digit, 83.73% ACC / 27.15% NMI on WebKB, and 49.45% ACC / 28.63% NMI on BBCSport, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.4–9.6% in ACC and up to ~13% in NMI. Even at 90% noise, AIRMVC maintains 58.20% ACC on UCI-digit and 59.18% ACC on WebKB, where most baselines drop below 30% ACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIRMVC outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and noise level tested. The largest margins appear on NMI: on BBCSport (10% noise), AIRMVC's 28.63 NMI exceeds SCE-MVC (15.96) by 12.67 points and MVCAN (18.99) by 9.64 points. On UCI-digit (10% noise), AIRMVC (93.95 ACC / 89.08 NMI) beats DIVIDE (88.20 / 80.28) by 5.75 ACC and 8.80 NMI, and MVCAN (86.50 / 79.00) by 7.45 ACC. Among noise-robust baselines, RMCNC and MVCAN are the closest competitors on WebKB (78.12 and 77.83 ACC vs. AIRMVC's 83.73), but both degrade sharply at higher noise (e.g., RMCNC drops to 52.71 ACC at 70% noise vs. AIRMVC's 62.89). Classical deep MVC methods (TGM-MVC, SCE-MVC, DealMVC, SURE, MFLVC) show the steepest performance collapse under noise, confirming that without explicit noise identification and rectification, multi-view fusion amplifies rather than mitigates corruption. At the extreme 90% noise setting, AIRMVC still leads by 1.5–10+ points in ACC over the best competing method on every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIRMVC extends the noise-robust multi-view clustering line initiated by RMCNC (Sun et al., 2024) and MVCAN (Xu et al., 2024), which focused on enhancing feature robustness through noise-tolerant losses or unshared architectures. The key departure is the explicit reformulation of noise identification as an anomaly-detection problem solved via a two-component GMM, coupled with a hybrid interpolation-based rectification strategy—neither of which appears in prior noise-robust MVC methods. The contrastive mechanism builds on the soft-prediction-guided pair construction idea from DIVIDE (Lu et al., 2024) and CANDY (Guo et al., 2024), but adds a confidence threshold on cross-view prediction agreement to filter unreliable pairs. Conceptually, the paper repurposes classical GMM/EM machinery inside a deep autoencoder-contrastive pipeline, bridging statistical anomaly detection and modern representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that noisy multi-view clustering can underperform single-view clustering (Table 4, Fig. 3) is a useful caution for practitioners who assume more views always help. The GMM-based identification-then-rectification pipeline offers a principled, interpretable alternative to purely learned denoising, and the information-theoretic guarantee (Theorem 4.1) provides a rare theoretical grounding for contrastive representations in noisy settings. However, the assumption that the first view is clean is a significant practical limitation that restricts applicability to scenarios where at least one view is reliably noise-free. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to structured or correlated noise, incomplete multi-view settings, and larger-scale datasets beyond the 13K-sample STL10 used here.
