## Problem

Infinite-width neural networks in the lazy training regime are well-described by static kernel machines (NTK, NNGP), but in the rich feature-learning regime where internal representations adapt to data, it was unknown whether kernel predictors still exist and what characterizes them. This paper shows that two distinct infinite-width limits in the rich regime do yield nonparametric kernel predictors, but with data-dependent (adaptive) kernels that evolve during training, closing a gap between kernel theory and the feature-learning behavior of modern deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset of P input-label pairs, a test point, and per-layer hyperparameters (feature-learning strength γ₀, inverse temperature β, and ridge coefficients λ_ℓ). It produces a scalar prediction f(x) in the form of a kernel regression f(x) = σ(Σ_μ Δ_μ K(x, x_μ)), where the kernel K is a P×P matrix that adapts to the training data. Internally, the solver outputs adaptive feature kernels Φ^ℓ and dual kernels Φ̂^ℓ at each hidden layer ℓ, which encode non-Gaussian preactivation densities and low-rank label-aligned spikes absent in lazy kernels.

## Method

The paper identifies two novel infinite-width limits of noisy gradient-flow dynamics under μP parameterization that yield adaptive kernel predictors. The first (aNBK) takes t→∞ then N→∞ at fixed temperature, interpreting the stationary distribution as a Bayesian posterior; the partition function is exponentially dominated by saddle points of a min-max optimization over kernel and dual-kernel order parameters, where the single-site densities Z_ℓ are non-Gaussian and depend on the dual kernels. The second (aNTK) takes β→∞ then N→∞ then t→∞, using DMFT fixed-point equations for gradient flow with weight decay, where the non-Markovian stochastic field dynamics converge to a fixed kernel predictor. In both cases, the feature-learning strength γ₀ > 0 drives kernels to develop data-dependent structure (low-rank spikes aligned with labels, non-Gaussian preactivations), and the lazy NNGP/NTK kernels are recovered as the γ₀→0 limit. For deep linear networks with whitened data, the saddle-point equations reduce to scalar recursions yielding exact exponential scaling of kernel-label overlaps with depth.

## Results

The paper derives two adaptive kernel predictors (aNBK and aNTK) for infinitely wide MLPs and CNNs in the feature-learning (μP) regime, showing that the final network predictor at convergence is a kernel machine with data-adaptive kernels. For deep linear networks with whitened data, the kernel-label overlap c_ℓ grows exponentially with layer depth ℓ, with scaling c_L ~ Lγ₀²/ln(Lγ₀²) in the large-depth limit. Numerically, on a two-layer MLP trained on CIFAR-10 (airplane vs. automobile), the aNTK and aNBK predictors achieve lower test loss than the lazy NNGPK at large sample sizes P, while matching the performance of a finite-width N=5000 network trained with Langevin dynamics. For a 5-layer Tanh MLP on MNIST (P=50), the aNBK theory reproduces empirical layer kernels with alignments of 97%, 81%, 73%, and 89% at layers 1–4 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its adaptive kernels (aNBK, aNTK) against four baselines: the lazy NNGPK, the lazy NTK, the full NNGP (with posterior variance), and finite-width trained networks. On CIFAR-10 (two-layer MLP, squared loss), both aNTK (γ₀=0.3) and aNBK (γ₀=0.3) achieve lower test loss than NNGPK at large P, with a clear performance gap that widens as sample size increases; however, at very small P, lazy learning can be competitive due to overfitting. The NNGP baseline (blue curve in Fig. 3a) performs similarly to NNGPK for the mean predictor but includes an additional variance term from averaging over random weights. The adaptive kernel predictors closely track the empirical predictors of finite-width N=5000 (MLP) and N=1028 (CNN) networks trained with Langevin dynamics or gradient flow with weight decay, confirming the infinite-width theory at moderate widths. For CNNs on CIFAR-10 (animate/inanimate), the two-layer CNN with kernel size k=8 outperforms the two-layer MLP at large P for the same feature-learning strength γ₀, and the DMFT theory matches the empirical CNN predictor closely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width kernel-machine characterization of neural networks (NTK, NNGP) from the lazy regime to the feature-learning (rich) regime under the μP parameterization. It builds directly on the DMFT framework of Bordelon & Pehlevan (2022) for feature-learning dynamics, but reinterprets the fixed points as kernel predictors rather than time-evolving fields. Crucially, it departs from proportional-limit adaptive kernel theories (Fischer et al. 2024, Seroussi et al. 2023) that treat feature learning as an O(1/N) perturbation: here, with P fixed and N→∞, kernels deterministically and non-perturbatively adapt to data, and the preactivation densities are genuinely non-Gaussian at infinite width.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that both the Bayesian (noisy gradient flow) and deterministic (gradient flow + weight decay) infinite-width limits yield kernel predictors with data-adaptive kernels provides a unifying representer-theorem description of feature-learning networks, opening the door to training infinitely-wide networks directly through their kernel equivalents. The deep linear analysis yielding exact exponential alignment propagation across depth is a clean analytical result with clear scaling laws. Open questions include the computational cost of solving the min-max problem for deep nonlinear networks, whether the kernel description remains accurate at moderate finite widths, and extension to architectures beyond MLPs and CNNs (e.g., transformers).
