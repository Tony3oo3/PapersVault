## Problem

During multi-task fine-tuning of MLLMs, open-response tasks such as image captioning exhibit progressive performance degradation due to over-reliance on language priors, while fixed-choice tasks remain stable. Existing anti-overfitting strategies (partial update masks, stiff penalty regularization) are designed for single-task adaptation and fail to address this task-specific fitting inconsistency in multi-task settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250997/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a textual instruction prompt as input to an MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B or VILA-1.5-3B) and produces a free-form language response (caption) or a single-choice letter (VQA). Unusually, the method also constructs a second noisy visual input by mixing the original image with Gaussian noise sampled from its own feature-space statistics at a 50% ratio, and processes both branches in parallel. The confidence alignment loss is applied selectively to open-response samples (identified by ground-truth label length > 2 tokens), while fixed-choice samples (label length = 2, i.e., one letter + EOS) are excluded.

## Method

NRCA (Noise Resilient Confidence Alignment) introduces a dual-branch training objective that forces the MLLM to produce consistent token prediction confidence under both clean and Gaussian-noise-corrupted visual inputs. The Noisy Visual Mixup component generates distorted images by sampling from a per-image Gaussian distribution (mean and variance extracted from the image's own features) and blending at ratio δ=0.5, avoiding semantic drift that plagues standard augmentations like color jitter. The Token Confidence Alignment component computes the average softmax probability assigned to ground-truth tokens across all positions for both branches, then penalizes their relative difference weighted by the empirical cross-entropy loss, making the regularization adaptive to sample difficulty. This avoids the instability of full distribution alignment over a 32K-vocabulary space with variable-length outputs. The final loss is L_CE + λ·L_NRCA with λ=2, and the normal-branch signals are detached to prevent optimization degradation.

## Results

On the primary VILA-3B architecture (E=3), NRCA achieves an average CIDER/Top-1 score of 83.24 on Flickr30k+ScienceQA, 105.96 on COCO-Cap+ScienceQA, 81.49 on Flickr30k+IconQA, and 103.51 on COCO-Cap+IconQA, outperforming the strongest baseline (Mag Mask) by 0.01–3.77 points across combinations. On the larger LLaVA-7B model, NRCA yields even larger gains, e.g., 79.67 AVG on Flickr30k+ScienceQA vs. 77.41 for L2-Reg and 75.71 for Full FT. The method's advantage is most pronounced on open-response captioning tasks (CIDER), where Full FT degrades with more epochs while NRCA maintains or improves performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRCA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every task combination and both architectures. The closest competitors are Ran Mask and Mag Mask (partial update mask methods), which achieve 83.02 and 83.23 AVG on VILA Flickr30k+ScienceQA respectively, versus NRCA's 83.24 — a narrow margin on VQA-heavy combinations but a much larger gap on captioning (76.00 vs. 75.37 CIDER on Flickr30k). Stiff penalty methods (Grafting, L2-Reg) trail Full FT on several configurations, e.g., Grafting scores 79.69 vs. Full FT's 79.47 on VILA Flickr30k+ScienceQA but drops to 77.84 on Flickr30k+IconQA, confirming the paper's claim that these methods are hyper-parameter sensitive. On the larger LLaVA-7B model, NRCA's advantage widens substantially: it gains +3.26 AVG over the best baseline (L2-Reg, 77.41) on Flickr30k+ScienceQA and +7.22 on COCO-Cap+ScienceQA, while baselines show inconsistent or degraded performance relative to Full FT. The paper also notes that at E=5, Full FT's open-response scores drop (e.g., Flickr30k CIDER falls from 70.33 to 64.67 on VILA), whereas NRCA maintains or improves (70.87), directly demonstrating the overfitting mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the contrastive learning paradigm (augmentation invariance) from representation learning into the MLLM fine-tuning setting, but departs from both existing anti-overfitting strategies for MLLMs. Prior work addresses overfitting via Partial Update Mask (Random Mask, Magnitude Mask) or Stiff Penalty Regularization (L2-Reg, Grafting), which the authors argue apply uniform penalties across all samples and fail to account for the fitting-condition asymmetry between open-response and fixed-choice tasks. Additionally, while contrastive decoding methods (e.g., VCD, RIT) use noisy visual inputs at inference time to suppress hallucinations, NRCA inverts this by treating the noisy branch as a training-time signal for confidence alignment, thereby strengthening visual representations rather than merely correcting outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical and previously under-examined failure mode: open-response tasks (captioning) degrade during multi-task MLLM fine-tuning while fixed-choice tasks remain stable, attributed to language prior overfitting. NRCA offers a lightweight (O(B×(|x̃^v|+|z̃|)) vs. O(|θ|) for parameter-based methods) and model-agnostic solution that does not require storing pre-trained weights. However, the evaluation is limited to 3B and 7B models across four datasets, and the method's reliance on label-length heuristics to identify open-response samples may not generalize to more diverse task taxonomies. Open questions include whether confidence alignment scales to larger MLLMs, whether alternative noise formulations (beyond Gaussian) yield better robustness, and whether the approach transfers to video or audio-visual settings.
