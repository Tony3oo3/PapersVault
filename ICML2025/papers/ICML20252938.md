## Problem

SNNs inherently encode information in spike timing across timesteps, yet existing methods either fail to fully exploit this temporal dimension or introduce substantial computational overhead that negates SNNs' energy efficiency advantage. No prior approach fuses spike features across different timesteps within a single timestep, leaving long-term temporal dependencies underutilized and initial/final timestep information partially lost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252938/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event streams (128×128 for CIFAR10-DVS), which are rate-encoded into binary spike trains over T timesteps (typically 1–16). The TS module operates on intermediate spike feature tensors of shape [T, C, H, W], shifting channel groups along the temporal axis. The network outputs a single class label per input, with accuracy reported as top-1 over 3 trials.

## Method

The TS module splits the C channels of a spike feature tensor into C_k groups (default C_k=32), randomly selects two split indices g₁ and g₂, then shifts one group forward by one timestep, another backward by one timestep, and leaves the remainder unchanged—fusing past, present, and future spike information within each timestep without any multiplications or additions. A residual combination Z′ = α ⊙ Z + X (with α ∈ [0.2, 0.5]) adds the shifted features back to the original input to prevent information loss and stabilize training. The module introduces only one learnable parameter (α) and can be inserted as a plug-and-play block into any SNN architecture (ResNet-18/19/20/34, Spikeformer). Training uses spatio-temporal backpropagation with surrogate gradients, and the TS module is applied consistently during both training and inference to maximize temporal modeling benefit at zero additional FLOPs.

## Results

TS-SNN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all four benchmarks: 96.72% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=2), 80.28% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19, T=2), 70.61% on ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), and 83.90% on CIFAR10-DVS (ResNet-20, T=16). Notably, these results are obtained with fewer timesteps than competing methods (e.g., 2 timesteps on CIFAR-10/100 vs. 4–6 for most baselines, and 10 timesteps on CIFAR10-DVS vs. 16–20 for others). Energy consumption is also significantly lower, with TS-SNN consuming only 5.857 mJ per ImageNet sample compared to 13.10 mJ for SEW-ResNet18 under the same protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-SNN outperforms all 14 compared baselines on the majority of dataset-architecture-timestep configurations. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=2), TS-SNN reaches 96.72%, surpassing the closest competitor MPBN (96.47% at T=2) and PFA (95.60% at T=2) by 0.25% and 1.12% respectively, while using only half the timesteps of PFA's best result (T=6, 95.70%). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19, T=2), TS-SNN achieves 80.28%, beating MPBN (79.51% at T=2) and PFA (76.70% at T=2); the sole exception is at T=1 where MPBN edges ahead by 0.10% (78.71% vs. 78.61%). On ImageNet (ResNet-34, T=4), TS-SNN achieves 70.61%, a substantial 2.92% improvement over the next best method Real Spike (67.69%) and 3.57% over SEW-ResNet (67.04%). On CIFAR10-DVS, TS-SNN with only 10 timesteps (83.80%) surpasses Spikeformer (80.90% at T=16) and STSA (79.93% at T=16) by 2.90% and 3.87% respectively, while using fewer timesteps. The TS module also generalizes to Transformer-based architectures, improving Spikeformer-4-384 by +0.09% on CIFAR-10 and +0.38% on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the Temporal Shift Module (TSM) from video understanding (Lin et al., ICCV 2019) to spiking neural networks, repurposing the channel-splitting and forward/backward/unchanged shift operation on spike tensors. Within the SNN training literature, it builds on the standard LIF neuron model trained via spatio-temporal backpropagation with surrogate gradients, and is compatible with recent architectural improvements like MPBN and SEW-ResNet. The key departure from TSM is the addition of a residual combination with a learnable penalty factor α to prevent information loss from shifting, and the observation that the shift operation introduces zero additional multiplications—making it uniquely suited to the energy-constrained SNN setting. The authors position this as filling a clear gap: no prior SNN work explicitly fuses features across timesteps via a shift mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TS module's plug-and-play design (one learnable parameter, no extra FLOPs) makes it an attractive drop-in for any SNN architecture, and the demonstrated energy savings (5.86 mJ vs. 13.10 mJ for SEW-ResNet18 on ImageNet) are meaningful for neuromorphic and edge deployment. However, the T=1 improvement—where the shift is theoretically nullified yet accuracy still rises—is attributed to the α scaling factor in a somewhat hand-wavy manner, leaving open whether this is a genuine temporal effect or merely a learned feature re-weighting. Open questions include how the random split-point sampling affects reproducibility at scale, whether the module benefits asynchronous or event-driven SNNs, and whether the modest 0.2–0.5% gains over strong baselines like MPBN persist in deeper or larger architectures beyond ResNet-34.
