## Problem

Existing Huber regression-based algorithms for heavy-tailed linear bandits (notably HEAVY-OFUL) achieve optimal instance-dependent regret but require storing all historical data and performing a full optimization pass at each round, yielding O(t log T) per-round computational cost. This makes them infeasible for large-scale or real-time applications. The paper asks whether a one-pass Huber-loss-based algorithm can match the optimal regret guarantees while updating using only the current data point.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a feature vector X_t from a feasible set (‖X_t‖₂ ≤ L), an observed reward r_t, and the (1+ε)-th moment bound ν_t of the noise. It outputs an arm selection via a UCB rule, a parameter estimate θ̂_t, and a confidence radius β_t. The per-round computation is O(d²) for the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury matrix update plus O(d³) for projection, independent of t and T, and requires only O(1) storage (no historical data). If ν_t is unavailable, a uniform bound ν suffices, trading the instance-dependent regret for the minimax O~(dT^{1/(1+ε)}) rate.

## Method

Hvt-UCB replaces the full-batch Huber regression of HEAVY-OFUL with a single Online Mirror Descent step under a local-norm regularizer ψ_t(θ) = ½‖θ‖²_{V_t}, where V_t = λI + (1/α)Σ_{s≤t} X_s X_s^T/σ_s² is updated incrementally. Two key design choices enable the analysis: (i) a recursive normalization factor σ_t that incorporates the previous round's confidence radius β_{t-1}, ensuring the current estimate stays in the quadratic region of the Huber loss with high probability; and (ii) a robustification parameter τ_t = τ₀(√(1+w_t²)/w_t)·t^{(1-ε)/(2(1+ε))} that grows with t to accommodate heavy-tailed noise. The estimation error decomposes into a stability term, a generalization gap term, and a negative quadratic term; the negative term cancels the positive O~(√T) bias introduced by the adaptive normalization, yielding the optimal O~(t^{(1-ε)/(2(1+ε))}) confidence radius. The UCB arm selection then gives the instance-dependent regret O~(dT^{(1-ε)/(2(1+ε))}√(Σν_t²) + dT^{(1-ε)/(2(1+ε))}).

## Results

Hvt-UCB achieves an instance-dependent regret of Õ(dT^((1-ε)/(2(1+ε))) √(Σν_t²) + dT^((1-ε)/(2(1+ε)))) with O(1) per-round computational cost, matching the regret of HEAVY-OFUL while reducing its per-round cost from O(t log T) to O(1). When only a general moment bound ν is available, the regret degrades to Õ(dT^(1/(1+ε))), matching the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors. In experiments with T=18,000 rounds, Hvt-UCB achieves comparable cumulative regret to HEAVY-OFUL under both Student-t (df=2.1) and Gaussian noise, while delivering a speedup exceeding 800× in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HEAVY-OFUL (Huang et al., 2023), Hvt-UCB achieves the same order of instance-dependent regret bound but reduces per-round computational cost from O(t log T) to O(1), yielding a measured speedup exceeding 800× at T=18,000 rounds while maintaining comparable cumulative regret under both heavy-tailed and Gaussian noise. Against OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011), Hvt-UCB is slightly slower per round due to the projection step, but OFUL's regret degrades significantly under heavy-tailed Student-t noise, whereas Hvt-UCB remains robust; under Gaussian noise both perform comparably. Against the one-pass truncation-based CRTM and MOM-based CRMM (Xue et al., 2023), Hvt-UCB achieves a tighter regret bound (instance-dependent vs. worst-case Õ(dT^(1/(1+ε)))) and does not require the bounded absolute moment assumption or fixed arm set with repeated pulling that CRTM and CRMM depend on; in the Gaussian noise experiment, CRTM shows suboptimal performance due to excessive reward truncation, while Hvt-UCB maintains competitive regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Huang et al. (2023, NeurIPS), whose HEAVY-OFUL algorithm uses adaptive Huber regression (Sun et al., 2020) to achieve optimal instance-dependent regret for heavy-tailed linear bandits but requires storing all historical data and performing a full O(t log T) optimization pass each round. The key departure is replacing this offline estimator with an Online Mirror Descent (OMD) update inspired by Zhang et al. (2016)'s ONS approach for logistic bandits, adapted here to handle the partially-linear structure of the Huber loss. Unlike prior one-pass heavy-tailed bandit algorithms (CRTM, CRMM from Xue et al., 2023) that rely on truncation or median-of-means and require specific noise assumptions or fixed arm sets, the Huber-loss-based approach maintains generality across dynamic contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 800× per-round speedup (O(t log T) → O(1)) makes Huber-based heavy-tailed bandit learning feasible for large-scale or real-time applications where HEAVY-OFUL was computationally infeasible, while preserving the same optimal and instance-dependent regret guarantees. The paper identifies two concrete downstream applications—heavy-tailed linear MDPs and online adaptive control—where the one-pass property is essential because repeated sampling of states or contexts is infeasible. An open question the authors highlight is whether the requirement for prior knowledge of the moment bound ν_t can be removed, following recent progress in heavy-tailed multi-armed bandits.
