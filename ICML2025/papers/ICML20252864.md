## Problem

Existing neural controllers with stability certificates (e.g., Neural Lyapunov Control) require continuous updating of the control signal, incurring prohibitive communication costs on digital platforms. While event-triggered control reduces this burden by updating only at discrete instants, prior work has not addressed the joint problem of guaranteeing stability and optimizing the scheduling (minimal triggering times, maximal inter-event time) for general nonlinear dynamics. This paper tackles both challenges simultaneously for arbitrary complex systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a known ODE model f, the current state measurement x, and an augmented error state e = x(t_k) − x(t) that tracks the deviation between the last triggered state and the current state. The output is a control signal u(x(t_k)) that is held constant between triggering events, a discrete event schedule {t_k} determined implicitly by the zero-crossing of a learned event function h, and a Lyapunov function V serving as a global stability certificate. The event-triggered dynamics are reformulated as a closed-loop ODE in the augmented (x, e) space with a reset operation e(t_{k+1}) = 0, enabling standard ODE solvers to be applied during training.

## Method

Neural ETC parameterizes the controller u_φ and Lyapunov function V_θ (via ICNN) and designs an event function h = ∇V·(f(x,u(x+e)) − f(x,u(x))) − σV(x) that guarantees exponential stability with decay rate (1−σ). Two complementary training algorithms are provided: (1) a Path Integral approach that uses ODESolveEvent (root solver + adjoint method) to compute gradients of the implicit triggering times, with a time-invariance trick reducing M batch trajectories to MK single-step solves; and (2) a Monte Carlo approach that avoids ODE backpropagation entirely by leveraging a theoretical lower bound on inter-event time (Theorem 3.3) and instead regularizing the Lipschitz constants of u_φ and the inverse of a learned class-K function α. A key theoretical contribution is an analytical projection operator π(u, U(V)) = u − max(0, L_{f_u}V − V)/‖∇V‖² · ∇V that maps any candidate controller into the stable controller space without solving an optimization problem, and whose Lipschitz constant minimization yields the necessary condition for optimal scheduling (Theorem 4.2).

## Results

On the three benchmark systems (2-D GRN, 3-D Lorenz, 100-D Cell), Neural ETC-MC achieves the fewest triggering events across all tasks: 4 triggers on GRN, 13 on Lorenz, and 2 on Cell, compared to 12–1914 for baselines. The minimal inter-event time is also maximized: 15.52 on GRN and 27.18 on Cell (vs. ≤0.37 for all other methods). Neural ETC-PI achieves the best MSE under finite triggers (≤10 events) on all three systems (0.05, 0.11, 5e-8), demonstrating superior stabilization quality under strict communication budgets. Training time for Neural ETC-MC is 30–75× faster than Neural ETC-PI (e.g., 32 s vs. 1230 s on GRN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural ETC dominates all eight baselines on triggering count across every system: on Lorenz, Neural ETC-PI uses only 20 triggers vs. 1602 (NLC), 2000 (LQR, IRL ETC), 1120 (ETS), and 340 (PGDNLC); on Cell, Neural ETC-MC uses just 2 triggers vs. 449 (LQR), 551 (Quad-NLC), 370 (IRL ETC), and 330 (Critic-Actor ETC). On minimal inter-event time, Neural ETC-MC achieves 15.52 on GRN and 27.18 on Cell, exceeding the next-best (PGDNLC at 0.37 on GRN, ETS at 0.02 on Cell) by orders of magnitude; Neural ETC-PI leads on Lorenz (0.02 vs. 0.002 for Critic-Actor ETC). On MSE under ≤10 triggers, Neural ETC-PI is best on all three systems (0.05/0.11/5e-8), while Neural ETC-MC is competitive on GRN (0.07) and Lorenz (0.29) but weaker on Cell (1.66 vs. 5e-8). The online QP-based BALSA method is the strongest non-neural baseline on GRN (12 triggers, 0.29 inter-event time) but is outperformed by both Neural ETC variants on all metrics. The RL-based methods (IRL ETC, Critic-Actor ETC) suffer from high trigger counts and near-zero inter-event times on Lorenz, indicating Zeno-like behavior, whereas Neural ETC maintains positive lower bounds guaranteed by Theorem 3.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: neural certificate-based control (NLC, Quad-NLC, PGDNLC) that learns stabilizing controllers with Lyapunov guarantees, and event-triggered control (Tabuada 2007, Heemels 2012) that reduces communication by updating only at state-dependent instants. The key departure is that prior neural controllers require continuous control updates (prohibitive communication cost on digital platforms), while prior ETC works focus on stabilization and Zeno-behavior avoidance but not on optimizing the scheduling itself. The authors explicitly position their contribution as the first to jointly learn a neural ETC with optimal scheduling (minimal triggering times / maximal inter-event time) for general nonlinear dynamics, combining ICNN-based Lyapunov construction, spectral norm regularization, and either a path-integral ODE-solver approach or a Monte Carlo approach derived from a new lower-bound theorem on inter-event time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: deploying learned controllers on resource-constrained wireless embedded systems or biological platforms (e.g., drug administration in gene networks) requires minimizing communication events, and this paper provides both a learning framework and theoretical guarantees (exponential stability via projection, optimality via Lipschitz regularization) for doing so. Limitations include the affine-actuator requirement for the projection guarantee, the ODE-solver bottleneck for stiff or high-dimensional systems in the PI variant, and the acknowledged inability to extend to SDEs where triggering times become random stopping times. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to already-stabilized controllers (avoiding the trade-off between event loss and stabilization loss), adaptive ODE solvers, and stochastic dynamics.
