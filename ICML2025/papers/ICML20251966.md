## Problem

Existing ECG-text cross-modal pre-training methods rely predominantly on contrastive learning with standard encoders (ResNet, BERT), overlooking the generative potential of masked auto-encoders for capturing fine-grained intra-modal features. Multi-modal masked auto-encoder architectures are inherently biased toward reconstruction, limiting their discriminative power for downstream tasks like zero-shot classification, and the critical role of negative sample selection in contrastive learning is largely unaddressed in medical settings where many text reports are near-duplicates.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12-lead ECG signals (500 Hz, 10 s) with random lead masking (p=0.5) and input dropout (p=0.1), alongside clinical text reports tokenized by the Flan-T5 tokenizer. It produces 768-dimensional discriminative embeddings from separate ECG and text projection heads, plus fused cross-attention representations, which serve as the basis for linear probing, full fine-tuning, and zero-shot classification across 100+ cardiac conditions. The ECG encoder uses a convolutional front-end followed by 8 transformer layers, while the text encoder is a fine-tuned Flan-T5-base.

## Method

D-BETA integrates a transformer-based ECG encoder and Flan-T5 text encoder within a masked auto-encoder architecture, connected by a cross-attention fusion module that feeds three task heads: masked language modeling (MLM), masked ECG modeling (MEM), and ECG-text matching (ETM). The key discriminative contribution is the ETS loss, a sigmoid-based pairwise loss (inspired by SigLIP) applied to separate projection heads on each encoder, which avoids the costly global softmax normalization of standard contrastive losses while directly enhancing per-encoder discriminative power. A second innovation, Nearest-Neighbor Negative Sampling (N3S), uses FAISS to select negative text reports by maximum cosine distance in pre-computed Flan-T5-small embedding space, preventing false negatives that arise from the high duplication rate in clinical reports (e.g., >100k identical 'sinus rhythm normal ecg' entries). The model is jointly trained on 779,891 MIMIC-IV-ECG pairs by optimizing all four losses simultaneously, and the resulting frozen ECG encoder achieves 15% average AUC improvement in linear probing at 1% data and 2% zero-shot gains over MERL without any task-specific training.

## Results

On PhysioNet 2021 (full fine-tuning), D-BETA achieves 85.7 CinC score for diagnosis classification with 12 leads, surpassing the best baseline (W2V+CMSC+RLM) by 12.5 points, and 65.4% patient identification accuracy vs. 57.7% for the best baseline. In linear probing with only 1% of training data, D-BETA achieves an average AUC improvement of ~15% over MERL, with the largest gains on PTBXL-Rhythm (86.61 vs. 53.33 AUC) and CSN (80.04 vs. 66.60 AUC). In zero-shot classification, D-BETA reaches 77.1% average AUC across six datasets, outperforming MERL (75.3%) by 2%, and on CODE-test achieves 96.79 AUC, exceeding both the in-domain DNN (96.59) and three cardiologist groups (92.07–93.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-BETA outperforms all 15 baselines across nearly every evaluation setting. In full fine-tuning on PhysioNet 2021, D-BETA's 85.7 CinC (12-lead Dx) exceeds W2V+CMSC+RLM (73.2) by 12.5 points, and even its 1-lead result (76.5) surpasses the best 12-lead baseline. In linear probing at 1% data, D-BETA dominates MERL by 12.8 points on PTBXL-Sub (77.74 vs. 64.90), 11.8 on PTBXL-Form (70.10 vs. 58.26), 33.3 on PTBXL-Rhythm (86.61 vs. 53.33), 15.1 on CPSC2018 (85.46 vs. 70.33), and 13.4 on CSN (80.04 vs. 66.60); at 100% data the gap narrows but D-BETA still leads on all six datasets (e.g., 96.71 vs. 88.34 on PTBXL-Rhythm). In zero-shot classification, D-BETA achieves 77.1% average AUC vs. MERL's 75.3%, winning on five of six datasets but losing on CPSC2018 (80.1 vs. 82.8). On CODE-test, D-BETA's zero-shot AUC of 96.79 marginally exceeds the in-domain DNN (96.59) and substantially outperforms cardiologist annotations (92.07–93.61). Among unimodal SSL baselines (SimCLR, BYOL, MoCo-v3, CLOCS, CRT, ST-MEM, ASTCL), D-BETA leads by 5–33 AUC points depending on dataset and training ratio, with the largest margins at low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-BETA extends the multi-modal masked auto-encoder paradigm (Chen et al., 2022, originally for medical vision-language) to the ECG-text domain, but departs from purely generative or purely contrastive SSL by unifying both within a single architecture. It builds on the ECG SSL lineage (CLOCS, 3KG, W2V+CMSC+RLM for contrastive; ST-MEM, CRT for generative) and the emerging ECG-text cross-modal line (MERL, ETP, ESI), while introducing the ETS sigmoid loss (inspired by SigLIP) and nearest-neighbor negative sampling (N3S) as targeted fixes for the discriminative weakness of masked auto-encoders. The use of Flan-T5 as the text encoder is, to the authors' knowledge, novel for the ECG domain, replacing the BERT/ResNet encoders common in prior ECG-text work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a hybrid generative-discriminative pre-training strategy can substantially reduce the labeled-data requirement for ECG classification (15% AUC gain at 1% data) and achieve competitive zero-shot performance without any task-specific training, which is practically significant given the scarcity of expert-annotated ECG data. The N3S finding—that over 100,000 of ~800,000 MIMIC-IV text reports are identical, making random negative sampling actively harmful—highlights a domain-specific pitfall that other medical NLP/SSL work may share. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other physiological signal-text pairs (e.g., EEG, fMRI), how the model behaves under true clinical distribution shift beyond the tested datasets, and whether the Flan-T5 dependency introduces licensing or deployment constraints for hospital settings.
