## Problem

Existing fair clustering algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: pre- and post-processing methods (e.g., fairlet-based SFC) sacrifice clustering utility by not jointly optimizing matchings and cluster centers, while in-processing methods (e.g., VFC) suffer from numerical instability and fail to achieve near-perfect fairness, especially at high fairness levels. No prior method simultaneously guarantees approximately optimal clustering utility for any given fairness level while remaining numerically stable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of n d-dimensional points each labeled with a binary sensitive attribute (s ∈ {0,1}), a pre-specified number of clusters K, and optionally a fairness level ε ∈ [0,1] for the FCA-C variant. It outputs K cluster centers in R^d and probabilistic assignment functions A_0, A_1 that map each point to a distribution over clusters, where the assignments are guaranteed to satisfy the proportional fairness constraint (balance ≈ min(n_0/n_1, n_1/n_0) for perfect fairness). For large datasets, each group is randomly partitioned into L subsets of size m (default m=1024) to reduce the linear-programming complexity from O(n³) to O(nm²).

## Method

FCA's key theoretical contribution is a decomposition of the perfectly fair K-means objective into a transport cost (measuring how far matched pairs from different groups are) plus a clustering cost in an aligned space defined by the weighted alignment map T^A(x_0, x_1) = π_0 x_0 + π_1 x_1. The algorithm alternates two phases: Phase 1 solves a modified Kantorovich problem (linear programming over a coupling matrix Γ with cost matrix C + D, where D encodes the clustering cost to current centers) to find the optimal joint distribution between the two groups, and Phase 2 runs standard K-means++ on the weighted aligned points {T^A(x_i, x_j)} with weights γ_{i,j}. FCA-C extends this by introducing a subset W of pairs (controlled by ε) that bypass the fair alignment and use standard K-means instead, enabling continuous control of the fairness-utility trade-off with a (τ+2)-approximation guarantee.

## Results

On the three tabular benchmarks (ADULT, BANK, CENSUS) with L2 normalization, FCA achieves near-perfect fairness (Bal ≈ Bal*) while attaining the lowest clustering Cost among all fair methods: 0.328 (Bal=0.493) on ADULT, 0.264 (Bal=0.645) on BANK, and 0.477 (Bal=0.962) on CENSUS. In the visual clustering setting, FCA reaches 89.0% ACC with Bal=1.000 on RMNIST and 67.6% ACC with Bal=0.270 on OFFICE-31, matching or exceeding the state-of-the-art FCMI on fairness while remaining competitive on utility. FCA also demonstrates superior numerical stability, achieving Bal values within 1% of Bal* where VFC plateaus at ≤90% of Bal*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCA outperforms all four tabular baselines (SFC, FCBC, FRAC, and standard K-means) on every dataset in Table 1, achieving both the lowest Cost and the highest Bal simultaneously. Against SFC (Backurs et al., 2019), the fairlet-based pre-processing method, FCA reduces Cost by 39% on ADULT (0.328 vs. 0.534) and 53% on CENSUS (0.477 vs. 1.015) while also achieving higher Bal, confirming that joint optimization of matching and centers beats the sequential fairlet-then-cluster pipeline. Against FCBC (Esmaeili et al., 2021), FCA dominates on all three datasets, e.g., Cost drops from 0.314 to 0.328 on ADULT while Bal rises from 0.443 to 0.493. Against FRAC (Gupta et al., 2023), FCA achieves lower Cost on ADULT (0.328 vs. 0.340) and CENSUS (0.477 vs. 0.537) with higher Bal. In the in-processing comparison (Table 2), FCA reaches near-perfect fairness (Bal within 1% of Bal*) on all datasets, whereas VFC (Ziko et al., 2021) plateaus at 88–90% of Bal* and suffers numerical overflow on CENSUS without L2 normalization. In visual clustering (Table 3), FCA slightly edges out FCMI (Zeng et al., 2023) on RMNIST (89.0 vs. 88.4 ACC, 1.000 vs. 0.995 Bal) but trails FCMI and DFC (Li et al., 2020) on OFFICE-31 ACC (67.6 vs. 70.0 and 69.0) while still achieving the highest Bal (0.270 vs. 0.226 and 0.165).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Kantorovich optimal transport framework (Kantorovich, 2006; Villani, 2008) and repurposes it as the core mechanism for fair clustering, introducing a novel decomposition of the fair K-means objective into a transport cost between protected groups and a standard clustering cost in an 'aligned space.' Conceptually, it departs from fairlet-based pre-processing methods (Chierichetti et al., 2017; Backurs et al., 2019) by jointly optimizing the group-to-group matching and cluster centers rather than solving them sequentially, and from constrained in-processing methods (Ziko et al., 2021) by eliminating explicit fairness constraints entirely through the alignment map construction. The theoretical equivalence (Theorem 3.3) between optimal perfectly fair clustering and unconstrained clustering in the aligned space is the key positioning result that distinguishes FCA from prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCA provides a practically stable and theoretically grounded alternative to existing fair clustering algorithms, particularly addressing the numerical instability that plagues constrained optimization approaches (e.g., VFC) at high fairness levels. The FCA-C variant offers a simple hyperparameter (ε) for controlling the fairness-utility trade-off with a provable approximation guarantee (τ+2). However, the method is currently limited to proportional (group) fairness with a binary sensitive attribute (though extended to multiple groups), and the empirical gains over strong baselines like FRAC are moderate. Open questions include extension to model-based clustering (e.g., Gaussian mixtures), other fairness notions (individual fairness, social fairness), and whether the alignment-space perspective generalizes beyond K-means-type objectives.
