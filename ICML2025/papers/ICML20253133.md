## Problem

Synthetic context extension—fine-tuning LLMs on synthetically-generated long-context data to extend their effective context window—is widely used but poorly understood. It remains unclear what properties of synthetic data (realism of needle concepts, diversity of surrounding context, degree of symbolization) make it effective or ineffective for downstream long-context retrieval and reasoning, and no mechanistic account explains the performance gaps between synthetic and real-data fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a long context C (extended from 8K to 32K tokens via RoPE rescaling) and a query q; the model must retrieve one or more needle concepts embedded in C and produce either a short answer string (MDQA, MuSiQue) or bracketed document IDs like '[3][7]' (SummHay Citation). The synthetic training data varies along two axes—concept expression (real entities vs. 4-character symbolic tokens vs. templated sentences) and context diversity (LLM-generated distractor paragraphs vs. repeated padding sentences)—and includes purely symbolic dictionary/list-lookup variants. Only attention heads are fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8), with MLP layers frozen.

## Method

The paper constructs a systematic grid of synthetic datasets across three long-context tasks (MDQA, MuSiQue, SummHay Citation) by varying concept expression and context diversity, then uses retrieval heads (Wu et al., 2025) as a mechanistic probe to explain performance differences. Retrieval heads are detected by computing per-head retrieval scores that measure how often an attention head places its maximum attention mass on the correct answer tokens in the context during decoding. The key finding is that the cosine similarity between retrieval-score vectors of synthetic-trained and real-trained models strongly correlates with downstream F1 (Spearman R up to 1.0 for SummHay on Llama-3), and that synthetic data inducing fewer retrieval heads tends to produce subsets of the real-data heads. Cross-model activation patching of the intersection heads (present in both real and synthetic models) outperforms patching complement or random heads, showing that synthetic data targets the right subnetwork but learns it less effectively—likely because upstream representation shifts cause the same heads to operate on different input distributions.

## Results

Across three long-context retrieval and reasoning tasks (MDQA, MuSiQue, SummHay Citation), fine-tuning on the best synthetic dataset still lags behind real data: on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, the gap is 33% F1 on MDQA (0.49 vs. 0.83), 4% on MuSiQue (0.41 vs. 0.45), and 2% on SummHay Citation (0.79 vs. 0.81). No single synthetic data construction strategy (varying concept expression and context diversity) is best across all tasks; for example, Low/High synthetic data wins on MDQA while High/Low wins on MuSiQue. The cosine similarity between retrieval head scores on synthetic vs. real data strongly correlates with downstream F1 (Spearman R = 0.75 for MuSiQue, R = 1.0 for SummHay Citation on Llama-3), and patching intersection retrieval heads from a real-data model into a synthetic-data model yields the largest performance gains (e.g., +0.25 F1 on MDQA Symbolic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead ablates synthetic data construction strategies (High/Low concept expression × High/Low context diversity, plus a fully Symbolic variant) against two real-data baselines (Full and Limited) and a no-fine-tuning baseline. Real Data (Full) dominates all synthetic variants on every task and model, with the largest gap on MDQA (0.83 vs. best synthetic 0.49 on Llama-3). Among synthetic variants, the best-performing construction is task-specific: Low concept expression + High context diversity wins on MDQA (0.49) and SummHay Citation (0.79), while High concept expression + Low context diversity wins on MuSiQue (0.41). The Symbolic (dictionary key-value / list retrieval) variant is competitive on MDQA (0.48) but underperforms on MuSiQue (0.32) and SummHay (0.54). Retrieval head analysis reveals that synthetic-data models recruit fewer retrieval heads than real-data models (e.g., 74 vs. 129 heads on MuSiQue Symbolic vs. Real for Llama-3), and the recall of synthetic heads against real heads correlates with F1, providing a mechanistic explanation for the performance gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: synthetic context extension (Xiong et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024), which uses synthetic data to extend LLM context windows post-pretraining, and mechanistic interpretability of attention heads (Wu et al., 2025; Olsson et al., 2022; Prakash et al., 2024), which identifies specialized subnetworks responsible for specific capabilities. While prior work has shown that synthetic data can be effective for context extension and that retrieval heads are important for long-context factuality, no prior work had connected these two threads to explain *why* some synthetic data works and others does not. The paper repurposes the retrieval head framework from Wu et al. (2025) as a diagnostic tool for evaluating synthetic data quality, extending the concept to multi-hop and citation settings, and uses activation patching (following Prakash et al., 2024) to establish causal links between specific heads and downstream performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic lens for understanding synthetic context extension, showing that retrieval head recall correlates strongly with downstream F1 (Spearman R=1.0 for SummHay, 0.75 for MuSiQue on Llama-3), offering a principled way to predict whether a synthetic dataset will be effective before committing to full training. The key limitation is that retrieval heads are necessary but not sufficient: even when synthetic data activates the same heads as real data, it does so less effectively, likely due to upstream representation shifts that the paper identifies but does not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond 32K context, whether non-attention components (MLP layers, position embeddings) play an equally important role, and how to design synthetic data that not only targets the right heads but also trains them as strongly as real data. The task-specificity of optimal synthetic data construction (no universal strategy works across MDQA, MuSiQue, and SummHay) limits the practical takeaway to a diagnostic framework rather than a recipe.
