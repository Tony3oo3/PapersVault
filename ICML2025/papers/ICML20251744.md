## Problem

While approximate recovery of sparse binary vectors from linear measurements was characterized by Reeves et al. (2019), the exact recovery sample complexity for generalized linear measurements (one-bit compressed sensing, logistic regression, sparse linear regression) remained open. In particular, it was unclear whether a statistical-computational gap exists for these problems, and no tight information-theoretic lower bounds matching efficient algorithm guarantees were known for the binary sparse setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sensing matrix A ∈ R^{m×n} (entries iid N(0,1), satisfying the power constraint E[(A_i^T x)^2] ≤ k) and a measurement vector y ∈ R^m (continuous for SparseLinearReg, binary ±1 for 1bCSbinary and logistic regression) as input, and outputs the support set S_x ⊆ [1:n] of size k identifying the nonzero coordinates of the unknown binary vector x. The recovery guarantee is worst-case over all k-sparse binary vectors, requiring max_{x: |x|_H=k} P(φ(A,y) ≠ x) → 0 as n → ∞.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) computes the inner product l_i = ⟨y, A^(i)⟩ for each column of A, sorts the resulting vector in decreasing order, and returns the top-k indices as the estimated support. The key insight is that for i ∈ S_x, the measurement y is correlated with column A^(i) (with E[y_i A_{i,j}] = E[g'(A_i^T x)] > 0 by Stein's lemma), while for j ∉ S_x the correlation vanishes, making top-k selection a valid recovery procedure. The upper bound analysis leverages subexponential concentration (since y_i·A_{i,j} is a product of subgaussian variables) to show m = O((k+σ²)log n) suffices for 1bCSbinary and SparseLinearReg, and m = O((k+1/β²)log n) for logistic regression. Lower bounds are derived via Fano's inequality with mutual information bounds, yielding matching Ω((k+σ²)log(n/k)) and Ω((k+1/β²)log(n/k)) rates. For SparseLinearReg, a separate MLE-based analysis provides nearly matching upper and lower bounds in the form of an optimization over Hamming distance l, showing the Gaussian channel capacity ½log(1+k/σ²) governs the fundamental limit.

## Results

For noisy one-bit compressed sensing (1bCSbinary), the paper establishes a tight sample complexity of O((k+σ²)log n) measurements for exact recovery via a simple linear estimation + top-k algorithm, matching the information-theoretic lower bound of (k+σ²)/2 · log(n/k). For logistic regression, the analogous tight characterization is O((k+1/β²)log n) upper and ½(k+1/β²)log(n/k) lower. For sparse linear regression (SparseLinearReg), the same O((k+σ²)log n) sample complexity is achieved, with almost-matching MLE-based upper and lower bounds of the form max_l nN(l) / ½log(1 + l/(2σ²)), where N(l) involves binary entropy terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's linear estimation algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves the same O((k+σ²)log n) sample complexity as Lasso and OMP for SparseLinearReg, but with lower computational cost of O((k+σ²)n log n) versus the higher cost of convex optimization-based methods. Compared to the black-box application of Plan et al. (2017), which yields a suboptimal O(k² log(2n/k)) sample complexity for binary vectors, this paper's specialized analysis improves the bound to O((k+σ²)log n). For 1bCSbinary and logistic regression, the paper shows there is no statistical-computational gap (unlike the conjectured gap for SparseLinearReg by Gamarnik & Zadik, 2017), since the efficient algorithm matches the information-theoretic lower bound up to constants. The MLE upper bound for SparseLinearReg (Theorem 2.9) closely matches the new lower bound (Theorem 2.10), with the gap being only a constant factor in the log denominator, improving upon the earlier approximate-recovery bounds of Reeves et al. (2019) which applied only to Gaussian designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper specializes the linear estimation algorithm of Plan, Vershynin, and Yudovina (2017) to the setting of sparse binary vectors, providing a tighter analysis that yields O((k+σ²) log n) samples versus the O(k² log(2n/k)) that a black-box application of their result would give. The MLE upper bound for SparseLinearReg follows the analysis framework of Reeves, Xu, and Zadik (2019), adapted from approximate to exact recovery. The paper also departs from the conjecture of Gamarnik and Zadik (2017) regarding a statistical-computational gap for SparseLinearReg, showing that no such gap exists for the related problems of 1bCSbinary and logistic regression. The noisy one-bit compressed sensing formulation (1bCSbinary) is introduced as a new problem, motivated by the sparse probit model, bridging one-bit CS and logistic regression under a unified GLM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides tight (matching up to constants) sample complexity characterizations for three important measurement models—noisy one-bit CS, logistic regression, and sparse linear regression—when the unknown signal is binary and sparse, resolving the exact recovery question with both efficient algorithmic upper bounds and information-theoretic lower bounds. The key practical implication is that for SparseLinearReg, retaining only the sign of each measurement (discarding amplitude) incurs no sample complexity penalty, which simplifies hardware and communication requirements. Open questions include whether a design matrix and algorithm can beat the (k+σ²) log n barrier for SparseLinearReg in the noisy regime, and whether the small constant-factor gap between the MLE upper and lower bounds (Theorems 2.9 and 2.10) can be closed.
