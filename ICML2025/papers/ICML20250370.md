## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for multi-modal data assume all modalities experience distribution shift simultaneously, but in practice many real-world corruptions (e.g., fog affecting cameras, wind affecting microphones) impact only a single modality. This paper identifies the under-explored 'uni-modal distribution shift' setting and shows, both theoretically and empirically, that blindly applying TTA to all modalities causes negative transfer on unshifted modalities and disrupts cross-modal attention fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 10-second video clips and audio signals, processed by a CAV-MAE pretrained backbone with separate transformer encoders for each modality. At test time, the model receives unlabeled streaming batches and must produce a class prediction (50 classes on Kinetics50, 309 on VGGSound) while simultaneously updating itself. A key constraint is that the model does not know which modality is shifted, so it must dynamically decide per-batch whether to adapt the video features, the audio features, or neither.

## Method

The proposed selective adaptation schema adds two lightweight components on top of a frozen multi-modal transformer: (1) modality-specific adapter matrices Φ applied residually (z(Φ+I)) to align shifted features, and (2) a learnable router parameterized by a modality shift semaphore S that passes through Gumbel-Softmax to produce soft selection weights, convexly combining original and adapted features per modality. A theoretical analysis proves that under zero-mean additive shift, the variance of intra-modal attention logit changes exceeds that of cross-modal changes, explaining why the shifted modality's self-attention becomes 'peaked' and starves cross-modal fusion. Adaptation is driven by self-training (thresholded pseudo-label cross-entropy plus a negative-entropy balance loss) in an end-to-end fashion, and a traverse inference schema ensembles predictions from all adapter configurations to ensure robustness during early adaptation before the router converges.

## Results

On Kinetics50-C with corrupted video modality (attention-based fusion), the proposed method achieves 64.5% average top-1 accuracy across 15 corruption types, outperforming the strongest baseline READ (62.5%) by 2.0 points and single-modality TTA methods Tent (59.4%), ETA (60.1%), and SAR (59.8%) by 4.4–5.1 points. On Kinetics50-C with corrupted audio, the method reaches 71.5% average accuracy vs. READ's 71.1%. On VGGSound-C with corrupted audio, the method achieves 34.7% vs. READ's 32.4%, while Tent collapses to 4.5% (source: 25.6%), demonstrating severe negative transfer in existing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three main evaluation settings. On Kinetics50-C with video corruption (the dominant-modality shift scenario), it leads READ by 2.0 points (64.5 vs. 62.5) and single-modality TTA methods Tent/ETA/SAR by 4.4–5.1 points, with the largest per-corruption gains on noise types (e.g., 52.6 vs. 49.4 for Gaussian noise). On Kinetics50-C with audio corruption, the margin over READ narrows to 0.4 points (71.5 vs. 71.1), while Tent and SAR show minimal gains over the source model. On VGGSound-C with audio corruption, the method achieves 34.7% vs. READ's 32.4%, while Tent catastrophically degrades to 4.5% (source: 25.6%), and MM-TTA drops to 7.6% in late fusion, confirming the negative transfer phenomenon the paper identifies. On VGGSound-C with video corruption, the method ties READ at 56.9% average, indicating less headroom when the shifted modality is not dominant. MM-TTA is only evaluated under late fusion and shows severe degradation on VGGSound-C (32.0% avg vs. 49.1% source), further motivating the selective adaptation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing uni-modal distribution shift as a distinct problem from the previously studied global (all-modality) shift in multi-modal TTA. It repurposes lightweight adapter networks (common in parameter-efficient fine-tuning) and Gumbel-Softmax discrete routing (from differentiable combinatorial optimization) into a selective test-time adaptation framework, departing from prior TTA methods (Tent, ETA, SAR, READ, MM-TTA) that uniformly adapt all modality-specific encoders or the fusion module. The theoretical analysis builds on the self-attention mechanism and additive noise models from domain adaptation literature to show why uniform adaptation fails under uni-modal shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard TTA methods cause negative transfer on unshifted modalities is practically important for deploying multi-modal systems (e.g., autonomous driving with camera+LiDAR) where environmental corruptions often affect only one sensor. However, the router's imperfect identification of the shifted modality (44–47% misrouting in some cases) and the limited evaluation to two modalities and two datasets leave open questions about scalability to more modalities, simultaneous multi-modal shifts, and convergence guarantees for the router in streaming settings. The moderate performance gains over READ (1–3 points) suggest the selective adaptation idea is sound but the current implementation has room for improvement.
