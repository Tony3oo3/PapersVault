## Problem

Zero-shot prediction via foundation models (e.g., CLIP) achieves remarkable downstream performance without task-specific labels, yet the unique mechanisms enabling this generalization—prompting, cross-modal translation, and the indirect prediction path through a text modality—lack a principled theoretical account. The paper asks: what quantities does ZSP actually learn, and which conditional independence structures between image, label, and caption variables govern its generalization limits?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251754/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single image x and a collection of m text prompts per class y (e.g., 'a photo of a cat'), encodes both via pre-trained encoders α and β, and outputs a class label by selecting the y maximizing the average inner product ⟨α(x), β(z_k^y)⟩. The pre-training distribution Q_{X,Z} pairs images with natural-language captions, while the prompting distribution ρ_{Y,Z} is user-defined (template-based or LLM-generated). The theoretical analysis treats the prediction as a two-stage regression: first mapping Z→Y via the prompt distribution, then mapping X→Z via the pre-training distribution, with the final output being a scalar prediction η_ρ(x) that can be thresholded for classification.

## Method

The paper introduces an 'indirect predictor' η_ρ(x) = E_{Q_{X,Z}}[g_ρ(Z)|X](x) that routes prediction from image to label through a text modality, and proves a three-term error decomposition: prompt bias (mismatch between the prompt distribution ρ_{Y,Z} and the true conditional P_{Y|Z}), residual dependence (the conditional mean-square contingency I(X;Y|Z) measuring information lost by not observing Z directly), and estimation error from finite pre-training and prompt samples. Two equivalent population-level representations of η_ρ are identified—the conditional mean operator M_{Z|X} and the Radon-Nikodym information density R = dQ_{X,Z}/dQ_XQ_Z—leading to two distinct learning approaches with different statistical rates. The conditional mean approach estimates a vector-valued regression function via spectral regularization (yielding rates governed by singular decay exponents of M_{Z|X}), while the information density approach estimates R via kernel Radon-Nikodym differentiation (yielding a parametric M^{-1} prompt rate but incurring a χ²-divergence penalty for caption distribution mismatch). The practical CLIP-style predictor (1) is shown to be a finite-dimensional SVD truncation of the information density, connecting the theory directly to deployed architectures.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper; empirical results validate the generalization decomposition rather than establish new SOTA. In the residual-dependence simulation (Fig. 2), the indirect, CLIP, and VICReg predictors all converge to the direct predictor's accuracy as E_{P_Z}[I(X;Y|Z)]→0. In the unbiased-prompting experiment (Fig. 3), sampling from the true P_{Z|Y} outperforms the 18 default CLIP templates at approximately M=10 prompts per class, yielding up to ~15% absolute top-1 accuracy gain (ResNet50, Sub-Task 1) that saturates near M=100. In the LLM-based class-conditional prompting experiment (Fig. 4), CuPL with Llama 3 achieves a nearly 20% increase in top-5 accuracy on DTD (ResNet50 and ViT-B/32), though Flowers 102's single well-chosen template often matches or exceeds the LLM approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new predictor but rather provides a theoretical lens under which existing methods are analyzed. Against CLIP (Radford et al., 2021): the CLIP predictor in the simulation (Fig. 2) closely tracks the theoretical indirect predictor η_ρ, confirming that CLIP's zero-shot behavior is well-captured by the framework; in the prompting experiments, CLIP-based encoders (ResNet50, NLLB-CLIP, ViT-B/32) serve as the backbone, and the comparison is between prompting strategies rather than between CLIP and a competing encoder. Against VICReg (Bardes et al., 2022): in the simulation, the VICReg predictor exhibits the same convergence trend toward the direct predictor as residual dependence vanishes, validating that the theory applies beyond contrastive objectives. Against CuPL / Pratt et al. (2023): the paper re-implements CuPL using Llama 3 (instead of GPT-3) and finds that LLM-generated class-conditional prompts substantially outperform default templates on DTD (~20% top-5 gain) and SUN397, but the single Flowers 102 template ('a photo of a ___, a type of flower') frequently matches or beats the LLM approach, suggesting template quality matters as much as prompt count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the FSL theory lineage (Saunshi et al., 2019; Wang and Isola, 2020; Tosh et al., 2021), which explains SSL success by treating downstream labels as latent variables in pre-training data (X⊥Z|Y). For ZSP, the authors argue the relevant conditional independence is X⊥Y|Z (the caption mediates the image-label relationship), fundamentally changing the prediction path. The analysis leverages kernel methods as analytical tools—specifically vector-valued spectral regularization (Meunier et al., 2024) for the conditional mean approach and regularized Radon-Nikodym derivative estimation (Nguyen et al., 2024) for the information density approach—rather than claiming foundation models are kernel methods. The paper positions itself against Chen et al. (2024), whose CLIP-specific bounds increase with batch size and lack explicit prompt dependence, and Oko et al. (2025), who assume idealized prompting (zero prompt bias and residual dependence).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive generalization theory specifically for ZSP (as opposed to FSL), providing a clean three-term error decomposition (prompt bias, residual dependence, estimation error) that practitioners can use to diagnose bottlenecks in their pipelines. The residual dependence term formalizes the fundamental information-theoretic cost of using natural language as a proxy for image classification, while the prompt bias term gives theoretical justification for why LLM-generated class-conditional prompts (CuPL-style) outperform fixed templates. Open questions include extending the analysis to distribution shifts between pre-training and downstream data, and connecting the framework to causal generative modeling. A limitation is that the statistical guarantees are derived for RKHS-based estimators, so mapping specific deep SSL objectives (CLIP, VICReg) to the framework's assumptions remains somewhat informal.
