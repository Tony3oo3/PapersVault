## Problem

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) place learnable B-spline activation functions on every edge, inflating the trainable parameter count by a factor of (G+k+1) over an equivalent MLP. This parameter overhead creates significant memory and training-cost barriers that limit KANs' scalability to larger datasets and deeper architectures, despite their superior function-approximation and interpretability properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253122/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MetaKANs consume the same inputs as standard KANs—vectors for symbolic regression and PDE solving, or RGB images for classification—and produce identical outputs (function values, PDE solutions, or class logits). The structural difference is internal: instead of storing a (G+k+1)-dimensional coefficient vector per edge, the network stores a single scalar prompt per edge plus a compact meta-learner, so the forward pass generates spline weights on-the-fly via the meta-learner before applying the B-spline activation.

## Method

The key insight is that all univariate activation functions in a KAN draw from a shared functional class (the same B-spline basis), so their weights obey a common generation rule that can be learned by a small meta-learner. MetaKANs replace each per-edge weight vector w ∈ R^(G+k+1) with a scalar learnable prompt z, and a two-layer MLP meta-learner M_θ(z) → w that predicts the spline coefficients at inference time. For deep KANs, layers are clustered by channel dimension (via k-means) and each cluster is assigned a dedicated meta-learner to capture layer-dependent weight-generation patterns. Training is fully end-to-end: prompts and meta-learner parameters are jointly optimized by gradient descent on the downstream task loss. The resulting parameter count is approximately Σ(n_l × n_{l+1}) + C·(d_hidden+1)·(G+k+1), which scales like a standard MLP and is model-agnostic—applicable to FastKAN, WavKAN, and ConvKAN variants by simply changing the meta-learner's output dimension.

## Results

On the Feynman symbolic regression benchmark (16 equations), MetaKANs achieve lower MSE in 11/17 functions at G=5 with 21–47% fewer parameters, and in 13/16 cases at G=20 with ~18% fewer parameters on average. On convolutional image classification (Table 3), MetaKANConv reduces parameters by 89% (392,887 vs. 3,494,958 for 4-layer CIFAR-10) while improving accuracy from 41.92% to 45.97%. For 8-layer architectures, MetaFastKANConv reaches 78.73% on CIFAR-10 with 78% fewer parameters than FastKANConv (9,051,033 vs. 40,695,648), and MetaKAGNConv achieves 57.76% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 56.41% baseline) with 80% parameter reduction. Across fully-connected classification (Table 5), MetaKANs consistently achieve 85–94% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy (e.g., 78.34% vs. 51.16% on SVHN at G=3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original KAN (Liu et al., 2024), MetaKANs achieve comparable or superior MSE on 11/17 Feynman equations at G=5 and 13/16 at G=20, while using only 1/3 to 1/9 of the trainable parameters; on CIFAR-10 (4-layer), MetaKANConv improves accuracy from 41.92% to 45.97% with 89% fewer parameters. Against FastKAN (Li, 2024), MetaFastKANConv slightly trails on CIFAR-10 (78.73% vs. 79.84%, 8-layer) but surpasses it on CIFAR-100 (52.02% vs. 50.80%) while using 78% fewer parameters. Against WavKAN (Bozorgasl & Chen, 2024), MetaWavKAN achieves 73.62% on SVHN (Mexican Hat) vs. 67.05% baseline with ~66% parameter reduction, and 49.52% on CIFAR-10 (8-layer) vs. 44.81% baseline. Against ConvKAN (Bodner et al., 2024), MetaKANConv wins on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (4-layer) but slightly trails on MNIST (96.03% vs. 98.43%). Against KALN (SS et al., 2024), MetaKALNConv dramatically outperforms on MNIST 8-layer (99.53% vs. 69.02%) and CIFAR-10 8-layer (70.23% vs. 29.14%). Against KAGN (Bresson et al., 2024), MetaKAGNConv matches or slightly exceeds on MNIST (99.21% vs. 99.15%) and CIFAR-10 (73.49% vs. 72.08%, 4-layer) with 80% fewer parameters, and surpasses on CIFAR-100 8-layer (57.76% vs. 56.41%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), which replace fixed activations with learnable spline-parametrized univariate functions, and hypernetworks, which use a smaller auxiliary network to generate the weights of a larger one. The key conceptual departure from prior hypernetwork work is the grounding in KANs' specific structure: the authors observe that all activation functions in a KAN belong to a shared function class, reframing weight learning as a multi-task problem over that class, and use learnable scalar prompts as task identifiers rather than heuristic layer-level templates. The extension to deep KANs via layer clustering (grouping layers by channel dimension and assigning dedicated meta-learners) is a practical design choice not present in standard hypernetwork formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing KAN parameter counts to approximately MLP-level (1/3 to 1/9 of original KAN parameters), MetaKANs address the primary practical barrier to scaling KANs to larger datasets and deeper architectures, while preserving the interpretability that motivates KANs in the first place. The model-agnostic design (applicable to FastKAN, WavKAN, ConvKAN) broadens the impact across the rapidly growing KAN variant ecosystem. However, the paper acknowledges that both KANs and MetaKANs still underperform established baselines on image classification benchmarks, and the authors identify transferring weight-prediction rules across novel KAN training tasks as an open direction. The condition that MetaKANs only save parameters for sufficiently large networks (Section 3.4) limits applicability to small-scale function fitting.
