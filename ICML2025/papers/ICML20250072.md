## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for asynchronous stochastic optimization under arbitrary (possibly adversarial) delays depend on the average delay, which is fragile to the outliers common in large-scale distributed settings, and no accelerated rates have been established for convex smooth objectives. Moreover, most prior methods require a known upper bound on the average delay or the number of workers, and their analyses are ad-hoc rather than reusable across optimization scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method is a black-box wrapper: it takes any standard K-query stochastic first-order optimization algorithm (SGD, accelerated SGD, projected SGD) together with a batch size parameter, and operates in an asynchronous loop where each round delivers a stochastic gradient computed at a potentially stale iterate with an arbitrary delay. It outputs an optimized point whose convergence rate depends on the q-quantile delay τ_q rather than the average delay; the quantile-adaptive variant (Algorithm 2) requires no prior delay bound and automatically matches the best quantile in hindsight via a doubling sweep over batch sizes.

## Method

The core mechanism is asynchronous mini-batching with stale-gradient filtering: for each inner query of the wrapped algorithm, the server accumulates B gradients while discarding any whose delay exceeds the current iteration window (ensuring the gradient was computed at the correct iterate), thereby reducing stochastic variance by a factor of 1/B without waiting for the slowest worker. The batch size is set to the q-quantile delay bound, so the effective number of inner steps scales as qT/(1+2τ_q), yielding rates of O(τ_q/(qT) + σ/√(qT)) for non-convex and O(τ_q²/(qT)² + σ/√(qT)) for convex smooth problems. For quantile adaptivity, Algorithm 2 runs a geometric sweep over doubling batch sizes and epoch lengths, selecting the last completed epoch's output; a key lemma bounds the total rounds T in terms of the final epoch's batch size and the q-quantile delay, enabling an infimum-over-q guarantee without any prior knowledge of the delay distribution. The approach is fully black-box, requiring only the standard convergence analysis of the inner algorithm rather than a specialized asynchronous analysis.

## Results

On FashionMNIST with a simulated asynchronous setting (Poisson-distributed compute times), Async-MB-SGD outperforms vanilla Async-SGD as the number of workers scales: at 40 workers (7,500 steps) the gap is marginal (84.57% vs 84.49%), at 160 workers (30,000 steps) Async-MB-SGD leads by 0.35% (86.20% vs 85.85%), and at 640 workers (120,000 steps) the gap widens to 1.14% (87.37% vs 86.23%). Async-MB-SGD also supports significantly larger learning rates (e.g., 0.10 vs 0.002154 at 640 workers) and exhibits smoother training curves, attributed to gradient filtering reducing effective delay and mini-batching reducing variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is vanilla asynchronous SGD (Async-SGD), which applies each received gradient immediately without filtering or mini-batching. Async-MB-SGD matches Async-SGD at 40 workers (84.57% vs 84.49%) but pulls ahead as asynchrony increases: +0.35% at 160 workers and +1.14% at 640 workers. The advantage grows with scale because gradient filtering discards the most stale gradients and mini-batching averages over B workers, both of which reduce the effective delay and variance that degrade vanilla Async-SGD under high worker counts. Notably, Async-MB-SGD can use learning rates 10–50× larger than Async-SGD at the same worker count, reflecting its reduced sensitivity to stale gradients. The paper's primary contribution, however, is theoretical: Table 1 shows that the proposed black-box conversion yields convergence rates depending on the q-quantile delay τ_q (e.g., O(τ_q/(qT) + σ/√(qT)) for non-convex) rather than the average delay τ_avg used by prior work (Cohen et al., 2021; Mishchenko et al., 2022; Koloskova et al., 2022), and provides the first accelerated O(τ_q²/(qT)² + σ/√(qT)) rate for convex smooth problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of asynchronous stochastic optimization under arbitrary delays (Cohen et al., 2021; Aviv et al., 2021; Mishchenko et al., 2022; Koloskova et al., 2022; Feyzmahdavian & Johansson, 2023) by replacing dependence on the average delay with dependence on an arbitrary quantile delay, achieved through a black-box asynchronous mini-batching conversion of standard first-order methods. It generalizes the fixed-compute-time mini-batching framework of Tyurin & Richtarik (2023) to fully arbitrary (possibly adversarial) delay sequences, and departs from prior work by requiring no known delay bound in its quantile-adaptive variant (Algorithm 2). The black-box design means that any new convergence result for a standard stochastic method immediately yields an asynchronous counterpart, avoiding the ad-hoc per-scenario analyses that characterized earlier asynchronous optimization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from average-delay to quantile-delay guarantees is practically meaningful because the median (or lower quantiles) can be orders of magnitude smaller than the average in settings with a few very slow workers, and the quantile-adaptive algorithm automatically selects the best quantile in hindsight without any prior knowledge. The first accelerated O(1/T²) rate for convex smooth objectives under arbitrary delays is a notable theoretical milestone. However, the experimental validation is limited to a single small-scale classification task (FashionMNIST) with modest accuracy gains (≤1.1%), and the paper does not address heterogeneous data distributions, Markovian sampling, or the practical overhead of the doubling procedure in Algorithm 2, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages translate to large-scale distributed training.
