## Problem

Personalized federated learning (PFL) trades off statistical accuracy against communication cost via a personalization parameter, yet the quantitative influence of this parameter on both sides of the trade-off remained unexplored. Prior work analyzed Problem (2) purely from an optimization perspective without establishing statistical convergence rates, and existing statistical analyses (e.g., Chen et al., 2023c) could not match the minimax lower bound. This paper fills the gap by providing the first minimax-optimal statistical rate for the PFL formulation and a unified characterization of how the personalization degree governs the accuracy-communication trade-off.

## Input / Output

Each of m clients holds n local samples drawn i.i.d. from a potentially heterogeneous distribution D_i, and the algorithm takes a scalar personalization parameter λ that interpolates between pure local training (λ→0) and fully collaborative global training (λ→∞). The output is a pair of models per client: a personalized local model w^(i) and a shared global model w^(g), with the local model's statistical error explicitly bounded as a function of λ, the total sample size N=mn, and the heterogeneity measure R. The bilevel structure means the global model is optimized at the outer level while each local model is the proximal (Moreau envelope) minimizer at the inner level, so the output is not a single shared model but a coupled global-local pair.

## Method

The paper reformulates the PFL objective as a bilevel problem where the outer objective F(w^(g)) is the average Moreau envelope of the local losses, with gradient ∇F_i(w^(g)) = λ(w^(g) − w_⋆^(i)(w^(g))). The proposed algorithm FedCLUP performs gradient descent on the global model at the outer level while approximating the inner-level proximal operator via K warm-started local gradient steps per communication round, yielding a communication complexity of O(κ(λ+μ)/(λ+L) · log(1/ε)) and computation cost independent of λ. On the statistical side, the analysis is algorithm-independent: it directly bounds the estimation error of the Problem (2) solution using strong convexity, smoothness, and bounded gradient variance, decomposing the error into a collaborative term O(1/N + R²) that vanishes as λ→∞ and a local-training term O(1/n) that dominates as λ→0. By choosing λ appropriately relative to R and n, the paper proves the resulting rate matches the minimax lower bound, establishing the first minimax-optimality guarantee for this PFL formulation.

## Results

On MNIST with a 5-layer CNN, FedCLUP at low personalization achieves 94.21% test accuracy, approaching GlobalTrain (94.33%) while far exceeding LocalTrain (76.33%). On CIFAR-10 with a 3-layer CNN, FedCLUP at low personalization reaches 73.02%, outperforming LocalTrain (62.38%) but trailing GlobalTrain (80.41%). On EMNIST with a 5-layer CNN, FedCLUP at low personalization (66.72%) actually surpasses GlobalTrain (66.11%), demonstrating that personalized collaboration can exceed full pooling under moderate heterogeneity. The communication complexity of FedCLUP scales as O(κ(λ+μ)/(λ+L) · log(1/ε)), reducing from O(κ log(1/ε)) at full collaboration to O(log(1/ε)) at full personalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCLUP consistently outperforms pFedMe (T Dinh et al., 2020) across most dataset-model combinations: on MNIST Logit, FedCLUP Low (83.12%) beats pFedMe Low (82.91%); on MNIST CNN2, FedCLUP Low (90.32%) exceeds pFedMe Low (87.49%) by nearly 3 points; on CIFAR-10 CNN3, FedCLUP Low (73.02%) trails pFedMe Low (77.32%), the one clear loss. On MNIST CNN5, pFedMe Low (94.40%) marginally edges out FedCLUP Low (94.21%), while on EMNIST CNN5 pFedMe Low (67.17%) slightly leads FedCLUP Low (66.72%). Against GlobalTrain, FedCLUP at low personalization approaches or matches full pooling on MNIST (94.21% vs 94.33%) and even surpasses it on EMNIST (66.72% vs 66.11%), but falls short on CIFAR-10 (73.02% vs 80.41%) where higher heterogeneity makes full collaboration more beneficial. Against LocalTrain, FedCLUP at any personalization degree substantially improves accuracy (e.g., +17.88 points on MNIST CNN5, +10.64 on CIFAR-10 CNN3), confirming the value of even modest collaboration. The theoretical contribution positions FedCLUP as the first method to simultaneously provide minimax-optimal statistical guarantees and explicit communication complexity bounds for Problem (2), whereas prior methods (FedProx, L2GD, AL2SGD, Algorithm3 of Chen et al.) either lack statistical error analysis or have looser bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Moreau-envelope-based personalized FL formulation (Problem 2) introduced by Hanzely & Richtárik (2020) and T Dinh et al. (2020), and leverages bilevel optimization techniques from Ji et al. (2022) to design the FedCLUP algorithm. It departs sharply from the dominant optimization-only perspective of prior PFL work (FedProx, pFedMe, L2GD, AL2SGD) by providing the first non-asymptotic statistical accuracy analysis for Problem (2), and from Chen et al. (2023c) by achieving minimax-optimal rates where prior analyses could not match the lower bound. The paper is positioned as the first to jointly characterize how the personalization parameter λ simultaneously governs statistical accuracy and communication complexity, filling a gap that purely algorithmic analyses left open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax-optimality result for Problem (2) is the first of its kind and establishes that the widely-used regularization-based PFL formulation is statistically efficient under appropriate personalization, giving practitioners confidence in the formulation itself. The quantitative trade-off characterization provides actionable guidance for choosing λ based on data heterogeneity R and communication budget, which is directly relevant to deploying PFL in resource-constrained settings. Key limitations include the reliance on strong convexity and L-smoothness for the theoretical guarantees (though non-convex generalization is empirically validated), and the assumption of uniform client weights and sample sizes. Open questions include per-client personalization (heterogeneous λ_i), non-convex theoretical guarantees, and the practical viability of the proposed dynamic λ tuning strategy at scale.
