## Problem

Contextual dueling bandits with adversarial preference feedback remain largely unexplored: prior work (Agarwal et al., 2021) is confined to finite-armed settings and assumes a weaker adversary that cannot observe the selected action pair before corrupting labels. This paper addresses the gap by studying a strong adversary who sees both the chosen actions and the true preference, and by extending the problem to infinite action spaces with contextual information, as needed for applications like RLHF.

## Input / Output

Each round the algorithm receives a context vector x_t, a finite action set A with a known bounded feature map φ(x,a) (‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1), and a binary preference label o_t ∈ {0,1} that may have been flipped by a strong adversary who observes the selected pair (a_t, b_t) and the true label before deciding to corrupt. The algorithm outputs a selected action pair (a_t, b_t) maximizing an estimated reward plus an exploration bonus, and maintains a parameter estimate θ_t via a weighted MLE. The total number of adversarial flips C is bounded but may be unknown to the learner.

## Method

RCDB integrates uncertainty-dependent weights w_i = min{1, α/‖φ_i‖_{Σ_i^{-1}}} into the MLE, where the weight downweights observations with high posterior prediction variance—intuitively, data points that are harder to predict are more likely to be adversarial. The covariance matrix Σ_t accumulates weighted outer products of feature differences, and action selection maximizes the estimated reward (φ(x,a)+φ(x,b))^T θ_t plus a confidence bonus β‖φ(x,a)−φ(x,b)‖_{Σ_t^{-1}}. This yields a regret of Õ(d√T/κ + dC/κ), matching a matching lower bound and thus achieving near-minimax optimality. For the sigmoid link function, RCDB-S replaces the uniform derivative lower bound κ with a refined local derivative estimate v_i = max{κ, σ̇(Δ̂_i)} constructed from a confidence set, eliminating the κ-dependence in the leading T-term and reducing the exponential dependence on the parameter radius B to a polynomial one (B^{1.5}).

## Results

RCDB achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T}/\kappa + dC/\kappa)$ for contextual dueling bandits with adversarial feedback, where $d$ is the context dimension, $T$ is the number of rounds, $C$ is the total adversarial feedback count, and $\kappa$ is the lower bound on the link function derivative. A matching lower bound of $\Omega((d\sqrt{T}+dC)/\kappa)$ is established, confirming near-minimax optimality. For the sigmoid link function, the improved algorithm RCDB-S achieves $\tilde{O}(dB^{1.5}\sqrt{T} + dBC/\kappa)$, eliminating the $\kappa$ dependence in the leading $T$-term and reducing the exponential dependence on parameter radius $B$ to polynomial. The paper also reports experiments (Appendix E) showing RCDB outperforms state-of-the-art dueling bandit algorithms under various adversarial feedback types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The two closest baselines for dueling bandits with adversarial feedback are WIWR (Agarwal et al., 2021) and Versatile-DB (Saha & Gaillard, 2022), both of which operate in the non-contextual K-armed setting. RCDB is the first algorithm to handle the contextual dueling bandit setting with adversarial feedback, achieving $\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T}+dC)$ regret, which is the natural contextual analogue of the $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT}+KC)$ bound achieved by CW-OFUL for linear contextual bandits. Unlike WIWR, whose regret depends on the minimum gap $\Delta_{\min}$ and scales as $K^2$, RCDB's bound depends on the context dimension $d$ and the horizon $T$ in a $\sqrt{T}$ fashion, making it applicable to large action spaces. Versatile-DB achieves a best-of-both-worlds guarantee for non-contextual dueling bandits but does not extend to the contextual setting. The paper's experiments (Appendix E) confirm that RCDB outperforms these state-of-the-art dueling bandit algorithms under various adversarial feedback patterns, validating the theoretical advantage of the uncertainty-weighted MLE approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uncertainty-weighted MLE framework of He et al. (2022) from linear contextual bandits to the dueling bandit setting, adapting the weight design to account for the generalized linear model structure of preference feedback. It builds on the arm-selection rules and regret definition from Di et al. (2023) for stochastic contextual dueling bandits, and borrows the local-derivative estimation technique from Abeille et al. (2021) for the sigmoid-specific improvement. The key departure from prior adversarial dueling bandit work (Agarwal et al. 2021; Saha & Gaillard 2022) is the move from finite-armed to contextual settings and the adoption of a strong adversary model where the adversary observes the selected action pair before deciding to flip the label.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve nearly minimax optimal regret for contextual dueling bandits under adversarial preference feedback, directly addressing a vulnerability in RLHF pipelines where adversaries can manipulate human preference labels. The sigmoid-specific result (RCDB-S) eliminates the κ dependence from the leading regret term, reducing an exponential dependence on the parameter radius B to a polynomial one—a meaningful theoretical improvement for practical RLHF settings where B can be large. Open questions include extending to nonlinear reward functions (e.g., neural networks), handling computationally efficient alternatives to per-round MLE, and whether the strong-adversary trade-off (sublinear regret only when C ≤ C̄) can be further tightened.
