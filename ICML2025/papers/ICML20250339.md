## Problem

Scaling Transformer hidden dimensions uniformly inflates both parameter count and computational cost, yet prior work has focused on sparsity in the intermediate (FFN) dimension while leaving hidden-dimension sparsity largely unexplored. The paper identifies that hidden states exhibit strong long-tail activation sparsity with a consistent split between dimensions shared across tokens and dimensions unique to individual tokens, motivating a sparse architecture that can expand hidden-dimension capacity without proportionally increasing activated parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250339/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard text token sequences (length 4096) during pretraining on 50B tokens from RedPajama and produces next-token predictions via cross-entropy loss. At inference, each token's hidden representation is sparsified: only K = ⌊δN⌋ out of N sub-dimensions receive non-zero routing weights, so the effective computation per token scales with the sparsity ratio δ rather than the full hidden dimension d. The resulting pre-trained model is evaluated zero-shot or few-shot on 10 NLP benchmarks spanning commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, and language modeling.

## Method

MOHD partitions each layer's hidden space into N sub-dimensions of size d_e and splits them into a shared portion (φN sub-dimensions, always active for all tokens) and a specialized portion ((δ−φ)N sub-dimensions, selected per token via Top-k routing over softmax scores computed against learned sub-dimension centroids). To counteract information loss from dimensional downsampling, an activation scaling factor α = Σg_i·N restores magnitude, and a Grouped Fusion Layer built from Monarch matrices (receptive field r, complexity O(d/r)) projects the sparsified output back to the full d-dimensional space. A load-balance loss (adapted from DeepSeek MoE) regularizes the router to prevent collapse onto a few sub-dimensions. The sparsification is applied jointly to both Attention (Q, K, V, O projections) and FFN (up, gate, down matrices), with residual connections and pre-norm preserving the activation flow pattern observed empirically in LLaMA2-7B.

## Results

On 10 NLP tasks (SciQ, PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC-E, ARC-C, HellaSwag, LogiQA, BoolQ, LAMBADA, MMLU), MOHD×3-3.41B with only 1B activated parameters achieves 47.9 average, surpassing LLaMA2-2.7B (2.54B activated, 46.2 avg) by 1.7 points. In the expansion setting, MOHD×2-2.27B (1B activated) reaches 47.2 avg, a 3.0-point gain over LLaMA2-1.13B (1B activated, 44.2 avg). In the compression setting, MOHD 50%-1.13B (503M activated) achieves 45.9 avg, exceeding the full LLaMA2-1.13B baseline (44.2) while using only 50% of the activated parameters. At the 495M scale, MOHD×3-1.48B (396M activated) attains 46.0 avg, a 2.2-point improvement over LLaMA2-495M (396M activated, 43.8 avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA2 baselines, MOHD consistently wins at equal activated parameters: MOHD×3-3.41B (1B activated) achieves 47.9 avg vs. LLaMA2-1.13B (1B activated) at 44.2, a 3.7-point gain, and even surpasses the much larger LLaMA2-2.7B (2.54B activated, 46.2 avg). In the compression setting, MOHD 50%-1.13B (503M activated) reaches 45.9 avg, exceeding the full LLaMA2-1.13B (44.2) while activating only half the parameters. Against DeepSeek MoE at the 355M scale (Table 2), MOHD outperforms MoE by approximately 2 points on average across all sparsity levels: MoHD 75% scores 46.3 vs. MoE 75% at 44.3 (+2.0), MoHD×2 scores 48.2 vs. MoE×2 at 46.0 (+2.2), and MoHD×3 scores 48.4 vs. MoE×3 at 46.5 (+1.9). The advantage is most pronounced in the expansion setting, where MOHD's hidden-dimension sparsity provides greater representational capacity per activated parameter than MoE's intermediate-dimension expert routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOHD extends the sparsely-activated Transformer lineage (MoE, Switch Transformers, DeepSeek MoE) by shifting the sparsity axis from the FFN intermediate dimension to the hidden dimension itself, applying sparse conditional activation across all projection matrices (Q, K, V, O, up, gate, down) simultaneously. It builds on activation sparsity observations (Liu et al. 2024, You et al. 2022) but uniquely identifies the shared-vs-specialized structure of hidden dimension activations across consecutive tokens, motivating the mixed routing design. The use of Monarch matrices for group fusion and the theoretical grounding via Barron-space approximation and Rademacher complexity analysis distinguish it from purely empirical MoE variants, positioning MOHD as a complementary mechanism to intermediate-dimension sparsity rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOHD opens a previously underexplored scaling axis (hidden dimension) with controllable compute cost, offering a principled alternative to uniform parameter expansion. The theoretical analysis (Appendix B) provides the first formal argument that mixed shared/specialized activation strictly dominates token-only activation in risk bounds, lending architectural justification beyond empirical gains. However, all experiments are limited to ≤1.13B parameters and 50B tokens on RedPajama, leaving open whether the 3–8% gains persist at 7B+ scale or with longer training. A key open question is the interaction with MoE: the paper argues complementarity but provides no joint architecture, and the routing overhead at very large hidden dimensions (4× expansion) remains uncharacterized for inference latency.
