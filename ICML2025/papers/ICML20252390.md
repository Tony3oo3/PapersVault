## Problem

Existing controllable text-to-music generation methods based on ControlNet-style fine-tuning require duplicating half the diffusion backbone as a trainable copy, leading to high parameter counts (572M+) and increased training/inference costs. Additionally, no prior fine-tuning method simultaneously supports both time-varying musical attribute conditions (melody, rhythm, dynamics) and reference audio conditions for inpainting/outpainting within a single framework.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus any combination of time-varying conditions: melody (128-bin CQT with top-4 pitch retention and 261.2 Hz high-pass filter), rhythm (beat/downbeat probability pairs from a recurrent beat detector), dynamics (Savitzky-Golay-smoothed spectrogram energy in dB), and optionally a VAE-encoded reference audio latent for inpainting/outpainting. Conditions are processed through separate 1D CNN feature extractors, interpolated to match the query sequence length, and concatenated into a single condition vector. Output is a 47-second stereo audio clip at 44.1 kHz generated via 50 denoising steps of the Stable Audio Open diffusion Transformer.

## Method

MuseControlLite augments the decoupled cross-attention mechanism (from IP-Adapter) with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) applied to query, key, and value vectors, enabling the model to learn time-varying conditions with only 85M trainable parameters (duplicated W'k and W'v projections plus feature extractors and zero-initialized 1D convolutions). The key empirical finding is that RoPE is essential: without it, melody accuracy collapses from 58.6% to 10.7%, because positional information is what allows the decoupled attention to align condition tokens with the correct temporal positions in the audio sequence. Two independent adapter sets are trained—one for musical attributes and one for audio conditioning—using complementary masks so the model learns to attend to distant tokens for smooth boundary transitions. At inference, multiple classifier-free guidance with separate scales (λ_text=7.0, λ_attr=2.0, λ_audio=1.0) allows flexible control strength per condition type, preventing over-fixation that degrades audio quality.

## Results

On the Song Describer benchmark for melody-conditioned generation, MuseControlLite-Melody achieves 61.1% melody accuracy and 76.42 FD, outperforming Stable Audio Open ControlNet (56.6% melody accuracy, 97.73 FD) and MusicGen-Stereo-Large-Melody (43.1% melody accuracy, 193.66 FD). This is accomplished with only 85M trainable parameters—6.75× fewer than Stable Audio Open ControlNet's 572M—while using less training data (1.7K hr vs. 2.2K hr). In subjective evaluation, MuseControlLite scores 4.21/5 on melody similarity, marginally exceeding Stable Audio Open ControlNet (4.17/5) and far surpassing MusicGen (2.67/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MusicGen-Stereo-Large-Melody, MuseControlLite wins decisively on every metric: melody accuracy improves from 43.1% to 61.1%, FD drops from 193.66 to 76.42, and KL decreases from 0.436 to 0.289, while using roughly 39× fewer trainable parameters (85M vs. 3.3B). Against Stable Audio Open ControlNet, MuseControlLite achieves a 4.5 percentage-point gain in melody accuracy (61.1% vs. 56.6%) and a 21.3-point improvement in FD (76.42 vs. 97.73), but trails slightly on KL (0.289 vs. 0.265) and CLAP (0.372 vs. 0.396), which the authors attribute to using a less diverse training set (MTG-Jamendo only vs. four datasets). In the audio outpainting task (Table 6), MuseControlLite with Text+Audio+Melody achieves the best FD (138.07) and melody accuracy (0.57) among all methods, while the Naive masking baseline (from DITTO) achieves the highest CLAP (0.39) but the worst smoothness value (−0.40), indicating abrupt boundary transitions. In subjective evaluation, MuseControlLite is essentially on par with Stable Audio Open ControlNet (overall preference 3.63 vs. 3.65; melody similarity 4.21 vs. 4.17) while requiring 6.75× fewer trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the decoupled cross-attention mechanism from IP-Adapter (originally for image conditioning) to the music domain, combining it with rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) to handle time-varying musical attributes. It departs from the dominant ControlNet-based approach (Music ControlNet, Stable Audio Open ControlNet), which duplicates half the diffusion model as a trainable copy, by instead training only lightweight W'k and W'v projections in separate cross-attention layers. The zero-initialized 1D convolution for combining outputs follows the ControlNet design, and the base architecture is Stable Audio Open's diffusion Transformer. The key conceptual departure is the finding that positional embeddings—seldom used in the cross-attention conditioner for text conditions—are essential when the condition is a function of time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.75× reduction in trainable parameters (85M vs. 572M) makes fine-grained music controllability accessible on consumer hardware (single RTX 3090), lowering the barrier for creative tool development. The first unified handling of both attribute and audio conditions enables practical workflows like melody-guided inpainting that were previously impossible. However, the model is trained solely on MTG-Jamendo (predominantly electronic music), limiting generalizability to other genres, and the multi-classifier-free-guidance approach adds inference overhead. Open questions include whether the RoPE-in-cross-attention finding generalizes to other temporal conditioning tasks beyond music, and whether the complementary masking strategy between attribute and audio conditions can be made more flexible.
