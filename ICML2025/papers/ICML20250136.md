## Problem

Existing integrated learning and optimization (ILO) methods such as SPO+ and SLO guarantee optimal decision performance only when the hypothesis class is well-specified (i.e., contains the true cost function). In practice, models are often misspecified due to incomplete features or unmodeled dependencies, yet no prior approach provides provable global optimality for decision cost under misspecification. This paper closes that gap by introducing a surrogate loss whose minimizers coincide with those of the true decision error regardless of misspecification level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a historical dataset of n pairs (x_i, c_i) where x_i ∈ R^k is a context vector and c_i ∈ R^d is a cost vector, along with a bounded convex decision set W ⊂ R^d. It produces predictor parameters θ ∈ R^m (for a linear hypothesis c̃_θ(x) = Φ(x)θ) that define a decision policy w(θ) mapping contexts to decisions in W. A key practical detail is that the user must perform a line search over an auxiliary threshold β ∈ [β_min,P, β_SPO+] to select the constraint level, and the method requires W to be a polyhedron for the strongest consistency and landscape guarantees.

## Method

The core contribution is the CILO loss ℓ_P^β(θ), defined as the gap between the optimal expected predicted cost under a constrained policy set (requiring expected true cost ≤ β) and the unconstrained optimal predicted cost. This loss is proven consistent: its global minimizers are exactly the minimizers of the true decision error ℓ_P, even under arbitrary misspecification. Structurally, ℓ_P^β is a difference of two convex (DC) functions, which the paper smooths via the Moreau envelope to obtain the s-CILO loss r_{P_n}^β. For linear hypothesis sets, r_{P_n}^β is everywhere differentiable and every stationary point is a global minimum, making plain gradient descent sufficient. A log-barrier variant (log-CILO) is introduced to handle the degenerate case where gradient descent converges to θ=0, ensuring the recovered solution is bounded away from zero and satisfies the non-vanishing condition required for consistency.

## Results

The paper evaluates regret (ℓ_P(θ) − β_min,P) across varying levels of misspecification (s = 5 to s = 20, where s controls the dimensionality gap between the true cost function and the hypothesis class). In the well-specified setting (s = 5), SLO achieves the lowest regret, consistent with prior findings. As misspecification increases (s > 5), the proposed CILO method achieves the lowest regret, with the gap widening as s grows, while SLO degrades to the worst-performing method. Results are presented as box plots over 96 independent experiments per setting, showing both the central tendency and variability of regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CILO against two baselines: SLO (Hu et al., 2022) and SPO+ (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022). In the well-specified regime (s = 5), SLO achieves the lowest regret, outperforming both SPO+ and CILO, which aligns with the known result that prediction-focused methods excel when the hypothesis class contains the true cost function. In the misspecified regime (s > 5), CILO consistently achieves the lowest regret, outperforming both SPO+ and SLO, with the advantage growing as the misspecification level increases. SLO performs worst in the misspecified setting because it optimizes prediction accuracy rather than decision quality, while SPO+ improves over SLO but still fails to match CILO because its surrogate loss lacks a consistency guarantee under misspecification. The key differentiator is that CILO is the first method with a provable global optimality guarantee for the true decision loss ℓ_P under arbitrary levels of misspecification, whereas SPO+ and SLO only guarantee optimality in the well-specified case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Integrated Learning and Optimization (ILO) framework, building directly on SPO+ (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022) and SLO (Bertsimas and Kallus, 2020; Hu et al., 2022), which are the two dominant paradigms for contextual optimization. The key departure is that all prior ILO surrogate losses (SPO+, maximum optimality margin, etc.) guarantee consistency only when the hypothesis set is well-specified; this paper introduces the CILO loss, a difference-of-convex (DC) surrogate that remains consistent under misspecification. For optimization, it repurposes Moreau envelope smoothing for DC programs (Sun and Sun, 2021) and DC constrained minimization (Pang et al., 2017) to handle the non-convex, non-smooth structure of the CILO loss, adding a log-barrier trick to avoid the degenerate θ=0 solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to provably minimize the true decision-cost objective under model misspecification, closing a gap that prior ILO work explicitly left open. The conceptual distinction between prediction misspecification and decision misspecification reframes how practitioners should think about model adequacy in predict-then-optimize pipelines. However, global optimality guarantees are restricted to linear hypothesis sets, the β parameter requires a line search over a potentially large interval, and all experiments are synthetic with a single linear setup—real-world validation and extension to non-linear predictors remain open. The paper also acknowledges that the stability assumption (Assumption 5) requiring W to be a polyhedron may limit applicability to general convex decision sets.
