## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods (GroupDRO, logit correction, IRM) are designed for subpopulation shifts where all attribute groups are present but imbalanced. They fail under the harder compositional shift, where entire attribute combinations are absent from training but appear at test time, because standard ERM assigns zero probability to unseen groups. This paper asks whether a classifier can provably extrapolate to novel attribute combinations that were never observed during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-attribute inputs (RGB images at 224×224 or text sentences) where each sample is labeled with a vector of m categorical attributes (e.g., bird class + background type, or toxicity + demographic identity). It outputs joint group probabilities over all attribute combinations via softmax, from which marginal class predictions are obtained by summing over spurious attributes. Crucially, at test time the model must produce non-trivial probabilities for attribute combinations that had zero training samples, using only the learned per-attribute energy components.

## Method

CRM trains an additive energy classifier where the logit for group z=(z₁,…,zₘ) is computed by summing per-attribute energy vectors via broadcasting (E₁ + E₂ + … rather than a single linear projection), plus a log-prior and a learnable per-group bias B(z). In step 1, this classifier is trained by standard cross-entropy on training groups. In step 2, the classifier is adjusted for test: the train prior is replaced with the test prior (uniform if unknown), and the bias for unseen groups is extrapolated via B*(z) = log E_{x~p}[exp(-⟨σ(z),Ê(x)⟩) / Σ_{z̃∈Z^train} exp(-⟨σ(z̃),Ê(x)⟩ + log p(z̃) - B̂(z̃))], an expectation computed over training data. The key theoretical result is that under the additive energy distribution assumption, CRM provably recovers the Bayes-optimal test classifier for all groups in the discrete affine hull of the training support, and this hull grows to cover the full Cartesian product with only O(md + d log d) training groups.

## Results

CRM achieves the best Worst Group Accuracy (WGA) across all six benchmarks under compositional shift (one group removed from training): 78.7% on Waterbirds, 81.8% on CelebA, 73.4% on MetaShift, 58.7% on MultiNLI, 67.9% on CivilComments, and 40.3% on NICO++. Average accuracy is competitive with the best logit-adjustment baselines (e.g., 87.1% vs. sLA's 89.3% on Waterbirds), while WGA margins over the strongest baseline (sLA) range from +1.4 pp (Waterbirds) to +24.4 pp (CelebA). In the worst-case compositional shift scenario, CRM's advantage over logit adjustment grows substantially (e.g., 73.0% vs. LC's 69.0% on Waterbirds; 31.0% vs. sLA's 19.7% on MultiNLI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRM outperforms all four main baselines (G-DRO, LC, sLA, and ERM) on Worst Group Accuracy across every dataset. The largest WGA gains are on CelebA (+14.0 pp over G-DRO, +24.4 pp over LC/sLA) and MultiNLI (+4.7 pp over sLA, +24.4 pp over G-DRO), where compositional shift is most damaging to standard methods. On Waterbirds, CRM's WGA advantage over the closest competitor (sLA) is modest (+1.4 pp on average) but widens to +4.0 pp in the worst-case shift scenario. G-DRO, which minimizes worst-case group loss, suffers the largest WGA degradation when a group is entirely absent from training (e.g., 87.3→42.3 on Waterbirds), confirming its reliance on seeing all groups. IRM, VREx, and Mixup (reported in Appendix G.1) are consistently outperformed by CRM on WGA, with CRM's advantage particularly pronounced on text datasets (MultiNLI, CivilComments). On Average Accuracy, CRM is within 1–2 pp of the best logit-adjustment baselines, indicating that its WGA gains do not come at the cost of overall predictive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes additive energy distributions—previously used in generative compositional models (Du et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022a)—for discriminative classification, introducing the discrete affine hull as a new mathematical object to characterize extrapolation. It departs from prior provable compositional generalization results (Dong & Ma, 2022; Wiedemer et al., 2023; Lachapelle et al., 2024), which assume deterministic additive decoders, by working with stochastic additive energy distributions that model richer input distributions. The paper also extends the standard subpopulation shift setting (Sagawa et al., 2019) to the harder compositional shift where entire attribute groups are absent from training, and contrasts its approach with logit-adjustment methods (LC, sLA) and GroupDRO by showing that a principled bias extrapolation step, grounded in the additive energy structure, is necessary for robust generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRM provides the first provable compositional generalization guarantees for discriminative tasks under flexible (stochastic) data models, with a sharp characterization showing extrapolation is possible exactly within the discrete affine hull and no further. The finding that the affine hull covers all d^m combinations with only O(md + d log d) training groups is a notable theoretical result with practical implications for data efficiency. Key limitations include the requirement for labeled attributes (not always available) and the assumption that the additive energy form holds; extending CRM to generative tasks and to settings without explicit attribute labels are identified as important open directions.
