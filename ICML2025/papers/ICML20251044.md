## Problem

Language models lack a notion of interchangeable tokens—symbols that are semantically equivalent yet distinct, such as bound variables in formal logic. Standard embedding architectures cannot accept tokens unseen during training, preventing generalization to larger vocabularies and making it impossible to recognize alpha-equivalence (renaming bound variables preserves meaning). This paper formalizes the problem and introduces alpha-covariance, a metric for evaluating robustness to such transformations.

## Input / Output

The method is evaluated on three autoregressive language-modeling tasks: (1) copying, where a token string is input and the identical string is produced; (2) LTL solving, where a formula in Polish (prefix) notation is input and a satisfying symbolic trace in lasso form u v^ω (with position delimiters ';' and loop markers '{','}') is generated; (3) propositional logic assignment prediction, where a formula is input and a partial Boolean assignment is output as an alternating sequence of atomic propositions and 0/1 values. In all tasks, the critical constraint is that the model must handle atomic propositions (interchangeable tokens) not present in the training vocabulary.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-part token embedding strategy where each interchangeable token's embedding is split into a shared learnable component (d_α dimensions, a single vector α common to all interchangeable tokens) and a randomized component (d_β dimensions, a unique vector β_i per token drawn from one of three distributions: standard normal, neighboring grid points, or hypercube vertices). During training, the β_i vectors are resampled at every forward pass, forcing the model to distinguish tokens based on the randomized part rather than memorizing specific values; during inference, embeddings are generated once. The embedding matrix is reconstructed on-the-fly and shared via three-way weight tying (encoder, decoder, and output projection), with L2 normalization applied to both embeddings and feature vectors before projection, paired with a modified AdaCos loss adapted for the sequence-length dimension. This design keeps the parameter count constant regardless of vocabulary size while enabling generation of embeddings for arbitrarily many new interchangeable tokens.

## Results

On the perturbed LTLRandom35 dataset, the proposed method achieves 95.94% correct predictions and 98.29% alpha-covariance at 5 APs, compared to 34.13% correct and 40.91% alpha-covariance for the standard baseline, and 24.33% correct for Llama 3.2 3B. In the vocabulary generalization experiment (trained on 5 APs, tested up to 10 APs and formula length 50), the proposed method performs only slightly worse than a full-vocabulary baseline trained on 10 APs, while outperforming both the vanilla and alpha-renaming baselines by a considerable margin. On propositional logic (PropRandom35, perturbed), the proposed method reaches 93.25% correct and 92.98% alpha-covariance at 5 APs, versus 41.57% and 10.65% for the standard baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Hahn et al. (2021) baseline (traditional embeddings), the proposed method trades a small in-distribution cost (95.94% vs. 98.23% correct on the normal dataset) for dramatic robustness gains: under dataset perturbation, the baseline collapses to 34.13% correct while the proposed method retains 95.94%, and its alpha-covariance at 5 APs is 98.29% versus 40.91%. In the vocabulary generalization setting (trained on 5 APs, tested on up to 10 APs), the proposed method outperforms both the vanilla baseline and the alpha-renaming baseline by a considerable margin, approaching the performance of a full-vocabulary model trained on 10 APs. Against Llama 3.2 3B, the proposed method achieves a 71.61-point improvement in correctness on LTL (95.94% vs. 24.33%) and a 64.22-point improvement on propositional logic (93.25% vs. 29.03%), while also substantially outperforming it on alpha-covariance (e.g., 98.29% vs. 62.34% at 5 APs on LTL). The alpha-renaming baseline, which also generalizes to larger vocabularies, achieves slightly higher alpha-covariance in-distribution (98.86% vs. 98.29% at 5 APs on perturbed LTL) but is outperformed by the proposed method in the out-of-distribution generalization setting (Table 3), where the proposed method edges ahead at most AP counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based formal reasoning line (Hahn et al. 2021 for LTL solving, Lample & Charton 2019 for symbolic integration) by identifying a structural limitation in standard token embeddings: they cannot represent interchangeable tokens (e.g., atomic propositions, bound variables) that are semantically equivalent yet must remain distinguishable. The dual-part embedding (shared learnable component + randomized component) is a novel architectural modification, and the paper adapts AdaCos loss (Zhang et al. 2019) to the sequence-length dimension for the first time. It also introduces alpha-renaming as a data augmentation baseline, which the authors note is new to the literature, and positions its contribution against prior extensible-vocabulary work (Morazzoni et al. 2023, Wei et al. 2016) that relies on external information rather than the interchangeable-token structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, low-overhead mechanism (13% training cost) for vocabulary generalization in formal reasoning, where the number of atomic propositions or bound variables is unbounded in practice. The alpha-covariance metric offers a reusable evaluation tool for any domain with interchangeable tokens. Key limitations include the need to manually specify which tokens are interchangeable, the requirement to train from scratch (incompatible with pretrained models), and a slight in-distribution performance drop. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to natural language settings where tokens carry non-interchangeable semantic content, and whether the randomization strategy can be improved to close the remaining gap with full-vocabulary baselines in out-of-distribution settings.
