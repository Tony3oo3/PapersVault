## Problem

Time-series foundation models achieve strong zero-shot forecasting but lack the in-context learning ability that makes LLMs so adaptable: they can only be prompted with the target series' own history, not with related examples from the target domain. Fine-tuning on target data recovers accuracy but breaks the zero-shot paradigm that makes foundation models appealing to practitioners. The paper asks whether providing in-context examples from related time-series at inference time can recover the benefits of domain-specific fine-tuning without any gradient updates.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a context window containing the target time-series history (up to 512 time-steps) plus up to 49 additional in-context examples from related time-series (each up to 512 time-steps), separated by learnable separator tokens. It produces a forecast over a configurable horizon (up to 128 steps) for the target series only. The architecture is decoder-only and handles arbitrary numbers of in-context examples (0–50) and varying history/horizon lengths, with padding masks ensuring correct handling of series not aligned to the patch length of 32.

## Method

Starting from a pretrained TimesFM (base) checkpoint, the authors continue pretraining a modified decoder-only transformer on contexts of n=50 time-series examples. Three architectural changes enable in-context learning: (1) a shared learnable separator token is inserted after each example to prevent naive concatenation from confusing the model (e.g., multiple linear trends appearing as a triangular wave); (2) causal cross-example attention allows each token to attend to all previous patches and separator tokens, so the model can track example boundaries and borrow patterns across examples; (3) positional encodings are removed (NoPE) to improve length generalization when the context grows from a single 512-step history to 50 concatenated examples. Training contexts are generated by grouping 50 segments of length 640 via two strategies—time-series-level (splitting one long series) and dataset-level (sampling across series in the same dataset)—with 5 examples drawn from the immediate history of the target and the remaining 45 sampled randomly from other series in the same dataset.

## Results

On the OOD Benchmark (23 out-of-domain datasets), TimesFM-ICF achieves a Geometric Mean Scaled MASE of 0.777, improving 6.8% over the base TimesFM model (0.834) and 5% over the next-best baseline PatchTST (0.753), while matching the per-dataset fine-tuned TimesFM-FT (0.776) without any gradient updates at inference. On the ETT long-horizon rolling-window benchmark (horizons 96–720), TimesFM-ICF achieves MAE of 0.405 (ETTh1), 0.378 (ETTh2), 0.378 (ETTm1), and 0.307 (ETTm2), rivaling or outperforming TimesFM-FT on 3 of 4 datasets. Inference on the full OOD Benchmark takes 25 minutes for TimesFM-ICF versus 418 minutes for TimesFM-FT (16× faster).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimesFM-ICF outperforms the base TimesFM model on all four ETT datasets (e.g., 0.405 vs 0.417 MAE on ETTh1) and on the OOD Benchmark (0.777 vs 0.834 Scaled MASE GM), confirming that in-context examples provide substantial gains over history-only prompting. Against the per-dataset fine-tuned TimesFM-FT, TimesFM-ICF matches performance on the OOD Benchmark (0.777 vs 0.776) and wins on 3 of 4 ETT datasets (ETTh1: 0.405 vs 0.407; ETTh2: 0.378 vs 0.381; ETTm2: 0.307 vs 0.306), while narrowly losing on ETTm1 (0.378 vs 0.371). Against zero-shot foundation models, TimesFM-ICF beats Moirai (Large) on all ETT datasets (e.g., 0.307 vs 0.320 on ETTm2) and outperforms Chronos-T5, LLMTime, and Lag-Llama on the OOD Benchmark. Among task-specific supervised methods, TimesFM-ICF surpasses PatchTST by 5% on the OOD Benchmark and by 0.02–0.12 MAE on ETT, and also outperforms iTransformer, TimesNet, DLinear, FEDformer, and Crossformer across all ETT datasets. The key practical advantage is that TimesFM-ICF achieves these results in a fully zero-shot manner (no per-dataset training), completing the OOD Benchmark 16× faster than TimesFM-FT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TimesFM decoder-only foundation model (Das et al., 2024) by introducing an in-context fine-tuning mechanism borrowed conceptually from LLM few-shot prompting. Prior time-series foundation models (Chronos, MOIRAI, TimesFM) only accept the target time-series history in their context window; this work adds related time-series examples as in-context prompts, requiring architectural modifications (learnable separator tokens, cross-example attention, and NoPE positional encoding) plus continued pretraining on grouped multi-example contexts. The approach is positioned as the time-series analogue of instruction tuning or few-shot prompting in NLP, where a pretrained model adapts to a new domain at inference time without gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that practitioners can avoid building per-dataset fine-tuning pipelines: providing ~50 related in-context examples at inference time matches the accuracy of explicit per-dataset fine-tuning while being 16× faster overall. Open questions include whether the technique transfers to other base architectures (the paper only validates on TimesFM), whether more sophisticated example selection (beyond random + immediate history) yields further gains, and how the approach scales to multivariate or cross-domain settings. The paper also notes that in-context fine-tuning outperforms simply training a longer-history model (6.8% vs 2.4% improvement), suggesting the mechanism captures distributional information that longer context alone cannot.
