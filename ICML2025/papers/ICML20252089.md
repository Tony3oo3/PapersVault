## Problem

Efficiently evaluating multiple policies across multiple reward functions in an online discounted MDP has not been systematically studied under (ε,δ)-PAC guarantees. Prior work either addresses single-reward or single-policy evaluation, provides only MSE-based exploration without PAC sample-complexity bounds, or gives instance-dependent bounds that ignore how reward structure interacts with transition dynamics to affect evaluation difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252089/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates online on a finite-state, finite-action discounted MDP with unknown transitions, given a set of deterministic target policies and per-policy reward sets that may be finite or convex (including the full [0,1]^S reward-free case). It outputs, at a data-dependent stopping time τ, value vector estimates V̂_r^π for every policy-reward pair (π, r) simultaneously, with the guarantee that all estimates are within ε in the sup-norm with probability at least 1−δ. Unlike prior OPE work that estimates only the expected value under the initial state distribution, this method evaluates the full value vector across all states.

## Method

The paper derives an instance-specific sample complexity lower bound by casting evaluation as a goodness-of-fit test between the true MDP and a set of 'confusing' alternative models that differ by more than 2ε in value. The bound is governed by a one-step value deviation ρ_r^π(s,s′) = V_r^π(s′) − E_{ŝ∼P(s,π(s))}[V_r^π(ŝ)], and the optimal exploration distribution ω* maximizes the information-gathering rate against the worst-case confusing model. Since the exact optimization over alternative models is non-convex, the authors prove a convex relaxation U_ε(ω;M) (Theorem 4.7) that is convex in ω and, for convex reward sets, reduces to a finite collection of standard convex programs (or a closed-form solution in the reward-free case). The resulting algorithm, MR-NaS for policy evaluation, uses certainty equivalence: at each step it solves the convex relaxation under the current MDP estimate to obtain ω*_t, induces a behavior policy from it, mixes with a decaying forcing policy to ensure ergodicity, and stops when t ≥ U_{ε/2}(N_t/t; M_t)·β(N_t,δ). The key structural insight is that sample complexity scales with the worst-case policy-reward pair rather than the sum over all pairs, and the optimal allocation is roughly proportional to the squared value deviation at each state.

## Results

The paper evaluates MR-NaS on four tabular MDP environments (Riverswim, Forked Riverswim, DoubleChain, NArms) across three scenarios: multi-policy evaluation with finite reward sets, reward-free multi-policy evaluation, and reward-free single-policy evaluation (30 seeds, 95% confidence intervals). MR-NaS achieves consistently low estimation error across all environments and scenarios, while baselines show mixed performance. In the reward-free setting (Figure 4), MR-NaS tracks the average error over the canonical basis for each policy and converges faster than all baselines. The paper does not report a single numerical summary table; results are presented as error-vs-samples plots with shaded confidence intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MR-NaS against four baselines: SF-NR (McLeod et al., 2021), GVFExplorer (Jain et al., 2024), Noisy Policy-Uniform, and Noisy Policy-Visitation. MR-NaS outperforms all baselines across all four environments in both the finite-reward multi-policy setting (Figure 3) and the reward-free multi-policy setting (Figure 4). SF-NR, which is not designed to optimize a behavior policy, shows mixed performance and generally higher error than MR-NaS. GVFExplorer, a variance-driven exploration method, also underperforms MR-NaS, particularly in environments where certain rewards are hard to obtain under a uniform policy, because it neglects the forward equations when optimizing the behavior policy. The two heuristic baselines (Noisy Policy-Uniform and Noisy Policy-Visitation) serve as simple references and are consistently outperformed by MR-NaS, confirming the benefit of the adaptive, instance-dependent exploration strategy derived from the relaxed characteristic time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Best Policy Identification (BPI) framework—originally developed for finding optimal policies (Al Marjani et al., 2021; Russo & Vannella, 2024)—to the distinct problem of policy evaluation, where the goal is to estimate value functions rather than identify the best policy. It departs from prior policy evaluation work (Hanna et al., 2017; Mukherjee et al., 2022; Jain et al., 2024) that guided exploration via MSE or variance minimization without PAC guarantees, and from multi-policy single-reward PAC evaluation (Dann et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024) that lacked instance-dependent reward structure. The key conceptual move is casting multi-reward multi-policy evaluation as a hypothesis testing problem over confusing models, yielding an instance-specific lower bound governed by a value deviation measure ρ, and then designing an adaptive exploration policy (MR-NaS) that achieves asymptotic optimality up to a constant factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first instance-specific sample complexity characterization for multi-reward multi-policy evaluation, revealing that complexity is governed by the worst-case policy-reward pair's value deviation rather than an aggregate over all pairs—a structural insight that informs where to allocate exploration effort. The convex relaxation U_ε provides a tractable optimization for designing the exploration policy and admits a closed-form solution in the reward-free case, making the approach practically implementable. Open questions include quantifying the gap between the exact lower bound T_ε and its convex relaxation U_ε, extending the analysis to non-tabular or function-approximation settings, and understanding how the value deviation measure interacts with sparse-reward structures in large-scale MDPs. The framework is directly relevant to applications like LLM alignment with multiple preference signals and multi-objective robotics.
