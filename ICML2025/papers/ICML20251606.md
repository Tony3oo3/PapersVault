## Problem

Sample-level analysis reveals that no single time series forecasting model consistently ranks first across test samples—each model excels on only a fraction of inputs while others dominate in different regimes. Existing ensemble strategies (mean/median, top-k selection) apply static weights that cannot adapt to the specific temporal characteristics of each input, leaving complementary model strengths untapped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The fusor consumes a 24-dimensional meta-feature vector extracted from the input multivariate time series (covering statistical, temporal, spectral, and multivariate descriptors) along with the prediction tensor from k base models in the model zoo. It outputs a softmax-normalized weight vector over the k models and the resulting fused forecast as a weighted sum of base model predictions. Because meta-features are task-agnostic and low-dimensional, the same fusor architecture operates across datasets with different numbers of variates, input lengths, and prediction horizons without architectural changes.

## Method

TIMEFUSE comprises a meta-feature extractor and a learnable single-layer linear fusor (with softmax) that maps 24 task-agnostic descriptors to per-model fusion weights. The fusor is trained with Huber loss on held-out validation sets to minimize fused prediction error, decoupling its meta-training from base model training. Cross-task meta-training is enabled by oversampling all datasets to the largest size and alternating training batches from different tasks within each step, which balances sample counts and prevents overfitting to any single task distribution. This design yields zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets, interpretable weight assignments tied to input properties (e.g., stationarity, spectral complexity), and negligible inference overhead (~0.145 ms) relative to base models.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over prediction lengths 96/192/336/720, input 96), TIMEFUSE achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 7 datasets, with an average MSE reduction of 3.61%, 6.88%, 11.77%, and 8.39% over TimeXer, TimeMixer, PAttn, and iTransformer respectively. On short-term PEMS traffic forecasting, TIMEFUSE delivers the best MAE, RMSE, and MAPE across all four datasets, achieving an average MAPE reduction of 20.46% over TimeXer and 9.89% over iTransformer. Zero-shot generalization (fusor trained without the target dataset) still outperforms the best individual model on 6 of 7 long-term datasets and all 4 PEMS datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMEFUSE achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 7 long-term forecasting datasets, outperforming every individual baseline including TimeXer (best on most tasks), TimeMixer, PAttn, iTransformer, TimesNet, PatchTST, DLinear, FedFormer, Non-stationary Transformer, LightTS, InFormer, and AutoFormer. The margin is most pronounced on Traffic (MSE 0.419 vs. 0.422 for iTransformer, the closest competitor) and Electricity (MSE 0.163 vs. 0.171 for TimeXer). On short-term PEMS forecasting, TIMEFUSE dominates all three metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) across all four datasets, with the largest gap over FedFormer (e.g., MAE 16.0 vs. 31.4 on PEMS03) and a 20.46% average MAPE improvement over TimeXer. Against static ensemble baselines (mean/median with top-k model selection), TIMEFUSE consistently outperforms, and the optimal static ensemble size varies drastically across datasets (10 models on ETTh1 vs. 2 on Traffic), highlighting the advantage of sample-level adaptivity. In comparisons with AutoGluon-TimeSeries and Chronos-Bolt-Base (Table 10), TIMEFUSE achieves the best average performance across all three task categories, while AutoGluon suffers from prohibitive inference times on high-dimensional datasets and Chronos produces degenerate predictions on long-horizon or multivariate tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both the dominant paradigm of developing ever-more-powerful individual forecasting architectures (TimeXer, TimeMixer, iTransformer, etc.) and from prior time-series ensemble work (Kourentzes et al., Oliveira & Torgo, Choi & Lee) that focused on homogeneous ensembles of same-architecture models with static aggregation. It repurposes the meta-learning idea of learning-to-predict from task descriptors, but applies it at the sample level using hand-crafted meta-features (statistical, temporal, spectral, multivariate) rather than learned representations, and trains a deliberately simple linear fusor. The framework is positioned as orthogonal to and composable with any base model, contrasting with AutoML systems (AutoGluon-TimeSeries) that bundle fixed model libraries and with foundation models (Chronos) that attempt universal generalization through scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TIMEFUSE adds negligible inference overhead (~0.15 ms) on top of any existing model zoo, is trivially extensible as new architectures emerge, and achieves near-universal sample-level improvements (up to 95.1%) across 16 real-world benchmarks. The zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets and the interpretability of learned fusion weights (e.g., stationarity → Non-stationary Transformer) make it a deployable tool rather than a research curiosity. Open questions include how the fusor behaves under distribution shift between meta-train and test sets, whether more expressive fusors would help as model diversity grows, and how to incorporate spatial/graph structure for sensor-network applications. The current oversampling strategy for cross-task balancing is acknowledged as a simplification that may not be optimal.
