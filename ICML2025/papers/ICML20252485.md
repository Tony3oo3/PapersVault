## Problem

Existing generative inference approaches for visual understanding are limited to single-label classification and cannot handle broader scene understanding tasks (object discovery, multi-object perception) on scenes substantially more complex than those seen during training. Discriminative models, while widely used, are shown to fail under even mild distribution shifts. The paper asks how generative models can be leveraged compositionally to infer scene structure from images with more objects or different attributes than encountered at training time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural image as input, encoded into a 4-channel 8×8 latent space via a pretrained VAE (images resized to 64×64 for CLEVR/CLEVRTex). It produces a variable number of visual concepts: continuous 2D object center coordinates normalized to [0,1], binary facial attribute labels (e.g., Black Hair, Eyeglasses, Smiling), or discrete object category labels from a finite prompt set. Notably, the number of concepts K is itself inferred rather than specified, and for the zero-shot setting the conditioning is provided by text prompts fed to a pretrained Stable Diffusion model rather than learned parameters.

## Method

The core contribution is an inverse generative modeling framework that factorizes the joint image likelihood p(x|c¹,…,cᴷ) as a product of per-concept energy-based models, each parameterized by a diffusion denoising function ε_θ(xᵗ, t|cᵏ). During training, the composed denoising function ε_θ^comb = Σₖ ε_θ(xᵗ, t|cᵏ) is optimized directly with the standard denoising diffusion objective, avoiding error accumulation from test-time composition. At inference, scene understanding becomes an optimization problem: find the concept parameters cᵏ that minimize the average denoising error over sampled timesteps and noise. For discrete concepts, all Mᴷ configurations are enumerated (or a continuous relaxation with gradient descent is used); for continuous concepts like object coordinates, stochastic gradient descent with multiple random initializations and pruning of low-likelihood paths prevents convergence to local minima. The number of concepts K is determined by selecting the value that yields the lowest denoising error across a range [K_min, K_max].

## Results

On CLEVR object discovery (trained on 3–5 objects, tested on 6–8), IGM with multiple initialization achieves 85.3% perception rate and 3.5e-4 estimation error out-of-distribution, compared to 58.7% and 1.2e-3 for the best generative baseline (Generative Classifier). On CelebA facial attribute prediction (trained on female faces, tested on male faces), IGM reaches 65.6% accuracy versus 62.2% for ResNet-50 and 61.7% for GC. For zero-shot multi-object perception using pretrained Stable Diffusion, IGM achieves 87.3% accuracy, outperforming Diffusion Classifier (70.4%) by 16.9 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGM outperforms all five baselines across every task and evaluation setting. Against ResNet-50, the margin is overwhelming: 94.7% vs. 5.3% in-distribution and 85.3% vs. 2.9% out-of-distribution on CLEVR object discovery, and 65.6% vs. 62.2% on CelebA OOD facial attributes. Against SlotAttn and DINOSAUR (object-centric discriminative methods), IGM gains 12–32 percentage points in perception rate on OOD CLEVR, while also reducing estimation error by roughly 3–4×. Against the closest generative baseline, Generative Classifier (GC), IGM achieves a 12.5-point in-distribution and 26.6-point OOD perception-rate advantage on CLEVR, and a 3.9-point OOD accuracy gain on CelebA. For zero-shot multi-object perception, IGM (87.3%) beats Diffusion Classifier (70.4%) and its variant (73.2%) by 14–17 points, demonstrating that explicit compositional modeling of individual object prompts is substantially more effective than compound-prompt or independent-prompt strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends compositional generative modeling (Du & Kaelbling, 2024; Liu et al., 2022), which factorizes a joint image distribution into a product of conditional energy-based models, from the generation side to the inference side. It departs from recent generative classifiers (Li et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023a) that use a single diffusion model for single-label classification by composing multiple denoising functions, each conditioned on an individual visual concept, and inverting the composed model to recover those concepts from an image. The key conceptual shift is treating scene understanding as an inverse problem: rather than generating an image from known concepts, the model finds the concept parameters that best explain an observed image, enabling flexible conditioning on continuous (object coordinates) or discrete (facial attributes, text prompts) variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional formulation provides a principled mechanism for generalizing to scenes with more objects or different object types than seen during training, which discriminative models and single-model generative classifiers struggle with. The zero-shot multi-object perception result using pretrained Stable Diffusion without fine-tuning suggests a practical path toward leveraging foundation models for structured scene parsing. However, the assumption of full concept independence ignores inter-object interactions, and the exponential cost of discrete concept enumeration limits scalability to many attributes. The experiments, while well-controlled, are confined to relatively simple synthetic (CLEVR) and single-domain (CelebA) settings, leaving open whether the approach scales to complex natural scenes with hundreds of object categories and spatial relationships.
