## Problem

Existing comparisons of feature embedding models rely on aggregate downstream-task metrics (e.g., classification accuracy) that lack interpretability and do not reveal which specific sample groups are clustered differently by each model. This paper addresses the need for a fine-grained, explainable comparison that identifies and quantifies the sample clusters captured by one embedding but not another, and further leverages this comparison to align embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251054/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference dataset of n samples, two pre-trained embedding models (which may map to spaces of different dimensions), and kernel parameters (e.g., Gaussian bandwidth σ and number of RFF features m for shift-invariant kernels). It produces the principal eigenvectors of the kernel difference matrix—each identifying a sample cluster differentially captured by the two embeddings—along with eigenvalues quantifying the magnitude of each cluster difference. A scalar SPEC-diff score (the spectral radius of the kernel difference matrix) summarizes overall mismatch, and the SPEC-align procedure outputs updated embedding parameters that reduce clustering discrepancies.

## Method

SPEC constructs the normalized kernel difference matrix Λ = (K_ψ1 − K_ψ2)/n and performs eigendecomposition, where principal eigenvectors identify sample clusters strongly grouped by one embedding but weakly by the other (formally guaranteed by Theorem 1 under bounded cross-block and within-block kernel norms). To avoid O(n³) cost, the paper proves Λ shares its non-zero eigenvalues with a (d₁+d₂)×(d₁+d₂) differential covariance matrix Γ, reducing computation to O(n(d₁+d₂)² + (d₁+d₂)³); for infinite-dimensional shift-invariant kernels like the Gaussian, Random Fourier Features bound the feature dimension to m, yielding O(m²n + m³) complexity. For alignment, SPEC-align adds the spectral radius of Γ as a penalty to the embedding's original loss, with gradients computed in O(n_B) per batch via the top left and right eigenvectors of Γ obtained through the power method, enabling stochastic gradient descent to minimize clustering mismatches.

## Results

The headline quantitative result is the SPEC-align experiment on ImageNet-1K: aligning CLIP to DINOv2 via the SPEC-diff penalty improves CLIP's frozen-backbone top-1 accuracy from 67.20% to 73.93% (a +6.73 pp gain), narrowing the gap to DINOv2 (78.99%) from 11.79 pp to 5.06 pp. Computationally, the Cholesky-decomposition-based eigenspace computation scales to d=10,000 RFF dimensions in 85 seconds, whereas the direct eigendecomposition of the differential covariance matrix fails beyond d≈5,000. The remaining results are qualitative: SPEC identifies interpretable cluster differences (e.g., CLIP clusters by overlaid text while DINOv2 clusters by visual content on ImageNet classes with typographic overlays) across AFHQ, FFHQ, MS-COCO, ImageWoof, and text datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is the SPEC-align experiment: SPEC-aligned CLIP (73.93%) substantially outperforms standard CLIP (67.20%) on ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy, closing roughly half the gap to DINOv2 (78.99%). Against DINOv2, the method does not surpass the reference but brings CLIP meaningfully closer, validating the alignment objective. For the qualitative embedding comparisons, SPEC reveals that CLIP and DINOv2 cluster ImageNet dog breeds differently (CLIP by posture/gesture, DINOv2 by breed) and that CLIP is susceptible to typographic attacks (clustering by overlaid text) while DINOv2 is not. On text embeddings, CLIP clusters GPT-4o-generated sentences by objects (e.g., cameras, guitars) while RoBERTa clusters by profession and gender; E5-V2 additionally captures multi-animal interaction captions on MS-COCO that CLIP misses. Inception-V3 and SWAV are compared pairwise on AFHQ and MS-COCO, with SWAV showing a distinctive strength in clustering grid-like structural patterns. Overall, the paper positions SPEC as a complementary, explainable diagnostic tool rather than a method that uniformly outperforms all baselines on a single metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kernel PCA (Schölkopf et al., 1998) from single-embedding dimensionality reduction to pairwise embedding comparison by analyzing the eigendecomposition of the *difference* of two kernel matrices. For scalability, it leverages Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) to handle shift-invariant kernels, and introduces a differential covariance matrix formulation that reduces eigendecomposition from O(n³) to O(n·d² + d³). Conceptually, it departs from prior embedding comparison work (downstream-task benchmarks, probing classifiers, information sufficiency metrics) by providing an interpretable, cluster-level view of *where* two embeddings disagree, and from Vargas et al. (2024) by operating on kernel differences rather than PCA of concatenated vectors, enabling comparison across different embedding dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPEC framework gives practitioners a diagnostic tool to understand *why* two embeddings differ on specific sample groups, which is valuable for model selection, debugging, and building hybrid systems. The SPEC-align method demonstrates practical utility by improving CLIP's ImageNet-1K linear-probe accuracy from 67.20% to 73.93% (approaching DINOv2's 78.99%), suggesting kernel-difference-based alignment could become a lightweight fine-tuning strategy for cross-modality models. Key limitations include the assumption that cluster differences are spectrally separable, the pairwise (not multi-embedding) comparison setting, and the reliance on a reference dataset whose distribution may not match deployment. Extending to non-spectral cluster structures and multi-embedding comparison are natural open directions.
