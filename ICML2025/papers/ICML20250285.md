## Problem

Existing algorithmic approaches for shortest path distance (SPD) queries on terrain DEMs require hundreds of hours of preprocessing even for 180K-node terrains, and recent neural approaches (NeuroGF, GeGNN) fail to scale to the millions-of-nodes scale now produced by LiDAR. The paper asks whether a neural data structure can be trained efficiently on terrains with up to 16 million nodes while maintaining sub-1% relative error and millisecond query times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250285/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a terrain graph G_h = (V, E, X_h, w) induced by an N₁×N₂ grid, where each node v has a 3D lift (x, y, h(x,y)) as its feature, edges connect each node to its 8 grid neighbors, and edge weights are Euclidean distances between lifts. Given a pair of vertices (u, v), the model outputs a scalar approximate geodesic distance d_{G_h}(u,v). The trained model has ~70K parameters and answers each query in ≤10 ms; embeddings for all nodes are precomputed once (scaling linearly with graph size, e.g., ~107 s for 16M nodes) and cached for repeated queries.

## Method

The core contribution is a decoupled two-stage training strategy for the NeuroGF pipeline (Siamese GAT embedding module φ + MLP distance-computation module ρ). In Stage 1, only φ is trained as a Siamese network with L₁ loss on a coarsened (downsampled) terrain graph; in Stage 2, φ is frozen and ρ is fine-tuned on the full-resolution graph to correct embedding errors. This mixed coarse-to-refined (M-CTR) strategy exploits the fact that the GNN forward pass (O(|V|+|E|)) is the computational bottleneck, so training it on a smaller graph while reserving the cheap MLP fine-tuning for the full graph enables training on 16M-node terrains in hours. The decoupling also supports efficient updates: when the height function changes, only the lightweight ρ needs retraining while the frozen φ embeddings are reused, reducing retraining cost by a factor proportional to the number of terrain instantiations.

## Results

On the largest terrain (Los Angeles, 16M nodes), the proposed M-CTR strategy achieves 0.88% average relative error and 94.41% accuracy (queries within 2% error) in 183 minutes of training, while the full-resolution GAT+L1 baseline did not finish training after 30+ hours. On Norway (4M nodes), M-CTR achieves 0.90% relative error and 91.1% accuracy in 183 min, slightly outperforming Full-GAT+L1 (0.94% error, 90.5% accuracy) while training 3.7× faster. On the 1M-node terrains (Philadelphia and Holland), M-CTR reduces relative error by 75.4% and 58.3% respectively compared to Coarse-GAT+L1, with accuracy gains of +64.3% and +25.7%. All models answer SPD queries in at most 10 ms at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two SOTA neural baselines: NeuroGF (Zhang et al., 2023, represented as GAT+MLP) and GeGNN (Pang et al., 2023). On the Norway-250 benchmark (Table 2), the decoupled approach achieves 0.84% relative error and 92.3% accuracy, outperforming NeuroGF/GAT+MLP (1.32% error, 89.4% accuracy) by ~36% in error and GeGNN (13.10% error, 34.5% accuracy) by a factor of ~15× in error. GeGNN is dramatically outperformed on all metrics, suggesting its hierarchical pooling architecture does not scale to terrain graphs. Against the original NeuroGF end-to-end pipeline, the decoupled training consistently reduces relative error by 10–15% across synthetic and real terrains, with the gap widening as terrain complexity (doubling dimension) increases. The M-CTR strategy also substantially outperforms the simple Coarse-GAT+L1 baseline (which uses the same coarse embedding but without the fine-tuned distance MLP), achieving 15–75% lower relative error at comparable training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NeuroGF framework (Zhang et al., 2023), which introduced a Siamese embedding module followed by an MLP distance-computation module for geodesic distance estimation on meshed surfaces. The key departure is a decoupled two-stage training strategy: the embedding module (GAT) is trained first on a coarsened terrain graph using L1 distance as the loss, then frozen while the distance-computation MLP is fine-tuned on the full-resolution graph. This contrasts with the end-to-end training of NeuroGF and the hierarchical pooling architecture of GeGNN (Pang et al., 2023), and extends the problem from smooth 3D meshes (ShapeNet-scale) to massive terrain DEMs with up to 16 million nodes, a regime where prior neural and algorithmic approaches were infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that neural data structures can now handle terrain graphs at a scale (16M nodes) that previously required days of preprocessing with non-neural methods, reducing this to hours of training with sub-10ms query times. The decoupled coarse-to-fine strategy is practically important for dynamic terrain settings where height functions change due to measurement updates or natural processes, since only the lightweight MLP needs retraining. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across terrains without per-terrain retraining, how accuracy degrades on very flat terrains (where relative perturbations are larger), and whether the L1-over-L2 finding in latent space generalizes beyond terrain graphs.
