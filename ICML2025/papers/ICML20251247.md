## Problem

The Schrödinger Bridge (SB) framework and its Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) solver have been theoretically justified only for continuous state spaces (R^D), leaving a gap for the large class of inherently discrete data such as text tokens, VQ codebook entries, and categorical features. Without a convergence guarantee, the discrete-time IMF (D-IMF) procedure cannot be reliably applied to discrete spaces, and no practical algorithm existed to implement it in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete categorical data of the form S^D (e.g., 256×1024 VQ-GAN tokens for CelebA, 256^3072 discrete RGB pixels for MNIST, or 8192^100 SentencePiece tokens for text) and produces a learned bidirectional stochastic process (forward and backward transition models) that maps a source sample to a target-domain sample. Because the state space grows exponentially in D, the method never materializes full transition matrices; instead it parameterizes transitions via a factorized, dimension-wise neural network that predicts a D×S row-stochastic matrix per input state, with a single shared network conditioned on the timestep.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that on a finite discrete space with a full-support Markov reference process, the dynamic SB solution is the unique process that is both Markovian and reciprocal, which immediately guarantees D-IMF convergence in KL. The resulting CSBM algorithm alternates two projections: a reciprocal projection that resamples intermediate states from the analytically tractable reference bridge q^ref(x_in | x_0, x_1), and a Markovian projection that trains a neural network to match the one-step transition probabilities of the reciprocal process by minimizing a KL objective (Proposition 3.3). The key parameterization (Eq. 9) computes each transition q_θ(x_{t_n}|x_{t_{n-1}}) by first drawing a predicted endpoint x̃_1 from a learnable factorized distribution and then sampling from the reference bridge q^ref(x_{t_n}|x_{t_{n-1}}, x̃_1), closely mirroring D3PM but with intermediate states sampled from the reference bridge rather than the marginal noising process. Two reference processes are provided—uniform (for unordered categories) and Gaussian-like (for ordered categories)—each controlled by a stochasticity parameter α that trades off similarity to the input against target-distribution fidelity.

## Results

On the CelebA 128×128 male→female translation task, CSBM achieves FID of 10.60 (low stochasticity, α=0.005) and 14.68 (high stochasticity, α=0.01), substantially outperforming ASBM (16.86 / 17.44) and DSBM (24.06 / 92.15). CSBM also leads on CMMD (0.165 / 0.212 vs. 0.216 / 0.231 for ASBM) and LPIPS (0.175 / 0.170 vs. 0.242 / 0.294 for ASBM). On the Amazon Reviews sentiment transfer task, CSBM achieves the best NLL (5.4) and BLEU (72.5–74.8) among all methods, though its sentiment accuracy (76.5–79.3%) trails the top baselines (91.5–93.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, CSBM decisively outperforms both ASBM (Gushchin et al., 2024b) and DSBM (Shi et al., 2023) across all three metrics (FID, CMMD, LPIPS) in both low- and high-stochasticity settings, with the gap widening dramatically at high stochasticity (e.g., FID 14.68 vs. 92.15 for DSBM). The authors note CSBM uses N=100 D-IMF steps versus N=3 for ASBM, so the comparison is not perfectly controlled. On Amazon Reviews sentiment transfer, CSBM achieves the lowest NLL (5.4) and highest BLEU (72.5–74.8) by a large margin over all seven text baselines (NLL 42–116.8, BLEU 0–14.7), indicating superior content preservation and fluency. However, CSBM's sentiment accuracy (76.5–79.3%) is lower than BST (93.5%), Seq2SentiSeq (92.4%), and PST (91.5%), though the authors argue manual inspection shows most generations do convey correct polarity. Overall, CSBM wins on image quality metrics and text fluency/similarity, but trades off some sentiment classification accuracy in the text domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Discrete-time Iterative Markovian Fitting (D-IMF) framework from Gushchin et al. (2024b) and the original IMF procedure from Shi et al. (2023) from continuous state spaces (R^D) to discrete categorical spaces (S^D). The theoretical core (Theorem 3.1) fills the previously empty cell in Table 1 for discrete space + discrete time, proving that the SB solution is the unique Markovian and reciprocal process under a general Markov reference. For the practical algorithm, the authors adopt the posterior-sampling and per-dimension factorization parameterization from D3PM (Austin et al., 2021), adapting its training methodology to the D-IMF projection steps, and apply it in VQ-GAN latent space for image translation rather than raw pixel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A large fraction of modern data (text tokens, molecular graphs, VQ codebook entries) is inherently discrete, yet SB-based generative methods were previously restricted to continuous spaces; this work provides both the theoretical justification and a working algorithm to close that gap. The finding that even N=1 intermediate steps is theoretically sufficient (Corollary 3.2) contrasts with DDSBM's reliance on large discretized N, though in practice N=100 is needed for competitive CelebA results. Key limitations include the per-dimension factorization that discards inter-feature dependencies (inherent to most discrete diffusion/flow methods), the somewhat ad hoc construction of reference processes where α must be retuned with N, and the fact that the N=100 vs. N=3 comparison against continuous-space baselines is not entirely apples-to-apples. Open questions include whether discrete EOT solvers (Sinkhorn, gradient-based) can be adapted to generative scenarios in discrete spaces, and how to construct reference processes that do not require α to shrink as N grows.
