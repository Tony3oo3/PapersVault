## Problem

Bivariate causal discovery under unmeasured confounding relies on proxy variables (negative control outcomes) to test whether a causal relation X→Y exists by examining an integral equation. Existing methods either require parametric assumptions or discretize continuous variables, causing information loss, and it remains unclear when the integral equation is guaranteed to fail under the alternative hypothesis. The paper shows that when the NCO has a strong direct effect on the outcome, the integral equation can have a solution even under H₁, rendering the causal relation unidentifiable with only the NCO.

## Input / Output

The method takes continuous observed data (X, Y, W) in the single-proxy setting or (X, Y, W, Z) in the two-proxy setting, where W is the negative control outcome and Z is the negative control exposure. It produces a causal hypothesis test decision (reject or fail to reject H₀: X ⊥ Y | U) along with a test statistic value Δ_{φ,m}. The procedure uses Gaussian kernels with median-distance bandwidth initialization, selects the Tikhonov regularization parameter λ via cross-validation over 50 values, and determines critical values through a Mammen wild bootstrap with B replications.

## Method

The core contribution is the Proxy Maximum Characteristic Restriction (PMCR), a non-parametric procedure that tests the integral equation p(y|x) = ∫h(w,y)p(w|x)dw by replacing the first-order moment restriction with a characteristic-function-based restriction E{φ(Y,t) − H(W,t) | X} = 0 for all t, capturing all-order moments for greater power. The solution H(W,t) is estimated in an RKHS via Tikhonov regularization of a kernel-based empirical risk, and the test statistic is constructed as a maximum over t of integrated squared conditional moment residuals, with asymptotic level and power established under global and local alternatives. A key theoretical finding is that under a linear Gaussian model, when the NCO's effect on Y (γ_W) exceeds a threshold, the integral equation admits a solution even under H₁, causing all single-proxy methods to lose power. To recover identifiability, the paper introduces a second integral equation p(y|z,x) = ∫h(w,y)p(w|z,x)dw using the NCE Z, which can discriminate H₀ from H₁ even when the first equation is solvable under the alternative.

## Results

In the single-proxy setting (W ↛ Y), the proposed PMCR procedure controls the type-I error rate at approximately α = 0.05 as sample size n increases, while both KCI and Liu et al. (2023) fail to maintain valid size. Power of PMCR approaches 1 with increasing n, outperforming both baselines. In the two-proxy setting (W → Y), the single-proxy procedure suffers from near-zero power (staying at α ≈ 0.05) due to the existence of a solution to the integral equation under H₁, but adding the NCE (Z) restores power to approximately 1. In the comparison with the first-moment MMR method, PMCR achieves power → 1 under H₁ while MMR remains at α ≈ 0.05, demonstrating the advantage of leveraging all-order moments via the characteristic function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Liu et al. (2023), PMCR wins on both type-I error control and power: Liu's discretization approach (with l_X=14, l_W=12 bins) exhibits inflated type-I error due to discretization and probability estimation errors, while PMCR maintains valid size and achieves higher power without any discretization. Against KCI (Zhang et al., 2012), PMCR clearly outperforms on type-I error, as KCI conditions on W but cannot eliminate the unmeasured confounding bias, leading to uncontrollable false positive rates. Against Miao et al. (2018) in the two-proxy setting, PMCR with Z achieves power approaching 1, whereas Miao's discrete procedure (l_X=3, l_W=2, l_Z=2) is limited by its discrete-variable design. Against the first-moment MMR method (Mastouri et al., 2021), PMCR demonstrates a decisive power advantage: in the counterexample (B.3) where the first-order moment equation is solvable under H₁, MMR's power remains at α ≈ 0.05 while PMCR's power reaches 1, confirming that capturing all-order moments via the characteristic function is essential for detecting causal relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-variable causal discovery line initiated by Miao et al. (2018) for discrete variables and continued by Liu et al. (2023) and Miao et al. (2023) for continuous variables. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it replaces discretization (Liu) and parametric goodness-of-fit (Miao 2023) with a non-parametric kernel method (PMCR) that leverages characteristic functions rather than first-order moments, building on the RKHS-based moment restriction framework of Mastouri et al. (2021) and Zhang et al. (2020); and (2) it identifies a fundamental identifiability gap in the integral equation framework and proposes a two-proxy (NCO + NCE) remedy. The method is positioned as a continuous, non-parametric counterpart to the discrete linear-regression tests of Miao et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that the integral equation underlying all prior proxy-based causal discovery can have a solution under the alternative hypothesis when the NCO's effect on the outcome is sufficiently strong—challenges a foundational assumption in the field and implies that existing methods can silently fail to detect true causal relations. The two-proxy extension provides a practical remedy, but the non-identifiability analysis is currently restricted to linear Gaussian models, and all empirical validation is on synthetic data. Open questions include whether the identifiability gap persists in non-Gaussian or high-dimensional settings, whether bidirectional NCO–outcome edges (acknowledged as future work) further complicate identification, and how common the failure regime is in real-world applications.
