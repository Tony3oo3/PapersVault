## Problem

Existing proximal methods for Mean-Field Games (e.g., Online Mirror Descent, Policy Mirror Ascent) either operate in a restricted no-manipulation regime that ignores population dynamics or impose overly restrictive assumptions such as uniformly bounded-away-from-zero policies and absolute continuity with respect to the uniform distribution. No trust-region-based algorithm with rigorous finite-sample convergence guarantees had been established for computing Mean-Field Nash Equilibria in ergodic MFG with finite state-action spaces.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in finite state-action spaces under a ν-restart oracle paradigm, where the agent can reset to a distribution ν and step forward with a chosen action and mean-field parameter μ—stronger than trajectory-only access but weaker than a generative model. The key output metric is exploitability φ(π, μ), measuring the maximum unilateral improvement an agent could achieve; the sample-based variant requires Õ(1/ε⁶) environment interactions to produce a pair (π, μ) with φ(π, μ) ≤ ε.

## Method

MF-TRPO is a two-loop algorithm: the inner loop runs entropy-regularized TRPO (a mirror descent method) to compute an approximate best-response policy via closed-form softmax updates derived from Q-function evaluations, while the outer loop updates the population distribution μ using an M-step monotonicity condition that guarantees contraction toward the unique equilibrium. The entropy regularization is structurally critical—it makes the proximal policy update admit a softmax closed form, keeping policies in the interior of the simplex without explicit projection and enabling the Õ(1/L) convergence rate. The sample-based variant replaces exact Q-function computation with trajectory-based estimates under the ν-restart paradigm, using martingale concentration inequalities to control estimation error with high probability across iterations. This yields Õ(1/ε⁶) sample complexity for the model-free variant under weaker assumptions (unichain ergodicity, Lipschitz continuity of MDP parameters) than prior MFG learning work.

## Results

The paper establishes that Exact MF-TRPO converges to an ε-MFNE with exploitability bounded by ε_K = C·exp(−τ/4 · Σβ_j) + C·√(log L / L), yielding a Õ(1/L) rate in the inner policy-optimization loop. The Sample-Based MF-TRPO variant requires at most Õ(1/ε⁶) environment interactions (under the ν-restart paradigm) to reach an ε-MFNE, with high-probability guarantees via martingale concentration. Numerical experiments on a 5×5 Grid-Based Crowd Modeling game confirm that exploitability decreases monotonically and converges within a few hundred iterations for both η=0.05 and η=0.3, matching the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head empirical comparison against competing MFG solvers; the numerical experiments (Figures 1–2) showcase only the proposed Sample-Based MF-TRPO's exploitability trajectory and mean-field distribution evolution. The positioning is primarily theoretical: relative to Pérolat et al. (2022) and Yardim et al. (2023), which analyze Online Mirror Descent in a restricted no-manipulation regime, MF-TRPO explicitly incorporates population updates and the monotonicity condition (A-2) to stabilize the ergodic dynamics. Compared to Shani et al. (2020), the Õ(1/ε⁶) sample complexity matches the standard RL rate but is now extended to the coupled policy–population MFG setting. The paper also relaxes assumptions made by Yardim et al. (2023) and Angiuli et al. (2022; 2023)—specifically dropping the requirement that policies be uniformly bounded away from zero and replacing absolute-continuity-with-uniform-distribution with a weaker unichain ergodicity condition—while retaining the Õ(1/L) per-iteration error bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trust-region policy optimization (TRPO) framework from Schulman et al. (2015) and its convergence analysis in Shani et al. (2020) to the mean-field game setting, where the policy must be optimized against an evolving population distribution. It builds directly on the proximal/mirror-descent methods for MFG introduced by Pérolat et al. (2022) and Yardim et al. (2023), but departs from them by explicitly incorporating population dynamics updates (via a monotonicity condition) and by relaxing their restrictive assumptions—specifically removing the requirement for policies uniformly bounded away from zero and absolute continuity with respect to the uniform distribution. The exploitability metric and the discounted-as-ergodic approximation follow Laurière et al. (2022), while the ν-restart interaction paradigm is inherited from Kakade (2003).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-sample convergence guarantees for a trust-region method in MFG, closing a gap where prior MFG-RL approaches (OMD, Q-learning, policy mirror ascent) either lacked population-update analysis or imposed overly strong structural assumptions. The O(1/ε⁶) sample complexity and O(1/L) optimization rate give practitioners a concrete computational budget for computing approximate MFNE in model-free settings. However, the scope is limited to finite state-action spaces with a discounted approximation of the ergodic problem, and the empirical validation is confined to a small 5×5 grid game. Open questions include extending to continuous state spaces, removing the unichain assumption, and connecting the framework to mean-field control (cooperative) settings as suggested by the authors.
