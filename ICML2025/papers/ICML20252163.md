## Problem

Existing TalkingFace generation methods struggle to maintain identity consistency, natural head motion, and accurate lip synchronization over extended video sequences. GAN-based approaches suffer from training instability and motion artifacts, while current diffusion-based methods rely on only 2–4 historical frames or static reference UNets, leading to rapid error accumulation, identity drift, and rigid expressions in long-term generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252163/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image, a speech audio track, and optionally a driving video's facial landmarks as input, and produces a temporally coherent talking-face video at 512×512 resolution. Training clips consist of 16 frames, but the model is designed for autoregressive long-sequence generation (tested up to 7,200 frames). The landmark input is optional and can be randomly dropped during training to support landmark-free inference.

## Method

MCDM builds on a Stable Diffusion v1.5-style denoising UNet where each Transformer block contains four attention layers: self-attention, two parallel spatial cross-attentions (archived-clip and present-clip reference), and a temporal-wise memory-efficient attention. The archived-clip motion-prior encodes a reference frame and historical frames via frozen VAEs and learnable patchify layers, then applies frame-aligned attention (reference as Key, archived frames as Value, computed frame-wise) to inject long-term identity context. The present-clip motion-prior diffusion model predicts decoupled motion tokens for head, lip, and expression using Wav2Vec audio features, CLIP image tokens, and custom lip/expression encoders, processed through a FiLM layer, a multimodal causal transformer, and a temporal interaction transformer. The memory-efficient temporal attention replaces standard self-attention with fast attention (Performer) and maintains a running exponential-moving-average memory (α=0.1) of archived motion features, which is concatenated with present-clip features before temporal processing to mitigate error accumulation. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) archived-clip motion-prior, (2) present-clip motion-prior, and (3) full model with only the present-clip reference and temporal attentions unfrozen.

## Results

On HDTF, MCDM achieves FID 26.45, FVD 543.28, Sync-C 7.49, Sync-D 8.04, SSIM 0.824, and E-FID 1.51, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On CelebV-HQ, MCDM records FID 67.29, FVD 784.53, Sync-C 7.25, and E-FID 2.31. On the newly introduced TalkingFace-Wild dataset (200+ hours, 10 languages), MCDM achieves FID 42.08, FVD 656.71, Sync-C 7.84, Sync-D 6.69, SSIM 0.779, and E-FID 1.97, again leading all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCDM outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets (HDTF, CelebV-HQ, and TalkingFace-Wild). The closest competitors are the other diffusion-based methods: on HDTF, MCDM improves FID from 31.37 (MegActor-Σ) and 31.44 (EchoMimic) to 26.45, and raises Sync-C from 6.87/6.96 to 7.49. On TalkingFace-Wild, the gap widens further—MCDM achieves FID 42.08 versus 48.57 (MegActor-Σ) and 49.20 (EchoMimic), with Sync-C of 7.84 versus 7.22 and 7.06 respectively. GAN-based methods (Audio2Head, V-Express, AniPortrait, SadTalker) trail substantially, with FID scores 20–50 points higher and Sync-C scores 1–4 points lower than MCDM. Hallo, while competitive among diffusion methods, still lags MCDM by ~5 FID points and ~1 Sync-C point on HDTF. The user study (20 participants, rank-3 preference) confirms MCDM's superiority in identity consistency, motion synchronization, and overall video quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCDM builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion v1.5 and adapts temporal attention mechanisms from AnimateDiff and Animate Anyone, but departs from the dominant Reference UNet paradigm (used in MegActor-Σ, Hallo, EchoMimic) by replacing it with a frozen VAE encoder plus a novel frame-aligned attention that integrates long-term archived frames. The two-stage design—first predicting decoupled motion states (head, lip, expression) via a dedicated motion-prior diffusion model, then generating video conditioned on those tokens—extends the Kandinsky-style feature-prediction idea from static T2I to the temporal TalkingFace setting. The memory-efficient temporal attention with a dynamic update mechanism (using Fast Attention/Performers) addresses the GPU memory bottleneck that limits prior autoregressive or semi-autoregressive approaches to short frame histories (2–4 frames).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of TalkingFace-Wild (200+ hours across 10 languages) fills a notable gap in multilingual, high-quality TalkingFace data, and the memory-efficient architecture makes long-sequence generation (7200+ frames) practically feasible where Reference-UNet-based methods hit GPU overflow. However, the method still relies on a single reference image and does not yet handle multi-reference or full-body scenarios, which the authors flag as important future directions. The moderate but consistent gains over strong diffusion baselines (MegActor-Σ, EchoMimic) suggest the motion-prior decomposition is a useful inductive bias, but whether it generalizes beyond talking-head to broader character animation remains open.
