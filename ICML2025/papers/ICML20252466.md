## Problem

No single LLM excels uniformly across all domains, and existing test-time knowledge fusion methods (e.g., token-wise or character-wise ensemble decoding) struggle to balance strong knowledge integration with inference efficiency. The paper asks whether speculative decoding can be repurposed not merely for speedup but as a principled mechanism to fuse complementary LLM knowledge at inference time without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252466/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoSD takes a prompt text sequence, a draft LLM, and an assistant LLM as inputs and produces a single fused text sequence. The two models may use different tokenizers, in which case the draft sequence is decoded by the draft tokenizer and re-encoded by the assistant tokenizer before parallel verification. The verification step consumes only the top-token probabilities from each model (not hidden states or embeddings), making the method compatible with black-box API access.

## Method

CoSD repurposes speculative decoding for knowledge fusion: the draft model generates K tokens autoregressively, then the assistant model verifies them in parallel. The key innovation is the verification criterion, which replaces the standard speculative-decoding acceptance test with a domain-aware decision. In Rule-Based CoSD, a draft token is replaced only when three conditions hold simultaneously: the tokens differ, the draft probability falls below threshold α, and the assistant probability exceeds β times the draft probability. In Tree-Based CoSD, a lightweight decision tree trained on a small domain-specific dataset (as few as 3 samples) maps the probability pair (M_p, M_q) to a binary accept/reject label, enabling domain-adapted fusion. When a replacement occurs, all subsequent draft tokens are discarded and generation restarts from that position, iterating until the full sequence is accepted or the EOS token is reached.

## Results

Across four model-pair scenarios and five benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, Hellaswag, TruthfulQA), CoSD-Rule achieves the best average score in three of four settings: 52.41 (Pair 1, complementary knowledge), 56.77 (Pair 2, complementary knowledge), and 52.16 (Pair 3, catastrophic forgetting recovery), surpassing the strongest individual model and all baselines. In the capacity-imbalance scenario (Pair 4), CoSD-Rule (35.71) is comparable to Speculative Decoding (35.65) while still outperforming Average Decoding (33.68) and Co-LLM (35.00). The largest gain is in catastrophic forgetting recovery, where CoSD-Rule improves the average by ~11 points over Speculative Decoding (52.16 vs. 40.87). For different-tokenizer pairs, CoSD-Rule outperforms Char-ED by up to 6.4 points (e.g., 50.65 vs. 44.29 on MMLU for Pair 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Speculative Decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), CoSD-Rule wins decisively in the complementary-knowledge and catastrophic-forgetting scenarios: 52.41 vs. 46.23 (Pair 1) and 52.16 vs. 40.87 (Pair 3), while the two methods are nearly tied in the capacity-imbalance setting (35.71 vs. 35.65, Pair 4), where Speculative Decoding's aggressive token replacement is more appropriate. Against Co-LLM (Shen et al., 2024), CoSD-Rule outperforms on every pair's average (52.41 vs. 50.90, 56.77 vs. 54.85, 52.16 vs. 48.29, 35.71 vs. 35.00), and additionally requires no per-domain training, giving it superior transferability. Against Char-ED (Gu et al., 2024) on the different-tokenizer pairs, CoSD-Rule leads on MMLU for both Pair 5 (50.65 vs. 44.29) and Pair 6 (57.33 vs. 52.51), and on HumanEval for Pair 6 (59.88 vs. 59.04), though it trails on GSM8K for Pair 5 (44.88 vs. 37.54 is actually a win; the paper reports 44.88 for CoSD-Rule vs. 37.54 for Char-ED). Overall, CoSD-Rule is the top or near-top method in 11 of 20 benchmark-pair cells, with the largest margins in the catastrophic-forgetting scenario where it recovers performance lost to fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSD repurposes the speculative decoding paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023; Xia et al., 2023) from its original purpose of accelerating single-model inference into a mechanism for test-time knowledge fusion between two LLMs. It departs from prior fusion methods like Co-LLM (Shen et al., 2024), which relies on hidden states and a trained classifier, and Char-ED (Gu et al., 2024), which operates at the character level, by using only token-level probabilities as the decision signal. The verification step—deciding when to accept the assistant model's token over the draft model's—is the novel contribution, implemented as either a simple threshold rule or a lightweight decision tree, rather than a learned neural module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSD offers a practical, interpretable, and training-free (or near-training-free) path for users to combine complementary LLMs via API calls alone, addressing a real deployment need without requiring white-box access or retraining. Key limitations remain: confidence-based decisions do not guarantee correctness (the assistant can be overconfident and wrong), and CoSD provides no benefit when one model uniformly dominates the other. Open questions include whether cross-validation or ensemble-of-confidence mechanisms could mitigate the confidence-accuracy mismatch, and whether the approach scales gracefully beyond two models without combinatorial verification overhead.
