## Problem

Tensor regression suffers from severe sample scarcity when the number of input-output pairs is far smaller than the tensor parameter dimension, making reliable estimation infeasible. While transfer learning from related source tasks can alleviate this, it introduces model shifts, covariate shifts, and the practical constraint of decentralized data storage—challenges that have not been jointly addressed in the tensor regression setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252549/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N_T target pairs and K × N_S source pairs, each consisting of a d₁×d₂×d₃ tensor predictor and a scalar response; in tensor completion the predictors reduce to standard basis elements with only a sparse subset of entries observed, and in compressed sensing they are Gaussian linear measurements. The output is the estimated target parameter tensor of the same d₁×d₂×d₃ shape (assumed low tubal rank r ≪ d₂), which in completion/sensing tasks directly yields the reconstructed tensor. A distributed variant (D-LoRT) additionally requires only local estimators from source nodes rather than raw data, reducing communication to a single tensor per source task.

## Method

LoRT employs a two-step estimation strategy built on the t-SVD framework. Step 1 (joint low-rank learning) minimizes a regularized empirical loss over all tasks with a novel fusion regularizer R(W⃗) = ‖W⁽⁰⁾‖_* + Σₖ aₖ‖W⁽⁰⁾ − W⁽ᵏ⁾‖_*, where ‖·‖_* is the tubal nuclear norm; this simultaneously enforces low tubal-rank structure in the target parameter and approximately low-rank model shifts between source and target, then produces a weighted-average initial estimate. Step 2 (target-specific refinement) solves a TNN-regularized least-squares correction using only target data, acting as a denoising step that removes bias from uninformative or misaligned sources. The distributed variant D-LoRT replaces the centralized joint optimization with local source-node estimators aggregated at the target node, preserving the same statistical rate with O(K) tensor transmissions. Non-asymptotic error bounds of order rd₁d₃/N + h̄√(d₁/N_T) are established, demonstrating robustness to both model and covariate shifts.

## Results

On the akiyo video completion task (128×128×3 tensors, 5% target sampling rate), LoRT Step 2 achieves 40.32 dB PSNR with K=8 source frames, compared to 14.24 dB for the TNN baseline and 27.37 dB for D-LoRT. The relative error (RE) drops from 0.159 (K=1) to 0.0197 (K=8) for LoRT Step 2, while TNN remains near 0.39. On the UCF-101 tensor compressed sensing task (40×40×3, 1500 target measurements), LoRT Step 2 reaches 34.69 dB PSNR (K=3, Eye Make-up) versus 13.88 dB for TNN, demonstrating a >20 dB improvement under extreme undersampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRT (both Step 1 and Step 2) substantially outperforms the TNN baseline (Lu et al., 2019b) across all datasets and settings, with PSNR gaps of 20–26 dB on akiyo and 15–20 dB on UCF-101 compressed sensing. The k-Sup baseline (Wang et al., 2021) performs comparably to TNN in the synthetic compressed sensing experiments, as both are target-only methods that cannot leverage source tasks. D-LoRT consistently outperforms TNN but trails centralized LoRT by 5–13 dB, reflecting the information loss from local estimation before aggregation. In the extended UCF-101 comparison (Table 6), LoRT Step 2 also dominates all other tensor completion baselines—Balanced TNN (Qiu et al., 2024), OITNN (Wang et al., 2023b), ℓ1-ℓ2 (Tan et al., 2023), CP (Zhou et al., 2013), Tucker (Li et al., 2018), and Convex (Raskutti et al., 2019)—by 10–18 dB, confirming that the transfer learning framework provides gains that no target-only low-rank method can match under extreme sparsity. The two-step refinement (Step 2 over Step 1) yields modest but consistent gains (0.03–0.4 dB), with the largest improvements appearing under high model shift or many source tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRT extends the TransFusion framework (He et al., 2024a), which studied fusion-based transfer learning for sparse vector regression under covariate shift, to the tensor regression setting where both predictors and parameters are multi-dimensional tensors. The tensor algebraic foundation relies on the t-SVD and tubal nuclear norm machinery from Kernfeld et al. (2015) and Lu et al. (2019b), which LoRT repurposes as a fusion regularizer to capture low-rank structure in both target parameters and inter-task model shifts. The paper positions itself as the first to systematically address four coupled challenges—sample scarcity, model shift, covariate shift, and decentralized data—within a single transfer tensor regression framework, rather than treating them in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first non-asymptotic theoretical guarantees for transfer tensor regression under distribution shifts, filling a gap between tensor recovery theory and transfer learning theory. The two-step design that provably removes the source-alignment term (δ*) from the error bound offers a clean mechanism for protecting against negative transfer, which is practically important in neuroimaging and spatiotemporal applications where source-target heterogeneity is common. Key open questions include the computational cost of repeated TNN proximal calls (O(d₃·min{d₁,d₂}·d₁d₂) per iteration), the lack of experiments on real data without ground truth, and whether the i.i.d. sampling assumption can be relaxed to handle structured spatiotemporal dependencies.
