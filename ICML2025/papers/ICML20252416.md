## Problem

While one-pass SGD is known to achieve the minimax-optimal Θ(1/√n) population loss in stochastic convex optimization, the out-of-sample behavior of multi-pass SGD—widely used in practice—remained largely uncharacterized. This paper reveals a sharp phase transition: using the canonical step size η = Θ(1/√n) that is optimal after one pass, a single additional pass can already drive the population excess risk to Ω(1), i.e., trivially large overfitting.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a training set of n i.i.d. samples from a population distribution, a fixed step size η, a number of epochs K (with T = nK total steps), and a sequence of data permutations (single-shuffle, multi-shuffle, or arbitrary), and produces a suffix-averaged iterate ŵ_{T,τ}. The lower-bound constructions operate in dimension d = O(n) (overparameterized regime) with 4-Lipschitz convex losses over the unit ball, and the resulting tight bound Θ(1/(ηT) + η√T) holds uniformly across all permutation schemes and for any suffix-averaging parameter τ.

## Method

The lower bound is built on a sample-dependent oracle framework from Livni (2024), where the gradient at step t may depend on all previously observed examples. The loss is decomposed as f(w,V) = g(w,V) + αh(w): the term g is a variant of Feldman's function that guarantees the high-probability existence of a 'bad vector' u₀ (an empirical risk minimizer with large population loss), while h is a carefully designed convex function whose subgradients steer SGD iterates toward u₀ in a block-wise manner. During the first epoch the oracle outputs zero (staying at the origin), effectively memorizing the training set; from the second epoch onward it activates h to drive the iterate toward u₀, achieving Ω(η√T) population loss. A reduction lemma (from Livni 2024) then converts the sample-dependent oracle into a standard first-order oracle by encoding past-example information into the iterates themselves. The matching upper bounds follow from a standard decomposition into a uniform-stability term (from Bassily et al. 2020) and an empirical-optimization-error term, yielding O(1/(ηT) + η√T + ηT/n).

## Results

The paper establishes a tight Θ(1/(ηT) + η√T) bound on the population excess risk of multi-pass (without-replacement) SGD over T = nK steps, matching for both single-shuffle and multi-shuffle variants (Theorems 3.1, 3.3). The dramatic consequence: with the canonical step size η = Θ(1/√n) that is minimax-optimal after one pass, population excess risk can reach Ω(1) after just two passes. For with-replacement SGD, the same Θ(1/(ηT) + η√T) bound holds after O(n log n) steps (Theorems 3.2, 3.4). Additionally, a new Ω(η√n) lower bound on the generalization gap of one-pass SGD is proven in dimension d = Õ(n), improving prior quadratic-dimension constructions (Theorem 4.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical bounds. The paper's one-pass generalization gap lower bound of Ω(η√n) in dimension d = Õ(n) improves upon Koren et al. (2022), which required dimension exponential in n, and Schliserman et al. (2024), which required dimension quadratic in n. The multi-pass population risk lower bound extends the GD overfitting constructions of Livni (2024) and Amir et al. (2021) to the SGD setting, where the first epoch provably generalizes optimally (by Nemirovskii–Yudin), creating a sharp phase transition at the second epoch. The upper bounds leverage stability results from Bassily et al. (2020) for non-smooth functions and optimization convergence results from Koren et al. (2022), showing that the Ω(η√T) overfitting term is unavoidable and that existing stability-based generalization bounds are tight only from the second pass onward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on sample complexity of gradient methods in stochastic convex optimization (Feldman 2016; Amir et al. 2021; Koren et al. 2022; Schliserman et al. 2024; Livni 2024) from full-batch gradient descent to multi-pass SGD, adapting the sample-dependent oracle framework to account for SGD's sequential, memory-limited nature. It builds directly on Livni's (2024) reduction from sample-dependent oracles to standard SCO and on Koren et al.'s (2022) stability and optimization bounds for without-replacement SGD, but departs from prior multi-pass SGD literature (Safran & Shamir 2020; Nagaraj et al. 2019; Cha et al. 2023) by analyzing population risk rather than empirical risk convergence. The upper bounds leverage Bassily et al.'s (2020) stability results for non-smooth losses, while the lower bounds require a novel adaptation of Feldman's function to handle SGD's inability to see future examples during the first pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a sharp phase transition in SGD's generalization behavior: the canonical step size η=Θ(1/√n) that is minimax optimal after one pass can yield Ω(1) population loss after just one additional pass, fundamentally challenging the intuition that more training in a convex, bounded setting should not catastrophically degrade out-of-sample performance. This tight Θ(1/(ηT)+η√T) characterization (matching upper and lower bounds across without-replacement, single-shuffle, multi-shuffle, and with-replacement variants) provides the first complete picture of when and how multi-pass SGD overfits. Key open questions include whether similar results hold in the smooth case, whether the phenomenon persists under uniform convergence (where generalization gap is small), and what the precise overfitting rate is during the second pass rather than after it completes.
