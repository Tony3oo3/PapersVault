## Problem

Despite strong visual quality, current video generation models produce temporally incoherent motion, violating basic physics and failing at even simple repetitive movements. The paper identifies the root cause as the standard pixel-reconstruction objective, which is nearly invariant to temporal perturbations (e.g., frame shuffling) during early denoising steps, causing models to prioritize appearance over motion dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as the sole user-facing input and produces a 128-frame video at 24 fps (5 seconds) at 256×256 resolution, operating in the latent space of a Temporal AutoEncoder (8× temporal, 8×8 spatial compression). During training, an additional optical flow signal (computed via RAFT and encoded as RGB where hue encodes direction and opacity encodes normalized magnitude) is jointly noised and fed alongside the video. At inference, the model simultaneously outputs both the video and its optical flow, with the flow serving as an internal guidance signal rather than a user-provided condition.

## Method

VideoJAM modifies a pre-trained DiT with only two added linear layers: an extended input projection W_in⁺ that concatenates noised video and noised optical flow latents into a single joint representation, and an extended output projection W_out⁺ that decodes both appearance and motion predictions from that shared latent. The training loss is extended to a joint appearance-motion velocity prediction under Flow Matching, and the model is fine-tuned on just 3M samples (<3% of the original training set). At inference, the key contribution is Inner-Guidance: a novel score-modification formulation that uses the model's own evolving noisy motion prediction d_t as a dynamic guidance signal, directly steering the sampling distribution toward the joint appearance-motion distribution. Unlike CFG or InstructPix2Pix, Inner-Guidance does not assume independence between the motion condition and the model weights (since d_t is generated by the model itself), requiring a new derivation that accounts for this dependency. Motion guidance is applied only during the first 50 of 100 denoising steps, targeting the early steps where coarse motion structure is determined.

## Results

On VideoJAM-bench, VideoJAM-30B achieves a VBench motion score of 92.4 (vs. 88.1 for the DiT-30B base model) and an appearance score of 73.4, while VideoJAM-4B reaches 93.7 motion (vs. 78.3 for DiT-4B). In human evaluation (2AFC), VideoJAM-30B is preferred over its base model DiT-30B in 72.7% of motion comparisons, 74.2% in quality, and 71.9% in text faithfulness. Against proprietary models, VideoJAM-30B wins motion preference over Sora (68.5%), Kling 1.5 (63.8%), and RunWay Gen3 (77.3%), despite being trained at a lower resolution (256×256 vs. 768+) and fine-tuned on only 3M samples (<3% of training data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoJAM-30B outperforms all eight baselines on motion coherence in both human and automatic evaluations. Against the direct base model DiT-30B, VideoJAM wins 72.7% of human motion comparisons and improves the VBench motion score from 88.1 to 92.4. Among proprietary models, VideoJAM-30B beats Sora (68.5% motion preference), Kling 1.5 (63.8%), and RunWay Gen3 (77.3%), though it trails them in visual quality (e.g., Kling scores 76.8 vs. 73.4 on VBench appearance) due to the lower 256×256 training resolution. Against open-source models, VideoJAM-30B dominates CogVideo5B (85.9% motion preference) and Mochi (74.2%). For the 4B variant, VideoJAM-4B surpasses CogVideo2B (96.1% motion preference), PyramidFlow (82.8%), and its own base DiT-4B (82.0%), while also outperforming the larger CogVideo5B (68.8% motion preference) despite being 25% smaller. Notably, VideoJAM also improves text faithfulness and perceived quality over its base models, indicating that the joint appearance-motion objective does not sacrifice other generation aspects.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoJAM builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone with Flow Matching, extending the standard pixel-reconstruction objective to a joint appearance-motion prediction by adding only two linear projection layers. Its Inner-Guidance mechanism departs from Classifier-Free Guidance and InstructPix2Pix by recognizing that the motion signal is generated by the model itself (hence dependent on model weights and other conditions), invalidating the independence assumptions those methods rely on. The paper also distinguishes itself from prior motion-conditioned video generation work (motion prompting, trajectory control, content-motion separation) by teaching the model an internal temporal prior rather than consuming motion as an external input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard pixel-based objectives are nearly invariant to frame permutation until late denoising steps provides a concrete diagnostic for why video models fail at motion, and the remedy (two linear layers + <3% of training data) is remarkably lightweight and generic. VideoJAM surpasses proprietary models (Sora, Kling, Runway Gen3) in motion coherence while also improving visual quality and text faithfulness, suggesting appearance and motion are complementary rather than competing objectives. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (experiments are at 256×256), whether more explicit physics encoding could address the remaining object-interaction failures, and whether the joint representation generalizes beyond optical flow to other motion signals.
