## Problem

Existing graph generative models either rely on adjacency matrices (requiring O(n²) variables and limiting architectures to permutation-invariant GNNs) or use diffusion-based iterative denoising (suffering from compounding decoding errors and requiring many sampling steps). The paper asks whether a token-based edge-list representation can unlock standard autoregressive Transformer architectures for graph generation, analogous to how LLMs model text, while maintaining competitive or superior generative quality.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a start-of-graph token [SOG] and autoregressively emits a token sequence: first all node tokens (type, index pairs), a separator token a_Δ, then all edge tokens (source index, destination index, edge type), terminated by [EOG]. For property prediction, the full graph token sequence is fed through the Transformer and the final token's hidden state is passed to an MLP head to produce a classification label. The edge ordering within the sequence is determined by reversing a degree-based edge-removal process, which constructs a dense core first and appends peripheral edges later, reducing sequence length to O(n + m) versus O(n²) for adjacency matrices.

## Method

G2PT represents each graph as a unified token sequence where node indices, node types, edge types, and special tokens share a single vocabulary, then trains a standard Transformer decoder via next-token prediction (negative log-likelihood). The key architectural insight is that the edge-list representation avoids the quadratic blowup of adjacency matrices and does not require permutation invariance, enabling generic Transformer backbones. For goal-oriented generation, the paper introduces two fine-tuning strategies: rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) with self-bootstrap (iteratively tightening acceptance thresholds) and KL-regularized PPO with a shared-architecture critic and pre-training loss to prevent degeneration. For graph property prediction, the final token activation from the pre-trained Transformer serves as a graph embedding, fine-tuned with an MLP classifier on MoleculeNet benchmarks.

## Results

On MOSES, G2PTbase achieves 96.4% validity, 100% uniqueness, and 0.97 FCD, outperforming all baselines on validity and FCD (next best: DeFoG at 92.8% validity and 1.95 FCD). On GuacaMol, G2PTbase reaches 93.4 FCD and 96.0 KL divergence, substantially exceeding DeFoG (73.8 FCD) and DiGress (68 FCD). On QM9, G2PTbase attains 0.06 FCD, the best among all compared methods. On generic graph generation (Planar, Tree, Lobster, SBM), G2PTbase achieves 11 out of 24 best MMD scores and ranks top-2 on 17 out of 24 metrics, with 100% V.U.N. on Planar, Lobster, and SBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2PTbase outperforms all five molecular generation baselines (DiGress, GEEL, DisCo, Cometh, DeFoG) on MOSES validity (96.4% vs. 85.7–92.8%) and FCD (0.97 vs. 1.19–1.95), and on GuacaMol FCD (93.4 vs. 59.7–73.8). On QM9, G2PTbase achieves the best FCD of 0.06, beating Cometh (0.11) and DeFoG (0.12). For generic graph generation, G2PTbase surpasses autoregressive baselines (GRAN, BiGG, BwR) and diffusion baselines (DiGress, HSpectre, DeFoG) on most MMD metrics, achieving 100% V.U.N. on Planar, Lobster, and SBM where several baselines score 0–77.5%. On MoleculeNet property prediction, G2PTbase (73.3 avg ROC-AUC) matches GraphMAE (73.3) and outperforms GraphMVP (71.7), GraphCL (70.6), ContextPred (70.9), AttrMask (70.2), and InfoGraph (70.1), despite using only 2D graph structure without 3D geometry or chirality features. The main trade-off is that G2PTbase scores lower on MOSES novelty (86.0) and scaffold similarity (3.3) compared to some baselines, which the authors attribute to better goodness-of-fit on the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2PT revisits the autoregressive/sequential paradigm for graph generation pioneered by GraphRNN and DeepGMG, but departs from both the adjacency-matrix-based sequential models (GraphRNN, GRAN) and the action-based edge-generation frameworks (DeepGMG, BiGG) by introducing a unified token vocabulary that encodes nodes and edges as a single sequence amenable to standard Transformer decoders. It explicitly contrasts with the dominant permutation-invariant diffusion-based approaches (DiGress, DeFoG, Cometh), arguing that the architectural constraint to GNNs and the compounding decoding error of iterative denoising are unnecessary. The work is best understood as transplanting the GPT next-token-prediction paradigm from NLP to graphs, leveraging the edge-list representation (rather than full adjacency matrices) to keep sequences short and computation linear in the number of edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single, architecturally simple Transformer can serve as both a generative model and a representation learner (via fine-tuning for property prediction), and can be adapted to goal-oriented generation using off-the-shelf NLP fine-tuning techniques (RFT, PPO) without task-specific architectural changes. The main limitation acknowledged is order-sensitivity: the degree-based edge ordering works well for the tested domains but may not transfer universally, and the authors flag more expressive or domain-agnostic orderings as an open direction. Broader open questions include whether the approach scales to very large graphs (current max is ~187 nodes) and whether the lack of permutation invariance becomes a bottleneck for tasks requiring equivariant reasoning.
