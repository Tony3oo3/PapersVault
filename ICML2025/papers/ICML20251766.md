## Problem

Neural operators have been developed primarily through their parametric compositional form, lacking a unifying optimization-theoretic perspective that connects layer design to a principled variational problem. This gap limits the ability to systematically design new architectures, understand expressivity, or leverage the extensive literature on proximal and monotone operator theory to analyze depth scaling and stability properties of operator layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251766/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on functions in separable Banach spaces (C(D,ℝⁿ), Lᵖ(D,ℝⁿ), or W^{m,p}(D,ℝⁿ)) over a bounded domain D⊂ℝᵈ. For PDE initial value problems, the input is a scalar or vector-valued initial condition function u₀(x) and the output is the solution u(·,τ̄) at a target time; for the Darcy problem, the input is a spatial viscosity function and the output is the steady-state pressure field. A key structural constraint is that the lifting operator P must map into dom ∂Φ₁ (e.g., by applying the activation σ to the lifted input), and the Bregman variant requires an invertible monotone activation—practically realized as SoftPlus with β=10³ to approximate ReLU while preserving invertibility.

## Method

Each operator layer is redefined as the minimizer of a Bregman-regularized functional optimization problem: L_t(v) = prox_{g_t}^{Φ_t}(∇̃Φ_t(M_t v) + K_t(v) + b_t), where Φ_t is a convex integral functional built from a Legendre function ψ and K_t is a bounded linear integral operator. When Φ_t reduces to the Euclidean norm (½|·|²), the framework recovers classical neural operators with activation σ = (ψ*)′; for a general Legendre ψ, it yields the Bregman neural operator L_t(v) = σ(σ⁻¹(v) + K_t v + K_t^ac(v) + b_t), which adds an inverse-activation term and corresponds to a mirror descent step with mirror map ∇̃Φ_t. This extra term makes the layer reduce to the identity when all weights vanish, providing implicit regularization that stabilizes training of deeper networks. The paper proves a universal approximation theorem for Bregman neural operators with sigmoidal activations and demonstrates empirically that the Bregman variant of FNO (BFNO) scales more effectively with depth than FNO, ResFNO, or F-FNO on PDEBench datasets.

## Results

On the PDEBench suite (2D Navier-Stokes ν=10⁻⁴, 1D Burgers, 2D Darcy, and others), BFNO consistently achieves the lowest ℓ² relative test error across all cross-validated layer counts T∈{4,8,16,32}. On the depth-scaling study (2D Navier-Stokes, ν=10⁻⁴), BFNO maintains decreasing error up to T=64, whereas FNO degrades from T=8, ResFNO from T=16, and F-FNO plateaus at T=16. BFNO also outperforms FNO in low- and mid-frequency error bands, with substantial reductions on simpler datasets and marginal gains on the most complex cases. The sole exception is 2D Darcy (viscosity-to-steady-state mapping), where F-FNO's additional MLP layers yield slightly better performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFNO outperforms the standard FNO (Li et al., 2021) on all datasets except 2D Darcy, with the most notable gains on 2D Navier-Stokes (ν=10⁻⁴) and 1D Burgers, where FNO's error grows with depth while BFNO's continues to decrease. Against F-FNO (Tran et al., 2023), BFNO wins on most benchmarks but loses on 2D Darcy, where F-FNO's extra MLP layers better capture the viscosity-to-solution mapping; F-FNO also plateaus earlier (T=16 vs. T=64 for BFNO) on depth-scaling. The Bregman variant of WNO (Tripura & Chakraborty, 2023) is studied in the appendix and shows similar qualitative improvements over its non-Bregman counterpart. A key structural advantage of BFNO is that its layers reduce to the identity when all weights are zero (Remark 3.6), and the learned weight distribution is sharply peaked around zero (Laplace-like), providing implicit regularization that stabilizes training of deeper models compared to the Gaussian-like distributions observed in FNO, ResFNO, and F-FNO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-dimensional Bregman neural network architecture of Frecon et al. (2022) to Banach function spaces, requiring new formulations of Legendre functions, Bregman divergences, and proximity operators in infinite dimensions. It departs from the standard kernel-based compositional viewpoint of neural operators (Kovachki et al., 2021) by recasting each operator layer as the minimizer of a Bregman-regularized optimization problem, showing that classical FNO layers emerge as the special case where the Bregman distance reduces to the Euclidean distance. The framework also draws on the proximal optimization literature (Nguyen, 2017; Combettes & Pesquet, 2020a) to connect activation operators to proximity operators, and the resulting Bregman variant corresponds to a mirror descent step with the activation's gradient as mirror map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bregman proximal viewpoint provides a unifying lens that connects neural operator design to the rich body of proximal and monotone operator theory, potentially enabling transfer of convergence and stability results from optimization to deep operator architectures. The practical finding that BFNO scales with depth far better than FNO (improving to T=64 vs. degrading from T=8) and its Laplace-like weight distribution suggest the inverse activation term acts as implicit regularization, which could guide future architecture design. A key open gap is that the universal approximation theorem requires sigmoidal activations while practice uses ReLU-like functions (approximated here by SoftPlus with β=10³), leaving the theoretical guarantee disconnected from the actual training setup. Extending the framework to non-monotone activations (e.g., GELU, SwiSH) and to more complex operator parametrizations beyond Fourier/wavelet bases remains future work.
