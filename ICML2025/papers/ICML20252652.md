## Problem

Autoregressive VLA models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) frame action prediction as next-token prediction, which discretizes continuous actions and is inherently slow for real-time control, while diffusion-based policies generate robust, high-frequency actions but lack the reasoning capabilities needed for complex, multi-step tasks. The paper asks whether the reasoning strength of autoregressive models and the action-generation robustness of diffusion models can be unified in a single framework without incurring the computational overhead of iterative reasoning-action loops.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (typically three camera views: two external Zed cameras and one wrist-mounted Realsense 435i) along with a natural-language instruction. Images are encoded by a shared SigLIP backbone per view, then concatenated into a fixed set of visual tokens fed into Qwen2-VL. The model produces two outputs in a single forward pass: (1) autoregressive reasoning text (e.g., 'grabbing the toy car') and (2) continuous robot joint-space actions generated by a diffusion denoising process, with the reasoning embedding modulating the action decoder via FiLM.

## Method

DiffusionVLA builds on a pre-trained Qwen2-VL backbone (2B/7B/72B) and appends a Diffusion Policy action head, trained jointly with a combined loss L = L_diff + 10·L_ntp. The key architectural contribution is a reasoning injection module: the final hidden-state embedding of the autoregressively generated reasoning phrase is injected into the policy network via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), so that the reasoning signal directly conditions every layer of the action decoder without requiring a second forward pass or recursive input-output cycling. For new embodiments, only a fresh MLP projection layer is initialized rather than copying an entire action decoder, preserving pre-trained knowledge. The model is pre-trained on Droid (and OXE for the 72B variant) with GPT-4o-augmented reasoning annotations, then fine-tuned per task using LoRA on the VLM while freezing the visual encoder, achieving 82 Hz inference for the 2B model on a single A6000 GPU.

## Results

On the multi-task learning benchmark (5 tasks, Franka robot), DiVLA-2B achieves 83.6% average in-distribution success rate and 57.8% under visual generalization (distractors, new backgrounds, colored lighting), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On zero-shot bin picking with 102 unseen objects, DiVLA reaches 63.7% success rate versus 28.4% for OpenVLA-7B and 8.9% for Diffusion Policy. Inference speed is 82 Hz for DiVLA-2B and 42 Hz for DiVLA-7B on a single A6000 GPU, compared to 5 Hz for OpenVLA-7B. On the bimanual table bussing task, DiVLA-2B achieves 72.9% (seen) and 70.8% (mixed) success, while OpenVLA scores 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiVLA-2B dominates all four baselines across every evaluated task despite using only 39K pre-trained trajectories (vs. 970K for Octo and OpenVLA, a 25× data advantage for the baselines). On in-distribution multi-task learning, DiVLA-2B scores 83.6% average, beating OpenVLA-7B (39.4%) by 44.2 points, TinyVLA (45.5%) by 38.1 points, Octo (24.3%) by 59.3 points, and Diffusion Policy (27.9%) by 55.7 points. Under visual generalization (distractors, novel backgrounds, colored lighting), the gap widens further: DiVLA-2B maintains 57.8% while the best baseline (TinyVLA) drops to 28.9%. On zero-shot bin picking of 102 unseen objects, DiVLA achieves 63.7% versus 28.4% (OpenVLA), 23.5% (TinyVLA), 19.6% (Octo), and 8.9% (Diffusion Policy). On the bimanual table bussing task, DiVLA-2B reaches 72.9% (seen) and 70.8% (mixed), while OpenVLA fails completely at 0% and Diffusion Policy manages only 45.8%. Inference speed is also a decisive advantage: DiVLA-2B runs at 82 Hz and DiVLA-7B at 42 Hz, compared to 5 Hz for OpenVLA-7B (a 16× and 8× speedup respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two dominant VLA paradigms: autoregressive next-token prediction models (RT-2, OpenVLA) that discretize actions into tokens, and diffusion-based policies (Diffusion Policy, π0) that model actions as denoising processes. It departs from both by assigning autoregression exclusively to language reasoning and diffusion exclusively to continuous action generation, then bridging them via a FiLM-based reasoning injection module that embeds self-generated reasoning phrases directly into the policy head. Conceptually, this is closest to Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2024), which unifies next-token prediction and diffusion in a single transformer for text and image generation, but DiffusionVLA adapts this unification to embodied control and adds the explicit reasoning-to-action coupling mechanism that prior robotics work (ECoT, YAY) handled only through iterative prompting or external conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the data efficiency: DiVLA-2B is pre-trained on only 39K trajectories (Droid) yet outperforms OpenVLA and Octo, which use 970K trajectories (OXE), by 20+ points on multi-task benchmarks. This suggests that the reasoning component substitutes for raw action data volume, a hypothesis that could reshape how VLA pre-training data is curated. Open questions include whether the reasoning injection module's contribution (≈33-point ablation gap) holds at 72B scale, whether the approach generalizes beyond Franka/AgileX to mobile or dexterous platforms, and whether the FiLM conditioning is truly necessary versus simpler cross-attention alternatives. The 82Hz inference speed also opens the door to real-time deployment of reasoning-augmented policies, which prior AR-based VLAs could not achieve.
