## Problem

Linear RNNs offer constant-memory, linear-time inference ideal for edge streaming, but their deployment in resource-constrained environments requires hardware-aware compression that has remained under-explored. Specifically, it was unclear whether unstructured sparsity in weights and activations could be translated into real latency and energy gains on hardware that natively exploits it, and whether highly sparse models could outperform dense baselines at matched compute budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251971/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes noisy audio as STFT frames (32 ms window, 8 ms hop, yielding 257-dimensional complex-valued input vectors) and produces denoised STFT frames of the same shape, processed token-by-token in a streaming fashion with an 8 ms per-frame latency budget. Evaluation is via scale-invariant signal-to-noise ratio (SI-SNR) on the Intel N-DNS Challenge test set. A secondary keyword-spotting experiment uses SpeechCommands V2-35 with the same STFT encoding.

## Method

The paper applies a three-stage compression pipeline to the S5 linear RNN: (1) iterative magnitude pruning with an Erdős-Rényi-Kernel sparsity schedule to reach 90% unstructured weight sparsity, (2) ReLU-fication—replacing GELU with ReLU and inserting additional ReLUs after residual adds and on the real component of the S5 hidden state—to induce activation sparsity without cross-channel synchronization, and (3) quantization-aware training with static W8A16 fixed-point quantization (8-bit weights, 16-bit activations, 16-bit recurrent diagonal). The compressed model is then ported to the Intel Loihi 2 neuromorphic chip, where complex arithmetic is split into real/imaginary synaptic layers and element-wise operations are fused into programmable neurons. The key empirical finding is that wider sparse S5 models dominate the Pareto front over dense models (2× less compute, 36% less memory iso-accuracy), and that deploying the sparse quantized model on Loihi 2 yields 42× lower latency and 149× lower energy per token compared to an iso-accuracy dense model on a Jetson Orin Nano GPU.

## Results

On the Intel N-DNS Challenge (audio denoising), sparse S5 models achieve iso-accuracy with 2× less compute and 36% less memory than dense S5 baselines, and the sparse-11 model matches the previous state-of-the-art Spiking-FullSubNet XL (15.2 dB SI-SNR) with 3.2× less compute and 5.37× lower memory. When deployed on Intel Loihi 2 with W8A16 fixed-point quantization, the sparse model achieves 42× lower latency (76 μs vs. 3224 μs) and 149× lower energy per token (13 μJ vs. 1936 μJ) compared to the iso-accuracy dense model on a Jetson Orin Nano in real-time chunked inference. In offline sample-by-sample processing, Loihi 2 delivers 3.7× higher throughput (16.58 vs. 4.28 samples/s) with 8× less energy per sample (185.80 vs. 1512.60 mJ/sam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Spiking-FullSubNet XL (Hao et al., 2024), the Track 1 winner of the Intel N-DNS Challenge with 15.2 dB SI-SNR, the sparse-11 S5 model matches accuracy while requiring 3.2× less compute and 5.37× lower memory, establishing a new efficiency-performance Pareto front. Against dense S5 models of the same architecture, sparse variants (90% weight sparsity + ReLU-fication) consistently dominate the Pareto front across all compute budgets: for example, sparse-8 achieves the same SI-SNR as dense-3 with 2× fewer MACs and 36% less memory. In the hardware comparison, the sparse quantized S5 on Loihi 2 decisively outperforms the iso-accuracy dense S5 on Jetson Orin Nano in real-time settings: 42× lower latency and 149× lower energy per token in chunked (10-step) inference, and 35× lower latency with 1200× lower energy in single-token fall-through mode. The Jetson only surpasses Loihi 2 in energy per sample when processing 256 sequences in parallel (5.89 vs. 185.80 mJ/sam), a regime where the GPU is fully utilized and Loihi 2's per-core resource constraints become limiting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established compression techniques—iterative magnitude pruning with ERK layer scaling (Zhu & Gupta, 2018; Evci et al., 2020), ReLU-fication for activation sparsity (Mirzadeh et al., 2024), and quantization-aware training—into a unified pipeline applied to the S5 linear RNN architecture (Smith et al., 2023). The key departure from prior work is the hardware-aware framing: rather than targeting GPU-compatible structured sparsity, the paper exploits the fact that neuromorphic processors like Intel Loihi 2 natively support unstructured sparsity in both weights and activations, making extreme (≥90%) sparsity practically realizable. The work also extends prior fixed-point deployment of linear RNNs (Meyer et al., 2024; Abreu et al., 2024) by combining quantization with sparsity and validating on actual neuromorphic silicon rather than simulation alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end demonstration that unstructured sparsity in linear RNNs translates into orders-of-magnitude hardware gains (42× latency, 149× energy) on neuromorphic processors, moving the field beyond theoretical MAC-count arguments. However, the comparison is asymmetric (sparse W8A16 on Loihi 2 vs. dense FP32 on Jetson Orin Nano), and the fixed-point mismatch that accumulates linearly with depth in recurrent models remains an unresolved challenge. Open questions include whether the Pareto-front dominance of sparse models holds for larger architectures and more complex tasks (language, multimodal), and whether the simulation-to-hardware gap can be closed with advanced data types like FP8.
