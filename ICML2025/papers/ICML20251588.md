## Problem

Helpfulness votes on online platforms are systematically distorted by position bias (top-ranked answers attract more votes due to visibility) and herding bias (users conform to the visible majority opinion), creating a cascading feedback loop that entrenches early popularity. Existing debiasing methods such as the Chinese Voting Process (CVP) can only mitigate herding bias and lack a causal framework to simultaneously account for both biases, leaving no principled way to recover true content quality from observational voting data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-ordered voting trajectory per question (a matrix of answers × time steps with +1/−1 vote entries), the display rank of each answer at each time step, the running positive-vote ratio, and the relative length of each response. It produces a scalar de-biased quality score per answer (the sum over time of the expected positive-vote probability under a uniform counterfactual vote-and-rank distribution), community-level coefficients quantifying position bias (β) and herding bias (λ), and a reranked answer list that can replace the platform's default vote-difference ordering.

## Method

CVA frames each individual vote as a causal treatment effect: the 'treatment' is the pair (existing vote distribution, display rank) and the 'outcome' is the binary vote, with content features (relative length) serving as the sole confounder under an ignorability assumption validated by a directed acyclic graph. A single parametric logistic model P(V=1) = sigmoid(q + λR + ν_i·L + β/(1+D)) is fit by maximum likelihood with L2 regularization over the full voting trajectory, jointly learning the per-answer quality q, the community-level herding coefficient λ, the question-level length coefficient ν_i, and the community-level position coefficient β. The final quality estimate is obtained via backdoor adjustment: it integrates the expected positive-vote probability over the population distribution of vote configurations and ranks, thereby marginalizing out both the herding and position effects while holding the response text fixed. This yields a counterfactual-invariant score that is provably optimal under a KL-divergence criterion, and unlike CVP it requires no independence assumption between the two bias mechanisms.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic dataset (where ground-truth quality is known), CVA achieves the highest Kendall's Tau rank correlation across all 8 representative communities, e.g., 0.4106 on reactjs(SOF), 0.4880 on politics, and 0.5640 on codegolf.meta, consistently outperforming both the system vote-difference ranking and the CVP baseline. On real StackExchange data evaluated by GPT-4o helpfulness scores, CVA achieves the best KT in 7 of 8 communities (e.g., 0.2573 on politics vs. 0.2138 for CVP and 0.0824 for voteDiff) and the lowest sum of squared residuals in 7 of 8. Across all 120 largest communities, CVA beats both baselines simultaneously in 77.5% of communities on residual and 75% on KT when using GPT-4o as ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CVA against two baselines: (1) the system's native vote-difference ranking (voteDiff) and (2) the Chinese Voting Process (CVP, Lee et al. 2016), which models herding bias via voting trajectories but does not account for position bias. CVA outperforms voteDiff in virtually all communities on both KT and Res metrics; for example, on the politics community with GPT-4o ground truth, CVA achieves KT 0.2573 versus 0.0824 for voteDiff (a >3× improvement). Against CVP, CVA wins in most communities, with the largest gaps appearing in communities with strong position bias (e.g., askubuntu: KT 0.1823 vs. 0.1116; politics: 0.2573 vs. 0.2138 under GPT-4o evaluation). CVA's advantage over CVP is smaller or absent in meta-communities (math.meta, codegolf.meta) where position bias is naturally low, since CVP's lack of a position term is less penalized there. At the community level (120 communities), CVA beats both baselines simultaneously in 78.3% of communities on residual and 74.2% on KT using comment sentiment, and in 77.5% and 75.0% respectively using GPT-4o helpfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinese Voting Process (CVP) framework of Lee et al. (2016), which modeled herding bias in sequential voting but lacked an explicit mechanism for position bias. CVA departs from CVP by introducing a position-bias term into the logistic voting model and applying Pearl's backdoor adjustment to jointly de-bias both effects from observational voting trajectories, rather than treating them independently. It also positions itself against instrumental-variable approaches (Dev et al., 2019) that could not isolate social influence from position effects, and against snapshot-based ML/DL helpfulness predictors that ignore the sequential, causal structure of voting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CVA provides a principled causal framework for fairer content ranking that is directly applicable to any platform with vote history, and the cross-community behavioral map across 120 StackExchange forums offers actionable insights into how different expert communities exhibit distinct bias profiles. Key limitations include the assumption that relative length is the sole confounder (B), the exclusion of read-but-not-voted interactions, and reliance on GPT-4o as a ground-truth proxy whose alignment with human judgment remains unvalidated. Open questions include extending the framework to richer presentational features (images, code, author reputation), handling the read-but-not-voted gap, and bridging the gap between LLM-evaluated and human-evaluated quality.
