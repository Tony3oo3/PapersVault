## Problem

Score matching and its major extensions (truncated, sliced, denoising) are widely used for density estimation, diffusion models, and graphical model learning, yet no general framework exists for applying them when data is partially missing. Existing approaches either restrict the score model to neural networks (MissDiff, Ambient Diffusion) or rely on EM-like procedures with unclear convergence properties and biased Monte Carlo estimates (Uehara et al., 2020). This paper fills the gap by providing two flexible, parameter-preserving methods that work with any parameterised score model under missing completely at random (MCAR) corruption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of partially observed d-dimensional samples, where each sample carries a binary mask indicating which coordinates are present; the missingness pattern can differ across samples and is assumed MCAR. The output is a parameterised score function s_θ(x) = ∇_x log q_θ(x) (the gradient of an unnormalised log-density), from which one can recover density estimates, mean/precision parameters for Gaussian models, or graphical model edge structures via L1-regularised precision estimation. Unlike NN-based competitors, the score model can be any explicit parametric family (e.g., Gaussian, ICA-inspired), making the approach interpretable and applicable to energy-based modelling and GGM estimation.

## Method

The key insight is to define marginal scores s_λ(x_λ) = ∇_{x_λ} log ∫ q(x) dx_{-λ} and show that minimising the Fisher divergence between true and model marginal scores yields a valid, θ-observable objective (Proposition 4.3). Two estimation strategies are then developed: (1) Marginal IW Score Matching, which approximates the intractable marginal score integral via importance weighting with r samples from a proposal over the missing coordinates, yielding finite-sample convergence bounds in bounded domains; and (2) Marginal Variational Score Matching, which takes the parameter gradient first and rewrites the objective in terms of expectations under the conditional p_θ(x_{-λ}|x_λ), then trains a variational conditional p'_φ to approximate it via KL or Fisher divergence, avoiding the nested-expectation bias of IW. Both methods extend directly to truncated, sliced, and denoising score matching, and the variational approach is shown to dominate in high-dimensional and structured settings (e.g., star-shaped GGMs, S&P 100, yeast data) while IW excels in lower-dimensional, small-sample regimes.

## Results

On the non-Gaussian parameter estimation task (1000 samples, 50% missing), Marg-Var achieves the lowest Fisher divergence across all dimensions, with the performance gap widening as dimensionality increases. On the S&P 100 GGM edge detection task (100 stocks, 5 years of data), Marg-Var clearly outperforms all other methods in AUC across all missingness levels. On the Yeast gene expression task (106 genes, 262 segregants), Marg-Var again achieves the highest AUC, with all other methods performing comparably to each other. In lower-dimensional settings (10-dim truncated Gaussian, 1000 samples, 20% missing), Marg-IW and EM perform best with Marg-Var approaching asymptotically, while Zeroed Score Matching fails to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baselines: the EM approach of Uehara et al. (2020) and Zeroed Score Matching (a naive adaptation of MissDiff that zeros out missing score dimensions). Marg-IW and EM perform nearly identically across all experiments, which the authors attribute to both using self-normalised importance weighting to approximate conditional expectations. Marg-Var consistently outperforms both baselines in high-dimensional and structurally complex settings (non-Gaussian models with increasing dimension, star-shaped GGMs, S&P 100, and Yeast data), with the advantage growing as dimensionality and structural complexity increase. Zeroed Score Matching performs worst in structured settings (failing to converge on the truncated Gaussian, showing large errors on star graphs) but performs moderately on unstructured dense graphs where naive marginalisation is a reasonable approximation. The key finding is that Marg-Var's advantage is most pronounced when the graph has strong local structure (few high-degree nodes) and high dimensionality, while in low-dimensional or unstructured settings all methods converge to similar performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score matching framework (Hyvärinen, 2005) and its major variants—truncated, sliced, and denoising score matching—to the setting of partially missing data, a gap the authors identify as largely unexplored. It departs from prior missing-data score matching work (Uehara et al., 2020) by avoiding the biased nested Monte Carlo of EM, and from diffusion-specific approaches (MissDiff, Ambient Diffusion) by remaining compatible with any parameterized score model rather than requiring neural networks. The variational approach builds directly on the bi-level score matching framework of Bao et al. (2020, 2021) and Vértes & Sahani (2016), generalizing their latent-variable results to the missing-data setting with arbitrary model classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Score matching underpins diffusion models, energy-based models, and graphical model estimation, yet real-world data is frequently incomplete; this paper provides the first general, parameter-preserving framework for score matching under MCAR (and MNAR) missingness with finite-sample guarantees. A key open question is how the marginal Fisher divergence used in the objective relates to the full Fisher divergence, which limits the interpretability of the theoretical bounds. The authors also identify natural extensions to diffusion-based generative modelling with missing data and to refined variational inference that accounts for the randomness of the missingness mask, both of which could have broad practical impact.
