## Problem

Content creators and benchmark curators lack a practical, statistically sound method to prove whether their data was included in LLM pretraining corpora. Existing approaches either impair machine readability (unicode substitutions), require hard-to-obtain validation sets, or make invalid distributional assumptions when comparing human-written content against LLM rephrasings—assumptions the paper shows are violated in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires gray-box access to the target LLM (token probabilities for any sequence) and two sets of watermarked rephrases per document: one public version released online and m private versions kept confidential. Both sets are generated by the same instruction-tuned LLM (Llama3-70B) with identical prompts, differing only in the KGW watermarking hash key. The output is a one-sided p-value from a paired t-test, where a small p-value indicates statistically significant evidence that the public version was seen during pretraining. Top 5% perplexity outliers are clipped before testing to prevent a few extreme samples from undermining the test.

## Method

STAMP repurposes the KGW watermarking scheme not for text attribution but as a mechanism to embed distinct, key-dependent signals into rephrased documents, thereby enhancing the statistical power of a membership test. In Stage 1, an instruction-tuned LLM generates one public rephrase (watermarked with a designated public key) and m private rephrases (each watermarked with a distinct private key) per document; the public version is released while private versions are kept secret. In Stage 2, the method computes per-document perplexity differences between the public version and the average of m private versions, then applies a one-sided paired t-test to determine whether the model systematically assigns lower perplexity to the public version. The critical design choice over prior work (PaCoST) is that both compared versions are LLM-generated with the same prompt, eliminating the confound of LLM self-preference bias; the watermarking further amplifies detection sensitivity by up to three orders of magnitude by increasing sequence memorizability during training.

## Results

On four benchmarks (TriviaQA, ARC-C, MMLU, GSM8K) each constituting <0.001% of a 6.7B-token pretraining corpus with duplication rate 1, STAMP achieves p-values of 1.2×10⁻⁴, 2.8×10⁻⁴, 7.0×10⁻⁴, and 6.6×10⁻⁶ respectively, all statistically significant at p<0.05. In real-world case studies, STAMP detects EMNLP 2024 paper abstracts with p≈10⁻¹² and AI Snake Oil blog posts with p=2.4×10⁻³. Watermarked rephrases preserve benchmark utility (relative LLM rankings unchanged) and achieve P-SP semantic similarity scores of 0.83–0.95, exceeding the 0.76 average for human paraphrases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAMP outperforms PaCoST (Zhang et al., 2024a) on all four benchmarks: PaCoST achieves significance only on TriviaQA (p=1.6×10⁻³) while STAMP reaches p-values 1–3 orders of magnitude lower across the board. Against LLM DI (Maini et al., 2024), STAMP dominates in both validation-set settings; LLM DI (different) reaches significance on only TriviaQA (p=0.02) and MMLU (p=0.03), whereas STAMP is significant on all four. All five membership inference attacks (PPL, Zlib, Min-K, Min-K++, DC-PDD) achieve AUROC ≈ 0.5 (random chance) on every dataset, confirming their inability to detect membership at the sequence level. In a direct comparison with the radioactivity-based approach of Sander et al. (2025) under matched conditions (δ=2, duplication=4, 2B tokens), STAMP achieves vanishingly small p-values (≈0) on ARC-C and MMLU while the radioactivity method yields non-significant p-values of 0.61 and 0.13. For utility preservation, STAMP watermarked benchmarks maintain near-identical model performance and rankings (e.g., ARC-C 0-shot: 26.3 vs. 26.1 original for Pythia-1B), whereas the UNICODE substitution watermark of Wei et al. (2024) degrades scores by up to 20% and disrupts relative rankings.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAMP repurposes the KGW LLM watermarking scheme—originally designed for attributing generated text to a specific model—as a mechanism to create semantically equivalent but algorithmically distinguishable document versions for membership detection. It departs from PaCoST (Zhang et al., 2024a) by eliminating the critical confound that LLMs systematically prefer their own outputs over human-written text, ensuring both public and private versions share the same data-generating process and differ only in watermark key. Compared to LLM DI (Maini et al., 2024), which requires an unseen validation set from the same distribution, STAMP needs only the privately held watermarked rephrases. It also contrasts with contemporaneous work by Sander et al. (2025), which detects contamination via token-level overfitting ('radioactivity') and requires the rephrasing and target models to share a tokenizer, whereas STAMP's perplexity-based paired test is tokenizer-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAMP provides content creators and benchmark curators with a practical, statistically grounded tool to prove unauthorized inclusion of their data in LLM pretraining corpora, addressing a growing legal and scientific-integrity concern. Key limitations include the requirement to watermark content before public release (making it inapplicable to already-published material), the need for gray-box token-probability access, and evaluation restricted to a 1B-parameter model via continual pretraining rather than from-scratch training. Open questions include the optimal watermarking strength trade-off between detectability and content quality, extension to other modalities (code, speech, images), and whether results generalize to larger models trained from scratch on trillions of tokens.
