## Problem

Implicit regularization for tensor factorizations has only been rigorously established for gradient flow or in the lazy training regime, with no prior result for gradient descent with small random initialization in the tensor setting. The closest prior work (Liu et al., 2024) for tubal tensor recovery required a strong spectral initialization assumption, leaving open whether vanilla gradient descent with a small random initialization can provably recover low tubal-rank solutions without such a requirement.

## Input / Output

The method takes m linear measurements y = A(X*X^T) of a tubal positive semidefinite tensor X*X^T ∈ S_+^{n×n×k} (where X ∈ R^{n×r×k} has tubal rank r ≤ n) along with a small random initialization U_0 ∈ R^{n×R×k} whose entries are i.i.d. N(0, α²/R) for a sufficiently small scale α. The measurement operator A must satisfy RIP(2r+1, δ) with δ ≤ cκ^{-4}r^{-1/2}, requiring m ≥ O(κ^8 r^2 nk) random sub-Gaussian measurements. The output is a factorization U*U^T ≈ X*X^T with tubal rank r, recovered after O(1/(μσ_min(X)^2) · log(1/α)) gradient descent iterations, with reconstruction error scaling as α^{21/16} times a condition-number-dependent constant.

## Method

The paper proves that gradient descent on the overparameterized loss ℓ(U) = ‖A(U*U^T) − y‖² with R ≥ 3r and small random initialization implicitly regularizes toward the low tubal-rank ground truth. The proof decomposes the trajectory into two stages: a spectral stage where GD iterates closely approximate the tensor power method (I + μA*A(X*X^T))^{*t} * U_0, aligning the tensor-column subspaces of U_t with those of X, and a convergence stage where a signal-noise decomposition (via the t-SVD of V_X * U_t) shows the signal term converges geometrically to X*X^T while the noise term remains bounded. A key technical challenge absent in the matrix setting is that gradient descent in the tubal tensor domain does not decompose into independent Fourier-slice problems (unlike the power method), forcing nontrivial cross-slice estimations to control interactions between Fourier components. The reconstruction error can be made arbitrarily small by decreasing α, at the cost of only logarithmically more iterations.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that gradient descent with small random initialization (entries i.i.d. N(0, α²/R)) on an overparameterized tubal tensor factorization (R ≥ 3r) converges to a low tubal-rank solution after Ût ≲ (1/(μσ_min(X)²))·ln(C₁κn·‖X‖/(min{n,R}·kα)) iterations, with relative Frobenius error bounded by O(k^{61/32}·r^{1/8}·κ^{-3/16}·(min{n,R}−r)^{3/8}·[C₂κ²√n/√min{n,R}]^{21κ²}·(α/‖X‖)^{21/16}). Numerical experiments (n=10, k=4, r=3, m=254 measurements, μ=10⁻⁵) confirm the predicted two-stage convergence: a spectral alignment stage (t ≲ 3000) where iterates align with the tensor power method, followed by a geometric convergence stage. The test error follows an approximately polynomial relationship with initialization scale α, consistent with the theoretical α^{21/16} rate, and increasing overparameterization R from 10 to 400 accelerates convergence and reduces both training and test error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method benchmark comparisons; the closest prior work (Liu et al., 2024) analyzes factorized gradient descent for low-tubal-rank recovery but requires a carefully designed spectral initialization that already well approximates the solution, whereas this paper proves that a small random Gaussian initialization suffices to achieve the same low-tubal-rank convergence without any such assumption. The numerical experiments compare different overparameterization levels (R = 10, 50, 100, 200, 400) and initialization scales (α varying over several orders of magnitude), showing that larger R consistently accelerates convergence and reduces the final error floor, while smaller α yields lower test error at the cost of more iterations (logarithmic in α). The paper also validates that gradient descent iterates in the spectral stage closely track the tensor power method iterates (modulo normalization), confirming the theoretical approximation. No competing tensor recovery algorithms (e.g., alternating minimization or t-Schatten norm minimization) are directly benchmarked, as the focus is on characterizing the implicit bias of gradient descent rather than comparing recovery algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis of Stöger & Soltanolkotabi (2021) for overparameterized matrix sensing to the tubal tensor factorization setting, where the key technical challenge is that gradient descent in the tubal domain does not decompose into independent per-slice matrix problems in the Fourier domain. It departs from the closely related work of Liu et al. (2024) on factorized gradient descent for low-tubal-rank recovery by eliminating the need for a carefully designed spectral initialization, instead showing that a small random Gaussian initialization naturally produces the same spectral alignment through a 'spectral stage' analogous to the tensor power method (Gleich et al., 2013). Prior tensor implicit bias results (Razin et al., 2021) were limited to gradient flow, making this the first rigorous gradient descent analysis for tensor factorization beyond the lazy regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result fills a notable gap in the implicit regularization literature by providing the first gradient descent (as opposed to gradient flow) convergence guarantee for overparameterized tensor factorization with small random initialization, validating the practical intuition that overparameterization plus small initialization is sufficient for structured tensor recovery. However, the analysis is restricted to the tubal rank notion and requires an RIP condition on the measurement operator, leaving open whether similar results hold for CP-rank or Tucker-rank factorizations and for more general (non-RIP) measurement models. The exponential dependence on the condition number in the initialization scale requirement, while consistent with matrix analogs, suggests that ill-conditioned tensors may require impractically small initializations, and whether this dependence is fundamental or an artifact of the proof remains an open question.
