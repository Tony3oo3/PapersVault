## Problem

LLM-based recommender systems represent users and items using in-vocabulary tokens (e.g., numeric IDs), whose semantics are inherited from natural language pre-training and fail to capture collaborative user-item relationships. Existing OOV token approaches like CID improve item distinguishability but cannot effectively memorize collaborative correlations during fine-tuning, leaving a gap between diversity and memorization in ID representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes user-item interaction sequences (ordered item IDs with ratings from Amazon reviews) and converts them into instruction-tuning prompts where user and item identifiers are replaced by META ID token sequences of the form ⟨Item⟩⟨CT_i⟩⟨CI_i⟩. Outputs are task-specific: recommended item IDs generated via constrained beam search (all-item setting), integer ratings (1–5), or free-text explanations/summaries. A key design choice is that both users and items receive OOV token IDs (not just items), and the OOV tokens are shared across users and items with similar collaborative properties.

## Method

META ID constructs OOV tokens through a three-stage pipeline: (1) sampling U-I-U meta-paths from the interaction graph where adjacent users assigned the same rating to an item, then training a skip-gram model on these paths to obtain shared user/item embeddings; (2) applying K-Means clustering (G=100–200 clusters) to these embeddings and assigning each user/item a coarse-grained cluster token ⟨CT_i⟩ plus a fine-grained in-cluster token ⟨CI_i⟩ ordered by distance to the centroid; (3) expanding the LLM vocabulary with these OOV tokens and initializing their embeddings via a linear transformation layer F(·) seeded with the cluster centroid vectors, which provides a meaningful starting point rather than random initialization. The LLM (T5-small or LLaMA2-7b with LoRA) is then fine-tuned on instruction-tuning prompts for sequential recommendation, direct recommendation, rating prediction, explanation generation, and review summarization, with the training objective jointly optimizing the LLM parameters and the OOV token transformation layer.

## Results

On sequential recommendation, META ID (T5 backbone) achieves H@5 of 0.0322, 0.0510, and 0.0533 on Sports, Beauty, and Toys respectively, outperforming all baselines across all three datasets. With the LLaMA2-7b backbone, META ID reaches H@5 of 0.0392 on Sports (best overall) but underperforms TIGER on Toys (0.0387 vs. 0.0521). The largest gains over in-vocabulary token baselines (RID, SID) occur on Toys, where META ID (T5) achieves 0.0533 H@5 versus 0.0044 (RID) and 0.0050 (SID), a >10× improvement. On direct recommendation, META ID similarly leads all methods, reaching H@5 of 0.0357, 0.0480, and 0.0564 on Sports, Beauty, and Toys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

META ID (T5) achieves the best H@5 and N@5 on all three datasets, beating the strongest non-LLM baseline S³-Rec by 28% (Sports), 32% (Beauty), and 20% (Toys) on H@5, and the strongest LLM-based baseline TIGER by 22% (Sports), 12% (Beauty), and 2% (Toys). Against the closest LLM-based competitor CID, META ID (T5) gains 19% on Sports, 52% on Beauty, and 210% on Toys H@5, demonstrating that meta-path-guided OOV tokens substantially outperform spectral-clustering-based collaborative IDs. In-vocabulary token methods (RID, SID) collapse on Toys (H@5 ≈ 0.004–0.005), confirming the paper's claim that limited in-vocabulary tokens cannot distinguish items at scale. META ID (LLaMA2-7b) leads on Sports (H@5=0.0392) but trails TIGER on Toys (0.0387 vs. 0.0521), which the authors attribute to differences in fine-tuning methodology (LoRA vs. full fine-tuning). On direct recommendation, META ID extends its lead, reaching H@5 of 0.0564 on Toys versus 0.0076 for CID and 0.0025 for SID, a >7× margin over the best in-vocabulary baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the P5 instruction-tuning paradigm for LLM-based recommendation by rethinking how users and items are tokenized. It builds directly on CID's introduction of out-of-vocabulary tokens for item IDs, but departs in two key ways: (1) it assigns OOV tokens to both users and items rather than items alone, and (2) it grounds the token construction in meta-path-based collaborative representations (via skip-gram on U-I-U paths) rather than random or spectral-cluster initialization. The meta-path sampling and skip-gram training are borrowed from metapath2vec, repurposed here to produce the embedding space that K-Means clustering then discretizes into hierarchical OOV tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, interpretable recipe for constructing LLM-compatible IDs that balance memorization (capturing collaborative structure) and diversity (distinguishing items), validated by the introduced MS/DS metrics whose sum correlates with NDCG@10. However, the evaluation is limited to three Amazon subsets with ~12–18K items, the two-level hierarchy is acknowledged as insufficient for trillion-scale systems, and cold-start for new users/items remains unaddressed. A key open question is whether the meta-path + K-Means pipeline scales gracefully or whether the fixed cluster count (G=100–200) becomes a bottleneck as catalog size grows.
