## Problem

Training-free length extrapolation methods for LLMs are severely constrained by memory bottlenecks that prevent 8B models from processing contexts beyond 128K tokens on a single GPU, and they suffer from the attention sink phenomenon where attention concentrates on initial or final tokens. While chunk-based parallel attention offers a path to longer contexts, the unique attention biases that emerge in parallel attention (distinct from classical attention) remain unexplored, and no existing method simultaneously addresses both the memory constraint and the parallel-specific attention distortion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text sequence (up to 128K+ tokens) and a query chunk, splitting the sequence into chunks of the model's native maximum context length (4K for Llama-2-7B, 8K for Llama-3-8B). Each chunk is processed independently through local attention, then the KV caches are compressed via token-level eviction before a global attention pass over the query. The output is autoregressively generated text, with the compressed KV cache fitting within a single A100 80GB GPU (or AMD MI210 64GB), enabling 23.50x prefill acceleration over the uncompressed baseline.

## Method

ParallelComp operates in three stages: (1) parallel local attention over chunks of the model's native context length, (2) a two-level eviction mechanism — chunk-level eviction using self-information scores (negative log-likelihood of the query given each chunk) to retain only the most relevant chunks via a fixed-size priority queue, and token-level parallel KV cache eviction that removes both low-cumulative-attention tokens (R_L) and excessively high-attention tokens (R_H) per head to calibrate the attention distribution, and (3) a global attention pass over the concatenated compressed KV caches for the query. The key insight is that parallel attention exhibits a multi-peak bias pattern (attention sink at chunk boundaries, recency bias, and middle bias) that differs fundamentally from classical attention, and that evicting high-attention tokens in specific layer ranges (recency tokens in layers 9–16, sink tokens in layers 25–32, middle tokens in layers 1–8) effectively calibrates the distribution without performance loss. The paper further provides a theoretical sparsity bound (Theorem 3.1) showing that the number of effective attention entries per row is upper-bounded by w − exp(O(log²(εw)/R²))·δ/(wd), establishing that attention collapse is inherent to parallel attention and motivating the eviction strategy.

## Results

On LongBench (Table 1), ParallelComp-calibration achieves 37.21 average on Llama2-7B-chat-hf (4K context) and 45.21 on Llama3-8B-instruct (8K context), surpassing the FullKV baseline (33.21 and 42.29 respectively) and all other extrapolation methods. On InfiniteBench (Table 2), the method achieves 51.27 average on Llama2-7B and 68.30 on Llama3-8B, reaching 91.17% of GPT-4's performance on ultra-long context tasks (125K–273K tokens) with an 8B model trained on only 8K context. The method also achieves a 23.50× prefill speedup and 1.76× chunk throughput improvement via parallel KV cache compression, while maintaining perplexity stability from 2K to 128K tokens on NarrativeQA (PPL ~5–12 vs. >10² for baselines at 128K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParallelComp-calibration outperforms all training-free extrapolation baselines on LongBench: it beats InfLLM (43.18) by 2.03 points and ChunkLlama (42.47) by 2.74 points on Llama3-8B, and beats InfLLM (32.91) by 4.30 points on Llama2-7B. Position-encoding-based methods (Dynamic-PI, NTK-Aware, AttenCalibration-NTK) consistently underperform chunk-based methods, with NTK-Aware dropping to 41.33 on Llama3-8B due to TREC collapse (50.50 vs. 72.50 for Ours). On InfiniteBench ultra-long tasks (125K–273K), ParallelComp is the only method achieving >90% on all three retrieval tasks (R.PK 100%, R.Num 98.64%, R.KV 22.80%), surpassing GPT-4's 91.17% relative performance and outperforming closed-source Kimi-Chat and Claude-2. Against RAG-based ChatQA-2, ParallelComp achieves 37.74 vs. 36.16 average on LongBench and 86.81 vs. 84.05 on InfiniteBench. On long-context models (LLaMA3.1, Qwen2.5) under a 24K KV cache budget, ParallelComp improves InfiniteBench average from 26.36→59.55 (Llama3.1) and 53.72→70.64 (Qwen2.5), while APE and StarAttention, despite hyperparameter tuning, only surpass ParallelComp by 0.32% on LongBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on chunk-based parallel attention (ChunkLlama, An et al. 2024), which splits long inputs into fixed-size chunks and reuses position encodings for training-free extrapolation. It departs from prior chunk-based methods (InfLLM, ChunkLlama) by introducing a two-stage KV cache eviction mechanism—evicting both low-relevance tokens (for compression) and abnormally high-attention tokens (for calibration)—and by providing the first theoretical analysis of attention sparsity and bias specifically in the parallel attention setting, extending the attention collapse framework of Dong et al. (2021). Unlike position-encoding-based methods (NTK-aware, PI) that modify RoPE, ParallelComp keeps the model architecture unchanged and instead manages memory through selective token eviction, positioning itself as a complementary rather than competing approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: enabling an 8B model trained on only 8K context to handle 128K tokens on a single A100 80GB GPU with a 23.50× prefill speedup addresses a real deployment bottleneck for long-context inference. The theoretical characterization of parallel attention bias (distinct from classical attention sink) and the layer-specific ablation showing that sink, recency, and middle tokens serve different functional roles at different depths provide actionable insights for future KV cache management. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 128K tokens, how it interacts with long-chain reasoning or in-context learning over 100K+ tokens, and whether the manually-set eviction thresholds can be made adaptive.
