## Problem

Self-training pipelines for LLM mathematical reasoning plateau or degrade as more self-generated data is added, because vanilla exploration produces spurious correlations across reasoning steps and fails to assign credit effectively to later stages. The core gap is that the exponentially growing search space over reasoning steps makes it intractable to find training data that isolates errors at specific stages, limiting the scalability of self-generated training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250712/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as a text query and produces a chain-of-thought solution ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. Unusually, the training signal is not computed on full responses but on completions generated from intermediate reasoning states (prefixes of a guidance solution), where each state's value is estimated via Monte Carlo rollouts. The guidance solution itself is a self-generated response selected per query, and the explored data consists of (query, intermediate state, completion, reward) tuples rather than standard (query, full response) pairs.

## Method

MARGE performs hit-guided exploration: for each query, it selects a self-generated 'hit' solution (correct for hard queries with estimated value < 0.5, incorrect for easy ones) and generates completions from every intermediate state of that solution, creating rollouts that share common prefixes and thereby isolating credit assignment to later reasoning steps. State values are estimated on-policy via Monte Carlo simulation (no separate value model), and the policy is updated iteratively using either DPO with correct/incorrect pairs per state or RL with GRPO-style group relative advantages plus a KL penalty. The guidance hit is refreshed each iteration from the latest policy outputs to maintain on-policy data, and the entire loop is repeated over multiple episodes. Theoretical analysis shows this strategy provably increases the number of valid preference pairs and reduces policy gradient variance compared to vanilla exploration.

## Results

On Qwen2-7B-Instruct, MARGE-RL achieves 61.08% pass@1 on MATH (+7.90% over base), 68.20% on MATH500, 88.70% on GSM8k, 35.77% on CollegeMath, and 25.88% on OlympiadBench, outperforming all baselines. The method also improves exploration diversity: MATH500 pass@64 reaches 68.20% vs. 63.58% for the best baseline (REINFORCE++), and the pass@64 over pass@1 gap widens with k, indicating enhanced solution diversity rather than a trade-off. Gains are consistent across four backbone models (Qwen2-7B, MetaMath-Mistral-7B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) and a math-specific model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), where MARGE-RL reaches 84.46% MATH pass@1 and 89.92% MATH500 pass@64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARGE-RL outperforms all vanilla-exploration baselines (SFT, DPO, PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO) on every benchmark for Qwen2-7B-Instruct, with the largest margins on MATH (+1.19% over GRPO) and MATH500 (+5.28% over REINFORCE++). Against externally supervised StepDPO (Lai et al., 2024), MARGE-RL leads by 3.30% on MATH and 4.66% on MATH500, while StepDPO slightly edges MARGE on OlympiadBench (22.91% vs. 25.88% is actually MARGE winning; StepDPO wins on no metric here). On MetaMath-Mistral-7B, MARGE-RL surpasses the MCTS-based MCTS-DPO (Xie et al., 2024) by 2.21% on MATH and 3.90% on MATH500, demonstrating that guided exploration is more effective than tree-search-based exploration without requiring a separate value model. Against concurrent works on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (Table 2), MARGE-RL achieves 84.46% MATH pass@1 and 89.92% MATH500 pass@64, beating ACEMath-7B (Liu et al., 2024) on MATH pass@1 (84.46 vs. 83.13) and MATH500 pass@1 (85.04 vs. 83.42), while PRIME-EURUS-7B (Cui et al., 2025) retains a lead on MATH500 pass@64 (58.96 vs. 49.49) and OlympiadBench pass@64 (51.97 vs. 49.02). Notably, MARGE improves pass@64 by a larger margin than pass@1 across all models, indicating it enhances exploration diversity rather than trading it off for single-shot accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARGE builds on the DPO and GRPO training objectives but introduces a fundamentally different data collection strategy: rather than generating full responses from scratch (vanilla exploration) or relying on external step-level supervision (StepDPO, Setlur et al.) or MCTS-based search (Xie et al., Chen et al.), it continues generation from intermediate states of a self-generated 'hit' solution. This connects conceptually to reverse curriculum learning (Xi et al., 2024) and demonstration-conditioned RL (Salimans & Chen, 2018; Florensa et al., 2017), but applies these ideas to the specific problem of credit assignment across reasoning steps in LLM self-training. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to test-time scaling methods (o1, QwQ, DeepSeek-R1) and complementary to them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies ineffective exploration as the core bottleneck preventing self-generated training data from scaling, and provides both theoretical (variance reduction, pair-counting) and empirical evidence that guided exploration addresses this. The finding that MARGE improves both pass@1 and pass@k simultaneously—rather than trading off diversity for accuracy as typical alignment methods do—is a useful conceptual contribution for the self-training literature. However, gains are moderate (1–4 points on MATH), performance converges after a few iterations, and the method has only been validated on mathematical reasoning, leaving open whether the exploration strategy generalizes to other long-horizon reasoning domains.
