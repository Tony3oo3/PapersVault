## Problem

Non-private scaling laws (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.) prescribe how to allocate compute among model size, dataset size, and iterations, but DP training introduces fundamentally different dynamics—gradient clipping, Gaussian noise, and hard privacy/data budget constraints—that invalidate these prescriptions. The paper asks: given a fixed compute budget, privacy budget (ε), and data budget (N), how should compute be optimally split among model size, batch size, and training iterations to minimize cross-entropy loss?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251745/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three scalar budgets as input: total FLOPs (C), privacy parameter ε (at fixed δ=10⁻⁸), and number of data individuals (N). It outputs the optimal training configuration (model size M, batch size B, iterations T) and the predicted cross-entropy loss for that configuration. Unusually, the output is not a single trained model but a continuous mapping over the budget space, obtained by enumerating all constant-compute (M, B, T) triples, converting each to a noise-batch ratio via privacy accounting, and querying a pre-fitted loss surface. The fitted surface is defined over M ∈ [4.5M, 778M], T up to 128K, and σ̄ ∈ [0.5²³, 0.5⁶].

## Method

The paper trains BERT models (4.5M–778M parameters) with DP-Adam at a fixed physical batch size of 1024, sweeping 18 noise-batch ratios, 6 model sizes, and up to 128K iterations on the standard BERT pre-training corpus. The key methodological insight is decoupling noise calibration from batch size: since Gaussian noise dominates minibatch sampling variance, the loss is modeled as a function of the noise-batch ratio σ̄ rather than the privacy budget directly. A semi-parametric fitting pipeline is then applied: rolling-average smoothing, isotonic regression to enforce monotonicity in T and σ̄, parametric extrapolation of training curves (L = E + A/T^α), and trilinear interpolation in log-space over (M, T, σ̄). For any query (C, ε, N), the method enumerates constant-compute configurations, computes σ̄ via the dp_accounting library, and queries the interpolated surface to predict cross-entropy, enabling optimal allocation without additional training runs.

## Results

The paper establishes that under DP, the compute-optimal model size is at least an order of magnitude smaller than non-private scaling laws predict: at 10^22 FLOPs, ~10^8 parameters are optimal under DP versus ~10^10 non-privately. The token-to-model ratio under DP is typically 1,000–100,000 (versus the 20× recommended by Chinchilla scaling laws), and increasing compute beyond a 'critical compute budget' yields negligible loss reduction unless the privacy or data budget is also increased. Compute-optimal DP training configurations save 5× to 100× compute compared to natural baselines while retaining comparable privacy and utility. For example, with N=10^8 and ε=1, the best cross-entropy (2.26) is achieved at ~5.2×10^21 FLOPs with a 130M-parameter model, 58K iterations, and batch size 220K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its compute-optimal DP training configurations against three natural baselines, all at 10^19 FLOPs with N=10^7: (1) BertLarge (335M params, 7500 steps, batch 1295) predicted by non-private Chinchilla scaling laws, (2) BertMedium (41M params, 5000 steps, batch 15,879), and (3) BertTiny (4.5M params, 2500 steps, batch 283,061) reflecting a DP expert's preference for large batch sizes. The non-private scaling law prediction (BertLarge) yields very low utility across most privacy budgets, confirming that private scaling laws are fundamentally distinct from non-private ones. The compute-optimal configuration with only 2×10^18 FLOPs achieves similar cross-entropy to the best baseline models using 5× the FLOPs across the reasonable range of privacy budgets. In the most extreme comparison, compute savings over the BertLarge baseline exceed 100×. The paper also shows that the optimal training configuration shifts substantially with the privacy budget, meaning that naively fixing a single configuration across all ε values (as is common in the DP literature) leaves significant utility on the table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-private scaling law framework of Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022) to the differentially private setting by introducing privacy budget (ε) and data budget (N) as additional axes alongside compute budget. It builds on DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and the observation that the noise-batch ratio dominates gradient variance (Ponomareva et al., 2023), using it as the key intermediate variable to decouple privacy accounting from empirical training. The closest prior work is Sander et al. (2023), who studied DP scaling for vision tasks but did not account for a bounded compute budget or model the full compute-privacy-utility trade-off for language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic framework for allocating compute under DP constraints, showing that non-private scaling law predictions are far from optimal (optimal models are ~10× smaller, token-to-model ratios are 1000–100,000× vs. 20×) and that compute budgets saturate under DP—a qualitative departure from non-private scaling. Practical impact is significant: compute-optimal configurations save 5×–100× FLOPs over natural baselines. Key limitations include restriction to BERT pre-training with fixed sequence length and a single physical batch size, and open questions include whether findings transfer to fine-tuning, decoder-only architectures, larger model scales, and better DP mechanisms.
