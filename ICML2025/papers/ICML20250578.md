## Problem

While normalization layers in deep networks account for the mean and variance of activations, no prior work has prescribed what distribution these activations should take or provided a mechanism to achieve it. The normal distribution is uniquely advantageous from an information-theoretic standpoint—it maximizes mutual information as a signal, is the worst-case noise distribution, and equates uncorrelatedness with independence—yet no normalization technique explicitly encourages normality in feature representations.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in replacement for existing normalization layers, consuming the pre-activation values of a unit (a vector of N values per channel per minibatch) after the standard mean/variance normalization step. It produces gaussianized, noise-perturbed activations that feed into the conventional affine transformation (γ, β), introducing no additional learnable parameters. At test time, running averages of μ, σ², and λ (analogous to batch normalization) replace the minibatch statistics.

## Method

Normality normalization inserts two steps between the standard normalization and affine transformation: (1) a Yeo-Johnson power transform that gaussianizes the normalized activations, with the transform parameter λ estimated in closed form via one step of Newton-Raphson on a quadratic series expansion of the negative log-likelihood centered at λ₀=1 (the identity transform), and (2) additive Gaussian noise scaled per-channel by the L1 norm of the zero-centered post-transform values. The power transform is applied after normalization so that μ=0 and σ²=1 simplify the λ estimation, and the noise is treated as a constant during backpropagation so it regularizes without directly affecting gradients. The approach is motivated by the mutual information game, which shows the normal distribution is simultaneously the optimal signal and worst-case noise encoding, and is validated across ResNet, WideResNet, and Vision Transformer architectures on eight image classification benchmarks.

## Results

On a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, LayerNormalNorm (LNN) consistently outperforms LayerNorm (LN) across all six evaluated datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (+3.72 pp, 70.12% vs 66.40%) and Food-101 (+5.86 pp, 79.11% vs 73.25%). On ImageNet, LNN achieves 75.25% top-1 accuracy versus 71.54% for LN (+3.71 pp). For ResNet architectures, BatchNormalNorm (BNN) similarly outperforms BatchNorm (BN) across all seven dataset/model combinations, with the largest improvement on STL-10 (ResNet34: 63.86% vs 58.82%, +5.04 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerNormalNorm outperforms LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016) on all six ViT datasets, with gains ranging from +1.17 pp (SVHN) to +5.86 pp (Food-101). BatchNormalNorm outperforms BatchNorm (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) on all seven ResNet/WideResNet dataset combinations, with the largest margin on STL-10 (+5.04 pp, ResNet34). The method also extends to InstanceNorm (Ulyanov et al., 2016) and GroupNorm (Wu & He, 2018), where InstanceNormalNorm and GroupNormalNorm both outperform their respective baselines on STL-10 (ResNet34, Figure 1). Decorrelated BatchNormalNorm outperforms Decorrelated BatchNorm (Huang et al., 2018) on CIFAR-10 (+0.84 pp), CIFAR-100 (+2.42 pp), and STL-10 (+2.60 pp). Finally, the proposed additive Gaussian noise with scaling outperforms Gaussian dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014) on STL-10 (ResNet34, Figure 7), attributed to the additive (rather than multiplicative) nature of the noise preventing it from corrupting backpropagated gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the family of normalization layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm, GroupNorm) by inserting a Yeo-Johnson power transform and scaled additive Gaussian noise between the standardization and affine transformation steps, motivated by information-theoretic properties of the normal distribution (maximum entropy, mutual information game, uncorrelatedness-implying-independence). While prior normalization layers account for first and second moments of activations, this work provides the first explicit prescription for the *shape* of the activation distribution and a mechanism to enforce it. The theoretical grounding draws on the Gaussian channel framework (Cover & Thomas, 2006) and the power transform literature (Box & Cox, 1964; Yeo & Johnson, 2000), while the noise component connects to regularization via noise (Bishop, 1995; Srivastava et al., 2014) and noise-robustness-as-generalization (Arora et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, information-theoretic justification for why normality specifically (beyond zero-mean/unit-variance) is the desirable activation distribution, and demonstrates that enforcing it via a parameter-free power transform yields consistent 1–5% accuracy gains across CNNs and Vision Transformers, all normalization types, and a range of model widths, depths, and batch sizes. The noise-robustness results (Table 4) showing substantially lower relative error propagation for normality-normalized models substantiate the theoretical motivation. Open questions include the runtime overhead (noted in Appendix A.2), whether the Gaussian-process connection at finite width can be formalized, whether gaussianizing weights (e.g., augmenting weight normalization) yields further gains, and how this interacts with adversarial robustness beyond the distributional sense.
