## Problem

Existing PEFT methods such as SSF apply layer-wise scaling vectors that struggle to effectively shift the pre-trained parameter space under large domain gaps, as the one-dimensional vectors tend to stretch excessively and limit expressiveness. While the low-rank shifting property observed in PEFT closely mirrors Neural Engram and Long-Term Potentiation/Depression (LTP/D) phenomena in biological neural networks, no prior method explicitly exploits the cross-layer propagation of scaling information that characterizes these biological mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250463/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SAN is a plug-and-play module operating on internal feature representations without altering input or output formats: it accepts RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ViT) and outputs class labels for vision tasks, text prompts and generates text answers for language tasks, and image-text pairs producing text responses for visual-language tasks. The method introduces zero additional trainable parameters beyond those of the base PEFT method (SSF, LoRA, or DoRA), maintaining identical parameter budgets while modifying how frozen pre-trained weights are reparameterized during fine-tuning.

## Method

SAN computes a scaling vector γ_l at each layer by taking the element-wise ratio between the PEFT-adjusted output y'_l and the original pre-trained output y_l, then explicitly propagates this vector to scale the frozen pre-trained weights of the subsequent layer (W'_{l+1} = γ_l ⊙ W_{l+1}). This mechanism is inspired by LTP/D in biological neural networks, where frequent activation of anterior neurons modulates posterior synapse development. The explicit propagation overcomes SSF's strong assumption that all rows within a layer share the same scaling factor, achieving a unique adjustment per parameter without extra trainable cost. When scaling vectors propagate through multiple layers, their quadratic influence (γ_l²) acts as an implicit regularization that discourages extreme deviations from the initial value of 1, promoting training stability. SAN is compatible with both feature-scaling methods (SSF) and low-rank adaptation methods (LoRA, DoRA), and supports either locational proximity (next layer in the same block) or functional proximity (corresponding layer in the next block) propagation strategies.

## Results

On vision tasks (25 datasets) with ViT-B, SAN+SSF achieves 91.6% mean accuracy on FGVC, 77.7% on VTAB-1k, and 83.8% on ImageNet-1k, outperforming FFT by up to 8.7% (VTAB-1k) and LoRA-16 by up to 6.8% (FGVC). On commonsense reasoning (8 datasets) with LLaMA3-8B, SAN+LoRA reaches 85.4% mean accuracy, surpassing ChatGPT (CoT) by 8.4% and LoRA-32 by 4.6%. On visual-language tasks (7 datasets) with LLaVA1.5-7B, SAN+DoRA achieves 68.9% mean accuracy, exceeding FFT by 2.4% and even outperforming the larger LLaVA1.5-13B with FFT (67.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAN consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines across all three task domains without adding trainable parameters beyond the base method. Against SSF (its closest vision baseline), SAN+SSF improves FGVC by +0.9% (91.6 vs. 90.7) and VTAB-1k by +1.6% (77.7 vs. 76.1), with the largest single-dataset gain being +6.5% on CLEVR-Count. Against LoRA, SAN+LoRA achieves +3.2% on LLaMA-7B (78.0 vs. 74.7) and +4.6% on LLaMA3-8B (85.4 vs. 80.8), while matching or slightly exceeding DoRA-32 on most language models (e.g., +0.8% on LLaMA-7B, +0.3% on LLaMA3-8B). Against FFT, SAN+SSF surpasses full fine-tuning by 8.7% on VTAB-1k (77.7 vs. 69.0) and 3.1% on FGVC (91.6 vs. 88.5), and SAN+DoRA exceeds FFT by 2.4% on LLaVA1.5-7B (68.9 vs. 66.5), even beating the larger 13B FFT model (67.8%). Against ChatGPT (CoT) on commonsense reasoning, SAN+LoRA with LLaMA3-8B surpasses it by 8.4% (85.4 vs. 77.0), while LLaMA-7B SAN+LoRA matches ChatGPT (78.0 vs. 77.0). The only notable exception is CLEVR-Distance, where SAN+SSF slightly underperforms SSF (61.4 vs. 62.3, −0.9%), suggesting minor instability on certain structured prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAN extends the PEFT lineage from SSF (feature scaling) and LoRA (low-rank adaptation) by introducing explicit cross-layer propagation of scaling vectors, drawing a direct analogy to Long-Term Potentiation/Depression in biological neural networks. While SSF and DoRA independently identified the low-rank nature of parameter shifts, SAN goes further by arguing that scaling components learned in anterior layers should be explicitly reapplied to posterior weights, rather than left to propagate implicitly through the network. This positions SAN as a plug-and-play wrapper compatible with existing PEFT methods (SSF, LoRA, DoRA) rather than a standalone alternative, and grounds the design in the Neural Engram hypothesis that the brain reuses existing synaptic pathways for new knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-regulation property (quadratic scaling effect acting as implicit regularization) and the plug-and-play compatibility with multiple PEFT backends make SAN a practical, low-cost enhancement that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. The neuroscience framing, while primarily conceptual, opens a productive research direction: if PEFT's parameter shifts genuinely mirror engram formation, then mechanisms from BNNs (e.g., heterosynaptic plasticity, engram interference) could inform continual and multitask learning in ANNs. Open questions include whether the near-linearity assumption for propagation holds in very deep or highly non-linear architectures, and whether the moderate gains (0.3–4.6%) scale further with model size or task difficulty.
