## Problem

Diffusion models frequently fail at compositional generation, producing images that miss objects or exhibit attribute inconsistencies when prompts contain multiple conditions. Existing remedies either require training/finetuning the model or rely on external verifier models, leaving a gap for a training-free, model-agnostic criterion that can verify per-condition satisfaction using only the diffusion model itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252920/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a generated sample (image or 2D point) together with a set of individual conditions {c_i} and a composed prompt c_compose, and produces a binary accept/reject decision per sample. For text-to-image, the image is encoded to a latent z of shape (m,m) via a VAE encoder, and the lift score is computed per latent pixel before being aggregated by counting activated pixels above a threshold τ=250. The overall pipeline generates a batch of candidates (e.g., 5 per prompt) and filters them, returning only samples that satisfy all compositional conditions.

## Method

CompLift approximates the lift score lift(x|c) = log p(x|c)/p(x) via the ELBO difference: E_{t,ε}[||ε − ε_θ(x_t,∅)||² − ||ε − ε_θ(x_t,c)||²], requiring only the original diffusion model's noise predictions with no extra training. For compositional prompts, individual lift scores are combined through a Compose function that encodes logical algebra (AND requires all positive, OR requires at least one positive, NOT requires non-positive). For text-to-image, the sampled noise ε is replaced by ε_θ(z_t, c_compose) to cancel model bias and reduce estimation variance, and the per-pixel lift is aggregated by counting activated latent pixels rather than averaging, making object detection more robust. A caching variant reuses the unconditional and per-condition predictions already computed during classifier-free-guided generation, reducing extra forward passes from (n+1)·T to zero under Composable Diffusion.

## Results

On the Attend-and-Excite text-to-image benchmark, CompLift (T=200) boosts SD 1.4 from CLIP 0.310/IR −0.191 to 0.322/0.293 on animal pairs, and SD 2.1 from 0.330/0.532 to 0.340/0.975, making each model comparable to the next generation (e.g., SD 2.1+CompLift matches vanilla SDXL on animal combinations). On 2D synthetic compositional generation, CompLift (T=1000) raises product accuracy from 56.5% (Composable Diffusion) to 99.9%, mixture from 70.7% to 100.0%, and negation from 7.9% to 78.7%. On CLEVR Position with 5-object compositions, accuracy improves from 78.7% to 90.3% while FID remains comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Composable Diffusion (Liu et al., 2022), CompLift delivers the largest gains: on 2D synthetic data, product accuracy jumps from 56.5% to 99.9% and negation from 7.9% to 78.7%, while on CLEVR 5-object compositions accuracy rises from 78.7% to 90.3%. Against EBM/MCMC baselines (Du et al., 2023), CompLift outperforms all four MCMC variants (ULA, U-HMC, MALA, HMC) on 2D synthetic accuracy and Chamfer Distance across all three algebras, and on CLEVR it achieves 90.3% vs. 11.0% (U-HMC) and 19.0% (ULA) for 5-object compositions. Against vanilla Stable Diffusion 1.4/2.1/XL (Rombach et al., 2022; Podell et al., 2023), CompLift consistently improves both CLIP and ImageReward scores, with the notable effect that SD 1.4+CompLift matches or exceeds vanilla SD 2.1 on object-animal and object combinations, and SD 2.1+CompLift matches vanilla SDXL on animal combinations. Against Attend-and-Excite (Chefer et al., 2023), CompLift adds further gains on top (e.g., A&E SD2.1 IR 1.225 → 1.337 with CompLift on animals), confirming it is complementary to attention-based guidance. The cached variant achieves ~30% of the perfect best-of-n selector's gain at near-zero extra latency, while the vanilla T=200 variant reaches ~40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompLift builds directly on the Diffusion Classifier insight (Li et al., 2023) that a pretrained diffusion model's ELBO can approximate a classifier's log-likelihood without additional training, and on Composable Diffusion (Liu et al., 2022) which provides per-condition denoising predictions during compositional generation. It departs from prior resampling methods (Karthik et al., 2023; Qi et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2025) that rely on external quality-assessment models, instead using the diffusion model's own denoising predictions as a self-contained acceptance criterion. The paper also positions itself against CFG/Composable Diffusion by framing CompLift as solving the primal constrained optimization directly (accept/reject) rather than the dual soft-regularization that CFG implicitly performs, and distinguishes itself from CAS (Hong et al., 2024) by decomposing a composed prompt into per-condition lift scores rather than scoring the whole prompt monolithically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CompLift offers a simple, training-free, model-agnostic rejection criterion that can be plugged into any existing compositional diffusion pipeline with near-zero additional compute (via caching), making it practically attractive for practitioners who want to improve condition alignment without retraining or adding external verifiers. The strong 2D synthetic results (near-perfect accuracy) and the demonstration that SD 1.4 + CompLift approaches SD 2.1 on some text-to-image categories suggest the criterion captures a real signal about condition satisfaction. However, limitations remain: performance is bounded by the underlying generator's quality, the criterion is weak on fine-grained attributes like color, and OR/NOT algebras for text-to-image lack systematic evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video/music generation and whether more sophisticated per-pixel criteria (e.g., leveraging segmentation models) could close the remaining gap on attribute-level composition.
