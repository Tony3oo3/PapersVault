## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics foundation models either ignore spatial coordinates entirely (Nicheformer) or encode them only through simple sinusoidal position embeddings (CellPLM), failing to capture the multi-scale biological information inherent in ST data. ST slices simultaneously encode macro-scale tissue morphology, micro-scale cell-cell interactions, and gene-scale expression profiles, and no prior model integrates all three scales with an architecture and self-supervised objectives designed for this purpose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ST slice as a 2D point cloud of N cells, each with an n-dimensional gene expression vector and a 2D coordinate. Before model input, the slice is partitioned into sub-slices of ~1,000 cells by spatial proximity, and ~50 virtual cells (cluster centroids from Leiden clustering over combined PCA-2 embeddings and positions) are injected into every sub-slice to carry macro-scale context. The model outputs SE(2)-invariant cell representations (invariant to 2D translation and rotation) and pairwise representations, which serve as transferable features for downstream heads.

## Method

SToFM combines a domain-adapted Geneformer cell encoder (fine-tuned on ST data via masked gene modeling plus contrastive learning) with an SE(2) Transformer that consumes a distance matrix as spatial input. Multi-scale integration is achieved by dividing each slice into ~1,000-cell sub-slices (micro-scale) and injecting Leiden-cluster virtual cells into every sub-slice (macro-scale), while the Geneformer embeddings carry gene-scale information. The SE(2) Transformer uses a Gaussian module to project the distance matrix into initial pair representations, which then act as attention bias in each layer, enabling SE(2)-invariant modeling of intercellular interactions. Pretraining uses two self-supervised objectives: Masked Cell Modeling (reconstruct 10% of masked cell embeddings) and Pairwise Distance Recovery (reconstruct unperturbed distances for 10% of cells with Gaussian-perturbed coordinates), with a staged strategy that freezes the cell encoder before jointly training both components.

## Results

On cross-slice tissue region semantic segmentation, SToFM achieves a macro F1 of 0.4588 on human embryo (vs. 0.3985 for the best baseline CellPLM) and 0.6437 on DLPFC (vs. 0.5953 for CellPLM), representing an average 11.6% relative improvement over the strongest baseline. On cell type annotation, SToFM reaches macro F1 of 0.4951 on Brain1 (25→21 classes, Stereo-seq) and 0.8362 on Brain2 (8→6 classes, SeekSpace), surpassing the best baseline by 14.34% on average. SToFM also leads on spatial deconvolution (Pearson 0.7546 cross-slice on liver) and gene imputation (Pearson 0.4877 cross-slice on skin), with the largest margins appearing in cross-slice transfer settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SToFM outperforms all four baselines across every downstream task. CellPLM, the only prior ST foundation model that jointly encodes gene expression and spatial coordinates, is the strongest baseline but is consistently outperformed: by 3.2–4.1 points in average F1 on embryo/DLPFC segmentation and by 7.7–9.9 points in cross-slice F1. Nicheformer, which pretrains on ST gene expressions without spatial information, underperforms even the non-spatial scGPT and Geneformer on several tasks (e.g., DLPFC average F1 of 0.5238 vs. 0.6178 for scGPT), suggesting that ST-specific pretraining without spatial modeling can be detrimental. Geneformer and scGPT, both single-cell-only models, perform competitively on same-slice evaluation but degrade sharply in cross-slice transfer (e.g., embryo cross-slice F1 drops to 0.37–0.39), highlighting their inability to capture spatially transferable tissue morphology. The largest SToFM gains appear in cross-slice settings, where macro-scale tissue structure and micro-scale cell-cell interaction patterns provide transferable signals that gene-expression-only models lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

SToFM extends the single-cell foundation model paradigm (Geneformer, scGPT) to spatial transcriptomics by adding explicit multi-scale spatial modeling. It departs from prior ST foundation models—Nicheformer (which ignores spatial coordinates entirely) and CellPLM (which uses only sinusoidal position embeddings)—by structuring spatial information into three distinct scales: gene-scale via domain-adapted Geneformer embeddings, micro-scale via sub-slice division and an SE(2) Transformer (borrowed from Uni-Mol), and macro-scale via Leiden-clustered virtual cells inspired by multi-scale GNN/CV approaches. The pretraining objectives (masked cell modeling and pairwise distance recovery) are novel combinations that jointly supervise both transcriptomic and spatial reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SToFM and its 88M-cell, 6-technology corpus (SToCorpus-88M) provide the field with the first large-scale, multi-scale ST pretraining resource, enabling transferable representations across sequencing platforms and tissue types. The notably larger cross-slice performance gap versus baselines suggests that macro-scale morphology and micro-scale interaction patterns are key transferable signals that prior models missed. Open questions include whether additional scales (e.g., image pyramid, sub-cellular), causal gene regulatory modeling, or multi-modal integration (pathology images, ligand-receptor pairs) can further improve representations, and whether the SE(2) invariance assumption holds for all tissue geometries.
