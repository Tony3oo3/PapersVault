## Problem

Robust versions of classical machine learning problems (regression, PCA, clustering) that discard the m largest loss values lack scalable algorithms; existing methods have high-order polynomial running times. The paper asks: under what conditions on the underlying loss function can one efficiently construct a small strong ε-coreset for the robust objective F^(m) that approximates the loss for every candidate solution x?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251041/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a finite weighted set of non-negative functions F (equivalently, a data matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} and response vector b ∈ R^n for regression), along with approximation parameter ε and outlier count m. It outputs a weighted multi-subset of F of size Õ(mT/ε) + S (where T is the total sensitivity and S is the vanilla coreset size) that guarantees (1±ε)-approximation of the robust loss L^(t) for all t ≤ m and all x. For downstream applications, the coreset is further processed via random diagonal masking and row duplication to yield an approximate regression solution x̃ or PCA solution Û with (1+14ε) approximation guarantee.

## Method

The construction is a two-stage algorithm. In the first stage, it identifies 'contributing' functions (those with f(x) ≥ (ε/m)·L^(m)(A;x) for some x) by repeatedly sampling each function with probability 1/m and retaining those whose sensitivity within the sample exceeds ε/4; after Θ(m log(Tm/ε)) rounds, all contributing functions are captured with constant probability, yielding a set S of size O(Tm/ε · log(Tm/ε)). In the second stage, a vanilla ε-coreset D is computed for F\S, and a sensitivity-flattening refinement splits each function f in D into ⌈(m/ε)·σ_D(f)⌉ copies with proportionally reduced weights, ensuring that removing any m copies incurs at most O(ε) relative error. The key insight is that two mild conditions—existence of a small vanilla coreset and bounded total sensitivity—are sufficient for a near-tight robust coreset, with the bound shown to be Ω(md/ε)-tight for ℓ₂ subspace embeddings.

## Results

On the Appliances Energy Prediction dataset (19,735×28), the proposed coreset achieves a mean relative error of 0.01–0.02 on the robust regression objective using only 7.1%–10.6% of the full data, with runtimes of 1.5–1.9 s versus 13.98 s for the full dataset. On the Gas Turbine Emission dataset (36,733×11), the mean relative error drops from 0.02 to 0.01 as the coreset size grows from 2% to 2.7% of the data, with runtimes of 0.28–0.40 s versus 7.54 s. For subspace embedding verification (m=10, ε=0.25), about 10% of rows on Energy and 5% on Emission yield a distortion parameter ≤0.15 and ≤0.1 respectively, substantially below the preset ε=0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical baseline is uniform sampling, compared on both subspace embedding distortion and robust regression objective approximation. On the Energy dataset, the proposed sensitivity-based coreset achieves a mean distortion 40%–50% lower than uniform sampling at the same coreset size, with roughly one standard deviation smaller variance. For robust regression on Energy, the coreset yields a mean relative error of 0.01–0.02 on the objective function, outperforming uniform sampling by at least one standard deviation. On the Emission dataset, the coreset again shows a mean distortion about one standard deviation below uniform sampling, and the objective-function relative error (0.01–0.02) is consistently lower than uniform sampling. In terms of runtime, the coreset approach reduces solving time by roughly 7–25× compared to running FastLTS on the full dataset, while maintaining comparable or better solution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the robust coreset paradigm from clustering-specific settings (Huang et al., ICLR 2023; Feldman & Schulman, SODA 2012) to a universal framework applicable to any loss function with bounded total sensitivity. It builds directly on the sensitivity sampling machinery of Feldman & Langberg (STOC 2011), showing that the two conditions already required for vanilla coresets—bounded total sensitivity and existence of a small vanilla coreset—are also sufficient for robust coresets. The key departure is the two-stage construction (identifying 'contributing' functions via repeated 1/m-sampling, then refining a vanilla coreset via sensitivity-based splitting), which had no analogue in prior robust coreset work that was either exponential in size or restricted to bi-criteria guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-tight coreset size bound for ℓ₂ subspace embeddings (Õ(md/ε) + Q, with a matching Ω(md/ε) lower bound) settles a natural open question about the cost of robustness in sparsification. The sensitivity flattening technique yields the first near-linear-time algorithms for robust regression and robust PCA with m outliers, improving on the n^{O(d²)} runtime of Simonov et al. (2019) for fixed m. However, the exponential dependence on m/ε in the final optimization step limits practicality for large outlier budgets, and the empirical validation is confined to two small UCI datasets (≤37K rows) with a heuristic solver (FastLTS), leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees translate to large-scale or high-dimensional settings.
