## Problem

Existing time series XAI evaluation metrics remove top-K points simultaneously, causing positive and negative attributions to cancel out and unfairly penalizing directional methods like Integrated Gradients. Additionally, naively applying IG to time series data ignores temporal dependencies and introduces out-of-distribution samples along the zero-baseline integration path, leading to unreliable attributions.

## Input / Output

TIMING takes a multivariate time series sample x ∈ R^{T×D} and a pre-trained classifier F: R^{T×D} → [0,1]^C, and produces a signed T×D attribution matrix where each entry quantifies the directional contribution of that time-step/feature to the predicted class probability. The method is entirely post-hoc (no training or fine-tuning of the classifier is required), and the signed nature of the output distinguishes features that reinforce versus suppress the model's prediction, unlike unsigned baselines that only report magnitude.

## Method

TIMING extends Integrated Gradients by replacing the zero baseline with a partially masked baseline (1−M)⊙x, where M is a binary mask that retains certain segments of the original signal. Rather than masking individual points randomly, TIMING selects n contiguous segments of lengths in [s_min, s_max] to preserve temporal structure, mitigating OOD issues and capturing effects when temporal relationships are disrupted. A key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) shows that the randomness over masks can be folded into the IG integration path itself, so a single pass with per-step random segment unmasking approximates the full expectation without repeated IG computations. TIMING retains the sensitivity and implementation-invariance axioms of IG but explicitly sacrifices completeness, trading the guarantee that attributions sum to the output difference for a richer, multi-baseline-context interpretation of feature contributions.

## Results

On MIMIC-III mortality prediction, TIMING achieves the best Cumulative Prediction Difference (CPD) scores of 0.366 (K=50) and 0.505 (K=100), outperforming IG (0.342/0.469) and GradSHAP (0.327/0.447). Across six real-world datasets (Table 3), TIMING consistently leads on zero-substitution CPD, with particularly large gains on Boiler (+109.8% relative over IG) and Wafer (+34.8%). On standard metrics (Acc, CE, Suff, Comp), masking-based methods like Extrmask and ContraLSP retain higher accuracy after 20% masking, but their CPD scores are dramatically lower (e.g., ContraLSP: 0.013 vs. TIMING: 0.366), revealing that they fail to capture directional feature contributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMING outperforms all 13 baselines on the proposed CPD metric across all datasets. The closest competitor is vanilla IG (0.342 vs. 0.366 CPD at K=50 on MIMIC-III), with GradSHAP a further step behind (0.327). Masking-based methods that dominate standard metrics—Extrmask (Acc 0.932) and ContraLSP (Acc 0.921)—score dramatically lower on CPD (0.204 and 0.013 respectively), indicating they assign near-zero importance to negatively contributing features. On the Boiler dataset, TIMING achieves a 109.8% relative improvement in zero-substitution CPD over IG (1.578 vs. 0.752), the largest gap observed. Among time-series-specific baselines, WinIT, Dynamask, FIT, TimeX, and TimeX++ all score below 0.10 CPD on MIMIC-III, far trailing both IG and TIMING. TIMING also achieves the best Sufficiency score (0.242×10²) among all methods, confirming its attributions retain the most predictive information when only top-ranked features are kept.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIMING extends Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) by replacing the fixed zero baseline with temporality-aware stochastic segment masking, addressing two specific failures of naive IG on time series: inability to capture disrupted temporal dependencies and out-of-distribution intermediate points along the integration path. Conceptually, the paper departs from the recent wave of time series XAI methods (ContraLSP, TimeX++, Extrmask, Dynamask) that adopt unsigned attribution schemes, arguing instead that directional (signed) attribution is more faithful and has been systematically undervalued by existing evaluation metrics. The proposed CPD and CPP metrics represent a methodological departure from the standard simultaneous-masking evaluation protocol (Acc, CE, Suff, Comp) used in prior time series XAI benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 2017 gradient-based method (IG) outperforms recent SOTA time series XAI methods under corrected evaluation challenges the field's implicit assumption that more complex, learned perturbation strategies are necessary for faithful attribution. The CPD/CPP metrics offer a more principled evaluation framework that could be adopted broadly, though their practical adoption depends on the community accepting the critique of existing metrics. A key limitation is that TIMING sacrifices the completeness axiom of IG, and the performance gain over plain IG is modest (e.g., 0.366 vs. 0.342 CPD on MIMIC-III), suggesting the segment-based masking provides refinement rather than a qualitative leap. Open questions include whether the metrics generalize to multivariate forecasting tasks and whether the incompleteness trade-off is acceptable in safety-critical deployment settings.
