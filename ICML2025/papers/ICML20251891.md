## Problem

Transformers produce strong semantic representations but require massive data and compute, making them impractical for low-resource languages. Structured recursive models like SELF-STRAE offer data-efficient learning through explicit compositional semantics but still underperform large pre-trained transformers. The paper asks whether a structured model can close this performance gap while retaining its extreme parameter efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251891/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

BANYAN consumes a batch of tokenised sentences (each a sequence of subword tokens from a BPE vocabulary) and produces 256-dimensional sentence and word embeddings alongside a learned entangled tree graph. The entangled tree is the key structural output: identical constituent spans across all sentences in the batch are collapsed into single shared nodes, so the graph grows sub-linearly with batch size. The model contains only 14 non-embedding parameters (K=128 channels, U=2), making inference feasible on CPU.

## Method

BANYAN introduces two changes over SELF-STRAE. First, it replaces per-sentence binary trees with entangled trees: a global frontier spans all leaf nodes across the batch, and greedy agglomerative merging (by cosine similarity) assigns a unique identity to each constituent span so that repeated spans map to a single graph node. Downward embeddings for each node are then averaged over all contextual instances, explicitly amortising meaning across contexts and eliminating false negatives in the contrastive objective. Second, it replaces the linear composition and decomposition functions with diagonalised (element-wise sigmoid-gated) functions, reducing parameters by a factor of U and enforcing a hierarchical decay that respects the tree's compression order. Training uses cross-entropy over reconstructed leaf tokens rather than the prior contrastive loss, which the authors show works well because the diagonal functions constrain the representation space to support effective amortisation without needing explicit contrastive pressure.

## Results

On English sentence-level STS (Spearman's ρ × 100), BANYAN achieves an average score of 62.97, outperforming ROBERTA+SimCSE (59.02), Sent2Vec (53.28), and its predecessor SELF-STRAE (46.59) by 3.9, 9.7, and 16.4 points respectively, using only 14 non-embedding parameters versus ~10M for ROBERTa-medium. On retrieval (BEIR), BANYAN leads on Quora NDCG@1 (57.74 vs. 43.26 for ROBERTA+SimCSE) and Arguana NDCG@10 (28.28 vs. 17.60). In multilingual evaluation across 9 languages, BANYAN scores 60.01, surpassing Paraphrase XLM-R (59.16), fine-tuned XLM-R (55.84), and Llama-3.1 8B (53.06) while being trained on only ~10M tokens per language.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BANYAN outperforms all baselines on the aggregate English STS score (62.97), beating the strongest transformer baseline ROBERTA+SimCSE (59.02) by ~4 points despite having 14 non-embedding parameters versus ~10M. Against its direct predecessor SELF-STRAE (46.59), BANYAN gains 16.4 points, with the largest per-task improvements on STS-13 (+15.7) and STS-14 (+25.5). On word-level tasks, BANYAN (48.53) trails Sent2Vec (50.56) but surpasses GLOVE (47.00) and ROBERTA (45.73), with a notable strength on WordSim-R (67.73 vs. 54.49 for Sent2Vec). In retrieval, BANYAN dominates Quora (NDCG@1: 57.74 vs. 43.26 for ROBERTA+SimCSE) and Arguana (NDCG@10: 28.28 vs. 17.60), though it slightly underperforms on MRPC F1 (79.48 vs. 80.83). In the multilingual setting, BANYAN (60.01) edges out Paraphrase XLM-R (59.16) and substantially outperforms fine-tuned XLM-R (55.84) and Llama-3.1 8B (53.06), with particularly large margins on Hausa (49.68 vs. 14.68 for XLM-R-FT) and Amharic (66.40 vs. 59.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

Banyan is a direct successor to SELF-STRAE (Opper et al., 2023b), a self-structuring autoencoder that learns compositional semantics through greedy agglomerative merging of token embeddings. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing disjoint per-sentence trees with a globally entangled graph that shares constituent nodes across contexts, and (2) replacing the linear composition/decomposition layers with diagonalised (sigmoid-gated element-wise) functions borrowed from the fast-weights literature (Ba et al., 2016). This places Banyan within the broader recursive neural network lineage (Socher et al., 2011; Tree-LSTMs) while explicitly positioning against both static embeddings (GLOVE, Sent2Vec) and transformer-based encoders (RoBERTa, XLM-R) as the dominant approaches to semantic representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is striking: a model with 14 non-embedding parameters, trainable in under 50 minutes on a single GPU for roughly 30 cents, matches or exceeds RoBERTa+SimCSE on English STS and outperforms XLM-R and Llama-3.1 8B on several low-resource languages (Hausa, Amharic, Telugu) using only ~10M tokens per language. This offers a genuinely viable alternative for communities and languages where transformer-scale pre-training is infeasible. However, the structure induction remains a greedy heuristic (cosine-similarity-based agglomerative clustering), which the authors acknowledge is sub-optimal; learning the merge procedure end-to-end is an open direction. The approach is also limited to embedding tasks and has not been tested for generation or downstream fine-tuning at scale.
