## Problem

Existing continual learning methods protect old-task knowledge by constraining or isolating specific parameters, but this approach scales poorly as the number of tasks grows and fragments the network into non-communicating subnetworks. The paper argues that what truly preserves old-task knowledge is not individual parameters but the activation pathways through the network, and that pathway-level protection can be achieved without isolating tasks from one another.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250356/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels. It operates in a data-free setting—no exemplars from previous tasks are stored—using a shared feature extractor with multiple appended classification heads (one per task). In task-agnostic evaluation, all heads are scored jointly and the global maximum determines the prediction; in task-aware evaluation, only the relevant task's head is queried.

## Method

LwI trains a fresh model on each new task, then fuses it with the accumulated old model via channel-aligned model fusion rather than simple weight averaging. The alignment is formulated as a layer-wise graph matching problem solved with the Sinkhorn optimal-transport algorithm: channels in each layer are treated as nodes and inter-layer connections as edges. The key design choice is asymmetric matching—shallow layers use maximization of similarity to consolidate shared features and promote inter-task collaboration, while deeper layers use minimization of similarity to deliberately misalign task-specific channels, preserving each task's distinct activation pathway. Knowledge distillation from the old model is applied during new-task training to maintain feature-space consistency before fusion. This layer-by-layer decomposition reduces the graph-matching complexity from O(N⁴) to O(N⁴/N_L³), making it tractable for standard CNN architectures.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet18, LwI achieves 51.95% task-agnostic accuracy (5 splits) and 81.10% task-aware accuracy (5 splits), outperforming all data-free baselines. On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet18, LwI reaches 34.33% task-agnostic (5 splits) and 62.97% task-aware (5 splits). The method also surpasses exemplar-based approaches (iCaRL, LUCIR) in the majority of settings, particularly on the more complex Tiny-ImageNet dataset where task-specific pathway allocation provides greater benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LwI outperforms the strongest regularization-based baseline LwF by 7.29% on task-agnostic accuracy (51.95% vs. 44.66%) and by 1.14% on task-aware accuracy (81.10% vs. 79.96%) on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 (5 splits). Against the best architecture-based method WSN, LwI gains approximately 2.45% on task-aware accuracy (84.90% vs. 83.08% at 10 splits) while also being applicable in task-agnostic settings where WSN cannot operate. On Tiny-ImageNet/ResNet18, LwI surpasses LwF by 7.57% task-agnostic (34.33% vs. 26.76%) and 3.31% task-aware (62.97% vs. 59.66%) at 5 splits. Compared to exemplar-based methods, LwI (using zero stored data) outperforms iCaRL by 5.52% task-agnostic and 7.26% task-aware on Tiny-ImageNet (5 splits), and beats LUCIR by 4.16% task-agnostic and 2.72% task-aware on the same setting. On CIFAR-100/ResNet18, LwI slightly trails LUCIR on task-agnostic accuracy (51.95% vs. 55.67% at 5 splits) but matches or exceeds it on task-aware accuracy (81.10% vs. 81.22% at 5 splits, 84.90% vs. 84.41% at 10 splits), demonstrating that pathway protection can rival data-replay approaches without any stored exemplars.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parameter-protection paradigm in architecture-based continual learning (Piggyback, PackNet, WSN, GPM, SPU), which relies on pruning or masking to isolate task-specific weights. Instead, it reframes the problem as pathway protection: rather than freezing individual parameters, it aligns channels across models via graph matching (building on Su et al., 2021 and the Sinkhorn optimal-transport framework) before fusing them, leveraging the sparsity of activation channels and the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis. The key conceptual departure is the layer-wise asymmetric matching strategy—maximizing similarity in shallow layers for shared feature collaboration while minimizing similarity in deep layers for task-specific pathway segregation—which has no direct precedent in prior CL fusion work. Knowledge distillation from the old model is retained as an auxiliary training signal, connecting the method to the LwF lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LwI offers a data-free continual learning approach that works in both task-agnostic and task-aware settings, addressing a practical gap: parameter-isolation methods like WSN and GPM require task identifiers at test time, while rehearsal-based methods raise data-privacy concerns. The pathway-protection framing is a useful conceptual contribution that preserves network integrity (no unstructured pruning) and scales without linearly growing parameter storage. However, validation is limited to small architectures (ResNet32, ResNet18, AlexNet) on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet; the authors acknowledge the lack of validation on larger models, and the graph-matching step introduces non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the asymmetric matching strategy generalizes to transformer architectures, whether the approach scales to hundreds of tasks without degradation, and whether the Sinkhorn-based soft matching can be replaced by faster sparse-matrix techniques.
