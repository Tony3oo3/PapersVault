## Problem

Standard online learning algorithms assume all mistakes are recoverable, requiring only sublinear regret (average regret per step → 0). This is unacceptable in high-stakes settings where a single mistake can be catastrophic and irreparable. The paper asks: under what conditions can an agent formally guarantee that the total probability of catastrophe goes to zero (subconstant regret) in online learning, and what role can a mentor play in enabling this guarantee?

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequence of inputs x_t ∈ R^n and has access to a policy class Π containing the mentor policy π^m. Unusually, the agent never observes payoffs directly; the only feedback channel is querying the mentor, which reveals π^m(x_t) and implicitly the payoff. The agent outputs either an action y_t ∈ Y or a mentor query, and the objective is to maximize the product of payoffs (overall survival probability) rather than their sum. The algorithm requires only a nearest-neighbor distance oracle over the input space and does not need to know the local generalization constant L or the smoothness parameter σ.

## Method

Algorithm 1 combines two components: (1) a modified Hedge algorithm (from Russo et al. 2024's label-efficient feedback variant) that updates weights only on query time steps and issues a query with probability p = 1/√(εT), and (2) an out-of-distribution check where, if the input is farther than ε^{1/n} from any previously queried input with the same proposed action, the agent queries the mentor instead of acting. Local generalization ensures that any single mistake costs at most Lε^{1/n} in payoff, while Hedge bounds the total number of mistakes. The query bound uses a novel action-conditioned packing argument: out-of-distribution queries for a fixed action form an ε^{1/n}-packing within the set of inputs mapped to that action, yielding sublinear total queries. Setting ε = T^{-2n/(2n+1)} yields subconstant regret O(T^{-1/(2n+1)} log T) and sublinear queries O(T^{(4n+1)/(4n+2)}), matching the difficulty of standard online learning.

## Results

The paper establishes both a negative and a positive theoretical result. The negative result (Theorem 4.1) shows that any algorithm with sublinear mentor queries has unbounded worst-case regret of Ω(L√(T/(E[|Q_T|]+1))), meaning catastrophe is nearly guaranteed without extensive supervision. The positive result (Theorem 5.2) shows that when the mentor policy class has finite VC dimension d (with σ-smooth inputs) or finite Littlestone dimension d, Algorithm 1 achieves subconstant multiplicative regret E[R_T^×] ∈ O(dL/(σμ_0^m) · T^(−1/(2n+1)) · log T), subconstant additive regret E[R_T^+] ∈ O(dL/σ · T^(−1/(2n+1)) · log T), and sublinear queries E[|Q_T|] ∈ O(T^((4n+1)/(4n+2)) · (d/σ · log T + E[diam(x)^n])). The key conceptual advance is reducing regret from sublinear (standard online learning) to subconstant (total probability of catastrophe → 0) by a factor of T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, so comparisons are conceptual and structural. Against standard online learning (Hedge, Littlestone/VC dimension results), the paper's key advantage is achieving subconstant total regret rather than sublinear regret — a full factor of T improvement — at the cost of requiring a mentor and local generalization. Against Bayesian approaches (Cohen et al. 2021, Kosoy 2019, Mindermann et al. 2018), the paper avoids the need for a tractable posterior or a finite set of environments, handling uncountable policy classes and continuous unbounded input spaces. Against safe RL (Liu et al. 2021, Stradi et al. 2024), the paper requires no prior safe policy, no periodic resets, and no observation of safety costs. Against Maillard et al. (2019), which also uses mentor queries for irreversible MDPs, the paper achieves sublinear (rather than linear) regret and query bounds, and handles maximally bad actions without dependence on a γ* parameter. The paper also contrasts with Barman et al. (2023) on multiplicative objectives, noting that their geometric-mean regret implies only average catastrophe probability → 0, while the paper guarantees total catastrophe probability → 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Hedge algorithm (Freund & Schapire, 1997) and its label-efficient variant from Russo et al. (2024), which handles the case where losses are only observed in response to queries. It also relies on smoothed analysis results from Haghtalab et al. (2024) showing that finite VC dimension suffices for sublinear regret under σ-smooth inputs. The key departure from prior work is the problem formulation itself: instead of additive regret with full feedback, the paper studies multiplicative regret (product of payoffs) with no payoff observation, a mentor that can be queried, and a novel 'local generalization' assumption replacing Bayesian inference. This positions the work as fundamentally different from both standard online learning and safe RL, which typically assume known safe policies, periodic resets, and observed safety costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal guarantee of catastrophe avoidance in online learning without Bayesian inference, which is significant for high-stakes AI applications (autonomous weapons, medical AI, robotics) where trial-and-error is unacceptable. The negative result—that even a perfect mentor cannot prevent catastrophe if the agent asks too few questions—challenges the common assumption that having a good supervisor is sufficient. Open questions include whether the problem is tractable for finite VC dimension with fully adversarial inputs, the computational efficiency of the algorithm (currently Ω(|Π̃|) per step), whether standard OOD detection methods satisfy local generalization, and extension to non-communicating single-episode MDPs where the agent must simultaneously avoid catastrophe and maximize reward.
