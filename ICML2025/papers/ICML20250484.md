## Problem

Prior algorithms for learning Gaussian DAGs under the equal-variance assumption achieve O(d log n) sample complexity but implicitly require the covariance matrix condition number κ to be bounded independently of n, which fails in natural settings (e.g., path or tree graphs) where κ grows polynomially or exponentially with n. This paper asks whether sample complexity can be made truly independent of κ and provides an affirmative answer with near-tight upper and lower bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250484/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes m i.i.d. samples from an n-node Gaussian DAG with maximum in-degree d and minimum edge weight b_min, both of which must be known to the learner (adaptive variants exist). It outputs the full directed edge set by first recovering a valid topological ordering, then identifying each node's parent set. The sample complexity depends on τ(G) = 1 + max_j Σ_{l: j→l} b_{jl}², a graph-specific quantity always bounded by κ but potentially much smaller (e.g., constant for a directed path with fixed edge weights while κ grows as k^{n-1}).

## Method

The key technical insight is replacing direct estimation of covariance subblocks (which requires condition number bounds) with the MSE of OLS regression to estimate conditional variance, since for Gaussians Var(X_i | X_J) equals the population MSE of regressing X_i on X_J. Phase 1 iteratively builds the topological order by exploiting the variance gap: if all parents are in the conditioning set, the conditional variance is exactly 1, otherwise it is at least 1 + b_min², requiring O(b_min^{-4} d log(n/d)) samples. Phase 2 identifies parents using an OLS coefficient-based strategy adapted from Misra et al. (2020) for undirected GGMs: a candidate parent set J is validated by checking that regressing on J ∪ K for all K yields small coefficients on K, achieving the tighter O(τ/b_min²) dependence rather than the O(τ²/b_min⁴) from variance gaps alone. A polynomial-time variant (Algorithm 2) replaces exhaustive subset search with LASSO regression under the additional assumption that all node variances are bounded by R², at the cost of a d⁴τ³ factor in sample complexity.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an algorithm (Algorithm 1) that recovers the DAG topology with sample complexity O(max(b_min^{-4}, τ·b_min^{-2}) · d·log(n/d)), which is independent of the condition number κ of the covariance matrix. A matching lower bound of Ω(max(b_min^{-4}, τ·b_min^{-2}) · log(n)) is established via Fano's inequality, giving an almost tight characterization up to a factor of d. In simulations (n=10–25, d=4, b_min=0.5, b_max=1, m=200–1000), Algorithms 1 and 2 outperform the prior state-of-the-art (Gao et al., 2022) once m exceeds ~400, with the latter failing to converge as n grows due to its implicit dependence on κ (which grows superlinearly), while the paper's methods remain consistent since τ grows only sublinearly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two algorithms against the algorithm of Gao et al. (2022) on synthetic DAGs with n=10,15,20,25 nodes, d=4, b_min=0.5, b_max=1, and m=200–1000 samples. For small sample sizes (m ≤ 400), Gao et al. (2022) achieves slightly higher accuracy, likely due to a better constant in its sample complexity. However, once m exceeds ~400 and especially as n increases, Algorithms 1 and 2 clearly outperform Gao et al. (2022), which fails to be consistent (its accuracy plateaus below 100%) because its sample complexity scales polynomially with the condition number κ, which grows superlinearly with n. In terms of false positive edges, all three methods decrease toward zero, but Algorithms 1 and 2 converge faster than Gao et al. (2022) when m > 600. Theoretically, the paper's upper bound O(τ·d·log(n/d)) strictly improves upon Gao et al.'s O(M⁵·d·log(n)) (where M² ≥ κ), and the lower bound Ω(τ·b_min^{-2}·log(n)) is stronger than Gao et al.'s Ω(log(n)/b_min²) since M²−1 ≥ d·b_min² always holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning Gaussian DAGs under the equal-variance assumption (Peters & Buhlmann 2014; Ghoshal & Honorio 2017; Chen et al. 2019; Gao et al. 2022), which established O(d log n) sample complexity but always with a hidden polynomial dependence on the condition number κ of the covariance matrix. The key departure is identifying a weaker graph-dependent quantity τ(G) ≤ κ that governs the true sample complexity, and proving that κ is unnecessary. The algorithmic framework (iterative topological ordering via conditional variance) follows Gao et al. (2022), but the Phase 2 parent-identification technique is borrowed from Misra et al. (2020) for undirected GGMs, where OLS coefficient analysis replaces the condition-number-dependent covariance estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant because κ can grow polynomially or even exponentially with n in natural structures (directed paths, trees), rendering all prior algorithms infeasible in high dimensions while the true sample complexity remains O(d log n). The near-tight upper/lower bound characterization (matching up to a d-factor) provides a clean theoretical benchmark. Open questions include closing the d-factor gap (a known limitation shared with undirected GGM literature), extending to unknown b_min and d, and whether the bounded-variance assumption for the efficient algorithm can be further relaxed. The paper also establishes a conceptual separation between directed (governed by τ) and undirected (governed by β_min) Gaussian structure learning.
