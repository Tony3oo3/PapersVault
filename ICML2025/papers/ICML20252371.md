## Problem

Subpopulation shift—where subgroup proportions differ between training and deployment—degrades model performance on underrepresented groups. Existing robustness methods (gDRO, JTT, CnC, DFR) rely on subgroup annotations or explicit minority-group identification, which are rarely available or sufficiently granular in real-world datasets. This paper asks whether robust worst-group performance can be achieved without any prior knowledge of subgroup number, identity, or distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed by a frozen ResNet-50) or text passages (processed by a frozen BERT) and outputs a single class label per input. The DPE classifier operates exclusively on the frozen feature vectors from the backbone, trained on a small class-balanced (or group-balanced, when annotations exist) subset of the validation set—no subgroup labels are required during training. The ensemble aggregates N per-class prototypes by averaging their softmax distance-based probabilities before taking the argmax over K classes.

## Method

DPE replaces the standard linear classification head with an ensemble of N learnable prototypical classifiers per class, where each prototype is a vector in the frozen feature space and classification is performed via scaled Euclidean distance with a softmax over classes. Two complementary diversification mechanisms prevent redundant decision boundaries: an explicit inter-prototype similarity (IPS) loss that penalizes the absolute inner product between prototypes of the same class, and implicit diversification through bootstrap aggregation, where each ensemble member is trained on a different random class-balanced subset of the validation data. Prototypes are trained sequentially—at each stage the new prototype is optimized via SGD on the sum of the classification loss and IPS loss while all previously learned prototypes are frozen—ensuring each new member must capture a distinct subpopulation. The final prediction averages the per-member softmax probabilities across all N prototypes before selecting the highest-scoring class.

## Results

The primary metric is Worst-Group Accuracy (WGA). Without subgroup annotations, ERM*+DPE achieves an average WGA of 73.9% across nine datasets, substantially outperforming ERM (57.7%) and the strongest prior methods DFR (65.2%) and RWY (67.5%). With subgroup annotations available, ERM*+DPE reaches an average WGA of 83.0%, with standout results of 94.1% on Waterbirds, 90.3% on CelebA, and 91.7% on MetaShift. DPE also excels on the most challenging shift types: attribute generalization (NICO++: 50.0% vs. 35.0% ERM; LIVING17: 63.0% vs. 48.0% ERM) and attribute imbalance (CheXpert: 76.8% vs. 41.7% ERM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPE (ERM*+DPE) outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of datasets. Against the ERM baseline from Subpop-Bench (Yang et al., 2023), DPE gains 25 points on Waterbirds (94.1 vs. 69.1) and 27 points on CelebA (84.6 vs. 57.6). DPE surpasses CRT/ReWeightCRT (Kang et al., 2020) by 17.8 points on Waterbirds and 14.0 on CelebA, and beats DFR (Kirichenko et al., 2022) by 5.1 and 10.9 points respectively. Against the strongest recent methods, DPE edges out RWY (Idrissi et al., 2022) by 8.0 points on Waterbirds and AFR (Qiu et al., 2023) by 3.7 points. In the annotated setting (Table 2), DPE (94.1 Waterbirds, 90.3 CelebA) narrowly beats GAP (Rudner et al., 2024) by 0.3 and 0.1 points, and clearly outperforms Group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2019) by 2.7 and 1.4 points, JTT (Liu et al., 2021) by 7.4 and 9.2 points, and CnC (Zhang et al., 2022) by 5.6 and 1.5 points. The closest competitor overall is GAP, which DPE matches or slightly exceeds on Waterbirds and CelebA but surpasses more decisively on MetaShift (91.7 vs. not reported) and CheXpert (76.0 vs. not reported).

## Relation to Prior Work

DPE sits at the intersection of three research threads: prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017), which provide distance-based classification in latent space; the last-layer retraining paradigm (Kirichenko et al., 2022; Izmailov et al., 2022), which showed that freezing ERM features and retraining only the classifier on a balanced validation subset is sufficient for subpopulation robustness; and ensemble diversification methods (Pagliardini et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2022), which encourage multiple models to capture complementary decision boundaries. The key departure from prior subpopulation-shift methods (gDRO, JTT, CnC, DFR, GAP) is that DPE does not require subgroup annotations, explicit minority-group identification, or reweighting schedules; instead, it replaces the linear head with an ensemble of prototypical classifiers whose diversity is enforced through an inter-prototype similarity loss and bootstrap sampling, allowing the model to implicitly discover subpopulation structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPE offers a practical, modular solution to subpopulation shift that can be plugged onto any ERM-trained backbone without requiring group labels, making it applicable to real-world settings where subgroup annotations are unavailable or too coarse. The consistent worst-group accuracy gains across nine diverse datasets (vision and NLP) and all four shift types (spurious correlation, attribute/class imbalance, attribute generalization) strengthen its case as a general-purpose robustness layer. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of a theoretical explanation for why prototype diversification improves worst-group accuracy, and the method still inherits ERM's limitations in low-data or weak-label regimes. Open questions include whether self-supervised or contrastive feature extractors could further improve DPE, and whether the observed prototype-subgroup alignment (shown qualitatively via ChatGPT analysis) can be formalized.
