## Problem

Existing automated metrics for text-to-video evaluation (FVD, CLIP-Sim, IS) and even general-purpose MLLMs lack high-level semantic understanding, multi-step reasoning, and explainability, producing only opaque scores without rationale. This makes them inadequate for assessing dimensions like rationality, safety, and creativity, and limits their usefulness for diagnosing and improving T2V generation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluator consumes an AI-generated video (represented as three keyframes: initial, middle, and final), the original text prompt (required for Alignment and Rationality dimensions), and a dimension-specific instruction with rating criteria. It outputs a structured four-stage Chain-of-Thought response—Overview, Description, Analysis, Assessment—culminating in a 1–5 integer score with an explicit rationale, rather than a bare number. The seven evaluation dimensions span low-level perception (Quality, Aesthetic, Consistency) to high-level reasoning (Rationality, Safety, Creativity), each with its own key aspects and scoring rubric.

## Method

GRADEO fine-tunes Qwen2-VL-7B via LoRA (lr=1e-5, 10 epochs, 4×RTX 3090) on GRADEO-Instruct, a 3.3k-video instruction-tuning dataset built from 16k human annotations across seven dimensions, where GPT-4o converts raw annotator rationales into a standardized four-step CoT format (Overview → Description → Analysis → Assessment). The key design insight is that forcing the model to plan its reasoning (Overview), describe the video content (Description), systematically analyze against criteria (Analysis), and only then assign a score (Assessment) mimics human 'slow thinking' and yields both higher human-alignment correlation and interpretable outputs. The training loss jointly optimizes the reasoning tokens and the final score token, and the ablation confirms that removing any reasoning stage degrades performance, with the full CoT achieving SROCC 0.692 vs. 0.512 for score-only output. A GPT-4o-assisted pipeline ensures score consistency by discarding and regenerating CoT chains whose final score disagrees with the human label.

## Results

On the GRADEO-Instruct test set (340 samples), GRADEO achieves the highest Spearman rank correlation (SROCC) with human evaluations across all seven dimensions, ranging from 0.601 (Alignment) to 0.797 (Creativity), with corresponding MAE values between 0.341 and 0.560. On pairwise preference accuracy across four external datasets (T2VQA-DB, GenAI-Bench-Video, TVGE-Quality, TVGE-Alignment), GRADEO scores 0.790–0.840, outperforming the strongest baseline VideoScore-v1.1 (0.690–0.795) by 5–10 percentage points. The ablation confirms that the full four-step CoT (Overview → Description → Analysis → Assessment) with instruction-tuning yields SROCC 0.692 vs. 0.512 for the base model without any reasoning steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRADEO outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated dimension. Against automatic metrics (PIQE, BRISQUE, CLIP-Score, ImageReward, HPS-v2.1), GRADEO's SROCC exceeds 0.60 on all dimensions while these metrics remain below 0.40, confirming that low-level perceptual features cannot capture high-level semantic evaluation. Among image MLLMs, GPT-4o achieves the highest Aesthetic SROCC (0.656) but drops to 0.403 on Quality and 0.438 on Creativity, whereas GRADEO maintains 0.717 and 0.797 respectively. Among video MLLMs, Gemini-1.5-Pro is the closest competitor, reaching 0.792 SROCC on Creativity (vs. GRADEO's 0.797) and 0.663 on Aesthetic (vs. 0.717), but it collapses to 0.071 on Rationality and 0.026 on Creativity for Gemini-1.5-Flash, exposing the fragility of general-purpose MLLMs on specialized evaluation tasks. On pairwise preference accuracy (Table 3), GRADEO leads all baselines: 0.815 on T2VQA-DB Video Fidelity (vs. VideoScore's 0.790), 0.790 on GenAI-Bench-Video (vs. 0.760), 0.780 on TVGE Alignment (vs. 0.690), and 0.840 on TVGE Quality (vs. 0.795). The consistent margin over VideoScore—the strongest dedicated video evaluation model—demonstrates that multi-step reasoning with instruction-tuning provides a meaningful advantage over score-only prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-dimensional T2V evaluation frameworks (VBench, EvalCrafter) and MLLM-based scoring approaches (VideoScore, Evaluation Agent) by introducing a reasoning-based evaluation paradigm. Where prior methods either rely on pre-trained feature extractors for low-level metrics or prompt general-purpose MLLMs without task-specific training, GRADEO fine-tunes a video MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B) with LoRA on human-annotated Chain-of-Thought data, explicitly structuring evaluation into four reasoning stages (Overview, Description, Analysis, Assessment). It also departs from prior work by incorporating high-level semantic dimensions—rationality, safety, and creativity—that were largely absent from earlier benchmarks, and by requiring the model to produce interpretable rationales alongside scores rather than outputting scores alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRADEO provides the first instruction-tuned, reasoning-based evaluator that achieves strong correlation with human judgments across all seven dimensions, including high-level semantics that automated metrics cannot assess. The benchmarking of eight T2V models reveals that even the best current systems score below 50/100 on rationality and below 36/100 on creativity, exposing fundamental gaps in generating physically plausible and imaginative content. Key limitations include the relatively small training set (3.3k videos, 5 annotators per sample), susceptibility of MLLMs to prompt-induced hallucinations during evaluation, and the narrow scope of seven dimensions. Open questions include whether the reasoning-based approach scales to longer videos, whether more diverse annotator pools would shift the evaluation landscape, and how to mitigate hallucination in the evaluator itself.
