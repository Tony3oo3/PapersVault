## Problem

Existing theoretical imitation learning algorithms achieve sample complexity guarantees via exploration bonuses tightly coupled to tabular or linear transition structure, making them inapplicable to continuous control with neural network function approximation. Conversely, practical SAC-based IL methods (GAIL, CSIL, ML-IRL, RKL) lack a principled exploration mechanism, relying solely on entropy regularization which is known to be inefficient. The paper bridges this gap by proposing an ensemble-based optimistic critic that is both provably efficient in the tabular setting and directly implementable with neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed dataset of expert trajectories (either state-only or state-action pairs) and performs online interaction with the environment to collect additional rollouts. In the tabular setting, the output is a mixture policy whose occupancy measure equals the time-average of the K iterated policies, deployable by sampling a random iteration index at the start of each episode. In the continuous setting, the output is a standard SAC actor network trained with the optimistic Q estimate, with no architectural changes beyond replacing the single critic with an ensemble of L critics.

## Method

SOAR is a primal-dual template that alternates cost updates (algorithm-dependent, e.g., OGD for ML-IRL, discriminator-based for GAIL) with policy updates using an optimistic actor-critic. The key mechanism replaces SAC's single critic with an ensemble of L critics, each trained on an independent subset of the replay buffer, and aggregates them via mean-minus-standard-deviation (clipped at a threshold σ) to produce an optimistic Q-value that underestimates the true Bellman backup. This optimism drives the policy to visit under-explored state-action pairs, as unvisited pairs have high critic disagreement and thus receive a larger bonus. In the tabular instantiation, the policy update uses multiplicative weights (online mirror descent) and the transition estimates use Laplace smoothing, yielding a provable O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity matching known lower bounds. The number of critics L is set to 36 log(|S||A|K/δ) in theory and 4 in practice, balancing the probability of guaranteed optimism against the inflation of temporal difference error that scales with L.

## Results

Across 4 MuJoCo environments (Ant, Hopper, Walker2d, Humanoid) with 16 expert trajectories, SOAR consistently improves the normalized return of all tested SAC-based IL algorithms (RKL, ML-IRL, CSIL) using L=4 critic networks. The headline finding is that SOAR reduces the number of episodes needed to reach a given performance level by approximately 50% compared to the base algorithms. In the harder exploration task (a worst-case MDP from Moulin et al., 2025), L=1 critic fails to reach expert performance, L=2 succeeds with high variance, and L=3 achieves the best sample efficiency, confirming the theoretical tradeoff. The advantage of SOAR is most pronounced in higher-dimensional environments (Ant-v5, Humanoid-v5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOAR is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of existing SAC-based IL algorithms rather than as a standalone method. Against ML-IRL (Zeng et al., 2022), CSIL (Watson et al., 2023), and RKL/f-IRL (Ni et al., 2021), the SOAR-enhanced versions (ML-IRL+SOAR, CSIL+SOAR, RKL+SOAR) consistently achieve higher normalized return across all four MuJoCo environments, reaching the same performance in roughly half the episodes. Against GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and SQIL (Reddy et al., 2019) in state-action settings, CSIL+SOAR and ML-IRL+SOAR achieve superior final performance. Against OPT-AIL (Xu et al., 2024), which uses a more complex deep exploration technique requiring non-concave maximization, SOAR outperforms it in the state-only setting while being significantly simpler to implement. Against HYPE (Ren et al., 2024), the SOAR-enhanced versions also show consistent gains. In the dedicated hard-exploration experiment, the ablation over L confirms that the ensemble size must be at least 2 for reliable optimism, with L=3 being optimal before the temporal-difference error bound (which scales with L) degrades performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOAR-IL sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) SAC-based imitation learning algorithms (GAIL, AIRL, CSIL, ML-IRL, RKL, HYPE) that use a single critic pair for policy updates, and (2) optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty exploration from the bandits literature. Prior theoretical IL work with exploration bonuses (Shani et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2023; Viano et al., 2024) achieves guarantees but relies on structure-specific bonuses (visit counts, linear features) that cannot be implemented with neural networks. SOAR departs from both lines by using an ensemble of L critics aggregated via mean-minus-std to produce a provably optimistic Q-estimate, which is structure-agnostic and drops directly into any SAC-based IL pipeline. The primal-dual (alternating cost/policy update) framing unifies several existing IL algorithms under one template.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: no prior method simultaneously offered (a) matching best-known sample complexity in the tabular setting and (b) a drop-in neural-network-compatible exploration mechanism for continuous control. The practical finding that SOAR halves the episodes needed across four MuJoCo environments and multiple base algorithms (CSIL, ML-IRL, RKL, HYPE) makes it immediately useful for practitioners. Key limitations include theoretical guarantees restricted to the tabular (binarized) MDP setting, a non-trivial tradeoff in the number of critics L (too few breaks optimism, too many inflates TD error), and the need for per-environment tuning of the std-clipping threshold σ. Open questions flagged by the authors include extending the analysis to general function approximation, applying ensemble exploration to visual IL, and exploring the connection to LLM fine-tuning via IL.
