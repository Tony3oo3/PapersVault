## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers for non-Euclidean domains (Geo-FNO, Transolver) rely on extrinsic spatial coordinates or learned parametrizations to Euclidean grids, leaving the intrinsic geometry of the manifold—particularly curvature—unexplored. Since curvature dominates the physical laws governing PDEs on manifolds, and because tangent vectors at different points live in incompatible local coordinate systems, standard message-passing and attention mechanisms cannot be directly applied to discrete manifolds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251486/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 2D discrete manifolds represented as graphs with node coordinates (e.g., wrinkle, torus, sphere, paraboloids, Stanford Bunny), consuming the PDE solution field u(x,t) at the current time step (plus u(x,t−1) for second-order wave equations) and the source term f(x,t). Internally, scalar fields are converted to tangent vector fields via a discrete gradient operator, and the output is the predicted solution u(x,t+1) as a scalar field. Evaluation uses L² and H¹ norms computed through finite-element mass matrices and Whitney-basis discrete gradients on the manifold.

## Method

The core contribution is a curvature-aware graph attention that replaces vanilla self-attention in message-passing frameworks. Node representations are treated as tangent vectors; parallel transport along geodesic edges moves neighbor representations into the target node's tangent space, naturally encoding path curvature into the transported vector. Linear mappings are generalized to (1,1)- and (0,2)-tensor fields defined via the Riemannian inner product, with the metric tensor estimated from curvature via Jacobi field Taylor expansion. To make parallel transport tractable, each edge is locally embedded into one of three constant-curvature surfaces (sphere, plane, pseudosphere) selected by the sign of the local Gaussian curvature, yielding closed-form O(1) transport formulas (rotation on S², identity on R², 2×2 matrix product on H² via Möbius isometries). A subtree partition scheme broadcasts a single set of tensor-field parameters across nodes within each subtree via parallel transport, controlling parameter count while preserving geometric consistency, and multi-head attention is realized through diverse subtree partitions.

## Results

On the wrinkle manifold (which contains positive, zero, and negative curvature regions), Curv-GT achieves the lowest relative error across all three PDE benchmarks: 0.025% L² and 0.101% H¹ for isotropic diffusion, 1.577% L² and 3.212% H¹ for p-Laplacian diffusion, and 0.035% L² and 0.039% H¹ for the wave equation. These results outperform the strongest baselines (GNOT, Transolver, Geo-FNO) by margins of 1.6×–10× depending on the PDE and metric. On the Stanford Bunny heat equation, Curv-GAT achieves 0.0058% L² loss, roughly halving the error of GAT (0.0116%) and GNOT (0.0106%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Curv-GT wins against all 11 baselines on every PDE and metric in the main comparison (Table 1). The closest competitor is GNOT, which achieves 0.040% L² on isotropic diffusion vs. Curv-GT's 0.025%, and 0.351% L² on the wave equation vs. 0.035%—a 10× gap. For p-Laplacian diffusion, Transolver (1.935% L²) and Geo-FNO (1.936% L²) are the strongest baselines, both outperformed by Curv-GT (1.577% L²). Non-graph methods (ResNet, DeepONet) and spectral methods (GCN, GINO) perform poorly, with GINO reaching 42.8% L² on p-Laplacian, indicating that ignoring local spatial structure is catastrophic on curved manifolds. On the Stanford Bunny heat equation (Table 6), Curv-GAT (0.0058% L²) outperforms GAT (0.0116%), GNOT (0.0106%), and Transolver (0.0102%), confirming the advantage generalizes to complex real-world geometry. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that simply concatenating curvature values or using linear mappings yields substantially worse results (e.g., 6.92% vs. 3.41% L² for GAT), validating the need for parallel transport and tensor-field-based attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the standard graph message-passing and attention framework (GAT, Graph Transformer) to Riemannian manifolds by replacing linear mappings with tensor fields and introducing parallel transport to align tangent spaces across nodes. It departs from prior manifold-PDE solvers (Geo-FNO, Transolver, GINO) that either project to Euclidean space or rely on extrinsic spatial coordinates, by explicitly encoding intrinsic Gaussian curvature into the attention mechanism via closed-form parallel transport on constant-curvature surfaces (sphere, plane, pseudosphere). The subtree partition for parameter sharing and the multi-head design via diverse partitions are practical extensions that make the tensor-field attention trainable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mathematically principled, drop-in replacement for vanilla attention that explicitly encodes path curvature and discrete gradient information, which prior manifold-PDE methods overlooked. This opens a path toward geometry-aware operator learning where the solver's inductive bias matches the underlying physics (curvature-dominated phenomena). Limitations include restriction to 2D manifolds, random subtree partitioning that may be suboptimal for highly non-uniform geometries, and the need for a curvature threshold ε that balances numerical stability against information loss. Open questions include extension to higher-dimensional manifolds, adaptive partition strategies, and whether the constant-curvature local approximation remains accurate for highly oscillatory or singular curvature fields.
