## Problem

Despite impressive empirical successes in concept learning, the field lacks general theoretical guarantees that recovered concepts are correct. Prior identifiability results require restrictive parametric assumptions—linearity, additivity, specific concept types, or sparse generating functions—that rarely hold universally in real-world data. The paper asks: in the most general nonparametric setting, which hidden concepts can be provably recovered from diverse classes of observations, and under what minimal conditions?

## Input / Output

The method takes observed variables x ∈ R^m (e.g., image pixels) paired with multi-hot class vectors c indicating which class each sample belongs to, and produces latent concept variables z = (z_A, z_B) split into class-dependent and class-independent parts, along with a binary connective structure matrix M encoding which concepts are linked to which classes. The generating function f: Z → X is an unknown diffeomorphism with no distributional assumptions on z, and estimation uses regularized maximum likelihood (ℓ_1 norm on the estimated Jacobian support) implemented via normalizing flows. The output is identifiable only up to a permutation and element-wise invertible transformation for class-dependent concepts, and up to a block-wise invertible transformation for class-independent ones.

## Method

The core mechanism is learning by comparison: the paper proves that the Jacobian D_c g of the class-dependent concept function, combined with observational equivalence, constrains the transformation between ground-truth and estimated models to a row permutation of the support structure. Theorem 1 establishes that for any pair of classes, concepts unique to each class are disentangled from the rest; Corollary 1 extends this to arbitrary class subsets (local comparison), enabling partial identifiability when global conditions fail. Theorem 2 then shows that under a Structural Diversity condition—each class-dependent concept is uniquely associated with one class within some subset—all class-dependent concepts are identifiable up to element-wise invertible transformation and permutation, without any assumptions on concept type, functional form, or parametric model. Proposition 1 further identifies class-independent concepts under sparsity conditions on the z_B-to-x Jacobian, and Proposition 2 recovers the binary class-concept structure matrix M nonparametrically. The key technical tool is analyzing the support of the matrix-valued function T in D_c ĝ = T D_c g, showing that invertibility of T plus ℓ_0 regularization forces the estimated support to be an exact row permutation of the true support.

## Results

On synthetic datasets, the proposed method (with structural conditions on M and F) achieves consistently higher Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC) than the base model (no structural conditions) across varying numbers of concepts, in both the class-dependent-only setting (Fig. 5) and the full-concept setting (Fig. 6), with lower variance across 10 random trials. On five real-world datasets (Fashion-MNIST, EMNIST, AnimalFace, Flower102, FFHQ), the method recovers semantically meaningful concepts (e.g., 'sleeve length,' 'heel height,' 'Blooming') that align with human understanding of the corresponding classes, validated through reconstructed image visualizations. No quantitative comparison against external baselines is reported; the primary quantitative comparison is between the proposed structural-condition model and an unstructured base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external method baselines. The sole quantitative comparison is between the proposed model (with structural diversity and sparsity conditions) and a 'Base' model that imposes no structural conditions on the connective matrix M or the generating function F, while keeping all other settings identical. On both synthetic settings (class-dependent concepts and all concepts), the proposed model achieves higher MCC and lower variance than the Base model, demonstrating that the structural conditions are necessary for successful disentanglement. The Base model fails to disentangle and recover most concepts, confirming the necessity of the proposed conditions. On real-world datasets, no numerical baseline comparison is provided; results are presented qualitatively through concept visualizations and heat maps of pixel differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the nonlinear ICA and identifiability literature (Kong et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2022) but departs fundamentally from prior concept-learning identifiability results that require parametric constraints such as linearity, additivity, or specific concept types (Brady et al., 2023; Lachapelle et al., 2023; Wiedemer et al., 2024). Instead of imposing sparsity on the generating function between latent and observed variables, the paper introduces a 'Structural Diversity' condition on the class-concept connective matrix, leveraging the cognitive principle of learning through comparison across diverse classes. The pairwise comparison theorem (Thm. 1) and its extension to arbitrary subsets (Cor. 1) provide a flexible, local identifiability guarantee that prior global-assumption frameworks do not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first frameworks providing nonparametric identifiability guarantees for latent concepts without restricting concept types, functional forms, or generative models, potentially explaining the broad empirical success of concept learning methods that previously lacked theoretical grounding. The partial identifiability via local comparison is practically significant: real-world data rarely satisfies ideal conditions for all concepts simultaneously, and this theory recovers as many concepts as the available diversity permits. Open questions include extending the framework to compositional generalization, controllable generation, and foundation models, as well as handling scenarios where even structural diversity is absent (e.g., all classes share overlapping features).
