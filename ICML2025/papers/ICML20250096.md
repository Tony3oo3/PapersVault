## Problem

GP prior VAEs capture temporal and cross-subject correlations in longitudinal data but suffer from cubic-time complexity, and existing scalable alternatives (inducing points, low-rank approximations) struggle with discrete covariates that resist gradient-based optimization. Additionally, the standard amortized variational inference introduces an unavoidable amortization gap that limits posterior quality. No efficient general solution for handling mixed continuous and categorical covariates in GP prior VAEs had been proposed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-dimensional samples (28×28 or 36×36 grayscale images, 64×64×3 RGB sprites, or 36-dimensional tabular vectors) paired with mixed-domain covariates: continuous variables (time, age, rotation angle) and categorical variables (subject ID, gender, disease status, ICU type). Because the parameterization is global, there is no per-sample encoder; instead, each sample's latent representation is computed as a linear combination of pre-computed basis functions evaluated at its covariate values. Outputs include generated samples at unseen time points or covariate combinations, learned latent functions decomposed by additive component, and Sobol indices quantifying each covariate's contribution to output variance.

## Method

DGBFGP replaces the exact additive GP prior with a Hilbert space eigenfunction approximation for continuous covariates (using the Wiener-Khinchin spectral density of the SE kernel) and an exact eigendecomposition for categorical kernels, yielding a global linear parameterization z = Σ_r A^(r) φ(x^(r)) where the weight matrices A^(r) carry Gaussian priors whose covariances are determined by spectral densities evaluated at Laplacian eigenvalues. This eliminates explicit kernel matrices, reduces time complexity to O(N Σ_r B^(r)), and removes the need for inducing points or amortized encoders. Training proceeds via variational inference with a factorized posterior q(A)q(σ)q(ℓ) over global parameters, where kernel hyperparameters (length-scale, amplitude) are treated as latent variables with log-normal priors and sampled via Monte Carlo within the ELBO. The resulting ELBO is directly amenable to standard mini-batch SGD, and Sobol indices computed from the additive structure provide per-component interpretability of the latent space.

## Results

On Rotated MNIST temporal interpolation, DGBFGP achieves 0.009±0.0001 MSE, outperforming the next-best method L-NODE (0.017) by 47% and SGP-BAE (0.023) by 61%. On the real-world Physionet ICU dataset, DGBFGP reaches 0.619±0.009 MSE versus 0.718 for LVAE and 0.732 for BRITS. On SPRITES conditional generation with unseen attribute combinations, DGBFGP achieves 0.0024±0.0003 MSE, more than 2× better than LVAE (0.0062). DGBFGP is also the fastest method in all experiments, running at 0.5 s/epoch on Rotated MNIST versus 7.7 s for LVAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGBFGP outperforms all five baselines across every experiment. On Rotated MNIST, it beats L-NODE (0.017) by 47% and SGP-BAE (0.023) by 61%, while BRITS is orders of magnitude worse (0.989) due to its non-generative RNN design. On Physionet, DGBFGP (0.619) surpasses LVAE (0.718) by 14% and BRITS (0.732) by 15%, with SVGP-VAE (0.802) and SGP-BAE (0.781) performing close to the trivial mean-prediction baseline (0.904). On SPRITES, DGBFGP (0.0024) achieves a 61% improvement over LVAE (0.0062) and a 70% improvement over SVGP-VAE (0.0081), demonstrating superior conditional generation with unseen attribute combinations. In terms of runtime (Table 10), DGBFGP is consistently the fastest: 0.5 s/epoch on Rotated MNIST vs. 7.7 s for LVAE and 1.8 s for SVGP-VAE, and 13.1 s on Physionet vs. 85.2 s for LVAE, confirming its linear-time advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGBFGP sits at the intersection of GP prior VAEs (GPPVAE, GP-VAE, SVGP-VAE, LVAE, SGP-BAE) and Hilbert-space basis function approximations for GPs (Solin & Särkkä, 2020; Timonen & Lähdesmäki, 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing inducing-point-based sparse GP approximations—which incur O(NM²+M³) complexity and resist gradient-based optimization for categorical covariates—with a global eigenfunction parametrization that yields O(N·ΣB) linear-time computation and eliminates the amortization gap inherent in encoder-based variational posteriors. The additive GP structure for mixed-domain covariates follows Ramchandran et al. (2021) and Ong et al. (2024), but the basis-function realization is the novel contribution, enabling probabilistic treatment of kernel hyperparameters and Sobol-index interpretability without explicit kernel matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-time scaling and elimination of the amortization gap make GP prior VAEs practical for large longitudinal datasets (e.g., ICU monitoring with thousands of subjects) where inducing-point methods become prohibitive. The global parameterization also enables straightforward fine-tuning for new subjects (only L additional parameters) rather than re-optimizing inducing points, which is a practical advantage in clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed number of basis functions (M=10) is sufficient for highly non-stationary or long-range temporal dependencies, how the method extends to non-stationary kernels beyond input warping, and whether the probabilistic treatment of kernel hyperparameters (log-normal priors) is necessary or whether point estimates would suffice in practice.
