## Problem

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) methods have been extensively studied, but their behavior under missing data—particularly nonignorable (MNAR) missingness where dropout depends on unobserved outcomes—remains theoretically uncharacterized. Standard OPE estimators that simply discard missing observations are shown to be biased under MNAR, yet no prior work provides a consistent estimator or valid statistical inference for value estimation in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory data with monotone missingness (binary response indicators η_t marking observed vs. dropped-out time points), a target policy π, a discount factor γ, and a shadow variable Z_t that is conditionally independent of dropout given the outcome but correlated with the unobserved outcome. It outputs a Q-function parameter estimate β̂_π, an integrated policy value V̂^π(G), and a two-sided confidence interval with asymptotically correct coverage. The Q-function is approximated via linear sieves (tensor-product B-splines or wavelets with L basis functions), and the dropout propensity is estimated via parametric or semi-parametric GMM before being plugged into the IPW estimating equation.

## Method

The core contribution is an Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) value estimator that augments the standard Bellman equation estimating equation with a weight η_{t+1}/(1−λ_{t+1}(ψ̂)) to correct the distributional shift induced by MNAR dropout. The dropout propensity λ(S_t, A_t, R_{t+1}, S_{t+1}; ψ) is estimated by solving a moment equation E[m(Z_t, S_t, A_t, R_{t+1}, S_{t+1}; ψ)]=0 using a shadow variable, supporting both a fully parametric logistic model and a semi-parametric exponential tilting model where the non-parametric component g(·) is profiled via kernel estimation and the tilting parameter ψ is solved by GMM. The paper proves that the complete-case estimator is consistent under MAR but biased under MNAR (Theorem 4.5), that the IPW estimator is bidirectionally consistent under MNAR (Theorem 4.6), and derives the asymptotic normality of the IPW value estimate to construct valid confidence intervals (Theorem 4.7). The IPW correction is also shown to extend naturally to other OPE methods such as Fitted Q Evaluation by incorporating the inverse weight into the Bellman residual loss.

## Results

Under MNAR missingness with n=1000, the Complete Case (CC) estimator exhibits a bias of −0.614 and an Empirical Coverage Probability (ECP) of only 0.820, while the proposed IPW estimator (parametric) achieves a bias of −0.023 and ECP of 0.940, and the semi-parametric IPW achieves a bias of 0.003 and ECP of 0.932. Under MAR, both CC and IPW remain consistent with negligible bias (|bias| < 0.03) and ECP above 0.94. On the MIMIC-III sepsis synthetic dataset with known MNAR dropout, CC underestimates the true policy value by 53% (0.504 vs. 1.075 for the Behavior policy), while IPW recovers 92% of the true value (0.988).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the Complete Case (CC) estimator (which naively uses only observed transitions) and the proposed IPW estimator (with parametric or semi-parametric dropout propensity estimation). Under MAR missingness, both CC and IPW perform comparably, confirming the theoretical result that CC remains valid when missingness is ignorable. Under MNAR, CC suffers severe bias (−0.598 to −0.655 across settings) and its confidence intervals undercover substantially (ECP drops to 0.780–0.904), while IPW(P) and IPW(SP) maintain near-zero bias (|bias| ≤ 0.072) and ECP close to the nominal 0.95 level. On the MIMIC-III sepsis synthetic experiment with a known MNAR dropout mechanism, CC underestimates all four policy values by 30–53% relative to the no-dropout baseline, whereas IPW recovers values within 8–10% of the true values. The semi-parametric IPW is slightly less accurate than the parametric version (as expected from greater model flexibility) but remains far superior to CC under MNAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-horizon OPE framework of Shi et al. (2021b), which uses linear sieve approximation of the Q-function via Bellman equation estimating equations, by incorporating inverse probability weighting to handle monotone missingness. For the MNAR component, it adapts the shadow-variable identification framework of Miao et al. (2024) and the semiparametric exponential tilting model of Shao & Wang (2016), both of which were previously restricted to single-stage decision-making. The key conceptual departure is bridging these two literatures: prior OPE work assumes complete trajectories, while prior MNAR work does not address sequential decision-making or value function estimation, leaving a gap that this paper fills with the first theoretical treatment of OPE under nonignorable missingness in infinite-horizon MDPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical guarantee that standard OPE estimators remain valid under MAR but become biased under MNAR, and offers a practical IPW-based correction with confidence intervals—addressing a silent failure mode in offline RL applications like healthcare and recommendation systems where missingness is pervasive. Key limitations include the requirement of a shadow variable (which is difficult to identify and justify in practice), the restriction to moderate state dimensions due to sieve approximation, and the need for domain knowledge to classify the missingness mechanism. Open questions include extending the framework to policy *learning* (not just evaluation) under MNAR, integrating with deep function approximators beyond linear sieves, and handling more complex missingness patterns such as intermittent or pre-action dropout.
