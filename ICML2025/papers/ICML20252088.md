## Problem

Existing zero-shot imitation learning methods decompose expert demonstrations into sequential single-goal tasks solved by goal-conditioned policies, but this hierarchical decomposition is provably myopic under goal abstraction: the agent's greedy pursuit of each individual goal can leave it in an unrecoverable state for subsequent goals. The paper formalizes this failure mode (Proposition 3.1) and shows it causes catastrophic performance degradation in hard-to-control environments, motivating a holistic occupancy-matching approach that considers all goals simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method receives a single expert demonstration as a rough (unordered in time) and partial (goal-space only, not full state) sequence of goals g_{0:M}, plus an initial state s_0. The offline pre-training dataset D_β contains full state-action trajectories without reward labels. The output is not a fixed neural policy but a non-Markovian deterministic action computed on-the-fly via MPC: at each step, a zero-order optimizer (iCEM) searches over H-step action sequences to minimize the OT cost, executing only the first action before replanning. The method runs at 2–4 Hz on an RTX 4090, with the OT cost matrix and Sinkhorn solver being the primary computational bottleneck.

## Method

ZILOT directly optimizes the occupancy matching objective by formulating it as a discrete Optimal Transport problem between the policy's state occupancy (approximated by rolling out a learned dynamics model P̂) and the expert's goal occupancy (the given demonstration). The key insight is lifting the goal-conditioned value function V to a state-goal distance d(s,g) = −V(s,g) (the learned first-hit time), which serves as the OT cost and incorporates MDP dynamics into the matching. The entropically regularized OT is solved via Sinkhorn's algorithm, and the resulting objective is optimized over action sequences using iCEM in a receding-horizon MPC loop. Three components are trained offline from D_β via a modified TD-MPC2 with HER: the dynamics model, the state-goal value function V (using a two-stream architecture for fast pairwise distance computation), and a goal-goal value function W that estimates inter-goal travel times for horizon truncation. This avoids the myopic decomposition entirely, as all goals within the planning horizon are simultaneously coupled to states in the OT transport plan.

## Results

ZILOT achieves the lowest W_min (Wasserstein-1 distance to expert trajectory) across all five environments, with the most dramatic improvement in fetch_slide_large_2D (0.098 vs. 0.205 for MPC+Cls and 0.250 for ER_FB). On GoalFraction (fraction of goals achieved in order), ZILOT leads in 4 of 5 environments, reaching 0.72 on fetch_pick_and_place, 0.75 on fetch_push, 0.70 on fetch_slide_large_2D, and 0.82 on halfcheetah, while slightly trailing the myopic baselines on pointmaze (0.94 vs. 1.00). The method runs at 2–4 Hz on an RTX 4090, compared to 25–72 Hz for the MPC+Cls baseline, due to the cost of Sinkhorn iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the hierarchical goal-following baselines from Pathak et al. (2018) (Pi+Cls and MPC+Cls), ZILOT achieves substantially lower W_min in every environment, with the largest gap in fetch_slide_large_2D (0.098 vs. 0.238/0.205) where myopic planning causes the agent to lose control of the cube. ZILOT also improves GoalFraction in 4 of 5 environments (e.g., 0.70 vs. 0.45 on fetch_slide_large_2D), though it slightly underperforms on pointmaze GoalFraction (0.94 vs. 1.00), where the baselines sacrifice trajectory fidelity to hit every waypoint exactly. Against the Forward-Backward baselines ER_FB and RER_FB from Pirotta et al. (2024), ZILOT dominates on both metrics across all environments; the W_min gap is particularly large in fetch_slide_large_2D (0.098 vs. 0.250) and halfcheetah (1.325 vs. 1.498), and GoalFraction improves from 0.24 to 0.70 on fetch_slide_large_2D. The authors attribute the FB baselines' weakness to their regularized f-divergence objective, which can be exploited by degenerate policies that remain in the most likely expert state, a failure mode avoided by ZILOT's unregularized Wasserstein objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZILOT departs from the dominant hierarchical decomposition approach to zero-shot IL (Pathak et al., 2018; Hao et al., 2023), which sequentially solves single-goal reaching problems, by instead directly optimizing the occupancy matching objective via Optimal Transport. The paper formally proves (Proposition 3.1) that the hierarchical approach is fundamentally myopic under goal abstraction, and replaces it with a holistic OT-based planning objective that considers all goals simultaneously. The method builds on TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024) for offline world-model learning and goal-conditioned value estimation, and on zero-order MPC (iCEM, MPPI) for inference-time optimization, while the OT formulation connects to prior work using Wasserstein distances in RL (Durugkar et al., 2021) and OT-based imitation (Dadashi et al., 2021; Haldar et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2023), though those use OT as a training reward rather than a planning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal characterization of myopia in hierarchical zero-shot IL (Proposition 3.1) identifies a fundamental failure mode that practitioners have encountered but not rigorously understood, and the OT-based solution provides a principled fix that generalizes across environments. The finding that the learned value-function distance d outperforms the true goal metric h is practically important, as it removes the need to specify h and incorporates MDP dynamics into the transport cost. Key limitations include reliance on world-model accuracy (forcing finite-horizon planning that can reintroduce mild myopia) and the non-Markovian nature of the resulting policy, which is theoretically unnecessary but practically effective. Open questions include how the method scales as world models improve, whether the OT objective can be learned end-to-end rather than optimized at inference, and whether the approach extends to high-dimensional visual observations.
