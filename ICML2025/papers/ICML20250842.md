## Problem

Extrapolative generation—producing sequences whose properties exceed the range observed in training data—is essential for tasks like protein design and sentiment control, but existing methods either rely on slow stochastic search (MCMC) or are sensitive to hyperparameters (ICE). The paper asks whether one can distill the knowledge in MCMC trajectories into a model that greedily extrapolates in a small number of deterministic steps, avoiding the inference-time cost of iterative sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial sequence (e.g., a wildtype protein of 83 amino acids, a Yelp review, or a Reddit post) and a sequence-level scoring function as input. At inference, the model autoregressively generates a short chain of intermediate states x_1, …, x_n, each conditioned on all prior states and their associated scores (either real or predicted), until it emits a <stop> token; the final state x_n is the extrapolated output. The scoring function is only needed during training and optionally at inference for intermediate states, making the learned model largely scorer-free at test time.

## Method

The approach is two-phase: first, a Metropolis-Hastings sampler with a masked language model as proposal distribution explores an energy-based surrogate (a product-of-experts combining a task scorer and a Hamming-distance prior) to produce Markov chains; second, an autoregressive model q_θ is fine-tuned (via LoRA on Prot-T5-XL, T5, or Llama-3.1) on pruned state sequences extracted from these chains. Training episodes are constructed by sub-sampling states using strategies such as fixed-length Δ-energy selection (picking transitions with the largest score improvement), uniform thinning, or first/best (single-step). The model is trained with teacher forcing to predict each intermediate state token-by-token conditioned on the full history of prior states and scores, analogous to reward-to-go in sequence-modeling RL. At inference, the model iteratively refines the sequence in 1–5 steps, optionally scoring or predicting the score after each intermediate state, and terminates upon generating <stop>—achieving extrapolation in a fraction of the iterations required by MCMC.

## Results

On the ACE2 protein stability task, q_θ (trained with fixed-length Δ-energy episodes) achieves 74.8% of generated mutants below ddG = −5, 61.6% below −6, and 46.4% below −7, dramatically outperforming MCMC (27.0%, 4.1%, 0.5%) and ICE with scorer (36.1%, 9.8%, 1.9%) in the extrapolation range while requiring only 3 iterations vs. 83 for MCMC. On sentiment extrapolation, q_θ reaches 73.4% extrapolation rate with just 1 iteration and 0.132% fluency degradation, compared to MCMC's 80.9% at 496 iterations with 0.746% fluency loss. On anonymization, q_θ achieves EER 0.221 and SBERT 0.839 in 4 iterations, matching MCMC's semantic similarity (0.835) while requiring ~1100× fewer iterations (4 vs. 4498).

## Baselines & Comparisons

q_θ decisively outperforms all extrapolation baselines (ICE, Genhance, Iterative sampling) in the protein extrapolation range: at ddG = −5, q_θ achieves 74.8% vs. ICE w/scorer's 36.1% and Genhance's 15.9%, while using only 3 iterations. On sentiment, q_θ (73.4% extrapolation, 1 iteration) beats ICE w/scorer (61.0%, 10 iterations) and FUDGE (23.7%, 1 iteration) in extrapolative capacity, and substantially outperforms MCMC in efficiency (1 vs. 496 iterations) and fluency preservation (0.132% vs. 0.746% perplexity change). For anonymization, q_θ matches or exceeds GPT-4 (EER 0.221 vs. 0.238) and DIPPER (EER 0.221 vs. 0.206) while achieving far higher semantic similarity (SBERT 0.839 vs. 0.698 and 0.641 respectively); Round Trip MT preserves the most semantics (SBERT 0.921) but fails at anonymization (EER 0.110 vs. q_θ's 0.221). Notably, q_θ outperforms MCMC itself on protein extrapolation (74.8% vs. 27.0% at ddG = −5), and the paper shows this gap cannot be closed by running MCMC for 10× more steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extrapolative controlled generation line initiated by ICE (Padmakumar et al., 2023), which uses iterative MLM denoising to push sequences beyond the training range. The key conceptual departure is treating MCMC not as an inference-time search tool but as a data-generation engine: Markov chains produced by Metropolis-Hastings sampling (with MLMs as proposals, following Goyal et al., 2021) are pruned and used to train an autoregressive model that directly learns the sequence-to-sequence transformation. The autoregressive parametrization with score conditioning draws on sequence-modeling RL formulations (Janner et al., 2021), while the energy-based product-of-experts surrogate follows Mireshghallah et al. (2022). For the anonymization task, the MCMC setup adapts the style-transfer framework of Khan et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for sample-efficient extrapolation across diverse sequence domains (proteins, text) with a single learned model replacing hundreds to thousands of MCMC steps at inference. A notable open question is whether the MCMC data-generation cost can be reduced (e.g., via annealing or better proposals), since the paper shows performance degrades when training on shorter chains. The authors also note that on-policy fine-tuning after initialization could further improve the learned policy, and that the approach has not been tested against prompting-based SOTA systems on extrapolation tasks due to the requirement that methods not be tuned on extrapolation-range data. Broader applicability to molecule design and other sequence domains with pre-trained models is an exciting but untested direction.
