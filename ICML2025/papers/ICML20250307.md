## Problem

Existing alignment-stage defenses against harmful fine-tuning (HFT) treat all alignment data samples uniformly, overlooking the fact that certain subsets of alignment examples are consistently more susceptible to forgetting across different fine-tuning tasks and harmful data ratios. This paper identifies and characterizes this uneven, data-dependent vulnerability and asks how to leverage it to design more effective alignment-stage protections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250307/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a curated alignment dataset of prompt–response pairs (2,000 safe samples from BeaverTails) and a proxy fine-tuning dataset (Alpaca mixed with 10% harmful data) used solely to estimate per-example vulnerability via a pseudo fine-tuning run. It produces a fully aligned LLM whose parameters are robust to subsequent harmful fine-tuning; the adversarial sampler learned during training is discarded, leaving only the LLM weights. The vulnerability grouping is fully data-driven and does not require access to the actual downstream fine-tuning distribution.

## Method

VAA operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Group Estimation) simulates HFT on a proxy dataset, tracks how many times each alignment example's harmful score increases over training, and partitions examples into 'vulnerable' (ForgotNum > 0) and 'invulnerable' groups. Stage 2 (Vulnerability-Aware Alignment) formulates training as a two-player game under a Group DRO framework: an adversarial sampler, parameterized by a probability simplex over the two groups, selects the currently worst-performing group via mirror ascent (EXP3 update), applies a group-specific first-order parameter perturbation ε_i ≈ α·∇ℓ_i/‖∇ℓ_i‖, and the LLM minimizes a composite objective f_i(θ) = (1−λ)ℓ_i(θ) + λℓ_i(θ+ε_i) on the sampled batch. A curriculum schedule gradually increases λ from 0 to 1, transitioning from standard supervised learning to full robustness optimization. This explicitly counteracts the gradient starvation that ERM causes when the invulnerable group dominates the vulnerable one, driving the model toward equal performance across groups.

## Results

On the four fine-tuning tasks (SST-2, AG News, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), VAA achieves the lowest average Harmful Score (HS) of 24.82, reducing it by 12.9%, 12.0%, 10.6%, and 3.4% compared to SFT (34.51) on each dataset respectively. VAA also attains the highest average Fine-tuning Accuracy (FA) of 56.77, matching or exceeding SFT (56.23) on every task. Under varying harmful ratios (0%–20%) and HFT epochs (1–5), VAA consistently maintains the lowest HS while preserving FA, and the vulnerability grouping transfers across model architectures (Llama2-7B → Qwen2.5-7B) without re-clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAA outperforms all three alignment-stage baselines (RepNoise, Vaccine, Booster) on Harmful Score across every fine-tuning task. On GSM8K, VAA reduces HS to 31.08 versus 41.83 (RepNoise), 34.66 (Vaccine), and 41.04 (Booster), while also achieving the highest FA (8.60 vs. 6.20–6.80). On AlpacaEval, both RepNoise (34.66) and Booster (40.24) actually *increase* HS relative to SFT (30.48), whereas VAA reduces it to 27.09. Vaccine is the closest competitor on average HS (31.28 vs. VAA's 24.82) but lags by 6.4 points and also trails on FA. Booster matches SFT on average HS (34.76 vs. 34.51) and offers no meaningful safety improvement, particularly failing on complex tasks. VAA's advantage is most pronounced on harder tasks (GSM8K, AlpacaEval), where baselines that rely on making harmful data unlearnable struggle with the complex task structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Group DRO framework from Sagawa et al. (2020) and the EXP3 adversarial bandit algorithm from Auer et al. (2002) to the LLM safety alignment setting, where they had not previously been applied. It departs from prior HFT defenses (Vaccine, RepNoise, Booster) that treat all alignment examples uniformly by introducing a data-centric perspective: the paper first empirically identifies that forgetting during HFT is uneven across alignment examples, then uses this vulnerability partition as a structural prior in the optimization. The adversarial sampler with group-dependent perturbations is a novel instantiation of the DRO min-max game tailored to the discrete group structure of alignment data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that vulnerability patterns are transferable across fine-tuning tasks and even across model architectures (Llama2 → Qwen2.5) provides a new lens for understanding alignment fragility that prior work had not identified. VAA is explicitly orthogonal to existing alignment-stage defenses, suggesting it can be composed with methods like Vaccine or Booster for stronger protection. However, the binary vulnerable/invulnerable partition is coarse, the grouping relies on a proxy fine-tuning step that adds pipeline complexity, and the method does not eliminate HFT risk entirely—open questions include whether continuous vulnerability spectra, uncertainty-based grouping, or integration with watermarking could yield more robust and scalable defenses.
