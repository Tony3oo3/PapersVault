## Problem

Existing graph-based representation learning methods for spatially resolved transcriptomics rely on spatially local neighbors, but the continuous nature of biological gene expression means neighboring spots exhibit minimal feature differences regardless of true spatial domain, making local information insufficient—especially for boundary spots. Additionally, multi-slice integration suffers from batch effects that cause same-slice spots to cluster together irrespective of biological relevance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial coordinates (N_s × 2) and gene expression profiles (N_s × N_g) to construct a spatial nearest-neighbor graph, which is block-diagonal across slices in multi-slice settings. It produces 64-dimensional spot representations (via a 2-layer GCN encoder) and reconstructed gene expression matrices (via a 2-layer MLP decoder) that serve as inputs to downstream tasks including K-means clustering for spatial domain identification, pseudotime computation for trajectory inference, and similarity-based matching for cross-slice alignment.

## Method

Spotscape uses a siamese architecture with two stochastically augmented views of the SNN graph (node feature masking and edge masking at 20% rate), encoded by a shared 2-layer GCN. The key innovation is the Similarity Telescope module, which enforces a relation consistency loss (L_SC) that minimizes the MSE between the cosine similarity matrices computed from the two augmented views, thereby capturing global pairwise relationships across all spots rather than only local neighbors. For multi-slice tasks, Spotscape adds a prototypical contrastive learning loss (L_PCL) that uses K-means prototypes at three granularities [K, 1.5K, 2K] to group same-domain spots across slices, and a similarity scaling loss (L_SS) that explicitly matches the mean of top-k similarity values between intra-slice and inter-slice neighbors to counteract batch effects. The total loss combines reconstruction, relation consistency, PCL, and similarity scaling terms with fixed balancing parameters (λ_Recon=0.1, λ_SC=1.0, λ_PCL=0.01, λ_SS=1.0), trained for 1000 epochs with PCL activated after a 500-epoch warm-up.

## Results

On single-slice spatial domain identification, Spotscape achieves the best ARI, NMI, and CA across all 16 slices in four datasets. Representative numbers include ARI 0.73 / NMI 0.78 / CA 0.75 on MTG Control (vs. SpaceFlow's 0.66/0.74/0.70) and ARI 0.57 / NMI 0.57 / CA 0.74 on NSCLC (vs. SpaceFlow's 0.53/0.52/0.75). For heterogeneous multi-slice integration on MTG, Spotscape reaches ARI 0.72 / NMI 0.76 / CA 0.81 while also achieving the best batch-effect correction (Silhouette batch 0.69, Graph connectivity 0.86). On Mouse Embryo alignment, Spotscape attains LTARI 0.51, surpassing the alignment-specific method SLAT (0.41) and STAligner (0.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spotscape consistently outperforms all ten baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. The closest competitor is SpaceFlow, which uses spatial regularization to map distances into representation space; Spotscape surpasses it by 0.07 ARI on MTG Control (0.73 vs. 0.66) and 0.14 ARI on MTG AD (0.68 vs. 0.54), attributed to the Similarity Telescope module directly optimizing inter-spot similarities rather than relying on a single regularization term. STAGATE, which uses graph attention to handle boundary heterogeneity, is outperformed by 0.19 ARI on MTG Control (0.73 vs. 0.54), confirming the paper's argument that attention alone cannot resolve the redundancy problem at domain boundaries. SpaCAE and GraphST trail significantly (e.g., 0.37 and 0.38 ARI respectively on MTG Control vs. 0.73 for Spotscape). For multi-slice heterogeneous integration, Spotscape beats CAST (0.48 ARI) and STAligner (0.38 ARI) by 0.24 and 0.34 ARI respectively on MTG, while also achieving superior batch-effect correction (Silhouette batch 0.69 vs. CAST's 0.45). On the alignment task, Spotscape (LTARI 0.51) outperforms the alignment-specific SLAT (0.41) and PASTE2 (0.21), demonstrating general applicability beyond dedicated alignment tools.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based representation learning lineage for spatial transcriptomics (SEDR, STAGATE, SpaCAE, SpaceFlow, GraphST) by arguing that the continuous nature of biological gene expression renders local spatial neighbors inherently similar regardless of domain membership, making attention-based or local-graph approaches fundamentally limited for boundary spots. It departs from the attention/edge-weighting paradigm (STAGATE) by introducing a global similarity consistency loss (Similarity Telescope) that directly optimizes pairwise cosine similarities across all spots, and from prior multi-slice integration methods (PASTE, STAligner, CAST) by proposing a simple top-k similarity scaling loss to balance intra- and inter-slice distances. The prototypical contrastive learning component draws on PCL (Li et al., 2020a) for grouping semantically similar spots across slices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even oracle edge weights fail to improve boundary-spot clustering challenges the prevailing assumption that better attention or edge weighting solves the heterophily problem in SRT, redirecting the field toward global relational structure learning. The unified single-slice/multi-slice framework with a simple similarity scaling strategy offers a practical, scalable tool (handles 100k+ spots) for the growing SRT community. Open questions include whether explicitly constructing edges from learned global similarities (graph structure learning) could further improve representations, and whether integrating histology images—which the authors acknowledge as a limitation—would complement the gene-expression-only approach.
