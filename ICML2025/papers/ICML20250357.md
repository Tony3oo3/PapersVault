## Problem

Training Vision Transformers in MXFP4 (4-bit microscaling floating point) precision, which is natively supported by Blackwell GPUs for 2× speedup over FP8, still suffers from significant accuracy degradation (up to 4% on DeiT-Tiny). The existing Microscaling training method has two unaddressed issues: its backward-pass gradient computation is biased due to inconsistent quantization group orientations, and a weight oscillation phenomenon in the forward pass—where latent weights fluctuate around quantization thresholds—prevents convergence even at near-zero learning rates. No prior work has systematically identified or resolved this oscillation problem in the context of fine-grained per-group quantization during pre-training from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K and trains Vision Transformer architectures (DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base, Swin-Tiny/Small) from scratch for 90 epochs. All linear-layer weights, activations, and gradients in both forward and backward passes are quantized to MXFP4 (E2M1 mantissa with E8M0 per-group scale, group size 32), while non-linear operations (attention softmax, LayerNorm, GELU) remain in BF16. The output is a fully MXFP4-quantized model deployable on Blackwell hardware, achieving 2× matrix-multiply speedup over FP8 and 4× over BF16.

## Method

TetraJet corrects the MXFP4 training pipeline with three key fixes: (1) truncation-free scaling that uses ceiling instead of floor for the E8M0 scale factor, ensuring no value exceeds the E2M1 representable range; (2) double quantization, where the already-quantized forward-pass activation/weight is re-quantized with the transposed group orientation (1×32 → 32×1) for the backward pass, yielding an unbiased STE gradient; and (3) stochastic rounding for all backward-pass quantizers to preserve gradient unbiasedness. To address the identified weight oscillation bottleneck, Q-EMA maintains an exponential moving average (β≈0.998) of each weight and uses it to break ties between the two nearest FP4 values during quantization, so oscillating weights settle to a stable choice. Q-Ramping periodically computes an oscillation ratio R_w = dist_Q/dist_W for each weight element over a short window, then adaptively amplifies the effective batch size (gradient accumulation) and proportionally scales the learning rate for high-R_w elements, reducing their update frequency and pushing them away from quantization thresholds.

## Results

On 90-epoch ImageNet-1K pre-training, TetraJet (MXFP4, per-group) achieves 59.75% top-1 on DeiT-T and 71.03% on DeiT-S, consistently outperforming the Microscaling baseline (58.56% / 70.10%). With the oscillation-reduction add-ons, TetraJet + Q-EMA reaches 60.00% on DeiT-T, 72.25% on DeiT-S, and 77.32% on DeiT-B — the latter exceeding full-precision DeiT-B (75.57%). The paper reports reducing the accuracy degradation of MXFP4 training by more than 50% relative to the Microscaling baseline across all five Vision Transformer variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the INT4 per-tensor baseline (Xi et al., NeurIPS 2023), TetraJet's per-group MXFP4 quantization yields a massive improvement: 59.75% vs. 40.14% on DeiT-T and 71.03% vs. 60.07% on DeiT-S, confirming that fine-grained per-group scaling is essential at 4-bit. Against the original Microscaling training method (Rouhani et al., 2023), TetraJet wins on all five models, with gains of +1.19 pp (DeiT-T), +0.93 pp (DeiT-S), +0.37 pp (DeiT-B), +0.25 pp (Swin-T), and +0.06 pp (Swin-S); the Q-EMA variant extends the DeiT-B lead to +2.78 pp over Microscaling. Against Nagel et al.'s (ICML 2022) oscillation-reduction methods, Q-EMA and Q-Ramping clearly outperform: their 'Dampen' regularizer provides no measurable benefit (59.75% vs. 59.75% on DeiT-T), while their 'Freeze' method catastrophically degrades to 16.45% on DeiT-T, confirming that freezing weights is incompatible with pre-training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Microscaling (MX) data format and its proposed MXFP4 training pipeline (Rouhani et al., 2023), correcting its gradient computation via double quantization and truncation-free scaling to achieve unbiased gradient estimation. It extends the weight oscillation literature from QAT/fine-tuning with per-tensor LSQ (Nagel et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023) to the substantially different setting of pre-training with fine-grained per-group MXFP4 quantization, where prior oscillation-reduction methods (Dampen, Freeze) either fail or cause catastrophic degradation. The broader lineage traces through the low-precision training progression from FP16/BF16 → FP8/INT8 → 4-bit formats, positioning MXFP4 as the next hardware-native step enabled by Blackwell GPUs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MXFP4 is the native 4-bit format on Blackwell GPUs offering 2× speedup over FP8 and 4× over BF16, so making it viable for training has direct practical impact on compute efficiency. The paper's identification of forward-pass weight oscillation as the dominant degradation source (rather than backward-pass quantization) provides a useful diagnostic framework for low-precision training more broadly. However, the evaluation is limited to Vision Transformers on ImageNet-1K; whether the oscillation phenomenon and its remedies transfer to LLM pre-training, other architectures, or longer training schedules remains an open question. The fact that Q-EMA on DeiT-B (77.32%) slightly exceeds full-precision training (75.57%) is intriguing but may reflect training-dynamics artifacts rather than a generalizable advantage.
