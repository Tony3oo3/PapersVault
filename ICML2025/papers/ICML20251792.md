## Problem

Existing OOD feature-shaping methods (ReAct, VRA, ASH, FS-OPT) are empirically motivated rule-based heuristics that lack a unifying theoretical framework, making it unclear under what distributional assumptions each method is valid and how to systematically derive new shaping functions. This paper asks: can OOD features be derived as the optimizer of a principled loss functional defined over the underlying ID/OOD statistical distributions?

## Input / Output

The method consumes penultimate-layer feature vectors from a pre-trained network (ResNet-50, MobileNet-v2, ViT-B-16, ViT-L-16 on ImageNet-1k at 256×256×3; ViT-B-16 or MLP-Mixer-Nano on CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32) and applies an element-wise piecewise linear shaping function μ(z) learned offline via variational optimization. The shaped features are then passed to an energy score (Liu et al., 2021) and thresholded to produce a binary ID/OOD label. Hyperparameters of the shaping function are tuned on a validation set of ID data plus Gaussian noise (ImageNet) or iSUN (CIFAR) by minimizing FPR95 via Bayesian optimization.

## Method

The paper formulates OOD features as random variables Z̃ with conditional distribution p(z̃|z), and defines a loss functional L = −D_KL + α·IB where the first term is the symmetrized KL divergence between p(z̃|Y=0) and p(z̃|Y=1) (maximizing ID/OOD separation) and the second is an Information Bottleneck term I(Z;Z̃) − β·I(Z̃;Y) (compressing features while preserving OOD-relevant information). Because the loss is defined over probability distributions, optimization is infinite-dimensional; the authors derive functional gradients via calculus of variations and perform gradient descent under element-wise independence and Gaussian p(z̃|z) = N(μ(z), σ_c(z)) assumptions, reducing the problem to 1D per feature component. The theory shows that different OOD distributional assumptions (Gaussian, Laplacian, Inverse Gaussian) naturally recover clipping (ReAct), small-value suppression (VRA/ASH), and negative-slope shaping (FS-OPT), and predicts a new piecewise linear shaping family that encompasses all three regimes. This predicted shaping function achieves state-of-the-art FPR95 among element-wise feature-shaping methods on ImageNet-1k and CIFAR-10/100 benchmarks.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1k benchmark, the proposed piecewise-linear shaping function achieves the best FPR95 (FP) and AUROC (AU) among all element-wise feature-shaping methods across four architectures: ResNet-50 (FP=35.82, AU=89.36), MobileNet-v2 (FP=46.97, AU=87.49), ViT-B-16 (FP=67.73, AU=81.06), and ViT-L-16 (FP=66.67, AU=83.92). On CIFAR-10 with DenseNet-101, the method achieves FP=24.08 and AU=95.26, outperforming the next-best element-wise method FS-OPT (FP=28.90, AU=94.12). Inference cost is on the order of microseconds per 256×256×3 image, comparable to other piecewise-linear shaping approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among element-wise feature-shaping methods, the proposed piecewise-linear shaping consistently outperforms all competitors on ImageNet-1k: it beats ReAct by 6.5 FP points on ResNet-50 (35.82 vs 42.29), VRA-P by 2.15 FP (35.82 vs 37.97), and FS-OPT by 3.93 FP (35.82 vs 39.75), while also achieving the highest AUROC. On CIFAR-10 with DenseNet-101, the method achieves FP=24.08, beating FS-OPT (28.90) by nearly 5 points and ReAct (82.00) by a wide margin. VRA-P and ASH variants show unstable or poor performance on ViT architectures (e.g., VRA-P drops to FP=98.39 on ViT-B-16), whereas the proposed method maintains competitive scores. ASH-S, which uses intermediate-layer pruning and re-routing (not element-wise shaping), marginally outperforms the proposed method on ResNet-50 (FP=34.70 vs 35.82) and CIFAR-10 DenseNet-101 (FP=23.93 vs 24.08), but the authors note this is a fundamentally different approach outside the scope of their theory. On CIFAR-100 with MLP-Nano, the method (FP=78.33) is slightly behind Energy (FP=79.90) and ReAct (FP=79.99) in AU but competitive in FP, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on larger-scale benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of optimization-based feature-shaping work (Xu et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024) by replacing deterministic shaping functions with random features (conditional probability distributions) and formulating the problem as a variational optimization over infinite-dimensional function spaces. The core algorithmic ingredient is the Information Bottleneck (Tishby et al., 2000), repurposed here as a regularization term that prevents the KL-divergence maximization from being ill-posed while preserving OOD-relevant information. Unlike prior optimization-based approaches that only separate distribution means, this work separates full distributions and uses calculus of variations to derive gradients, yielding a principled connection between OOD distributional assumptions (Gaussian, Laplacian, Inverse Gaussian) and the qualitative properties of existing shaping functions (clipping, zeroing, negative slopes).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a simple piecewise linear shaping function that achieves state-of-the-art results among element-wise feature-shaping methods on ImageNet-1k and CIFAR benchmarks, with the added benefit of a theoretical explanation for why it works. The framework provides a diagnostic lens: practitioners can now reason about which shaping function to use based on the expected OOD distribution and noise level, rather than relying on empirical trial-and-error. Key limitations include the restriction to element-wise (not vector) shaping, the fact that only the mean of the random feature is exploited algorithmically (the full distribution remains unused), and the absence of a theory for the downstream score function. Open questions include whether the full random feature distribution can be leveraged, whether the approach extends to non-element-wise shaping (as in ASH), and whether the distributional assumptions hold for novel OOD regimes.
