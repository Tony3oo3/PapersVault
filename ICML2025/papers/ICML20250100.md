## Problem

Long-range graph tasks are typically validated empirically via benchmarks like LRGB, whose long-rangedness is justified by graph size, diameter, or domain intuition rather than a formal definition. There is no principled, quantitative measure of how strongly distant nodes interact in a given task or model, making it impossible to rigorously assess whether a benchmark task is truly long-range or whether an architecture genuinely captures long-range dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained differentiable GNN (any architecture), a graph with node features, and a chosen distance metric (shortest-path or resistance distance), and produces a scalar range value at node, graph, and dataset granularities. For node-level tasks the range is derived from the Jacobian of output w.r.t. input features; for graph-level tasks it uses the Hessian since the Jacobian lacks pairwise interaction terms. Computationally, the Jacobian is sub-sampled over nodes and channels (e.g., 50% output nodes, 50% channels for LRGB) to keep memory tractable on graphs up to 256 nodes, and for PEPTIDES the discrete AtomEncoder is replaced with a differentiable one-hot variant to enable backpropagation through the input.

## Method

The paper derives a unique node-level range measure rho_u(L) = sum_v |L_uv| * d_G(u,v) from four axiomatic properties (Locality, Unit Interaction, Additivity, Homogeneity) on linear maps, then extends it to arbitrary differentiable GNNs by applying the formula to the Jacobian. A normalized variant rho_hat_u is defined as the expectation of distance under the influence distribution I_u(v) proportional to |dF_u/dx_v|, which admits fast stochastic estimation and is invariant to the number of interacting nodes. For graph-level tasks where the Jacobian is a vector rather than a matrix, an analogous Hessian-based measure eta_u is derived from the second-order Taylor term. The authors validate the measure on synthetic tasks with known ground-truth range (showing model range converges to task range as training error approaches zero) and then apply it to LRGB, finding that VOCSUPERPIXELS exhibits a positive range-performance correlation (genuinely long-range) while PEPTIDES-FUNC and PEPTIDES-STRUCT show a negative correlation (effectively local tasks).

## Results

On VOCSUPERPIXELS (a node-level task), MPNNs (GCN, GINE, GatedGCN) converge to a normalized SPD range of ~2–3 hops, while GPS and GT reach ~10 hops; range positively correlates with F1 score, confirming the task is genuinely long-range. On PEPTIDES-FUNC and PEPTIDES-STRUCT (graph-level tasks), MPNNs maintain a range of ~1 hop with no increase during training, while GPS/GT exhibit ranges of ~19 hops yet perform worst, indicating a negative correlation between range and performance and suggesting these tasks are fundamentally local. On CORA (a known short-range task), the GCN stays at ~1 hop and outperforms the GT, which initially learns a high range but converges to ~1 hop before overfitting. On heterophilic datasets (AMAZON-RATINGS, ROMAN-EMPIRE), GCN+VN induces long-range interactions but yields zero performance gain over a standard GCN, suggesting heterophily is not driven by long-range dependencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new architecture but uses six GNN architectures as probes to measure task range. GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017) consistently exhibits the shortest range (~1–2 hops) and performs well on PEPTIDES but poorly on VOCSUPERPIXELS, confirming its limited long-range capacity. GINE (Xu et al., 2018) and GatedGCN (Bresson & Laurent, 2017) are slightly more expressive and reach marginally higher ranges (~2–3 hops) due to reduced over-smoothing, but remain fundamentally local MPNNs. GPS (Rampášek et al., 2022) achieves the highest range (~10 hops on VOCSUPERPIXELS, ~19 on PEPTIDES) via global attention and is the best-performing model on VOCSUPERPIXELS but the worst on PEPTIDES, demonstrating that its long-range capacity is beneficial only when the task actually requires it. The pure Graph Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) mirrors GPS in range and performance pattern, while GCN+VN (virtual node) creates a 2-hop shortcut between all nodes, increasing range substantially but yielding no performance gain on PEPTIDES or heterophilic tasks—indicating that simply increasing range without matching the task's local structure is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the influence-function lineage (Koh & Liang, 2017; Xu et al., 2018b) and Alon & Yahav's (2020) problem radius, but departs by deriving a family of range measures from four axiomatic properties (locality, unit interaction, additivity, homogeneity) rather than proposing an ad-hoc definition. It subsumes the influence-based measure of Liang et al. (2025) as a special case and extends the Hessian-based mixing analysis of Di Giovanni et al. (2023b) into a principled range framework applicable at node, graph, and dataset granularities. The empirical component positions itself as a critical re-examination of the LRGB benchmark (Dwivedi et al., 2022) in light of Tönshoff et al.'s (2023) hyperparameter-sensitivity critique, using the new measure as a diagnostic rather than a performance metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The range measure offers a principled, architecture-agnostic diagnostic for evaluating whether a GNN or a benchmark task is genuinely long-range, moving the field beyond empirical performance gaps as the sole evidence. The finding that PEPTIDES tasks show a negative correlation between model range and performance—contradicting their inclusion in a 'long-range' benchmark—raises important questions about how LRGB tasks were originally justified and whether graph size/diameter alone suffices. Open questions include extending the Hessian-based graph-level measure to large real-world tasks, analyzing how specific architectural components (rewiring, residual connections, positional encodings) modulate range, and whether the model-range-as-task-range proxy holds under distribution shift or adversarial conditions.
