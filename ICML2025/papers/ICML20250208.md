## Problem

Generative Flow Networks face four practical barriers in continuous and imitation-learning settings: the flow-matching loss is intractable without handcrafted backward policies, imitation learning requires a separate reward model, acyclicity constraints conflict with natural RL environments, and divergence-based losses are predicted to be unstable in the presence of 0-flows (ergodic invariant measures). These limitations have prevented GFNs from matching the expressivity and ease-of-use of diffusion or normalizing-flow baselines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target distribution specified either as an unnormalized reward density (RL) or a dataset of samples (IL) on a compact state space (flat torus T^d or round sphere S^d), together with a small fixed set of globally-defined diffeomorphisms (e.g., 4 affine maps or 4 rotations). It produces samples via a Markov chain with a learned random stopping time τ, and simultaneously yields a tractable closed-form density estimate f̂_term that serves as an implicit reward model. Unlike diffusion models requiring a fixed number of denoising steps, the sampling trajectory length is adaptive and learned during training.

## Method

EGFs replace the sequentially-composed parameterized diffeomorphisms of normalizing flows with a learned policy α→ that at each state selects among finitely many (as few as 4) globally-defined diffeomorphisms Φ_i. This finite-choice structure gives a closed-form backward policy and star inflow via Jacobian determinants, making the flow-matching loss tractable without auxiliary estimators. Universality is guaranteed by requiring the group generated by the Φ_i to be topologically ergodic with summable L²-mixing coefficients (spectral gap), which is satisfied by 4 affine maps on tori (from SL_d(Z) generators) and 4 algebraic rotations on spheres (from dense free subgroups of SO_{d+1}). For imitation learning, the paper introduces the KL-weakFM loss, which couples a cross-entropy term on the virtual terminal density f̂_term = max(0, f_init + f←* − f→*) with a weak FM penalty on only the negative part of the flow-matching defect, eliminating the need for a separate reward model while provably controlling total-variation sampling error via a quantitative sampling theorem.

## Results

On the NASA earth science datasets (sphere S²), EGF achieves the best negative log-likelihood (NLL) across all three tasks: Volcano (−2.31), Earthquake (−0.12), and Flood (0.56), outperforming Moser Flow (−2.02, −0.09, 0.62) and Riemannian NFs (−0.97, 0.19, 0.90). EGF uses a 256×5 MLP architecture (vs. 512×6 for Moser Flow) and achieves these results with 10× shorter training time. On toy 2D torus tasks, EGFs with minimal 32×3 MLPs and only four affine transformations successfully reproduce target distributions where Moser Flow fails entirely at the same model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGF outperforms Moser Flow (Rozen et al., 2021) on all three sphere datasets, with the largest margin on Volcano (−2.31 vs. −2.02 NLL) and Flood (0.56 vs. 0.62), while using a model roughly 30× smaller (256×5 vs. 512×6 MLPs) and 10× less training time. Against Riemannian continuous normalizing flows (Mathieu & Nickel, 2020), EGF achieves substantially better NLL on all datasets (e.g., −2.31 vs. −0.97 on Volcano), demonstrating that the ergodicity-based approach with simple rotations surpasses handcrafted Riemannian transformations. On toy 2D torus distributions, EGF matches or exceeds DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) sample quality with tiny 32×3 MLPs, while additionally providing a tractable density estimate that DDPM cannot offer; Moser Flow fails to train at this model size. In RL experiments, the stable FM loss used by EGF converges reliably while the divergence-based loss (as used in prior GFN work) exhibits blow-up in flow size and sampling time, confirming the 0-flow instability predicted by Brunswic et al. (2024).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generative Flow Network (GFN) framework from Bengio et al. (2021, 2023) and the non-acyclic continuous GFN theory of Brunswic et al. (2024) and Lahlou et al. (2023) by restricting the forward policy to finitely many globally defined diffeomorphisms, yielding a tractable backward policy and flow-matching loss. The transformation blocks are borrowed from normalizing flows on tori and spheres (Rezende et al., 2020), but the key departure is that EGFs treat these as a stochastic Markov chain with a random stopping time rather than a deterministic sequential composition, and invoke ergodicity of the generated group to prove universality. The KL-weakFM loss is a new contribution that couples cross-entropy with a one-sided FM penalty, enabling imitation learning without a separate reward model—a gap identified in prior GFN-for-IL work (Zhang et al., 2022; Lahlou et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universality guarantee with only four simple transformations (two generators plus inverses) on tori and spheres is a clean theoretical result that suggests EGFs could be a lightweight, provably expressive alternative to Moser Flow or diffusion models for structured state spaces. However, all experiments are confined to 2D (torus T² and sphere S²), and the L²-mixing summability condition required for universality is difficult to verify or enforce in higher dimensions, leaving the practical applicability of EGFs beyond low-dimensional settings largely open. The KL-weakFM loss addresses a real practical pain point (eliminating the separate reward model in IL), but its behavior under more complex targets and at scale remains untested. A systematic study of spectral-gap regularization and the minimal number of transformations needed in higher dimensions would be the most impactful next step.
