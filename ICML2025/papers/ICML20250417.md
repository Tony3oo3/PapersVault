## Problem

Existing methods for training LLMs to generate fine-grained, sentence-level citations rely heavily on costly human annotations or proprietary API-based data pipelines (e.g., LongCite-45K), making it difficult to further improve citation quality beyond the initial supervised fine-tuning stage. The paper asks whether an LLM can autonomously evaluate and improve its own citations without any external supervision, by leveraging its own output probabilities as a self-supervised reward signal.

## Input / Output

The model receives a long context partitioned into individually indexed sentences (each prepended with a <C{i}> identifier) followed by a query, and produces a multi-statement response where each statement is immediately followed by a citation tag in the format <cite>[i1-i2][i3-i4]...</cite> referencing sentence spans from the context. Citations may be empty, reference a single sentence, or span multiple sentences, and are constrained to at most 384 tokens of cited text (unless the citation is a single long sentence). The response is generated autoregressively with top-p sampling (p=0.7, T=0.95), while best-of-N resampling of citation spans uses higher diversity (p=0.9, T=1.2).

## Method

SelfCite computes a self-supervised reward for each citation via context ablation, combining two metrics: a necessity score (probability drop, measuring how much the LM's probability of generating the statement decreases when cited sentences are removed from the full context) and a sufficiency score (probability hold, measuring whether the cited sentences alone maintain the statement's probability). The final reward simplifies to log p(r_i | E_i) − log p(r_i | C \ E_i), requiring only two forward passes per citation. This reward is used in two ways: (1) best-of-N sampling, where N=10 citation candidates are resampled per statement and the highest-reward candidate is selected, and (2) SimPO preference optimization, where BoN-refined citations serve as 'chosen' responses against the original citations as 'rejected' responses, internalizing the improvement into the model weights without a reference model. A length-balancing procedure prevents the model from exploiting longer citations as a shortcut during SimPO training.

## Results

On the LongBench-Cite benchmark (five long-form QA tasks), SelfCite's best configuration (LongCite-8B + SimPO then BoN) achieves an average citation F1 of 79.1, improving over the LongCite-8B baseline (73.8) by 5.3 points. Best-of-N sampling alone raises F1 to 77.5, while SimPO fine-tuning alone reaches 77.9, matching BoN without the inference-time reranking cost. The method also produces shorter citations (94.7 tokens avg) compared to GPT-4o (220 tokens) while achieving substantially higher F1 (79.1 vs. 65.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfCite (SimPO then BoN, F1=79.1) outperforms all proprietary prompting baselines by a wide margin: GPT-4o (65.6), Claude-3-sonnet (67.2), and GLM-4 (65.4), while also producing shorter citations (94.7 vs. 132–220 tokens). Against the fine-tuned LongCite-8B baseline (72.0), SelfCite gains 7.1 F1 points; against the NLI-reward SimPO variant (75.0), it still leads by 4.1 points, confirming that the context-ablation reward provides a more accurate signal than NLI-based entailment. The contributive attribution method ContextCite (256 calls, F1=68.9) is outperformed by 10.2 points, largely because SelfCite reranks LLM-generated citation candidates rather than estimating importance from scratch via linear regression. In the fully self-supervised setting (starting from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with ContextCite-generated SFT data), SelfCite reaches 71.5 F1, closing most of the gap to the supervised LongCite-8B (73.8). On the zero-shot chunk-level ALCE benchmark, SelfCite (SimPO w/ SelfCite) also outperforms both the LongCite-8B baseline and the NLI-reward SimPO variant in citation recall and precision, despite being trained on out-of-distribution sentence-level data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfCite builds directly on LongCite (Zhang et al., 2024), which established the sentence-level citation generation task and provided the LongCite-45K SFT data and LongCite-8B/9B models as starting points. The key conceptual departure is replacing externally-supervised citation rewards (human annotations, proprietary API distillation, or NLI-model-based scores) with a self-supervised reward computed from the LLM's own output probabilities under context ablation, drawing on interpretability techniques from ContextCite (Cohen-Wang et al., 2024) and rationalization (Lei et al., 2016). The reward is then applied through two standard alignment mechanisms—best-of-N sampling and SimPO preference optimization (Meng et al., 2024)—making the contribution primarily a novel reward design rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that an 8B open-source model, after self-supervised alignment, reaches citation F1 (79.1) within ~2 points of the Claude Citations API (81.3) built on a much larger proprietary model, while requiring no human annotations or external NLI models for the alignment stage. This offers a scalable path to improving citation quality in RAG systems without the cost of annotation pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method requires access to model log-probabilities (infeasible for closed-source APIs), still needs an SFT kick-start to generate structured citations, and the off-policy SimPO training shows degradation beyond 2K examples. Open questions include whether on-policy methods (PPO) can further close the gap, and whether fully self-supervised citation generation from scratch (without any SFT) is achievable.
