## Problem

The connection between softmax attention and modern Hopfield networks established by Ramsauer et al. (2021) was framed entirely around exact memory retrieval, yet transformers in practice solve a much broader range of tasks. This paper asks two specific questions that the retrieval-only framing leaves open: (1) how does the memorization–generalization tension manifest when the model must denoise a novel sample rather than recall a stored one, and (2) why does the attention-to-Hopfield correspondence involve exactly one energy-minimization step rather than many? Introducing in-context denoising as a controlled testbed, the paper shows that one step is not a limitation but the Bayes-optimal choice.

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt of L clean tokens drawn i.i.d. from a task-specific distribution over R^n (a random d-dimensional subspace, a d-sphere of fixed radius, or a K-component isotropic Gaussian mixture with centers on a sphere) concatenated with a single query token corrupted by additive isotropic Gaussian noise Z ~ N(0, σ_Z² I). The output is a single n-dimensional vector estimate of the original uncorrupted query. The task is unsupervised in the sense that the underlying distribution is never given explicitly; it must be inferred entirely from the L context tokens within the prompt, and the loss is mean squared error between the estimate and the true token.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form Bayes optimal denoisers (posterior means) for three restricted tasks and proves that a one-layer transformer with a single attention head and scaled identity weights W_PV = αI, W_KQ = βI converges almost surely to these optima as context length L → ∞ (Theorem 3.1 for sphere-supported distributions; a linear-attention analogue for the subspace case). Empirically, standard Adam training from random initialization recovers these scaled-identity weights and reaches the Bayes-optimal MSE across all three tasks. The key mechanistic insight is that the trained softmax attention layer is exactly one gradient-descent step (with step size γ = α) on a context-aware dense associative memory energy E(X_{1:L}, s) = (1/2α)||s||² − (1/β) log Σ_t exp(β X_t^T s), where the L context tokens sculpt the landscape and the corrupted query serves as the initial state. Iterating this update beyond one step can degrade performance by driving the state toward a query-independent minimum, explaining why the Ramsauer correspondence is naturally single-step. For the linear-manifold case, the corresponding energy reduces to a 'Naive Spherical Hopfield' model, linking the result to classical Hopfield theory.

## Results

Across all three denoising tasks (linear manifolds, nonlinear spheres, Gaussian mixtures) with n=16, L=500, one-layer transformers trained from random initialization converge to scaled identity weights (W_PV ≈ αI, W_KQ ≈ βI) and achieve MSE within a few percent of the Bayes optimal bound. For the linear case (σ_0²=2, σ_Z²=1, d=8), the learned weight product ⟨w_KQ⟩⟨w_PV⟩ ≈ 0.327 ≈ 1/3 = 1/(σ_0²+σ_Z²), matching the theoretical optimum. For the sphere case (R=1, σ_Z²=0.1), weights approach α≈1, β≈1/σ_Z²=10; for the Gaussian mixture case (σ_0²=0.02, σ_Z²=0.1), α≈1 and β≈5.127. Softmax and linear attention perform comparably in all three settings, and a model trained on d=8 subspaces generalizes to different subspace dimensions at inference time with only mild performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares trained one-layer transformers against two baselines: the trivial zero predictor and the Bayes optimal denoiser (posterior mean). In all three tasks, the trained model substantially outperforms the zero predictor and closely approaches the Bayes optimal MSE, with the gap shrinking as context length L increases (Fig. 4a). Softmax attention and linear attention are compared directly: for the linear manifold case they perform nearly identically (explainable via the small-argument softmax expansion), while for the Gaussian mixture case softmax attention slightly outperforms linear attention (Fig. 7). The trained weights in all cases fall within the same broad valley of the 2D MSE loss landscape as the analytically derived optimal weights (Fig. 6), confirming that standard training from random initialization recovers the theoretically predicted structure. The paper also demonstrates that the one-step attention update corresponds to a single gradient descent step on a context-aware DAM energy landscape, and that naive iteration of this update (as in standard Hopfield networks) can degrade performance by converging to query-independent minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-to-dense-associative-memory (DAM) correspondence first identified by Ramsauer et al. (2021), which showed that one step of a modern Hopfield network update (Demircigil et al., 2017) is mathematically equivalent to softmax attention. Prior work on this connection focused almost exclusively on exact retrieval (converging to a stored memory), while this paper introduces in-context denoising as a task that probes the correspondence beyond retrieval. It also aligns with the growing ICL literature (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Ahn et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2023) showing that transformers internally emulate gradient descent on context-specific objectives, providing a concrete denoising instantiation of that broader phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key mechanistic insight is that a single gradient descent step on the context-aware DAM energy landscape is not merely a convenient approximation but is actually optimal for denoising, and that naive iteration (the standard Hopfield protocol) can degrade the estimate by driving it toward query-independent minima. This resolves a natural question about why the Ramsauer et al. correspondence involves only one step. Open questions include whether multi-layer architectures with tied or varying weights can perform beneficial multi-step updates on more complex tasks, and how these findings extend to the high-dimensional, anisotropic settings of practical transformers. The scope is currently limited to small-dimensional (n=16) toy tasks, so generalization to realistic settings remains to be established.
