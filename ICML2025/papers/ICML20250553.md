## Problem

Standard reinforcement learning assumes a fixed, immutable action space, yet in many real-world sequential decision problems (e.g., robot control with safety constraints), the set of feasible actions varies stochastically over time. While this 'sleeping' phenomenon has been extensively studied for multi-armed bandits, it remained entirely unaddressed in the RL literature. This paper introduces the Sleeping Reinforcement Learning (SleRL) paradigm and establishes the first theoretical guarantees for learning under varying action availability.

## Input / Output

The agent observes a state s and, depending on the disclosure protocol, either the full schedule of available action sets A_{k,h}(s) for all stages and states at the start of each episode (per-episode), or only the current stage's available set A_{k,h} just before action selection (per-stage). The transition model P is unknown and must be estimated from interaction. The algorithm outputs an action from the available subset and provides minimax regret guarantees: Õ(H√(SAT)) for per-episode and independent per-stage settings, while a lower bound of Ω(H√(SAT·2^{A/2})) is proven for the Markovian per-stage case, revealing an unavoidable exponential barrier in the number of actions.

## Method

The paper develops three algorithmic and theoretical results. For per-episode disclosure, AR-UCBVI modifies UCBVI by computing optimistic value functions over the current episode's action sets while reusing all past samples, ensuring monotonicity of estimates despite changing availabilities. For per-stage disclosure with Markovian availability, the authors prove a minimax lower bound of Ω(H√(SAT·2^{A/2})) via a novel 'sleeping lattice' construction attached to an A-ary tree, showing that temporal correlation in action availability creates an exponential statistical barrier. For the tractable independent per-stage case, S-UCBVI extends UCBVI with two distinct optimistic bonuses: one (b^Q) for transition model uncertainty and a second (b^V) for action availability distribution uncertainty, where the value function is computed as an expectation over the estimated availability distribution. This dual-bonus design achieves Õ(H√(SAT)) regret, matching the standard RL lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes matching upper and lower regret bounds for Sleeping MDPs across two disclosure settings and two availability models. For per-episode disclosure, AR-UCBVI achieves regret of Õ(H√(SAT)), matching the minimax lower bound Ω(H√(SAT)) up to logarithmic factors. For per-stage disclosure with independent action availability, the proposed S-UCBVI also achieves Õ(H√(SAT)), again matching the lower bound. For the more challenging Markovian per-stage case, the paper proves an unavoidable exponential lower bound of Ω(H√(SAT)·2^(A/2)) via a novel sleeping lattice construction, while the augmented-MDP approach yields an upper bound of Õ(H√(SAT)·2^A).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against standard RL regret bounds and across different problem settings. The key finding is that per-episode disclosure and independent per-stage disclosure both achieve the same Õ(H√(SAT)) regret as standard UCBVI (Azar et al., 2017), meaning the sleeping structure does not increase the fundamental learning complexity in these tractable cases. In contrast, the Markovian per-stage setting introduces an unavoidable exponential factor of 2^(A/2) in the lower bound (Theorem 4.2), establishing a statistical barrier that no algorithm can overcome. The paper also formally proves the ordering V_PE* ≥ V_PS* ≥ V_LLC* (Section 6), showing that per-episode disclosure strictly dominates per-stage, which in turn dominates the low-level controller approach, using Jensen's inequality and policy-space containment arguments respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Sleeping Reinforcement Learning (SleRL), explicitly extending the well-studied Sleeping Multi-Armed Bandit literature (Kleinberg et al., 2008; Kanade et al., 2009; Saha et al., 2020) to the episodic MDP setting, which the authors note 'remains unaddressed despite its practical relevance.' Algorithmically, it builds directly on UCBVI (Azar et al., 2017) and a refined bonus analysis (Drago et al., 2025), adapting the optimistic value iteration to handle stochastic action availability. The lower bound constructions reuse the A-ary tree instances from Domingues et al. (2021), augmented with a novel 'sleeping lattice' structure to capture Markovian temporal correlations in action availability. The paper also positions itself as a stochastic generalization of deterministic action masking in RL (Vinyals et al., 2017; Huang & Ontañón, 2022), where availability is a fixed function of state rather than a random variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper cleanly delineates the boundary between tractable and intractable Sleeping RL: per-episode disclosure and per-stage independent availability both achieve the standard RL minimax rate Õ(H√(SAT)), while Markovian per-stage availability incurs an unavoidable exponential factor 2^(A/2) in the lower bound. This exponential barrier is a fundamental negative result showing that temporal correlation in action availability is not merely a practical nuisance but a statistical impossibility for polynomial-time learning. Open questions include whether intermediate availability structures (e.g., bounded memory) can avoid the exponential blowup, and whether instance-dependent bounds can capture when the problem is easier than worst-case. The practical motivation—low-level controllers in robotics that override infeasible actions—makes this formulation directly relevant to safe RL deployment.
