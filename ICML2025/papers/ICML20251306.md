## Problem

Sparsifying neural networks typically causes performance degradation due to reduced capacity, and while sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) has been shown to improve generalization by finding flat minima, its integration with the sparsification process remains at an early stage. Existing approaches either enforce sparsity or flatness separately, or combine them through ad-hoc heuristics without a principled optimization framework that jointly balances both objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense neural network (VGG-19, ResNet-20/32 for vision; LLaMA-2-7B/13B, LLaMA-3-8B for language) along with standard training data and a target parameter count d specifying the desired sparsity level. It produces a sparse network with exactly d non-zero parameters, ready for inference without costly retraining—only a lightweight batch-norm tuning step (a few forward passes) is applied post-projection. For LLMs, the method operates block-wise on the reconstruction error objective, processing each transformer layer sequentially with 128 calibration samples of sequence length 2048.

## Method

SAFE formulates pruning as the min-max problem min_{||x||_0 ≤ d} max_{||ε||_2 ≤ ρ} f(x+ε), seeking a sparse solution that is simultaneously flat. It solves this via an augmented Lagrangian with variable splitting: x handles the sharpness-aware objective minimization (using SAM's perturbed gradient update), z handles the sparsity constraint via hard thresholding, and a dual variable u enforces their agreement through a quadratic penalty λ/2||x−z+u||². This penalty acts as a smooth bridge that gradually pulls x toward sparsity during training rather than imposing abrupt pruning, and the dual ascent on u maintains feasibility once reached. The extension SAFE+ replaces the Euclidean projection with a generalized P-weighted projection, where the positive definite diagonal matrix P encodes saliency scores (e.g., diagonal Hessian for Optimal Brain Damage, gradient magnitude for SNIP, or activation norms for Wanda), allowing diverse pruning heuristics to be integrated within the same constrained optimization framework. A convergence proof establishes that SAFE converges to a δ-stationary point of the original sparsity-constrained problem under standard smoothness and weak convexity assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG-19, SAFE achieves 94.65% validation accuracy at 90% sparsity and maintains 93.56% at 99.5% sparsity, outperforming ADMM (88.53%) and GMP (90.63%) at extreme sparsity. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-32, SAFE reaches 73.89% at 90% sparsity and 51.45% at 99.5%, versus ADMM's 12.34% at the same sparsity. For LLM pruning, SAFE+ achieves the best perplexity across all settings, e.g., 6.56 on Wikitext2 for LLaMA-2-7B at 50% sparsity (vs. ALPS 6.87, Wanda 6.92, SparseGPT 6.99). Under 75% label noise at 90% sparsity, SAFE retains 64.72% accuracy on CIFAR-10/ResNet-20 while ADMM drops to 36.84%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE consistently outperforms all image classification baselines (GMP, PBW, MLPrune, LTH, ADMM) across CIFAR-10/100 at every sparsity level, with the gap widening dramatically at extreme sparsity: at 99.5% on CIFAR-100/ResNet-32, SAFE achieves 51.45% vs. ADMM's 12.34% and GMP's 42.93%. For LLM pruning, SAFE+ surpasses SparseGPT, Wanda, and ALPS on all models and sparsity settings (e.g., LLaMA-3-8B at 2:4 structured sparsity: SAFE+ 13.39 vs. ALPS 14.53, Wanda 23.03 on Wikitext2), while also being ~2.5× faster than ALPS. Against SAM-inspired pruners, SAFE outperforms IMP+SAM (cubic) at 99.5% sparsity (79.55% vs. 73.73%) and CrAM (10.00%) by a large margin; SAFE+SG further extends this to 85.85% at 99.5%, surpassing CrAM+ (81.30%). Under 75% label noise, SAFE maintains 64–67% accuracy across sparsity levels while ADMM collapses to 33–40%, a gap of 25–30 percentage points. SAFE also improves adversarial robustness (PGD) by ~6–13 points over ADMM at 90–99.5% sparsity on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFE unifies two well-established optimization tools—sharpness-aware minimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) and the augmented Lagrangian / ADMM framework (Boyd et al. 2011)—into a single principled algorithm for sparsity-constrained optimization. Prior attempts to bring sharpness awareness into pruning (Na et al. 2022, CrAM by Peste et al. 2022, Bair et al. 2023) applied SAM-like updates in ad-hoc or loosely coupled ways; SAFE instead embeds the SAM min-max objective directly inside the ADMM x-update and treats sparsity as a hard constraint handled by the z-projection, yielding a convergence guarantee. The SAFE+ extension further generalizes the projection step to a weighted metric, subsuming OBD, SNIP, and Wanda saliency scores within the same constrained-optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFE provides one of the first theoretically grounded (convergent) pruning methods that simultaneously enforces sparsity and flatness, and its robustness to label noise, common corruptions, and PGD adversarial attacks suggests flatness is a more reliable regularizer than sparsity alone under distribution shift. The generalized projection (SAFE+) offers a clean unifying lens for diverse saliency scores, which could simplify the design space for future pruning methods. Limitations include evaluation primarily on CIFAR-scale vision models and LLaMA-2/3 (no ImageNet or 70B-scale LLMs), the convergence proof relying on standard smoothness/weak-convexity assumptions that are hard to verify in practice, and the vision experiments requiring full training rather than post-training pruning. Open questions include whether the flatness–sparsity interaction persists at much larger scale and whether the ADMM penalty schedule can be made adaptive.
