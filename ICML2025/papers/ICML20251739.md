## Problem

Existing robust conformal prediction methods rely on randomized smoothing or formal verification solvers, which are either computationally intractable at scale (e.g., ImageNet) or produce overly conservative prediction sets that are too large for practical use. Additionally, no prior work provides certifiable coverage bounds for vanilla CP under adversarial attacks that hold simultaneously across all perturbation budgets without assumptions on the attacker.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251739/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces two types of outputs: (1) robust conformal prediction sets of class labels that maintain 1−α coverage under ε-bounded ℓ₂ perturbations, and (2) high-probability lower and upper coverage bounds (γ⁻_m, γ⁺_m) for vanilla CP sets under attack, valid simultaneously for all ε > 0. The conformal score is a sigmoid-based LAC score s(x,y) = 1 − sigmoid((f(x)_y − b)/T), and the method requires only a single forward pass per class at inference, yielding O(1) per-sample complexity at both calibration and test time.

## Method

The lip-rcp method exploits the global Lipschitz constant of 1-Lipschitz-by-design networks to compute tight conservative and restrictive conformal scores in closed form: s(x,y) ± L_n·L_s·ε bounds the score under any ε-bounded perturbation, eliminating the need for Monte Carlo sampling or verification solvers. The networks use orthogonality-constrained layers with GroupSort2 activations and are trained with Optimal Transport-inspired Hinge Kantorovich-Rubinstein losses to simultaneously promote robustness and tight Lipschitz bounds. For auditing vanilla CP, the paper derives Theorem 3.3, which provides high-probability binomial confidence intervals on coverage that hold uniformly over all attack budgets ε, correcting a subtle overfitting error in prior work (Gendler et al., 2022). The unified calibration procedure leverages the translation equivariance of quantile computation to yield identical results whether robustness is enforced at calibration time or inference time, with negligible computational overhead compared to vanilla CP.

## Results

On ImageNet (ε=0.02, α=0.1), lip-rcp achieves a robust CP set size of 111.0 with 97.4% coverage in 0.012s per sample, compared to CAS's set size of 1000.0 with 100.0% coverage in 7.920s per sample — a ~9× reduction in set size and ~660× speedup. Across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet (Figure 3), lip-rcp consistently produces smaller robust CP sets while maintaining coverage close to the target 1−α, outperforming VRCP, CAS, and RSCP+ methods. For vanilla CP auditing, Lipschitz-based coverage bounds are less pessimistic than CROWN formal verification bounds (Figure 4, left), with negligible computational overhead relative to vanilla CP (Figure 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VRCP (Jeary et al., 2024), lip-rcp produces smaller robust CP sets with comparable or better coverage on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet (Figure 3), while being O(1) at both calibration and inference versus VRCP-I's O(t_v) test-time solver cost. Against CAS (Zargarbashi et al., 2024), the advantage is dramatic on ImageNet: lip-rcp achieves a set size of 111.0 versus CAS's 1000.0 (a ~9× improvement) at 97.4% coverage, with a 660× per-sample speedup (0.012s vs 7.920s). Against CROWN formal verification (Zhang et al., 2018) for vanilla CP coverage auditing, Lipschitz-based bounds are tighter (less pessimistic) than CROWN bounds on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4, left), attributed to CROWN's limited scalability to larger networks. The paper also notes that smoothing-based methods (RSCP, RSCP+) suffer from finite-sample Monte Carlo estimation errors that inflate their sets, and that VRCP's verification solvers become intractable at ImageNet scale, which is why only CAS could be benchmarked there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Lipschitz-by-design neural networks (Anil et al., 2019; Boissin et al., 2025) and robust conformal prediction (Gendler et al., 2022; Jeary et al., 2024; Zargarbashi et al., 2024). It departs from both smoothing-based (RSCP, CAS) and verification-based (VRCP, CROWN) approaches to robust CP by exploiting global Lipschitz bounds to achieve O(1) score estimation, and it introduces a complementary auditing framework for vanilla CP under attack that provides the first sound coverage bounds valid simultaneously across all perturbation budgets. The paper also identifies and corrects a subtle overfitting-type error in Theorem 2 of Gendler et al. (2022) that was subsequently reproduced in Zargarbashi et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: lip-rcp scales to ImageNet where verification methods are intractable and smoothing methods produce degenerate set sizes (1000 vs. 111 classes), while maintaining negligible computational overhead over vanilla CP. The simultaneous-all-ε coverage guarantee removes the need to pre-specify an attack budget, which is valuable for deployment in safety-critical settings. Key limitations include restriction to ℓ2 perturbations and dependence on Lipschitz-by-design architectures (not fully model-agnostic), and open questions include generalization to other norms, higher perturbation budgets, and regression settings.
