## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is the default technique for improving sample quality in visual generative models, but it requires joint inference from both a conditional and an unconditional model at every sampling step, doubling computational cost and complicating downstream tasks such as distillation and RLHF. Unlike language models, where a single model can represent sampling distributions across temperatures, visual models lack an equivalent single-model mechanism for temperature-controlled generation. The paper asks whether visual generative models can be trained to directly produce CFG-quality samples using only one model, from scratch.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a class label (ImageNet C2I) or text prompt (COCO T2I) together with a pseudo-temperature β ∈ [0,1] and produces a single synthetic image (256×256 for ImageNet, 512×512 for COCO). The β parameter replaces CFG's guidance scale s via the relation β = 1/(1+s) and is fed to the network as an additional conditioning input through a Fourier embedding and small MLP, enabling continuous diversity-fidelity control without any second model call. For VAR, a pyramid β schedule is applied across decoding steps to mirror VAR's original pyramid CFG behavior.

## Method

GFT reformulates CFG's sampling equation by expressing the conditional model as a linear interpolation of a target sampling model and the unconditional model: ε^c = β·ε^s + (1−β)·ε^u. This allows training the sampling model ε^s directly with the standard conditional diffusion loss (or cross-entropy loss for discrete models), since the interpolation recovers the learnable conditional distribution from real data. The unconditional term is computed in evaluation mode with stop-gradient, adding only ~19% training overhead and negligible extra GPU memory compared to CFG. During training, β is randomly sampled from [0,1], so the network learns a continuous family of sampling distributions parameterized by temperature, giving inference-time flexibility analogous to CFG's guidance scale. The same interpolation is applied in logit space before softmax for autoregressive and masked-prediction models, yielding a standard cross-entropy objective that requires only a few lines of code modification to existing CFG codebases.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, GFT fine-tuned DiT-XL/2 achieves FID 1.99 without guidance, surpassing the CFG baseline (FID 2.11) and guidance distillation (FID 2.11). For autoregressive models, GFT fine-tuned VAR-d30 reaches FID 1.91 (vs. CFG 1.92) and LlamaGen-3B reaches FID 2.21 (vs. CFG 2.22), both without any guidance at inference. On zero-shot text-to-image (COCO 2014), GFT fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5 achieves FID 8.10, slightly outperforming guidance distillation (8.16) while remaining close to CFG (7.87). All GFT models halve sampling cost by requiring only a single forward pass per step instead of the dual conditional+unconditional inference used by CFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), GFT matches or slightly exceeds the guided FID on most models: DiT-XL/2 (1.99 vs 2.11), VAR-d30 (1.91 vs 1.92), and LlamaGen-3B (2.21 vs 2.22), while being 50% cheaper at inference. Against Guidance Distillation (Meng et al., 2023), GFT wins on DiT-XL/2 (1.99 vs 2.11) and Stable Diffusion 1.5 on COCO (8.10 vs 8.16), and requires no pretrained teacher model. Against Condition Contrastive Alignment (Chen et al., 2024b), GFT substantially outperforms on both AR models: LlamaGen-3B (2.21 vs 2.69) and VAR-d30 (1.91 vs 2.54), and additionally extends to diffusion and masked models where CCA is inapplicable. Against the base DiT-XL/2 (Peebles & Xie, 2023), VAR-d30 (Tian et al., 2024), LlamaGen-3B (Sun et al., 2024), and MAR-B (Li et al., 2024) with CFG, GFT fine-tuning achieves comparable or better FID with less than 5% of pretraining epochs. The one case where GFT is slightly behind CFG is MAR-B (2.39 vs 2.27), though the from-scratch GFT variant (Table 4) closes this gap to 2.27. For Stable Diffusion 1.5 (Rombach et al., 2022) on COCO, GFT (8.10) is marginally behind CFG (7.87) but ahead of distillation (8.16), with the gap attributed to the text-conditional setting's higher sensitivity to guidance scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

GFT builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), reusing its maximum-likelihood training objective but reparameterizing the conditional model as a linear interpolation between a sampling network and an unconditional network, so that the sampling network is trained end-to-end without a separate guidance step at inference. Prior attempts to eliminate guided sampling—Guidance Distillation (Meng et al., 2023) for diffusion and Condition Contrastive Alignment (Chen et al., 2024b) for AR models—both require a pretrained CFG teacher and operate as a two-stage pipeline; GFT departs from this by enabling single-stage, from-scratch training with the same convergence guarantees as standard conditional modeling. The pseudo-temperature β parameterization, which conditions the model on a continuous temperature input, draws an analogy to temperature scaling in language models but is shown to be the correct mechanism for visual generation where naive logit-temperature division fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GFT offers a drop-in replacement for CFG that halves inference cost while matching or exceeding quality across five distinct architectures (diffusion, AR, masked-prediction), making it immediately practical for any existing CFG codebase with only a few lines of modification. The finding that from-scratch GFT training can outperform CFG (e.g., LlamaGen-L: FID 2.52 vs 3.06) suggests the joint conditional-unconditional parameterization provides a useful inductive bias rather than merely replicating CFG's behavior. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation and other modalities, whether the β scheduling heuristics (pyramid, power-cosine) can be made more principled, and whether the 10–20% additional training compute is acceptable at the largest scales where CFG's dual-model overhead is most costly.
