## Problem

Diffusion policies achieve strong performance in imitation learning and offline RL, but their standard training procedure (denoising score matching) requires data samples from the target distribution, which is impossible in online RL where the optimal policy is defined by an unknown Q-function. Backpropagating policy gradients through the full reverse diffusion process is computationally expensive and unstable, and existing workarounds introduce biased estimations or prohibitive memory costs. The paper asks: can we train diffusion policies in online RL with the same computational efficiency as denoising score matching, without needing to sample from the optimal policy?

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a state vector s and the learned Q-function Q(s, a) as the energy function, rather than data samples from the target policy. For DPMD, actions a_0 are sampled from the current policy π_old and noised to a_t; for SDAC, a_t is sampled from a user-chosen distribution h_t (e.g., uniform or Gaussian) and a pseudo-clean action ẃ_0 is drawn from a conditional Gaussian φ_{0|t}. At deployment, the score network s_θ(a_t; s, t) drives a reverse diffusion process to produce a continuous action vector, with a batch of candidate actions scored by Q and the best one selected (plus tunable Gaussian noise for exploration).

## Method

The core contribution is Reweighted Score Matching (RSM), a general loss family that reweights the vanilla score matching objective by an arbitrary strictly positive function g(a_t; s), preserving the optimal solution (matching the noise-perturbed score ∇_{a_t} log p_t) while enabling tractable computation. By choosing g_MD = Z_MD(s)·p_t(a_t|s), the loss reduces to an expectation over the current policy π_old weighted by exp(Q/λ), yielding DPMD; by choosing g_MaxEnt = h_t(a_t|s)·Z(s)·p_t(a_t|s) and applying a reverse sampling trick that exploits the proportionality between q_{t|0} and the conditional Gaussian φ_{0|t}, the loss becomes an expectation over h_t and φ_{0|t} weighted by exp(Q/λ), yielding SDAC. Both algorithms require only the same forward-noising and score-matching computation as standard DDPM training, avoiding recursive gradient backpropagation through the reverse process. Practical stabilizations include Q-value normalization via EMA (DPMD), logsumexp over multiple ẃ_0 samples (SDAC), and batch action selection with adaptive Gaussian noise for exploration-exploitation balance.

## Results

On 10 OpenAI Gym MuJoCo v4 tasks, DPMD and SDAC outperform all baselines across nearly all environments. The headline improvements over SAC are 36.0% (HalfCheetah, SDAC 12210 vs. 8981), 41.7% (Walker2d, DPMD 4365 vs. 3233), 127.3% (Ant, DPMD 5683 vs. 2500), and 143.5% (Humanoid, DPMD 6959 vs. 2858). DPMD achieves at least 6.4%–43.4% improvement over the best diffusion-policy baseline (QSM/DACER) depending on the task. In terms of efficiency, DPMD uses 1192 MB GPU memory and 21.1 min training time, comparable to the lightweight QSM (997 MB, 14.2 min) and far below QVPO (5219 MB, 30.9 min) or DPPO (1557 MB, 95.2 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classic model-free baselines, both DPMD and SDAC substantially outperform SAC on all locomotion tasks (e.g., DPMD 6959 vs. SAC 2858 on Humanoid; DPMD 5683 vs. SAC 2500 on Ant) and match or exceed PPO/TD3 across the board. Among diffusion-policy baselines, DPMD and SDAC dominate: QSM fails entirely on InvertedDoublePendulum (2186 vs. 9360) and Ant (938 vs. 5683) due to inaccurate score estimation from differentiating a learned Q-function; QVPO collapses on Reacher (−30.59) and Pusher (−129.06) because it cannot handle negative Q-values; DACER fails on InvertedPendulum (801 vs. 1000) due to gradient instability from recursive backpropagation through the reverse diffusion; DIPO and DPPO are consistently weaker, with DPPO particularly poor on HalfCheetah (1173) and Ant (60). SDAC matches or slightly exceeds DPMD on HalfCheetah (12210 vs. 11924) and Swimmer (119.1 vs. 79.3) but underperforms on Ant (1391 vs. 5683), making DPMD the more consistently strong variant overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising score matching (DSM) framework from DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) by introducing Reweighted Score Matching (RSM), which reweights the loss to preserve the optimal solution while eliminating the need to sample from the target distribution. It builds on the energy-based model interpretation of diffusion models (Song & Ermon, 2019) to connect diffusion policies with the closed-form energy-based policies arising from policy mirror descent (Tomar et al., 2021) and max-entropy RL (Haarnoja et al., 2018). Unlike prior diffusion-policy online RL methods (QSM, QVPO, DACER, DIPO, DPPO) that either backpropagate through the reverse diffusion process, maintain separate diffusion buffers, or approximate the policy as MLE, RSM achieves tractable training with computation comparable to standard DSM by leveraging a reverse sampling trick and Q-function reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSM provides a principled and computationally efficient pathway to train expressive diffusion policies in online RL, closing a significant gap between the theoretical appeal of energy-based policies and their practical deployment. The >120% improvement over SAC on Humanoid and Ant, combined with lower GPU memory and faster training than competing diffusion-policy RL methods, demonstrates that diffusion policies can be both expressive and practical in online settings. Open questions remain around the stability of diffusion policies during training, the design of efficient exploration schemes, and whether the RSM framework extends to other probabilistic modeling problems beyond RL (e.g., general Boltzmann sampling). The generality of the reweighting approach suggests potential applications to any setting where only energy functions are available.
