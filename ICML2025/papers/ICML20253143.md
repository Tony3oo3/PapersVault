## Problem

Existing attribution methods (Shapley, Banzhaf, etc.) compute variable importances given a predefined partition but offer no theoretical guidance on whether grouped variables form a meaningful coalition. The paper identifies that the attribution of a coalition S need not equal the sum of its members' individual attributions (the 'conflict of attributions'), yet no prior work explains the mathematical mechanism behind this discrepancy or provides principled criteria for evaluating coalition faithfulness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained AI model v(·), an input sample x with n input variables (tokens for NLP, 8×8 image regions for vision, or stones on a Go board), and a candidate coalition S ⊆ N. It outputs the coalition attribution φ(S), a decomposition of each member's Shapley value into a shared component (1/|S|·φ(S)) and a conflicting component U_{i,S̄}, plus three faithfulness scores R(i), R′(i), and Q(S) in [0,1]. The AND-OR interaction parameters {γ_L} are learned via a LASSO-like sparsity objective over all subsets, and the universal-matching property guarantees exact reconstruction of v(S) for every masked subset.

## Method

The core insight is reformulating the Shapley value as a uniform allocation of AND-OR interaction effects: φ(i) = Σ_{S⊇i} (1/|S|)[I_and(S)+I_or(S)] (Theorem 3.2). Building on this, the paper defines a coalition attribution φ(S) = Σ_{T⊇S} (|S|/|T|)[I_and(T)+I_or(T)] that allocates each interaction's effect proportionally to the fraction of T covered by S. Theorem 3.4 then proves the conflict arises exclusively from interactions T that overlap S partially (∅ ≠ T∩S ≠ S), giving a closed-form decomposition ∑_{i∈S}φ(i) = φ(S) + φ_conflict(S). Three faithfulness metrics—R(i) measuring the dominance of the shared term, R′(i) measuring the fraction of i's total interaction strength routed through the full coalition, and Q(S) measuring the coalition-level analogue—are derived from this decomposition and validated on toy functions, BERT/LLaMA on SST-2, VGG/ResNet on MNIST/CIFAR-10, and KataGo's Go value network.

## Results

On toy functions with known ground-truth interactions, the three proposed faithfulness metrics (R(i), R'(i), Q(S)) correctly identify coalition types: purely faithful coalitions score ~0.94 on all three metrics, purely unfaithful coalitions score ~0.01–0.03, and partially faithful coalitions score ~0.47–0.61. On SST-2 with BERT-large and LLaMA, a semantically coherent coalition ('mesmerizing performances') achieves Q(S)=0.743/0.746, while an incoherent one ('rivaling blair') drops to Q(S)=0.425/0.312. The theoretical decomposition is verified numerically: approximation errors for recovering Shapley values from coalition attributions are on the order of 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸ across all coalition sizes (m=1 to 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing attribution methods in a head-to-head accuracy sense; instead it positions its contribution as a theoretical explanation where prior methods (Faith-Shap, Joint Shapley) address the attribution conflict only in an engineering manner (e.g., adding a squared-loss penalty). Table 1 contrasts these approaches conceptually: Shapley and Banzhaf values satisfy efficiency only for the full set N, Joint Shapley extends axioms to feature sets but still estimates per-interaction attributions, and Faith-Shap pushes φ(S) toward Σφ(i) via a loss term. The proposed method instead proves the conflict is mathematically unavoidable and quantifies its exact source (interactions T with ∅≠T∩S≠S). Empirically, the faithfulness metrics are validated against ground-truth interactions on synthetic functions (clear separation: 0.94 vs. 0.01) and against human-annotated coalitions on SST-2, where semantically meaningful phrases consistently receive higher Q(S) scores than random groupings across both BERT-large and LLaMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shapley value framework (Shapley, 1953) and the AND-OR interaction formalism (Li & Zhang, 2023) to define a new attribution metric for variable coalitions, φ(S). Unlike prior attribution methods (Shapley, Banzhaf, Joint Shapley) that compute attributions given a fixed partition, and unlike Faith-Shap (Tsai et al., 2023) which patches the resulting conflict with a squared-loss regularizer, this paper provides a theoretical decomposition showing the conflict is an unavoidable consequence of interactions covering only partial variables in a coalition. The work thus shifts the question from 'how to eliminate the conflict' to 'how to quantify and interpret it,' positioning coalition faithfulness as a first-class evaluation criterion rather than a constraint to be enforced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving that the attribution conflict is mathematically inherent (Theorem 3.4) rather than an artifact of a particular method, the paper gives practitioners a principled basis for choosing variable groupings in attribution analyses—critical for tasks like image segmentation granularity or NLP tokenization. The three faithfulness metrics (R(i), R'(i), Q(S)) offer actionable diagnostics, validated across synthetic functions, NLP (BERT, LLaMA), image classification (VGG-11, ResNet-20), and the Go game (KataGo). However, the exponential computational cost of AND-OR interactions limits practical use to small variable sets (n≤10 in experiments), and whether the faithfulness metrics remain discriminative in high-dimensional settings (e.g., full-resolution images or long documents) remains an open question.
