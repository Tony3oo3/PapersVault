## Problem

Collaborative decoding techniques (e.g., weighted ensembling, contrastive decoding) improve LLM output quality by combining outputs from multiple models at each generation step, but each model must perform a separate forward pass per token, substantially slowing inference. The paper asks whether collaborative decoding can be accelerated without compromising the quality of the combined distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250683/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a set of 2–3 LLMs (e.g., Llama-2-7B + Vicuna-7B, or Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + Coder + Math variants) as input, along with a combination function (weighted ensemble at the probability level or contrastive decoding at the logits level) and a proposal length γ. It produces a sequence of text tokens whose distribution exactly matches the combined distribution r(x) = C(q(x), p(x)) of the participating models, guaranteeing lossless quality relative to standard collaborative decoding.

## Method

CoS extends speculative decoding to collaborative decoding via two mechanisms. First, Naive-CoS replaces the target model's distribution in the acceptance-rejection test with the combined distribution r(x), so the acceptance criterion becomes u_j ≤ min(1, r(x)/q(x)) and rejected tokens are resampled from norm max(0, r−q), ensuring generated tokens follow r(x) exactly. Second, the Alternate Proposal Framework treats the bonus token (naturally produced when all proposals are accepted) as a new proposal from the verifier, which is then verified by the proposer, so the two models alternate roles each cycle and each invocation yields two distributions in the optimal case. The framework generalizes to n models where each model scores others' proposals in parallel and generates its own bonus token. Theoretically, CoS is proven to be never slower than standard collaborative decoding (Corollary 3.7), with a guaranteed acceptance-rate lower bound of λ in the weighted-ensemble setting, and empirically achieves 1.11×–2.23× speedups across code generation, math reasoning, multi-task understanding, and summarization tasks.

## Results

CoS achieves speedups of 1.34x–1.85x over standard weighted ensemble (WE) collaborative decoding and 1.11x–2.23x over standard contrastive decoding (CD), across four tasks (HumanEval, GSM8K, MMLU, CNNDM) and multiple LLM pairs. In the three-model WE setting, CoS reaches up to 1.85x speedup on HumanEval, while vanilla SD actually degrades to 0.96x. The highest single speedup is 2.23x (Llama-3 CD, T=0, HumanEval), where CoS outperforms SD (2.04x) by 12%. CoS is guaranteed to never be slower than standard collaborative decoding, whereas SD can drop below 1.0x (e.g., 0.94x for Llama-2 CD, T=1, HumanEval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoS against three baselines: standard collaborative decoding (WE/CD), parallel collaborative decoding (WE-P/CD-P), and vanilla Speculative Decoding (SD, based on Leviathan et al. 2023 / Xia et al. 2023). CoS consistently outperforms all baselines across every model pair, task, and temperature setting. Against SD, CoS achieves 1.11x–2.23x speedup in CD (e.g., 2.23x vs. 2.04x for Llama-3 T=0 HumanEval) and 1.34x–1.85x in WE (e.g., 1.58x vs. 1.27x for Llama-Vicuna HumanEval). Critically, SD can be slower than standard collaborative decoding (0.94x for Llama-2 CD T=1 HumanEval; 0.96x for 3-model WE HumanEval), while CoS never drops below 1.0x. Parallel collaborative decoding (WE-P/CD-P) is uniformly slower than the sequential baseline (0.39x–0.82x), confirming that naive parallelism is counterproductive. The theoretical guarantee (Corollary 3.7) that CoS is never slower than standard collaboration is empirically validated across all 16+ experimental configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Speculative Decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Xia et al., 2023) from single-model acceleration to the multi-model collaborative decoding setting, where the verification distribution is generalized from the target model's distribution to an arbitrary combined distribution of all participating models. The closest prior work is Speculative Contrastive Decoding (Yuan et al., 2024), which the authors identify as a special case of their Naive-CoS. The key conceptual departure is the Alternate Proposal Framework, which repurposes the bonus token (normally discarded in collaborative settings because it follows the verifier's distribution rather than the combined one) as a proposal from the verifier to be verified by the proposer, enabling each model invocation to yield two distributions in the optimal case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoS provides a drop-in acceleration layer for any collaborative decoding method (ensemble, contrastive decoding, realignment, or future variants) with a theoretical guarantee of never underperforming the standard approach, which is valuable for practitioners who rely on multi-model collaboration for quality. The interpretable quality-speed tradeoff via the weighted ensemble weight λ in SD is a useful side contribution that gives practitioners a principled knob to balance inference cost against output fidelity. Open questions include how the method behaves when model sizes differ by orders of magnitude (the theoretical lower bound on acceptance rate relies on comparable model sizes for the weighted ensemble case), and whether the n-model generalization maintains the same guarantees as the two-model case at scale.
