## Problem

Existing federated node-level graph learning methods suffer from two critical limitations: they heavily rely on labeled graph samples that are difficult to obtain in real-world applications, and partitioning a complete graph into subgraphs inevitably destroys cross-subgraph links (over 55% on CiteSeer), leading to sub-optimal node representations. This paper is the first to address the link-missing problem in federated node-level clustering under fully unsupervised settings.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a subgraph with node attribute matrix X̄ ∈ R^{N̄×d} and adjacency matrix Ā ∈ R^{N̄×N̄}, and produces node cluster assignments Y via K-means on the final GNN embeddings. Rather than uploading raw node features, clients transmit a masked matrix R (generated from Gaussian noise with shape matching the selected representative nodes) and trained MLP parameters, which the server uses to infer global samples. A hyperparameter k (typically 1%–20%) controls the fraction of representative nodes selected per cluster for upload, balancing information fidelity against privacy and noise.

## Method

FedNCN operates in three stages. On each client, a GNN encodes the subgraph, K-means extracts cluster prototypes, and an MLP projector is trained in a denoising-like manner to reconstruct the selected representative node attributes from Gaussian noise, implicitly preserving clustering properties while producing hard-to-reverse signals. On the server, the uploaded noise and MLP parameters are used to infer global samples; intra-cluster links are established via cosine similarity, inter-cluster links are identified through graph kernel affinity (COS, WL, or SP), and an improved N-Cut criterion prunes redundant edges to yield a mended global graph. Finally, a GNN generator on the mended graph learns consensus prototypes by minimizing MSE reconstruction loss and KL-divergence clustering loss (using Student's t-distribution soft assignments), and the updated prototypes and GNN parameters are backpropagated to clients for the next round of local training.

## Results

FedNCN achieves substantial improvements over all baselines across five datasets under unsupervised federated node-level clustering. On the 10-client setting, FedNCN reaches 58.99% ACC on CiteSeer, 64.95% on PubMed, 71.21% on Amazon-Computer, 73.48% on Amazon-Photo, and 91.27% on Questions, outperforming the strongest baseline (FedGTA) by 32.65, 18.08, 50.25, 40.80, and 3.20 percentage points in ACC, respectively. The method also shows robustness to increasing client count, with only modest ACC drops (e.g., 58.99%→57.47% on CiteSeer from 10 to 20 clients) while baselines degrade more sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five FGL baselines (FedSage+, FedPUB, FedTAD, FedGTA, FedIIH) are originally supervised classification methods adapted to the unsupervised clustering setting, and FedNCN outperforms every one of them on all five datasets and all three client partitions. FedGTA is the strongest baseline, achieving 26.34% ACC on CiteSeer (10 clients) and 88.07% on Questions, yet FedNCN still surpasses it by 32.65 and 3.20 points respectively; the gap is even more dramatic on Amazon-Computer (71.21% vs. 20.96%) and Amazon-Photo (73.48% vs. 32.68%). FedPUB and FedIIH perform particularly poorly, often yielding near-zero NMI and ARI scores, indicating they fail to produce meaningful cluster structure without labels. Against the three classical FL aggregation strategies (FedAvg, FedProx, FedPer) applied to the same local model, FedNCN's cross-subgraph link mending yields average improvements of 9.7%, 6.8%, 18.0%, 22.4%, and 6.2% across the five datasets, confirming that the proposed global aggregation is far more effective than simple parameter averaging or proximal regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of federated graph learning and unsupervised graph clustering. Prior FGL methods (FedSage+, FedPUB, FedTAD, FedGTA, FedIIH) all rely on labeled node data to guide cross-client information sharing, and none explicitly address the structural damage caused by graph partitioning in an unsupervised setting. FedNCN repurposes prototype learning and denoising autoencoder ideas (MLP projector) to extract privacy-preserving clustering signals, then combines graph kernel similarity estimation with an N-Cut refinement to reconstruct cross-subgraph topology — a pipeline not present in any prior FGL work. The global knowledge-sharing stage draws on deep embedded clustering (Student-t distribution, KL divergence) but applies it to a server-side mended graph rather than a centralized one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: in real-world federated deployments (medical, transportation, social networks), graphs are partitioned across devices and labels are scarce, yet prior methods either require supervision or ignore the structural damage from partitioning. The demonstrated restoration of topology similarity from 44.74% to 78.45% provides a concrete mechanism for recovering lost inter-client structure without sharing raw data. Limitations include the relatively narrow evaluation (five citation/product/QA graphs, up to 20 clients), the somewhat hand-wavy privacy argument (relying on MLP and GNN parameters being architecturally distinct), and the absence of a formal threat model. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs, robustness to non-IID subgraph distributions, and whether the link-mending approach generalizes to link prediction or graph-level tasks.
