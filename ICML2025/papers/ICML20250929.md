## Problem

The classical multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge for trajectory inference uses Brownian motion as its reference process, producing rough, noisy trajectories that are hard to interpret and that fail to borrow strength across adjacent time points for particle tracking. Efficient algorithms for the multi-marginal problem rely on the Markov property of the reference process, yet smooth Gaussian processes—which would yield regular, interpretable paths—are inherently non-Markov, creating an apparent fundamental tension between smoothness and tractability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of K+1 point cloud observations (finite sets X_k ⊂ R^d of size n at times t_0,...,t_K) along with Matérn kernel parameters (smoothness ν = m − 1/2 and length-scale ℓ) that define the Gaussian autoregressive prior. It outputs the optimal scaling functions β_k that parameterize the joint distribution over all time steps, from which individual particle trajectories are reconstructed either by sequential sampling or argmax over the pairwise marginals p(z_k, z_{k+1}). The continuous phase-space variables (derivatives of the trajectory) are discretized via a Haar wavelet basis with M coefficients, and the number of message-passing iterations T controls the approximation accuracy.

## Method

The key insight is that a Gaussian autoregressive process (GAP) of order m—whose covariance is a Matérn kernel with ν = m − 1/2—is not Markov, but its lifted version η = (ω, dω/dt, …, d^{m-1}ω/dt^{m-1}) in phase space R^{dm} is a Gauss-Markov process. This allows the multi-marginal SB problem to be reformulated as an optimization over phase space with a tree-structured (pairwise) cost, making it amenable to belief propagation on a hidden Markov model with observed position nodes and hidden velocity/derivative nodes. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 3) approximates the continuous messages using a finite Haar wavelet expansion, reducing each message-passing step to matrix-vector multiplications of size nM × nM, yielding overall complexity Õ(K²n²M²C_max/ε). Theorem 4.1 establishes that this belief propagation is exactly equivalent to Sinkhorn's algorithm for the original multi-marginal entropic OT problem, guaranteeing convergence in Õ(KC_max/ε) iterations.

## Results

On the One-By-One particle tracking task, Smooth SB achieves 99.9% 5-step accuracy on the N-Body dataset (vs. 64.1% for Brownian SB and 64.9% for W₂ matching) and 99.1% on the 2D Gaussian Process dataset (vs. 61.2% for Brownian SB). On the Leave-One-Timestep-Out (LOT) trajectory inference task, Smooth SB is best or second-best across all four datasets (Petal, Embryoid Body, Dyngen Tree, Dyngen Cycle), typically reducing W₁ distance by 2–5× compared to MIOFlow and DMSB. For example, on Petal (j=2), SSB achieves W₁ = 2.70e-2 vs. 2.22e-1 for MIOFlow and 2.10e-1 for DMSB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Brownian Motion Schrödinger Bridge (BMSB, Lavenant et al. 2024), Smooth SB delivers dramatic gains on particle tracking: on the N-Body dataset, 5-step accuracy jumps from 64.1% to 99.9% and mean ℓ₂ error drops from 5.43e-1 to 5.48e-4 (a ~1000× improvement). Against MIOFlow (Huguet et al. 2022), Smooth SB wins on all four LOT datasets, with W₁ reductions of 8× (Petal), 1.6× (EB), 2.4× (Dyg Tree), and 2.2× (Dyg Cycle). Against DMSB (Chen et al. 2023), Smooth SB is consistently superior where DMSB converges and DMSB fails entirely on both Dyngen datasets. Against F&S (Chewi et al. 2021), the comparison is closest: SSB matches or slightly beats F&S on W₁ for Petal and EB, while F&S edges out SSB on M_G and M_I for EB and Dyg Tree; on Dyg Cycle, SSB clearly wins on W₁ (1.94e-1 vs. 2.84e-1) and M_G but ties on M_I.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge framework for trajectory inference (Lavenant et al., 2024; Chizat et al., 2022) by replacing the standard Brownian motion prior with smooth Gaussian autoregressive processes (GAPs), including Matérn kernels. The key conceptual departure is resolving what the authors identify as an inherent tension: prior efficient algorithms for multi-marginal SB rely on the Markov property of the reference process, yet Lemma C.1 proves that no non-trivial Markov Gaussian process has differentiable sample paths. The paper overcomes this by lifting the optimization to phase space, where the GAP becomes a Gauss-Markov process, enabling belief propagation. This subsumes the 'momentum Schrödinger bridge' of Chen et al. (2019, 2023) as the special case of integrated Brownian motion (order-2 GAP), and connects to the belief propagation literature on tree-structured costs (Haasler et al., 2021a; Teh & Welling, 2001).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, statistically grounded framework for smooth trajectory inference within the Schrödinger Bridge formulation, offering a quasi-Bayesian interpretation that prior spline-based or physics-inspired methods lack. The main limitation is the curse of dimensionality: the number of orthonormal coefficients M scales exponentially with the product of GAP order and observation dimension, restricting practical use to roughly 10 dimensions. Open questions include rigorous approximation guarantees for the truncated belief propagation algorithm, whether the exponential dimensional scaling is fundamental (as with Wasserstein barycenters), and extensions to noisy or partially observed data. For single-cell RNA-seq and particle tracking applications in low-to-moderate dimensions, this offers a compelling alternative to the rough Brownian-prior SB.
