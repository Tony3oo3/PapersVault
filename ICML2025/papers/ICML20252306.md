## Problem

Optimistic optimization algorithms such as SequOOL and SOO require a fixed hierarchical partitioning scheme (typically axis-aligned rectangles) that is agnostic to the function's structure. For black-box functions with low-dimensional ridge structure embedded in high-dimensional spaces, this default partitioning wastes evaluations on irrelevant dimensions and yields suboptimal regret rates. The paper asks whether one can adaptively learn a partitioning scheme that exploits the function's intrinsic low-dimensional geometry while preserving the theoretical guarantees of optimistic optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only pointwise evaluations of a black-box function f: [-1,1]^d → R (no gradients or analytic form), along with a total evaluation budget n and a target subspace dimension m. It outputs an estimated maximizer x̂(n) with a simple regret guarantee r_n = f* − f(x̂(n)). In the LLM quantization application, the input is the OPT-1.3B model weights plus a calibration dataset, and the output is a jointly optimized vector of 3M scaling factors α* ∈ [0,1]^{3M} that minimize perplexity, replacing AWQ's 3M independent 1-D grid searches with a single high-dimensional optimization.

## Method

The core method is a two-stage (or interleaved) pipeline: first, a single hidden-layer neural network is fitted via SGD to function evaluations at random points, and the top m right singular vectors of its hidden-layer weight matrix are extracted as an estimate Â of the true subspace matrix A; second, SequOOL is applied on an adaptive partitioning scheme defined by trisecting the m-dimensional projected space spanned by Â, with an optional lookahead direction-selection strategy that greedily chooses which axis to split at each depth based on the current surrogate. The key theoretical contribution is proving that for multi-index functions f(x) = g(Ax), this adaptive partitioning reduces the near-optimality dimension by a factor β = 1 + (d−m)/(2m−1) and improves the SequOOL parameter from ρ to ρ^β, yielding regret Õ(n^{−β/η}) versus Õ(n^{−1/η}) for the default scheme. The proof technique relates near-optimal cell counts across partitioning schemes via lattice covering arguments and bounds the impact of subspace estimation error through the subspace distance dist(A, Â).

## Results

On the OPT-1.3B LLM quantization task (72-dimensional search over per-layer scaling factors), Algorithm 2 (interleaved SequOOL on learned partition) achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 16.68 and calibration-set perplexity of 14.29, reducing the perplexity gap relative to the unquantized model (14.47) from 2.45 (AWQ grid search) to 2.21 — a ~10% improvement in quantization degradation. On benchmark functions, the method achieves zero regret on Rastrigin (vs. 4.22×10⁻⁵ for SequOOL and 5.5×10⁻³ for RESOO) and on Styblinski-Tang with ~900 evaluations (vs. 2000 for RESOO). Theoretically, for m-dimensional multi-index functions in d dimensions, the regret bound improves from Õ(n^{-1/η}) (SequOOL) to Õ(n^{-β/η}) where β = 1 + (d−m)/(2m−1) > 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the optimistic optimization baselines, the proposed method clearly wins on Rastrigin (zero regret vs. 4.22×10⁻⁵ for SequOOL and 5.5×10⁻³ for RESOO) and Styblinski-Tang (zero regret at ~900 evals vs. 2000 for RESOO), and matches or beats SOO, DiRect, and plain SequOOL across most benchmark functions. On the Ellipsoid and Sharp Ridge functions, the method performs slightly worse than RESOO, likely due to the 650-sample overhead of the subspace-learning stage within a 2000-evaluation budget. Against Bayesian optimization baselines (REMBO, HeSBO) and evolutionary/annealing methods (CMA-ES, Dual Annealing), the method is competitive or superior on the multi-modal and non-differentiable benchmarks (Rastrigin, Branin, Sharp Ridge) where gradient-free partitioning-based search excels. On the LLM quantization task, Algorithm 2 outperforms the AWQ grid-search baseline by ~10% in perplexity degradation (16.68 vs. 16.92 on WikiText-2) and also beats plain SequOOL (16.83), confirming the benefit of the learned adaptive partitioning even when the function does not strictly satisfy the multi-index assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistic optimization framework (SOO, SequOOL, DiRect) by replacing the fixed axis-aligned partitioning scheme with one that is learned adaptively from function evaluations. It draws on the multi-index function class from Fornasier et al. (2012) and the neural network subspace learning guarantees from Mousavi-Hosseini et al. (2023) to identify the relevant low-dimensional subspace, then applies SequOOL's search procedure on the resulting rotated partitioning. Unlike random embedding methods (REMBO, RESOO, HeSBO) that project into a random low-dimensional space and require a priori knowledge of the subspace dimension, this approach learns the subspace directions and their splitting schedule from data, and provides regret bounds that improve by a factor of β = 1 + (d−m)/(2m−1) over default SequOOL for multi-index functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable regret improvement over SequOOL for a structured function class by learning the partitioning geometry, bridging a gap between the theoretical optimistic optimization literature and practical high-dimensional black-box optimization. The LLM quantization application (OPT-1.3B, ~10% perplexity improvement over AWQ) demonstrates practical relevance, though the gain is modest. Key limitations include the multi-index assumption (when violated, improvement over SequOOL is minimal), the need to estimate the subspace dimension m, and the budget cost of the learning phase (650 samples in experiments). Open questions include extending to noisy evaluations, broader low-dimensional function classes beyond multi-index, and theoretical guarantees for the lookahead direction selection strategy.
