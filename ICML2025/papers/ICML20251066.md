## Problem

Diffusion models can memorize training samples, raising privacy risks, yet existing detection methods either require full 50-step sampling, are restricted to the final denoising step (e.g., LID-based approaches), or lack a principled theoretical grounding. This paper asks whether memorization can be uniformly characterized through the sharpness of the learned probability landscape, enabling both early-stage detection at the first sampling step and a principled inference-time mitigation strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251066/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an initial noise vector x_T in the latent space (16,384-dimensional for Stable Diffusion) and produces a generated image plus a scalar memorization detection score. Detection requires only a single DDIM sampling step (t=T-1) and a single Hessian-vector product via automatic differentiation, avoiding explicit Hessian formation. For the SAIL mitigation, the initial noise is optimized over a few gradient steps before standard 50-step DDIM sampling proceeds, with a Gaussian prior regularization and early-stopping threshold to keep x_T near the isotropic prior.

## Method

The core insight is that memorized samples occupy regions of high sharpness in the log probability density, quantified by large negative Hessian eigenvalues of log p_t(x_t|c). The paper proves three lemmas linking score-based quantities to Hessian statistics: the score norm equals the negative Hessian trace (Lemma 4.1), Wen's score-difference metric equals a sum of squared eigenvalue differences between conditional and unconditional Hessians (Lemma 4.2), and a Hessian-weighted score-difference norm equals the negative trace of the cubed Hessian (Lemma 4.3). The enhanced metric ||H^Δ(x_t,c) s^Δ(x_t,c)||² amplifies curvature in high-sharpness directions, achieving AUC 0.998 at step 1 with only 4 generations—matching Wen et al.'s 5-step, 16-generation result. For mitigation, SAIL optimizes x_T by minimizing a Taylor-expanded approximation of the sharpness gap plus a Gaussian prior term, requiring only score evaluations and no model retraining or prompt modification.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4, the proposed Hessian-scaled metric ‖H_θ^Δ(x_T)s_θ^Δ(x_T)‖² achieves AUC 0.998 and TPR@1%FPR 0.982 using only 4 generations at the first sampling step (t=T−1), matching Wen et al.'s score-difference metric that requires 16 generations across all 50 steps. On SD v2.0, the same metric attains AUC 0.991 with 4 generations at step 1. For mitigation, SAIL reduces SSCD memorization scores while maintaining or improving CLIP prompt-image alignment compared to all text-modification baselines on both SD v1.4 and v2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Hessian-scaled metric substantially outperforms Carlini et al.'s tiled ℓ₂ distance (AUC 0.940 vs. 0.998 on SD v1.4) and Ren et al.'s attention-entropy baselines (LE: AUC 0.832; AE: AUC 0.598), which rely on full 50-step sampling or specific attention patterns. Against Chen et al.'s Bright Ending (BE) metric, which achieves comparable AUC (0.997) but requires completing all 50 sampling steps to obtain the end-token attention mask, the proposed method reaches the same performance at step 1 with only 4 generations. The closest competitor is Wen et al.'s score-difference norm, which achieves AUC 0.998 on SD v1.4 but only with 16 generations at step 50; the paper's metric matches this with 4 generations at step 1, a 4× reduction in generations and 50× reduction in sampling steps. For mitigation, SAIL outperforms Somepalli et al.'s RTA/RNA prompt perturbation and Wen et al.'s text-embedding optimization on both SSCD (lower memorization) and CLIP (better prompt alignment), because SAIL modifies only the initial noise rather than altering text conditioning, preserving user intent and image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a sharpness-based theoretical framework that unifies and justifies the score-difference memorization metric of Wen et al. (2024) by showing it measures Hessian eigenvalue differences between conditional and unconditional densities. It extends the geometric memorization analysis of LID-based methods (Ross et al., 2024; Kamkari et al., 2024), which are restricted to the final denoising step, by demonstrating that sharpness signatures persist across all timesteps via Hessian eigenvalue statistics. The SAIL mitigation strategy departs from prior approaches that modify prompts or attention weights by instead optimizing the initial diffusion noise, drawing on the Fisher-Rao geometric structure of the Gaussian covariance manifold to motivate the optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to detect memorization at the very first sampling step (t=T-1) with near-perfect AUC (0.998) using only a single Hessian-vector product is practically significant for real-time deployment, eliminating the need for full 50-step generation before flagging risky outputs. SAIL's approach of adjusting only the initial noise—without altering prompts, text embeddings, or model weights—offers a model-agnostic mitigation that preserves user intent and generation quality, addressing a key limitation of prior prompt-modification strategies. Open questions include whether the Gaussian approximation underlying the theoretical results holds for more complex or multi-modal latent spaces, how SAIL scales to larger models (e.g., SDXL, FLUX), and whether the sharpness framework generalizes beyond diffusion to other generative paradigms like flow matching or autoregressive models.
