## Problem

Jointly trained multimodal models suffer from modality laziness (the strong modality suppresses the weak one) and modality clash (inconsistent gradient directions across modalities cause cross-modal interference during batch gradient descent). Existing methods address laziness through loss reweighting, gradient modulation, or unimodal decomposition, but none identify or resolve the batch-level origin of modality clash, leaving multimodal learning both imbalanced and insufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251685/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired audio and video clips (10-second videos on Kinetic-Sounds, short video clips with audio on CREMA-D) and outputs a single class label (6 emotion classes or 31 action classes). During training, one modality is dynamically masked to zero per sample based on KL-divergence evaluation, and batches are reassembled so each contains data from only one active modality; at inference, no masking or reassembly is applied, so the model operates on full multimodal input with zero additional overhead.

## Method

Data Remixing consists of two coupled steps applied after a warm-up phase with full multimodal data. First, sample-level decoupling computes the KL divergence between each modality's prediction distribution and a uniform distribution per sample, then retains only the worst-performing modality (masking the other to zero), which naturally allocates more training samples to the weak modality to counteract laziness. Second, batch-level reassembling partitions the decoupled dataset into non-overlapping subsets by retained modality and constrains each training batch to draw exclusively from one subset, ensuring all gradient updates within a batch share a consistent optimization direction and eliminating cross-modal interference. The two steps alternate per epoch, the dataset is never expanded, and the approach is agnostic to the fusion architecture (concatenation, summation, decision fusion, FiLM, MMTM, CentralNet) and backbone (ResNet-18).

## Results

On CREMA-D, Data Remixing with Concatenation fusion achieves 72.72% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (G-Blend at 69.89%) by 2.83 points and the simple Concatenation baseline (64.52%) by 8.20 points. On Kinetic-Sounds, it reaches 55.63% accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (Resample at 55.17%) by 0.46 points and the Concatenation baseline (50.23%) by 5.40 points. When combined with MLA or Resample, the method further boosts accuracy to 74.19% and 73.25% on CREMA-D, and 57.75% and 58.40% on Kinetic-Sounds, respectively, without expanding the training set (factor = 1 for Remix alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Data Remixing outperforms all six imbalanced multimodal learning baselines on both datasets. On CREMA-D, it beats G-Blend (69.89%) by 2.83 points, OGM-GE (68.15%) by 4.57 points, PMR (68.95%) by 3.77 points, Greedy (68.28%) by 4.44 points, MLA (68.01%) by 4.71 points, and Resample (67.61%) by 5.11 points. On Kinetic-Sounds, it surpasses Resample (55.17%) by 0.46 points, MLA (54.66%) by 0.97 points, G-Blend (53.55%) by 2.08 points, OGM-GE (52.66%) by 2.97 points, PMR (51.93%) by 3.70 points, and Greedy (51.20%) by 4.43 points. Crucially, Remix achieves these gains without dataset expansion (factor = 1), whereas MLA requires 2× data and Resample requires ~1.9× data. Training time is also lower: Remix takes 2537 s on CREMA-D versus 4525 s for Resample and 6128 s for MLA. When stacked with MLA or Resample, Remix adds a further 1.47–5.63 points on CREMA-D and 3.09–3.23 points on Kinetic-Sounds, confirming its complementary nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on modality laziness in multimodal learning (Wang et al. 2020, Du et al. 2021, Fan et al. 2023) and unimodal-input training strategies (Wu et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2024b, Wei et al. 2024) by introducing a second, distinct failure mode: modality clash at the batch level. It builds most directly on Wei et al. (2024)'s sample-level modality valuation framework but replaces Shapley-value-based resampling with a lighter KL-divergence criterion that avoids dataset expansion. The key conceptual departure is the argument that even after achieving modality balance, inconsistent gradient directions across modalities within a shared batch cause cross-modal interference, and that this can be resolved by reassembling batches to contain only one modality's samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Data Remixing method offers a simple, architecture-agnostic training strategy that simultaneously addresses modality laziness and the newly articulated modality clash without dataset expansion or inference overhead, making it broadly applicable to existing multimodal pipelines. The ~6.5% improvement on CREMAD and ~3.4% on Kinetic-Sounds over strong baselines, combined with compatibility with complex fusion architectures (MMTM, CentralNet), suggests practical utility. However, the evidence is limited to two small-scale audio-visual datasets with ResNet-18, and the authors acknowledge a limitation when one modality is primarily auxiliary with limited information. Open questions include whether the batch-level reassembly principle generalizes to more than two modalities, to larger-scale or text-involving multimodal settings, and whether the KL-divergence criterion remains reliable as model capacity increases.
