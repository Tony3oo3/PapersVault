## Problem

The diffusion Fisher (negative Hessian of the marginal log-density) is essential for likelihood evaluation, adjoint optimization, and optimal transport analysis in diffusion models, yet current access methods rely on auto-differentiation (VJP) of the score network, which is computationally expensive (O(d²) for trace) and provides no theoretical accuracy guarantee. No prior work had identified an inherent algebraic structure of the diffusion Fisher that could enable gradient-free, bounded-error access.

## Input / Output

Both algorithms consume the noisy sample x_t, time step t, the trained score network's y-prediction output ȳ_θ(x_t,t), and the noise schedule parameters (α_t, σ_t). DF-TM additionally requires a trained scalar network t_θ(x_t,t) and outputs a single scalar (the Fisher trace) for log-likelihood integration. DF-EA is training-free, additionally uses the endpoint sample x_0 (already computed during adjoint guidance), and outputs a vector (Fisher-vector product) expressed as a scalar-weighted combination of λ_t, x_0, and ȳ_θ — no matrix operations or backpropagation are needed.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is proving that the diffusion Fisher lies entirely within the span of outer products of initial data points (or an integral thereof in the general setting), yielding the closed-form F_t = (1/σ_t²)I − (α_t²/σ_t⁴)[Σw_i y_i y_i^T − (Σw_i y_i)(Σw_i y_i)^T]. DF-TM exploits this by noting the trace reduces to a weighted norm sum, then trains a lightweight scalar U-Net (with MLP head) to predict the single unknown term (1/d)Σw_i‖y_i‖², reducing trace evaluation from O(d²) VJP calls to one forward pass. DF-EA exploits the observation that as t→0 the weight w_i concentrates on the nearest data point, so the weighted outer-product sum is approximated by the single endpoint outer-product x_0 x_0^T, collapsing the Fisher-vector product into three inner products with no gradients. Both methods come with explicit approximation error bounds (Propositions 7 and 8) in terms of the score network's approximation error δ_2 and the DF-TM network's error δ_1.

## Results

DF-TM reduces the per-iteration trace evaluation time from 2195.48 s (VJP) to 0.072 s (a 99% reduction) on the SD-v1.5 model, while DF-EA reduces the Fisher-vector product time from 0.155 s to 0.063 s (59% reduction). On 2-D chessboard data with the VE schedule, DF-TM achieves a relative NLL error of 3.41%–5.81% across timesteps, compared to VJP's 6.68%–70.95%, with the gap widening dramatically at low noise levels. Both proposed methods come with rigorous approximation error bounds (Propositions 7 and 8), whereas the VJP baseline offers no theoretical guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the VJP-based diffusion Fisher access (Song & Lai, 2024), which approximates the Fisher via auto-differentiation of the learned score network. DF-TM decisively outperforms VJP on trace accuracy: on 2-D chessboard data, DF-TM maintains a stable 3.4%–5.8% relative NLL error across all timesteps, while VJP degrades from 6.7% at t=1.0 to 71.0% at t=0.0. In terms of speed, DF-TM is roughly 30,000× faster per iteration (0.072 s vs. 2195.48 s) because it replaces d backpropagation passes with a single forward pass through a scalar network. DF-EA is 59% faster than VJP for the Fisher-vector product (0.063 s vs. 0.155 s) and, in adjoint guidance experiments (Figure 3), consistently achieves higher SAC/AVA aesthetic scores, Pick-Score, and lower CLIP/Face-ID losses across both SD-1.5 and SD-2base. Crucially, both DF-TM and DF-EA carry provable error bounds, a property the VJP baseline entirely lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adjoint guidance framework of Pan et al. (AdjointDPM) and the PF-ODE likelihood evaluation framework of Song et al. (NeurIPS 2021), both of which require access to the diffusion Fisher but rely on expensive VJP-based auto-differentiation. While Lu et al. (2022a) and Benton et al. (2024) established that the DF can be expressed in terms of the score and its covariance, those formulations remained theoretical and did not yield efficient computational algorithms. The key departure here is the derivation of an explicit outer-product span structure for the DF, revealing it is entirely determined by initial data and the noise schedule without any gradient operations, which enables the two proposed gradient-free access algorithms (DF-TM and DF-EA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: trace evaluation drops from O(d²) to O(d) (2195s → 0.072s on SD-1.5), making per-sample NLL evaluation feasible for the first time at commercial scale, and adjoint guidance becomes faster and more accurate without backpropagation. The outer-product span insight also enables the first numerical OT verification of general PF-ODE maps, confirming prior theoretical results for Gaussian/affine data and demonstrating failure for non-affine data. Open questions include whether the DF-EA endpoint approximation (replacing the weighted outer-product sum with a single x₀x₀ᵀ) remains accurate at intermediate timesteps, and whether the outer-product structure can be further exploited for other second-order tasks beyond trace and matrix-vector products.
