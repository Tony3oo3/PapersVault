## Problem

Existing empirical studies on the effect of width scaling on catastrophic forgetting in continual learning produce contradictory conclusions, and theoretical models of CF are limited to the lazy (fixed-feature) regime. The paper addresses the open question of whether and when scaling ameliorates forgetting by identifying the degree of feature learning as the missing variable that reconciles these observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential task images (Split-CIFAR10 with 5×2 classes, Permuted-MNIST with 5 permuted tasks, Split-TinyImageNet with variable task/class splits) and produces a catastrophic forgetting rate (CFr), a feature evolution measure via 1−CKA across residual blocks, and an optimal parameterization factor γ₀* that balances plasticity and stability. A key unusual aspect is that the input is not just the data but also the architectural parameterization: γ₀ smoothly interpolates between the lazy (γ₀→0) and rich (γ₀=1) regimes, and the infinite-width simulations use a 30-sample subset of MNIST with 2 tasks due to cubic scaling in samples and tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces a variable parameterization of residual networks where the output scale γ = γ₀√N and learning rate η = η₀γ₀²N interpolate between NTP (lazy) and μP (rich) training regimes, enabling controlled study of feature learning degree. It extends dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) to the non-stationary multi-task setting, deriving a closed system of self-consistent stochastic equations (Proposition 4.1) that exactly characterize infinite-width network dynamics under sequential task training, including the cross-task NTK that governs forgetting. Empirically, the authors discover a sharp lazy-rich transition (LRT) at γ₀ ≈ 0.1 where feature evolution and CFr jump non-linearly, and show that the optimal γ₀* for the stability-plasticity tradeoff transfers across widths and (with μP+1/√L) across depths. They further demonstrate that task similarity shifts the LRT toward higher γ₀, explaining why stationary training benefits from full feature learning while highly non-stationary settings require laziness.

## Results

On Split-CIFAR10 (ResNet, N=64 base), the optimal degree of feature learning γ₀* ≈ 0.1 minimizes the average error, transferring consistently across widths up to N=4096. Below the lazy-rich transition (γ₀ < γ₀^LRT ≈ 0.1), width scaling monotonically reduces the Catastrophic Forgetting Rate (CFr) under NTP, while under μP (γ₀=1) width provides no reduction in CFr. The infinite-width DMFT simulation (2-layer MLP, Permuted-MNIST, 30 samples) reproduces the finite-width CFr(γ₀) curve with striking precision, confirming the theoretical prediction that the NTK across tasks governs forgetting dynamics. On Split-TinyImageNet, increasing task similarity (more classes per task) shifts γ₀* toward 1, and a 'pretraining effect' emerges where high-similarity tasks exhibit non-monotonic CFr(γ₀) with width becoming beneficial again.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other continual learning methods (e.g., EWC, experience replay); instead, it compares two architectural parameterizations—Neural Tangent Parameterization (NTP) and Maximal Update Parameterization (μP)—as the key 'baselines' for understanding scale. Under NTP (equivalent to PyTorch's standard parameterization), width scaling reduces CFr, consistent with Mirzadeh et al. (2022a). Under μP (γ₀=1), width scaling does not reduce CFr, and forgetting curves for different widths nearly coincide, matching the width-consistency observed by Vyas et al. (2024). The paper reconciles the contradictory finding of Wenger et al. (2023)—that longer training eliminates the benefit of scaling—by showing that extended training implicitly pushes NTP models into a richer regime (more NTK evolution), effectively mimicking μP dynamics. The DMFT infinite-width theory (Proposition 4.1) is validated against finite-width ResNet and MLP experiments, with the theoretical CFr curves converging to the empirical ones as width increases, confirming that the lazy-regime theory of Doan et al. (2021) and Bennani et al. (2020) is insufficient to capture CF in the feature-learning regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) framework of Bordelon & Pehlevan (2022) from stationary to non-stationary multi-task training, providing the first exact infinite-width characterization of catastrophic forgetting in the rich feature-learning regime. It builds on the scaling-limits literature (NTP vs. μP parameterizations from Yang & Hu, 2020) and the lazy-regime CF theory of Doan et al. (2021) and Bennani et al. (2020), which the authors explicitly note do not extend to feature learning. The key conceptual departure is using the γ₀ parameter of μP to interpolate continuously between lazy and rich regimes, thereby reconciling contradictory empirical findings from Mirzadeh et al. (2022a), Wenger et al. (2023), and Ramasesh et al. (2022) about whether width scaling reduces forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing contradiction in the CL literature by showing that scale is neither inherently beneficial nor harmful for forgetting—its effect is entirely mediated by the degree of feature learning, with an optimal γ₀* ≈ 0.1 that transfers across widths. This has direct practical implications: one can tune the feature-learning level at small scale and transfer it to large models without re-tuning. Open questions include a thorough characterization of depth scaling (only preliminary results are provided), the connection between the lazy-rich transition and loss-landscape geometry (the negative multi-task Hessian trace at high γ₀ is suggestive but not fully explained), and whether mitigation methods like EWC or replay interact differently with the γ₀ regime. The infinite-width simulations are limited to 30 samples and 2 tasks due to cubic computational scaling, leaving the theory's validity on larger, more realistic task sequences unverified.
