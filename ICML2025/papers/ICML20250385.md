## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) suffer from two scalability limitations: all parameters are densely activated at every denoising timestep regardless of relevance, and the monolithic architecture treats early-stage coarse structure and late-stage fine detail identically. Prior MoE adaptations for diffusion partition experts along either the temporal (denoising stage) or spatial (token) axis, but none jointly integrates both dimensions, leaving untapped performance gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (one of 1000 ImageNet categories) and random Gaussian noise as input, producing 256×256 RGB images. Images are encoded by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE into 32×32×4 latent tokens before patchification and transformer processing. Evaluation uses 50K generated samples with 250 DDPM steps and classifier-free guidance (CFG scale 1.0 or 1.5 depending on the comparison setting).

## Method

Diff-MoE replaces the dense MLP in DiT with a spatially-routed Mixture-of-Experts (8 task-specific + shared experts, Top-K activation per token) and introduces two key mechanisms: (1) expert-specific timestep conditioning, where each expert receives its own AdaLN modulation from the diffusion timestep embedding, decoupling temporal adaptation from spatial routing so the router focuses purely on token content; (2) a global feature recalibration module that average-pools all tokens into a global context vector and applies channel-wise multiplication inside each expert's GLU, injecting whole-image awareness into locally-specialized experts. Low-rank decomposition (rank r=64) is applied to both the timestep projection and global context weights to keep parameter overhead minimal. The architecture also incorporates RoPE 2D positional encoding, GLU feedforward, and a depthwise convolution layer before the MoE block for local feature extraction.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation (400K iterations, CFG=1.5), Diff-MoE-XL/2 achieves a FID of 2.69 and IS of 262.22, reducing FID by 27% over DiT-MoE-XL/2 (3.42) at the same 1.5B activated parameters. Against dense DiT baselines (CFG=1.0), Diff-MoE-L/2 reaches FID 13.98 / IS 90.72, outperforming SiT-LLaMA-L/2 (FID 14.32 / IS 86.85) with comparable activated parameters. Against temporal MoE baselines (CFG=1.5, no rectified flow), Diff-MoE-B/2 achieves FID 11.97, a 26% reduction over Switch-DiT-B/2 (16.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dense DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023), Diff-MoE consistently wins at every scale: at the small scale, Diff-MoE-S/2 achieves FID 44.27 vs DiT-S/2's 66.59 (CFG=1.0). Against SiT (Ma et al., 2024) and SiT-LLaMA (Chu et al., 2024), Diff-MoE-B/2 reduces FID from 29.53 to 24.88 and IS from 50.13 to 59.57 over SiT-LLaMA-B/2. Against temporal MoE baselines DTR (Park et al., 2023) and Switch-DiT (Park et al., 2024), Diff-MoE-B/2 (no rectified flow) achieves FID 11.97, beating Switch-DiT-B/2 (16.21) by 26% and DTR-B/2 (16.58) by 28%. Against the spatial MoE baseline DiT-MoE (Fei et al., 2024), Diff-MoE dominates across all scales: at XL, FID drops from 3.42 to 2.69 (27% reduction) with identical activated parameters, and at B+ scale, FID drops from 7.19 to 4.00 (44% reduction). Diff-MoE wins every head-to-head comparison reported in the paper, with the largest margins observed against spatial MoE baselines at larger model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diff-MoE extends the DiT architecture by replacing its dense MLP layers with a spatially-routed Mixture-of-Experts, following the shared-expert design popularized by DeepSeekMoE. It departs from prior MoE-diffusion work (DTR, Switch-DiT for temporal partitioning; DiT-MoE, EC-DiT for spatial partitioning) by jointly optimizing expert selection across both temporal and spatial axes through expert-specific timestep conditioning, which decouples denoising-stage adaptation from token-level routing. The global feature recalibration mechanism is a novel addition tailored to image generation, injecting pooled global context into each expert's GLU channel mixing to compensate for the local-only nature of token-wise expert processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint spatiotemporal expert coordination offers a principled path toward scaling diffusion transformers without proportionally increasing per-token compute, which is critical as these models push toward multi-billion parameter regimes. However, the evaluation is confined to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 at 400K iterations, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that large-scale variants (XL at 7M steps) remain unvalidated, leaving open whether the gains persist under longer training or transfer to text-to-image generation. The low-rank decomposition trade-off (28% parameter reduction at a small FID cost) also raises questions about the optimal rank for different model scales.
