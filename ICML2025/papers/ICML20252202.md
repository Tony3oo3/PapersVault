## Problem

Pretraining data selection for LLMs has relied heavily on human-crafted heuristics (rule-based filters, domain classification) or computationally expensive methods (controlled training, data influence models) to identify which documents contribute effectively to learning. The paper asks whether one can directly estimate a document's learning contribution by measuring how well model compression losses on that document predict downstream performance rankings, without any controlled training or strong human priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252202/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text documents from a pretraining corpus (RefinedWeb or C4). During the one-time setup phase, it samples 900K documents (300 per each of the 3,000 most frequent domains) and computes normalized per-character losses using 6 Llama models (7B–65B). At deployment, only a lightweight fastText scorer is needed to assign a binary selection score to every document in the full corpus, enabling scalable filtering without GPU resources. The selected subset (typically 10% of the pool) is then used for standard LLM pretraining.

## Method

PRESELECT defines a document's *predictive strength* as the degree to which the ranking of 6 Llama models by their normalized losses on that document matches the ranking of those same models by their averaged downstream benchmark scores, computed via a pairwise matching score (Eq. 1) that is more robust to noise than Pearson correlation. Documents with the highest predictive strength (~200K) serve as positive examples and those with the lowest (~200K) as negative examples to train a fastText classifier. At deployment, this fastText scorer is applied to the entire corpus to select high-predictive-strength documents for pretraining. The approach operates at individual document granularity (rather than domain level as in Perplexity Correlation), uses only same-family models to avoid cross-architecture evaluation noise, and requires no controlled training or data influence models—making it both principled and extremely lightweight relative to prior model-based selection methods.

## Results

On the 1B model trained on 30B tokens (10% selection from RefinedWeb), PRESELECT achieves an average accuracy of 33.4 across 7 representative tasks, outperforming random selection (28.1) by 5.3% and the strongest baseline DCLM (31.2) by 2.2%. In the Math and Code domains, PRESELECT reduces bits-per-character to 0.830 and 0.744 respectively, compared to 1.023 and 0.901 for random selection. At the 3B scale (100B tokens), PRESELECT reaches 39.5 average accuracy versus 34.7 for random and 37.8 for DCLM. Notably, PRESELECT trained on only 30B tokens surpasses a model trained on 300B tokens without selection, achieving a 10× reduction in compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRESELECT outperforms all 9 baselines on the 1B×30B-token setting. Against DCLM (the strongest baseline), PRESELECT wins by 2.2% average accuracy and by 0.027/0.029 BPC on Math/Code, with particularly large gains on BBH (16.2 vs 12.6) and RACE (27.7 vs 24.3). FineWeb-Edu shows competitive results on exam-style tasks (ARC-E: 48.3 vs 48.0) but underperforms PRESELECT on understanding (LAMBADA: 18.2 vs 23.5) and reasoning (BBH: 12.8 vs 16.2). Both Perplexity Correlation variants (DD and DP) significantly underperform PRESELECT by 4.8% and 4.1% respectively, with DD even dropping below random on HellaSwag due to reduced domain diversity. PPL Filtering provides only marginal gains over random (29.1 vs 28.1 avg). On the C4/Pythia setting, PRESELECT also beats MATES (44.9 vs 44.0 at 410M; 46.8 vs 45.8 at 1B) and all other baselines (DSIR, DsDm, QuRating). At 3B scale, PRESELECT extends its lead over DCLM to 1.7% average accuracy and 0.018/0.016 BPC on Math/Code.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRESELECT builds directly on two recent threads: Huang et al.'s empirical finding that compression efficiency on domain-aligned text linearly predicts downstream performance, and Thrush et al.'s Perplexity Correlation method that uses model-loss correlations for domain-level data selection. It departs from Thrush et al. in three key ways: operating at document granularity rather than domain level, using a simple rank-matching score instead of Pearson correlation across ~90 diverse models, and restricting to 6 same-family Llama models to avoid cross-family evaluation noise. The deployment mechanism (a fastText classifier trained on top/bottom predictive-strength documents) follows the practical pattern established by DCLM and FineWeb-Edu, but the selection signal is derived from a principled compression-intelligence link rather than human heuristics or SFT-referenced labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× compute reduction (30B selected tokens outperforming 300B unselected tokens at 1B scale) is a practically significant efficiency gain for pretraining, and the method's extreme lightness at deployment (a single fastText scorer, no GPU needed) makes it broadly adoptable. A notable open question is whether the predictive-strength signal generalizes beyond the Llama family used for scoring, since the authors explicitly chose same-family models to avoid noise but this limits the external validity of the metric. The paper also acknowledges that positive data includes adult/literary content, flagging content filtering as an orthogonal but necessary preprocessing step. Whether the document-level rank-matching advantage over domain-level correlation persists at much larger scales (10B+ parameters) or on non-English corpora remains untested.
