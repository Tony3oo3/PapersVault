## Problem

Repeated eigenvalues in the normalized graph Laplacian limit the expressiveness of spectral GNNs by restricting the number of distinct filter coefficients and causing loss of frequency components. Prior remedies (orthogonal Jacobi bases, eigenvalue correction) either operate only in linear settings or compromise the topological information encoded in the Laplacian by reducing its spectral gap. This paper extends the expressiveness analysis to nonlinear spectral GNNs and proposes a perturbation-based solution that increases eigenvalue diversity while preserving the original graph topology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix (n × f₁). Before spectral convolution, features are linearly projected and reshaped from n × f₁ to nd × f₃ to match the dimensionality of the nd × nd perturbed sheaf Laplacian, where d is the stalk dimension (typically 2–4). After the final aggregation layer, the nd × f_last output is reshaped back to n × df_last and linearly mapped to n × c node class predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is the Perturbed Sheaf Laplacian (PSL) matrix, constructed by treating the block form of the normalized graph Laplacian (L ⊗ I_d) as a base and applying carefully constrained perturbations via restriction maps. Each restriction map is parameterized as a reflection matrix I − 2uuᵀ/‖u‖², where the learnable vector u has components bounded in [η/10, η] for a small η, ensuring the perturbation norm stays below the minimum eigenvalue gap (per Weyl's inequality) so that repeated eigenvalues split into distinct values. The PSL matrix is then substituted into existing spectral GNN architectures (GCN, GPRGNN, APPNP, BernNet, Graph-Heat, JacobiConv) in place of the standard normalized Laplacian, and is rebuilt every 10 training epochs. Theoretical analysis establishes that PSL-GNNs have strictly more distinct eigenvalues than their vanilla counterparts, yielding stronger one-dimensional prediction ability, fewer lost frequency components, and more diverse frequency processing, with eigenvalue perturbation bounded by 2ε/(1−2ε)·λ̃_max.

## Results

On the seven benchmark node classification datasets, PSL-GNN variants consistently outperform their corresponding spectral GNN baselines. The vertical average gain (VAG) across all models ranges from 0.86% (Photo) to 1.96% (Cornell), with the horizontal average gain (HAG) per model between 1.05% and 1.22%. PSL-GPR achieves the strongest overall performance, reaching 89.11% on Pubmed, 94.23% on Photo, and 88.67% on Cornell. The number of distinct eigenvalues of the learned PSL matrix significantly exceeds that of the normalized graph Laplacian (e.g., 2436 vs. 2262 on Cora; 145 vs. 73 on Texas), confirming the perturbation effectively splits repeated eigenvalues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSL-GNN variants outperform all six corresponding spectral GNN baselines (GCN, APPNP, Graph-Heat, BernNet, GPRGNN, Jacobi) on every dataset, with gains of 0.86–1.96% in VAG. GCN shows the largest relative improvement (HAG 1.93%), because its fixed filter g(λ)=1−λ zeros out all frequency components at eigenvalue 1, which has the highest multiplicity; the PSL reduces this multiplicity, preserving more information. PSL-GPR achieves the best absolute accuracy on most datasets (e.g., 94.23% on Photo, 88.67% on Cornell), surpassing GPRGNN by 1.1–2.35%. In the ablation (Table 2), PSL-GNN matches or slightly exceeds the general sheaf Laplacian (GSL) of Bodnar et al. (2022), confirming that the constrained perturbation captures similar structure with fewer parameters. In the fully-supervised split (Tables 8–9), PSL-GNN also outperforms the eigenvalue-correction (EC) variants of Lu et al. (2024) on all datasets, e.g., PSL-GPR reaches 90.13% on Cora vs. EC-GPR's 89.41%, and PSL-Jacobi reaches 95.69% on Photo vs. EC-Jacobi's 95.54%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of spectral GNN theory and cellular sheaf topology. It directly addresses the repeated-eigenvalue limitation identified by Wang & Zhang (2022) and Lu et al. (2024), but departs from both by constraining the sheaf restriction maps to small perturbations of the identity (reflection matrices with bounded parameters) rather than using fully learnable orthogonal maps (as in Bodnar et al.'s Neural Sheaf Diffusion) or adding diagonal eigenvalue corrections. The perturbation bounds are grounded in Weyl's inequality and relative perturbation theory for diagonally dominant matrices (Dailey et al., 2014), while the expressiveness analysis extends the linear-spectral-GNN results of Wang & Zhang to the nonlinear regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSL provides a lightweight, theoretically grounded mechanism to increase eigenvalue diversity in spectral GNNs without the parameter overhead of full sheaf Laplacians, and the perturbation bound (Theorem 5.5) gives practitioners a principled way to control how far the modified operator deviates from the original graph topology. However, the gains are modest (typically 1–2% accuracy), the method has only been validated on node classification, and the authors acknowledge that graph-level tasks and further reduction of eigenvalue multiplicity remain unexplored. The finding that GCN benefits most—because its fixed filter g(λ)=1−λ discards all components at the highest-multiplicity eigenvalue λ=1—offers a useful diagnostic for understanding when spectral GNNs underperform.
