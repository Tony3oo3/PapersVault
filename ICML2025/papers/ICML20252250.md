## Problem

Existing dual-target molecule generation methods reduce the inherently multi-objective optimization problem to scalarized weighted sums, which fails to capture trade-offs between target affinity, drug-likeness, and synthetic accessibility, often yielding molecules with imbalanced or unrealistic properties. Additionally, most generative approaches do not integrate synthetic planning, producing compounds that are computationally attractive but practically unsynthesizable. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating dual-target design as a Pareto multi-objective search over a synthesizable chemical space.

## Input / Output

Given a target protein pair (e.g., GSK3β-JNK3, EGFR-MET, PIK3CA-mTOR) and known active inhibitors, the method extracts target-aware fragments via FGIB and maps them to Enamine REAL Space building blocks using a Tanimoto similarity threshold (0.4), yielding ~14k blocks and ~25M possible reaction products. The search is guided by four vectorized objectives: predicted dual-target activity (Chemprop D-MPNN) and dual-target docking scores (QuickVina-GPU-2.1). Output is a set of dual-target molecules (SMILES) organized into ranked Pareto fronts, each annotated with its full property vector, rather than a fixed-size generation batch.

## Method

CombiMOTS extends Pareto Monte Carlo Tree Search (PMCTS) to combinatorial chemistry by replacing the scalar UCB with a vectorized Pareto UCB (PUCB) formula that operates element-wise over D objectives, enabling non-dominated node selection from local Pareto fronts rather than greedy highest-score traversal. The search tree nodes represent tuples of building blocks; expansion generates children either by applying all matching reaction templates to current blocks (producing reaction products) or by incorporating all compatible reactants (producing larger block sets). A key design choice is the search space reduction step: FGIB extracts property-informative fragments from known dual-active compounds via a graph information bottleneck objective, which are then aligned to industry-available Enamine REAL Space blocks, ensuring every generated molecule is synthesizable in 1–2 reaction steps. Backpropagation updates node statistics using element-wise maximum of property vectors, and the algorithm terminates after a fixed number of rollouts (50k), returning all encountered terminal products ranked by Pareto front membership.

## Results

On all three dual-target tasks (GSK3β-JNK3, EGFR-MET, PIK3CA-mTOR), CombiMOTS achieves 100% validity and 100% uniqueness, with diversity of 88.67%, 90.75%, and 90.88% respectively—substantially outperforming all baselines. Novelty reaches 99.81–99.99%. In the Pareto front analysis (Table 2), CombiMOTS identifies the largest optimal sets (174, 268, 210 molecules) with competitive R²-distance to the utopia point (0.8075, 0.8419, 0.8143), confirming its ability to find well-balanced trade-offs across docking scores, QED, and synthetic accessibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CombiMOTS dominates on uniqueness (100% across all tasks) versus RationaleRL (48–92%), MARS (26–80%), and REINVENT (56–98%), and matches or exceeds their diversity by 10–25 percentage points (e.g., 88.67% vs. 70.92% for RationaleRL on GSK3β-JNK3). Against the Pareto MCTS baselines, CombiMOTS outperforms MolSearch on uniqueness (100% vs. 72–82%), novelty (99.8–99.99% vs. 74–100%), and diversity (88–91% vs. 76–84%), while Mothra produces fewer than 1% valid molecules (0.68–0.92% validity) and near-zero dual-activity success rates. On the data-scarce DHODH-RORγt task, CombiMOTS generates molecules with substantially better docking scores and QED than AIxFuse, whose scalarized objective leads to property imbalance. In the Pareto front analysis, CombiMOTS finds 1.5–2.5× more Pareto-optimal solutions than any baseline (e.g., 268 vs. 177 for REINVENT on EGFR-MET), confirming superior exploration of the multi-objective landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

CombiMOTS synthesizes three distinct research threads: the synthesizable search space paradigm from Swanson et al. (2024), which uses Enamine REAL Space building blocks and reaction templates with MCTS; the Pareto MCTS formulation from Yang et al. (2024a), which replaces scalarized UCB with vectorized Pareto selection; and the target-aware fragment extraction via FGIB from Lee et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure from prior dual-target methods (RationaleRL, MARS, REINVENT, AIxFuse) is the rejection of scalarized multi-objective rewards in favor of maintaining a vectorized Pareto front throughout the tree search, combined with a combinatorial (reaction-based) rather than autoregressive generation process. This positions the work at the intersection of multi-objective optimization, fragment-based drug discovery, and retrosynthesis-aware generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two well-recognized gaps in dual-target drug design: the loss of trade-off information when aggregating competing objectives into a single scalar, and the disconnect between in silico generation and practical synthesizability. By grounding generation in industry-available building blocks and reactions, CombiMOTS produces molecules that are, by construction, synthesizable in one or two steps, which is a meaningful practical advantage over generative models that produce structurally valid but potentially unmakeable compounds. Open questions include scalability to larger chemical spaces (e.g., Freedom Space), the computational cost (~4 days for 50k rollouts on a single GPU), the reliance on docking scores as a proxy for true binding affinity without wet-lab validation, and whether the Pareto formulation's advantages persist as the number of objectives grows beyond four.
