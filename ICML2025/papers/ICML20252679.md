## Problem

Existing subgraph representation learning methods primarily capture local neighborhood structures while overlooking the significant impact of global structural information, particularly multi-hop relationships beyond immediate neighborhoods. This creates two challenges: effectively encoding structural relationships between distant nodes, and preventing excessive aggregation of global information from diluting the discriminative ability of subgraph representations.

## Input / Output

GPEN takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrices and a set of labeled subgraphs S={S_1,...,S_k} (which may share nodes/edges and consist of multiple components) as input. It produces a fixed-size subgraph representation Z_{S_i} per subgraph via node-level message passing followed by readout aggregation, which is then passed to an MLP for label prediction. The global position encoding is a one-hot vector of length t_max+1 (tree depth) computed offline and concatenated to original node features, while the boundary-aware convolution uses a tunable balance factor b∈[0,1] to weight local vs. global information.

## Method

GPEN introduces two core modules. First, Global Position Encoding constructs a maximum spanning tree over the graph using PageRank-derived edge weights w_ij=R[i]+R[j], then encodes each node's position as a one-hot vector based on its shortest-path distance to the highest-importance root node, providing a unified multi-hop relationship signal for all nodes. Second, Boundary-aware Convolution computes difference vectors d_u^v = h_v - h_u between each node and its neighbors, then aggregates these with vanilla message-passing outputs via a balance factor b to selectively integrate global structural information while preserving subgraph boundary characteristics. An optional tree perturbation step generates augmented training samples by extracting the largest connected component of each subgraph within the tree, reducing variance across random seeds without introducing additional parameters.

## Results

GPEN achieves the best micro-F1 scores on all four real-world datasets: 0.644 on ppi-bp, 0.639 on hpo-metab, 0.691 on hpo-neuro, and 0.912 on em-user, outperforming the strongest baseline S2N (0.643, 0.639, 0.686, 0.890 respectively). On synthetic datasets, GPEN leads on cut-ratio (0.936) and coreness (0.876), ties on component (1.000), and is marginally behind S2N on density (0.956 vs. 0.963). Notably, GPEN consistently reports the lowest standard deviations across all datasets (e.g., ±0.009 on ppi-bp vs. ±0.041 for S2N), indicating more stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPEN outperforms all five main baselines on all four real-world datasets. Against the strongest competitor S2N (Kim & Oh, ICML 2024), GPEN gains +0.001 on ppi-bp, matches on hpo-metab (both 0.639 but with 8× lower variance), leads by +0.005 on hpo-neuro, and achieves a clear +0.022 margin on em-user (0.912 vs. 0.890). GLASS (Wang & Zhang, ICLR 2022) and SSNP (Jacob et al., CIKM 2023) trail GPEN by 0.022–0.025 on ppi-bp and 0.024–0.025 on em-user, while SubGNN (Alsentzer et al., NeurIPS 2020) and Sub2Vec (Adhikari et al., PAKDD 2018) lag further behind by 0.045 and 0.125 respectively on em-user. On synthetic datasets, GPEN wins on cut-ratio (0.936 vs. GLASS 0.935) and coreness (0.876 vs. GLASS 0.840), ties at 1.000 on component with GNN-seg/GLASS/S2N, and is narrowly behind S2N on density (0.956 vs. 0.963). Across all comparisons, GPEN's standard deviations are consistently the smallest (e.g., ±0.009 vs. ±0.072 for S2N on hpo-metab), supporting the paper's theoretical stability claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPEN extends the subgraph representation learning line initiated by SubGNN (2020) and advanced by GLASS (2022), SSNP (2023), and S2N (2024), all of which primarily capture local neighborhood structure or use sampling-based strategies for limited global context. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing a maximum spanning tree (weighted by PageRank-derived node importance) as a global position encoder, providing a unified hierarchical coordinate for every node rather than relying on fragmented random-walk views. The boundary-aware convolution module, which uses inter-node difference vectors to gate information flow, draws on edge-based GNN ideas but applies them specifically to preserve subgraph boundary identity during global aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe—tree-based position encoding plus boundary-aware convolution—that practitioners can plug into existing subgraph GNN pipelines to capture multi-hop structure without diluting local discriminative signals. However, the tree construction is offline and static, limiting applicability to dynamic or streaming graphs, and the theoretical guarantees (e.g., PageRank monotonicity with degree) rely on assumptions that may not hold in heterogeneous real-world networks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes, whether the maximum spanning tree choice generalizes beyond the tested datasets, and how the method interacts with more recent GNN architectures (e.g., graph transformers).
