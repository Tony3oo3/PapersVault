## Problem

While self-attention is central to Transformer architectures, the structural patterns encoded in the query-key weight matrix W_qk = W_q W_k^T during training remain uncharacterized. Existing interpretability methods (sparse autoencoders, circuit analysis, logit lens) analyze activations or individual components but do not reveal how the training objective—autoregressive versus bidirectional—shapes the geometry of W_qk itself, leaving a gap in understanding how information is structurally embedded through self-attention.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the W_q and W_k weight matrices of each self-attention layer in a pre-trained or in-training Transformer, computing the composite bilinear form W_qk = W_q W_k^T (a d×d matrix) without requiring forward-pass activations. It produces two scalar scores per layer—a symmetry score (ratio of symmetric to skew-symmetric Frobenius norm components) and a directionality score (normalized difference between high-norm row and column sums above a mean+γ·std threshold)—which are then aggregated as medians across layers. The framework is modality-agnostic, applied to text, vision, and audio Transformers of varying sizes.

## Method

The paper reformulates self-attention scores as a bilinear form ⟨x_i, W_qk x_j⟩, then derives that the implicit gradient update to W_qk decomposes into a sum of rank-1 outer products of token embeddings (Proposition 2.1). A key structural insight (Proposition 2.2) shows that a token used as context contributes all predicted-token embeddings to the column space but only its own embedding to the row space, and vice versa when it is the prediction target. Under autoregressive training, tokens at earlier positions appear more frequently as context than as predictions, so column updates accumulate more mass than row updates, provably inducing column dominance and directionality (Theorem 2.3). Under bidirectional training, every pair (i,j) contributes an approximately symmetric term β_ij K_ij + β_ji K_ij^T, provably inducing symmetry in W_qk (Theorem 2.4). These predictions are validated across BERT, GPT, LLaMA3, Mistral, ModernBERT, and vision/audio models, and exploited practically by initializing W_q and W_k so that W_qk is symmetric, which accelerates encoder-only convergence by up to 73% on language tasks.

## Results

Symmetric initialization of W_qk reduces final training loss across all three datasets (Jigsaw, Wikipedia, Red Pajama) for 4-, 12-, and 24-layer encoder-only models, with the largest gains on Wikipedia (e.g., 0.984 → 0.812 for 4-layer, 0.192 → 0.166 for 24-layer). Convergence speed-up reaches up to 73% for 4-layer models (Wikipedia), 35% for 12-layer (Red Pajama), and 44% for 24-layer (Wikipedia). Across pre-trained models (BERT, GPT, LLaMA3, Mistral, ModernBERT, Phi), encoder-only architectures consistently exhibit higher symmetry scores while decoder-only models show higher directionality scores, validating the theoretical predictions of Theorems 2.3 and 2.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it contrasts two initialization strategies (random vs. symmetric W_qk) and validates structural predictions across model families. Symmetric initialization outperforms random initialization on 8 of 9 dataset–depth combinations, with the sole exception being the 12-layer Jigsaw model where loss is marginally higher (1.430 vs. 1.419) and speed-up is 0%. The speed-up advantage is most pronounced for smaller models (4-layer: 26–73%) and diminishes but remains positive for larger models (24-layer: 14–44%). On the structural validation side, encoder-only models (BERT, ModernBERT) consistently show positive symmetry scores while decoder-only models (GPT, LLaMA3, Mistral, TinyStories GPT) show negative directionality scores, confirming the theoretical distinction between bidirectional and autoregressive training objectives. The authors note that symmetric initialization gains are less significant for vision Transformers compared to language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the W_qk embedding-space formulation introduced by Elhage et al. (2021) and Dar et al. (2023) by explicitly connecting the combined query-key matrix to bilinear forms and deriving the implicit gradient structure as a sum of rank-1 outer products. Within the broader mechanistic interpretability literature (logit lens, sparse autoencoders, circuit analysis), it carves out a distinct angle: analyzing the geometric structure of W_qk itself rather than individual features or attention patterns. The authors also position their symmetric initialization as a generalization of Trockman & Kolter's (2023) identity-matrix initialization for W_qk, framing the latter as a special case of the broader symmetry principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean mathematical explanation for a long-observed but unexplained asymmetry: encoder-only models develop symmetric W_qk matrices while decoder-only models develop directional, column-dominant ones. The practical consequence—symmetric initialization yielding up to 73% training speedup on small encoder models—offers a simple inductive bias practitioners can adopt. Key open questions include whether symmetric initialization scales to large models (experiments are limited to 4–24 layers), whether analogous structural priors can be exploited for decoder-only models' directionality, and whether the intriguing parallel to cortical connectivity patterns (symmetric for vision, directional for language) can be made rigorous.
