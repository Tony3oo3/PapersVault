## Problem

Existing federated learning methods, whether frequentist (e.g., FedAvg) or Bayesian (e.g., Partitioned Variational Inference), are inherently non-robust to model misspecification: under contaminated or misspecified likelihoods, Bayesian FL produces overconfident and biased posteriors, while frequentist FL provides no principled uncertainty quantification. No prior framework simultaneously offers provable robustness to likelihood misspecification, calibrated uncertainty, and computational tractability in the federated setting.

## Input / Output

Each client m holds a local partition {x_m, y_m} of the full dataset and communicates only a scalar-valued loss update Δ_m(θ) = -τ_m log(q_m/q_s) to the server, never raw data. The server maintains a global loss approximation ℓ_s(θ) and a prior π(θ), and the variational family Q (e.g., multivariate Gaussians for BNNs) constrains the posterior form. The final output is a server-side approximate posterior q_s(θ) from which class predictions with calibrated uncertainty are derived; for Gaussian variational families the KL and Alpha-Rényi divergences admit O(1) closed-form evaluations, keeping per-client computation comparable to standard PVI.

## Method

FEDGVI iterates between clients and server: the server broadcasts the current posterior q_s, each client computes a cavity distribution q^{\m} ∝ q_s / exp{-ℓ_m} to remove its own prior contribution, then minimises a local GVI objective (robust loss L_m + divergence D from the cavity) to obtain q_m, and sends the damped log-ratio update Δ_m = -τ_m log(q_m/q_s) back. The server aggregates all Δ_m into ℓ_s and minimises a global GVI objective (ℓ_s + D_s from the prior) to produce the next q_s. Robustness is achieved by choosing finite-supremum losses at clients (β-divergence, γ-divergence, score matching, or generalised cross-entropy) combined with KL or Alpha-Rényi divergences, which Theorem 4.12 proves yields an outlier-robust server posterior. The cavity distribution is shown to be necessary (Theorem 4.9) rather than heuristic, and for exponential-family likelihoods with weighted score-matching loss the client updates are conjugate Gaussians, giving closed-form O(1) per-client computation. Setting τ = 1/M recovers a logarithmic opinion pool (Proposition 4.3), and the fixed point of the algorithm is a local minimiser of the global GVI objective (Proposition 4.4).

## Results

On MNIST with 10% label contamination, FEDGVI with combined Alpha-Renyi divergence and generalised cross-entropy loss achieves 97.50% accuracy (10 clients) and 98.13% (3 clients), outperforming PVI (95.56%/96.68%) and FEDAVG (96.64%/96.34%). On FASHIONMNIST at 40% contamination, FEDGVI (δ=0.8) retains 87.2% accuracy while FEDAVG collapses to 49.0% and PVI drops to 82.8%. The single-client GVI upper bound is 98.13%, showing FEDGVI nearly matches centralized inference in the federated setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDGVI outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and contamination level tested. Against PVI (Ashman et al., 2022), FEDGVI gains ~2% on MNIST (97.50% vs 95.56%) and ~4.4% on FASHIONMNIST at 40% contamination (87.2% vs 82.8%), with the gap widening as contamination increases. Against FEDAVG (McMahan et al., 2017), FEDGVI leads by ~0.9% on MNIST but the advantage is dramatic on FASHIONMNIST at 40% contamination (87.2% vs 49.0%), where the frequentist method essentially fails. Against FEDPA (Al-Shedivat et al., 2021), FEDGVI improves by ~3.3% on MNIST (97.50% vs 94.25%) and ~1.8% on FASHIONMNIST at 40% contamination. Against β-PREDBAYES (Hasan et al., 2024), FEDGVI gains ~2.6% on MNIST (97.50% vs 94.90%) and ~1.4% on FASHIONMNIST at 40% contamination. On COVERTYPE (Figure 5), FEDGVI outperforms both DSVGD (Kassab & Simeone, 2022) and DSGLD (Ahn et al., 2014) in classification accuracy, attributed to FEDGVI down-weighting low-probability data points. Notably, FEDGVI with D_AR + L_GCE nearly matches the single-client GVI upper bound (98.13%), indicating the federated framework incurs minimal information loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDGVI generalises Partitioned Variational Inference (PVI, Ashman et al., 2022) by replacing the fixed negative log-likelihood and KL divergence with arbitrary robust losses and divergences, while recovering PVI and FEDAVG as special cases. It extends the non-distributed Generalised Variational Inference framework of Knoblauch et al. (2022) and the Generalised Bayesian Inference posterior of Bissiri et al. (2016) into the federated setting, where data is partitioned across clients. The key conceptual departure from prior Bayesian FL is abandoning the assumption that the likelihood and prior are well-specified, instead targeting GBI/GVI posteriors that remain calibrated under Huber contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable robustness theorem (Theorem 4.12) and the necessity of the cavity distribution (Theorem 4.9) provide theoretical grounding that was previously heuristic in PVI and absent in other Bayesian FL approaches, giving practitioners a principled recipe for robust federated inference. The conjugate-update result (Proposition 4.10) means robustness comes at no additional computational cost over standard PVI for exponential-family models. Open questions include extending to personalised and hierarchical FL settings, addressing adversarial/Byzantine attacks via robust aggregation, and the still-open problem of provable robustness to prior misspecification in GVI. The paper also notes that robust losses can underperform under correct specification, so the method is best suited to settings where misspecification is expected.
