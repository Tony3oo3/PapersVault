## Problem

Real-world multi-modal imaging suffers from diverse degradations (noise, blur, haze in visible; stripe noise in infrared) that severely degrade fusion quality. Existing approaches either cascade restoration and fusion sequentially—requiring separate models per degradation type and suffering from inter-task information loss—or use unified frameworks (DRMF, Text-IF) that fail to handle complex multi-degradation scenarios simultaneously. The paper addresses the gap of a single all-in-one model that robustly restores and fuses multi-modal images under arbitrary combinations of degradations without prior knowledge of the degradation type.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of degraded images—a grayscale infrared image (H×W×1) and an RGB visible image (H×W×3)—and produces a single fused RGB image (H×W×3). During training, images are randomly cropped to 128×128 pixels, and the model is trained under six degradation settings (high/medium/low Gaussian noise, haze, defocus blur, and stripe noise) in a single unified model. The infrared and visible branches share encoder and decoder weights, and the model simultaneously outputs restored intermediate images alongside the final fused result.

## Method

TG-ECNet builds on a Restormer-style U-shape Transformer backbone and introduces two task-aware gating mechanisms coupled with multi-expert collaboration (11 experts, 6 selected per task). In the encoder, a degradation-aware gating module dynamically identifies the input degradation type and generates task-specific prompts that route features through the most relevant expert sub-networks for restoration. In the decoder, a fusion-aware gating module combines restored visible and infrared features via learnable weights (α·F_V + β·F_I) and then applies a second expert-selection gate to aggregate the most complementary cross-modal information. A two-stage training strategy decouples the optimization: Stage 1 (30 epochs) trains the restoration pathway with pixel, gradient, and local-detail losses against clean ground truth, while Stage 2 (30 epochs) fine-tunes the fusion pathway with fusion-specific losses, minimizing interference between the two objectives. This design enables a single model to handle all degradation types and produce high-quality fused output without requiring explicit degradation labels at inference time.

## Results

On the DeMMI-RF dataset, TG-ECNet achieves a PSNR of 30.58 dB, MSE of 0.0570, CC of 0.5340, N_abf of 0.0385, and MS-SSIM of 0.2972, outperforming all nine baselines across every metric. On the EMS dataset, TG-ECNet reaches 29.80 dB PSNR and 0.4273 MS-SSIM, surpassing the best competitor (DenseFuse at 28.96 dB / 0.3313) by +0.84 dB and +0.096 respectively. In the most challenging multi-degradation setting (Noise50 + Defocus + Haze + Stripe), TG-ECNet attains 30.22 dB PSNR and 0.2870 MS-SSIM, while the next-best method (MGDN) reaches only 29.68 dB and 0.1750 MS-SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-ECNet outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest pure-fusion baseline MGDN, TG-ECNet gains +0.36 dB PSNR (30.58 vs. 30.22) and +0.054 MS-SSIM (0.2972 vs. 0.2431) on DeMMI-RF, and +1.09 dB PSNR (29.80 vs. 28.71) on EMS. The degradation-aware unified models (AWFusion, Text-IF, DRMF) consistently underperform: AWFusion lags by 1.12 dB PSNR on DeMMI-RF and 2.25 dB on EMS, while DRMF trails by 1.07 dB and 1.16 dB respectively, confirming that their diffusion/text-guided approaches struggle with multi-degradation scenarios. Among pure fusion methods, CDDFuse and SeAFusion are the closest competitors on PSNR (29.84 and 29.59 on DeMMI-RF), but TG-ECNet still leads by 0.74 and 1.00 dB. In the multi-degradation stress test (Table 2), the gap widens further: TG-ECNet achieves 30.22 dB PSNR versus MGDN's 29.68 dB and DRMF's 29.36 dB, with N_abf of 0.0110 versus 0.1570 and 0.2390, indicating substantially better structural fidelity under combined degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TG-ECNet builds directly on the Restormer U-shape Transformer as its backbone, repurposing it for a dual restoration-and-fusion pipeline rather than single-task restoration. Conceptually, it departs from the cascade paradigm (restore-then-fuse) and from recent unified approaches like DRMF (diffusion-based) and Text-IF (text-guided), instead introducing a task-aware gating mechanism with mixture-of-experts routing to handle heterogeneous degradation types within a single architecture. The two-stage training strategy draws from the all-in-one restoration literature (e.g., AdaIR, PromptIR) but applies it specifically to resolve the information-processing conflict between restoration and fusion objectives in a multi-modal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DeMMI-RF benchmark (30K+ multi-modal pairs across six degradation types, including UAV and driving viewpoints) fills a notable gap, as prior fusion datasets rarely account for realistic degradation. The finding that the two-stage training strategy dramatically reduces artifact generation (N_abf) for stripe noise specifically—while single-stage training inflates it by ~2.7×—suggests that task decoupling is not uniformly beneficial but is critical for certain degradation-modalities. Open questions include generalization to unseen or compound degradation types beyond the six predefined categories, and whether the expert selection mechanism can be made interpretable enough for safety-critical deployment in rescue or autonomous driving.
