## Problem

Despite the empirical dominance of next-token-prediction (NTP) pre-training in decoder-only language models, the theoretical understanding of how NTP affects generalization behavior has been largely absent. Existing generalization bounds for transformers target different pre-training paradigms (seq2seq, ICL, MAE) and do not account for the autoregressive, token-dependent structure inherent to NTP. This paper establishes the first fine-grained generalization theory specifically for NTP pre-training in decoder-only models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length token sequence of m tokens (all sequences are preprocessed to uniform length) from a vocabulary of size n_v, with a special begin-of-sequence token prepended. For each position j, the context T_{j-1} = {t_0, ..., t_{j-1}} is fed through the decoder-only model to produce a predicted next token t̂_j, which is scored against the ground-truth token t_j via cross-entropy loss. The output is a probability distribution over the full vocabulary obtained by applying a linear projection and softmax to the final hidden representation.

## Method

The paper decomposes the decoder-only model into a Representation-Learner (multi-layer transformer-decoder blocks) and a Token-Predictor (linear projection + softmax), then develops a novel Rademacher complexity decomposition (Proposition 4.8) that separates the generalization contributions of these two components. Token dependencies within each sequence are modeled via φ-mixing, yielding a two-level generalization bound: one term O(√(Θ/Nm)) governing generalization across N token sequences, and another O(√(1/m)) governing generalization among m tokens within a sequence. The key technical advance is the first covering number bound for multi-layer, multi-head transformer-decoder blocks that explicitly incorporates the causal mask matrix in self-attention under the Frobenius norm, achieving O(L) dependence on depth versus the C^{O(L)} in prior work. The final excess risk bound O(√(Θ/Nm) + √(C_{φ,r}/m)) is validated empirically by training GPT-2-style models of varying sizes (0.002B–0.31B parameters) on the MiniMind Chinese corpus and evaluating on DAMO_NLP.

## Results

The paper establishes a generalization bound of O(√(Θ/Nm) + √(C_{φ,r}/m)) for NTP pretraining, where Θ is the number of model parameters, N is the number of token sequences, m is the maximum sequence length, and C_{φ,r} captures token-level dependence via φ-mixing. Empirically, on the DAMO_NLP test set (m=512), the best model (miniNTP-9, 0.31B params, 100% data) achieves a PPL of 1.05, while the smallest model (miniNTP-7, 0.002B params) reaches 5.76. Reducing training data from 100% to 50% (miniNTP-5 vs. miniNTP-4) degrades PPL from 1.49 to 3.17, and shortening training sequence length from 512 to 64 (miniNTP-4 vs. miniNTP-1) causes PPL to explode from 1.49 to 316,024, confirming the theoretical dependence on both N and m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparisons are theoretical, contrasting the proposed NTP generalization bound O(√(Θ ln(1+NmC)/(Nm)) + √(C/m)) against four prior bounds in Table 1. Compared to Edelman et al. (2022) for seq2seq pretraining, the paper's covering number achieves O(L) dependence on the number of layers versus their C^{O(L)}, a significant improvement, and is the first to incorporate the mask matrix in self-attention under the Frobenius norm. Against Li et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023) for ICL pretraining, the NTP bound explicitly captures both sequence-level (√(Θ/Nm)) and token-level (√(C/m)) generalization, whereas ICL bounds depend on the number of prompts T rather than sequence length m. Compared to Deng et al. (2024) for MAE pretraining, the NTP bound avoids the (mC)^{O(L)} exponential-in-L term present in their MAE result. Empirically, the experiments confirm that the theoretical scaling predictions hold: increasing any of N, m, or Θ monotonically reduces test PPL, with m having the most dramatic effect (PPL drops from 316,024 at m=64 to 1.49 at m=512 for the same model size and data fraction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rademacher complexity framework for transformer generalization (Bartlett et al. 2017; Lin & Zhang 2019) to the specific NTP pretraining setting, building on covering number analyses for transformers by Edelman et al. (2022) and Deng et al. (2024). The key departures are: (1) a novel Rademacher complexity decomposition that separates the Representation-Learner from the Token-Predictor in composite function classes, and (2) the first covering number bound for masked self-attention under the Frobenius norm, reducing the layer dependence from C^O(L) to O(L). The use of φ-mixing (Mohri & Rostamizadeh 2010) to handle intra-sequence token dependence follows the non-i.i.d. learning theory tradition, while the R-L/T-P abstraction parallels Zhang et al. (2024a) and Deng et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical generalization bound specifically for NTP pretraining, the dominant paradigm behind GPT-3, Llama, DeepSeek, and other major LLMs, filling a notable gap where prior bounds covered seq2seq, ICL, or MAE pretraining but not NTP. The bound O(√(Θ/Nm) + √(1/m)) provides actionable guidance that both the number of sequences and the maximum sequence length independently matter for generalization, not just total token count. However, the φ-mixing assumption is difficult to verify empirically, the bound remains quite loose in practice, and validation is limited to small models (0.002B–0.31B) on Chinese text. Open questions include extending the analysis to multimodal settings, using alternative frameworks (stability-based or information-theoretic), and developing language-specific data distributions beyond generic mixing processes.
