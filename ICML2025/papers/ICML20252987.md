## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous data silos, the aggregated global model suffers significant accuracy degradation and increased prediction uncertainty compared to local models, particularly when silos exhibit large distributional shifts. Existing discrete-representation approaches for FL handle minor distributional shifts but fail to generalize to significantly divergent distributions and require full model retraining to accommodate new data sources, limiting their adaptability in dynamic real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252987/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes images (grayscale 28×28 for MNIST/FMNIST, RGB 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable-size for GTSRB) from multiple data silos with different feature distributions (introduced via rotation angles). Internally, images are encoded into latent feature vectors that are segmented into s sub-vectors, each independently mapped to a codeword. The model outputs a class label (Top-1 prediction) and a predictive entropy uncertainty estimate computed via 20 Monte Carlo Dropout forward passes with dropout rate 0.1, which serves as the control signal for codebook extension.

## Method

UEFL introduces an extensible codebook that begins with a small shared set of v codewords (initialized Gaussian) and iteratively grows by adding v new codewords per client when that client's MC Dropout uncertainty exceeds (1+γ)× the minimum uncertainty across all clients. New codewords are initialized via K-means clustering on the trained encoder's latent features, aligning them with the actual data distribution and accelerating convergence. The codebook is segmented: each feature vector is split into s sub-vectors, each quantized independently, exponentially increasing representational capacity without proportional codebook growth. Critically, extended codewords are client-specific and excluded from FedAvg aggregation, preserving explicit distributional differentiation in the global model. The training loop alternates between standard FedAvg-style local updates (with VQ-VAE stop-gradient codebook loss) and uncertainty-triggered codebook extension, typically converging within 1–3 extension rounds.

## Results

On five heterogeneous datasets (MNIST, FMNIST, GTSRB, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), UEFL achieves mean top-1 accuracy of 0.920, 0.850, 0.942, 0.720, and 0.326 respectively, outperforming FedAvg by 3%–22.1% and DisTrans by up to 22.1% in accuracy. Mean predictive entropy (uncertainty) is reduced by 38.83%–96.24% compared to FedAvg (e.g., 0.0239 vs. 0.636 on GTSRB; 0.0222 vs. 0.177 on CIFAR-10). For domain generalization, UEFL reaches 95.3% average accuracy on Rotated MNIST and 84.5% on PACS, surpassing FedSR and FedIIR. The method adds only 3.34% parameters and 3.58% runtime overhead over FedAvg on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg, UEFL wins on all five multi-domain datasets in both accuracy and uncertainty, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (accuracy 0.326 vs. 0.088; uncertainty 0.655 vs. 1.91) and GTSRB (accuracy 0.942 vs. 0.660; uncertainty 0.0239 vs. 0.636). Against DisTrans, UEFL achieves higher accuracy on every dataset (e.g., 0.920 vs. 0.815 on MNIST; 0.942 vs. 0.898 on GTSRB), while DisTrans cannot report uncertainty due to its lack of dropout layers. For domain generalization (Table 2), UEFL outperforms FedSR (95.3 vs. 94.7 on Rotated MNIST; 84.5 vs. 83.4 on PACS) and FedIIR (95.3 vs. 95.0; 84.5 vs. 83.7). In the 100-client scalability setting (Table 4), UEFL beats FedCR on all four datasets (e.g., 98.29 vs. 97.47 on EMNIST-L; 63.37 vs. 62.96 on CIFAR-100). For label heterogeneity (Table 12), UEFL outperforms VHL on CIFAR-10 (62.67 vs. 61.23) but falls short of FedBR (62.67 vs. 64.61), indicating that the extensible codebook is most effective for feature rather than label heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE codebook mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the discrete-representation approach to FL heterogeneity (Liu et al., 2021), but departs from both by making the codebook *extensible*—starting small and growing via K-means-initialized codewords—rather than fixed. It further integrates Monte Carlo Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) as a privacy-preserving uncertainty signal to decide *when* and *for whom* to extend the codebook, a coupling not present in prior discrete-FL or uncertainty-estimation work. The federated training loop follows FedAvg, and the domain-generalization experiments position the method alongside FedSR and FedIIR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extensible-codebook-plus-uncertainty framework offers a lightweight (≈3% parameter overhead) and privacy-respecting mechanism for handling feature heterogeneity in FL, which is a persistent practical bottleneck. However, the heterogeneity is introduced via geometric rotation, a relatively simple and controlled shift; whether the approach generalizes to more complex, real-world distributional changes (e.g., sensor differences, style shifts) remains an open question. The method also requires multiple communication rounds for codebook convergence, and its benefit on label heterogeneity is more modest, suggesting the core contribution is most relevant to feature-space shifts.
