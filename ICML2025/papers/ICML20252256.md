## Problem

Equivariant Gaussian process kernels that encode physical symmetries (e.g., rotational equivariance of vector fields or molecular dipole moments) typically require evaluating double Haar integrals over the symmetry group, making them computationally prohibitive for practical GP inference. This paper addresses the gap between the theoretical requirement of stochastic equivariance and the computational cost of existing integration-based kernel constructions, providing both a rigorous kernel characterization of stochastic equivariance and a class of kernels that enforce it without any group integration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial coordinates (d-dimensional points for vector fields) or flattened atomic position vectors (3s-dimensional for s-atom molecules) as inputs, and produces a p×p matrix-valued kernel evaluated at pairs of inputs. The kernel is constructed by first projecting each input onto a fundamental region of the group action via a section map, evaluating a base kernel (e.g., diagonal squared-exponential) on the projected coordinates, and then left- and right-multiplying by the corresponding group representation matrices. The resulting GP yields vector-valued posterior means and covariances that are stochastically equivariant, meaning every posterior sample respects the group symmetry exactly.

## Method

The paper establishes (Theorem 3.1) that stochastic equivariance of a centred second-order random field is equivalent to its matrix-valued kernel satisfying K(g⋆x, h⋆x') = ρ_g K(x,x') ρ_h^⊤ for all group elements g,h. Building on this, Proposition 4.1 constructs an integration-free equivariant kernel K_Π by projecting inputs onto a fundamental region A of the group action via a section s, evaluating a base kernel K_Ā on the projected points, and wrapping the result with the representation matrices ρ_{s(x)}^⊤ and ρ_{s(x')}. This replaces the expensive double Haar integral of the classical construction with a single projection and two matrix multiplications, yielding a 500× speedup in practice. The approach is demonstrated on SO(2)-equivariant velocity fields, SO(3)-equivariant water molecule dipole moments (with additional translation and permutation invariances handled via a coordinate transform φ), and mixed equivariant/non-equivariant ocean drifter data, where the integration-free kernel matches or exceeds the integration-based kernel in predictive accuracy while being orders of magnitude faster.

## Results

On the rotation-equivariant vector field F¹, the proposed integration-free kernel K_Π achieves RMSE 0.11 [0.06] and LogS −6.03 [0.51], outperforming the Helmholtz kernel (RMSE 0.23, LogS 88.44 with extreme variance) and the naive squared-exponential kernel (RMSE 0.27, LogS 2.06). On F², K_Π achieves RMSE 0.13 [0.05] and LogS −7.41 [0.81], beating the Haar-integration kernel K_∫ (RMSE 0.22 [0.2], LogS −3.41 [2.06]) while being 500× faster to compute (55 s vs. 45 h for 500 test locations with 100 training points). On the water molecule dipole moment task, K_Π significantly outperforms all non-equivariant baselines across all training set sizes, with RMSE small enough for practical quantum chemistry use.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Helmholtz kernel (Berlinghieri et al., 2023), K_Π wins decisively on both RMSE and LogS for both synthetic fields, and critically provides stochastically equivariant posterior samples (Corollary 5.1) whereas the Helmholtz GP only guarantees equivariance in the posterior mean. The Helmholtz kernel also exhibits extreme LogS variance (e.g., 256.96 on F¹), indicating severe miscalibration. Against the Haar-integration kernel K_∫ (following Reisert & Burkhardt, 2007), K_Π is 500× faster in computation and achieves comparable or better predictive performance: on F¹, K_∫ has a marginal RMSE edge (0.08 vs. 0.11) but K_Π is far more stable; on F², K_Π clearly wins on both RMSE (0.13 vs. 0.22) and LogS (−7.41 vs. −3.41), with K_∫ suffering numerical instability near domain boundaries due to integration approximation errors. On the Gulf of Mexico ocean data (Table 2), a mixture of the Helmholtz and K_Π kernels (GP 5) achieves the best LogS for most values of the equivariance fraction α, confirming that combining equivariant and non-equivariant components yields the most calibrated probabilistic predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fundamental-region projection approach of Ginsbourger et al. (2012) from scalar-valued invariance to vector-valued equivariance, and provides a clean theoretical characterization (Theorem 3.1) linking stochastic equivariance of centred second-order random fields to the equivariance condition on their matrix-valued kernel as defined by Reisert & Burkhardt (2007). It departs from the standard Haar-integration construction (Eq. 3) by showing that projecting inputs onto a fundamental region and sandwiching a base kernel with representation matrices achieves the same equivariance guarantee without the computationally prohibitive double group integral. The paper also positions itself against the Helmholtz kernel (Berlinghieri et al., 2023), which is equivariant only in the posterior mean rather than stochastically, and against symmetry-adapted GPs (Grisafi et al., 2018) that rely on expensive double sums.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× speedup over integration-based equivariant kernels makes stochastically equivariant GP modelling practically feasible for moderate-to-large datasets, and the dipole moment experiment demonstrates direct applicability to quantum chemistry where equivariance is a physical necessity. Key open questions include scaling to large molecule datasets (the authors note sparse equivariant GPs are ongoing work), the sensitivity to pathological fundamental region choices (disconnected regions degrade performance), and whether the approach extends cleanly to non-compact or non-Lie groups. The paper also highlights that mixing equivariant and non-equivariant kernels (as in the ocean data experiment) offers a flexible framework for data with partial equivariance, an underexplored direction.
