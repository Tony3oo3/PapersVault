## Problem

Graph Transformers suffer from quadratic time and memory complexity due to pairwise self-attention computations, limiting their scalability to large graphs. Existing efficient alternatives—linear attention (Performer, BigBird) and sparse attention (Exphormer)—either introduce additional computational overhead that dominates on medium-sized graphs or degrade to quadratic complexity as graph density increases. The paper asks whether the quadratic pairwise structure can be fundamentally eliminated rather than merely approximated or sparsified.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{d×N}, the graph adjacency structure (encoded via positional encodings such as LapPE, RWSE, or SPE), and a learnable virtual-node projection that aggregates global graph information. Outputs are node embeddings of dimension d_o (via a compatibility matrix W_c applied to concatenated primal projection scores), which feed downstream heads for graph classification, node classification, edge ranking (MRR), or multi-property regression (MAE). The data-dependent projection f_X = F + BX·1_N·1_Ns^T makes the attention basis graph-specific while preserving permutation equivariance, distinguishing it from sequence-based primal attention that relies on uniform token sampling.

## Method

Primphormer reinterprets graph self-attention as a dual-space kernel machine and constructs an equivalent primal representation with O(Nps) time and O(2N_s·s + 2Np) memory complexity (N_s ≪ N). The key mechanism is an asymmetric kernel trick (handling the non-Mercer attention scores) combined with a virtual-node data-dependent projection f_X that aggregates global graph information into the projection weights W_e and W_r, forming a data-adaptive output basis in the dual space. The primal-dual equivalence is established via a variational optimization problem whose KKT conditions yield a generalized eigenvalue system; rather than solving this cubic-complexity dual system, the implementation jointly minimizes the task loss plus a regularization term η·Σ_l J_l² that drives the primal objective to zero, thereby recovering the standard self-attention in the dual space. Theoretically, Primphormer is shown to be a universal approximator for continuous functions on both sequences and graphs, and to match the 1-WL expressivity of standard Transformers in distinguishing non-isomorphic graphs.

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB), Primphormer achieves the best results on 4 of 5 datasets: PascalVOC-SP F1 (0.4602), COCO-SP F1 (0.3903), Peptides-Func AP (0.6612), and PCQM-Contact MRR (0.3757), outperforming Exphormer by 6.3, 4.5, 0.9, and 1.2 points respectively. On the large-scale OGBN-Products dataset (~2.4M nodes, 61M edges), Primphormer achieves 74.89% accuracy while using only 13.35 GB peak memory and 22.1 s/epoch, compared to Exphormer's 31.09 GB and 25.4 s/epoch. On the BREC expressivity benchmark, Primphormer with SPE positional encoding scores 219.4/270 (ALL), matching or slightly exceeding the standard Transformer (213/270) and substantially outperforming Prim-Atten (171.4/270).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Primphormer clearly outperforms linear attention mechanisms (BigBird, Performer) and the sequence-specific Prim-Atten across all LRGB and GNN benchmarks, with the largest gaps on PascalVOC-SP (0.4602 vs. 0.3724 for Performer) and COCO-SP (0.3903 vs. 0.2762 for BigBird). Against the closest graph-specific competitor Exphormer, Primphormer wins on PascalVOC-SP (+6.3 F1), COCO-SP (+4.5 F1), Peptides-Func (+0.9 AP), PCQM-Contact (+1.2 MRR), and OGBN-Products (+0.22% ACC), while Exphormer retains a narrow edge on CIFAR-10 (74.69 vs. 74.13) and MalNet-Tiny (94.02 vs. 93.62). In terms of efficiency, Primphormer matches Exphormer's runtime but uses substantially less peak memory (13.35 GB vs. 31.09 GB on OGBN-Products), since its complexity depends only on node count rather than edge count. On the BREC expressivity benchmark, Primphormer with SPE (219.4/270) slightly exceeds the standard Transformer (213/270) and dramatically outperforms Prim-Atten (171.4/270), confirming that the primal representation preserves 1-WL-level expressivity. Overall, Primphormer achieves the best or near-best accuracy on 10 of 12 evaluated datasets while consistently offering the lowest memory footprint among all attention-based GTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework for self-attention from sequences (Chen et al., 2023) to graphs by addressing two key weaknesses: permutation equivariance (nodes are unordered) and output-basis flexibility. It builds on the GPS architecture (Rampasek et al., 2022) as its experimental backbone and adopts the virtual-node concept (Cai et al., 2023) to construct a data-dependent projection that aggregates global graph information. Conceptually, it departs from both linear-attention approximations (Performer, BigBird) and sparse-attention strategies (Exphormer) by eliminating pairwise computations entirely through a primal-space representation, rather than approximating or restricting the attention matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Primphormer provides a principled path to linear-complexity graph Transformers with matching expressivity (1-WL) and universal approximation guarantees for functions on graphs, which prior efficient GTs lacked. The demonstrated efficiency on ogbn-products (~2M nodes, 61M edges) — 13.35 GB peak memory vs. OOM for full attention and 31.09 GB for Exphormer — makes large-scale graph Transformer training practically feasible. Open questions include how to incorporate edge features into the primal representation (the authors note this as future work) and whether the regularization-based KKT approximation (Eq. 2.9) can be tightened or replaced with exact solutions without incurring cubic dual-space complexity.
