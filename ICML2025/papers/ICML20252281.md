## Problem

Noise conditioning—providing the noise level t as an explicit input to the denoising network—is widely regarded as indispensable for denoising diffusion and flow-matching models to function. However, no prior work has systematically examined what happens when this conditioning is removed, nor provided a theoretical characterization of the error it introduces. This paper challenges the assumption by asking whether denoising generative models can operate effectively without knowing the noise level, drawing an analogy to the long-studied problem of blind image denoising.

## Input / Output

The network receives a noisy data point z = a(t)x + b(t)ε (an RGB image at 32×32, 64×64, or 256×256 resolution) and outputs a denoised estimate or flow-field prediction. In the noise-unconditional variant studied here, the t-embedding input is simply zeroed out, so the network sees only z without any indication of the corruption level. At inference, the model starts from pure Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises over N steps (ranging from 2 for consistency models to 500 for SDE samplers) to produce a final generated image, evaluated by FID against 50,000 real samples.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified reformulation that isolates the neural network NN_θ from method-specific schedule functions (a(t), b(t), c(t), d(t)), enabling a clean comparison of noise-conditional vs. noise-unconditional behavior across iDDPM, DDIM, ADM, EDM, Flow Matching, and Consistency Models. The theoretical analysis shows three key results: (1) the posterior p(t|z) is sharply concentrated with variance ≈ t²/2d for high-dimensional data, (2) the error between the noise-conditional effective target R(z|t) and the noise-unconditional target R(z) is O(1) while the target norm is O(d), and (3) an accumulated error bound for the iterative sampler that depends only on the schedules and dataset statistics, computable without training. Empirically, most models degrade gracefully without noise conditioning, flow-based methods (Rectified Flow) even improve, and only DDIM with an ODE sampler fails catastrophically (FID 3.99→40.90), consistent with its orders-of-magnitude-larger theoretical error bound. The paper also proposes uEDM, a simple modification of EDM that sets the output scaling c_out(t)=1 (removing the t-dependent scale the network must model), achieving FID 2.23 on CIFAR-10 without noise conditioning—narrowing the gap to EDM's 1.97.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 class-unconditional generation, the proposed uEDM model achieves an FID of 2.23 without noise conditioning, narrowing the gap to the noise-conditional EDM baseline (1.99 FID). Flow-based models (FM/1-RF) surprisingly improve without noise conditioning, going from 3.01 to 2.61 FID with the Euler sampler. Most other methods exhibit graceful degradation (e.g., iDDPM 3.13→5.51, EDM 1.99→3.36, ADM 2.70→5.27), while only DDIM with the ODE sampler fails catastrophically (3.99→40.90 FID). The theoretical error bound (Statement 3) correlates strongly with empirical behavior: DDIM's bound is ~3×10⁶ versus ~10² for FM and uEDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a single method that 'beats' all baselines; rather, it evaluates the impact of removing noise conditioning across 8 model families. Flow-based models (FM/1-RF, Liu et al. 2023) are the most robust, actually improving FID from 3.01 to 2.61 without conditioning, likely because their regression target is t-independent. EDM (Karras et al. 2022) degrades modestly (1.99→3.36 FID with Heun), and the proposed uEDM variant narrows this gap to just 0.19 FID (2.04→2.23). iDDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal 2021) and ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021) show moderate degradation (~2 FID points). Consistency models iCT (Song & Dhariwal 2024) and ECM (Geng et al. 2025) degrade by ~0.7 FID, contradicting prior findings that iCT is highly sensitive to t-conditioning. DDIM (Song et al. 2021) with the ODE sampler is the sole catastrophic failure (3.99→40.90 FID), but adding stochasticity (SDE sampler) restores reasonable performance (3.18→5.41). The classifier-free guidance experiment on ImageNet 256×256 with SiT-B/2 (Ma et al. 2024) confirms that removing noise conditioning incurs almost no degradation across guidance scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual bridge between decades of blind image denoising research (noise-level estimation, denoising without known noise parameters) and modern denoising-based generative models, asking whether the noise conditioning that has been standard since NCSN and DDPM is truly necessary. It builds on the EDM framework to propose uEDM, a noise-unconditional variant, while systematically evaluating the impact of removing noise conditioning across diffusion (iDDPM, DDIM, ADM, EDM), flow matching (Rectified Flow), and consistency models (iCT, ECM). The theoretical analysis—effective regression targets, posterior concentration of p(t|z), and accumulated error bounds—is novel in this generative-modeling context, though the individual mathematical tools (concentration of measure, Lipschitz bounds) are standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that noise conditioning is not essential for most denoising generative models—and that flow matching can even improve without it—the paper opens a new design axis for the field and reconnects score-based models to the classical single-energy-function EBM formulation with Langevin dynamics. The error bound, computable purely from schedules and dataset statistics without training, offers a practical pre-training diagnostic for model robustness. Open questions include whether purpose-built noise-unconditional architectures can close the remaining gap to noise-conditional SOTA, how the findings extend to higher-order samplers and video generation, and whether the flow-matching improvement reflects a fundamental property of t-independent regression targets.
