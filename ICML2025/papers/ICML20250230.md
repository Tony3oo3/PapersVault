## Problem

Random cropping for visual augmentation in vision-language models (e.g., WCA) can inadvertently crop background objects unrelated to the target category, and the similarity-based weighting used to filter such crops is unreliable because it is confounded by crop size. This causes the model to overemphasize spurious local details while losing global semantic context, degrading zero-shot classification and out-of-distribution generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a set of class labels, each expanded into M LLM-generated fine-grained text descriptions (M=50 in experiments). At inference time, the image is processed through both a DINO ViT (for attention maps) and a CLIP ViT (for feature extraction), producing 2N features per image (N from raw-space crops, N from feature-space crops). The output is a single predicted class label determined by the highest weighted cosine similarity score across all crops and descriptions. The method is entirely training-free and requires no downstream task data.

## Method

ABS comprises three components operating at inference time. First, Attention-Based Raw Space Selection uses DINO's last-layer attention map to identify top-k salient patches, samples N crop centers via probability-weighted sampling over these patches, and performs random-size cropping (ratio α to β of image dimensions) centered on each sampled patch, ensuring crops target main objects rather than background. Second, Attention-Based Feature Selection feeds the original (uncropped) image through CLIP up to the penultimate transformer layer, crops the intermediate feature map at the same spatial positions and sizes as the raw-space crops, resizes each crop back to full resolution via bicubic interpolation, and passes it through the final layer—preserving global category semantics that raw-space cropping would lose. Third, Soft Matching computes the cosine similarity between each of the 2N crop features and all LLM descriptions across all K categories, applies softmax to obtain per-crop description weights, and uses these weights to filter out irrelevant descriptions before computing the final classification score as a weighted sum of similarities.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution generalization benchmark (5 ImageNet variants), ABS achieves average top-1 accuracy of 57.49% (ViT-B/32), 66.00% (ViT-B/16), and 75.28% (ViT-L/14), improving over the strongest zero-shot baseline WCA by +2.38%, +1.97%, and +0.80% respectively. The largest single-dataset gain is +6.21% on ImageNet-A (ViT-B/32). On the zero-shot classification benchmark (6 datasets), ABS achieves the best result on 5 out of 6 datasets, with notable gains on fine-grained categories (e.g., DTD: 54.26% vs. WCA's 52.79% with ViT-B/16). As a training-free method, ABS (66.00% avg on OOD) outperforms all tested few-shot and test-time adaptation methods, including TDA (65.01%) and DiffTPT (62.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABS outperforms all 14 baselines on the OOD generalization benchmark (ViT-B/16 average). Against the closest zero-shot competitor WCA (Li et al., 2024), ABS gains +1.97% average (66.00% vs. 64.03%), with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (+5.67%). Against CuPL (Pratt et al., 2023), the gain is +4.07% average. Against the basic CLIP and CLIP-D baselines, improvements range from +6.92% to +7.92% average. Among finetuning methods, ABS (training-free) beats TDA (65.01%), DiffTPT (62.58%), UPT (62.51%), KgCoOp (62.33%), CoCoOp (62.13%), CoOp (61.72%), ProGrad (61.79%), TPT (61.59%), and GDA (60.37%) on average; the only dataset where ABS trails a finetuning method is ImageNet, where UPT scores 72.63% vs. ABS's 71.92%. On the zero-shot classification benchmark, ABS leads on 5 of 6 datasets (ImageNet, CUB, Oxford Pets, DTD, Place365) but is slightly behind WCA on Food101 (85.66% vs. 86.40% with ViT-B/16), which the authors attribute to Food101's multi-object, single-label annotation ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WCA (Li et al., 2024) framework, which pairs random image cropping with LLM-generated fine-grained descriptions for zero-shot VLM classification, by replacing the random cropping with DINO attention-guided selection to avoid background artifacts. It further departs from WCA by introducing a feature-space cropping operation that preserves global semantic context lost in raw-space crops, and a soft matching mechanism to filter irrelevant text descriptions per crop. The text-side foundation (LLM description generation) is inherited from CuPL (Pratt et al., 2023), and the overall approach sits within the broader family of training-free prompt/augmentation strategies for CLIP-style models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABS provides a practical, training-free recipe that consistently outperforms both zero-shot baselines and fine-tuning methods (CoOp, UPT, TPT, etc.) on OOD generalization, suggesting that careful visual augmentation can substitute for parameter updates in many settings. The complementary-gain finding from the ablation (raw-space and feature-space crops each add ~0.3% alone but ~1.3% together) highlights a useful design principle: local and global representations are not redundant. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video or multi-modal settings, whether segmentation models like SAM could replace the single DINO attention map, and whether the crop-based augmentation assumption generalizes to VLMs pretrained with different augmentation strategies.
