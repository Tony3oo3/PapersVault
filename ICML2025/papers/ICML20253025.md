## Problem

Existing hierarchical graph clustering pooling methods (e.g., DiffPool, MinCutPool) determine node-to-cluster assignments via learnable matrices that depend primarily on graph connectivity, failing to exploit the rich geometric information inherent in geometric graphs such as molecular structures. This limits their ability to capture higher-order spatial relationships that are critical for distinguishing subtle structural variations in densely packed point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253025/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular or chemical graphs (atoms as vertices, bonds as edges) with 3D atomic coordinates embedded in R³ and atom-type one-hot features as node attributes. For non-geometric graphs (e.g., social networks), a spectral embedding via the normalized graph Laplacian's three smallest non-trivial eigenvectors provides surrogate 3D coordinates. The output is a graph-level embedding computed as H^(L)ᵀ·(H^(L)W), passed through a 1-layer MLP to yield 2-dimensional classification scores (or a scalar for regression).

## Method

RTPool performs hierarchical pooling via Rhomboid Tiling (RT) clustering, which leverages high-order Voronoi tessellations and their dual Delaunay complexes to define geometrically meaningful clusters at successive orders. The clustering matrix C_{k₁}^{k₂} = (I_{k₂})ᵀ·I_{k₁} is constructed from incidence matrices between Delaunay complex vertices and maximal rhomboids, where each entry N(Q,Q′) counts the number of depth-(d+1) rhomboids simultaneously containing both vertices, naturally encoding geometric proximity as a weight. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.2) constrains the step size Δk = k₂−k₁ to 1 or 2 in R³ to guarantee complete clustering. At each pooling layer, node features are aggregated via Z^(l+1) = Ĉ_l·H^(l) (row-normalized clustering matrix), then updated by a GIN layer on a 'generated graph' that preserves the original molecular bond semantics rather than using the Delaunay 1-skeleton. This geometry-driven, non-learnable clustering mechanism replaces the learnable assignment matrices of prior pooling methods, enabling the model to capture higher-order geometric structure without additional trainable parameters for cluster assignment.

## Results

RTPool achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on all 7 TUDataset graph classification benchmarks, with headline results of 88.29% (BZR), 92.76% (COX2), 94.74% (MUTAG), 78.86% (PTC_MR), 82.94% (PTC_MM), 77.72% (PTC_FM), and 82.29% (PTC_FR). The runner-up method Wit-TopoPool is outperformed by an average relative improvement of 5.94%, with the largest absolute gap of 8.29 percentage points on PTC_MR (78.86% vs. 70.57%). On the molecular regression benchmarks (Esol, FreeSolv, Lipo), RTPool also achieves the best or near-best RMSE, e.g., 3.6666 on FreeSolv and 1.0789 on Lipo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTPool outperforms all 21 baselines across all 7 TUDataset classification benchmarks, with Wit-TopoPool consistently the closest competitor. The largest absolute gains are on the PTC datasets: PTC_MR (78.86% vs. 70.57% for Wit-TopoPool, +8.29 pp), PTC_MM (82.94% vs. 79.12%, +3.82 pp), PTC_FM (77.72% vs. 71.71%, +6.01 pp), and PTC_FR (82.29% vs. 75.00%, +7.29 pp). On COX2, RTPool achieves 92.76% versus Wit-TopoPool's 87.24% (+5.52 pp), while on MUTAG the gap is smaller (94.74% vs. 93.16%, +1.58 pp) and on BZR it is minimal (88.29% vs. 87.80%, +0.49 pp). Among traditional GNN baselines, GIN is the strongest (e.g., 89.39% on MUTAG, 85.60% on BZR), still trailing RTPool by 5–13 percentage points. Topology-based methods (PersLay, MPR, FC-V, SIN) and pooling methods (DiffPool, MinCutPool, HaarPool, HopPool, MvPool) are all substantially outperformed, confirming that the geometric information captured by rhomboid tiling provides a meaningful advantage over purely connectivity-driven or topological approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the rhomboid tiling structure from computational geometry (Edelsbrunner & Osang, 2021) as a fixed, non-learnable hierarchical clustering mechanism for graph pooling, departing fundamentally from the learnable cluster-assignment paradigm of DiffPool, MinCutPool, and SAGPool. It generalizes the Alpha shape concept (Edelsbrunner & Mücke, 1994) by leveraging the natural hierarchy of high-order Delaunay complexes to define multi-level clusters, with the homotopy equivalence results from Corbet et al. (2023) providing the theoretical bridge between tiling layers. Unlike Wit-TopoPool, which uses witness complexes for topological pooling, RT clustering is driven entirely by the geometric positions of points rather than graph connectivity, making it a geometry-first rather than topology-first approach to hierarchical graph coarsening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for geometric graphs (molecular structures with 3D coordinates), exploiting spatial geometry through a principled tiling structure can consistently outperform connectivity-based pooling by a meaningful margin (~6% over the best baseline), suggesting that current GNN pooling methods underutilize available geometric information. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.1–3.4) provide actionable guidance on step-size selection, reducing the hyperparameter search space. However, the method's applicability is constrained: it requires explicit point coordinates (the spectral embedding workaround for non-geometric graphs like IMDB yields weaker results), and all experiments are on small TUDataset benchmarks (188–467 graphs), leaving open questions about scalability to larger molecular or materials datasets and whether the geometric advantage persists at scale.
