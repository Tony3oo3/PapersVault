## Problem

Anchor-based multi-view clustering methods typically exploit only anchor-sample proximity while ignoring the geometric structure among anchors themselves, and they suffer from cross-view anchor misalignment that existing alignment schemes address only by selecting a baseline view. Additionally, the standard pipeline of forming a spectral embedding and then partitioning it introduces non-zero variance into clustering results, degrading stability and interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252918/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes v multi-view data matrices {X_p ∈ R^{d_p × n}} with view-specific dimensions d_p and a user-specified number of clusters k, and directly outputs a discrete binary cluster indicator matrix C ∈ {0,1}^{k×n} where each column contains exactly one 1 (hard assignment). No spectral embedding, k-means post-processing, or baseline-view selection is required; the number of anchors m is set equal to k to keep the permutation search tractable.

## Method

DTP-SF-BVF introduces three coupled mechanisms within a single alternating-optimization objective: (1) self-expression learning (‖A_pT_p − A_pT_pS_p‖_F²) captures anchor-anchor geometry, which is then injected into the anchor-sample graph via a point-point guidance term Tr(B_p^T L_s B_p CC^T) that penalizes row-wise disagreement in the anchor graph weighted by S_p; (2) a learnable binary permutation matrix T_p per view rearranges anchors in their native dimension space, eliminating the need for a baseline view while jointly optimizing with anchor generation; (3) the anchor graph is factorized as B_pC and the consensus matrix C is optimized under a binary column-sum constraint, directly producing hard cluster labels without forming a spectrum. View importance is adaptively weighted by α_p, and the full objective is solved by a six-step block-coordinate scheme with linear complexity in n.

## Results

DTP-SF-BVF achieves the best or near-best results across all 7 datasets and 3 metrics (ACC, NMI, F-score). On DERMATO it reaches 85.47% ACC, 89.97% NMI, and 87.92% F-score, improving F-score by 6.91% over the second-best method. On CALTE7 it attains 80.66% ACC and 78.12% F-score, substantially outperforming all 17 classical baselines. A distinctive property is zero variance (±0.00) across all 20 runs on every dataset, attributed to the spectrum-free binary learning strategy that directly outputs discrete cluster indicators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTP-SF-BVF consistently outperforms all 17 classical MVC baselines on DERMATO, CALTE7, Reuters, and FasMNI4V across all three metrics; on DERMATO it gains 6.91% F-score over the runner-up (MSCIAS at 80.90%). Against anchor-based competitors that only model anchor-sample proximity (FPMVS, FASTMI, SFMC), DTP-SF-BVF wins on all 7 datasets by exploiting the additional anchor-anchor track via self-expression learning. Compared to alignment methods requiring a baseline view (FMVACC, 3AMVC, AEVC), it achieves 27.32% higher ACC on CALTE7 (80.66% vs. 53.34% for FMVACC) while avoiding the need to select a reference view. Against deep learning baselines (ADAGAE, DEMVC, MFLVC, DSMVC), DTP-SF-BVF leads on DERMATO (85.47% vs. 80.73% for MFLVC), Cora (52.44% vs. 31.02% for MFLVC), and REU7200 (26.22% vs. 25.42% for MFLVC), though DEMVC slightly edges it on FasMNI4V NMI (59.74% vs. 59.21%). All reported results carry zero standard deviation (±0.00), in contrast to baselines like PMSC (±9.96 ACC on DERMATO) or FPMVS (±7.05 ACC), highlighting the stability advantage of the spectrum-free design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based multi-view clustering framework (Eq. 1) by addressing three specific gaps in prior methods: (1) incorporating anchor-anchor geometric structure via self-expression learning (borrowed from subspace reconstruction) and feeding it into the anchor-sample graph through a point-point guidance term; (2) replacing baseline-view-dependent alignment schemes (e.g., FMVACC, 3AMVC, AEVC) with per-view learnable permutation matrices that jointly optimize with anchor generation; and (3) eliminating the spectral embedding step entirely by directly learning a binary cluster indicator matrix. The permutation idea has precedent in MPAC (Kang et al., 2019) for partition alignment, but here it is repurposed for anchor reordering in the original feature space without requiring a common projection dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-variance clustering output (a direct consequence of the binary constraint) offers a practical stability advantage over spectral methods, and the baseline-view-free alignment removes a heuristic design choice that has plagued prior anchor-matching approaches. However, the O(m!) traversal search for the permutation matrix forces the number of anchors to equal the number of clusters, which is a significant scalability constraint that limits the method's applicability to problems with many clusters. The authors acknowledge the need for a non-parametric version and more flexible view-weighting schemes as future directions, and the method remains confined to the anchor-based MVC paradigm rather than offering a general-purpose clustering framework.
