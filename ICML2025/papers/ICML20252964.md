## Problem

Standard cross-entropy loss in language models treats all tokens as nominal categories, so predicting '3' instead of '2' incurs the same penalty as predicting '9' instead of '2'. This fundamental limitation means LMs lack any inductive bias for numerical proximity, causing them to struggle with arithmetic and quantitative reasoning even on simple tasks like three-digit multiplication. The paper asks whether a regression-like loss can be applied directly at the token level to fix this without architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the predicted probability distribution over number tokens (a subset of the vocabulary identified by a token-to-float mapping) and the numerical value of the ground truth token, producing a scalar regression-like loss that is added to the standard CE objective. It yields zero loss for all non-numerical tokens, so it can be applied uniformly across any sequence without masking. The only requirement is a map from token strings to their numerical values, making it compatible with both digit-level and multi-digit tokenization schemes.

## Method

The paper proposes two flavors of Number Token Loss (NTL). NTL-MSE computes the dot product of predicted token probabilities with their numerical values to obtain a real-valued prediction, then applies a standard regression loss (MSE, MAE, Huber) against the ground truth value. NTL-WAS instead computes the Wasserstein-1 distance between the predicted probability distribution over number tokens and the one-hot ground truth distribution, using Euclidean distance between numerical token values as the transport cost; when the label is one-hot this reduces to a weighted sum of absolute differences, and when tokens are sorted and equidistant it can be computed via the CDF. NTL-WAS is preferred because it eliminates the non-unique minima problem of NTL-MSE, where probability mass split symmetrically around the target can yield zero loss for an incorrect prediction. Both variants are scaled by λ (default 0.3) and added to CE, are model-agnostic (applied to T5, GPT-2, and IBM Granite), and introduce only ~1% overhead in loss computation for NTL-WAS.

## Results

On the DeepMind Mathematical Q&A dataset (interpolation), NTL-WAS with T5-Base achieves 75% accuracy and MAE of 0.10, outperforming vanilla CE (64% accuracy, MAE 0.13) by over 10 percentage points. On the extrapolation test, NTL-WAS reaches 43.2% accuracy versus 36.7% for CE. Scaling to T5-3B on GSM8K, NTL-WAS improves top-1 accuracy from 13.5% (CE) to 17.7%, surpassing LLaMA-2 7B (14.6%). On the rJokes regression task, NTL-WAS achieves RMSE 1.81, matching a dedicated regression head (1.82) while operating purely at the token level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTL-WAS outperforms the Regression Transformer (Born & Manica, 2023) on both interpolation (75% vs. 71% accuracy) and extrapolation (43.2% vs. 40.4% accuracy), while also achieving a substantially better R² on extrapolation (0.913 vs. 0.738). Against xVal (Golkar et al., 2023), NTL-WAS wins by a large margin (75% vs. 10% accuracy on interpolation), which the authors attribute to xVal's effective number range being limited to [-5, 5] due to layer normalization. Compared to Gaussian Cross Entropy (Wang et al., 2025), NTL-WAS alone (43% accuracy) slightly edges out GCE alone (42%), and the combination of NTL-WAS-CDF with GCE yields the best interpolation result (48% accuracy, MAE 0.76). On the rJokes regression benchmark, NTL-WAS (RMSE 1.81) matches the T5 regression-head model (RMSE 1.82) and improves over CE (2.01) by ~10%, though larger models with regression heads (BERT 1.62, RoBERTA 1.61, XLNet 1.74) retain a modest edge, which the authors attribute to their roughly 2× parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant strategy of improving numerical reasoning in LMs via architectural or representational changes (regression heads, xVal continuous encodings, numeracy-preserving embeddings, digit-order reversal) by instead modifying the training objective at the token level. It builds on the 'regression by classification' literature (Shah & Aamodt 2023; Stewart et al. 2023) and the concurrent RAFT loss (Lukasik et al. 2025, which proposed the NTL-MSE variant), but extends this line with a Wasserstein-distance formulation (NTL-WAS) that eliminates the non-unique minima problem of Lp-norm losses. The Gaussian Cross Entropy of Wang et al. (2025) is positioned as a complementary but less principled alternative that blurs labels rather than respecting the prediction distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NTL offers a drop-in, architecture-agnostic loss term with negligible overhead that consistently improves arithmetic performance across encoder-decoder and decoder-only models up to 3B parameters, making it a practical candidate for integration into LLM pretraining pipelines. The finding that a purely token-level loss can match a dedicated regression head on a regression task is notable, though demonstrated only at 60M scale. Key open questions include whether NTL helps in large-scale pretraining (vs. fine-tuning), how to account for decimal-place significance in multi-digit numbers, and whether it synergizes with other arithmetic-specific adaptations like chain-of-thought or verifier training.
