## Problem

Memory-efficient LLM training has developed two seemingly distinct families of methods: gradient compression (e.g., GaLore, which projects gradients into a lower-dimensional space) and adapter-based reparameterization (e.g., LoRA, which freezes base weights and trains low-rank updates). While recent works identified specific equivalences between one-sided LoRA and gradient sketching, no general theoretical framework unified these approaches, leaving open whether the connection extends beyond particular choices of projection matrices and optimizers.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a neural network's weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n} and their corresponding gradient matrices, applying a linear projection S ∈ R^{r×d} (where d = mn and r < d) to compress gradients before optimization. For practical use, S is constrained to a Kronecker-factored form S = R^T ⊗ L with L ∈ R^{d_L×m} and R ∈ R^{n×d_R}, reducing storage from O(mnr) to O(d_L·m + n·d_R). The output is the updated parameter matrix W^{(t+1)} = W^{(t)} + S^T·Δ, where Δ is the optimizer update computed in the r-dimensional space, yielding optimizer states of dimension O(r) instead of O(d).

## Method

The paper proves (Thm. 1) that for any differentiable loss and any optimizer maintaining state via a fixed update rule, training with linearly transformed gradients (project via S, optimize, map back via S^T) produces exactly the same optimization trajectory as reparameterizing the model as Θ + S^T·Λ and training only Λ initialized to zero. Proposition 2 specializes this to Kronecker-factored S = R^T ⊗ L, establishing equivalence with training W + L^T·A·R^T (only A learned), and Corollary 3 recovers GaLore as one-sided LoRA where one adapter matrix is frozen to the SVD projection P^T. Empirically, the paper shows that random sketching matrices (Gaussian, Rademacher, semi-orthogonal) perform comparably to SVD-based GaLore projections, enabling QLoRA-style quantization of base weights for further memory savings. A second study exploits the duality for distributed training, proposing a worker-aware orthogonal initialization of frozen LoRA sides (P_i·P_j^T = 0) that outperforms identical or independent random initialization across multiple rank/worker configurations.

## Results

At 200M scale (5B tokens on SlimPajama), one-sided gradient transformations achieve validation PPL of 20.13–21.34, with random semi-orthogonal (20.13) and Rademacher (20.24) slightly outperforming SVD-based GaLore (21.34). At 1.3B scale (10B tokens), the gap narrows: semi-orthogonal reaches 13.71 PPL vs. GaLore's 13.62, while full pretraining achieves 12.44. Adding INT8 quantization to the base weights (QLoRA-style) costs <0.1 PPL (e.g., Rademacher+INT8: 20.26 vs. 20.24 at 200M), whereas NF4 quantization incurs a 3+ PPL penalty. In distributed training, the proposed orthogonal worker initialization (Distributed Random) achieves 19.81 PPL at 200M, outperforming LTE (20.97) and approaching DiLoCo (18.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReLoRA (Lialin et al., 2024), one-sided random transformations (Rademacher 20.24, Semi-orthogonal 20.13 at 200M) slightly outperform ReLoRA (20.40), suggesting the extra flexibility of training both LoRA sides is unnecessary. Against GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024), random sketching matrices match or beat SVD-based projections (Semi-orthogonal 20.13 vs. GaLore 21.34 at 200M; 13.71 vs. 13.62 at 1.3B), while also saving memory since only an RNG seed must be persisted. Against Flora/Gaussian (Hao et al., 2024), Rademacher and Semi-orthogonal achieve marginally better PPL (20.24/20.13 vs. 20.57 at 200M). The QLoRA-style INT8 approach to GaLore (21.38 PPL, 0.81 GB) substantially outperforms QGaLore (Zhang et al., 2024) which quantizes weights and trains them directly (23.86 PPL, 0.94 GB). In distributed training, the proposed Distributed Random initialization (19.81 PPL at 200M) beats LTE (Huh et al., 2024) by 1.16 PPL and approaches DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2024) (18.00 PPL), while all one-sided methods remain below the full-training DiLoCo baseline as expected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes and unifies the equivalence between gradient compression (GaLore) and one-sided LoRA that was previously established in specific cases by Flora (Hao et al., 2024) for random projections and LoQT (Loeschcke et al., 2024) for SVD-based projections. The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that *any* linear gradient transformation is exactly equivalent to a linear adapter reparameterization, not just the Kronecker-factored or SVD-specific cases. The paper then leverages this duality in both directions: using the adapter view to enable QLoRA-style quantization of GaLore training, and using the gradient-transformation view to derive a distributed initialization scheme for parallel LoRA workers that improves upon LTE (Huh et al., 2024) and DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The duality provides a principled lens for designing memory-efficient training methods, suggesting that the choice of gradient projection matrix (SVD vs. random vs. semi-orthogonal) may matter less than previously assumed, and that random sketching matrices with cheap rematerialization are a practical default. However, the paper acknowledges a non-trivial performance gap between full pretraining and all memory-efficient variants at both 200M and 1.3B scales, and the experiments are limited to 1.3B parameters. Open questions include what characterizes a good projection matrix S, whether S can be learned via meta-learning, whether the equivalence extends to non-linear transformations, and how these techniques scale to much larger models and more extreme distributed settings.
