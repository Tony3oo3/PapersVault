## Problem

Diverse prompt learning for vision-language models (e.g., instance-dependent and fine-grained prompts) significantly boosts downstream performance by describing visual concepts from multiple perspectives, but it scales gradient backpropagation through the text encoder from O(M) to O(B×M) or O(Z×M), causing severe GPU memory and training-time overhead that remains unaddressed in prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251591/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a set of M class-name text labels, producing a final classification probability by averaging predictions from three complementary text-feature streams (global-invariant, instance-dependent, and fine-grained). The instance-dependent stream conditions on the per-sample visual feature, while the fine-grained stream uses Z learnable conditional tokens (Z=4) to select top-k local visual regions at multiple scales, making the output sensitive to both global and local image content.

## Method

SurPL replaces the expensive process of learning diverse text prompts from scratch with a lightweight Surrogate Feature Generator (SFG), a cross-attention module that takes a single basic prompted text feature (obtained via any standard prompt-learning baseline such as DVLP) as the query and flexible conditional signals as key/value to directly synthesize diverse prompted text features. For instance-dependent features, the per-sample visual feature serves as the conditional signal; for fine-grained features, Z learnable tokens act as conditional signals that automatically capture fine-grained cues during optimization. Because the SFG parameters are orders of magnitude smaller than the text encoder, the overall backpropagation cost remains O(M) rather than O(B×M) or O(Z×M), matching single-prompt learning efficiency. A generalizable variant (SurPL-G) further applies Sharpness-Aware Minimization to flatten the loss landscape and improve base-to-novel, cross-domain, and cross-dataset generalization without architectural changes.

## Results

On the 11-dataset few-shot benchmark (16-shot), SurPL achieves 85.12% average top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best diverse prompt method GalLoP (84.40%) by 0.72% and the best single prompt method LLaMP (83.81%) by 1.31%. For base-to-novel generalization, SurPL-G attains 81.03% harmonic mean (Base 86.37%, Novel 76.32%), improving over the prior SOTA DePT (80.42%) by 0.61%. In terms of efficiency, SurPL reduces GPU memory from OOM (CoCoOp, PLOT++, ALIGN, GalLoP on ImageNet) to 23.80G on a single RTX 3090, while cutting training time on FGVCAircraft from 5–31 min to 2 min 36 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurPL outperforms all 19 baselines on the 11-dataset few-shot average. Among diverse prompt methods, it beats GalLoP (84.40%) by 0.72%, ALIGN (82.48%) by 2.64%, PLOT++ (82.09%) by 3.03%, and CoCoOp (74.90%) by 10.22%; the largest per-dataset gains are on FGVCAircraft (+2.21% over GalLoP), EuroSAT (+1.27%), and UCF101 (+1.28%). Among single prompt methods, SurPL surpasses LLaMP (83.81%) by 1.31%, PSRC (82.87%) by 2.25%, MaPLe (81.79%) by 3.33%, DAPT (81.62%) by 3.50%, and CoOp (79.89%) by 5.23%. For base-to-novel generalization, SurPL-G (HM 81.03%) exceeds DePT (80.42%), TCP (79.51%), PSRC (79.73%), ALIGN (79.25%), MaPLe (78.55%), KgCoOp (76.94%), ProGrad (75.21%), CoCoOp (72.52%), CoOp (72.25%), and zero-shot CLIP (70.69%). In cross-dataset and cross-domain settings, SurPL-G also leads all baselines (target avg 66.61% vs. MaPLe 66.30%; cross-domain avg 61.37% vs. PSRC 60.65%). Against non-prompt PEFT methods (CLIP-Adapter 79.86%, Tip-Adapter 81.15%, TaskRes 80.75%, CLIPFit 81.27%, CLIP-LoRA 82.95%), SurPL's 85.12% average represents a 2.17–5.26% margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prompt learning lineage for CLIP-based VLMs, starting from CoOp's single learnable prompt and extending through diverse prompt approaches like CoCoOp (instance-dependent) and GalLoP (fine-grained). The key conceptual departure is replacing the optimization of multiple diverse prompts with a lightweight cross-attention module (SFG) that generates diverse text features from a single basic prompt plus conditional signals, thereby reducing backpropagation cost from O(B×M) or O(Z×M) to O(M). For generalization, it incorporates SAM (Foret et al., 2021) as an optimization strategy, following the broader trend of using flat-minima regularization for prompt generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SurPL eliminates GPU OOM failures on large-scale datasets (ImageNet) that plague existing diverse prompt methods, while matching or exceeding their accuracy. The SFG's modularity means it can be plugged into any single prompt learning baseline (demonstrated with CoOp, MaPLe, PSRC), making it a broadly applicable efficiency tool. Limitations include the SFG's added parameter overhead (1.64M vs. 0.06M for the DVLP baseline) and the authors' own acknowledgment that cross-attention is 'relatively outdated.' Open questions include whether more efficient feature-generation architectures can replace the cross-attention SFG, and whether new conditional signal designs beyond instance features and learnable tokens can unlock further gains.
