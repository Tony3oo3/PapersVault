## Problem

Gradient inversion attacks in federated learning have been extensively studied for unimodal image classification but remain largely unexplored for multimodal vision-language models. Document Visual Question Answering (DQA) models present unique challenges: reconstructing fine-grained text (occupying ~1% of the image) is far harder than recovering large objects, and text-based gradients are inherently sparse, limiting recoverable information. This paper is the first to systematically study gradient inversion on multimodal DQA architectures in an FL setting.

## Input / Output

The adversary receives the shared gradient vector from a single FL client, the current global model parameters, a publicly available document template matching the client's layout (with private fields blanked out), and a question-answer pair extracted analytically from the gradients. The output is a pixel-level reconstruction of the client's document image, evaluated both visually (PSNR, FFT₂D, MSE) and semantically via Trocr OCR (exact-match binary accuracy and character-level fuzz ratio). The template constraint is critical: without a matching layout, the search space is too large for successful inversion.

## Method

GI-DQA combines an analytic-based QA extraction step (leveraging prior work on gradient-to-text recovery) with an optimization-based document reconstruction that minimizes a combined gradient matching loss (per-layer MSE plus cosine similarity) plus three auxiliary priors: a Laplacian-based text prior for edge enhancement, a Gaussian smoothing prior for noise suppression, and a combined spatial-plus-channel Total Variation prior that enforces both local smoothness and grayscale (R=G=B) consistency. A key design choice is an exponential-decay scheduler φ(t)=α·exp(−λt) that weights auxiliary priors heavily in early iterations and gradually shifts emphasis to gradient matching, stabilizing optimization from the start rather than delaying priors as in GradViT. The paper further shows that only pre-fusion gradients (before cross-modal attention) carry sufficient signal for reconstruction, while post-fusion gradients are far less informative, revealing multimodal fusion as an implicit privacy safeguard.

## Results

On the PFL-DocVQA dataset, GI-DQA achieves 70.1% exact word reconstruction (Binary) and a Fuzz Ratio of 0.909 on the Donut model, with a PSNR of 24.19 dB. On the OCR-based LayoutLMv3 model, the attack reaches 82.1% Binary match and a Fuzz Ratio of 0.681 (PSNR 22.71 dB). The best prior method, APRIL, manages only 5.8% binary match on Donut and 0.6% on LayoutLMv3, while DLG and iDLG perform at the level of a random baseline (0% binary match).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GI-DQA decisively outperforms all four baselines on both models across every metric. DLG [44] and iDLG [42] are effectively indistinguishable from a random initialization (0% binary match, PSNR ≈ 7 dB), confirming that standard gradient-matching losses designed for unimodal image classification cannot exploit the cross-modal structure of DQA models. Inverting Gradients [11], which adds cosine-similarity and total-variation regularization, improves PSNR to 11.4 dB on Donut but still recovers zero words exactly. APRIL [23], the strongest prior method and the only one designed for transformer architectures, reaches 5.8% binary match on Donut (PSNR 13.65) and 0.6% on LayoutLMv3 (PSNR 9.83), a gap of roughly 12× in exact-word recovery compared to GI-DQA's 70.1% and 82.1%. The paper attributes this gap to GI-DQA's use of pre-fusion gradients, a document-template prior that narrows the search space, and domain-specific priors (Laplacian text filter, channel-wise TV) that are absent from all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient inversion literature in federated learning—originally developed for unimodal image classification (DLG, iDLG, Inverting Gradients) and later adapted to vision transformers (APRIL, GradViT)—to the multimodal Document Visual Question Answering domain. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) it introduces document-specific auxiliary priors (Laplacian text prior, channel-wise TV, Gaussian smoothing) and an early-weighted scheduler that differ from the delayed-prior strategy of GradViT; (2) it requires analytic extraction of the question-answer pair before document reconstruction, a step unnecessary in unimodal attacks; and (3) it leverages publicly available document templates as structural priors, a threat-model assumption absent from prior gradient inversion studies. The paper positions itself as the first gradient inversion attack targeting deeply fused, jointly trained multimodal architectures, distinguishing itself from Liu et al. (2024), which uses independently trained modality-specific models coordinated via knowledge distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete privacy vulnerability in multimodal FL deployments for document processing (medical records, invoices, legal contracts), demonstrating that 70%+ of private words can be recovered from gradients alone when a template is available. The finding that pre-fusion gradients carry the most recoverable signal while post-fusion gradients are significantly less revealing offers a targeted architectural insight for defense design. However, practical impact is tempered by notable limitations: the attack degrades sharply after early training iterations (<10% by iteration 30), fails at batch sizes ≥4, and depends on template availability. Open questions include whether adaptive defenses can protect pre-fusion layers without sacrificing utility, whether the approach generalizes to non-template documents, and how it interacts with differential privacy or secure aggregation.
