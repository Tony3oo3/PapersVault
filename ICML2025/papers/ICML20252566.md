## Problem

Foundation models for Relational Databases (RDBs) remain underexplored despite the widespread practical importance of relational data. Existing approaches either handle single-table data well but cannot capture inter-table relationships, or apply GNNs to individual RDB tasks without proposing a universal, generalizable model. The paper tackles how to build a single model that unifies diverse input types (categorical, numerical, text), handles both classification and regression, and transfers across RDBs with varying schemas, sizes, and domains.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a temporally constrained rooted subgraph sampled from an RDB, where each node (row) carries a variable-length feature tensor x_i ∈ R^{L_i×d} and a metadata tensor m_i ∈ R^{L_i×d} (column names), edges carry relation-type vectors e_r ∈ R^d, and a task embedding t ∈ R^d derived from the target column name. Only nodes with timestamps earlier than the target row are included, enforcing temporal causality. The output is a fixed-length vector z ∈ R^d that is decoded into either a class probability distribution (softmax over inner products with text embeddings of candidate categories) or a numeric value (via a pretrained float decoder followed by denormalization).

## Method

Griffin's architecture comprises three unified components: (1) a data encoder that maps all categorical/text features through a pretrained Nomic text encoder and numerical features through a pretrained MLP float encoder (trained with L1 reconstruction loss), plus metadata and task embeddings; (2) a 4-layer MPNN where each layer applies task-conditioned cross-attention over a node's column features (query from node+task representation, keys from column-name metadata, values from cell embeddings) followed by hierarchical message passing (mean aggregation within each relation type, then max aggregation across relation types weighted by edge-type vectors); (3) a unified decoder that uses text embeddings of target labels as classification logits and the pretrained float decoder for regression. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: self-supervised completion pretraining (randomly mask a cell, minimize cosine distance between predicted and true embeddings), joint supervised fine-tuning on ~200 single-table datasets plus RDB datasets from 4DBInfer and RelBench (covering >150M nodes), and task-specific fine-tuning. The first-layer cross-attention is replaced with self-attention over column features to avoid degenerating to mean aggregation when task-relevant information is not yet available.

## Results

Across 24 tasks spanning 14 RDBs (covering classification, regression, and multi-hop relational prediction), Griffin-pretrained achieves the best overall rank of 3.042, outperforming the strongest GNN baseline SAGE (4.792) and the best DFS-enhanced single-table model DFS+FT-Transformer (5.917). Griffin-unpretrained (3.708) also surpasses all baselines, confirming the architectural advantage independent of pretraining. On regression tasks the gains are especially large: Griffin-pretrained reduces Amazon/rating RMSE from 0.9563 (GAT) to 0.5994, and Rel-avito/ad-ctr MAE from 1.3565 (SAGE) to 0.6586.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Griffin-pretrained dominates all four GNN baselines (SAGE, GAT, PNA, HGT) and all four DFS-enhanced single-table baselines (DFS+MLP, DFS+DeepFM, DFS+FT-Transformer, DFS+XGB) in overall rank, with the best GNN baseline SAGE trailing by 1.75 rank points (4.792 vs. 3.042). The largest absolute gains appear on regression tasks: Griffin-pretrained cuts Amazon/rating RMSE by ~38% relative to GAT (0.5994 vs. 0.9563) and Rel-avito/ad-ctr MAE by ~51% relative to SAGE (0.6586 vs. 1.3565). On classification ROC-AUC tasks Griffin-pretrained wins on most tasks (e.g., Retailrocket/cvr 0.9643 vs. SAGE 0.8470) but is slightly behind SAGE on Rel-hm/user-churn (0.6804 vs. 0.6988). Griffin-unpretrained already outperforms every baseline, isolating the architectural contribution (cross-attention + hierarchical aggregation) from the pretraining benefit. In the few-shot comparison with TabPFNv2+DFS (Appendix E), Griffin and TabPFNv2 each excel on different domain subsets, with Griffin performing better on Commerce-2 and Others-2 tasks while TabPFNv2 leads on Commerce-1 and Others-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

Griffin carves new ground by proposing the first foundation-model-style architecture specifically for relational databases, unifying what prior work treated as separate task-specific GNNs (4DBInfer, RelBench, ATJNet, GFS) into a single model with shared encoders and decoders. It repurposes ideas from tabular foundation models (TPBerta, CARTE, TabPFN) and graph foundation models (OFA, GraphAny) but adapts them to the relational setting through a unified data encoder (pretrained text encoder for categorical/text, pretrained float encoder for numerical) and a cross-attention module that replaces the standard mean aggregation in MPNNs. The hierarchical aggregation (intra-relation mean followed by inter-relation max) and the use of RDB metadata as attention keys are departures from both single-table tabular models and prior RDB GNNs that flatten or one-hot encode features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a meaningful first step toward a general-purpose RDB foundation model, and the finding that single-table pretraining alone (no RDB data) improves downstream RDB performance is practically useful for practitioners who may lack large relational pretraining corpora. The similarity/diversity transferability analysis provides actionable guidance for selecting pretraining data. However, the 'foundation model' label is somewhat aspirational given only 24 downstream tasks across two benchmarks and a fixed 512-dim architecture; open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of domains, whether the unified text-encoder-based feature handling remains efficient at larger scale, and whether the cross-attention module's benefits persist with deeper or wider GNN backbones.
