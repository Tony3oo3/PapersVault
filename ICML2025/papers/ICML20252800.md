## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks offer significant energy savings over ANNs for few-shot learning, but existing SNN-based FSL methods struggle to capture complex spatiotemporal features from inputs and lack effective cross-class feature comparison mechanisms for accurately mapping query samples to the correct support set. This limits both classification accuracy and the practical deployment of SNNs in energy-constrained few-shot scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252800/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes pairs of support set images and query set images in an N-way K-shot episodic format. For static datasets (CUB, miniImageNet), each image is replicated across T time steps to form a T×C×H×W spiking input; for the neuromorphic N-Omniglot dataset, the temporal dimension is native. The output is a predicted class label for each query image, determined by nearest-prototype matching against support set embeddings in the learned feature space.

## Method

The proposed SSCF architecture uses a VGGSNN backbone (8 conv-bn-LIF layers without average pooling to preserve sparsity) followed by two key modules: a Self-Feature Extractor (SFE) that applies an unfolded operation to expand spatial positions, then a bottleneck convolution (1×1 → 3×3 → 3×3 → 1×1) with LIF activation to compute time-channel self-correlation patterns, combined with backbone features via residual addition; and a Cross-Feature Contrastive (CFC) module that builds a 4D cross-correlation tensor between query and support features, refines it with two 4D convolutional layers, and generates joint attention maps for relational classification. Training combines a Temporal Efficient Training (TET) loss—averaging cross-entropy over all T time steps to guide the spiking backbone—with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that separates positive and negative support prototypes via cosine similarity, balanced by a hyperparameter λ. This dual-loss strategy leverages the SNN's temporal dimension for efficient spike-train optimization while the contrastive component enhances discriminative power and noise robustness, achieving 98.9% on 5w5s N-Omniglot and competitive accuracy on CUB/miniImageNet at only 8.3% of the energy cost of comparable ANN methods.

## Results

On the neuromorphic N-Omniglot dataset, SSCF with VGGSNN backbone achieves 98.9% accuracy on 5-way 5-shot (T=8) and 95.3% on 5-way 1-shot (T=12), representing 11.5% and 25.0% improvements over MAML respectively. On static datasets, the model achieves 76.27% (5w1s) and 87.00% (5w5s) on CUB-200-2011, and 60.97% (5w1s) and 75.61% (5w5s) on miniImageNet, approaching state-of-the-art ANN performance. In terms of energy efficiency, SSCF consumes only 1.849 mJ per inference versus 22.264 mJ for the ANN baseline RENet (8.30% of ANN energy) at T=2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On N-Omniglot, SSCF (VGGSNN) substantially outperforms both Siamese and MAML baselines across all task settings, with the largest gap in 1-shot scenarios (e.g., 95.3% vs. 74.4% for MAML on 5w1s, a 25% improvement). On CUB-200-2011, SSCF (76.27% 5w1s, 87.00% 5w5s) surpasses ProtoNet, RelationNet, MAML, S2M2, FEAT, and DeepEMD, but remains below the top ANN method RENet (79.49%, 91.11%). On miniImageNet, SSCF (60.97% 5w1s, 75.61% 5w5s) trails the best ANNs (RENet at 67.60%, 82.58%) by 6–7 points but still exceeds MAML, Meta-SGD, adaResNet, and RelationNet. Among SNN-specific baselines, SSCF clearly dominates: it outperforms Knowledge-Transfer (67.8% vs. 64.1% on N-Omniglot 20w1s), OWOML (60.9% vs. 45.2% on miniImageNet 5w1s), and CESM/MESM (60.9% vs. 51.8%/53.6%). The key trade-off is that SSCF sacrifices 3–7% accuracy relative to the best ANNs on static datasets but achieves this at only 8.3% of the energy cost (1.849 mJ vs. 22.264 mJ for RENet), making it the most energy-efficient competitive FSL method reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of SNN-based few-shot learning and metric-learning architectures. It builds directly on the TET loss (Deng et al., 2022) for training the spiking backbone and adapts the cross-correlation attention mechanism from RENet (Kang et al., 2021) into a spiking context for the CFC module. Unlike prior SNN-FSL work that focused on online learning on neuromorphic hardware (Stewart et al., Scherr et al.) or multi-timescale meta-learning (Jiang et al.), this paper introduces a structured two-module architecture (self-feature extractor + cross-feature contrastive) with a single-stage TET+InfoNCE training objective, positioning SNNs as a viable backbone for standard episodic FSL rather than purely online adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.3% energy consumption relative to ANNs makes this practically relevant for edge and IoT deployment where few-shot adaptation is needed without sustained power budgets. However, the static-dataset results (76.27% CUB, 60.97% miniImageNet) still trail strong ANN baselines like RENet (79.49%, 67.60%), indicating that SNN-based FSL has not yet closed the accuracy gap on general-purpose image classification. Open questions include whether scaling to deeper spiking backbones or more sophisticated temporal encoding can close this gap, and whether the theoretical energy savings translate to real neuromorphic hardware under the episodic training protocol.
