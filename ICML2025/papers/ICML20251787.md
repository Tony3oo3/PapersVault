## Problem

Indiscriminately mixing generated images with real images during vision-language model training causes mode collapse, where the model overfits to synthetic artifacts and degrades on real-world data. Existing approaches fail to recognize that generated images constitute a distinct data modality with subtle but significant distributional differences from real images, and no prior method explicitly bridges this Gen-Real modality gap in a principled way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251787/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired (generated image, real image) tuples that share the same text caption, where generated images are synthesized by Stable Diffusion v2 (50 inference steps) from the original training captions. At training time, the real-image encoder f_r remains frozen while the generated-image encoder f_g is updated via LoRA (rank 4); at inference on real images, the original f_r encoder is used directly, so the model never sees generated artifacts during real-world deployment. The aligned embeddings feed into downstream VLMs (CLIPCap, LLaVA, LLaMA-3) to produce captions, retrieval rankings, or class predictions.

## Method

GMAIL introduces a dual-encoder architecture where a frozen real-image encoder f_r and a LoRA-fine-tuned generated-image encoder f_g operate as separate modalities, connected by a cross-modality contrastive alignment loss (Eq. 1) that pulls f_g(x_g) toward f_r(x_r) for same-caption pairs while pushing apart mismatched pairs in a shared latent space. This explicit separation prevents the generated encoder from contaminating the real-image representations, avoiding the mode collapse that arises when a single encoder must simultaneously accommodate both distributions. The alignment is trained for 50k steps with AdamW (lr=1e-4, τ=0.07, batch 256), and the resulting aligned f_g can be plugged into any VLM backbone for downstream tasks. The key design choice is that the text encoder h is expected to ignore synthetic-specific artifacts and capture only the shared semantics between R and S, while the separate f and g mappings allow each modality to retain its own characteristic features without forcing them into a single collapsed representation.

## Results

On COCO image captioning, GMAIL consistently improves all tested VLMs: ClipCap+GMAIL reaches 38.12 B@4 / 119.53 CIDEr (vs. 32.15 / 108.35 baseline), LLaVA+GMAIL reaches 43.26 B@4 / 146.38 CIDEr (vs. 39.67 / 134.29), and Llama3+GMAIL reaches 50.21 B@4 / 168.53 CIDEr (vs. 47.36 / 158.13). On zero-shot image retrieval (COCO), CLIP+GMAIL achieves 56.8 R@1 image-to-text and 37.5 R@1 text-to-image, outperforming CLIP by 5.0 and 4.8 points respectively. On zero-shot image classification across 8 benchmarks, CLIP+GMAIL improves top-1 accuracy by 2–10 points (e.g., DTD 65.26 vs. 55.20, Aircraft 37.85 vs. 32.88). Scaling from COCO to CC12M generated data further boosts Flickr30k retrieval R@1 from 47.1 to 50.9 (I2T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMAIL is applied as a plug-in on top of four captioning VLMs (ClipCap, IFCap, LLaVA, Llama3) and consistently wins on all six metrics; the largest absolute gain is on ClipCap (+5.97 B@4, +11.18 CIDEr) while even the strongest baseline Llama3 still benefits (+2.85 B@4, +10.40 CIDEr). For zero-shot retrieval, CLIP+GMAIL beats CLIP by 5.0 R@1 (I2T) and 4.8 R@1 (T2I) on COCO, and Long-CLIP+GMAIL beats Long-CLIP by 5.1 and 5.2 R@1 respectively; on Flickr30k the text-to-image gains are even larger (14.6 R@1 over CLIP). On zero-shot classification, CLIP+GMAIL outperforms CLIP on all 8 benchmarks (e.g., +10.06 on DTD, +4.97 on Aircraft), and SynCLR+GMAIL surpasses SynCLR on every dataset (e.g., +3.77 on DTD, +5.05 on Aircraft). Against SigLIP on COCO captioning, GMAIL adds +4.84 B@4 and +7.86 CIDEr. Compared to Task2Sim (a mixed real+synthetic training approach), GMAIL achieves +1.47 B@4 and +3.81 CIDEr, confirming that explicit modality alignment outperforms indiscriminate data mixing.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMAIL builds directly on CLIP's contrastive language-image pre-training framework, extending it with a dual-encoder architecture that treats generated images as a distinct modality rather than augmented real data. It departs from prior synthetic-data training approaches like SynCLR (Tian et al., 2024), which integrate generated images indiscriminately into the training pipeline, by introducing an explicit cross-modality alignment loss that bridges the latent-space gap between real and generated encoders. The method also connects to the broader diffusion-model literature (Stable Diffusion) as a scalable data source and leverages LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, positioning itself as a plug-in alignment layer for existing vision-language models such as CLIPCap, LLaVA, and LLaMA-3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important and increasingly urgent problem: as generative models produce ever-more-realistic images, training pipelines need principled ways to exploit this data without suffering mode collapse or overfitting to synthetic artifacts. The modular design (dual encoder + alignment loss) is easy to adopt across VLMs, and the positive scaling trend on CC3M/CC12M suggests the approach becomes more valuable as generated data volumes grow. However, the framework is validated primarily on CLIP-based backbones with Stable Diffusion v2 as the sole generator, leaving open whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other generative architectures (e.g., FLUX, autoregressive models) or to non-image modalities. The reliance on paired real-generated images sharing identical captions also constrains applicability to settings where such pairing is feasible.
