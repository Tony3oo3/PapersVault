## Problem

Vision-language pre-training models (VLMs) for text-to-visual retrieval suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) global contrastive training incorporates low-level visual redundancies (textures, background noise) that obscure core semantic concepts, and (2) the granularity of web-crawled training captions is too coarse to match user queries that range from high-level scene descriptions to fine-grained attribute queries. Existing remedies address these issues during training (retraining VLMs, augmenting datasets with LMM-generated captions, or fine-tuning LMMs as rerankers), which is computationally expensive and not plug-and-play.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual user query (short or long-context, up to ~217 tokens on IIW) and a gallery of images or videos as input. It first retrieves top-k candidates (k=20) using a standard VLM, then produces a 'visual abstraction' for each candidate: a concatenated text string combining a general LMM-generated description and QA-based answers. The final output is a reranked list where the score is the sum of the original VLM cross-modal score and a text-space similarity score computed by an off-the-shelf text retriever. For video inputs, frames are sampled at 3 FPS before being fed to the LMM.

## Method

VISA is a training-free, test-time reranking framework that transforms cross-modal retrieval into a text-only retrieval problem. It consists of two stages applied to the top-k VLM candidates: (1) General Description Generation, where an off-the-shelf LMM (e.g., LLaVA-v1.6-34B) produces a long-form natural language description that naturally filters out low-level visual redundancies; and (2) QA-Based Description Refinement, where a chain-of-thought pipeline first uses an LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) to extract key phrases from the query and generate three targeted questions, then an LMM (Qwen2-VL-7B) answers those questions for each candidate, with explicit instructions to respond 'Uncertain' when content is absent to prevent hallucination. The concatenated description and answers form the visual abstraction, which is scored against the query by a text retriever (gemma2-9B) in a unified text space. The final ranking combines the original VLM score with the text-space score via simple addition, making VISA a drop-in module orthogonal to any existing VLM architecture.

## Results

On short-context image retrieval, VISA applied to SigLIP boosts R@1 from 54.2% to 57.2% on COCO (+3.0) and from 83.0% to 85.1% on Flickr30K (+2.1); applied to EVA-CLIP-18B, it raises R@1 to 59.5% on COCO (+3.9) and 86.1% on Flickr30K (+3.0). On long-context retrieval, VISA on LoTLIP achieves a remarkable +12.1% R@1 gain on DCI (62.5% → 74.6%) and +8.7% on Urban1k (85.9% → 94.6%). On video retrieval, VISA on InternVideo2-C yields the largest single gain of +8.9% R@1 on DiDeMo (45.9% → 54.8%), while on InternVideo2-G it improves R@1 by +2.4% on MSR-VTT and +2.5% on DiDeMo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISA consistently outperforms all training-based baselines on short-context image retrieval: SigLIP+VISA (57.2 R@1 on COCO) surpasses UMG-CLIP (57.5), FLAIR (53.3), CoCa (51.2), and DreamLIP (41.1), while EVA-CLIP+VISA (59.5) exceeds even the combined SigLIP+EVA-CLIP ensemble (56.0) and InternVL-G (58.6). On long-context retrieval, VISA on LoTLIP dramatically outperforms purpose-built long-context methods: it beats FLAIR (66.2) by 8.4 R@1 on DCI, Long-CLIP (61.0) by 13.6, and LoTLIP alone (62.5) by 12.1, while also surpassing FLAME (93.2) on ShareGPT4v (97.8). On video retrieval, VISA on InternVideo2-C outperforms all listed baselines including VAST (49.3), mPLUG-2 (47.1), and LanguageBind (42.8) on MSR-VTT, and achieves the largest margin on DiDeMo (+8.9 over InternVideo2-C, +8.9 over VAST). Notably, VISA is training-free and plug-and-play, whereas competing methods like RAGVL require fine-tuning LMMs, FLAME requires distillation from frozen LLMs, and DreamLIP/FLAIR require retraining with LMM-generated captions. The only baseline VISA does not surpass is BLIP-2 on Flickr30K (89.7 vs 85.1/86.1), which benefits from COCO fine-tuning and zero-shot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISA carves new ground by repurposing the LMM captioning paradigm (explored in DreamLIP and FLAIR for training data augmentation) as a purely test-time reranking mechanism, eliminating the need for any model retraining. It departs from object-detection-based semantic extraction (UNITER, OSCAR) by using free-form natural language descriptions instead of finite object categories, and from fine-tuning-based LMM rerankers (RAGVL) by relying entirely on off-the-shelf models. The chain-of-thought QA refinement for granularity alignment is a novel mechanism not present in prior caption-augmentation or reranking work, positioning VISA as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to existing VLM architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of VISA makes it immediately deployable on top of any existing VLM, offering a practical recipe for practitioners who cannot afford retraining. The substantial long-context gains (e.g., +12.1% R@1 on DCI) suggest that text-space retrieval with LMM-generated abstractions is particularly effective when queries demand fine-grained, multi-granularity reasoning. Open questions include scalability to very large galleries (the offline description generation cost grows linearly), the reliability of LMM-generated descriptions in safety-critical domains (hallucination risk acknowledged in the Impact Statement), and whether the approach extends naturally to other cross-modal tasks like video grounding or composed image retrieval.
