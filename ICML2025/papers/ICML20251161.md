## Problem

Existing diffusion-based ultra-low bitrate image compression methods suffer from two critical gaps: (1) semantic redundancy, where full image captions are transmitted alongside compressed latents that already encode much of the same information, wasting precious bits at extremely low rates; and (2) a fixed diffusion denoising schedule that ignores the intrinsic correlation between compression ratio and noise level, leading to suboptimal reconstruction fidelity and unnecessary decoding latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a reconstructed image along with a two-component compressed bitstream: (a) a compressed latent feature z_c from a neural encoder-decoder operating in the Stable Diffusion latent space, and (b) a short semantic residual caption encoded as discrete CLIP text-embedding indices (not raw text strings) to minimize text bitrate. The system targets ultra-low bitrates below 0.01 bpp, with the text-to-latent bitrate ratio adapting dynamically: at very low bitrates the text carries more information, while at higher bitrates the residual caption shrinks toward zero.

## Method

ResULIC introduces two key modules. First, Semantic Residual Coding (SRC) uses an MLLM (GPT-4o) to generate captions for both the original image and the decoded compressed image, then computes their semantic difference to transmit only the information lost during latent compression; a Perceptual Fidelity Optimizer (Pfo) further refines these residual prompts via discrete CLIP-embedding-space optimization (analogous to PEZ but initialized from the residual caption) to maximize reconstruction consistency. Second, the Compression-aware Diffusion Model (CDM) establishes a theoretical mapping between bitrate and the diffusion noise-addition endpoint N_r, starting the reverse denoising process from a partially-noised compressed latent z_c rather than pure Gaussian noise, with N_r adaptively selected per compression level while retaining the original Stable Diffusion noise schedule (no retraining required). A Visual Adapter (ControlNet-XS Type B architecture) conditions the frozen diffusion UNet on the compressed latent, and the entire system is trained with a combined noise-prediction loss, MSE, LPIPS, and rate-distortion objective.

## Results

On CLIC-2020, ResULIC achieves BD-rate savings of −62.9% (FID), −68.5% (KID), −51.6% (DISTS), and −26.9% (LPIPS) relative to the MS-ILLM anchor. Compared to the strongest diffusion-based competitor PerCo, ResULIC delivers −80.7% BD-rate on LPIPS and −66.3% on FID. Decoding latency is 0.60 s (4-step) or 0.43 s (3-step) per 768×512 image, substantially faster than PerCo (2.13 s / 0.64 s) and DiffEIC (4.57 s / 1.96 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResULIC outperforms all 11 baselines on perceptual realism (FID, KID) and perceptual fidelity (DISTS, LPIPS) metrics. Against the primary diffusion-based competitor PerCo (Careil et al., ICLR 2024), ResULIC achieves −80.7% BD-rate on LPIPS and −66.3% on FID, while also improving PSNR and MS-SSIM. Versus DiffEIC (Li et al., 2024), ResULIC gains 34.5 pp on FID and 36.4 pp on LPIPS BD-rate. Compared to GAN-based methods (HiFiC, MS-ILLM), ResULIC dominates on all perceptual metrics while remaining competitive on distortion (PSNR 120.3% vs. 122.3% for HiFiC). Against the traditional VVC/VTM codec, ResULIC achieves substantially better perceptual quality at ultra-low bitrates (<0.05 bpp). The ANS baseline from PASD (Yang et al., ECCV 2024) shows only marginal gains over no-CDM ResULIC, whereas full CDM with 4/3 denoising steps yields −54.4% LPIPS BD-rate over ANS. On Kodak, ResULIC also surpasses CorrDiff (Ma et al., 2024) and GLC (Jia et al., CVPR 2024) at matched bitrate points, confirming consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: learned image compression (ELIC-style latent coding), diffusion-based generative reconstruction (PerCo, DiffEIC), and residual-guided diffusion for restoration (ResShift, RDDM). It departs from prior diffusion-based compressors in two key ways: (1) replacing full caption transmission with a semantic residual that encodes only what the compressed latent has lost, and (2) mapping bitrate to the diffusion starting timestep while retaining the original Stable Diffusion noise schedule, rather than designing new schedules as ResShift/RDDM do. The Visual Adapter follows ControlNet-XS, and the Pfo module adapts PEZ's discrete prompt optimization with a residual-initialized embedding and a CLIP auxiliary loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The -80.7% / -66.3% BD-rate savings over PerCo on LPIPS/FID at ultra-low bitrates (<0.01 bpp) represent a substantial step for bandwidth-constrained applications, and the modular Pfo component (demonstrated as a plug-in for PerCo and Text+Sketch without retraining) offers a broadly applicable tool for improving fidelity in any diffusion-based codec. However, the 181-second encoding time (dominated by Pfo's 500-step optimization) and the hard dependency on GPT-4o for semantic residual retrieval are significant practical barriers. Notably, PSNR and MS-SSIM BD-rates remain positive relative to MS-ILLM, confirming a deliberate fidelity-for-realism trade-off that limits applicability where pixel-level accuracy matters. Open questions include whether the MLLM call can be replaced by a lighter local model without quality loss, and whether the bitrate-to-timestep mapping generalizes to video or multi-frame compression.
