## Problem

Standard diffusion and flow matching models are restricted to modeling all dimensions simultaneously, making them unable to enforce compositional constraints such as synthesis pathways during generation or perform reward credit assignment in compositional space. Existing synthesis-aware GFlowNets are limited to 2D molecular graphs, missing the 3D protein-ligand interactions essential for binding affinity prediction. This paper bridges the gap by extending flow matching to handle compositional state transitions while jointly modeling continuous 3D states.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket's 3D structure (encoded hierarchically at atom and residue level), 38 bimolecular Enamine reaction rules, and a building block catalog (up to 1.2M synthons) as input, and jointly outputs a 3D binding pose (atomic coordinates) and a valid synthesis pathway (ordered sequence of synthon additions). Molecules are constrained to ≤40 heavy atoms and ≤2 synthesis steps (Enamine REAL Space). A synthon-based representation is used rather than reactant-based to prevent spurious leaving-group atoms from appearing in intermediate states during sequential construction.

## Method

CGFlow interleaves two processes: a Compositional Flow that sequentially adds synthons at fixed time intervals λ using a GFlowNet policy trained with the trajectory balance objective, and a State Flow that refines 3D atomic positions via flow matching with a component-wise local time schedule that assigns higher noise to recently added components. The key insight is that compositional state transitions are a straightforward extension of standard flow matching interpolation, where each component's continuous state is initialized from Gaussian noise only when its synthon is added and then refined over a fixed interpolation window t_window. The state flow model uses an E(3)-equivariant Semla architecture with hierarchical protein encoding (atom-level within residues, residue-level across the pocket) and protein-ligand message passing, while the compositional flow model uses a graph transformer over 3D protein-ligand complex graphs. Both models are conditioned on the current object state x_t and a self-conditioning prediction x̂_1^t from the previous step, ensuring interdependence between structure and pose throughout generation. The state flow is trained independently on CrossDocked protein-ligand complexes (supervised), while the compositional flow is trained online with the TB objective using docking scores as reward.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 pocket-conditional benchmark, 3DSynthFlow (high β) achieves the best average Vina docking score of -9.42 kcal/mol and the highest AiZynthFinder success rate of 36.1%, while also attaining the best QED (0.73) and 100% validity. On the LIT-PCBA pocket-specific benchmark, 3DSynthFlow achieves the best average Vina score of -10.89 kcal/mol across all 15 targets and a 68.58% AiZynth success rate. The method also demonstrates a 4.2× improvement in sampling efficiency over the 2D baseline RxnFlow (4,432 vs. 1,063 diverse high-scoring modes after 64,000 samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DSynthFlow outperforms all 11 baselines on the CrossDocked2020 benchmark in both Vina docking score and AiZynth success rate simultaneously — a first for any method. Against the closest 2D reaction-based GFlowNet (RxnFlow), 3DSynthFlow improves Vina from -8.85 to -9.42 and AiZynth from 34.8% to 36.1%, while maintaining comparable sampling time (6s vs. 4s). The strongest diffusion-based competitor, MolCRAFT-large, achieves a competitive Vina score (-9.25) but has a drastically lower AiZynth success rate (3.9% vs. 36.1%), highlighting the critical advantage of explicit synthesis pathway modeling. Among 2D GFlowNet baselines, SynFlowNet (v2024.10) and RGFN trail 3DSynthFlow by 0.5–1.7 kcal/mol in Vina on LIT-PCBA, while fragment-based methods (TacoGFN, FragGFN) are far behind in both affinity and synthesizability. On LIT-PCBA, 3DSynthFlow also achieves the best PoseCheck protein-ligand interaction score (0.50 sum vs. 0.30–0.33 for all baselines), confirming that 3D co-design yields more physically plausible binding poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGFlow extends flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) by introducing a component-wise local time schedule for the continuous state interpolation, drawing on the frame-level noise scheduling idea from video diffusion (Ruhe et al., 2024), while the discrete compositional structure is sampled via GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021) trained with trajectory balance. The 3DSynthFlow application directly builds on RxnFlow (Seo et al., 2024), a 2D synthesis-pathway GFlowNet, by adding a 3D state flow model (based on Semla, Irwin et al., 2024) that predicts binding poses for partially constructed ligands. This departs from prior SBDD methods (Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, MolCRAFT) that generate 3D structures without enforcing synthesizability, and from 2D synthesis-based GFlowNets (RGFN, SynFlowNet, RxnFlow) that cannot model 3D protein-ligand interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint co-design of 3D binding poses and synthesis pathways addresses a fundamental gap in target-based drug discovery: previously, methods optimized either binding affinity or synthesizability but not both simultaneously within a single generative model. The 4.2× sampling efficiency gain and the first simultaneous SOTA in both Vina Dock and AiZynth success rate on CrossDocked2020 suggest that 3D-aware synthesis-aware generation is a practical path toward experimentally viable drug candidates. Key limitations include the synthon-based action space excluding ring-forming reactions, the reliance on a pre-trained docking proxy for pocket-conditional rewards, and the current restriction to two synthesis steps. Open questions include whether CGFlow generalizes to longer synthesis trajectories, more complex reaction topologies, and other compositional domains beyond molecules.
