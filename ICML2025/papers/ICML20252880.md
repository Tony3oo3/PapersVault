## Problem

Existing dimensionality reduction methods (PCA, t-SNE, UMAP) for visualizing cross-modal embeddings focus on preserving feature distributions within a single modality, ignoring cross-modal metric scores like CLIPScore or HPS. When applied to metric-aware visualization, these methods produce dense neighborhoods where points with vastly different metric values overlap, and contour maps become highly inaccurate because local neighbors with mixed high and low values are collapsed into a single aggregated color. The paper addresses how to generate trustworthy 2D visualizations that faithfully capture the continuous landscape of cross-modal embedding metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252880/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional cross-modal embedding vectors (e.g., from CLIP) for prompt-image pairs, each paired with a scalar metric score (CLIPScore, HPSv2, PickScore, or Aesthetic Score), and produces 2D projected coordinates via a parametric 4-layer MLP. Because the projection is parametric, new unseen embeddings can be projected at inference time. The contour map is computed by evaluating a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression on a normalized [0,1]×[0,1] grid, with grid resolution dynamically adjustable for zoom; the scatterplot view colors each projected point by its metric value, and an overlay mode composites sampled points onto the contour map.

## Method

AKRMap jointly learns a 2D projection and a metric contour estimator through two coupled mechanisms. First, a kernel regression supervision loss is constructed by splitting data into 90% training and 10% validation each epoch: the Nadaraya-Watson regression in the projected space is fit only on training points, and the weighted MSE (w₁=1 on validation, w₂=0.3 on training) is backpropagated through the projection network, bridging nonparametric kernel regression with parametric DR. Second, the standard Gaussian RBF is replaced by an adaptive generalized t-distribution kernel K(x,α,β)=(1+α‖x‖^{2β})^{-1}, where α and β are learnable parameters inspired by the power-law scaling between high- and low-dimensional neighborhood radii, allowing the kernel decay rate to adapt to complex metric landscapes. The total objective combines this regression loss with a perplexity-free t-SNE KL-divergence neighborhood constraint (L = 0.125·MSE_r + KL), trained with Adam (lr=0.002) for 20 epochs on a single GPU.

## Results

On the HPD test set (out-of-sample), AKRMap achieves the lowest mapping error across all four cross-modal metrics. For HPSv2, AKRMap reduces MAE to 0.8108, a ~43% improvement over the best baseline t-SNE (1.4361). For CLIPScore, AKRMap achieves MAE of 1.8707 versus 4.0042 for PCA (best baseline), a ~53% reduction. Neuro-Visualizer performs worst on CLIPScore with MAE of 12.4201, indicating autoencoder-based methods struggle with cross-modal metric mapping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AKRMap outperforms all five baselines on every metric and every error measure (MAE, MAPE, RMSE) for both in-sample and out-of-sample points. Against t-SNE (the strongest traditional baseline), AKRMap reduces HPSv2 MAE from 1.4361 to 0.8108 (~43%) and CLIPScore MAE from 4.0241 to 1.8707 (~53%). PCA and UMAP show similar performance to t-SNE, with UMAP slightly better on Aesthetic Score (0.4463 vs 0.4725) but still well behind AKRMap (0.4305). The autoencoder-based SAE is consistently the weakest among the four traditional/encoder baselines on CLIPScore and PickScore, while Neuro-Visualizer collapses on CLIPScore (MAE 12.4201 vs ~4.0 for others), suggesting autoencoder decoders are poorly suited to cross-modal metric estimation. The gap between AKRMap and baselines is largest on CLIPScore, where the metric landscape is more complex, and smallest on Aesthetic Score, which is a unimodal metric with a simpler distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on parametric dimensionality reduction for embedding visualization (parametric t-SNE, parametric UMAP, autoencoder-based methods like SAE and Neuro-Visualizer) by introducing a fundamentally different optimization objective: preserving continuous cross-modal metric landscapes rather than neighborhood geometry. It draws on Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression and the power-law relationship between high-dimensional and low-dimensional local radii observed by Narayan et al. (2021), but departs from prior kernel-based DR (e.g., kernel PCA) by placing the kernel in the *projection* space as a learnable, adaptive component jointly optimized with the network. The supervised t-SNE framework of Hajderanj et al. (2019) provides the conceptual starting point, but AKRMap replaces label-based supervision with a cross-validation-style kernel regression loss that bridges parametric projection and nonparametric regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As cross-modal metrics like CLIPScore and HPS become standard for evaluating T2I models, the inability to inspect instance-level metric distributions undermines trust in aggregate scores; AKRMap addresses this gap with a practical, open-source tool supporting interactive zoom and overlay. The paper's limitation that neighborhood preservation is sacrificed for metric accuracy, and its uncertainty about handling sequence-based metrics (e.g., CodeBERTScore), leave open questions about generality. Scaling to billion-point pretraining datasets (e.g., LAION-5B filtering) and extending to multi-dimensional preference scores (MPS) are natural next steps that could significantly impact how the community audits large-scale generative models.
