## Problem

Existing SSM quantization methods either support only a single bit-width (Quamba: W8A8 on Mamba1) or degrade severely at lower bit-widths like W4A8 (MambaQuant), leaving no unified framework for diverse deployment scenarios. Moreover, prior work does not perform head-to-toe quantization (embedding through output head) and does not address the generalization gap that 4-bit weights introduce on large multitask benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained Mamba1 or Mamba2 checkpoint and a small calibration set (512 sentences sampled from the Pile dataset) to compute static scaling factors and sorting/clustering indices. It outputs fully quantized models in W8A8, W4A8, or W4A16 configurations spanning embedding, SSM blocks, and the language-model head, with offline-reordered weights, per-group scaling factors, and 8-bit cached SSM states ready for inference on CUDA hardware (A5000, Orin Nano 8G).

## Method

Quamba2 exploits two empirical properties of selective SSMs—channel order preserving (SSD computation is channel-wise, so input and output channel orders match) and activation/state persistence (activated channels and states remain consistent across time steps and samples)—to design two key quantization mechanisms. First, sort-and-cluster sorts SSM input channels by their calibrated maximum, then applies unsupervised clustering to group heads into m clusters and channels within each head into n clusters, yielding m×n scaling factors for 8-bit quantization of x. Second, per-state-group quantization assigns a single scaling factor per group of 128 states to the input-dependent parameters B and C, dramatically improving precision in low-activation groups. To maintain compute-invariance, the framework performs offline cluster-aware weight reordering (input projection, causal convolution, normalization, output projection) and fuses Hadamard matrices into the projection weights, so that online Hadamard transforms paired with quantization produce identical block outputs. For 4-bit weights, GPTQ is applied after Hadamard fusion, and an evolutionary search assigns layer-wise W4A8/W4A16 precisions (W4AX-mixed) to recover generalization on MMLU with only ~10% prefilling latency overhead.

## Results

Quamba2-8B (Mamba2) achieves 69.9% average zero-shot accuracy under W8A8, 69.4% under W4A8, and 70.2% under W4A16, compared to 70.8% for FP16 — a maximum drop of only 1.4%. In the W4A8 configuration, Quamba2 delivers 3.89× less memory, 1.39× faster prefilling (TTFT), and 3.05× faster generation (TPOT) for Mamba2-8B on an A5000 GPU. The framework enables head-to-toe 4-bit deployment of Mamba2-8B on an Orin Nano 8G at 13 tokens/second, where FP16 and W8A8 both fail with out-of-memory. On MMLU (5-shot), W4A16 retains 45.3% vs. 47.0% FP16, while the auto-searched W4AX mixed-precision model recovers to 44.0% with only 10% additional prefilling latency over pure W4A8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Quamba (Chiang et al., 2025), Quamba2 wins decisively on Mamba2-8B W8A8 (69.9% vs. 67.0%, a 2.9-point gap) and on latency (12.61 ms vs. 14.12 ms TPOT), while Quamba does not support Mamba2 or any 4-bit configuration at all. Against MambaQuant (Xu et al., 2025), Quamba2 wins at W4A8 on both Mamba1 sizes (56.7% vs. 54.3% at 1.4B; 61.0% vs. 58.5% at 2.8B) and is the only method supporting Mamba2; however, MambaQuant slightly edges Quamba2 at W8A8 on Mamba1-2.8B (62.1% vs. 61.8%) and Mamba1-1.4B (58.3% vs. 57.5%). In the accuracy-vs-latency Pareto front (Figure 12), Quamba2 models dominate both SSM baselines and Transformer baselines (QuaRot on Llama2, QServe on Llama3) across both A5000 and Orin Nano 8G, achieving the best accuracy at every latency point while also having the smallest memory footprint. The auto-searched W4AX mixed-precision model outperforms both handcrafted mixed-precision variants (HC-first, HC-last) by 0.9–1.9 points on MMLU at the same TTFT budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work Quamba (Chiang et al., 2025, W8A8-only for Mamba1) and MambaQuant (Xu et al., 2025, W8A8/W4A8 for Mamba1) by generalizing SSM post-training quantization to both Mamba1 and Mamba2 backbones across three bit-width configurations (W8A8, W4A8, W4A16) with head-to-toe coverage. The key conceptual departure is leveraging two newly identified SSM properties—channel order preserving in SSD computation and activation/state persistence—to design sort-and-cluster and per-state-group quantization, replacing the clipping-based approach used in Quamba. The offline cluster-aware weight reordering adapts the Transformer weight-reordering literature (RPTQ, QuaRot) to SSMs, where its applicability was previously unclear due to the linear recurrence structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Quamba2 provides the first unified, multi-bit-width PTQ framework for selective SSMs that enables practical edge deployment (Mamba2-8B on 8GB Orin Nano at 13 TPS) while maintaining within ~1.6% of FP16 accuracy, addressing a critical gap for SSM-based applications on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that W4A8 quantization incurs a 5.8% generalization drop on MMLU versus only 2.1% on LAMBADA reveals that standard zero-shot benchmarks understate the reasoning degradation from aggressive quantization, an important caveat for practitioners. Open questions include whether more advanced search algorithms can close the remaining generalization gap for 4-bit SSMs, whether the channel-persistence observations hold for other SSM variants (e.g., hybrid Transformer-Mamba models like Jamba), and whether the approach extends to sub-4-bit regimes.
