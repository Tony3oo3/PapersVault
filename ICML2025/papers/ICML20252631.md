## Problem

Deriving closed-form analytical solutions to differential equations has traditionally required deep mathematical expertise, while existing ML approaches either produce complex, hard-to-interpret expressions (genetic programming) or sacrifice symbolic interpretability entirely (PINNs, neural operators). The core challenge is simultaneously maintaining strict adherence to physical laws, ensuring computational efficiency, and preserving human-readable symbolic forms—current methods fail to achieve all three.

## Input / Output

SSDE takes as input a differential equation (ODE or PDE) with its boundary and initial conditions, a user-defined symbol library of permissible operators and functions, and the computational domain—no labeled data is required. It outputs a closed-form symbolic expression (an expression tree with optimized constants) that satisfies the physical constraints to machine precision. For high-dimensional PDEs, the solution is constructed as a nested parametric expression where each variable's contribution is discovered sequentially, and constants are represented as vectors that degenerate to scalars when their variance across sampled points falls below a threshold.

## Method

SSDE employs an RNN to autoregressively generate symbolic expression skeletons (pre-order traversals of expression trees) from a given symbol library, guided by parent and sibling node representations. A key innovation is Risk-Seeking Constant Optimization (RSCO): in each batch, only the top-ε fraction of expressions (by boundary/initial-condition loss) undergo full physics-constraint optimization via BFGS, while the rest are filtered early, eliminating wasted computation on infeasible skeletons. For high-dimensional PDEs, a recursive exploration strategy decomposes the solution into sequential single-variable rounds using parametric expressions, where each round's RNN discovers the skeleton for one variable while treating remaining variables' contributions as optimizable parameters. Training uses risk-seeking policy gradients (selecting only top-ε rewards) augmented with hierarchical entropy regularization to encourage exploration, with the reward computed from the RMSE of PDE residuals, boundary conditions, and initial conditions.

## Results

SSDE achieves a 100% complete recovery rate (P_RE) on all six PDE benchmarks (2D/3D Poisson, Heat, and Wave equations), with physics-constraint errors (L_PHY) ranging from 1.05×10⁻⁵ (Heat3D) to 8.17×10⁻⁶ (Wave3D). All three baselines—PINN+DSR, KAN, and PR-GPSR—achieve 0% recovery rate across every benchmark, with L_PHY values 2–5 orders of magnitude higher (e.g., KAN reaches 1.36×10¹ on Wave3D vs. SSDE's 8.17×10⁻⁶). On the 12-problem Γ dataset (ODEs and 2D PDEs derived from Nguyen expressions), SSDE again recovers 100% of ground-truth solutions, while PINN+DSR fails on 5 of 12 problems entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDE decisively outperforms all baselines on every benchmark. Against PINN+DSR (symbolic regression on PINN numerical solutions), SSDE achieves 100% vs. 0% recovery rate on all six PDEs, with L_PHY roughly 3–5 orders of magnitude lower; on the Γ dataset, PINN+DSR additionally fails completely on 5/12 problems. KAN (Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks) performs worst among the three main baselines, with L_PHY values reaching 1.36×10¹ on Wave3D—over 15 orders of magnitude above SSDE—and 0% recovery everywhere. PR-GPSR (physics-regularized genetic programming) is the closest competitor in L_PHY (e.g., 1.90×10⁻² on Poisson2D) but still 2–3 orders of magnitude worse than SSDE and achieves 0% recovery on all benchmarks. In supplementary comparisons (Table 6), SSDE also beats standalone PINN (best relative L₂ error 6.24×10⁻⁴ vs. SSDE's 1.55×10⁻⁵ on Poisson2D), FEX, DSR, and Mathematica (which fails to return solutions on all six PDEs). The recursive exploration strategy is critical: ablation shows that removing it drops SSDE's recovery rate to 0% on 3D problems, while removing RSCO significantly impairs convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSDE extends the risk-seeking policy gradient framework from Deep Symbolic Regression (Petersen et al., 2020) and DSO (Mundhenk et al., 2021) by repurposing it for a fundamentally different objective: rather than regressing expressions from observed data, it directly searches for closed-form solutions that satisfy physical constraints (PDE residuals, boundary conditions, initial conditions) via automatic differentiation. The key conceptual departure is the recursive exploration strategy, which decomposes high-dimensional PDE solving into sequential single-variable subproblems using parametric expressions—a decomposition not present in prior GP-based or RL-based symbolic regression for DEs. The RSCO two-stage optimization (satisfy deterministic conditions first, then optimize against the full PDE) draws on the hard-constraint embedding philosophy of Lu et al. (2021c) but applies it within an RL training loop rather than a network architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% complete recovery rate across all benchmarks (vs. 0% for all baselines) demonstrates that direct physics-constrained RL search can outperform the dominant paradigm of applying symbolic regression to PINN numerical outputs, avoiding error propagation from approximate solutions. The recursive decomposition for high-dimensional PDEs offers a practical path toward symbolic solution discovery in settings where the combinatorial search space would otherwise be intractable. However, the benchmarks are relatively simple (polynomial, exponential, and trigonometric solutions on small domains with small symbol libraries), and it remains open whether the approach scales to genuinely complex PDEs with large operator sets, irregular domains, or coupled systems. The method also requires the solution to be expressible within the given symbol library, limiting its applicability to problems with known functional form families.
