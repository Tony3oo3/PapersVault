## Problem

Despite LoRA's widespread adoption for fine-tuning large models, its theoretical understanding has remained limited, and no principled explanation exists for why it works or how to improve its sample efficiency. This paper shows that vanilla LoRA, when analyzed through a Mixture of Experts lens, suffers from suboptimal (exponential) sample complexity for low-rank matrix estimation, motivating a reparameterization strategy that provably reduces the required data to a polynomial scale.

## Input / Output

RepLoRA operates on pre-trained transformer backbones (LLaMA-7B/13B, ViT-B/16, Video Swin-B, VL-BART) by fine-tuning only the query and value projection matrices within each attention head. Inputs span text sequences, RGB images, video clips, and image/video paired with text; outputs are task-specific (generated text, class labels, action labels, captions, or answers). A key design constraint is that the reparameterization MLPs are discarded after training, so the deployed model stores only the standard low-rank matrices A_Q, A_V, B_Q, B_V, incurring zero additional inference cost over vanilla LoRA.

## Method

The paper first establishes that each multi-head self-attention head can be decomposed into a Mixture of Experts model, where LoRA's low-rank updates correspond to modifying both the expert functions and the softmax gating score functions. Under this MoE formulation, the authors prove via Voronoi-cell-based loss functions that estimating the low-rank matrices without shared structure yields convergence rates no faster than O_P(1/log^τ(n)), requiring exponential data for a target error ε. In contrast, imposing a shared reparameterization structure—where A_Q, A_V are outputs of a common MLP g_θA(A) and B_Q, B_V are outputs of g_θB(B) with shared hidden layers and distinct output heads—achieves parametric O_P(√(log n / n)) rates, reducing data requirements to O(ε^{-4}) or O(ε^{-2}). Practically, RepLoRA implements A and B as diagonal matrices fed through two-layer MLPs with non-linear activations, and the entire reparameterization is discarded post-training, leaving only the standard LoRA matrices for inference.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-7B, RepLoRA achieves 79.1% average accuracy (PPT 1.96), outperforming LoRA (74.1%, PPT 1.70) by 5.0 points and DoRA (78.4%) by 0.7 points. With LLaMA-13B, RepLoRA reaches 82.9% average (PPT 2.60) vs. LoRA's 80.2% (PPT 2.15). On fine-grained image classification (FGVC), RepLoRA achieves 90.7% average accuracy vs. LoRA's 84.8% (+5.9 points), and on VTAB-1K it reaches 75.9% vs. LoRA's 72.2% (+3.7 points). In the sample-efficiency experiment, RepLoRA matches LoRA's full-data performance using only 30% of training data and achieves a 40.4% gap at 1% data fraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepLoRA consistently outperforms LoRA across all four evaluated domains: +5.0% on commonsense reasoning (LLaMA-7B), +5.9% on FGVC image classification, +3.7% on VTAB-1K, +7.8% on SSv2 video recognition, and +4.3% on video-text understanding. Against DoRA, the closest competitor, RepLoRA leads by 0.7% on commonsense reasoning (7B), 3.5% on FGVC, and 2.4% on video-text tasks, while using a comparable number of trainable parameters (~1% of total). RepLoRA substantially surpasses Adapter (+8.3% on commonsense 7B, +5.1% on FGVC) and Prefix Tuning (+14.5% on commonsense 7B, +4.4% on FGVC), though Prefix Tuning narrowly edges out RepLoRA on the StanfordCars dataset (90.2% vs. 87.6%). The PPT metric confirms that RepLoRA's accuracy gains come at minimal parameter cost, achieving the highest PPT scores in nearly all settings (e.g., 2.60 vs. 2.15 for LoRA on LLaMA-13B commonsense reasoning).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by reparameterizing its low-rank matrices A and B as outputs of lightweight MLPs, drawing on reparameterization ideas from the prefix-tuning literature (Li & Liang, 2021; Le et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is a theoretical bridge between LoRA and Mixture of Experts models, building on the recent finding (Le et al., 2024) that each attention head can be decomposed into an MoE structure. Within the broader PEFT landscape, RepLoRA sits alongside DoRA, AdaLoRA, and VeRA as a LoRA variant, but uniquely provides a statistical learning theory justification (via Voronoi-cell-based MoE analysis) for why its reparameterization improves sample efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that vanilla LoRA requires exponentially many samples to achieve a given estimation error, while reparameterized LoRA needs only polynomially many, provides a principled explanation for why shared-structure reparameterization helps and could guide future PEFT design beyond LoRA. Practically, RepLoRA achieves up to 40% improvement over LoRA at 1% data and matches full-data LoRA with only 30% of training data, while incurring zero inference overhead since the MLP is discarded post-training. Open questions include whether the simplified theoretical setting (A_Q = A_V, B_Q = B_V) fully captures the practical method's behavior, whether the exponential-to-polynomial gap holds in more realistic non-i.i.d. or multi-task settings, and whether the MoE perspective can be extended to analyze other PEFT methods like DoRA or VeRA.
