## Problem

Existing deep count regression methods (Poisson and Negative Binomial DNNs) impose variance restrictions—equi-dispersion or overdispersion—that prevent them from achieving full heteroscedasticity, while Gaussian-based models violate the discrete nature of count targets by assigning probability mass to infeasible real values. This misspecification corrupts aleatoric uncertainty estimates and, by extension, epistemic uncertainty in deep ensembles, degrading both calibration and out-of-distribution detection. No prior method provides a fully heteroscedastic predictive distribution over non-negative integers for neural count regression.

## Input / Output

The network accepts heterogeneous inputs—tabular feature vectors, 224×224 RGB images, 3D point clouds, or tokenized text sequences—processed by modality-appropriate backbones (MLP, ViT-B-16, CountNet3D, or DistilBERT) into a shared hidden representation. From this representation, two separate affine heads produce log(μ̂) and log(γ̂), the log-scale parameters of a Double Poisson distribution over ℤ≥0. When ensembled, M independently trained DDPNs are combined as a uniform mixture of their predictive PMFs, yielding a joint predictive distribution whose total variance decomposes into aleatoric (mean of member variances) and epistemic (variance of member means) components.

## Method

DDPN outputs the parameters (μ, γ) of the Double Poisson distribution, which under Efron's moment approximations has mean μ and variance μ/γ, granting unrestricted variance over (0,∞) and thus full heteroscedasticity. The authors formally define 'learnable loss attenuation'—the property that a loss decomposes into a dispersion penalty d(φ̂) growing in φ̂ and an attenuation factor a(φ̂) shrinking in φ̂ multiplying a residual penalty—and prove the Double Poisson NLL satisfies this definition, explaining DDPN's robustness to outliers. To counter premature convergence where the network 'explains away' poor mean fit via inflated uncertainty, they introduce β-DDPN, a stop-gradient modification L^(β) = [γ̂^{-β}]·L that interpolates between the natural likelihood (β=0) and a mean-focused objective (β=1), tempering the attenuation strength. Training minimizes the Double Poisson NLL (with the normalizing constant set to 1 per Efron's approximation) via AdamW with cosine learning-rate annealing, and ensembles are formed by uniform mixture of M independently trained networks.

## Results

Across four diverse count regression datasets (tabular, image, point cloud, text), DDPN and its β-variants achieve the best MAE and CRPS in every setting. For ensembles, β₁.₀-DDPN attains MAE 0.959 / CRPS 0.712 on COCO-People, MAE 0.859 / CRPS 0.597 on Inventory, MAE 0.344 / CRPS 0.257 on Amazon Reviews, and DDPN achieves MAE 0.485 / CRPS 0.361 on Length of Stay—outperforming the best continuous baseline (β₀.₅-Gaussian) by 5–17% in MAE and 4–15% in CRPS. For OOD detection, β₀.₅-DDPN ensembles reach AUROC 0.887, AUPR 0.875, and FPR80 0.199, surpassing the best Gaussian baseline (AUROC 0.840, AUPR 0.812, FPR80 0.318).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPN outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. The discrete baselines (Poisson DNN and NB DNN) are the weakest performers: their equi-dispersion and overdispersion constraints prevent them from modeling under-dispersion, leading to inflated uncertainty and poor mean fit (e.g., Poisson DNN MAE 0.650 vs. DDPN 0.485 on Length of Stay). Among continuous baselines, β₀.₅-Gaussian is the strongest competitor, achieving the best non-DDPN MAE on COCO-People (1.019) and Inventory (0.879), yet DDPN still leads by 5–6% in MAE and 4–5% in CRPS. Faithful Gaussian and Natural Gaussian improve over vanilla Gaussian DNN by decoupling mean and variance gradients, but remain 10–15% behind DDPN in CRPS across datasets. The gap between Gaussian and DDPN models is attributed to the inductive bias mismatch: Gaussian models assign probability mass to infeasible continuous values, producing misaligned predictive CDFs. For OOD detection, DDPN ensembles dominate all baselines, with β₀.₅-DDPN achieving AUROC 0.887 vs. 0.840 for the best Gaussian (a 5.6% relative improvement) and FPR80 0.199 vs. 0.318 (a 37% relative reduction in false positives at 80% TPR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Double Poisson distribution from the generalized linear model literature (Efron, 1986) to deep neural count regression, filling a clear gap: existing discrete count regressors (Poisson, Negative Binomial) impose equi-dispersion or overdispersion restrictions that prevent full heteroscedasticity, while Gaussian regressors lack the discrete inductive bias. The β-modification for controllable loss attenuation is a direct discrete analog of Seitzer et al. (2022), and the ensemble strategy follows Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017). The paper also formalizes 'learnable loss attenuation'—a property previously only informally described for Gaussian NLL by Kendall & Gal (2017)—and proves DDPN satisfies it, providing a theoretical bridge between continuous and discrete heteroscedastic regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDPN provides the first fully heteroscedastic deep count regression model, which matters because count prediction is pervasive (crowd counting, inventory, traffic, healthcare) yet prior methods could not flexibly represent under-dispersion or arbitrarily low aleatoric uncertainty. The consistent SOTA across four modalities (tabular, image, point cloud, text) and the OOD detection gains suggest broad practical utility. Open questions include behavior at very high counts (where Gaussian approximation may suffice), the single-dataset OOD evaluation, and whether the Efron moment approximations degrade in extreme regimes. The formalization of learnable loss attenuation may also inform future work on robustness in other probabilistic regression settings.
