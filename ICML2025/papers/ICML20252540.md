## Problem

LLMs fundamentally lack physical awareness — the ability to understand real-world phenomena such as Doppler shifts, multipath reflections, and spatial relationships from sound. A critical barrier is data scarcity: collecting large-scale audio with diverse physical phenomena is prohibitively expensive, and annotating physical effects (e.g., reverberation time, frequency shift) is nearly infeasible by human labelers alone, requiring sophisticated measurement equipment.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw 16 kHz audio waveforms (10-second clips) and a natural-language question as input. For tasks like Doppler estimation and range estimation, two audio channels are provided (original source and received signal). The audio is converted via STFT (window 254, hop 10 ms) into three spectral channels — log-magnitude, sine of phase, and cosine of phase — rather than the mel-spectrogram used by Whisper. Output is a free-form textual answer: binary (Yes/No), multi-class (Rich/Moderate/Negligible), or a numerical estimate (e.g., Doppler shift percentage, TDoA in samples, distance in meters).

## Method

The core insight is decomposing the received signal into source and channel (y = h ⊛ s), enabling synthetic data generation by convolving real AudioSet sound sources with a physics-based channel simulator that independently models five components: LOS paths, early reflections, frequency-selective reverberation, Doppler-induced time-varying delay, and binaural microphone-array reception. This produces the AQA-PHY dataset of 1 million <Audio, Question, Answer> tuples with both closed-form and open-form QA pairs. The audio encoder extends Whisper-large-v2 by adding two phase channels (sin and cos of STFT phase) alongside magnitude, each processed through 3×3 1D convolutions before fusion and a 32-layer transformer. The encoder is initialized from Whisper (magnitude path) with the phase subnetwork trained from scratch, and the LLM backbone (Llama3.1-8B or Qwen2-7B) is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) using standard next-token prediction on the QA dataset.

## Results

On the AQA-PHY benchmark (Merged setting), ACORN with Qwen2-7B achieves 0.924 BCA for LOS detection, 0.181 MAE_f for Doppler estimation, 0.907 MAE_t for DoA estimation, 0.903 TCA for multipath analysis, and 1.599 REP for range estimation. With Llama3.1-8B, the corresponding values are 0.920, 0.791, 1.423, 0.890, and 1.764. In zero-shot real-world vehicle experiments, the model achieves 0.845–0.870 accuracy for LOS detection and 0.8975–0.925 accuracy for left/right DoA classification without any real-world training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Whisper-large-v2 encoder (magnitude-only), paired with the same two LLM backbones. ACORN's phase-aware encoder consistently outperforms Whisper across all five tasks and both LLMs. With Qwen2-7B, ACORN reduces Doppler MAE_f from 1.042 to 0.181 (5.8× improvement), cuts DoA MAE_t from 2.716 to 0.907 (66.6% reduction), and lowers range REP from 10.609 to 1.599 (6.6× improvement), while also gaining +4.3 pp on LOS BCA and +5.5 pp on multipath TCA. With Llama3.1-8B, the gains are similarly large: DoA MAE_t drops by 74.5% (5.585→1.423) and range REP by 7.1× (12.572→1.764). The paper does not compare against other audio-LLM systems (e.g., Pengi, LTU, Qwen-Audio, BAT) in the main results table, limiting the scope of external comparison to the encoder-level ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio LLM line of work (Pengi, LTU, Qwen-Audio, BAT) by shifting focus from semantic content recognition to physical phenomena understanding (Doppler, multipath, LOS, DoA, range). It builds directly on Whisper-large-v2 as the audio encoder backbone but departs by adding explicit sine/cosine phase channels alongside magnitude, arguing that physical effects manifest in phase relationships that magnitude-only encoders miss. The data generation strategy contrasts with environment-specific CIR reconstruction (SoundSpaces 2.0) by decomposing sound into source and channel, then synthesizing channels via component-wise signal processing (damped sinusoids, bandpass-filtered noise, time-varying delay), enabling systematic parameter control at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in LLM capabilities: the inability to reason about physical dynamics through sound, with direct implications for vehicle security (rejecting external voice commands), embodied AI (turning toward speakers), and autonomous driving (detecting sirens). The 1M-sample AQA-PHY dataset and the physics-based channel simulator are reusable assets for the broader audio LLM community. However, real-world validation is limited to a single vehicle with 600 total samples, and the sim-to-real gap (e.g., LOS accuracy dropping from 0.924 to 0.845–0.870) remains a significant open question. Future work on multi-turn interaction, internal reasoning chains, and scaling to more complex physical phenomena (e.g., multi-source, dynamic environments) is needed to move beyond the current single-turn, single-phenomenon paradigm.
