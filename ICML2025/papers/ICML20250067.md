## Problem

Existing differentially private online learning algorithms for prediction with expert advice are limited to static regret, comparing against a single fixed expert chosen in hindsight. This is unrealistic in non-stationary environments where the optimal expert shifts over time, motivating dynamic regret (tracking the best expert with at most S switches). This paper initiates the study of private online prediction under dynamic regret, providing algorithms and lower bounds for three adversary types: shifting stochastic, oblivious, and adaptive.

## Input / Output

At each of T rounds, the algorithm receives a loss vector ℓ_t: [N] → [0,1] and must output an expert J_t ∈ [N], with the constraint that the entire sequence of plays is (ε,δ)-differentially private with respect to any single-round change in the loss sequence. The comparison benchmark is the best sequence of experts with at most S switches, making the problem harder as S grows. The algorithm's performance is measured by expected dynamic regret, and the key regime-dependent result is that sub-linear regret is achievable for oblivious adversaries at ε ≤ √(S/T) but impossible for adaptive adversaries in the same regime.

## Method

The paper develops three distinct algorithmic strategies tailored to each adversary. For shifting stochastic adversaries, an SVT-based algorithm uses the sparse-vector-technique to detect distribution shifts (by monitoring whether internal adaptive regret exceeds a threshold) and restarts a lazy report-noisy-max subroutine, achieving O(√(ST log(NT)) + S log(NT)/ε) regret. For oblivious adversaries, a reduction constructs a class of meta-experts (all sequences of N experts with ≤ S switches, yielding (NT)^{2S} meta-experts) and applies existing private static-regret algorithms, giving O(√(ST log(NT)) + ST^{1/3} log(T/δ) log(NT)/ε^{2/3}) regret. For adaptive adversaries, a private variant of Lu & Zhang's clipped-simplex mirror descent adds independent Laplace(1/ε) noise to each loss vector before the Bregman-divergence update, achieving O(√(ST) log^{1.5}(NT)/ε + S log(NT)/ε) regret. A matching lower bound of Ω(min(T, S/(ε log(T/(S+1)))²)) establishes ε ≈ √(S/T) as a sharp threshold separating feasible from infeasible private learning under adaptive adversaries.

## Results

The paper establishes the first differentially private dynamic regret bounds for prediction with expert advice under three adversary models. For shifting stochastic adversaries, the SVT-based algorithm achieves expected dynamic regret O(√(ST log(NT)) + S log(NT) log(T)/ε), matching a lower bound of Ω(√(ST log N) + S log(N/S)/ε) up to logarithmic factors. For oblivious adversaries, a reduction from dynamic to static regret yields O(√(ST log(NT)) + ST^{1/3} log(T/δ) log(NT)/ε^{2/3}). For adaptive adversaries, the paper proves a sharp threshold at ε ≈ √(S/T): below this, any (ε,δ)-DP algorithm suffers linear regret Ω(T), while above it, sub-linear regret O(√(ST) log^{1.5}(NT)/ε + S log(NT)/ε) is achievable.

| Adversary | Upper Bound | Lower Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Shifting Stochastic | √(ST log N) + S log N / ε | √(ST log N) + S log(N/S) / ε |
| Oblivious | √(ST log N) + ST^{1/3} log N / ε^{2/3} | √(ST log N) + S log(N/S) / ε |
| Adaptive | √(ST) log^{1.5} N / ε + S log N / ε | √(ST log N) + S / (ε log(T/(S+1)))² |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between regret bounds across adversary models and against prior static-regret results. The shifting stochastic upper bound nearly matches the lower bound (gap is a log T factor), establishing near-optimality. The oblivious upper bound improves upon what would be obtained by naively applying static private algorithms per segment, leveraging the reduction from Asi et al. (2024) which achieves T^{1/3}/ε^{2/3} scaling. The adaptive adversary lower bound extends the static separation of Asi et al. (2023b) (which showed linear regret for ε ≤ 1/√T) to the dynamic setting with threshold ε ≤ √(S/T), showing that the dynamic problem is strictly harder. The adaptive upper bound is derived by privatizing the non-private clipped-simplex algorithm of Lu & Zhang (2019), introducing an additional 1/ε factor compared to the non-private √(ST log N) rate. No matching lower bound is known for the oblivious setting, leaving a gap between the upper and lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on differentially private online prediction with expert advice from the static regret setting (Asi et al., 2023b; 2024) to the dynamic regret setting, where the comparison sequence of experts is allowed to switch up to S times. For the stochastic adversary, it builds on the lazy report-noisy-max algorithm of Asi et al. (2023b) and augments it with the sparse-vector-technique to detect distribution shifts; for the adaptive adversary, it privatizes the clipped-simplex mirror-descent algorithm of Lu & Zhang (2019) by adding per-coordinate Laplace noise. The oblivious-adversary result is obtained via a meta-expert reduction that converts any private static-regret algorithm into a private dynamic-regret algorithm, instantiating the best known static bounds from Asi et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of private online learning under dynamic regret, establishing that the problem is fundamentally harder than its static counterpart and identifying a sharp privacy threshold ε ≈ √(S/T) below which learning under adaptive adversaries becomes infeasible. A key open question is the design of efficient algorithms for the oblivious setting: the current reduction constructs an expert set exponential in T, and it is unclear how to privatize the clipping operations in non-private dynamic-regret algorithms without incurring large privacy costs. The tension between lazy updating (which enables privacy) and frequent expert switching (which is necessary for tracking) remains a central unresolved challenge.
