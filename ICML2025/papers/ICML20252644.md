## Problem

Current large vision-language models (LVLMs) are expected to deliver accurate perception in a single pass, yet they frequently produce hallucinations and miss critical visual details. While LVLMs possess intrinsic self-reflection capabilities, these abilities are unstable and have not been effectively activated through training. The paper asks whether an iterative, reflection-based perception paradigm—mimicking how humans gradually refine their visual understanding—can systematically overcome these single-pass limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252644/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question or captioning instruction as input. During inference, the policy model receives the image, question, and the full history of previous responses and critic feedback (scores plus rationales) in a multi-turn dialogue format, producing a sequence of progressively refined text responses. The final output is the last-turn response, which is a more accurate and detailed caption or answer than the initial single-pass prediction.

## Method

RePer introduces a dual-model architecture where a policy model generates perceptual responses and a critic model evaluates them with scores and rationales, alternating over multiple reflection turns. Training is powered by Reflective Perceptual Learning (RPL), which first constructs a visual reflection dataset by generating diverse candidates via temperature sampling, scoring them with a hybrid VLM-based and rule-based reward system, and organizing them into multi-turn dialogues ordered from lowest to highest quality. The key training objective is reflective unlikelihood training, which balances a likelihood loss (weighted by normalized reward σ) and an unlikelihood loss (weighted by 1−σ) so that early low-quality responses are penalized while later high-quality responses are reinforced, preventing behavioral collapse. This formulation unifies listwise preference optimization with fine-grained feedback signals, outperforming DPO and LiPO while enabling object-level and token-level preference supervision through explicit critic rationales.

## Results

RePer (LLaVA-1.5 13B + RePer) achieves 54.73 CAPTURE on DetailCaps-4870 (+3.50 over LLaVA-1.5 13B), 51.00 aAcc on HallusionBench (+7.15), and 82.54 GAPE Total (+5.17). At 7B scale, RePer reaches 52.89 CAPTURE (+1.86), 38.70 aAcc (+3.05), and 80.88 GAPE Total (+5.72). The method also improves DetailCaps Recall by +12.17% (13B) and +8.30% (7B), indicating substantially better perception of visual details, while reducing hallucination rates on MMHal-Bench (0.52 vs. 0.58 at 13B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePer consistently outperforms all baselines at both 7B and 13B scales. At 13B, RePer surpasses LLaVA-1.5 13B by +7.15 on HallusionBench aAcc, +3.50 on DetailCaps CAPTURE, and +5.17 on GAPE Total; it also beats VOLCANO 13B (the closest self-feedback competitor) by +10.31 on HallusionBench aAcc and +4.37 on GAPE Total. At 7B, RePer improves over LLaVA-1.5 7B by +3.05 on HallusionBench aAcc and +5.72 on GAPE Total, and outperforms VOLCANO 7B by +12.20 on HallusionBench aAcc and +2.10 on GAPE Total. In the preference-optimization comparison (Table 5), RPL outperforms DPO by +4.20 on DetailCaps and +5.67 on HallusionBench, and beats LiPO by +2.42 on DetailCaps and +6.24 on HallusionBench, confirming that the unlikelihood-based reflective training is more effective than standard pairwise or listwise preference optimization for hallucination reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-correction/reflective paradigm from LLMs (SCoRe, RISE) to the vision-language domain, but departs from prior visual CoT approaches (Shikra, ChartThinker, Merlin) that perform perception in a single pass with specialized intermediate tasks. The core training mechanism builds on unlikelihood training (Welleck et al., 2019), repurposing it within a multi-turn reflective dialogue setting where reward-normalized weights balance likelihood and unlikelihood across turns. Conceptually, RPL positions itself as a unification of DPO and LiPO-style preference optimization, but with fine-grained critic feedback and explicit reward signals that those methods lack, while the inference-time policy-critic architecture draws on the broader RL agent literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and fundamental limitation of LVLMs—the unrealistic expectation of perfect first-pass perception—by providing a principled iterative refinement mechanism with measurable gains in hallucination reduction and caption detail. The finding that iterative reflection progressively aligns model attention with human visual focus offers a mechanistic explanation for why the approach works, beyond mere score improvement. However, the approach incurs inference-time latency (multiple policy-critic rounds), relies on an external critic model, and has only been validated on LLaVA-1.5 at 7B/13B scale; open questions include whether the paradigm scales to video understanding, complex multi-step visual reasoning, or settings where a strong critic is unavailable.
