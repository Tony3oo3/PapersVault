## Problem

Prior work posits that a single linear direction in the residual stream governs LLM refusal, yet whether refusal is actually mediated by higher-dimensional geometric structures and whether orthogonal directions are truly independent under intervention remain untested. The paper addresses both gaps by asking whether multi-dimensional cones of refusal directions exist and whether non-linear network transformations create indirect coupling that orthogonality alone cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen chat model, paired harmful/harmless prompts (SALADBENCH + ALPACA), and model-specific target sequences generated by ablating or adding the DIM direction to match each model's refusal style. It produces unit-norm direction vectors at a chosen residual-stream layer, orthonormal bases spanning polyhedral cones (up to 5D), and iteratively discovered sets of representationally independent directions. The retain loss constrains side effects via KL divergence over specific token positions, while the RepInd loss is computed over the first 90% of layers to avoid over-constraining near the unembedding.

## Method

Refusal Direction Optimization (RDO) uses gradient descent on a direction vector with three loss terms: a cross-entropy ablation loss (model answers harmful prompts when the direction is projected out), a cross-entropy addition loss (model refuses harmless prompts when the direction is added), and a KL-divergence retain loss preserving harmless-input behavior. This extends to Refusal Cone Optimization (RCO), which optimizes an orthonormal basis spanning a polyhedral cone where all positive combinations mediate refusal, using Monte Carlo sampling from the cone plus basis-vector losses and Gram-Schmidt projection after each step. The paper introduces Representational Independence (RepInd), a stricter criterion requiring that ablating one direction leaves the cosine similarity of another direction to activations unchanged at every layer, capturing non-linear coupling invisible to Euclidean orthogonality. RepInd is enforced via an additional loss penalizing cross-direction cosine-similarity changes, enabling iterative discovery of genuinely independent refusal mechanisms whose simultaneous ablation yields additive attack-success improvements.

## Results

On JailbreakBench, RDO achieves competitive directional-ablation ASR (75.7–80.3%) comparable to DIM (76.2–79.7%) across Gemma 2, Llama 3, and Qwen 2.5 models, while substantially outperforming DIM on activation-subtraction ASR (e.g., 75.6% vs. 66.6% on Qwen 2.5 14B). RDO reduces TruthfulQA degradation from directional ablation by 1.1–4.6 points over DIM (e.g., 67.9 vs. 63.3 on Qwen 2.5 14B, baseline 70.8), indicating more surgical interventions. The paper further identifies refusal concept cones up to 5 dimensions and multiple representationally independent directions whose simultaneous ablation yields additive ASR gains, surpassing the single DIM direction for k ≥ 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the DIM (Difference-in-Means) refusal direction from Arditi et al. (2024), extracted using the same protocol but with the paper's expanded dataset. RDO matches or slightly exceeds DIM on directional-ablation ASR (within ~1–2 points across models) and clearly wins on activation-subtraction ASR, particularly for Qwen 2.5 models (e.g., 75.6% vs. 66.6% on 14B; 65.2% vs. 55.0% on 1.5B). On side effects, RDO consistently preserves more benchmark performance: TruthfulQA scores are 1.1–4.6 points higher than DIM under ablation, and MMLU/ARC/GSM8K degradations are smaller in most model–benchmark combinations. The paper also demonstrates that RDO enables capabilities DIM cannot provide: identifying multi-dimensional refusal cones (up to 5D) and multiple representationally independent directions whose joint ablation yields additive ASR gains beyond the single DIM direction. On the XSTest over-refusal trade-off, RDO achieves a uniformly better Pareto frontier than DIM, refusing more harmful inputs at the same or lower benign over-refusal rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation engineering framework of Zou et al. (2023) by replacing the difference-in-means (DIM) extraction with a gradient-based optimization (RDO) that directly encodes desired refusal properties (monotonic scaling, surgical ablation) as loss functions, borrowing the formal property definitions from Park et al. (2023). Its most direct departure is from Arditi et al. (2024), who demonstrated that a single linear direction in activation space mediates refusal; here the authors show that refusal is instead governed by multi-dimensional polyhedral cones (up to 5D) and that orthogonality of directions does not guarantee functional independence under intervention, motivating their new representational independence criterion. The gradient-based approach also subsumes and generalizes prior activation-steering methods by allowing arbitrary side constraints (e.g., retain loss) to be incorporated into the optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that refusal is mediated by multiple representationally independent mechanisms rather than a single direction, this work has direct implications for both offensive research (multi-direction attacks are more robust) and defensive strategies (robust safety requires protecting multiple independent pathways, not just one). The representational independence framework is a generalizable tool applicable to any pair of interventions in LLM activation space, potentially useful beyond refusal for understanding other aligned behaviors. Key limitations include the use of shared optimization targets across directions (which may constrain the diversity of discovered mechanisms) and the inability to rule out additional undiscovered refusal regions; extending the method with diverse targets or judge-based rewards is identified as a natural next step.
