## Problem

Standard attention's scoring function uses a low-rank bilinear form (W_Q W_K^T with rank r << D), creating an information bottleneck that prevents transformers from solving inherently high-dimensional tasks like in-context regression unless the head dimension nearly matches the input dimension. Additionally, the same scoring function is applied uniformly to all token pairs regardless of their positional distance, wasting compute on long-range interactions that are often less informative than local ones. The paper asks whether structured matrices can simultaneously resolve both limitations while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequences of D-dimensional token embeddings (where D = H·r for H heads of dimension r). For in-context regression, the input is a prompt of N pairs (x_i, w^T x_i) with x_i ∈ R^{d_input} and the model predicts w^T x_N as a scalar. For language modeling, character-level tokenized text sequences of length T=1024 produce next-token distributions. For time series, 7-dimensional feature vectors over horizons of 96–336 steps produce future values. A key structural detail is that the scoring function itself is modified: instead of a single dot product through a shared low-rank projection, each token pair's score is computed through a structured matrix whose rank and block structure depend on the pair's relative position in the sequence.

## Method

The paper replaces the low-rank matrix W_Q W_K^T in the attention scoring function with high-rank structured matrices—Block Tensor Train (BTT) and Multi-Level Low Rank (MLR)—that require only O(D^{3/2}) parameters and FLOPs while achieving full or near-full rank, directly resolving the low-rank bottleneck. For the distance-dependent compute bias, the score matrix S itself is made MLR-structured: the sequence is hierarchically partitioned into blocks at L levels (with p_l = 2^{l-1} blocks at level l), and each token pair's score is computed as a sum of low-rank terms up to the level at which they share a block, so nearby tokens receive higher-rank (more expressive) scoring while distant tokens receive lower-rank scoring. This reduces the FLOPs for forming S from T²r to T²·Σ_l r_l/2^{l-1} and shrinks the KV cache during autoregressive generation by a factor of up to 4×. The authors also define Multi-Level Block Tensor Contraction (MLBTC) as a unifying family that subsumes both BTT and MLR (and hence Monarch, Butterfly, Kronecker, and low-rank matrices). Training uses μP for stable width scaling and QK LayerNorm for numerical stability, with the optimal tensor contraction order chosen to minimize FLOPs.

## Results

On in-context linear regression (d_input=128), Bilinear BTT attention with 8 heads solves the task at embedding dimension D=256, while standard 8-head attention fails even at D=512 due to the low-rank bottleneck; 1-head (full-rank) attention only succeeds at D=512. On OpenWebText language modeling (T=1024, r=64, 8-level MLR with rank allocation 32|8|6|4|4|4|2), MLR attention achieves lower validation loss than standard attention across all model widths D∈{256,384,512,768}, exhibiting a better scaling law. On ETTh1 time series forecasting, MLR attention improves oil temperature prediction by ~1% relative MAE over standard attention, with the gap widening as the prediction horizon grows from 96 to 336 hours. Additionally, 8-level MLR with uniform rank allocation (r_l=r/8) reduces the KV cache size by 4× compared to standard attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard multi-head attention (Vaswani et al., 2017), the paper's structured variants win on all three tasks: Bilinear BTT/MLR resolve the low-rank bottleneck on in-context regression (achieving the task at smaller model widths), 8-level MLR attention achieves consistently lower validation loss on OpenWebText across all model widths, and MLR attention improves time series MAE at longer horizons. Against sliding window attention (Child et al., 2019), MLR attention outperforms SWA on OpenWebText language modeling when controlling for compute, across all model widths tested. Against the Global+SWA hybrid (inspired by Longformer, Beltagy et al., 2020), where the first and fourth layers use standard attention and the rest use SWA with window size 128, MLR attention again achieves the lowest validation loss. The paper notes that sparse attention variants like SWA are 'brittle and underperform standard attention' in prior work, and MLR's structured (but not sparse) approach avoids this trade-off by allocating more FLOPs to local interactions while retaining a global receptive field at every level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on structured matrices (Monarch, Butterfly, BTT) from replacing individual linear layers to replacing the entire bilinear attention scoring function W_Q W_K^T with a single structured matrix, thereby unifying the design space for attention's inductive biases. It departs from prior locality-based attention variants (sliding window attention, Longformer) that impose hard sparsity patterns on the score matrix, instead using the hierarchical block structure of MLR matrices to encode a soft, distance-dependent compute allocation. The introduction of the MLBTC family provides a novel theoretical unification showing that BTT, MLR, Monarch, Butterfly, Kronecker, and low-rank matrices are all special cases of a single structured matrix class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled framework for customizing attention's inductive biases to match task structure, with practical benefits including KV cache reduction (up to 4×) and FLOP savings during autoregressive generation. However, the empirical gains on language modeling are modest ('mild improvements,' 'slightly outperforms') and evaluated at relatively small scale (6-layer, D≤768, character-level tokenization), leaving open whether the advantages persist at the scale of modern LLMs. The MLBTC unification is a useful theoretical tool, but the paper does not yet demonstrate that its greater flexibility over BTT or MLR alone yields practical benefits. Future work on applying these techniques to the value/output projection (W_V W_O^T), to different heads with different structures, and to fine-tuning or compression settings could broaden the impact.
