## Problem

Existing object-centric video prediction methods are limited to deterministic environments or require precise action annotations, preventing them from leveraging the large volume of available unlabeled video data. This gap is especially critical for robotic world models that must forecast multiple possible futures in stochastic, multi-object scenes without access to ground-truth actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PlaySlot takes a single reference frame (64×64 RGB) and a sequence of latent actions—either inferred from a reference video via the InvDyn module, provided by a user, or generated by a learned policy—and autoregressively produces future object slots, per-object images and masks, and composited video frames (15 frames from 6 seed frames in experiments). For behavior learning, the system additionally outputs executable robot actions via a lightweight action decoder trained on a small set of labeled data, while the core prediction pipeline remains fully unsupervised.

## Method

PlaySlot combines three key components: (1) SAVi with Slot Attention and GRU-based slot refinement to decompose each frame into N_S 128-dimensional object slots; (2) an InvDyn module (a 4-layer transformer encoder for single-agent or a shared MLP for multi-agent) that infers latent actions from consecutive slot pairs using a probabilistic Gaussian formulation; and (3) a cOCVP transformer predictor that autoregressively forecasts future slots conditioned on past slots and latent actions. The central design choice is a hybrid latent action parameterization—each action is the sum of a discrete action prototype (learned via vector quantization) and a continuous variability embedding—balancing interpretability and controllability with the flexibility to model stochastic dynamics. Training proceeds in three stages: SAVi pretraining via reconstruction, joint InvDyn+cOCVP training with image/slot/VQ losses (no teacher forcing), and finally behavior cloning of a latent policy plus action decoder regression on a small labeled set.

## Results

On the four evaluation environments, PlaySlot achieves the best LPIPS on BlockPush (0.066 vs. 0.090 for CADDY and 0.096 for SVG) and a dominant PSNR of 54.09 on GridShapes₂Objs (vs. 39.16 for CADDY and 38.10 for SVG), demonstrating superior performance in multi-object and interaction-heavy scenes. On ButtonPress, SVG leads in PSNR (32.23 vs. PlaySlot's 26.03), but PlaySlot still outperforms CADDY (24.58) and both object-centric baselines. On the real-world Sketchy dataset, PlaySlot achieves the best PSNR (24.43) and SSIM (0.815), marginally ahead of SVG (24.24) and CADDY (23.65). For behavior learning, PlaySlot matches the ResNet Oracle with as few as 500 expert demonstrations on ButtonPress and outperforms LAPO by a large margin on BlockPush across all data regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlaySlot clearly outperforms both object-centric baselines (SlotFormer and OCVP) across all four datasets, with PSNR gaps of 4–35 dB, confirming that unconditional slot-based predictors fail to model stochastic agent dynamics. Against the stochastic baseline SVG, PlaySlot wins on BlockPush (LPIPS 0.066 vs. 0.096) and GridShapes (PSNR 54.09 vs. 38.10) but loses on ButtonPress (PSNR 26.03 vs. 32.23), where the simpler single-agent task favors SVG's holistic latent distribution. Against CADDY, PlaySlot wins on BlockPush (LPIPS 0.066 vs. 0.090), GridShapes (PSNR 54.09 vs. 39.16), and Sketchy (PSNR 24.43 vs. 23.65), while CADDY retains an edge on ButtonPress. In the behavior-learning experiments, PlaySlot surpasses LAPO (Schmidt & Jiang, 2024) by a large margin on BlockPush and slightly on ButtonPress, and approaches the performance of oracle models (Slot Oracle, ResNet Oracle) with only 200–500 noisy expert demonstrations. Against model-based RL baselines DreamerV3 and SOLD, PlaySlot closes the gap as demonstrations increase, with object-centric methods (SOLD, Slot Oracle, PlaySlot) consistently outperforming their holistic counterparts (DreamerV3, ResNet Oracle, LAPO).

## Relation to Prior Work

PlaySlot sits at the intersection of two established research threads: object-centric video prediction (SlotFormer, OCVP) and latent-action-based controllable video generation (CADDY, Genie). It departs from CADDY/Genie by replacing holistic CNN features with structured slot-based object representations, and from SlotFormer/OCVP by adding an inverse dynamics module that infers latent actions from unlabeled video, enabling action-conditioned prediction. The hybrid discrete-continuous action parameterization draws on CADDY's design but replaces its multi-objective regularization with vector quantization, simplifying the training pipeline. The behavior learning component extends the inverse-dynamics pretraining line (LAPO, DynaMo) by grounding it in object-centric slots rather than monolithic feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PlaySlot demonstrates that object-centric structure combined with inferred latent actions yields a controllable world model that outperforms both holistic and unconditional object-centric baselines, particularly in multi-object and interaction-heavy scenes. The ability to learn robot behaviors from unlabeled expert demonstrations—matching oracle performance with as few as 200 demos on ButtonPress—suggests a practical path toward sample-efficient robotics without action annotations. However, the authors acknowledge that SAVi's decomposition fails on complex real-world scenes and that the single entangled latent action limits fine-grained control, leaving open the question of whether the framework scales to realistic multi-task manipulation. Future work on disentangled action vectors and stronger decomposition models (DINOSAUR, SOLV) is needed to bridge this gap.
