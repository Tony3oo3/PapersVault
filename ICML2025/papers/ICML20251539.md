## Problem

The Clique-Picking (CP) algorithm for counting Markov equivalent DAGs requires independently generating undirected connected components (UCCGs) for each of the m maximal cliques as root, incurring O(m(|V|+|E|)) cost. Despite substantial structural overlap between UCCG sets computed under different root cliques, the original algorithm recomputes them from scratch, leaving significant redundancy unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251539/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a UCCG (connected chordal graph) and a rooted clique tree T^{K_1} generated by the MCS algorithm as input, and outputs the complete collection of UCCG sets C_G(K_1), ..., C_G(K_m) for every maximal clique as root. These sets are then consumed by the recursive CP-Count procedure to compute the final MEC size. The processing follows the MCS clique ordering, and each iteration i only requires the parent clique's previously computed super clique set L^{(t)} and UCCG set C_G(K_t), avoiding any graph-level traversal.

## Method

The key insight is introducing 'super cliques'—groupings of a clique header with all its clique tails within a rooted clique tree—whose induced subgraphs on the corresponding 'super residuals' directly yield the UCCGs (Theorem 5.3). When transitioning from root K_t to its child K_i, only one edge direction in the clique tree changes (K_t→K_i becomes K_i→K_t), and the super clique structure transforms via three local operations: (1) the super clique of K_i splits into one per child, (2) super cliques of K_t's other children that become tails of K_t merge into a new super clique rooted at K_t, and (3) all remaining super cliques are unchanged. The SC-Trans-Op algorithm (Algorithm 4) implements this transfer in O(m) per iteration, reducing the total cost of generating all UCCG sets from O(m(|V|+|E|)) to O(m²), which is a substantial gain when m ≪ |V|+|E|.

## Results

The improved ICP algorithm consistently outperforms the original CP algorithm across all tested configurations. For the full algorithm, ICP achieves a 1.6–1.9× speedup (e.g., 707.5s vs. 1169.8s at |V|=4096, r=0.34; 387.5s vs. 660.4s at |V|=4096, r=0.08). When isolating Step 2 (UCCG generation), the advantage is dramatically larger: the ratio T_CP,2/T_ICP,2 ranges from 5.3× (|V|=1024, r=0.04) to 264.5× (|V|=2048, r=0.46), confirming the theoretical reduction from O(m(|V|+|E|)) to O(m²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original Clique-Picking (CP) algorithm of Wienobst et al. (2023), which is the state-of-the-art polynomial-time method for counting Markov equivalent DAGs. ICP wins on every tested configuration (all combinations of |V| ∈ {512, 1024, 2048, 4096} and r ∈ {0.04, 0.06, 0.12, 0.24, 0.34, 0.46, 0.08}), with no losses or ties. The overall algorithm speedup is moderate (1.6–1.9×) because Step 2 is only one component of the full recursive CP-Count procedure. However, when measuring Step 2 in isolation, ICP achieves up to a 264.5× speedup over CP at |V|=2048, r=0.46. The advantage grows with graph density r because denser graphs yield a smaller ratio θ = m/(|V|+|E|), making the O(m²) super-clique transfer increasingly favorable relative to the O(m(|V|+|E|)) cost of the original method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Clique-Picking (CP) algorithm of Wienobst et al. (2023), which was the first polynomial-time method for counting Markov equivalent DAGs via iterative root-clique selection and UCCG decomposition. The key departure is the introduction of super cliques and super residuals as higher-level structures within rooted clique trees, enabling the UCCG generation step (Step 2 of CP) to be computed in O(m²) rather than O(m(|V|+|E|)) by exploiting structural overlap across different root choices. The theoretical foundation draws on Leimer's (1993) RIP sequence permutation property and the MCS algorithm for chordal graph decomposition, while the broader counting literature (He et al. 2015, Ghassami et al. 2019, Ganian et al. 2022) provides context for why efficient MEC size computation remains an active problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Efficient MEC counting underpins practical causal inference tasks including intervention experiment design and average causal effect estimation, where knowing the size of the equivalence class is essential. The improvement is most impactful when the number of maximal cliques m is substantially smaller than |V|+|E|, a regime common in sparse-to-moderate chordal graphs arising from real-world causal discovery. However, the paper only optimizes one step of the recursive CP algorithm; the overall counting cost still depends on recursive subproblem solving, and the practical speedup in end-to-end runtime (roughly 2× in Table 2) is more modest than the Step-2-only ratio (up to 264× in Table 3). Open questions include whether the super clique transfer idea can be extended to sampling from MECs or to interventional essential graphs, and whether further structural exploitation can reduce the O(m²) bound.
