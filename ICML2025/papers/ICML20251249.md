## Problem

Reconstructing cell lineage trees from phenotypic data (e.g., transcriptomes) remains largely unexplored because no well-established stochastic model governs gene expression evolution along lineages, and comprehensive lineage annotations are scarce outside a few model organisms. Existing approaches rely on genetically engineered barcodes (e.g., CRISPR recorders) that are infeasible or ethically restricted in many systems, leaving open whether high-content molecular phenotypes alone contain sufficient information for tree reconstruction and what algorithm can recover it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of single-cell RNA-seq gene expression profiles (p-dimensional count vectors per cell, ~13,000 genes after filtering) as input, optionally augmented with partial lineage annotations such as high-level clade assignments or labeled mother-daughter pairs for a subset of leaves. It produces a d-dimensional embedding per cell whose pairwise Euclidean distances approximate an additive tree metric, from which a full unrooted lineage tree topology with edge weights is reconstructed via the Neighbor-Joining algorithm. The input cells are treated as an unordered set (no positional encoding), and the output tree is evaluated against ground truth using normalized Robinson-Foulds distance and quartet distance.

## Method

CellTreeQM uses a Transformer encoder (without positional encodings, since cells are permutation-invariant) to map gene expression vectors into a latent space, and trains this embedding via a quartet-based additivity loss derived from Buneman's four-point condition: for each quartet of leaves, the loss enforces that the two largest pairwise-distance sums are equal (L_close) and that the smallest sum is separated by a margin from their average (L_push). A Frobenius-norm deviation loss anchors the learned distances to the original feature-space distances, and a Gumbel-Softmax gating module adaptively down-weights non-heritable or noisy gene features via element-wise multiplication. The overall objective jointly optimizes the embedding function, the gating mask, and the additivity constraints, with the final tree topology extracted by Neighbor-Joining on the learned distance matrix. The framework supports three supervision levels—fully supervised (all quartet topologies known), weakly supervised (only known quartets from clade partitions or partial leaf labels contribute to the loss), and unsupervised (quartet ordering estimated data-driven from the latent space).

## Results

On the C. elegans Small dataset (102 leaves), direct reconstruction from raw transcriptomic data yields a high RF distance of 0.923, indicating almost no recoverable lineage signal. CellTreeQM reduces this to a Δ%RF improvement of 0.690 (0.757 with feature gating) and Δ%QD of 0.867, with near-zero training RF (0.000), demonstrating that the learned embedding faithfully preserves the global tree topology. In the weakly supervised high-level partition setting (Table 2), CellTreeQM achieves Δ%RF of 0.349 at partition level 3 and 0.274 at level 2, while also generalizing to unknown quartets (Δ%U-QD of 0.182 and 0.558 respectively), where Quadruplet actually underperforms raw data (negative Δ%U-QD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CellTreeQM consistently outperforms both the Triplet loss (Schroff et al., 2015) and Quadruplet loss (Chen et al., 2017) baselines across all evaluation settings. In the supervised setting on C. elegans Small, CellTreeQM achieves Δ%RF of 0.690 versus 0.179 for Triplet and 0.454 for Quadruplet, and Δ%QD of 0.867 versus 0.637 and 0.784 respectively. The gap is most pronounced in Train RF: CellTreeQM reaches 0.000 (perfect global topology preservation) while Triplet sits at 0.519, indicating Triplet's latent space fails to align with the global tree structure despite recovering moderate quartet-level topology. In the weakly supervised high-level partition setting, CellTreeQM generalizes to unknown quartets (Δ%U-QD = 0.182 at level 3) while Quadruplet actually degrades below raw data (Δ%U-QD = −0.065). In the partial-leaf labeled setting at 80% known fraction, CellTreeQM achieves Δ%RF of 0.448 versus 0.175 (Triplet) and 0.403 (Quadruplet), with the gap narrowing but persisting at 50% and 30% labeling fractions. Permutation experiments further confirm that CellTreeQM's advantage stems from its explicit four-point-condition loss rather than overfitting: it still embeds data with moderate fidelity under a random tree, while Triplet and Quadruplet struggle to align any arbitrary topology with the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical four-point condition from phylogenetics (De Soete, 1983) as a differentiable loss function for deep metric learning, bridging a gap between distance-based tree reconstruction and modern representation learning. It departs from standard contrastive objectives (triplet, quadruplet) by explicitly enforcing additive tree-metric geometry in the latent space rather than merely pulling similar pairs together. The work is closely related to Schlüter & Uhler (2025), which tests whether gene expression encodes lineage signal via permutation, but CellTreeQM goes further by directly reconstructing the tree topology and introducing a benchmark. The transformer backbone without positional encoding and the Gumbel-Softmax feature gating are design choices tailored to the unordered, high-dimensional nature of single-cell data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that transcriptomic profiles alone carry recoverable lineage signal, this paper opens a path toward lineage inference in organisms where CRISPR-based genetic barcoding is infeasible or ethically restricted. The introduced benchmark (synthetic Brownian-motion simulations plus three scales of C. elegans data) provides a much-needed evaluation framework for a task that previously lacked standardized tests. Key limitations include the narrow evaluation scope (C. elegans with invariant embryonic lineages), the reliance on Neighbor-Joining which can fail under complex noise, and the weak unsupervised results, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to organisms with convergent evolution or larger, noisier developmental atlases.
