## Problem

Adversarially trained networks achieve high clean test accuracy yet suffer a large robust generalization gap, a phenomenon the authors term Clean Generalization and Robust Overfitting (CGRO). Prior theoretical work attributed robust overfitting to sample complexity or robustness-accuracy tradeoffs, but did not identify the specific mechanism during the training process that simultaneously preserves clean generalization while causing robust overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the input is a D-dimensional binary classification instance; in the structured-data setting, each input decomposes into P patches of dimension d, containing exactly one meaningful signal patch (aligned with a unit vector w*) and noisy patches drawn from a low-variance Gaussian. The two-layer convolutional network maps these patch-structured inputs to a scalar score for binary classification, trained under an ℓ_p adversarial perturbation radius δ. Empirically, standard image datasets are used: MNIST (28×28 grayscale, ℓ∞ radius 0.3) and CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB, ℓ∞ radius 8/255), with PGD attacks for evaluation.

## Method

The paper provides two complementary theoretical analyses of CGRO. From representation complexity, it proves that a CGRO classifier requires only poly(D) + Õ(ND) ReLU parameters by leveraging robust memorization of training data, whereas a truly robust classifier demands exponential Ω(exp(D)) capacity in the worst case. From training dynamics, it analyzes a two-layer CNN of Õ(ND) width trained by adversarial training on structured patch data and proves a three-stage phase transition: (i) quadratic growth of the signal component yielding partial true feature learning, (ii) signal growth stagnation as noise components dominate the gradient, and (iii) quadratic growth of noise components yielding exact spurious feature memorization. The resulting model partially captures the true signal (enabling clean generalization) while memorizing data-specific random noise (causing robust overfitting on unseen perturbed data). The analysis employs ReLU^q activations, the tensor power method for convergence tracking, and a Lojasiewicz inequality to handle the non-convex optimization landscape.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ℓ∞ perturbation radius 8/255, the largest WideResNet-34 (width factor 10) achieves 86.05% clean test accuracy but only 50.08% robust test accuracy, while robust training accuracy reaches 99.57%—a clear demonstration of the CGRO phenomenon. On MNIST, LeNet-5 at size factor 16 achieves 94.85% clean test accuracy and 83.43% robust test accuracy with 99.5% robust training accuracy. On synthetic structured data (Definition 5.1), the two-layer CNN converges to 100% clean training accuracy, 98.5% clean test accuracy, 100% robust training accuracy, but only 17.5% robust test accuracy, confirming the theoretical prediction of partial true feature learning combined with exact spurious feature memorization (Theorem 5.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not compare against external adversarial training baselines (e.g., TRADES, Free, or standard PGD-AT). Instead, the empirical section verifies the paper's own theoretical predictions by varying model capacity. The key finding is a phase transition: on MNIST, LeNet-5 at size factors 1, 2, and 8 all collapse to a trivial solution (11.35% accuracy, chance level), while at factors 12 and 16 the model achieves high clean accuracy (~95%) with a large robust generalization gap (~12–17 points between robust train and robust test). On CIFAR-10, WideResNet-34 shows a smoother transition: clean test accuracy increases modestly from 82.56% to 86.05% as width grows from 1 to 10, while robust test accuracy rises from 43.39% to 50.08% and robust training accuracy jumps from 64.19% to 99.57%, confirming that larger models achieve robust memorization (low robust training error) without robust generalization. The synthetic data experiment (Table 2) provides the cleanest validation of Theorem 5.9, showing the predicted three-stage phase transition in feature learning dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning theory framework of Allen-Zhu & Li (2020a,b) to the adversarial training setting, adapting the Tensor Power Method and signal/noise component decomposition to analyze how a two-layer CNN learns under adversarial perturbations. It generalizes the exponential lower bound on robust classifier complexity from Li et al. (2022) (which assumed linear separability) to a more general polynomial-size clean classifier assumption. The CGRO concept departs from prior robust overfitting work (Rice et al., 2020; Schmidt et al., 2018) by explicitly formalizing the coexistence of clean generalization and robust overfitting, and by providing a mechanistic training-dynamics explanation rather than purely statistical bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical account of why adversarial training converges to the CGRO regime, with a clean representation-complexity separation (poly(D) + Õ(ND) for CGRO vs. Ω(exp(D)) for robust generalization) and a three-stage phase-transition mechanism (partial true feature learning → signal growth stalls → noise memorization). Key limitations include the restrictive structured-data assumption (single meaningful patch, perturbation aligned with the signal direction) and the two-layer CNN architecture, leaving open whether the three-stage dynamics persist in deeper networks, with multiple meaningful patches, or under unstructured perturbations. The global-flatness generalization bound (Appendix B) offers a practical diagnostic but its tightness in realistic settings remains unverified.
