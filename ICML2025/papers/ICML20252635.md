## Problem

Existing conditional flow matching (CFM) algorithms condition the probability path on one or two endpoints, which limits coverage of the (t,x) space, causes extrapolation errors in the learned vector field, and cannot naturally accommodate multiple correlated observations such as time series or grouped data. This paper asks whether conditioning at a finer-grained 'stream' level—on the full latent stochastic path between endpoints—can reduce estimation variance and flexibly integrate correlated data while preserving the simulation-free training property of CFM.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise as the source distribution and optionally covariates (e.g., starting images or starting LFP values) to separate streams for different subjects. Training data can be grayscale images (MNIST 28×28, HWD+ handwritten digits), RGB images (CIFAR-10 32×32), or multivariate time series (LFP with 5 time points × 7 brain regions per trial). A key structural flexibility is that correlated observations can be placed at arbitrary intermediate time points (e.g., t=0.5) along the stream, not just at the two endpoints, enabling a single unified model to generate smooth transformations across all observed time points.

## Method

The core contribution is a stream-level CFM algorithm where the latent stochastic path (stream) connecting source and target endpoints is modeled as a Gaussian Process. Because the derivative of a GP is also a GP, the joint distribution of (s_t, ṡ_t) given observed values is available in closed form, preserving the simulation-free training of CFM: the per-stream vector field is simply the stream velocity ṡ_t, and the marginal vector field is its conditional expectation given s_t = x. The GP covariance kernel (squared exponential with optional time-varying variance terms) controls the spread of streams, acting as a regularizer that expands coverage of the (t,x) space and reduces extrapolation error. Multiple correlated observations are incorporated by conditioning the GP on their values at intermediate time points, which again yields an analytic conditional GP. The training loss is the standard squared-error regression of a neural network (MLP or U-Net) against the sampled stream velocity, and the approach composes with endpoint coupling strategies such as optimal transport (OT-CFM).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (unconditional generation from standard Gaussian), GP-I-CFM achieves the best FID of 3.62 (SE 0.008), outperforming I-CFM (3.75), OT-CFM (3.74), and GP-OT-CFM (3.75). On MNIST, GP-OT-CFM achieves the lowest KID (0.0031, SE 0.0001) and FID (42.99, SE 0.14) with notably reduced standard errors compared to I-CFM (KID 0.0040, FID 44.50). In the 2-Gaussian simulation, GP-OT-CFM reduces the 2-Wasserstein distance to 1.35 (SE 0.05) versus 1.54 (SE 0.08) for I-CFM, demonstrating variance reduction in vector field estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against I-CFM (Lipman et al., 2023), the GP-stream variants consistently improve sample quality: GP-I-CFM reduces CIFAR-10 FID from 3.75 to 3.62 and MNIST FID from 44.50 to 43.55, while also halving the standard error on KID (0.0001 vs. 0.0002). Against OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2024), the GP-stream methods again win on all metrics in the low-dimensional 2-Gaussian simulation (W2: 1.35 vs. 1.43 for GP-OT-CFM vs. OT-CFM) and on MNIST (KID: 0.0031 vs. 0.0036; FID: 42.99 vs. 44.21). However, on high-dimensional CIFAR-10, the OT coupling strategy provides little benefit over independent coupling (OT-CFM FID 3.74 ≈ I-CFM 3.75), and combining both strategies (GP-OT-CFM, FID 3.75) does not improve over GP-I-CFM alone, suggesting minibatch OT approximation degrades in high dimensions. The GP-stream approach also reduces sample generation time slightly (1.27s vs. 1.30s for GP-I-CFM vs. I-CFM on CIFAR-10), and the combined GP-OT-CFM is fastest at 1.20s. Overall, the GP-stream model provides a consistent, if modest, improvement in both sample quality and estimation stability across all tested benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and Tong et al. (2024) by introducing a Bayesian latent variable layer: instead of conditioning the probability path on one or two endpoints, it conditions on an entire latent stochastic 'stream' connecting them, modeled as a Gaussian Process. The GP choice is motivated by the stochastic interpolant framework of Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden (2023), which showed that stochasticity in the path smooths the vector field and suppresses spurious intermediate modes; the GP provides a tractable, simulation-free way to realize this while preserving the closed-form joint distribution of position and velocity. The approach also subsumes prior linear interpolant paths (I-CFM, OT-CFM) as special cases of the GP construction, positioning GP-CFM as a strict generalization rather than an alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GP-stream formulation offers two practical advantages over existing CFM methods: (1) it reduces variance in the estimated marginal vector field by expanding the coverage region of the (t,x) space, thereby mitigating extrapolation errors that cause outlier samples, and (2) it naturally accommodates multiple correlated observations (e.g., time series, grouped data) by placing them along the stream between endpoints, enabling information sharing across time points. A key limitation is the Monte Carlo error introduced by sampling GP trajectories, which the authors note could be mitigated by importance sampling or antithetic sampling. Open questions include whether the GP-stream approach scales efficiently to very high-dimensional settings (the CIFAR-10 results show only modest FID gains over I-CFM), and whether the variance-reduction benefit persists as dataset size grows large enough that extrapolation becomes less of an issue.
