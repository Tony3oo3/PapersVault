## Problem

Topological Deep Learning (TDL) lacks the principled, standardized frameworks that underpin GNN development, having progressed by generalizing GNN architectures one-by-one to specific topological domains. This fragmentation slows the creation of new TDL models, limits cost-benefit analysis, and hinders adoption beyond expert communities. The paper asks: can any existing neural network be systematically transformed into its topological counterpart with minimal adaptation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251916/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a combinatorial complex (cells of ranks 0 through dim(C) with feature vectors) and a user-specified collection of neighborhood functions, which may be per-rank (e.g., only 1-cells exchange messages with their bounding 0-cells). The complex is expanded on-the-fly into an ensemble of strictly augmented Hasse graphs—one directed graph per neighborhood—each carrying the cell features as node features. Output is a set of updated cell embeddings (one per cell in the complex) that feed into a readout for graph-level classification/regression or node-level classification.

## Method

GCCNs expand a combinatorial complex into an ensemble of strictly augmented Hasse graphs (one per neighborhood function, preserving rank information unlike prior single-graph expansions), then apply a base neural network ω_N (any GNN, Transformer, or MLP imported from PyTorch Geometric/DGL) independently to each graph. Outputs are synchronized via a rank-wise inter-neighborhood aggregation, and a row-wise update function φ produces the next layer's embeddings. Two key innovations enable generality: (1) per-rank neighborhoods, which restrict message passing to specific cell ranks and reduce computational cost, and (2) the neighborhood message function ω_N is unconstrained—need not be message-passing—so spectral convolutions, attention, or any graph operator can be plugged in. The authors prove GCCNs formally subsume CCNNs (Prop. 4.1), are cell-permutation equivariant (Prop. 4.2), and are strictly more expressive than CCNNs by relating them to k-WL on augmented Hasse graphs (Prop. 4.3). TopoTune, a lightweight PyTorch module integrated into TopoBench, instantiates GCCNs from a configuration file specifying neighborhoods and base models.

## Results

Across 16 domain/dataset combinations, GCCNs exceed the best CCNN by more than 1σ in 11 cases. In the cellular domain, the best GCCN (ω_N=GIN) achieves 86.38% on MUTAG vs. 80.43% for the best CCNN, and 0.19 MAE on ZINC vs. 0.34 for CCNNs. In the simplicial domain, GCCNs dramatically outperform CCNNs on node-level tasks (e.g., 88.57% on Cora vs. 82.27%, 75.92% on CiteSeer vs. 71.24%). GCCNs are also more parameter-efficient, with some configurations using up to half the parameters of equivalent CCNNs while matching or exceeding performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the best CCNNs benchmarked on TopoBench (Telyatnikov et al., 2024), GCCNs consistently outperform in both cellular and simplicial domains: e.g., 86.38% vs. 80.43% on MUTAG (cellular), 85.96% vs. 76.17% on MUTAG (simplicial), and 88.57% vs. 82.27% on Cora (simplicial). GCCNs also match or exceed the best GNNs on most tasks, though in 2 of 16 combinations the best GNN still prevails. Against SCCN (Yang et al., 2022), GCCN counterparts achieve substantially better results on graph-level tasks (e.g., 82.13% vs. 70.64% on MUTAG) and node-level tasks (e.g., 88.06% vs. 82.19% on Cora). Against CWN (Bodnar et al., 2021a), GCCN counterparts are competitive or superior (e.g., 84.26% vs. 80.43% on MUTAG, 86.44% vs. 86.32% on Cora), while being up to 28% smaller in parameter count. The ensemble-of-strictly-augmented-Hasse-graphs formulation consistently outperforms the single-augmented-Hasse-graph variant, confirming the value of preserving neighborhood structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CCNN framework of Hajij et al. (2023), which defined combinatorial complexes and their message-passing neural networks, and on the augmented Hasse graph expansion that maps complexes to graphs. It departs from prior graph-based TDL approaches (Jogl et al., 2022a,b; Veličković, 2022) that collapse all neighborhoods into a single graph, losing rank and neighborhood structure, by introducing strictly augmented Hasse graphs (one per neighborhood) and per-rank neighborhoods. The resulting GCCN framework systematically subsumes CCNNs while allowing arbitrary GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE, Transformer) as neighborhood message functions, and is implemented in TopoTune, integrated with the TopoBench benchmarking infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical standardization gap in TDL by providing both a unified theoretical framework (GCCNs with proven strict expressivity over CCNNs) and accessible software (TopoTune), potentially accelerating adoption beyond the expert community. Key limitations include slower training on larger node-level datasets due to on-the-fly graph expansion, and reduced parameter-efficiency gains on high-dimensional node-level tasks. Open questions include whether the framework scales to sparse or multimodal topological data, how to best select neighborhood structures per dataset, and whether preprocessing the graph expansion can close the training-time gap with native CCNNs.
