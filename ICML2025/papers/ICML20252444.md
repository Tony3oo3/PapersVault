## Problem

Existing equivariant neural functional networks face a trade-off: graph-based message-passing approaches (e.g., Kalogeropoulos et al., 2024) achieve both permutation and scaling equivariance but incur very high memory and runtime costs, while parameter-sharing approaches (Tran et al., 2024a) are efficient but use only equivariant linear layers that update each hidden layer's weights solely from itself, limiting expressivity. The paper addresses the open challenge of designing a parameter-sharing-based equivariant NFN that is both computationally efficient and sufficiently expressive to capture cross-layer weight interactions.

## Input / Output

The method takes the full weight space of a fixed MLP or CNN architecture—specifically, all weight matrices [W]^(i) ∈ R^{d×n_i×n_{i-1}} and bias vectors [b]^(i) ∈ R^{d×n_i×1} for i=1,…,L—as a structured input object, and must produce outputs that are equivariant (or invariant) under the monomial matrix group G = {I} × M_{n_{L-1}}^{>0} × … × M_{n_1}^{>0} × {I}. In practice, the NFN is trained in supervised mode to produce class labels (INR classification), a scalar generalization score (CNN accuracy prediction via Kendall's τ), or a weight perturbation vector (style editing), with the group action applied to the input weights during training as data augmentation.

## Method

MAGEP-NFN constructs equivariant and invariant polynomial layers from a specialized class of 'stable polynomial terms'—matrix products like [W]^(s,t) = [W]^(s)·…·[W]^(t+1), [Wb]^(s,t)(t), [bW]^(s)(L,t), and [WW]^(s,0)(L,t)—that transform predictably under the monomial matrix group action (each term picks up at most a left and right group factor). By restricting layers to linear combinations of these stable terms and enforcing parameter-sharing constraints derived from the invariance/equivariance condition I(gU)=I(U), the authors obtain polynomial layers of degree at most L+1 that capture inter-layer weight relationships while maintaining the low memory and runtime of the parameter-sharing mechanism. A key theoretical contribution is a complete characterization of the linear dependencies among stable polynomial terms (Theorem 3.4), which determines exactly which coefficient constraints are needed for invariance. The resulting architecture generalizes the equivariant linear layers of Tran et al. (2024a) as its linear component, with the additional inter-layer polynomial terms providing the expressivity gain demonstrated in ablation studies.

## Results

On the INR classification task, MAGEP-NFN achieves 77.55% test accuracy on MNIST, 37.18% on CIFAR-10, and 62.83% on FashionMNIST, outperforming all baselines by 7.73%, 2.95%, and 1.68% respectively over the second-best model. On CNN generalization prediction (Small CNN Zoo, ReLU subset), MAGEP-NFN reaches a Kendall's τ of 0.933, surpassing Monomial-NFN (0.922) and HNP (0.926), while maintaining robustness under scale augmentations up to U[1,10⁴]. On weight-space style editing, MAGEP-NFN matches NP and Monomial-NFN (MSE 0.020 on CIFAR-10 contrast, 0.068 on MNIST dilation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NP and HNP (Zhou et al., 2024b), MAGEP-NFN achieves the largest margin on MNIST INR classification (77.55% vs. 69.82% and 66.02%), with more modest but consistent gains on CIFAR-10 (+3.44% over NP) and FashionMNIST (+4.62% over NP). Against Monomial-NFN (Tran et al., 2024a), the closest baseline sharing the same parameter-sharing mechanism, MAGEP-NFN wins on all three INR datasets (e.g., 77.55% vs. 68.43% on MNIST, a 9.12% gap) and on CNN generalization prediction (Kendall's τ 0.933 vs. 0.922), demonstrating that the polynomial inter-layer terms add meaningful expressivity over the linear-only layers. Against STATNet (Unterthiner et al., 2020), MAGEP-NFN achieves a Kendall's τ of 0.933 vs. 0.915 on the ReLU subset and 0.940 vs. 0.913 on the Tanh subset. On weight-space style editing, MAGEP-NFN ties NP and Monomial-NFN (MSE 0.020 on CIFAR-10 contrast; 0.068 on MNIST dilation), indicating that the polynomial terms do not degrade equivariant editing performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Tran et al. (2024a), which introduced Monomial-NFN using equivariant linear layers with a parameter-sharing mechanism, by replacing those linear layers with polynomial layers built from 'stable polynomial terms' that capture inter-layer weight relationships. It positions itself against two prior paradigms: graph-based equivariant NFNs (Kalogeropoulos et al., 2024) that achieve full permutation-and-scaling equivariance but at prohibitive memory and runtime cost, and permutation-only equivariant NFNs (Zhou et al., 2024b; Navon et al., 2023) that ignore scaling symmetries. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a restricted polynomial class whose terms are provably stable under the monomial matrix group action, enabling efficient parameter-sharing while recovering expressivity lost in the purely linear formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete trade-off in equivariant NFN design: the parameter-sharing mechanism of Tran et al. (2024a) was efficient but under-expressive because each hidden layer's weights were updated only by themselves, whereas graph-based approaches were expressive but computationally expensive. The stable polynomial framework provides a clean theoretical characterization (linear independence, equivariance/invariance conditions) that makes the construction principled rather than ad hoc. However, the practical gains are modest (e.g., +0.007 Kendall's τ on CNN generalization, +7.7% on MNIST INR classification), and the authors note the construction is tied to a specific architecture, limiting immediate broad applicability. Open questions include whether the polynomial degree can be increased beyond L+1 for further expressivity, and whether the framework extends cleanly to architectures with normalization layers or attention mechanisms.
