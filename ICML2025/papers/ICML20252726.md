## Problem

Existing traffic anomaly detection methods for autonomous driving prioritize detection accuracy while neglecting response time, yet in safety-critical driving scenarios even a few hundred milliseconds of delay can determine the difference between safe braking and a collision. This paper formalizes real-time anomaly detection by explicitly incorporating total response time (inference latency plus detection delay) into the evaluation framework, and demonstrates that current state-of-the-art methods incur unacceptable latencies for this use case.

## Input / Output

The model takes two parallel inputs: RGB frames resized to 224×224 pixels and asynchronous event streams (sparse pixel-level brightness-change events with coordinates, timestamps, and polarity). Since no existing traffic anomaly dataset contains real event-camera data, the V2E conversion technique synthesizes event streams from video frames. The output is object-level: per-object riskiness scores (via softmax), bounding boxes from YOLOX, and a binary anomaly indicator, all produced at approximately 579 FPS. The event stream is consumed asynchronously rather than in fixed temporal windows, enabling inter-frame anomaly detection between consecutive video frames.

## Method

The proposed Multimodal Asynchronous Hybrid Network uses a dual-branch backbone: a ResNet50 CNN extracts spatial appearance features from RGB images, while a Deep Asynchronous Graph Neural Network (DAGr) with spline convolution processes the sparse event stream, where events form graph nodes connected by spatial-temporal proximity edges. CNN features are fused unidirectionally into GNN node features (not reciprocally), enhancing event representation in sparse-event scenarios. At the object level, bounding box priors from YOLOX detection gate which events belong to which object; fused per-object features are processed by parallel GRUs (one for bounding box trajectories, one for fused features) to capture temporal accumulation of anomaly evidence. An attention mechanism then re-weights object-level hidden states to emphasize anomalous agents before a final softmax produces the risk score. The single-stage design (shared feature extraction for detection and anomaly scoring) outperforms a two-stage variant, and the asynchronous event processing is the key mechanism enabling detection of anomalies that occur between consecutive video frames, achieving millisecond-level response times.

## Results

On the ROL dataset, the proposed method achieves 87.9% AUC, 57.0% AP, 73.6% AUC-Frame, 2.80 s mTTA, and 1.17 s mResponse at 579 FPS. On DoTA, it reaches 89.6% AUC, 62.3% AP, 82.3% AUC-Frame, 2.78 s mTTA, and 1.21 s mResponse. The most striking advantage is inference speed: 579 FPS versus 3 FPS for TTHF and 77 FPS for STFE, yielding a mean response time roughly 2× lower than the next-best method (1.17 s vs. 1.88 s for MAMTCF on ROL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on AUC (both ROL and DoTA), mTTA, FPS, and mResponse. Against the strongest accuracy competitor TTHF (Liang et al., 2024), OURS gains +0.8 pp AUC on ROL and +0.5 pp on DoTA, and achieves 193× higher FPS (579 vs. 3) with mResponse nearly halved (1.17 s vs. 2.46 s on ROL); however, TTHF retains a narrow edge on AUC-Frame for DoTA (84.7% vs. 82.3%) and AP on ROL (58.5% vs. 57.0%). STFE (Zhou et al., 2022) is the closest on AP for ROL (57.9% vs. 57.0%) but trails by 1.7 pp AUC and runs at only 77 FPS. Among the real-time-specific baselines in Table 8, OURS surpasses MOVAD (Rossi et al., 2024) on AUC-Frame (73.6% vs. 71.9% ROL), mTTA (2.80 s vs. 2.47 s), and mResponse (1.17 s vs. 2.61 s ROL), while frame-level methods EfficientAD and AED-MAE are far behind on all traffic-specific metrics (AUC-Frame ≤ 65.2%). The FOL family (Yao et al., 2022) and earlier autoencoder baselines (ConvAE, ConvLSTMAE, AnoPred) are consistently outperformed by 3–16 pp in AUC and by 0.5–1.3 s in mTTA, confirming that the asynchronous event-stream architecture delivers both accuracy and the sub-second response times critical for safety-critical driving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the traffic anomaly detection (TAD) and anticipation (TAA) literature by explicitly incorporating response time as a first-class optimization objective, formalizing 'real-time anomaly detection' with a new mResponse metric. The event-processing branch is directly derived from DAGr (Gehrig & Scaramuzza, 2024) and the asynchronous GNN paradigm of Li et al. (2021), but the paper is the first to introduce event streams as a modality for road traffic anomaly detection, fusing them with RGB features via a unidirectional CNN-to-GNN augmentation. Prior TAD/TAA methods (FOL, AM-Net, STFE, TTHF) operate purely on RGB video and optimize for accuracy alone; this work departs from that by treating inference latency and detection delay as co-equal constraints, motivated by the safety-critical millisecond budget of autonomous driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 579 FPS inference speed with SOTA-level AUC (0.879 on ROL) demonstrates that event-camera-based asynchronous processing can close the gap between detection accuracy and real-time responsiveness that has constrained prior traffic anomaly methods. However, the reliance on V2E-synthesized event data (since no real DVS annotations exist for traffic anomalies) and the dependence on upstream object detector reliability (documented failure cases) are significant practical limitations. Open questions include whether real event-camera hardware in deployment will match V2E fidelity, how the approach scales to multi-vehicle dense traffic, and whether the single-stage architecture advantage generalizes beyond the tested datasets.
