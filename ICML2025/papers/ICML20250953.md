## Problem

Previous vision encoders for robot manipulation, pre-trained with single-image reconstruction or two-image contrastive learning, capture static visual information but neglect the dynamic aspects of physical interactions that are critical for embodied tasks. While video diffusion models (VDMs) demonstrate strong future-frame prediction and physical world understanding, their internal representations—which explicitly encode both current and predicted future states—have not been leveraged as visual features for robot policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a current RGB observation (256×256, optionally from multiple camera views such as a static and wrist-mounted camera) and a text instruction (encoded via CLIP into 20×512 tokens) as input, and outputs a robot action sequence (e.g., 10 steps × 7 DoF for arm tasks, 10 × 18 for dexterous hand). Unusually, the video diffusion model is consumed as a single-pass encoder rather than a multi-step denoiser: the current image is concatenated channel-wise with white noise and fed through one forward pass of the TVP model, yielding intermediate up-sampling layer features that serve as predictive visual representations. Action chunking of 10 steps enables 7–10 Hz closed-loop control on consumer GPUs.

## Method

VPP employs a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, the 1.5B-parameter Stable Video Diffusion model is fine-tuned into a manipulation-focused Text-guided Video Prediction (TVP) model by adding CLIP cross-attention for language conditioning and training on a mixture of internet human manipulation (Something-Something-v2), internet robot (RT-1, Bridge, BC-Z), and self-collected downstream task videos with weighted sampling ratios. In stage 2, the frozen TVP model is used purely as a vision encoder: a single forward pass with white-noise input produces up-sampling layer features (T×C×W×H) that explicitly represent current and future frames; these are interpolated to a common spatial size, concatenated along the channel dimension, and aggregated by a Video Former (spatial-temporal attention over learnable tokens) into a fixed-length representation. A Diffusion Transformer policy head then generates the action sequence via cross-attention on these tokens, effectively learning an implicit inverse dynamics model that tracks the robot's motion within the predicted future. This single-pass design avoids the multi-step denoising bottleneck of prior video-prediction-based policies, reducing inference to ~140 ms and enabling high-frequency closed-loop control.

## Results

On the Calvin ABC→D zero-shot long-horizon benchmark, VPP achieves an average task completion length of 4.33, a 18.6% relative improvement over the previous SOTA (RoboUniview, 3.65). On MetaWorld (50 tasks), VPP reaches 68.2% average success rate, outperforming GR-1 (57.4%) by 10.8%. In real-world dexterous hand manipulation, VPP achieves 75% success on seen tasks and 60% on unseen tasks, substantially exceeding the strongest baselines (Susie: 45%/28%, GR-1: 32%/15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPP outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On Calvin ABC→D, VPP (4.33) surpasses the previous SOTA RoboUniview (3.65) by 18.6%, VidMan (3.42) by 26.6%, and GR-1 (3.06) by 41.5%; even with only 10% training data, VPP (3.25) exceeds GR-1's full-data result (3.06). On MetaWorld, VPP (68.2%) beats GR-1 (57.4%) by 10.8% and Susie (41.0%) by 27.2%. In real-world Franka Panda experiments, VPP achieves 85% seen / 73% unseen success, compared to Susie (56%/46%) and GR-1 (52%/38%). On the dexterous hand platform, VPP dominates all categories: 75% seen vs. Susie 45%, 60% unseen vs. Susie 28%, and 68% tool-use vs. Susie 23%. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that replacing VPP's predictive VDM representations with Stable-VAE (2.58), VC-1 (1.23), or Voltron (1.54) causes substantial performance drops, validating the core hypothesis that video diffusion model internals encode superior predictive information for control.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPP extends the line of work using generative model representations for robot control (Gupta et al. 2024 for image diffusion, VidMan for video diffusion) by repurposing a fine-tuned video diffusion model (SVD) as a single-pass vision encoder whose internal features inherently encode both current and predicted future states. It departs from prior future-prediction-based policies (SuSIE, UniPi) that require full multi-step denoising to produce a goal image, and from autoregressive video-action models (GR-1) that generate one frame per forward pass, by extracting predictive representations in a single forward step. The action head builds on Diffusion Policy with a DiT architecture, while the Video Former module with spatial-temporal attention is a new aggregation mechanism not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving 7–10 Hz closed-loop control (vs. sub-Hz for denoising-based methods) while maintaining strong predictive guidance, making VDM-based policies viable for real-time dexterous manipulation. The data-efficiency result (10% Calvin data outperforming full-data baselines) and the single-view ablation beating depth-augmented 3D methods suggest the predictive representation is a compact, information-dense interface. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons beyond 16 frames, whether the single-step representation degrades for highly dynamic or contact-rich interactions, and whether the heavy 1.5B-parameter TVP model can be distilled for edge deployment.
