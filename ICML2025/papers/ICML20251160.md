## Problem

Traditional IRL assumes a single static reward function, and even recent time-varying IRL variants fail to capture a critical biological reality: animals make decisions based on their history, not just their current state. No existing IRL framework incorporates both decision-level history dependency (how past decisions and location shape future goal-switching) and action-level history dependency (how recent trajectory context influences within-goal behavior).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251160/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete state-action sequences from behavioral recordings (e.g., 127-node labyrinth visits, 9 MoSeq behavioral syllables, or 25-position gridworld states), with each trajectory spanning 300–500 time steps. It outputs, for each inferred hidden mode: a history-dependent reward function r_z(s_t, s_{t-1}, …, s_{t-L+1}, a_t), a corresponding policy, and state-dependent mode transition probabilities P_z(z_{t+1}|z_t, s_t). The number of hidden modes Z and history length L are hyperparameters selected via held-out log-likelihood.

## Method

SWIRL extends a Hidden-Mode MDP by introducing two levels of history dependency: (1) decision-level, where hidden-mode transitions depend on the current state P_z(z_{t+1}|z_t, s_t) rather than only the previous mode, capturing location-dependent goal switching; and (2) action-level, where both the reward and policy under each mode depend on the previous L states via state augmentation, yielding non-Markovian decision processes. Training proceeds via EM: the E-step computes posterior mode distributions using forward-backward message passing over the trajectory, while the M-step updates all parameters (rewards, mode transitions, initial distributions) by gradient descent on the EM auxiliary function. The IRL component is embedded in the policy term of the auxiliary function, where Soft-Q iteration computes optimal Q-functions from the current reward estimate and a Boltzmann distribution yields the stochastic policy, making the overall procedure compatible with the MaxEnt IRL framework. This design allows SWIRL to be combined with either model-based (Soft-Q iteration) or model-free (IQ-Learn) IRL solvers, with the model-based variant preferred for the small, data-limited neuroscience settings studied here.

## Results

Across three experiments (simulated 5×5 gridworld, 127-node water-restricted labyrinth, and mouse spontaneous behavior), the full SWIRL model (S-2, with both state-dependent mode transitions and L=2 history-dependent rewards) achieves the best held-out test log-likelihood and segmentation accuracy on the gridworld and labyrinth datasets. On the spontaneous behavior dataset, S-1 (state-dependent transitions, L=1 reward) is optimal, as the preprocessing (merging consecutive identical syllables) makes longer history windows poorly defined. All SWIRL variants consistently outperform the single-reward MaxEnt baseline and the dynamics-based ARHMM/rARHMM baselines on held-out test LL across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWIRL outperforms MaxEnt IRL (single static reward) on all three datasets, confirming that multiple hidden reward modes are necessary to capture long-term behavioral structure. Compared to Multi-intention IQL (I-1, no state-dependent transitions), adding state dependency to mode transitions (S-1) improves test LL and segmentation accuracy on the gridworld and labyrinth, showing that decision-level history dependency matters. Compared to Locally Consistent IRL (S-1, no action-level history), adding L=2 history-dependent rewards (S-2) further improves test LL and reward recovery on the gridworld and labyrinth, but slightly hurts performance on spontaneous behavior due to irregular time intervals after syllable merging. On the spontaneous behavior dataset, all four SWIRL variants (I-1, I-2, S-1, S-2) outperform both ARHMM (MoSeq) and rARHMM on held-out test LL, demonstrating that learning explicit reward functions via IRL provides better behavioral segmentation than pure autoregressive dynamics modeling. The paper also shows that S-2 recovers interpretable history-dependent reward maps (e.g., water port reward depends on whether the mouse was previously at the port) that no baseline can produce.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWIRL extends the multi-intention IRL lineage (Zhu et al., 2024; Nguyen et al., 2015) by adding two levels of history dependency: state-conditioned hidden-mode transitions (decision-level) and non-Markovian reward/policy functions over L previous states (action-level). It is built on the MaxEnt IRL framework (Ziebart et al., 2008) and uses Soft-Q iteration or IQ-Learn for the inner reward optimization. The paper also explicitly positions SWIRL as a principled generalization of dynamics-based behavioral analysis methods (ARHMM/MoSeq, SLDS), showing that in deterministic MDPs SWIRL reduces to ARHMM, thereby unifying two previously separate literatures in animal behavior modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SWIRL provides the first IRL framework that recovers interpretable, history-dependent reward maps and hidden-mode segments from long, non-stereotyped animal trajectories without prior knowledge of reward locations—a capability absent from prior IRL or dynamics-based approaches. The spontaneous behavior experiment revealed that action-level history dependency (L>1) can actually degrade performance when temporal structure is disrupted by preprocessing, positioning SWIRL as a hypothesis-testing tool rather than a universally superior model. Open questions include scalability to larger state spaces (RNN/transformer-based policies), extension to POMDPs for partially observable environments, and whether the dopamine-reward correlations observed here generalize across brain regions and species.
