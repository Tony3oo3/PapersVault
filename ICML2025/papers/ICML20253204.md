## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning networks suffer from progressive loss of plasticity due to primacy bias (overfitting to early experiences) and dead/dormant neurons, causing performance degradation as training progresses. Existing reset-based remedies either require prohibitively large replay buffers for recovery or selectively reset only non-contributing neurons, leaving a fundamental dilemma where critically important neurons become irresetable. The paper asks whether a mechanism can regenerate all neurons cyclically while preserving learned knowledge, achieving sustainable plasticity without performance collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard state-vector observations from MuJoCo and DMC environments (e.g., 17-dim Hopper, 45-dim Humanoid) and outputs continuous policy actions via a 3-layer tanh actor network. Unusually, the method also modifies the network itself during training: every F environment steps, a fraction of neurons in each layer are reinitialized and then recovered via distillation, so the 'output' includes a structurally updated policy network whose parameters have been partially regenerated. The distillation phase operates on PPO's own 8192-sample online replay buffer without additional environment sampling.

## Method

The Sustainable Backup Propagation (SBP) framework implements neuron regeneration through two coupled procedures. Cycle Reset periodically reinitializes p% of neurons per layer in a fixed cyclical order (so the longest-surviving neurons are always the next to reset), governed by reset frequency F and rate p. Inner Distillation then freezes the reset neurons and trains the remaining (non-reset) neurons against a temporary copy of the pre-reset policy using a novel α-weighted Double KL divergence (α-DKL) loss that combines Forward KL for knowledge transfer with Reverse KL to filter out harmful information, continuing until the distillation loss falls below threshold τ. This dual-phase design ensures plasticity recovery (reset neurons regain capacity) while the non-reset neurons absorb and redistribute the knowledge that would otherwise be lost. Integrated into PPO's actor network, the resulting P3O algorithm achieves full-network cyclic regeneration with only ~0.03–0.43 hours of additional distillation time over standard PPO training.

## Results

On six MuJoCo environments, P3O achieves the highest mean return across all tasks, with the most dramatic gains in Humanoid (6237 vs. PPO's 1163, a 436% improvement), HalfCheetah (6865 vs. 3626, +89%), and Ant (5522 vs. 3354, +64%). Even in environments where PPO already performs well (Hopper, HumanoidStandup, Walker), P3O still edges ahead by 1–8%. The ablation PPO+Cycle (reset without distillation) underperforms standard PPO in 4 of 6 environments, confirming that inner distillation is essential for the reset to be beneficial rather than destructive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P3O outperforms all three baselines (PPO, PPO+CBP, PPO+ReDo) on every one of the six MuJoCo environments. Against standard PPO, the gains range from a modest +1% on Hopper to a striking +436% on Humanoid, with large improvements on HalfCheetah (+89%) and Ant (+64%). Compared to PPO+CBP, P3O wins on all six tasks; CBP's contribution-based neuron selection helps on Ant and HalfCheetah over raw PPO but still falls short of P3O's full-network cycle reset with distillation. Against PPO+ReDo, P3O again dominates across the board, with ReDo's activation-score-based selection providing only marginal or inconsistent gains over PPO. The PPO+Cycle ablation (cycle reset without inner distillation) actually degrades performance below PPO in 4 of 6 environments (e.g., Walker drops from 6270 to 4993), demonstrating that reset alone is insufficient and that the α-DKL inner distillation is the critical component enabling sustainable plasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reset-based plasticity recovery methods (CBP, ReDo) by introducing a 'neuron regeneration' framework that pairs cyclic neuron resetting with a knowledge-recovery distillation step, addressing the core limitation of prior resets that discard learned knowledge. The distillation mechanism draws on the forward/reverse KL divergence analysis from policy distillation literature (Martins et al., 2021), repurposing it as an intra-network knowledge transfer tool rather than a teacher-student compression technique. The overall framework is instantiated within PPO, building directly on Schulman et al.'s clipped objective, and the Cycle Friction benchmark extends the Slip MuJoCo task (Dohare et al., 2024) to test cyclic environmental adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Plasticity loss is a fundamental bottleneck in long-horizon RL, and this paper demonstrates that combining systematic neuron cycling with targeted distillation can substantially improve sample efficiency, particularly in complex environments (436% on Humanoid, 89% on HalfCheetah). The finding that naive cyclic reset without recovery actually degrades performance below standard PPO underscores that the distillation component is not optional but essential. Open questions include optimal adaptive reset scheduling (current parameters are fixed and environment-dependent, as Walker requires different settings), theoretical guarantees for the regeneration process, and whether the approach generalizes beyond on-policy actor-critic methods to off-policy or model-based settings.
