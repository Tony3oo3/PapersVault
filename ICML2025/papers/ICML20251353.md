## Problem

While NTK theory characterizes training dynamics of over-parameterized networks, the spectral structure of the conjugate kernel (CK) and neural tangent kernel (NTK) matrices under class-structured inputs remained largely unexplored in the high-dimensional regime. Specifically, it was unknown whether isolated eigenvalues detach from the bulk of the limiting spectral measure, where they land, and whether their associated eigenvectors encode the group (class) structure of the input data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a p×n data matrix X whose columns are drawn from a K-class Gaussian mixture model (with class-specific covariance matrices C_a), random weight matrices with i.i.d. zero-mean entries, and entrywise activation functions (e.g., ReLU, Poly, Sigmoid) across L hidden layers. It produces (i) the asymptotic positions of isolated eigenvalues of the CK and NTK, determined as solutions to a K×K determinant equation involving Stieltjes transforms, and (ii) the entrywise almost-sure limit of the projection matrix onto the eigenspace associated with each isolated eigenvalue, which quantifies alignment with class-label indicator vectors. The analysis operates in the regime n, p → ∞ with p/n → c₀ ∈ (0,∞).

## Method

Building on the deterministic equivalents of Gu et al. (2024), the paper shows that the ℓ-th layer CK (NTK) is spectrally equivalent to α_{ℓ,1} X^T X + V A_ℓ V^T + α_{ℓ,0} I_n (with β coefficients for NTK), where V = [J/√p, ψ] encodes class labels and norm fluctuations. Isolated eigenvalues are then found by solving det H_1(z) = 0 (or det H_2(z) = 0 for NTK), a K×K matrix equation whose entries involve the Stieltjes transforms m_a(z) of the GMM bulk; a phase transition occurs when the solution ρ lies outside the bulk support S. For eigenvectors, the projection onto the isolated eigenspace is expressed as a contour integral of the resolvent, evaluated via Woodbury's identity and L'Hôpital's rule to yield the closed-form limit −h_ℓ^1(ρ) Γ(ρ) F(ρ), where F(ρ) is built from the left/right eigenvectors of H_1(ρ) at zero. This reveals that the eigenspace is informative (captures class structure) precisely when h_ℓ^1(ρ) ≠ 0, and non-informative when ρ approaches a zero of h_ℓ^1, linking activation function choice and inter-class covariance differences to feature propagation.

## Results

The paper establishes that isolated eigenvalues of the CK and NTK exist outside the bulk support when the determinant of a K×K matrix H_1(ρ) (or H_2(ρ) for NTK) vanishes, with asymptotic positions α_{ℓ,1}ρ + α_{ℓ,0} (CK) or β_{ℓ,1}ρ + β_{ℓ,0} (NTK). The corresponding eigenspace projection is characterized entrywise as −h_ℓ^1(ρ)Γ(ρ)F(ρ) + o_{a.s.}(1), where F(ρ) depends on the left/right eigenvectors of H_1(ρ) at zero. Empirically, on MNIST (digits 1 and 7, 1600 samples each), a three-layer network with activations [Sin, ReLU/10, Sin] yields four isolated eigenvalues, with the eigenvector of the largest being informative for class separation while the other three are non-informative. A phase transition is observed: switching all activations from Poly to ReLU (n=2000, p=3600, 4 classes) causes an isolated eigenvalue to emerge, confirming the role of activation function choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no method-to-method comparisons or benchmark tables. The contribution is to extend the deterministic equivalents of Gu et al. (2024) by identifying and characterizing isolated eigenvalues and their eigenspaces, rather than proposing a new algorithm to compare against. The paper contrasts its framework with Wang et al. (2024), who analyze spiked eigenvalue propagation through CK layers but require the input data to contain spiked eigenvalues and use the same activation at every layer. In contrast, this work shows that isolated eigenvalues can arise purely from the group (class) structure of GMM inputs without any spiked structure in the original data, and accommodates different activation functions per layer. The empirical validation (Figures 1–5) serves as self-consistency checks of the theory rather than comparisons to competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gu et al. (2024), which established deterministic equivalents for the CK and NTK in the high-dimensional regime, and on Benaych-Georges & Couillet (2016), which characterized the limiting spectral distribution of Gram matrices under GMM assumptions. It departs from Wang et al. (2024), which studied how spiked eigenstructures propagate through hidden layers, by showing that isolated eigenvalues can emerge purely from inter-class covariance differences in GMM data—without requiring any spiked eigenvalues in the input—and by allowing different activation functions across layers. The eigenvector alignment analysis (Theorem 3.4, Corollary 3.5) extends the spectral analysis task from eigenvalue characterization to a precise entrywise limit of the projection onto the isolated eigenspace, connecting it to unsupervised class structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous characterization of when and how group features in input data manifest as isolated eigenvalues in deep kernel matrices, with a phase transition governed by activation function choice and covariance separation. This offers a quantitative mechanism for understanding feature learning in randomly initialized DNNs, bridging random matrix theory and neural network interpretability. Key limitations include the GMM input assumption, restriction to fully-connected architectures, and analysis of expected (not empirical) kernels; the authors note that extending to heavy-tailed distributions and other architectures (residual, attention) are important open directions. The single MNIST experiment (2 classes, 1600 images) provides only preliminary empirical validation of the theoretical predictions.
