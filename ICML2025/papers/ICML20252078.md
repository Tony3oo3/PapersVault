## Problem

Existing generative replay methods for Federated Continual Learning (FCL) focus almost exclusively on privacy constraints, overlooking the non-IID nature of client data. This paper identifies two underexploited properties: (1) Client Expertise Superiority, where individual clients outperform the server on specific classes under non-IID settings, and (2) Client Forgetting Variance, where heterogeneous local distributions cause clients to forget at different rates. The central question is: beyond privacy, how can the unique role of clients be leveraged to improve synthetic data quality and replay effectiveness in FCL?

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet100 at 224×224) in a class-incremental federated setting with 5 clients over 5 or 10 sequential tasks. The generator takes 256-dimensional noise vectors as input and produces synthetic samples that serve as abstract 'meta data' representations rather than visually recognizable images. Each client receives a personalized replay buffer whose per-task allocation is determined by that client's forgetting profile, and the final output is a class label prediction from a CNN classifier trained jointly on new local data and the adaptive synthetic buffer.

## Method

CAN consists of two stages. In Expert-Driven Data Synthesis, an expertise map selects the highest-accuracy client per class as a domain expert; the generator is then trained with a joint loss combining a navigation loss (cross-entropy ensuring the expert correctly classifies the synthetic sample) and a gap loss (negative KL divergence between a reference model trained on synthetic data and the server model, encouraging capture of fine-grained features the server misses). In Adaptive Replay, each client's forgetting pattern is profiled by measuring per-task accuracy after a short training phase on new data; forgetting weights (inverse accuracy) are computed, and the replay buffer is reallocated proportionally so that tasks with greater forgetting receive more synthetic samples. This client-specific buffer allocation, combined with expert-guided generation, allows CAN to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy with smaller buffer sizes (500 samples) than baselines requiring 1250–2560 samples.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (5-task, IID), CAN with a 500-sample buffer achieves 46.15% average accuracy and 32.42 forgetting score, matching LANDER's accuracy (46.16%) while reducing forgetting by 2.59 points, and outperforming LANDER with a 1250-sample buffer on forgetting (32.42 vs. 37.23). Under the most challenging NIID(0.1) setting, CAN (500) attains 37.14% accuracy and 37.32 forgetting, remaining competitive with LANDER (1000) at 39.88%/32.21. On ImageNet100 (5-task, NIID(0.5)), CAN achieves 37.56% average accuracy versus LANDER's 28.28%, a 9.28-point gap that widens to 13.21 points under NIID(0.1) (28.18% vs. 14.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LANDER (the primary baseline), CAN with a 500-sample buffer matches accuracy (46.15% vs. 46.16% on CIFAR-100 IID 5-task) while reducing the forgetting score from 35.01 to 32.42; notably, CAN(500) also beats LANDER(1250) on forgetting (32.42 vs. 37.23) with 60% less buffer storage. Against TARGET, CAN achieves a 9.84-point accuracy gain (46.15% vs. 36.31%) on CIFAR-100 IID 5-task, demonstrating the benefit of client-expertise-guided synthesis over server-only knowledge. Against regularization-based baselines (FedEWC, FedWeIT, FedLwF), CAN outperforms by 15–30 accuracy points, confirming that generative replay with adaptive allocation is far more effective than parameter-penalty or distillation-only approaches under non-IID conditions. In the appendix comparison with DDDR (a diffusion-model-based method), CAN matches or slightly exceeds DDDR across all buffer sizes and heterogeneity levels (e.g., 44.92% vs. 44.21% on CIFAR-100 IID, buffer 500) without requiring any pretrained model, highlighting that client-aware strategies can close the gap to diffusion-based replay at substantially lower computational and storage cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAN extends the generative replay framework for federated continual learning established by TARGET (Zhang et al., 2023) and LANDER (Tran et al., 2024), reusing LANDER's generator architecture and the foundational deep generative replay paradigm of Shin et al. (2017). The key departure is a conceptual repositioning: rather than relying solely on server-side knowledge or external pretrained models (CLIP in LANDER, diffusion models in DDDR) to guide synthetic data generation, CAN introduces an 'expertise map' that selects per-class client experts to navigate generation, and an adaptive buffer that reallocates replay samples according to each client's measured forgetting profile. The adaptive scaling factors for balancing new and old knowledge are borrowed from R-DFCIL (Gao et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that client-specific expertise under non-IID distributions can substitute for pretrained models in guiding synthetic data quality, reducing both storage and computational overhead in FCL deployments. The adaptive replay mechanism addresses a practical gap—uniform buffer allocation ignores heterogeneous forgetting patterns—but the 'V' phenomenon observed with increasing buffer size suggests the method has not yet fully exploited larger buffers, pointing to open questions about how to better leverage scale. Broader implications include whether the client-as-navigator framing generalizes to settings with dynamic client participation, very large client populations, or non-class-incremental task structures.
