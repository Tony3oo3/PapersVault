## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning methods either require extensive labeled data (SFT), maximize a single reward while ignoring solution diversity (PPO), or incur high inference-time search costs (ToT, RAP). The paper addresses the gap of training LLMs to generate diverse, high-quality multi-step reasoning solutions with minimal training examples (as few as 15), rather than converging to a single optimal path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252926/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text-described initial state and goal specification, and produces multiple diverse reasoning trajectories where each step is a natural-language action (e.g., 'pickup blue' in BlocksWorld, an arithmetic equation in Game24). The LLM operates at the granularity of reasoning steps rather than individual tokens, and a transition function (LLM-based with greedy decoding or an external environment) maps each generated action to the next state. Trajectories are sampled with probabilities proportional to the unnormalized reward of the terminal state, so higher-reward solutions appear more frequently but lower-reward valid solutions are still accessible.

## Method

FOR formulates multi-step LLM reasoning as a Markovian flow on a DAG-structured reasoning graph, where each reasoning step is an edge (action) and each configuration is a node (state). The forward policy P_F is parameterized as the LLM itself (fine-tuned via LoRA on Llama-3-8B), and training uses the trajectory balance objective from GFlowNets with a log-variance approximation for the partition function Z, minimizing the variance of the log-ratio between forward and backward trajectory probabilities. The backward policy is set to uniform over the parents of each state, avoiding the need to learn a separate backward network. To efficiently explore the vast trajectory space, FOR combines on-policy sampling with tempered variants, an off-policy replay buffer prioritizing high-reward trajectories, ε-sampling, and a modified local search that truncates the latter portion of the highest-reward trajectory and reconstructs it with a random policy. This step-level (rather than token-level) GFlowNet formulation amortizes search-based inference into training, enabling diverse solution discovery across six reasoning benchmarks with as few as 15 training examples.

## Results

With only 15 training examples, FOR achieves 78.44% accuracy on 6-step BlocksWorld (vs. 34.68% for SFT and 40.73% for GFN-CoT), 10.87% on Rubik's Cube (vs. 4.92% for XoT), 50.37% on 1D-ARC (vs. 39.51% for CoT), 57.39% on GSM8K (vs. 52.69% for SFT), and 63.07% OOD prediction accuracy on ProntoQA (vs. 50.10% for STaR). FOR also consistently leads on diversity (e.g., 1.33 on 6-step BlocksWorld vs. 1.04–1.12 for SFT variants) and creativity (e.g., 82.61% on Rubik's Cube vs. 8.69% for SFT), while matching the inference speed of CoT and SFT-based methods and running 30× faster than ToT(BFS) and RAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOR outperforms all same-base-model baselines across all six tasks. On BlocksWorld 6-step, FOR reaches 78.44% accuracy, surpassing SFT (34.68%), SFT+PPO (24.58%), SFT+GFN-CoT (40.73%), and RAP (69.70%), while also achieving the highest diversity (1.33) and creativity (9.52%)—metrics where most baselines score 0. On Rubik's Cube, FOR (10.87%) more than doubles the best prompting baseline XoT (4.92%) and achieves 82.61% creativity vs. 8.69% for SFT. On 1D-ARC, FOR (50.37%) beats CoT (39.51%) and program-synthesis baselines (Hypo-Search 1.48%, Program-Only 0.74%). On GSM8K, FOR (57.39%) and FOR+PRM (62.62%) exceed SFT (52.69%) and CoT (45.72%) in both accuracy and diversity. On ProntoQA OOD, FOR (63.07% pred. acc., 28.88% proof acc.) substantially outperforms STaR (50.10%, 24.60%) and CoT (43.50%, 18.50%). The only methods that surpass FOR on raw accuracy are the much larger O1-mini (93.93% on 6-step BlocksWorld, 94% on Game24) and GPT-4o (67.67% on 6-step BlocksWorld, 59% on Game24), but even these lag behind FOR on diversity and creativity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021) from their established domains (molecule synthesis, combinatorial optimization) to multi-step LLM reasoning by formulating reasoning as a Markovian flow on a DAG. It specifically adapts the trajectory balance objective (Malkin et al., 2022a) and the log-variance approximation for Z (Zhang et al., 2023c), while introducing a modified local search (Kim et al., 2023) for efficient exploration during training. The key departure from prior GFlowNet-LLM work (GFN-CoT, Hu et al., 2023a) is the shift from token-level to reasoning-step-level modeling, and from reward maximization (PPO) to reward-proportional sampling, which is the core mechanism enabling diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOR addresses a genuine gap: existing LLM reasoning methods (SFT, PPO, CoT, ToT) optimize for the single best solution and produce near-zero diversity, while FOR with only 15 training examples discovers multiple unique valid solutions across six reasoning tasks. The practical implication is that diverse solution generation could improve robustness (via aggregation/ranking) and enable creative assistance in scientific discovery. However, the approach requires a well-defined DAG structure and a computable reward function, limiting applicability to open-ended or ill-structured reasoning; training cost is ~35× higher than SFT, and test sets are relatively small (30–183 examples per task), leaving open whether the data-efficiency and diversity gains hold at scale.
