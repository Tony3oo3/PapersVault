## Problem

Existing KBQA methods face three interrelated challenges: end-to-end approaches lack awareness of the KB environment (struggling with unseen entities and relations), step-by-step methods (CoT/ToT) suffer from local optima or combinatorially large search spaces, and training open-source LLMs for logical form generation depends heavily on expensive human annotation. No prior method simultaneously addresses all three while operating in low-resource settings with limited labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a natural language question and a Freebase-based knowledge graph, KBQA-o1 produces a stepwise logical form in S-expression notation built from 8 atomic operations (entity extraction, relation joining, merging, ordering, comparison, time constraint, counting, and finish), which is then converted to an executable SPARQL query returning an answer entity set. Each intermediate step is constrained to be executable over the actual KB graph, and the method operates in low-resource settings with only 40 training examples (GrailQA) or 100 (WebQSP, GraphQ).

## Method

KBQA-o1 combines a ReAct-based agent (Thought-Action-Observation loop) with Monte Carlo Tree Search driven by a policy model and a reward model. During MCTS expansion, the policy model generates candidate next steps via beam search, which are filtered against KB-executable options using SimCSE semantic matching, then reranked by the policy model's likelihood scores; UCT governs node selection balancing exploration and exploitation. The reward model scores complete logical forms while the policy model scores individual steps, combined via a weighted Q-value that back-propagates through the search tree. An incremental fine-tuning stage uses MCTS-generated auto-annotations (filtered by a reward threshold γ*) to improve both models without additional human labeling, enabling the system to surpass fully supervised methods on compositional and zero-shot splits.

## Results

On GrailQA (40-shot low-resource setting), KBQA-o1 with Llama-3.1-8B achieves 78.5% overall F1 and 71.9% overall EM, surpassing the previous best low-resource method ARG-KBQA (GPT-3.5-turbo) by 30.0 F1 points (78.5% vs. 48.5%). In the more challenging compositional and zero-shot splits, KBQA-o1 even exceeds fully supervised methods like TIARA (81.9% overall F1) on compositional F1 (77.6% vs. 74.8%) and zero-shot F1 (76.1% vs. 80.7% — slightly below TIARA but far above all low-resource baselines). On WebQSP (100-shot), KBQA-o1 (Llama-3.1-8B) reaches 59.8% F1, outperforming the best low-resource baseline ARG-KBQA (58.8%). On GraphQ (100-shot), KBQA-o1 achieves 48.7% F1, substantially exceeding the best low-resource baseline KB-Coder (31.1%) and all full-supervised baselines (max 31.8% for ArcaneQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three low-resource baselines (KB-BINDER, KB-Coder, ARG-KBQA, all using GPT-3.5-turbo), KBQA-o1 with Llama-3.1-8B dominates across all three datasets: on GrailQA it gains +30.0 F1 over ARG-KBQA (78.5 vs. 48.5), on WebQSP +1.0 F1 (59.8 vs. 58.8), and on GraphQ +17.6 F1 over KB-Coder (48.7 vs. 31.1). Against fully supervised methods, KBQA-o1 (Llama-3.1-8B) matches or exceeds TIARA on compositional F1 (77.6 vs. 74.8) and DecAF on compositional F1 (77.6 vs. 79.8, slightly below), while remaining well below on I.I.D F1 (85.5 vs. 91.2 for TIARA), confirming the gap is primarily in in-distribution settings. In the comparison analysis (Section 5.4), KBQA-o1's MCTS variant outperforms both the CoT-based step-by-step method (QueryAgent) and the ToT-based method (ToG) on all evaluation splits and logical operators, while the end-to-end variants (RG-E2E from DecAF, GR-E2E from ChatKBQA) only match or exceed the agent process on I.I.D and WebQSP but fall behind on compositional and zero-shot. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that removing MCTS drops GrailQA overall F1 from 78.5 to 48.5 (matching ARG-KBQA), and removing the KB environment drops it to 43.0, validating that both the heuristic search and environment grounding are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

KBQA-o1 sits at the intersection of three research threads: ReAct-style LLM agents (Yao et al., 2023b), Monte Carlo Tree Search as a planning heuristic (Swiechowski et al., 2022; RAP, Hao et al., 2023), and step-by-step KBQA methods that alternate generation and retrieval (QueryAgent, Think-on-Graph). It departs from prior end-to-end KBQA (retrieve-then-generate, generate-then-retrieve) by making the KB environment a first-class, per-step input to the agent rather than a one-shot retrieval signal, and from prior CoT/ToT step-by-step methods by replacing unguided sequential or exhaustive tree search with a policy- and reward-driven MCTS that balances exploration and exploitation. The incremental fine-tuning loop, where MCTS exploration on unlabeled data produces auto-annotations that further train the policy and reward models, is the paper's most distinctive architectural contribution, positioning it as the first 'heuristic' KBQA method in the authors' framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for teams that cannot afford large-scale human annotation or proprietary API access: an 8B open-source model with 40–100 shots of labeled data outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo-based low-resource baselines by 20–30 F1 points, and the auto-annotation pipeline reduces ongoing labeling cost. The dramatic ablation gap (78.5 → 43.0 F1 when KB environment feedback is removed) underscores that per-step environmental grounding, not just model scale, is the critical factor for compositional and zero-shot generalization. Open questions include scalability to KBs far larger than Freebase, whether the MCTS rollout budget can be further reduced without quality loss, and whether the reward model's SFT-based scoring generalizes to domains with no initial labeled data at all.
