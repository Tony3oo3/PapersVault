## Problem

Modern pre-training corpora derived from CommonCrawl contain trillions of tokens but lack any inherent structure, making it impossible to reason about their internal composition or systematically rebalance content types for downstream tasks. While quality filters have become the dominant curation tool, they operate at the document level and leave the domain-level data mixture opaque and uncontrolled. The paper asks whether constructing explicit, human-interpretable domains for monolithic web corpora can provide a principled and complementary axis for data curation beyond per-document quality scoring.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw web pages (text content truncated at 50K characters plus URL) from a 200B-token CommonCrawl-based corpus and produces two independent labels per document: a topic category (24 classes, e.g., Science & Technology, Politics) and a format category (24 classes, e.g., Academic Writing, Tutorial). Downstream, the annotated corpus is resampled according to optimized mixture proportions to yield a 29B-token training set for a 1B-parameter model. A key structural detail is that the 24×24 topic-format pairs are combined under an independence assumption (NMI ≈ 0.10), so the joint mixture is the product of separately optimized topic and format distributions rather than a full 576-way optimization.

## Method

WebOrganizer constructs two complementary taxonomies (topic and format, 24 categories each) through human-in-the-loop design, iteratively refining natural-language category definitions by prompting Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct on CommonCrawl samples and reviewing annotations. To scale annotation to web scale, the paper distills the 405B LLM's soft next-token probabilities into a 140M-parameter gte-base-en-v1.5 embedding model via two-stage fine-tuning: 1M annotations from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct followed by 80K high-quality annotations from Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct, achieving 93.5% average accuracy on confident LLM labels. For mixture optimization, the authors adapt RegMix by training 512 small 50M-parameter models on randomly sampled domain mixtures, fitting a LightGBM gradient-boosted regression to predict bits-per-byte loss on MMLU and HellaSwag, and searching for the optimal mixture with a soft KL constraint toward the corpus prior and an adaptive multi-step line search. The central empirical finding is that topic and format mixtures capture orthogonal aspects of web content (k-means clusters align with topics but not formats), so their product combination yields a 3.0-point average gain over the baseline—matching FineWeb-Edu—and further boosts FineWeb-Edu from 54.2% to 56.2% when the quality filter is applied independently within each domain subset.

## Results

On the 9-task OLMES evaluation suite (1B-parameter models, 29B tokens, DCLM 1b-1x setting), Topic×Format domain mixing achieves 54.6% average accuracy, a 3.0-point gain over the 51.6% baseline corpus and narrowly beating FineWeb-Edu (54.2%). When combined with quality filters, domain mixing boosts FineWeb-Edu from 54.2% to 56.2% (+2.0 pts) and DCLM-fasttext from 55.1% to 56.1% (+1.0 pts). The largest individual task gains come from HellaSwag (60.1% with Topic×Format vs. 57.5% baseline) and ARC-easy (69.7% vs. 62.2% baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FineWeb-Edu (Penedo et al., 2024), standalone Topic×Format domain mixing (54.6% avg) narrowly outperforms the quality filter (54.2%), with the gap driven by HellaSwag (60.1% vs. 56.0%) and ARC-easy (69.7% vs. 71.9% where FineWeb-Edu leads). When combined, FineWeb-Edu + Topic×Format reaches 56.2%, a 2.0-point improvement that nearly doubles FineWeb-Edu's original 2.6-point gain over the 51.6% baseline. Against DCLM-fasttext (Li et al., 2024), the quality filter alone is stronger (55.1% vs. 54.6%), but adding domain mixing still yields a 1.0-point boost to 56.1%. Notably, FineWeb-Edu underperforms the random-sampling baseline on HellaSwag (56.0% vs. 57.5%), a weakness that domain mixing corrects to 62.5%—a 5-point absolute gain over baseline. The paper also shows that FineWeb-Edu's implicit domain mixture recovers 84% of its performance gains, while DCLM-fasttext's implicit mixture recovers only 35%, indicating DCLM-fasttext relies more on document-level quality signals beyond domain effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data mixture optimization line (DoReMi, RegMix) to the setting of monolithic web corpora where no pre-defined domains exist, by introducing human-designed topic and format taxonomies and distilling Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct annotations into 140M classifiers for scalable labeling. It departs from k-means clustering approaches (Gururangan et al., 2023) by showing that embedding-based clusters align primarily with topic (NMI≈0.46) but not format (NMI≈0.13), motivating the two-dimensional structure. The paper also builds on quality filtering work (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM-fasttext) by reframing these filters as implicit domain mixers, a perspective not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WebOrganizer provides a practical, open-source tool for documenting and curating the internal composition of web-scale pre-training corpora, addressing a transparency gap that has grown as datasets shifted from curated multi-source collections to monolithic CommonCrawl crawls. The finding that 84% of FineWeb-Edu's gains are recoverable by domain mixing alone raises important questions about what document-level quality scoring adds beyond coarse-grained rebalancing, and suggests that 'quality' is highly task-dependent. Open questions include whether these mixture predictions transfer across model scales, whether hierarchical or multi-label taxonomies would capture more nuance, and how the approach extends to non-English or multimodal corpora.
