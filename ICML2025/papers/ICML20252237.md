## Problem

Designing high-affinity protein binders for arbitrary targets remains challenging because existing methods suffer from two primary failure modes: the designed sequence may not fold into the intended structure, and the designed structure may not bind to the target. Most computational approaches are specialized for particular interaction types or rely on limited hotspot residues, leaving many protein targets intractable without extensive wet-lab screening.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252237/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a target protein's amino acid sequence (length M) and its 3D Cα coordinates (M×3 matrix), PPDiff non-autoregressively generates a binder protein of variable length N, producing both its 20-residue amino acid sequence and Cα backbone coordinates (N×3). At inference, 100 candidates are generated per target and ranked by complex pLDDT; the binder length is not constrained to match the target, and informative guidance (kNN energy minimization for structure, DSSP secondary-structure fragments for sequence) replaces random noise initialization to improve candidate quality.

## Method

PPDiff is a denoising diffusion probabilistic model that jointly denoises discrete amino acid types (via multinomial/categorical diffusion) and continuous Cα coordinates (via Gaussian diffusion) in a single generative process. The denoising network, SSINC, stacks interleaved blocks of self-attention layers (capturing global sequence correlations) and kNN equivariant graph convolutional layers (modeling local 3D spatial interactions), topped with a single causal attention layer that enforces left-to-right sequential dependencies to simplify the intricate inter-residue coupling. The model is pretrained on PPBench (706,360 PDB-derived chain-pair complexes) and finetuned for specific applications such as mini-binder and antibody design. Training uses a variational lower bound decomposed into a categorical KL term for sequence and a weighted L2 term for structure, with 1,000 diffusion steps and ESM-2 pretrained initialization for the self-attention parameters (692M total parameters).

## Results

On the general protein-protein complex design task (PPBench), PPDiff achieves a top-1 success rate of 50.00% (ipTM=0.700, pTM=0.779, PAE=9.153, pLDDT=83.77), surpassing the strongest baseline SSINC Network (47.36%) and SEnc+ProteinMPNN (35.08%). After finetuning, PPDiff reaches 23.16% success rate on target-protein mini-binder design and 16.89% on antigen-antibody complex design. PPDiff also achieves notably high novelty (~89%) and diversity (~91%) scores, indicating it generates genuinely new binder sequences rather than memorizing training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPDiff outperforms all four baselines on success rate across every task. Against SEnc+ProteinMPNN (the weakest baseline), PPDiff gains +14.9 pp on general design (50.00% vs 35.08%), +16.0 pp on mini-binder (23.16% vs 7.12%), and +10.7 pp on antigen-antibody (16.89% vs 6.24%). Against InterleavingDiff (PPDiff without causal attention), PPDiff improves top-1 success rate by 4.4 pp (50.00% vs 45.61%) and mini-binder by 3.8 pp (23.16% vs 19.32%), while maintaining comparable novelty (~89%); the causal attention layer is the key architectural addition. Against SSINC Network (same architecture, no diffusion), PPDiff achieves higher success rate (50.00% vs 47.36%) and dramatically higher novelty (89.46% vs 37.46%), confirming that the diffusion process is essential for generating diverse designs. In the head-to-head comparison with RF+MPNN (Table 5), PPDiff edges out the average success rate (23.16% vs 21.46%) while achieving substantially higher novelty (91.39% vs 78.10%) and diversity (91.79% vs 25.71%), though RF+MPNN wins on individual targets like FGFR2 (28.07% vs 7.36%) and TrkA (100% vs 20%). PPDiff slightly trails SSINC Network on PAE and pLDDT for top-5/10 candidates but compensates with higher success rate and far superior diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPDiff extends the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) and multinomial diffusion for discrete tokens (Hoogeboom et al., 2021) to the joint protein sequence-structure generation setting, following the independent-modality diffusion formulation of Guan et al. (2023). Its core SSINC architecture combines the interleaving network from Song et al. (2024b) with E(n) equivariant graph convolutional layers (Satorras et al., 2021) and a novel causal attention component to capture sequential dependencies. Unlike prior protein design methods that handle structure (RFdiffusion, FrameDiff) or sequence (EvoDiff, DPLM) in isolation, or specialized binder designers (AlphaProteo, Bennett et al.) targeting specific proteins, PPDiff positions itself as a general-purpose, non-autoregressive co-designer for arbitrary targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 706K-complex PPBench dataset and the 50% top-1 success rate on general protein-protein complex design represent a meaningful step toward on-demand binder design without extensive wet-lab screening. The joint sequence-structure diffusion formulation directly addresses the two dominant failure modes (sequence not folding, structure not binding) that plague sequential design pipelines. However, the absence of wet-lab validation remains a critical gap, and the modest improvements over the SSINC Network baseline (47.36% → 50.00%) suggest the causal attention and diffusion components contribute incrementally rather than transformationally. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger binder lengths, whether the 50% success rate translates to experimentally validated binders, and whether the informative guidance strategy generalizes beyond the kNN-distance prior.
