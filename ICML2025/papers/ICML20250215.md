## Problem

Existing backward-compatible representation learning methods operate in Euclidean space and force new models to reconstruct old embeddings regardless of their quality, ignoring the uncertainty inherent in legacy representations. This rigid alignment constrains the new model's ability to learn richer representations, especially when old embeddings are suboptimal due to limited model capacity or out-of-distribution data. No prior work leverages the geometric structure of embedding spaces to naturally encode model evolution and representation confidence.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) as input and produces 128-dimensional hyperbolic embeddings in the Lorentz model (i.e., points on a one-sheeted hyperboloid in R^129). A standard Euclidean backbone (ResNet18 or ViT-B-16) first produces a Euclidean vector z, which is then lifted onto the hyperboloid via the exponential map at the origin. Retrieval is performed using the hyperbolic geodesic distance between query and gallery embeddings, and the old model's Euclidean embeddings are similarly lifted for cross-model compatibility evaluation.

## Method

HBCT lifts both old and new embeddings into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space, where the time-like coordinate naturally encodes model confidence and evolution. Two uncertainty-aware losses enforce compatibility: (1) an entailment cone loss that constrains new embeddings to lie within the cone of their old counterparts, with the cone aperture inversely proportional to the old embedding's norm so that uncertain old embeddings grant greater evolutionary freedom; and (2) a robust hyperbolic contrastive loss (RINCE variant) where the noise-robustness parameter q is set to the uncertainty of each old embedding, dynamically downweighting noisy positive pairs. The encoder is a hybrid Euclidean-Hyperbolic model: a standard CNN/ViT backbone produces a Euclidean vector that is projected onto the hyperboloid via the exponential map, and classification is performed with hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression. The total training objective combines the base classification loss with weighted entailment and contrastive alignment terms, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying encoder.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, HBCT achieves the highest backward-compatibility score (P_com) across all four update scenarios, improving CMC@1 compatibility by 21.4% and mAP compatibility by 44.8% on average over the strongest Euclidean baseline. In the most challenging 'Both' scenario (new architecture + new classes), HBCT reaches P_com of 0.324 (CMC@1) and 0.154 (mAP), compared to 0.245 and 0.111 for the best Euclidean method (AdvBCT). These compatibility gains come with minimal performance sacrifice (P_up ≈ 0), whereas Euclidean baselines like BCT often degrade the new model's own retrieval accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet, HBCT similarly leads, e.g., achieving P_com of 1.076 (CMC@1) in the Ext-data scenario versus 0.702 for BCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBCT outperforms all four Euclidean baselines on P_com in every scenario and both datasets. Against BCT (Shen et al., 2020), HBCT wins decisively in the 'Both' scenario (0.324 vs. −0.054 CMC@1 P_com on CIFAR-100), where BCT actually degrades compatibility below the old model's baseline; BCT's high P_com in Ext-data (0.900) comes at a P_up cost of −0.014, whereas HBCT maintains P_up ≈ −0.002. Against Hot-Refresh (Zhang et al., 2022), HBCT leads by 0.135–0.735 in CMC@1 P_com across scenarios, with the largest gap in New-Arch (0.937 vs. 0.517). Against AdvBCT (Pan et al., 2023), HBCT achieves 0.324 vs. 0.245 in the hardest 'Both' setting and 0.937 vs. 0.449 in New-Arch. Against HOC (Biondi et al., 2024), HBCT's advantage is most pronounced in the 'Both' scenario (0.324 vs. 0.116) and New-Arch (0.937 vs. 0.534), while HOC remains competitive in Ext-data where the gap narrows to 0.956 vs. 0.685. Notably, HBCT's P_up stays within ±0.005 of zero across all settings, indicating that the compatibility gains do not come at the expense of the new model's own retrieval quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes backward-compatible representation learning—a task established by Shen et al. (2020) and extended by Hot-Refresh, HOC, and AdvBCT in Euclidean/hyperspherical space—by lifting embeddings into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic geometry. It draws on entailment cones (Ganea et al., 2018; Desai et al., 2023) to impose a partial-order constraint between old and new embeddings, and adapts RINCE (Chuang et al., 2022) as an uncertainty-aware contrastive alignment loss. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the hyperbolic time-like dimension as a natural axis for model evolution and representation uncertainty, rather than forcing the new model to reconstruct old embeddings regardless of their quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HBCT addresses a practically important problem in large-scale retrieval systems: updating embedding models without re-indexing entire vector databases, which is costly and raises privacy concerns. The uncertainty-aware design—where uncertain old embeddings receive less alignment pressure—is a principled solution to the tension between compatibility and model improvement. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet, the method is restricted to supervised settings, and the authors acknowledge that norm clipping may become unstable over many sequential updates. Open questions include whether the approach extends to self-supervised or multi-modal foundation models and whether a more principled rescaling strategy can handle long-horizon model evolution.
