## Problem

Existing non-asymptotic confidence bounds (NCBs) for the optimal value of stochastic optimization problems are subject to at least one of two restrictive conditions: dependence on a global Lipschitz constant of the expected cost function, or the assumption that all moments of the underlying randomness exist (light tails). This paper asks whether computable NCBs can be constructed beyond the simultaneous imposition of both conditions, and provides the first affirmative answers for convex heavy-tailed, nonconvex light-tailed, and nonconvex heavy-tailed over-parameterized settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. sample ξ_1,…,ξ_N of the random parameter vector, a bounded closed feasible region X ⊆ R^d, and a significance level α ∈ (0,1), and produces two scalars L_{N,α} and U_{N,α} satisfying P[L_{N,α} ≤ F* ≤ U_{N,α}] ≥ 1−α. The bounds are anchored to the SAA optimal value F_N* (and the DRM optimal value F_γ* in nonconvex cases), with the critical property that they depend on the p-th central moment of the randomness and the q-norm diameter of X rather than on the Lipschitz constant of F. The interval length shrinks at rate O(N^{-1/2}), matching the parametric rate, and the dependence on dimensionality d is at most logarithmic (via Δ_{p,q} growing linearly in q = ln d).

## Method

For convex SO under heavy tails (Theorem 4.1), the paper replaces the standard Bernstein-type concentration with Marcinkiewicz's inequality, which only requires a finite p-th moment (p ≥ 2) of the stochastic gradient and cost at the optimal solution, yielding bounds that depend on σ_f, σ_g, and the feasible-region diameter D_q rather than any Lipschitz constant. For nonconvex SO under light tails (Theorem 4.7 + Lemma 4.6), the upper bound is constructed through the diametrical risk minimization (DRM) formulation, where Sudakov's minoration controls the covering number of X and Bernstein's inequality provides uniform concentration over the ε-net, while the lower bound follows directly from SAA. For nonconvex heavy-tailed over-parameterized models (Theorem 4.13), the key mechanism is constructing a bounded auxiliary problem by truncating the cost at Γ·N, applying the light-tailed DRM bound to this truncated problem, and then bounding the gap between the original and truncated objectives via Markov's inequality under the p-th moment assumption, leveraging the structural condition that the DRM yields zero optimal cost (as in over-parameterized classification with a positive margin).

## Results

For convex stochastic optimization under heavy tails (power-law with a=3.01, p=2, α=0.01), the proposed P-NCB* achieves length ratios r₁ of 0.281 at d=100 down to 0.064 at d=4000 relative to the Oliveira & Thompson (2023) benchmark, meaning the proposed confidence intervals are 3.6× to 15.6× narrower. The estimated-quantity variant P-NCB^E still achieves ratios of 0.599 to 0.170 across the same dimensionality range. For nonconvex over-parameterized models (two-layer LeakyReLU network, Student-t noise), the average length ratio r₂ ranges from 0.008 to 0.030, indicating 33× to 125× tighter bounds than the benchmark. Empirical coverage probabilities (ECP) remain at or above 0.9998 for all configurations, confirming valid coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the non-asymptotic confidence bound from Oliveira & Thompson (2023), which relies on a finite p-th moment of the Lipschitz constant and a generic chaining functional γ(𝒳) that grows at O(√d). The proposed P-NCB dominates this benchmark on the length-ratio metric across all tested dimensionalities (d=100 to 4000 for convex; d=41 to 1681 for nonconvex), with the advantage widening as d increases—precisely the regime where the benchmark's metric-entropy dependence becomes most costly. For the convex case, P-NCB* narrows the interval by a factor of ~15.6× at d=4000 versus the benchmark, while P-NCB^E (using only estimated quantities) still achieves ~5.9× improvement. For the nonconvex over-parameterized case, the proposed DRM-based bound is 33–125× tighter than the benchmark. Empirical coverage probabilities are comparable (≥0.9998 for all methods), confirming that the tighter intervals do not sacrifice validity. The key structural advantage is the elimination of the Lipschitz constant and, in the convex heavy-tailed case, the metric entropy term entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of non-asymptotic confidence bound (NCB) construction for stochastic optimization, building directly on the SAA framework of Guigues et al. (2017) and the diametrical risk minimization (DRM) formulation of Norton & Royset (2023). The key departure is from the simultaneous reliance on a global Lipschitz constant and light-tailed (sub-Gaussian/sub-exponential) assumptions that characterize all prior NCB results: Guigues et al. avoid Lipschitz dependence but require all moments to exist, while Oliveira & Thompson (2023) handle heavier tails but reintroduce Lipschitz-constant dependence. By leveraging Marcinkiewicz's inequality for heavy-tailed concentration and Sudakov's minoration for the DRM upper bound, the paper constructs bounds that are simultaneously non-Lipschitzian and valid under finite-moment (heavy-tailed) assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide the first computable NCBs that are free of both Lipschitz constants and light-tail assumptions across three settings (convex heavy-tailed, nonconvex light-tailed, and nonconvex heavy-tailed over-parameterized), which is practically significant for stopping criteria in stochastic algorithms and for applications where heavy-tailed data (e.g., financial returns, network traffic) is the norm. However, the bounds remain notably conservative (empirical coverage of 1.0000 against target 0.99 or 0.90), and the most general heavy-tailed nonconvex result requires strong structural assumptions (non-negative cost, zero DRM optimal value) that limit applicability. Open questions include tightening the constants, extending to settings without the zero-optimal-cost assumption, and reducing the remaining metric-entropy dependence in the nonconvex case.
