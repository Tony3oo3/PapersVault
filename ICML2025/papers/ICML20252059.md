## Problem

While in-context learning (ICL) has enabled transformers to perform Bayesian inference for univariate, discrete posterior predictive distributions (e.g., TabPFN), it remains unclear whether transformers can learn full Bayesian inference—yielding complex, high-dimensional continuous posterior distributions over latent variables—purely from context. This paper asks whether a transformer can learn the mapping x ↦ P^{z|x} for models like GLMs, factor analysis, and GMMs without any explicit parameter updates or parametric assumptions on the posterior.

## Input / Output

The method takes an entire tabular dataset x (e.g., K rows of covariates and a response for GLMs, or K multivariate observations for FA/GMMs) as a single context sequence, processed by a transformer encoder. It produces posterior samples of the latent variable z (e.g., regression coefficients β ∈ R^5 for GLMs, factor scores z ∈ R^{z_dim} for FA, or mixture means/variances for GMMs) by forward-solving a neural ODE starting from a standard Gaussian draw. The latent dimensionality varies by scenario (5 for GLMs, 3–5 for FA, up to 75 for GMMs), and the number of context points K ranges from 25 to 50.

## Method

The architecture pairs a TabPFN-style transformer encoder (8 layers, 512-dim, 8 heads) that encodes the dataset x with a diffusion transformer decoder that outputs a conditional vector field v_{t,x}^θ. The decoder is trained via conditional flow matching: given a sample (x, z^(1)) from the joint P^{x,z}, a base sample z^(0) ~ N(0,I), and a random time t ~ U[0,1], the network learns to predict the ground-truth vector field (z^(1) − ωz^(0)) at the interpolated point γ_t = (1−ωt)z^(0) + tz^(1), minimizing an MSE loss. At inference, sampling from P^{z|x} requires only a single encoder forward pass followed by solving the ODE with a Dopri5 solver (tolerance 10^{-7}), yielding samples in ~2 minutes for GLMs. The key theoretical contribution is Proposition 1, which shows that under a sufficient condition on the divergence d, the intractable posterior-matching objective R_θ can be replaced by a tractable surrogate R̃_θ computable from joint samples P^{x,z} alone, making the entire training procedure a supervised regression on synthetic data.

## Results

Across 7 GLM scenarios on 50 synthetic and 17 real-world datasets, the ICL approach achieves the best average C2ST (0.657 synthetic, 0.648 real-world), MMD (0.183 synthetic, 0.090 real-world), and W₂ (0.556 synthetic, 0.387 real-world) among all compared methods, with lower values indicating closer agreement with the HMC gold standard. On factor analysis, ICL achieves a near-theoretical-minimum C2ST of 0.568 on synthetic data (vs. ~0.99 for all baselines) and MMD of 0.057. On GMMs, ICL yields C2ST of 0.825 and MMD of 0.706 on synthetic data, substantially outperforming VI methods (C2ST ~0.99, MMD ~2.3–2.7), though the gap narrows on real-world data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The ICL approach outperforms all four VI variants (diagonal, full, structured, and IAF) and the Laplace approximation on GLMs across all three metrics (C2ST, MMD, W₂) on both synthetic and real-world data, with the largest margins on MMD (e.g., 0.183 vs. 0.929–1.648 on synthetic). On factor analysis, ICL dramatically outperforms all baselines on synthetic data (C2ST 0.568 vs. 0.987–0.999; MMD 0.057 vs. 3.2–3.4), though on real-world data the W₂ metric is higher for ICL (0.583 vs. 0.251–0.288), which the authors attribute to numerical issues in empirical W₂ computation. On GMMs, ICL achieves the best synthetic C2ST (0.825 vs. 0.985–0.995) and MMD (0.706 vs. 2.3–2.7), but the advantage on real-world W₂ is marginal (10.691 vs. 11.5–11.7). The paper notes that at higher latent dimensions (K=20, K=50), ICL no longer outperforms the other methods, likely due to the curse of dimensionality affecting the evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established threads: the prior-data fitted network (PFN) paradigm of training on synthetic joint distributions (Müller et al., 2021; Hollmann et al., 2022), the TabPFN transformer encoder for tabular data, and continuous normalizing flows via flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for flexible distribution modeling. The key departure from prior work is extending in-context learning from univariate or discrete posterior predictive distributions (as in TabPFN) to full multivariate continuous posteriors over latent variables, and from supervised ICL to unsupervised ICL for latent-variable models. The decoder architecture extends diffusion transformers (Peebles & Xie, 2023) by replacing self-attention with cross-attention to the encoder, and the training objective replaces the diffusion loss with a flow-matching loss, which the authors show is essential for matching HMC-quality posteriors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single forward pass through a pre-trained transformer can produce posterior samples matching Hamiltonian Monte Carlo quality, offering a fundamentally different computational trade-off: large up-front training cost in exchange for fast, flexible inference without per-dataset optimization. This opens the door to applying ICL to any probabilistic model with a simulable generative process, potentially beyond the reach of standard MCMC or VI. Key open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional posteriors (where the authors find ICL loses its edge at K=20–50), reducing the substantial training compute, and understanding how far the approach generalizes under distribution shift. The finding that ICL captures multimodal posteriors (GMMs) where even inverse autoregressive flow VI fails suggests a genuine structural advantage of the flow-matching formulation over parametric variational families.
