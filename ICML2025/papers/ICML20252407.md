## Problem

Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in generative tasks but have never been applied to clustering. Existing deep clustering methods suffer from model collapse, underutilize pretrained features, and rely on complex augmentation pipelines or multiple parallel heads, limiting their practical utility on high-dimensional data with intricate structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained DINO ViT feature vectors (ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16, 384-d or 768-d) extracted from RGB images, along with random Gaussian vectors z_T ~ N(0, F²I_d) in a d-dimensional latent space (d=32 or 64). At inference, B=4 stochastic diffusion samples are generated and their resulting cluster probability vectors p(k|x) are averaged to produce a final K-way assignment. The diffusion model is conditioned on the frozen feature vector throughout the denoising process, so no end-to-end feature learning occurs.

## Method

CLUDI formulates clustering as a latent variable model where a diffusion model generates assignment embeddings z_0 ∈ R^d conditioned on pre-trained features x, which are then projected through a logit matrix L and tempered softmax to yield cluster probabilities. Training follows a SimSIAM-style self-distillation paradigm with shared teacher-student weights: the teacher performs 25-step stochastic DDIM sampling from pure noise to produce diverse denoised embeddings, which are mapped back to embedding space via a predictor matrix E and normalized to form targets. The student denoises noisy versions of these targets (noise level sampled uniformly over t ∈ [0,T]) from feature-augmented inputs (20% dropout + Gaussian noise with σ² ∈ [0.1, 0.3]). The loss combines an MSE term on embeddings with a non-collapsing cross-entropy term that incorporates a uniform prior over the minibatch (borrowed from Self-Classifier), weighted by a Min-SNR-γ schedule to balance difficulty across timesteps. A sqrt noise schedule is used to account for reduced sensitivity of discrete labels to noise near t=0.

## Results

On ImageNet-200, CLUDI (ViT-B/16) achieves 86.1% NMI, 73.7% ACC, and 63.2% ARI, outperforming the strongest baseline TEMI (85.2% NMI, 73.1% ACC, 62.1% ARI) and substantially exceeding Self-Classifier* (78.3% NMI, 53.1% ACC, 44.1% ARI). On ImageNet-50, CLUDI reaches 91.2% NMI and 82.1% ACC, a 3.4-point NMI gain over the next-best method. On fine-grained datasets, CLUDI (ViT-B/16) achieves 81.5% NMI on Oxford 102 Flowers and 87.9% NMI on Caltech 101, consistently leading all baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUDI outperforms all five baselines (SCAN, Propos, TSP, TEMI, Self-Classifier*) across every dataset and all three metrics (NMI, ACC, ARI). The margin is most dramatic on ImageNet-200, where CLUDI (ViT-B/16) achieves 86.1% NMI versus 78.3% for Self-Classifier* (a 7.8-point gap) and 85.2% for TEMI. On ImageNet-50, CLUDI leads Self-Classifier* by 3.4 NMI points (91.2 vs. 87.8) and TEMI by 5.1 points. On fine-grained datasets the advantage is even larger: on Oxford 102 Flowers, CLUDI (ViT-B/16) reaches 81.5% NMI and 69.7% ACC, surpassing Self-Classifier* (72.5% NMI, 57.8% ACC) by 9 and 12 points respectively. TEMI is the closest competitor on ImageNet-200 but still trails CLUDI by ~1 point on NMI and ARI; on smaller datasets like CIFAR-10 and STL-10, CLUDI's lead over TEMI narrows to 1–2 NMI points.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLUDI repurposes the stochastic sampling machinery of DDIM (Song et al., 2021b) — originally designed for image generation — as a mechanism to produce diverse cluster assignment embeddings, a use case entirely absent from prior diffusion literature. The training paradigm is adapted from SimSIAM (Chen & He, 2021), where teacher and student share weights, but the teacher is replaced by a diffusion denoiser whose stochasticity provides a novel form of data augmentation. The non-collapsing cross-entropy loss with a uniform minibatch prior is borrowed directly from Self-Classifier (Amrani et al., 2022), and the pre-trained DINO ViT backbone follows the decoupled feature-learning approach of TSP and TEMI. The key departure from all prior work is the use of iterative denoising in a latent embedding space to generate and aggregate multiple stochastic cluster predictions, rather than a single deterministic forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that diffusion models can serve as effective clustering engines, opening a new direction at the intersection of generative modeling and unsupervised learning. The stochastic-sampling-as-augmentation framing offers a principled alternative to hand-crafted image augmentations or neighbor-mining pipelines, potentially simplifying training for future clustering systems. However, the method's sensitivity to the noise rescaling factor F² and embedding dimension d, and its acknowledged degradation at large cluster counts (K ≥ 200), leave open important questions about scalability and adaptive hyperparameter selection. Future work on hierarchical or multi-scale diffusion clustering could address these limitations and extend the approach to settings where the number of clusters is unknown.
