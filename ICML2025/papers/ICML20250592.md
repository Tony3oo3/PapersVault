## Problem

Existing NL-to-TL translation pipelines rely on causal language models that predict from full vocabularies (50k–100k tokens) for both atomic proposition grounding and formula generation, leading to poor handling of co-references, high data requirements, and sensitivity to training set size. No prior method exploits the context-free grammar of temporal logic or the discrete, low-cardinality nature of AP grounding to reduce the effective solution space at each decoding step.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language sentence (e.g., 'Go to the red room and push the box into the green room') and produces a well-formed temporal logic formula (e.g., '◇(red_room ∧ green_room)'). During AP grounding, each input token is assigned an integer label (0 for non-AP, 1–5 for the nth AP), so the grounded intermediate representation uses only 6 possible output tokens. The final TL output is guaranteed syntactically valid because the decoder is constrained at every step to emit only tokens permitted by the current state of the LTL grammar (1–20 valid tokens per position).

## Method

GraFT decomposes translation into two stages, each with a restricted output vocabulary. For AP grounding, a fine-tuned BERT (MLM) assigns integer IDs to tokens, reducing the prediction space from ~100k tokens to 6 labels and naturally handling co-references. For grounded NL-to-TL translation, a fine-tuned T5 (Seq2Seq) is trained with grammar-forced cross-entropy: at each position, the logit scores for all tokens outside the grammar-valid set V_t are set to −∞ before the softmax, using ground-truth tokens to track the parser state (teacher-forcing style). At inference, a logits processor (Algorithm 1) dynamically restricts valid tokens based on the model's own decoded prefix. The paper proves that this masking yields lower-or-equal cross-entropy and zero gradient on invalid tokens, and extends the argument to show a strictly smaller stochastic-gradient variance constant M_(g) < M in the SGD convergence bound, implying faster convergence with less data.

## Results

On the three NL-to-TL benchmarks (CW, GLTL, Navigation), GraFT achieves 99.90%, 99.80%, and 99.10% end-to-end translation accuracy with 2000 training examples, and 97.70%, 91.50%, and 85.00% with only 500 examples. The paper reports an average improvement of 5.49% in end-to-end translation accuracy and 14.06% in out-of-domain translation accuracy over state-of-the-art baselines. For AP grounding, BERT (MLM) reaches 99.99% accuracy on the Navigation dataset, substantially outperforming GPT-4 (87.28%) and GPT-4o-mini (83.97%) on the same task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraFT outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and data quantity. Against NL2TL (Chen et al., 2023), the closest competitor, GraFT leads by 4.70% on CW, 7.70% on GLTL, and 4.60% on Navi at 500 training examples, and by 1.70%, 2.40%, and 12.40% at 2000 examples (vs. the GPT-4o-mini grounding variant). Against NL2LTL (Fuggitti & Chakraborti, 2023) and NL2Spec (Cosler et al., 2023), which skip AP grounding entirely and rely on CLMs, GraFT's advantage is much larger: 18.8–27.8% on CW and 13.7–23.7% on GLTL. In out-of-domain evaluation (Table 4), GraFT trained on GLTL+Navi achieves 73.0% on Navi versus NL2TL's 55.3%, a 17.7-point gap, and GraFT trained on CW+Navi reaches 58.8% on CW versus NL2TL's 21.2%, demonstrating substantially better cross-domain generalization. For the AP grounding sub-task specifically, BERT (MLM) achieves 99.99% on Navi compared to GPT-4's 87.28% and GPT-4o-mini's 83.97%, confirming that the masked-language-model formulation is more suited to the token-labeling task than causal next-token prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NL2TL framework (Chen et al., 2023), which decomposed NL-to-TL translation into CLM-based AP grounding followed by Seq2Seq translation. GraFT departs from this lineage in two key ways: it replaces the causal language model for grounding with a masked language model (BERT) that assigns integer labels to tokens, and it introduces grammar-forced training and grammar-constrained decoding into the Seq2Seq translation step, restricting valid output tokens at each position to a small grammar-determined subset. The theoretical contribution formalizes why this restriction reduces cross-entropy and improves gradient alignment, extending the teacher-forcing paradigm with a grammar-aware variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The grammar-forcing principle—restricting the output vocabulary at each decoding step based on known syntactic structure—is a general technique that could transfer to other structured-output generation tasks beyond temporal logic (e.g., code generation, mathematical expression parsing). The finding that a small fine-tuned BERT outperforms GPT-4 for AP grounding challenges the default assumption that larger LLMs are necessary for extraction subtasks. However, the evaluation is limited to three small datasets (2K–11K examples), the grammar is specific to a fixed set of TL operators, and the paper does not explore more expressive logics (STL, LTL with past operators) or larger, more diverse domains, leaving open whether the approach scales to realistic industrial specification tasks.
