## Problem

Despite universal approximation guarantees for Deep Operator Networks (DONs) and Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs), no formal optimization convergence guarantees existed for training these neural operators with gradient descent. This paper closes that gap by establishing conditions under which GD provably decreases the empirical loss for both architectures, and shows that network width is a key factor enabling these guarantees.

## Input / Output

The input is a scalar function u sampled at R discrete locations (forming a vector in R^R for DONs, or R + d_x dimensions for FNOs after concatenating the evaluation point), and the output is the operator's value at a single evaluation point y (for DONs) or x (for FNOs). Unlike standard supervised learning between finite-dimensional vectors, the model learns a mapping between infinite-dimensional function spaces, with the empirical loss averaged over n input functions and their respective output evaluation grids. The analysis is restricted to scalar-valued inputs (d_u = 1), excluding vector-valued problems like Navier-Stokes.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified optimization framework requiring two conditions on the loss: restricted strong convexity (RSC) on a restricted set N_t and β-smoothness on a neighborhood B(θ_0), which together guarantee per-step loss reduction under GD with step-size η_t = ω_t/β. For DONs, the RSC analysis must handle cross-interaction Hessian terms between the branch and trunk networks, requiring a more complex restricted set Q_κ^t that couples both parameter blocks. For FNOs, the challenge is cross-derivatives between spatial-domain weights W and Fourier-domain weights R in the Hessian. In both cases, the RSC parameter α_t takes the form 2κ²‖∇G̅_t‖² − c/√m (or c(1/√m_f + 1/√m_g) for DONs), meaning wider networks lower the gradient-norm threshold needed for RSC to hold and enlarge the initialization neighborhood radius ρ where guarantees are valid. Empirical validation on antiderivative, diffusion-reaction, and Burgers' equation problems confirms that increasing width from 10 to 500 yields lower training losses and faster convergence for both architectures.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: proving that gradient descent on DONs and FNOs satisfies RSC and smoothness conditions, yielding guaranteed loss decrease when widths are sufficiently large. Empirically, increasing width from m=10 to m=500 consistently reduces training loss across all three operator learning problems (antiderivative, diffusion-reaction, Burgers' equation) for both DONs and FNOs over 80,000 epochs. For the linear antiderivative operator, wider DONs reach losses around 10⁻¹² and wider FNOs around 10⁻⁵. For the nonlinear Burgers' equation, the width benefit is more pronounced for FNOs than for DONs in terms of both final loss and convergence speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing neural operator architectures or optimization methods; its contribution is a theoretical convergence guarantee rather than a new model. The empirical section (Section 8) instead performs a width-scaling study, training DONs and FNOs at widths m ∈ {10, 50, 100, 200, 500} on three canonical PDE problems and reporting training loss curves. The key empirical finding is that wider networks consistently achieve lower final losses and faster convergence, supporting the theoretical prediction that over-parameterization relaxes the RSC condition (making α_t > 0 easier to satisfy) and enlarges the neighborhood around initialization where guarantees hold. No head-to-head comparison between DONs and FNOs is drawn, as the authors explicitly state the experiments are not intended to compare the two architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the restricted strong convexity (RSC) optimization framework of Banerjee et al. (2023a) and Cisneros-Velarde et al. (2025) from standard feedforward networks to two major neural operator architectures: Deep Operator Networks (DONs) and Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs). While the NTK-based approach (Jacot et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2021a) has been the dominant paradigm for optimization analysis of neural networks, this work deliberately uses the Hessian-based RSC route, which is necessary because the cross-interaction terms in DONs (inner product of two networks) and the Fourier-domain cross-derivatives in FNOs make NTK-style first-order analysis intractable. The paper unifies both architectures under a single two-condition framework (RSC + smoothness), showing that despite their substantial architectural differences, both satisfy these conditions when sufficiently wide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal optimization convergence guarantee for training DONs and FNOs with gradient descent, filling a notable gap given that universal approximation results for both architectures already existed. The explicit width dependence in the RSC parameter (α_t) and the neighborhood radius provides concrete theoretical justification for the over-parameterization practices common in neural operator training. Limitations include the restriction to scalar input functions, the requirement that iterates remain in a fixed neighborhood of initialization, and the probabilistic nature of the guarantees (holding with probability 1 - O(1/m)). Open questions include extending the analysis to vector-valued operators (e.g., Navier-Stokes), other neural operator families (GNOs, attention-based operators), and whether the RSC framework can yield tighter or deterministic guarantees.
