## Problem

Existing knowledge editing and unlearning methods, particularly those relying on output-tracing localizations (Causal Tracing, Attribution Patching), produce edits that are fragile to prompt reformatting, vulnerable to adversarial relearning, and leave latent knowledge intact in intermediate representations. The paper identifies a mechanistic explanation for this fragility: output-tracing methods preferentially target attribute extraction components (which read facts into output logits) rather than the fact lookup mechanism (where knowledge is sourced into the latent stream), so the underlying knowledge remains accessible through alternative extraction paths.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Gemma-7B, Gemma-2-9B, or Llama-3-8B), a forget set of facts to edit or unlearn, a retain set of facts to preserve, and a per-dataset component localization map identifying which MLP layers implement the fact lookup mechanism. The localization is derived differently per dataset: via logistic regression probes on intermediate activations for Sports Facts, or via two rounds of path patching (first identifying extraction components, then finding upstream MLP sources) for CounterFact. The output is a modified LLM where only FLU-localized MLP weights are updated, with the number of trainable parameters matched across all localization methods to ensure fair comparison.

## Method

The core insight is that robust editing should target the fact lookup (FLU) mechanism—specific MLP layers that enrich latent representations with subject attributes—rather than the attribute extraction mechanism that decodes those attributes into output logits. For Sports Facts, FLU layers are identified by training logistic regression probes on intermediate activations and selecting layers where probe accuracy rapidly increases (layers 2–7 for Gemma-7B, 2–8 for Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3-8B). For CounterFact, path patching first identifies extraction components with high direct logit-diff impact, then a second round patches paths between MLPs and those extraction components to find upstream MLPs that supply factual information. The editing procedure is localized fine-tuning: gradient descent on a combined loss (injection/forget cross-entropy + retain cross-entropy + Pile SFT loss) restricted exclusively to FLU-localized MLP parameters. This approach is validated through MCQ prompting, adversarial relearning, latent knowledge probing, and weight masking analyses, demonstrating that FLU edits generalize across prompt formats, resist relearning (only ~20% recovery vs. ~63% for OT methods), and actually perturb internal latent representations rather than merely masking output retrieval.

## Results

On the Sports-Unlearning task (Table 1), FLU localization achieves an MCQ forget accuracy of 0.110 (i.e., 89% forget error), compared to 0.767 for Attribution Patching and 0.849 for Causal Tracing, while maintaining MMLU accuracy of 0.613. In adversarial relearning on Sports-Athlete-Editing, OT methods (Causal Tracing, Causal Tracing MLPs) recover up to 63% of forgotten information, whereas FLU localization recovers only ~20%. At scale (editing up to 1000 CounterFact facts on Gemma-7B), FLU is the only localization that preserves MCQ edit accuracy above random chance, while baselines fall to chance level. FLU also exceeds other methods by more than 40% in MCQ Edit Accuracy on Sports-Athlete-Editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FLU (Fact Lookup) mechanistic localization against two Output-Tracing (OT) baselines: Causal Tracing (Meng et al., 2023) and Attribution Patching (Nanda, 2023). FLU decisively outperforms both OT methods on the key robustness metric (MCQ accuracy): in Sports-Unlearning, FLU achieves 0.110 MCQ forget accuracy versus 0.849 (Causal Tracing) and 0.767 (Attribution Patching), a gap of over 65–74 percentage points. In adversarial relearning, OT methods allow recovery of up to 63% of forgotten information, while FLU limits recovery to ~20%. On CounterFact-Editing, FLU is the only localization that maintains meaningful MCQ edit accuracy, while Causal Tracing and Attribution Patching fall to near-random performance. The paper attributes this gap to a mechanistic difference: OT methods preferentially modify attribute-extraction components (later-layer MLPs and attention heads) that format the output, leaving the underlying latent knowledge intact, whereas FLU targets the earlier-layer MLPs that enrich the residual stream with factual attributes, thereby removing the knowledge at its source.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the factual recall interpretability literature (Nanda et al., 2023; Geva et al., 2023) that decomposed factual recall into token concatenation, fact lookup, and attribute extraction stages, and on the localized fine-tuning paradigm from Lee et al. (2023) and Panigrahi et al. (2023). Its key conceptual departure is distinguishing between Output-Tracing (OT) localizations (causal tracing, attribution patching) that measure causal effects on final outputs, and mechanistic localizations targeting intermediate components where knowledge enters the latent stream. The paper confirms and extends Hase et al. (2023)'s skepticism about OT for editing by showing *why* OT fails: it preferentially targets the attribute extraction mechanism rather than the fact lookup mechanism, leaving latent knowledge intact and vulnerable to re-extraction or relearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, mechanistically grounded recipe for where to edit or unlearn in LLMs, with the practical implication that the 'most causally important for output' components are not the right place to edit for robustness. A key limitation is that FLU localization remains partially manual (probe-based for Sports Facts, path-patching-based for CounterFact), and the approach is validated only on factual recall tasks rather than broader capabilities. Open questions include whether fully automated FLU discovery is feasible at scale, whether the lookup-vs-extraction distinction generalizes to non-factual knowledge (procedural, relational), and whether the robustness gap persists under stronger adversarial attacks beyond the MCQ and relearning evaluations tested here.
