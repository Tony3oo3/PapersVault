## Problem

Existing time series forecasting methods augment models with either text (providing semantic context but lacking fine-grained temporal detail) or vision (capturing spatial-temporal patterns but lacking semantic interpretability), yet no prior work has unified both modalities with time series. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first framework that leverages pre-trained Vision-Language Models to jointly exploit temporal, visual, and textual modalities, particularly targeting data-scarce few-shot and zero-shot forecasting scenarios where single-modality approaches struggle to generalize.

## Input / Output

The method takes only raw multivariate time series as input (no external text or images required) and internally generates both a 64×64 three-channel image representation and structured textual prompts (statistics, periodicity descriptions, domain context) from the same sequence. Outputs are forecasts over horizons of 96–720 steps for long-term tasks and 6–48 steps for short-term M4 tasks, with output dimensionality matching the number of input variables (7 for ETT, 21 for Weather, 321 for Electricity, 862 for Traffic). The frozen VLM backbone (ViLT by default, 128.9M params) produces 156-token multimodal embeddings of dimension 768 that are projected into the model's 128-dim space for fusion.

## Method

Time-VLM comprises three trainable modules that feed a frozen VLM: (1) a Retrieval-Augmented Learner that extracts patch embeddings (patch length 16, stride 8) and refines them via a hierarchical memory bank combining local top-k cosine-similarity retrieval through an MLP with global multi-head self-attention, fused by a learnable gate; (2) a Vision-Augmented Learner that converts time series into three-channel images by concatenating FFT frequency features and sine/cosine periodicity encodings to the raw signal, then applying multi-scale 1D and 2D convolutions followed by bilinear interpolation to 64×64; (3) a Text-Augmented Learner that assembles structured prompts from extracted statistics (min/max, median, trend) and domain-specific descriptions. The VLM's vision and text encoders produce multimodal embeddings that are fused with temporal features via cross-modal multi-head attention (temporal as query, multimodal as key/value) followed by a sigmoid-gated residual fusion, and a final linear predictor generates forecasts. Only the RAL, VAL, and prediction head are trained end-to-end with MSE loss; the VLM remains frozen, keeping total parameters at 143.6M versus Time-LLM's 3405M.

## Results

On the 5% few-shot long-term forecasting benchmark (averaged over horizons 96–720), Time-VLM (143M params) achieves the best MSE on 5 of 7 datasets: ETTh1 (0.442), ETTh2 (0.354), ETTm1 (0.364), ETTm2 (0.262), and Weather (0.240), reducing MSE by up to 29.5% over the second-best method (Time-LLM, 3405M params). On the M4 short-term benchmark, Time-VLM attains the best SMAPE (11.894), MASE (1.592), and OWA (0.855). In zero-shot cross-domain transfer (e.g., ETTh1→ETTh2), Time-VLM achieves MSE 0.338 vs. Time-LLM's 0.353, while using only 1/24th the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 5% few-shot setting, Time-VLM decisively outperforms Time-LLM (3405M) on ETTh1 (MSE 0.442 vs. 0.627, −29.5%), ETTm1 (0.364 vs. 0.425, −14.4%), and Weather (0.240 vs. 0.260, −7.7%), while using only 1/24th the parameters. It also beats GPT4TS, DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, and FEDformer on all ETT and Weather datasets. However, Time-VLM loses to DLinear on ECL (MSE 0.218 vs. 0.176) and to PatchTST on Traffic (0.558 vs. 0.418), where the high dimensionality (321 and 862 variables) challenges the multimodal fusion. In zero-shot cross-domain transfer, Time-VLM matches or beats Time-LLM on 6 of 8 ETT transfer pairs (e.g., ETTh1→ETTh2: 0.338 vs. 0.353 MSE) and substantially outperforms LLMTime (0.992 MSE). On the full-data long-term benchmark, Time-VLM is competitive but slightly trails Time-LLM on Weather (MSE 0.224 vs. 0.225) and DLinear on ECL (0.172 vs. 0.166), confirming that the multimodal advantage is most pronounced under data scarcity. On M4 short-term forecasting, Time-VLM achieves the best SMAPE (11.894), MASE (1.592), and OWA (0.855), narrowly edging out Time-LLM (11.983 / 1.595 / 0.859).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to unify temporal, visual, and textual modalities through a pre-trained VLM for time series forecasting. It repurposes three existing threads—patch-based temporal processing (à la PatchTST), time-series-to-image transformation (à la TimesNet/VisionTS), and LLM-based textual augmentation (à la Time-LLM/GPT4TS)—and bridges them via a frozen VLM backbone (ViLT/CLIP/BLIP-2) that was previously unexplored for time series. The retrieval-augmented memory bank and cross-modal multi-head attention with gated fusion are novel architectural choices that have no direct precedent in the time series literature, though each component draws on established mechanisms from NLP and vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Time-VLM demonstrates that a 143M-parameter model can match or exceed a 3405M-parameter LLM-based forecaster (Time-LLM) in few-shot and zero-shot settings while being 20× more memory-efficient, suggesting that pre-trained cross-modal alignment is a more efficient inductive bias than raw language scale for time series. However, the ablation revealing that the text-augmented learner contributes only ~2% MSE improvement, and the modest full-data gains (sometimes slightly negative on Weather/ECL/Traffic), indicate that the VLM's textual pathway is underutilized and that domain-specific VLMs or stronger temporal-semantic alignment remain open problems. The framework's reliance on VLMs trained on natural image-text pairs (MSCOCO) rather than time series data raises questions about how much of the performance gain comes from genuine cross-modal reasoning versus generic visual pattern recognition.
