## Problem

Standard single-cell VAEs with negative binomial decoders do not enforce Euclidean geometry in their latent space, so linear latent interpolations used by downstream trajectory inference tools (e.g., OT-CFM) do not correspond to geodesic paths on the decoded statistical manifold. Existing flattening regularisations target only continuous-data Gaussian decoders, while geometric autoencoders for single-cell data either lack a discrete likelihood or require expensive pairwise distance computations, leaving a gap for principled latent geometry regularisation in discrete-likelihood VAEs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw single-cell RNA-seq count matrix (N×G) along with gene-specific inverse dispersion parameters θ_g treated as free parameters independent of cell state, and cell-specific size factors l derived from total counts. The encoder produces d-dimensional latent codes (d≪G) via a reparameterised Gaussian posterior, while the decoder outputs cell-specific NB means μ = l·softmax(ρ_φ(z)) parameterising the negative binomial likelihood. Downstream, the Euclidean latent embeddings feed into OT-CFM to produce a time-resolved velocity field for trajectory reconstruction, and linear latent interpolations can be decoded to recover intermediate gene expression profiles.

## Method

FlatVI introduces a flattening loss L_flat = E_{q_ψ(z|x)}‖M(z) − αI_d‖_F² that pushes the pullback metric of the stochastic decoder towards a spatially-uniform scaled identity, where α is a trainable scale parameter and λ weights the loss against the standard ELBO. The key theoretical contribution is an explicit closed-form pullback metric for the negative binomial case: M(z) = Σ_g [θ_g / (h_g(z)(h_g(z)+θ_g))] ∇_z h_g(z) ⊗ ∇_z h_g(z), derived from the Fisher information M(μ) = θ/(μ(μ+θ)) of the univariate NB distribution. This makes the regularisation tractable without pairwise distance computations, relying only on the decoder Jacobian (computed via JVP) and decoded means. The approach is grounded in two assumptions—geodesic convexity of the NB statistical manifold and local-to-global approximation under dense sampling—that justify enforcing local Euclidean geometry to recover global geodesic structure. When M(z) ≈ αI_d, straight lines in latent space become geodesics on the decoded manifold, making linear interpolations biologically meaningful for trajectory inference.

## Results

On the EB dataset, FlatVI achieves a latent 2-Wasserstein distance of 1.54±0.09 and decoded 2-Wasserstein of 41.99±0.04 for held-out time-point reconstruction via OT-CFM, outperforming NB-VAE (2.07 / 43.36) and GAE (2.16 / 70.29). On the larger MEF reprogramming dataset (165,892 cells, 39 time points), FlatVI reduces the latent 2-Wasserstein to 1.64±0.13 and decoded 2-Wasserstein to 97.12±0.01, again beating both baselines. On the Pancreas dataset, FlatVI's latent space enables CellRank to recover all 6 known terminal states, versus 5 for NB-VAE and 4 for GAE. On simulated data, increasing the flattening strength to λ=10 raises the 3-NN overlap between Euclidean and pullback-geodesic neighbourhoods from 0.66 (unregularised) to 0.80 while preserving parameter reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlatVI consistently outperforms the standard NB-VAE (Lopez et al., 2018) across all trajectory reconstruction metrics on both EB and MEF: on EB it reduces the latent 2-Wasserstein by 26% (1.54 vs 2.07) and the decoded 2-Wasserstein by 3% (41.99 vs 43.36); on MEF the gains are 21% latent (1.64 vs 2.07) and 6% decoded (97.12 vs 103.29). Against the Geodesic Autoencoder (Huguet et al., 2022), FlatVI shows larger margins, particularly in decoded space where GAE's continuous MSE-based decoder struggles with count data: on EB the decoded 2-Wasserstein drops from 70.29 to 41.99 (40% reduction), and on MEF from 106.83 to 97.12. For the Pancreas terminal-state task, FlatVI recovers all 6 biologically known endpoints via CellRank, while GAE and NB-VAE capture only 4 and 5 respectively, and FlatVI also yields higher velocity consistency across latent dimensions. In the qualitative decoded-interpolation comparison against GAGA (Sun et al., 2025), FlatVI's simple linear latent paths produce marker-gene trajectories that faithfully track expected developmental dynamics, whereas GAGA's neural-ODE interpolations exhibit recurrent failures including unstable optimisation, underestimation of peak marker expression, and overestimation of intermediate states.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlatVI sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the NB-VAE architecture for single-cell count data (Lopez et al., 2018), the FIM-based pullback metric for stochastic decoders (Arvanitidis et al., 2022), and the flattening loss that regularises latent geometry toward Euclidean (Chen et al., 2020). The key departure is that Chen et al. only applies to continuous-data AEs, while Arvanitidis et al. characterises the latent geometry of discrete-likelihood VAEs without imposing any regularisation toward a simple geometry. FlatVI bridges this gap by explicitly regularising the FIM-based pullback metric of a negative-binomial VAE toward a scaled identity, extending the flattening principle to discrete count data for the first time. It also contrasts with data-driven geometric regularisers like GAE (Huguet et al., 2022) and GAGA (Sun et al., 2025), which rely on pairwise distance computations in observation space; FlatVI instead leverages the analytic FIM of the decoder's likelihood, avoiding high-dimensional distance calculations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Euclidean latent geometry assumption is pervasive in single-cell trajectory inference pipelines (OT-CFM, CellRank, dynamic OT), yet standard VAEs provide no guarantee that straight latent lines correspond to geodesics on the decoded statistical manifold. FlatVI closes this gap with a theoretically grounded regularisation that is cheap to compute (Jacobian-vector products only) and compatible with existing downstream tools, making it a practical drop-in improvement for the single-cell community. However, the approach assumes geodesic convexity, which fails for cyclic processes like the cell cycle, and the flattening-reconstruction trade-off requires careful hyperparameter tuning per dataset. Open questions include extending the framework to other statistical manifolds (Poisson for chromatin accessibility), batch-corrected settings, and whether the local-to-global approximation holds under sparse sampling.
