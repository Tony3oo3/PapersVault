## Problem

Large-batch training of language models with AdamW causes a sharp increase in max attention logit, leading to one-hot attention distributions and poor generalization. While LAMB partially mitigates this via its weight-wise trust ratio, its l2-norm formulation is ineffective at constraining extreme individual values in query/key weight matrices, and its coarse weight-wise granularity ignores the strong row/column similarity structure present in trained weights, causing training instability in mid-depth attention layers.

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes standard per-parameter weight matrices (W ∈ R^{n×n}), their corresponding Adam-style update vectors (u = m/√(v+ε)), and a scalar learning rate at each step. It produces updated weight matrices where each element's update is scaled by an element-wise trust ratio (the maximum of its row-wise max-norm ratio, column-wise max-norm ratio, and a global weight-wise max-norm lower bound) and then clipped so that no single element's update magnitude exceeds 1. The computation adds only ~1% overhead over LAMB's l2-norm trust ratio.

## Method

MERIT replaces LAMB's l2-norm trust ratio with a max-norm (largest absolute value) ratio, which directly bounds the extreme values in query/key weight matrices and thereby constrains the upper bound of max attention logit (proven as O(√d · M_Q · M_K · C_X²)). It further introduces element-wise trust ratios: for each weight element (i,j), the ratio is max{row_i max-norm ratio, col_j max-norm ratio, weight-wise max-norm ratio}, leveraging the observed row/column similarity in transformer weights to provide local, structure-aware scaling. An element-wise clipping step (inspired by SignSGD) caps the maximum update magnitude at 1 across all dimensions to prevent outlier-driven instability. A convergence guarantee is established for the weight-wise variant (MERIT-W) in nonconvex settings, showing O(1/√T) gradient-norm convergence with β₂=0 and a sublinear rate with β₂>0.

## Results

On OpenWebText validation loss, MERIT achieves 3.280 (GPT-2 Small, 1K batch), 2.982 (GPT-2 Medium, 4K batch), and 2.897 (GPT-2 Large, 8K batch), consistently outperforming LAMB (3.355/3.068/2.971) and AdamW (3.470/3.172/3.039). The performance gap widens with model scale: MERIT's advantage over LAMB grows from 0.075 on 125M to 0.074 on 770M parameters. In the 48B-token setting, MERIT enables a 6K batch size for GPT-2 Medium that matches or exceeds the standard 480-batch AdamW baseline on zero-shot SuperGLUE (46.70 vs. 46.59 average accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERIT outperforms all five baselines across all three GPT-2 model sizes on validation loss. Against AdamW, MERIT reduces validation loss by 0.190 (Small), 0.190 (Medium), and 0.142 (Large), with the gap remaining substantial even at 770M where AdamW and LAMB converge. Against LAMB, MERIT improves by 0.075 (Small), 0.086 (Medium), and 0.074 (Large), confirming that the max-norm and element-wise ratio mechanisms address LAMB's inability to control mid-layer attention logits. Against Lion and Sophia-G, MERIT also achieves lower validation loss on all model sizes (Figure 4). In the 48B-token zero-shot evaluation (Table 1), MERIT at 6K batch for GPT-2 Medium (46.70 avg) slightly exceeds AdamW at 480 batch (46.59 avg), demonstrating that the larger batch introduces no generalization penalty. QK-Norm, while enabling higher learning rates for AdamW, actually degrades large-batch training performance compared to MERIT (Figure 8), as it restricts information flow in attention layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERIT is a direct modification of LAMB's trust ratio mechanism, replacing the l2-norm with max-norm to more effectively constrain the maximum attention logit in query/key weight matrices, and adding an element-wise ratio that exploits row/column weight similarities observed in transformer attention layers. The element-wise clipping mechanism draws from SignSGD's update strategy. The paper positions itself at the intersection of large-batch optimization (LARS, LAMB) and attention logit stability (QK-Norm, σ-Reparam), arguing that prior work addressed these concerns separately while MERIT unifies them through a single trust ratio design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MERIT demonstrates that a 6K batch size can match the generalization of a 480 batch size for GPT-2 Medium with 48B tokens, a practically significant result for training throughput. However, the evaluation is limited to models up to 770M parameters and two architectures (GPT-2, Llama), leaving open whether the max-norm and element-wise ratio benefits persist at the 7B+ scale where attention logit dynamics may differ. Cross-domain applicability (vision, multimodal, RL) remains untested, and the convergence proof only covers the weight-wise variant (MERIT-W) under simplified assumptions (β1=0, λ=0), so the theoretical guarantees for the full algorithm are incomplete.
