## Problem

LLM unlearning methods face a fundamental trade-off: aggressive removal of targeted knowledge (e.g., via gradient ascent) severely degrades general model performance, while regularization-based retention strategies weaken unlearning efficacy. Existing approaches can only shift this trade-off along a Pareto frontier, not break it, leaving practitioners unable to simultaneously achieve strong removal and reliable retention.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained LLM parameters (LLaMA-2-7B, Phi-1.5, Zephyr-7B, ICLM-7B) and requires an unlearning dataset of targeted sequences plus a retain dataset of non-targeted sequences from which mini-batch gradients are computed at each step. The retain gradients are tracked via exponential moving average to stabilize the projection direction without requiring large batch sizes. In the TRU variant, no external retain data is needed; instead, each unlearning sample uses the remaining unlearning set as its internal retention reference for task-vector rectification.

## Method

GRU replaces the standard gradient update during unlearning with a constrained projection: at each step, the unlearning gradient is re-projected so its inner product with the (EMA-smoothed) retain gradient is non-negative, ensuring the update does not increase the retain loss under a first-order approximation. This yields a closed-form solution that removes only the negatively-aligned component of the unlearning gradient along the retain direction, leaving the orthogonal (retention-safe) component intact. Gradient clipping further bounds the rectified gradient norm to prevent stochastic overshoot. The TRU extension applies the same rectification principle to task vectors in a retain-data-free setting, where each sample's task vector is projected against the gradient of the remaining unlearning set before normalized aggregation across all targeted samples.

## Results

On TOFU (LLaMA-2-7B, 5% unlearning), GRU dramatically improves GA from FQ −16.93 / MU 0.00 to FQ −3.52 / MU 0.519, and NPO from FQ −10.91 / MU 0.492 to FQ −9.96 / MU 0.541. On WMDP (Zephyr-7B), NPO+GD with GRU raises MMLU retention from 0.472 to 0.503 while reducing Bio unlearning accuracy from 0.271 to 0.264. On MUSE (ICLM-7B), NPO+GD with GRU lifts KnowMem-R retention from 23.56 to 30.75 while maintaining KnowMem-U at 0.00. Across all benchmarks and backbones, GRU yields uniform improvements in both removal and retention metrics for every baseline method tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRU consistently outperforms all six baselines on both removal and retention metrics. Against GA (Yao et al., 2023), the improvement is most dramatic: on LLaMA-2-7B 5% TOFU, MU jumps from 0.00 to 0.519 and FQ from −16.93 to −3.52, essentially rescuing a method that otherwise destroys model utility. Against WGA (Wang et al., 2024b), GRU lifts MU from 0.445 to 0.570 (LLaMA 5%) and FQ from −7.75 to −5.10. Against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), gains are more modest but consistent: MU improves from 0.492 to 0.541 (LLaMA 5%) and FQ from −10.91 to −9.96; on WMDP, NPO+GD with GRU achieves MMLU 0.503 vs. 0.472 without. In the retain-data-free setting, TRU substantially outperforms the Task Vector baseline (Ilharco et al., 2022): on LLaMA 5% TOFU, FQ is −7.34 vs. −22.13 and MU is 0.53 vs. 0.00. Against SimNPO (Fan et al., 2024), GRU improves MU from 0.553 to 0.617 (LLaMA 10%). Finally, against RMU (Li et al., 2024) on WMDP, GRU reduces Cyber accuracy from 0.31 to 0.28 and raises MMLU from 0.41 to 0.44.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRU extends the gradient projection lineage from continual learning (GEM, Lopez-Paz & Ranzato 2017) and multi-task learning (PCGrad, Yu et al. 2020) to the specific problem of LLM unlearning, where the two competing objectives are removal of targeted knowledge and retention of general capability. Unlike prior unlearning methods (GA, NPO, GD, WGA) that attempt to balance the trade-off through loss weighting or reweighting, GRU reframes the problem geometrically: it projects unlearning gradients onto the half-space orthogonal to retention gradients, thereby decoupling the two objectives at the update level. The TRU extension further repurposes task vectors (Ilharco et al., 2022) in a novel 'rectified task vector' formulation that uses the unlearning set itself as an internal retention reference, addressing the retain-data-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unlearning-retention trade-off is a fundamental bottleneck for practical LLM deployment: compromising removal risks privacy breaches, while compromising retention destroys commercial utility. GRU's plug-and-play design (applicable to any objective-based unlearning loss) and its theoretical guarantees (convergence preservation + retention non-degradation) make it a practical tool for practitioners. However, the method critically depends on the quality and representativeness of retain data, and the TRU extension only partially addresses distributional bias. A key open question the authors identify is whether reliable unlearning can be achieved without any retain data or surrogate, which remains unresolved.
