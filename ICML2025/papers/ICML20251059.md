## Problem

Existing block-wise quantization methods for LLMs focus on 'how to scale' activations (e.g., power-of-two shared exponents in MX formats) but fail to capture the nuanced magnitude distributions within individual blocks, leading to wasted or underestimated representable ranges. This is particularly problematic for activation quantization, which requires real-time processing, handles wide dynamic ranges, and must remain compatible with low-precision hardware arithmetic to achieve genuine energy savings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM weights (pre-quantized offline) and activations (quantized online during inference), partitioning each into fine-grained blocks of size 16–64 along multiplication dimensions (sub-token for activations, sub-channel for weights). Each data element is stored as 4 bits (1-bit sign + 3-bit index into the selected dialect's representable values), with an additional 4-bit dialect identifier per block. The quantized operands are dequantized to 5-bit unsigned integers (0.5 × [0–15]) for MAC operations, and partial sums are requantized back to 4-bit DialectFP4 between successive matrix multiplications in the full-path (A·W and A·A) setting.

## Method

BlockDialect introduces DialectFP4, a formatbook of 16 FP4 variants (dialects) that share the same 0.5 granularity and six smallest representable values but differ in their large-magnitude values, enabling each fine-grained block to be assigned the format that best matches its specific distribution. A two-stage online selection process replaces costly MSE computation: the first stage narrows to a pair of dialects whose maximum magnitude matches the block's maximum, and the second stage selects the better dialect by counting elements within its 'beneficial range' using pre-designed logical operations on 5-bit fixed-point intermediates. Because all representable values are integer multiples of 0.5, multiplications reduce to 4-bit unsigned integer arithmetic with a 2-bit right shift, yielding MAC units with area and power comparable to native FP4 while outperforming MXFP4 by 10.78% (LLaMA3-8B) in zero-shot accuracy at lower effective bitwidth.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B, BlockDialect-32 achieves 72.24% average zero-shot accuracy (linear scope) and 68.57% (full-path scope), with WikiText2 perplexity of 7.05 and 7.87 respectively, at an effective bitwidth of 4.28 bits. For full-path quantization, BlockDialect-32 is only 5.45% below full precision (74.45%) on LLaMA3-8B and 2.69% below on LLaMA2-7B (70.94%), while using fewer bits per element than MXFP4 (4.28 vs. 4.31). In the linear scope, the accuracy drop versus FP16 is just 1.76% (LLaMA3-8B) and 1.20% (LLaMA2-7B) with the mixed block-size configuration (64, dn:16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MXFP4 (the hardware-supported microscaling format), BlockDialect-32 achieves a 10.78% (LLaMA3-8B) and 7.48% (LLaMA2-7B) zero-shot accuracy gain in the full-path scope while using a lower effective bitwidth (4.28 vs. 4.31 bits). In the linear scope, BlockDialect-32 outperforms MXFP4-32 by 3.93 points on LLaMA3-8B (72.24% vs. 68.31%) and 3.80 points on LLaMA2-7B (69.74% vs. 65.94%). Against QuaRot (rotation-matrix-based W4A4KV4), BlockDialect-32 leads by 2.56 points on LLaMA3-8B full-path (68.57% vs. 66.01%) and by 0.18 points on Mistral-7B (72.15% vs. 71.72%), despite QuaRot retaining query and attention scores in FP16. LLM-FP4 (matrix-wise mixed format) performs substantially worse, with 41.92% average accuracy on LLaMA3-8B linear scope versus BlockDialect's 72.24%, a gap of over 30 points. The two-stage dialect selection approach (vs. exact MSE) incurs only ~0.61% accuracy drop in the linear scope and ~0.63% in the full-path scope, confirming practical viability for online activation quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Microscaling (MX) format's hardware-supported power-of-two shared exponent and fine-grained block structure, but shifts the design focus from 'how to scale' (the dominant paradigm in SmoothQuant, QuaRot, and MX itself) to 'how to represent' each block by assigning an optimal number format from a formatbook. Conceptually, it refines the mixed-format quantization line (LLM-FP4's matrix-wise format selection, MoFQ's layer-wise FP/INT choice) to a much finer per-block granularity, using closely related FP4 variants ('dialects') rather than entirely distinct formats. The requirement that representable values be scaled integers (multiples of 0.5) distinguishes DialectFP4 from lookup-based formats like NF4 and SF4, which achieve better distributional fit but require high-precision dequantization and arithmetic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling full-path 4-bit matrix multiplication (including attention's activation-activation products) with only ~5% accuracy loss versus full precision, a regime where MXFP4 degrades by over 16%. As hardware support for fine-grained block-wise quantization becomes standard (NVIDIA Blackwell, OCP MX spec), the per-block format selection strategy and the two-stage online dialect selection offer a concrete path to energy-efficient inference without sacrificing the integer-arithmetic efficiency that makes low-precision MACs attractive. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (evaluation is limited to 7–8B parameters), how it interacts with emerging rotation-based or distribution-flattening techniques (the SmoothQuant combination shows only marginal gains, suggesting non-trivial coupling), and whether the KV cache chunking strategy for sub-channel-wise value quantization remains practical at very long sequence lengths.
