## Problem

Most continual federated learning (CFL) methods rely on data rehearsal (caching or generating samples), which is memory-intensive for edge devices and raises privacy concerns. While regularization-based alternatives like Synaptic Intelligence (SI) avoid rehearsal, they fail under the heterogeneous (Non-IID) data distributions typical of federated settings because their importance scores are computed solely from local data, misaligning local and global optimization objectives.

## Input / Output

Each client receives streaming image tasks sequentially (RGB 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, RGB 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, grayscale 28×28 for Digit10, RGB 300×300 for Office31/Office-Caltech10) partitioned via Dirichlet distribution to induce heterogeneity. The method outputs a single classification model that predicts class labels across all previously seen tasks without requiring task IDs at inference. The global model parameters serve as an anchor for the personalized surrogate model, and no samples are cached or replayed at any point.

## Method

FedSSI introduces a Personalized Surrogate Model (PSM) trained locally before each new task arrives, using a proximal update rule v_k ← v_k − η(∇L + q(λ)(v_k − w_global)) where the hyperparameter λ controls the balance between local data fit and alignment with the global model (analogous to a momentum term). Parameter importance scores are computed from the PSM's training trajectory rather than the local model alone, so the resulting SI surrogate loss reflects both local and global data distributions. The target classification model is then trained on the new task with a combined loss of cross-entropy plus the SI penalty weighted by these synergistic importance scores. The PSM training costs only ~1/40 of the main training cost per task and requires no additional communication rounds, and the paper provides convergence guarantees showing the PSM converges at a rate proportional to the global model's convergence rate.

## Results

FedSSI achieves the best final accuracy A(f) and average accuracy Ā across all six datasets in both Class-IL and Domain-IL scenarios. On CIFAR-10 (Class-IL, α=0.1), FedSSI reaches 42.58% A(f) and 62.65% Ā, outperforming the strongest baseline FOT (40.18% / 61.41%) by 2.40% and 1.24% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet, the improvement over FOT is larger at 40.56% vs. 37.23% A(f) (+3.33%). The paper reports up to 12.47% improvement in final accuracy over baselines under the most heterogeneous data settings (α=0.1), with FedSSI maintaining stable performance as data heterogeneity varies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSSI outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and both metrics (A(f) and Ā). The strongest competitor on Class-IL datasets is FOT (orthogonal projection), which FedSSI surpasses by 2.40% on CIFAR-10, 1.81% on CIFAR-100, and 3.33% on Tiny-ImageNet in final accuracy. On Domain-IL datasets, FL+SI is the closest baseline, with FedSSI improving by 2.30% on Digit10 (72.09% vs. 69.79%) and 2.18% on Office31 (55.28% vs. 53.10%). Rehearsal-based methods (Re-Fed, FedCIL, GLFC) consistently underperform FedSSI, confirming that caching samples with a limited 300-sample buffer is insufficient under high heterogeneity. Notably, FL+SI—which FedSSI directly builds upon—suffers the largest performance drop under Non-IID data (e.g., 51.82% on Office-Caltech-10 vs. 62.57% for FedSSI), validating the paper's core motivation that standard SI fails without the personalized surrogate model. FedSSI also requires fewer communication rounds than most baselines (e.g., 304 vs. 319 for Re-Fed on CIFAR-10) while achieving higher accuracy, yielding a favorable accuracy-to-communication trade-off (Δ up to 44.70% on CIFAR-100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Synaptic Intelligence (SI) from centralized continual learning into the federated setting, building on the FedAvg/FedProx aggregation framework. It departs from the dominant CFL paradigm of data rehearsal (caching, generative replay, knowledge distillation) by adopting a purely regularization-based approach, and further departs from naive FL+SI by introducing a Personalized Surrogate Model (PSM) that injects global model information into the local importance computation via a proximal term. The PSM concept draws on federated optimization literature (Hanzely & Richtárik, 2020) and the Re-Fed framework's use of personalized informative models, but repurposes it specifically to compute parameter importance scores rather than to select replay samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedSSI addresses a practical tension in CFL: rehearsal-based methods that achieve good accuracy conflict with the storage and privacy constraints of edge devices, while non-rehearsal methods (FOT, FedWeIT) require task IDs at inference or introduce architectural overhead. The PSM adds only ~2.5% training cost per task and no communication overhead, making it deployable on resource-constrained clients. Key open questions include how to automatically select the hyperparameter λ without knowing the data heterogeneity level α in advance, whether the approach extends to non-convex or multi-modal settings, and whether the method generalizes beyond ResNet18 backbones and image classification to other modalities or larger-scale federated deployments.
