## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization algorithms either require strong convexity or uniqueness of the lower-level (LL) optimal solution, or rely on penalty methods that converge to stationary points rather than KKT points and exhibit slower empirical convergence. No first-order stochastic method had previously achieved approximate KKT points for bilevel problems where both the upper-level and LL objectives are nonconvex or weakly convex, leaving a gap in both theory and practical performance for real-world machine learning formulations.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic gradient estimates computed from constant-size mini-batches (batch size 1 or a fixed constant) of the UL and LL losses, along with hyperparameters controlling the Moreau envelope smoothing (γ), proximal strength (p), constraint tolerance (δ), and seven step-sizes (α, β, τ, ω, η, μ, θ). It outputs a triple (x, y, λ) satisfying three KKT conditions simultaneously: stationarity of the Lagrangian, constraint violation bounded by ε, and complementarity (slackness) bounded by ε, with the dual variable λ dynamically adjusting the relative weight of the LL constraint at each iteration.

## Method

TSP reformulates the bilevel problem via the Moreau envelope of the LL loss, converting it into a single-level constrained problem, then constructs a two-sided smoothed Lagrangian K(x, y, x̂, ŷ; λ) that adds proximal quadratic terms to both the UL and LL sides, ensuring strong convexity in each subproblem. The algorithm is a single-loop primal-dual scheme where the dual variable λ is updated via a two-stage moving average (an auxiliary variable h with dampening θ, followed by a projected gradient step and a second averaging with μ) to control stochastic noise, while primal variables x, y, and the Moreau envelope surrogate z are updated by SGD with smoothing factors ω on the reference points x̂, ŷ. This two-sided smoothing is the key structural difference from prior penalty-based or one-sided smoothed methods: it simultaneously stabilizes both the UL minimization and the LL constraint enforcement, enabling a high-probability descent lemma that bounds the dual variable and yields an O(ε⁻⁴) iteration and sample complexity for ε-KKT points—matching the lower bound of standard SGD for single-level nonconvex problems.

## Results

On the MNIST data hyper-cleaning task, TSP achieves a peak test accuracy of 90.4% (±0.0429%) and a peak F1 score of 92.4% (±0.100%), outperforming all penalty-based baselines. On the MNIST meta-learning task with multi-head architectures, TSP achieves the best generalization (test) accuracy among all compared methods, despite not attaining the highest meta-training accuracy. Theoretically, TSP achieves an O(ε⁻⁴) iteration complexity for finding ε-KKT points with high probability, matching the lower bound of standard SGD for single-level nonconvex problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP outperforms all three penalty-based baselines on the data hyper-cleaning task: it exceeds BOME by 0.34% in test accuracy and 0.41% in F1 score, PBGD by 0.39% and 0.45% respectively, and MEHA by 0.06% and 0.02% respectively. The margin over MEHA is small but TSP shows notably lower variance (±0.04% vs ±0.17% test accuracy), indicating more stable convergence. A key qualitative finding is that MEHA's test accuracy drops rapidly as iterations progress due to the penalty parameter continually increasing, causing overfitting to the lower-level problem—a known weakness of penalty methods. On the meta-learning task, TSP achieves the best test (generalization) accuracy compared to ANIL, BOME, PBGD, and MEHA, attributed to its ability to adjust dual variables individually for each task-specific head. TSP also converges faster in terms of iterations than the penalty-based methods, consistent with the known practical advantage of primal-dual over penalty approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Moreau envelope-based reformulation of bilevel optimization (Gao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a) and the smoothed Lagrangian framework for nonconvex constrained optimization (Zhang & Luo, 2020; Lu, 2024), combining them into a single-loop primal-dual algorithm. It departs from the dominant penalty-based approach (BOME, PBGD, MEHA) by using a Lagrangian/dual-variable mechanism that the authors argue provides better constraint enforcement and generalization. The key conceptual departure is applying two-sided smoothing—Moreau envelope on the LL side and proximal terms on the UL side—within a primal-dual structure, enabling stochastic first-order convergence to KKT points rather than merely stationary points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first stochastic first-order method with a proven O(ε^{-4}) rate for finding KKT points in nonconvex bilevel optimization where the LL objective is only weakly convex, matching the single-level SGD lower bound. The empirical demonstration that KKT solutions generalize better than stationary points (penalty methods) on data hyper-cleaning and meta-learning tasks suggests a practical advantage for multi-task and hyperparameter settings where the dual variable dynamically weights sub-problems. Open questions include whether the many step-size and smoothing hyperparameters (τ, μ, θ, η, α, β, ω, p) can be simplified, whether the rate extends to more complex constraint structures, and whether the boundedness assumptions (A3, coercivity) hold naturally in deep learning settings without explicit regularization.
