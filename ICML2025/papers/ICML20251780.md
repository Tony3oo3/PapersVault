## Problem

Traditional Cellular Potts Models require domain experts to handcraft analytical Hamiltonians for each new biological problem, and even well-designed Hamiltonians only partially capture the complexity of real multicellular dynamics. No prior method existed for learning an energy function directly from observational data while respecting the universal symmetries (permutation invariance over cells, translation invariance over the lattice) inherent to cellular dynamics modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes static snapshots of cellular configurations (discrete 2D lattice states, e.g. 100×100 or 149×149 grids, where each site is assigned a cell ID), not full trajectories, making it analogous to Neural Cellular Automata in data requirements. It produces a learned scalar-valued Hamiltonian H_θ(x) that parameterizes the CPM's MCMC dynamics; running the CPM with this Hamiltonian from an initial lattice state yields simulated cell dynamics trajectories. The Hamiltonian is a deep neural network with convolutional layers operating on one-hot encoded per-cell grids, pooled to a single scalar energy value.

## Method

The Neural Hamiltonian architecture enforces permutation and translation invariance through iterative NH layers: each layer applies a per-cell CNN (φ^l), aggregates cell embeddings via summation (permutation-invariant), processes the result with a cell-interaction CNN (ψ^l) alongside a global context lattice, and applies local spatial pooling. Training follows the energy-based model paradigm, minimizing negative log-likelihood with positive examples from data and negative examples from persistent MCMC chains using an approximate parallelized CPM sampler that proposes multiple boundary spin-flips simultaneously for speed. A key design choice is the biology-informed closure: the Neural Hamiltonian is added to a symbolic Hamiltonian (contact energy + volume constraint) with learned balancing weights, which constrains dynamics to biologically realistic configurations and acts as a regularizer that stabilizes otherwise divergent EBM training. The architecture uses SiLU activations, residual connections, and progressively increasing channel widths across NH layers.

## Results

On Cellular MNIST, the Neural Hamiltonian+closure model achieves a Classifier Score (CS) of 4.35 while maintaining perfect biological consistency (p_volume=1.00, p_unfragmented=1.00), substantially outperforming the analytical Cellsort Hamiltonian (CS=2.47) and Cellsort+External Potential (CS=3.11) on structural quality. On the bipolar axial organization task, NH+closure achieves an alignment RMSE of 37.3, a 4× improvement over the Cellsort Hamiltonian (147.2) and Cellsort+External Potential (154.4), while also matching their biological consistency metrics. The vanilla Neural Hamiltonian (without closure) achieves the highest CS (4.91) on Cellular MNIST but suffers from poor biological metrics (p_volume=0.11) and training divergence on the bipolar task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary analytical baseline is the prototypical Cellsort Hamiltonian of Graner & Glazier (1992), which achieves perfect biological consistency (p_volume=1.00, p_unfragmented=1.00) but fails to capture complex self-organized structures, scoring only CS=2.47 on Cellular MNIST and alignment RMSE=147.2 on bipolar organization. The Cellsort+External Potential variant improves CS to 3.11 but still falls well short of NeuralCPM. The CNN baseline, lacking permutation symmetry, produces unrealistic dynamics (p_volume=0.00, p_unfragmented=0.05) despite a moderate CS of 3.70. The 1-layer NH+CNN baseline, which pools over cells before applying a CNN, shows intermediate performance (CS=3.53, p_unfragmented=0.87), confirming the importance of deep equivariant representations. The vanilla Neural Hamiltonian achieves the highest CS (4.91) but has poor biological metrics and diverges during training on the bipolar task; adding the analytical closure term (NH+closure) restores biological realism (p_volume=1.00, p_unfragmented=1.00 on MNIST) and stabilizes training, yielding the best overall trade-off across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Cellular Potts Model (Graner & Glazier, 1992), a long-established stochastic simulation framework for multicellular dynamics, and deep energy-based models (Song & Kingma, 2021), which provide the training machinery. Prior ML approaches to cellular dynamics either accelerated physics-based simulators (neural operators) or learned autoregressive surrogates requiring full trajectory data (Minartz et al., 2022, 2023); NeuralCPM departs from both by learning the Hamiltonian itself from static snapshots, with a symmetry-respecting equivariant architecture tailored to the lattice-based CPM state space. The closure-model design—adding a neural residual on top of an analytical Hamiltonian—draws on the physics-informed neural network paradigm but is specifically adapted to the CPM's energy-function structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuralCPM removes the need for domain experts to hand-engineer a Hamiltonian for every new biological scenario, enabling data-driven modeling of cellular self-organization that analytical Hamiltonians cannot capture (e.g., bipolar axial organization). The finding that the analytical closure term stabilizes EBM training is practically important, as training deep EBMs is notoriously unstable. Key limitations include the restriction to ~100 cells, a global receptive field that is biologically implausible at tissue scale, and the equilibrium assumption that excludes actively migrating cells; scaling to large 3D tissues and incorporating history-dependent (non-Markovian) dynamics are the most pressing open questions.
