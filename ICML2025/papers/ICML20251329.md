## Problem

Despite state-of-the-art accuracy, the geometric structure of face recognition embedding spaces remains poorly understood: it is unclear how human-interpretable attributes (hair color, pose, illumination, etc.) influence the arrangement of identity clusters and the internal structure within each cluster. The paper asks whether and how these attributes shape the embedding geometry at two natural scales — the macroscale (inter-identity relationships) and the microscale (intra-identity structure) — and whether the degree of attribute invariance at the microscale correlates with recognition quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251329/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes pretrained FR models (FaceNet, ArcFace, AdaFace, SphereFaceR) applied to face images, plus controlled single-attribute variations generated by GAN-Control (pose, age, hair color, illumination, expression) and low-level augmentations (brightness, hue, image quality), all sampled along curves of length 3 at 7 discrete values per attribute. The macroscale output is a per-attribute KS statistic and intra-entropy score computed on a cleaned CelebA subset (~55K images, 1965 identities, 40 binary attributes); the microscale output is a multiscale invariance energy fingerprint (energy vs. scale ε) per attribute, averaged over 40 synthetic identities with over 120K embedded images total.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-scale geometric framework. At the macroscale, it tests whether an attribute's modality changes the distance distribution between identity point clouds using a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on intra- vs. inter-modality distances, and links the resulting KS statistic to intra-entropy (a proxy for discriminative power) via Spearman correlation. At the microscale, it defines a physics-inspired invariance energy: for each attribute, a one-parameter group of transformations induces a unit-norm vector field on the identity point cloud, and the energy is the expected cosine dissimilarity between vectors at points within a radius ε, analogous to the Hamiltonian of the n-vector spin model. Low energy indicates the model folds space to collapse attribute directions (invariance), while high energy indicates coherent displacement (sensitivity). The framework is validated by fine-tuning ArcFace and AdaFace on monovariant GAN-Control datasets and confirming that the energy metric increases most for the fine-tuned attribute, with the maximum of each rescaled energy column falling on the diagonal.

## Results

At the macroscale, KS-tests confirm that a large majority of CelebA's 40 binary attributes significantly shape the embedding space structure of all five FR models (p < 0.001). Spearman correlations between intra-entropy and KS statistics are significantly negative across all models (ranging from −0.566 to −0.656, all p ≤ 10⁻³), confirming that attributes most deterministically linked to identity have the strongest structural impact. At the microscale, the proposed invariance energy reveals that FaceNet exhibits substantially higher energy (greater attribute sensitivity) than ArcFace and AdaFace across all 8 attributes and all scales, while all models are most invariant to low-level attributes (contrast, illumination) and least invariant to complex ones (head angle, age). Fine-tuning ArcFace and AdaFace on a single attribute consistently increases the invariance energy for that same attribute (diagonal dominance in the energy matrix), validating the measure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analytical paper that characterizes existing FR models rather than proposing a new model to outperform them. The four compared models (FaceNet, ArcFace, AdaFace, SphereFaceR) are analyzed using the paper's proposed metrics. FaceNet (iResNetv1, Euclidean distance) shows the highest structural dependence on attributes at both scales: its KS statistics are generally larger than ArcFace's (Figure 1d), and its invariance energy is significantly higher across all attributes and scales (Figure 4b), indicating its embedding space retains more attribute information. ArcFace (ResNet18/50, cosine) and AdaFace (ResNet18, cosine) show similar and lower energy values, with AdaFace slightly more sensitive to color-related attributes (hair color, hue). SphereFaceR (iResNet100, cosine) has the tightest intra-class distances (0.276) and highest LFW accuracy (99.83%) but is not included in the microscale energy analysis. The fine-tuning experiments confirm that targeted attribute augmentation on ArcFace and AdaFace increases invariance energy specifically for the augmented attribute, with the effect being strongest on the diagonal of the attribute-by-fine-tuning energy matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the contrastive/metric learning framework for face recognition (FaceNet, ArcFace, AdaFace, SphereFace) and the data augmentation theory of Chen et al. (2020a), which frames augmentation as group transformations inducing invariance in learned representations. It extends prior interpretability efforts—Hill et al. (2019) on hierarchical structure in FR embeddings, Ren et al. (2024) on attribute recovery from embeddings, and Diniz & Schwartz (2020) on implicit attribute learning—by introducing a unified multi-scale geometric formalism: a KS-test-based macroscale analysis for categorical attributes and a physics-inspired invariance energy metric (analogous to the n-vector Hamiltonian) for continuous attributes at the microscale. The use of GAN-Control for controlled single-attribute variation connects to the controllable GAN literature but is repurposed here as a measurement instrument rather than a generation tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The invariance energy metric provides a concrete, quantitative diagnostic for assessing how well an FR model filters out non-identity variations, which is directly relevant to fairness, robustness, and security concerns in deployed biometric systems. The finding that FaceNet retains more attribute structure than angular-margin models at both scales offers practitioners a geometric rationale for model selection beyond accuracy benchmarks. Limitations include the restriction to 8 controllable attributes, reliance on synthetic GAN-Control data for microscale validation, and the macroscale analysis being limited to binary attributes. Open questions include whether the energy metric generalizes to other metric learning domains (e.g., retrieval, clustering) and whether it can be integrated into training as a regularizer to explicitly enforce attribute invariance.
