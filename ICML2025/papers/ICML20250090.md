## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs suffer significant performance degradation at low bit widths and typically offer only a fixed set of solutions at specific integer bit levels, requiring manual tuning. Moreover, they apply uniform bit-widths across all weight channels despite large disparities in per-channel quantization error, leading to disproportionate resource allocation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision (FP16) weight matrices, a small calibration set (100/128 C4 samples for computing gradient-squared weights), and a target bit level that can be any real value (e.g., 3.2 bits). It produces per-channel quantized labels (low-bit indices into a codebook), a set of cluster centroids whose count varies per channel according to the allocated bit, and a per-column scaling vector. The mixed-precision output means different channels within the same layer are stored at different precisions, breaking the fixed bit grid.

## Method

SKIM combines three components: (1) a greedy bit-allocation algorithm that solves a knapsack-like constrained minimization in O(n log n) time, assigning more bits to channels with higher quantization error; (2) weighted K-means clustering per channel, where the weights derive from the S-diag sensitivity-based objective (gradient-squared times input-norm-squared), enabling non-uniform quantization with channel-specific centroid counts; and (3) a trainable per-column scaling vector optimized via an iterative strategy that alternates between fixing the K-means grouping and backpropagating the L-full layer-wise reconstruction error through the dequantization to update the scaling. The paper's theoretical contribution unifies layer-wise and sensitivity-based quantization objectives into full/diag forms, showing they differ only by an output-gradient factor, which justifies using different objective forms for different sub-steps (L-full for scaling, S-diag for clustering) without causing optimization conflicts.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B WikiText2 perplexity, SKIM achieves 5.79 at 4-bit, 6.21 at 3-bit, and 8.99 at 2.25-bit, narrowing the gap to FP16 (5.68) by approximately 14% on average across bit levels. At the most aggressive 2.25-bit setting, SKIM reduces perplexity from 11.32 (SqueezeLLM) to 8.99, a 20% improvement. On LLaMA2-7B, SKIM similarly leads with 5.60 (4-bit), 6.09 (3-bit), and 10.10 (2.25-bit) WikiText2 perplexity. In benchmark accuracy (Table 3), SKIM at 3-bit achieves 55.1% average across PiQA, ARC-C, ARC-E, and MMLU, outperforming SqueezeLLM's 54.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKIM consistently outperforms all five baselines across the majority of settings. Against SqueezeLLM, the gap is most dramatic at 2.25-bit where SKIM achieves 8.99 vs. 11.32 WikiText2 perplexity on LLaMA-7B (a 20.6% reduction), and at 3-bit where SKIM reaches 6.21 vs. 6.32. Against OmniQuant, SKIM leads at every bit level, with the largest margin at 3-bit (6.21 vs. 6.48) and 2.x-bit (8.99 vs. 9.72). Against QuIP#, SKIM wins at 3-bit (6.21 vs. 6.29) and 2.x-bit (8.99 vs. 9.95), while matching at 4-bit (5.79 vs. 5.83). Against DecoupleQ (Appendix Table 7), SKIM wins at 4-bit (5.79 vs. 5.85) and 2.x-bit (8.64 at 2.30 bits vs. 8.65 at 2.25 bits), but loses at 3-bit (6.70 vs. 6.38). Against ABQ-LLM (Appendix Table 8), SKIM wins at both 4-bit (5.79 vs. 5.83) and 3-bit (6.21 vs. 6.29) on LLaMA-7B WikiText2.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKIM builds directly on the non-uniform K-means clustering quantization paradigm established by SqueezeLLM and GPTQ, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it replaces fixed or element-wise mixed precision (as in SqueezeLLM's sparse outlier retention) with an adaptive channel-wise bit allocation via a greedy knapsack-style algorithm, and it introduces a trainable scaling vector optimized through an iterative strategy that handles the non-differentiability of K-means grouping. The paper also provides a unified reformulation showing that layer-wise and sensitivity-based quantization objectives (used by GPTQ and SqueezeLLM respectively) reduce to the same structural form, differing only in whether output gradients are included, which justifies cross-objective optimization across SKIM's multi-step pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to quantize to arbitrary (including non-integer) bit levels is practically valuable for practitioners who need to fit a model within a specific GPU memory budget, and the ~14% average perplexity gap reduction over full precision at 3-bit is a meaningful improvement over prior SOTA. However, the method remains weight-only and requires calibration data, and the OPT experiments reveal that mixed precision can sometimes increase perplexity due to overfitting on calibration data, suggesting the approach may not generalize uniformly across architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends to activation quantization, whether the greedy allocation's approximation gap becomes problematic at larger scales, and whether the iterative scaling optimization converges reliably for very deep or heterogeneous models.
