## Problem

Models trained on distilled datasets remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks, yet existing robustness-enhancing approaches for dataset distillation either require costly adversarial retraining or impose a performance-robustness trade-off. The paper identifies that prior DD methods neglect the mutual information structure among inputs, latent representations, and outputs, leaving non-robust features unfiltered in the synthetic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253169/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ROME takes a source dataset of 32×32 RGB images with class labels (CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100), applies PGD adversarial perturbations (budget 8/255) to create a perturbed 'robust prior' dataset, and uses a pretrained adversarially robust ConvNet to provide logits and embeddings during distillation. The output is a compact synthetic dataset at IPC-1, IPC-10, or IPC-50 that, when used to train a fresh ConvNet via standard SGD, yields a model with both high clean accuracy and strong adversarial robustness without any adversarial fine-tuning step.

## Method

ROME reformulates dataset distillation through the Conditional Entropy Bottleneck, defining the objective as I(Y;Z) − β·I(X;Z|X̂), where X̂ is the adversarially perturbed source dataset serving as a robust prior. This yields two tractable loss terms: a performance-aligned term (cross-entropy between synthetic image logits from a pretrained robust model and true labels) that maximizes I(Y;Z), and a robustness-aligned term (squared L2 distance between class-conditional embedding means of synthetic and perturbed samples, derived by scaling Pinsker's inequality) that minimizes I(X;Z|X̂). The synthetic dataset is initialized by sampling from the perturbed source and updated via gradient descent on the weighted sum (1−α)L_Perf + α·L_Rob with α=0.2. Because robustness is encoded directly into the distilled images through the perturbation prior and feature alignment, no adversarial retraining of downstream models is needed, eliminating the computational overhead and accuracy-robustness trade-off of prior methods.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC-50), ROME achieves 97.44% I-RR and 63.32% I-CREI under white-box targeted attacks, surpassing the best baseline (DC) by 8.93% and 5.11% respectively. Under untargeted attacks, ROME reaches 67.01% I-RR and 43.62% I-CREI, outperforming the best baseline (DM) by 10.82% and 4.46%. On CIFAR-100, improvements are even larger: ROME gains 23.51% I-RR over DC under targeted attacks and 12.95% over DSA under untargeted attacks. Under black-box attacks (Table 2), ROME achieves near-perfect robustness (99.90% I-RR targeted transfer, 99.79% targeted query) and a 15.2% improvement over the second-best method on untargeted query-based attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROME outperforms all six baselines (DC, DSA, MTT, DM, IDM, BACON) across every metric and dataset in the white-box evaluation. On CIFAR-10 targeted attacks, ROME's 97.44% I-RR exceeds DC (88.51%), the strongest baseline, by 8.93 percentage points; on CIFAR-100 the gap widens to 23.51 points (100.65% vs. 77.14%). Under untargeted attacks, ROME beats DM (56.19% I-RR on CIFAR-10) by 10.82 points and DSA (33.29% on CIFAR-100) by 12.95 points. In the black-box setting (Table 2, CIFAR-10 IPC-50), ROME achieves 99.90% I-RR on targeted transfer attacks versus DSA's 94.09%, and 78.46% on untargeted query-based attacks versus BACON's 63.26%—a 15.2-point margin. Notably, ROME attains these gains without adversarial retraining, whereas baselines like DC and DM require an additional ~0.65 hours of adversarial fine-tuning to reach comparable (but still lower) I-CREI scores (Table 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

ROME extends the Information Bottleneck lineage (Tishby et al., 2000; Alemi et al., 2017) and specifically the Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (Fischer, 2020) into the dataset distillation setting, drawing direct inspiration from IBD (Kuang et al., 2023), which applied IB to knowledge distillation for robustness. It departs from prior robust DD efforts (BEARD, GUARD) that rely on adversarial retraining or curvature regularization with restrictive assumptions, instead embedding adversarial perturbations as a robust prior within the distillation objective itself. The method also builds on distribution-matching DD methods (DM, IDM, BACON) for the performance-aligned component while introducing a novel robustness-aligned term derived via Pinsker's inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROME addresses a practical gap: distilled datasets produce models that are significantly more vulnerable to adversarial attacks than full-data models, yet prior solutions require costly adversarial retraining that introduces a performance-robustness trade-off. By achieving strong robustness without retraining, ROME makes robust DD more accessible for resource-constrained settings. However, scalability remains a key open question—the authors acknowledge that the large search space limits applicability to ImageNet-scale or language/vision-language models. The introduced I-RR metric also provides the community with a more stable evaluation tool that avoids overestimating robustness when a single dominant attack inflates the worst-case ASR.
