## Problem

Protein language models lack factual biological knowledge (e.g., gene descriptions), and existing knowledge-enhanced approaches (OntoProtein, KeAP) only use protein knowledge graphs as implicit pre-training objectives. This leads to three critical failures: knowledge is not explicitly integrated at the token level, high-order graph structure is ignored, and knowledge is catastrophically forgotten during fine-tuning because downstream proteins often fall outside the PKG, creating inconsistent optimization objectives between pre-training and fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

Kara takes an amino acid sequence and a protein knowledge graph (ProteinKG25: ~600K proteins, 50K GO entities, 31 relations, 4.5M triplets) as input. Pre-trained embeddings of GO entities and relations are generated by a frozen PubMedBERT from text descriptions, and protein embeddings by a frozen ProtBert from sequences; these are stored in vector databases. The output is a knowledge-integrated protein embedding produced by mean-pooling the last-layer token embeddings (excluding virtual tokens) of a ProtBert backbone. For proteins absent from the PKG, a knowledge retriever predicts top-K candidate (relation, GO) triplets to construct virtual tokens on the fly, enabling seamless adaptation to knowledge updates.

## Method

Kara introduces contextualized virtual tokens—knowledge tokens (mean of MLP-projected [relation; GO] embeddings over 1-hop neighbors) and structure tokens (mean of MLP-projected protein embeddings over 2-hop neighbors)—concatenated before the amino acid token sequence so each residue can attend to graph context via standard Transformer self-attention. Pre-training jointly optimizes masked language modeling (15% masking) and a structure-based margin loss that pulls 2-hop-connected protein embeddings closer than negatives, unifying token-level knowledge fusion with sequence-level functional similarity. For fine-tuning, a TransE-based knowledge retriever scores candidate (protein, relation, GO) triplets using a relation-GO combination strategy (reducing search from 47K GO entities to ~2K per relation) and a cross-modal matching loss aligning text and sequence modalities; the top-K retrieved triplets replace ground-truth knowledge to construct virtual tokens for new proteins. This unified virtual-token + regularization pipeline is applied identically in both stages, so the optimization objective remains consistent and pre-trained knowledge is not catastrophically forgotten during task fine-tuning.

## Results

Across 6 downstream tasks, Kara achieves on average 5.1% improvements over existing knowledge-enhanced models. In amino acid contact prediction, Kara reaches 0.45 P@L (short-range), 0.39 (medium-range), and 0.31 (long-range), outperforming KeAP by up to 11.6% on long-range contacts. On protein homology detection, Kara achieves 0.32 accuracy (vs. 0.29 for KeAP and 0.13 for ESM-2), and on stability prediction it reaches 0.83 Spearman correlation (vs. 0.80 for both KeAP and ESM-2). In PPI identification on STRING, Kara scores 87.59 F1, surpassing KeAP (85.61) and ESM-2 (85.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kara consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art knowledge-enhanced model KeAP across all 6 tasks, with the largest gains in long-range contact prediction (+11.6% P@L) and homology detection (+10.3% accuracy), attributed to Kara's ability to incorporate knowledge during fine-tuning via the knowledge retriever. Against the strong non-knowledge baselines ESM-2 and ESM-1b, Kara wins on contact prediction, PPI, homology, and stability, while tying on binding affinity (0.50 MSE) and BP semantic similarity (0.41). Compared to OntoProtein, Kara achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., 0.32 vs. 0.24 on homology), demonstrating that explicit task-oriented knowledge integration via virtual tokens and structure-based regularization is more effective than implicit knowledge embedding through TransE objectives. Against sequence-based baselines (LSTM, ResNet, Transformer, ProtBert), Kara's advantages are most pronounced on long-range contacts and homology detection, where knowledge of functional similarity provides critical signal. On binding affinity prediction, Kara matches ESM-1b (0.50) but does not surpass it, which the authors attribute to the importance of 3D structural information that ESM-1b captures architecturally but Kara approximates only through PKG text descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kara extends the line of knowledge-enhanced protein language models initiated by OntoProtein (TransE-based alignment of protein and GO embeddings) and KeAP (cross-attention-guided masked token prediction with GO context). It departs from both by introducing a retrieval-augmented paradigm: a knowledge retriever predicts potential gene descriptions for proteins absent from the PKG, and contextualized virtual tokens (knowledge + structure) are prepended to amino acid sequences for explicit, token-level multi-modal fusion. The structure-based regularization, applied uniformly across pre-training and fine-tuning, distinguishes Kara from prior work that only incorporates knowledge during pre-training, addressing the inconsistent optimization objectives that cause catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Kara demonstrates that knowledge-enhanced protein models can actually underperform vanilla PLMs (e.g., ESM-1b) on several tasks when knowledge is only implicitly embedded during pre-training, highlighting a failure mode in the prior paradigm. The unified knowledge integration across both stages, combined with the ability to adapt to PKG updates without retraining, offers a more robust and maintainable framework for protein representation learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D structural information (acknowledged as future work), how performance holds on very long sequences where both Kara and KeAP struggle, and whether the knowledge retriever's generalization to truly novel gene descriptions (beyond the 8:2 split tested) is sufficient for rapidly evolving biological knowledge bases.
