## Problem

Existing diverse teammate generation methods for cooperative MARL (e.g., FCP, MEP, LIPO, Macop) operate purely at the policy level, producing teammates that lack semantic grounding in specific coordination behaviors. This leads to inefficient exploration of the teammate policy space and forces agents to rely on costly trial-and-error interactions for adaptation to unseen teammates, rather than leveraging the semantic information that naturally describes coordination preferences.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes environment observations (state vectors from LBF, PP, SMACv2, or GRF) and iteratively generates natural language coordination behaviors that are translated into executable Python reward functions. During execution, the agent receives a natural language description of the unseen teammate's preferred behavior (e.g., 'I prefer to score myself') before the episode begins, and the LLM selector outputs an index selecting one of the pre-trained policy heads. The final output is the joint action of all controllable agents, with the selected head determining the coordination strategy.

## Method

SEMDIV employs a three-stage iterative loop: (1) an LLM behavior generator proposes a novel coordination behavior in natural language, conditioned on previously generated behaviors and similarity feedback to ensure semantic diversity; (2) an LLM reward generator translates the behavior into an executable Python reward function, iteratively refined via grounding feedback (executability, task completion, non-constant returns, behavior alignment, and policy distinctness verified via a compatibility threshold); (3) a teammate policy is trained via self-play (MAPPO or VDN) maximizing a weighted sum of task reward and generated reward, with the generated reward weight decaying to zero. Agents use a multi-head architecture with a shared feature extractor (3-layer MLP + GRU, hidden dim 64) and per-teammate policy heads (3-layer MLP), trained with continual learning that freezes old heads and applies Lp-norm regularization on the feature extractor to prevent catastrophic forgetting. At test time, an LLM selector performs language-based reasoning over the task description, learned behaviors, and the new teammate's description to pick the optimal head, replacing trial-and-error adaptation.

## Results

Across four MARL environments (LBF, PP, SMACv2, GRF), SEMDIV achieves an average task success rate (R1) of 0.74±0.08 and a teammate-preference satisfaction rate (R2) of 0.68, outperforming the best baseline Macop by 19% on R1 and 39% on R2. The Oracle upper bound sits at 0.95 for both metrics. SEMDIV also generates teammates covering coordination behaviors unreachable by policy-level baselines, such as multi-pass tactics in GRF, and scales effectively with the number of training teammates thanks to its multi-head architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMDIV consistently outperforms all four baselines across all environments on both R1 and R2. Against FCP (Strouse et al., 2021), which trains diverse teammates via different random seeds, SEMDIV achieves 0.74 vs. 0.48 average R1 and 0.68 vs. 0.34 R2 — a gap of 26 and 34 percentage points respectively, reflecting FCP's inability to generate semantically meaningful diversity. MEP (Zhao et al., 2023) and LIPO (Charakorn et al., 2023) add entropy or incompatibility regularization terms and improve over FCP (0.58 and 0.55 R1), but still trail SEMDIV by 16 and 19 percentage points on R1. The closest competitor is Macop (Yuan et al., 2023), which iteratively generates teammates by minimizing compatibility with agents; SEMDIV beats Macop-R1 by 12 points on R1 (0.74 vs. 0.62) and by 20 points on R2 (0.68 vs. 0.48), with the largest gap appearing in PP (R2: 0.54 vs. 0.23) and SMACv2 (R1: 0.65 vs. 0.48). Notably, LLM-Agent (using GPT-4o directly as the policy without MARL) performs comparably to SEMDIV on simpler tasks (LBF: 0.88 vs. 0.90 R1) but collapses on complex environments (GRF: 0.14 vs. 0.67 R1), underscoring the necessity of task-specific reinforcement learning alongside LLM-based semantic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ad-hoc teamwork and zero-shot coordination literature (FCP, MEP, LIPO, Macop) by shifting diversity generation from the policy level to the semantic level, leveraging LLMs to propose coordination behaviors in natural language before translating them into trainable reward functions. It builds on LLM-to-reward-code approaches (Text2Reward, Eureka) for the reward generation step and on multi-head continual learning architectures (Kessler et al., Yuan et al.) to avoid catastrophic forgetting across teammates. The key departure is the closed-loop integration: the LLM not only generates behaviors and rewards but also verifies policy-behavior alignment and selects the appropriate policy head at test time via language-based reasoning, creating a unified semantic-RL pipeline absent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMDIV addresses a practical bottleneck in open multi-agent deployment: agents trained with policy-level diversity cannot efficiently adapt to teammates whose behaviors are described in language, a common scenario in human-AI and heterogeneous-agent settings. The 39% improvement in satisfying teammates' preferred coordination behaviors (R2) over the best baseline is meaningful for applications where respecting partner preferences matters as much as task completion. Limitations include reliance on a specific LLM (GPT-4o), evaluation on only four relatively small-scale environments, and a persistent gap to the Oracle upper bound. Open questions include whether the semantic-level exploration advantage persists in high-dimensional continuous control or real-world embodied settings, and how the approach scales when the number of distinct coordination behaviors grows substantially.
