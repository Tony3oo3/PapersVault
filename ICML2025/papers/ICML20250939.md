## Problem

In long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL), existing multi-expert methods like CPE train three classifiers for different unlabeled data distributions but fail to exploit each expert's distinct strengths: the long-tailed expert excels on head classes, the uniform expert on medium classes, and the inverse expert on tail classes. CPE uses all experts simultaneously for pseudo-labeling (introducing error labels) and only the uniform expert for testing, leaving expertises underutilized. The paper also identifies that deep features are more biased toward head classes while shallow features are less biased but less discriminative, a trade-off not previously addressed in LTSSL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images with ground-truth class labels and unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10-LT, 96×96 for SVHN-LT and STL-10-LT) through a shared WRN-28-2 encoder that extracts features at three depths. The DEA module outputs a 3-dimensional soft class membership probability vector per sample, and the aggregator produces a C-dimensional soft prediction (C=10 for all datasets) from which the final class label is selected by argmax. During training, a binary confidence mask (threshold t=0.95) gates which unlabeled samples contribute to the pseudo-label loss.

## Method

Meta-Expert builds on FixMatch with three auxiliary classifiers (experts) trained at different logit adjustment intensities, and introduces two novel modules. The Dynamic Expert Assignment (DEA) module is an MLP that takes the fused encoder feature and all three expert logits as input, outputting a soft probability vector w=[w₁,w₂,w₃] indicating which expert should handle each sample; it is trained with a cross-entropy loss against ground-truth class membership on labeled data and pseudo-membership on unlabeled data. The Multi-depth Feature Fusion (MFF) module aligns and adds shallow, medium, and deep encoder features to produce a representation that balances head-class bias (reduced by shallow features) with discriminative power (retained from deep features). An aggregator computes a weighted sum of expert logits using the DEA probabilities followed by softmax, ensuring the appropriate expert dominates pseudo-label generation and prediction per sample. Training proceeds with an 18-epoch warmup using only the base FixMatch loss, then jointly optimizes the base loss, DEA loss, and META loss end-to-end. A theoretical generalization error bound (Theorem 1) shows that the overall pseudo-labeling error ε is smaller under dynamic expert assignment than under CPE's uniform ensemble, yielding a tighter bound.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT with the most challenging setting (N₁=500, M₁=4000, γ=200), Meta-Expert achieves 74.39% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best CPE (67.45%) by 6.94 pp and ACR (71.92%) by 2.47 pp. Under the inverse distribution mismatch (γ_l=200, γ_u=1/200), Meta-Expert reaches 85.71%, outperforming CPE (84.07%) by 1.64 pp. On STL-10-LT (N₁=450, γ_l=15), Meta-Expert achieves 79.98%, beating CPE (78.71%) by 1.27 pp. On SVHN-LT (N₁=1500, M₁=3000, γ=200), Meta-Expert reaches 93.56%, marginally surpassing BaCon (93.30%) by 0.26 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-Expert outperforms all eight baselines on CIFAR-10-LT across every tested setting, with the largest margin over CPE (the closest multi-expert competitor) reaching 6.94 pp under the smallest labeled set (N₁=500, γ=200). Against ACR, the previous single-expert SOTA, Meta-Expert gains 2.47 pp in the same setting and 1.64 pp under the most severe inverse mismatch (γ_u=1/200). On SVHN-LT, Meta-Expert is competitive with SimPro (94.24% vs. 94.76% under inverse γ_u=1/150) but clearly surpasses CPE (93.06%) and ACR (92.03%) in that setting, while matching or exceeding BaCon under the consistent case. On STL-10-LT, where unlabeled labels are inaccessible, Meta-Expert leads CPE by 1.27 pp (N₁=450, γ_l=15) and ACR by 1.43 pp. The only near-tie is on SVHN-LT consistent (γ=200), where Meta-Expert (93.56%) edges out BaCon (93.30%) by just 0.26 pp, suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced under distribution mismatch and small labeled budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CPE (Ma et al., 2024), which first introduced three distribution-specific experts (long-tailed, uniform, inverse long-tailed) for LTSSL, and on FixMatch as the base SSL framework. It departs from CPE in two key ways: (1) instead of using all experts simultaneously for pseudo-labeling or only the uniform expert for testing, it proposes a Dynamic Expert Assignment (DEA) module that routes each sample to its best-suited expert based on estimated class membership; and (2) it introduces a Multi-depth Feature Fusion (MFF) module motivated by the novel empirical observation that shallow features are less biased toward head classes but less discriminative, whereas deep features show the opposite trade-off. The theoretical contribution—a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound showing that expert integration reduces pseudo-labeling error—extends the analysis framework of prior SSL generalization results to the multi-expert LTSSL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world LTSSL scenarios (e.g., medical imaging) often have mismatched labeled/unlabeled distributions, and prior multi-expert methods waste expert capacity by not routing samples to their strongest classifier. The DEA+MFF combination provides a principled, end-to-end solution that consistently improves over CPE and other baselines by 1–4 pp across CIFAR-10-LT, STL-10-LT, and SVHN-LT. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (≤10 classes), a fixed set of three experts, and the DEA module's reliance on an MLP that adds ~13% parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale long-tailed SSL, whether the number of experts should be adaptive, and whether the depth-fusion insight generalizes beyond WRN-28-2 architectures.
