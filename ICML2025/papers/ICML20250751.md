## Problem

Reaction yield is governed by the activation energy of the transition state, whose graph structure can only be inferred if the atom mapping between reactants and products is known. However, most yield prediction datasets contain neither transition states nor atom mappings, and computing the true mapping is an NP-hard graph matching problem that existing tools address only via expert-curated rules or fine-grained supervision. Prior yield predictors therefore bypass the transition state entirely, relying on reactant/product embeddings or SMILES representations without modeling the structural rearrangement that determines yield.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-step reactions where each elementary step provides reactant and product molecular graphs with node features (atomic number, degree, hybridization) and edge features (bond type), padded to a common node count N. It outputs a scalar yield prediction for the final product, while internally producing a doubly stochastic alignment matrix approximating the atom mapping and a continuous weighted adjacency matrix for the condensed graph of reaction (CGR). Training uses only the distant supervision of yield values—no ground-truth atom mapping or transition state labels are required.

## Method

YIELDNET first computes node and edge embeddings of reactants and products via a CMPNN-based GNN, then approximates the atom mapping through a differentiable node alignment network that applies iterative Sinkhorn normalizations to a cost matrix derived from the embeddings, yielding a doubly stochastic matrix P. This P is used to construct a continuous approximation of the CGR adjacency as max(Adj_R ⊙ M_R, P·Adj_I ⊙ M_I·P^T), where the edge embeddings M provide soft weights that model transient fractional bonds and strengthen gradient flow. A custom input-differentiable GNN (operating entirely in tensor space on the weighted adjacency) computes CGR node embeddings, which are then concatenated with step encodings and processed by a transformer to capture cross-step interactions before set-based aggregation and an MLP produce the final yield. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with MSE on yield plus a Frobenius-norm regularizer that penalizes alignment between atoms of different identities, requiring no explicit atom-mapping supervision.

## Results

YIELDNET achieves the lowest MAE on 7 out of 8 datasets, with particularly large gains on DF (6.941 vs. 12.470 for YieldBERT, a 44% reduction) and GP (23.152 vs. 27.837 for DeepReac+). The skyline variant YIELDNET(sky), which uses the true CGR available only in GP, further reduces MAE to 17.396, confirming the importance of the condensed graph representation. Gains are statistically significant (p < 0.05) on five of the eight datasets, with the remaining two (NS3, NS4) showing non-significant but still favorable margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YIELDNET outperforms all six yield-prediction baselines (GCN, HGT, TAG, GIN, DeepReac+, YieldBERT) on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin on DF where it achieves 6.941 MAE versus 12.470 for YieldBERT and 12.516 for GCN. Among the GNN baselines, GCN is the strongest competitor on NS1–NS5, while YieldBERT leads on SC and DF; YIELDNET beats both by 1.7–5.5 MAE points. In the atom-mapping comparison (Table 2), YIELDNET's learned alignment outperforms RDKit (9.245 vs. 9.910 on NS1), RXNMapper (9.245 vs. 9.610), and the attention-based method of Astero & Rousu (9.245 vs. 9.548), with the advantage attributed to the injective doubly-stochastic constraint. On GP, where ground-truth atom mapping is available, YIELDNET's learned alignment achieves a Frobenius distance of 3.708 from the true permutation, compared to 5.096 for the attention-based method, all without any explicit atom-mapping supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: differentiable permutation learning via Gumbel-Sinkhorn (Mena et al., 2018; Cuturi, 2013), graph neural networks for molecular representation (specifically CMPNN from Song et al., 2020), and reaction yield prediction. It departs from prior yield prediction work (YieldBERT, DeepReac+, GNN baselines) by explicitly modeling the condensed graph of reaction (CGR) as a transition-state surrogate, and from prior atom mapping work (RXNMapper, GraphormerMapper, AMNet) by learning the mapping implicitly under distant supervision of yield rather than requiring ground-truth atom mapping labels. The key conceptual departure is formulating atom mapping as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem solved via Sinkhorn iterations, then feeding the resulting soft permutation into a custom input-differentiable GNN to compute CGR embeddings end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that most real-world yield prediction datasets lack atom mappings and transition states, yet the CGR (and hence the transition state) is physically central to determining yield via activation energy. By learning the mapping purely from yield supervision, YIELDNET removes a major manual curation bottleneck. However, the approach is limited to reactions with ~200–250 atoms, single reaction paths, and 2D molecular graphs without 3D geometry or reaction conditions (temperature, time, purification). Open questions include whether the Sinkhorn-based alignment generalizes to larger or multi-path reactions, whether incorporating 3D coordinates or reaction conditions would close the remaining gap to the skyline (true CGR), and whether the input-differentiable GNN design transfers to other graph-matching applications beyond chemistry.
