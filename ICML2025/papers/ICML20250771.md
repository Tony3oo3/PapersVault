## Problem

Adaptive sparse attention mechanisms like α-entmax assign exactly zero probability to irrelevant tokens, offering a data-dependent alternative to softmax that can improve both interpretability and efficiency. However, existing α-entmax implementations do not exploit this sparsity for runtime or memory gains, making them slower than optimized softmax attention (e.g., FlashAttention-2) and unable to scale beyond short context lengths due to the lack of hardware-aware, GPU-optimized kernels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard attention inputs Q, K, V ∈ R^{n×d} along with a scalar sparsity parameter α > 1 (e.g., 1.5 or 2.0), and produces the attention output O ∈ R^{n×d} plus a per-row threshold vector τ ∈ R^n needed for the backward pass. Unlike fixed-pattern sparse attention, the sparsity pattern is entirely data-dependent and discovered at runtime; the method additionally constructs a binary block mask M ∈ {0,1}^{T_r×T_c} and two pointer-increment lookup tables (one per query block, one per key block) that identify which K/V blocks contribute nonzero attention, enabling selective skipping of null blocks in both forward and backward passes.

## Method

ADASPLASH combines two key innovations. First, a hybrid Halley-bisection root-finding algorithm computes the α-entmax threshold τ by using Halley's method (cubic convergence via first and second derivatives of f(τ)) when the update stays within bisection bounds, and falling back to bisection otherwise; this reduces the number of iterations from 23 (standard bisection) to 3 for machine-precision convergence. Second, custom Triton kernels implement a FlashAttention-style tiling-and-recomputation strategy adapted to α-entmax: Q, K, V are split into blocks of sizes B_r and B_c, the score matrix S is never materialized in HBM, and f(τ), f'(τ), f''(τ) are accumulated block-by-block in on-chip SRAM. After a few Halley-bisection iterations, a binary block mask M is constructed to identify null blocks (where all entries of P are zero), and pointer-increment lookup tables are built to skip loading K/V blocks that do not contribute to the output. The backward pass exploits the sparse Jacobian structure of α-entmax (Diag(u) − uuᵀ/‖u‖₁ where u_j = p_j^{2−α}) and reuses the same lookup tables to skip null blocks when computing dQ, dK, and dV, achieving O(n) memory complexity while approaching or surpassing FlashAttention-2 runtime at high sparsity levels.

## Results

ADASPLASH achieves a GPT-2 training step time of 1.03 s with 52.5 GB peak memory, matching FlashAttention-2 (0.98 s, 52.5 GB) while outperforming Torch bisection (7.78 s, 77.6 GB) and Torch sorting (3.61 s, 73.8 GB) by large margins. On the BEIR retrieval benchmark, ModernBERT with α=1.5 attains the best nDCG@10 across all four tasks (e.g., TREC-COVID 75.2 vs. 67.6 for dense ModernBERT). The Halley-bisection algorithm reduces α-entmax threshold computation from 23 iterations (bisection) to 3, yielding a 15× speedup (2.38 ms vs. 36.67 ms) and 1.75× memory reduction. Sparse GPT-2 (α=1.5) also achieves a slightly lower validation loss (3.263 vs. 3.283) and higher HellaSwag accuracy (30.6% vs. 30.4%) than its softmax counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention-2, ADASPLASH matches runtime (1.03 vs. 0.98 s/step) and memory (52.5 GB) for GPT-2, and at high input sparsity (Figure 1) it surpasses FA2 because FA2's runtime is constant while ADASPLASH exploits sparsity to skip null blocks. Against Torch's bisection implementation of α-entmax, ADASPLASH is 7.5× faster on GPT-2 (1.03 vs. 7.78 s/step) and uses 32% less memory (52.5 vs. 77.6 GB); on long-document classification at 8192 tokens the gap widens to 38 min vs. 4 h 13 min per epoch and 79.9 vs. 508.2 GB peak memory. Against Torch's sorting-based α=1.5 algorithm, ADASPLASH is 3.5× faster (1.03 vs. 3.61 s/step) with 29% less memory. On task quality, sparse ModernBERT (α=1.5) outperforms dense ModernBERT on all four BEIR retrieval tasks (e.g., SciFact 58.4 vs. 57.7, TREC-COVID 75.2 vs. 67.6) while matching it on GLUE (83.5 vs. 83.7 avg). Sparse RoBERTa (α=1.5) ties dense RoBERTa on GLUE (83.9 avg) and slightly exceeds it on long-document classification at 4096 tokens (F1 78.0 vs. 77.9). Sparse GPT-2 (α=1.5) marginally beats dense GPT-2 on both validation loss (3.263 vs. 3.283) and HellaSwag (30.6% vs. 30.4%), confirming that adaptive sparsity does not sacrifice downstream accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the hardware-aware tiling and recomputation strategy of FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) and the adaptive sparse probability transformation of α-entmax (Peters et al., 2019). Prior α-entmax implementations (bisection from Blondel et al., 2019; sorting-based exact algorithms) were memory-bound and could not scale beyond short contexts, while FlashAttention's efficiency gains were tied to the dense softmax structure. ADASPLASH departs from both by introducing a hybrid Halley-bisection root-finder (reducing iterations from 23 to 3) and custom Triton kernels that exploit the dynamic, input-dependent sparsity of α-entmax through block-level masking and pointer-increment lookup tables—something neither FlashAttention's fixed sparse patterns nor prior entmax implementations could do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADASPLASH makes long-context training with adaptive sparse attention practically viable for the first time, reducing what was an out-of-memory problem at 4k tokens (Torch bisection) to a regime comparable to FlashAttention-2 at 8k+ tokens. The finding that the method can match or surpass FlashAttention-2 runtime at high sparsity levels (Figure 1) suggests that adaptive sparsity is not merely a theoretical nicety but a genuine efficiency lever for encoder models and long-document tasks. Open questions include how well the approach scales to very large decoder-only models where attention is denser, whether the block mask M can be made more granular without losing the linear-memory guarantee, and how it interacts with emerging KV-cache compression and speculative decoding techniques.
