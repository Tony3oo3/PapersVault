## Problem

Neural combinatorial optimization solvers face a fundamental tension between two paradigms: Global Prediction (GP) methods produce overly smooth probability heatmaps at scale that hinder decoding, while Local Construction (LC) methods suffer from slow autoregressive step-by-step construction that limits scalability. No existing approach adaptively controls the decision granularity—i.e., how many variables are resolved per step—between these two extremes of one-shot and one-at-a-time determination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251700/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a masked array (y_t, t, G, M) where y_t is a hybrid of the noised solution and the current partial solution (selected by mask M), t is a diffusion time step, G is the graph, and M is a binary mask indicating which decision variables are already determined. The output is a probability heatmap H over all M decision variables (nodes for MIS/MVC/MCl/MCut, edges for TSP/ATSP), from which a determination operator greedily selects the most confident variables to fix in each round. The mask M is updated after each determination round, creating an iterative partial-to-full solving loop rather than a single inference.

## Method

COExpander instantiates the proposed Adaptive Expansion (AE) paradigm by combining a consistency-model-based global predictor (adapted from Fast-T2T) with a partial-solution prompting mechanism. During training, a random mask M (sampled from a mixture of Dirac-delta-at-zero and uniform distributions) reveals a subset of the optimal solution to the network, teaching it to predict the full Bernoulli distribution conditioned on partial guidance; the training loss is a supervised binary cross-entropy reformulated via the triangle inequality from the consistency objective. At solving time, the model iteratively generates heatmaps and a problem-specific determination operator D fixes the most confident variables (with constraint propagation) in each round, updating the mask to prompt the next inference. This 'partial-to-full' multi-step process replaces both the 'noise-to-full' one-shot GP decoding and the 'next-token' LC autoregression, achieving adaptive step sizes that balance global information with local constraint satisfaction.

## Results

COExpander reduces the average optimality drop across 6 COPs (MIS, MCl, MVC, MCut, TSP, ATSP) from 3.807% (previous SOTA) to 0.657%, with a 4.0× speedup. On TSP-1K, COExpander achieves 0.64% drop in 2.43s, outperforming Fast-T2T (0.74%, 1.93s) and DIFUSCO (1.31%, 7.89s). On ATSP-500, COExpander reaches 1.57% drop versus 9.02% for GOAL and 8.13% for GOAL+Beam-16. On ultra-large RB-GIANT (MCl), COExpander is the first neural solver to surpass Gurobi(3600s) by 1.9% with a 1134× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COExpander outperforms all GP baselines (DIFUSCO, Fast-T2T, DiffUCO, GCN, GNNGLS, DIMES, Meta-EGN) on every problem: e.g., on TSP-500 it achieves 0.25% drop vs. 0.50% for Fast-T2T and 0.92% for DIFUSCO, while on MIS-SATLIB it reaches 0.16% vs. 0.25% for Fast-T2T. Against LC baselines, COExpander is substantially stronger on ATSP (1.57% vs. 9.02% for GOAL on ATSP-500) and competitive on TSP (0.64% vs. 1.71% for BQ-NCO+Beam-16 on TSP-1K), while also being 4–10× faster. Among AE-family methods, COExpander beats LwD (5.34% vs. 12.80% drop on ER-700-800 MIS) and DualOPT (0.64% vs. 0.83% on TSP-1K). On ultra-large instances, COExpander is the first neural solver to exceed Gurobi(3600s) on MCl-RB-GIANT (84.12 vs. 81.52 objective) with a 1134× speedup. The one notable loss is on CVRP, where COExpander (5.34% drop on CVRP-500) underperforms RL4CO+Classic-LS (4.17%), which the authors attribute to limitations of the consistency-model backbone for complex capacity constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

COExpander positions itself as a third paradigm (Adaptive Expansion) bridging the two dominant approaches in neural combinatorial optimization: Global Prediction (one-shot heatmap decoding, e.g., DIFUSCO, Fast-T2T, DiffUCO) and Local Construction (autoregressive step-by-step building, e.g., AM, POMO, BQ-NCO, GOAL). Its backbone is the optimization-consistency model from Fast-T2T (Li et al., 2024), which itself builds on DIFUSCO's graph diffusion framework and the self-consistency principle of Song et al. (2023), while the GCN architecture follows Joshi et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure is the 'partial-to-full' prompting mechanism—conditioning the global predictor on a masked partial solution—and the adaptive determination operator that expands the set of fixed variables by variable amounts per step, rather than fixing all at once (GP) or one at a time (LC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a unified, model-agnostic paradigm (AE) with concrete SOTA results across six COPs (MIS, MCl, MVC, MCut, TSP, ATSP), reducing average optimality drop from 3.807% to 0.657% with a 4× speedup, and is the first neural solver to beat Gurobi(3600s) on ultra-large (10K-node) instances. The re-standardization of 29 benchmarks and re-implementation of baseline solvers (notably revealing that the widely-used LKH Python package silently disabled 2-opt/3-opt, causing 97–99% performance degradation) addresses a real reproducibility gap in the NCO literature. Open questions include whether AE extends to heavily constrained problems like CVRP (where COExpander currently underperforms), whether the iterative determination can be learned via RL rather than fixed greedy heuristics, and whether coupling outer mask updates with inner diffusion steps yields further gains.
