## Problem

Existing multi-modal model editing methods fail to dynamically determine the influence scope of each fact, leading to an imbalanced generality-locality trade-off: methods like IKE over-generalize by editing unrelated facts, while GRACE over-localizes by failing to generalize to related inputs. No prior work explicitly formulates or addresses this trade-off in the multi-modal setting, and no dedicated benchmark exists to evaluate it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on VQA-style inputs: an RGB image paired with a text question prompt, producing a text answer. For each edit, the system consumes a (image, text, new_answer) triple and internally generates a positive sample (rephrased text prompt via the backbone LLM, same image) and a negative sample (a pure black image) to calibrate the influence radius. The codebook stores averaged layer embeddings as keys, so at inference the input embedding from layer l-1 is compared against all stored keys using a distance function (Euclidean or cosine) to decide whether to activate an edited transformation or pass through the original layer.

## Method

BalancEdit wraps a single selected layer of a pre-trained VLM (e.g., layer 31 of MiniGPT-4 or BLIP-2 OPT) with a discrete codebook module that stores three components per edit: a key (averaged embedding from the preceding layer), a transformation (the fine-tuned weights of that layer encoding the new fact), and a dynamic influence radius. The radius is computed as ε = (1−α)·d(Pos, k) + α·d(Neg, k), where the positive sample is a GPT-4-rephrased text prompt and the negative sample is a black image, both processed through the frozen backbone to obtain embeddings. At inference, if the input embedding falls within any key's radius, the corresponding edited transformation replaces the original layer; otherwise the original layer processes the input unchanged. This mechanism requires no pre-training data, no modification of backbone weights, and supports sequential edits by appending new codebook entries, with conflict resolution that discards superseded entries.

## Results

BalancEdit achieves 100% editing success accuracy (Acc) across all dataset-backbone combinations, while attaining the highest harmonic mean (HM) of T-Gen, I-Gen, and Loc. On the challenging OKEDIT dataset, BalancEdit reaches HM of 71.58 (MiniGPT-4) and 71.85 (BLIP-2 OPT), outperforming the strongest baseline MEND by 10–20 percentage points. On the simpler MMEDIT dataset, BalancEdit scores 88.08 and 89.14 HM, surpassing MEND by ~3 points and GRACE by 10–35 points. The method requires no pre-training data and completes a single edit in ~8 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BalancEdit outperforms all three baselines (IKE, MEND, GRACE) on the harmonic mean (HM) metric across every dataset-backbone combination, confirming its superior balance between generality and locality. Against MEND (the strongest baseline), BalancEdit gains +2.65 HM on MMEDIT/MiniGPT-4 (88.08 vs 85.43), +3.85 on MMEDIT/BLIP-2 (89.14 vs 85.29), +10.51 on OKEDIT/MiniGPT-4 (71.58 vs 61.07), and +20.15 on OKEDIT/BLIP-2 (71.85 vs 51.70). Against GRACE, which prioritizes locality at the expense of generality, BalancEdit achieves substantially higher HM (e.g., 71.58 vs 37.84 on OKEDIT/MiniGPT-4) while also improving locality by 20–80% over other baselines. Against IKE, which over-generalizes by rephrasing the nearest fact, BalancEdit improves HM by 33–60 points (e.g., 71.58 vs 38.68 on OKEDIT/MiniGPT-4). Notably, BalancEdit requires no pre-training data (unlike IKE and MEND) and no pre-training time, while completing edits in ~8 seconds versus GRACE's ~33 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

BalancEdit directly extends GRACE (Hartvigsen et al., 2024), which introduced discrete key-value adaptors for lifelong model editing in language models, by porting the codebook architecture to vision-language models and adding a dynamic per-fact influence radius. It departs from prior multi-modal editing work (IKE, MEND, GRACE adapted to VLMs) by explicitly formulating the generality-locality trade-off as a first-class, per-edit property rather than treating all facts as having uniform influence scope. The OKEDIT dataset extends the earlier MMEDIT benchmark with higher-quality diffusion-generated images and semantically harder locality samples drawn from OKVQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly frame and evaluate the generality-locality trade-off in multi-modal model editing, providing both a principled mechanism (dynamic radius via positive/negative samples) and a dedicated benchmark (OKEDIT) for measuring it. The plug-and-play, weight-preserving codebook design makes it practical for spot-fixing deployed VLMs between retraining cycles. Key open questions remain: inference speed degrades with codebook size due to similarity search, multi-hop edits (where a fact change cascades through chained knowledge) are not handled, and the method is validated only on VQA with two backbones, leaving open whether the dynamic radius generalizes to other multi-modal tasks or larger model families.
