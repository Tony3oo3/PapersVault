## Problem

Existing generative ZSL methods synthesize unseen-class visual features from scratch (Gaussian noise) conditioned on expert-annotated attribute vectors, which suffers from two fundamental issues: insufficient data for the generator to capture high-dimensional distributions, and heavy reliance on labor-intensive annotations that prevent generalization to new scenes. The paper asks whether a generative ZSL model can instead *induce* unseen-class features from similar seen-class samples using only weak class semantic vectors (e.g., CLIP text embeddings of class names).

## Input / Output

The method consumes 512-dimensional CLIP visual features of seen-class training samples and CLIP text-encoder embeddings of class names as weak class semantic vectors. It produces 512-dimensional synthesized visual features for each unseen class (1600–5000 per class depending on dataset), which are then combined with real seen-class features to train a standard softmax classifier. Notably, the generation is inductive: the encoder takes actual referent-class samples (top-2 by cosine similarity, further mixed via 0.8/0.2 mixup) rather than pure noise, and the decoder is conditioned on the target class's refined semantic vector.

## Method

GenZSL replaces imagination-based generation with an inductive variational autoencoder (IVAE) that transforms referent seen-class samples into target-class features. Three key mechanisms enable this: (1) Class Diversity Promotion applies SVD to the matrix of CLIP text embeddings and removes the first principal component, making class semantic vectors nearly orthogonal (mean inter-class similarity drops from 0.57 to ~0.00002 on CUB) while preserving class relationships; (2) Semantically Similar Sample Selection picks the top-k seen classes most cosine-similar to each target class as referent sources; (3) The IVAE (MLP encoder/decoder) encodes referent samples conditioned on the target class vector, samples a latent code, and decodes target-class features, optimized by a KL divergence, a target-class reconstruction loss (vision-level boosting), and a CLIP-based contrastive information-boosting loss (semantic-level boosting) that maximizes similarity between synthesized features and the target class embedding. This inductive paradigm converges 60× faster than f-VAEGAN on AWA2 while achieving 24.7% higher accuracy, and requires no expert-annotated attributes.

## Results

Under the GZSL setting, GenZSL achieves harmonic mean (H) of 57.4% on CUB, 47.0% on SUN, and 87.4% on AWA2, setting new state-of-the-art on SUN and AWA2 while ranking second on CUB (behind ViFR at 60.1%). Under the CZSL setting, GenZSL reaches 73.5% accuracy on SUN and 92.2% on AWA2, outperforming all embedding-based and generative baselines. Notably, GenZSL uses only weak class semantic vectors (CLIP text embeddings of class names) while most competitors rely on expert-annotated attributes, and it trains 60× faster than f-VAEGAN on AWA2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenZSL achieves the best GZSL harmonic mean on SUN (47.0% vs. 45.5% for CLSWGAN+DSP) and AWA2 (87.4% vs. 74.7% for f-VAEGAN†), while ranking second on CUB (57.4% vs. 60.1% for ViFR). Against the primary imagination-based baseline f-VAEGAN (strong attributes), GenZSL gains +5.7% H on SUN and +23.9% H on AWA2, and even outperforms f-VAEGAN with CLIP features (†) by +22.1% H on CUB and +12.7% H on AWA2. In the CZSL setting, GenZSL leads all 32 baselines on both SUN (73.5% acc vs. 71.5% for CVsC) and AWA2 (92.2% acc vs. 82.5% for VADS), with a minimum 20.3% gain over imagination-based generative methods on AWA2. Compared to VLM-based methods using the same weak CLIP text embeddings (Table 4), GenZSL surpasses CLIP (55.0% H) and CoOp+SHIP (57.1% H) on CUB, while imagination-based methods like f-VAEGAN drop from 52.9% to 35.3% H when switching from strong to weak semantic vectors—highlighting that induction-based generation is far more robust to limited side information.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenZSL departs from the dominant generative ZSL paradigm (f-VAEGAN, CLSWGAN, and related GAN/VAE methods) that synthesizes unseen-class features from scratch (Gaussian noise) conditioned on expert-annotated attribute vectors. Instead, it reframes generation as an induction problem inspired by cognitive psychology: a variational autoencoder (building on the conditional VAE of Sohn et al., 2015) transforms referent seen-class samples into target-class samples, guided by weak CLIP text embeddings rather than strong semantic annotations. The class diversity promotion module (SVD-based removal of the principal component) is a lightweight preprocessing step that addresses the known issue of CLIP text embeddings lacking fine-grained discriminability, and the overall pipeline is architecturally simple (MLP encoder-decoder) compared to the multi-scale GAN architectures of prior generative ZSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the dependency on expert-annotated attributes, which makes generative ZSL applicable to arbitrary domains without costly annotation pipelines, and in the substantial training-speed advantage (up to 60× faster than f-VAEGAN) that lowers the barrier to iterative experimentation. The induction-versus-imagination framing offers a conceptual lens that could inform other data-augmentation or few-shot generation tasks beyond ZSL. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very fine-grained or open-vocabulary settings where CLIP text embeddings are weakest, whether the simple MLP backbone can be replaced by more expressive architectures without losing the efficiency advantage, and whether the induction paradigm generalizes to image-level (pixel-space) generation rather than feature-space synthesis.
