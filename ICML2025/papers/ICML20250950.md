## Problem

DDPMs require hundreds to thousands of sequential denoising steps, creating a major inference bottleneck for real-time applications such as robotics and interactive image generation. Prior parallelization approaches based on Picard/fixed-point iterations either leave a small but non-zero error in the samples or require restrictive smoothness assumptions (e.g., uniform Lipschitz continuity of the score function) for theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether DDPMs can be parallelized with zero quality loss and only a bounded second-moment assumption on the data distribution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained DDPM with no retraining or architectural modification, parallelizing only the inference loop. For image generation it consumes text prompts and produces images via latent diffusion (StableDiffusion-v2) or pixel-space diffusion (3×256×256 LSUN Church); for robot control it consumes camera observations and produces action sequences of length k=16 in d=7 or d=14 dimensions. The key operational parameter is the speculation length θ, which controls how many future denoising steps are predicted and verified in a single parallel round, with θ=∞ representing the theoretical upper bound on parallelism.

## Method

The paper proves a hidden exchangeability property: via the equivalence between DDPMs and Stochastic Localization (SL), the increments of the denoising trajectory become exchangeable random variables after an appropriate time/scale reparametrization, so the conditional distribution of any future increment equals that of the immediately next increment. This enables Autospeculative Decoding (ASD), which at each step a makes a single model call to compute the proposal mean, uses it to speculate all future target means in one parallel round, and then verifies each speculation via a Gaussian Rejection Sampler (GRS) that simultaneously samples from the target distribution and checks agreement in O(1) time using a reflection-coupling construction. No auxiliary draft model is needed—the DDPM speculates about itself—and the output is identically distributed to sequential DDPM, guaranteeing zero quality loss. Under a bounded second-moment assumption, ASD achieves a Õ(K^{1/3}) parallel speedup over K sequential steps, with empirical wall-clock speedups of 1.8–4× on image generation and robot control tasks.

## Results

ASD achieves 1.8–4× wall-clock speedup over sequential DDPM across image generation and robot control tasks, with up to 7× algorithmic speedup (reduction in sequential neural network calls) on Robomimic diffusion policies. On StableDiffusion-v2 (1000 denoising steps), ASD-∞ yields 1.9× algorithmic speedup and a peak 1.82× wall-clock speedup with θ=6 on 8 A40 GPUs. On the LSUN Church pixel-space model, algorithmic speedup reaches 3.1×. Critically, sample quality is preserved: CLIP scores (32.0–32.3 vs. 32.1 for DDPM), FID scores (13.2–14.3 vs. 14.3), and Robomimic success rates (within ±3% across all environments) are statistically indistinguishable from vanilla DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla sequential DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), which serves as both the quality reference and the speed reference. ASD matches DDPM on all quality metrics: CLIP scores on COCO2017 captions are 32.0–32.3 for ASD variants vs. 32.1 for DDPM (Table 1); FID on LSUN Church is 13.2 for ASD-4/6/8 and 14.3 for ASD-∞ vs. 14.3 for DDPM (Table 2); and Robomimic success rates are within the standard error of DDPM across Square, Transport, and Tool Hang (Table 3). On speed, ASD wins decisively: it reduces the number of sequential model calls by 2–7× (algorithmic speedup) and achieves 1.8–4× wall-clock acceleration depending on the task and hardware. The paper positions this as an error-free parallelization (exact sampler) in contrast to prior parallel methods (Shih et al., 2024; Gupta et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024) that introduce small approximation errors via Picard iterations, though those prior methods are not directly benchmarked in the experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts speculative decoding from LLMs (Leviathan et al., 2023) to diffusion models, but eliminates the need for a separate draft model by exploiting a newly discovered structural property: the hidden exchangeability of DDPM increments under a Stochastic Localization reparametrization (building on Eldan, 2013 and Montanari, 2023). It departs from prior parallel DDPM approaches (Shih et al., 2024; Pokle et al., 2022) that rely on Picard/fixed-point iterations and leave residual error, instead achieving provably error-free sampling. The adaptive complexity analysis draws on the parallel sampling framework of Anari et al. (2024a) for any-order autoregressive models, extended here to the continuous-space setting where the token space is infinite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hidden exchangeability property is a fundamental structural insight that reframes DDPMs as any-order autoregressive models over increments, potentially enabling the transfer of a broader toolkit of autoregressive optimization techniques to the diffusion setting. The error-free guarantee without Lipschitz continuity assumptions on the score function is a meaningful theoretical advance over prior parallel methods whose guarantees become vacuous for non-smooth distributions. Practical limitations include the modest 1.8–4× wall-clock speedup (constrained by multi-GPU data transfer overheads) and the sublinear O(K^{1/3}) theoretical speedup; open questions include extending the framework to discrete diffusion models and whether similar exchangeability structures exist in other generative model families.
