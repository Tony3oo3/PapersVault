## Problem

Existing multi-task model merging methods (sparsification, orthogonality promotion) alleviate task conflicts by discarding task-specific parameters, inevitably degrading performance as the number of tasks grows. They also overlook the fundamental merging objective—ensuring the merged model performs comparably to each task-specific model—and fail to explicitly preserve shared knowledge across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252637/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the pre-trained model weights θ₀ and the fine-tuned model weights {θᵢ} for n tasks (no training or test data required), computing task vectors τᵢ = θᵢ − θ₀ as the sole signal. It outputs a single merged model θ* = θ₀ + Σᵢ λᵢ(τᵢ + Δ) that is deployed for all tasks simultaneously at standard model size, supporting parallelized static inference. The approach is plug-and-play: it can be layered on top of existing merging strategies (Task Arithmetic, AdaMerging) without modifying their core logic, and operates layer-wise on linear layers only.

## Method

DOGE frames merging as a constrained optimization problem—minimizing the per-task loss gap between the merged model and individual models, subject to preserving the shared subspace—and solves it via adaptive projective gradient descent. A data-free objective is derived by first-order Taylor expansion around θ₀, with the gradient ∇Lⱼ(θ₀) approximated by −τⱼ (interpreting task vectors as accumulated gradients under the NTK assumption), yielding a loss optimizable purely from model parameters. A modification vector Δ is introduced to each task vector and optimized via gradient descent; to retain shared knowledge, the gradient is projected orthogonal to a shared subspace S_share constructed from the top-k SVD singular vectors of all task vectors (deduplicated via a second SVD), so updates only occur in directions that do not alter shared representations. Finally, task-aware layer-wise merging coefficients λᵢˡ = η/‖τᵢˡ‖ are computed training-free, treating λ as an adaptive learning rate that suppresses dominant tasks and balances gradient magnitudes across tasks and layers.

## Results

On the 8-task ViT-B/32 vision benchmark, DOGE TA achieves 80.7% average top-1 accuracy, improving over Task Arithmetic (69.1%) by 11.6 percentage points, while DOGE AM reaches 85.9%, surpassing AdaMerging (80.1%) by 5.8 points and approaching the Traditional MTL upper bound of 88.9%. On ViT-L/14, DOGE AM attains 92.6% average accuracy, nearly matching Traditional MTL (93.5%). For NLP, DOGE TA on Flan-T5-large achieves 88.0% average across GLUE tasks, closing the gap to individually fine-tuned models (89.6%) to just 1.6 points under fully data-free conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOGE TA outperforms all data-free baselines on ViT-B/32: it beats Task Arithmetic by 11.6 points (80.7 vs 69.1), Ties-Merging by 8.3 points, Concrete TA by 3.4 points, and AWD TA by 5.5 points. Among test-time adaptation methods, DOGE AM surpasses AdaMerging by 5.8 points (85.9 vs 80.1), AdaMerging++ by 4.8 points, AWD AM by 3.0 points, and Concrete AM by 1.9 points, while remaining fully data-free in its DOGE TA variant. On Flan-T5-base (GLUE), DOGE TA achieves 79.9% average, outperforming Task Arithmetic (77.4%), Ties-Merging (77.5%), and AdaMerging++ (78.3%) under data-free conditions. On the 13B LLM generative benchmark, the method reaches 90.0% normalized average, exceeding TIES+DARE (87.2%) and TA+DARE (84.0%). The method does not surpass the Traditional MTL upper bound (88.9% on ViT-B/32) or individually fine-tuned models (90.8%), but DOGE AM narrows the gap to just 3.0 points without requiring any training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOGE builds directly on the Task Arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the Taylor-expansion-based data-free objective from Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023), but departs from the dominant paradigm of resolving task conflicts by sparsifying or orthogonalizing task vectors (Ties-Merging, AWD). Instead, it reframes merging as a constrained optimization problem solved via projective gradient descent, introducing a modification vector Δ optimized within a shared SVD-derived subspace and reinterpreting merging coefficients as adaptive learning rates. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play complement to existing approaches, demonstrated by its application on top of both Task Arithmetic and AdaMerging (Yang et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled optimization formulation for model merging, DOGE offers a more systematic alternative to heuristic sparsification strategies, and the shared-subspace constraint addresses a previously overlooked failure mode: loss of shared knowledge when tasks are similar. The task-aware λ (inverse-norm scaling) is a simple, training-free mechanism that eliminates the need for grid search or test-time adaptation. Limitations include the reliance on the Neural Tangent Kernel approximation (which may degrade for large fine-tuning steps), restriction to homogeneous architectures, and the computational cost of SVD on large models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to heterogeneous model merging and whether the Taylor approximation remains valid under aggressive fine-tuning regimes.
