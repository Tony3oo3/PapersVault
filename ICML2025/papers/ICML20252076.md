## Problem

Safe RL with instantaneous hard constraints in non-star-convex decision spaces (e.g., collision-avoidance regions in autonomous driving) had no provably efficient algorithm, and the existing star-convex analysis by Amani et al. (2021) contained an error in its covering-number argument that invalidated the regret guarantee. The core difficulty is that the value function is maximized over a data-dependent safe set, so small changes in safety parameters can cause drastic shifts in the value function, breaking standard covering-number techniques from unconstrained RL.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a state observation s, a known feature map φ(s,a) ∈ R^d, a safety threshold τ, and a known zero-cost initial safe action a_s^0 per state. It outputs a deterministic safe policy π^k that selects actions satisfying c_h(s,a) ≤ τ at every step with high probability, and achieves a regret bound of Õ((1+1/τ)√(log(1/τ) d³ H⁴ K)) over K episodes. A distinguishing requirement is that the feature space F_s must satisfy either star-convexity (Assumption 3.1) or the weaker Local Point Assumption (Assumption 3.2), which only demands a small ball around the initial safe point and local connectivity near the constraint boundary.

## Method

NCS-LSVI is a two-phase model-free algorithm. In Phase 1 (pure safe exploration, K' episodes), the agent samples uniformly from the boundary of an ε-ball around φ(s, a_s^0) on the (d−1)-dimensional hyperplane, stabilizing the estimated safe set so that small perturbations in safety parameters no longer cause drastic changes. In Phase 2 (safe exploitation-exploration), the agent constructs an estimated safe set via Recursive Least Squares on the feature difference φ(s,a) − φ(s,a_s^0), and selects actions by maximizing an optimistic Q-function (with a standard exploration bonus plus a constraint-distance bonus g_{h,ν}^k) over the estimated safe set. The key technical contribution is the Objective-Constraint Decomposition (OCD) technique, which bounds the covering number of the value-function class by introducing an intermediate value function that shares the objective of one function and the constraint of another, thereby separating the effect of objective changes from constraint changes. Under star-convexity, OCD applies directly (K'=0); under the Local Point Assumption, it applies only after the pure exploration phase has stabilized the safe set, yielding the first regret guarantee for non-star-convex settings.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left((1+1/\tau)\sqrt{\log(1/\tau)\, d^3 H^4 K}\right)$ for star-convex settings and $\tilde{O}\left((1+1/\tau)\sqrt{\log(1/\tau)\, d^3 H^4 K} + 1/(\epsilon^2\iota^2)\right)$ for non-star-convex settings, with zero safety constraint violations throughout learning (with high probability). In the autonomous driving merging simulation (K=1000 episodes, H=3, d=6), NCS-LSVI with K'=2000 pure exploration episodes achieves sublinear regret, while a safe baseline restricted to an ε-neighborhood of the initial policy remains at a constant regret level. The unconstrained LSVI-UCB baseline achieves lower regret but accumulates linearly growing constraint violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NCS-LSVI against two baselines in the autonomous driving merging scenario. LSVI-UCB (Jin et al., 2020) achieves lower cumulative regret than NCS-LSVI but violates the lane-keeping safety constraint at every episode, with cumulative violations growing linearly over K episodes (Figure 9). A sub-optimal safe baseline that restricts actions to an ε-neighborhood of the initial safe policy never violates constraints but incurs constant (non-decreasing) regret, as it never explores beyond the safe neighborhood (Figure 7). NCS-LSVI with K'=2000 achieves sublinear regret while maintaining zero constraint violations across all episodes (Figures 3, 6), demonstrating that the two-phase design (pure safe exploration followed by safe exploitation-exploration) successfully balances safety and learning efficiency in the non-star-convex setting. In the star-convex variant (no collision-avoidance module), NCS-LSVI with K'=0 also achieves sublinear regret with zero violations, confirming that the pure exploration phase is unnecessary when star-convexity holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safe RL framework of Amani et al. (2021), which handled instantaneous hard constraints in star-convex decision spaces within linear MDPs, by (i) correcting a fundamental error in their covering-number analysis via the novel Objective-Constraint Decomposition (OCD) technique, and (ii) generalizing to non-star-convex feature spaces through the Local Point Assumption and the two-phase NCS-LSVI algorithm. The Q-function estimation and optimistic exploration machinery are inherited from Jin et al. (2020) for unconstrained linear MDPs, but the key departure is recognizing that data-dependent feasible sets break the standard contraction-based covering argument, necessitating entirely new bounding techniques. The paper also distinguishes itself from the large body of constrained RL work with cumulative (expected) constraints by focusing on instantaneous hard constraints that must hold at every time step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient algorithm for safe RL with instantaneous hard constraints in non-star-convex decision spaces, a setting that naturally arises in autonomous driving and robotics where collision-avoidance constraints create disjoint feasible regions. The identification that covering numbers can become infinite without structural assumptions (Lemma 5.3) is a fundamental insight that clarifies why prior star-convex-based methods cannot simply be extended. Open questions include whether the 1/(ε²ι²) dependence in the regret bound is fundamental, whether the approach extends beyond linear MDPs to nonlinear function approximation, and whether the Local Point Assumption can be further relaxed. The correction of Amani et al. (2021) also has broader implications for the constrained RL literature that relied on their analysis.
