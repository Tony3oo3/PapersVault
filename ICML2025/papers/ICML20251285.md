## Problem

Sobolev IPM is a central tool in theoretical machine learning (e.g., GAN convergence rates, ReLU discriminator bounds), yet no efficient algorithmic approach exists to compute it, blocking practical use. This paper closes that gap by showing that a natural regularization of the Sobolev norm constraint yields a closed-form, fast-to-evaluate distance for probability measures on a graph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251285/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two probability measures whose supports lie on the nodes of a connected, undirected, geodetic graph (unique shortest paths from a fixed root node z₀) with positive edge lengths, and outputs a scalar distance Ŝ_p(μ,ν). In the discrete case (Theorem 3.5), the computation reduces to a sum over edges e∈E with precomputed coefficients β_e, giving O(|E_{μ,ν}|) per pair after a one-time Dijkstra preprocessing of O(|E|+|V|log|V|). The distance can also be exponentiated into positive definite kernels k_{Ŝ_p}(μ,ν)=exp(−t·Ŝ_p(μ,ν)) for use in kernelized SVM classification.

## Method

The key insight (Theorem 3.2) is that the Sobolev norm ‖f‖_{W^{1,p}} is equivalent to the weighted L^p-norm of the graph derivative f′ with an explicit weight ŵ(x)=1+λ(Λ(x)), where Λ(x) is the subgraph of points whose shortest path from the root passes through x. Replacing the intractable Sobolev-ball constraint with the unit ball of this weighted L^{p′}-norm on f′ (Definition 3.3) turns the IPM supremum into a dual-norm evaluation, yielding the closed-form Ŝ_p(μ,ν)^p = ∫ ŵ(x)^{1−p}|μ(Λ(x))−ν(Λ(x))|^p λ(dx) (Theorem 3.4). For node-supported measures with the length measure, this further collapses to a finite edge sum with coefficients β_e that depend only on the graph topology and edge lengths (Theorem 3.5), making the distance independent of the physical-graph assumption. The paper additionally proves Ŝ_p is a metric, equivalent to the original Sobolev IPM and to Sobolev transport, equals W₁ on trees, and is negative definite for 1≤p≤2, which via Berg's theorem guarantees positive definite and infinitely divisible kernels.

## Results

The regularized Sobolev IPM kernels achieve SVM classification accuracy comparable to or exceeding Sobolev transport (ST), optimal transport (OT), and tree-Wasserstein (TW) kernels across all six datasets (TWITTER, RECIPE, CLASSIC, AMAZON, Orbit, MPEG7) on both G_Log and G_Sqrt graphs. The headline computational advantage is dramatic: on the Orbit dataset, evaluating the OT kernel Gram matrix takes over 39 hours, while the regularized Sobolev IPM kernels (for p = 1, 1.5, 2) complete in under 34 minutes — a speedup of several orders of magnitude. The computation time for the regularized Sobolev IPM is comparable to Sobolev transport, confirming scalability to large-scale settings (e.g., >29 million measure pairs on AMAZON).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (1) Sobolev transport (ST) kernels k_ST^1.5 and k_ST^2, (2) standard OT kernel k_OT with ground cost d_G, and (3) tree-Wasserstein kernel k_TW. In terms of speed, the regularized Sobolev IPM is several orders of magnitude faster than OT (39+ hours vs. <34 minutes on Orbit) and comparable to ST. In terms of classification accuracy, the regularized Sobolev IPM kernels perform on par with or slightly better than ST kernels across most datasets. The OT kernel, while sometimes competitive in accuracy, is empirically indefinite (requiring Gram matrix regularization) and its performance is degraded in most experiments due to this indefiniteness. The TW kernel, which is positive definite but uses only partial graph information, underperforms OT on RECIPE but outperforms it on CLASSIC and AMAZON for both graph types. Overall, the regularized Sobolev IPM kernels match or exceed all three baselines in accuracy while being significantly faster than OT and on par with ST in runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Sobolev transport framework of Le et al. (2022), reusing its graph-based Sobolev space, graph derivative, length measure, and geodetic graph setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing the intractable Sobolev norm constraint on the critic function with a weighted L^p norm on its graph derivative, an equivalence established via a new norm-comparison theorem (Theorem 3.2). This regularization strategy is analogous in spirit to how Sobolev GAN (Mroueh et al., 2018) and Fisher GAN (Mroueh & Sercu, 2017) constrain critics by gradient or function norms, but here it is applied to the full Sobolev IPM on a graph to unlock a closed-form solution. The paper also connects to tree-Wasserstein (Le et al., 2019) by showing exact equality at p=1 on tree topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form expression resolves a long-standing computational barrier for Sobolev IPM, making it practical for large-scale distribution comparison (e.g., 29M+ measure pairs in the AMAZON dataset) where standard OT is infeasible. The negative-definiteness result (for p ≤ 2) and the resulting positive-definite kernels with infinite divisibility open a path to kernelized learning on graph-supported measures without the indefinite-kernel issues that plague OT-based kernels. Open questions include extending to unbalanced measures (different total masses), going beyond Sobolev geometry to Besov or other function-space norms, and characterizing the exact relationship between the regularized Sobolev IPM and p-Wasserstein distance for p > 1.
