## Problem

Theoretical analysis of diffusion models has been confined to single-worker settings, where accurate score function estimation is assumed. In distributed training with resource-constrained workers, this assumption breaks down due to sparse training, data heterogeneity, and straggler effects, yet no generation error bound existed for such scenarios. This paper establishes the first theoretical performance guarantee for distributed diffusion models under resource limitations.

## Input / Output

The method starts from a standard Gaussian noise vector Y_{n,0} ~ π_d in R^d and iteratively applies a discretized reverse SDE (exponential integrator) over K time intervals to produce generated image samples approximating each worker's data distribution q_{n,δ}. In experiments, the U-Net score estimator is trained on 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, SVHN) and 28×28 grayscale images (Fashion-MNIST), partitioned across 10 workers. Each worker trains only a pruned subset of the D-dimensional parameter vector θ based on its local resource mask, making the effective model size heterogeneous across workers.

## Method

The paper proposes a distributed diffusion training mechanism where each worker independently performs forward noising and reverse denoising with locally scheduled discrete-time noise, learning a shared score function s_θ(·) via denoising score matching. To accommodate resource heterogeneity, each worker generates a binary mask m_{r,n} ∈ {0,1}^D, prunes the global model to θ_{r,n,0} = θ_r ⊙ m_{r,n}, performs S local gradient steps on the pruned subset, and the server aggregates coordinate-wise (averaging only over workers that retain each coordinate). The theoretical core uses Girsanov's theorem to decompose the KL divergence between the ideal and actual data distributions into three components: time discretization error (bounded via Itô calculus), distributed training error (bounded via a novel convergence analysis of pruned distributed SGD with coordinate-wise aggregation), and a constant offset from denoising score matching substitution. By carefully selecting hyperparameters (learning rate η, discretization step κ, total time T, and number of intervals K), the generation error is shown to be dominated by distributed training dynamics, yielding a bound linear in data dimension d that matches state-of-the-art single-worker results.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the full-model FedDM-F achieves IS 4.59 and FID 73.73, while aggressive random pruning (R-FedDM-S) degrades to IS 3.60 and FID 111.21. Top-k pruning is more robust, with T-FedDM-M retaining IS 4.54 and FID 80.42, close to the full model. On Fashion-MNIST, higher pruning levels paradoxically improve FID (R-FedDM-S reaches FID 49.94 vs. 87.59 for the full model), attributed to a regularization effect on simpler data distributions. On SVHN, random pruning at level L reduces FID from 163.36 to 93.78, while Top-k pruning at level S inflates FID to 193.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external diffusion model baselines; instead, it ablates pruning strategy (Random vs. Top-k) and pruning level (F, L, M, S) within the FedDM framework. On complex datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN), the full model (FedDM-F) consistently achieves the best IS, and Top-k pruning preserves quality far better than random pruning at equivalent sparsity (e.g., on CIFAR-10, T-FedDM-M retains IS 4.54 vs. R-FedDM-M's 4.01, and FID 80.42 vs. 104.53). On the simpler Fashion-MNIST dataset, the trend reverses: random pruning at higher levels (S) yields the best FID (49.94), outperforming the full model (87.59), which the authors attribute to a regularization effect that eliminates redundant parameters. Top-k pruning on Fashion-MNIST, however, degrades FID as pruning increases (from 87.85 at L to 109.83 at S), suggesting that retaining the largest-magnitude weights is less beneficial when the data distribution is simple. Overall, Top-k pruning is recommended for complex datasets, while random pruning is more suitable for resource-constrained settings with simpler tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate theoretical threads: (1) the Girsanov-based generation error bounds for single-worker diffusion models established by Benton et al. (2024) and Chen et al. (2023a,b), and (2) the distributed SGD convergence analysis with pruning from Lian et al. (2017) and Zhou et al. (2024). It departs from prior work by being the first to incorporate distributed training dynamics—specifically coordinate-wise model aggregation under arbitrary per-worker pruning masks—into the diffusion model generation error bound, extending the single-worker OU-process analysis to a multi-worker, resource-heterogeneous setting. The practical framework builds directly on FedDM (Vora et al., 2024) and DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), adding a sparse-training mechanism to handle worker resource constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical guarantee that distributed diffusion model training under resource heterogeneity can achieve generation error bounds linear in data dimension, matching the best single-worker results, which gives practitioners confidence that federated diffusion training does not fundamentally degrade sample quality. The hyperparameter selection guidance (Remark 4.12) showing that the bound can be made dominated by distributed training dynamics rather than diffusion-specific terms is practically useful for tuning. Open questions include whether the results extend beyond the OU/Variance-Preserving SDE to other noise schedules, whether the pruning error assumption (Assumption 4.2) holds for structured pruning in practice, and how the bounds behave with non-IID data distributions across workers. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments (10 workers, CIFAR-10/SVHN/Fashion-MNIST), leaving scalability to larger models and datasets untested.
