## Problem

Existing tuning-free image personalization methods encode multiple reference images through image-independent operations (averaging or concatenating embeddings), which cannot perform interaction among images to capture consistent visual elements and causes attribute confusion and subject hallucination. Tuning-based methods like LoRA can extract consistent elements but require test-time finetuning for each distinct image group, eliminating zero-shot generalization. The paper addresses the gap of enabling instruction-controlled, multi-reference consistent generation without per-group finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253249/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary number of reference images (each processed at up to 336×336 pixels by the MLLM, with arbitrary aspect ratios preserved) along with a text instruction specifying which consistent visual elements (style, facial identity, character identity, object identity) to capture. It produces a single generated image via Stable Diffusion XL that preserves the specified consistent elements across the reference group. Training supports up to 16 reference images per group, and inference degrades gracefully beyond this limit while maintaining acceptable latency even with 56 references due to the fixed 64-token aggregation.

## Method

EasyRef uses Qwen2-VL-2B as a unified encoder that jointly processes all reference images and the instruction, leveraging the MLLM's multi-image comprehension and instruction-following to extract consistent visual elements. The key architectural contribution is an efficient reference aggregation strategy: 64 learnable reference tokens are appended to the context at the final LLM layer, where bi-directional self-attention followed by an MLP propagates and compresses information across all reference images into a fixed-size representation, projected via a trainable MLP into the diffusion latent space. This conditioning vector is injected into SDXL's U-Net through decoupled cross-attention layers (following IP-Adapter). Training follows a progressive three-stage scheme: (1) alignment pretraining on 13M image-text pairs optimizing only the final LLM layer, reference tokens, and adapters; (2) single-reference finetuning that unfreezes the full MLLM and adds LoRA to the U-Net, augmented with 4M cropped human face images for fine-grained identity preservation; (3) multi-reference finetuning on curated image groups where one image is the target and the rest serve as conditioning inputs with random shuffling and truncation augmentation.

## Results

On the COCO 2017 validation set (single-reference), EasyRef achieves CLIP-I 0.876, CLIP-T 0.621, and DINO-I 0.873, surpassing IP-Adapter-SDXL (0.836/0.617/0.650) by a large margin on DINO-I (+0.223). On the proposed MRBench held-in split (multi-reference), EasyRef scores CLIP-I 0.856, CLIP-T 0.739, DINO-I 0.675, outperforming both LoRA (0.831/0.715/0.654) and IP-Adapter-SDXL (0.768/0.632/0.527). On the held-out split, EasyRef (0.838/0.715/0.653) beats IP-Adapter-SDXL (0.813/0.646/0.611) while LoRA fails entirely, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization. On DreamBench, EasyRef achieves DINO-I 0.651, the best among all tuning-free methods, approaching DreamBooth's 0.668.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO (single-reference), EasyRef dominates all 10 baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over IP-Adapter-SDXL on DINO-I (0.873 vs. 0.650, +0.223) and a modest lead on CLIP-T (0.621 vs. 0.617). On MRBench held-in, EasyRef beats the tuning-based LoRA by +0.025 CLIP-I and +0.021 DINO-I, and crushes IP-Adapter-SDXL by +0.088 CLIP-I and +0.148 DINO-I, confirming that instruction-guided multi-image encoding via MLLM outperforms both per-group finetuning and simple embedding averaging. On MRBench held-out, LoRA fails completely (cannot generalize without per-group finetuning), while EasyRef still leads IP-Adapter-SDXL by +0.025 CLIP-I and +0.042 DINO-I. On DreamBench, EasyRef's DINO-I of 0.651 is the best among all tuning-free methods, surpassing Kosmos-G (0.618), MoMA (0.618), SSR-Encoder (0.612), and IP-Adapter (0.612), though it trails DreamBooth (0.668) by 0.017; on CLIP-T, EasyRef (0.302) is outperformed by MoMA (0.348) and IP-Adapter (0.330), indicating a slight weakness in text alignment for subject-driven generation. Overall, EasyRef wins or ties on the primary image-consistency metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-I) across all three benchmarks while sacrificing a small amount of text alignment (CLIP-T) in the DreamBench setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

EasyRef builds directly on IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention mechanism for injecting image conditions into the U-Net, but replaces IP-Adapter's single-image CLIP embedding pipeline with a multimodal LLM (Qwen2-VL-2B) that jointly encodes multiple reference images and a text instruction. This departs from the broader tuning-free lineage (IP-Adapter, InstantStyle, InstantID) that relies on task-specific encoders (CLIP for style, ArcFace for faces, DINOv2 for subjects) and from tuning-based methods (LoRA, DreamBooth) that require per-group finetuning. The key conceptual shift is treating multi-reference consistency as an instruction-following problem solvable by a single MLLM, rather than as a feature-fusion problem requiring specialized architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, zero-shot nature of EasyRef removes the per-group finetuning bottleneck of LoRA/DreamBooth while avoiding the attribute-confusion and subject-hallucination artifacts of embedding-averaging approaches, making multi-reference personalization practical at scale. The introduction of MRBench (300 curated images across 5 consistency categories with held-in/held-out splits) fills a gap left by DreamBench, which only covers subject-driven generation. Open questions include whether performance can be maintained beyond the 16-image training constraint, whether the progressive training scheme is strictly necessary or a single-stage approach suffices with larger data, and how the method generalizes to video or 3D generation where temporal/spatial consistency across references is even more demanding.
