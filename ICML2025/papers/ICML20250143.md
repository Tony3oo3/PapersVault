## Problem

Vanilla gradient descent applies the raw gradient directly to weights without accounting for the heterogeneous curvature of the loss function across different directions in weight space. While duality maps from optimization theory (mirror descent, natural gradient, steepest descent on normed spaces) address this, no principled, architecture-agnostic procedure existed for constructing such maps for general deep neural networks. The paper also seeks to unify two seemingly disparate methods—maximal update parameterization (μP) for scalability and Shampoo for speed—under a single theoretical framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-layer gradient tensors (d_out × d_in for Linear/Embed, d_out × d_in × k × k for Conv2D) along with architectural metadata: layer type, input/output dimensions, module masses, and sensitivities. It produces a dualized gradient of the same shape as the input, where Linear and Conv2D layers yield scaled semi-orthogonal matrices (all singular values equal in magnitude) and Embed layers yield column-wise RMS-normalized matrices. The dualized gradient is then scaled by a learning rate and subtracted from weights, replacing the raw gradient in the standard update rule.

## Method

Modular dualization is a three-step recursive procedure: (1) assign an induced operator norm to each layer based on its input/output semantics (RMS→RMS for Linear, ℓ₁→RMS for Embed, max-over-kernels RMS→RMS for Conv2D); (2) derive the corresponding layerwise duality map, which for Linear reduces to G → √(d_out/d_in) · UVᵀ via the reduced SVD; (3) compose layerwise maps into a full-architecture map using weighted-max (L∞) combination rules for module composition and concatenation, where module masses control relative importance. The key unifying insight is that the single Linear.dualize map simultaneously recovers μP's spectral update scaling (the √(d_out/d_in) factor) and Shampoo's orthogonal projection (the UVᵀ mapping). For GPU-efficient computation, the SVD-based projection is approximated by a rectangular Newton-Schulz iteration—a cubic or quintic polynomial applied iteratively to the spectrally-normalized gradient—that converges to UVᵀ without computing inverse matrix roots, remaining numerically stable even on low-rank or ill-conditioned matrices.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with MLPs of varying width, the duality-based optimizer maintains a stable optimal learning rate across widths (unlike standard parameterization + Adam, where the optimal LR drifts) while reaching substantially lower final training loss than both SP+Adam and μP+Adam in the same number of steps (Figure 1). In a width-1024 MLP trained for 1000 steps, dualized gradient descent achieves higher training accuracy than spectrally-normalized non-dualized GD and 'erases' a watermarked initial weight pattern, demonstrating that weights move substantially further from initialization in Frobenius norm at large width (Figure 2). The Muon optimizer, built on the Newton-Schulz duality map, set NanoGPT training speed records and was scaled to a 1.5B-parameter transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the duality-based optimizer against three baselines. Against standard parameterization (SP) + Adam, the duality method matches the learning-rate-transfer property (stable optimal LR across width) but reaches noticeably lower final training loss at every width tested. Against μP + Adam (Yang & Hu, 2021), the duality method achieves comparable LR stability but reaches lower loss in the same number of steps, suggesting the duality map captures more of the beneficial geometry than μP's initialization-only correction. Against spectrally-normalized non-dualized gradient descent at width 1024, the dualized method achieves higher training accuracy at the highest stable learning rate and induces substantially larger weight movement (erasing a visible watermark in the weight matrix), challenging the prior belief that very wide networks cannot stray from initialization. The paper positions μP and Shampoo as partial approximations to the full duality map, with the duality-based method subsuming both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modular norm framework of Large et al. (2024) by constructing the full duality map (rather than the simpler modular normalization they used), and unifies two previously separate lines of work—maximal update parameterization (μP) and Shampoo—as partial approximations to a single RMS→RMS duality map. It departs from prior spectral descent (Carlson et al., 2015b) by providing a fully recursive, all-layer construction via the modular norm's L∞ combination, and from duality structure gradient descent (Flynn, 2017) by using max rather than sum to combine layerwise norms, yielding conventional all-layer optimizers. The rectangular Newton-Schulz iteration for computing the SVD-based projection is adapted from the matrix sign function iteration in Higham (2008).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of μP and Shampoo under a single duality map provides a principled, architecture-agnostic recipe for designing fast and scalable optimizers, and the resulting Muon optimizer has already set NanoGPT speed records and scaled to 1.5B-parameter transformers. The finding that duality-based updates cause weights to move substantially further from initialization (erasing a watermark) challenges the widely-held NTK-era belief that wide networks remain near initialization, suggesting the 'numerical paradox' is optimizer-dependent rather than fundamental. Open questions include whether the Newton-Schulz approximation degrades at very large scale, whether the modular type system can be fully automated, and whether the framework extends cleanly to architectures beyond Linear/Embed/Conv2D (e.g., attention with softmax, normalization layers).
