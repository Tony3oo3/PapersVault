## Problem

Ordering operations such as sorting, ranking, and top-k selection are inherently non-differentiable, blocking direct gradient-based training in neural networks. Existing differentiable relaxations suffer from quadratic or worse time complexity, high memory usage that limits scalability to large sequence lengths and k values, lack of closed-form solutions, or inability to return proper probability vectors. The paper asks whether a single, unified, closed-form construction can solve all soft-order problems simultaneously with near-optimal O(n log n) complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real-valued score vector r ∈ ℝⁿ, a selection threshold k ∈ (0, n) that need not be integer, and a learnable temperature parameter α ≠ 0. For top-k it outputs a probability vector p ∈ (0,1)ⁿ with ∑pᵢ = k (elements near 1 for the k largest scores, near 0 otherwise); for ranking it outputs a soft rank vector in (0, n−1)ⁿ; for sorting it outputs a soft-sorted sequence; and for permutations it outputs an n×n doubly stochastic matrix. All outputs are differentiable with respect to r, k, and α, and the α parameter can be jointly optimized during training to control the hardness of the relaxation.

## Method

LapSum constructs all soft-order operations from a single primitive: the sum of Laplace CDFs, Lap-Sum_α(r, x) = Σᵢ Lap_α(x − rᵢ). The key theoretical result is that for the Laplace distribution specifically, Lap-Sum admits a closed-form piecewise expression on each interval between sorted inputs, with coefficients (aᵢ, bᵢ, cᵢ) computable via simple recurrences in O(n) time (parallelizable via prefix scan), and its inverse has an explicit formula involving a square root. This yields O(n log n) total complexity for forward and backward passes (dominated by the initial sort). The derivative of the top-k output with respect to the input has the structured form D = sqᵀ − diag(s), where sᵢ = (1/α)min(pᵢ, 1−pᵢ) and q is a softmax of negative absolute distances, enabling O(n) vector-matrix products instead of materializing the full n×n Jacobian. The same framework extends to soft sorting (via multiple inverse evaluations) and soft permutations (via differences of CDF values at consecutive inverse points), all sharing the same O(n log n) derivative computation.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18, P_j=[0,0,0,0,1]), LapSum achieves 58.07% top-1 and 87.50% top-5 accuracy, outperforming all baselines. On ImageNet-21K-P (ResNeXt-101, P_j=[0,0,0,0,1]), LapSum reaches 37.93% top-1 and 70.67% top-5 accuracy, the best among all compared methods. On ImageNet-1K, LapSum is competitive (85.47% top-1, 97.83% top-5), within 0.2% of the best SinkhornSort result. In runtime and memory benchmarks with k=n/2, LapSum achieves O(n log n) time and O(n) memory, outperforming or matching all competing methods for high-dimensional inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LapSum clearly outperforms NeuralSort (Petersen et al., 2022a) and SoftSort (Prillo & Eisenschlos, 2020) across all datasets, with particularly large margins on CIFAR-100 (58.07% vs 22.58% and 1.01% top-1) and ImageNet-21K-P (37.93% vs 15.87% and 33.61% top-1). Against the strongest competitor SinkhornSort (Cuturi et al., 2019), LapSum wins on CIFAR-100 (58.07% vs 55.62% top-1) and ImageNet-21K-P (37.93% vs 36.93% top-1), while being marginally behind on ImageNet-1K (85.47% vs 85.65% top-1). LapSum also surpasses DiffSortNets (Petersen et al., 2022b) on CIFAR-100 (58.07% vs 52.81%) and ImageNet-21K-P (37.93% vs 35.96%), and Smooth top-k (Berrada et al., 2018) on CIFAR-100 (58.07% vs 53.07%) and ImageNet-21K-P (37.93% vs 34.03%). In the kNN setting (Table 3), LapSum matches or exceeds kNN+OT (Xie et al., 2020) on CIFAR-10 (92.2% vs 84.8%) while being competitive on MNIST (99.4% vs 99.0%). For soft permutation on Large-MNIST, LapSum achieves the best permutation accuracy at n=3 (94.2% vs 93.0% for deterministic NeuralSort from Grover et al., 2019), though it trails slightly at larger n values. In runtime/memory benchmarks, LapSum outperforms or matches all methods including Sander et al.'s Dykstra-based approach, with some competitors failing to complete due to memory limits at high dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the landscape of differentiable order-based operations (soft ranking, soft sorting, soft top-k, soft permutation) under a single CDF-sum framework, then shows that the Laplace distribution specifically yields closed-form inverses with O(n log n) complexity. Prior approaches address these tasks through separate mechanisms—smooth approximations (Berrada et al., 2018), optimal transport (Cuturi et al., 2019; Xie et al., 2020), Sinkhorn iterations (Petersen et al., 2022b), or monotonic sorting networks (Petersen et al., 2022a)—each with different trade-offs in closed-form availability, computational cost, and whether they output probability vectors. LapSum departs from all of these by deriving a single distribution-based construction that simultaneously solves every soft-order problem with a closed-form solution, parallelizable prefix-scan computation, and differentiability with respect to all parameters including the temperature α.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in matching the O(n log n) time and O(n) memory complexity of plain sorting while remaining fully differentiable, which removes the primary bottleneck for applying soft-order losses to high-dimensional problems (e.g., ImageNet-21K-P with 10,450 classes) where prior methods hit memory walls. The trainable α parameter offers an additional degree of freedom for controlling softness that prior methods lack. Limitations include modest accuracy gains over strong baselines (1–2 points on CIFAR-100, ~1 point on ImageNet-21K-P) and the fact that on the Large-MNIST permutation task, LapSum trails stochastic NeuralSort at larger n (33.9% vs 41.8% at n=15). Open questions include whether the Laplace-specific closed form extends to multivariate or structured ordering problems, and whether the efficiency advantage persists under mixed-precision GPU training at extreme scales.
