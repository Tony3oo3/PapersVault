## Problem

Score-matching diffusion models that perfectly learn the ideal score function can only memorize training examples, yet trained convolutional diffusion models generate highly original images far from their training data. This theory-experiment gap remains unexplained: what inductive biases prevent optimal score-matching, and how do they produce structured, creative outputs? The paper identifies two simple CNN biases—locality and translational equivariance—and derives a fully analytic theory that quantitatively predicts individual outputs of trained CNN diffusion models on a case-by-case basis.

## Input / Output

The ELS machine takes an isotropic Gaussian noise sample φ_T and a dictionary of all P×P local patches extracted from the training set, and produces a generated image by numerically integrating the reverse flow over 20 steps. The locality scale P is a single time-dependent hyperparameter that shrinks from large to small (coarse-to-fine) over the reverse process, calibrated to match the effective receptive field of the trained network. All experiments use 32×32 images (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR10, CelebA), and the theory is evaluated by comparing its outputs pixel-by-pixel against trained ResNet or UNet outputs given identical initial noise, reporting per-sample r² values.

## Method

The core contribution is deriving the minimum mean squared error (MMSE) approximation to the ideal score function under two constraints: Ω-locality (each pixel's score depends only on a local P×P neighborhood) and translational equivariance (the same function maps every local patch to a score). This yields the Equivariant Local Score (ELS) machine, in which each pixel independently computes Bayesian posterior weights over all training set patches (not just co-located ones), pulling the pixel toward the center of its most likely source patch. Zero-padding at image boundaries partially breaks equivariance, restoring positional knowledge so that interior, edge, and corner patches only flow to training patches with matching boundary overlap. The mechanism produces 'locally consistent patch mosaics'—exponentially many novel images where every pixel equals the center of its L2-closest training patch—explaining both combinatorial creativity and the notorious fine-scale spatial inconsistencies (e.g., wrong number of limbs) as consequences of excessive locality at late reverse-flow times.

## Results

The boundary-broken ELS machine predicts trained CNN diffusion model outputs with median pixelwise r² of 0.94 (MNIST), 0.90 (FashionMNIST), 0.90 (CIFAR10), and 0.96 (CelebA) for ResNets, and 0.89, 0.93, 0.90, and 0.90 (LS machine) for UNets, respectively. On CIFAR10, the ELS machine also partially predicts a pretrained self-attention-enabled UNet with median r² ≈ 0.77, substantially exceeding the IS machine baseline of r² ≈ 0.48. The ELS machine uniformly outperforms the memorizing IS machine across all datasets and architectures, confirming that locality and equivariance are the key inductive biases driving creative generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Ideal Score (IS) machine, which perfectly inverts the forward diffusion and can only reproduce memorized training examples. The ELS machine uniformly and substantially outperforms the IS machine across all four datasets and both architectures (e.g., r² 0.94 vs. much lower IS values on MNIST ResNet), confirming that the creative outputs of trained CNNs are far from memorization. Between the two theory variants, the ELS machine (partial equivariance breaking via zero-padding boundaries) best explains ResNet outputs on all datasets and UNet outputs on MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR10. However, on CelebA the UNet is better described by the fully non-equivariant LS machine (r² ≈ 0.90) than by the ELS machine, because the UNet's large receptive field allows it to infer absolute pixel location from boundary information, while the ResNet's smaller 17×17 receptive field cannot. For the pretrained UNet+SA model on CIFAR10, the ELS machine achieves median r² ≈ 0.77 versus 0.48 for the IS baseline, revealing that attention carves semantically coherent objects from the ELS patch mosaic rather than fundamentally altering the local generation mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-matching diffusion framework (Song & Ermon 2019; Song et al. 2020a,b) and the DDIM deterministic parameterization, but departs fundamentally from prior theoretical work that assumes models learn (or nearly learn) the ideal score function, which provably leads to memorization. It positions itself against concurrent and prior work on diffusion model generalization (Kadkhodaie et al. 2023a,b; Zhang et al. 2023; Niedoba et al. 2024; Wang & Vastola 2024) by showing that two simple inductive biases—locality and translational equivariance—are not merely limiting constraints but are sufficient to derive a fully analytic theory (the ELS machine) that quantitatively predicts individual trained model outputs case-by-case, a level of prediction prior works did not achieve. The patch mosaic mechanism also echoes early nonparametric texture synthesis (Efros & Leung 1999), but is here derived from first principles of optimal score approximation under architectural constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analytic theory to explain the creative outputs of trained CNN diffusion models with high quantitative accuracy (median r² ≈ 0.94–0.96 across four datasets and two architectures), providing a mechanistic account of how exponential creativity emerges from locally consistent patch mosaics. The theory also explains the notorious spatial inconsistencies (wrong number of limbs) as a consequence of excessive locality at late reverse-flow times, and partially predicts attention-enabled UNet outputs (r² ≈ 0.77), suggesting attention carves semantic coherence from local patch mosaics. Key limitations include restriction to small CNN-only models on 32×32 images, the need to calibrate a time-dependent locality scale empirically, and the unresolved question of why 150-step discretization matches theory better than 20-step. A full quantitative theory of attention's role in diffusion model creativity remains open.
