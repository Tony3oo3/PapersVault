## Problem

A widely used analytical formula in the alignment literature claims the KL divergence between the best-of-n policy and the reference policy equals log(n) − (n−1)/n, but this formula is actually only an upper bound that can be unboundedly loose (e.g., for low-entropy prompts where the best outcome has high reference probability). This gap undermines the reliability of reward-vs-KL tradeoff curves reported in the literature and obscures how much capability is truly preserved during alignment.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a text prompt x, draws n i.i.d. samples from a reference policy π_ref(·|x), scores each with a scalar reward r(x,y), and outputs the highest-reward sample. Beyond the selected response, the paper's key outputs are: (1) a practical KL divergence estimator d_n(ε_n) that depends only on the reference probability ε_n of the selected sample, (2) theoretical upper and lower bounds on the gap between the analytical formula and true KL, and (3) win rate bounds of the form n/(n+1) with explicit gap characterizations. The estimator is a random variable whose variance is bounded by log(n), requiring O(log n · log(1/δ)) samples for δ-precision.

## Method

The paper derives the exact PMF of the best-of-n policy via the CDF of the maximum of n uniform random variables (Lemma 2.3), then uses this to prove the analytical formula log(n)−(n−1)/n is an upper bound on true KL (Theorem 3.1). It characterizes the gap through Rényi-2 entropy: the gap is small when n²δ ≪ 1 for a δ-bound model (all outcomes have probability ≤ δ), but grows as log(nε_∞) when the top-reward outcome has high reference probability. A new estimator d_n(ε_n) is proposed that decomposes the KL integral into a closed-form term over the 'bulk' outcomes plus an exact term for the selected sample, tracking true KL closely across all regimes. The paper further proves win rate is upper bounded by n/(n+1) with gap bounded by (n−1)/2 · e^{−H₂}, and compares best-of-n against rewind-and-repeat rejection sampling, showing best-of-n achieves superior win-rate-vs-KL tradeoffs while rewind-and-repeat is more compute-efficient for driving reward up.

## Results

The paper proves that the widely used analytical formula log(n) − (n−1)/n for the KL divergence of the best-of-n policy is only an upper bound, not the exact value. In Example 1 (binary output, equiprobable), the true KL divergence is bounded by log(2) ≈ 0.693 for all n, while the analytical formula grows unbounded as n → ∞. The win rate of best-of-n against the reference policy is upper bounded by n/(n+1), and the paper shows that very good win-rate-vs-KL tradeoffs are achievable with n < 1000. The proposed KL estimator (Eq. 25) closely tracks the exact KL divergence across uniform and non-uniform distributions, outperforming both the analytical formula and a looser alternative bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares best-of-n against two baselines on win-rate-vs-KL-divergence tradeoff curves. Against rewind-and-repeat (Kim et al., 2025), best-of-n consistently achieves better tradeoffs: in both Example 2 (ternary model, π_ref = (0.3, 0.6, 0.1)) and Example 3 (low-probability outcomes), the best-of-n curve dominates the rewind-and-repeat curve, meaning it achieves higher win rate at the same KL divergence (Figures 8, 9). Against the win-rate-optimal policy (the closed-form KL-regularized RL solution from Korbak et al., 2022), best-of-n is 'strikingly close' in Example 2 and 'almost optimal' in the asymptotic regime of Example 3, confirming recent findings by Yang et al. (2024a) and Gui et al. (2024). The paper also shows that tradeoff curves reported in prior literature using the analytical formula are conservative — the actual best-of-n tradeoff is guaranteed to be no worse than what was previously reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a rigorous theoretical treatment of the best-of-n alignment policy, building directly on the sampling-based alignment framework of Nakano et al. (2021) and Stiennon et al. (2020), and the KL-regularized RL formulation of Christiano et al. (2017) and Ouyang et al. (2022). It departs from prior work by disproving a widely-used analytical formula for the KL divergence of best-of-n (log(n) − (n−1)/n, attributed to Hilton & Gao, 2022 and used by Gao et al., 2023; Go et al., 2023; Scheurer et al., 2023), showing it is only an upper bound, and providing tight gap bounds via Rényi entropy. The analysis also extends to blockwise best-of-n decoding (Mudgal et al., 2024) and compares against rewind-and-repeat rejection sampling (Kim et al., 2025), situating best-of-n within a broader family of test-time rejection sampling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Correcting the KL divergence formula has direct practical implications: alignment practitioners can now guarantee that best-of-n preserves more of the reference model's capabilities than previously estimated, which matters for safety compliance and capability retention. The proposed estimator (Eq. 25) offers a practical tool for monitoring drift during inference-time alignment without requiring knowledge of the full distribution. The open question the paper highlights is how to achieve Pareto-optimal tradeoffs among compute budget, reward, and KL divergence — potentially by combining rewind-and-repeat (which drives reward up efficiently) with best-of-n (which offers better KL tradeoffs) — and the dual-use concern that the same mechanism enabling effective alignment also enables best-of-n jailbreaking.
