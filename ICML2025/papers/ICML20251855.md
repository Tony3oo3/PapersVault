## Problem

Dynamic fusion via self-attention in multimodal Transformers is expected to adaptively weight modalities based on input quality, but the paper shows this adaptability is lost during training: a self-reinforcing cycle causes the model to over-rely on one modality regardless of data characteristics, widening the key distribution gap and deactivating the attention mechanism. Consequently, dynamic fusion can underperform simple static concatenation, as demonstrated on Kinetic-Sound (67.0 vs. 68.0 accuracy).

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal inputs—audio spectrograms (257×1004 or 257×299 depending on dataset) plus video frames processed through a 4-layer ViT-B/16 backbone, or pre-extracted text features for CMU-MOSEI—and produces a single class label via a linear classifier applied to the [class] token. The RollingQ algorithm operates on the query vector of the fusion attention layer, requiring only an additional d×d rotation matrix (≈1% parameter overhead) and negligible FLOPs increase (0.1%).

## Method

RollingQ detects the self-reinforcing cycle using an Attention Imbalance Rate (AIR) indicator, defined as the expected cosine similarity difference between the query and each modality's average key. When |AIR| exceeds a threshold β, the algorithm computes a rebalance anchor q_b as a weighted combination of the normalized expectations of both modalities' average key distributions, with weights determined by a tanh function of AIR that down-weights the biased modality. A rotation matrix R_b is then obtained via SVD of [E[Q], q_b], and the query is rotated as q_r = q·R_b, redirecting the query's learning toward a region that allocates more attention to the underutilized modality. This grants the weaker modality greater gradient during backpropagation, gradually equalizing feature quality and narrowing the key distribution gap, thereby restoring the attention mechanism's ability to dynamically respond to per-sample modality quality.

## Results

On Kinetic-Sound (Audio+Visual), Vanilla MT+RollingQ achieves 69.3% accuracy, a 2.3-point improvement over the vanilla baseline (67.0%) and surpassing static fusion (Concat: 68.0%). On CREMA-D (Audio+Visual), RollingQ reaches 51.9% accuracy (↑3.1 over baseline), and on CMU-MOSEI (Visual+Text), it achieves 63.2% (↑0.5). When applied to the more complex MMML architecture, RollingQ further pushes Kinetic-Sound to 70.7% and CREMA-D to 52.7%, while adding only ~1% extra parameters and 0.1% GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RollingQ consistently outperforms its own vanilla baseline across all three datasets, with the largest gain on CREMA-D (+3.1%) and Kinetic-Sound (+2.3%). Against static-fusion baselines, Vanilla MT+RollingQ beats OGM (51.9 vs 51.2 on CREMA-D; 69.3 vs 68.2 on Kinetic-Sound) and PMR (51.9 vs 50.1; 69.3 vs 68.2), and edges out simple Concat on all datasets. Among dynamic-fusion baselines, RollingQ surpasses JMT on all three datasets (e.g., 69.3 vs 67.7 on Kinetic-Sound) and MulT on MOSEI (63.2 vs 62.4), but MBT retains a lead on Kinetic-Sound (72.2 vs 69.3) due to its heavier 2-layer bottleneck architecture. When RollingQ is applied to MMML, it improves Kinetic-Sound from 69.8 to 70.7 and CREMA-D from 52.0 to 52.7, demonstrating that the self-reinforcing cycle is a pervasive issue across different multimodal Transformer designs and that query rotation is a broadly applicable remedy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of multimodal fusion and Transformer attention analysis. While prior work on modality imbalance (OGM, PMR, etc.) addresses the static fusion paradigm by modulating gradients or adding auxiliary objectives, this paper identifies a distinct failure mode in the dynamic fusion paradigm: the self-attention mechanism itself deactivates due to a self-reinforcing cycle that widens the key distribution gap across modalities. The RollingQ algorithm departs from these optimization-level fixes by directly manipulating the query vector via SVD-based rotation, targeting the attention allocation mechanism rather than the encoder training dynamics. The theoretical grounding draws on the greedy nature of multimodal learning (Wu et al., 2022) and the cosine-similarity decomposition of attention scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention-based dynamic fusion can underperform simple static concatenation challenges a core assumption in multimodal Transformer design and provides a mechanistic explanation (self-reinforcing key distribution gap) that is actionable. The RollingQ solution is remarkably lightweight (one rotation matrix, ~1% parameter overhead) and generalizes across backbones (ViT, ResNet) and fusion paradigms (late, mid, early). Key limitations include the single-layer theoretical analysis (multi-layer dynamics remain unexplored), the fact that RollingQ does not directly improve unimodal encoder quality, and modest absolute gains (0.5–3.1%). Open questions include whether combining RollingQ with gradient-modulation methods for static fusion yields compounding benefits, and how the self-reinforcing cycle manifests in deeper, pre-trained multimodal Transformers.
