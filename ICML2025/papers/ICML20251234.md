## Problem

Traditional LLMs scale primarily along data size and model size, but data availability has become a critical bottleneck for continued improvement. This paper asks whether introducing explicit latent thought vectors with inference-time computation can unlock new scaling dimensions (inference steps and latent size) that improve sample and parameter efficiency beyond what autoregressive or diffusion models achieve at the same compute budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text sequences with a short context window of 256 tokens (max sequence length 1024, GPT-2 tokenizer), and for each sequence it first runs 16–64 steps of fast variational inference to obtain a per-instance posterior over latent thought vectors before generating tokens autoregressively. For conditional generation it takes a 50-token prefix and produces 50-token completions; for arithmetic reasoning it takes GSM8K word problems (optionally with 5-shot in-context demonstrations) and outputs candidate solutions evaluated by pass@5. The latent thought vectors are instance-specific local parameters (not shared across samples), which distinguishes the I/O from standard autoregressive models where all parameters are global.

## Method

LTMs introduce layered latent thought vectors z = (z_1, …, z_L) drawn from an isotropic Gaussian prior N(0, I), where each z_l cross-attends to layer l of a Transformer decoder (z_l provides keys/values, input tokens provide queries). Training uses a dual-rate variational Bayes algorithm: a fast loop (Adam, lr ≈ 0.3, T_fast = 16–64 steps) optimizes per-sequence variational parameters (μ, σ²) of the posterior q(z|x) to maximize the ELBO, while a slow loop (AdamW, lr = 4×10⁻⁴ with cosine decay) updates the global decoder parameters β. The short context window (k = 256) forces the latent vectors to carry long-range information that would otherwise be disconnected. This design introduces two new scaling axes—number of inference steps and number of latent vectors per layer (N_z)—that are orthogonal to model size and data size, and the paper shows that increasing T_fast improves both sample and compute efficiency, while larger N_z provides additional headroom for further gains.

## Results

LTM-Large (76M parameters, 0.9B tokens) achieves a state-of-the-art validation perplexity of 3.05 on OpenWebText and zero-shot perplexity of ≤3.48–4.56 across all seven benchmarks (PTB, WikiText, LM1B, LAMBADA, AG News, PubMed, Arxiv), dramatically outperforming GPT-2-Large (34.26–161.33) and all diffusion baselines. LTM-Medium (51M parameters) matches GPT-2-Large's trFLOPs/tok while using only 6.7% of its parameters, achieving ≤17.32–32.06 perplexity. On conditional generation, LTM-Large achieves 0.974 MAUVE with standard multinomial sampling, surpassing GPT-2-Medium (0.955 with nucleus sampling) and SEDD (0.957). On GSM8K, LTM-Small (38M) with 5-shot in-context demonstrations surpasses GPT-2-Medium finetuned and test-time search baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019), LTM-Large achieves 91.7% lower perplexity on LAMBADA (≤3.48 vs. 34.26) and 97.3% lower on PTB (≤4.43 vs. 161.33) while using only 10% of the parameters; LTM-Medium matches GPT-2-Large's trFLOPs/tok with just 6.7% of its parameters. Against SEDD (Lou et al., 2024), LTM-Large outperforms on all reported perplexity benchmarks (e.g., ≤3.66 vs. ≤29.98 on WikiText) and on conditional generation MAUVE (0.974 vs. 0.957). Against MD4 (Shi et al., 2024), LTM-Large achieves substantially lower perplexity across all shared benchmarks (e.g., ≤3.92 vs. ≤51.45 on LM1B). Against SEDD/MDLM from Sahoo et al. (2024), LTM-Large dominates on all seven perplexity benchmarks despite using far fewer training tokens (0.9B vs. 524B). On GSM8K arithmetic reasoning, LTM-Small (38M) with 5-shot in-context demonstrations surpasses both GPT-2-Medium finetuned on GSM8K and GPT-2-Medium with test-time search (Li et al., 2022), demonstrating emergent few-shot capability at a scale far below GPT-3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical variational Bayes framework (Jordan et al., 1999; Blei et al., 2017) and the Transformer decoder architecture, but departs from both amortized VAEs (which suffer from posterior collapse) and post-training latent chain-of-thought methods (Zelikman et al., 2022; Phan et al., 2023) by integrating non-amortized variational inference directly into pre-training via a dual-rate optimization scheme. The latent thought vectors with an explicit prior in latent space draw conceptual inspiration from cognitive science—declarative-procedural memory (Ullman, 2004) and the language of thought hypothesis (Fodor, 1975)—while the inference-time computation connects to the test-time adaptation literature (Graves, 2016; Krause et al., 2018). Unlike discrete diffusion models (SEDD, MDLM, MD4) that operate in token space, LTMs introduce a continuous latent space as an additional generative axis, creating new scaling dimensions (inference steps, latent size) orthogonal to traditional model-size and data-size scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of inference steps and latent size as new scaling dimensions that improve both sample and compute efficiency offers a potential path around the data bottleneck limiting LLM scaling, though the evidence is currently confined to GPT-2 scale (38M–76M parameters) and a single dataset (OpenWebText). The emergent few-shot in-context reasoning at 38M parameters—dramatically below the GPT-3 scale where this was first observed—suggests that gradient-based latent inference may enable more efficient pattern discovery than token-level autoregressive generation, but this claim rests on a single benchmark (GSM8K pass@5) and warrants validation at larger scales. Key open questions include whether learnable structured priors (e.g., latent reasoning chains) and reward/verifier models in latent space can further unlock reasoning capabilities, and whether the scaling laws observed here generalize beyond the short-context, small-model regime studied.
