## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation (SSMIS) methods rely on complex training strategies such as consistency regularization and contrastive learning to exploit unlabeled data, but none model the underlying data structure information in a graph representation. This gap is particularly costly when labeled data is extremely scarce, as the distribution mismatch between the small labeled set and the large unlabeled set remains unaddressed from a structural perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical image volumes (C×W×H×D) split into a small labeled set and a much larger unlabeled set. During training, bidirectional copy-paste generates two mixed images per iteration by cropping a foreground region from one image and pasting it onto another (inward and outward), which are fed to the student network. The output is a voxel-wise segmentation mask with k classes, supervised by ground-truth labels on the labeled portion and teacher-generated pseudo-labels on the unlabeled portion.

## Method

GraphCL builds on the Bidirectional Copy-Paste teacher-student framework and introduces two graph mechanisms inside the encoder. First, a Data Structure Analyzer (DSA) produces structure scores from CNN features, which form a dense instance graph (adjacency = X_sa · X_sa^T) over the mini-batch; a GCN then propagates features across this graph to align samples of the same category. Second, a clustering graph is constructed from a patch-level similarity matrix (W = F·F^T − Max(F·F^T)/τ), processed by a single-layer GCN followed by an MLP with softmax to yield soft cluster assignments. A correlation clustering loss L_CC = −Tr(W·S·S^T) enforces intra-cluster coherence and inter-cluster separation without requiring the number of clusters k to be specified. The total training objective combines the bidirectional segmentation losses with κ·L_CC, and ablations show that placing the GCN at the fifth (deepest) encoder layer yields the best performance.

## Results

On the LA dataset with 5% labeled data, GraphCL achieves a Dice score of 88.80%, Jaccard of 80.00%, 95HD of 7.16, and ASD of 2.10, outperforming the second-best method (BCP) by 1.73% in Dice. With 10% labeled data, GraphCL reaches 90.24% Dice and 82.31% Jaccard. On the ACDC dataset (5% labeled), GraphCL achieves 88.68% Dice and 3.12 95HD, and on Pancreas-NIH (20% labeled), it reaches 83.15% Dice and 71.42% Jaccard, consistently surpassing all baselines across all four evaluation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphCL outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. On the LA dataset (5% labeled), it beats the strongest competitor BCP by 1.73% in Dice (88.80 vs. 87.07) and by 1.67 in 95HD (7.16 vs. 8.83); the gap widens to 2.47% Dice over SS-Net (86.33). On ACDC (5% labeled), the advantage is most dramatic: GraphCL achieves 88.68% Dice versus SS-Net's 65.83% (a 22.85% gap) and BCP's 86.83%, while also reducing 95HD from 8.71 (BCP) to 3.12. On Pancreas-NIH (20% labeled), GraphCL leads BCP by 2.03% in Dice (83.15 vs. 81.12) and CoraNet by 3.48% (79.67), with a 95HD of 6.87 versus BCP's 8.11. The only metric where GraphCL does not strictly dominate is ASD on Pancreas-NIH, where it reports 2.12 versus BCP's 2.34 (still better) but the paper notes a 0.22 increase over the second-best in one sub-metric, though overall it remains the best. Supervised-only baselines (V-Net, U-Net) are far behind, confirming the value of the semi-supervised graph-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphCL builds directly on the Bidirectional Copy-Paste (BCP) teacher-student framework (Bai et al., 2023) for semi-supervised medical image segmentation, extending it with two graph-based mechanisms: a structure-aware alignment module using a Data Structure Analyzer and GCN, and a correlation clustering loss. While graph neural networks have been applied to supervised semantic segmentation (GraphNet, A²GNN) and to object detection/tracking, the authors position this as the first work to explicitly model data structure information via graphs in the semi-supervised medical image segmentation setting. The method repurposes standard GCN propagation and correlation clustering from the graph learning literature, adapting them to operate on CNN-extracted features from 3D medical volumes within a self-training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating graph structural information into the SSMIS pipeline yields consistent gains across three anatomical benchmarks, with the largest improvements appearing under the most label-scarce conditions (5% labeled), suggesting graph-based structure modeling is particularly valuable when labeled supervision is minimal. The k-less clustering strategy removes a practical hyperparameter burden, and the correlation clustering loss provides a principled mechanism for enforcing intra-cluster coherence without specifying cluster count. Open questions remain around the robustness of the graph construction to noisy pseudo-labels (acknowledged by the authors as future work), whether the approach generalizes beyond the three tested datasets, and how the method scales to larger 3D volumes or more complex multi-organ tasks.
