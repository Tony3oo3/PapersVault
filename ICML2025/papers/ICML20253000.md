## Problem

Visual tokens in VLMs impose substantial memory and compute overhead (e.g., 2304 tokens for a 672×672 image) despite being informationally sparser than text tokens. Existing sparsification methods either modify the vision encoder/projector or prune tokens during decoding without leveraging the question prompt, and most require additional training data or parameters. The paper addresses the gap of a training-free, text-guided mechanism that adaptively prunes visual tokens based on the specific question being answered.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on existing VLMs (LLaVA, Mini-Gemini, Qwen2-VL, Video-LLaVA) without any fine-tuning. It consumes the self-attention matrices already computed within the LLM decoder layers and the pre-decoder visual/text embeddings, then outputs a reduced set of visual tokens (e.g., 576→192 or 576→64) that continue through the remaining decoder layers to produce the final text response. The sparsification ratio is not fixed but adapts per layer based on the rank of the attention sub-matrix, and the method is compatible with FlashAttention via a dual-pass forward with a specially constructed V matrix.

## Method

SparseVLM first selects visually relevant text tokens ('raters') by computing cross-attention between visual and text embeddings and retaining only those exceeding the mean correlation threshold. It then extracts the language-query × vision-key sub-matrix P from the self-attention logits to score each visual token's significance, and adaptively determines how many tokens to prune per layer via N = λ × (L_v − rank(P)), where the rank reflects redundancy. Pruned tokens are not simply discarded: the top-τ% of deleted tokens are aggregated using k-nearest-neighbor density peak clustering (Rodriguez, 2014) and reconstructed into compact cluster-center tokens via element-wise summation, minimizing information loss. The entire pipeline introduces no extra parameters, requires no training data, and adds only O(L_t × L_v) FLOPs for significance estimation and O(L_t × L_v × min(L_t, L_v)) for rank computation per layer.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 (576→128 visual tokens, 77.8% reduction), SparseVLM retains 96.7% of the vanilla model's average accuracy across 8 benchmarks while reducing FLOPs from 4.62T to 1.72T (−62.8%) and CUDA latency from 57.82ms to 33.28ms (−42.4%). At the most aggressive setting (576→64 tokens, 88.9% reduction), SparseVLM still achieves 89.3% average accuracy, far exceeding ToMe (71.1%) and FastV (72.0%). On Video-LLaVA (2048→194 tokens, 90.5% reduction), SparseVLM reaches 47.0% average accuracy versus FastV's 40.5%, a 14.7% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseVLM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every token budget. Against FastV (ECCV'24), the gap widens with increasing sparsity: +11.2% at 192 tokens, +14.3% at 128 tokens, and +17.3% at 64 tokens on LLaVA, and +14.7% on Video-LLaVA, because FastV's text-agnostic pruning discards question-relevant visual regions. Against ToMe (ICLR'23), SparseVLM leads by 10.2–18.2% in average accuracy; ToMe's direct token merging degrades performance most severely at high sparsity (71.1% at 64 tokens vs. SparseVLM's 89.3%). Against PDrop (CVPR'25), the closest competitor, SparseVLM gains 3.2% at 192 tokens and 15.9% at 64 tokens in accuracy, though PDrop achieves marginally lower FLOPs (2.03T vs. 2.14T at 192 tokens) and higher latency (36.74ms vs. 36.50ms). The paper attributes SparseVLM's advantage to its text-guided rater selection (which avoids irrelevant prompt tokens polluting the significance score) and its token recycling mechanism (which recovers 1.2–7.2% additional accuracy over direct discarding, growing with sparsity).

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseVLM extends the line of decoding-stage visual token sparsification (FastV, VoCo-LLaMA, PDrop) by introducing text-guided, training-free pruning that leverages self-attention matrices to rate visual token significance relative to the question prompt. It departs from prior text-agnostic methods by selecting only visually relevant text tokens as 'raters' and by using the rank of the attention matrix to adaptively set per-layer sparsification ratios. For token recycling, it repurposes the density peak clustering algorithm (Rodriguez, 2014) to compress pruned tokens into compact cluster representations, a mechanism not previously applied to VLM inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a plug-and-play, training-free module, SparseVLM offers immediate practical value for deploying VLMs on resource-constrained hardware, achieving 4.5× compression with ~97% accuracy retention across LLaVA, Mini-Gemini, Qwen2-VL, and Video-LLaVA. The consistent 11–17% accuracy advantage over the trained FastV baseline challenges the assumption that learned pruning networks are necessary. Open questions include whether the rank-based adaptive strategy scales gracefully to very long video sequences (thousands of frames), how the method interacts with speculative decoding or other inference accelerators, and whether the density-peak recycling overhead becomes prohibitive at extreme sparsity ratios.
