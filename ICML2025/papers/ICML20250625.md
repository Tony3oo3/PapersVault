## Problem

LoRA adapters are tightly coupled to their base diffusion model, making them unusable when the base model is updated, distilled, or pruned. Retraining adapters on the new model is often impractical due to resource constraints or unavailability of the original training data, limiting the longevity and portability of PEFT adaptations across evolving model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LoRA (or DoRA, FouRA, LCM-LoRA) adapter from a source model along with the full weight matrices of both the source and target diffusion models, requiring no training data or gradient updates. It produces a transferred adapter of the same shape as the source adapter, ready to be plugged into the target model for text-to-image generation. The approach handles dimension mismatches between source and target modules via linear projection into a common subspace, and supports both linear and convolutional layers.

## Method

ProLoRA performs training-free, closed-form transfer of LoRA adapters in three steps: (1) identifying correlated module pairs between source and target models via SVD-based column/row subspace similarity (threshold 0.8), (2) decomposing the source adapter ΔW_s into a subspace component (projected onto the column/row spaces of W_s) and a null-space component (projected onto the orthogonal complements), and (3) projecting each component into the corresponding subspace and null space of the target model weights W_t. The key insight is that preserving both the subspace and null-space effects of the adapter is critical for faithful style transfer—ablation shows CSD-MMD degrades from 0.0245 to 0.1344 when the null-space projection is omitted. Unlike LoRA-X, which restricts adapters to the weight subspace and requires source-model training, ProLoRA transfers standard LoRAs with full expressiveness directly, and the initial SVD cost is amortized over multiple adapter transfers.

## Results

On the primary text-to-image transfer benchmark (Table 1), ProLoRA achieves HPSv2 scores within 0.01–0.05 of LoRA trained from scratch across all three style datasets (BlueFire, Paintings, Origami) and four target models (RV-v3.0, SD Eff-v1.0, RVXL-v3.0, SSD-1B). For example, on BlueFire→SSD-1B, ProLoRA reaches HPSv2 0.318 vs. 0.323 for native LoRA, with a shared CSD-MMD of 0.0207 confirming style preservation. On the LCM-LoRA acceleration task (Table 4), ProLoRA matches the trained LCM-LoRA (HPSv2 0.315 vs. 0.329; LPIPS 0.497 vs. 0.494) while achieving a higher DINOv2 correlation (0.944 vs. 0.885 for naive copy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA trained from scratch on the target model, ProLoRA matches within ~0.01–0.05 on HPSv2 and LPIPS across all dataset/model combinations, with the shared CSD-MMD confirming that the transferred adapter preserves the target style distribution. Against X-Adapter (Ran et al., 2023), ProLoRA wins on every metric: HPSv2 0.281 vs. 0.271, LPIPS 0.443 vs. 0.403, DINOv2 0.961 vs. 0.884, and inference time 3.7 s vs. 16.1 s (4.3× faster). Against LoRA-X (Farhadzadeh et al., 2025), ProLoRA at rank 32 achieves comparable or slightly better HPSv2/LPIPS than LoRA-X at rank 320 (e.g., BlueFire: 0.318/0.413 vs. 0.300/0.392), despite a 10× lower adapter rank, and avoids the ~400 s extra source-model training that LoRA-X requires. On the LCM-LoRA acceleration task, ProLoRA outperforms naive copy (DINOv2 0.944 vs. 0.885) and closely matches the trained LCM-LoRA (HPSv2 0.315 vs. 0.329), demonstrating that the subspace+nullspace projection generalizes to convolutional layers used by LCM-LoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProLoRA extends the subspace similarity framework introduced in LoRA-X (Farhadzadeh et al., 2025) by incorporating null space projection, enabling transfer of standard LoRA adapters that affect both the column/row subspace and the null space of base model weights. It departs from prior adapter-transfer approaches—Trans-LoRA (Wang et al., 2024) which requires synthetic data, X-Adapter (Ran et al., 2023) which trains a per-target mapper, and LoRA-X which restricts adapters to the subspace and requires source-model training—by offering a fully training-free, closed-form projection that preserves the full expressiveness of existing adapters. The method also generalizes beyond LoRA to DoRA, FouRA, and LCM-LoRA, and extends to text generation with TinyLlama, broadening the scope of prior work that focused primarily on style LoRAs in diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProLoRA addresses a practical bottleneck in the diffusion model ecosystem: LoRA adapters are locked to their base model, and retraining is often infeasible due to data unavailability or compute constraints. The ablation showing that null space projection is critical (CSD-MMD degrades ~5.5× without it) challenges the implicit assumption in much PEFT literature that the subspace captures the essential adaptation signal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to architecturally dissimilar models (beyond the SDXL→SSD-1B family), how it behaves with very low-rank adapters across diverse tasks, and whether the SVD pre-processing cost becomes prohibitive for very large or frequently updated model families.
