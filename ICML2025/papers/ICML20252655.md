## Problem

Autoregressive language models generate tokens without lookahead, making it difficult to enforce global text properties like non-toxicity, persona, or topic. Existing controllable generation methods either retrain the LM per attribute (expensive, risks fluency loss) or approximate the Expected Attribute Probability (EAP) via sampling or trained discriminators, which is slow, high-variance, and unreliable for rare attributes. No prior approach combines exact EAP computation with rapid per-attribute adaptation without modifying the base model.

## Input / Output

TRACE takes a text prompt, a frozen base LM (GPT-2 Large or Gemma-2B), a one-time distilled HMM (h=4096 hidden states for detoxification, h=256 for personalization), and a lightweight per-token weight vector w(x_i) fitted to a target attribute. The output is an autoregressively generated continuation whose next-token distribution is reweighted by the HMM-computed EAP, incurring only ~1.2× the baseline decoding time. The factorized classifier constraint (p(s|x)=∏w(x_i)) is what makes the EAP sum over all future sequences tractable, and composition of multiple attributes is achieved by simply multiplying their token-weight vectors.

## Method

TRACE distills a Hidden Markov Model from the base LM via latent-variable EM (one-time, attribute-agnostic), then trains a log-linear factorized classifier per attribute using log-MSE loss against oracle scores (e.g., Perspective API), with an optional affine logit-space transformation to sharpen the target distribution. During decoding, the constrained next-token probability is p_LM(x_t|x_<t)·p_HMM(s|x_<t,x_t), where the EAP term is computed exactly via a forward pass for p(z_t|x_≤t) and a precomputed backward pass for E_HMM[∏_{i>t}w(x_i)|z_t], both in O(h²+hV) per step. The backward expectations are cached once per sequence length and reused across all generations, making the per-token overhead negligible relative to the LM forward pass. For compositional control (e.g., political AND nontoxic), the token weights of individual classifiers are multiplied, and the same forward-backward machinery applies unchanged.

## Results

On RealToxicityPrompts with GPT-2 Large, TRACE achieves the lowest average maximum toxicity (0.163) and probability of any toxic generation (0.016) among all baselines, while maintaining diversity (Dist-2: 0.85, Dist-3: 0.85) close to the uncontrolled baseline (0.87/0.86). On Gemma-2B, TRACE reduces average max toxicity from 0.359 to 0.189 with only a 1.2× inference overhead, outperforming DPO (0.222) which degrades diversity to 0.74/0.77. TRACE requires only 10 seconds of classifier training per attribute versus hours to 16 hours for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE outperforms all 10 baselines on average maximum toxicity for GPT-2 Large (0.163 vs. DPO 0.180, Quark 0.196, PPO 0.218, MuCoLa 0.308, FUDGE 0.302, DExperts 0.314, GeDi 0.363, DAPT 0.428, PPLM 0.520). The key differentiator is that RL methods (DPO, PPO, Quark) reduce toxicity at the cost of significant diversity loss (DPO drops Dist-2 from 0.87 to 0.76), whereas TRACE preserves diversity at 0.85/0.85. On Gemma-2B, TRACE (0.189 avg max tox, 0.86/0.85 diversity) beats DPO (0.222, 0.74/0.77) on both toxicity and diversity. In terms of efficiency, TRACE's 1.2× inference overhead and 10-second per-attribute training time are dramatically lower than MuCoLa (15–20×), PPLM (40×), Mix and Match (7.5×, 2 hr training), DAPT (16 hr), and GeDi (5 hr). The paper also shows TRACE's conditional entropy (52.54) matches the base LM (52.06) while DPO's drops to 39.52, confirming RL-induced mode collapse that TRACE avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE extends the HMM-based tractable control framework of Zhang et al. (2023, 2024), which enforced logical constraints (e.g., keyword presence via DFAs) during autoregressive generation, to the substantially harder setting of semantic attributes like toxicity, persona, and political content. It leverages the latent variable distillation technique of Liu et al. (2023) to obtain the HMM from a base LM in a one-time step, then pairs it with a factorized log-linear classifier to enable exact Expected Attribute Probability (EAP) computation via a forward-backward algorithm. This departs from prior decoding-based control methods (GeDi, FUDGE, DExperts) that train large per-attribute discriminators, and from sampling-based methods (MuCoLa, Mix and Match) that estimate EAP with high variance, by shifting the approximation burden entirely to the one-time HMM distillation and achieving exact inference at decoding time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE offers a practically compelling efficiency story: 10-second per-attribute adaptation versus hours for baselines, and only 1.2× inference overhead versus 7.5–40× for competing decoding methods, making it attractive for dynamic or low-resource deployment. The finding that a simple factorized classifier combined with exact HMM inference outperforms more expressive neural classifiers with approximate EAP estimation challenges the implicit assumption that semantic attributes require complex per-attribute models. Open questions include whether the factorized classifier assumption limits performance on attributes with strong long-range dependencies, whether improved HMM distillation (the paper shows HMM quality directly correlates with control effectiveness) can close the remaining gap to RL-based methods on perplexity, and whether the approach extends to attributes that are genuinely non-factorizable beyond what the log-MSE loss can capture.
