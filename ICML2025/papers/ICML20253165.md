## Problem

Existing subgraph neural networks either apply simple pooling over GNNs (failing to capture essential subgraph properties) or rely on rigid subgraph definitions, and none explicitly model long-range dependencies between subgraphs. Moreover, no implicit model has been designed for subgraph representation learning, and conventional implicit GNNs become unstable when naively adapted to subgraph-level tasks due to the complex aggregation step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253165/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base graph G with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and a set of m labeled subgraphs D = {(S_i, y_i)}, where each subgraph is induced by a node subset. It constructs a hybrid graph by adding supernodes (one per subgraph) with features defined as the mean of member node features, plus membership edges and label-aware subgraph-subgraph edges. The output is a class label per subgraph, obtained by applying attention over the joint node and supernode embeddings learned at the fixed point of the implicit operator.

## Method

ISNN constructs a hybrid graph combining original nodes with supernodes representing subgraphs, where subgraph-subgraph edges are built via a label-aware strategy (connecting k most distant training supernodes within each class after pretraining). The implicit model uses the EIGNN formulation f(Z, Ĝ; ξ, W) = αAZ·h(W) + ψ_ξ(X̂) with a non-expansive operator (α chosen small enough). Training is formulated as a bilevel optimization: the upper level minimizes subgraph classification loss while the lower level enforces the fixed-point constraint ‖Z − f(Z, Ĝ)‖²_F = 0. The key algorithmic contribution replaces gradient descent in the inner loop with K steps of fixed-point iteration, reducing gradient oracle calls from K+1 to 1 per outer iteration while maintaining a provable convergence guarantee (sample complexity Õ(ε^{-1.5})) under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition on the lower-level objective.

## Results

ISNN achieves the best Micro-F1 score across all four datasets: 0.731 on PPI-BP, 0.646 on HPO-METAB, 0.688 on HPO-NEURO, and 0.914 on EM-USER. On AUROC, ISNN leads on three of four datasets (0.924 on PPI-BP, 0.919 on HPO-METAB, 0.896 on HPO-NEURO) but trails GLASS by a marginal 0.001 on EM-USER (0.959 vs. 0.960). The largest gains over the strongest baseline (SSNP) are on PPI-BP (+9.5 F1 points) and HPO-METAB (+5.9 F1 points), where label-aware subgraph-level information is most impactful. Runtime is competitive, with ISNN completing 1500 epochs in 105–161 seconds versus 130–204 seconds for SSNP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISNN outperforms all six baselines on Micro-F1 across all four datasets. Against the strongest node-pooling baselines, ISNN beats SSNP by 9.5 F1 points on PPI-BP (0.731 vs. 0.636) and 5.9 points on HPO-METAB (0.646 vs. 0.587), while the gap narrows to 0.6 points on HPO-NEURO (0.688 vs. 0.682) and 2.6 points on EM-USER (0.914 vs. 0.888). GLASS, the next-best method, is surpassed by ISNN on F1 in every dataset (e.g., +11.3 on PPI-BP) and on AUROC in three of four (losing by only 0.001 on EM-USER). SubGNN, which explicitly models subgraph-level structure via anchor patches, is outperformed by ISNN by 7.3–13.5 F1 points across datasets, suggesting that the implicit fixed-point formulation plus label-aware hybrid graph construction captures subgraph relationships more effectively than SubGNN's multi-channel message passing. The plain implicit baselines (IGNN-plain, EIGNN-plain) perform poorly (F1 0.21–0.43), confirming that naively applying implicit GNNs to subgraph tasks is unstable without the proposed bilevel optimization and hybrid graph construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ISNN sits at the intersection of two research threads: subgraph representation learning (Sub2Vec, SubGNN, GLASS, SSNP) and implicit graph neural networks (IGNN, EIGNN). It departs from prior subgraph methods by introducing a hybrid graph that jointly encodes node-level and label-aware subgraph-level (supernode) information, and from prior implicit GNNs by being the first to apply the implicit/fixed-point paradigm to subgraphs. The training algorithm builds on the V-PBGD penalty-based bilevel framework (Shen & Chen, 2023) but replaces the inner-loop gradient descent with fixed-point iteration, reducing gradient oracle calls while preserving convergence guarantees under a non-expansive operator assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens the implicit-model direction for subgraph learning, showing that long-range dependencies between subgraphs can be captured without unbounded message-passing depth, and that a bilevel formulation is necessary for stable training (naive IGNNs degrade severely on subgraph tasks). A notable limitation is that the label-aware edge construction relies on class labels at training time, which constrains applicability to unsupervised or few-label settings; the multi-label dataset (HPO-NEURO) shows the label-conversion heuristic does not fully capture inter-subgraph structure. Open questions include whether the hybrid-graph + implicit formulation extends to subgraph generation, link prediction within subgraphs, or dynamic subgraph tracking, and whether the fixed-point inner loop can be replaced by closed-form solvers for further efficiency gains.
