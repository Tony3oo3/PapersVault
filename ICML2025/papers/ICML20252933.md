## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) strategies are designed for single-task learning, leaving multi-task learning (MTL) data and models largely unprotected. The core difficulty is that MTL requires perturbations to simultaneously introduce effective spurious features across K tasks while maintaining low intra-class variance, a challenge that existing surrogate-dependent methods fail to address due to independent per-sample optimization, and that surrogate-free patch-based methods cannot sustain as task count grows.

## Input / Output

The method takes clean multi-task images (RGB at 70×70 for CelebA, 256×256 grayscale for ChestX-ray14, 140×140 for UTKFace, or 288×384 for NYUv2) along with K task labels, and produces sample-specific perturbations bounded by ℓ∞ ≤ 8/255 that are added to the original images. For dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth, normal estimation on NYUv2), the discrete class-wise embeddings are replaced by task-specific embedding modules that map dense label maps to spurious feature maps. The generator is trained once on the full dataset and then applied to produce the unlearnable dataset, requiring only ~3M parameters regardless of dataset size.

## Method

MTL-UE replaces per-sample perturbation optimization with a generator-based architecture: an encoder (9 conv layers, 0.5× downsampling) extracts a latent z from the input image, and K sets of learnable class-wise feature embeddings (each 16×H/2×W/2) are selected based on the sample's task labels. The concatenation of z and the selected embeddings is decoded by a 4-layer ConvTranspose2d network (2× upsampling) to produce the perturbation, which is clipped to the ℓ∞ bound. Two embedding regularizations are added: Intra-ER minimizes cosine similarity between embeddings within each task to maximize inter-class separation, and Inter-ER minimizes absolute cosine similarity across tasks to enforce geometric independence and reduce feature redundancy. The framework is plug-and-play, using any surrogate-dependent UE method (EM, TAP, SEP) as the base loss L_b in the generator's training objective, and extends to dense prediction by swapping discrete embeddings for learned embedding modules that map dense labels to spurious features.

## Results

On CelebA (40 binary tasks, ResNet-18), MTL-UE-TAP reduces MTL model accuracy from 91.11% (clean) to 59.51% and STL accuracy to 68.65%, while MTL-UE-SEP achieves 58.73% (MTL) and 76.39% (STL). On UTKFace (3 multi-class tasks), MTL-UE-TAP drops MTL average accuracy to 19.84% and STL to 25.59%, compared to clean performance of 78.97%/78.92%. On NYUv2 (dense prediction), MTL-UE-EM reduces segmentation mIoU from 53.05 to 2.37 and pixel accuracy from 75.01 to 16.04 for MTL models. MTL-UE also reduces intra-class feature standard deviation from 2.191 (EM) to 1.715 (MTL-UE-EM) on average, confirming tighter spurious feature control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTL-UE consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and model type. Against the strongest surrogate-free baseline AR (Patch), MTL-UE-TAP improves MTL accuracy on CelebA from 73.12% to 59.51% (−13.6 pp) and on UTKFace from 27.41% to 19.84% (−7.6 pp), while also extending the attack to STL models where AR(Patch) only achieves 84.41% on CelebA versus MTL-UE-TAP's 68.65%. Against the surrogate-dependent baselines, MTL-UE-TAP and MTL-UE-SEP substantially improve over their respective base methods (TAP: 85.24%→59.51% MTL on CelebA; SEP: 84.25%→58.73%), while MTL-UE-EM offers a more modest MTL gain (75.66%→74.38%) but a large STL gain (89.91%→74.26%). The averaging-based fusion variants of LSP and AR are nearly ineffective (within 0.1 pp of clean on CelebA MTL), confirming that naive multi-task perturbation combination fails. On NYUv2 dense prediction, MTL-UE-EM achieves mIoU of 2.37 versus EM's 26.45, a 24-point improvement, while MTL-UE-TAP and MTL-UE-SEP excel on depth and normal estimation respectively, showing complementary strengths across the three MTL-UE variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) literature, which has focused almost exclusively on single-task learning (STL), to the multi-task learning (MTL) setting. It builds directly on three surrogate-dependent UE methods—EM (Huang et al., ICLR 2021), TAP (Fowl et al., NeurIPS 2021), and SEP (Chen et al., ICLR 2023)—by wrapping them in a plug-and-play encoder-decoder generator that replaces per-sample perturbation optimization with class-wise feature embedding injection. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of intra-task and inter-task embedding regularization to control intra-class variance of spurious features, addressing a failure mode the authors identify in both surrogate-dependent and surrogate-free baselines when task count grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As foundation models and generalist systems increasingly train on multi-task datasets (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging), the inability of existing UE methods to protect such data is a practical gap this paper addresses. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt MTL-UE without re-deriving attack objectives, and the extension to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth, normals) broadens applicability beyond classification. Open questions remain: STL models resist MTL-UE more effectively at high task counts (performance degrades beyond ~15 tasks on CelebA), partial data protection is limited (effectiveness drops sharply when only a fraction of data is poisoned), and the method's robustness to future, more sophisticated defenses is not yet established.
