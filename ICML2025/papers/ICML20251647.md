## Problem

Existing token-merging methods for diffusion models (ToMeSD, ToFu) rely on GPU-inefficient primitives like sorting and scattered memory writes, which negate their theoretical FLOP savings when paired with optimized attention kernels such as FlashAttention. Once attention is no longer the bottleneck, the overhead of unoptimized merge/unmerge logic dominates runtime, preventing any practical speedup. The paper asks how to redesign token reduction so that its overhead is negligible relative to the attention computation it accelerates.

## Input / Output

ToMA is a training-free, plug-and-play module inserted into the transformer blocks of existing diffusion models (SDXL-base, Flux.1-dev). It takes the standard text prompt and random noise latent, operates on the latent-space token sequence within each block, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image after the usual denoising loop. The merge ratio (fraction of tokens retained) is a user-configurable hyperparameter (25%, 50%, 75%), and the method is architecture-agnostic, supporting both UNet-based and DiT-based backbones without retraining.

## Method

ToMA reformulates token merging as a GPU-aligned pipeline with three stages. First, destination tokens are selected via greedy maximization of a Facility Location submodular function over cosine similarities, yielding a (1−1/e)-approximation guarantee and a diverse, representative subset. Second, merging is expressed as a column-wise softmax attention (SDPA) between destination queries and all source keys/values, producing a dense weight matrix whose matrix multiplication replaces the scatter/gather operations of prior methods; unmerge is the simple transpose of this matrix (an approximation to the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse justified by near-orthonormality of the rows). Third, two redundancy-exploiting strategies minimize overhead: spatial locality is leveraged by restricting selection and merging to non-overlapping tile or stripe windows (giving 1/k and 1/k² speedups respectively), and temporal/layer redundancy is exploited by reusing destination indices across 10 denoising steps and merge weights across 5 steps and within same-type transformer blocks.

## Results

ToMA achieves up to 24% wall-clock speedup on SDXL-base (6.07→4.34 s/img at 75% merge) and 23% on Flux.1-dev (21.03→16.12 s/img on RTX6000 at 75% merge), while keeping DINO degradation below 0.07 at the 50% ratio. Critically, prior token-merging methods (ToMe, ToFu) actually *increase* latency by 11–44% over the unmodified baseline when paired with FlashAttention, because their GPU-inefficient sort/scatter operations dominate once attention is already optimized. At 50% merge, ToMA reduces FID from 25.3 to 28.9 and CLIP-T from 29.89 to 29.64 on SDXL, with a DINO shift of only 0.068.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (ToMeSD), ToMA is the only method that actually accelerates generation: ToMe is 34–44% *slower* than the unmodified baseline at every merge ratio due to its GPU-inefficient sort-based token pairing, while ToMA achieves 17–28% speedup. On quality, ToMe edges out ToMA slightly on DINO at 75% merge (0.084 vs 0.098) and FID (41.2 vs 58.6), but the practical irrelevance of ToMe's speed penalty makes this moot. Against ToFu, ToMA dominates on both axes: ToFu is 11–14% slower than baseline and suffers catastrophic FID degradation (142.1 at 50%, 161.5 at 75% vs ToMA's 28.9 and 58.6), indicating severe visual artifacts. Against ToDo (evaluated only at 75% merge), ToMA is 4.2× faster in relative speedup (−28.5% vs −6.6%) and achieves lower FID (58.6 vs 68.6) and higher CLIP-T (27.96 vs 27.60), though ToDo has a marginally better DINO (0.093 vs 0.098). On Flux.1-dev (where no baselines are compatible due to RoPE), ToMA alone delivers up to 23.4% speedup with DINO Δ of 0.064 at 75% merge.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToMA builds directly on the token merging paradigm introduced by Bolya et al. (ICLR 2023) and adapted to diffusion by ToMeSD (Bolya & Hoffman, 2023), but departs fundamentally in its implementation strategy. Where prior methods (ToMeSD, ToFu, ToDo) rely on GPU-inefficient primitives—sorting, index_select, index_add_—for source-destination pairing and feature redistribution, ToMA reformulates destination selection as a submodular facility-location problem and implements merge/unmerge as dense attention-like matrix multiplications. This positions the paper as a system co-design contribution: the algorithmic guarantee from submodular optimization is paired with hardware-aligned linear algebra to close the gap between theoretical FLOP savings and actual wall-clock speedups when attention is already optimized (e.g., FlashAttention2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical practical failure mode: widely-adopted token merging methods (ToMeSD, ToFu) actually increase generation latency by 11–44% when paired with FlashAttention, because their scatter/gather overhead dominates once attention is no longer the bottleneck. ToMA's training-free, plug-and-play design (24–28% speedup on SDXL/Flux with negligible quality loss) offers a deployable fix, and its compatibility with both UNet and DiT architectures broadens applicability. Open questions include whether custom tiled/stripe attention kernels (explicitly deferred as future work) could close the remaining gap to the theoretical lower bound, and whether the locality and sequential-redundancy assumptions hold for video diffusion or higher-resolution generation where token counts are substantially larger.
