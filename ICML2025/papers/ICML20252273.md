## Problem

The existing theoretical framework for weak features learning (WFL) with discrete WFs relies on 0-1 loss properties that cannot be extended to continuous WFs, leaving a gap in the unified treatment of continuous weak features such as missing, noisy, or interval-valued observations. Prior analyses of impute-then-regress (ItR) for missing data lacked finite-sample generalization error bounds valid for arbitrary distributions and could not accommodate diverse types of continuous WFs within a single framework. This paper establishes the missing theoretical foundation for continuous WFL, providing generalization error analyses and consistency guarantees that parallel the discrete WFL results.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular data partitioned into weak features (continuous values that are missing, noisy, or given as intervals) and ordinary features (complete observations), along with labels for training. The feature estimation model g maps ordinary features to point estimates of the weak features, and the label prediction model f consumes both the estimated weak features and the ordinary features to produce a predicted label. In experiments, both g and f are two-layer perceptrons with 500 hidden units and ReLU activations, trained with logistic loss for binary classification on four OpenML datasets with 50% MCAR missingness.

## Method

The core contribution is a risk-based formulation that replaces the 0-1 loss (used in discrete WFL) with mean squared error (MSE) to measure estimation errors of the feature estimation model g, enabling a unified treatment of arbitrary continuous WFs. The key analytical tool is a novel inequality bounding |R_l(f) − R_{l,g}(f)| via the Hellinger distance between the true conditional distribution p*(X^w|X^o,Y) and a Gaussian noise model q_g centered at g's output, which is then specialized to express the bound directly in terms of MSE (Lemma 4.2). This yields a learning algorithm class (LAC-cWFL) with three steps—weakly supervised learning of g, ERM for f with g fixed, and structural risk minimization for g with f fixed—encompassing both sequential and iterative learning. Generalization error bounds derived via Rademacher complexity show that the MSE of g controls the convergence rate of f's error (O_p(1/n^{1/4}) with fixed g, improving to O_p(1/(nm)^{1/4}) when g is learned), and explicit consistency conditions for both sequential and iterative learning are established. The framework is further extended to the case where all features are continuous and potentially missing (ItR) and to an integrated WFL setting using probabilistic models and Hellinger/KL divergences.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation in Section 5 confirming that Theorem 4.3's error bound accurately tracks the generalization error R_{l,g}(f) across four real-world datasets (Jets, Electricity, Mv, Run-or-Walk) under 50% MCAR missingness. The key empirical finding is that lower MSE of the feature estimation model g (controlled in the range [0.001, 0.5]) yields a faster decrease in R_{l,g}(f) as training sample size n increases, and the theoretical bound from Theorem 4.3 exhibits the same qualitative behavior. The main theoretical contributions are consistency guarantees: Theorem 4.4 shows sequential learning (steps i and ii) is consistent under mild conditions, and Theorem 4.6 extends this to iterative learning (alternating steps ii and iii).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods; it is a theoretical framework paper whose empirical section validates its own error bound rather than benchmarking against baselines. The experiments use two-layer perceptrons (width 500, ReLU) with logistic loss on four OpenML datasets, with g's MSE artificially controlled via a Gaussian sampling procedure (Eq. 5.15) to test the theoretical prediction. The validation confirms that the O_p(1/n^{1/4}) convergence rate predicted by Theorem 4.3 (dominated by g's MSE term) is observed in practice, and that the bound's shape matches the observed R_{l,g}(f) curves. The paper positions its contribution as extending the discrete WFL framework (Sugiyama & Uchida, 2025) to continuous WFs, with results corresponding in parallel to the discrete case rather than competing with it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the discrete WFL framework of Sugiyama & Uchida (ICML 2025) to continuous weak features, replacing the 0-1 loss with MSE and introducing a Gaussian noise model to derive analogous generalization error bounds via Hellinger distance. It also addresses a gap in the impute-then-regress (ItR) literature (Josse et al. 2024; Bertsimas et al. 2021; Le Morvan et al. 2021), which established Bayes-rule characterizations but lacked finite-sample generalization error analyses for arbitrary distributions. The key technical departure is a novel proof technique using a probabilistic reparameterization of the deterministic feature estimator, enabling the derivation of upper bounds on |R_l(f) − R_{l,g}(f)| in terms of MSE rather than 0-1 loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the WFL framework is universally applicable across discrete and continuous weak features, providing consistency guarantees for both sequential and iterative learning that were previously unavailable for continuous settings. This gives practitioners theoretical justification for impute-then-regress and complementary features learning pipelines under broad distributional assumptions. However, the integrated WFL (mixed discrete + continuous WFs) remains open due to the inability to decompose Hellinger distance into per-feature estimation errors, and the error bounds do not capture the relative importance of individual WFs for the downstream task. Experimental validation is limited to four tabular datasets under MCAR with a fixed 50% missing rate, leaving open questions about performance under MNAR mechanisms and higher missing rates.
