## Problem

Boltzmann Exploration (B-exp), the standard RL exploration strategy for embedding-based action selection, requires computing softmax probabilities over all n actions, making it intractable for catalogs of millions. Truncated B-exp (TB-exp) sidesteps this by restricting sampling to a small ANN-retrieved candidate set, but this artificially caps the exploration radius based on engineering constraints rather than convergence properties. No existing method simultaneously achieves scalability to millions of actions, an unrestricted exploration radius, and order preservation with respect to embedding similarity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state vector V and a set of n action embeddings X_n, all constrained to the unit hypersphere S^{d-1} (unit Euclidean norm), along with a scalar concentration parameter κ ∈ ℝ⁺. It produces a single sampled action index by first drawing a continuous vector Ṽ ~ vMF(V, κ) and then performing one approximate nearest-neighbor query against X_n. The theoretical analysis additionally characterizes the full probability P_vMF-exp(i | V, X_n, κ) for every action i, showing it equals the vMF mass over i's Voronoi cell on the hypersphere, which is always strictly positive (unrestricted radius).

## Method

vMF-exp replaces B-exp's O(n) discrete softmax sampling with a two-step continuous-then-discrete procedure: (1) sample a vector Ṽ from a von Mises-Fisher distribution centered on the state V with concentration κ, and (2) retrieve Ṽ's nearest neighbor in X_n via an ANN engine, selecting that action for exploration. The vMF PDF f_vMF(Ṽ|κ,V,d) ∝ e^{κ⟨V,Ṽ⟩} mirrors the Boltzmann weight structure, and the probability of selecting action i equals the vMF mass over i's Voronoi cell in the hyperspherical tessellation induced by X_n. Under the assumption that embeddings are i.i.d. uniform on S^{d-1}, the paper proves that P_vMF-exp(a|n,d,V,κ) / P_B-exp(a|n,d,V,κ) → 1 as n → ∞, with explicit convergence rates of O(1/n²) for d=2 and O(1/n^{1+2/(d-1)}) for d>2. For high dimensions (d ≥ 20), a refined second-order approximation P₁ incorporating the Beta and Gamma functions captures the small but systematic deviation where vMF-exp explores slightly more broadly than B-exp for the same κ. The entire sampling step costs O(1) in n (one d-dimensional vMF draw plus one ANN query), making it scalable to millions of actions.

## Results

The paper's primary result is Proposition 4.1, establishing that lim_{n→∞} P_B-exp(a)/P_vMF-exp(a) = 1, i.e., vMF-exp asymptotically matches Boltzmann Exploration's sampling probability for each action. Monte Carlo simulations (8 million repetitions, 95% confidence intervals) confirm this equivalence: for small dimensions (d=2,3,4), P_vMF-exp is indistinguishable from P_B-exp on a 1e-5 probability scale, while for d≥16 the second-order correction P_1 from Proposition 4.4 becomes necessary. On the 1-million-vector GloVe dataset, vMF-exp simultaneously satisfies all three desired properties (scalability, unrestricted radius, order preservation) with sampling latency of a few milliseconds, and the production deployment on Deezer's music streaming service achieved a positive worldwide online A/B test over millions of candidate songs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against Boltzmann Exploration (B-exp, Cesa-Bianchi et al., NeurIPS 2017). vMF-exp asymptotically matches B-exp's per-action sampling probability (ratio → 1 as n→∞), but achieves this in O(1) time with respect to n (sampling a d-dimensional vMF vector + one ANN lookup) versus B-exp's O(n) softmax computation. In terms of convergence rate, vMF-exp actually reaches its asymptotic regime faster in 2D (O(1/n²) vs. B-exp's o(1/(n√n))), though for high dimensions (d≥16) the gap widens and vMF-exp explores slightly more extensively than B-exp for the same κ (P_B-exp(a) ≥ P_vMF-exp(a) when ⟨V,A⟩>0). Truncated B-exp (TB-exp), the practical workaround used by YouTube and others, is noted to satisfy scalability but fails the unrestricted-radius property by restricting candidates to a fixed-size ANN subset. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical benchmark table; instead, equivalence is established theoretically and validated via Monte Carlo simulation and the GloVe/Deezer experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Boltzmann Exploration (B-exp), the standard RL exploration strategy that samples actions via a softmax over embedding similarities, and its truncated variant (TB-exp) used in production systems like YouTube. The key conceptual departure is replacing the discrete n-way softmax with a continuous von Mises-Fisher distribution sampled on the unit hypersphere, followed by a single ANN lookup — transforming an O(n) computation into O(1) sampling plus sublinear retrieval. The method also draws on directional statistics (Fisher, 1953), Voronoi tessellation theory, and extreme value theory (Fisher-Tippett-Gnedenko) to establish its asymptotic equivalence to B-exp, and notes a structural parallel to Thompson Sampling in bandit settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

vMF-exp provides the first theoretically grounded, scalable exploration method that simultaneously satisfies scalability (P1), unrestricted radius (P2), and order preservation (P3) for hyperspherical embedding action sets of millions — properties that no prior method (B-exp, TB-exp, ε-greedy) achieves jointly. Its successful multi-month deployment on Deezer's production recommender system validates practical viability. However, theoretical guarantees are currently restricted to i.i.d. uniform embeddings on the hypersphere; extending them to clustered or non-uniform embedding distributions (common in practice, e.g., music genre clusters) remains open, as does characterizing the impact of ANN approximation errors at extreme scale.
