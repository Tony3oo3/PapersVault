## Problem

Existing pseudo-labeling methods select predicted labels with high confidence and high training stationarity, overlooking non-stationary predictions that the paper shows can carry greater information gain for decision boundaries. The key question is how to exploit the significant information from predicted labels with non-stationary training dynamics for pseudo-labeling while minimizing the risk of misclassification that such labels inherently carry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251943/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled set, a pool of unlabeled samples (RGB images or graph nodes), and a memory bank of epoch-wise softmax predictions recorded during base-model training (|T| ≈ 50 epochs). Beyond producing standard pseudo-labels, the output includes the two bimodal categories (c₁, c₂) for each selected sample, which are required by the specialized two-term loss function. The 2-phasic metric is designed to act as a plug-in booster: it is applied in a third training stage after a baseline pseudo-labeling method has already augmented the labeled set.

## Method

The core contribution is the 2-phasic metric, which identifies two-phase labels—predictions that switch from one category in early training to another in later epochs—by combining a spatial measure (Leave-Maximum-Out entropy, which quantifies bimodality of the averaged prediction distribution by excluding the highest-probability class) and a temporal measure (sigmoid-gated deltas capturing small, directional probability shifts confined to the two bimodal categories). The paper argues two-phase samples sit near decision boundaries and encode both false correlations (simple, non-exclusive patterns from phase 1) and correct correlations (complex, class-exclusive patterns from phase 2). A specialized binary cross-entropy loss is designed with two terms: a c₂ term that reinforces correct correlations and a c₁ term that actively suppresses false correlations. The method is validated as a booster across four image and four graph datasets, yielding average accuracy gains of 1.73% and 1.92% respectively over five baselines, with the c₁ term contributing most when labeled data is scarce.

## Results

The proposed 2-phasic metric acts as a universal booster for existing pseudo-labeling methods, achieving an average classification accuracy gain of 1.73% on image datasets (CIFAR-100, EuroSAT, STL-10, Semi-Aves) and 1.92% on graph datasets (Cora, Citeseer, PubMed, AmazonComputers). The 2-phase trials consistently outperform baseline trials across all six baselines, all eight datasets, and both base models (GCN for graphs, ViT for images). Gains are most pronounced under extreme label scarcity, e.g., on Cora with only 3 labeled nodes per class, where Confidence improves from 66.21% to 70.41% (+4.20%) and DR-GST from 71.01% to 73.88% (+2.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 2-phasic metric improves every baseline it is combined with, confirming its role as a complementary booster rather than a standalone replacement. Against Confidence score (Lee et al., 2013), the largest gains appear on graph data with minimal labels: +4.20% on Cora (L/C=3) and +4.18% on PubMed (L/C=3). Against MoDis (Pei et al., 2024b), which already leverages training dynamics for stationarity, the 2-phasic metric still adds +1.49% on Cora and +1.54% on CIFAR-100, demonstrating that non-stationary dynamics carry information beyond what stationarity captures. Against DR-GST (Liu et al., 2022a), the strongest graph baseline, gains of +2.87% (Cora) and +4.18% (PubMed) show that distribution-recovered self-training still misses two-phase samples. On image data, UPS (Rizve et al., 2021) and SoftMatch (Chen et al., 2023) are both improved by 1.2–1.6%, with SoftMatch benefiting from the added boundary information despite already combining pseudo-labeling with contrastive learning. AUM (Sosea & Caragea, 2022) shows the smallest relative gains (+3.45% on Cora, +1.19% on PubMed), suggesting its margin-based selection partially overlaps with two-phase samples. Overall, the two-phase labels are largely non-overlapping with confidence-selected labels (IoU as low as 0.16 on CIFAR-100), confirming they capture a distinct and informative subset of unlabeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the training-dynamics-based pseudo-label selection paradigm (exemplified by MoDis and time-consistency methods) that selects labels with high stationarity—i.e., predictions that remain consistent throughout training. Instead, it targets the complementary regime of non-stationary predictions, specifically those exhibiting a two-phase switching pattern, and formalizes their detection via a novel 2-phasic metric combining a Leave-Maximum-Out (LMO) entropy for spatial bimodality and directional-change measures for temporal structure. The specialized loss function, which simultaneously reinforces correct class-exclusive patterns and suppresses false simple-pattern correlations, extends the standard cross-entropy pseudo-labeling objective in a way that prior confidence- or uncertainty-based methods do not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that non-stationary, two-phase predictions carry higher information gain than the high-confidence stationary labels they complement challenges the prevailing assumption in training-dynamics literature that prediction consistency is a proxy for reliability. Practically, the 2-phasic metric acts as a plug-in booster (average +1.7–1.9% accuracy) across diverse baselines and both image and graph domains, making it broadly applicable without replacing existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more complex models (e.g., foundation models), whether the memory-bank overhead becomes prohibitive at scale, and whether the hand-tuned parameters (ε_bi, ε_¬bi, η, φ) can be learned automatically. The method's reliance on a small number of sampled epochs (|T|=50) is encouraging but untested on very long training schedules.
