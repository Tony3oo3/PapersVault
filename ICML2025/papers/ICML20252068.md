## Problem

Linear-attention models reduce inference cost to O(T) but suffer from a recency bias that causes them to underrepresent historical context, while full self-attention incurs O(T^2) compute and linearly growing KV-cache memory. Existing hybrid or distilled architectures use a fixed layer layout, so they cannot dynamically trade accuracy for efficiency under fluctuating production workloads. The paper asks whether one can (i) fix the recency bias in linear attention and (ii) convert Transformer layers to linear attention on the fly at inference time, guided by real-time resource pressure.

## Input / Output

At training time the method consumes a pre-trained Transformer (Llama2-7B) and 1.6B tokens from SlimPajama (truncated to 4K) to distill per-layer DSLA replacements. At inference time, DSLA-Serve ingests incoming text prompts together with live GPU memory and prompt-length signals; it outputs the same generated text as the base model but with a dynamically chosen subset of layers running in DSLA mode (12.5%-50% conversion depending on prompt length and load), yielding 2.29x lower per-token latency on replayed Azure traces.

## Method

DSLA replaces the single recurrent state of GLA with two parallel states (S1 for history and S2 for recency), each updated by its own data-dependent forget gate, then blended at the output by a per-layer learnable scalar gamma. A contrastive penalty on the cosine similarity of the two gating matrices, combined with KL-divergence distillation from the teacher's self-attention, forces the states to specialize: S1 retains far-context while S2 tracks local tokens. Layers are ranked by attention entropy (lower entropy means less sensitive to linearization) and distilled in ascending-sensitivity order via chained fine-tuning, where each newly converted DSLA layer is fine-tuned with all previously converted layers frozen in place, guaranteeing train-test consistency for every partial configuration the system may deploy. At serving time, DSLA-Serve monitors GPU memory and prompt length, progressively swaps layers from the least-sensitive end of the queue into DSLA form until a memory threshold is met or a quality SLO is at risk, and reverts when load subsides.

## Results

DSLA-Serve achieves a 2.29× reduction in end-to-end per-token generation latency (93.64 ms → 40.83 ms) over Llama2-7B and a 3.0× reduction over the hybrid Zamba-7B, while maintaining competitive downstream performance. On long-context multi-document QA, DSLA[25%] (25% of layers converted) surpasses Llama2-7B on HotpotQA (11.07 vs. 5.63) and 2WikiMQA (14.20 vs. 10.24), and outperforms Mamba-7B by up to 72.23% (TriviaQA: 87.46 vs. 15.23). On summarization, DSLA[25%] achieves 27.98% average ROUGE, nearly matching Llama2-7B (28.09%) and Zamba-7B (27.88%), while on lm_eval commonsense reasoning it reaches 56.3% average vs. Llama2-7B's 57.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the quadratic baseline Llama2-7B, DSLA[25%] wins decisively on multi-document QA (HotpotQA +5.44, 2WikiMQA +3.96) and TriviaQA (+1.27), but trails slightly on WikiText-2 perplexity (9.26 vs. 8.79) and TREC (55.0 vs. 59.0). Against purely linear models, DSLA[25%] dominates: it outperforms GLA-7B by 7.46 points on HotpotQA and 29.78 on TriviaQA, and beats Mamba-7B by up to 72.23 points (TriviaQA: 87.46 vs. 15.23). Versus the hybrid Zamba-7B, DSLA[25%] leads on HotpotQA (+3.17), 2WikiMQA (+6.23), LCC (+26.21), and TriviaQA (+5.27), while Zamba retains an edge on TREC (64.0 vs. 55.0) and Repobench is close (51.53 vs. 43.20 in DSLA's favor). In the direct linearization-method comparison (Table 7, Llama3-8B teacher), DSLA achieves 72.7% average lm_eval accuracy vs. LoLCATs' 70.7%, with the largest gap on MMLU (68.9% vs. 52.8%). At 1.5B scale (Table 8), DSLA distilled from Phi-1.5 matches or exceeds Phi-Mamba on all five lm_eval tasks (e.g., ARC-C: 44.5 vs. 44.1) while requiring only 800M tokens of fine-tuning versus 3.0B for Phi-Mamba.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Gated Linear Attention (GLA) by replacing its single recurrent hidden state with two specialized states (history and recency), directly addressing the recency bias that prior linear-attention and SSM analyses (Wang et al. 2024b, Ben-Kish et al. 2024) identified as a fundamental limitation. It departs from static distillation pipelines (Bick et al. 2024, Zhang et al. 2024a, Wang et al. 2024a) and fixed hybrid architectures (Zamba, Jamba, Samba) by introducing an on-the-fly, sensitivity-ordered layer conversion framework (DSLA-Serve) that adapts the degree of linearization at inference time based on real-time memory pressure. The chained fine-tuning strategy is a key methodological departure from prior work that distills layers in isolation, ensuring consistency across partial-conversion configurations at deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.3× end-to-end latency reduction over Llama2-7B and 3.0× over Zamba-7B while retaining comparable downstream accuracy makes this practically relevant for production LLM serving where KV-cache memory is the dominant bottleneck. The adaptive 'pay-as-you-go' conversion strategy addresses a real operational gap: static hybrids cannot flexibly trade accuracy for memory under fluctuating loads, and full linearization degrades long-range reasoning. Key limitations include the need to hold both Transformer and DSLA weights in memory simultaneously, performance degradation beyond ~75% conversion, and evaluation limited to 7B and 1.5B models. Open questions include whether the dual-state design and sensitivity ordering generalize to much larger models (70B+), whether the contrastive regularization is necessary at scale, and how the framework interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantization or speculative decoding.
