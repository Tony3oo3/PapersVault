## Problem

Non-stationarity in multivariate time series creates a fundamental tension: it causes spurious regressions in short-term modeling due to random fluctuations, yet it is essential for capturing long-term cointegration relationships across variates. Existing methods either uniformly eliminate or retain non-stationarity without addressing these distinct and opposing impacts on short-term versus long-term dependency modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251595/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series of shape C×I (C variates, I=720 input steps) and produces predictions of shape C×O where O ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} for long-term tasks or O=12 for short-term traffic forecasting. The input is partitioned into N non-overlapping patches of length S per variate, which are linearly embedded into D-dimensional tokens; after downsampling to M patches (M<N), the final output is obtained by unpatching and projecting the M downsampled tokens to the target horizon.

## Method

TimeBridge is a four-stage Transformer framework that applies asymmetric non-stationarity handling: (1) Patch Embedding segments each variate into non-overlapping patches and embeds them via a linear layer; (2) Integrated Attention first detrends each patch by subtracting a moving-average trend, then computes attention using the detrended patches as Query/Key and the original patches as Value, yielding a stationary attention map that captures intra-variate temporal dependencies without spurious regression; (3) Patch Downsampling reduces N patches to M via an MLP and uses cross-attention to aggregate global long-term information into each downsampled token; (4) Cointegrated Attention operates on the downsampled patches across all C variates at the same time position, deliberately preserving non-stationarity to model inter-variate cointegration. The theoretical justification, grounded in Brownian motion analysis, shows that non-stationary inputs produce attention scores that grow quadratically with time (causing spurious attention), while detrending destroys the cointegration signal needed for long-term equilibrium modeling.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over prediction lengths 96–720), TimeBridge achieves the best overall MSE/MAE across 8 datasets, with headline numbers of 0.149/0.245 on Electricity, 0.360/0.255 on Traffic (862 channels), and 0.219/0.249 on Weather. Compared to the strongest baselines, TimeBridge reduces MSE by 1.85% over DeformableTST, 5.56% over ModernTCN, and 13.66% over TimeMixer (with corresponding MAE reductions of 2.49%, 4.12%, and 7.58%). On short-term PeMS traffic forecasting, TimeBridge attains the best MAE on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., 14.52 on PeMS03, 20.43 on PeMS07). On financial forecasting, TimeBridge achieves the highest ARR (0.285 on CSI 500, 0.326 on S&P 500), Sharpe ratio (1.405, 1.927), and Information Ratio (1.317, 1.842) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeBridge outperforms all 11 long-term baselines on 7 of 8 datasets for both MSE and MAE; the sole exception is ETTh2, where ModernTCN (MSE 0.322) and DeformableTST (0.328) edge out TimeBridge (0.341), likely due to ETTh2's only 7 channels limiting the benefit of Cointegrated Attention. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitor DeformableTST, TimeBridge wins on 7/8 datasets with an average MSE reduction of 1.85%; against the MLP-based TimeMixer the gap widens to 13.66% MSE reduction, most pronounced on Traffic (0.360 vs. 0.409) and Electricity (0.149 vs. 0.185). On short-term PeMS forecasting, TimeBridge achieves the best MAE on PeMS03 (14.52), PeMS07 (20.43), and PeMS08 (14.98), while TimeMixer narrowly wins on PeMS04 MAE (19.21 vs. 19.24) and RMSE (30.92 vs. 31.12); DUET achieves a marginally lower RMSE on PeMS03 (22.99 vs. 23.10). Channel-independent baselines (PatchTST, DLinear) degrade substantially on PeMS due to strong inter-variable dependencies, confirming the need for the Cointegrated Attention component. On financial forecasting, TimeBridge dominates all 12 baselines on both CSI 500 and S&P 500 across ARR, ASR, CR, and IR, with the largest margin over iTransformer (ARR 0.285 vs. 0.214 on CSI 500) and Crossformer (ARR 0.326 vs. 0.284 on S&P 500), while traditional strategies (BLSW, CSM) and classic deep models (LSTM, Transformer) trail significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeBridge carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads in time series forecasting: normalization-based methods (RevIN, Dish-TS, SAN) that eliminate non-stationarity, and dependency-modeling methods (CI vs. CD) that capture intra- or inter-variate relationships. Rather than treating non-stationarity as uniformly harmful or beneficial, the paper argues it has opposite effects at different temporal scales—causing spurious regressions in short-term intra-variate modeling but enabling cointegration in long-term inter-variate modeling. This motivates a dual-attention architecture (Integrated Attention with detrending for short-term, Cointegrated Attention without detrending for long-term) that no prior method explicitly combines, building on patch-based representations from PatchTST and the attention framework of standard Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded (via Brownian motion analysis) explanation for why a single normalization strategy is suboptimal, offering practitioners a clear design principle: remove non-stationarity where you model local temporal dependencies, preserve it where you model cross-variate equilibrium. Limitations include the need for dataset-specific tuning of downsampling rates and the observation that low-channel datasets (ETT, C=7) gain little from the Cointegrated Attention component. Open questions include whether the Brownian-motion-based theoretical justification extends to more complex non-stationary processes, and whether the CI/CD split for short/long-term modeling generalizes to other architectural families beyond Transformers.
