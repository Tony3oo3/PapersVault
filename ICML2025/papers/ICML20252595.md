## Problem

Patchification has been treated as a de facto default in ViT-like architectures since 2021, but its impact on information loss and its potential as a scaling dimension for visual models has not been systematically studied. This paper asks whether reducing the patch size (lowering spatial compression) consistently improves visual understanding, and whether the gains stem from unlocking compressed information or merely from longer token sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at resolutions ranging from 64×64 to 224×224 (and up to 800 short-side for COCO), tokenized at patch sizes from 16×16 down to 1×1, producing sequences of up to 50,176 tokens. Outputs span class labels (ImageNet-1k), per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE20k), and bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO). A notable design choice is that for semantic segmentation, the standard UperNet decoder is replaced by a single linear projection layer to test whether decoder heads remain necessary at low compression rates.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic patchification scaling study, reducing the patch size from 16×16 to 1×1 across two architecture families: DeiT (quadratic-complexity self-attention) and Adventurer (linear-complexity Mamba-based), across four vision tasks and multiple input resolutions. The key finding is a smooth, consistent scaling law: test loss decreases monotonically as patch size shrinks, reaching its limit at pixel-level tokenization. An ablation isolating sequence length (by interpolating existing tokens rather than reducing patch size) shows that the gains come primarily from unlocking previously compressed visual information, not from longer contexts. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that at sufficiently small patch sizes, task-specific decoder heads (e.g., UperNet) become dispensable for dense prediction, suggesting a path toward decoder-free, encoder-only visual foundation models.

## Results

The paper demonstrates a consistent scaling law: reducing patch size from 16×16 to 1×1 monotonically improves performance across all evaluated tasks. The headline result is 84.6% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with Adventurer-Base (100M params) at 224×224 input and 1×1 patch size, processing a 50,176-token sequence — a 2.0% gain over the standard 16×16 baseline (82.6%). On ADE20k semantic segmentation, removing the UperNet decoder entirely still yields 46.8 mIoU at 2×2 patch (vs. 45.7 with UperNet at 16×16), and on COCO object detection, Adventurer-Base improves from 44.1 AP^b at 64×64 to 50.3 AP^b at 4×4 patch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external methods but instead establishes patch size as a new scaling dimension, comparing each model against its own standard 16×16-patchification baseline. Across all four tasks (classification, semantic segmentation, object detection, instance segmentation) and both architectures (ViT/DeiT and Mamba-based Adventurer), smaller patches strictly outperform the 16×16 baseline: e.g., DeiT-Base at 64×64 improves from 68.2% to 81.3% (+13.1%), and Adventurer-Base at 224×224 improves from 82.6% to 84.6% (+2.0%). For dense prediction, the standard UperNet decoder (13M params) at 16×16 patch is outperformed by a simple linear projection (0.2M params) at 2×2 patch on ADE20k (46.8 vs. 45.7 mIoU for Adventurer-Base), indicating the decoder becomes unnecessary once compression is low. On COCO, Adventurer-Tiny gains +4.0 AP^b (44.7→48.7) and Adventurer-Base gains +6.2 AP^b (44.1→50.3) when moving from the largest to smallest feasible patch size. An ablation (Table 5) confirms that the gains come from reduced information compression rather than merely longer sequences: interpolating tokens to extend sequence length without reducing patch size yields only +0.1–0.2% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions patchification—the spatial compression layer introduced with ViT and adopted as a default in subsequent architectures (DeiT, ConvNeXt, Vision Mamba, Adventurer)—as a compromise rather than a necessity, and systematically scales it down to 1×1 to test this hypothesis. Conceptually, it extends the NLP scaling laws framework (Kaplan et al., 2020) to a new dimension in vision: spatial compression rate, rather than parameter count or data volume. It is most closely related to Pixel Transformers (Nguyen et al., 2024), which explored pixel-level tokens but only at very low resolutions (32×32); this work scales the idea to standard 224×224 inputs and 50,176-token sequences using both quadratic-complexity ViT and linear-complexity Mamba-based Adventurer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that patchification is not architecturally necessary but rather a compute-driven compromise challenges a foundational design assumption in modern vision models and opens a new, practically accessible scaling dimension that does not require new data or training strategies. The decoder-free dense prediction result suggests a path toward universal encoder-only visual foundation models, potentially simplifying the architecture landscape. Key open questions include whether these gains persist at larger data scales (beyond ImageNet-1k), in video or multi-modal settings, and whether the quadratic-complexity ViT can practically exploit this dimension at full resolution without prohibitive cost. The 50,000 A100 GPU-hour compute requirement, while modest by frontier-model standards, still limits immediate adoption for smaller research groups.
