## Problem

State-of-the-art LLMs cannot reliably indicate when their outputs are incorrect: token probabilities are poorly calibrated, verbalized confidence is unstable and prompt-sensitive, and existing uncertainty estimates correlate weakly with actual prediction correctness. This paper asks how to train LLMs to express confidence in a way that is directly aligned with answer correctness, and how such confidence can be leveraged for downstream routing to stronger models and for rejection learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250418/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (multiple-choice or free-form) and produces a text answer followed by a special confidence token (<CN> or <UN>) generated autoregressively after the answer. The continuous confidence score is extracted as P(<CN>)/(P(<CN>)+P(<UN>)), giving a value in [0,1] that can be thresholded for routing or rejection decisions. Unusually, the confidence token is conditioned on both the input query and the model's own generated answer, rather than on the query alone, making the confidence assessment output-dependent.

## Method

Self-REF is a three-step fine-tuning framework: (1) confidence token annotation, where the base LLM generates predictions on training data and each instance is augmented with <CN> (correct) or <UN> (incorrect) tokens appended to the model's own prediction; (2) LoRA fine-tuning on the augmented data, where two special confidence tokens with trainable embeddings are introduced and gradients from incorrect answers in <UN> samples are masked out to prevent the model from reinforcing wrong predictions; (3) confidence score extraction via normalized token probabilities. The key distinction from prior routing work is that confidence is learned end-to-end within the LLM and conditioned on its own generated output during autoregressive decoding, rather than relying on an external router or query-only features. The training objective is aligned against prediction correctness rather than response consistency, and the approach requires no new loss functions or architectural modifications beyond the two added tokens.

## Results

Self-REF achieves the lowest routing rate (i.e., the smallest fraction of queries needing to be sent to the 70B model) across all four datasets and both local LLMs. For Llama3-8B-Instruct, routing rates to match Llama3-70B-Instruct accuracy are 39% (MMLU), 49% (OpenbookQA), 65% (GSM8K), and 40% (MedQA); for Mistral-7B-Instruct they are 70%, 40%, 75%, and 70% respectively. This translates to up to 2.03× per-token latency improvement (e.g., 0.145 s vs. 0.292 s on MMLU with Llama3-8B) while maintaining comparable overall accuracy. On the rejection learning task, Self-REF dominates all baselines on ROC curves for both MMLU and OpenbookQA without any explicit rejection training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-REF consistently achieves the lowest routing rate across all 8 model–dataset combinations, outperforming every baseline. Against Verbalizing Uncertainty (Tian et al., 2023), Self-REF reduces the required routing rate from 100% to 39% on MMLU (Llama3-8B) and from 100% to 40% on OpenbookQA (Mistral-7B). Against Zero-shot Logits (Mahaut et al., 2024), the gap is similar: 39% vs. 100% on MMLU and 40% vs. 100% on MedQA for Llama3-8B. The closest competitor is Fine-tuned Logits (Liu et al., 2024), which achieves 90% routing on MMLU and 70% on OpenbookQA/GSM8K for Llama3-8B, still substantially worse than Self-REF's 39% and 49%/65%. On calibration (ECE), Self-REF is best or second-best in most settings but not uniformly; Fine-tuned Logits achieves lower ECE on MedQA for Mistral-7B (0.023 vs. 0.037), confirming the paper's observation that better calibration does not guarantee better routing utility. On the rejection learning task (ROC curves), Self-REF dominates all four baselines on both MMLU and OpenbookQA for both local models, with no baseline coming close across any threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

Self-REF departs from the dominant paradigm of LLM routing, which relies on external classifiers or scoring functions operating on query features (Ding et al., 2024; Ong et al., 2024; Stripelis et al., 2024) or on post-hoc calibration of token probabilities (Tian et al., 2023; Mahaut et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024). Instead, it integrates confidence estimation directly into the autoregressive decoding process by training the LLM to emit special <CN>/<UN> tokens conditioned on its own generated answer, making the confidence signal output-dependent rather than query-dependent. This is conceptually adjacent to Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2024), which also uses learned special tokens for routing decisions, but Self-REF aligns those tokens against prediction correctness rather than retrieval relevance, and applies them to both routing and rejection without introducing new loss functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, lightweight recipe (LoRA + two special tokens + gradient masking) that practitioners can apply to any fine-tunable LLM to obtain a continuous confidence score usable for cost-aware routing or abstention, without degrading base task performance. A key open question the paper surfaces is the decoupling between calibration quality and downstream routing utility: Self-REF often does not achieve the best ECE or Brier Score yet consistently achieves the best routing rate, suggesting that existing calibration metrics are insufficient proxies for routing decisions. Limitations include evaluation on only four multiple-choice/short-answer datasets, two local model families, and a single (non-fine-tuned) 70B target, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to open-ended generation, multi-model routing, or sentence-level confidence.
