## Problem

Riemannian optimization on the Stiefel manifold requires a retraction at every iteration to maintain the orthogonality constraint, but all known retractions (QR, polar, Cayley, exponential) incur at least O(np²) cost from non-standard linear algebra operations. This makes Riemannian solvers impractical for large-scale problems where both n and p are large. The paper asks whether the per-iteration retraction cost can be reduced while preserving convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an objective function F: St(n,p) → R, an initial column-orthonormal matrix X_0 ∈ St(n,p), and a submanifold dimension r < n that controls the trade-off between per-iteration cost and convergence rate. The output is an optimized orthonormal matrix X ∈ St(n,p). The framework generalizes directly to quotient manifolds (Grassmann, flag) where the input is a representative of an equivalence class of orthogonal matrices rather than a single Stiefel point.

## Method

The key insight is to parameterize each update as X_{k+1} = U_k X_k with U_k ∈ O(n), leveraging the transitive action of the orthogonal group on the Stiefel manifold, then restrict the optimization to a random r-dimensional submanifold via U_k(Y) = P_k^T [Y 0; 0 I_{n-r}] P_k where P_k is a random orthogonal or permutation matrix and Y ∈ O(r). The Riemannian gradient is computed on O(r) and the retraction is performed on O(r) (cost O(r³)) instead of St(n,p) (cost O(np²)), eliminating the non-standard linear algebra bottleneck. Two sampling strategies are provided: uniform orthogonal sampling (Haar measure, per-iteration cost O(npr)) and uniform permutation sampling (per-iteration cost O(nr²)), with the latter being cheaper per iteration but suffering weaker high-probability convergence bounds due to a binomial factor. The paper establishes convergence guarantees for general nonconvex functions, Riemannian PL conditions, and stochastic settings, and shows that RSDM with r=2 and permutation sampling recovers Riemannian coordinate descent as a special case.

## Results

The proposed RSDM achieves O(n²/r²k) convergence for general nonconvex functions and O(exp(−r²/n²·k)) under the Riemannian PL condition, matching the total complexity O(n³ε⁻²) of standard Riemannian gradient descent (RGD) when p ≥ Cn. Per-iteration cost is reduced from O(np²) (full retraction) to O(npr) (orthogonal sampling) or O(nr²) (permutation sampling). Empirically, RSDM converges fastest on PCA (n=2000,p=1500 and n=3000,p=2500) and the Quadratic Assignment Problem (n=1000), and consistently outperforms RGD in test accuracy when training an orthogonal ViT on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RGD (Boumal, 2023), RSDM achieves faster per-iteration cost (O(npr) vs. O(np²)) and empirically converges fastest on PCA and QUAD problems, switching from sublinear to linear convergence near the optimum. On O-ViT training (CIFAR-10), RSDM-P consistently outperforms RGD in test accuracy throughout training with a non-negligible gap. Against RCD (Han et al., 2024a) and TSD (Gutman & Ho-Nguyen, 2023), RSDM wins decisively in wall-clock runtime because coordinate descent requires looping over all n² index pairs per iteration, making it GPU-unfriendly; RSDM with r=2 is theoretically equivalent to RCD but scales to larger r for better parallelism. Against infeasible methods PCAL (Gao et al., 2019) and Landing (Ablin & Peyré, 2022), RSDM achieves the fastest convergence on PCA and QUAD, while on the simpler Procrustes problem all feasible methods (RGD, RSDM, Landing) converge competitively. Orthogonal sampling (RSDM-O) outperforms permutation sampling (RSDM-P) on QUAD and provides tighter high-probability convergence bounds, at the cost of O(npr) vs. O(nr²) per-iteration complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Riemannian optimization framework for Stiefel manifolds (Absil et al., 2008; Boumal, 2023) and the line of coordinate descent methods on the Stiefel manifold (Shalit & Chechik, 2014; Han et al., 2024a; Gutman & Ho-Nguyen, 2023), which update only a few rows/columns per iteration. The key departure is a unified parameterization via the orthogonal group action on a random r-dimensional submanifold, which subsumes coordinate descent as a special case (r=2, permutation sampling) while also generalizing Euclidean random subspace gradient descent (Kozak et al., 2021) to the Riemannian setting. Unlike infeasible/retraction-free methods (Gao et al., 2019; Ablin & Peyré, 2022) that sacrifice constraint satisfaction, RSDM maintains feasibility while reducing retraction cost from O(np²) to O(r³).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical path to scaling Riemannian optimization to large-scale problems where the retraction step dominates computational cost (the p ≥ Cn regime), matching RGD's total complexity while being significantly cheaper per iteration. The trade-off analysis between permutation sampling (cheaper per iteration, O(nr²), but worse high-probability convergence) and orthogonal sampling (more expensive, O(npr), but tighter guarantees) gives practitioners a clear decision framework. A key limitation is that the matching-complexity guarantee only holds when p = Ω(n); extending to the p ≪ n regime where retraction is already cheap remains open. The observed switch from sublinear to linear convergence near optimality (attributed to random projection improving local landscape) is empirically compelling but lacks formal theoretical justification.
