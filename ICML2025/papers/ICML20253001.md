## Problem

Autoregressive models, the dominant paradigm in language generation, are poorly suited to tabular data due to two fundamental mismatches: (1) next-token prediction assumes discrete tokens, yet tabular data contains continuous columns that require non-parametric distribution modeling, and (2) tabular columns are permutation-invariant, so fixed generation orders limit flexibility for conditional tasks like missing data imputation where target and conditioning columns vary. Existing solutions either discretize continuous features (losing information) or enforce rigid column orderings (losing generality).

## Input / Output

The model takes a tabular dataset where all continuous columns are grouped into a single token (standardized to zero mean, unit variance) and each discrete column is label-encoded into its own token, forming a sequence of length 1+D_dst. During training, a random subset of positions is masked and the model predicts their conditional distributions given the unmasked context. At inference, it produces full synthetic rows (continuous values via diffusion sampling, discrete values via categorical sampling) or, for imputation, fills only the missing entries using the RePaint technique to condition on observed continuous values within the denoising loop.

## Method

TabNAT nests a conditional diffusion model inside a bidirectional masked Transformer. A two-layer MLP encodes the continuous columns into a single d-dimensional embedding, while each discrete column uses a learnable category embedding; position-specific mask embeddings replace masked positions, and learnable column-specific positional encodings preserve permutation invariance. The bidirectional Transformer produces output embeddings z for each position, which condition two separate heads: a four-layer MLP denoising network (with x-encoder, z-encoder, and sinusoidal timestep embeddings projected to a shared d_diff=512 space) for continuous columns trained via score-matching under a Variance-Exploding SDE with σ(t)=t, and per-column MLP categorical heads for discrete columns trained via cross-entropy. Training randomly samples the number and positions of masked tokens each step, enabling the model to learn p(x_target | x_S) for arbitrary subsets S without committing to a fixed generation order. At inference, the model supports both parallel generation (all continuous columns at once via a single diffusion pass) and sequential autoregressive generation in any user-specified column order, and for imputation it applies RePaint-style iterative refinement that re-noises known continuous values at each reverse step while denoising the unknown ones.

## Results

On ten tabular datasets, TabNAT achieves the best average fidelity across five of six metrics: Marginal 1.05%, Joint 1.71%, α-Precision 2.01%, C2ST 2.03×10⁻², and JSD 0.09×10⁻², outperforming the second-best method (TabSyn or DP-TBART) by 10.5%–39.5%. On Machine Learning Efficiency, TabNAT yields a 0.72% AUC gap and 0.09 RMSE gap, a >30% improvement over the most competitive baselines. The only metric where TabNAT is not best is β-Recall (48.4%), where the interpolation method SMOTE (16.7%) dominates due to its inherent proximity to real samples. For missing data imputation at 30% MCAR, TabNAT achieves the lowest average MAE on continuous features and highest average accuracy on discrete features across five heterogeneous datasets, surpassing dedicated imputation methods (KNN, GRAPE, MOT, HyperImpute, Remasker).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabNAT outperforms all 10 generation baselines on Marginal, Joint, α-Precision, C2ST, and JSD fidelity metrics, with the closest competitor being TabSyn (Marginal 1.36%, Joint 1.91%) and DP-TBART (Marginal 3.24%, Joint 2.71%). On MLE, TabNAT's 0.72% AUC gap and 0.09 RMSE gap represent a >30% improvement over TabSyn (1.19% / 0.13) and DP-TBART (2.95% / 0.15). The sole exception is β-Recall, where SMOTE (16.7%) substantially outperforms TabNAT (48.4%) because interpolation-based methods inherently produce samples within the real data support. For missing data imputation at 30% MCAR, TabNAT achieves the best average MAE on continuous features and best average accuracy on discrete features across Adult, Default, Shoppers, News, and Beijing, surpassing dedicated imputation methods including Remasker (ICLR 2024), HyperImpute (ICML 2022), MOT (ICML 2020), GRAPE (NeurIPS 2020), and KNN. GAN-based (CTGAN) and VAE-based (TVAE, GOGGLE) methods show the weakest fidelity, with Marginal errors of 15–18% and Joint errors of 17–29%, while LLM-based GReaT performs poorly on joint correlation (44.3%) despite reasonable marginal fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabNAT sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2021; Karras et al., 2022) for continuous data, and masked/bidirectional transformer architectures (Gulati & Roysdon, 2023; Castellon et al., 2023) for discrete sequence modeling. Prior tabular generation methods either apply diffusion to all columns (TabDDPM, TabSyn, STaSy, CoDi) and struggle with discrete features, or discretize continuous columns and use autoregressive/masked transformers (DP-TBART, Tab-MT) at the cost of information loss. TabNAT departs from both by nesting a conditional VE-SDE diffusion model inside a bidirectional masked transformer, using the transformer's output embeddings as conditioning for the diffusion process on continuous columns while directly optimizing categorical distributions for discrete columns. The RePaint technique (Lugmayr et al., 2022) is repurposed for partial continuous-column imputation during the reverse denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental architectural tension in tabular generation: autoregressive models excel at discrete tokens but require fixed ordering and parametric assumptions for continuous values, while diffusion models handle continuous data naturally but lack explicit dependency modeling. TabNAT's order-agnostic bidirectional attention enables a single model to serve both unconditional generation and flexible conditional imputation without retraining, which is practically valuable for privacy-preserving data sharing and ML pipeline augmentation. Limitations include sampling time dominated by the diffusion process (~15.7s vs ~2.1s for DP-TBART on Adult), and the approach has not been tested on very high-dimensional tables (max 46 continuous columns in News). Open questions include whether the hybrid design scales to tables with hundreds of columns, whether the diffusion component can be replaced by lighter alternatives without fidelity loss, and how the method performs under differential privacy constraints beyond the DP-TBART comparison.
