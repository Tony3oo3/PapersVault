## Problem

Training semi-implicit variational inference (SIVI) models in high dimensions requires estimating the intractable score gradient ∇_z log q_z. The only known unbiased estimator (UIVI) relies on an inner MCMC loop targeting the intractable conditional q_{ε|z}, which is often multimodal and computationally prohibitive, leading the community to dismiss UIVI as impractical. This paper revisits UIVI and shows its MCMC loop can be effectively replaced by importance sampling with a learned proposal, restoring the ELBO as a viable training objective for SIVI.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target density (e.g., a Bayesian posterior defined by a likelihood and prior over a continuous variable z ∈ R^{d_Z}) and produces samples from a learned semi-implicit distribution q_z, parameterized by a neural sampler f_φ that maps latent variables ε to parameters of a conditional density q_{z|ε}. A key structural detail is that the score gradient is estimated via importance sampling over k latent samples ε_i drawn from a conditional normalizing flow τ_{ε|z}, and the batch-level aggregation scheme (using logaddexp) allows k to be arbitrarily large while keeping memory constant, decoupling the number of importance samples from the backpropagation cost.

## Method

AISIVI alternates between two optimization steps: (1) training a conditional normalizing flow τ_{ε|z} (built from affine coupling layers) by minimizing the expected forward KL divergence E_{z~q_z}[D_KL(q_{ε|z}‖τ_{ε|z})], whose gradient is unbiased because q_{z,ε} does not depend on the flow parameters; and (2) training the SIVI neural sampler by minimizing the reverse KL D_KL(q_z‖p_z) via the path gradient estimator, where the intractable score term ∇_z log q_z is estimated by an importance-sampling Monte Carlo estimator s_{IS,k} using samples from τ_{ε|z}. The paper proves that choosing τ_{ε|z} = q_{ε|z} (the global optimum of the forward KL) debiases the score gradient estimate, and that the estimator is consistent for any proposal with sufficient support. A constant-memory batch aggregation algorithm (logaddexp-based) enables processing arbitrarily many importance samples without increasing backpropagation cost, making the method scalable to high-dimensional problems (up to 100 dimensions in experiments).

## Results

On the 100-dimensional conditioned diffusion process, AISIVI achieves a log marginal likelihood of 74,062 in 10K iterations, approaching the state-of-the-art KSIVI (74,521) which requires 100K iterations. AISIVI outperforms IWHVI (67,667), BSIVI (60,556), PVI (53,121), and UIVI (40,207) by 6,400, 13,500, 20,900, and 33,800 log-likelihood points respectively. On the 22-dimensional Bayesian logistic regression (WAVEFORM), all methods produce comparable marginal and pairwise density estimates, with PVI and KSIVI showing a marginally tighter spread in correlation coefficients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AISIVI nearly matches KSIVI (74,062 vs. 74,521 log ML) while requiring only 10% of the training iterations (10K vs. 100K), making it the most efficient method to reach near-gold-standard accuracy. Against IWHVI, AISIVI gains ~6,400 log-likelihood points, demonstrating the benefit of a more expressive CNF proposal over IWHVI's conditional Gaussian under sequential (rather than joint) training. AISIVI substantially outperforms PVI by ~20,900 log-likelihood points, suggesting that the particle-based Wasserstein gradient flow approach is less effective in this high-dimensional setting. Compared to the original UIVI, AISIVI improves by ~33,800 log-likelihood points; however, UIVI was limited to an inner batch size of 2 due to its MCMC overhead, so this gap partly reflects computational constraints rather than a pure algorithmic comparison. On the 22-dim WAVEFORM benchmark, all methods perform comparably, with PVI and KSIVI showing a marginally better fit in pairwise correlation coefficients, but no method dominates clearly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits UIVI (Titsias & Ruiz, 2019), which was largely dismissed due to its computationally prohibitive MCMC inner loop, and replaces that loop with importance sampling using a conditional normalizing flow (CNF) as the proposal distribution. It builds on the path gradient estimator from Roeder et al. (2017), which Titsias & Ruiz derived as an intermediate result but whose implications for SIVI training were never fully exploited. The sequential (alternating) training of the CNF proposal and the SIVI model departs from Sobolev & Vetrov's (2019) joint optimization, making expressive conditional models like CNFs feasible in practice. The overall framework repositions the ELBO as a viable SIVI training objective, competing with newer kernel Stein discrepancy (KSIVI) and particle-based (PVI) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a CNF-learned importance sampling proposal can make the ELBO competitive with kernel-based and particle-based SIVI methods, this work offers a scalable, kernel-free alternative for high-dimensional variational inference that is easier to implement and tune. The memory-efficient batch aggregation technique (Section 3.2) is a practical contribution enabling arbitrarily large inner sample counts without proportional memory growth. However, the method lacks inherent exploration capabilities for multimodal targets, and the authors acknowledge that alternative NF architectures or a principled annealing strategy could further improve performance. The gap to KSIVI remains small but nonzero, and whether the approach maintains parity at even higher dimensions or with more complex targets remains an open question.
