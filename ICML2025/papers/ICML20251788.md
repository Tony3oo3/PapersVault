## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks such as NIAH, RULER, and BABILong contain literal lexical matches between the question and the relevant context, allowing models to exploit surface-level matching rather than genuine associative reasoning. This makes it impossible to determine whether models truly understand latent associations in long contexts or merely pattern-match on shared keywords. The paper asks: how do models perform on long-context retrieval when such literal matches are removed?

## Input / Output

The input consists of a long haystack (concatenated book snippets up to 128K tokens) with a single needle sentence embedded at one of 26 evenly spaced positions, paired with a question asking which character possesses a specific attribute. The question and needle share no literal keywords; they are linked only through latent associations (e.g., 'Dresden' in the question connects to 'Semper Opera House' in the needle via world knowledge). The output is the character name, and a test is correct if the returned answer contains the correct name. Each context-length experiment comprises 7,540 tests (58 question-needle pairs × 26 placements × 5 haystacks).

## Method

NOLiMA introduces a carefully designed needle set of 58 question-needle pairs across 5 template groups, where the query keyword (W_q) and needle keyword (W_n) are connected through one-hop or two-hop associative links (world knowledge or commonsense) with zero lexical overlap. The haystack is constructed from 10 open-license books and processed through a two-stage filtering pipeline: Contriever embeddings identify and remove semantically similar distractor words, and a Llama 3.3 70B-based redaction step detects and removes text spans that could serve as false answers. The benchmark is evaluated across 13+ LLMs at context lengths from 250 to 128K tokens, with additional analyses on latent hop difficulty, word-order inversion, needle placement depth, CoT prompting, reasoning-based models (GPT-o1, DeepSeek-R1), and ablations showing that literal matches as cues simplify the task while literal matches as distractors severely impair performance.

## Results

On NOLiMA, 13 LLMs claiming 128K+ context support are evaluated on associative-retrieval accuracy. At 32K tokens, 11 of 13 models drop below 50% of their short-context base scores; even GPT-4o (the best performer) falls from a 99.3% base to 69.7%. Most models have an effective context length of ≤2K tokens (defined as the longest length where accuracy stays above 85% of base), far below their claimed 128K–2M limits. Two-hop (multi-step associative) examples degrade performance further, with Llama 3.3 70B dropping to 25.9% at 32K versus 56.2% for one-hop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOLiMA is positioned against five existing long-context benchmarks primarily via ROUGE precision scores (Table 1) measuring literal overlap between question and relevant context. NOLiMA achieves R-1 = 0.069, R-2 = 0.002, R-L = 0.067, dramatically lower than Vanilla NIAH (0.905/0.789/0.855), RULER QA (0.809/0.437/0.693), ∞Bench QA (0.966/0.545/0.960), HELMET RAG (0.689/0.304/0.555), and BABILong 0K (0.553/0.238/0.522), confirming minimal surface-level matching. In terms of effective context length for Llama 3.1 70B, NOLiMA yields only 2K, compared to 16K on BABILong and 32K on RULER, showing that removing literal matches exposes a much shorter true retrieval horizon. The paper also shows that adding literal matches back (via direct or multiple-choice ablations) restores accuracy to ~98% even at 32K, confirming that the performance gap versus NIAH-style baselines is driven by the absence of surface cues rather than task complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) line of work (Kamradt, 2023) and its complexity-increasing variants (RULER, BABILong, HELMET) by identifying a shared confound: existing long-context benchmarks contain literal lexical matches between questions and relevant context, allowing models to exploit surface-level attention rather than genuine associative reasoning. NOLiMA deliberately removes this overlap by designing needle sets where questions and needles are linked only through latent associations (world knowledge or commonsense), requiring one- or two-hop inference. The haystack filtering pipeline (using Contriever embeddings and LLM-based redaction) is a methodological contribution that ensures no distracting or false-answer content contaminates the irrelevant context, going beyond the simple random-text haystacks used in prior NIAH work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 11 of 13 models claiming 128K+ context windows have effective lengths of only 1–8K tokens when literal matches are removed has direct implications for RAG systems and search engines, where relevant documents often have lexical gaps with user queries. The ablation showing that *distracting* literal matches (irrelevant overlaps) further degrade performance highlights a practical failure mode: models may latch onto superficially similar but irrelevant content while missing the truly relevant fact. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., sparse attention, retrieval-augmented decoding) can close this gap, whether the two-hop and inverted-order findings generalize beyond the 58-pair needle set, and whether the results hold for non-English or multilingual settings.
