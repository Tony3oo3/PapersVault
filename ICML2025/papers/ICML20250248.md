## Problem

The Metric Nearness Problem requires projecting a noisy or incomplete distance matrix onto the space of valid metric matrices, but the O(N³) triangle inequality constraints make existing projection-based methods (TRF, HLWB, PAF) prohibitively expensive and poorly scalable beyond a few thousand points. No prior approach simultaneously guarantees metric compliance by construction, supports real-time incremental updates, and scales to 10⁵×10⁵ matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-metric distance matrix D ∈ R^{N×N} (which may violate symmetry, non-negativity, or the triangle inequality, and may contain missing entries or Gaussian noise) and produces a metric-compliant matrix that minimizes the weighted Frobenius distance to D. Crucially, it also outputs a compact embedding matrix A ∈ R^{N×k} (k ≪ N) whose tropical inner product with its transpose reconstructs the full distance matrix, reducing storage from O(N²) to O(Nk). For the MetricPlug variant, the input is a pair of embedding matrices from any neural network, and the output is a guaranteed-metric distance matrix computed via ReLU-activated tropical inner product.

## Method

The core insight is that the max-tropical inner product (max-plus matrix multiplication) of two non-negative matrices inherently satisfies the triangle inequality (Theorem 5), so any matrix of the form (A ⊙_max A^T)^off with A ≥ 0 is automatically a valid metric matrix—eliminating the need for explicit constraint enforcement. MetricEmbedding is a multi-layer network where each layer replaces standard matrix multiplication with max-tropical multiplication and bias addition with element-wise max (tropical addition), with all parameters constrained non-negative; the final output is passed through (· ⊙_max ·^T)^off to produce the metric matrix. Training minimizes the Frobenius norm between the predicted metric matrix and the target D via backpropagation through tropical operations with AdamW, and a minibatch variant reduces space to O(Nk) by sampling point pairs. The TA-metric representation theorem (Theorem 8) formally characterizes the representational space, showing it equals the set of zero-diagonal non-negative matrices satisfying a specific min-based triangle inequality condition. A separate MetricPlug module inserts a single tropical inner product layer into any existing metric or contrastive learning pipeline, guaranteeing the output distance matrix satisfies all metric axioms without architectural changes.

## Results

On synthetic complete-graph distance matrices (N=100 to 1000), MetricEmbedding achieves up to ~75× speedup over baselines (e.g., 26.73 s vs >2000 s for TRF/HLWB at N=1000) while maintaining 0% triangle inequality violations. The NMSE is slightly higher than baselines (0.084 vs 0.059 for TRF at N=100; 0.136 vs 0.058 at N=1000), reflecting a modest accuracy trade-off for the large speed gain. The method scales to 100,000×100,000 matrices within 12 hours, far exceeding the ~10,000-point limit of HLWB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetricEmbedding decisively outperforms all three baselines on computation time: at N=1000 it runs in 26.73 s versus >2000 s for TRF and HLWB and 1619.68 s for PAF, a 60–75× speedup. On triangle inequality violations, MetricEmbedding ties HLWB and PAF at 0% but clearly beats TRF, which retains 4.5–4.85% violations at all tested sizes. On reconstruction accuracy (NMSE), MetricEmbedding is consistently worse than all baselines—the gap widens with N (0.084 vs 0.059 at N=100; 0.136 vs 0.058 at N=1000 vs TRF)—indicating a speed-accuracy trade-off. Against PAF specifically, MetricEmbedding is ~31× faster at N=100 and ~60× faster at N=1000 while incurring roughly 2× higher NMSE. The paper also reports that MetricEmbedding scales to 100,000-point matrices (12 h), whereas HLWB is limited to ~10,000 points and TRF/PAF to ~2,000–3,000 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes tropical (max-plus) algebra—a tool long used in optimization and shortest-path computation—into a neural network architecture for the Metric Nearness Problem, a task previously addressed by iterative projection methods (TRF, PAF, HLWB) that enforce O(N³) triangle inequality constraints explicitly. The key conceptual departure is the TA-metric representation theorem, which shows that the max-tropical inner product of non-negative matrices inherently satisfies the triangle inequality, eliminating the need for projection-based constraint enforcement. The MetricPlug extension further adapts this algebraic structure as a drop-in replacement for Euclidean/cosine distance in contrastive learning, drawing on the GRACE framework but substituting the similarity metric rather than the network architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60× speedup and ability to scale to 10⁵×10⁵ matrices with O(N²) space (vs. O(N³) for TRF/PAF) make metric repair practical for large-scale applications like recommendation systems and real-time path planning where prior methods were infeasible. However, the quality-speed tradeoff is notable: NMSE is consistently 30–130% worse than TRF/HLWB baselines (e.g., 0.136 vs. 0.058 at N=1000), and all experiments use synthetic complete-graph data, leaving open how the method performs on real-world noisy, sparse, or asymmetric distance matrices. The contrastive learning gains from MetricPlug are marginal (79.4% vs. 79.2% test accuracy on Cora), and the min-tropical variant is shown to *not* satisfy the triangle inequality, limiting the generality of the algebraic framework. Open questions include whether the quality gap can be closed with deeper architectures or better initialization, and whether the approach extends to directed or weighted-graph settings beyond complete graphs.
