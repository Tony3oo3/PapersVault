## Problem

Existing federated learning mechanisms that incentivize strategic data sharing (e.g., data-share-maximizing or welfare-maximizing mechanisms) achieve efficiency at Nash equilibrium but are not reciprocally fair: some agents are forced to contribute more to the federation than they receive in return. The paper asks whether a budget-balanced mechanism can simultaneously guarantee full reciprocity (each agent's payoff equals her Shapley-value contribution) and admit efficient Nash equilibria with strong data-gain and welfare guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each agent i contributes s_i data samples (interpreted as the number of local training batches) drawn from a private distribution D_i, along with a payoff function a_i(s) capturing model accuracy and a private cost function c_i(s_i). The mechanism is cost-agnostic: the central server never needs to observe or verify c_i. In the practical FedBR-Shap protocol, agents share only gradient updates (not raw data) and locally train two models (with s_i and s_i+ε batches) to estimate utility derivatives. The output is a jointly trained global model, a zero-sum payment vector p_i(s) = φ_i^A(s) − a_i(s) per agent, and the Nash-equilibrium contribution vector s*.

## Method

The proposed mechanism M^Shap sets each agent's payment to p_i(s) = φ_i^A(s) − a_i(s), where φ_i^A(s) is the Shapley value of agent i's marginal contribution to the cumulative accuracy A(s) = Σ_i a_i(s). This makes the mechanism fully reciprocal by construction (a_i + p_i = φ_i^A), budget-balanced (payments sum to zero via the efficiency property of Shapley values), and cost-agnostic. NE existence follows from Kakutani's fixed-point theorem under standard concavity/convexity assumptions, and stochastic best-response dynamics converge to an ε-approximate NE in O(log(1/ε)) iterations with a step size depending on strong-concavity and Lipschitz constants. For canonical accuracy functions a(s) = 1 − α(‖s‖₁+1)^{−β} with linear costs, the mechanism achieves DataGain ≥ n^{1/(β+1)} ≥ √n and AccuracyGain > 1, both improving with the number of agents. The distributed FedBR-Shap protocol implements M^Shap without requiring closed-form accuracy functions: at each round the server aggregates intermediate models from subsets of agents' gradient updates, enabling each agent to approximate the derivative of its Shapley-based utility and update its batch count accordingly.

## Results

M^Shap achieves full reciprocity (1.00) across all five datasets, while baselines M^0 and M^BG range from 0.47 to 0.95. In terms of data sharing, M^Shap yields a 9.51× data gain on MNIST, 13.43× on FashionMNIST, and 3.89× on CIFAR-10 relative to M^0, with data shares reaching 54.9%, 54.8%, and 99.6% respectively (vs. 5.8%, 4.1%, and 25.6% for M^0). Average model accuracy also improves: 90.4% on MNIST (vs. 88.3% M^0, 87.6% M^BG), 63.3% on FashionMNIST (vs. 60.9%, 61.5%), and 48.5% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 43.6%, 44.7%). On the synthetic quadratic regression dataset, M^Shap achieves a 31.18× data gain and 1.36× accuracy gain (90.8% vs. 67.2% and 65.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares M^Shap against two baselines: M^0 (no payments, agents act purely on accuracy minus cost) and M^BG (the welfare-maximizing mechanism from Murhekar et al., 2023). M^Shap dominates both baselines on reciprocity, achieving exactly 1.00 (fully reciprocal) while M^0 ranges from 0.47–0.93 and M^BG from 0.02–0.95, meaning the baselines force some agents to contribute more than they receive. On data gain, M^Shap substantially outperforms both: e.g., 9.51× vs. 1.32× (M^BG) on MNIST, and 13.43× vs. 1.51× on FashionMNIST. On accuracy, M^Shap wins on all five datasets, with the largest margin on the synthetic quadratic task (90.8% vs. 67.2% for M^0 and 65.8% for M^BG). Notably, on the Lumpy Skin Disease dataset, M^BG's reciprocity drops to 0.02, indicating severe unfairness where the high-data-quality agent is heavily exploited, whereas M^Shap maintains 1.00 and correctly incentivizes the lower-quality-data agent to contribute more (55.4% vs. 38.1% data share).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FL mechanism-design lineage initiated by Karimireddy et al. (2022) and Murhekar et al. (2023), which established the strategic data-sharing framework and proved existence of Nash equilibria under concave payoffs and convex costs. The key departure is the introduction of a fairness criterion—reciprocity measured via the Shapley value—as the payment rule, in contrast to prior mechanisms that optimize purely for data-sharing volume or utilitarian welfare. The stochastic best-response dynamics with convergence guarantees generalize the deterministic BR dynamics of Murhekar et al. (2023), and the cost-agnostic design eliminates the need for the central server to verify agents' private cost functions, a practical limitation of the welfare-maximizing mechanism M^BG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that a fully reciprocal (Shapley-based) mechanism simultaneously achieves strong efficiency guarantees (Ω(√n) data gain, Pareto-optimality in the data-vs-welfare tradeoff) without requiring knowledge of agents' costs—resolves a perceived tension between fairness and efficiency in FL mechanism design. The cost-agnostic property has direct practical value: it removes the incentive for agents to misreport acquisition costs and eliminates a verification burden on the server. Open questions include extending the theoretical bounds beyond the canonical accuracy form a(s)=1−α·(‖s‖₁+1)^−β, designing r-reciprocal mechanisms for r<1 that trade some fairness for higher data gain, and scaling Shapley-value computation to cross-device FL settings with thousands of clients where the current Monte-Carlo approximation becomes costly.
