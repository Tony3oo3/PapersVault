## Problem

Score-based diffusion models lack principled tools for controlling inference-time behavior: common heuristics like classifier-free guidance or score addition for product-of-experts do not actually sample from the prescribed intermediate or final distributions, and existing corrector schemes (Langevin steps, MCMC) are either only exact in the infinite-step limit or require expensive retraining. The paper asks whether one can derive lightweight, principled correction terms that make a batch of simulated particles faithfully approximate annealed, product, or geometric-averaged target distributions without retraining the underlying score models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained score functions ∇log q_t(x) from one or more diffusion models (e.g., conditional and unconditional EDM2 for image generation, or two target-conditioned models for dual-protein ligand design) and produces samples from a modified target density such as q_t(x)^β, q_t^1(x)·q_t^2(x), or a geometric average. It operates on a batch of K particles simultaneously; the final output is a single sample selected via weighted resampling from the batch. For image generation the input is a text prompt and the output is a 512×512 RGB image; for drug design the input is a pair of protein binding-pocket structures and the output is a 3D ligand with docking scores to both targets.

## Method

The core contribution is a set of conversion rules (Table 1) that decompose the Fokker-Planck PDE of a target density (annealed, product, or geometric average) into a standard SDE drift/diffusion part plus an explicit reweighting term g_t(x), yielding a Feynman-Kac weighted SDE whose weighted particles are properly distributed under the target. For linear drifts f_t(x) common in diffusion models, the correction weights depend only on score differences or inner products already computed during simulation, adding zero extra model evaluations. To control weight variance, the paper applies Sequential Monte Carlo resampling (systematic or birth-death-clock) over a user-specified active interval [t_min, t_max], where particles with low accumulated weight are replaced by copies of high-weight particles. This turns the batch size K into an inference-time scaling knob: larger K improves fidelity to the target distribution at the cost of K× score evaluations per step, with no retraining required.

## Results

On ImageNet-512 with EDM2-XS, adding FKC resampling to CFG improves CLIP score from 28.74–28.81 to 28.97–29.14 and ImageReward from −0.25 to +0.05 across noise levels and step counts (best: CLIP 29.14, IR 0.05 at γ=40, N=32). For dual-target molecule generation, PoE+FKC (β=2.0) achieves the lowest maximum docking score (−7.720) and the highest fraction of molecules better than known reference binders (45.0%), outperforming both TargetDiff (−6.961, 32.1%) and DualDiff (−7.030, 30.6%). On the LJ-13 Boltzmann sampling task, train-then-anneal with FKC reduces the 2-Wasserstein distance to MCMC reference from 0.103 (DEM at T_S=0.8) to 0.048 (Target Score+FKC), and from 0.268 (DEM at T_S=1.5) to 0.066 (Tempered Noise+FKC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2021), FKC resampling consistently improves both CLIP score (+0.2 to +0.31 points) and ImageReward (+0.22 to +0.26 points) across all tested noise levels and step counts, with the largest gains at lower step counts (N=32). Against DEM (Akhound-Sadegh et al., 2024) on the LJ-13 system, the train-then-anneal FKC approach substantially outperforms direct training at the target temperature: at T_S=0.8, Target Score+FKC achieves Distance-W2 of 0.048 vs. DEM's 0.103, and at T_S=1.5, Tempered Noise+FKC achieves 0.066 vs. DEM's 0.268. Against TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023) and DualDiff (Zhou et al., 2024) on dual-target drug design, PoE+FKC (β=2.0) achieves the best maximum docking score (−7.720 vs. −6.961 and −7.030) and the highest percentage of molecules beating known reference binders (45.0% vs. 32.1% and 30.6%), though at a slight cost in diversity (0.806 vs. 0.886 and 0.883) and uniqueness (0.862 vs. 0.918 and 0.943). Overall, FKC wins on all primary quality metrics across all three experimental settings, with the trade-off being modest reductions in sample diversity for the molecule generation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion model framework (Song et al., 2021) and the classifier-free guidance heuristic (Ho & Salimans, 2021), identifying that standard score-combination methods (CFG, product-of-experts) do not actually sample from their prescribed target marginals. The key conceptual departure is the use of the Feynman-Kac formula to derive exact reweighting terms that, combined with Sequential Monte Carlo resampling (Naesseth et al., 2019), convert these heuristics into principled samplers. This connects to the Jarzynski equality and free-energy computation literature, and differs from predictor-corrector approaches (Bradley & Nakkiran, 2024) which target a different guidance weight β′ = (1+β)/2 rather than the original β.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FKC framework provides a general, theoretically grounded recipe for composing or tempering pretrained diffusion models at inference time without retraining, which is practically important for drug discovery, multi-objective generation, and controlled image synthesis. The train-at-high-temperature-then-anneal strategy offers a viable alternative to the notoriously difficult task of directly training samplers at low temperatures. Limitations include the requirement for accurate score estimates, computational overhead from maintaining K > 1 particles, and observed weight degeneracy at final steps that reduces diversity. Open questions include optimal particle counts at scale, extension to latent diffusion models (briefly explored in the appendix), and whether the framework can handle more complex compositions beyond pairwise products.
