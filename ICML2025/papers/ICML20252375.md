## Problem

Models trained on imbalanced tabular data inherit spurious correlations with non-causal features, leading to poor worst-group performance under distribution shift. Existing reweighting methods either require unavailable group labels or rely solely on classification boundaries P(Y|X), failing to balance the overall data distribution P(X) and thus leaving underrepresented regions underweighted.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw tabular features and binary class labels as input, requiring no group annotations or prior distributional assumptions. It produces two outputs: per-sample reweighting scores (derived from SimDiff, a score-similarity difference measure) and final class predictions. At inference time, only the VAE encoder and a lightweight MLP classifier are deployed; the score models are used exclusively during the weight-computation stage.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages: (1) a β-VAE encodes tabular features into latent representations, and class-conditional EDM-style score models (4-layer MLPs with σ-dependent preconditioning) are trained separately on each class's latent data to capture the joint distribution P(X,Y); (2) for each sample, an aggregated similarity is computed by averaging the reconstruction error of the score model across T fixed timesteps and K noise samples, and a SimDiff score is obtained by subtracting the own-class similarity from the other-class similarity, which simultaneously captures covariate imbalance (X-shift) and label imbalance (Y-shift); (3) softmax-normalized weights derived from SimDiff (with a temperature parameter τ) are applied to train a weighted cross-entropy classifier. The key insight is that score-similarity serves as a stable proxy for relative density—avoiding the 'density explosion' problem of exact log-likelihood—because in high-density regions, neighboring score vectors align directionally toward the sample, yielding lower reconstruction error.

## Results

On five tabular datasets (Adult, Bank, Default, Shoppers, Taxi), the proposed latent score-based reweighting method achieves the highest worst-group accuracy across all datasets, with an average worst-case accuracy of 51.74% compared to the best baseline (FAM) at 48.71%—a 3.03-point improvement. The method also attains the best mean accuracy on two of five datasets (Adult: 74.33%, Shoppers: 79.68%) while maintaining competitive mean accuracy elsewhere. On the ACS PUMS generalization experiment (cross-state transfer), the method achieves 65.23% average worst-case accuracy, outperforming all baselines by at least 4 points. When integrated with tree-based models (CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost), the score-derived weights consistently improve worst-group accuracy by 4–14 points over unweighted baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on worst-group accuracy across every dataset. Against the strongest baseline FAM (avg worst 48.71%), the improvement is +3.03 points on average, with the largest gap on Adult (+5.20 pts) and Taxi (+0.90 pts). KL-DRO is the most inconsistent: it achieves the second-best worst accuracy on Shoppers (59.74%) but collapses to 19.39% on Default and 47.93% on Taxi, yielding the lowest average worst accuracy (43.22%). EIIL, which uses environment inference to create implicit boundaries, shows the weakest worst-case performance overall (38.75% avg), particularly on Bank (21.69%) and Default (28.91%). JTT, the boundary-based reweighting method the paper most directly contrasts with, trails the proposed method by 4.58 points on average worst accuracy (47.16% vs. 51.74%), confirming the paper's argument that focusing solely on P(Y|X) decision boundaries fails to balance the overall data distribution P(X). On mean accuracy, the proposed method wins on Adult (74.33%) and Shoppers (79.68%) but is slightly behind FAM on Bank (69.50% vs. 71.03%) and Default (62.62% vs. 62.50%), indicating the robustness gains come at minimal cost to overall accuracy. SRDO (stable learning via sample reweighting), the closest method in spirit, is outperformed by 7.11 points on average worst accuracy, which the authors attribute to SRDO's reliance on feature decorrelation under linear assumptions rather than full joint distribution modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes score-based generative models (Song & Ermon, 2019; Song et al., 2021) and the EDM preconditioning framework (Karras et al., 2022) for a fundamentally different purpose: estimating relative sample density for reweighting, rather than generation. It adopts the latent diffusion paradigm from TabSyn (Zhang et al., 2024c) and Rombach et al. (2022) to operate in a VAE-compressed space suited to tabular data. Conceptually, it departs from boundary-based reweighting methods (JTT, EIIL) that optimize over P(Y|X) and from DRO approaches (CVaR-DRO, KL-DRO) that require group labels or expand the search space, by instead modeling the full joint distribution P(X,Y) and using a score-similarity proxy (SimDiff) to avoid the numerical instability of exact log-likelihood computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in tabular ML: achieving robustness across multiple non-causal covariates without requiring group annotations, which are often unavailable in real deployments. Its compatibility with tree-based models (CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost) is notable given their dominance in industrial tabular settings. Limitations include the additional compute cost of training VAEs and per-class diffusion models, the current restriction to binary classification, and the need to tune the temperature τ. Open questions include extension to multi-class settings, scalability to very high-dimensional tabular data, and whether the SimDiff proxy generalizes to continuous-valued targets or multi-task scenarios.
