## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA represent Multi-Head Attention (MHA) projection layers as 2D matrices, which by definition cannot capture correlations beyond the second axis and thus severely limit representation capacity for the inherently high-dimensional head structure. While tensor-based methods such as FacT partially address this by stacking layers into 3D tensors, they still do not explicitly model the head dimension, and the Canonical-Polyadic Decomposition (CPD) form has not been explored as a PEFT mechanism for Vision Transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CaRA operates on standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for VTAB-1k and FGVC datasets) and produces class labels via the ViT's classification head. The method is a fine-tuning strategy: it takes a frozen pretrained ViT (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/16 on ImageNet-21k) and adds only a small set of trainable CPD factor matrices (≈0.06–0.08M parameters for ViT-B). During inference, the learned low-rank updates are merged into the pretrained weights, incurring zero additional latency or memory overhead compared to the original model.

## Method

CaRA proposes a novel two-tensor decomposition of the ViT: a 4D tensor W^mha ∈ R^{3l×d_model×h×d_h} stacking all Q, K, V projections across l blocks and h heads, and a 3D tensor W^ffn ∈ R^{9l×d_model×d_model} stacking the output projection and feed-forward layers. The low-rank update for each tensor is expressed in Canonical-Polyadic Decomposition form—a sum of R rank-1 outer products with learned scaling factors—rather than the matrix product (LoRA) or Tensor-Train/Tucker decompositions (FacT). This 4D MHA representation explicitly captures inter-head correlations that 2D or 3D formulations cannot, while CPD's linear parameter scaling (R + R·Σ dimensions) avoids the exponential growth of Tensor-Train/Tucker with rank. The method is trained with standard gradient descent on the CPD factors (with specific initialisation: random normal for the block-index factor, zeros for the model-dimension factor, and orthogonal matrices for the remaining factors), and the resulting updates are merged into pretrained weights at inference time.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16 backbone), CaRA achieves 74.14% overall mean top-1 accuracy with only 0.06M trainable parameters, outperforming LoRA (72.25%, 0.29M) by 1.9% with 5× fewer parameters and matching SPT-LoRA (74.07%, 0.54M) with 9× fewer parameters. On the FGVC benchmark, CaRA reaches 90.46% mean accuracy with 0.08M parameters, surpassing SPT-LoRA (90.04%, 0.54M). On ViT-Large fine-tuning, CaRA achieves 88.77% mean accuracy with 0.076M parameters, outperforming DoRA (87.60%, 0.860M) by 1.2% with 11× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaRA outperforms LoRA by 1.9% overall mean on VTAB-1k (74.14% vs. 72.25%) while using 5× fewer trainable parameters (0.06M vs. 0.29M). Against the tensor-based FacT variants, CaRA achieves +1.1% over FacT-TT (73.04%) and +0.9% over FacT-TK (73.23%) with comparable parameter budgets (0.06M vs. 0.04M/0.07M). CaRA matches SPT-LoRA (74.14% vs. 74.07%) while requiring only 1/9th of its parameters (0.06M vs. 0.54M), and surpasses all adapter-based methods (VPT-Deep 69.43%, SSF 73.10%, RepAdapter 73.84%) by 0.3–4.7%. On FGVC, CaRA (90.46%) beats SPT-LoRA (90.04%) and VPT-Deep (89.10%) with 7× fewer parameters, achieving SOTA on 3 of 5 datasets. On ViT-Large, CaRA (88.77%) outperforms DoRA (87.60%) and PiSSA (86.24%) with 11× and 10× fewer parameters respectively. On the GLUE NLU benchmark, CaRA (85.9 mean) surpasses LoTR (77.2–79.0) and LoRETTA_adapter (85.2) with fewer parameters, though it remains slightly below LoRA (87.2) and FT (86.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

CaRA extends the tensor-based PEFT line initiated by FacT (Jie & Deng, 2023), which first stacked all ViT projection layers into a single 3D tensor and applied Tensor-Train or Tucker decompositions for low-rank updates. The key departure is twofold: (1) CaRA decouples the transformer into a 4D MHA tensor (explicitly preserving the head dimension) and a 3D FFN tensor, rather than collapsing everything into one 3D tensor, and (2) it replaces Tensor-Train/Tucker with Canonical-Polyadic Decomposition (CPD), which the authors argue is less susceptible to the curse of dimensionality. The additive low-rank update paradigm itself is inherited directly from LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), with the CPD factor matrices playing the role of LoRA's A and B matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaRA achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on VTAB-1k and FGVC with roughly 5× fewer trainable parameters than LoRA (0.06M vs 0.29M) and matches SPT-LoRA at ~9× fewer parameters, making it a compelling default for parameter-constrained fine-tuning of ViTs. The explicit 4D MHA tensorisation is a clean conceptual contribution: it shows that the head dimension carries structure that 2D matrix updates fundamentally cannot capture, and that CPD's linear parameter scaling in rank (vs. exponential for Tensor-Train) makes the method robust to rank selection. Open questions include whether a weight-normalised CPD variant (analogous to DoRA) can close the remaining gap to full fine-tuning, and whether hardware-optimised tensor kernels can eliminate the ~25% training-time overhead over LoRA.
