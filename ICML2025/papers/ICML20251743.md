## Problem

While parallel data collection is a standard technique in on-policy RL, the trade-off between scaling the number of parallel environments (N_envs) versus rollout length (N_RO) under a fixed data budget remains underexplored. Additionally, the connection between how data is structured during collection and network-level optimization pathologies—such as feature collapse, dormant neurons, and loss of plasticity—lacks systematic empirical characterization.

## Input / Output

The agents consume RGB game frames (Atari-10, Procgen) or low-dimensional state vectors (Isaac Gym) and output discrete actions (Atari, Procgen) or continuous actions (Isaac Gym), with PPO additionally producing value estimates. The critical structural detail is that each training batch is formed as B = N_envs × N_RO, where N_envs environments each roll out for N_RO timesteps; the paper's central question is how to allocate a given data budget between these two factors, since they induce different bias-variance and diversity profiles in the collected trajectories.

## Method

This is a systematic empirical study that varies N_envs and N_RO under fixed and scaling data budgets using PPO (policy-based) and PQN (value-based) on Atari-10, Procgen, and Isaac Gym benchmarks. The authors track a suite of learning-dynamics metrics—feature rank, dormant neuron fraction, weight norm, gradient kurtosis, policy variance, effective sample size, and UMAP-based state-space coverage—to link data collection structure to optimization stability and plasticity. The key finding is that, for a fixed batch size, scaling N_envs (e.g., 128 envs × 8 steps) outperforms scaling N_RO (e.g., 8 envs × 128 steps), and that this advantage persists when the total data budget is increased or when the number of training epochs is raised. They further show that the gains are robust to learning-rate and discount-factor choices, are amplified by decoupled actor-critic encoders, and are most pronounced in policy-based methods compared to value-based PQN.

## Results

On the Atari-10 benchmark (100M timesteps, 5 seeds, human-normalized IQM), scaling N_envs from 8 to 128 (while reducing N_RO from 128 to 8 to maintain a fixed data budget) consistently outperforms the reverse configuration, yielding higher final returns and better sample efficiency. When the data budget is increased, scaling N_envs produces larger performance gains than scaling N_RO. Increasing epochs from 4 to 8 causes performance degradation, but this decline is 'completely overcome by doubling the number of environments.' Learning dynamics metrics confirm the benefit: higher N_envs leads to increased feature rank, reduced dormant neuron percentage, lower weight norm, lower gradient kurtosis, lower policy variance, and higher effective sample size (ESS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline algorithms; instead, it ablates the data collection configuration of PPO (and PQN) under fixed or scaled data budgets. The primary comparison is between scaling N_envs (more parallel environments, shorter rollouts) versus scaling N_RO (fewer environments, longer rollouts) at equal total batch size: N_envs scaling wins on final performance, sample efficiency, and all optimization-stability metrics (feature rank, dormant neurons, weight norm, gradient kurtosis, policy variance, ESS). A secondary comparison is PPO (policy-based) versus PQN (value-based): both collect on-policy parallel data, but PQN shows only marginal improvements from scaling N_envs, which the authors attribute to the difference in loss functions. A third comparison examines shared versus separate actor-critic encoders: separate encoders alone cause performance collapse at default settings, but scaling N_envs mitigates this collapse and unlocks the full benefit of decoupled representations. Hyperparameter sensitivity analyses (learning rate, discount factor γ) confirm that the N_envs scaling advantage is robust and not an artifact of a particular hyperparameter choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and PQN (Gallici et al., 2024) as its primary algorithms, situating itself at the intersection of two active research threads: parallel data collection in RL (A3C, IMPALA, Sample Factory) and the recently characterized plasticity loss in on-policy deep RL (Sokar et al., 2023; Nikishin et al., 2022; Juliani & Ash, 2024; Moalla et al., 2024). Prior work has shown that scaling data volume helps but that the *structure* of that data matters (Singla et al., 2024; Obando Ceron et al., 2024), yet the specific decomposition of batch size into N_envs versus N_RO and its mechanistic link to optimization stability metrics (feature rank, dormant neurons, weight norm, gradient kurtosis) had not been systematically isolated. The paper departs from prior scaling studies by holding total data budget fixed and varying only the allocation between environments and rollouts, thereby cleanly attributing performance differences to data diversity versus temporal depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway — that scaling parallel environments is more effective than increasing rollout length for a fixed data budget, and that this specifically mitigates overfitting from multiple training epochs — gives RL practitioners a clear, low-cost lever for improving agent performance without architectural changes. The connection between N_envs and plasticity-related metrics (reduced dormant neurons, lower weight norm, lower gradient kurtosis) provides a mechanistic explanation that goes beyond 'more data helps,' potentially guiding future algorithm design. Open questions include whether N_envs and N_RO should be adjusted dynamically during training (the authors acknowledge fixed values are likely suboptimal), why PQN shows markedly weaker benefits than PPO despite similar data collection, and whether the findings extend to continuous control at scale or to settings with non-stationary environments.
