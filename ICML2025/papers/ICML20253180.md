## Problem

Existing adversarial training methods for MARL overfit to the strongest adversarial perturbations of observations, causing performance degradation on clean or weakly perturbed inputs. Additionally, when the actor-director decomposition from single-agent robust RL is extended to multi-agent settings, the observation perturbations generated by the actor frequently misalign with the policy-level influence intended by the director, leading to training instability. The paper also notes that prior methods fail to leverage the protagonist agent's policy information when constructing the adversary.

## Input / Output

The protagonist agents receive state-vector observations from SMAC (StarCraft II) or CAV (autonomous driving) environments, which may be perturbed by an ℓ∞-bounded adversary (budget 0.25 for 3m/3s_3z, 0.1 for 8m, 0.05 for CAV). The stochastic adversary's SDor component outputs policy perturbation directions (vectors in the simplex-constrained space of the protagonist's discrete action distribution), while STor converts these into concrete observation-space perturbations via a single FGSM step. The trained protagonist agents output robust joint policies under the CTDE paradigm, evaluated by win rate (SMAC) or cumulative reward and crash rate (CAV).

## Method

ATSA decomposes the observation adversary into a Stochastic Director (SDor) and an SDor-guided generaTor (STor). SDor is trained with a maximum-entropy objective that simultaneously minimizes the expected team reward of the protagonists and maximizes the entropy of its own policy, learned via factorized soft policy iteration (joint soft Q-evaluation alternating with individual soft policy improvement) under the individual-global-optimal condition. STor then solves a per-agent optimization that minimizes the KL divergence between the protagonist's policy evaluated on the adversarial observation and SDor's suggested policy perturbation, using a single FGSM step with clipping to the ℓ∞ ball. A novel SDor-STor loss function (cross-entropy between the protagonist's action distribution under adversarial observations and SDor's policy suggestion) is added to the SDor's policy gradient to enforce coherence between the two components. The paper proves that SDor's soft policy converges to a global optimum and that the SDor-STor combination induces the optimal stochastic observation adversary in the OD-POMDP.

## Results

On SMAC, ATSA achieves the highest average win rate across all three maps (3m, 3s_3z, 8m) for both VDN and QMIX frameworks, reaching 0.91±0.09 (3m VDN), 0.99±0.02 (3m QMIX), 0.90±0.11 (3s_3z VDN), 0.98±0.02 (3s_3z QMIX), 0.97±0.04 (8m VDN), and 0.99±0.02 (8m QMIX). ATSA secures 42 statistically significant wins (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p<0.05) over 11 baselines across the three scenarios. On the CAV environment, ATSA achieves the best average cumulative reward (59.81±10.38 for VDN, 63.74±6.87 for QMIX) and lowest average crash rate (0.01±0.02 for VDN, 0.01±0.01 for QMIX) among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATSA outperforms all 11 baselines on the average win rate across all six SMAC configurations (3 maps × 2 frameworks). Against the strongest adversarial-training baselines, ATSA (0.99±0.02 on 3m QMIX) beats PAAD (0.94±0.03) by 5 percentage points and RAP (0.96±0.02) by 3 points; on 8m QMIX, ATSA (0.99±0.02) surpasses PAAD (0.93±0.03) and RAP (0.93±0.03) by 6 points. FGSM-trained agents show high robustness against their own perturbations but degrade sharply under cross-attacks (e.g., 0.74±0.24 on 3m QMIX AVG vs. ATSA's 0.99±0.02), confirming overfitting to a single attack distribution. ATLA and ROMANCE-p/s perform poorly overall (AVG ≤ 0.53), struggling with high-dimensional action spaces and sparse attack budgets respectively. Robust-learning baselines (PR, PR-REP, ERNIE) achieve moderate robustness but fail to match ATSA's consistency: PR drops to 0.24±0.17 on 3s_3z QMIX, and ERNIE reaches only 0.69±0.19 on 3m QMIX. In CAV, ATSA achieves the best average reward and lowest crash rate under both VDN and QMIX, with the gap over PAAD being most pronounced under ATLA and PAAD adversaries (e.g., 59.81 vs. 44.18 average reward for VDN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAAD actor-director framework (Sun et al., 2022b) from single-agent to multi-agent adversarial training, replacing the deterministic strongest adversary with a stochastic one trained via factorized maximum-entropy policy iteration (Zhang et al., 2021b). The maximum-entropy objective is derived through the probabilistic inference lens of Levine (2018), and the individual-global-optimal condition from FOP ensures the factorized soft policies converge to a global optimum. The key departure from PAAD is twofold: (1) the director (SDor) is stochastic rather than deterministic, preventing overfitting to a single strongest perturbation, and (2) a novel SDor-STor loss explicitly aligns the observation perturbation generated by STor with the policy perturbation intended by SDor, addressing a misalignment gap identified in prior MARL adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode in MARL adversarial training: deterministic strongest adversaries cause overfitting to specific perturbation patterns, degrading clean-environment performance. The theoretical guarantee that the stochastic director converges to a global optimum, combined with the optimality result linking SDor+STor to the optimal stochastic observation adversary, provides a principled foundation rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. Limitations include restriction to discrete action spaces and deterministic protagonist policies; the authors note extension to continuous action spaces and model-based settings as future work. The SDor-STor loss, while empirically important (ablation confirms its role), lacks a closed-form justification for the optimal κ weighting, leaving a tuning burden.
