## Problem

Federated ensemble distillation mitigates client data heterogeneity by training a server model on pseudo-labels derived from weighted client predictions, but the choice of weighting function has lacked theoretical grounding. Existing methods (uniform, variance-based, entropy-based, domain-aware) are heuristic, and no prior work provides a provably optimal weighting scheme or a tight generalization bound for the resulting ensemble. This paper asks: what weighting of client predictions constitutes an optimal model ensemble, and can it be computed in practice without knowing client data distributions?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes client labeled RGB image datasets (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet100) split non-IID via Dirichlet(α) with α ∈ {0.1, 0.05}, plus either a server-held unlabeled image dataset or a generator that produces synthetic images in the data-free setting. A generator (WGAN-GP trained from scratch, StyleGAN-XL pretrained on ImageNet, or a FedGAN-trained model) is distributed to clients, who train 4-layer CNN discriminators against it. The output is a trained global classifier (ResNet-18, VGG11, or ResNet-50) whose pseudo-labels are computed as softmax-weighted sums of client predictions using the odds of client discriminator outputs, with the odds constrained to [1, e] via a double-sigmoid to prevent weight dominance.

## Method

FedGO's core insight is that the optimal ensemble weighting w_k*(x) = π_k·p_k(x) / Σ π_i·p_i(x) (Theorem 3.4) can be computed without knowing p_k by exploiting the GAN optimal discriminator result: the odds of an optimal discriminator D_k against a fixed generator G equal p_k(x)/p_g(x), so the ratio of odds across clients recovers w_k* (Theorem 3.6). The algorithm decouples generator/discriminator preparation (a one-time pre-FL stage) from the main FL loop, where each round performs standard FedAVG parameter averaging followed by ensemble distillation on the server's unlabeled (or generated) dataset using the discriminator-derived weights. This decoupling makes the additional communication, privacy, and computational overhead negligible compared to methods like DaFKD that exchange generators and models every round. The paper further proves (Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3) that a server model knowledge-distilled from an optimal ensemble achieves near-optimal loss, with the gap bounded by the distillation loss and the distribution discrepancy between the average client distribution and the server dataset distribution.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with α=0.1, FedGO achieves 79.62% server test accuracy at 100 communication rounds, closing the gap to central training (85.33%) to within 5.7%p and outperforming the strongest ensemble-distillation baseline DaFKD (71.52%) by over 8%p. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.05) and ImageNet100 (α=0.05), FedGO reaches 41.04% and 31.70% respectively, still leading all baselines but with a smaller margin (1.5–2.5%p over DaFKD). In terms of convergence, FedGO reaches the target accuracy in 3.0 rounds on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) versus 5.4–5.6 rounds for FedDF/FedGKD+/DaFKD, and 25.4 rounds on CIFAR-100 (α=0.05) versus 39.8–78.0 rounds for the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGO outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against non-distillation baselines (FedAVG, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedDisco, FedUV, FedTGP), the gains are largest on CIFAR-10: FedGO achieves 79.62% vs. FedAVG's 58.65% (+21%p) and FedProx's 64.69% (+15%p) at α=0.1. Among ensemble-distillation baselines, FedGO's advantage is most pronounced on CIFAR-10, where it beats DaFKD (71.52%) by 8.1%p and FedGKD+ (72.59%) by 7.0%p at α=0.1; the gap narrows on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet100 to roughly 0.5–2.5%p, which the authors attribute to larger distillation loss as the number of classes increases. In convergence (Table 4), FedGO reaches target accuracy in 3.0 rounds on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) versus 5.4–5.6 for FedDF/FedGKD+/DaFKD, and in 25.4 rounds on CIFAR-100 (α=0.05) versus 39.8–78.0 for the same baselines. The paper also ablates the weighting function itself (Figure 2), showing FedGO's GAN-based discriminator weighting dominates uniform, variance-based, entropy-based, and domain-aware weighting across all communication rounds on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGO sits at the intersection of federated ensemble distillation (introduced by FedDF, Lin et al. 2020) and GAN theory (Goodfellow et al. 2014). While DaFKD (Wang et al. 2023a) already used client discriminators to weight pseudo-labels, it did so heuristically and coupled generator training with every FL round. FedGO's key departure is deriving the *exact* optimal weighting formula from the GAN optimal-discriminator theorem (Theorem 2.1), showing that the odds of a client discriminator output equals the theoretically optimal ensemble weight (Theorem 3.6), and decoupling generator preparation from the main FL loop to make the overhead negligible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a provably near-optimal weighting scheme for federated ensemble distillation with a tight generalization bound independent of client data heterogeneity, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in prior analyses. The practical decoupling of generator training (only 5 rounds via FedGAN, or a one-time off-the-shelf generator) makes the method deployable without the per-round communication cost of DaFKD. However, the performance advantage diminishes on harder datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet100) due to a larger distillation loss, and the optimality guarantee only holds on the support of the generator distribution—open questions include improving distillation from generated images and extending to model-heterogeneous settings.
