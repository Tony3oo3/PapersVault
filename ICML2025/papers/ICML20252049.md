## Problem

Stability-based memorization (Feldman, 2020) provides a rigorous theoretical measure of how much a model memorizes a specific training sample, but computing it requires training 2,000–10,000 models, making it impractical for most applications. Existing lightweight proxies (learning time, model confidence, input loss curvature) lack a formal theoretical connection to stability-based memorization and fail to capture properties like bi-modality. This paper seeks a memorization proxy that is simultaneously formally grounded in learning dynamics and computationally free to obtain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with class labels (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and tracks the per-sample cross-entropy loss at every SGD iteration throughout training. The output is a single scalar Cumulative Sample Loss (CSL) per training example — the sum of its loss across all T_max iterations — which serves as a memorization score. Because CSL is computed by simply accumulating loss values already produced during a standard forward pass, it incurs zero additional computational overhead beyond the base training cost of 3F·T·m (one forward-backward pass per sample per epoch).

## Method

The paper defines CSL(z_i) = Σ_{t=0}^{T_max-1} ℓ(w_t, z_i) and establishes a three-link theoretical chain: (1) Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 prove that the input-space gradient norm is bounded by the weight-space gradient norm via a scaling factor k_g, so SGD convergence in weight space implies convergence of the per-sample input gradient; (2) Theorem 5.4 shows that learning time T_{z_i} (the first time the average input gradient norm drops below threshold τ) is upper-bounded by CSL, yielding κ_T·E[T_{z_i}] ≤ (E[CSL(z_i)] − ξ)/L; (3) Theorem 5.3 then bounds expected memorization by expected learning time, and Theorem 5.5 composes the two to give E[mem(z_i)] ≤ (E[CSL(z_i)] + β − ξ)/L ≤ 1 + β/L. Empirically, CSL achieves cosine similarity of 0.93–0.94 with Feldman & Zhang's stability-based scores on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet, outperforms all baselines in mislabeled detection (AUROC 0.9845–0.9897) and duplicate detection (AUROC 0.9821–0.9886), and is 4 orders of magnitude cheaper than stability-based memorization and ~14× cheaper than input loss curvature.

## Results

CSL achieves the highest cosine similarity with stability-based memorization on ImageNet (0.94 on Top-50k, 0.79 on All samples), outperforming all competing proxies. For mislabeled detection, CSL attains AUROC of 0.9845–0.9869 on CIFAR-10 and 0.9891–0.9897 on CIFAR-100 across 1–10% symmetric label noise, setting a new state of the art. For duplicate detection, CSL reaches 0.9821 AUROC on CIFAR-10 and 0.9886 on CIFAR-100. Computationally, CSL requires zero additional overhead beyond training (3FTm), versus 2,000–30,000× for stability-based memorization and 3.6–14.3× for input loss curvature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSL consistently outperforms all seven baselines on both mislabeled and duplicate detection. Against the closest competitor, input loss curvature (Garg et al., 2024), CSL gains 0.3–1.3% AUROC on mislabeled detection (e.g., 0.9845 vs. 0.9715 at 1% noise on CIFAR-10) and 2.9–2.5% on duplicate detection (0.9821 vs. 0.9536 on CIFAR-10). Against SSFT (Maini et al., 2022), CSL leads by 2.2–12.3% AUROC on mislabeled detection, and against Confident Learning (Northcutt et al., 2021) by 11.9–13.6%. On memorization similarity (cosine similarity with Feldman & Zhang scores), CSL is slightly below Loss Sensitivity on CIFAR-100 Top-5k (0.93 vs. 0.97) but clearly superior on ImageNet Top-50k (0.94 vs. 0.79) and on the full-dataset 'All' subset for both datasets (0.79 vs. 0.81 on CIFAR-100; 0.79 vs. 0.49 on ImageNet). Threshold-based Learning Time (Jiang et al., 2021) performs near chance level (~0.49–0.53 AUROC), confirming that simple thresholding of loss trajectories is far less effective than the cumulative approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization estimation literature initiated by Feldman (2020) and empirically validated for deep networks by Feldman & Zhang (2020), which established stability-based memorization as a rigorous but computationally prohibitive measure. It departs from recent proxy-based approaches—input loss curvature (Garg et al., 2024; Ravikumar et al., 2024a), learning time (Jiang et al., 2021), loss sensitivity (Arpit et al., 2017), and forgetting frequency (Toneva et al., 2019)—by proposing Cumulative Sample Loss (CSL) and, crucially, providing the first formal theoretical chain (CSL → learning time → memorization) that explains *why* a simple running sum of per-sample loss tracks memorization, rather than merely demonstrating empirical correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSL offers a zero-overhead memorization proxy obtainable during standard training, making rigorous memorization analysis feasible at scale where stability-based methods (requiring 2,000–10,000 model retrainings) are intractable. The theoretical framework also clarifies the role of learning dynamics in memorization, potentially guiding future work on when and why models memorize. Open questions include whether the near-linear CSL–memorization relationship holds under non-SGD optimizers, with data augmentation, or in non-vision domains, and whether the L-bounded loss assumption can be relaxed without degrading the bounds. The SOTA results on mislabeled and duplicate detection suggest immediate practical utility for data curation pipelines.
