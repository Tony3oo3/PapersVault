## Problem

Solving PDEs for flow simulation via numerical methods (e.g., DNS) demands fine spatiotemporal grids and small time steps, incurring prohibitive computational cost. Pure neural approaches accelerate this but suffer from weak generalizability, heavy data dependency, and error accumulation in long-term prediction. The paper addresses how to fuse PDE structure directly into a neural architecture so that accurate, generalizable, long-horizon predictions are possible on coarse grids with only a handful of training trajectories.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a coarse-grid velocity field (e.g., 64×64 for NSE, downsampled 1024× spatially and 128× temporally from a 2048² DNS), along with the macro time step Δt, spatial resolution dx, a Reynolds-number embedding Re_emb = (1/Re)·(a⊗b) using trainable vectors, and the external forcing term f. It outputs the next macro-step coarse velocity field u_{k+1}, and by autoregressive rollout produces long-term spatiotemporal trajectories. All computations are anchored to the coarse grid; no fine-grid data is needed at inference, and the pressure field is computed on the fly via a spectral Poisson solver rather than being provided as a label.

## Method

MultiPDENet decomposes each macro time step into M micro-steps (M=4) handled by a Physics Block, followed by a macro-scale UNet corrector (MaNN). The Physics Block embeds the governing PDE inside the network: a Correction Block (2-layer FNO) refines the coarse state, a learnable symmetric 5×5 convolutional filter (only 6 free parameters per filter, preserving central-difference stencil symmetry and satisfying Order-of-Sum Rules for up to 4th-order accuracy) estimates spatial derivatives, and these feed into an RK4 integrator that marches the PDE residual forward. A lightweight FNO (MiNN) corrects residual error at each micro-step, while the MaNN UNet absorbs accumulated drift at the macro scale. Training is purely supervised with MSE loss on 3–5 coarse trajectories; the PDE structure is encoded architecturally rather than as a loss term, giving the model interpretability and strong generalization across unseen ICs, Reynolds numbers, forcing terms, and domain sizes.

## Results

MultiPDENet achieves 61–95% improvement over the best baseline across all four PDE systems (KdV, Burgers, Gray-Scott, Navier-Stokes) on RMSE, MAE, and MNAD, while also substantially extending the High Correlation Time (HCT). On the most challenging NSE (Kolmogorov flow, Re=1000), MultiPDENet reduces RMSE from 0.8224 (UNet, best baseline) to 0.1379 and extends HCT from 3.96 s to 8.36 s. On Burgers, the improvement is most dramatic: RMSE drops from 0.0832 (PINO) to 0.0057 (a 93.1% reduction), and HCT increases from 0.55 s to 1.40 s. Several baselines (DeepONet, LI, TSM, UNet on GS) diverge entirely (NaN) on at least one dataset, whereas MultiPDENet remains stable across all systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiPDENet outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and every metric. On KdV, the closest competitor is PINO (RMSE 0.4120), which MultiPDENet beats by 62.7% (RMSE 0.1536) and by a factor of ~4× in HCT (39.8 s vs. 13.9 s); DeepONet diverges entirely. On Burgers, PINO is again the best baseline (RMSE 0.0832), but MultiPDENet achieves a 93.1% RMSE reduction (0.0057) and a 2.5× HCT extension (1.40 s vs. 0.55 s); PeRCNN, despite its physics encoding, is outperformed by 94.8% on MNAD. On Gray-Scott, PeRCNN is the strongest baseline (RMSE 0.1763, HCT 640 s), yet MultiPDENet still halves the RMSE (0.0573) and more than doubles the HCT (1400 s); FNO diverges catastrophically (RMSE 8774) and UNet produces NaN. On the NSE (Kolmogorov flow), UNet is the best surviving baseline (RMSE 0.8224, HCT 3.96 s), while LI and TSM both diverge (NaN); MultiPDENet reduces RMSE by 83.2% (0.1379) and extends HCT by 111% (8.36 s). The consistent pattern is that pure neural operators (FNO, DeepONet) and hybrid methods (LI, TSM) fail to maintain stability over long rollouts on coarse grids, whereas MultiPDENet's PDE-embedded architecture with multiscale correction preserves accuracy across all tested systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiPDENet sits at the intersection of physics-encoded learning (PeRCNN, PhyMPGN) and hybrid numerical-ML methods (Kochkov et al. 2021, TSM), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike PINNs that embed PDEs in the loss function, or PeRCNN that uses polynomial feature-map multiplication, MultiPDENet embeds a learnable RK4-based PDE solver directly in the forward pass with symmetric convolutional filters inspired by finite-difference stencils. The multiscale time-stepping scheme—combining a fine-scale Physics Block with a coarse-scale U-Net corrector—is the paper's central architectural departure from prior hybrid methods, which typically rely on a single-scale NN correction and suffer from error accumulation in long rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–7× speedup over GPU-accelerated DNS while matching or exceeding DNS-1024 accuracy, combined with generalization across Reynolds numbers (up to Re=4000), external forcings, and domain sizes without retraining, makes this practically relevant for industrial flow simulation. The ablation showing that both the micro-scale Physics Block and macro-scale U-Net are individually necessary (removing either causes severe degradation or NaN) validates the multiscale decomposition as a principled design choice. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to 1D/2D problems on regular grids, and the authors acknowledge that 3D extension and irregular-grid support (via GNNs) are open problems. Whether the multiscale correction strategy transfers to other PDE classes beyond the four tested remains to be established.
