## Problem

Existing reward-guided text generation (RGTG) methods suffer from two critical flaws: they require a separate reward model call for each candidate token at every decoding step (incurring ~10× the inference cost of the base LLM), and their partial-sequence value functions can provably prefer prefixes that lead to suboptimal full sequences. The paper provides formal counterexamples (Theorems 1 and 2) showing that both PARGS and Controlled Decoding learn value functions that score a prefix extendable to the optimal response lower than a prefix whose extensions are all suboptimal.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the reward model receives the prompt concatenated with the partial generated sequence and outputs a single vector of |D| scores (one per vocabulary token) in one forward pass, rather than requiring k separate calls for top-k candidates. The adjusted logit for each token is the sum of the frozen LLM's log-probability and β times the corresponding reward score, from which the next token is sampled. This design makes inference cost independent of the top-k parameter k, reducing total model calls from ~600 (ARGS/PARGS/CD) to ~100 (FaRMA) on TL;DR.

## Method

FaRMA (Faster Reward Model for Alignment) introduces a reward model with a causal LM backbone that, instead of producing a single scalar per sequence, outputs a full vocabulary-sized score vector in one forward pass. Training alternates between two losses: (a) a standard Bradley-Terry loss on full-sequence preference pairs to anchor the reward scale, and (b) a novel max-over-vocabulary constraint loss that enforces V(y_{1:i}|x) = max_{y_{i+1}} V(y_{1:i+1}|x), which is equivalent to the Bellman optimality equation for a deterministic MDP where tokens are actions and partial sequences are states. The paper proves (Theorem 3) that this constraint guarantees the learned value function scores any prefix extendable to an optimal full sequence at least as high as any other prefix, resolving the sub-optimality shown for PARGS and CD. The key architectural insight is that computing the max over the vocabulary requires only a single forward pass through the reward model, eliminating the per-candidate inner loop that dominates RGTG inference cost.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization task, FaRMA achieves the highest average reward among all RGTG methods (2.05±0.15) while completing generation in 5 minutes, compared to 29–32 minutes for ARGS, PARGS, and CD. On the HH dialogue task, FaRMA-2.8b achieves 1.80±0.18 reward in 5 minutes, outperforming all other RGTG baselines. In terms of inference efficiency, FaRMA requires only 106.54 total model calls per response on TL;DR versus 642–663 for other RGTG methods, a roughly 6× reduction. FaRMA also produces the most diverse generations (lowest ROUGE-L: 0.21 on TL;DR, 0.24 on HH) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaRMA outperforms all RGTG baselines on both reward and inference time. On TL;DR, FaRMA (2.05) beats ARGS (1.46), PARGS (1.56), CD (1.15), and CARDS (1.73) in average reward while being 6–10× faster (5 min vs. 17–32 min). On HH, FaRMA-2.8b (1.80) surpasses ARGS (1.41), PARGS (1.63), and CD (1.24) in reward with 5× less inference time, though it trails CARDS (1.92) and PPO (1.92) by a small margin. Against the offline RLHF baselines, FaRMA is competitive: it matches PPO exactly on TL;DR (2.05 vs. 2.05) and is within 0.03 of DPO (2.05 vs. 2.08), without requiring any LLM fine-tuning. The key practical advantage is that FaRMA's reward model can be scaled down (400M parameters still yields 1.49 reward on HH, well above π_ref's 1.18) while DPO and PPO require full LLM retraining at 3× the training time and 3–4× the peak memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaRMA extends the reward-guided text generation (RGTG) framework introduced by Khanov et al. (2024), which combines a frozen LLM's logits with reward model scores at each decoding step, and the partial-sequence Bradley-Terry value model of Rashid et al. (2024) (PARGS). It departs from both by (i) redesigning the reward model architecture to output scores for all vocabulary tokens in a single forward pass rather than requiring one call per candidate token, and (ii) introducing a max-over-vocabulary constraint (Eq. 7) that is explicitly derived from Bellman's equation for deterministic MDPs, connecting RGTG to Q-learning. The paper also positions itself against Controlled Decoding (Mudgal et al., 2024) and Value Augmented Sampling (Han et al., 2024), showing via Theorems 1–2 that their value functions can prefer suboptimal prefixes, while Theorem 3 guarantees FaRMA's value function scores optimal-extendable prefixes at least as high as any other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6–7× inference speedup (e.g., 2.99 s vs. 19.4 s on TL;DR) makes RGTG a far more practical alternative to offline RLHF for settings where retraining the LLM is infeasible, and the theoretical counterexamples against PARGS and CD provide a clear diagnostic for why prior tokenwise reward models underperform. A key open question is whether the local one-step max constraint (Eq. 5) remains sufficient for very long generations or highly branching decision trees, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to multimodal or agentic settings where the 'vocabulary' of actions is structured rather than flat. The reliance on GPT-4 as a human-evaluation proxy and the modest model sizes tested (≤7 B) leave room for validation at scale.
