## Problem

In multi-agent sequential decision making, the total counterfactual effect of an agent's action quantifies how much an alternative action would have changed the outcome, but provides no insight into *through what mechanisms* the effect propagates. Prior mediation analysis decomposes effects along causal paths, but in multi-agent MDPs there can be exponentially many such paths without clear operational meaning. The paper asks: how can we decompose the total counterfactual effect into interpretable contributions attributable to individual agents' subsequent behaviors and to the environment's state transitions?

## Input / Output

The method takes a realized trajectory from an MMDP-SCM (states, joint actions, and noise variables over a finite horizon), an intervention specification (agent i at time-step t set to alternative action a_{i,t}), and a response variable Y, and produces a bi-level decomposition: a first-level split of TCFE into tot-ASE (effect propagating through all subsequent agents) and r-SSE (effect propagating through state transitions), followed by second-level per-agent Shapley scores summing to tot-ASE and per-state ICC scores summing to r-SSE. Counterfactual identifiability requires the MMDP-SCM to satisfy weak noise monotonicity, and all effects are estimated via posterior sampling (100 samples for effects, 20 for conditional variance).

## Method

The core contribution is a causal explanation formula (Theorem 3.3) establishing TCFE = tot-ASE − r-SSE, where tot-ASE measures the effect propagating through all subsequent agents' natural responses to the intervention, and r-SSE (the *reverse* state-specific effect) measures the effect that would be lost or gained had the intervention not propagated through state transitions. For the agent-level decomposition, the paper defines agent-specific effects over subsets of agents and applies Shapley values (ASE-SV), proving uniqueness under efficiency, invariance, symmetry, and contribution monotonicity axioms. For the state-level decomposition, the paper adapts intrinsic causal contributions (ICC) to the MMDP-SCM setting, measuring each state variable's contribution as the proportional reduction in variance of the counterfactual outcome difference when conditioning on that state's noise variables, and proves the scores fully allocate r-SSE (Theorem 4.2). The approach is validated on a Gridworld with LLM-assisted RL actors and a two-agent sepsis management simulator, demonstrating that the decomposition identifies which agents and which specific time-steps drive the counterfactual effect.

## Results

In the Sepsis management simulator, the decomposition attributes 45.6% of the total counterfactual effect (TCFE ≈ 0.82) to the total agent-specific effect (tot-ASE ≈ 0.374) and 54.4% to the reverse state-specific effect (−r-SSE ≈ 0.446). In the Gridworld environment, the paper empirically demonstrates that the naive decomposition TCFE = tot-ASE + SSE fails, while the proposed causal explanation formula (Theorem 3.3: TCFE = tot-ASE − r-SSE) holds exactly. ASE-SV efficiently attributes tot-ASE to individual agents (e.g., full attribution to actor A₂ in Gridworld), whereas the naive sum of individual agent-specific effects can deviate by up to 95% from tot-ASE in the Sepsis setting (8,728 alternative actions evaluated). The r-SSE-ICC method exhibits sparsity: Gini coefficient analysis over 437 alternative actions reveals that only a small subset of state variables carry significant intrinsic contribution to r-SSE, regardless of trajectory length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a methodological paper that introduces a new decomposition framework rather than benchmarking against competing attribution methods, so no direct baseline comparisons are reported. The key empirical validation is against the naive decomposition (TCFE = tot-ASE + SSE), which the paper shows fails in the Gridworld environment (Plot 2b), while the proposed formula TCFE = tot-ASE − r-SSE (Theorem 3.3) is empirically confirmed. For agent attribution, ASE-SV is contrasted with the naive sum of individual agent-specific effects: in the Sepsis setting, the naive sum deviates from tot-ASE by up to 95%, while ASE-SV always satisfies the efficiency axiom (scores sum exactly to tot-ASE) as guaranteed by Theorem 5.3. In the Sepsis trust-parameter sweep, ASE-SV recovers the expected trend—clinician contribution decreases to zero and AI contribution increases to full as trust μ rises—consistent with the agent-specific effects framework of Triantafyllou et al. (2024). In Gridworld, estimated counterfactual quantities are validated against ground-truth values obtained by direct computation, with all estimates falling within standard error bounds (Appendix L).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's (2001) causal mediation formula from general SCMs to the multi-agent MDP setting, replacing path-based decomposition with a bi-level attribution over agents and state variables. It builds directly on the MMDP-SCM framework and agent-specific effects from Triantafyllou et al. (2024) for the agent-level decomposition, and adapts the intrinsic causal contributions (ICC) of Janzing et al. (2024) for the state-level decomposition. The key conceptual departure from prior mediation analysis is that, rather than attributing effects to exponentially many causal paths, the paper attributes them to interpretable entities (individual agents and state variables) that have clear operational meaning in multi-agent decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework for decomposing counterfactual effects in multi-agent sequential decision making, with direct applications to accountability in human-AI collaboration (e.g., healthcare). The practical finding that r-SSE-ICC is sparse—only a few state variables carry significant contribution—suggests that accurate counterfactual estimation may require inferring only a small subset of counterfactual states. Key open questions include scalability to many agents or long horizons (exponential Shapley computation, linear ICC computation), reliance on the noise monotonicity assumption for counterfactual identifiability, and integration with existing blame-attribution methods for multi-agent systems.
