## Problem

Existing AUC maximization methods assume identical training and test distributions and require labeled negative data, both of which are violated in many imbalanced classification tasks where only positive and unlabeled (PU) training data are available and a covariate shift exists between training and test. No prior method can maximize AUC under this combined setting of PU data, covariate shift, and absence of class-prior information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data sources: a small set of positive-labeled training instances, a larger set of unlabeled training instances, and unlabeled test instances (e.g., 50 positive + 3,000 unlabeled training, 3,000 unlabeled test in the main experiments). Inputs are 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST, FashionMNIST), 32×32 RGB images (SVHN, CIFAR-10), or high-dimensional tabular vectors (epsilon: 2,000-d, Hreadmission: 183-d). The output is a real-valued positivity score s(x) = u(h(x)) that ranks instances by likelihood of being positive; a threshold on this score yields the binary class label. Notably, the method requires neither negative labels nor class-prior values (π_tr, π_te) at training time.

## Method

The paper theoretically derives two importance-weighted estimators of the smoothed AUC risk on the test distribution: one computed from importance-weighted PU training data (Eq. 17) and another from importance-weighted positive training data paired with unlabeled test data (Eq. 19). The key algebraic insight is that the sigmoid symmetry σ(z)+σ(−z)=1 causes the positive-positive pairwise term to collapse to a constant, eliminating the need for negative-conditional densities or class priors. The training loss is a weighted sum of both estimators (controlled by hyperparameter β), and importance weights are modeled via a relative density-ratio w_α(x) = p_te(x)/(α·p_te(x)+(1−α)·p_tr(x)) to ensure boundedness. A dynamic training procedure (Algorithm 1) alternates between updating the weight-estimation head v (fixing the shared feature extractor h) and updating the score head u and h (fixing the weights), following the iterative scheme of Fang et al. (2020) to avoid error propagation from a separate two-step weight estimation.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best or statistically comparable test AUC in all 12 experimental settings (4 datasets × 3 class-priors) in Table 1. Headline AUC values include 0.817 on MNIST (π=0.01), 0.925 on FashionMNIST (π=0.01), 0.715 on SVHN (π=0.01), and 0.896 on CIFAR-10 (π=0.01). The method also performs well under class-prior shift (Table 3, e.g., 0.938 on MNIST with π_tr=0.01, π_te=0.01) and without any distribution shift (Table 4, e.g., 0.927 on MNIST), matching trAUC in the no-shift setting. On tabular data (Table 5), it achieves 0.530 on epsilon and 0.694 on Hreadmission, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across the 12 main experimental settings. Against trPU (Kiryo et al., 2017), the gap is large (e.g., 0.817 vs. 0.677 on MNIST at π=0.01) because trPU uses a classification risk that ignores class imbalance. Against trAUC (Xie & Li, 2018), the proposed method gains 1–5 AUC points (e.g., 0.817 vs. 0.795 on MNIST, 0.715 vs. 0.548 on SVHN at π=0.01) by incorporating importance weighting and test-distribution data. Against UDAUC (Yang et al., 2023), which learns invariant features via CORAL loss, the proposed method wins by 1–17 AUC points (e.g., 0.715 vs. 0.549 on SVHN at π=0.01), suggesting that explicit importance-weighted AUC risk minimization is more effective than feature-invariance for covariate shift. Against PAUC (Kumagai et al., 2024), the gap is dramatic (e.g., 0.896 vs. 0.364 on CIFAR-10 at π=0.01) because PAUC assumes a positive distribution shift rather than covariate shift. Against CPU (Sakai & Shimizu, 2019), the proposed method wins by 10–40 AUC points (e.g., 0.896 vs. 0.508 on CIFAR-10 at π=0.01) because CPU optimizes classification risk without addressing class imbalance. In the large-data regime (Table 9), PURA (Jiang et al., 2023) also underperforms (e.g., 0.808 vs. 0.849 on MNIST at π=0.01) since it does not account for distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: AUC maximization for imbalanced classification, covariate shift adaptation via importance weighting, and positive-unlabeled (PU) learning. It builds directly on the dynamic importance-weighting framework of Fang et al. (2020, 2023), which iterates between weight estimation and classifier learning with a shared feature extractor, and on the relative density-ratio formulation of Yamada et al. (2013) for bounded, stable weight estimation. The key departure is from prior covariate-shift PU methods (Sakai & Shimizu, 2019) and AUC-under-shift methods (Kumagai et al., 2024), which either minimize instance-wise classification risk or assume a different shift type; here the authors derive two complementary pairwise AUC risk estimators that exploit the sigmoid symmetry to eliminate the need for negative labels and class priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: in cybersecurity and medical applications, negative labels are often unavailable due to privacy or expertise constraints, and input distributions shift over time, yet AUC is the appropriate metric for imbalanced data. The elimination of class-prior requirements is a meaningful practical simplification, since priors are notoriously difficult to estimate. The main limitation is the assumption of covariate shift specifically; the authors acknowledge that other shift types may break the method and call for automatic shift-type detection. Open questions include whether the two-estimator formulation generalizes to multi-class or ordinal settings, and whether the dynamic approach's advantage over two-step weighting (clear on SVHN, absent on MNIST) can be better characterized theoretically.
