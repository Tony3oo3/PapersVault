## Problem

Byzantine-robust decentralized learning on sparse (non-fully-connected) communication networks remains poorly understood: existing methods either assume full connectivity, achieve robustness guarantees that vanish with network size or spectral gap, or rely on inaccessible information. A fundamental open question is how many Byzantine nodes can be tolerated on an arbitrary sparse graph before any communication protocol necessarily fails, and whether practical algorithms can approach this theoretical limit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250731/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each honest node i consumes its own parameter x_i, the parameters of its neighbors x_j, the edge weights w_ij, and a global upper bound b on the total Byzantine weight in any honest node's neighborhood. The F-RG update applies the aggregation rule F to the *differences* (w_ij, x_i − x_j) rather than to raw neighbor parameters, which is critical for avoiding heterogeneity-induced bias on sparse graphs. The output is a robustly updated parameter x_i^+ per honest node, with convergence guarantees parameterized by the algebraic connectivity μ₂ of the honest subgraph and the spectral gap γ.

## Method

The F-RG (Robust Gossip) framework replaces the standard weighted-sum gossip update with a robust aggregation rule F applied to pairwise differences (x_i − x_j), decoupling robustness design from the decentralized communication structure. The paper introduces (b,ρ)-robust summation as a new criterion and proves that F-RG is r-robust whenever 2ρb < μ_min (the algebraic connectivity of the honest subgraph), while Theorem 3.5 shows no algorithm can be r-robust when 2b ≥ μ_min, establishing a near-optimal breakdown point. The practical instantiation CS+-RG uses an adaptive clipping threshold set to the 2b-th largest norm among neighbor differences, achieving ρ = 2 and thus tolerating up to 1/5 Byzantine weight on fully-connected graphs (versus 1/3 for the oracle case). The framework recovers NNA and ClippedGossip as special cases, and the paper further introduces a Spectral Heterogeneity attack that exploits the Fiedler vector of the communication graph to maximize long-term variance among honest nodes.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contribution is theoretical: CS+-RG achieves ρ=2 (robust when 4b < μ_min), which is near-optimal compared to the information-theoretic upper bound of 2b < μ_min (Theorem 3.5). In experiments on MNIST (α=1, Two Worlds graph, μ₂=16), CS+-RG maintains stable training loss under the Spectral Heterogeneity attack while GTS-RG, ClippedGossip, and IOS all degrade significantly. On the averaging task, CS+-RG sustains low relative MSE up to b≈4 (matching its 4b<16 guarantee), whereas GTS-RG and ClippedGossip (both ρ=4) fail earlier, consistent with their 8b<16 threshold. The novel Spectral Heterogeneity (SpH) attack proves more disruptive than Dissensus or ALIE against all baselines, exploiting the Fiedler vector to partition the graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS+-RG (ρ=2) doubles the tolerable Byzantine weight compared to GTS-RG/NNA (ρ=4) and ClippedGossip/CS_He-RG (ρ=4), achieving a breakdown condition of 4b<μ_min versus 8b<μ_min. In the MNIST experiments (Figure 1, α=1, Two Worlds graph), CS+-RG maintains near-clean training loss under the Spectral Heterogeneity attack, while GTS-RG, ClippedGossip, and IOS all show significant loss degradation. The paper also shows that ClippedGossip's adaptive clipping rule (CS_He) is theoretically weaker than CS+ despite its oracle-like appearance (both achieve ρ=4, but CS+ does so without requiring knowledge of the honest set S). IOS (Wu et al., 2023) has a breakdown condition of 8b√|H| ≤ μ_min, which degrades with network size, making it the weakest baseline for large sparse graphs. The new Spectral Heterogeneity attack specifically exploits the Fiedler vector of the honest subgraph and is shown to be more effective than Dissensus (He et al., 2023) and ALIE (Baruch et al., 2019) at disrupting all three baselines, while CS+-RG remains resilient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies existing Byzantine-robust gossip algorithms—NNA (Farhadkhani et al., 2022) and ClippedGossip (He et al., 2023)—under a single F-RG framework that applies robust aggregation rules to parameter differences rather than parameters directly. It builds on the (α,λ)-reduction abstraction from Farhadkhani et al. (2023) for D-SGD convergence, but introduces a new (b,ρ)-robustness criterion that is strictly weaker than the (f,κ)-robustness used in prior work, enabling tighter breakdown guarantees on sparse networks. The key departure from prior work is the characterization of the breakdown point via algebraic connectivity of the honest subgraph, generalizing the classical 1/3 Byzantine fraction bound from fully-connected to arbitrary sparse topologies, and showing that existing methods (He et al., Wu et al.) have guarantees that degrade with network size or connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight breakdown characterization (2b < μ_min as a necessary condition, 4b < μ_min achieved by CS+) gives practitioners a concrete, graph-dependent criterion for how many Byzantine nodes a decentralized system can tolerate, replacing the coarse node-count heuristic. The Spectral Heterogeneity attack, which exploits the Fiedler vector to partition the graph, represents a new threat model for decentralized ML that prior defenses were not designed to counter. Open questions include whether ρ=1 (the optimal breakdown) can be achieved with a practical rule, and how robustness behaves when the Byzantine weight constraint cannot be satisfied globally but only within local neighborhoods. The experimental validation is limited to small networks (≤26 nodes) with a single seed, so the practical gap between CS+-RG and baselines on larger, more realistic topologies remains to be confirmed.
