## Problem

Real-world time series contain complex, nonstationary patterns with infrequent repetitions and weak temporal correlation, making it difficult for models to memorize all patterns in their weights without sacrificing generalizability. Existing forecasting architectures rely entirely on learned parameters to capture every pattern, with no mechanism to externally retrieve relevant historical context at inference time. This paper asks whether a retrieval-augmented approach—borrowed from RAG in NLP—can reduce the learning burden by explicitly providing past pattern information as input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lookback window x ∈ R^{C×L} (L timesteps, C variates) as the query and the entire training time series S ∈ R^{C×T} as the retrieval pool, which is unusual compared to standard models that only see the lookback window. The full training series is pre-processed into key-value patch pairs via a sliding window (stride 1), and the last timestep value is subtracted as an offset before similarity computation. The output is a multivariate forecast y ∈ R^{C×F} for F future timesteps, with the offset restored by adding back x^L to the raw prediction.

## Method

RAFT is a shallow MLP architecture augmented with retrieval modules that operate across multiple downsampled periods {1, 2, 4}. For each period, the input query and all key patches are offset-normalized, and Pearson correlation is used to measure similarity between the query and key patches extracted from the full training series via a sliding window. The top-m most similar key patches are selected, their corresponding value patches (the F timesteps immediately following each key) are aggregated via a temperature-scaled softmax weighted sum, and the result is projected to a common dimension. Retrieval results from all periods are summed, concatenated with the processed input, and passed through a linear predictor to produce the final forecast. Training uses standard MSE loss, and the retrieval pre-computation is done once per dataset, making inference lightweight.

## Results

RAFT achieves the best average MSE on 6 out of 10 benchmark datasets (ETTh1: 0.420, ETTh2: 0.359, ETTm1: 0.348, ETTm2: 0.254, Electricity: 0.160, Traffic: 0.434), outperforming all 9 baselines including TimeMixer, PatchTST, TimesNet, and DLinear. The paper reports an overall win ratio of 86% across all dataset-horizon-baseline combinations. On the remaining 4 datasets (Exchange, Illness, Solar, Weather), RAFT is competitive but not the best, with TimeMixer or PatchTST holding the top position.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAFT (a shallow MLP + retrieval module) outperforms all Transformer-based baselines (Autoformer, Informer, FEDformer, PatchTST, TimesNet) on 9–10 out of 10 datasets, with the largest margins over Informer (e.g., 0.420 vs 1.040 on ETTh1) and Autoformer (0.348 vs 0.588 on ETTm1). Against the strongest lightweight baseline TimeMixer, RAFT wins on 6/10 datasets (all ETT subsets, Electricity, Traffic) but loses on Exchange (0.441 vs 0.386), Illness (2.097 vs 2.024), Solar (0.231 vs 0.216), and Weather (0.241 vs 0.240, a near-tie). Against DLinear, RAFT wins on 7/10 datasets, losing only on Exchange, Illness, and Solar. Against MICN, RAFT wins on 7/10, with MICN holding the edge on Exchange (0.315 vs 0.441), Illness, and Solar. The retrieval module also improves Autoformer when added as a post-hoc component (e.g., ETTm1: 0.588 → 0.454), confirming its architecture-agnostic benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAFT adapts the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm from NLP to time series forecasting, but departs from prior retrieval-based time series work (Iwata & Kumagai, 2020; Yang et al., 2022) that operated across multiple time series entities by performing retrieval within a single time series. The method builds on the offset-subtraction preprocessing from DLinear (Zeng et al., 2023) and the multi-periodicity downsampling strategy from TimeMixer (Wang et al., 2024), but replaces the learned temporal modeling with an explicit key-value retrieval over the full training history. Conceptually, RAFT positions retrieval as an external inductive bias that offloads pattern memorization from model weights to a lookup mechanism, contrasting with transformer variants that learn all dependencies within a fixed lookback window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a shallow MLP augmented with a simple Pearson-correlation-based retrieval module can consistently outperform complex transformer architectures, suggesting that explicit access to historical patterns may be more effective than implicit learning for nonstationary, infrequent patterns. The synthetic experiments cleanly isolate when retrieval helps most (rare, temporally uncorrelated patterns), providing actionable guidance for practitioners. Open questions remain around scaling the O(N²) pre-computation, designing similarity measures that capture nonlinear and nonstationary structure, and determining optimal retrieval strategies (when, where, how many) tailored to specific dataset characteristics. The architecture-agnostic nature of the retrieval module (validated on AutoFormer) suggests broad applicability, but the current Pearson-correlation similarity is a relatively simple starting point.
