## Problem

Existing Federated Multi-View Clustering (FMVC) methods suffer from three critical limitations: communication cost scales with the number of optimization iterations because variables are exchanged at every update step; privacy is compromised by transmitting sensitive embeddings or full n×n similarity matrices; and most approaches assume complete multi-view data, failing when views are partially missing due to sensor failures or incomplete collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local incomplete multi-view feature matrix X^(v) ∈ R^(dv×n) and a binary index matrix G^(v) ∈ {0,1}^(n×nv) marking which samples are available in that view. The method outputs cluster assignments via k-means on the left singular vectors of the learned global anchor graph Z ∈ R^(m×n). Crucially, the only variables transmitted between clients and server are the compact anchor graph Z^(v) (m×n) and anchor similarity matrix S^(v) (m×m), where m ≪ n, rather than raw features or sample-level representations.

## Method

EFIMVC decouples local and global optimization so that communication occurs only after each side converges locally, rather than at every iteration. On the client side, a single-view NMF-like factorization (X^(v)G^(v) ≈ A^(v)Z^(v)G^(v)) with column-sum and non-negativity constraints learns view-specific anchors and an incomplete anchor graph, regularized by a λ-weighted alignment term to the server's global anchor graph. On the server side, a dual-anchor alignment mechanism fuses view-specific graphs: an orthogonal Procrustes matrix P^(v) enforces structural (row-level) consistency, while a β-weighted manifold term using anchor similarity S^(v) preserves feature-level neighborhood structure. The anchor update has a closed-form solution, while anchor graph columns are solved as independent quadratic programs, and the overall convergence is guaranteed by monotone decrease of a combined objective.

## Results

EFIMVC achieves the best or near-best ACC across all seven datasets, with particularly large margins on 100Leaves (72.88% vs. 53.73% for the best incomplete MVC baseline DVSAI) and YTF10 (84.95% vs. 80.55% for SCBGL). Against federated baselines, EFIMVC outperforms the second-best method by 32.9% ACC on ProteinFold (31.71% vs. 23.85% FMCSC) and 19.7% on WebKB (90.64% vs. 75.70% FedMVFPC). On large-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST), EFIMVC matches or slightly exceeds centralized incomplete MVC methods (96.62% vs. 96.56% on CIFAR-10; 98.66% vs. 98.70% on MNIST) while operating in a federated setting. NMI follows the same pattern, with EFIMVC reaching 84.76% on 100Leaves and 91.52% on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four centralized incomplete MVC baselines (FIMVC, SCBGL, DVSAI, DAQINT), EFIMVC wins on 5 of 7 datasets in ACC, with the largest gap on 100Leaves (+19.15 pp over DVSAI) and YTF10 (+4.40 pp over SCBGL); it trails FIMVC by <1 pp on WebKB (90.64 vs. 91.51) and MNIST (98.66 vs. 98.70), and DVSAI by <1 pp on ProteinFold (31.71 vs. 32.36) and CCV (20.04 vs. 20.98). Against the four federated MVC baselines (FMVC, HFMVC, FMCSC, FedMVFPC), EFIMVC dominates across nearly all datasets: on 100Leaves it achieves 72.88% ACC versus 47.97% (FMCSC, the best federated method), and on CIFAR-10 it reaches 96.62% versus 69.80% (FedMVFPC). FMVC is only evaluated on the three smallest datasets (ProteinFold, WebKB, 100Leaves) and is outperformed by EFIMVC on all three. The federated baselines that cannot natively handle missing views require zero-padding, which severely degrades their performance on larger datasets (e.g., FMCSC drops to 18.73% ACC on CIFAR-10), whereas EFIMVC handles missing views natively via the index matrix G.

## Relation to Prior Work

EFIMVC sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: anchor-based incomplete multi-view clustering (building directly on the view-independent anchor formulation of Liu et al., 2022) and federated multi-view clustering (FMVC, HFMVC, FMCSC, FedMVFPC). The key departure from prior FMVC methods is the choice of anchor graphs as communication variables rather than embeddings, similarity matrices, or partition matrices, combined with a decoupled local-global optimization that transmits variables only after local convergence. The dual-anchor alignment mechanism (structural + feature consistency via an orthogonal alignment matrix P) is novel, with the optimization of P following the Procrustes-style approach of Zhang et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored combination: federated clustering with genuinely missing views, where prior FMVC methods simply zero-pad and degrade severely. The anchor-graph-as-communication-variable design is a clean solution to the privacy-bandwidth tradeoff, and the convergence proof for the decoupled optimization is a useful theoretical contribution. However, the federated setting remains somewhat idealized: the paper does not discuss non-IID data distributions across clients, client dropout, Byzantine robustness, or the case where different clients hold different subsets of views. The large performance gaps over federated baselines are partly an artifact of those baselines being forced to zero-pad missing entries, making direct comparison less informative about the true methodological advantage.
