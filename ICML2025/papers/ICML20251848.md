## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam store momentum and second-moment states that consume up to twice the model's parameter count in memory, becoming a critical bottleneck for LLM-scale training. Existing memory-efficient alternatives (GaLore, FLORA, LoRA) either lack convergence guarantees, require strong assumptions, sacrifice significant performance, or demand extensive hyperparameter tuning. This paper addresses the gap by providing memory-efficient optimizer modifications that maintain or improve performance while offering high-probability convergence guarantees under standard non-convex smoothness and sub-gaussian noise assumptions.

## Input / Output

The methods operate on standard 2D parameter matrices (weight matrices of shape m×n with m≥n) and their stochastic gradients, compatible with FSDP-style distributed training where parameters are flattened to 1D tensors. Subset-Norm partitions each parameter's coordinates into subsets (grouping along the smaller dimension by default), reducing the second-moment state from mn to m elements per parameter. Subspace-Momentum projects the gradient onto a rank-k subspace via SVD of a gradient snapshot, storing momentum only in that subspace (rn elements) while applying plain SGD in the orthogonal complement, making the overall parameter update full-rank despite the compressed momentum state.

## Method

The paper introduces two modular, complementary techniques for optimizer-state compression. Subset-Norm (SN) generalizes AdaGrad-Coordinate (c=d) and AdaGrad-Norm (c=1) by partitioning d coordinates into c subsets that each share a single accumulated gradient-norm step size, reducing adaptive step-size memory from O(d) to O(c) ≈ O(√d) with a simple heuristic of grouping along the smaller dimension of 2D weights. Subspace-Momentum (SM) restricts momentum to a low-dimensional subspace U = rowspan(P) while performing plain SGD in the orthogonal complement U⊥, keeping the update full-rank and reducing momentum memory from O(d) to O(k). Combined as SNSM, these reduce Adam's 2d optimizer-state memory to k + √d (e.g., from 4.99 GB to 0.84 GB for a 1B model). The key theoretical contribution is high-probability convergence guarantees for both techniques under non-convex L-smooth objectives with coordinate-wise sub-gaussian noise, where SN achieves improved dimensional dependence over both AdaGrad-Coordinate and AdaGrad-Norm, and SM matches vanilla SGD's rate while using only O(k) momentum memory.

## Results

On LLaMA 1B pre-training (13.1B tokens, C4), AdamSNSM achieves a validation perplexity of 13.96, substantially outperforming Adam (16.00) while reducing optimizer-state memory from 4.99 GB to 0.84 GB (83% reduction). AdamSN alone reaches 14.96 perplexity with 2.62 GB memory (47% reduction over Adam). Across all four model scales (60M–1B), every SN variant outperforms its coordinate-wise counterpart, and combining SN with Subspace-Momentum (SNSM) consistently yields the best perplexity at the lowest memory footprint among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adam (the strongest standard baseline), AdamSNSM improves 1B-model perplexity from 16.00 to 14.05 (−1.95) while cutting optimizer memory by 83%; AdamSN alone improves to 14.96 with 47% less memory. Against GaLore (the main memory-efficient competitor), AdamSNSM achieves lower perplexity at every scale (e.g., 14.05 vs 16.76 on 1B) while using fewer tunable hyperparameters (5 vs 6) and less memory (0.84 GB vs 1.61 GB on 1B). Against FLORA, LoRA, and ReLoRA (Table 8), AdamSNSM outperforms all three on every model size tested (e.g., 13.96 vs 19.21 for LoRA on 1B), whereas those methods sacrifice perplexity relative to Adam to save memory. Among the authors' own variants, AdaGradSNSM achieves the single best perplexity (13.96 on 1B) with only 4 tunable parameters, and RMSPropSN reaches the minimal memory footprint (√d, 0.12 GB on 1B) while still beating vanilla RMSProp by 1.06 perplexity points. On the SFT task (LLaMA 7B, UltraFeedback), AdamSNSM (r=32) achieves a minimum validation perplexity of 2.390, marginally better than Adam (2.401) and LoRA (2.410), at 42.89 GB peak memory versus Adam's 77.11 GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: adaptive step-size optimization (AdaGrad, AdaGrad-Norm, RMSProp) and low-rank gradient projection for memory-efficient training (GaLore, FLORA, GRASS). Subset-Norm (SN) is a principled interpolation between AdaGrad-Coordinate (c=d) and AdaGrad-Norm (c=1), unifying their analyses under a single partition framework with noise-adapted convergence guarantees. Subspace-Momentum (SM) departs from GaLore's joint compression by applying momentum only in a chosen subspace while performing full-rank SGD in the orthogonal complement, making it modular and composable with any adaptive step size. Unlike prior memory-efficient methods that are largely heuristic-driven, both techniques come with high-probability convergence proofs under standard non-convex smoothness and sub-gaussian noise assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: combining SN and SM reduces Adam's optimizer-state memory by over 80% while matching or exceeding Adam's perplexity in roughly half the training tokens, with minimal additional hyperparameter tuning and FSDP compatibility. The counterintuitive finding that restricting momentum to a subspace can *improve* performance (rather than merely save memory) opens a new direction for understanding when and why momentum helps in stochastic optimization. Key open questions include: a convergence proof for the combined SNSM algorithm, a theoretical explanation for why subspace momentum outperforms full momentum, optimal (non-equipartition) subset strategies for SN, and generalization to higher-order tensors and architectures beyond transformers.
