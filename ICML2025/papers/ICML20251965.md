## Problem

Standard Bayesian inference methods (SGLD, SVGD, variational inference) sample ensemble particles independently from the posterior, offering no mechanism to model inter-particle interactions during training. This leads to mode collapse where particles converge to similar solutions, severely limiting ensemble diversity and generalization. The paper asks: can we explicitly model particle interactions to promote diversity while simultaneously guaranteeing distributional robustness?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251965/cropped_page7_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pre-trained foundation models via LoRA: ViT-B/16 (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K) for 224×224 RGB image classification, and LLaMA-2-7B for text-based commonsense reasoning with multiple-choice answers. During training, K=4 LoRA particle models interact through a shared divergence loss; at inference, particles are sampled independently from the learned mixture-of-Gaussians posterior and their predictions are averaged. The Bayesian variables are the lightweight LoRA matrices (A, B) in the attention layers, not the full model weights.

## Method

IBDR defines a joint distribution Q^K over K particles and introduces a DPP-inspired divergence loss l_div = det([f̃_{-y}^i]^T [f̃_{-y}^i]) that maximizes the volume spanned by normalized non-maximal prediction vectors, encouraging particles to disagree on wrong classes while agreeing on the correct one. A Wasserstein-based distributional robustness upper bound (Theorem 4.1) connects the population loss to an empirical min-max objective with a SAM-like adversarial ascent step, where λ controls the perturbation radius ρ. The approximate posterior is parameterized as a mixture of Gaussians Q = (1/K)Σ N(μ_i, σ²I), and the practical algorithm alternates gradient updates on particle means μ_{1:K} (via reparameterization and one-step ascent for θ_i') and the DRO multiplier λ (via projected gradient descent to maintain λ ≥ 0). This yields a dual optimization that simultaneously enforces flatness (low sharpness) and diversity (high l_div) across the ensemble.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16 + LoRA), IBDR achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 73.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline SVGD (70.9%) by 2.7 points and the deterministic SAM (70.5%) by 3.1 points. IBDR also attains the best mean ECE of 0.14 across all 19 datasets, compared to 0.16 for SAM and 0.17 for LoRA. On six commonsense reasoning tasks with LLaMA-2-7B, IBDR reaches 79.66% mean accuracy, surpassing the best Bayesian baseline BLoB (78.75%) by 0.91 points and the deterministic MLE (77.72%) by 1.94 points, while maintaining a competitive mean ECE of 12.27.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBDR outperforms all 11 baselines on the VTAB-1K mean accuracy. The largest gaps are against FFT (+11.3 pts), DeepEns (+6.6 pts), and SA-BNN (+5.4 pts), confirming that inter-particle interaction via the DPP-based divergence loss is critical beyond what independent sampling or single-model sharpness-aware training provides. Among Bayesian baselines, SVGD is the closest competitor (70.9% vs. 73.6%), but IBDR still leads by 2.7 points on average and by up to 5.3 points on individual datasets (e.g., SVHN: 91.3 vs. 84.3). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-2-7B, IBDR (79.66% mean ACC) beats BLoB (78.75%), MCD (78.10%), and MLE (77.72%), with the largest margin over Laplace-LoRA (76.78%, +2.88 pts). However, IBDR does not achieve the best ECE on commonsense reasoning: Laplace-LoRA (11.85) and BLoB (12.12) edge out IBDR (12.27), though IBDR still substantially outperforms MLE (18.05), MAP (18.46), and Deep Ensembles (19.34) on calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBDR sits at the intersection of three research threads: Bayesian ensemble methods (SGLD, SVGD, Deep Ensembles), Sharpness-Aware Minimization and its Bayesian extension (SA-BNN, Nguyen et al. 2023), and Wasserstein-based Distributional Robustness Optimization (Blanchet & Murthy 2019; Gao & Kleywegt 2023). The key departure from prior work is the explicit modeling of inter-particle interactions during training via a DPP-inspired divergence loss over the joint product distribution Q^K, rather than treating particles as i.i.d. draws. The theoretical contribution generalizes the DRO dual from single-model spaces to product spaces (Theorem 4.1), with SA-BNN recovered as a special case when the divergence loss is removed (Corollary 4.2). For the fine-tuning setting, the method operates on LoRA parameters, building on the Bayesian PEFT line (BLoB, BayesTune, Laplace-LoRA) but adding the interactive robustness mechanism absent from those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for promoting ensemble diversity without sacrificing calibration, which is a persistent tension in Bayesian deep learning. The clean reduction of SA-BNN to a special case validates the generality of the framework and suggests the interactive DRO perspective could be extended to other ensemble settings beyond LoRA fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the DPP-based diversity loss (defined on prediction probabilities rather than parameter space) is the optimal choice, how the method scales beyond 4–8 particles given the linear runtime increase, and whether the min-max inner optimization remains stable for very large models. The approach also inherits the limitation that particles interact only during training and are sampled independently at inference, which may not capture all relevant dependencies.
