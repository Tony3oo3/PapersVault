## Problem

MoE models reduce inference FLOPs by activating only a subset of experts, but all experts must still reside in VRAM, making large MoE models (e.g., Mixtral-8×7B at 46B parameters) infeasible on single GPUs. Existing expert-offloading strategies that load experts on-demand introduce severe latency because dynamic routing requires transferring hundreds of millions of parameters per decoding step, and batched generation worsens the problem as different samples select different experts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes token IDs and produces generated text sequences, as in standard autoregressive language modeling. The key I/O distinction is that during inference, the routed experts are replaced by pre-computed lookup tables indexed solely by token ID (vocabulary size ~50k), so the per-token data transfer is only d×N vectors (e.g., 0.15M parameters for MoLE-16E) rather than full expert weight matrices. The LUTs themselves (d×N×|V| parameters) are stored offloaded on CPU RAM or disk and are never loaded in full.

## Method

MoLE modifies the standard MoE architecture in two ways during training: (1) routed experts receive embedding-layer outputs (token embeddings) rather than intermediate hidden states, restricting their input space to a finite set of |V| possibilities; and (2) all N experts are activated simultaneously (no top-k sparsity) since the experts will become computation-free at inference. After training, each expert FFN_j is reparameterized into a lookup table by pre-computing v_j^i = FFN_j(Embedding(i)) for every token ID i, yielding a LUT of shape |V|×N×d per layer. At inference, the MoE layer computes h' = Σ_j g_j·v_j^i + FFN_shared(h) + h, where only the N×d vectors for the current token ID are fetched from offloaded storage—reducing per-token parameter transfer by ~1/1500–1/2000 compared to MoE expert offloading. The router and shared expert still operate on intermediate features, preserving contextual sensitivity, while the LUT-based experts contribute token-level knowledge that modulates downstream attention.

## Results

Across three model scales (160M, 410M, and 1B activated parameters), MoLE achieves average zero-shot accuracy of 40.8–41.9 (160M), 44.6–45.7 (410M), and 47.4 (1B), consistently outperforming the Dense baseline (38.8, 41.8, 44.3) by 2–3 points. Critically, MoLE loads only 0.037M–0.39M parameters per token during inference, compared to 57M–537M for MoE with expert offloading—a reduction of roughly 1/1500 to 1/2000. This means MoLE's per-step communication overhead is negligible, enabling inference latency comparable to dense models while matching or exceeding MoE accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Dense (Pythia-style) baseline, MoLE wins at every scale: +2.0 to +3.1 points in average accuracy at 160M, +2.8 to +3.9 at 410M, and +3.1 at 1B, while keeping inference FLOPs identical to Dense. Against the MoE (Mixtral-style, top-2 routing) baseline, MoLE-4E outperforms MoE-10E in all three scales (40.8 vs 40.3, 44.6 vs 43.9, 47.4 vs 46.6), and MoLE-16E matches or beats MoE-34E at 160M (41.9 vs 41.8) but trails slightly at 410M (45.7 vs 46.6). The decisive practical advantage is in per-token parameter transfer: MoLE loads 0.037M–0.39M parameters per token versus 57M–537M for MoE, a 1500–2000× reduction, which translates to decoding latency comparable to Dense rather than the 0.7s+ per-step overhead of MoE with CPU offloading. In batched generation (batch size 8 or 32), MoE's latency grows substantially as more distinct experts must be loaded, while MoLE's latency remains nearly flat since it only retrieves pre-computed vectors of fixed size.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoLE extends the sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts lineage (Shazeer et al., 2017; Mixtral, Jiang et al., 2024) by making two structural departures: routed experts consume embedding tokens rather than intermediate hidden states, and all experts are activated during training since the LUT reparameterization eliminates the need for sparse gating at inference. It directly addresses the expert-offloading problem identified by Eliseev & Mazur (2023) and Xue et al. (2024), where dynamic expert selection forces costly per-step parameter transfers, by converting experts into pre-computed lookup tables that require only a single index-based retrieval. The reparameterization step (FFN → LUT) is the key conceptual bridge between training-time MoE and inference-time data lookup, a transformation not present in prior MoE or offloading work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoLE offers a concrete path to deploying MoE-scale models on VRAM-constrained hardware (edge devices, single GPUs) without the latency penalty that plagues expert offloading, with per-token data transfer reduced by 3–4 orders of magnitude. However, all experiments are at 160M–1B activated parameters, leaving open whether the embedding-token-only input to experts and the LUT reparameterization hold up at 7B+ scale where contextual interactions are more critical. The LUT storage footprint (2.4–7.4× larger than offloaded MoE experts) and the demonstrated NF4/NF3 quantization headroom suggest further compression is possible but unexplored. Future work on more diverse discrete input spaces and larger-scale validation would be needed before this becomes a production-ready alternative to standard MoE offloading.
