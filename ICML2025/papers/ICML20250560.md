## Problem

Multi-sample aggregation strategies (majority voting, best-of-N) are widely used to improve LLM accuracy, but the sampling temperature—a critical hyperparameter controlling output diversity—has no principled, data-free selection method. Existing practice either fixes a single temperature across all models and tasks or requires labeled validation data for per-task tuning, both of which are suboptimal and impractical when validation data are scarce.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled set of task input instances (e.g., 200 MATH problems or 100 MBPP prompts), a language model, and an aggregation method as input, and outputs a single predicted temperature value (e.g., 0.7 or 0.9). Notably, only a small number of generation samples (as few as 40) are needed to estimate the entropy curve with low variance, making the procedure computationally lightweight. The predicted temperature is then used directly for the downstream multi-sample aggregation inference.

## Method

The core contribution is the Entropy Turning Point (EntP): the temperature at which the log-scale token-level entropy curve transitions from concave to convex, marking a sudden spike that signals sample quality collapse. The TURN algorithm sweeps through candidate temperatures (interval 0.1, up to t_max), generates samples at each temperature, computes average token-level entropy (over top-1000 tokens), takes the logarithm, and identifies the first point where the second derivative becomes positive. A small aggregation adaptation factor β is then added (0 for majority voting, +0.1 for best-of-N) to account for the fact that best-of-N tolerates more quality degradation since only one correct sample suffices. The paper further provides a stochastic process model with proper/improper token dynamics and a noise tolerance rate α that explains the entropy spike mechanistically: before EntP, higher temperature increases diversity among proper tokens, but after EntP, the compounding error rate causes improper tokens to dominate, producing the observed sharp entropy rise and rapid accuracy drop.

## Results

TURN achieves a hit rate of 12/13 on MATH (majority voting) and 11/13 on MBPP (best-of-N), with average temperature gaps of only 0.023 and 0.015 from the ε-optimal range, respectively. The average performance drop compared to grid-searched optimal temperatures is just 0.32% on MATH and 0.59% on MBPP. TURN outperforms the best fixed temperature by 0.5% on MATH and 0.4% on MBPP in average accuracy, with a combined margin of 0.75%. Efficiency is high: only ~40 samples are needed for temperature estimation, yielding a mere 0.2% performance drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TURN against a fixed-temperature baseline, where a single temperature is selected from {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.1} to maximize overall accuracy across all models. No prior method automatically adjusts temperature for multi-sample aggregation, so fixed temperature is the only available baseline. On MATH, the best fixed temperature is T=0.7 (avg. drop 0.8%), while TURN achieves 0.3% avg. drop — a 0.5% improvement. On MBPP, the best fixed temperature is T=0.9 (avg. drop 0.9%), while TURN achieves 0.5% — a 0.4% improvement. When both tasks are combined, TURN's average drop of 0.4% beats the best fixed temperature's 1.15% by 0.75%. The aggregation adaptation factor β (set to +0.1 for best-of-N) provides a small but consistent gain over the unadapted TURN-Ada. variant, particularly on MBPP where it reduces the average drop from 1.1% to 0.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temperature-tuning literature from single-sample decoding (Zhang et al. 2024b; Li et al. 2024; Xie et al. 2024) to the multi-sample aggregation setting (majority voting, best-of-N), where prior work either used a fixed default temperature or required labeled validation data (Zhang et al. 2024a). The key conceptual departure is using token-level entropy as a self-assessment proxy for model-task distance, eliminating the need for any validation set. The stochastic process model providing a mechanistic explanation for the entropy spike is a novel theoretical contribution not present in prior temperature-selection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TURN offers a practical, low-cost procedure (≈40 samples suffice) for practitioners who deploy multi-sample aggregation across diverse models and tasks without access to labeled validation data. The strong negative correlation between model-task entropy distance and optimal temperature (−0.895 on MATH) provides a useful heuristic: task-finetuned models tolerate higher temperatures than general-purpose ones. Open questions include whether the entropy spike phenomenon persists at larger model scales (tested only up to ~9B), whether it generalizes to other aggregation strategies beyond majority voting and best-of-N, and whether the small adaptation factor β transfers cleanly to new task domains.
