## Problem

Existing ANN-to-SNN conversion methods rely on rate coding, where the firing rate must approximate the activation value over many time-steps. This causes a fundamental decay problem: as time progresses, the influence of earlier spikes diminishes, requiring increasingly more spikes to maintain precision, which inflates both energy consumption and latency. The paper asks whether an alternative coding scheme can transmit activation information more efficiently by encoding only the change (difference) rather than the absolute rate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ANN (CNN or Transformer) and a calibration dataset, and produces a converted SNN with no weight retraining. At inference, the SNN consumes standard RGB images (224×224 ImageNet) and outputs class labels, but internally communicates via sparse binary spikes across T time-steps (as few as 2–8 for Transformers, 4–8 for CNNs). The conversion requires only a forward pass over the calibration set to compute per-layer activation statistics (mean and variance) for threshold determination.

## Method

The core contribution is differential coding, where each spike encodes a correction to the current encoded activation value rather than contributing to a running average. Specifically, the encoded value r[t] updates as r[t] = r[t-1] + x[t]/t only when a spike fires, eliminating the per-step decay inherent in rate coding and allowing neurons to stop firing once sufficient precision is reached. For nonlinear layers (GeLU, LayerNorm, Softmax), differential graded units compute the change in the nonlinear function output scaled by t. For spiking neurons, an additional membrane potential m_r tracks the difference between input and output encoded values, adjusting the input current to maintain correct encoding. A threshold iteration method derives the optimal ReLU threshold by minimizing expected quantization error under a Gaussian activation assumption, iterating θ ← k₁·θ until convergence. Multi-threshold (MT) neurons with power-of-2 thresholds enable hardware-friendly implementation via bit-shifting, and the entire pipeline is training-free.

## Results

On ImageNet, DCGS achieves near-ANN accuracy with extremely short time-steps: ResNet34-4/1 reaches 76.04% top-1 at T=8 (vs. 76.42% ANN, 0.38% loss), VGG16-4/1 reaches 73.17% at T=8 (vs. 73.25% ANN, 0.08% loss), and ViT-S-8/4 reaches 81.11% at T=4 (vs. 81.38% ANN, 0.27% loss). Energy consumption ratios are as low as 0.22× the ANN for VGG16 at T=8 and 0.37× for ResNet34 at T=8. For larger Transformers, EVA02-L-8/4 achieves 89.72% at T=4 (vs. 90.05% ANN), demonstrating the method scales to 305M-parameter models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For CNNs, DCGS substantially outperforms all prior rate-coding conversion methods while using 8× fewer time-steps: on ResNet-34, DCGS (76.04% at T=8) surpasses QCFS (72.35% at T=64) by 3.69%, MMSE (71.12% at T=64) by 4.92%, and SRP (68.61% at T=64) by 7.43%. On VGG-16, DCGS (73.17% at T=8) beats TS (70.97% at T=64) by 2.20% and SNM (71.50% at T=64) by 1.67%. For Transformers, DCGS ViT-B-8/4 (83.98% at T=4) outperforms STA ViT-B/32 (82.79% at T=256) by 1.19% with 64× fewer time-steps, and exceeds ECMT ViT-S/16 (77.07% at T=10) by 4.04% at T=4. Against SpikeZIP-TF on comparable ViT-Scale models, DCGS achieves 81.11% at T=4 versus 81.45% at T=64 — a 0.34% accuracy gap but with 16× fewer time-steps, yielding significantly lower energy. The method also extends to EVA02-L (305M params), achieving 89.72% at T=4, a setting not covered by any prior baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion literature by replacing the standard rate coding scheme with a differential (delta) coding scheme, where spikes encode changes in the activation value rather than the activation itself. It builds directly on the multi-threshold neuron framework from Huang et al. (2024) and the expectation compensation idea for graded units, while departing from prior rate-based methods (Cao et al. 2015, Bu et al. 2022, Li et al. 2021) that require T=64+ time-steps. The threshold iteration method is a novel analytical contribution that derives optimal spiking neuron thresholds from the assumed Gaussian distribution of pre-activations, complementing the coding scheme change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× reduction in required time-steps (T=4–8 vs. T=64) while matching or exceeding prior accuracy is practically significant for neuromorphic deployment, where latency and energy are the primary constraints. The training-free nature means no retraining cost, and the hardware-friendly MT neuron implementation (bit-shift based, no argmin) makes this deployable on existing neuromorphic chips. Key open questions remain: the lack of an optimal threshold method for Transformers limits performance there (energy ratios exceed 1.0), and the requirement for at least one negative threshold per neuron constrains the neuron design space. Whether differential coding can be combined with surrogate-gradient training for end-to-end SNN optimization is unexplored.
