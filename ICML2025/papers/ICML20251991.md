## Problem

Existing cross-modal hashing (CMH) methods assume all modalities are available simultaneously and must be jointly trained, requiring full retraining whenever new modality data arrives. In practical streaming-media scenarios where modalities are collected asynchronously at different time points, this static paradigm limits scalability and flexibility. No prior CMH method addresses the problem of learning hash codes for sequentially arriving, potentially unpaired multi-modal data without retraining historical modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted fixed-length features from up to five modalities (4096-d VGG for images, 300-d Doc2Vec for text, 29-d or 672-d MFCC for audio, 4700-d LightField for 3D models, 4096-d C3D for video) and produces binary hash codes of configurable length (16–128 bits) via three-layer MLP sub-networks of shape [d* → 4096 → 4096 → L]. Crucially, each modality's sub-network is trained independently and sequentially as data arrives, so the system never requires simultaneous access to all modalities. Retrieval is performed by Hamming distance between binary codes across modalities.

## Method

SHE introduces a three-module streaming training pipeline built around a learnable prototype knowledge library. The Knowledge Library Mining (KLM) module assigns K learnable binary prototypes per class per modality and pulls each sample's hash code toward its nearest same-class prototype via a max-similarity log-loss, while a Knowledge Library Regularization (KLR) term prevents prototype collapse by enforcing inter-class separation. The Knowledge Library Transfer (KLT) module, active only for the second and subsequent modalities, aligns the new modality's prototypes with the first modality's historical prototypes using a clamped cosine-similarity boundary σ (default 0.95), thereby preserving cross-modal semantic consistency without any joint training or retraining of prior sub-networks. Finally, a Discriminative Hashing Learning (DHL) contrastive loss maximizes intra-class hash-code similarity and minimizes inter-class similarity in Hamming space. The overall loss is a weighted sum of these terms, with the KLT term appearing only when m ≥ 2, enabling each new modality to be trained in isolation against the frozen historical knowledge library.

## Results

On Wikipedia (128-bit), SHE achieves 59.4% MAP for I2T and 77.9% for T2I, outperforming the best supervised hashing baseline SCH (56.3% / 77.2%) by 3.1 and 0.7 points respectively. On NUS-WIDE (128-bit), SHE reaches 69.8% (I2T) and 69.2% (T2I), surpassing SCH (61.6% / 65.9%) by 8.2 and 3.3 points. On the 5-modality XMedia dataset, SHE achieves 75.0% average MAP, exceeding the only other streaming-capable method MARS (72.0%) by 3.0 points. On the larger XMediaNet dataset, SHE attains 51.0% average MAP versus MARS's 46.1%, a 4.9-point margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHE outperforms all 14 baselines across all four datasets. Among unsupervised hashing methods, SHE leads UCCH (the strongest unsupervised baseline) by 26.8 points on Wikipedia I2T (59.4 vs 32.6) and 20.4 points on NUS-WIDE I2T (69.8 vs 49.4). Among supervised hashing methods, SHE surpasses the best competitor SCH by 3.1 points on Wikipedia I2T, 8.2 points on NUS-WIDE I2T, and 21.4 points on XMedia average MAP (75.0 vs 53.6). Against the real-valued streaming-capable method MARS, SHE gains 3.0 points on XMedia (75.0 vs 72.0) and 4.9 points on XMediaNet (51.0 vs 46.1), confirming that the knowledge-library-based hashing strategy is more effective than MARS's shared label parsing module. GNN4CMR and HOPE, while competitive on image-text pairs, degrade substantially on the 5-modality datasets (58.1 and 62.6 on XMedia; 33.4 and 39.4 on XMediaNet) because they rely on paired-instance construction, whereas SHE handles unpaired streaming data natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scalable cross-media retrieval paradigm introduced by MARS (Wang & Peng, 2021), which independently trains modality-specific sub-networks to handle asynchronously arriving data, into the hashing domain for the first time. While MARS and subsequent real-valued methods (GNN4CMR, HOPE) achieve flexibility by avoiding joint training, they suffer from high computational cost at retrieval time; SHE replaces real-valued representations with binary hash codes while preserving the independent-training property. The knowledge library mechanism (KLM/KLT) is a novel contribution that repurposes class prototypes as a compact, evolving semantic anchor to maintain cross-modal consistency without requiring direct modality interaction, departing from the joint-optimization assumption of supervised CMH methods like MIAN, SCH, and DHaPH.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: real-world multimedia systems (medical imaging, surveillance, content platforms) rarely receive all modalities simultaneously, yet existing CMH methods require full retraining when new modalities arrive. By combining hashing efficiency with streaming flexibility, SHE offers a more deployable solution for large-scale asynchronous retrieval. Key limitations include the assumption of clean labels (noisy annotations in streaming settings are unaddressed), the dependence on a well-represented first modality for knowledge library initialization (as shown by the Audio-first degradation on XMedia), and the use of label-based pseudo-pairs to evaluate paired-oriented baselines on unpaired datasets, which may not reflect true deployment conditions. Open questions include whether the knowledge library approach extends to unsupervised or few-shot streaming settings and how it scales beyond five modalities.
