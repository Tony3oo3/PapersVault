## Problem

Image segmentation models degrade significantly under natural corruptions (adverse weather, lighting changes, sensor noise) that are difficult to simulate or parameterize ahead of time. While sensitivity analysis can identify which perturbations a model is most vulnerable to, existing approaches like AdvSteer require ~48 hours of offline data generation and 2.4 TB of storage, making them impractical for online training or large-scale deployment. The paper addresses this gap by making sensitivity analysis fast enough to run on-the-fly during training as an augmentation policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253153/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces pixel-level semantic segmentation masks, operating within existing segmentation architectures (SegFormer-b0, ViT-S with SAM or DINOv2 weights). During training, the sensitivity analysis evaluates the model on a small validation subset (not the full set) using KID computed via a pre-trained Inception network, requiring only ~0.2×(number of SA updates)×(evaluation time) overhead. Augmentations are applied on-the-fly at sampled intensity levels rather than pre-generating perturbed datasets, reducing storage to just the original clean dataset (~12 GB vs. 2.4 TB for AdvSteer).

## Method

The core mechanism defines sensitivity as the ratio of model accuracy drop to KID increase (ΔMA/ΔKID) across perturbation intensity levels, then formulates an optimization to find L intensity values that maximize the minimum sensitivity interval while enforcing spacing via a regularization term (λ=2). Because the resulting function g(α) is approximately monotonically increasing, the optimal levels are equally spaced along g, enabling iterative estimation via PCHIP spline fitting: the algorithm starts with two anchor points, evaluates the candidate level with greatest uncertainty, refits the spline, and repeats until all uncertainties fall below a threshold (0.05). During training, after a clean-data warmup period, the method periodically (every r_SA iterations) recomputes sensitivity curves for 24 basis perturbations (photometric: ±RGB, ±HSV, noise, blur; geometric: ±shear, ±translate, ±rotate) at 5 levels each, then samples augmentations using a Beta-Binomial distribution that upweights levels where the model performs worst. This adaptive, model-free policy requires no separate policy network and runs ~9.3× faster with ~200× less storage than prior sensitivity analysis.

## Results

On zero-shot adverse weather evaluation (ACDC), the method achieves mIoU of 62.50 (Fog), 49.36 (Rain), 20.72 (Night), and 48.16 (Snow), yielding relative mIoU improvements of up to 2.81% (Fog), 3.85% (Rain), and 6.20% (Snow) over the next-best method. On the overall ACDC benchmark, the method reaches 45.45 mIoU versus 41.67 for the next-best (IDBH), a 9.07% relative gain. The sensitivity analysis runs ~9.3× faster and uses ~200× less storage than the prior AdvSteer approach (9.6 min vs. 90 min; 12 GB vs. 2.4 TB), enabling practical online use during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest competitor is IDBH (Li & Spratling, ICLR 2023), which uses random occlusion and a broader augmentation set; the proposed method outperforms it on mIoU in all four ACDC weather scenarios (e.g., 48.16 vs. 45.35 on Snow, 49.36 vs. 45.64 on Rain) despite not using RandomErase or RandomFlip. AugMix (Hendrycks, ICLR 2020) is the next-closest on Rain mIoU (47.53) but trails by 1.83 points; it also achieves slightly higher aAcc on Rain (89.28 vs. 88.87) due to class imbalance favoring large 'sky' regions. VIPAug (Lee, AAAI 2024) wins on Night aAcc (61.10 vs. 58.85) but loses on Night mIoU (17.40 vs. 20.72) and is outperformed on all other weather mIoU scores. AutoAugment, RandAugment, and TrivialAugment are consistently the weakest baselines, with AutoAugment dropping below 77 aAcc on Fog and Rain, indicating that learned policy-based augmentations from ImageNet transfer poorly to segmentation under natural corruptions. On the domain-shift IDD dataset, the method achieves the best mAcc (63.03) but is slightly behind AutoAugment (40.74) and AugMix (40.50) on mIoU (40.33), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced in adverse-weather rather than pure domain-shift settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the sensitivity analysis framework of Shen et al. (2021, AdvSteer), which first showed that training on 'basis perturbations' identified via sensitivity analysis improves zero-shot robustness to unseen corruptions. The key departure is making this analysis adaptive and online: instead of exhaustively evaluating a uniformly sampled grid of intensities (requiring offline data generation and ~2.4 TB storage), the authors use iterative PCHIP-based curve fitting to estimate sensitivity with ~10× less compute and ~200× less storage, enabling on-the-fly augmentation policy updates during training. The method also positions itself between random augmentation (RandAugment, TrivialAugment) and policy-based augmentation (AutoAugment, AugMix, IDBH), claiming to capture the targeting precision of sensitivity analysis with the scalability of random methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making sensitivity analysis practical for online training, this work removes a major barrier to using model-aware augmentation in real-time perception systems (autonomous driving, robotics) where natural corruptions are the primary failure mode. The method is architecture- and domain-agnostic, demonstrated across six segmentation datasets and even classification, and complements foundation-model fine-tuning (SAM, DINOv2) rather than competing with it. Limitations include degraded performance on visibility-dominated corruptions (Night, AdvSteer benchmark) and reliance on a fixed Inception model for KID computation; open questions include whether the approach extends to video segmentation, 3D point clouds, or corruptions that are genuinely non-stationary over time.
