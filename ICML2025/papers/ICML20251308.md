## Problem

Scaling up model size and computation in reinforcement learning frequently leads to overfitting and unstable optimization due to non-stationary data distributions, unlike supervised learning where larger models reliably improve performance. The root cause is uncontrolled growth of feature, weight, and gradient norms during training, which destabilizes optimization in multiple compounding ways. Previous regularization techniques (normalization layers, weight decay, reward scaling, periodic reinitialization) address these norm instabilities in isolation without a unified framework, making coordinated scaling difficult.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw observation vectors from continuous control environments (MuJoCo, DMC, MyoSuite, HumanoidBench) and action vectors, producing a stochastic policy over continuous actions and a distributional Q-value represented as a categorical distribution over 101 discrete return atoms spanning [-5, 5]. Observations are first standardized via running statistics, then augmented with a constant shift coordinate before being projected onto the unit hypersphere, preserving magnitude information that plain l2-normalization would discard. The distributional critic outputs per-action probability vectors over the atoms rather than a single scalar Q-value, enabling smoother gradients via KL-divergence loss.

## Method

SimbaV2 builds on Soft Actor-Critic with three unified norm-stabilizing modifications: (1) all LayerNorm layers are replaced with l2-normalization, constraining intermediate features to the unit hypersphere; (2) weight decay is replaced by projecting weight vectors onto the unit hypersphere after each gradient step, maintaining consistent effective learning rates across layers; and (3) a categorical distributional critic with KL-divergence loss is combined with reward scaling (normalizing rewards by running discounted-return variance) to stabilize gradient norms under varying reward magnitudes. Architecturally, each residual block uses an inverted-bottleneck MLP followed by l2-normalization, with the residual connection replaced by a learnable linear interpolation (LERP) that approximates a Riemannian retraction on the hypersphere. Weights are initialized orthogonally and scalers are decoupled into initialization and learning-rate components to preserve unit norm at initialization while allowing adaptive scaling during training. This unified constraint on all three norm types eliminates the need for weight decay tuning or periodic weight reinitialization, enabling smooth scaling with both model width/depth and update-to-data ratio.

## Results

SimbaV2 achieves a state-of-the-art average normalized return of 0.892 across 57 continuous control tasks (MuJoCo, DMC, MyoSuite, HumanoidBench) at UTD=2, surpassing the previous best of 0.780 (Simba) and 0.749 (TD-MPC2). At higher compute (UTD=8), SimbaV2 further improves to 0.911 without requiring periodic weight reinitialization, while Simba plateaus at 0.818 and BRO at 0.807. On the most challenging HBench-Hard subset, SimbaV2 continues to improve with increased model width (up to d_h=1024) where Simba plateaus, and successfully solves all five complex tasks at depth L=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimbaV2 outperforms all 22 baselines on the overall 57-task average at UTD=2 (0.892 vs. 0.780 for Simba, 0.749 for TD-MPC2, 0.731 for BRO). The largest gains are on MuJoCo (1.617 vs. 1.570 for TD7, 1.475 for CrossQ) and MyoSuite (0.847 vs. 0.743 for Simba, 0.714 for BRO), where the distributional critic and reward scaling are most impactful. At high UTD (≥8), SimbaV2 (0.911) maintains its lead over BRO (0.807) and Simba (0.818) without periodic reinitialization, whereas Simba requires reinitialization to avoid overfitting. On DMC-Easy, SimbaV2 (0.874) is competitive with TD-MPC2 (0.889) and MR.Q (0.868) but does not clearly dominate, making it the one domain where the advantage is marginal. For offline RL on D4RL MuJoCo, SimbaV2+BC achieves 86.7 average, statistically matching TD7+BC (87.2) and outperforming all other offline baselines (CQL 73.0, IQL 74.1, IDQL 79.1, DQL 82.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

SimbaV2 extends the Simba architecture (Lee et al., 2024c), which introduced pre-layernorm residual blocks and weight decay for RL scaling, by replacing all normalization and regularization mechanisms with a unified hyperspherical constraint. It draws on the ngpt line of work (Loshchilov et al., 2024) for weight projection onto the unit sphere and decoupled scaler initialization, and on distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017) for the categorical critic. The key conceptual departure from prior work is unifying three previously isolated norm-stabilization techniques—feature normalization, weight decay, and reward scaling—into a single architectural principle (hyperspherical normalization), rather than applying them as independent patches. This contrasts with the broader RL literature where techniques like spectral normalization, batch normalization, and periodic reinitialization have been developed and applied in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap: RL has not benefited from scaling in the way supervised learning has, and the community has relied on increasingly complex regularizations (periodic resets, ensembles, dormant-neuron reinitialization) to make larger models work. SimbaV2's finding that a single architectural constraint can replace periodic reinitialization at high UTD ratios is practically significant, as resets cause performance drops and are impractical for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to model-based RL, visual RL, and the increasingly important setting of RL for training large language models, where the paper notes the potential benefits of stricter normalization remain unexplored.
