## Problem

In RL with trajectory feedback, the agent only observes the cumulative reward after completing a full trajectory rather than per-step rewards, making it suitable for costly-reward scenarios like healthcare or autonomous driving. Prior algorithms (Efroni et al., 2021) achieved √K-type regret but with a suboptimal S² dependence on the number of states, far from the minimax lower bound of Ω(√(SAH³K)). This paper closes that gap by proving the first asymptotically nearly optimal regret bound for this setting.

## Input / Output

The agent operates in a finite-horizon episodic MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H. In each of K episodes, it commits to a deterministic policy, observes the full state-action trajectory (s₁,a₁,…,s_H,a_H), and receives a single scalar cumulative reward Y = Σ r_h at episode end — no per-step reward signals are available. The algorithm outputs a sequence of policies whose total regret over K episodes is bounded by Õ(√(SAH³K)) with high probability, matching the minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Method

The algorithm combines two key technical ingredients within an online policy-elimination framework. First, it constructs a tighter reward confidence region by exploiting the fact that the number of trajectories (SA)^H is much smaller than the number of deterministic policies A^(SH), and that any policy's occupancy vector is a convex combination of trajectory indicator vectors — this avoids a union bound over all policies and yields a per-trajectory concentration event. Second, it generalizes the Kiefer-Wolfowitz optimal design theorem to select a mixed exploration policy that minimizes the maximum information deficit across all remaining policies, ensuring the information matrix grows at the optimal rate. A reference transition model (learned via a Raw-Exploration procedure adapted from Zhang et al., 2022b) handles the unknown transition kernel, enabling both the exploration design and the empirical transition estimates needed for policy elimination. The overall procedure runs in O(log log K) batches with geometrically increasing episode counts, each batch performing reward regression followed by policy elimination to shrink the candidate set while preserving the optimal policy.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 5.6) establishes that Algorithm 1 achieves a regret bound of Õ(√(SAH³K) + √(S³A²H³)K^(3/8) + √(S¹¹A³H¹⁹)K^(1/4) + √(S¹⁷A³H²⁷)) in K episodes for an episodic MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H. The leading term Õ(√(SAH³K)) matches the known minimax lower bound Ω(√(SAH³K)) (Jin et al., 2018; Domingues et al., 2021) up to logarithmic factors, making it asymptotically nearly optimal. This is the first asymptotically nearly optimal regret bound for RL with trajectory feedback, improving upon the prior best of Õ(√(S²A²H⁴K)) from Efroni et al. (2021).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's regret bound Õ(√(SAH³K)) is asymptotically nearly optimal, matching the minimax lower bound Ω(√(SAH³K)) that holds even in the standard RL setting with full per-step reward access. Compared to Efroni et al. (2021), which achieved Õ(√(S²A²H⁴K)), this paper improves the dependence on S from S² to S and on H from H⁴ to H³, by exploiting the structure of the trajectory set (|T| = (SA)^H is much smaller than the number of policies A^(SH)) to construct a tighter confidence region via a generalized Kiefer-Wolfowitz optimal design. Compared to Cassel et al. (2024), whose linear MDP result translates to Õ(√(S⁵A⁵H⁷K)) in the tabular case, this paper achieves dramatically tighter dependence on all parameters. The key conceptual contribution is showing that RL with trajectory feedback is statistically no harder than standard RL: the regret cost of learning the reward from trajectory feedback is upper bounded by the cost of learning the transition kernel, which already dominates the regret in the standard setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-with-trajectory-feedback framework introduced by Efroni et al. (2021), who connected the problem to linear bandits but achieved a regret bound with suboptimal dependence on the number of states (S²A²H⁴ vs. the optimal SAH³). The key technical departure is constructing a tighter confidence region by exploiting the fact that each policy's occupancy is a convex combination of trajectory indicators, combined with a generalized Kiefer-Wolfowitz optimal design for exploration. For learning the transition kernel, the paper adapts the multi-batch policy elimination framework of Zhang et al. (2022b), which was designed for standard RL and does not require the tighter reward confidence region developed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that RL with trajectory feedback achieves the same asymptotically optimal regret bound as standard RL—establishes that the seemingly harder feedback model carries no additional statistical cost, which is reassuring for applications like healthcare or autonomous driving where per-step rewards are prohibitively expensive to obtain. However, the algorithm is computationally inefficient (exponential in S and H due to maintaining and optimizing over sets of deterministic policies), leaving open the important question of whether a computationally efficient algorithm can achieve the same regret bound. Generalizing the techniques to function approximation settings (e.g., linear MDPs) and to the more general logistic feedback model of Chatterji et al. (2021) are natural next steps.
