## Problem

Existing 3D reasoning benchmarks assume real-time access to scene data (e.g., point clouds), which is impractical given the high cost and complexity of frequent 3D scene updates. This paper asks whether foundation models can perform hypothetical reasoning—mentally simulating scene changes from a textual description and reasoning about the imagined state—without direct perceptual access to the modified scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance is a tuple ⟨S, F, c, q⟩: a 3D scene representation S (point cloud, RGB-D video, top-view map, or scene caption), an anchor-based world frame F that fixes directional terms to a global reference, a context change description c (one of five types: movement, removal, attribute, addition, replacement), and a question q. The model must first mentally rotate the scene to align with F, then imagine the scene after applying c, and finally produce a short-phrase or single-word answer. Questions span three reasoning categories—scale-based (proximity/size), direction-based (orientation), and semantic (object attributes)—with answers that cannot be inferred from the original scene alone or from commonsense knowledge.

## Method

Hypo3D is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a new model architecture. The dataset (7,727 context changes, 14,885 QA pairs across 700 indoor scenes from ScanNet and 3RScan) is built through a five-stage pipeline: scene curation, anchor-based world frame annotation, hybrid human+GPT-4o context change generation, GPT-4o question generation with heavy human filtering (91% of raw questions discarded), and SBERT-based deduplication with GPT-4-Turbo grammar refinement. A key design choice is the anchor-based world frame, which defines all directional terms relative to a fixed reference object in each scene, resolving ambiguities present in both object-centric and observer-centric definitions used by prior datasets. Ten foundation models (LLMs, 2D VLMs, 3D VLMs) are evaluated zero-shot using EM and PM metrics. The central empirical finding is a large human-model gap (humans >91% EM vs. best model ~45% EM), with models showing severe hallucination—altering answers even when the context change is irrelevant to the question—and consistent performance degradation when required to imagine a changed scene versus reasoning directly on the provided one.

## Results

On the Hypo3D benchmark (7,727 context changes, 14,885 QA pairs across 700 indoor scenes), the best-performing model, GPT-4o with semantic top-view maps, achieves only 45.50% EM and 48.82% PM overall, lagging 45.5 percentage points behind human evaluators (91.00% EM, 92.50% PM). Movement changes are the hardest category, with the best model scoring 9.82% lower in EM than addition changes. Direction-based questions show the largest model-human gap, with most models scoring below 25% EM on pure directional questions even when the scene is pre-aligned to the world frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 10 foundation models as baselines on the Hypo3D benchmark. 2D VLMs with semantic top-view maps clearly outperform both LLMs (text-only) and 3D VLMs: GPT-4o (45.50% EM) and Qwen2-VL 72B (44.25% EM) lead, while LLaVA-3D 7B (31.56% EM) and LEO 7B (14.83% EM) trail significantly despite receiving richer geometric inputs. Among 2D VLMs, open-source LLaVA-OV 72B (43.01% EM) approaches closed-source GPT-4o (45.50% EM), and on non-semantic maps LLaVA-OV 72B (36.38% EM) actually outperforms both GPT-4o (33.58% EM) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (20.42% EM). Llama 3.2 3B (26.08% EM) is the weakest LLM, while GPT-4o text-only (35.54% EM) shows that scene captions alone are insufficient. A critical finding is that all models except Llama 3.2 3B show 4–7% EM degradation when reasoning in hypothetically changed scenes versus unchanged scenes, and all 2D/3D VLMs hallucinate by altering answers even when the context change is irrelevant to the question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established 3D VQA benchmark lineage (3DQA, ScanQA, SQA3D, MRSA, SPARTUN3D), all of which assume answers are derivable from a static, fully accessible scene. Hypo3D introduces a fundamentally different formulation: models must first mentally apply a described context change to the scene before answering, a task the authors term 'hypothetical reasoning.' The anchor-based world frame also breaks from both object-centric (3DQA, ScanQA) and observer-centric (SQA3D, MRSA) directional conventions, proposing a scene-anchored global reference that resolves ambiguities with symmetrical or amorphous objects. The evaluation draws on the current generation of LLMs, 2D VLMs, and 3D VLMs, positioning the benchmark as a stress test for the imagination capability these models are expected to possess.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a 45.5% EM gap between the best model (GPT-4o) and humans, and more critically reveals that models systematically hallucinate—altering answers even when the described scene change is provably irrelevant to the question. This negative result challenges the working assumption that foundation models can selectively attend to task-relevant information and ignore distractors, with implications well beyond 3D reasoning. Limitations include indoor-only scenes, object-level (not scene-level) changes, and text-only change descriptions, leaving open whether multimodal change specifications or outdoor/structural changes would yield different conclusions. The anchor-based world frame standardization is a practical contribution likely to be adopted by future 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks.
