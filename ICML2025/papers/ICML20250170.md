## Problem

Solving nonlinear drift-diffusion PDEs on metric graphs (networks of 1D edges with Kirchhoff-Neumann coupling at vertices) traditionally requires graph-specific numerical schemes, and parameter identification on such networks demands many forward and adjoint solves. No graph-agnostic deep learning framework existed that could both simulate PDE dynamics on arbitrary graph topologies and solve inverse problems at near-zero additional cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250170/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each edge-level DeepONET takes a vector of sensor measurements (initial condition samples, inflow/outflow flux values, edge velocity) as branch-net input and a (t,x) coordinate pair as trunk-net input, producing the density ρ_e(t,x) on that edge. For the full-graph coupling and inverse problem, the graph topology (adjacency, edge lengths, vertex types) plus optional midpoint sensor readings of density and flux are consumed, and the output extends to recovered initial conditions and per-edge velocities. The coupling variables are parameterized by only 10 RBF coefficients per vertex, keeping the optimization lightweight.

## Method

The core contribution is a Lego-like domain decomposition where three physics-informed DeepONETs (inflow, inner, outflow edge types) are trained independently on single-edge PDE residuals plus edge-specific boundary/flux losses, using a modified MLP branch net and a Fourier trunk net (5 random frequencies, 7 hidden layers, tanh). Training data come from finite-volume solves with Gaussian-process-sampled boundary and initial conditions, and the physics-informed loss eliminates the need for pointwise solution labels. At inference, the three pre-trained models are assembled on any graph topology without retraining; unknown inter-edge flow parameters at vertices are parameterized via a 10-center RBF interpolation and optimized to enforce Kirchhoff-Neumann flux conservation and continuity conditions. For inverse problems (recovering initial conditions and edge velocities from noisy sensor data), the coupling loss is simply augmented with data-misfit terms, making the inverse solve cost comparable to the forward solve.

## Results

For forward simulation on four test graphs (G1–G4), the best model (width 200, 20K training data) achieves absolute space-time L² errors of 4.68e-03 to 7.91e-03 and relative errors of 1.06e-02 to 1.84e-02 against the FVM reference, averaged over 1000 random runs. For the parameter identification problem at the lowest noise level (ε=0.01), the method recovers initial conditions with absolute L² error of 3.99e-02–5.03e-02 and edge velocities with error of 1.58e-02–3.32e-02 across graphs G1–G4, including a large graph with 1034 edges. The computational complexity for the inverse problem scales linearly in the number of edges (O(N_edges)) versus quadratic for a traditional FVM-based adjoint approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other machine learning methods (e.g., PINNs, FNOs, or other operator learning approaches) on the same metric graph drift-diffusion problem; the accuracy benchmark is the finite volume method (FVM) reference solution. The PI DeepONET achieves visually indistinguishable solutions from the FVM on all test graphs, with relative L² errors below 2% for the forward problem. The key differentiator is computational cost for inverse problems: the authors provide an analytical complexity comparison showing the PI DeepONET approach scales as O(N_GradientSteps · N_edges) versus O(N_GradientSteps · N_t · (n_e · N_edges + N_vertices)²) for a traditional FVM-based adjoint method, representing a reduction from quadratic to linear complexity in the number of edges. The authors note that dedicated FVM-based inverse solvers do not exist for this specific metric graph setting, making a direct empirical comparison infeasible. The method's graph-agnostic training (three edge-type surrogates reusable on arbitrary topologies) is a structural advantage over domain-specific numerical schemes that would require re-discretization for each new graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-informed DeepONet framework (Wang et al., 2021) to the setting of PDEs on metric graphs, drawing on the domain decomposition philosophy of XPINNs (Jagtap & Karniadakis, 2020) and classical graph-based domain decomposition (Leugering, 2017). The key departure from prior work is the 'Lego-like' graph-agnostic training: three edge-type surrogates (inflow, inner, outflow) are trained once on representative graphs, then coupled for arbitrary topologies via a cheap node-level optimization, rather than retraining or tailoring a solver per graph. The finite volume reference solver builds directly on the authors' earlier work (Blechschmidt et al., 2022) for drift-diffusion on metric graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is that parameter identification on large graphs (demonstrated at 1034 edges) reduces to adding data-misfit loss terms to the coupling optimization, avoiding the repeated forward/adjoint solves required by classical PDE-constrained optimization. This makes real-time or near-real-time applications (e.g., traffic flow prediction) feasible. Limitations include: the method is currently validated only on drift-diffusion with a specific flux function, the coupling optimization relies on a small RBF parameterization (n_β=10) that may limit temporal resolution, and velocity recovery accuracy degrades for edges far from boundary nodes under high noise. Open questions include extending to more complex PDEs (e.g., full traffic models beyond drift-diffusion), handling time-dependent graph topologies, and providing rigorous error bounds for the coupled surrogate system.
