## Problem

Expected Improvement (EI) and information-theoretic acquisition functions such as Max-value Entropy Search (MES) are widely regarded as fundamentally distinct paradigms in Bayesian optimization, with EI directly maximizing potential improvement and MES reducing uncertainty about the optimum. This paper challenges that separation by showing EI is actually a special case of MES under a specific variational approximation, revealing a deeper theoretical connection that has gone unrecognized. The practical question is whether this unified perspective can yield a new acquisition function that inherits the strengths of both families.

## Input / Output

The method operates on noiseless black-box function evaluations modeled by a zero-mean GP with a 5/2-Matern ARD kernel, initialized with 20 uniform random samples. At each BO iteration, it consumes the current observation set D_t and the GP posterior to produce the next evaluation point x_{t+1} via an EM-style loop (5 inner iterations with early stopping at tolerance d·10^{-5}). Expectations in the ESLBO are estimated via pathwise conditioning with 128 posterior samples, and a clamping mechanism (z_x* ≥ 10^{-10}) ensures numerical stability of the log terms.

## Method

The VES framework derives a variational lower bound (ESLBO) of the MES acquisition function using the Barber-Agakov bound, replacing the intractable conditional entropy H[y*|D_t, y_x] with an expectation over a chosen variational density q(y*|D_t, y_x). The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that restricting q to exponential distributions makes the ESLBO algebraically equivalent to EI, proving EI is a special case of MES. VES-Gamma generalizes this by parameterizing q as a shifted Gamma distribution with shape k and rate β, whose hyperparameters are auto-determined at each location x by solving a closed-form system involving the digamma function (with L2 regularization toward k=1 for stability). The resulting ESLBO contains the EI term scaled by β plus additional terms that act as an 'anti-EI' component, dynamically balancing exploitation and exploration. The optimization alternates between solving for (k*, β*) given x and maximizing the ESLBO over x, converging in a few EM iterations.

## Results

VES-Gamma is evaluated against EI (LogEI) and MES across 12 benchmarks spanning 2D synthetic functions to 388D real-world problems. On synthetic benchmarks, VES-Gamma achieves the best log simple regret on Branin (d=2) and Hartmann (d=6), and is competitive with EI on Levy (d=4) and Griewank (d=8). On 100D GP prior samples, VES-Gamma outperforms both EI and MES by a wide margin for length scales ℓ=0.05, 0.1, 0.25, while EI matches VES-Gamma at ℓ=0.5. On real-world benchmarks, VES-Gamma significantly outperforms all methods on the 388D SVM problem and is competitive with EI on Mopta08 (124D) and Lasso-DNA (180D), though MES achieves the best result on Rover (60D). Runtime per BO iteration is approximately 10.9s for VES-Gamma (N=5 inner iterations) versus 1.6s for EI and 1.1s for MES.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VES-Gamma against two baselines: EI (implemented as LogEI, Ament et al. 2023) and MES (Wang & Jegelka, 2017). Against EI, VES-Gamma wins on Branin, Hartmann, SVM (388D), and all GP prior samples with ℓ≤0.25, ties on Levy, Griewank, Rover, Mopta08, and Lasso-DNA, and is matched by EI on GP ℓ=0.5. Against MES, VES-Gamma wins on the majority of benchmarks including all synthetic functions (except a near-tie on Levy), GP samples, SVM, Mopta08, and Lasso-DNA; MES only wins on the 60D Rover problem. The KS two-sample test confirms VES-Exp (the exponential-family VES equivalent to EI) produces statistically indistinguishable function evaluations from EI, with passing rates of 92.6%–99.8% across six benchmarks, validating the theoretical equivalence in Theorem 3.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Max-value Entropy Search (MES) framework of Wang & Jegelka (ICML 2017) and the Barber-Agakov variational bound (NeurIPS 2004) to derive a variational lower bound (ESLBO) of MES. The key departure from prior MES-based methods (which approximate p(y_x | y*, D_t) with truncated Gaussians) is that VES instead approximates p(y* | D_t, y_x) via variational inference, revealing that Expected Improvement emerges exactly when the variational family is restricted to exponential densities. This reframes EI—not as a heuristic but as a VI approximation of an information-theoretic objective—challenging the long-standing view in the BO community that EI and information-theoretic AFs are fundamentally distinct paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides a principled design space for new acquisition functions: by choosing richer variational families (here, Gamma), one can interpolate between pure exploitation (EI) and pure information gain (MES) in a theoretically grounded way. However, the practical impact is currently limited by a ~7× computational overhead per BO iteration (10.9s vs. 1.6s for EI), and the paper acknowledges that the interpretation of individual ESLBO terms remains unclear. Open questions include extending the framework to noisy observations, finding more flexible variational families, and reducing computational cost via the VarPro strategy outlined in the appendix.
