## Problem

Transfer RL has shown empirical success but lacks theoretical guarantees for realistic task relationships. Prior work either assumed purely low-rank transition models or made direct assumptions on value/reward similarity, failing to capture the common scenario where tasks share core dynamics yet diverge along sparse, task-specific dimensions. This paper provides the first provable regret bounds for transfer Q-learning under a composite (low-rank + sparse) transition structure in high-dimensional feature spaces where p, q ≫ N and the transition core is not itself low-rank.

## Input / Output

The method takes feature functions φ ∈ ℝ^p and ψ ∈ ℝ^q (with p, q potentially far exceeding the number of trajectories N), N₀ episodes of source-task data, and N episodes of target-task data. It produces a target-task policy via optimistic Q-values, along with estimated transition components (L̂, Ŝ) and a regret bound of Õ(√(eH⁵N)) that depends on the sparse-difference sparsity e rather than the ambient dimension d = max{p,q}. A key structural requirement is the incoherence of L*'s singular vectors and a sparsity constraint s ≤ max{p,q}/(4C_S μr³) ensuring low-rank/sparse separation.

## Method

UCB-TQL decomposes each task's transition matrix as M* = L* + S*, where L* is a shared low-rank incoherent component and S* is a task-specific sparse component. The algorithm operates in two stages: (I) a batch estimation of L* and the source sparse component S^(0) from N₀ source episodes via a hard-constrained least-squares program enforcing rank-r incoherence on L and ℓ₀-sparsity on S; (II) an online correction of the sparse difference D = S^(1) − S^(0) using target data, constrained to have at most e nonzero entries. The key technical contribution is a fine-grained confidence region that separately bounds the source estimation error (radius β_{N₀}) and the sparse-difference correction (radius β_n^(1)), with an explicit sparsity constraint ‖D‖₀ ≤ e on the decision variable, yielding a tighter one-step error than a naive combined region. The regret analysis combines a separation lemma for controlling cross terms between low-rank and sparse estimation errors, matrix Freedman concentration for martingale noise, and a restart mechanism to ensure strong convexity of the design matrix across correlated stages.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical regret bounds rather than empirical benchmarks. For single-task UCB-Q-Learning under high-dimensional composite MDPs (Theorem 3.7), the cumulative regret over N episodes is bounded by Õ(√(NH⁵)), with the constant depending on the rank r, sparsity s, and log(dNH) but not on the ambient dimension d = max{p,q}. For the transfer setting (Theorem 4.6), UCB-TQL achieves Regret(NH) ≲ C′_reg · N/√N₀ + C′_φ C_ψ H² √(e C_φψ² NH log(dNH)), where e is the sparsity of the task-specific difference. When the source sample size satisfies N₀ ≍ N², the regret simplifies to Õ(√(eH⁵N)), which is independent of the ambient dimension d and scales only with the sparse difference e ≪ d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) Against single-task RL (Yang & Wang, 2020), the transfer bound Õ(√(eH⁵N)) improves over the single-task bound Õ(√(sH⁵N)) by replacing the full sparsity s with the much smaller task-difference sparsity e, and by eliminating dependence on the ambient dimension d. (2) Against a naive confidence region that treats the combined (L, S) jointly, the paper's fine-grained confidence region (13) that separately constrains the sparse difference D with ‖D‖₀ ≤ e yields a tighter one-step error bound involving C′_φ (the entrywise norm) rather than C_φ (the full ℓ₂ norm), since C_φ ≥ C′_φ. (3) The phase transition at N₀ ≥ N(rC_φ² + s) (neglecting logs) marks the point where the source estimation error becomes negligible and the regret is dominated solely by the sparse-difference correction term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank MDP literature (FLAMBE, REP-UCB) and transfer RL work (Agarwal et al. 2023, Cheng et al. 2022) by introducing a composite transition structure that is the sum of a low-rank shared component and a sparse task-specific deviation, rather than assuming purely low-rank dynamics. It builds directly on the separation lemma from Chai & Fan (2024) for structured matrix recovery and on the feature-space RL framework of Yang & Wang (2019, 2020), but departs critically by operating in a high-dimensional regime (p, q >> N) where the transition core M* is not low-rank, rendering existing linear and low-rank MDP algorithms inapplicable. The UCB-TQL algorithm inherits the optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty principle from standard UCB-Q-learning but introduces a novel fine-grained confidence region that explicitly parameterizes the sparse difference D between tasks, going beyond the naive joint confidence ball.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical treatment of transfer RL under a composite (low-rank + sparse) transition structure in high-dimensional feature spaces, bridging a gap between the empirical success of transfer RL and its limited theoretical grounding. The dimension-independent regret bound O~(sqrt(eH^5N)) when source data is abundant (N_0 ≍ N^2) demonstrates that structural similarity in transition dynamics can provably reduce sample complexity without scaling with ambient dimension. Key open questions include whether the incoherence and sufficient-sparsity assumptions can be relaxed, whether alternative sparse structures (row, column, group sparsity) yield different rates, and whether the hard-constrained optimization can be replaced by computationally tractable convex relaxations. The absence of any empirical validation limits immediate practical impact, though the framework provides a template for future algorithmic work.
