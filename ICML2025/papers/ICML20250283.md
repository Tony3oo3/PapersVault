## Problem

Existing multi-objective LLM alignment methods treat all preference dimensions (helpfulness, harmlessness, humor, etc.) as objectives to be simultaneously maximized via weighted combinations, ignoring how humans actually make decisions. Bounded rationality research shows humans use satisficing strategies—optimizing a primary goal while ensuring secondary criteria merely meet acceptable thresholds—yet no prior alignment framework operationalizes this principle at inference time without costly finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250283/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SITAlign takes a text prompt, a set of N reward models (one designated as the primary objective, the rest as threshold-constrained secondary criteria), pre-defined threshold values β for each secondary reward, a token-level baseline policy π_BL, and trajectory-level baseline models ρ_i^BL pre-aligned to each reward. At each decoding step, it samples top-k tokens (k=10) from the baseline policy, evaluates Transfer Q* for each candidate token under all N rewards, and outputs a single next token drawn from the reweighted policy. The full response is generated token-by-token up to a maximum of 2048 tokens, with prompts capped at 128 tokens.

## Method

SITAlign formulates alignment as a constrained controlled-decoding problem: maximize the expected primary reward minus a KL divergence penalty from the baseline policy, subject to expected secondary rewards exceeding pre-set thresholds. It solves this via Lagrangian duality, deriving a closed-form optimal token policy π*(z|s_t) ∝ π_BL(z|s_t)·exp((1/β₁)Σᵢ λᵢ·TQ*_i(s_t,z)) where the Lagrange multipliers λ* are estimated in closed form via a quadratic approximation of the dual objective (inverting the Hessian of the partition function Z_λ at λ=0). The action-value functions Q* are estimated using Transfer Q*, which samples trajectories from pre-trained but unaligned baseline policies rather than requiring the intractable optimal policy. This yields a fully inference-time algorithm requiring no model finetuning, with theoretical suboptimality bounds in both primal (KL-divergence-based) and dual (Lipschitz-constant-based) terms.

## Results

SITAlign is evaluated using GPT-4 win-tie rates across three setups, with the target-reward constraint standardized to a 50% win-tie rate. On PKU-SafeRLHF (helpfulness primary, harmlessness threshold), SITAlign improves the helpfulness win-tie rate by 22.3% over the state-of-the-art multi-objective decoding method MOD while maintaining the harmlessness threshold. On Anthropic-HH (helpfulness primary, humor threshold), SITAlign achieves approximately a 10% improvement in helpfulness win-tie rate over MOD. Across all three setups, SITAlign consistently outperforms both single-reward baselines (ARGUS-style decoding) and MOD on the primary reward while satisfying the secondary threshold constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SITAlign against two families of baselines. First, single-reward controlled decoding following Khanov et al. (ARGUS, 2024), which can only optimize one criterion at a time; SITAlign outperforms these on the primary reward while simultaneously satisfying the secondary threshold, something the single-reward baselines cannot do. Second, and most importantly, SITAlign is compared against MOD (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2024), the state-of-the-art multi-objective decoding method that treats all criteria as a weighted scalar objective. SITAlign wins on the primary (baseline) reward across all three setups—by 22.3% on PKU-SafeRLHF helpfulness and ~10% on Anthropic-HH helpfulness—while still meeting the 50% win-tie rate constraint on the target reward. MOD, by contrast, achieves higher win-tie rates on the target reward (because it maximizes it rather than thresholding it) but sacrifices the primary reward, confirming the paper's central claim that satisficing (threshold-based) treatment of secondary objectives is more effective than joint maximization. The qualitative example (dismemberment prompt) further illustrates that MOD produces safe but unhelpful refusals, whereas SITAlign generates responses that are both harmless and informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

SITAlign builds directly on the controlled decoding framework (Mudgal et al., 2023; Khanov et al., 2024) and the Transfer Q* estimator (Chakraborty et al., 2024) for token-level alignment at inference time. Its closest competitor is MOD (Shi et al., 2024), which formulates multi-objective decoding as a weighted-sum maximization over all reward dimensions. The key conceptual departure is importing the satisficing principle from bounded rationality (Simon, 1956): rather than simultaneously maximizing all objectives, SITAlign maximizes one primary reward while enforcing threshold constraints on secondary rewards, solved via Lagrangian duality with a closed-form dual approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The satisficing framing offers a more cognitively grounded and practically flexible alternative to weighted multi-objective alignment, since thresholds can be adjusted per user or context without finetuning. The 22.3% helpfulness win-tie improvement over MOD while maintaining the harmlessness threshold is a meaningful practical gain, and the ablation showing target-reward win-rates plateau beyond a threshold empirically validates the core hypothesis. Open questions include whether satisficing alignment can mitigate reward overoptimization (explicitly flagged by the authors), how threshold selection generalizes across reward models, and whether the quadratic dual approximation remains tight when many constraints are simultaneously active.
