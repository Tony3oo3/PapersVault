## Problem

Existing 3D language Gaussian Splatting methods project 2D CLIP features onto 3D Gaussians and rely on rendered 2D feature maps for perception, but this paradigm breaks down for direct 3D understanding because a single 3D object exhibits different semantics from different viewpoints (view-dependent semantics). The paper quantifies this gap: intra-object semantic similarities are often lower than inter-object similarities, and roughly 50% of 2D features fail to retrieve their corresponding 3D object completely, causing false positives and false negatives in 3D retrieval.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (used to train the underlying 3D-GS model), SAM-extracted 2D masks at up to three granularity levels (subpart, part, whole), and CLIP ViT-B/16 visual features (512-dim) computed from masked image crops. At inference, a free-form text prompt is encoded by CLIP and matched against per-object semantic descriptors. The output is a binary per-Gaussian segmentation label (foreground/background), which can be rendered into 2D masks or directly assigned as point-cloud semantic labels; a 3D bilateral filter and a 0.6 relevance threshold are applied to the raw scores before binarization.

## Method

LaGa first performs contrastive 3D scene decomposition: it trains 32-dim Gaussian affinity features via a masked-average-pooling contrastive loss, then clusters all multi-view mask prototypes with HDBSCAN to group 2D masks into coherent 3D objects, and assigns each Gaussian to an object by nearest-prototype matching. For each object, it collects all associated multi-view CLIP features and applies adaptive K-means clustering (K selected by silhouette score, K_max=20) to extract a compact set of semantic descriptors that capture view-dependent variation. At inference, each descriptor's relevance to the text query is weighted by a product of directional consistency (cosine alignment with the object's global mean feature) and internal compactness (L2 norm of the descriptor), and the maximum weighted relevance across descriptors yields the object's final score—enabling direct 3D open-vocabulary segmentation without rendering 2D feature maps.

## Results

On the LERF-OVS dataset, LaGa achieves 64.0% mean mIoU in direct 3D Gaussian-level segmentation, improving by +18.7% over the previous 3D SOTA (OpenGaussian, 45.3%) and surpassing the best 2D-rendered-feature method (VLGS, 62.0%) by +2.0%. On 3D-OVS, LaGa reaches 95.3% mean mIoU, competitive with the strongest 2D baselines (VLGS: 97.1%). On ScanNet point-cloud segmentation, LaGa achieves 32.5/49.1 mIoU/mACC (19 classes), outperforming OpenGaussian (24.7/41.5) by +7.8 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaGa decisively outperforms all 3D baselines on LERF-OVS: it beats OpenGaussian (45.3%) by +18.7 mIoU, SuperGSeg (35.9%) by +28.1, and SAGA (30.7%) by +33.3, with the largest per-scene gain on Waldo Kitchen (+33.7 over OpenGaussian) where view-dependent semantics are most severe. Against 2D-rendered-feature methods, LaGa surpasses VLGS (62.0%) by +2.0 and OccamLGS (61.3%) by +2.7 in mean mIoU, while also exceeding N2F2 (54.4%) and LangSplat (51.4%) by double-digit margins. On 3D-OVS, LaGa (95.3%) trails VLGS (97.1%) by 1.8 mIoU but edges out OccamLGS (95.0%) and N2F2 (93.9%), with the gap attributable to the dataset's simpler forward-facing scenes where view-dependency is less impactful. On ScanNet, LaGa (32.5 mIoU, 19 classes) improves over OpenGaussian (24.7) by +7.8 and over LangSplat (3.8) and LEGaussians (3.8) by nearly 30 points, confirming that 2D-rendered-feature methods fundamentally fail at point-level 3D perception. The paper also notes that OpenGaussian's rigid single-feature-per-object assignment causes frequent misclassifications (e.g., Figurines-Miffy, Ramen-Egg), whereas LaGa's multi-descriptor aggregation preserves complementary semantics across viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-GS-based open-vocabulary scene understanding line (LangSplat, LEGaussians, N2F2, GOI) by shifting from 2D-rendered feature map inference to direct 3D object-level understanding. It builds on 3D scene decomposition methods (Garfield, OmniSeg3D, SAGA) but repurposes them to establish cross-view semantic connections rather than merely partitioning the scene. The closest prior work, OpenGaussian, also decomposes 3D scenes for open-vocabulary perception but rigidly assigns a single representative 2D feature per object; LaGa departs by adaptively clustering multi-view semantics into weighted descriptors, explicitly addressing the view-dependency gap that OpenGaussian overlooks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and quantification of 'view-dependent semantics' as a fundamental limitation of projecting 2D features onto 3D Gaussians provides a useful diagnostic framework for the field, explaining why 2D-trained features degrade in 3D retrieval. The +18.7% mIoU gain over OpenGaussian and +8.8% over SOTA 2D methods demonstrate that multi-view semantic aggregation is both necessary and effective. However, the method inherits CLIP's compositional-semantic limitations (bag-of-words effect) and SAM's context-stripping, leaving open the question of how to handle relational or part-level queries. Future work could integrate LLMs for compositional grounding or explore learned view-invariant semantic representations.
