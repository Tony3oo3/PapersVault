## Problem

In federated multi-armed bandits, agents are heterogeneous and naturally group into clusters that share the same underlying bandit problem, but the cluster structure is unknown a priori. Existing federated bandit work either assumes all agents solve the same problem, handles heterogeneity without explicit clustering, or focuses on regret minimization rather than pure exploration. This paper addresses the gap of identifying the best arm for each of N agents under a δ-probably correct framework while exploiting the (unknown) cluster structure to reduce both sample and communication complexity.

## Input / Output

The learner receives 1-subGaussian reward samples from K arms pulled by N agents, along with a known separation parameter η (lower bound on the gap between best arms of different bandits) and error threshold δ. The agent-to-bandit mapping M: [N]→[M] is unknown. The output is a vector of best-arm indices (one per agent), with the guarantee that the probability of any agent receiving an incorrect best arm is at most δ. Communication is strictly through a central learner (no agent-to-agent gossip), and the paper tracks both sample complexity (total arm pulls) and communication cost (measured in real-number and bit units).

## Method

The paper proposes two two-phase algorithms built on Successive Elimination (SE). C1-BAI first runs SE for a limited number of rounds at every agent to obtain active sets and empirical means, then clusters agents via a graph whose edges connect agents with similar estimated means on shared active arms; in the second phase, one representative per cluster runs full SE to identify the best arm. BAI-Cl reverses the order: it randomly samples agents (coupon-collector style, requiring O(M log M) samples) to identify the set S of M distinct best arms, then for all remaining agents runs SE only on S rather than all K arms. An improved variant BAI-Cl++ adds a symmetric separability assumption (Assumption 6.1) and uses a modified SE procedure (ŌSE) in the second phase that leverages estimated means from the first phase to terminate early, reducing the per-agent cost from O(M·η⁻²) to O(M·η⁻² + η₁⁻²). The paper proves δ-PC guarantees, derives instance-dependent sample complexity bounds, establishes a minimax lower bound via change-of-measure arguments, and shows BAI-Cl++ is order-wise optimal when M is constant.

## Results

On the MovieLens-1M dataset (100 users, 6 age-group clusters, 316 movies), BAI-Cl++ achieves a 72% reduction in sample complexity compared to the naive cluster-oblivious scheme, while a 65% improvement is observed on the Yelp dataset. Theoretically, BAI-Cl++ is shown to be order-wise minimax optimal when the number of clusters M is constant: its sample complexity of N·Δ⁻² matches the lower bound max{M·(K−M), N}·log(1/δ)/Δ². In synthetic experiments with M=20, K=100, BAI-Cl and BAI-Cl++ consistently outperform both the naive scheme and C1-BAI across all three problem instances, with the gap widening as N increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four schemes: (1) a naive single-phase scheme where each agent independently runs successive elimination without exploiting cluster structure, (2) C1-BAI (cluster first, then identify best arm per cluster), (3) BAI-Cl (identify best arms first via coupon-collector sampling, then cluster remaining agents), and (4) BAI-Cl++ (BAI-Cl with an additional mean-estimation step enabling a more efficient second-phase procedure). BAI-Cl++ dominates in sample complexity whenever M ≪ N,K, since its dominant term is N·M·η⁻² versus N·K·η⁻² for C1-BAI. C1-BAI outperforms the naive scheme when the separability parameter η is large relative to individual arm gaps (e.g., dataset 1 where η=0.3 vs. minimum gaps of 0.01–0.14), but both C1-BAI and the naive scheme degrade when η equals the arm gaps (dataset 3). BAI-Cl/BAI-Cl++ are sensitive to skewed cluster sizes (power-law with increasing α), as the coupon-collector first phase becomes harder to complete when some clusters are very small, whereas C1-BAI and the naive scheme are invariant to cluster-size distribution. In terms of communication cost, BAI-Cl is more efficient than C1-BAI (O(N·M·log K·c_b) vs. O(N·K·c_r)) whenever M·log K ≤ K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Successive Elimination framework (Even-Dar et al., COLT 2002) as its core subroutine, embedding it within the Federated Multi-Armed Bandit (F-MAB) paradigm introduced by Shi & Shen (2021). It departs from prior F-MAB pure-exploration work (Réda et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2023) that either assumes agents share a single bandit or each has an independent problem, by introducing an intermediate clustered structure where agents within a cluster share a bandit but clusters differ. It also distinguishes itself from clustered bandit regret-minimization work (Chawla et al. 2024, Pal et al. 2023) by focusing on fixed-confidence best-arm identification rather than cumulative regret, and from single-agent BAI literature (Kaufmann et al. 2016, Jamieson & Nowak 2014) by adding the distributed, multi-agent, and clustering dimensions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical treatment of BAI under agent heterogeneity, establishing that the problem decomposes into two sub-tasks (identifying the set of best arms and matching agents to bandits) and that BAI-Cl++ achieves order-wise minimax optimality when the number of clusters is constant. A key open question is removing the requirement to know the separation parameter η a priori; the authors sketch a multi-phase halving approach but acknowledge the stopping criterion remains unresolved. For practitioners in recommendation systems and ad placement, the 65–72% sample-complexity reductions on MovieLens and Yelp demonstrate concrete value, though the methods assume a central server and 1-sub-Gaussian rewards, limiting direct applicability to fully decentralized or heavy-tailed settings.
