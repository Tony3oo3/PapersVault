## Problem

Existing multi-task diffusion planners require either task-specific expert demonstrations or per-task reward labels, both of which are costly to obtain. This paper asks whether a versatile diffusion planner can be learned from large-scale sub-optimal (inferior) trajectories without any reward labels or task descriptions, and then rapidly adapted to specific downstream tasks with minimal online interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250604/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner takes a window of T_o historical states (or point-cloud visual features extracted via a DP3 encoder for image-based settings) and outputs an action sequence of length H (planning horizon), of which the first T_a steps are executed before re-planning. During pre-training, the model is conditioned only on historical states with no task ID or reward signal (guidance-free generation); during fine-tuning, task-specific rewards from online environment interaction provide the optimization signal. The pre-training dataset consists of 50M sub-optimal transitions (first 50% of a SAC agent's replay buffer) pooled across all 50 Meta-World tasks.

## Method

SODP is a two-stage framework. In pre-training, a U-Net diffusion model with FiLM conditioning is trained via standard denoising loss to predict future action sequences from historical states on a mixed multi-task sub-optimal dataset, capturing broad multi-modal action distributions without any task or reward conditioning. In fine-tuning, the denoising process is reformulated as a K-step MDP, and a PPO-style clipped importance-sampling objective (Eq. 12) is optimized via policy gradient to maximize task-specific returns. A key design choice is the BC regularization term: instead of KL-divergence to the pre-trained model (which traps the policy in sub-optimal regions), the target policy μ is approximated by the best recent proficient actions collected during online interaction, allowing the model to preserve acquired skills while exploring toward higher-reward regions. This combination of reward-driven policy gradient and proficient-action BC regularization enables rapid convergence with as few as 100k online steps per task.

## Results

On Meta-World 50 tasks with random goals (MT50-rand), SODP achieves a 60.56±0.14% average success rate using sub-optimal offline data, outperforming the strongest multi-task baseline HarmoDT-F (57.20±0.68%) by 3.4% and the leading diffusion-based method MTDIFF-P-ONEHOT (48.94±0.95%) by 11.6%. In the efficiency experiment with only 100k fine-tuning steps per task, SODP retains 59.26% success rate while offline-online baselines drop sharply (Cal-QL: 24.60%, IBRL: 17.08%, RLPD: 7.62%). On the image-based Adroit benchmark, SODP achieves 73.9% average success rate, surpassing DP3 (68.3%) and Diffusion Policy (31.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SODP outperforms all 14 baselines on Meta-World MT50-rand. Against offline-online RL methods (RLPD, IBRL, Cal-QL), SODP leads by 25–50 percentage points, as these methods struggle to extract useful priors from low-quality sub-optimal data. Against multi-task RL baselines, SODP surpasses HarmoDT-F/M (57.20%) by ~3.4% and MTDIFF-P-ONEHOT (48.94%) by ~11.6%, with the gap over MTDIFF attributed to SODP's ability to model multi-modal action distributions without requiring task-specific prompts or expert data. In the 100k-step efficiency test, SODP (59.26%) remains competitive with HarmoDT-F (57.37%) while MTDIFF-P collapses to 27.06%, demonstrating that SODP's pre-trained action priors enable rapid adaptation with minimal online interaction. On Adroit, SODP (73.9%) beats DP3 (68.3%) and Diffusion Policy (31.7%), confirming generalizability to high-dimensional dexterous manipulation with point-cloud inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SODP builds on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) for action-sequence generation and on the DDPO/DPOK line of work (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2024) that reformulates the denoising process as an MDP for policy-gradient fine-tuning. It departs from multi-task diffusion planners like MTDIFF (He et al., 2023), which require task-specific conditions (prompts, return labels) and expert data, by performing guidance-free pre-training on sub-optimal transitions without reward labels or task descriptions. The key methodological departure is the BC regularization term that targets the best recent online actions rather than constraining divergence to the pre-trained model (KL) or the pre-training loss, a design motivated by the observation that standard text-to-image fine-tuning regularizers trap sub-optimal pre-trained planners in low-reward regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large-scale sub-optimal data—readily available without expert annotation or reward engineering—can serve as a sufficient foundation for a versatile multi-task planner, reducing the data collection burden that has limited practical deployment of multi-task RL. The finding that KL and pre-training-loss regularization (standard in diffusion model alignment) actively harm performance when the pre-trained model is weak, while a BC regularizer targeting the best recent actions enables effective exploration, offers a transferable insight for fine-tuning any under-trained generative model. Limitations include higher computational cost than baselines (11–13 h vs. 4.5–10 h per task), dependence on online interaction for fine-tuning, and evaluation restricted to simulated manipulation benchmarks; scaling to real-world embodied tasks with continuous, high-dimensional action spaces remains open.
