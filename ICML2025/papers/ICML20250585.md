## Problem

Optimal vaccination to minimize epidemic extinction time in SIS models is closely linked to the NP-hard Spectral Radius Minimization problem, but all prior work assumes the contact graph is known. The only existing SIS structure learning method (Barbillon et al., 2020) requires the restrictive assumption that a vertex is infected by at most one neighbor at a time, and no prior work has addressed vaccination when the graph must first be inferred from observed infection data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250585/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-series of binary infection states (0 = susceptible, 1 = infected) for all n vertices over T rounds, along with the infection probability p_inf, maximum degree Δ, and a global vaccination budget K. The first T/2 rounds are used for graph learning; the learned undirected edge set E is then passed to the vaccination module, which outputs a set of at most K vertices whose infection probability is permanently reduced by a factor α ∈ [0,1] for all subsequent rounds. The learning algorithm additionally requires a stationarity assumption on the data, justified empirically by the rapid convergence of SIS processes to a meta-stable regime.

## Method

The approach decomposes the VUG problem into two stages. First, SISLearn recovers the graph via an inclusion-exclusion procedure inspired by Bresler's Ising model learning: Direct Influence (DI) estimates identify a superset of true neighbors (since DI ≥ p_inf for neighbors), then Conditional Influence (CI) prunes non-neighbors by exploiting the local Markov property (CI = 0 for non-neighbors when conditioning on a separator set, while CI ≥ p_inf(1−p_inf)^(Δ−1) for true neighbors). This yields the first SIS structure learning algorithm with formal sample-complexity guarantees under a stationarity assumption. Second, vaccination is formulated as Spectral Radius Minimization: for graphs of bounded treewidth ω, a dynamic programming algorithm on a nice tree decomposition achieves polynomial-in-n runtime O(n^O(1) K^O(1) 2^O(ω) log(Δ/ε)) via binary search on the spectral radius threshold with feasibility checking; for general graphs, a greedy heuristic iteratively removes the vertex that most reduces the spectral radius in O(Kn³) time.

## Results

On the augmented chn.2009.flu.1.00 dataset (n=40, treewidth≈8, K=7, α=0.2), the proposed DP algorithm achieves the lowest proportion of infected nodes across all post-vaccination rounds, with the Greedy heuristic closely trailing. SISLearn achieves F1 scores exceeding 0.9 for graph recovery with sufficient learning rounds (≥400), and the combined pipeline outperforms all baselines even with as few as 100 learning rounds. On the larger usa.2009.flu.1.00 graph (n=286, treewidth≈58, K=50, α=0.25), Greedy performs best among all tested strategies, followed by LD and GW. Computationally, SISLearn scales to n=10,000 vertices in ~9 minutes, DP handles treewidth-10 graphs with n=2,000 in ~20 minutes, and Greedy scales to n=1,000 in ~10 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Random, Largest Degree (LD), Priority Order (PO, Scaman et al. 2016), and Closed Walks (GW, Saha et al. 2015). Both proposed methods (DP and Greedy) consistently outperform all baselines in reducing the proportion of infected nodes over time on both the chn.2009.flu.1.00 and usa.2009.flu.1.00 datasets. LD ranks third, confirming that degree-based targeting is effective but inferior to spectral-radius-aware strategies. Notably, PO (Scaman et al., 2016) performs the worst of all methods, even underperforming random vaccination, likely because it was designed for continuous-time SIS 'healing' rather than discrete-time one-time vaccination. GW (Saha et al., 2015) outperforms random but is clearly dominated by both DP and Greedy, particularly on the larger usa.2009.flu.1.00 graph where Greedy achieves the lowest infection levels. The paper also notes that the MLE-based SIS structure learning of Barbillon et al. (2020) could not be meaningfully compared, as its single-infection-source assumption causes it to always return a complete graph under the paper's more general multi-neighbor infection model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the inclusion-exclusion structure learning framework from Bresler (2015) for Ising models to the SIS epidemic setting, replacing the single-neighbor infection assumption of the only prior SIS learning work (Barbillon et al., 2020) with a more general multi-neighbor model. For the vaccination side, it builds on the spectral radius minimization (SRM) formulation from Van Mieghem et al. (2011) and the modified ergodic Markov chain analysis of Van De Bovenkamp & Van Mieghem (2014), but departs by handling the case where the graph is unknown and by providing the first polynomial-in-n exact algorithm for SRM on bounded-treewidth graphs—a result that was previously open since the SRM decision problem cannot be expressed in monadic second-order logic. The VUG problem itself, combining both sub-tasks, is introduced as a new formulation absent from prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end framework for epidemic control when the contact network is unknown, with sample-complexity guarantees for graph recovery and a provably polynomial-time optimal vaccination algorithm on bounded-treewidth graphs. The practical impact is demonstrated on real 2009 H1N1 outbreak data, where the combined approach significantly outperforms degree-based and walk-based baselines. Key open questions include establishing approximation guarantees for the greedy heuristic, understanding which network structures are learned first (to optimize the learning-vs-vaccinating tradeoff), and scaling to larger, denser real-world networks where treewidth may be unbounded.
