## Problem

Prior circuit representation learning has focused on front-end logic design and task-specific back-end models that require large, hand-annotated datasets, leaving no general framework to learn from the abundant unlabeled placement data available in industrial flows. The key challenge is that post-placement circuits combine topological connectivity with geometric distribution in ways that neither standard graph masking nor image-style patch masking can adequately capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253107/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a post-placement circuit as a heterogeneous graph where cell nodes carry 5 features (x, y coordinates, height, width, pin count) and net nodes carry 3 features (degree, x-span, y-span), with edges encoding cell-to-cell and cell-to-net connectivity. During pre-training, a 50% grid-based mask zeros out cell coordinates while preserving size and pin features, and topological edges are retained to guide reconstruction. The encoder produces node-level embeddings that feed task-specific decoders: a U-Net for 2D congestion maps and an MLP for scalar post-routing wirelength estimates.

## Method

DeepLayout employs an asymmetric encoder-decoder with a task-agnostic encoder that alternates Multi-Scale Geometric Attention (MSGA) and Heterogeneous GNN (HGNN) modules at four spatial scales (1×, 2×, 4×, 8×). MSGA reduces global attention complexity from O(n²d) to O(npd) via learnable proxy queries, then maps features to a 2D plane for sparse convolution to capture local spatial structure; HGNN performs message passing over the heterogeneous cell-net graph to capture topological relationships. The self-supervised objective combines two losses: (1) MSE reconstruction of masked cell coordinates, and (2) prediction of a Routing Probability Algorithm (RPA) field that encodes interconnect demand without requiring actual routing labels. A layout-oriented masking strategy partitions the floorplan into grids of 30 routing track lengths, masks only non-empty grids at 50% ratio, and preserves topological edges through masked regions to mimic incremental placement/ECO workflows.

## Results

On the CircuitNet-N28 dataset, DeepLayout achieves the best congestion prediction across all data scales (5/10/20 fine-tuning samples), reaching a Pearson correlation of 0.4418, RMSE of 0.0349, and SSIM of 0.7909 with 20 samples. For post-routing wirelength estimation, DeepLayout attains the highest Pearson score of 0.3961 and lowest RMSE of 0.1682 with 20 samples, consistently outperforming baselines as labeled data increases. A 50% mask ratio during pre-training yields optimal downstream performance for both tasks, outperforming 20% and 80% ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepLayout outperforms CircuitGNN (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) on both downstream tasks. In congestion prediction, DeepLayout achieves a Pearson of 0.4270–0.4418 versus CircuitGNN's 0.2155–0.2978, a gap of roughly 0.13–0.21 in correlation, while also improving SSIM by 0.02–0.03. In wirelength estimation, DeepLayout edges out CircuitGNN with Pearson 0.3704–0.3961 vs. 0.3683–0.3691 and slightly lower RMSE (0.1682 vs. 0.1727 at 20 samples). Against Net² (Xie et al., ASPDAC 2020) on wirelength estimation, DeepLayout shows a substantial margin, achieving Pearson 0.3704–0.3961 compared to Net²'s 0.1155–0.3141, with RMSE roughly 0.01–0.02 lower. The advantage over CircuitGNN is most pronounced in congestion prediction, where the self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled placement data provides a clear representation benefit that a purely supervised graph network cannot match with only 5–20 labeled samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepLayout adapts the masked autoencoder paradigm from vision (MAE) and NLP (BERT) to the domain of post-placement circuit layouts, but departs fundamentally from prior circuit representation work that targeted front-end logic design (DeepGate, DeepGate3, Wang et al.) by focusing on geometric and topological properties rather than functional behavior. The hybrid encoder combining multi-scale geometric attention (inspired by Voxel Set Transformer) with heterogeneous GNN message passing is a novel architectural synthesis not seen in prior circuit GNNs (CircuitGNN) or point-cloud approaches (CircuitPoint). The layout-oriented masking strategy—grid-based, size-adaptive, and topology-preserving—repurposes the MAE masking concept to respect the physical constraints of standard-cell placement, and the RPA-based routing supervision signal is a new self-supervised target specific to this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single self-supervised pre-trained encoder can outperform task-specific SOTA methods on both congestion prediction and post-routing wirelength estimation, DeepLayout validates the feasibility of general-purpose back-end circuit representation learning and reduces the need for expensive task-specific labeled datasets. However, evaluation is limited to two downstream tasks on a single public dataset (CircuitNet-N28), and the framework's scalability to full-chip SoC designs with millions of cells, its applicability to other back-end stages (routing, CTS), and whether the 50% mask ratio finding generalizes across process nodes remain open questions. The work opens a promising direction but the evidence base is still narrow for broad adoption.
