## Problem

Test-time adaptation on graphs—adapting a pretrained GNN to unlabeled test graphs under distribution shift without accessing training data—remains largely unexplored. Existing test-time adaptation methods target Euclidean data and fail to handle the structural complexity of graphs, where shifts in both node attributes and graph topology make pseudo-labels unreliable and naive self-training prone to catastrophic forgetting of source-domain knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253138/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained GNN model (two GIN layers + mean pooling + two-layer MLP) trained on labeled source graphs, along with a batch of unlabeled test graphs, and produces an adapted model that outputs per-graph class probabilities. Adaptation is performed iteratively over epochs with a curriculum schedule that linearly decreases the fraction of selected graphs from 80% to 60%, and the entire process runs in seconds per epoch (0.04–0.21 s/epoch) on a single GPU, converging in roughly 10–30 epochs.

## Method

ASSESS combines two mechanisms in an iterative loop. First, Adaptive Subgraph-based Selection (ASBS) estimates the mutual information between each test graph and a randomly sampled subgraph using a Jensen-Shannon discriminator, then converts this into a graph-specific confidence threshold (higher MI → lower threshold), enabling individual-level, structure-aware selection of reliable graphs via temporal ensembling. Second, Regularized Prototype Supervision (RPS) initializes class prototypes from the pretrained classifier weights and optimizes them under a Bayesian objective: the prior (Gaussian centered at initial weights) regularizes prototypes against drift, while the likelihood is approximated by a soft-assignment self-training loss where assignments are computed via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations over the batch. The final loss jointly optimizes the RPS term on selected graphs and the MI term on all test graphs, with a curriculum that gradually tightens the selection threshold.

## Results

ASSESS achieves consistent improvements over all baselines across five graph classification datasets under test-time adaptation. On FRANKENSTEIN, it reaches 60.3% average accuracy (vs. 59.0% for the best baseline RNA), and on Mutagenicity it achieves 73.5% (vs. 70.6% for TAST). The largest gains appear on PROTEINS (78.9% vs. 75.4% for GraphCL) and NCI1 (75.5% vs. 71.6% for GraphCL), while on IMDB-BINARY it attains 67.8% (vs. 65.2% for SHOT). All improvements are measured as classification accuracy under offline test-time adaptation with distribution shifts induced by graph density splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASSESS outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. Among vanilla GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN, GAT), the best (GIN) trails ASSESS by 2.3% on FRANKENSTEIN and 4.6% on Mutagenicity, confirming that standard architectures suffer under distribution shift without adaptation. Among unsupervised/semi-supervised methods, GraphCL is the strongest on PROTEINS (75.4%) and NCI1 (71.6%) but still falls 3.5% and 3.9% short of ASSESS, while MeanTeacher is competitive on PROTEINS (69.1%) but weaker elsewhere. Among test-time adaptation methods, TAST is the closest competitor on Mutagenicity (70.6% vs. 73.5%) and SHOT leads on IMDB-BINARY among baselines (65.2% vs. 67.8% for ASSESS), but both are designed for Euclidean data and do not account for graph structural complexity. RNA, the most recent graph-specific source-free adaptation method, is competitive on FRANKENSTEIN (59.0%) but lags by 5.9% on Mutagenicity and 8.7% on PROTEINS, indicating that its rank-and-align strategy is less effective under the density-based shifts used here.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASSESS extends the test-time adaptation paradigm from Euclidean data (images, tabular) to non-Euclidean graph-structured data, building on two key threads: (1) prototype-based domain generalization (Iwasawa & Matsuo, 2021), which this paper reformulates through a Bayesian prior-likelihood decomposition with Sinkhorn-Knopp soft assignments, and (2) graph mutual information estimation (Infograph, Sun et al., 2020a), repurposed here not as a training objective but as a per-graph confidence signal for adaptive thresholding. The method departs from prior self-training TTA approaches (SHOT, TAST) by replacing uniform or class-wise confidence thresholds with individual-level, structure-aware thresholds derived from subgraph mutual information, and by regularizing prototype updates against the pretrained classifier weights rather than freezing the head entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method purposefully designed for test-time adaptation on graphs under the source-free (no training data access) setting, addressing a practical gap where institutions release pretrained models but keep training data private. The convergence guarantee under the Tsybakov condition and PL assumption provides theoretical grounding that is rare in TTA literature. However, the evaluation is limited to five small-scale graph datasets (≤4,337 graphs) in an offline setting with a single GIN backbone, leaving open whether the approach scales to large-scale graph benchmarks, online/streaming adaptation, or heterogeneous GNN architectures. The moderate but consistent gains (1–5% over strongest baselines) suggest the method is a solid first step rather than a definitive solution to graph TTA.
