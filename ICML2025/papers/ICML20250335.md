## Problem

Traditional stratified sampling (Neyman allocation) minimizes data variance but ignores the additional variance introduced by differential privacy (DP) noise mechanisms. When DP is incorporated into survey design, privacy amplification by subsampling means each group requires a different nominal privacy budget to achieve uniform protection, fundamentally altering the optimal sample allocation. This paper is the first to formulate and solve the DP-aware optimal stratified sampling problem for private mean estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250335/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the population group sizes N_i, group variances σ_i², total sample size η, privacy budget ε, sensitivity Δf, and number of groups k. It outputs the optimal integer sample sizes (n_1,...,n_k) that sum to η, where each n_i is bounded by N_i. A key structural constraint is that the DP noise variance γ²(n_i, N_i, ε) depends on the subsampling ratio n_i/N_i, coupling the allocation decision to the privacy mechanism's noise level in a non-trivial way that makes the objective non-separable across groups.

## Method

The paper formulates the DP-aware stratified sampling design as a constrained integer program minimizing a variance objective that combines data variance (σ_i²/n_i) with DP noise variance that depends on the subsampling rate n_i/N_i through the privacy amplification formula. The central theoretical contribution is proving strong convexity of the variance objective under Laplace, Discrete Laplace, and Truncated-Uniform-Laplace mechanisms, which guarantees a unique continuous optimum and enables a quadratic lower bound. For the population mean case, closed-form continuous solutions are derived for DLap and TuLap (proportional to τ_i·N_i), and the pure-Laplace variance is shown to be minimized by proportional allocation. Algorithm 1 exploits strong convexity to bound the integer-optimal design within a ball of radius r = √(2(g(n_init)−g(x*)))/λ around the continuous optimum, reducing the search from O(η^(k−1)) to O((2r)^(k−1)) where r is empirically small, making the method scalable to sample sizes of 10⁵.

## Results

The paper's headline metric is the ratio of the DP variance objective (Neyman allocation vs. DP-optimal design), where 1 is optimal and higher is worse. Under the Laplace mechanism, the ratio ranges from 1.83 (ε=0.1) to 2.31 (ε=√10), meaning ignoring DP during design inflates variance by up to ~2.3×. Under TuLap, the inflation is more severe, reaching 4.08 at ε=10. In simulation (Section 5.1), the naive subsampling variance for population mean under Laplace can be up to 2.5× the optimal design variance within 1 < ε < 10, and up to 4× under TuLap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical Neyman allocation (Neyman, 1934), which minimizes data-only variance but ignores DP-induced noise. The DP-optimal design consistently outperforms Neyman allocation on the DP variance objective across all tested ε values and mechanisms. Under Laplace noise, the Neyman design incurs a 1.8×–2.3× variance penalty relative to the DP-optimal design; under TuLap, the penalty is larger, reaching 2.4×–4.1×. For the A-optimal design objective, the gap is somewhat smaller (up to 1.8× under Laplace) but still substantial. Notably, for the Discrete Laplace mechanism applied to population mean estimation, the DP-optimal design coincides exactly with the Neyman allocation (ratio = 1), meaning no variance inflation occurs in that specific case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Neyman allocation framework for optimal stratified sampling by incorporating the variance induced by differential privacy mechanisms and the privacy amplification effect of subsampling. It builds directly on the subsampling privacy amplification results of Kasiviswanathan et al. (2011) and Dong et al. (2022), the parallel composition lemma of McSherry (2009), and the specific noise distributions of Awan & Slavković (2018, TuLap) and Inusah & Kozubowski (2006, DLap). The key departure from prior work is that traditional stratified sampling design (Neyman, 1934) minimizes only data variance, whereas this paper formulates a joint optimization over data variance and mechanism-specific DP noise, where the noise level depends on the per-group subsampling rate through the privacy amplification formula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that ignoring DP effects during survey design can inflate estimator variance by up to 4× (TuLap) or 2.5× (Laplace), providing a concrete motivation for DP-aware design in sensitive survey domains (health, public policy). The strong convexity result and the resulting algorithm (reducing search from O(η^(k-1)) to O((2r)^(k-1))) make the approach practically implementable for large sample sizes. Key limitations include the assumption of known group variances, the restriction to ε-DP with three specific mechanisms and subsampling without replacement, and the curse of dimensionality when the number of groups k is large. Open directions include extending to shuffling-based amplification, central DP with a trusted curator, other mechanisms (Gaussian, Staircase), and alternative privacy frameworks (zCDP, f-DP).
