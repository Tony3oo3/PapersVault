## Problem

Standard isotropic diffusion (DDPM) degrades all frequency components of a time series identically, but time series carry most of their informative structure in low frequencies. The authors empirically show that this causes a steep, early collapse of low-frequency energy during the forward process, producing contradictory gradient directions across diffusion steps and destabilizing training. No prior time series diffusion model had addressed this frequency-domain asymmetry in the transition design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For forecasting, the model takes a look-back window of 96 steps as a condition and produces a target window of L ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} steps; for super-resolution it takes a down-sampled time series (at a factor that divides the original length L=576) and reconstructs the full-resolution signal. All inputs are first instance-normalized to zero mean and unit variance before entering the diffusion process. Denormalization is task-specific: forecasting uses a learned statistics predictor conditioned on the look-back window, while super-resolution re-scales the standard deviation using the dataset-based noise schedule at the corresponding diffusion step.

## Method

MA-TSD replaces DDPM's diagonal transition matrix with a non-isotropic moving-average matrix K_t whose kernel sizes are the factors of the time series length, interpolated across T diffusion steps to form a continuous coarse-to-fine degradation. A dataset-based noise schedule β_t = √(1 − γ_t²) compensates for the variance loss caused by moving averaging, ensuring the terminal distribution is standard Gaussian. The backward process follows a DDIM-style non-Markovian formulation with a 'factor-only' acceleration strategy that selects only the diffusion steps nearest to the original moving-average kernels, reducing inference cost while preserving quality. Because the forward process is inherently multi-resolution, the backward process starting from a mid-diffusion step directly yields super-resolution without retraining per scale. For forecasting, a hybrid loss jointly optimizes the denoising network and a small statistics-prediction network for mean and standard deviation, which the authors prove is an upper bound on the standard conditional diffusion loss without instance normalization.

## Results

On time series forecasting (primary task), MA-TSD achieves the best average MSE on all 6 datasets (Electricity 0.340, ETTh2 0.212, ETTm2 0.124, Exchange 0.372, Traffic 0.166, Weather 0.207), yielding an overall rank of 1.17 versus 2.67 for the next-best diffusion model (MR-DIFF). For probabilistic forecasting, MA-TSD attains the best CRPS on 5/6 datasets (rank 1.67), with the sole exception being Weather where TMDM leads (0.138 vs. 0.150). On super-resolution, MA-TSD reduces Consistency error by 5–10× over conditional DDPM (e.g., 0.0032 vs. 0.0291 on MFRED at 3×) and achieves the lowest Context-FID on most dataset-scale combinations without requiring per-scale retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five diffusion-based forecasting baselines, MA-TSD wins on all 6 datasets for MSE and on 5/6 for CRPS; the only CRPS loss is on Weather (0.150 vs. TMDM's 0.138), which the authors attribute to the dataset's high 10-min resolution where both informative high-frequency signal and stochastic noise are suppressed by the moving-average filter. The largest gains are over CSDI (e.g., 0.340 vs. 0.458 on Electricity) and D3VAE (e.g., 0.124 vs. 3.345 on ETTm2), while the margin over the strongest competitor MR-DIFF is more modest (e.g., 0.372 vs. 0.480 on Exchange, 0.166 vs. 0.247 on Traffic). For super-resolution, MA-TSD outperforms both the conditional DDPM and FM-VP baselines on Consistency across all three datasets and all three scales (3×, 6×, 12×), with Context-FID also superior except on Solar at 12× where FM-VP edges ahead (0.862 vs. 1.414). In the synthesis task, MA-TSD beats both KoVAE and ImagenTime on Discriminative Score, Predictive Score, and Context-FID across ETTh2, Exchange, and ECG. Against non-diffusion SOTA (Autoformer, Non-stationary Transformer, PatchTST), MA-TSD achieves the best overall rank (1.50 vs. PatchTST's 1.83), though PatchTST is slightly better on ETTh2 (0.202 vs. 0.212) and Weather (0.189 vs. 0.207).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework to time series by replacing the isotropic diagonal transition matrix with a non-isotropic moving average matrix, motivated by the observation that low-frequency components carry more information in time series than in images. It is conceptually closest to Blurring Diffusion Models (Hoogeboom & Salimans, 2023), which also use low-pass filters as transitions, but departs by adopting a non-Markovian (DDIM-style) formulation with a dataset-based noise schedule rather than forcing the filter into a per-frequency Markovian chain. Among time series diffusion models (CSDI, SSSD, D3VAE, TMDM, mr-diff), all of which rely on standard isotropic DDPM, MA-TSD is the first to explicitly encode the frequency asymmetry of time series into the forward process itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that standard DDPM produces contradictory gradients across diffusion steps when applied to time series provides a concrete diagnostic for why prior time series diffusion models may underperform, and the moving average transition offers a principled fix that also yields a free super-resolution capability without retraining per scale. Limitations include the dependence of the factor-only backward strategy on the time series length having useful divisors, the marginal improvement on high-resolution Weather data where informative high-frequency content is also suppressed, and the dataset-specific noise schedule that must be recomputed per dataset. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to multivariate or irregularly-sampled time series, and whether the non-Markovian formulation can be further accelerated beyond the factor-only subset.
