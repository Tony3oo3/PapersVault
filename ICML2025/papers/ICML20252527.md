## Problem

Existing GNN model extraction attacks either ignore realistic budget and batch-size constraints or fail to account for structural dependencies between graph nodes. This paper formulates the problem of GNN Model Extraction With Limited Budgets for node-level graph learning, where bulk queries are prohibited, only a small set of initial nodes is available, and the total query budget is strictly capped—making prior active-learning-based extraction methods inapplicable to graph-structured data.

## Input / Output

The extractor receives node attribute vectors and the subgraph structure G_a (a subset of the target graph), plus API access to the target GNN f_T, but no labels. Queries are issued iteratively in small batches (κ=1 node per cycle in experiments) under three constraints: an initial budget I (2C nodes, one per class), a per-cycle budget κ, and a total budget B (ranging from 2C to 20C). The output is the accumulated set of queried nodes and a trained 2-layer GCN extracted model f_a whose predictions should match f_T's on unseen test nodes.

## Method

CEGA is an iterative active-sampling framework that selects the next node to query by combining three complementary ranking criteria: (1) representativeness via PageRank on the subgraph structure, (2) uncertainty via the entropy of the interim GCN model's softmax predictions (with a perturbation-based alternative for lower-complexity settings), and (3) diversity via K-Means clustering of node embeddings to penalize over-represented prediction classes. The three ranks are combined through cycle-dependent weights that shift from structure-dominated (early cycles, when the interim model is unreliable) to history- and diversity-dominated (later cycles), following exponential decay/growth schedules. After each query, a 2-layer GCN interim model is retrained on all accumulated queried nodes, providing updated predictions that guide subsequent selections. Theoretical analysis establishes O(CN + N log N) additional time complexity for the entropy-based uncertainty step and proves the existence of a perturbation intensity ε that preserves interim model stability.

## Results

Using 20C queried nodes across six benchmark graph datasets, CEGA achieves the best or near-best performance on accuracy, fidelity, and F1 score. Headline numbers include 93.90% accuracy and 95.83% fidelity on Coauthor-Physics, 85.98% accuracy and 90.81% fidelity on Amazon-Computer, and 73.29% accuracy and 78.50% fidelity on DBLP. On the most challenging dataset (Cora-Full with 70 classes), CEGA reaches 52.74% accuracy, 77.90% fidelity, and 31.20% F1, outperforming all baselines by 1–3 percentage points. CEGA also exhibits the lowest performance gap relative to the full-subgraph oracle model, indicating superior information recovery under budget constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEGA is compared against three active-learning baselines: AGE (Cai et al., 2017), GRAIN (NN-D), and GRAIN (ball-D) (Zhang et al., 2021), all evaluated under identical query-budget constraints (2C to 20C nodes). CEGA outperforms AGE on 5 of 6 datasets across all three metrics (accuracy, fidelity, F1), with the largest margins on Amazon-Computer (+0.85% accuracy, +0.71% fidelity, +4.20% F1) and Cora-Full (+2.15% accuracy, +2.23% fidelity, +1.92% F1); AGE edges out CEGA only on Coauthor-CS by a narrow 0.11% in accuracy. Against GRAIN (NN-D), CEGA wins on 5 of 6 datasets for every metric, with particularly large F1 gains on Amazon-Computer (+2.28%) and Cora-Full (+2.27%). Against GRAIN (ball-D), CEGA similarly dominates on 5 of 6 datasets, with the most notable advantage on Amazon-Computer F1 (+8.10%) where GRAIN (ball-D) degrades substantially. Overall, CEGA achieves the best result on 5 out of 6 datasets for each of the three metrics, with AGE as the closest competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: GNN-based model extraction attacks (Wu et al. 2022, Shen et al. 2022) and active learning for graph neural networks (AGE, Cai et al. 2017; GRAIN, Zhang et al. 2021). Prior GNN MEA studies assume unconstrained or high-volume querying, while prior GNN active learning methods do not frame the problem as replicating a black-box target model. CEGA repurposes active learning criteria (representativeness via PageRank, uncertainty via entropy, diversity via K-Means clustering) into the model extraction setting, and introduces explicit budget constraints (initial, per-cycle, and total query limits) that prior MEA work on graphs has largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing budget-constrained GNN model extraction, the paper provides a more realistic threat model for MLaaS security and simultaneously offers a practical tool for researchers who need to acquire GNN functionality when expert labeling is prohibitively expensive (e.g., biomedical knowledge graphs). Key limitations include the transductive assumption (no inductive generalization to unseen nodes), restriction to node-level classification, and the fact that edge information is not leveraged in early query cycles. Open questions include extending the framework to inductive GNNs, incorporating edge-level queries, and evaluating defenses against the specific budget-constrained attack patterns CEGA enables.
