## Problem

Current video-language models are constrained by short context lengths and rely on sparse frame subsampling for longer sequences, causing information loss and limiting long-video understanding. Existing solutions either sacrifice transformer representational depth for efficiency, require expensive fine-tuning on long-video datasets, or assume the spatio-temporal module is intrinsic to the LLM, limiting specialization. The paper asks how to enable models to fully process arbitrarily long videos in a single pass without losing critical details or requiring additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video as a stream of frame chunks (256 frames per chunk for Video-LLaMA with EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14, or 16 frames per chunk for VideoChat2 with UMT-L) alongside a text question, and produces a text answer. Video frames are average-pooled over spatial patches to form a per-frame embedding sequence, then processed sequentially in a single pass. The Q-former outputs R visual tokens per chunk, which are incrementally averaged across all chunks (Eq. 17) so that only a fixed-size token set is passed to the LLM regardless of video length, enabling unbounded context without storing all chunk embeddings.

## Method

The core contribution augments the video Q-former's cross-attention with a continuous-time long-term memory (LTM) that consolidates information across chunks. Each chunk is processed by the existing Q-former (short-term memory), while the LTM maintains a continuous signal x(t) over [0,1] fitted via multivariate ridge regression with N basis functions. Attention over the LTM uses a Gibbs PDF derived from the continuous query-key similarity function, replacing the Gaussian model of the original ∞-former. At each consolidation step, the past LTM is contracted by a forgetting factor τ, the new chunk is appended, and ridge regression is recomputed; T evaluation points are sampled either uniformly or via 'sticky memories,' where locations are drawn from a histogram of prior attention density to allocate higher granularity to relevant video segments. The final Q-former output is a weighted combination Z = α·Z_STM + (1−α)·Z_LTM, and the entire pipeline is training-free, using pretrained Video-LLaMA or VideoChat2 weights without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On NeXT-QA, ∞-Video LLaMA with sticky memories achieves 41.1% accuracy, outperforming MovieChat+ (35.2%) and the base Video-LLaMA (30.7%) by 5.9 and 10.4 points respectively. On EgoSchema (3-minute egocentric videos), the same configuration reaches 46.8% accuracy, a +6.0 point gain over the no-LTM variant (40.8%). On the MovieChat-1K open-ended benchmark (~8-minute videos), ∞-Video LLaMA (sticky) attains 72.2% accuracy and a 3.88 GPT-3.5 score, surpassing MovieChat (67.8%, 3.81) and MovieChat+ (66.4%, 3.67). On VideoMME (up to 1-hour videos), ∞-VideoChat2 (sticky) achieves 42.4 average, beating all open-source baselines including Qwen-VL-Chat (41.1) and Chat-UniVi-v1.5 (40.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Video-LLaMA-based training-free methods, ∞-Video LLaMA (sticky) clearly wins: it outperforms MovieChat+ by 5.9 points on NeXT-QA and 9.4 points on EgoSchema, and beats the base Video-LLaMA by 10.4 and 26.6 points respectively. Against GPT-4-based proprietary systems (LLOvi, VideoAgent, VideoTree), the open-source ∞-Video LLaMA trails substantially (41.1 vs. 67.7–75.6 on NeXT-QA), but ∞-VideoChat2 (sticky) narrows the gap to within 0.5 points of VideoTree on NeXT-QA (78.1 vs. 75.6) and is competitive on EgoSchema (64.8 vs. 66.2). On VideoMME, ∞-VideoChat2 (sticky) at 42.4 average surpasses all open-source baselines—Qwen-VL-Chat (41.1), Chat-UniVi-v1.5 (40.6), Video-LLaVA (39.9), ShareGPT4Video 8B (39.9), and ST-LLM (37.9)—by 1.3 to 4.5 points. On the MovieChat-1K open-ended benchmark, ∞-Video LLaMA (sticky) leads all Vicuna-7B-based methods with 72.2% accuracy, beating VideoChat (61.0%), Video-ChatGPT (44.2%), MovieChat (67.8%), and MovieChat+ (66.4%). For the VideoChat2 category, gains from adding LTM are modest (≤0.6 points), which the authors attribute to VideoChat2 already being well-optimized for in-domain tasks; however, replacing STM entirely with LTM (α=0) yields the best VideoChat2 results on MovieChat-1K (66.5% accuracy, 3.85 score).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ∞-former's continuous-time long-term memory (originally developed for text) to the video domain by augmenting video Q-formers' cross-attention with a continuous LTM module, replacing the original Gaussian attention model with a Gibbs PDF that better mirrors the discrete softmax. It departs from prior long-video approaches (MovieChat, VideoTree, VideoAgent) that either rely on sparse frame subsampling, heuristic frame merging, or GPT-4 backbones, by processing all frames in a single pass with a training-free memory consolidation mechanism. The 'sticky memories' sampling strategy, which allocates more memory resolution to high-attention regions, draws on cognitive science concepts of memory reconsolidation and non-local replay, distinguishing it from the uniform sampling in the original ∞-former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the approach is practically significant: it enables existing short-video models (Video-LLaMA, VideoChat2) to handle arbitrarily long videos without fine-tuning or additional training data, reducing both computational cost and data requirements. However, improvements are modest and architecture-dependent—VideoChat2, already a strong model, shows minimal gains, and uniform LTM sampling sometimes underperforms the no-LTM baseline, suggesting the mechanism's benefit is conditional. Open questions include whether deeper integration with cognitive consolidation theories (e.g., offline replay during 'sleep' states) could yield larger gains, whether the approach generalizes beyond Q-former architectures, and how interpretability analyses of the continuous attention maps might inform understanding of episodic memory in biological systems.
