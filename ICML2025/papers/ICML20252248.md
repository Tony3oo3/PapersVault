## Problem

State-of-the-art ML-powered iterative combinatorial auctions (ICAs) achieve high efficiency via value queries (VQs), but practical auctions like the CCA rely on demand queries (DQs) because they are cognitively easier for bidders. No prior mechanism effectively combined both query types, and the paper proves that DQs alone cannot exceed 50%+ε efficiency on infinitely many instances, while VQs alone are impractical in early rounds. The paper also identifies that naively mixing DQs and VQs can cause efficiency to drop arbitrarily close to 100%, requiring careful design to avoid this pitfall.

## Input / Output

The auction operates in sequential rounds: bidders first receive item price vectors (DQs) and respond with their utility-maximizing bundles, then receive specific bundle queries (VQs) and respond with their value for that bundle. The mechanism is constrained to a total budget of 100 queries (40 DQs + 60 VQs for MLHCA, versus 100 of a single type for baselines). The output is a feasible allocation of items (respecting capacity constraints) to bidders along with payments computed via VCG or VCG-nearest rules, with the final allocation determined by solving the Winner Determination Problem over all elicited reports.

## Method

MLHCA is a three-phase hybrid auction: (1) an initial CCA-style DQ phase with fixed price increments, (2) an ML-powered DQ phase where mMVNNs are trained on demand responses and NEXTPRICE generates the next price vector via convex optimization for highest clearing potential, and (3) an ML-powered VQ phase where NEXTALLOCATION selects bundles to query based on trained models. The key technical contributions are: a mixed training algorithm that simultaneously optimizes a DQ loss (utility-gap between predicted and reported argmax bundles) and a VQ regression loss on mMVNNs, and a 'bridge bid'—a single VQ for the WDP-optimal bundle after the DQ phase—that provably guarantees the final efficiency is no less than the DQ-only efficiency. The critical design insight is query ordering: DQs provide global, price-driven information about the allocation space and are superior in early rounds, while VQs provide targeted, precise value information that becomes advantageous once models are sufficiently trained, yielding up to 10× lower efficiency loss than the previous SOTA with 58% fewer queries.

## Results

On the SATS spectrum auction test suite (GSVM, LSVM, SRVM, MRVM domains), MLHCA (40 DQs + 60 VQs) achieves 0.00% efficiency loss in GSVM and SRVM, 0.04% in LSVM, and 4.81% in MRVM after 100 total queries. In LSVM, MLHCA reduces efficiency loss by a factor of ~10 compared to BOCA (0.04% vs. 0.39%), and in MRVM it outperforms all baselines by over 2 percentage points. MLHCA requires at most 74 queries (40 DQs + 34 VQs) to statistically outperform the final efficiency of both BOCA (100 VQs) and ML-CCA (100 DQs), and in three of four domains it surpasses ML-CCA's 100-DQ efficiency with only 40 DQs + 2 VQs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLHCA outperforms all three baselines across all four SATS domains. Against BOCA (the previous SOTA VQ-based auction, 100 VQs), MLHCA achieves a 10× reduction in efficiency loss on LSVM (0.04% vs. 0.39%) and a 38% reduction on MRVM (4.81% vs. 7.77%), while matching or exceeding BOCA on GSVM and SRVM. Against ML-CCA (the SOTA DQ-based auction, 100 DQs), MLHCA reduces efficiency loss by 98% on GSVM (0.00% vs. 1.77%), 99.5% on LSVM (0.04% vs. 8.36%), and 31% on MRVM (4.81% vs. 6.94%). Even ML-CCA supplemented with the clock-bids-raised heuristic (up to 100 additional VQs) is outperformed by MLHCA in every domain, with the largest gap on LSVM (0.04% vs. 3.61%). Against the classical CCA, MLHCA reduces efficiency loss by 99.6% on GSVM, 99.8% on LSVM, and 36% on MRVM. Notably, MLHCA achieves these gains with fewer total queries: it needs only 42 queries to beat ML-CCA in GSVM and SRVM, and at most 74 queries to beat both BOCA and ML-CCA in MRVM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of ML-powered iterative combinatorial auctions: the DQ-based CCA/ML-CCA lineage (which uses demand queries with ML-driven price updates) and the VQ-based BOCA/MLCA lineage (which uses value queries with Bayesian-optimization-style allocation selection). The key conceptual departure is a theoretical framework showing that DQs provide superior information in early rounds (global, utility-maximizing bundles) while VQs are necessary and sufficient for achieving full efficiency in later rounds, and that the ordering is critical. The bridge bid—a single VQ for the WDP-optimal bundle after the DQ phase—is a novel mechanism-design element that resolves the non-monotonicity problem when transitioning between query types, and the mixed training algorithm that provably extracts all information from both DQ and VQ responses is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLHCA's practical impact is substantial: in the most realistic domain (MRVM, simulating the 2014 Canadian spectrum auction worth USD 5.27B), a 2-point efficiency gain corresponds to over USD 100M in welfare, and the mechanism achieves this with fewer queries, reducing bidders' cognitive load. The non-monotonicity result—that adding DQs can reduce efficiency arbitrarily close to 100%—challenges the standard assumption that more elicited information always helps, with implications beyond this specific mechanism. Open questions include dynamically determining the optimal DQ-to-VQ switch point, incorporating epistemic uncertainty into the ML models, and extending the incentive analysis (the VQ phase is not strategyproof, though the paper argues for strong practical incentives via marginal economies and activity rules).
