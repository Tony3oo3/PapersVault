## Problem

Existing locally differentially private (LDP) graph neural networks suffer severe utility degradation because the privacy budget ε is split across high-dimensional node features, injecting substantial noise that degrades downstream tasks like node classification. No prior work has systematically identified the factors governing this estimation error or proposed a unified framework to mitigate it while preserving LDP guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250883/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server receives LDP-perturbed node feature vectors x' (produced client-side via multi-bit or piecewise mechanisms) and has public access to the graph topology (V, E), but never sees the original features x. The framework outputs node embeddings and classification labels for semi-supervised node classification, with the perturbation applied exactly once per node to maintain ε-LDP compliance throughout training and inference via the post-processing theorem.

## Method

UPGNET introduces two plug-and-play layers motivated by a theoretical bound showing aggregation estimation error scales as O(√(d log(d/δ)) / (ε√|N(v)|)): (1) a High-Order Aggregator (HOA) layer that expands the effective neighborhood size through personalized multi-hop aggregation with decaying weights, using Dirichlet energy analysis to provably mitigate over-smoothing (energy ratio Φ_K → 0 as K → ∞); and (2) a Node Feature Regularization (NFR) layer that reduces effective feature dimension via L1-regularization solved in closed form through proximal gradient descent, yielding a soft-thresholding operator for feature selection. The two layers compose into H-N or N-H architectures that can be integrated with any GNN backbone (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT), and the entire pipeline remains LDP-compliant since all server-side processing is post-processing of the single local perturbation.

## Results

UPGNET consistently outperforms BASE, LPGNN, and Solitude across all four datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, LastFM, Facebook) and three GNN backbones (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT) for ε ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0}. On CiteSeer with GraphSAGE at ε=3.0, UPGNET achieves approximately 5% higher test accuracy than both LPGNN and Solitude. On Facebook, UPGNET's accuracy closely approaches the NonPriv (no-privacy) baseline, indicating near-lossless utility under LDP. The NFR layer alone yields 2.5–4.9% overall accuracy gains over the base LDP mechanisms (MBM/PM) across all four datasets, with the largest improvements at the smallest privacy budget (ε=0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPGNET outperforms all three baselines (LPGNN, Solitude, and the base LDP mechanisms) across every dataset and GNN architecture tested. Against LPGNN (Sajadmanesh & Gatica-Perez, CCS 2021) and Solitude (Lin et al., IEEE TIFS 2022), UPGNET achieves roughly 5% higher test accuracy on CiteSeer (GraphSAGE) at ε=3.0, and on Facebook its accuracy nearly matches the NonPriv upper bound, whereas the baselines remain noticeably below it. Against the piecewise mechanism (PM, Wang et al., ICDE 2019) used as a standalone perturbation, adding UPGNET's NFR layer (PM*) improves overall accuracy by 2.3–4.6% across the four datasets, with the largest gain (+6.7%) on Facebook at ε=0.01. The HOA layer further extends these gains: in the HOA-vs-SKA ablation (Fig. 6), HOA maintains steady accuracy improvement as the aggregation step K grows to 64, while SKA's accuracy collapses below 40% on LastFM at K=64 due to over-smoothing. UPGNET's plug-and-play design means these improvements hold regardless of whether the backbone is GCN, GraphSAGE, or GAT, though GAT shows slightly larger degradation under very small ε due to its attention mechanism's sensitivity to feature noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented LDP-GNN literature (LPGNN, Solitude, and others using MBM or PM) into a single three-stage pipeline (perturbation, calibration, aggregation) and identifies two structural factors—feature dimension and neighborhood size—that govern estimation error. Rather than proposing yet another LDP mechanism, it repurposes classical L1 regularization (via proximal gradient descent) and Dirichlet energy analysis from the over-smoothing literature as plug-and-play utility-enhancement layers (NFR and HOA) that can be attached to any existing LDP mechanism and any GNN backbone. This positions UPGNET as a general-purpose enhancement layer on top of prior LDP-GNN work rather than a replacement for the underlying privacy mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified pipeline and Theorem 3 give practitioners a clear, actionable diagnostic: utility loss in LDP-GNN is governed by d/|N(v)|, so reducing effective dimensionality or expanding effective neighborhood are the two levers to pull. The plug-and-play design means existing LDP-GNN deployments can adopt NFR or HOA without redesigning the privacy protocol. Limitations include focus solely on node-feature privacy (orthogonal to link privacy), restriction to node classification, and the one-off (non-iterative) NFR solution that may underperform on highly structured features. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other graph tasks (link prediction, community detection), whether it composes with link-LDP mechanisms, and how it behaves under adaptive or sequential privacy budgets.
