## Problem

Existing large-scale neural modeling approaches are task-specific, focusing exclusively on either encoding (predicting neural activity from behavior) or decoding (predicting behavior from neural activity), which limits their ability to capture the bidirectional relationship between the two. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a unified multimodal framework that performs both encoding and decoding within a single model, enabling seamless translation between neural activity and behavior at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253056/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NEDS consumes trial-aligned spike counts (20 ms bins, 100 bins per 2 s trial) from up to 27,380 neurons and four behavioral variables: continuous whisker motion and wheel speed (20 ms resolution) plus discrete choice (left/right) and block prior (3 levels). Continuous modalities are tokenized temporally while discrete variables are replicated across all T time bins to align resolution. Outputs include predicted spike rates under a Poisson emission model, continuous behaviors via MSE, discrete behaviors via cross-entropy, and per-neuron embeddings formed by concatenating rows of the session-specific input and output matrices.

## Method

NEDS is a multimodal, encoder-only transformer that processes a unified token sequence from all modalities through a shared self-attention stack, with modality-specific linear tokenizers on input and modality-specific linear decoders on output. The core training innovation is a multi-task-masking strategy that randomly alternates per-sequence among four schemes: full neural masking (learns E[X|Y] for encoding), full behavioral masking (learns E[Y|X] for decoding), within-modality random masking (captures intra-modal dependencies), and cross-modal random masking (encourages joint cross-modal representations). Session-specific input matrices and session embeddings enable multi-animal pretraining, while rotary position embeddings encode temporal order. The model is pretrained on 73 animals (74 sessions) and fine-tuned on 10 held-out animals, with the masking scheme sampled uniformly at each training step so that a single trained model can perform either encoding or decoding at inference by simply choosing which modality to mask.

## Results

On the IBL repeated site dataset (10 held-out animals, pretrained on 74 sessions), multi-session NEDS achieves 0.27±0.03 bits-per-spike for neural encoding and outperforms all baselines on every decoding task: 91% choice accuracy, 86% block accuracy, 0.64 wheel-speed R², and 0.59 whisker-motion R². Multi-session pretraining yields a 24% improvement in encoding and 4–11% improvement in decoding over single-session NEDS. Additionally, NEDS neuron embeddings achieve 83% brain-region classification accuracy without any explicit region supervision during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEDS outperforms all three multi-session baselines on every decoding task. Against POYO+ (the strongest decoding-only baseline), NEDS gains +1% on choice (0.91 vs 0.90), +5% on block (0.86 vs 0.81), +13% on wheel speed (0.64 vs 0.51), and +7% on whisker motion (0.59 vs 0.52). Against NDT2, the margins are larger: +10% choice, +7% block, +17% wheel, and +13% whisker. Against the linear RRR decoder, NEDS leads by +8% choice, +2% block, +19% wheel, and +18% whisker. Critically, NEDS is the only method in the comparison that also performs neural encoding (0.27 bps), whereas POYO+, NDT2, and RRR are restricted to decoding. On the single-session setting, NEDS also beats the linear and reduced-rank baselines on all tasks except block decoding, and the multimodal variant improves over its unimodal counterpart by 5% in encoding and 2–7% in decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEDS extends the multi-task-masking paradigm from UL2 (Tay et al., 2022) and multimodal masked modeling (He et al., 2022; Mizrahi et al., 2023) to the neurobehavioral domain, using a transformer architecture with modality-specific tokenizers and session-specific input matrices inherited from sequential autoencoder work (Pandarinath et al., 2018). It departs from prior large-scale neural models—POYO+ (decoding-only) and NDT2 (encoding/self-prediction with limited decoding)—by unifying both directions in a single model through alternating neural, behavioral, within-modality, and cross-modal masking. Unlike the concurrent M-VAE approach (Schulz et al., 2025), which uses a sequential RNN-VAE architecture, NEDS employs a parallel transformer that scales to multi-animal datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEDS provides the first unified, scalable framework for bidirectional neural-behavioral modeling, removing the need to train separate models for encoding and decoding. The emergent brain-region separability in neuron embeddings (83% classification accuracy without explicit anatomical supervision) suggests the model captures meaningful neuroanatomical structure, opening avenues for cell-type and region-agnostic analysis. Key limitations include reliance on trial-aligned data (restricting pretraining scale), limited hyperparameter tuning at full 73-animal scale, and evaluation on a single task/dataset. Future directions include unaligned-data pretraining, additional modalities (LFP, electrophysiological features), and extension toward a true brain foundation model.
