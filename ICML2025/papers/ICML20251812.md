## Problem

The Mahalanobis distance is a widely used post-hoc OOD detector, but its performance varies dramatically across models with similar classification accuracy. The paper identifies that this inconsistency stems from strong violations of the method's core Gaussian assumption: feature norms vary much more across and within classes than a shared-covariance Gaussian model would predict, causing the feature norm to become a confounder that correlates with the Mahalanobis score regardless of whether a sample is ID or OOD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251812/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-logit feature vectors (e.g., 1024-dim for SwinV2-B) extracted from a frozen, pretrained network applied to RGB images. No architectural or training modifications are required; the only change is applying l2-normalization to both the training features (for estimating class means and the shared covariance matrix) and the test features (for scoring). The output is a scalar OOD score defined as the negative minimum Mahalanobis distance to any class mean, with a rejection threshold calibrated to achieve 95% true-positive rate on in-distribution validation data.

## Method

Mahalanobis++ projects pre-logit features onto the unit sphere via l2-normalization before estimating class-conditional means and the shared covariance matrix, and before computing the Mahalanobis distance for test samples. The key insight is that discarding the feature norm removes the dominant source of variance that violates the shared-covariance Gaussian assumption: the paper proves (Lemma 3.1) that under a true Gaussian model, feature norms should be tightly concentrated, yet empirically they exhibit heavy tails and strong class-dependent variation for most pretrained models. After normalization, QQ-plots show features align more closely with a Gaussian, class variances become more uniform (reducing the variance-deviation score), and the correlation between feature norm and OOD score is broken, so OOD samples with small norms are no longer systematically missed. The method is purely post-hoc, requires no hyperparameters, and consistently outperforms the unnormalized Mahalanobis distance in 41 of 44 tested models, improving average FPR by 7.6 points over the OpenOOD benchmark.

## Results

Across 44 ImageNet models evaluated on five OpenOOD datasets, Mahalanobis++ achieves an average FPR@95% of 34.9%, outperforming the conventional Mahalanobis distance (42.5%) by 7.6 points and the strongest non-Mahalanobis baseline ViM (41.9%) by 7.0 points. On the cleaned NINCO benchmark, the improvement over Mahalanobis widens to 10.9 FPR points (42.9% vs. 53.8%), with the best single-model result of 18.4% FPR for ConvNeXtV2-L-In21k. On CIFAR-100, Mahalanobis++ achieves 44.1% average FPR versus 52.5% for Mahalanobis and 51.3% for ViM. Normalization also eliminates all 17 failed noise-distribution unit tests for ConvNeXtV2, SwinV2, and ViT-CLIP models (reducing failures from 5–14 to 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mahalanobis++ (Maha++) consistently outperforms the conventional Mahalanobis distance (Maha) in 41 out of 44 models, with the largest gains on models where feature norms vary strongly (e.g., SwinV2-B: 41.6→26.7 FPR, a 14.9-point improvement). The only exceptions are two ViT-augreg models whose feature norms are already well-behaved, where normalization provides no benefit. Among non-Mahalanobis baselines, ViM (Wang et al., 2022) is the most competitive, achieving 41.9% average FPR, but Maha++ still wins by 7.0 points on average and is the best method for 4 of the top-5 models. KNN (Sun et al., 2022) and NNguide (Park et al., 2023a), both of which operate in normalized feature space, are consistently outperformed by Maha++ across all models. Relative Mahalanobis (Ren et al., 2021) at 41.8% FPR is surpassed by Maha++ by 6.9 points, while relative Maha++ (38.9%) improves over its unnormalized counterpart in 39/44 cases. Logit-based methods (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, GEN) and activation-shaping methods (AshS, fDBD) are substantially worse, with average FPRs ranging from 43.6% to 90.7%, confirming that feature-based Mahalanobis scoring with normalization is the strongest post-hoc approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mahalanobis distance OOD detection framework of Lee et al. (2018) and the relative Mahalanobis variant of Ren et al. (2021) by diagnosing why these methods fail for many models: strong feature norm variations violate the shared-covariance Gaussian assumption. Prior work on feature norms (Park et al. 2023, Regmi et al. 2024, Haas et al. 2024) and spherical embeddings (Ming et al. 2023, Sehwag et al. 2021) either required training modifications or used normalization only as a byproduct of contrastive objectives. The key departure here is showing that l2-normalization as a purely post-hoc, training-free step before Mahalanobis estimation resolves the issue across diverse architectures and pretraining schemes, without any architectural or training changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Mahalanobis distance is one of the most widely deployed post-hoc OOD detectors, and this paper provides a one-line fix (l2-normalize features) that consistently improves it across 44 models, resolving both the cross-model inconsistency and the brittleness on noise distributions. The diagnostic analysis—showing that feature norm correlates with the OOD score regardless of ID/OOD status—gives practitioners a clear understanding of when and why the method fails. A limitation is that the fix does not help for augreg-trained ViTs, whose feature norms are already well-behaved, and the paper does not explain why that specific training scheme induces such favorable geometry. Open questions include whether the normalization benefit persists in non-visual domains or with different feature extraction layers, and whether the augreg training insight can be generalized to other architectures.
