## Problem

When deploying a model across multiple data distributions (e.g., cameras at different angles, image classes, or question types), a limited annotation budget must be allocated strategically to guarantee worst-case performance rather than merely average performance. Existing active learning methods require a pre-existing unlabeled pool and optimize mean accuracy, while bandit-based robust learning methods assume oracle access to fresh distribution samples each round. This paper addresses the gap of adaptively collecting and annotating data from multiple sources under a fixed budget, starting from an empty training set, with provable minimax regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm starts with an empty training set and, over T rounds, adaptively selects one of K data sources to sample a fixed batch of M annotated instances from (e.g., M images from a specific camera, M images containing a specific object class, or M question-answer pairs of a given type). The collected samples are accumulated into a growing training set that is reused across rounds for gradient estimation. The output is the sequence of model parameters θ_t, with the time-averaged parameter θ̄ serving as the final model; the objective is to minimize the maximum expected loss across all K sources rather than their weighted average.

## Method

The proposed UCB-OGD algorithm alternates between a UCB-based data source selection step and an Online Gradient Descent update step. In the selection step, each source k is assigned a confidence radius r_k(S) = C√(α ln t / (2|S_k|)) that shrinks as more samples are collected from that source; the algorithm selects the source maximizing the sum of its empirical loss estimate and confidence radius, balancing exploitation of high-loss sources with exploration of under-sampled ones. In the update step, the model parameters are updated via OGD using the empirical gradient computed from a random batch drawn from the accumulated samples of the selected source, with learning rate η_t = 1/(2L√t). The key insight is that previously collected samples are reused for loss estimation at new parameter values, providing information about the current context without requiring fresh oracle samples—this bypasses the limitations of standard contextual bandit approaches that rely on similarity structure in the context space. The paper proves a minimax regret bound of O(T^(1/2)(K ln T)^(1/2)), matching a lower bound up to a ln T factor, and extends the analysis to non-convex losses via time-smoothed OGD yielding convergence to Pareto-stationary points.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10k budget), UCB-OGD achieves 52.3% minimum class accuracy and 66.5% mean accuracy, matching DBAL (52.7% min) and outperforming all other AL baselines on the worst-case metric. On PASCAL VOC2012 (3k budget), UCB-OGD reaches 44.7% min mAP@50 and 53.0% mean mAP@50, surpassing MDN (42.2/47.2) and Rand-OGD (40.6/51.3). On the real-world COSMOS testbed (2.5k images), UCB-OGD achieves 61.7% min AP@50 vs. 57.0% for Rand-OGD, and reaches the same 57.0% AP@50 milestone with only 2,160 samples (13% budget savings). The theoretical minimax regret bound of O(√T·√(K ln T)) is shown to be optimal up to a ln T factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pool-based AL baselines from Munjal et al. (CVPR 2022), UCB-OGD achieves the second-best minimum accuracy on CIFAR-10 (52.3% vs. DBAL's 52.7%), while UC (49.3%), BALD (45.0%), and EN (53.0% min but 68.1% mean) are all outperformed on the worst-case metric; however, UC and DBAL achieve higher mean accuracy (68.7% and 68.6% respectively) than UCB-OGD (66.5%), indicating a trade-off between worst-case and average performance. Against BADGE (Ash et al., ICLR 2020), UCB-OGD dominates on both metrics: 52.3% vs. 40.0% min accuracy and 66.5% vs. 61.0% mean accuracy on CIFAR-10. Against MDN (Choi et al., ICCV 2021) on VOC2012 object detection, UCB-OGD wins on both min mAP@50 (44.7% vs. 42.2%) and mean mAP@50 (53.0% vs. 47.2%), and the paper shows MDN requires 40% of the budget as an initial labeled pool to match UCB-OGD, whereas UCB-OGD starts from scratch. Across all tasks including the real-world COSMOS testbed, UCB-OGD consistently outperforms the Rand-OGD baseline by 4.7–16 percentage points on the minimum metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-studied areas—multi-armed bandit exploration (UCB, ε-greedy from Auer et al., 2002) and online gradient descent (Hazan, 2016)—into a unified adaptive data collection framework for minimax robust learning. It departs from pool-based active learning by requiring no pre-existing unlabeled pool, and from oracle-based bandit approaches in group-DRO (Haghtalab et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023) by reusing collected samples rather than drawing fresh i.i.d. samples each round. The key conceptual departure is treating data source selection as a contextual bandit problem where the 'context' is the current model parameter, enabling the algorithm to evaluate losses over already-collected data without pulling new arms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax regret guarantee (O(√T·√(K ln T))) for adaptive data collection under a fixed annotation budget, with a matching lower bound up to a ln T factor, giving practitioners a principled alternative to heuristic active learning for expensive annotation tasks like object detection. However, the main theoretical results require convex loss, and the non-convex case only yields convergence to a Pareto-stationary point—a significant gap for practical DNN training. Open questions include tightening the ln T gap, incorporating Bayesian priors for more efficient exploration, and validating the framework in dynamic, non-stationary environments such as evolving traffic patterns.
