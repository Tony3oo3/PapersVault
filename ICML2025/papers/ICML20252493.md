## Problem

Existing multi-objective alignment methods (MORLHF, MaxMin-RLHF) require training multiple reward models and running reinforcement learning from scratch to balance competing human preferences, making them computationally expensive and training-unstable. The paper asks whether the optimal multi-objective policy can be obtained as a simple post-processing step over already-trained single-objective policies, bypassing reinforcement learning entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252493/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K pre-trained single-objective policies π_k(y|x) (each obtained via standard RLHF or DPO on a separate preference dataset) and a text prompt x as input. It produces a unified policy π*(y|x) expressed as a normalized geometric mean of the input policies, π*(y|x) ∝ ∏_k π_k(y|x)^{λ_k}, where the weights λ are either learned via optimization (max-min setting) or user-specified (MORLHF setting). The output is a text response sampled from this mixed policy, and the entire computation operates on log-probabilities of existing models rather than raw parameters, requiring no gradient updates to the language model itself.

## Method

MPO establishes a closed-form equivalence between reward aggregation and policy aggregation: the optimal max-min policy is a geometric mean of single-objective policies with weights λ* that minimize the expected log-partition function over the prompt distribution. A normalization operator P_π_ref (subtracting β·log E_π_ref[exp(r/β)]) projects heterogeneous reward functions onto a shared scale, ensuring no single objective dominates. To solve for λ* under the max-min setting, the paper introduces Batch Stochastic Mirror Descent (BSMD), which samples prompts and responses from the reference model, computes gradient estimates of the log-partition function via automatic differentiation, and updates λ via a mirror-descent step that naturally enforces the simplex constraint without costly projections. Convergence is guaranteed at rate O(1/√T) under standard bounded-gradient assumptions, with a KL-divergence error bound on the resulting policy under a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition. For the MORLHF variant with a predefined preference vector λ, the solution is immediate—no optimization is needed—and the method reduces to a single geometric-mean combination of policies.

## Results

On the Helpful Assistant task (HH-RLHF, Qwen 2.5-7B), MPO achieves the highest minimum win rate across all three objectives: 46.3% under β=0.1 and 53.1% under β=0.5, outperforming MaxMin-RLHF (44.6% and 46.1%) and Reward Soups (44.8% and 50.0%). In the exploratory sentiment-conciseness experiment (LLaMA 3.2-3B, IMDb), MPO with learned weight λ₁=0.386 achieves the best max-min objective value compared to grid-searched weights and single-reward RLHF. Computationally, MPO requires only ~2.5 A100 GPU hours (for weight optimization via BSMD) versus ~10 A100 GPU hours for RL-based baselines (PPO), a roughly 4× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPO is compared against two baselines: Reward Soups (Ramé et al., 2023), which linearly interpolates single-objective policies with uniform weights λ=[1/3,1/3,1/3], and MaxMin-RLHF (Chakraborty et al., 2024), which trains a policy via PPO to optimize the worst-case reward. On the min win rate metric (the natural objective for max-min alignment), MPO outperforms Reward Soups by 1.5 pp (β=0.1: 46.3 vs 44.8) and 3.1 pp (β=0.5: 53.1 vs 50.0), and outperforms MaxMin-RLHF by 1.7 pp (β=0.1: 46.3 vs 44.6) and 7.0 pp (β=0.5: 53.1 vs 46.1). The advantage over MaxMin-RLHF is particularly pronounced under the looser KL constraint (β=0.5), where MPO's adaptive weight learning (λ₃≈0, effectively dropping the humorous objective) yields a more balanced policy. MPO achieves these gains while requiring only ~2.5 A100 GPU hours for the BSMD weight optimization step, compared to ~10 A100 GPU hours for the PPO-based RL training used by both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPO builds on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) for its normalization operator and the closed-form policy–reward relationship, and on the conditional stochastic optimization literature (Hu et al., 2020b) for the Batch Stochastic Mirror Descent algorithm used to optimize preference weights. Conceptually, it departs from both MORLHF (linear scalarization + PPO) and MaxMin-RLHF (min-aggregation + PPO) by showing that the optimal multi-objective policy is a geometric mean of single-objective policies, reducing the problem to a lightweight weight-optimization step rather than full reinforcement learning. The MORLHF special case (Lemma 3.9) recovers the decoding-time alignment result of Shi et al. (2024), positioning MPO as a generalization that also handles the max-min setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the RL stage entirely, MPO offers a ~4× compute reduction (2.5 vs. 10 A100 GPU-hours) while matching or exceeding MaxMin-RLHF on min-win-rate metrics, making multi-objective alignment substantially more accessible. Key limitations include increased memory for storing multiple policies, the need to re-optimize λ when adding new objectives, and reliance on GPT-based evaluation that is sensitive to prompt design. An important open direction is extending MPO to unobserved or continuous preference distributions rather than a fixed set of labeled preference subsets, which would improve generalizability to real-world heterogeneous user populations.
