## Problem

Existing offline model-based RL methods fail on sparse-reward, long-horizon goal-reaching tasks (e.g., AntMaze) because they synthesize transitions in raw state space, where Euclidean proximity does not reflect true temporal distance through obstacles. This leads to over-generalization in out-of-support regions and an inability to learn the long-horizon structure needed to navigate toward distant goals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline trajectory dataset of (s, a, s') tuples along with goal state labels assigned via hindsight experience replay (20% random state, 50% future state, 30% fully random). For pixel-based tasks, 64×64×3 RGB frames are first compressed through a frozen IMPALA CNN to 512-d features before entering the autoencoder. The output is a skill-conditioned goal-reaching policy π(a|s,w) that, at test time, adapts its latent skill variable w zero-shot toward the target goal's latent direction, plus a set of augmented (s, â, ŝ') transitions decoded from latent rollouts.

## Method

TempDATA learns a geometric autoencoder whose encoder f maps states into a latent space Z preserving temporal distance at two scales: a trajectory-level (macroscopic) constraint via expectile-regression Bellman optimality that encodes shortest-path cost to a goal, and a transition-level (microscopic) constraint bounding the latent distance between consecutive states by a moving cost d₀. A Gaussian latent dynamics model ζ(z'|z,a) is then trained in this representation space via negative log-likelihood, enabling rollouts that respect the learned temporal geometry and avoid generating transitions through walls or obstacles. Augmented transitions are decoded back to state space and mixed with the original dataset at a fixed ratio σ to train an offline policy (IQL + AWR) using an intrinsic reward defined as the reduction in latent temporal distance to the goal. The entire pipeline—autoencoder pre-training, latent dynamics training, and iterative policy extraction with periodic latent rollouts—runs without ensemble models, reducing wall-clock time while outperforming prior MBRL baselines by ~200 points on AntMaze.

## Results

On D4RL AntMaze (8 tasks), TempDATA achieves a total success rate of 520.2 (including ultra) and 431.7 (excluding ultra), outperforming the best MBRL baseline ROMI (272.6) by ~200 points and the best TARL baseline GTA (376.5) by ~55 points. On multi-goal FrankaKitchen and CALVIN, TempDATA scores 185.7 total, surpassing the strongest GCRL baseline HIQL (176.5). On pixel-based Visual FrankaKitchen, TempDATA reaches 114.6, matching GC-IQL (116.5) while RepB-SDE fails entirely (0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TempDATA wins on all 8 AntMaze tasks against every MBRL baseline. The closest MBRL competitor, ROMI (272.6 total), is outperformed by ~200 points; MOPO and RepB-SDE achieve 0.0 across all AntMaze variants. Against TARL baselines, TempDATA surpasses GTA (376.5) by ~55 points and S4RL (350.5) by ~81 points on the 6-task total. On multi-goal FrankaKitchen and CALVIN, TempDATA (185.7) edges out HIQL (176.5), winning on kitchen-partial (70.0 vs 65.0) and CALVIN (50.4 vs 43.8) but slightly trailing on kitchen-mixed (65.3 vs 67.7). All other GCRL baselines (GCSL 102.5, GC-CQL 52.8, GC-IQL 98.3, GC-POR 58.7) are substantially outperformed. On pixel-based Visual Kitchen, TempDATA (114.6) matches GC-IQL (116.5) while RepB-SDE fails completely (0.0), demonstrating that latent-space augmentation overcomes the high-dimensional input challenge that defeats state-space MBRL.

## Relation to Prior Work

TempDATA sits at the intersection of three research threads: offline MBRL (MOPO, COMBO, RAMBO, ROMI), goal-conditioned RL (GC-IQL, HIQL, GCSL), and temporal-distance representation learning (quasimetric learning, bisimulation). The key departure from prior offline MBRL is that transition augmentation is performed in a learned latent space that encodes temporal distance rather than in raw state space, which the authors argue prevents overgeneralization in out-of-support regions. The autoencoder's Bellman-based temporal distance objective draws on value-metric equivalence (Wang & Isola, 2022; Wang et al., 2023) and expectile regression from IQL, while the policy extraction reuses HIQL's skill-conditioned architecture with test-time skill adaptation. Unlike diffusion-based trajectory augmentation (S4RL, SynthER, GTA), TempDATA generates single-step transitions via a Gaussian latent dynamics model, avoiding the computational cost of iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first offline MBRL method to achieve competitive performance on sparse-reward, long-horizon goal-reaching benchmarks (AntMaze, Kitchen, CALVIN) and pixel-based variants, a setting where prior MBRL methods largely fail. The practical implication is that model-based data augmentation becomes viable for tasks previously reserved for model-free or diffusion-based approaches, with lower training cost (no ensembles, no iterative sampling). Open questions the authors flag include: how far rollouts can venture beyond empirical support, whether symmetric temporal metrics suffice for temporally asymmetric tasks, extension to multi-agent and partially observable settings, and whether strictly offline pre-training is optimal. The method's modularity (compatible with any offline RL algorithm) suggests broad applicability, though validation beyond the tested benchmarks remains to be seen.
