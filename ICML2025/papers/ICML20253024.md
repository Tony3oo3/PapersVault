## Problem

Existing locate-then-edit knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, PMET) perform well on single-hop fact recall but consistently fail on multi-hop factual recall involving newly edited knowledge. The paper identifies the root cause through mechanistic interpretability: single-hop prompts only update shallow MLP layers, while multi-hop reasoning retrieves the same fact from deeper MLP layers via an implicit subject mechanism, leaving those deeper layers unedited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an edit instance as a triplet (s, r, o→o*), a single-hop edit prompt (e.g., 'The capital of Spain is'), and a constructed multi-hop edit prompt formed by prepending a preceding fact (s', r', s) sourced from WikiData SPARQL queries or the model itself. It produces modified LLM weights where both shallow MLP layers (e.g., layers 3–8 in GPT-J) and deeper MLP layers (e.g., layers 16–20) are updated, yielding a model that correctly answers both single-hop and multi-hop queries involving the edited fact.

## Method

IFMET is a two-stage locate-then-edit framework grounded in mechanistic interpretability findings. Using LogitLens and causal intervention experiments, the paper shows that in multi-hop tasks the implicit subject accumulates in middle layers and causally drives the final answer through deeper MLP layers, whereas single-hop tasks retrieve knowledge from shallow MLPs at the subject token position. Stage 1 applies the standard single-hop edit prompt to shallow MLP layers using PMET's key-value memory optimization (closed-form weight update via Δ = R K_E^T (C_0 + K_E K_E^T)^{-1}). Stage 2 (furtherance edit) applies the constructed multi-hop edit prompt to deeper MLP layers at the last token position, targeting the layers where implicit-subject-driven retrieval occurs. The multi-hop prompt is built by finding a valid preceding triple (s', r', s) from WikiData or the model itself, forming a two-hop chain C = (s', r', s) ⊕ (s, r, o→o*), ensuring the editing mechanism matches the retrieval mechanism used during multi-hop inference.

## Results

On MQuAKE-3K (few-shot setting with CoT), IFMET achieves 31.01% average multi-hop accuracy, nearly doubling PMET's 17.04% and outperforming all other baselines (ROME 18.1%, MEMIT 12.3%, MEND 11.5%). The improvement is most pronounced in complex reasoning scenarios: for 3-edit instances, IFMET reaches 24.30% vs. PMET's 11.19%, and for 3-hop questions, IFMET achieves 23.58% vs. PMET's 12.76%. In the Post-type multi-hop tasks (where the edited fact is an implicit reasoning step), IFMET+CoT improves from PMET's 12.63% to 28.90%, while Pre-type performance remains comparable (43.66% for both).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFMET outperforms all four compared baselines across every subset of MQuAKE-3K. Against PMET (the strongest baseline and the method IFMET builds upon), IFMET improves average multi-hop accuracy from 17.04% to 31.01% (+14 points, ~82% relative gain), with the largest gains in the Post-type implicit multi-hop scenarios (12.63% → 28.90%). ROME (18.1%) and MEMIT (12.3%) perform comparably to PMET on average but degrade sharply on 3+ edit or 3+ hop instances, confirming that single-stage shallow-layer editing is insufficient for complex multi-hop recall. MEND (11.5%), which uses meta-learning rather than locate-then-edit, is the weakest baseline, suggesting that gradient-based weight updates without targeted layer identification are particularly poorly suited to multi-hop factual recall. Notably, IFMET's 31.01% still falls short of the unedited Base model's 42.83% on the unedited answer, indicating that knowledge editing inherently introduces some degradation even with the two-stage approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the locate-then-edit paradigm pioneered by ROME and MEMIT, which identified factual knowledge storage in shallow MLP layers for single-hop recall, and builds directly on PMET as its editing backbone. The key departure is the use of mechanistic interpretability tools (LogitLens, causal intervention) to reveal that multi-hop reasoning retrieves knowledge from a fundamentally different set of components—deeper MLP layers activated by implicit subject information at the last token position—rather than the shallow layers targeted by all prior single-hop editing methods. The method also draws on the key-value memory interpretation of FFNs (Geva et al., 2021) to formulate the weight-update objective, and on WikiData/SPARQL for constructing the multi-hop edit prompts that drive the second editing stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for a well-known failure mode of locate-then-edit methods on multi-hop queries, moving the field beyond empirical observation to a causal account of why shallow-layer-only editing is insufficient. The two-stage IFMET approach is practical (≈2.5× time overhead over PMET) and generalizes across architectures (GPT-J-6B, LLaMA-2-7B) and edit batch sizes. However, absolute multi-hop accuracy remains low (~31% on MQuAKE-3K), specificity drops noticeably, and the method has only been validated on 2–4 hop chains with relatively simple relation types, leaving open whether the shallow/deep layer separation generalizes to longer or more compositional reasoning chains.
