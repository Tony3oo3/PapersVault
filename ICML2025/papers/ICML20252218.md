## Problem

Foundation models like CLIP and SAM are brittle to diverse real-world viewing conditions (unusual viewpoints, lighting shifts, rotations) due to photographer's bias in internet training data. Existing robustness approaches—data augmentation and equivariant architectures—hard-wire invariances at training time, requiring prior knowledge of relevant transformations and failing under distribution shifts. The paper asks whether invariance can instead be achieved as a test-time optimization problem using the visual priors already embedded in foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or a 3D scene) that may be transformed by rotations, color/contrast shifts, viewpoint changes, or day-night relighting, and produces a canonicalized (visually-typical) version of that image. For 3D viewpoint canonicalization, TRELLIS generates 60 candidate renders on a spherical grid, and the selected canonical view is passed to OV-Seg (a CLIP variant for background-removed images) for classification. For segmentation, the canonicalized image is fed to SAM with a box prompt, and the output mask is inverse-transformed back to the original orientation. In the active vision setting, the input is a 6-DoF camera pose in a Gaussian splatting scene, and the output is an optimized pose that minimizes FOCAL energy.

## Method

FOCAL implements a 'vary and rank' strategy: it first generates candidate transforms of the input (parametric for 2D rotations/color, generative via TRELLIS for 3D viewpoints, or latent-space interpolation for day-night), then ranks them by minimizing a combined energy function E_FOCAL = γ₁·E_CLIP + γ₂·E_diff. The CLIP energy uses a weighted combination of mean and max class logits as a semantic prior, while the diffusion energy (5–10 denoising steps of SD-2-base) acts as a general appearance-based prior favoring in-distribution images. For continuous or high-dimensional transform spaces (color, 6-DoF camera poses), Bayesian Optimization with a Gaussian Process and Expected Improvement acquisition function efficiently finds the energy minimum in 50–100 evaluations. The entire pipeline is training-free and architecture-agnostic: the canonicalized image is simply passed to any downstream classifier or segmenter, and invariance emerges as a property of the test-time optimization rather than from hard-wired constraints.

## Results

On 3D viewpoint canonicalization, FOCAL improves worst-viewpoint accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS from 12.0% to 62.0% and boosts CO3D accuracy by 43.8% (threshold 0.3) and 26.8% (threshold 0.5) over finetuned CLIP (OV-Seg). On 2D rotations, FOCAL matches or exceeds PRLC's trained canonicalizers across CIFAR-10/100 and STL-10 with both ResNet-50 and ViT, and generalizes to ImageNet with up to +11.4% rotated accuracy over PRLC. For lighting transformations, FOCAL improves CLIP accuracy by 9.9% on color shifts and 4.1% on contrast shifts, with gains reaching 15% and 12% for extreme transformations. On COCO segmentation with SAM, FOCAL matches PRLC's 65.9 mAP while achieving +2.1% better pose accuracy, all without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PRLC (Mondal et al., 2023), FOCAL matches or outperforms on every 2D rotation setting: +0.4–2.4% upright accuracy and +0.4–2.2% rotated accuracy across CIFAR-10/100 and STL-10 with both ResNet-50 and ViT, all zero-shot versus PRLC's trained canonicalizers. On ImageNet, where PRLC's small-dataset-trained canonicalizers degrade severely (−16.7% upright accuracy for ResNet-50), FOCAL achieves +11.4% rotated accuracy for ViT (71.2% vs. 60.5%) and +4.3% for CLIP. On COCO segmentation with SAM, FOCAL ties PRLC at 65.9 mAP while gaining +2.1% pose accuracy (88.9% vs. 86.8%). Against OV-Seg (finetuned CLIP) on 3D viewpoints, FOCAL dramatically outperforms: 49.5% vs. 5.71% at threshold 0.3 and 55.3% vs. 28.5% at threshold 0.5 on CO3D. Against TRELLIS as a canonicalizer, FOCAL gains +3.6% (t=0.3) and +1.9% (t=0.5) on CO3D, though TRELLIS itself provides surprisingly strong baseline performance. On the RCC color-correction task, FOCAL loses to Barron & Tsai (6.4° vs. 1.3° median angular error), as FOCAL targets in-distribution typicality rather than exact color neutrality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCAL builds directly on the learned canonicalization framework of Kaba et al. (2022), which formalized invariance as energy minimization over transformations, and on the energy-based model perspectives of Grathwohl et al. (2020) (classifiers as EBMs) and Graikos et al. (2022) (diffusion models as image priors). The key departure from prior canonicalization work (Kaba et al., PRLC/Mondal et al.) is that FOCAL replaces the trained, dataset-specific canonicalizer with pre-trained foundation models (CLIP + Stable Diffusion) used as energy functions at test time, eliminating the need for any training or architectural modification. It also extends the scope from the 2D rotations studied in prior work to 3D viewpoint shifts, lighting/color changes, day-night transitions, and 6-DoF active vision, using Bayesian Optimization to handle continuous and high-dimensional transformation spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing invariance as a test-time optimization problem grounded in internet-scale visual priors, FOCAL offers a plug-and-play robustness mechanism that avoids the rigidity of training-time data augmentation and the architectural constraints of equivariant networks. Two main limitations remain: the computational cost scales linearly with the number of candidate transforms (up to 56× for 2D rotation, 13.3s per image for 3D), and the user must still specify which transformation generator to apply. Open questions include automatic transformation selection at test time, gradient-based optimization for higher-dimensional spaces, and a System-1/2 scheme that invokes canonicalization only when needed to reduce overhead.
