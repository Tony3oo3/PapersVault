## Problem

Offline RL agents suffer from distributional shift and value overestimation due to limited static datasets, while existing video-enhanced RL methods either require action/reward annotations on auxiliary videos, operate in raw action space, or fail to directly transfer behavioral knowledge for sequential decision-making. The paper asks whether unlabeled, out-of-domain internet videos can provide meaningful high-level control guidance to an offline RL agent without explicit action labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB frames from an offline trajectory dataset (with actions and rewards) plus unlabeled video clips from a source domain (e.g., BridgeData-V2 robot videos, NuScenes driving scenes, or Minecraft gameplay). It outputs continuous or discrete control actions and value estimates, with the policy and value networks additionally conditioned on a discrete latent behavior token (one of K=50 codes) that encodes a high-level behavioral abstraction. The two-stream world model simultaneously generates short-horizon real-action rollouts and long-horizon latent-behavior rollouts from the same initial state.

## Method

VeoRL introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Behavior Abstraction Network (BAN) applies VQ-VAE-style vector quantization over concatenated CNN encodings of consecutive video frames to learn a discrete latent action space of K behavior codes, trained jointly with a video prediction loss and an MMD domain-alignment term; (2) a two-stream world model comprises a trunk net (predicting next state from real actions, trained only on the target offline dataset) and a plan net (predicting long-horizon state transitions from latent behaviors, trained jointly on target and source data using only 'behavior-change' trajectory segments where the latent code switches); (3) model-based actor-critic learning conditions both the policy π(a|s,ā) and value v(s,ā) on the latent behavior, and introduces a goal-conditioned intrinsic reward r̄ = −‖s_t − s̄_t‖₂ that penalizes divergence between trunk-net and plan-net state rollouts, thereby anchoring the value network to video-derived behavioral priors and mitigating overestimation bias.

## Results

On Meta-World, VeoRL achieves an average success rate of 0.53 across six manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming DreamerV2 (0.32), VIP (0.27), DrQ+CQL (0.14), APV (0.04), and LOMPO (0.00). On CARLA autonomous driving, VeoRL outperforms DreamerV2 by up to 350% in episode return with the smallest standard deviation. On MineDojo, VeoRL improves success rates by 250% (Harvest log in plains: 0.10→0.35), ~130% (Harvest water with bucket: 0.36→0.83), and ~100% (Harvest sand: 0.27→0.55) over DreamerV2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeoRL consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks. Against DreamerV2 (the primary model-based baseline), VeoRL gains 66% in average Meta-World success rate (0.53 vs 0.32), up to 350% in CARLA episode return, and 100–250% in MineDojo success rates. Against the video-enhanced baselines APV and VIP, VeoRL's advantage is most pronounced: APV achieves only 0.04 average success rate on Meta-World and ~−500 episode return on CARLA, while VIP reaches 0.27 on Meta-World but still trails VeoRL by 26 points. Against the offline-specific baselines, DrQ+CQL (model-free) scores 0.14 average success rate on Meta-World, and LOMPO (model-based) fails entirely (0.00 on all six tasks). On MineDojo, VeoRL also surpasses VPT (0.55 vs 0.20 on Harvest sand), which relies on inverse-dynamics pseudo-labeling and requires domain consistency. Finally, VeoRL outperforms DreamerV3 (a stronger backbone) on Meta-World, confirming that the video-enhancement contribution is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

VeoRL builds directly on the DreamerV2 model-based RL framework (RSSM-based world model with actor-critic policy learning) and extends it with a two-stream architecture: a trunk net for real-action transitions and a plan net for latent-behavior transitions. The latent behavior abstraction draws on VQ-VAE-style vector quantization to create a discrete, domain-agnostic action space, departing from prior video-enhanced RL methods (APV, VIP) that perform action-free representation pretraining, and from VPT which requires inverse dynamics models assuming source-target domain consistency. The intrinsic reward mechanism that aligns trunk-net rollouts with plan-net predictions is a novel loss design not present in prior offline RL methods (CQL, LOMPO) or video-pretraining approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that truly unlabeled, out-of-domain video data can be converted into actionable policy guidance for offline RL without any action annotations, addressing a practical bottleneck in deploying RL to new domains. The consistent >100% gains across three structurally different environments (robotic manipulation, autonomous driving, open-world gaming) suggest the latent behavior abstraction captures transferable physical-world knowledge rather than domain-specific artifacts. Key limitations include the computational overhead of the dual-branch world model and the reliance on MMD for domain alignment, which may not scale to highly heterogeneous video sources. Open questions include whether the learned latent behaviors remain semantically meaningful at larger codebook sizes or in continuous (non-discrete) formulations, and whether the approach extends to multi-agent or language-conditioned settings.
