## Problem

Existing knowledge editing benchmarks are small-scale (~20K samples), synthetic, and predate the knowledge cutoffs of modern LLMs, leaving it unclear whether specialized editing techniques can handle the large volumes of real-world factual updates needed for practical deployment. This paper asks whether lifelong knowledge editing methods can actually incorporate hundreds of thousands of sequential real-world edits while maintaining prior knowledge, generalization beyond memorization, and locality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252355/cropped_page5_idx11.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes periodic Wikidata knowledge graph snapshots (subject-relation-object triplets) and produces a lifelong benchmark of 502,382 question-answer pairs across eight bi-monthly timesteps (February–July 2024), spanning ~6.9M tokens. Each timestep includes five evaluation subsets: the update set, rephrased variants, persona-styled variants (detective, pirate, philosopher, etc.), two-hop reasoning quintuples, and locality probes with semantically similar but distinct objects. Evaluation yields accuracy scores across all five axes per method per timestep, enabling tracking of retention, generalization, and forgetting over the full sequential update stream.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated seven-step extraction pipeline that compares consecutive Wikidata snapshots to identify changed triplets, filters for quality (removing ~76% of initial changes via circularity, character, length, and ambiguity checks), derives locality probes and multi-hop reasoning tuples, and uses GPT-4o-mini/GPT-3.5 with few-shot prompting to generate diverse question-answer pairs with post-hoc verification. The empirical study evaluates four knowledge editing methods (ROME, R-ROME, MEMIT, WISE) against retrieval-augmented generation (top-2 retrieval with all-mpnet-base-v2 embeddings via Annoy) and continual LoRA finetuning with interpolation-based model merging (α=0.25) across five 7B LLMs. The key finding is that local weight-modification methods collapse within hundreds of edits, WISE converges to pre-update accuracy within 10K edits, while RAG nearly triples update accuracy at 2× inference cost and LoRA+merging outperforms all specialized editing methods at equivalent inference cost after ~100K edits.

## Results

On WikiBigEdit (502K QA pairs across 8 timesteps), RAG achieves near-perfect update accuracy (96–99% across models) and strong generalization (85–86% rephrase, 70–75% personas), but multi-hop reasoning remains near pre-update levels (~50%). LoRA-Merge (continual finetuning with interpolation-based model merging) achieves 60–67% update accuracy and 53–58% locality, consistently outperforming all specialized knowledge editing methods (ROME, R-ROME, MEMIT, WISE) which collapse or converge to pre-update accuracy within 10K edits. Pre-update LLMs solve only ~41–50% of the 500K real-world factual updates, with 82% remaining unsolved across all five tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four specialized knowledge editing methods lose decisively to both RAG and LoRA-Merge at scale. ROME and R-ROME (local weight modification) collapse within the first few hundred sequential edits, with accuracy dropping to near-zero on update accuracy. MEMIT, designed for mass editing, shows similar rapid degradation and model collapse under 10K+ sequential updates. WISE, which stores adapted weight matrices for retrieval during inference to avoid direct model modification, avoids catastrophic failure but steadily declines and converges back to pre-update accuracy within 10K edits. In contrast, RAG nearly triples update accuracy (e.g., 96.17% vs. ~50% pre-update for Llama-2-7b) and achieves 84.92% rephrase and 72.62% persona accuracy, though at roughly 2× inference cost. LoRA-Merge, at equivalent inference cost to the base model, achieves 67.36% update and 58.40% locality for Llama-2-7b, outperforming all knowledge editing methods on every evaluation axis after ~100K edits while showing minimal catastrophic forgetting and even positive backward transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wikidata-snapshot-based knowledge editing benchmarks of TemporalWiki (Jang et al., 2022) and WikiFactDiff (Khodja et al., 2024) by scaling from ~20K to 500K+ question-answer pairs, adding a fully automated extraction pipeline for continuous benchmark expansion, and introducing multi-hop reasoning and persona-based generalization evaluation axes absent in prior work. On the method side, it positions itself against the established knowledge editing lineage (ROME, MEMIT, WISE, R-ROME) but deliberately contrasts these specialized approaches with generic modification techniques—retrieval-augmented generation and LoRA-based continual finetuning with interpolation-based model merging—that have not previously been evaluated as baselines in the knowledge editing literature at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that purpose-built knowledge editing methods collapse within hundreds of sequential edits while simple RAG or LoRA+merging outperform them across all standard editing evaluation axes fundamentally challenges the field's investment in specialized editing architectures. The WikiBigEdit benchmark itself (500K+ pairs, automated pipeline, multi-hop and locality evaluation) provides the first practically-scaled testbed for lifelong editing, making it a likely reference standard for future work. Open questions include whether more sophisticated RAG (e.g., HippoRAG) can close the multi-hop reasoning gap, whether model merging can be further tuned to eliminate residual forgetting, and whether any editing method can match RAG's near-perfect retention without the 2× inference cost.
