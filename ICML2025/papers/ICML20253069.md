## Problem

Mixture of Experts (MoE) models are highly susceptible to adversarial attacks, yet existing robustness methods either treat the MoE as a monolithic network without analyzing component-level vulnerabilities, or they apply standard adversarial training that significantly degrades clean-data accuracy. This paper asks how to robustify MoEs in a targeted, efficient manner while preserving high standard accuracy, and how to formally bound the achievable robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253069/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 TinyImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet) and outputs a class label via a weighted sum of expert predictions. The MoE layer replaces the final classification head, using a fully connected top-1 (or top-2) router and E=4 expert networks (ResNet18 or ViT-small). In the dual-model setting, the final prediction is a convex combination (1−α)·F_S(x) + α·F_R(x) of a standard and a robust MoE, with α ∈ [0.5, 1] controlling the robustness–accuracy trade-off.

## Method

The paper first identifies that expert networks (not the router) are the primary vulnerability in MoEs, then proposes RT-ER, a robust training loss that augments standard adversarial training with a KL-divergence term forcing the second-top expert (the expert with the second-largest router weight) to produce consistent outputs on clean and adversarial inputs. Building on this, a dual-model linearly combines a standard MoE and an RT-ER-trained robust MoE, and the authors derive certified ℓ_p-robustness bounds for both the single MoE and the dual-model under Lipschitz continuity assumptions on experts and router. To further close the accuracy–robustness gap, JTDMoE introduces a bi-level alternating training scheme: the lower level updates the robust MoE with the RT-ER loss, while the upper level updates both MoEs to minimize cross-entropy on the combined dual-model output, thereby enlarging the robust MoE's classification margin and simultaneously improving both robust and standard accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet18 experts, PGD ε=8/255), RT-ER achieves 69.09% robust accuracy (RA) versus 53.74% for standard adversarial training (AT), while retaining 77.81% standard accuracy (SA). The joint-trained dual-model (JTDMoE) at α=0.7 further improves to 92.29% SA and 74.62% RA, surpassing both the standard MoE (92.14% SA, 52.54% RA) and AT (79.08% SA, 53.74% RA) on both metrics simultaneously. On TinyImageNet (ViT-small experts, PGD ε=2/255), JTDMoE reaches 84.91% SA and 57.39% RA. On ImageNet (ViT experts), RT-ER achieves 68.38% SA and 56.16% RA, outperforming AT (60.32%/44.64%) and TRADES (61.94%/45.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard adversarial training (AT, Madry et al. 2017), RT-ER improves RA by 15.35 percentage points on CIFAR-10 (69.09% vs. 53.74%) and by 5.85 points on TinyImageNet (56.79% vs. 50.94%), while incurring only a ~1.3% SA drop. JTDMoE further surpasses AT on both SA and RA simultaneously (92.29%/74.62% vs. 79.08%/53.74% on CIFAR-10). Compared to TRADES (Zhang et al. 2019), RT-ER achieves 16.55% higher RA on CIFAR-10 (69.09% vs. 52.54%) at the cost of 6% lower SA (77.81% vs. 83.81%); under AutoAttack the gap narrows to 15.46% RA improvement (54.36% vs. 38.90%). Against AdvMoE (Zhang et al. 2023), which is designed specifically for CNN-based MoE, RT-ER gains 10.42% RA on CIFAR-10 (69.09% vs. 58.67%) with comparable SA (77.81% vs. 76.83%). On ImageNet, RT-ER outperforms both AT and TRADES by approximately 12% in RA and 8% in SA, confirming scalability to larger models and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard adversarial training framework (Madry et al., 2017) to the Mixture-of-Experts setting by identifying a component-level vulnerability: expert networks are far more susceptible to adversarial perturbations than the router. It departs from prior MoE robustness work (Puigcerver et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023), which applied end-to-end adversarial training or imposed architectural restrictions, by introducing a targeted KL-divergence loss (RT-ER) that robustifies only the second-top expert. The dual-model strategy and its certified robustness bound are directly motivated by the adaptive smoothing framework of Bai et al. (2024a), which the authors adapt to the MoE context and augment with a bi-level joint training procedure (JTDMoE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures become the backbone of increasingly large language and vision models (GLaM, Switch Transformers), understanding and mitigating their adversarial vulnerabilities is practically critical. The tunable dual-model offers a deployment-friendly knob for the robustness-accuracy trade-off, and the component-level vulnerability analysis provides actionable guidance for MoE designers. However, the evaluation is primarily on small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10, TinyImageNet) with modest architectures (ResNet18, ViT-small); the ImageNet results in the appendix, while positive, use a single MoE layer. Open questions include whether the bi-level joint training scales to hundreds of experts in production MoE systems, whether the Lipschitz-based certified bounds remain informative at scale, and whether the approach transfers to NLP MoE where routing dynamics differ substantially.
