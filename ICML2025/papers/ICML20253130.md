## Problem

Existing automated jailbreaking methods (adversarial prompt search, fine-tuning, decoding parameter optimization) are computationally expensive, requiring many forward and backward passes that become prohibitive for large models (e.g., 70B+). More fundamentally, the paper identifies that current safety alignment is shallow—safe and unsafe models diverge primarily in their initial token distributions but converge as generation proceeds—yet no efficient inference-time attack exploits this structural weakness to jailbreak large models with minimal adversarial resources.

## Input / Output

The attack consumes a harmful text query and the next-token logits from three models simultaneously: a strong safe target (e.g., Llama2-70B), a weak safe reference (e.g., Llama2-7B), and a weak unsafe model (adversarially fine-tuned 7B or even a 1.3B pruned variant). The strong and weak models must share the same vocabulary, though token alignment via dynamic programming can bridge tokenizer mismatches. The output is a full harmful text generation from the strong model, requiring only one forward pass on the target per example, with the two small models adding roughly 20% (or as little as 3.7% with pruning) overhead.

## Method

The key insight comes from KL-divergence analysis showing that safe and unsafe models share over 50% of their top-10 tokens, with overlap increasing as the prefix lengthens—meaning safety alignment primarily gates the first few tokens. The attack modifies the strong model's decoding distribution via log-probability algebra: each token probability is multiplied by (unsafe_weak / safe_weak)^α, where α is an amplification factor (α=1 suffices; higher values increase harmfulness). This is the same mathematical structure as DExperts/contrastive decoding, but weaponized: the weak unsafe model (obtained by fine-tuning on ~100 adversarial examples) provides initial harmful guidance, while the mismatch ratio converges toward 1 over longer generations, allowing the strong model's own capabilities to produce more detailed and harmful content than the weak model alone. The approach requires no backward passes, no prompt optimization, and no modification of the target model's weights, achieving >99% attack success rates across five LLMs from three organizations.

## Results

On Llama2-13B-Chat, the weak-to-strong attack (α=1.5) achieves 99.4% ASR on AdvBench and 99.0% on MaliciousInstruct, with Harm Scores of 3.85 and 4.29 respectively. On Llama2-70B-Chat, it reaches 99.2% ASR on AdvBench and 100.0% on MaliciousInstruct, with GPT-4 harm scores of 4.07 and 4.22. The attack requires only one forward pass per example in the target model, and even using the unaligned base model as the weak unsafe model (Weak-to-Strong†) still yields 39.8% ASR on AdvBench-13B. Across five model families (Llama2, Vicuna, Baichuan2, InternLM), the attack consistently achieves >99% ASR on both primary benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The weak-to-strong attack decisively outperforms all six baselines on both ASR and harm scores. Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the gap is largest on Llama2-70B where GCG achieves only 56.2% ASR on AdvBench versus 99.2% for weak-to-strong. Prompt-based methods (Prefix Injection, SelfCipher, DeepInception) are particularly ineffective on the 70B model, with ASR dropping to 1–3%, while weak-to-strong maintains near-perfect rates. The closest competitor is Adversarial Decoding (Huang et al., 2023), which reaches 95.9% ASR (Best Top-K) on Llama2-13B AdvBench but produces substantially less harmful outputs (Harm Score 2.60 vs. 3.85). Compared to Adversarial Fine-tuning (Yang et al., 2023; Qi et al., 2023), which achieves 93.7% ASR on Llama2-13B AdvBench, weak-to-strong not only surpasses it in ASR (99.4%) but also generates more harmful content (Harm Score 3.85 vs. 3.73), demonstrating that inference-time logit manipulation can exceed the effectiveness of permanently modifying model weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the log probability algebra from DExperts (Liu et al., 2021) and contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2023b)—techniques previously used for controlled generation or alignment—into an adversarial attack vector. It departs from prior jailbreaking methods (GCG, adversarial fine-tuning, adversarial decoding) by requiring only one forward pass on the target model and no gradient optimization, instead leveraging the observation that safe and unsafe models' token distributions converge rapidly after initial tokens. The mechanistic analysis via KL divergence and top-K overlap rates provides a new diagnostic lens on why alignment fails, complementing prior work that identified failure modes (Wei et al., 2023a; Wolf et al., 2023) without explaining the temporal structure of the vulnerability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety alignment is concentrated in initial tokens and can be overridden by a 7B model's logit ratio during decoding of a 70B model exposes a fundamental fragility in current alignment paradigms, with >99% attack success rates across five models from three organizations. The proposed gradient ascent defense reduces ASR by only 5–10% for this specific attack, suggesting that deeper, more structural alignment mechanisms are needed. Key limitations include the white-box requirement, shared vocabulary constraint, and dependence on logit access, which restricts immediate applicability to closed-source APIs. Open questions include whether this vulnerability persists in newer alignment methods (e.g., DPO, constitutional AI) and whether the shallow-alignment pattern is an artifact of current RLHF pipelines or a more general property of instruction-tuned models.
