## Problem

Model merging enables training-free multi-target domain adaptation by averaging fine-tuned weights, but quantization—essential for deploying on resource-constrained devices—introduces discretization that misaligns weights and degrades merging quality. No prior work systematically analyzed how quantization noise affects the error barrier between merged models or proposed quantization strategies that preserve merge compatibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained source model (e.g., ResNet-101 for segmentation, ResNet-50 for classification), target-domain data for single-target adaptation, and the original source weights (needed for distance regularization). After domain adaptation, quantization is applied via 20,000 iterations of block-wise reconstruction with batch norm layers folded. The output is a set of quantized, merge-compatible weights per target domain; these are then combined via midpoint weight averaging (with noise-sampling-based rounding) into a single merged model that produces segmentation masks or class labels across all target domains simultaneously.

## Method

HDRQ extends the error barrier analysis to quantized weights, showing via second-order Taylor expansion that quantization noise inflates the barrier through Hessian-dependent terms at the merged point and through inter-model distance. To counter this, HDRQ introduces two regularizations during block-wise reconstruction: (1) noise-based Hessian regularization, where quantization noise ε ~ U[-Δ/2, Δ/2] is sampled and added to weights instead of using deterministic rounding, making the expected loss penalize sharp curvature (the first-order term vanishes since E[ε]=0); and (2) weight distance regularization, an L2 penalty between source and target weights that bounds inter-model distance via the triangle inequality. For the merging step itself, HDRQ resolves the rounding ambiguity (arising when step sizes are similar and the merged integer falls between two adjacent values) by sampling noise before averaging and selecting the sample with highest cosine similarity to the original interpolation vector.

## Results

On the semantic segmentation merging task (GTA→Cityscapes + GTA→IDD), HDRQ achieves 45.75 mIoU (harmonic mean) at W4A4, outperforming QDrop (41.54) by 4.21 mIoU and BRECQ (31.95) by 13.80 mIoU. At W6A6, the gap narrows to 1.19 mIoU over QDrop (53.70 vs. 52.51). On Office-Home multi-target classification, HDRQ consistently leads at low bit-widths: at W3A3, it achieves 64.70% harmonic mean accuracy on R→A,C,P versus 62.99% (QDrop) and 43.66% (BRECQ), with similar margins across all four domain configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDRQ is compared against two PTQ baselines: BRECQ (block-wise reconstruction) and QDrop (block-wise reconstruction + partial dropout). On semantic segmentation merging at W4A4, HDRQ (45.75 mIoU) beats QDrop (41.54) by 4.21 mIoU and BRECQ (31.95) by 13.80 mIoU; at W6A6 the advantage over QDrop shrinks to 1.19 mIoU. On Office-Home at W3A3, HDRQ outperforms QDrop by 1.71% (R→A,C,P), 1.07% (A→R,C,P), 0.62% (C→R,A,P), and 0.31% (P→R,A,C), while BRECQ collapses catastrophically (e.g., 43.66% vs. 64.70% on R→A,C,P). At higher bit-widths (W8A8), all three methods are within ~0.5% of each other, indicating HDRQ's advantage is most pronounced under aggressive quantization where merging degradation is most severe. The ablation confirms noise-based quantization contributes +1.22% and distance regularization adds +0.49% over the QDrop baseline at W3A3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of post-training quantization (PTQ) and training-free model merging for multi-target domain adaptation (MTDA), two threads that had been developed largely in isolation. It builds directly on Li et al. (2024) for the merging-based MTDA framework, on BRECQ and QDrop for the block-wise reconstruction and partial-dropout PTQ pipeline, and on noise-based quantization (NICE, Bit-shrinking) for the Hessian-regularizing mechanism. The key departure is extending the error-barrier analysis of Frankle et al. (2020) to explicitly incorporate quantization noise, thereby providing a theoretical justification for why standard PTQ degrades merging and how targeted regularization can restore merge-friendliness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap: deploying quantized models on resource-constrained devices while retaining the ability to merge domain-adapted variants, a scenario that is increasingly relevant for on-device adaptive AI. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CNN backbones (ResNet-50/101) and two task families (segmentation, classification), and the distance-regularization term requires access to source weights, which may not always be available. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to transformer architectures, scales to more than two target domains, and whether the noise-sampling rounding strategy remains effective under aggressive bit-widths (e.g., W2A2).
