## Problem

The Transducer architecture's input-synchronous decoding aligns each target token with a specific source token, making it effective for monotonic tasks like speech recognition but poorly suited to non-monotonic alignment tasks such as simultaneous translation where word reordering is common. Adding cross-attention to the predictor to access source history makes predictor states dependent on the specific READ/WRITE path, creating an exponentially large state space that breaks the forward-backward algorithm needed for end-to-end training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming speech audio as 80-dimensional log mel-filterbank coefficients (10ms frames, 25ms window) processed in chunks of 320–1280 ms, and produces either translated text (subword tokens from a 10k SentencePiece vocabulary) or translated speech (discrete units from mHuBERT + K-means quantization) in an autoregressive, token-by-token fashion. The streaming constraint means the model must emit target tokens before the full source utterance is received, with the chunk size directly controlling the latency–quality trade-off.

## Method

MonoAttn-Transducer augments the standard Transducer predictor with a monotonic cross-attention module that lets each predictor state attend to all previously received encoder states. The key training insight is to use the forward-backward algorithm to compute posterior alignment probabilities π(u,t) between predictor and encoder states, then estimate the expected context vector via a weighted soft attention over encoder states (Eq. 8–9), thereby avoiding enumeration of the exponential READ/WRITE path space. A diagonal prior alignment (Eq. 12) breaks the circular dependency between posterior and context estimation, and a chunk-synchronization step (Eq. 13) collapses per-frame alignment mass onto chunk boundaries to match inference behavior. Training follows a curriculum: offline pretraining with full-sentence attention, then streaming finetuning with Algorithm 1. The result maintains O(U) predictor forward passes at inference and O(1) attention memory overhead, matching the base Transducer's efficiency while outperforming CAAT (O(T) memory) and TAED (O(U+T) inference, O(T) memory).

## Results

On MuST-C simultaneous speech translation, MonoAttn-Transducer (Posterior) improves over the Transducer baseline by an average of 0.75 BLEU / 0.95 COMET on En→Es and up to 2.06 COMET on En→De across chunk sizes, with the largest gains (0.88 BLEU / 1.77 COMET) at chunk sizes ≥960 ms and offline. For example, at 1280 ms chunk, En→De BLEU rises from 22.96 to 23.74 and COMET from 66.26 to 68.54, while LAAL remains nearly unchanged (2305 vs 2312 ms). On CVSS-C Fr→En speech-to-speech translation, MonoAttn-Transducer achieves 18.3 ASR-BLEU at 320 ms chunk (vs 17.1 for Transducer) while reducing LAAL from 984 to 918 ms, and reaches 19.3 ASR-BLEU offline (vs 18.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Transducer baseline, MonoAttn-Transducer (Posterior) consistently wins on both BLEU and COMET at every chunk size for both En→Es and En→De, with the gap widening as chunk size increases (e.g., +1.34 BLEU on En→De at 1280 ms vs only +0.23 at 320 ms). The Prior-only variant degrades sharply at 320 ms (23.00 BLEU on En→Es, below the Transducer's 24.33), confirming that posterior alignment inference is critical for small-chunk streaming. Among Transducer-based competitors, MonoAttn-Transducer outperforms CAAT (Liu et al., 2021) in less latency-constrained settings thanks to its tightly coupled self- and cross-attention in the predictor, while TAED (Tang et al., 2023) shows the opposite trend—larger gains at small chunk sizes—but at O(U+T) inference cost versus MonoAttn-Transducer's O(U). In the broader SOTA comparison (Figure 2), MonoAttn-Transducer achieves the best BLEU–AL trade-off curve, surpassing AED-based methods (Wait-k, RealTrans, MU-ST, Seg2Seg, EDAtt, AlignAtt), the CTC-based NAST, and the LLM-based LSG across both language pairs. On speech-to-speech (CVSS-C Fr→En), MonoAttn-Transducer improves ASR-BLEU by 1.2 points at 320 ms and 1.3 points offline over Transducer while also reducing LAAL by 66 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transducer architecture (Graves, 2012; Zhang et al., 2020) by integrating monotonic cross-attention into the predictor, building on the cumulative-sum rewriting trick from MILk (Arivazhagan et al., 2019) for efficient expected-context computation. It departs from prior Transducer+attention approaches—CAAT (Liu et al., 2021) separates cross-attention from self-attention to preserve state independence, while TAED (Tang et al., 2023) updates all predictor states per new source chunk, incurring O(T+U) inference cost. The key conceptual departure is using the forward-backward algorithm to compute posterior alignment probabilities that estimate the monotonic attention context, thereby keeping self-attention and cross-attention tightly coupled while maintaining O(U) inference complexity and O(1) attention memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled solution to the exponential state-space explosion that has blocked the integration of full cross-attention into Transducer training, making it the first Transducer variant with tightly coupled monotonic attention at vanilla Transducer's computational cost. This is practically important for latency-sensitive streaming deployment where TAED's O(T+U) overhead is prohibitive. Limitations include the chicken-and-egg bootstrap (requiring a diagonal prior to initialize posterior inference), 1.33× training slowdown, and evaluation restricted to two language pairs and one speech-to-speech setting. Open questions include whether the posterior-alignment trick generalizes to other streaming architectures (e.g., CTC-based) and whether the diagonal prior remains adequate for highly non-monotonic language pairs.
