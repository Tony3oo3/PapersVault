## Problem

Autoregressive models require a fixed ordering over data dimensions, which is natural for text but arbitrary for structured data like molecular graphs. Any-order ARMs (AO-ARMs) remove this constraint by using a uniform distribution over all L! permutations, but they underperform because the model must fit all conditional distributions simultaneously without learning any preference. The paper asks: can we learn a data-dependent, context-sensitive generation ordering that adapts to the partially generated state?

## Input / Output

The model starts from a fully masked state where every data dimension (node or edge in a molecular graph, pixel in MNIST) is assigned a special mask token, and sequentially unmasks one dimension at a time until the full data vector is revealed. For molecular graphs, molecules are represented as dense adjacency matrices with node vectors, padded to a fixed size; the total number of dimensions scales as O(n²) for n-atom molecules. At each step, the order-policy selects which masked dimension to fill, and a classifier assigns its categorical value (atom type or bond type).

## Method

LO-ARM replaces the uniform prior over orderings in AO-ARMs with a trainable order-policy, a Plackett-Luce distribution over remaining dimensions that conditions on the already-unmasked data values. Training maximizes a variational lower bound (ELBO) on the log-likelihood using an amortized variational distribution q_θ(z|x) that conditions on the full data vector, with gradients computed via a REINFORCE leave-one-out (RLOO) estimator using two sampled orderings. The classifier (Graph Transformer for molecules, UNet for images) predicts categorical values for each dimension, while the order-policy is parametrized either by an extra shared-torso linear layer or by entropy-based uncertainty of the classifier. A key design choice is that the variational distribution uses a single forward pass to produce all L logits, enabling fast parallel sampling of full permutations via the Gumbel-top-k trick, making training scalable.

## Results

On ZINC250K, LO-ARM-st-sep achieves a state-of-the-art FCD of 3.229, substantially outperforming CatFlow (13.21), GDSS (14.66), and GraphARM (16.26), while also reaching 96.26% validity and 100% uniqueness. On QM9, LO-ARM-st-sep achieves FCD 0.240 (vs. CatFlow 0.441 and GraphARM 1.22) with 99.85% validity. On chemistry-specific metrics for ZINC250K, LO-ARM matches the ground-truth QED (0.75) and achieves SAS of 3.08 (vs. GT 2.76) and pairwise similarity of 0.34 (vs. GT 0.35), exceeding or matching all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiGress (Vignac et al., ICLR 2023), LO-ARM-st-sep achieves a dramatically lower FCD on ZINC250K (3.229 vs. not directly reported in Table 2, but DiGress is listed in Table 1 on QM9 with NLL 69.6 and no FCD); on QM9, LO-ARM-st-sep achieves FCD 0.240 versus DiGress's unreported FCD but with comparable validity (99.85% vs. 99.00%). Against CatFlow (Eijkelboom et al., NeurIPS 2024), LO-ARM-st-sep wins decisively on FCD for both datasets: 3.229 vs. 13.21 on ZINC250K (a 4× improvement) and 0.240 vs. 0.441 on QM9 (an 1.8× improvement), while CatFlow retains a slight edge on validity (99.21% vs. 96.26% on ZINC250K). The paper's own AO-ARM baseline (uniform ordering) shows that the learned order-policy is critical: on ZINC250K, moving from AO-ARM (FCD 6.541, validity 32.93%) to LO-ARM-st-sep (FCD 3.229, validity 96.26%) yields a 2× FCD reduction and a 63-point validity gain, confirming that the learned ordering is the primary source of improvement rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends any-order autoregressive models (AO-ARMs, Uria et al., 2014) by replacing the uniform ordering prior with a learnable, data-dependent order-policy, bridging the gap between fixed-order ARMs and order-agnostic masked diffusion models. Conceptually, it is closest to Variational Order Inference (VOI, Li et al., 2021), which also learns orderings via variational inference, but LO-ARM achieves this with a fully tractable Plackett-Luce variational distribution and standard REINFORCE leave-one-out gradients, avoiding VOI's intractable normalizing constant and Bethe approximation. In the graph generation literature, prior work (You et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2021; Kong et al., 2023) has explored fixed or partially learnable node orderings, but LO-ARM provides a general framework where both node and edge dimensions are ordered flexibly without architectural inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic validity jump from AO-ARM (33%) to LO-ARM (96%) on ZINC250k demonstrates that the generation order is not a minor hyperparameter but a critical structural choice for autoregressive graph generation, and that it can be learned end-to-end without domain-specific priors. The 99.9% consistency of the discovered edge-first ordering (bonds → no-edges → atoms) provides an interpretable, chemically meaningful generation strategy that emerges purely from ELBO optimization. Open questions include closing the remaining validity gap with diffusion-based methods (which may exploit fragment-level denoising), scaling to higher-dimensional data like high-resolution images or proteins, and incorporating complex molecular constraints directly into the order-policy.
