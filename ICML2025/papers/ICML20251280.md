## Problem

Most SAE-based interpretability methods analyze features at a single layer or focus exclusively on the residual stream, leaving the multi-layer dynamics of feature emergence, transformation, and disappearance largely unexplored. This paper asks how SAE features propagate, originate, or vanish across consecutive transformer layers and modules, and whether the resulting cross-layer structure can be leveraged for more effective and interpretable model steering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251280/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only pretrained SAE decoder weight matrices (no activation data or forward-pass statistics are required), specifically from SAEs trained at three positions per layer: residual stream, MLP output, and attention output. It requires that SAEs at all positions share the same hidden dimension (e.g., Gemma Scope's 2304-dim SAEs), which rules out packs like Gemma Scope's attention SAEs trained at a different dimension. Outputs include per-feature origin classifications (From RES, From MLP, From ATT, combinations, or 'From nowhere'), composed flow graphs tracing a feature's semantic evolution from layer 0 to the final layer, and steered text generations where multi-layer feature interventions are applied to hidden states during inference.

## Method

The core mechanism is a data-free cosine similarity matching: for a target feature (a column of the residual SAE decoder at layer L), the method computes the maximum cosine similarity against every column of the decoder matrices for the previous residual, MLP output, and attention output SAEs at the same layer, yielding three similarity scores s^(R), s^(M), s^(A) that classify the feature's origin. By composing these short-range matches across consecutive layers, the authors build flow graphs that trace how a feature's semantics evolve from early to late layers. For steering, the flow graph identifies a set of related features across multiple layers; interventions (activation or deactivation via rescaling hidden states by scaled decoder columns) are then applied cumulatively across layers with linear or exponential decay of steering coefficients, which the paper shows reduces hyperparameter sensitivity and achieves better thematic control than single-layer interventions. The approach is validated through deactivation experiments (causal intervention on predecessor features) and compared against permutation-based and Pearson-correlation-based matching, achieving comparable performance without requiring any data.

## Results

In the feature deactivation experiment (Table 1), the proposed top-1 cosine similarity matching achieves a mean activation change of 0.75 and a 65% success rate, matching the top-1 Pearson correlation baseline (0.74 AC, 65%) and approaching the exhaustive-search upper bound (0.83 AC, 73%). For model steering, cumulative multi-layer interventions using flow-graph-discovered features outperform single-layer interventions on both deactivation (suppressing 'Scientific concepts and entities') and activation tasks, with reduced sensitivity to the rescaling hyperparameter r. The method also identifies effective steering layers (e.g., layer 5 for 'Research methodology') that differ from the initial feature's layer (layer 12), demonstrating the added value of cross-layer feature tracking.

| Method | Mean Activation Change | Success Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Top-1 Cosine (ours) | 0.75 | 65% |
| Top-1 Pearson | 0.74 | 65% |
| Exhaustive Search (upper bound) | 0.83 | 73% |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the permutation-based matching of Balagansky et al. (2024), the proposed top-1 cosine similarity method is tested as one of four strategies in the deactivation experiment; the random-sampling variant of top-5 sharply reduces deactivation success (Figure 6), confirming that the cosine-similarity top-1 choice is causally informative, while the permutation method performs comparably. Against the Pearson correlation baseline (associated with Balcells et al. 2024 and Wang et al. 2025), the data-free cosine method ties on mean activation change (0.75 vs. 0.74) and success rate (65% vs. 65%) in Table 1, but the correlation method reduces the 'From nowhere' group and improves attention-module predecessor identification at the cost of requiring 100K tokens of activation data. Notably, the correlation method performs worse on out-of-distribution Python code, whereas the cosine method maintains consistent performance across all four evaluation datasets (FineWeb, TinyStories, AutoMathText, PythonGithubCode). In the steering experiments, the paper does not directly compare against a prior multi-layer steering baseline (none exists), but shows that flow-graph-guided cumulative interventions outperform single-layer interventions on the same initial feature set, with the advantage growing at lower rescaling coefficients r.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-matching line initiated by Balagansky et al. (2024), which used permutation matrices to align SAE features across residual-stream layers, by generalizing to a many-to-one cosine similarity over decoder weights that also covers MLP and attention module SAEs. It builds on the SAE monosemanticity framework of Bricken et al. (2023) and the transcoder-based circuit discovery of Dunefsky et al. (2024), but departs from both by requiring no activation data and no backward pass. Unlike Chalnev et al. (2024), who used SAE features for single-layer steering in a data-dependent manner, this work constructs cross-layer flow graphs and applies them to multi-layer intervention, positioning itself as the first to use SAE features from different layers jointly for controlling generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free nature of the method makes cross-layer feature tracking practical at scale, and the flow graphs offer a concrete, interpretable picture of how semantic content is born, refined, and propagated through a transformer. The multi-layer steering results are promising but limited to small-scale experiments on Gemma 2 2B and a handful of themes; the authors themselves note that features remain 'tangled' across layers and that efficient deactivation would require changing the convergence direction of hidden states at early stages rather than local perturbations. Open questions include whether the three-part layer partition (early uncertainty, mid stability, late propagation) generalizes across architectures, how to disentangle overlapping causal pathways, and whether the approach scales to much larger models where SAE training quality may degrade.
