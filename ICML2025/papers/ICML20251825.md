## Problem

Current quantization methods treat each target bit-width as an independent optimization problem, forcing practitioners to maintain separate models for int8, int4, and int2 deployment. Moreover, extremely low precisions like int2 are known to severely degrade model quality. The paper asks whether a single model can be trained that simultaneously yields accurate models at multiple precisions by exploiting the inherent nested structure of integer data types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251825/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weights (Gemma-2 2B/9B, Mistral 7B) and a set of target bit-widths (default: int8, int4, int2) as input, operating on FFN parameters (or all Transformer parameters in extended experiments). The output is a single int8-quantized model whose most significant bits directly encode int4 and int2 representations via right-shift slicing, enabling free extraction of interpolated precisions (int6, int3) and layer-wise Mix'n'Match combinations without additional training. The quantized weight distribution is shifted toward higher-valued buckets compared to standard quantization, a side effect that particularly benefits the int2 slice.

## Method

MatQuant leverages the inherent Matryoshka (nested) structure of integer types—int4 is the top 4 MSBs of int8, int2 is the top 2 MSBs of int4—by jointly optimizing a weighted sum of quantization losses across all target precisions simultaneously. The slicing operator S(q^c, r) extracts r MSBs from a c-bit quantized vector via right-shift, round, clamp, and left-shift, and the overall objective (Eq. 7) sums the base algorithm's loss (cross-entropy for QAT, layer-wise L2 reconstruction for OmniQuant) over each sliced precision with reweighting factors λ_r. This multi-scale training acts as a general wrapper around existing learning-based quantization methods without modifying their core mechanisms. A key empirical finding is that joint optimization shifts the quantized weight distribution toward higher-valued buckets, giving int2 access to more informative quantization levels. The paper further introduces co-distillation (using int8 outputs as supervision for int2), Single Precision MatQuant (optimizing only int2 loss within an 8-bit representation for up to 1% additional gain), and Extra Precision MatQuant (using one extra bit to capture outliers, yielding 6% improvement for int2).

## Results

On Gemma-2 9B with OmniQuant, MatQuant achieves 63.35% average downstream accuracy at int2, outperforming the explicitly trained OmniQuant baseline (60.24%) by 3.11%, while remaining within 0.5% at int8 (74.05 vs. 74.59) and int4 (73.83 vs. 74.33). With QAT as the base algorithm, the int2 gains are even larger: 62.29% vs. 56.02% on Gemma-2 9B (+6.27%) and 61.97% vs. 54.95% on Mistral 7B (+7.02%). Interpolated bit-widths (int6, int3) obtained by slicing the MatQuant int8 model match or approach explicitly trained baselines without any additional training (e.g., int3: 72.87% vs. 73.23% on Gemma-2 9B). Log perplexity on C4 validation follows the same trend, with MatQuant int2 achieving 3.187 vs. 3.292 on Gemma-2 9B (OmniQuant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatQuant is compared against two baselines: (1) explicitly trained OmniQuant/QAT models at each target precision, and (2) naive slicing of an int8-quantized model to lower precisions. Against the explicitly trained OmniQuant baseline, MatQuant is within 0.5% on int8 and int4 but significantly outperforms it on int2 by 1.04% (Gemma-2 2B), 3.11% (Gemma-2 9B), and 3.01% (Mistral 7B) in average downstream accuracy. Against the QAT baseline, the int2 advantage is even more pronounced: +4.46%, +6.27%, and +7.02% respectively. On interpolated bit-widths (int6, int3) that were never explicitly optimized, MatQuant matches the explicitly trained baselines (e.g., int3 on Gemma-2 9B: 72.87% vs. 73.23%), whereas naive slicing of int8 collapses catastrophically (int3: 54.18%, int2: 38.11%). The key takeaway is that MatQuant's multi-scale joint training is essentially free at higher precisions while delivering substantial gains at the most challenging low-bit regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatQuant extends the Matryoshka (nested) representation learning paradigm from Kusupati et al. (2022) and multi-scale model training (Rippel et al., 2014; Yu et al., 2018) to the bit-width structure of integer data types, where int4 and int2 are inherently nested within int8's most significant bits. It is a general-purpose wrapper around existing learning-based quantization methods (QAT, OmniQuant) that replaces their single-precision objective with a joint multi-precision loss over sliced MSBs. The closest prior work, Yu et al. (2019) on any-precision DNNs, demonstrated the concept on small ConvNets but did not scale to LLMs or exploit the integer nesting structure explicitly. The layer-wise Mix'n'Match strategy draws on Devvrit et al. (2023) for elastic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatQuant offers a practical path to deploying a single quantized model that can be served at int8, int4, int2 (and interpolated int6, int3) without retraining, eliminating the need to maintain and manage multiple precision-specific models in production. The 4–7% int2 accuracy gains over independently trained baselines are meaningful for the most aggressive compression regime, and the free interpolative bit-widths plus Mix'n'Match densely span the accuracy-vs-memory Pareto frontier for hardware-constrained serving. Open questions include extending the approach to floating-point representations (FP8/FP4) where the nested bucket structure breaks down, and hardware-software co-design to support elastic bit-widths on-the-fly. The FFN+Attention extension reveals that baseline QAT destabilizes at int2/int3, suggesting MatQuant-style joint training may be a necessary stabilizer rather than just an accuracy booster for full-model quantization.
