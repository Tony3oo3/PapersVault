## Problem

Standard neural world models are monolithic and lack explicit boundaries between distinct environmental modes (e.g., water vs. land dynamics), making them difficult to reuse across environments with different spatial configurations of the same modes. This paper asks whether one can unsupervisedly discover such mode structure from low-level continuous observations and assemble it into a modular, reusable world model that supports efficient model-based policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250905/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory datasets from multiple POMDPs (continuous, non-visual observations such as LiDAR readings, motor positions, and GPS) plus a small amount of active-POMDP data, a reward function, and the number of modes (assumed known). It produces a neurosymbolic FSM world model whose states are MLP forward models (32-32 hidden units, ReLU) specialized per mode and whose transitions are decision-tree predicates (max depth 8), along with a Soft Actor-Critic policy optimized via alternating real and model-based RL sessions. The FSM operates purely on observations rather than latent states, and model-based rollouts are kept short (30 steps) to limit compounding error.

## Method

SWMPO synthesizes the FSM in three stages. First, it learns a joint neural architecture where a mode-encoder m_{θ2}(o_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, o_t) feeds a first-order forward model f_{θ1} that predicts the observation change (o_{t+1} − o_t), regularized by a mutual-information term that minimizes I(m_{θ2}, O_{t+1}) to enforce minimality of the mode variable; k-means then clusters the continuous mode vectors into discrete labels. Second, a greedy pruning pass removes spurious mode transitions whose elimination increases prediction error by less than a tolerance ε. Third, for each pair of modes, a decision-tree classifier is trained on the labeled transitions to synthesize the symbolic transition predicate δ_{i,j}. The resulting FSM is then used in a model-based RL loop (Algorithm 3) that alternates short real-environment SAC sessions with model-based SAC rollouts, reusing offline-trained neural primitives and only learning environment-specific transition structure online.

## Results

SWMPO is evaluated on four simulated environments (Point Mass, LiDAR Racing, Salamander, Bipedal Walker) using Levenshtein distance between predicted and ground-truth mode labels (lower is better), aggregated over four test trajectories per terrain across eight terrains. SWMPO outperforms all baselines in Point Mass and Salamander, significantly outperforms SLDS and rSLDS in LiDAR Racing (marginally beating HMM), and marginally beats all baselines in Bipedal Walker where all models struggle. In the model-based RL experiment on Point Mass, SWMPO achieves higher cumulative reward than both model-free SAC and a monolithic model-based RL baseline, without training any neural model online—instead using online data solely to synthesize FSM transitions between pre-trained primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the HMM baseline (Baum & Petrie, 1966), SWMPO outperforms in Point Mass and Salamander, marginally outperforms in LiDAR Racing, and marginally beats in Bipedal Walker. Against SLDS (Ackerson & Fu, 1970) and rSLDS (Glaser et al., 2020), SWMPO significantly outperforms in LiDAR Racing and outperforms in Point Mass and Salamander; in Bipedal Walker all methods struggle similarly. In the RL experiment (Figure 8), SWMPO outperforms both the model-free SAC baseline (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and the model-based monolithic neural baseline on cumulative reward in Point Mass, with the key distinction that SWMPO trains no neural network online—only the offline-trained primitives are reused while transition predicates are synthesized from active POMDP data. The paper notes that SWMPO's advantage is most pronounced in environments with clear mode boundaries (Point Mass, Salamander) and diminishes in more complex locomotion tasks (Bipedal Walker).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the FSM structure previously used for policy synthesis (Inala et al., 2020) as a world model, combining it with unsupervised mode discovery from continuous observations. It departs from HMMs and switching dynamical systems by using non-linear neural primitives within each mode and learning transition predicates via decision trees rather than probabilistic emission/transition matrices. The mutual-information-minimization objective for identifying the latent mode variable draws on information-theoretic identifiability criteria, while the overall neurosymbolic pipeline (neural dynamics + symbolic transitions) follows the hybrid-systems tradition of piecewise-dynamics modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular primitive-reuse mechanism offers a practical path toward reducing sample complexity in model-based RL for environments with recurring dynamical regimes (e.g., amphibious robotics), since only boundary predicates need to be relearned per environment. However, the framework relies on strong assumptions—known mode count, deterministic observations, and the global-modes condition—that limit generality, and the RL evaluation is restricted to a single environment (Point Mass) due to resource constraints. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or stochastic observation spaces, whether the number-of-modes assumption can be relaxed, and whether the FSM structure can be exploited more directly during policy optimization (e.g., via product MDPs).
