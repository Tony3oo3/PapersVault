## Problem

Existing precipitation nowcasting methods face a fundamental accuracy-perception tradeoff: deterministic models (ConvLSTM, SimVP) minimize MSE but produce smoothed, detail-free predictions with intensity decay, while probabilistic models (STRPM, DiffCast) generate realistic textures but sacrifice spatial accuracy. No prior method simultaneously achieves low prediction error and high perceptual quality across long forecast horizons, particularly for high-echo regions where distributional divergence grows with time.

## Input / Output

The model takes 13 consecutive VIL radar frames (128×128, 5-minute intervals spanning 65 minutes) as input and produces 36 future precipitation frames (128×128, 180 minutes) as output. Internally, frames are patch-embedded to a 4× downsampled 16C-channel latent representation, and the rectified flow component reshapes the temporal dimension into the batch dimension so each frame is individually transported with a frame-indexed embedding conditioning the U-Net velocity field.

## Method

PercpCast reformulates nowcasting as a posteriori MSE problem under a distribution-matching constraint (p_Ŷ = p_Y), solved via a two-component end-to-end architecture. A ConvLSTM precipitation estimator first produces the posteriori mean sequence, then a frame-sampled rectified flow (U-Net) learns the straight-line velocity field from each posteriori frame to its corresponding target frame, with gradients stopped between the two components. The key architectural novelty is per-frame modeling: instead of transporting the entire sequence jointly, each frame is sampled individually using a temperature-distance weighted strategy (exponential base weights w_i = exp(k·i) modulated by a dynamic temperature τ) that progressively increases attention on distant frames. An LPIPS loss is applied at each flow step to enforce perceptual consistency, and the whole system is trained end-to-end with the estimator continuously providing augmented conditional samples to the flow model, yielding a theoretical 2MMSE upper bound on prediction error.

## Results

On SEVIR, PercpCast achieves the best CSI (0.267), HSS (0.360), SSIM (0.722), and LPIPS (0.268) among all compared methods, with an MSE of 0.0092. On MeteoNet, it similarly leads in CSI (0.209), HSS (0.305), SSIM (0.820), and LPIPS (0.237), with an MSE of 0.0049. The paper reports average improvements of ~5% in CSI and ~10% in LPIPS over the strongest baselines, with the largest gains (15–22%) in high-echo CSI thresholds (>74 on SEVIR, >24 on MeteoNet). MSE is competitive but not always the lowest (e.g., ConvLSTM achieves 0.0084 on SEVIR vs. 0.0092 for PercpCast).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PercpCast outperforms all eight baselines on CSI, HSS, SSIM, and LPIPS across both SEVIR and MeteoNet. Against the strongest deterministic baseline (ConvLSTM, CSI 0.240 / LPIPS 0.304 on SEVIR), PercpCast improves CSI by ~9.6% and reduces LPIPS by ~11.8%; against the strongest probabilistic baseline (DiffCast, CSI 0.244 / LPIPS 0.286), the gains are ~9.4% in CSI and ~6.3% in LPIPS. On MeteoNet, the advantage over DiffCast (CSI 0.199, LPIPS 0.241) is ~5% in CSI and ~1.7% in LPIPS. The only metric where PercpCast is not the best is MSE: ConvLSTM (0.0084) and MAU (0.0029) achieve lower MSE on SEVIR and MeteoNet respectively, though the paper notes its MSE is within 2× of any method. In high-echo CSI thresholds (e.g., CSI133 on SEVIR), PercpCast leads by 15–22% over all baselines, indicating superior handling of intense precipitation events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deterministic precipitation nowcasting (ConvLSTM, SimVP, Earthformer) and probabilistic/generative approaches (STRPM, DiffCast, PreDiff), which have historically been treated as competing paradigms. It builds directly on the rectified flow framework of Liu et al. (2022) for distribution transport and the distortion-perception tradeoff theory of Freirich et al. (2021) for the 2MMSE optimal transport decomposition, repurposing both for a spatiotemporal forecasting setting. The key conceptual departure is using a deterministic estimator (ConvLSTM) to produce the posteriori mean as the *source* distribution for a frame-sampled rectified flow, rather than starting from noise as in diffusion-based nowcasters, thereby preserving physical motion dynamics while still aligning the output distribution with ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded resolution to the long-standing accuracy-perception tradeoff in precipitation nowcasting, with the 2MMSE bound offering a clear optimality guarantee that prior generative nowcasters lacked. Its consistent improvements across all five metrics (CSI, HSS, SSIM, MSE, LPIPS) on two datasets suggest the posteriori-mean-as-source strategy is broadly applicable to other spatiotemporal prediction tasks. However, the authors acknowledge the model cannot handle sudden convective storms without additional atmospheric variables (temperature, humidity, wind), and the reliance on a single deterministic backbone (ConvLSTM) limits exploration of how estimator quality interacts with the flow module. Whether the frame-sampled rectified flow with temperature scheduling generalizes beyond radar nowcasting to other video prediction domains remains an open question.
