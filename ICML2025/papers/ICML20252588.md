## Problem

Existing algorithms for convex finite-sum coupled compositional optimization (FCCO) require both inner and outer functions to be smooth, exhibit poor convergence rates with unfavorable dependence on batch sizes and condition numbers, and rely on nested inner loops whose iteration counts depend on problem-specific constants. This paper identifies a structured subclass (cFCCO) where the outer functions are convex and monotonically non-decreasing, enabling a single-loop algorithm that handles non-smooth outer functions and achieves near-optimal convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic oracles g_i(x;ζ_i) sampled from per-group distributions P_i (one distribution per summand), deterministic convex outer functions f_i: R^m → R, and a convex regularizer r(x). Each iteration samples a mini-batch S of S groups from [n] and two independent batches of B samples from each sampled group's distribution, requiring only O(1) oracle calls per iteration. The output is a model parameter vector x* minimizing the composite objective, instantiated as a robust classifier for group DRO or a binary classifier for pAUC maximization on imbalanced data.

## Method

ALEXR reformulates cFCCO via the convex conjugate f_i* into a convex-concave min-max problem, then applies a single-loop primal-dual block-coordinate scheme. The dual variable y is updated via block-coordinate stochastic mirror ascent with extrapolation: only the S sampled blocks y^(i) for i∈S_t are updated using an extrapolated estimator g̃_t^(i) = g_i(x_t; B_t^(i)) + θ(g_i(x_t; B_t^(i)) − g_i(x_{t-1}; B_t^(i))), while unsampled blocks remain fixed. The primal variable x is updated via stochastic proximal gradient descent using an independent mini-batch for the compositional gradient. The block-coordinate design (updating only S of n dual blocks per step) combined with the extrapolation parameter θ yields O(1/ε) iteration complexity for strongly convex problems and O(1/ε²) for merely convex problems with O(1) batch size, and lower complexity bounds are proven to establish near-optimality within a broad class of first-order stochastic methods.

## Results

On the GDRO task, ALEXR achieves a mean worst-(αn)-group test accuracy of 66.80% across Adult and CelebA datasets (α=0.1 and 0.15), outperforming BSGD (66.46%), SOX (66.46%), MSVR (62.20%), OOA (61.35%), SGD-UW (49.46%), and vanilla SGD (2.56%). On the pAUC maximization task with restricted TPR (α=0.5 and 0.75) across four imbalanced datasets (Covtype, Cardiomegaly, Lung-mass, Higgs), ALEXR achieves the highest validation pAUC throughout training, surpassing both FCCO baselines and the SOTA pAUC-specific algorithm of Zhu et al. (2022). ALEXR also converges faster than all baselines in validation loss curves for GDRO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALEXR outperforms all six baselines on the GDRO task in mean worst-group accuracy (66.80%), with the largest margins over MSVR (+4.60 pp, driven by a 8.8 pp gap on Adult) and OOA (+5.45 pp, with a 9.4 pp gap on CelebA at α=0.1). Against the closest FCCO competitors BSGD and SOX (both 66.46% mean), ALEXR gains a small but consistent edge on CelebA (75.79 vs 75.30/75.04 at α=0.1) and Adult at α=0.1 (56.58 vs 55.81/56.34), though BSGD narrowly edges ALEXR on Adult at α=0.15 (58.58 vs 58.52). On the pAUC maximization task, ALEXR achieves the highest validation pAUC across all four imbalanced datasets and both TPR thresholds (0.5, 0.75), surpassing BSGD, SOX, MSVR, SGD with over-sampling, and the dedicated SOTA pAUC algorithm of Zhu et al. (2022). ALEXR also converges faster in validation loss than all FCCO baselines on GDRO, and its single-loop design with O(1) batch size per iteration gives it a practical advantage over the double-loop MSVR and the large-batch BSGD.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALEXR is positioned as a single-loop primal-dual block-coordinate algorithm that unifies and extends three prior lines of work: SOX (Wang & Yang, 2022) via its moving-average dual estimator, MSVR (Jiang et al., 2022) via a modified correction-term scaling that avoids the large β factor, and SAPD (Zhang et al., 2024) via a block-coordinate dual update that avoids updating the full high-dimensional dual variable. The key conceptual departure is the min-max reformulation using the convex conjugate of f_i, which enables a mirror-prox dual step with problem-specific distance-generating functions (f_i* for smooth outer functions, quadratic for non-smooth ones), combined with an extrapolated stochastic estimator for the inner function. Unlike prior cFCCO algorithms that required both f_i and g_i to be smooth and used nested loops, ALEXR handles non-smooth outer functions and operates in a single loop with O(1) batch size per iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-optimality lower bounds (Theorem 4) are the strongest theoretical contribution, establishing that no first-order stochastic algorithm within the abstract scheme can beat ALEXR's rates, and notably showing that strong convexity of the objective does not yield a faster rate when the outer function f_i is non-smooth—a departure from standard optimization intuition. This resolves a gap for non-smooth cFCCO problems (e.g., GDRO with CVaR divergence) that were previously intractable with existing algorithms. The main open question the authors identify is extending these results to concave outer functions (e.g., logarithmic), which appear in applications like multi-task GDRO and contrastive learning. Practical impact is moderate: the algorithm is applicable to a specific but important class of problems (GDRO, pAUC, bipartite ranking) where prior methods had suboptimal rates or inapplicable smoothness assumptions.
