## Problem

Existing Multi-Label Online Continual Learning (MOCL) methods address catastrophic forgetting, missing labels, and class imbalance through pseudo-labeling, distillation, and resampling, but all rely on extracting a single feature vector per image. This unified representation suffers from co-occurrence bias between head and tail classes, making discriminative feature learning ineffective under the combined pressure of missing labels and long-tailed distributions. The paper identifies that label-specific region identification—a fundamental principle in offline multi-label learning—has been overlooked in the online setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming RGB images (224×224×3) annotated only with the current task's label subset, processed through a DINO v1 pre-trained ViT-S/16 backbone that produces patch-level feature maps. Beyond standard multi-label binary predictions, the method outputs cropped single-label object regions (bounding boxes derived from MCut masks) that are selectively stored in a fixed-size memory buffer (1000 slots) using class-balanced reservoir sampling, effectively converting full-image replay into multiple single-label sub-image classification tasks.

## Method

CUTER (CUT-out-and-Experience-Replay) operates in three stages: (1) it uses iterative Mask Cut (MCut) on the pre-trained model's patch feature graph to segment foreground objects, then re-feeds each cropped object through the classifier to verify single-label correspondence via a confidence threshold (top-1 > τ, top-2 < 0.5); (2) verified objects are stored in a memory buffer with a modified reservoir sampling strategy that uses dual confidence thresholds (τ₁ < τ₂) based on class frequency to maintain balance; (3) a low-rank regularization term (nuclear norm of the patch similarity adjacency matrix) is added to the training loss to prevent degradation of the model's innate localization capability during continual learning. The paper further provides a theoretical foundation via Theorem 2.3, proving that the Fiedler value of the feature graph Laplacian is upper-bounded by the perturbation norm from an ideal block-diagonal structure, and uses the averaged Fiedler value as an annotation-free metric to select pre-trained models with strong zero-shot localization (showing DINO outperforms MoCo, MAE, and iBOT).

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007, CUTER w/Rl achieves 82.07±0.53 Avg mAP and 67.89±1.28 Last mAP, outperforming the best MOCL baseline AGCN++ (74.14 Avg mAP, 55.34 Last mAP) by ~8 and ~12.5 points respectively. On MS-COCO, CUTER reaches 60.14±0.60 Avg mAP and 47.82±0.60 Last mAP, surpassing the strongest MOCL method AGCN (56.45 Avg mAP, 37.48 Last mAP) by ~3.7 and ~10.3 points. On NUS-WIDE, CUTER attains 51.14±0.72 Avg mAP and 37.57±1.46 Last mAP, leading the best baseline AGCN (49.16 Avg mAP, 33.49 Last mAP) by ~2 and ~4 points. The advantage is most pronounced in Last mAP (performance on all classes after full training), where CUTER gains 10–22 points over OCL baselines like NsCE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUTER w/Rl outperforms all 10 baselines across all three datasets on both Avg and Last mAP. Against the strongest OCL baseline NsCE, CUTER gains ~6.8 Avg mAP and ~11.0 Last mAP on VOC, ~8.9 and ~21.6 on COCO, and ~5.4 and ~10.4 on NUS-WIDE. Among MOCL-specific methods, CUTER surpasses AGCN++ (best MOCL on VOC) by 7.9 Avg mAP and 12.5 Last mAP, AGCN (best MOCL on COCO) by 3.7 Avg mAP and 10.3 Last mAP, and AGCN (best MOCL on NUS-WIDE) by 2.0 Avg mAP and 4.1 Last mAP. The MLCIL method APPLE is the closest competitor on VOC (76.24 vs 82.07 Avg mAP) but still trails by 5.8 points; on COCO and NUS-WIDE the gap widens to 11.5 and 3.6 Avg mAP respectively. Notably, CUTER's advantage is largest in Last mAP (all-classes performance after full training), indicating superior retention of previously learned classes, while the gap in Avg mAP (seen-classes only) is more moderate, suggesting the cut-out-and-replay mechanism primarily benefits long-term memory rather than current-task accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-label online continual learning (MOCL) and self-supervised pre-training, departing from prior MOCL methods (PRS, OCDM, AGCN, KRT) that address missing labels and class imbalance through pseudo-labeling, knowledge distillation, or resampling of whole images. Its key conceptual departure is leveraging the innate zero-shot localization capability of contrastive pre-trained models (particularly DINO) to identify and crop label-specific object regions, then replaying those regions instead of full images—effectively transforming multi-label replay into multiple single-label sub-image classification tasks. The theoretical grounding draws on graph spectral theory (Fiedler values, Cheeger constants, NCut/MCut) to both evaluate pre-trained models' localization quality in an annotation-free manner and to motivate a low-rank regularization that preserves localization during continual training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CUTER's plug-in versatility—demonstrated by its ability to complement PRS, OCDM, KRT, and AGCN—makes it a practical component for existing MOCL pipelines rather than a standalone replacement. However, the approach is tightly coupled to ViT backbones (performance degrades notably with ResNet), and the multi-round MCut operations introduce non-trivial computational overhead that may limit deployment in high-velocity streaming settings. Open questions include whether the localization-preserving regularization generalizes to non-patch-based architectures, whether the Fiedler-value-based model selection transfers to domains beyond natural images, and how the method scales to very large label spaces (hundreds or thousands of classes) where per-object cropping becomes increasingly expensive.
