## Problem

Existing intrinsically interpretable GNNs implicitly assume that rationale subgraphs are nearly invariant across instances of the same graph category, which fails in real-world settings where rationales vary significantly in form, size, and topology (e.g., different functional groups producing the same bioactivity). This restrictive assumption leads to unreliable interpretations and poor generalization when the true causal substructures are variform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252749/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled graph with node and edge features and learns a per-edge importance score in [0,1] via a GNN-based filtration functional. The output is threefold: a class label prediction, a rationale subgraph identified by thresholding the learned filtration (edges with high scores form G_X, low scores form G_ε), and persistent homology barcodes (0-th and 1-st) computed from the induced graph filtration of each subgraph. The persistent homology features are permutation-invariant representations that are concatenated with the pooled GNN graph embedding before the final classification MLP.

## Method

TOPING models graph interpretation as an autoregressive generation process where the rationale subgraph is 'grown' first, followed by the complement, and uses persistent homology to track topological feature lifecycles across this filtration. The key loss component is a self-adjusted topological discrepancy: the 1-Wasserstein distance between the distributions of persistent homologies of G_X and G_ε under the bottleneck metric, approximated via Kantorovich duality with k=8 learnable 1-Lipschitz Rational Hat vectorization functions and a 2-head attention mechanism to select the top-2 maxima. A two-mixture Gaussian prior on edge filtrations (fixed at means 0.25 and 0.75) acts as an unsupervised clustering regularizer that prevents attention collapse without requiring hyperparameter tuning. The backbone GNN (GIN or CINpp) is shared between the filtration head and the prediction head, and the persistent homology features are fed into the prediction MLP alongside the standard pooled graph representation. The paper proves that under the condition |E_X| < |E_ε| and minimality of G_X*, the loss is uniquely optimized by the ground-truth filtration f*(e)=1{e∈G_X*}.

## Results

On interpretation quality (ROC AUC), CINpp+TOPING achieves 100.00% on BA-2Motifs, BA-HouseAndGrid, BA-HouseOrGrid, and Benzene, and 95.08% on SPmotif0.5, substantially outperforming the best intrinsic baselines (GSAT: 78.20% on SPmotif0.5; GMT-LIN: 83.23%). On prediction accuracy, CINpp+TOPING reaches 79.30% on the Spurious Motif dataset (b=0.5), beating GSAT (74.70%) and GMT-LIN (73.16%), while achieving 100% on Benzene. On the variform rationale dataset BA-HouseOrGrid-nRnd, TOPING maintains 100% AUC as rationale complexity increases, whereas GSAT and GMT-LIN degrade sharply. On Mutag, TOPING (96.38% AUC) is slightly below GSAT (99.38%), which the authors attribute to the trivially simple rationale structure (two edges) that offers little topological signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against post-hoc explainers (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, MatchExplainer, Mage), TOPING dominates on nearly all datasets, with the largest gaps on SPmotif (95.08 vs. 62.62 for GNNExplainer) and BA-HouseOrGrid (100.00 vs. 53.21). Among intrinsic interpretable baselines, TOPING (CINpp) outperforms GSAT by 16.88 AUC points on SPmotif0.5 (95.08 vs. 78.20) and 30.70 points on BA-HouseOrGrid (100.00 vs. 69.30), and beats GMT-LIN by 11.85 points on SPmotif0.5 (95.08 vs. 83.23). On prediction accuracy for Spurious Motif (b=0.5), CINpp+TOPING achieves 79.30%, exceeding GSAT (74.70%) and GMT-LIN (73.16%), confirming better resistance to spurious correlations. The one notable loss is on Mutag interpretation, where GSAT (99.38% AUC) and GMT-LIN (99.87%) slightly edge out TOPING (96.38%), which the authors explain by the trivially simple two-edge rationale structure that provides minimal topological signal for the persistent homology gap to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPING extends the intrinsically interpretable GNN lineage (GSAT's stochastic attention with information bottleneck, GMT's subgraph multilinear extension, DIR's invariant rationale discovery) by replacing the assumption of near-invariant rationale subgraphs with a topological framework based on persistent homology. The method reinterprets the graph attention mechanism as a learnable filtration process and introduces a novel topological discrepancy loss—a 1-Wasserstein distance between distributions of persistent homology barcodes—that acts as a self-adjusted constraint separating rationale from noise subgraphs. The prior regularization via a two-mixture Gaussian on edge filtrations is positioned as a more stable alternative to GSAT's Bernoulli information bottleneck, which the authors show can degenerate to a trivial constant solution, particularly on topologically expressive backbones like CINpp.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing interpretable GNNs degrade sharply when rationale subgraphs vary in form, size, or topology across instances (the 'variform rationale' challenge), a situation common in molecular biology and social networks. The theoretical guarantee that the loss is uniquely optimized by the ground truth without invariance assumptions is a meaningful step toward principled interpretability. However, the computational cost of persistent homology (O(n^ω) in theory, with practical GPU bottlenecks) limits scalability, and the MUTAG results reveal that the topological machinery can be counterproductive for structurally simple rationales. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to higher-dimensional simplicial complexes and whether approximation methods can close the efficiency gap.
