## Problem

Existing zero-shot coordination (ZSC) methods rely on population-based training (PBT) to expose agents to diverse partner strategies on a single task, but these agents fail to generalize to even minor environmental variations, requiring retraining for each new layout. The paper asks whether training across a diverse distribution of environments with a single self-play partner can produce more general cooperative skills that transfer to both novel partners and novel tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents consume a 9×9×26 grid observation encoding wall structure, object positions (pots, onion piles, plate piles, goals), and both agents' locations in Overcooked, or a 5×5 fully/partially observable grid in the Dual Destination toy problem. The output is a discrete action from a small action space (6 actions in Overcooked: four directions, stay, and interact; 5 actions in Dual Destination: four directions and stay). The recurrent LSTM core maintains state across timesteps within an episode, resetting at episode boundaries, enabling the agent to track evolving task progress and partner behavior.

## Method

Cross-Environment Cooperation (CEC) replaces partner diversity with environment diversity: a single cooperator policy is trained via self-play (IPPO with PPO) to maximize expected reward across a procedurally generated distribution of coordination tasks, rather than against a population of partners on one fixed task. The Jax-based procedural generator samples wall structures from five base Overcooked layouts, then randomly places goals, pots, onions, and plates on reachable walls, producing up to 1.16×10^17 solvable configurations at 10M steps/minute on a single GPU. The key architectural choice is an LSTM recurrent core (256 units) that enables meta-learning across the rapidly changing environment distribution, which the paper shows is necessary for convergence. The training objective (Eq. 2) samples a new environment each episode and plays the policy against itself, eliminating the need to maintain a partner population. An optional fine-tuning step (CEC-FT) applies 100M additional self-play steps on a single held-out layout with a reduced learning rate to recover task-specific performance while retaining cross-environment generalization.

## Results

On the Dual Destination problem, CEC achieves 0.931 normalized reward (SE 0.013) with novel partners on procedurally generated layouts, within 2.5% of the oracle (0.955), while FCP and IPPO fail to coordinate. On 100 held-out procedurally generated Overcooked layouts, FCP and E3T receive 0 reward, whereas CEC significantly outperforms all baselines (p < 0.0001). In the human-AI study, CEC outperforms FCP on success rate (p < 0.001) and CEC-Finetune significantly outperforms both FCP and E3T on subjective cooperation ratings (p < 0.01), while also achieving the lowest human-AI collision rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IPPO (de Witt et al., 2020), CEC wins decisively: IPPO agents fail to coordinate with novel partners even on the single task they were trained on (near-zero XP in Dual Destination), while CEC achieves 0.931 normalized reward on unseen layouts. Against FCP (Strouse et al., 2022), CEC wins on both partner and environment generalization: FCP adapts to partner diversity on a fixed task but receives 0 reward on all 100 procedurally generated Overcooked layouts, and CEC significantly outperforms FCP in the human study (p < 0.001 on success rate). Against E3T (Yan et al., 2023), the comparison is more nuanced: E3T achieves the highest raw reward in human-AI play on its trained layout (it is the SOTA single-task method), and CEC falls slightly short on score. However, CEC-FT significantly outperforms E3T on all 7 subjective cooperation metrics (p < 0.01, t = 3.1233), and CEC achieves the lowest human-AI collision rate. Critically, E3T also receives 0 reward on the 100 held-out procedural layouts, whereas CEC generalizes effectively, demonstrating that environment diversity alone can match or exceed partner-diversity methods for cross-task coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PBT paradigm (FCP, E3T) for zero-shot coordination, which trains a single cooperator against a diverse population of partners on one fixed task. It also extends the procedural-environment-generation line of work (Cobbe et al., McKee et al., Ruhdorfer et al.), which has shown that diverse environments improve generalization but typically evaluates agents with the same trained team. The key conceptual move is replacing partner diversity with environment diversity: a single self-play policy trained across a distribution of procedurally generated tasks, eliminating the need for partner populations while simultaneously addressing both partner and environment generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result challenges the field's working assumption that self-play is inherently insufficient for ZSC in cooperative games, suggesting that the bottleneck was environment coverage rather than partner coverage. This opens a practical route toward generalist cooperative agents that can interact with novel humans in novel settings without requiring human demonstration data. Open questions include how to best combine partner and environment diversity (the paper's CEC-E3T ablation suggests naive combination is counterproductive), whether the findings extend beyond grid-based cooperative games, and what the upper ceiling of CEC performance is given that learning curves had not converged at 3B steps.
