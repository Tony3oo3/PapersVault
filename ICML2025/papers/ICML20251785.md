## Problem

The training dynamics of equivariant neural networks remain poorly understood, particularly how they compare to data-augmented non-equivariant counterparts — a question at the center of an active empirical debate with no conclusive theoretical resolution. While neural tangent kernels (NTKs) provide a powerful analytical framework for studying wide-network training dynamics, no NTK theory existed for equivariant architectures based on group convolutions, leaving the relationship between manifest equivariance and augmentation-based symmetry learning unexplored at the kernel level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature maps over a symmetry group G: for images, 32×32 RGB patches (histological, MNIST, CIFAR-10) lifted onto C_n ⋊ R²; for molecules, per-atom spherical signals (29 atoms × 10 channels at bandlimit L=6, downsampled to L=3) lifted onto SO(3). The output is a pair of kernel matrices — the NTK Θ and NNGP K — indexed by both input positions and group elements (g, g′), which after group pooling reduce to scalar kernels. These kernels are then used in the closed-form NTK regression predictor μ(x) = Θ(x,𝒳)Θ(𝒳,𝒳)⁻¹𝒴 to produce class labels or molecular energy values.

## Method

The paper derives layer-wise recursive relations for the NTK and NNGP of group convolutional layers, lifting layers, group pooling layers, and pointwise nonlinearities, extending the standard NTK recursion to feature maps on a general symmetry group G. For roto-translations C_n ⋊ R², the recursions reduce to efficient computations via the A-operator (a convolution-based averaging over filter support), while for SO(3) they become purely algebraic equations in Wigner-matrix Fourier coefficients. A key theoretical result proves that a non-equivariant network trained with full G-data augmentation yields the same expected prediction as a corresponding group convolutional network trained without augmentation, at all training times and even off-manifold, in the infinite-width limit. The authors implement these recursions as new layers in the neural-tangents package and validate convergence of finite-width Monte-Carlo NTK estimates to the analytical expressions.

## Results

The paper's main empirical results are presented as plots rather than a single numerical table. For histological image classification (9 tissue classes, 32×32 pixels), the C₄×ℝ²-invariant GCNN NTK achieves higher test accuracy than the conventional CNN NTK across all training set sizes, with the gap widening as more data is available (Figure 2). For molecular energy regression on QM9, the SO(3)-invariant GCNN NTK yields considerably lower MAE than the conventional MLP NTK across a range of training set sizes (Figure 3). Additionally, the Monte-Carlo NTK estimates for finite-width equivariant networks converge to the analytical infinite-width expressions as width grows (Figure 1), and the L² distance between data-augmented CNN ensembles and GCNN ensembles on MNIST decreases with increasing ensemble size, confirming the theoretical equivalence at finite width (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares equivariant NTKs against their non-equivariant counterparts in two settings. For histological image classification (C₄×ℝ² symmetry), the rotation-invariant GCNN NTK outperforms the conventional CNN NTK (from Arora et al., 2019) in test accuracy at all training set sizes, with the equivariant kernel showing better scaling behavior as more training data is added (Figure 2). For molecular energy prediction on QM9 (SO(3) symmetry), the SO(3)-invariant GCNN NTK achieves a considerable MAE reduction over the standard MLP NTK (from Jacot et al., 2018) across the tested range of training set sizes (Figure 3). In both cases, the equivariant kernel's advantage is attributed to the built-in symmetry constraint, which is particularly beneficial when the symmetry group is continuous (SO(3)) and thus cannot be fully captured by finite data augmentation. The paper also validates that at finite width, ensembles of data-augmented CNNs progressively converge to ensembles of GCNNs, with the L² distance between their logits decreasing as ensemble size grows on both MNIST and CIFAR-10 (Figure 4 and Appendix F.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK framework from Jacot et al. (2018) and the CNN-specific NTK of Arora et al. (2019) to the general setting of group convolutional layers (Cohen & Welling, 2016), deriving layer-wise recursive relations for group convolutions, lifting layers, group pooling, and pointwise nonlinearities. It complements Gerken & Kessel (2024), who showed that ensembles of data-augmented networks yield equivariant mean predictions, by proving a stronger and more specific result: the ensemble mean of an augmented non-equivariant network equals the ensemble mean of a particular GCNN architecture at all training times. The specialization to SO(3) and C_n × R² builds on the Fourier-space machinery of Cohen et al. (2018) and the efficient A-operator from Xiao et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing empirical debate between equivariant architectures and data augmentation by showing they are theoretically equivalent at infinite width (in expectation, at all training times, even off-manifold), providing a clean theoretical foundation for the field. Practically, the equivariant NTK implementations for roto-translations and SO(3) offer a new kernel method that outperforms non-equivariant counterparts on histological image classification and molecular energy prediction. Key limitations include the infinite-width restriction of the equivalence theorem, the restriction to invariant (not equivariant) outputs in the main theorems, and the fact that finite-width agreement is only approximate. Open questions include extending the equivalence to equivariant (non-invariant) outputs, which would require new layer types beyond those presented.
