## Problem

Procrustes-Wasserstein (PW) was introduced as an OT distance invariant to rigid transformations, but it was never formally proven to satisfy the axioms of a metric on any well-defined space, and PW barycenters had never been defined or estimated from data. This gap limits PW's applicability to tasks requiring mean shapes or clustering in the space of point clouds, where standard Wasserstein is sensitive to pose and Gromov-Wasserstein barycenters remain abstract distance matrices rather than geometric point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D or 3D point clouds (matrices X ∈ R^{n×d}) each paired with a probability histogram p ∈ Σ_n, assumed pre-centered at their Euclidean barycenter and scaled to the unit ball (no translation or scaling invariance is handled). For barycenter computation, r such point clouds with their weights are provided, and the algorithm returns a barycenter point cloud X* ∈ R^{n×d} with weights p, along with the r optimal transport plans Γ*_j and orthogonal alignment matrices P*_j. For the archaeological application, inputs are ~10k-vertex 3D scans of astragalus bones, and the output is a sequence of interpolated 3D shapes parameterized by η ∈ [0,1].

## Method

The paper first defines a quotient space M_d/~ of discrete probability measures, where two measures are equivalent if their supports differ by a row permutation and a rigid transformation (orthogonal matrix), and proves PW₂ is a genuine distance on this quotient (Theorem 2.1). The PW barycenter is then defined as the minimizer of the average PW² distance to r input measures; the location update uses a Newton-Raphson step that computes weighted barycenters of each input cloud's points via the optimal transport plan and then applies the corresponding optimal orthogonal alignment P*, yielding the closed-form update X ← diag(p⁻¹)Γ*YP*. The weights are optimized via a dual LP formulation analogous to Cuturi & Doucet (2014). The inner PW sub-problem is solved by alternating EMD (for the transport plan) and SVD (for the orthogonal matrix), and the authors benchmark four initialization strategies—Euc-GW, Geo-GW, Fiedler-vector Wasserstein, and UPCA-Wasserstein—finding Fiedler-W to be the most robust for 2D/3D point cloud matching.

## Results

On the MNIST point-cloud clustering task (50 clouds, k=5), Procrustes-Wasserstein (PW) achieves an Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 0.7669 and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) of 0.8361, substantially outperforming all baselines. PW also produces more representative centroids for all five digit classes, with particularly strong separation for digits 0, 1, and 3. Computationally, PW runs in 130 s, which is 5.2× faster than Euc-GW (675 s) and 2.9× faster than Geo-GW (379 s), though slower than EMD (9 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PW decisively outperforms both baseline families on both clustering metrics. Against EMD (standard Wasserstein / Earth Mover's Distance), PW improves ARI from 0.4069 to 0.7669 (+0.36) and NMI from 0.5652 to 0.8361 (+0.27); EMD consistently fails to identify digit 3. Against Gromov-Wasserstein with Euclidean distances (Euc-GW), PW gains +0.22 ARI (0.7669 vs 0.5500) and +0.15 NMI (0.8361 vs 0.6815) while being 5.2× faster. Against Gromov-Wasserstein with geodesic distances (Geo-GW), the margin is even larger: +0.39 ARI and +0.26 NMI, with PW also running 2.9× faster. The paper notes that PW's advantage over EMD is particularly notable because the input point clouds are already roughly aligned (no random rotations applied), highlighting that the optimal rotation search in PW still yields meaningful gains even in near-aligned settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Procrustes-Wasserstein framework originally developed for aligning word embeddings (Grave et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2017) to the geometric setting of 2D/3D point clouds, and builds directly on the Wasserstein barycenter machinery of Cuturi & Doucet (2014) for the location and weight updates. It positions PW as a middle ground between standard Wasserstein (which is sensitive to isometries) and Gromov-Wasserstein (which is isometry-invariant but computationally expensive and yields barycenters as distance matrices rather than point clouds). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a quotient space of discrete measures modulo rigid transformations and permutations, on which PW is formally proven to be a distance — a result absent from all prior PW formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing PW as a bona fide distance and defining PW barycenters, the paper fills a foundational gap that limits the use of PW in any task requiring metric properties (clustering, interpolation, k-means). The practical advantage over GW barycenters — producing point clouds directly in the original feature space rather than distance matrices requiring MDS/TSNE — is significant for shape analysis where geometric fidelity matters. Limitations include the assumption of centered and unit-scaled data (no translation or scaling invariance), the non-convexity of the barycenter problem requiring local minima, and the requirement of a fixed number of barycenter points. Open questions include whether entropic regularization (sketched in the appendix) yields meaningfully smoother barycenters at scale, and whether the Fiedler-vector initialization advantage generalizes beyond the tested 2D/3D shapes.
