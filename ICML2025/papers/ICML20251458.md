## Problem

Existing benchmarks comparing RAG and long-context (LC) LLMs produce contradictory conclusions due to systematic flaws: insufficient context lengths (often under 10k tokens), data leakage from training corpora, problematic truncation of over-length contexts, and unreliable automated metrics like F1/EM. This paper identifies these specific pipeline failures and asks whether a properly designed benchmark can yield actionable routing guidelines for when to use RAG versus LC.

## Input / Output

Each test case pairs a naturally occurring long document (novel, financial statement, or academic paper) at either ~32k or ~128k tokens with a single question from one of four task types. For LC evaluation, the full document is fed to the model; for RAG, the document is chunked at 600 tokens with 100-token overlap, and 5 chunks are retrieved via hybrid search (GTE-large-en-v1.5 embeddings + BM25) before generation. The output is a binary correctness judgment produced by GPT-4o as judge, validated against human annotation via Cohen's Kappa (κ > 0.89 across all task-model combinations).

## Method

LaRA comprises 2,326 QA pairs across three context types and four tasks (location, reasoning, comparison, hallucination detection), constructed via a GPT-4o in-context learning pipeline with iterative seed refinement until a 90% human-validated pass rate is reached. To prevent data leakage on novels, a three-stage GPT-4o entity replacement pipeline extracts character names (including aliases and epithets), disambiguates them using the novel title, and substitutes consistent fictitious names. Long-context annotation is decomposed into ~10k-token segments to ensure uniform answer distribution and enable 'lost in the middle' analysis. The empirical study evaluates 11 LLMs (7 open-source, 4 proprietary) under both RAG and LC at 32k and 128k, revealing that the optimal routing depends on model strength, context length, task type, and context type—e.g., RAG outperforms LC by up to 38% for weak models at 128k, while LC dominates for strong models on reasoning and comparison tasks.

## Results

On LaRA (2,326 test cases, 4 tasks, 3 context types), the average accuracy gap between LC and RAG reverses with context length: at 32k, LC outperforms RAG by 2.40% overall, while at 128k, RAG outperforms LC by 3.68%. The effect is model-dependent: weaker models gain substantially from RAG at 128k (e.g., Mistral-Nemo-12B improves 38.12% with RAG over LC; Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct improves 6.48%), whereas strong models like GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet favor LC. Task-level gaps are large: RAG leads hallucination detection by 22.36% at 128k, while LC leads comparison by 14.30% and reasoning by 1.39% at the same length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is RAG (GTE-large-en-v1.5 embeddings + BM25 hybrid search, 600-token chunks, 5 chunks retrieved) versus full long-context (LC) input, evaluated across 11 LLMs. At 32k context, LC wins overall by 2.40% on average, driven by strong margins in comparison (+15.22%) and reasoning (+4.66%), while RAG wins hallucination detection (−10.38%). At 128k, the overall gap flips to RAG's favor (−3.68%), with hallucination detection showing the largest RAG advantage (−22.36%) and comparison still favoring LC (+14.30%). Model strength is the dominant moderator: Mistral-Nemo-12B gains 38.12% overall accuracy from RAG at 128k (15.21% LC → 53.33% RAG), and Llama-3.2-3B gains 6.48%, whereas GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet remain 3–9% better with LC. The paper also re-evaluates ∞-bench, showing that after fixing truncation and using LLM-as-judge instead of F1/EM, Qwen-2.5-7B can outperform GPT-4o on En.MC (87.9% vs. 76.0%), undermining prior conclusions that LC universally dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the RAG-vs-long-context comparison space by identifying and fixing specific methodological flaws in prior benchmarks (∞-bench, Qasper, NarrativeQA, QuALITY) rather than building on a single prior method. It departs from Xu et al. (ICLR 2024), Li et al. (EMNLP 2024), and Yu et al. (2024), which reached contradictory conclusions due to issues like insufficient context lengths, data leakage, middle-truncation artifacts, and unreliable F1/EM metrics. LaRA also distinguishes itself from concurrent long-context RAG work (LONG²RAG, Loong) by using naturally occurring 32k/128k documents, LLM-as-judge evaluation validated via Cohen's Kappa, and four task types designed to probe distinct capabilities rather than homogeneous query patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that no single routing strategy (always-RAG or always-LC) is optimal, and that the best choice depends on model strength, context length, and task type—gives system designers concrete criteria for building hybrid RAG/LC routers. The finding that RAG consistently outperforms LC on hallucination detection even for frontier models like GPT-4o (56.34% accuracy) challenges the assumption that stronger models inherently know their epistemic limits. Limitations include a single RAG configuration (GTE-large-en-v1.5, 600-token chunks, 5 chunks), English-only contexts, and evaluation limited to 11 models; open questions include whether the routing guidelines generalize to multilingual settings, streaming/interactive contexts, and whether more sophisticated retrieval (e.g., iterative or agentic RAG) would close the comparison-task gap.
