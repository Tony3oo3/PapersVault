## Problem

Prototypical self-supervised learning methods rely on a single learnable prototype per pseudo-cluster, which under-represents the feature space region it is meant to describe. The common remedy of over-clustering (K ≫ C) reduces images per prototype, biases toward simpler features, and still leaves gaps in coverage. The paper asks whether a non-parametric, multi-embedding representation of each region can replace single prototypes while eliminating the need for Sinkhorn-Knopp or centering regularizers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-crop RGB views (224×224 and 96×96) through a ViT encoder, producing a [CLS] token and L patch embeddings per view. Rather than comparing views to fixed learnable prototypes, it maintains two FIFO memory banks (E_C of size 65536 for [CLS] tokens, E_P of size 8192 for patch tokens) that are updated each iteration. At every step, anchors are randomly sampled from these memories and k nearest-neighbor support embeddings are retrieved via spherical k-NN, forming dynamic, non-parametric SOP structures that change across iterations.

## Method

SOP replaces each learnable prototype with a set of k+1 non-parametric support embeddings (one anchor plus k nearest neighbors) drawn from a running memory bank. The view-to-SOP probability is computed as a softmax over cosine similarities to all SEs, then aggregated per SOP using soft contribution weights proportional to each SE's similarity to its anchor, yielding a weighted vote that better characterizes the local region. Two non-parametric losses are combined: a global [CLS] contrastive loss (Eq. 2) that aligns view distributions across augmented views, and a novel SOP-MIM loss (Eq. 4) that reconstructs masked patch tokens by matching their non-parametric SOP-based distributions to those of unmasked tokens. Random anchor selection at each iteration acts as a built-in regularizer against collapse, removing the need for Sinkhorn-Knopp, centering, or sharpening, and the method is robust to the number of SOPs (1024–16384) without the over-clustering sensitivity of prototypical baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1M k-NN evaluation, SOP with ViT-L/16 achieves 79.2% top-1 accuracy, surpassing iBOT (78.0%) by +1.2% and matching I-JEPA's ViT-H/14 (79.3%) with fewer parameters. On linear probing, SOP ViT-L/16 reaches 81.2% top-1, outperforming iBOT (81.0%) and I-JEPA ViT-L/16 (77.5%). On image retrieval (Revisited Oxford/Paris), SOP's frozen ViT-B features achieve 42.7/17.5 mAP on Medium/Hard splits, improving over iBOT by +5.9/+3.2 mAP. On COCO object detection, SOP achieves 51.4 AP^b and 44.3 AP^m with Mask R-CNN, edging out iBOT (51.2/44.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOP consistently outperforms iBOT on k-NN across all backbone sizes: +0.1% (ViT-S), +1.1% (ViT-B), and +1.2% (ViT-L), with the gap widening as model complexity increases. On linear probing, SOP matches iBOT on ViT-S (77.9%) but leads on ViT-B (+0.4%) and ViT-L (+0.2%). Against DINO, SOP gains +1.1% k-NN on ViT-B and +2.1% on ViT-L, while on linear probing the advantage is larger (+1.7% ViT-B). On image retrieval, SOP's frozen features dramatically outperform iBOT by +5.9 mAP on Revisited Oxford Medium and +3.2 mAP on Hard splits, and similarly on Revisited Paris (+3.2/+4.7 mAP). SOP also surpasses the supervised DeiT baseline on k-NN for ViT-B (78.2 vs 81.0 is a loss, but on 1% semi-supervised SOP leads 69.5 vs 75.6 is a loss; however on 100% fine-tuning SOP matches at 84.2 vs 84.0 for iBOT). Against I-JEPA, SOP's ViT-L/16 (81.2% linear) exceeds I-JEPA's ViT-L/16 (77.5%) by +3.7% and approaches I-JEPA's larger ViT-H/14 (79.3%) with fewer parameters. On dense prediction (COCO detection, ADE20K segmentation), SOP achieves modest but consistent gains over iBOT (+0.2 AP^b, +0.6 mIoU fine-tuned), and on robustness (ImageNet-9 background changes) SOP leads iBOT by +1.4 to +3.4 points across six of seven corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-parametric SSL line initiated by Silva et al. (ICML 2024), which replaced learned prototypes with memory-bank representations, by introducing Self-Organizing Prototypes (SOPs) that group multiple semantically similar support embeddings (SEs) around a randomly sampled anchor to represent a local region in feature space. It directly adapts the two-task framework of iBOT (cluster assignment + Masked Image Modeling) into non-parametric forms, replacing the learnable online tokenizer with a FIFO memory of patch embeddings and the parametric prototype loss with a weighted combination of SE similarity scores. The key conceptual departure from DINO/iBOT/DINOv2 is that SOPs are dynamic, stochastic, and non-parametric: rather than optimizing fixed prototypes that must encode all features of a cluster, SOPs exploit local data structure to bootstrap complementary feature sets, thereby addressing the under-representation and over-clustering problems inherent in prototypical SSL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that non-parametric SSL can be stable without the Sinkhorn-Knopp, centering, or sharpening regularizers that prototypical methods require, and that it alleviates the over-clustering problem (1024 SOPs suffice where iBOT needs 65536 prototypes). The finding that performance gains increase with model scale (ViT-S → ViT-B → ViT-L) and that SOP achieves SOTA retrieval performance (up to +3.2 mAP on Hard splits) suggests the approach may be particularly well-suited for large-scale pre-training. However, gains on dense prediction tasks remain modest (+0.2 AP^b, +0.4 mIoU), and the method still relies on a teacher-student momentum setup, leaving open whether fully non-parametric training without any moving-average teacher is feasible. The non-parametric MIM loss outperforming its parametric counterpart by 7.3% k-NN points is a notable result that may inform future MIM designs.
