## Problem

Controllable diffusion models like ControlNet rely on ad-hoc heuristics to select which network blocks receive conditional control signals, with no principled mechanism to determine the optimal block configuration per task or per denoising timestep. This leads to either redundant computation (activating all blocks) or insufficient control (activating too few), and the optimal design varies unpredictably across different spatial conditioning tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatial conditioning image (depth map, canny edge, or segmentation mask at 512×512 for SD1.5 or 1024×1024 for SD3.0) and a text prompt, producing a generated image via 20 denoising steps. Unusually, the computational path is input-dependent: at each timestep, a router unit inspects the current latent feature and decides per-block whether to activate the corresponding control branch, so the number of FLOPs consumed varies across timesteps and across different conditioning samples.

## Method

FlexControl extends ControlNet by copying all diffusion blocks (not just encoder blocks) into a trainable control branch with zero modules, then inserts a lightweight router unit before each control block. Each router produces a binary activation mask via a Gumbel-Sigmoid mechanism conditioned on the current latent feature, enabling differentiable end-to-end training while allowing hard skip decisions at inference. A computation-aware loss term penalizes deviation from a target sparsity γ (the ratio of active-block FLOPs to full-branch FLOPs), encouraging the router to activate control signals only where they benefit generation quality. The router architecture is adapted to the backbone: global average pooling plus an MLP for UNet, and a dual global/local feature encoding with weighted fusion for DiT. The entire router accounts for less than 1% of total parameters and is plug-and-play across dual-stream and LoRA-based control architectures.

## Results

On SD1.5 with γ=0.5 (≈50% sparsity), FlexControl achieves an average FID of 12.61 and average CLIP score of 0.3039 across depth map, canny edge, and segmentation mask tasks, outperforming ControlNet-Large (FID 14.05, CLIP 0.3026) which uses twice the FLOPs. For controllability, FlexControl reduces RMSE by 6.30% over ControlNet and 4.74% over ControlNet++ on depth maps, while improving mIoU by 9.87% and 3.16% respectively on segmentation masks. On the DiT-based SD3.0 backbone, FlexControl γ=0.7 achieves FID 20.54 and SSIM 0.4775, surpassing ControlNet-Large (FID 21.64, SSIM 0.4828) at lower FLOPs (4.35T vs 6.22T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexControl γ=0.5 (SD1.5) outperforms all baselines on FID across all three conditioning types: it beats ControlNet (17.76→11.65 FID on depth), ControlNet++ (16.66→11.65), T2I-Adapter (22.52→11.65), and GLIGEN (18.36→11.65). Against the strongest baseline ControlNet-Large, which replicates the full SD model as a control branch, FlexControl still wins on FID (12.45→11.65 depth, 16.78→14.80 segmentation) and CLIP score (0.2796→0.2842 segmentation) while using only ~50% of the FLOPs (280G vs 561G). On controllability metrics, FlexControl achieves the best RMSE (0.2358) and mIoU (0.3751) but is marginally behind ControlNet-Large on SSIM (0.5612 vs 0.5642) for canny edges. When extended to the lightweight ControlNeXt architecture, FlexControlNeXt γ=0.7 improves FID from 24.16 to 23.71 and mIoU from 0.2825 to 0.2841. On the LoRA-based OminiControl (FLUX.1), FlexOminiControl γ=0.7 reduces FID from 22.84 to 22.39 and improves SSIM from 0.4125 to 0.4146, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the router across dual-stream and LoRA-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexControl extends the ControlNet dual-stream architecture (Zhang et al., 2023b) by replacing its fixed, heuristic block-selection strategy with a trainable, per-timestep router that dynamically decides which control blocks to activate. It draws on the dynamic neural network literature (early-exit, token sparsification) for the routing mechanism and on Gumbel-Sigmoid for differentiable discrete decisions, while introducing a novel computation-aware loss that explicitly targets a desired FLOPs sparsity. The method is demonstrated as a plug-and-play extension across UNet (SD1.5), DiT (SD3.0), lightweight (ControlNeXt), and LoRA-based (OminiControl/FLUX.1) architectures, positioning it as a general-purpose layer atop existing controllable diffusion pipelines rather than a standalone model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a ~50% sparsity level (FlexControl γ=0.5) matches or exceeds the quality of ControlNet-Large (all blocks active) while roughly halving the trainable-branch FLOPs, suggesting that most fixed control signals in current pipelines are redundant. This opens the door to cheaper, task-adaptive controllable generation without manual architecture search. Open questions include whether the router generalizes to unseen conditioning types without retraining, whether the global FLOPs-ratio loss can be replaced by per-block or per-timestep granularity for finer control, and how the approach behaves under very few denoising steps (e.g., 4–10) where the early/late stage distinction compresses.
