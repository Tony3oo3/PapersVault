## Problem

Existing one-shot LLM pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) rely on heuristic approximations of loss change or weight-activation products, lacking any theoretical guarantee of optimality. Optimization-based alternatives (ADMM, FISTA) either still lack convergence guarantees or introduce bias by relaxing the non-convex ℓ₀ problem to an ℓ₁ surrogate. This paper closes the gap by formulating pruning as an ℓ₀-penalized optimization and providing rigorous convergence, convergence-rate, and statistical risk bounds for the resulting algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method operates layer-by-layer on a pre-trained LLM: for each layer ℓ, it receives the original weight matrix (d₁×d₂), a calibration matrix X (128 rows from C4, where each row is the activation output of the preceding pruned layers, column-normalized via (XᵀX)^{-1/2}), and a target sparsity level s. The output is a sparse weight matrix with at most s non-zero entries per layer, assembled into a fully pruned model. Sparsity is controlled adaptively through a per-iteration penalty coefficient λ that increases or decreases based on the gap between current and target sparsity, rather than being fixed a priori.

## Method

mAIHT solves the ℓ₀-penalized objective ½‖XŴ − XW‖²_F + λ‖W‖₀ via a proximal gradient scheme where the proximal operator of λ‖·‖₀ reduces to element-wise hard thresholding at √(2αλ). The key acceleration introduces a momentum term Y^k combining the previous two iterates, and a monitoring variable V^{k+1} (a plain proximal gradient step from W^k) that gates acceptance: the accelerated step Z^{k+1} is used only if its objective value does not exceed V^{k+1}, guaranteeing monotone descent. An adaptive λ update rule (λ^{k+1} = λ^k(1 + (‖W^k‖₀ − s)/(d₁d₂))) drives the solution toward the exact target sparsity without requiring a grid search. After the iterative phase stabilizes the support, a short projected gradient descent refines the retained weights. Theoretically, the paper proves convergence to α-fixed points, an O(1/k) rate for vanilla IHT, a faster sub-linear rate for mAIHT via the KL property, and population risk upper bounds under the Restricted Isometry Property showing the excess risk is bounded by a constant multiple of the optimal sparse risk plus vanishing optimization and generalization terms.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B pruned to 50% sparsity, mAIHT achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 7.072, outperforming ADMM (7.083), SparseGPT (7.240), Wanda (7.259), and Magnitude Pruning (17.286). Across zero-shot tasks, mAIHT attains the best mean accuracy at every sparsity level from 0.2 to 0.5, reaching 59.79% at 50% sparsity versus 59.05% (ADMM), 58.68% (SparseGPT), and 58.35% (Wanda). At 10% sparsity, mAIHT (5.693) is marginally behind ADMM (5.692) but still ahead of SparseGPT and Wanda.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mAIHT outperforms all four baselines at sparsity levels 0.2–0.5 on both WikiText2 perplexity and zero-shot mean accuracy. Against Magnitude Pruning, the advantage is dramatic: at 50% sparsity, mAIHT achieves 7.07 perplexity versus MP's 17.29, and 59.79% mean zero-shot accuracy versus MP's 50.75%. Against Wanda and SparseGPT, mAIHT consistently leads by 0.17–0.19 perplexity points at 50% sparsity (7.07 vs. 7.26 and 7.24) and by 1.1–1.4 percentage points in mean zero-shot accuracy. Against ADMM (the closest competitor), mAIHT wins at 0.2–0.5 sparsity by small but consistent margins (e.g., 7.072 vs. 7.083 perplexity at 50%), while at 10% sparsity ADMM edges out mAIHT by 0.0003 perplexity points (5.6925 vs. 5.6928), a negligible difference. On individual zero-shot tasks, mAIHT achieves the top or second-best score on most benchmarks across all sparsity levels, with particularly strong gains on RTE (e.g., 58.12% vs. 54.51% for ADMM at 50% sparsity) and OBQA (41.80% vs. 40.80%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the iterative hard-thresholding (IHT) framework of Blumensath & Davies (2008) and the monotone accelerated proximal gradient technique of Li & Lin (2015), applying them to the ℓ₀-penalized layer-wise pruning formulation introduced by Meng et al. (2024a, ALPS). It departs from the dominant one-shot pruning paradigm (SparseGPT, Wanda) that relies on heuristic approximations of loss change, and from ADMM-based methods that relax ℓ₀ to ℓ₁, by instead solving the non-convex ℓ₀ problem directly via proximal gradient with hard thresholding. The key conceptual departure is providing a full theoretical treatment—convergence, convergence rates via KL properties, and risk upper bounds under RIP—for a non-convex ℓ₀-penalized pruning objective, a gap that prior heuristic and relaxed-optimization methods left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantees (convergence to α-fixed points, sub-linear convergence rates, and RIP-based risk upper bounds) give practitioners confidence that the pruning solution is near-optimal rather than merely heuristic, which is valuable for safety-critical or resource-constrained deployments. However, the empirical gains over SparseGPT and Wanda are modest (e.g., ~0.17 perplexity improvement at 50% sparsity on LLaMA-7B), and the method is roughly 9× slower than Wanda, limiting its practical adoption where speed is paramount. Open questions include extending the theoretical analysis to structured sparsity (n:m, block, row), non-uniform layerwise sparsity schedules, and higher sparsity regimes (>50%) where the method's advantage diminishes. The connection that one iteration of mAIHT with normalization reduces to Wanda is a useful unifying observation but does not fundamentally change the landscape.
