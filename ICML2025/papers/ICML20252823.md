## Problem

Unsupervised Graph Domain Adaptation (UGDA) must handle simultaneous distribution shifts in both node attributes and graph topology, but existing methods either entangle these two shifts in a single embedding alignment or rely on topology structure models (e.g., CSBM) and pseudo-label estimation for topology alignment. The expressiveness of the topology model and the inaccuracy of pseudo-labels fundamentally limit the quality of topology alignment, creating a gap that this paper addresses by decoupling the two alignment objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (node attribute matrix, adjacency matrix, and node class labels) and an unlabeled target graph (node attribute matrix and adjacency matrix) as input, and produces node-level class labels for the target graph. The spectral GNN backbone is parameterized by K-order Bernstein polynomial coefficients θ_k that define the frequency response of the graph filter, and these coefficients are the primary parameters aligned across domains. The method operates at the node level and requires no pseudo-labels on the target graph.

## Method

DGSDA disentangles the graph domain adaptation problem into two sequential steps: (1) node attribute alignment via MMD in the embedding space, and (2) topology alignment converted into model alignment by directly aligning the Bernstein polynomial coefficients of a spectral GNN (BernNet) between source and target domains. Rather than aligning topology structures with a separate model, the method aligns the K-order Bernstein polynomial coefficients θ_k that parameterize the spectral filter h(λ), which captures low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass frequency responses. The overall loss combines source classification, spectral coefficient alignment (with L1 regularization on |θ_k|), MMD for attribute alignment, and target-domain entropy minimization. Theoretical analysis proves that the Lipschitz constant of the spectral filter is determined by the ground-truth function being approximated (not the polynomial itself), justifying the coefficient regularization as a mechanism to tighten the domain adaptation bound.

## Results

On citation networks (Table 1), DGSDA achieves the best mean accuracy across all six domain pairs, with headline results of 83.57% on A→C and 82.92% on D→C, outperforming the strongest baseline A2GNN by 2.89% and 3.03% respectively. On social networks (Table 2), DGSDA wins 3 of 4 tasks, with a particularly large 17.45% gain over the second-best method on Blog B1→B2 (65.64% vs. 48.19%). On airport traffic networks (Table 3), DGSDA leads on 4 of 6 domain pairs but trails JHGDA on the Brazil↔Europe pairs due to the limited scale of those graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGSDA outperforms all ten baselines on every citation network domain pair, with the largest margin being 6.33% over JHGDA on D→C (82.92% vs. 76.59%). The closest competitor is A2GNN (propagation-tailored GDA), which DGSDA surpasses by 2.9–3.0% across citation tasks; structure-shift methods (StruRW, JHGDA, KBL, PairAlign) trail by 4–10% on most pairs. On social networks, DGSDA dominates the Blog tasks by over 17% (65.64% vs. 48.19% for StruRW on B1→B2), while on Twitch DE↔EN it leads by 1–3% over the best baseline. On airport traffic networks, DGSDA wins 4 of 6 pairs but loses to JHGDA on B→E (49.92% vs. 54.39%) and E→B (61.37% vs. 70.12%), which the authors attribute to the small graph sizes (131 and 399 nodes) causing over-adaptation to dominant hubs. Source-only baselines (GCN, GAT) and node-embedding GDA methods (DANE, UDAGCN, AdaGCN) are consistently the weakest, confirming that structural alignment is critical for graph domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGSDA builds on the UGDA literature (StruRW, PairAlign, JHGDA, KBL) that addresses topology shifts, but departs fundamentally by replacing explicit topology structure alignment with spectral filter (model) alignment. It leverages BernNet's Bernstein polynomial approximation to parameterize graph spectral filters, and draws on the theoretical DA bound framework from SpecReg (You et al., 2023) to justify coefficient regularization. The key conceptual departure is the explicit disentanglement of attribute and topology distribution shifts, converting the topology alignment problem into a parameter alignment problem that avoids the need for topology structure models (e.g., CSBM) and pseudo-label estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement strategy offers a cleaner and more generalizable framework for UGDA by decoupling well-studied attribute alignment from the harder topology alignment, and the spectral filter alignment avoids the fragility of pseudo-labels (confirmed empirically to hurt performance). The theoretical result that Bernstein polynomial Lipschitz constants are determined by the ground-truth function rather than the polynomial itself provides a principled justification for the regularization term. Limitations include restriction to node-level classification, dependence on spectral GNNs as the backbone, and degraded performance on very small-scale graphs (Brazil-Europe airport). Open questions include whether the spectral filter alignment idea extends to graph-level or edge-level tasks, and whether non-spectral GNNs can benefit from analogous parameter alignment.
