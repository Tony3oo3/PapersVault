## Problem

ViT-based 3D pose estimators suffer from two persistent weaknesses: predictions frequently misalign with 2D image evidence (e.g., globally flipped hands or wrongly oriented bodies under occlusion and blur), and the architecture is restricted to single-frame inference, ignoring temporal context that would stabilize video predictions. Existing video-based methods work around this by stacking separate temporal modules on static frame features, failing to fully exploit spatiotemporal interactions at every layer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (or T-frame video sequences) alongside 2D skeleton pose images rendered from off-the-shelf ViTPose estimates, where joints appear as colored circles connected by gradient-colored bones on a blank background with confidence encoded via alpha blending. When T=1 the task reduces to image-based HPE; when T>1 it becomes video-based HPE with temporal attention. Outputs are SMPL/MANO pose parameters θ, shape β, 3D mesh vertices, 3D joint coordinates, and per-frame camera projection parameters, all decoded from the enhanced ViT features.

## Method

EXTPOSE extends a pre-trained ViT backbone (HMR2.0 or HaMeR) with two orthogonal attention extensions while sharing all parameters and introducing no extra modules. First, a dual-stream cross-modal attention concatenates image and 2D-skeleton-image token streams before self-attention, so each token attends to both intra- and cross-modal tokens in a unified space, enabling the image stream to query precise keypoint locations from the skeleton and the skeleton stream to recover missing RGB texture for depth disambiguation. Second, for video, 2D spatial attention is inflated to 3D spatiotemporal attention over T×M tokens, with an attention mask (Eq. 11) that restricts cross-frame attention to within each modality stream while cross-modal attention operates within each frame. The 2D pose is represented as a skeleton image rather than 1D coordinates or heatmaps, allowing it to pass through the same patch embedding and positional encoding as RGB, which yields faster convergence and 4.6% lower PA-MPJPE. Training uses standard HPE losses (3D joint, parameter, 2D reprojection, adversarial) with each modality randomly masked at 50% probability to preserve single-modality robustness.

## Results

On 3DPW (human pose), EXTPOSE achieves 35.5 mm PA-MPJPE in the image setting (T=1), improving over HMR2.0 (44.4 mm) by 12.3%, and 34.0 mm in the video setting (T=16), outperforming WHAM (35.9 mm) by 5.3%. On FreiHAND (hand pose), EXTPOSE reaches 4.9 mm PA-MPJPE, a 14.0% improvement over the ViT baseline HaMeR (6.0 mm). On HO3D video, EXTPOSE (T=16) achieves 6.9 mm PA-MPJPE, a 26.6% improvement over Deformer (9.4 mm), and on Human3.6M it reaches 27.2 mm PA-MPJPE with 16 frames, surpassing HMR2.0 (33.6 mm) by 19.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXTPOSE outperforms all model-based 3D HPE baselines across every benchmark. On 3DPW image, it beats the closest ViT-based competitor HMR2.0 (44.4 mm) by 8.9 mm PA-MPJPE and TokenHMR (44.3 mm) by 8.8 mm; on 3DPW video, it surpasses WHAM (35.9 mm, which also uses 2D poses) by 1.9 mm. On FreiHAND, EXTPOSE (4.9 mm) improves over HaMeR (6.0 mm) by 1.1 mm and MeshGraphormer (5.9 mm) by 1.0 mm PA-MPJPE. On HO3D video, the 26.6% PA-MPJPE gain over Deformer (9.4 mm → 6.9 mm) is the largest relative improvement in the paper. On Human3.6M, EXTPOSE with only 16 frames (27.2 mm) matches or exceeds lifting methods that use 243 frames (e.g., MixSTE at 30.6 mm, P-STMO at 34.4 mm). The one comparison where EXTPOSE trails is the HInt 2D PCK metric, where the dedicated 2D estimator ViTPose (66.5/86.5/93.1) still outperforms EXTPOSE's 2D projection (59.6/84.8/92.7), which the authors attribute to the generalization gap inherent in model-based 3D HPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

EXTPOSE extends the ViT-based HPE lineage (HMR2.0 for humans, HaMeR for hands) by augmenting their self-attention with two orthogonal extensions: cross-modal attention between RGB image tokens and 2D skeleton-image tokens, and cross-frame attention for video sequences. It departs from prior video-based HPE methods (VIBE, TCMR, WHAM) that extract static frame features and bolt on separate temporal modules (RNNs, additional transformers), instead performing full 3D spatiotemporal attention within a single shared backbone. The use of rendered skeleton images as the 2D pose representation is inspired by conditional generation (ControlNet, Animate Anyone), but repurposed here for pose estimation rather than synthesis, and the dual-stream cross-modal fusion replaces prior channel-concatenation, late-fusion, or lifting-paradigm approaches with a parameter-free attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical 'extension' paradigm: practitioners can upgrade existing ViT HPE models to handle video and 2D-assisted inference without retraining from scratch, reusing pre-trained weights and adding no new parameters. The finding that video-based EXTPOSE outperforms its image-based counterpart (contrary to the prior trend of video underperforming image methods due to over-smoothing) suggests that full spatiotemporal attention within a unified ViT can resolve the depth/occlusion ambiguities that plague frame-by-frame inference. Open questions include whether the approach scales to whole-body reconstruction, whether more powerful 2D priors (e.g., SAM 2 segmentation) can further close the gap on challenging failures (missing keypoints, severe blur), and how the method interacts with emerging efficient-attention and multi-view formulations.
