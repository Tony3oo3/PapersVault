## Problem

The two gold-standard dissimilarity measures for polygonal curves each have a critical flaw: the Fréchet distance is a true metric but is completely dominated by a single outlier vertex, while DTW is robust to outliers but violates the triangle inequality by a factor that grows with curve length. No existing measure simultaneously provides metric-like behavior and outlier robustness, and no dimension-free learning bounds existed for clustering curves under any of these measures.

## Input / Output

The method takes two polygonal curves in R^d (each a sequence of m vertices) and an integer parameter k (typically k ≪ m) and returns a single non-negative dissimilarity score. For the learning-theoretic results, the input is an i.i.d. sample of n curves from an arbitrary distribution supported on the unit Euclidean ball, and the output is a median curve minimizing the expected k-DTW cost. In the experimental setting, k-DTW scores feed into l-nearest-neighbor classification (predicting pass/fail on OULAD clickstream curves of complexity 294) and hierarchical agglomerative clustering with single or complete linkage.

## Method

k-DTW is defined as the minimum over all monotone traversals of the sum of the k largest vertex-matching distances, interpolating between Fréchet (k=1) and DTW (k ≥ 2m−1). The exact algorithm performs a parametric search over the z distinct pairwise vertex distances: for each candidate threshold E[l], it modifies the distance matrix by subtracting E[l] (clipped at zero) and runs standard O(m′m″) DTW, adding k·E[l] to recover the top-k sum; the minimum over all iterations yields the exact k-DTW in O(m′m″z) time. A (1+ε)-approximation reduces z to O(log(k/ε)/ε) by rounding distances to powers of (1+ε) using the discrete Fréchet distance as a k-approximation anchor, achieving near-quadratic time. On the learning-theory side, the paper proves the first dimension-free Rademacher and Gaussian complexity bounds for median-curve learning under k-DTW via terminal embeddings (reducing ambient dimension d to O(ε⁻² log(mn))) combined with a chaining argument, and shows a separation: k-DTW's complexity is strictly smaller than DTW's by a factor of Ω̃(√m) when k ≪ m.

## Results

On the OULAD dataset (n=275 clickstreams, complexity m=294), k-DTW with k=76 achieves the best AUC of 0.79772, outperforming Fréchet (0.73737) by 8.2% and standard DTW (0.78360) by 1.8%. The best k-DTW variant (k=72) also leads in accuracy (0.85520) and F1-score (0.91775). In synthetic agglomerative clustering experiments, k-DTW with k=O(log m) produces pure clusters with small intra-cluster distances, while DTW fails to separate curve types and Fréchet suffers from outlier-dominated inter-cluster distances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

k-DTW clearly outperforms Fréchet distance (Eiter & Mannila, 1994) on the OULAD classification task, achieving up to 8.2% higher AUC (0.79772 vs. 0.73737) and 1.8% higher accuracy, attributed to Fréchet's sensitivity to outlier vertices. Against standard DTW (Vintsyuk, 1968), k-DTW gains up to 1.8% AUC (0.79772 vs. 0.78360), with the advantage stemming from k-DTW's stronger relaxed triangle inequality (factor k vs. factor m^{1/4}). In synthetic clustering, k-DTW with k=O(log m) cleanly separates three curve types where DTW cannot distinguish type-A from type-C curves and Fréchet produces intra-cluster distances comparable to inter-cluster distances. The paper also benchmarks against weak Fréchet (Buchin et al., 2019c), edit distance with real penalty (Chen & Ng, 2004), partial window DTW (Sakoe & Chiba, 1978), and partial segment DTW (Tak & Hwang, 2007), finding k-DTW competitive with or slightly ahead of these baselines across all classification metrics. The authors note that a small set of k values {ln(m), √m, m/10, m/4} suffices in practice, with at least one k-DTW variant matching or beating all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces k-DTW as a principled interpolation between the Fréchet distance (k=1) and DTW (k ≥ 2m−1), drawing conceptual inspiration from the Ky-Fan norm for matrices and algorithmic structure from the top-k optimization framework of Bertsimas & Sim (2003). It departs from prior learning-theoretic work on curves (Conradi et al., 2024; Driemel et al., 2021), which relied on VC-dimension arguments with at least linear dependence on ambient dimension d, by establishing the first dimension-free Rademacher/Gaussian complexity bounds via terminal embeddings and chaining. The robustness analysis extends the classical breakdown-point framework of Lopuhaa & Rousseeuw (1991) from point estimators to curve-of-top-k-median estimators, a notion not previously considered for curves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-free learning bounds and the complexity separation (k-DTW strictly smaller than DTW by Ω̃(√m) when k ≪ m) provide the first theoretical justification for why a 'sparser' curve distance should generalize better with fewer samples, which is practically relevant for time-series and trajectory learning. The main open problem is algorithmic: the exact k-DTW algorithm runs in O(m²z) time (quartic in worst case), and the paper explicitly identifies improving the top-k optimization framework as a key challenge whose resolution would yield faster algorithms for k-DTW and related problems. Empirically, k-DTW yields modest but consistent improvements over both Fréchet and DTW (up to 8.2% AUC on OULAD), suggesting practical utility, though the gains are not dramatic and the parameter k requires some tuning.
