## Problem

Training normalizing flows with the reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence to sample Boltzmann distributions of molecular systems is prone to mode collapse at typical target temperatures (300 K), where free energy minima are too far separated for the mode-seeking objective to cover the full configurational space. Existing variational alternatives like FAB avoid collapse but require expensive annealed importance sampling with HMC transitions, making them computationally costly. The paper asks whether a simpler, more efficient variational strategy can resolve metastable states in increasingly complex molecular systems without mode collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a random latent vector drawn from a Gaussian/uniform prior and an unnormalized molecular energy function (a parameterized force field evaluated on internal coordinates: bond lengths, angles, and dihedral angles). It outputs 3D molecular conformations in internal coordinate space that approximate the Boltzmann distribution at the target temperature (300 K). The internal coordinate representation is system-specific and not transferable across molecules, and the flow constrains chirality and hydrogen permutation symmetries by restricting spline output ranges.

## Method

TA-BG is a two-phase training procedure for a normalizing flow built from 16 monotonic rational-quadratic spline coupling layers. In phase 1, the flow is trained at a high temperature (1200 K) using the reverse KLD, where reduced barrier heights between free energy minima prevent mode collapse. In phase 2, the distribution is iteratively annealed to the target temperature via a reweighting-based forward KLD objective: at each step, samples are drawn from the flow at the current temperature, importance weights are computed to transition to the next lower temperature, the dataset is resampled according to these weights, and forward KLD training is performed on the resampled buffer. The temperature schedule follows a geometric progression (9 annealing steps from 1200 K to 300 K), and a final fine-tuning iteration at the target temperature improves overlap. A regularized energy function caps extreme van der Waals values to stabilize training, and the largest energy contributions per batch are removed to prevent divergence.

## Results

On the three peptide systems (alanine dipeptide, tetrapeptide, hexapeptide), TA-BG achieves the best NLL across all systems (−213.665, −330.114, −501.511) while requiring up to ~3× fewer target energy evaluations than FAB (7.56×10⁷ vs 2.13×10⁸ for dipeptide/tetrapeptide; 3.08×10⁸ vs 4.2×10⁸ for hexapeptide). For the hexapeptide, TA-BG is the only variational method that accurately resolves all metastable states, achieving a RAM KLD of 8.63×10⁻³ versus 2.09×10⁻² for FAB. On the 2D GMM system, TA-BG (NLL 6.91, ESS 96.89%) matches FAB with neural splines (NLL 6.92, ESS 97.09%) and outperforms diffusion-based baselines (PIS: NLL 7.72; DDS: NLL 7.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FAB (the strongest variational baseline), TA-BG achieves better NLL on all three peptide systems (e.g., −501.511 vs −501.268 on hexapeptide) and better RAM KLD on tetrapeptide (2.61×10⁻³ vs 6.89×10⁻³) and hexapeptide (8.63×10⁻³ vs 2.09×10⁻²), while FAB retains a slight edge on RAM KLD for dipeptide (1.50×10⁻³ vs 1.97×10⁻³) and RAM KLD w. RW for tetrapeptide (1.25×10⁻³ vs 1.94×10⁻³). Critically, TA-BG requires only ~1/3 the target energy evaluations of FAB for dipeptide and tetrapeptide (7.56×10⁷ vs 2.13×10⁸) and ~73% for hexapeptide (3.08×10⁸ vs 4.2×10⁸). Against direct reverse KLD training at 300 K, TA-BG dramatically outperforms on RAM KLD (e.g., 8.63×10⁻³ vs 5.41×10⁻¹ on hexapeptide), confirming that the annealing workflow avoids the severe mode collapse that plagues direct reverse KLD. On the 2D GMM, TA-BG (NLL 6.91, ESS 96.89%) is essentially tied with FAB using neural splines (NLL 6.92, ESS 97.09%) and clearly outperforms diffusion-based samplers PIS (NLL 7.72), DDS (NLL 7.43), and iDEM (NLL 6.96, ESS 73.4%). The forward KLD baseline (trained on MD data) achieves comparable NLL but requires 50–55× more energy evaluations, highlighting TA-BG's data-free efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the normalizing flow framework (Dinh et al., 2015; Durkan et al., 2019) and the variational sampling paradigm for molecular systems, most directly extending the FAB method (Midgley et al., 2023b) which was the first to successfully sample multimodal Boltzmann distributions without mode collapse. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the reverse KLD—typically avoided due to its mode-seeking behavior—as a high-temperature pre-training objective, then switching to a mass-covering forward KLD with importance-weighted resampling for iterative annealing to the target temperature. The geometric temperature schedule draws from replica-exchange molecular dynamics (Sugita & Okamoto, 1999), and the approach is positioned against concurrent temperature-conditioned flow methods (Wahl et al., 2025; Schebek et al., 2024) that require MD samples or partition function estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a conceptually simple two-phase workflow (reverse KLD at high T, then reweighting-based forward KLD annealing) can match or exceed the more complex FAB method while requiring up to three times fewer energy evaluations, and is the only variational approach that accurately resolves metastable states in the hexapeptide. The introduction of alanine tetrapeptide and hexapeptide as new benchmarks significantly extends the field beyond the long-used alanine dipeptide. Open questions include whether the importance-sampling-based annealing scales to higher-dimensional systems (the authors discuss replacing IS with AIS), whether the approach transfers to more expensive energy evaluations (DFT, foundation models), and whether combining TA-BG's annealing with FAB's α-divergence training at high temperature could yield further gains.
