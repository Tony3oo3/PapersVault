## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods (e.g., NPO, RMU) are highly vulnerable to downstream fine-tuning: even fine-tuning on data entirely unrelated to the unlearning objective can rapidly recover forgotten knowledge, undermining the permanence that unlearning is supposed to provide. The paper asks how to define and integrate 'invariance' into LLM unlearning so that erased knowledge remains irretrievable through parameter updates from arbitrary future fine-tuning tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Zephyr-7B-beta or LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct), a forget set of targeted content to erase, a retain set for utility preservation, and a single unrelated fine-tuning dataset (e.g., GSM8K) used solely for invariance regularization during training. It outputs an unlearned LLM whose forget quality is preserved even after subsequent fine-tuning on new, unseen downstream tasks (math, sentiment analysis, paraphrase detection, etc.), while also maintaining or improving fine-tuning accuracy on those tasks. Notably, only one unrelated dataset is required at training time, and the resulting robustness generalizes to fine-tuning datasets never seen during invariance training.

## Method

ILU extends the standard LLM unlearning objective (NPO or RMU) with an invariance regularization term derived from IRMv1: it penalizes the squared ℓ₂-norm of the gradient of a downstream fine-tuning loss with respect to a virtual scalar predictor w, evaluated at w=1, effectively forcing the unlearned model to be stationary under fine-tuning perturbations. The key design choice is using a single fine-tuning dataset that is unrelated to the forget set (e.g., GSM8K for WMDP unlearning), which the paper shows outperforms both using the forget set itself (which creates an objective conflict) and using multiple fine-tuning sets (which introduces optimization complexity). Task vector analysis reveals the mechanism: ILU's unlearning direction remains near-orthogonal to the post-fine-tuning direction (cos ≈ 0.09), preserving the unlearning effect, whereas NPO's unlearning direction flips toward the fine-tuning direction (cos ≈ −0.41), causing knowledge recovery. The regularization is lightweight and integrates as a single additional gradient computation per training step, avoiding the computational overhead of meta-learning approaches like TAR.

## Results

On the WMDP benchmark with Zephyr-7B-beta, ILU(GSM8K) achieves an average robust accuracy (RA) of 0.65 for RMU-based unlearning and 0.56 for NPO-based unlearning, compared to 0.42 and 0.47 for the respective baselines without invariance regularization — a 23% average RA improvement over RMU across six downstream fine-tuning tasks. Fine-tuning accuracy (FA) is preserved or slightly improved (e.g., 82.32 vs. 82.43 for RMU+ILU vs. RMU), and pre-finetune forget quality (FQ) and MMLU utility remain comparable. On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, NPO+ILU matches TAR's average RA of 0.70 while requiring only 118 minutes of training versus TAR's 7,442 minutes (≈63× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NPO and RMU (the two primary SOTA unlearning baselines), ILU(GSM8K) substantially improves robust accuracy: RMU+ILU raises average RA from 0.42 to 0.65, and NPO+ILU from 0.47 to 0.56, while keeping fine-tuning accuracy within ~1 point. Against LAT (latent adversarial training), NPO+ILU achieves a higher average RA of 0.70 vs. 0.64 on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, indicating stronger fine-tuning resilience. Against TAR (tamper-resistant safeguards via meta-learning), NPO+ILU matches the average RA of 0.70 but at 118 minutes of training versus TAR's 7,442 minutes — a 63× computational advantage. Against SAM-based robustness variants (Fan et al., 2025), ILU shows slightly larger FQ degradation under relearning attacks (drop of 0.06 for NPO+ILU vs. 0.02 for NPO+SAM), though SAM explicitly optimizes sharpness with prior knowledge of the attack space, whereas ILU promotes invariance implicitly without attack-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a novel conceptual bridge between Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) and LLM unlearning, treating downstream fine-tuning as an 'environment' whose invariance can be promoted via a lightweight gradient-norm regularization. It builds directly on the IRMv1 formulation (Arjovsky et al., 2019) and integrates it with existing unlearning objectives (NPO, RMU) as a drop-in regularizer, avoiding the computationally expensive meta-learning / gradient-unrolling approach of TAR (Tamirisa et al., 2024). The paper positions itself against the growing body of evidence on unlearning fragility (Lucki et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024; Lynch et al., 2024) and against concurrent robustness-enhancing methods like SAM-based unlearning (Fan et al., 2025) and latent adversarial training (Sheshadri et al., 2024), arguing that invariance offers a more general and scalable alternative to attack-specific defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: in industrial pipelines, unlearning is almost never the final optimization step, and the demonstrated vulnerability (unrelated fine-tuning recovering forgotten knowledge) undermines the utility of all current unlearning methods. The finding that a single unrelated dataset suffices for invariance regularization and generalizes to unseen fine-tuning tasks makes ILU highly deployable without requiring knowledge of future fine-tuning plans. Open questions include: (1) whether invariance can be extended to protect safety alignment operations more broadly, (2) whether ILU can be further strengthened against targeted relearning attacks (where SAM-based methods still hold an edge), and (3) the theoretical characterization of why invariance regularization preserves the unlearning direction in weight space.
