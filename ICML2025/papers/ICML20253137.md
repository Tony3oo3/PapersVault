## Problem

Pre-training large neural networks from scratch demands extensive GPU memory, and existing parameter-efficient methods face fundamental limitations: LoRA's fixed low-rank subspace cannot capture the full spectral structure needed during pre-training, while ReLoRA and similar periodic-merging methods suffer from saddle points (zero gradients) at each reinitialization. No prior method achieves memory-efficient pre-training that closely tracks full-rank training dynamics across diverse architectures and geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253137/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard training data (text corpora such as OpenWebText and C4, graph-structured data, or image datasets) and operates on the weight matrices of all linear layers in a neural network. At initialization and at each periodic re-SVD, the full weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} is decomposed into U, Σ, V^T; during training, only r columns of U and V^T (plus all m singular values) are updated per step. The output is a trained model whose weights are stored in SVD form (U, Σ, V^T) and can be collapsed back into a single dense matrix W = UΣV^T for inference at full-rank computational cost.

## Method

SST replaces each linear layer's weight matrix with its SVD (U, Σ, V^T) and updates all singular values at every step while selectively updating only r singular vectors from U and V^T via multinomial sampling with a mixed uniform-plus-singular-value-weighted distribution. An enhanced gradient formula decouples direction learning (U, V^T) from magnitude (Σ), preventing small singular values from suppressing vector updates. Periodic re-SVD at the end of each round restores orthogonality and prevents degeneration into a low-rank subspace. A memory-efficient implementation partitions U and V^T into active and frozen segments, storing optimizer states only for the active (sampled) vectors and swapping newly sampled vectors in, reducing memory to roughly LoRA-level while maintaining full-rank expressiveness.

## Results

On LLaMA-1.3B pre-training (13.1B tokens, r=128/2048), SST achieves a validation perplexity of 14.59 with only 251M trainable parameters (18.7% of full-rank), nearly matching full-rank training (14.54) and reducing the perplexity gap versus LoRA (16.50) and ReLoRA* (17.32) by 97.4%. On IWSLT'14 machine translation (Euclidean Transformer, dim=64, r=8), SST achieves BLEU 22.28 vs. LoRA 18.08 and ReLoRA* 18.12 (full-rank: 24.27), reducing the BLEU gap by 66.7% on average. On hyperbolic graph neural networks (r=1), SST reduces the performance gap versus LoRA by 73.7% in node classification and 82.5% in link prediction, and even outperforms full-rank training on the Disease link prediction task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SST consistently outperforms LoRA across all evaluated tasks: on LLaMA-1.3B it achieves PPL 14.59 vs. LoRA's 16.50 (a 1.91-point gap), and on IWSLT'14 (dim=64, r=8) it reaches BLEU 22.28 vs. LoRA's 18.08. Against ReLoRA*, SST also wins uniformly — on OPT-125M it achieves PPL 26.98 vs. ReLoRA*'s 35.80, and on hyperbolic GNNs (r=1) it reduces the node classification gap by 73.7% and link prediction gap by 82.5% relative to LoRA, while ReLoRA* suffers periodic saddle points that SST avoids through SVD-based initialization. Compared to GaLore (Appendix G), SST outperforms on OPT-125M (PPL 26.98 vs. 32.17) and on IWSLT'14 across most dimension/rank settings (e.g., BLEU 22.28 vs. 18.08 at dim=64, r=8), though GaLore edges out SST at the largest setting (dim=256, r=32: 24.01 vs. 23.97). Notably, SST's advantage over LoRA and ReLoRA* is most pronounced at small ranks (r ≤ 8), where the low-rank constraint is most restrictive, and SST's gradient norm correlates with full-rank training at 0.85 versus ReLoRA*'s 0.58, confirming closer approximation of full-rank dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA → ReLoRA/COLA/PLoRA → GaLore lineage of parameter-efficient pre-training by shifting the optimization from a fixed low-rank additive subspace to the spectral domain of the weight matrix itself. Unlike LoRA (frozen base + low-rank delta) or ReLoRA* (periodic merge-and-reinitialize with zero-init), SST decomposes weights via SVD, updates all singular values at every step, and selectively updates singular vectors through multinomial sampling weighted by singular value magnitude. The key conceptual departure is using SVD both for initialization and periodic reinitialization to avoid the saddle-point pathology that plagues zero-initialized low-rank restarts, and the first application of parameter-efficient pre-training to hyperbolic neural networks (HyboNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SST offers a practical drop-in replacement for LoRA/ReLoRA in pre-training pipelines, reducing the perplexity gap to full-rank by 50–97.4% across OPT and LLaMA scales while keeping memory comparable to LoRA. The demonstration that a low-rank spectral method can outperform full-rank training in hyperbolic settings (by constraining the search space to prevent overfitting in exponentially growing volume) opens a new direction for parameter-efficient training in non-Euclidean geometries. Open questions include whether the periodic optimizer-state reset can be eliminated for faster convergence, whether SST extends to embedding layers, and whether the SVD reinitialization overhead remains negligible at much larger scales (e.g., 70B+ parameters).
