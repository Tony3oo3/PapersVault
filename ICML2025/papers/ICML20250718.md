## Problem

Training long-context LLMs requires high-quality long documents, which are scarce in most domains and become increasingly harder to obtain as target context lengths grow. Existing synthesis methods (random concatenation, KNN, Quest) extend document length but lack a mechanism to explicitly reinforce long-range dependency modeling, leaving the model unable to discriminate relevant distant context from distracting content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes short documents (meta-documents, predominantly under 8K tokens from Cosmopedia v2 and FineWeb-Edu) and a deduplicated pretraining corpus as input. It produces synthesized long-context documents (128K or 512K) in which each meta-chunk is followed by k retrieved hard-negative chunks, with k adaptively computed from the target length, chunking granularity s (optimal at 2048 tokens), and tokenizer encoding rate. The synthesized documents are then consumed by standard next-token prediction training, yielding a long-context LLM that generalizes to 512K context.

## Method

NExtLong operates in two stages. In Negative Document Extension, a meta-document is chunked into meta-chunks of granularity s (split by newlines, then grouped to fill s tokens), and a FAISS index is built from the deduplicated pretraining corpus (also chunked at granularity s). For each meta-chunk, the top-k most semantically similar chunks are retrieved as hard negatives and concatenated after the meta-chunk to form an extended chunk [m_i, n_i1, ..., n_ik]; all extended chunks are then concatenated into a single long document. In Long-Range Dependence Modeling, the model is trained with standard next-token prediction loss on these synthesized documents. The critical design choice is that hard negatives are semantically similar yet non-identical (guaranteed by corpus deduplication), forcing the model to learn to discriminate the true dependent meta-chunks from distracting content across long distances, thereby explicitly reinforcing long-range dependency modeling rather than merely increasing context length.

## Results

On the HELMET benchmark (averaged over 8K–128K context lengths), NExtLong achieves an average score of 62.58, surpassing the best prior synthesis method Quest (55.25) by +7.33% and the Standard random-concatenation baseline (52.85) by +9.73%. The largest gains appear in Recall (82.56 vs. 69.13 for Quest, +13.43%) and Re-Ranking (31.47 vs. 22.35, +9.12%). On RULER, NExtLong scores 81.50 versus 76.63 for Quest. When extended to 512K context, Llama-3-8B-NExtLong-512K-Base reaches 65.76 average on HELMET, outperforming Llama-3.1-8B (61.07) and ProLong-512K-Base (60.34) without using any naturally occurring long documents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExtLong outperforms all four data synthesis baselines (Standard, KNN, ICLM, Quest) across every HELMET sub-task and RULER, with the largest margins in Recall (+13.43% over Quest) and Re-Ranking (+9.12% over Quest). Against SOTA open-source models trained on naturally occurring long documents, Llama-3-8B-NExtLong-512K-Base surpasses Llama-3.1-8B by +4.69% and ProLong-512K-Base by +5.42% on average HELMET score, despite using only synthesized data from short documents. On the ICL tasks specifically, NExtLong matches or exceeds GPT-4o as the number of shots increases, outperforming it on four of five ICL sub-tasks at 128K context. The method also achieves 100% accuracy on the Needle-in-a-Haystack task up to 500K tokens. On short-text benchmarks (HellaSwag, ARC, PIQA, etc.), NExtLong shows no significant degradation compared to the base model, confirming that the long-context training does not harm general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

NExtLong repurposes the hard negative mining technique from contrastive learning (Robinson et al., 2020) for a novel application: document synthesis for long-context training. It departs from prior long-context data synthesis methods (KNN, ICLM, Quest) that concatenate short documents based on similarity or keyword relevance but lack an explicit mechanism to reinforce long-range dependency modeling. The key conceptual shift is inserting semantically similar yet distracting content *between* dependent chunks, transforming the training signal from simple next-token prediction over coherent text into a discrimination task that forces the model to identify relevant context amid noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NExtLong demonstrates that carefully synthesized data with hard negatives can match or surpass models trained on naturally occurring long documents, significantly reducing the field's dependence on scarce long-document corpora as context windows continue to grow. This opens a practical path toward ultra-long context models (512K+) without requiring prohibitively rare training data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Llama-3-8B architecture family, whether generative methods for creating harder/more diverse negatives (as the authors suggest) yield further gains, and whether the benefits persist at 1M+ token scales where the current 128K/512K evaluations do not reach.
