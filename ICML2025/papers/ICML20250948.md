## Problem

In multi-agent systems with information asymmetry, a principal must learn optimal policies while agents strategically manipulate feedback based on unobserved private types, creating confounders that invalidate standard RL estimation. Prior work (Yu et al., 2022) addressed this only in an offline i.i.d. setting without distribution shift, leaving open whether non-i.i.d. exploration data can still support causal identification of confounded dynamics, and how knowledge transfer from a source to a different target population affects sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes sequentially collected trajectories (s_h, a_h, e_h, r_h, s_{h+1}) from a source population under time-varying exploration policies, along with the known target population distribution P^t, the feedback manipulation distribution F, and specified function classes (model class for rewards/transitions, and two discriminator classes F and G). It outputs an ε-optimal Markov policy for the principal in the target population, with a high-probability confidence set over aggregated models. Notably, the source distribution P^s is unknown, and the data is inherently non-i.i.d. due to adaptive exploration, which is the key departure from prior offline analyses.

## Method

The core algorithm (OPME-G) uses the principal's state-action pair (s_h, a_h) as an instrumental variable in a nonparametric IV framework to build conditional moment equations that identify the true reward and transition functions despite unobserved confounders. Estimation proceeds via minimax optimization with Fenchel-Rockafellar duality: a discriminator class F handles the reward estimation, while a second discriminator class G (value-targeted regression) handles transition dynamics. For the non-i.i.d. online setting, the paper develops a novel martingale concentration analysis using Freedman's inequality to construct high-probability confidence sets, replacing the i.i.d. concentration bounds used in prior offline work. Knowledge transfer is achieved by estimating the underlying model under the source distribution, then aggregating it under the known target distribution to form the planning MDP, after which optimistic planning selects the next exploration policy. The resulting sample complexity is Õ(1/ε²), with explicit dependence on the distributional Eluder dimension, an ill-posedness measure τ_h quantifying confounding difficulty, and a knowledge transfer multiplicative term C_h^f capturing distributional shift.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.4) establishes that the proposed model-based algorithm OPME-G learns an ε-optimal policy with sample complexity Õ(Σ_{h=1}^H B² d_{V,h} τ_h C_h^f log(|R×P×G×F|/δ) · ε^{-2}), where d_{V,h} is the distributional Eluder dimension, τ_h is the ill-posedness measure, and C_h^f is the knowledge transfer multiplicative term. For linear MDPs (Example 5.5), this specializes to Õ(Σ B² d τ_h C_h^f log(·) · ε^{-2}). Remark 5.6 confirms this matches the known lower bound of Õ(ε^{-2}·log(1/δ)) from Domingues et al. (2021) up to logarithmic factors, establishing near-optimality in ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The closest theoretical comparison is with Yu et al. (2022), who studied strategic MDPs in an offline setting with i.i.d. data and a restricted nonlinear dynamical system transition class. The present work extends to the online (non-i.i.d.) setting, general transition classes, and adds knowledge transportability, requiring new martingale concentration analysis (Freedman's inequality) instead of the i.i.d. concentration used by Yu et al. The paper also notes that its confidence set construction (restricting only the empirical risk value, Eq. 4.7) is cleaner than Yu et al.'s approach (restricting value differences) and eliminates their symmetric/star-shaped assumption on the discriminator class. The sample complexity in ε is shown to be minimax-optimal via the lower bound of Domingues et al. (2021), though the dependence on problem-dependent parameters (τ_h, C_h^f, d_{V,h}) is left as an open question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline strategic MDP framework of Yu et al. (2022) to an online setting with knowledge transportability, breaking the i.i.d. data assumption that underpinned prior work. The causal identification machinery draws directly on the nonparametric instrumental variable (NPIV) econometric tradition (Ai & Chen, 2003; Newey & Powell, 2003), while the minimax estimation step leverages Fenchel-Rockafellar duality (Dai et al., 2018; Nachum & Dai, 2020) and the value-targeted regression idea from Ayoub et al. (2020). A key technical departure from Yu et al. is the use of martingale concentration (Freedman's inequality) in place of i.i.d. concentration, and a simpler confidence set construction that eliminates the symmetric/star-shaped assumption on the discriminator class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably efficient online algorithm for strategic RL with both unobserved confounders and distribution shift, achieving a tight O~(1/ε²) sample complexity that matches known lower bounds. This bridges three previously separate literatures—principal-agent economics, causal inference, and model-based RL—into a unified framework with clear quantification of how confounding (ill-posedness measure τ_h) and distribution shift (concentrability C_h^f) degrade sample efficiency. Open questions include whether the NPIV approach extends to partially observable settings (POMDPs) and whether value bridge functions can replace the current discriminator-based transition estimation. A practical limitation is the requirement that the target type distribution and feedback manipulation distribution be known a priori.
