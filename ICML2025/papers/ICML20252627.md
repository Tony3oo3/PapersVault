## Problem

Autoregressive LLM decoding is inherently sequential, limiting hardware parallelism, which becomes critical as models grow larger and outputs grow longer (e.g., long CoT reasoning). Existing acceleration methods—speculative decoding (requires an auxiliary drafter model with matching tokenizer, increasing memory overhead) and layer skipping (introduces missing KV caches that cause output discrepancies)—fail to simultaneously avoid auxiliary models, preserve original model parameters, and guarantee exact output parity with standard decoding.

## Input / Output

AdaDecode takes a standard text prompt formatted with the backbone model's chat template and produces a generated text sequence that is bit-for-bit identical to what vanilla autoregressive decoding would produce. The method operates on instruction-tuned transformer LLMs (Llama3.1-8B, CodeLlama-13B, CodeLlama-34B) and requires only a small set of trained lightweight LM heads (48M parameters total for three heads on Llama3.1-8B) as additional components; all original model weights remain frozen. A hyperparameter γ (default 0.75) controls the confidence threshold for triggering early predictions, and a maximum of 5 concurrent early predictions is enforced to balance speedup against rejection risk.

## Method

AdaDecode introduces lightweight LM heads at selected intermediate layers, each decomposed as E^(i) = E*·T^(i) where E* is the frozen final-layer head and T^(i) is a learnable d×d transformation (16M params per head vs. 0.5B for a full head). These heads are trained via KL-divergance distillation against the final-layer predictions on task-specific data, with all backbone parameters frozen. At inference, when an intermediate layer's predicted token probability exceeds threshold γ, the next token is immediately committed and its remaining-layer KV cache computations are deferred; these deferred computations are then executed in parallel with subsequent token processing (adaptive layer parallelism), breaking the sequential bottleneck. A final verification step using modified rejection sampling (accept with probability min{1, p*/p_θ}) checks all early predictions against the completed full-layer outputs, resampling from the adjusted residual distribution if a rejection occurs, thereby guaranteeing exact output parity with standard autoregressive decoding.

## Results

AdaDecode achieves up to 1.73× decoding speedup over vanilla autoregressive decoding (CodeLlama-34B-Instruct on HumanEval), consistently outperforming all baselines across three backbone models (8B–34B) and three tasks (XSum, HumanEval, GSM8K). On CodeLlama-34B, AdaDecode reaches 1.38× (XSum), 1.73× (HumanEval), and 1.60× (GSM8K) speedup, compared to the best baseline (LookAhead) at 1.14×, 1.39×, and 1.41× respectively. The method requires only 48M additional parameters (three lightweight LM heads) while guaranteeing output parity with standard autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDecode outperforms all four baselines (SpecDecode, Self-SpecDecode, LookAhead, SWIFT) across every model size and task. Against SpecDecode, the advantage is most pronounced on CodeLlama-13B where SpecDecode with a 7B drafter actually *slows down* XSum (0.97×) while AdaDecode achieves 1.37×; on HumanEval, AdaDecode reaches 1.69× vs. SpecDecode's 1.08×. Against Self-SpecDecode, AdaDecode leads by a wide margin (e.g., 1.69× vs. 1.14× on HumanEval with 13B; 1.73× vs. 1.23× with 34B), largely because Self-SpecDecode requires task-specific Bayesian optimization to select skip layers and suffers from high rejection rates. Against LookAhead, AdaDecode wins on all settings (1.73× vs. 1.39× on 34B HumanEval), with the gap widening for larger models where LookAhead's surplus FLOPs from Jacobi decoding become a bottleneck. Against SWIFT, AdaDecode again leads consistently (1.73× vs. 1.40× on 34B HumanEval; 1.57× vs. 1.08× on 34B GSM8K), with SWIFT's speedup being particularly weak on mathematical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaDecode sits at the intersection of two established acceleration paradigms: speculative decoding (which uses an auxiliary drafter model verified by the main model) and layer skipping/early exiting (which bypasses intermediate layers to reduce per-token computation). It departs from speculative decoding by eliminating the need for a separate drafter, and from layer skipping by deferring rather than permanently omitting skipped-layer computations, thereby preserving KV cache integrity. The closest prior method is EESD, which also combines early exiting with verification for output parity, but AdaDecode introduces dynamic-depth exiting (vs. fixed-depth), a parameter-efficient LM head decomposition (E*·T, 31× fewer parameters), and a KL-divergence training objective rather than cross-entropy with Thompson sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaDecode offers a practical, drop-in acceleration method (up to 1.73× speedup) that requires no auxiliary model, no changes to original model weights, and guarantees output parity with standard autoregressive decoding—addressing key adoption barriers of both speculative decoding and layer skipping. The parameter-efficient lightweight head design (48M vs. 1.5B parameters) makes the method accessible even for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions remain around integrating vertical (per-step) and horizontal (cross-step) acceleration in a non-naive way, extending to quantized or pruned models, and whether relaxing strict output parity could yield further speedups by avoiding rejection overhead.
