## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods are designed for classification and use global prompts that mix foreground and background information, making them poorly suited for object detection where fine-grained instance localization is critical. When applied to medical imaging, the conceptual gap between natural and medical domains, combined with the dilution of prompt information by the large number of image-text tokens, further degrades detection performance. Moreover, most continual object detection research focuses on natural domains, leaving data-scarce medical applications largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes medical images across 8 modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, PET, endoscopy, light microscopy, histopathology, ultrasound) and text prompts consisting of class names as input, processed through a frozen GLIP model with Swin-T backbone and BERT text encoder. It outputs COCO-format bounding boxes with class labels for medical objects (lesions, tumors, organs). The method operates in a sequential class-incremental setting where each of 13 tasks introduces new object categories, and is evaluated under full-data and few-shot (1/10/50-shot) conditions with at least k annotated instances per class.

## Method

iDPA comprises two modules: Instance-level Prompt Generation (IPG) and Decoupled Prompt Attention (DPA). IPG extracts instance-level features via RoI pooling from a pre-trained model, then uses a cross-attention Query-Answer framework to decouple l concept vectors from M instance representations per category, producing task-specific prompts that focus on dense prediction rather than global scene information. DPA reformulates the original prompt attention by separating it into the standard vision-to-text attention and a separate prompt-to-text knowledge injection branch combined via a residual connection with a learnable scaling factor λ, eliminating the coupling that causes prompt information to be overshadowed by the much larger token sequence. Knowledge injection is applied at the cross-modality fusion encoder rather than only at the backbone, where fine-grained detection reasoning occurs. The approach trains only 1.4% of parameters (3.22M) while keeping the base model frozen, and uses weight transfer between successive tasks to enable continual concept perception.

## Results

On the ODinM-13 benchmark (13 medical datasets, 8 modalities, 9 organs), iDPA achieves a Final Average AP (FAP) of 50.28%, Cumulative Average AP (CAP) of 54.10%, and Final Forgetting Percentage (FFP) of 2.48% under full-data training, using only 1.4% trainable parameters. In few-shot settings, iDPA achieves FAP of 12.19% (1-shot), 23.78% (10-shot), and 38.65% (50-shot), outperforming the best baselines by 4.83%, 12.88%, and 4.59% respectively. The method remains within 4.39% of the joint-training upper bound (54.67% FAP) while requiring no rehearsal data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iDPA outperforms all 12 baselines on FAP across every setting. Against the previous prompt-based SOTA NoRGa, iDPA gains +5.44% FAP (50.28 vs. 44.84) and reduces FFP by 2.44 points (2.48 vs. 4.92). Against the strongest non-prompt baseline ER (which requires 10-shot rehearsal data and full model tuning), iDPA improves FAP by +10.37% and reduces FFP by 16.77 points. The gap over ZiRa (the only prior COD method adapted to VLOD) is dramatic: +46.62% FAP, attributed to the large medical–natural domain gap that ZiRa's regularization cannot bridge. Among prompt-based methods, iDPA leads CODA by +8.20% FAP, DIKI by +7.77%, and L2P by +10.40%, while also achieving the lowest FFP (2.48%) among all prompt-based approaches. In the 10-shot few-shot setting, iDPA's advantage is most pronounced (+12.88% FAP over ZiRa at 10.90%), highlighting its strength in data-scarce clinical scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prompt-based continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, NoRGa, DIKI) from classification to object detection, identifying that global prompt learning and naive prepending of prompts to image-text tokens are ill-suited for fine-grained instance localization. It builds on Vision-Language Object Detection architectures (GLIP, Grounding DINO) as the frozen backbone and departs from prior COD work (ILOD, CL-DETR, ZiRa) by introducing instance-level prompt generation via RoI pooling and cross-attention, plus a decoupled attention mechanism that separates prompt-to-vision and prompt-to-text knowledge injection from the original cross-modal attention. The paper also introduces ODinM-13, a 13-dataset multi-modality, multi-organ benchmark specifically for incremental medical object detection, filling a gap left by natural-domain COD benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ODinM-13 benchmark provides the first comprehensive evaluation suite for incremental medical object detection across 8 imaging modalities and 9 organs, addressing a practical need in data-scarce clinical settings where retraining all-in-one models is infeasible. The finding that knowledge injection at the fusion encoder (post-backbone) outperforms backbone-level injection challenges the common practice in prompt-based CL and suggests that fine-grained detection reasoning benefits from later-stage knowledge integration. Open questions include whether the DPA mechanism generalizes to other VLOD architectures beyond GLIP, whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric medical imaging, and whether the large performance gap over ZiRa reflects a genuine methodological advantage or primarily a domain-mismatch artifact.
