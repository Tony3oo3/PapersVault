## Problem

Existing quantized LoRA methods (QLoRA, LoftQ, PiSSA) are confined to 2–4 bits per parameter and diverge or fail to converge below 2 bits, limiting deployment on ultra-resource-constrained hardware. Three fundamental bottlenecks prevent sub-2-bit fine-tuning: coarse-grained (layer- or matrix-level) precision assignment, reliance on a single global data-distribution assumption for quantization format selection, and absence of hardware-efficient low-bit execution primitives. LowRA addresses all three to enable accurate LoRA fine-tuning down to 1.15 bits per parameter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained LLM weights, a task-specific training corpus, and a user-specified total bit budget (e.g., 1.15 or 1.75 bits/parameter). It produces per-output-channel mixed-precision quantized base weights (each channel assigned 1, 2, or 4 bits), per-channel quantization mappings and thresholds, and trained LoRA adapter tensors. Notably, the quantization is data-free and one-shot: no calibration set is required, and the resulting base weights remain task-agnostic and reusable across multiple downstream adapters, with only the low-rank tensors updated during fine-tuning.

## Method

LowRA combines three components. First, a Weighted Lloyd-Max algorithm searches per-output-channel quantization mappings and thresholds by treating each 64-element group's absmax as a weight, minimizing weighted MSE so that high-magnitude blocks receive proportionally more quantization fidelity. Second, a two-level hierarchical Integer Linear Program assigns each output channel a precision (1, 2, or 4 bits): a cluster-level ILP allocates bit budgets across K-Means clusters of channels (grouped by parameter size and MSE features), then an intra-cluster ILP assigns specific channels to bitwidths while minimizing total SSE under the budget constraint. Third, custom CUDA quantize/dequantize kernels execute the mixed-precision, per-channel operations with channel-specific lookup tables and groupwise absmax rescaling, integrated into bitsandbytes. The quantized base weights are initialized via LoftQ's iterative quantize-then-SVD procedure to absorb quantization errors into the low-rank adapters, and only the adapters are trained during fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B WikiText-2 at 2-bit quantization, LowRA achieves 6.60 perplexity, outperforming QLoRA (9.17) and LoftQ (8.63) by 2.57 and 2.03 points respectively. LowRA is the first method to enable stable LoRA fine-tuning below 2 bits, reaching 1.75 bits on LLaMA-2-7B/13B and BART-large, and 1.15 bits on LLaMA-3-3B (8.00 ppl on WikiText-2) and LLaMA-65B (7.49 ppl). On average, LowRA achieves comparable performance to 4-bit QLoRA while reducing precision by 0.86 bits per parameter, cutting fine-tuning memory by 30–50% and inference memory by up to 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LowRA consistently outperforms both QLoRA and LoftQ at every sub-4-bit precision level across all models and tasks. At the challenging 2-bit setting on LLaMA-2-7B, LowRA reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 2.57 over QLoRA (6.60 vs. 9.17) and by 2.03 over LoftQ (6.60 vs. 8.63), while on Open-Assistant it improves by 1.72 over QLoRA and 1.33 over LoftQ. Critically, LowRA is the only method that remains stable below 2 bits: QLoRA and LoftQ either fail to converge (DNC) or simply do not support precisions below 2 bits, whereas LowRA achieves 7.76 ppl at 1.75 bits on LLaMA-2-7B and 8.00 ppl at 1.15 bits on LLaMA-3-3B. On BART-large summarization, QLoRA degrades catastrophically below 3 bits (ROUGE-L drops from 31.09 at 4-bit to 4.36 at 2-bit on CNN/DailyMail), while LowRA maintains 26.67 ROUGE-L at 2-bit and 25.99 at 1.75-bit. At 4-bit, LowRA matches LoftQ and slightly outperforms QLoRA, confirming no accuracy penalty from the additional quantization machinery.

## Relation to Prior Work

LowRA extends the quantized LoRA fine-tuning lineage (QLoRA → LoftQ/PiSSA) by addressing three limitations the authors identify: coarse-grained (layer-level) precision assignment, fixed-distribution quantization formats (e.g., NormalFloat), and reliance on simulated quantization without hardware support. Its core technical contributions—a Weighted Lloyd-Max formulation for per-output-channel adaptive mapping/threshold search and a two-level hierarchical ILP for channelwise precision assignment—are novel combinations of classical quantization theory and combinatorial optimization applied to the LoRA setting. The use of LoftQ (rather than PiSSA) as the low-rank initializer, and the data-free one-shot post-training quantization design, position LowRA as a practical, task-agnostic system rather than a task-specific QAT method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling accurate LoRA fine-tuning down to 1.15 bits per parameter with 30–50% memory reduction, LowRA makes LLM adaptation feasible on devices previously out of reach (e.g., a single 16 GB T4 for LLaMA-3-3B, or a Raspberry Pi 4 for LLaMA-2-7B inference). The 3.4× throughput gain at 1.5 bits over 4-bit QLoRA on consumer GPUs suggests practical serving benefits beyond memory savings. Open questions include whether the per-output-channel + ILP approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or to settings where base weights are not shared across adapters, and whether the data-free quantization assumption degrades on highly domain-specific fine-tuning tasks. The one-time preprocessing cost (minutes to ~11 minutes for the ILP solver) is negligible but could matter in rapidly iterating multi-adapter serving pipelines.
