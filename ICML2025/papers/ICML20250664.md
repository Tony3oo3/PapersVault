## Problem

Message-Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) suffer from a limited effective receptive field (ERF), where the contribution of distant nodes to a node's output decays exponentially with graph distance, analogous to the underutilized theoretical receptive field in CNNs. This limits MPNNs' ability to capture long-range dependencies and exacerbates over-squashing, particularly on graphs with large diameters. The paper formalizes this ERF limitation for MPNNs and proposes an architectural remedy that expands the ERF without increasing asymptotic complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature vectors and an adjacency matrix as input, and produces node-level or graph-level predictions (classification labels or regression values) depending on the downstream task. The graph is hierarchically coarsened S times via Graclus node-pairing, yielding S+1 nested graphs of decreasing size (each roughly half the nodes of the previous). At inference, features from all scales are recursively unpooled and concatenated back onto the original graph nodes before a task-specific head, so the final output always resides on the original node set.

## Method

IM-MPNN introduces an interleaved multiscale architecture built on three components: (i) an arbitrary MPNN backbone (GCN, GINE, GatedGCN, GAT) applied independently at each of S+1 coarsened graph scales; (ii) Graclus-based pooling/unpooling that pairs adjacent nodes to halve graph size at each coarsening step; and (iii) scale-mix layers that, at every block, let each node receive weighted contributions from its children (finer scale) and parent (coarser scale) via learnable lower-to-higher and higher-to-lower projections. The theoretical analysis shows that coarsening by a factor of two multiplies the effective diffusion constant κ by four, so a single hop on a coarser scale covers roughly four hops on the original graph while each intermediate node is aggregated only once, mitigating the exponential decay. The overall time complexity remains O(|V|+|E|) because the sum of work across geometrically shrinking scales is bounded by a constant factor times the original graph size.

## Results

On the Long Range Graph Benchmark, IM-GCN with 4 scales achieves 0.2929 F1 on PascalVOC-SP and 0.1960 F1 on COCO-SP, representing up to 41% relative improvement over the GCN baseline (0.2078 and 0.1338 respectively). On City-Networks (100k–570k nodes), IM-GCN (4 scales) improves accuracy by 10–16.5% over GCN, e.g., 58.9% on London vs. 43.8%. On heterophilic node classification, IM-CO-GNN(μ,μ) achieves 92.00% on Roman-Empire, 54.43% on Amazon-Ratings, 97.39 AUC on Minesweeper, 85.77 AUC on Tolokers, and 78.92 AUC on Questions, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art Graph Transformers and heterophily-specific GNNs while retaining linear complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IM-MPNN consistently outperforms its corresponding non-multiscale backbone across all benchmarks: on LRGB PascalVOC-SP, IM-GCN (4 scales) reaches 0.2929 F1 vs. GCN's 0.2078 (+41% relative), and on City-Networks London, IM-GCN achieves 58.9% vs. GCN's 43.8% (+15.1%). On heterophilic node classification, IM-CO-GNN(μ,μ) matches or slightly exceeds the strongest MPNN baseline CO-GNN(μ,μ) on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 97.39 vs. 97.31 AUC on Minesweeper, 85.77 vs. 84.45 on Tolokers, 54.43 vs. 54.17 on Amazon-Ratings) while also beating all Graph Transformer baselines (Exphormer, GOAT, GPS, GT-sep) on Roman-Empire (92.00 vs. 89.03 for Exphormer) and Minesweeper (97.39 vs. 90.74). IM-GatedGCN achieves 97.32 AUC on Minesweeper, matching CO-GNN(μ,μ) and surpassing all Graph Transformers. On the graph transfer task, IM-GCN with 3 scales maintains 100% accuracy at distance k≈37 on CliquePath, whereas standard GCN fails at k≈7. The method also outperforms the hierarchical Graph U-Net (0.1801 F1) and the rewiring method DRew (0.3909 F1) on PascalVOC-SP, with IM-GatedGCN reaching 0.4332 F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the over-squashing problem in MPNNs through the lens of Effective Receptive Fields (ERF), drawing a direct parallel to the well-studied ERF limitation in CNNs (Luo et al., 2016). It builds on the diffusion-based interpretation of message-passing (Chamberlain et al., 2021b; Eliasof et al., 2023b) to show that node contributions decay exponentially with distance, and adapts the multiscale convolution strategy from CNNs (Finder et al., 2024) to graphs via hierarchical coarsening with Graclus (Dhillon et al., 2007). Unlike prior over-squashing mitigations that rely on dense propagation (transformers, graph rewiring) or non-local operators, IM-MPNN maintains O(|V|+|E|) complexity by interleaving message-passing across coarsened scales with learnable scale-mixing weights, distinguishing it from hierarchical GNNs like Graph U-Net (Gao & Ji, 2019) that use pooling for feature extraction rather than ERF expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical foundation (binomial distribution tail bounds and diffusion PDE analysis) for why standard MPNNs fail at long-range tasks, and offers a practical, backbone-agnostic fix that can be dropped into any existing MPNN (GCN, GINE, GatedGCN, GAT) with minimal architectural change. The 41% relative improvement on LRGB and the graph transfer results (100% accuracy at k=37 vs. GCN failing at k=7) demonstrate that the ERF limitation is a primary bottleneck, not just a secondary effect of over-squashing. Open questions include: how the optimal number of scales scales with graph diameter, whether the theoretical analysis extends to non-uniform or directed graphs, and whether the approach interacts favorably with other over-squashing mitigations (e.g., non-dissipative dynamics). The transductive nature of the City-Networks evaluation and the fixed 500K parameter budget in LRGB experiments limit the generality of the comparisons.
