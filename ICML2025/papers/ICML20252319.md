## Problem

Extending a pretrained LLM to a new domain (e.g., a new language) with standard parameter-efficient methods like finetuning or LoRA does not formally add model capacity, creating an inherent trade-off between learning the new domain and catastrophic forgetting of the original one. While adapters do add capacity, they still suffer significant forgetting because their outputs are not constrained to be neutral on the original distribution. The paper asks how to design adapter blocks that provably output near-zeros on the original domain while remaining fully expressive on the new one.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained LLM (e.g., Gemma-2B or an internal 1B English-only model) and new-domain text data (e.g., a target language extracted from CommonCrawl), plus a small fraction (p=0.1) of data approximating the original pretraining distribution (e.g., English Wikipedia). It produces an extended model with parallel gated FFN adapter blocks inserted at each transformer layer, adding ~20% extra learnable parameters while keeping all backbone weights frozen. The extended model is evaluated on both perplexity and downstream benchmarks (Belebele, HellaSwag, ARC, CSQA, MMLU) in both the original and target languages.

## Method

Neutral residues adds parallel FFN adapter blocks (rather than MHA) to each transformer layer, each wrapped by a ReLU-based block gate that scales the adapter's contribution. The key training signal is a local ℓ1 sparsity loss applied only on original-distribution data, which forces the gated adapter outputs to be near-zero when the input follows the pretraining distribution, while remaining unconstrained on new-domain data. This gating mechanism is critical: without it, the adapter's GLU gating weights become co-linear (as shown via spectral analysis), preventing effective learning of the new domain. The method also uses a low-variance initialization (variance 1/(d·L) instead of the standard He 2/d) to keep the extended model close to the original for longer during early training. Among two gating strategies tested (ReLU + ℓ1 vs. sigmoid + cross-entropy), the ReLU variant with sparsity regularization achieves the best trade-off between learning and forgetting, and the approach is stable across a wide range of learning rates unlike finetuning or LoRA.

## Results

On Gemma-2B extended to four target languages (French, Danish, Hungarian, Slovak), neutral residues achieves the best trade-off between preserving English knowledge and learning the new language. For French, it scores 53.3 on English forgetting (nearly matching the 53.2 backbone) and 48.2 on French learning (vs. 44.6 backbone), outperforming LoRA (51.2/44.9) and vanilla adapters (52.8/46.0). For Hungarian, it reaches 52.6/38.8 vs. backbone 53.2/33.1, LoRA 48.6/38.0, and adapters 52.0/38.4. The method is notably stable across a one-order-of-magnitude range of learning rates, unlike finetuning and LoRA which are highly sensitive to this hyperparameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neutral residues consistently outperforms both LoRA and vanilla adapters on both forgetting and learning across all four target languages. Against LoRA, it improves English forgetting by 1.1–3.3 points (e.g., 53.3 vs. 51.2 for French, 52.6 vs. 48.6 for Hungarian) while also improving target-language learning by 0.2–3.3 points (e.g., 48.2 vs. 44.9 for French). Against vanilla adapters, the gains are smaller but consistent: +0.5 to +3.7 points on forgetting and +0.4 to +2.2 points on learning. Full finetuning achieves the best target-language scores (e.g., 49.9 for French) but at the cost of the worst English forgetting (49.8), confirming the learning–forgetting trade-off. The paper also shows that adapters (which add explicit capacity) outperform LoRA (which does not) on this knowledge-addition task, validating the architectural choice of parallel FFN adapter blocks over low-rank matrix updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adapter architecture of Houlsby et al. (2019) and Rebuffi et al. (2017), but departs from them by adding a ReLU-based block gate with an L1 sparsity loss that forces adapter outputs to near-zero on the original distribution, and by using a more aggressive low-variance initialization (1/(d·L) vs. the standard 2/d). It is conceptually adjacent to MoE-style model extension work (Zhong et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024) but deliberately avoids softmax gating, which the authors argue prevents the model from producing zero-valued residual outputs. Unlike LoRA, which adds no formal capacity, neutral residues adds ~20% extra FFN parameters, positioning it closer to the block-expansion approach of Wu et al. (2024) but with a lighter, parallel-adapter design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, resource-efficient path to extending LLMs to new languages without the cost of full retraining, and the stability across a wide learning-rate range (one order of magnitude) makes it more robust to hyperparameter tuning than finetuning or LoRA. However, the math-domain experiment (Table 12) reveals a significant limitation: when the new domain's language overlaps with the original (mathematical English vs. general English), the sparsity signal becomes ambiguous and forgetting increases substantially. The approach has been validated only on 1B–2B parameter models and alphabetic/non-Latin scripts; whether the gating mechanism and initialization strategy scale to 70B+ models or to fundamentally different modalities remains open.
