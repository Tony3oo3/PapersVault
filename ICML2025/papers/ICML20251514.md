## Problem

Existing XAI methods for graph neural networks are designed for 2D graphs and either cannot handle the tensor product-based message passing central to spherical equivariant GNNs or fail to properly attribute importance to geometric features such as edge distances and directions. This limits the ability to understand how 3D positional data influences predictions in equivariant models used across molecular science, protein research, and materials science.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251514/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a trained spherical equivariant GNN (e.g., TFN) and a 3D geometric graph with node features, coordinates, and a radial-cutoff adjacency matrix, EquiGX back-propagates the scalar prediction score through every layer to produce three types of relevance scores: per-node importance, per-edge distance relevance, and per-edge direction relevance. Unlike standard LRP where all scores propagate to the input, edge distance and direction scores are accumulated across message-passing layers rather than backpropagated further, since they represent geometric properties fixed at each layer. The final node importance combines the node's own relevance with half the relevance of each connected edge.

## Method

EquiGX extends the Deep Taylor Decomposition framework to derive layer-wise relevance propagation rules specifically for tensor product (TP) based message passing in spherical equivariant GNNs. The key insight is that the bilinearity of the tensor product makes the Jacobian of the message with respect to hidden features independent of the Taylor root point, enabling an exact first-order decomposition. For each TP message, the relevance is split equally (1/3 each) among the three trilinear components—source node hidden features, edge distance (via radial basis function weights), and edge direction (via spherical harmonics)—following the trilinear attribution principle. Linear layers use LRP-ε propagation, while norm-based non-linearities (e.g., sigmoid of feature norm) use a normalized reversal with a global scaling factor λ to preserve the conservation property. The method is applied recursively from the output layer back to the input, with only hidden-feature relevance continuing to propagate while geometric relevance scores are accumulated per layer.

## Results

EquiGX achieves the best AUROC and Average Precision across all four datasets (Shapes, Spiral Noise, SCOP, ActsTrack). On the synthetic Shapes dataset, it reaches 84.31 AUROC and 91.00 AP; on Spiral Noise, 83.57 AUROC and 86.82 AP; on the real-world SCOP protein dataset, 81.51 AUROC and 82.69 AP; and on ActsTrack, 76.96 AUROC and 60.47 AP. The TFN models used for explanation achieve 100% accuracy on both synthetic datasets and 84.35% accuracy on SCOP, confirming that the explanations are generated from well-trained models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiGX outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four datasets. On Shapes, it edges out the closest competitor PG-Explainer (82.83 AUROC) by 1.5 points and GNN-Explainer (80.85) by 3.5 points. On Spiral Noise, the gap widens substantially: EquiGX (83.57 AUROC) surpasses GNN-Explainer (79.69) by nearly 4 points and PG-Explainer (69.09) by over 14 points. On SCOP, EquiGX (81.51 AUROC, 82.69 AP) leads GNN-Explainer (77.26, 72.37) by 4.3 and 10.3 points respectively, with the AP gap being particularly large. On ActsTrack, the advantage is most dramatic: EquiGX (76.96 AUROC, 60.47 AP) exceeds the best baseline Grad-CAM (62.11, 44.95) by 14.9 and 15.5 points, while PG-Explainer (52.16, 29.43) and Grad (55.84, 31.87) perform near or below random. The LRI variants (Bernoulli and Gaussian noise injection) consistently underperform, confirming that input-output-only methods without access to internal model structure are insufficient for equivariant GNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Taylor Decomposition / Layer-wise Relevance Propagation framework (Montavon et al., 2017; Bach et al., 2015) to the specific challenge of spherical equivariant GNNs, where the central message-passing operation is a tensor product involving Clebsch-Gordan coefficients and spherical harmonics. It departs from prior GNN explainability work (GNN-Explainer, PG-Explainer, Grad-CAM) that targets 2D graphs and cannot handle positional/geometric features, and from the only prior 3D-specific method (LRI, Miao et al., 2023) which relies on input-output perturbation without internal model access or equivariance awareness. The trilinear decomposition strategy for splitting message relevance among hidden features, distance, and direction follows the equal-split principle from Achtibat et al. (2024) for attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Equivariant GNNs are increasingly central to molecular property prediction, protein structure analysis, and materials science, yet their internal reasoning has remained opaque; EquiGX provides the first layer-wise, architecture-aware explanation method that explicitly accounts for tensor product operations and geometric positional data. The method's demonstrated ability to recover known structural motifs (cubes, tetrahedra, β-sheets, α-helices) validates its scientific utility. Key limitations include reliance on norm-based non-linearities, testing only on TFN, and open questions about extending to attention-based equivariant transformers (EquiformerV2, MACE) or scalarization-based models (HEGNN) that require new LRP rules for inner-product operations.
