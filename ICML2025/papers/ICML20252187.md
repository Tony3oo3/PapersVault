## Problem

Existing learning-based global routing methods (e.g., HubRouter, PRNet) generate routes via generative models but cannot guarantee connectivity, requiring a second-stage RL or heuristic post-processing step to enforce it. This two-stage pipeline breaks end-to-end training-inference alignment and introduces additional complexity. DSBRouter addresses this by directly generating connected routes in a single forward pass without any post-processing correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3-channel 64×64 grid image where channel 1 encodes pin locations, channel 2 encodes horizontal congestion, and channel 3 encodes vertical congestion. It produces a 3-channel 64×64 output where channels 2 and 3 are passed through unchanged and channel 1 contains the predicted connected routing map. Unlike standard diffusion models that denoise from Gaussian noise, DSBRouter transitions between two meaningful data distributions (pins+congestion as prior, routes as data), using a symmetric non-monotonic noise schedule rather than a standard VP/VE schedule.

## Method

DSBRouter employs a terminal-reparameterized Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (DSB) with a uvit-b backbone (13 ViT layers, 4×4 patches, 512-dim embeddings) to learn bidirectional transitions between the pin distribution p_s and the route distribution p_r, alternating optimization of forward and backward networks across epochs. During inference, the backward process (pins → routes) is guided at each step by an evaluation-based gradient feedback mechanism: the model constructs a Steiner map from the current predicted route, generates a suboptimal RST via a greedy disjoint-set algorithm, and computes the gradient of an overflow-oriented energy function (measuring the gap between expected and actual overflow) to refine the route via a classifier-guidance-style proposition. This guidance is realized without additional trained networks, using only the DSB backbone's reparameterized outputs and a Gibbs distribution over the energy function. The result is a truly end-to-end router that produces 100% connected routes with SOTA overflow reduction (up to 100% on several ISPD98/ISPD07 cases) without any post-processing step.

## Results

On ISPD98 (ibm01–ibm05) and ISPD07 (ada03, ada04), DSBRouter achieves the lowest overflow across all seven benchmarks, reaching zero overflow on ibm02, ibm05, ada03, and ada04. Compared to the SOTA ML-based method NeuralSteiner, DSBRouter achieves an average overflow reduction of 90.4% (e.g., 1430 vs. 2200 on ibm01; 0 vs. 728 on ada03). On wirelength, DSBRouter is competitive on ISPD98 (within 3.2% of NeuralSteiner, outperforming it on ibm01 and ibm04) but produces longer routes on the much larger ISPD07 cases (ada03: 29.5M vs. 9.5M; ada04: 24.3M vs. 9.0M). On the ISPD07 correctness-rate evaluation (Table 2), DSBRouter achieves 100% connectivity across all four test splits, while all HubRouter variants without RL post-processing achieve 0–44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three HubRouter variants (HR-VAE, HR-DPM, HR-GAN), DSBRouter dramatically outperforms on overflow: e.g., on ibm02, DSBRouter achieves 0 OF versus 9919 (VAE), 14117 (DPM), and 7481 (GAN); on ibm05, 0 versus 909, 2390, and 419. On wirelength, DSBRouter is competitive with HR-GAN on ISPD98 (e.g., 61435 vs. 61056 on ibm01) but significantly better on correctness rate (100% vs. 0–44% without RL post-processing). Against the classical routers GeoSteiner, Labyrinth, and FLUTE+ES, DSBRouter reduces overflow by 57–100% (e.g., 1430 vs. 3342 on ibm01; 0 vs. 7399 on ibm02) while maintaining wirelength within ~5% on ISPD98. Against the SOTA ML-based NeuralSteiner, DSBRouter achieves an average 90.4% overflow reduction and hits zero overflow on four of seven benchmarks, but incurs substantially longer wirelength on the large-scale ISPD07 cases (ada03/ada04) due to the evaluation-based guidance favoring congestion avoidance over shortest paths. DSBRouter's main trade-off is generation time: 4491 s on ibm01 versus 1–12 s for classical methods and 10–12 s for HR-VAE, attributed to the multi-step DSB sampling with gradient recomputation at each step.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSBRouter applies the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (De Bortoli et al., 2021; Tang et al., 2024) to global routing for the first time, establishing a bidirectional transition between pin distributions and route distributions rather than the standard noise-to-data mapping used in prior generative routers like HubRouter. It adapts classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) to the DSB backward process, introducing an evaluation-based gradient feedback mechanism that directly optimizes overflow during sampling. Unlike HubRouter and NeuralSteiner, which decompose routing into hub/point generation followed by a separate connectivity-enforcement stage (RL or classical post-processing), DSBRouter learns the full pin-to-route mapping in a single end-to-end model, leveraging the DSB's inherent constraint that both endpoints are meaningful data distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generative model can produce fully connected routes without any post-processing step, addressing a persistent gap in learning-based global routing where connectivity was always enforced by a second-stage algorithm. However, the practical impact is significantly limited: inference takes thousands of seconds (vs. seconds for classical routers), and wirelength degrades substantially on large-scale ISPD07 instances (relative error of 3.15× the lower bound on ada03). Open questions include whether distillation-based acceleration (as the authors suggest) can close the inference-time gap, and whether the DSB framework can be extended to handle the non-monotonic noise schedules required for meaningful endpoint distributions without sacrificing quality.
