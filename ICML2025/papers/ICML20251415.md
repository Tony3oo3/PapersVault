## Problem

Existing counterfactual fairness notions require decisions to remain unchanged for all individuals when protected attributes change, regardless of whether the attribute actually affects the outcome of interest. This is inappropriate in cases like selecting runner athletes based on leg disability, where the attribute genuinely influences performance. The paper asks: which individuals should be protected, and how should fairness be enforced when the protected attribute has a real causal effect on the outcome?

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed tabular data (binary protected attribute A, covariates X, binary outcome Y) along with a pre-trained decision model D(·) that produces binary decisions. It outputs a binary fairness verdict (satisfies or violates principal counterfactual fairness) via an optimization feasibility check, and when a violation is detected, produces post-processed decisions D' along with per-subgroup epsilon adjustment parameters. All variables are assumed binary and covariates discrete, though the framework extends to other types; the key constraint is that only one potential outcome per individual is observed, making principal strata partially identified.

## Method

The paper proposes principal counterfactual fairness (PCF), which restricts individual-level counterfactual fairness to the principal stratum where Y(a)=Y(a'), i.e., individuals for whom the protected attribute has no individual causal effect on the outcome. To evaluate PCF, it formulates a linear optimization problem over joint potential-outcome probabilities w(x) with constraints from observed data, and declares a violation if the feasible region is empty or if the optimized interval for any w term excludes zero. For estimation, it uses doubly robust (DR) estimators combining outcome regression and inverse propensity scoring to estimate the required conditional probabilities P(D(a)=d, Y(a)=y | X=x). When a violation is detected, a post-processing step introduces non-negative epsilon parameters that force decision flips with minimal total change, solved via a constrained optimization that preserves the PCF constraints. Theoretical results establish asymptotic normality of the DR estimator, consistency of the estimated epsilon to the optimal solution, and convergence of the fairness violation to zero at rate O_P(1/√n).

## Results

On the OULAD real-world dataset (32,593 students, disability as sensitive attribute), post-processing achieves PCF improvements of +1.79% to +9.59% and CF improvements of +1.35% to +5.22% across subgroups where the optimization is infeasible. On synthetic data across four models (LR, SVM, RF, NB) and three estimators (OR, IPS, DR), PCF gains range from +5.15% to +9.31% while CF gains range from +1.47% to +8.88%. A consistent finding across all settings is that PCF improvements exceed CF improvements, confirming the method selectively targets the principal stratum (Y(0)=Y(1)) rather than the full population.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing fairness methods; instead, it evaluates the pre- and post-processing performance of the same decision model under two metrics: standard counterfactual fairness (CF = P(D(0)=D(1))) and the proposed principal counterfactual fairness (PCF = P(D(0)=D(1) | Y(0)=Y(1))). Across all 12 synthetic configurations (4 models × 3 estimators), PCF gains (+5.15% to +9.31%) consistently exceed CF gains (+1.47% to +8.88%), confirming that the post-processing selectively corrects unfairness on the principal stratum. On the OULAD dataset, the largest PCF improvement (+9.59%) occurs in the X₁<120, X₂>0 subgroup, where the optimization infeasibility is most severe (w₀₁₀₀ interval [0.176, 0.220] entirely positive). The paper also notes that finer subgrouping (4 subgroups vs. 2) produces larger ε values and larger metric changes, indicating stronger constraint violations in smaller subpopulations. No direct comparison with prior fairness notions (e.g., counterfactual equalized odds, path-specific fairness) is provided empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends counterfactual fairness (Kusner et al., 2017) by importing the principal stratification framework from causal inference (Frangakis & Rubin, 2002; Pearl, 2011) to restrict the fairness requirement to individuals whose protected attribute has no individual causal effect on the outcome. It departs from prior group-level principal fairness (Imai & Jiang, 2020), which still relies on statistical independence within strata, by formulating an individual-level counterfactual condition. The paper also positions itself against path-specific counterfactual fairness (Chiappa, 2019), which restricts fairness to certain causal paths but still demands it for all individuals, and against counterfactual equalized odds (Mishler et al., 2021), which conditions on counterfactual outcomes but retains group-level independence constraints. The doubly robust estimation machinery for the optimization-based evaluation follows standard causal inference practice (Chernozhukov et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored question—when and for whom should fairness be enforced—by tying the fairness requirement to the causal structure between the protected attribute and the outcome. However, a critical limitation is that principal counterfactual fairness is only partially identified: the paper provides statistical bounds and a falsification test rather than point estimates, which limits practical auditability. The empirical validation is narrow (one real-world dataset, one sensitive attribute, one decision model), and the post-processing approach, while theoretically sound, has not been stress-tested across diverse causal structures or high-dimensional settings. Open questions include developing identification strategies under weaker assumptions and integrating causal discovery to automatically determine relevant strata.
