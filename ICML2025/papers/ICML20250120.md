## Problem

While polynomial-length chain-of-thought (CoT) scratchpads are known to extend transformer expressivity from TC⁰ to PTIME, the minimal CoT length required for specific algorithmic problems remained poorly understood. Empirical evidence shows transformers struggle with even TC⁰ problems like PARITY and MULTIPLICATION without CoTs, yet no systematic lower bounds on CoT length existed. This paper initiates the study of provable lower bounds on the number of CoT steps needed across a range of algorithmic problems in the unique-hard-attention (UHAT) regime.

## Input / Output

The paper studies four tasks: PARITY (a binary string of length N → even/odd bit), MULTIPLICATION (a pair of N-digit binary integers → 2N product digits), MEDIAN (a list of N B-bit numbers → the ⌊N/2⌋-th order statistic), and DAG REACHABILITY (a directed graph edge list plus a query vertex pair → yes/no reachability label). The central output is a lower bound on the CoT length |g(x)| as a function of input length N, where the CoT is an autoregressive sequence of intermediate tokens appended to the input that the transformer must generate before producing the final answer. Bounds are stated in the worst case over all inputs of a given length.

## Method

The core technical tool is a generic CoT lower bound (Theorem 3.3) proved via random restrictions, a technique analogous to the classical Switching Lemma for AC⁰ circuits. The key insight is that if a UHAT-expressible CoT of sublinear length o(N) exists, then there must exist a restriction fixing a constant fraction of input positions while rendering the target function constant; for high-sensitivity functions (average sensitivity Θ(N)), no such restriction exists, forcing linear CoT length. The paper applies this to PARITY, the middle digits of MULTIPLICATION, MEDIAN, and a PARITY-reducible subfamily of DAG REACHABILITY, yielding Ω(N) lower bounds. Matching upper bounds up to logarithmic factors are constructed explicitly: prefix-parity CoTs for PARITY, an O(N log N) FFT/NTT-based scratchpad for full multiplication, sorted-prefix enumeration for MEDIAN, and BFS-based CoTs for reachability. Experiments with small softmax-attention transformers and LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, o1-mini) confirm that these CoT lengths are both necessary and sufficient in practice.

## Results

The paper proves that any UHAT-expressible CoT for PARITY, the middle digits of MULTIPLICATION, MEDIAN, and DAG REACHABILITY must have length Ω(N) (or Ω(|E| log|V|) for reachability), establishing linear lower bounds in the input size. These bounds are tight up to logarithmic factors: PARITY and MEDIAN have matching Θ(N) upper bounds, MULTIPLICATION has a Θ(N log N) upper bound via a Number-Theoretic-Transform-based scratchpad, and REACHABILITY has a matching O(|E| log|V|) upper bound via breadth-first search. For dot-by-dot CoTs (repeated single tokens) on PARITY, the required length is superpolynomial ω(N^k) for all k ∈ ℕ, yielding a superpolynomial separation from full CoTs. Experiments confirm that transformers trained without CoTs fail on all four tasks, while CoTs implementing the theoretical upper bounds achieve near-perfect accuracy (e.g., 100% on 16-digit multiplication with the O(N log N) scratchpad, and perfect accuracy on DAG reachability up to 35 vertices).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical lower-bounds paper, so there are no direct method-vs-method comparisons. Instead, the key comparison is between the proven lower bounds and existing CoT constructions: for MULTIPLICATION, the standard grade-school algorithm requires Θ(N²) CoT steps, while the paper's NTT-based construction achieves Θ(N log N), matching the lower bound up to a log factor. For PARITY, the straightforward prefix-parity CoT of length Θ(N) is shown to be optimal, and the paper further demonstrates that dot-by-dot (pause-token) CoTs cannot achieve polynomial length, requiring ω(N^k) for any k. Empirically, transformers without CoTs perform at chance level on DAG reachability and fail to generalize on multiplication beyond short inputs, while the theoretical CoT constructions are successfully learned by small 2-layer, 2-head transformers. The paper also verifies that frontier LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, o1-mini) produce CoTs of at least linear length on PARITY, MULTIPLICATION, and MEDIAN, consistent with the theoretical lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random-restrictions technique from Hahn (2020) and the classical Switching Lemma (Hastad et al., 1994; Furst et al., 1984) from no-CoT transformers to transformers with chain-of-thought, yielding the first systematic lower bounds on CoT length. It leverages the sensitivity-based framework of Hahn & Rofin (2024) to identify which algorithmic problems are fundamentally hard for transformers, and the UHAT model from Hao et al. (2022) as the formal setting. Prior work on transformer expressivity focused on model-size bounds without CoT (Merrill & Sabharwal, Strobl, etc.) or on upper bounds showing CoT universality (Pérez et al., 2019; Bhattamishra et al., 2020b); this paper fills the gap by proving that for high-sensitivity problems, no sublinear-length CoT exists in UHAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These lower bounds establish fundamental limits on CoT compression: for PARITY, MULTIPLICATION, MEDIAN, and REACHABILITY, linear-length CoTs are provably necessary, constraining how much inference-time compute can be saved by fine-tuning shorter reasoning traces. The superpolynomial separation between full CoTs (Θ(N)) and dot-by-dot/pause-token CoTs (ω(poly(N))) for PARITY is a notable negative result showing that hidden computation cannot substitute for explicit intermediate steps on high-sensitivity tasks. Key open questions include whether other formal models (e.g., log-precision transformers) might admit shorter yet learnable CoTs, and whether average-case bounds can be established. The results suggest that architectural changes (deeper transformers, SSMs) or tool use may be necessary to overcome these representational barriers.
