## Problem

Fine-tuning vision foundation models on a downstream task causes concept forgetting on other tasks, and existing regularization methods (weight-space L2, point-wise feature matching) impose overly strong per-sample constraints that ignore the local neighborhood structure in feature space. This paper argues that the rich knowledge encoded in feature neighborhoods (e.g., shared visual attributes across classes) is lost under point-wise alignment, and that no prior method explicitly preserves this structural knowledge during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired feature vectors from a pre-trained model f_θ̂ and a fine-tuned model f_θ over a hard-class-mined mini-batch (B=64, m=16 classes × n=4 samples), where classes are greedily selected to maximize inter-class feature similarity. It produces (1) an FDA regularization loss added to the task loss, and (2) instance-wise synthetic proxies (positive and negative sets) generated online by a 23.6k-parameter network (one attention layer + two conv layers) that virtually expands the batch from 64 to ~90 feature points. The proxies are convex combinations of true features via learned softmax-normalized pooling weights, ensuring they remain on the feature manifold.

## Method

FDA constructs a k-nearest-neighbor graph (K>n to span multiple classes) over pre-trained batch features, then transfers both neighbor indices and cosine similarity weights to the fine-tuned feature space, using a noise-resistant sigmoid loss to pull pre-trained neighbors together and push non-neighbors apart in the fine-tuned space. Proxy-FDA augments this with a lightweight proxy generator that adaptively pools positive and negative feature sets to synthesize diverse proxies (n^{p+}=s·K, n^{p-}=s·(B-K-1), s=0.4), trained online with a manifold-constraint loss plus a variance-diversity penalty. The key insight is validated empirically via OTDD (Optimal Transport Dataset Distance), a structure-aware distributional metric that correlates more strongly with concept forgetting than point-wise L2 distance, confirming that structure-wise alignment is the right regularization objective. Hard class mining (greedy selection of classes with high FDA loss) ensures meaningful kNN search within each batch, and the entire pipeline adds only 17–21% training overhead without affecting inference.

## Results

On end-to-end fine-tuning of CLIP ViT-B/32 across 10 classification datasets, Proxy-FDA achieves a mean ΔLP of +1.54 (positive forward transfer), outperforming FDA (+1.39), LDIFS (+0.86), L2SP (+0.29), LP-FT (−2.59), and naive fine-tuning (−4.37), while maintaining a mean ALP of 91.82% (vs. 91.90% for naive). In few-shot prompt tuning (16 shots/class, 11 datasets), Proxy-FDA boosts ΔNew from −10.99 to −0.55 for CoOp and from 1.88 to 3.23 for PromptSRC, achieving a harmonic mean AH of 80.81 (PromptSRC+Proxy-FDA) that surpasses all regularization-free baselines. The paper further demonstrates that OTDD (a structure-aware distributional distance) correlates more strongly with concept forgetting than point-wise L2 feature distance, validating the structure-wise alignment approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proxy-FDA consistently outperforms all point-wise regularization baselines: on end-to-end fine-tuning, it achieves +1.54 mean ΔLP vs. LDIFS (+0.86) and L2SP (+0.29), while naive fine-tuning and LP-FT both suffer negative transfer (−4.37 and −2.59). In few-shot prompt tuning, Proxy-FDA plugged into CoOp improves ΔNew from −10.99 to −0.55 (a 10.4-point gain), and when added to the strongest regularization baseline PromptSRC, it further lifts ΔNew from 1.88 to 3.23 and AH from 79.97 to 80.81. For continual fine-tuning, Proxy-FDA achieves positive ΔLP on all three task sequences (e.g., +0.93 on SVHN after SVHN→CIFAR10→RESISC45), outperforming LDIFS (+0.61) and all classic continual learning methods (LwF, iCaRL, D+R, ZSCL). In knowledge distillation, Proxy-FDA matches the best prior method WTTM and outperforms relational KD baselines CNA and ITRD. The paper also shows Proxy-FDA surpasses LLM-based prompt methods ProText and ArGue-N on OOD generalization without requiring external language models, and outperforms OGEN and CLAP in all metrics under both base-to-new and domain generalization settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from point-wise feature-space regularization (LDIFS, Mukhoti et al. 2024) by introducing structure-wise alignment via nearest-neighbor graph transfer between pre-trained and fine-tuned feature spaces, using the sigmoid loss formulation from Zhai et al. (2023) as the underlying contrastive objective. The proxy generation mechanism repurposes ideas from deep metric learning (proxy anchor loss) and feature augmentation (Manifold Mixup, VOS/NPOS), but applies them to synthesize informative synthetic features that enrich FDA rather than serving as class prototypes or outlier detectors. The use of OTDD (Alvarez-Melis & Fusi, 2020) as a diagnostic metric connects the method to optimal transport theory, and the high-level idea of distilling feature relations is closely related to relational knowledge distillation (CNA, ITRD), though Proxy-FDA distills both neighbor indices and similarities simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Proxy-FDA provides a practical, plug-and-play regularization that can be added to existing prompt tuning or end-to-end fine-tuning pipelines with modest compute overhead (17–21%), making it broadly applicable to the common workflow of adapting vision foundation models. The demonstrated correlation between OTDD and forgetting (rather than L2 distance) offers a useful diagnostic for practitioners to monitor alignment quality during fine-tuning. However, the method's reliance on hard class mining and batch-level kNN computation limits its applicability to very small batches or streaming settings, and the paper leaves open whether external data retrieval could complement proxy generation for even more diverse FDA. The impact statement also raises a valid concern: preserving pre-trained structure may also preserve and amplify unintentional biases embedded in the foundation model.
