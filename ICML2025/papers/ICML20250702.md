## Problem

Existing Actor-Critic methods for average-reward RL with general policy parametrization achieve only a suboptimal O(1/T^{1/4}) global convergence rate, while direct Policy Gradient methods reach the optimal O(1/√T) rate but require precise knowledge of mixing and hitting times and do not scale to large or infinite state spaces. The paper asks whether a practical Actor-Critic approach can achieve the order-optimal O(1/√T) rate without these dependencies.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an ergodic MDP (S, A, r, P) with a d-dimensional policy parameter θ and a bounded linear feature map φ(s) with ‖φ(s)‖ ≤ 1. It generates trajectories of geometrically-distributed lengths (capped at T_max) rather than fixed-length episodes, and the critic is restricted to linear function approximation. The output is a sequence of policy parameters θ_k whose average suboptimality satisfies J* − (1/K)Σ E[J(θ_k)] ≤ O(√ε_app + √ε_bias) + Õ(t_mix³ T^{-1/2}), with no dependence on |S| or |A|.

## Method

MLMC-NAC alternates between a Critic subroutine (H inner-loop SGD steps estimating the average reward η and critic parameter ζ via TD-based updates) and a Natural Policy Gradient subroutine (H inner-loop SGD steps solving a convex subproblem for the NPG direction ω), followed by a single policy update θ_{k+1} = θ_k + αω_k. Both subroutines use Multi-Level Monte Carlo gradient estimators with geometrically-distributed trajectory lengths, which achieve the same bias order as averaging T_max samples while requiring only O(log T_max) samples on average, thereby eliminating the need to space samples t_mix apart. The key analytical contribution is a refined decomposition (Lemma 1) that bounds the global error by the bias and second-order error of the NPG estimator, and a generic stochastic linear recursion analysis (Theorem 2) that handles Markovian noise by exploiting the distinction between the full error E‖x_H − x*‖² and the mean error ‖E[x_H] − x*‖², the latter being significantly smaller and yielding the optimal T^{-1/2} rate. Standard SGD with carefully chosen learning rates (β = 4 log H / (λH), γ = 2 log H / (μH)) replaces the AdaGrad updates used in prior MLMC-AC work, exploiting the strong convexity structure of the NPG and critic subproblems.

## Results

The MLMC-NAC algorithm achieves a global convergence rate of Õ(1/√T) for average-reward MDPs with general policy parametrization, where T is the horizon length. This is the first Actor-Critic method to reach this rate, improving upon the previous state-of-the-art Õ(1/T^{1/4}) for AC approaches. The bound does not scale with the size of the state or action space, making it applicable to infinite state spaces, and depends on the mixing time only as t_mix^3 (a weaker dependence than direct PG methods that require samples spaced Õ(t_mix) apart). The convergence guarantee also includes an irreducible error term of O(√ε_app + √ε_bias) from critic approximation and policy expressivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest AC baselines—NAC-CFA (Wang et al., 2024) and MAC (Patel et al., 2024)—MLMC-NAC improves the global convergence rate from Õ(1/T^{1/4}) to Õ(1/√T) while maintaining the same favorable properties (infinite state spaces, general parametrization, model-free). Compared to the direct PG method PHPG (Ganesh et al., 2025b), which also achieves Õ(1/√T), MLMC-NAC additionally supports infinite state spaces and does not require precise knowledge of mixing or hitting times to decorrelate samples. Relative to PPGAE (Bai et al., 2024), MLMC-NAC improves the rate from Õ(1/T^{1/4}) to Õ(1/√T) and eliminates the dependence on action space size and hitting time. Against tabular model-based methods (UCRL2, PSRL) and the tabular model-free MDP-OOMD, MLMC-NAC matches the Õ(1/√T) rate while extending to general (non-tabular) policy parametrization and infinite state spaces. The key analytical improvement over prior AC works is refining the critic error term from E‖ξ_t − ξ*‖ to ‖E[ξ_t] − ξ*‖, which is significantly smaller and enables the optimal rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLMC-based actor-critic framework introduced by Suttle et al. (2023) and Patel et al. (2024), which achieved O(1/T^{1/4}) global convergence for average-reward RL with general policy parametrization. The key departures are: (1) replacing AdaGrad with standard SGD to exploit the strong convexity structure of the NPG and critic subproblems, (2) refining the analysis to bound the bias term via ‖E[ξ_t] − ξ*‖ rather than E‖ξ_t − ξ*‖, and (3) leveraging a generic stochastic linear recursion analysis (Theorem 2) to unify the NPG and critic error bounds. The MLMC estimator itself is adapted from Dorfman & Levy (2022) and Beznosikov et al. (2023), while the NPG convergence proof draws on Mondal & Aggarwal (2024) from the discounted-reward setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to achieve the order-optimal O(1/√T) global convergence rate for actor-critic methods in average-reward MDPs with general policy parametrization, closing the gap with direct policy gradient methods (Ganesh et al., 2025b) while retaining the practical advantages of AC (lower variance, applicability to infinite state spaces, no mixing-time oracle). The convergence rate is independent of state-space size, making it applicable to infinite-state MDPs. Key limitations include the restriction to linear critics (neural critics remain an open extension to the average-reward setting) and the ergodicity assumption. The paper also notes that the approach could potentially transfer to discounted MDPs, where the current SOTA for AC is O(T^{-1/3}).
