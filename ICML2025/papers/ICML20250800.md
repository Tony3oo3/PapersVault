## Problem

Existing IQA backbones face a fundamental trade-off: CNNs capture local features but lack long-range dependency modeling, while Transformers model global context at quadratic computational cost. Whether the recently introduced State Space Model (Mamba), which offers linear-complexity sequential modeling, can match or surpass these architectures for low-level visual perception in image quality assessment remained entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250800/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images as input, which are randomly cropped into ten 224×224 patches during training and evaluation. It outputs a single scalar quality score per image, evaluated via PLCC and SRCC against human subjective ratings. The framework is designed to operate in three modes: task-specific (trained and tested on one dataset), universal (mixed training across six datasets spanning synthetic, authentic, and AIGC distortions), and transferable (trained on one domain, fine-tuned with StylePrompt, then tested on other domains).

## Method

QMamba adapts the VMamba architecture by replacing global horizontal/vertical cross-scanning with a local window-based scanning strategy (inspired by LocalMamba), performing horizontal scans within local windows followed by window-level scans, applied both horizontally and vertically to preserve local distortion perception while retaining global awareness through hierarchical composition. Three scale variants (Tiny, Small, Base) are provided with 4–15 encoder blocks and 96–128 embedding dimensions. For transferable IQA, the paper introduces StylePrompt, a lightweight tuning mechanism that freezes the entire pre-trained QMamba and learns only ~4% of parameters: a StylePrompt Generation module produces N learnable 1×1×C prompt components weighted by input-dependent softmax gating, and a StylePrompt Injection module maps the fused prompt through linear layers to affine parameters (γ, β) that modulate feature mean and variance along the channel dimension, enabling domain-adaptive recalibration without full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the task-specific IQA benchmark across 8 datasets, LQMamba-B (93.79M params) achieves the best average SRCC of 0.896, while the lightweight QMamba-T (27.99M params, 4.47G GFLOPS) reaches 0.893 average SRCC—surpassing Swin-B (0.872) and ViT-B (0.854) with substantially fewer parameters and lower computational cost. On the most challenging dataset TID2013 (24 distortion types), LQMamba-B achieves 0.964 SRCC versus 0.933 for Swin-B. In transferable IQA, StylePrompt (3.83M trainable params, ~4% of total) achieves 0.901 average SRCC, nearly matching full-parameter fine-tuning (0.908) while using only 4% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QMamba consistently outperforms all compared baselines on average SRCC. Against CNN-based IQA methods, QMamba-T (0.893) surpasses DBCNN (0.852), MetaIQA (0.815), and BRISQUE (0.713) by 4–18 SRCC points. Against Transformer-based IQA methods, QMamba-T beats DEIQT (0.884) by 0.9 SRCC with lower GFLOPS (4.47G vs 5.41G), and outperforms MUSIQ (0.832) and TReS (0.858) by 5–6 SRCC points. Against generic vision backbones, QMamba-T (27.99M, 4.47G) exceeds Swin-B (86.74M, 15.47G) by 2.1 SRCC and ViT-B (85.80M, 17.58G) by 3.9 SRCC, while using roughly one-third the parameters and compute. In the transferable IQA setting, StylePrompt (3.83M params) achieves 0.901 average SRCC, outperforming SSF (6.1M, 0.735) and CrossAttn-Prompt (12.17M, 0.772) by 13–17 SRCC points, and approaching full fine-tuning (0.908) with only 4% of trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt the Mamba/State Space Model architecture to image quality assessment, building directly on VMamba's cross-scan visual SSM and LocalMamba's window-based scanning. It departs from the dominant CNN-based (DBCNN, MetaIQA, HyperIQA) and Transformer-based (MUSIQ, DEIQT, TReS) IQA literature by replacing quadratic-complexity attention with linear-complexity selective state space modeling. The key architectural departure from prior visual Mamba work is the adoption of local window scanning over global horizontal/vertical cross-scanning, motivated by the observation that IQA-relevant artifacts are predominantly local. The StylePrompt tuning strategy further distinguishes this work from standard fine-tuning and other prompt methods (SSF, cross-attention prompts) by targeting feature statistics (mean/variance) rather than content-level adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work validates Mamba as a viable and efficient backbone for low-level visual perception, opening a new architectural direction for IQA that avoids the quadratic cost of Transformers while matching or exceeding their accuracy. The StylePrompt mechanism, achieving ~99% of full-tuning performance with only 4% of parameters, offers a practical recipe for efficient cross-domain transfer that could generalize beyond IQA to other perception tasks. However, the method still relies heavily on ImageNet-1K pre-training, and the paper acknowledges that computational demands remain substantial for real-time or very large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the local-scanning advantage persists for video quality assessment, whether the approach generalizes to domains with very different statistics (e.g., medical imaging), and whether the modest performance gaps over strong Transformer baselines (DEIQT, Swin-B) widen or narrow at larger scales.
