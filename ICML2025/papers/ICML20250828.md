## Problem

Existing algorithms for stochastic contextual bandits with general constraints either require Slater's condition (yielding O~(√T/δ) bounds that degrade as constraints tighten) or achieve suboptimal O~(T^{3/4}) regret and violation without it. The open question is whether an efficient algorithm can attain the optimal O~(√T) guarantee for both regret and constraint violation in the absence of Slater's condition, which is often impractical to verify or quantify in real-world applications.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a context x_t drawn i.i.d. from a known distribution P(·), selects an action a_t from a finite set A, and observes a noisy reward r_t ∈ [0,1] and cost c_t ∈ [-1,1]. The algorithm maintains a policy π_t over the action simplex and a scalar dual variable Q_t; the dual update uniquely requires the context distribution P(·) to compute E_x[⟨π_t, ĝ_t(x)⟩], distinguishing it from standard primal-dual methods that only use the realized context. The cost functions are general (can be positive or negative), covering knapsack, fairness, and group-fairness constraints within a single formulation.

## Method

Optimistic³ integrates three layers of optimism into a primal-dual framework: (1) optimistic parameter learning via UCB reward and LCB cost estimators from online regression oracles; (2) optimistic primal decision via KL-regularized mirror descent (yielding a closed-form Exp3-style update) on the surrogate L̂(x_t,a) = f̂_t(x_t,a) − Q_t ĝ_t(x_t,a); and (3) an optimistic dual update Q_{t+1} = (Q_t − E_x[⟨π_{t−1},ĝ_{t−1}(x)⟩] + 2E_x[⟨π_t,ĝ_t(x)⟩])⁺ that introduces a momentum correction term to smooth dual fluctuations. The key analytical tool is a unified 'regret-plus-drift' bound (Lemma 5.3) combining the pushback property of Bregman divergences with a cross-term inequality (Lemma 5.7) that controls the interaction between consecutive dual estimates, ultimately yielding O~(√T) regret and violation without any Slater's condition assumption. For knapsack constraints, the violation bound translates into a stopping-time analysis giving O((1+ν*/b)√T) regret in a universal budget regime, and for non-contextual MAB the framework recovers best-of-both-worlds guarantees for stochastic and adversarial constraints with gradient-type updates rather than per-round linear programming.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 5.1: Optimistic³ achieves Õ(√T) regret and Õ(√T) constraint violation for contextual bandits with general constraints, without requiring Slater's condition or knowledge of the Slater constant δ. For knapsack constraints (Theorem 6.1), the regret bound is Õ((1+ν*/b)√T) in a universal budget regime B=Ω(1). In experiments on MSLR-WEB30K, Optimistic³ outperforms both LOE2D and LagrangeCBwLC in averaged reward and constraint violation (Figure 1), with results averaged over 50 trials at 95% confidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Guo & Liu 2024 (LOE2D), Optimistic³ improves the no-Slater bound from Õ(T^{3/4}) to Õ(√T) and the with-Slater bound from Õ(√T/δ²) to Õ(√T), eliminating the δ-dependency entirely. Against Slivkins et al. 2023 (LagrangeCBwLC), Optimistic³ removes the Õ(√T/δ) dependency on the Slater constant, which is critical when constraints are tight (δ→0). For knapsack constraints, Optimistic³ recovers the state-of-the-art O((1+ν*/b)√T) bound of Chzhen et al. 2024 and Guo & Liu 2025, but without requiring Slater's condition or prior knowledge of δ. In the MAB extension, Optimistic³ matches the best-of-both-worlds guarantees of Bernasconi et al. 2024a (Õ(√T) for stochastic, Õ(√T) β-regret for adversarial) while using a gradient-based update instead of per-round linear programming, yielding significantly lower computational overhead. Empirically on MSLR-WEB30K, Optimistic³ dominates both LOE2D and LagrangeCBwLC in both reward and violation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework for contextual bandits with general constraints established by Slivkins et al. (2023) and Guo & Liu (2024), whose LagrangeCBwLC and LOE2D algorithms respectively achieved O~(√T/δ) (requiring Slater's condition) and O~(T^{3/4}) (without it). The key departure is the 'triple-optimistic' design: in addition to standard UCB/LCB parameter estimation and KL-regularized mirror descent for the primal, the paper introduces an optimistic dual update with a momentum-like correction term that smooths the dual variable trajectory. For the MAB extension, the paper adopts the adaptive weighted cost estimator from Bernasconi et al. (2024a) but replaces their per-round linear-programming-based feasible set construction with a gradient-type update, recovering the same best-of-both-worlds guarantees at lower computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a stated open question by achieving the optimal O~(√T) regret and violation for contextual bandits with general constraints without Slater's condition, eliminating the 1/δ dependency that becomes problematic when constraints are tight. A notable limitation is that the optimistic dual update requires knowledge of the context distribution; the authors acknowledge this as an important direction for future work. The framework's generality—covering knapsack, fairness, and group-fairness constraints under a single analysis—makes it a useful template for future constrained bandit algorithms, though the experimental validation is limited to a single dataset (MSLR-WEB30k) with a simple cost model.
