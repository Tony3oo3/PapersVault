## Problem

Standard self-attention in Transformers produces row-stochastic matrices that can overconcentrate on a few tokens, degrading information flow. While Sinkformer enforces doubly-stochastic attention via iterative Sinkhorn normalization, this requires sequential iterations (O(SN²)) that become a bottleneck. The paper asks whether doubly-stochastic attention can be enforced in a fully parallelizable, non-iterative manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252149/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes query, key, and value matrices (Q, K ∈ R^{m×N}, V ∈ R^{d×N}) derived from input tokens—image patches (224×224, patch size 16), 3D point clouds (5000 points), or text sequences—and produces an attention-weighted output VG where G is a doubly-stochastic attention matrix. The axis-aligned slicing (Θ = I_m) means each of the m query/key dimensions is treated as a learnable slice, and the inverse temperature τ controls how many tokens each token attends to. At inference, soft sorting can be replaced with hard sorting, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(LN log N).

## Method

ESPFormer interprets queries and keys as uniform empirical measures and constructs a doubly-stochastic transport plan via Expected Sliced Transport Plans (ESP). For each of m axis-aligned slices, projected keys and queries are soft-sorted using a temperature-based SoftSort relaxation, yielding soft permutation matrices A_l and B_l; the per-slice transport plan is U_l = (1/N) A_l^T B_l. These plans are aggregated as G = Σ_l σ_l^τ U_l, where σ^τ is a softmax over per-slice transport costs weighted by an inverse temperature τ, ensuring G is doubly-stochastic by construction. The entire computation is parallelizable across slices (no iterative loop), giving O(mN(N+d)) complexity versus O((S+m)N²) for Sinkhorn. During training, soft sorting provides differentiability; after exponential temperature annealing, hard sorting at inference yields exact doubly-stochastic matrices and further reduces cost.

## Results

On the Cats and Dogs dataset with 100% training data, ESPFormer (Hard Sort) achieves 81.23% accuracy, outperforming Sinkformer (79.12%), DiffTransformer (78.85%), and Transformer (78.49%). On ModelNet40, Set ESPFormer reaches 89.4% mean accuracy versus 88.3% for Set Sinkformer and 85.8% for the original Set Transformer. On IWSLT'14 German-to-English NMT, ESPFormer attains the best BLEU scores of 34.64 (Transformer backbone) and 34.83 (DiffTransformer backbone) after fine-tuning, surpassing all baselines. ESPFormer also improves sentiment analysis accuracy on IMDb (85.47% vs. 85.25% Transformer) and TweetEval (72.36% vs. 71.31% Transformer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESPFormer consistently outperforms the vanilla Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) across all evaluated tasks, with the largest gap on Cats and Dogs at 1% data (58.52% vs. 49.71%, a ~9-point improvement) and a more modest but consistent gain on IMDb sentiment analysis (85.47% vs. 85.25%). Against Sinkformer (Sander et al., 2022), ESPFormer wins on Cats and Dogs (81.23% vs. 79.12% at 100% data), ModelNet40 Set Transformer (89.4% vs. 88.3% mean), and NMT fine-tuning (34.64 vs. 34.61 BLEU), while matching on Point Cloud Transformer (92.7% mean for both). Compared to DiffTransformer (Ye et al., 2025), ESPFormer achieves higher accuracy on Cats and Dogs (81.23% vs. 78.85%), ModelNet40 Set Transformer (89.4% vs. 88.7%), and NMT (34.83 vs. 34.78 BLEU with DiffTransformer backbone). On ModelNet40, ESPFormer also surpasses the original Set Transformer (Lee et al., 2019) by 3.6 points (89.4% vs. 85.8%) and matches the Point Cloud Transformer (Guo et al., 2021) at 92.7% mean. A key efficiency advantage over Sinkformer is that ESPFormer's computation is fully parallelizable across slices, whereas Sinkhorn requires sequential iterations; ESPFormer outperforms Sinkformer in wall-clock time for S > 3 iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESPFormer sits at the intersection of two research threads: doubly-stochastic attention (initiated by Sinkformer, which replaces softmax with iterative Sinkhorn normalization) and sliced optimal transport (which provides efficient 1D projections of high-dimensional distributions). The key conceptual departure from Sinkformer is replacing the sequential, iterative row/column normalization with a fully parallelizable aggregation of lifted transport plans from axis-aligned slices, using the Expected Sliced Transport Plan (ESP) framework of Liu et al. (2025) as the theoretical backbone and SoftSort (Prillo & Eisenschlos, 2020) as the differentiable sorting primitive. The paper also positions itself relative to Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2025), demonstrating that the ESP attention mechanism is composable with differential attention rather than competing with it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the iterative bottleneck of Sinkhorn normalization, yielding a fully parallelizable attention with O(mN(N+d)) training complexity and O(LN log N) inference complexity after temperature annealing to hard sorting. This makes doubly-stochastic attention viable at sequence lengths where Sinkformer's S-iteration loop becomes prohibitive. However, the method is incompatible with causal/autoregressive architectures (enforcing both lower-triangularity and doubly stochasticity collapses to the identity), and training memory scales linearly with the number of slices. Open questions include whether the axis-aligned slicing assumption remains optimal for very high-dimensional or anisotropic key/query spaces, and whether the modest accuracy gains observed here persist at ImageNet-scale or with much longer sequences.
