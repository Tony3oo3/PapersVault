## Problem

Open-world Prompt Tuning (OPT) requires models to both detect whether an input belongs to the base or new domain and classify it within that domain, but existing evaluation metrics (HM, OverallAcc, AUROC) each fail to simultaneously assess detection, classification, and remain insensitive to unknown base/new sample ratios. This inconsistency means models with identical metric scores can have vastly different real-world performance, and no single metric guides end-to-end optimization of the two-stage pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder, with text prompts (learnable tokens in a template like 'a photo of [CLASS]') fed to the frozen text encoder. At inference, the model first produces a scalar domain-detection score r(x) in [0,1] (higher = base domain), then routes the sample to either the base-domain classifier g or the new-domain classifier h to output a class label. Training operates in a 16-shot few-shot regime on base classes only, with new-domain samples entirely unavailable.

## Method

The paper introduces OpenworldAUC, a pairwise ranking metric defined as the joint probability that (1) a base instance receives a higher detection score than a new instance and (2) both instances are correctly classified in their respective domains, making it simultaneously sensitive to detection, classification, and distribution-free. To optimize this metric, Gated Mixture-of-Prompts (GMoP) assigns three specialized learnable prompts—one for the detector r, one for the base classifier g, and a fixed zero-shot prompt for the new classifier h—and couples them via a sigmoid gating mechanism that weights each base-new pair by the classifiers' confidence in the ground-truth channel, enabling an AUROC-style squared ranking loss to be differentiable. Pseudo base-to-new partitions (K random splits of the known base classes) simulate the new domain during training, with each partition assigned its own sub-detector whose maximum score is used at test time. A generalization bound (Thm. 5.2) shows that moderate training set size and partition number K suffice for satisfactory test performance.

## Results

On the open-world recognition task across 11 benchmarks, GMoP achieves an average OpenworldAUC of 60.94, outperforming the strongest baselines DePT-Kg (59.00), TCP (58.90), and DeCoOp (58.77) by approximately 2%. The method also demonstrates robustness to domain imbalance, achieving 68.84 average OpenworldAUC on 10 imbalanced benchmarks (1:5 base/new ratio), and leads on domain generalization to ImageNet variants (e.g., 62.67 on ImageNet-R vs. 61.01 for DeCoOp). The paper reports results averaged over 5 random seeds using 16-shot prompt tuning with ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMoP outperforms all 10 baselines on average OpenworldAUC across the 11 recognition benchmarks, with the largest margins over HM-oriented methods (CoOp: +3.35, KgCoOp: +5.87) and OOD-oriented methods (LoCoOp: +8.32, Gallop: +4.04). Among the strongest competitors, DePT-Kg (59.00) and TCP (58.90) are the closest, with GMoP leading by ~2 points; DeCoOp (58.77), the first dedicated OPT framework, trails by 2.17 points. On individual datasets, GMoP wins on 9 out of 11 benchmarks, with notable gains on Flowers102 (+2.51 over DeCoOp) and SUN397 (+1.54 over DeCoOp), while Aircraft remains a challenging outlier where all methods score below 13. The method also consistently leads on domain-imbalanced settings (Table 3, avg. 68.84 vs. DeCoOp's 66.87) and domain generalization to ImageNet variants (Table 4, e.g., +1.66 on ImageNet-R over DeCoOp), confirming that the OpenworldAUC-oriented optimization transfers well beyond the in-distribution recognition task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Open-world Prompt Tuning (OPT) framework introduced by DeCoOp (Zhou et al., 2024), which established the two-stage divide-and-conquer pipeline of base-to-new detection followed by domain-specific classification. It also extends the broader prompt tuning lineage (CoOp, MaPLe, KgCoOp, TCP) by addressing a gap those methods leave: none of them jointly optimize detection and classification under a single unified objective. The key departure is the proposal of OpenworldAUC, a pairwise ranking metric that simultaneously captures detection quality, base/new classification accuracy, and distribution insensitivity—properties that the paper proves no single existing metric (HM, OverallAcc, AUROC) satisfies. The Gated Mixture-of-Prompts optimizer then operationalizes this metric by assigning separate prompts to the detector and each classifier, connected through a sigmoid gating mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metric gap identified here is practically significant: models optimized for HM can have large performance differences in the OPT setting while scoring identically, making model selection unreliable. OpenworldAUC's distribution insensitivity is particularly valuable for deployment where base/new ratios are unknown a priori. However, the method's reliance on pseudo base-to-new partitions (which approximate but do not replicate true unseen classes) and a fixed zero-shot CLIP prompt for the new-domain classifier limits its ceiling. Open questions include whether the gating mechanism generalizes beyond CLIP-based architectures, whether the K-partition strategy scales to very large class spaces, and whether the theoretical generalization bound's assumptions (moderate N_b, proper K) hold in practice for the most challenging open-world scenarios.
