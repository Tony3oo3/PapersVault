## Problem

DPO's core advantage is bypassing RL entirely, yet MCTS-based reasoning approaches inherently require RL-learned value functions for UCB-guided search, creating a fundamental conflict that prevents seamless integration of tree-search reasoning with RL-free preference optimization. Additionally, standard MCTS suffers from cold-start exploration expressiveness (discrete integer visit counts limit policy diversity) and sparse-action improvement (unvisited actions receive no gradient signal), which are particularly problematic in the exponentially large token-level state space of language generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with preferred and rejected responses (standard DPO-style preference data) and produces two trained language models: a token-selection policy π_θ for generation and a stochastic tree-search policy π_φ for exploration-guided decoding. A key structural detail is that both LMs are initialized from the same SFT checkpoint and trained in an alternating fashion, with π_θ frozen during π_φ updates. For reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH, Game24), the input is reconfigured from question-answer pairs into preference pairs, and the action space shifts from token-level to sentence-level (terminated by newline tokens).

## Method

The paper reformulates AlphaZero-like MCTS as a stochastic policy via reverse KL-divergence regularization (Theorem 3.3), proving asymptotic equivalence between the empirical visit distribution and a continuous simplex-constrained policy. Instead of solving this policy per-state via dichotomic search (infeasible in exponential state spaces), IT-PO introduces a second LM π_φ as a universal approximator of the ITS policy, trained with a new preference loss (Eq. 15/18) that constructs Bradley-Terry logits from the ratio π_θ(a|s)/π̄_φ(a|s) rather than explicit Q-values, eliminating both reward and value modeling. Training alternates between cDPO updates on π_θ and IT-PO loss updates on π_φ, with self-improved preference policy augmentation (resampling K responses from π_φ, ranking by the ITS preference score, and feeding top pairs back into cDPO). At inference, ITS-α decoding replaces MCTS visit-count exploration with the learned stochastic policy's temperature-scaled distribution, while ITS-rollout performs test-time meta-updates of π_φ on unseen prompts before decoding.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH preference alignment benchmark, IT-PO_φ achieves 69.12% accuracy and 5.315 entropy, outperforming the strongest DPO variant TDPO₂ (67.33% acc, 4.915 ent) and standard DPO (59.43% acc, 3.196 ent). On mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, Path@1), ITS-α reaches 53.2% accuracy with 561 tokens, surpassing MCTS-α (51.9%) and BFS-V (52.5%). On Game24 planning, ITS-Rollout achieves 73.2% with 646 tokens, beating MCTS-Rollout (71.3%) and BFS-V (64.8%). On the harder MATH benchmark, ITS-α (39.8%) and ITS-rollout (40.2%) outperform their MCTS counterparts (36.0% and 36.7%) by roughly 3–4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO-family baselines on Anthropic HH, IT-PO_φ wins on both accuracy (69.12% vs. DPO's 59.43%, RTO's 61.43%, and TDPO₂'s 67.33%) and diversity (5.315 vs. 3.196, 3.314, and 4.915 respectively), with IT-PO_θ also exceeding DPO and RTO in accuracy while matching TDPO₂. On GSM8K (Path@1), ITS-α (53.2%) outperforms MCTS-α (51.9%), BFS-V (52.5%), and CoT-greedy (41.4%) from the TS-LLM baseline suite, while ITS-Rollout (51.6%) is competitive with MCTS-Rollout (47.8%). On Game24, the advantage is more pronounced: ITS-Rollout reaches 73.2% vs. MCTS-Rollout's 71.3% and BFS-V's 64.8%, and ITS-α (67.6%) beats MCTS-α (63.3%) and BFS-V (64.8%). On MATH, both ITS variants consistently outperform their MCTS counterparts by ~3–4 points (39.8% vs. 36.0% for α; 40.2% vs. 36.7% for rollout), and notably a smaller φ model (LLAMA3-8B) still yields competitive results (37.9%/38.2%) compared to the full-size φ (Qwen1.5-32B). The paper also shows that IT-PO methods are more robust to temperature scaling at inference, maintaining >50% GPT-4 win rate in 7 out of 10 temperature settings where T-DPO variants degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: DPO-family preference optimization (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and AlphaZero-like MCTS for LLM reasoning (Feng et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2024a). The key theoretical insight is borrowed from Grill et al. (2020b), who showed that MCTS exploration is asymptotically equivalent to a stochastic policy solved by reverse-KL-regularized local optimization. The paper's departure from prior MCTS-based LLM work is eliminating the RL-learned value function that standard UCB-based search requires, replacing it with a second LM that approximates the implicit tree search policy via neural universal approximation. This resolves the fundamental tension between DPO's RL-free design and MCTS's dependence on learned value functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling MCTS-like reasoning benefits within a purely RL-free training pipeline, which is attractive for practitioners who want DPO's stability without sacrificing reasoning capability. The finding that a substantially smaller model (LLAMA3-8B) can serve as the ITS policy approximator alongside a 32B main model suggests the approach may be more computationally tractable than training two equally-sized LMs. However, open questions remain: the asymptotic equivalence between ITS and MCTS requires sufficient simulation rounds, the two-LM architecture increases memory and training cost, and the empirical gains over strong MCTS baselines (e.g., ~2 points on Game24) are modest, leaving unclear whether the added complexity is justified at scale. The connection to GRPO (DeepSeek) hints at broader relevance but is only briefly discussed.
