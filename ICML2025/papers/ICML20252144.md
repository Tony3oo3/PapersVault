## Problem

Two dominant paradigms exist for scaling test-time compute in LLMs: verifier-free (VF) methods that distill or clone expert search traces via supervised fine-tuning, and verifier-based (VB) methods that use reward signals (0/1 outcome labels or trained verifiers) to guide RL or search. It remains unclear which class scales more efficiently as the test-time token budget grows, and no prior work provides a theoretical separation between them under realistic assumptions on the pre-trained base model.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a base pre-trained LLM policy π_b, a distribution of problems ρ, and a test-time token budget H. VF methods consume n expert solution traces (e.g., from rejection sampling or distillation), while VB methods consume n reward annotations on rollouts sampled from π_b itself. The output is a fine-tuned policy evaluated under a bi-level reward: 0 until the correct answer appears in the trace, then 1 for all subsequent tokens, so that performance captures both correctness and token efficiency within the H-token budget.

## Method

The paper proves an information-theoretic lower bound showing that any VF algorithm incurs suboptimality Ω(H/√n) when the base policy is heterogeneous (i.e., solution traces for a given problem vary widely in token efficiency), building on second-order suboptimality bounds from Foster et al. (2024a). In contrast, a simple VB algorithm—train a verifier on n reward annotations, then perform pessimistic policy optimization against the learned reward—achieves suboptimality O(H/n), yielding a √H performance gap as test-time compute scales. The separation holds under two conditions on the base LLM: heterogeneity (high variance in the reward distribution) and anti-concentration (constant probability mass on rewards exceeding the mean by σ√κ), both verified empirically for Llama-3.1/3.2 models. Empirically, on MATH with 3B/8B Llama models, VB best-of-N search outperforms VF SFT by 8× in compute scaling and 6× in data efficiency, and the gap grows super-linearly when both n and H are scaled together, matching the theoretical prediction.

## Results

On MATH with Llama-3.1-8B, the verifier-based Best-of-N (BoN) approach scales test-time compute by 8× over verifier-free SFT at a fixed data budget of 2^14 samples, and achieves 6× better data efficiency at a fixed test compute of 2^12 tokens. When both data n and test compute H are scaled proportionally, the accuracy gap between RL (VB) and SFT (VF) grows super-linearly in log H, matching the theoretical Ω(√H) prediction from Theorem 5.8. On the S1 model (trained via verifier-free distillation from Gemini Thinking), BoN with a trained outcome verifier outperforms S1's budget-forcing approach across all tested compute budgets on both MATH500 and AIME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is S1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025), a model trained via verifier-free supervised distillation from Gemini Thinking traces. In a compute-matched evaluation on MATH500 and AIME, the paper's simple VB method (BoN with a trained 0/1 outcome verifier) outperforms S1's budget-forcing approach across all tested test-time compute budgets (Figure 6), demonstrating that even a single step of verifier-based policy improvement beats a fully distilled VF model. Internally, the paper compares VF SFT on manually stitched sequential revision traces (following Qu et al., 2024 and Snell et al., 2024) against VB BoN and RL. On MATH, BoN wins by 8× in compute scaling and 6× in data efficiency, and the gap widens super-linearly as both H and n grow. On the didactic planted-subsequence task, RL matches the expert policy closely while SFT's suboptimality grows with H, confirming the theoretical Ω(H/√n) lower bound for VF methods. Notably, when base-policy heterogeneity σ_x is low (Figure 7), VF SFT can outperform BoN due to verifier inaccuracy, but since real LLMs are predominantly heterogeneous, VB methods win on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on information-theoretic lower bounds from imitation learning (Foster et al., 2024a) to establish a fundamental separation between verifier-based and verifier-free approaches to scaling test-time compute in LLMs. It extends the planted subsequence problem from Setlur et al. (2024b) to a contextual setting for controlled experiments, and positions its analysis at the intersection of test-time compute scaling (Snell et al., 2024; Sardana et al., 2023) and the growing literature on RL with outcome rewards (DeepSeek-R1, Kimi K1.5) versus distillation-based approaches (S1, Stream of Search). The key conceptual departure is formalizing 'asymptotic test-time compute efficiency' (Definition 4.2) and proving that the performance gap between the two method families grows with the token budget H, rather than shrinking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result showing that verification is not merely helpful but fundamentally necessary for efficient test-time compute scaling, with the advantage of VB over VF methods widening as budgets grow (Ω(√H) gap). The anti-concentration condition, while abstract, is verified to hold for practical LLMs (Llama-3.1/3.2), grounding the theory in reality. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to dense/process rewards versus sparse outcome rewards, other reward function classes (e.g., generative verifiers), and very long contexts (>32k tokens) where training becomes prohibitively expensive. The practical implication is clear: practitioners scaling test-time compute should invest in verifiers and RL rather than relying solely on distilling expert traces.
