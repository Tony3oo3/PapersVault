## Problem

Existing VLM safety benchmarks suffer from low harmfulness levels, ambiguous image-text pairs that fail to elicit harmful responses, and limited diversity in image-text combinations. Their automated evaluation methods (e.g., StrongREJECT) overestimate jailbreak success because VLMs often produce specific, convincing but genuinely unharmful image descriptions, which are misclassified as successful attacks. This makes it difficult to determine whether a model was truly compromised or merely described the input image.

## Input / Output

The ELITE evaluator consumes a triple of (input image, forbidden text prompt, victim model response) and outputs a continuous safety score in [0, 25] computed as (1 − refused) × (specific + convincing)/2 × toxicity, where refused is binary, specific and convincing are scored 1–5, and toxicity is scored 0–5. A score ≥ 10 is classified as unsafe. At the benchmark level, the 4,587 image-text pairs are organized across 11 AILuminate-aligned taxonomies, and the primary reported metric is E-ASR (percentage of pairs scoring ≥ 10) per taxonomy and in aggregate, evaluated against 18 proprietary and open-source VLMs.

## Method

The core technical contribution is extending the StrongREJECT rubric-based evaluator with an explicit toxicity score (0–5) as a multiplicative factor, so that merely descriptive or unhelpful responses receive low scores even when the model does not refuse. For benchmark construction, the authors apply this evaluator to filter existing benchmarks (VLGuard, MLLMGuard, MM-SafetyBench, SIUO, Figstep, SPA-VL, JailbreakV-28k) using a threshold of 10, requiring at least 2 of 3 victim models (Phi-3.5-Vision, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision, Pixtral-12B) to exceed the threshold. They additionally generate 1,054 new image-text pairs via four in-house methods—Role Playing, Fake News, Blueprint, and Flowchart—that cover all four image-text pair types (safe-safe, safe-unsafe, unsafe-safe, unsafe-unsafe), with images created using Flux AI and Grok 2 and prompts validated against the three victim models. Taxonomy balancing is performed by removing lowest-scoring pairs from over-represented categories to maintain distributional balance across the 11 hazard categories.

## Results

The ELITE evaluator (GPT-4o) achieves an AUC of 0.77 on the 963-sample human evaluation dataset, substantially outperforming StrongREJECT (GPT-4o) at 0.46. In safeguard model comparisons, ELITE (GPT-4o) attains 72.6% accuracy and 0.637 F1, surpassing LlamaGuard3-Vision-11B (60.3%, 0.233 F1), LlavaGuard-13B (53.6%, 0.346 F1), and the OpenAI Moderation API (62.4%, 0.412 F1). On the 4,587-pair ELITE benchmark, GPT-4o is the most safety-aligned model with an E-ASR of 15.67%, while Pixtral-12B is the most vulnerable at 79.86%. The ELITE benchmark yields markedly higher E-ASR than prior benchmarks (e.g., 63.59% for LLaVa-v1.5-7B on ELITE vs. 27.75% on VLGuard and 45.06% on MM-SafetyBench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the StrongREJECT evaluator, ELITE wins decisively: AUC rises from 0.46 to 0.77 on the human-labeled set, and even ELITE with the smaller InternVL2.5-26B (AUC 0.65) surpasses StrongREJECT with GPT-4o. Against safeguard models, ELITE (GPT-4o) outperforms LlamaGuard3-Vision-11B by ~12.3 points in accuracy and 0.404 in F1, LlavaGuard-13B by ~19 points in accuracy and 0.291 in F1, and the OpenAI Moderation API by ~10.2 points in accuracy and 0.225 in F1; ELITE also leads across all 11 taxonomies in per-category F1 (Table 7). In benchmark comparisons (Table 4), the ELITE benchmark produces substantially higher E-ASR than VLGuard, MM-SafetyBench, and MLLMGuard for every tested model (e.g., LLaVa-v1.5-13B: 69.68% vs. 28.40%, 46.61%, 27.26%), confirming that existing benchmarks contain many ambiguous pairs that fail to elicit harmful responses. The ELITE benchmark (generated) subset further boosts E-ASR (e.g., 78.46% for LLaVa-v1.5-13B), validating the in-house generation methods. Notably, the ELITE benchmark also achieves higher standard ASR than prior benchmarks, demonstrating it is not overfitted to the ELITE evaluator metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELITE extends the StrongREJECT evaluator (Souly et al., 2024), a rubric-based jailbreak evaluation for LLMs, to the vision-language setting by introducing a multiplicative toxicity score that distinguishes genuinely harmful outputs from merely descriptive ones. It positions itself against a family of existing VLM safety benchmarks (VLGuard, MLLMGuard, MM-SafetyBench, JailbreakV-28K, SPA-VL, SIUO, Figstep) that the authors argue suffer from low harmfulness, ambiguous image-text pairs, and limited diversity in pair combinations. The benchmark construction pipeline also departs from prior work by incorporating safe-safe image-text pairs and four in-house generation methods (Role Playing, Fake News, Blueprint, Flowchart) to cover all four image-text pair types, whereas most prior benchmarks focus predominantly on unsafe-unsafe or safe-unsafe combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, higher-quality benchmark (4,587 pairs) and a simple evaluator modification that VLM safety researchers can adopt to get more reliable safety assessments than the ASR-based or safeguard-model approaches currently in use. The finding that a majority of filtered-out pairs from existing benchmarks produced only descriptive (non-harmful) responses underscores a quality gap in the current evaluation landscape. Open questions include whether the rubric-based approach generalizes to multi-turn jailbreaks, whether the toxicity score's effectiveness depends on the evaluator model's own safety alignment, and whether the 10-point threshold is robust across different model families and deployment settings.
