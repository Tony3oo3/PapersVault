## Problem

Existing trusted multi-view classification methods suffer from two critical limitations: (1) feature-level opacity, where the model acts as a black box and cannot explain which input features drive the decision, and (2) distribution-unaware uncertainty estimation, where the uncertainty mass depends only on the total sum of evidence rather than its distribution across classes, causing counterintivially constant uncertainty when evidence becomes more diffuse (e.g., under noise).

## Input / Output

The method consumes two views of a 30-second single-channel EEG epoch: the raw time-domain signal (100 Hz or 125 Hz sampling) and its STFT spectrogram (256-point FFT, 50% overlap, yielding 129 frequency bins). Beyond the standard class label, the model outputs per-class belief masses, a distribution-aware uncertainty mass, and per-Gabor-kernel importance scores computed via gradient-based sensitivity. A multi-epoch Bi-LSTM wrapper takes 10 consecutive single-epoch outputs (±5 epochs) to model inter-epoch transition rules, with Gabor kernel frequencies clamped to 0–35 Hz.

## Method

TMCEK embeds trainable Gabor kernels (parameterized by center, width, and frequency) as the first convolutional layer of the time-domain branch, explicitly encoding expert-known EEG patterns (slow waves ~1 Hz, theta 3–7 Hz, alpha 8–13 Hz, spindles 15–18 Hz, K-complexes) to yield interpretable feature-level evidence. The key theoretical contribution is a distribution-aware subjective opinion that replaces the conventional uncertainty mass u = K/S with u = Kd/S, where d = (1 + Gini(e))/2 captures how concentrated the evidence is across classes; three propositions prove this formulation correctly increases uncertainty under evidence dispersion and that the modified mass aggregation is equivalent to dispersion-weighted evidence pooling. The two views (1D CNN on raw EEG + 2D CNN on STFT) produce independent Dirichlet-parameterized opinions that are fused via a modified Dempster-Shafer rule incorporating the distribution parameter d. Training uses an annealed loss combining adjusted cross-entropy, KL divergence to a uniform Dirichlet, and inter-view consistency terms (probability disagreement weighted by confidence plus evidence cosine similarity), with a multi-epoch Bi-LSTM head for temporal context.

## Results

On the three sleep staging datasets, TMCEK achieves the best performance across all metrics: 85.0% Acc / 80.2% MF1 / 0.80 Kappa on Sleep-EDF 20, 81.4% Acc / 77.5% MF1 / 0.75 Kappa on Sleep-EDF 78, and 84.3% Acc / 78.0% MF1 / 0.79 Kappa on SHHS. On standard multi-view benchmarks, TMCEK also leads: 98.80% on HandWritten, 74.84% on Scene15, 94.67% on CUB, and 97.06% on PIE. The method additionally demonstrates superior uncertainty estimation under noise (σ up to 100) and improved robustness compared to non-trusted variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMCEK outperforms all sleep staging baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitors are DFSC (84.44% Acc on EDF20) and AttnSleep (84.2% Acc on SHHS), which TMCEK surpasses by 0.56 and 0.1 percentage points respectively in accuracy, while leading by a larger margin in MF1 (80.2 vs. 78.25 on EDF20; 78.0 vs. 75.3 on SHHS). Among multi-view trusted methods, TMCEK beats the strongest baseline CCML by 0.05% on HandWritten, 2.24% on Scene15, 0.09% on CUB, and 2.50% on PIE. RCML, the most recent conflict-aware method, is outperformed on all four multi-view datasets and additionally misclassifies noisy samples where TMCEK remains correct, while RCML's uncertainty paradoxically decreases under noise. The Gabor-knowledge-based MISC (Niknazar & Mednick, 2024) is consistently outperformed on all three sleep datasets (e.g., 85.0 vs. 81.9 Acc on EDF20), confirming that the distribution-aware uncertainty mechanism provides gains beyond expert-knowledge embedding alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trusted multi-view classification (TMC) line initiated by Han et al. (ETMC, 2023) and the conflictive multi-view learning framework of Xu et al. (RCML, 2024) by replacing the standard subjective opinion with a distribution-aware variant that incorporates a Gini-coefficient-based evidence concentration measure. For feature-level interpretability, it adapts the Gabor kernel parameterization strategy from Niknazar & Mednick (MISC, 2024) but embeds it within a dual-view (time-domain + STFT) evidential architecture rather than a single-stream design. The evidential deep learning foundation (Sensoy et al., 2018) is retained, but the uncertainty mass formula is modified so that u depends on both the total evidence sum and its distributional spread, addressing what the authors term the 'Evidence Distribution-unaware Problem.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-aware subjective opinion offers a clean, theoretically grounded fix to a known limitation in evidential deep learning (uncertainty insensitive to evidence spread), and the Gini-based formulation is simple enough to drop into existing EDL pipelines. However, the approach is instantiated and validated almost exclusively on single-channel EEG sleep staging, and the Gabor kernel interpretability is tightly coupled to sleep-domain expert knowledge (slow waves, spindles, K-complexes), limiting immediate transfer to other modalities. Open questions include whether the distribution-aware opinion generalizes to high-dimensional or non-Dirichlet settings, whether the Gini coefficient is the optimal dispersion measure (vs. entropy, variance), and whether the modest 1–3% accuracy gains over strong baselines (e.g., CCML, RCML) persist at larger scale or with more views.
