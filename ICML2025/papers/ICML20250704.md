## Problem

Classical infinite-horizon average-reward RL is known to incur Ω(√T) regret across T rounds, and no quantum speedup had been demonstrated for this setting. The paper asks whether quantum statistical estimation primitives—specifically quantum mean estimation with its O(1/n) convergence rate—can be integrated into a model-based RL framework to break this classical lower bound, while navigating the fundamental obstacle that quantum measurement collapses the state and prevents repeated use of a single sample.

## Input / Output

At each round the agent observes a classical state-action pair (s_t, a_t) and receives both a classical next-state/reward signal and a quantum sample |ψ⟩^t, which is a coherent superposition over all successor states weighted by the true transition probabilities P(s'|s_t, a_t). Because measurement collapses the quantum state, each |ψ⟩^t can be measured exactly once; the algorithm therefore defers all quantum measurements to epoch boundaries. The output is a stationary policy π_e(a|s) computed by solving an optimistic linear program at each epoch, together with a provable regret bound of Õ(1) (constant in T), in contrast to the classical Õ(√T) bound.

## Method

Q-UCRL is an epoch-based, optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty algorithm that replaces classical empirical transition estimates with quantum mean estimates produced by the QBounded subroutine (Cornelissen et al., 2022), which achieves O(1/n) estimation error versus the classical O(1/√n) rate. A key technical novelty is a momentum-based estimator (Eqs. 21–25) that fuses each epoch's fresh quantum mean estimate with all prior estimates using count-based weights, thereby preserving information that would otherwise be destroyed by quantum state collapse and enabling accuracy to accumulate across epochs. At each epoch boundary the agent solves a convex optimistic LP over transition probabilities within a confidence ball around the fused estimate, yielding an optimistic policy. The regret analysis is entirely martingale-free: it decomposes regret into long-term averaged Bellman errors (following Agarwal et al., 2022b), bounds those errors via the quantum estimation guarantee and the MDP mixing time, and avoids the classical martingale concentration inequalities that have no quantum analogue, ultimately yielding the Õ(1) regret bound.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that Q-UCRL achieves a regret bound of O(S⁵A⁴T_mix · log³(T/(SA)) · log^{1/2}(T√S) · log(S²AT)) across T rounds, which is Õ(1) — effectively constant regret independent of T in the leading order. This breaks through the classical theoretical lower bound of Ω(√T) established by Jaksch et al. (2010). The key intermediate result is that quantum mean estimation via QBounded yields a per-epoch transition probability error of O(log(S/δ)/ν_e(s,a)) (Lemma 2), a quadratic improvement over the classical O(√(log/ν)) rate, which propagates through the Bellman error analysis to produce the exponential regret improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are made at the level of regret bounds rather than empirical metrics. Q-UCRL's Õ(1) regret bound breaks the classical lower bound of Ω(√T) that applies to all classical infinite-horizon average-reward RL algorithms, including UCRL (Auer et al., 2008) and UC-CURL (Agarwal et al., 2022b), both of which achieve Õ(√T) regret. The speedup originates from the quantum mean estimation subroutine (QBounded, Cornelissen et al. 2022), which converges at rate O(1/n) versus the classical O(1/√n), yielding a quadratic improvement in the Bellman error bound (Eq. 39 vs. the classical counterpart). The paper also notes that its analysis is martingale-free, avoiding the concentration inequalities (e.g., Weissman et al. 2003) that are standard in classical RL regret proofs, and explicitly accounts for quantum state collapse upon measurement — a constraint absent in classical settings. No direct head-to-head empirical comparison is provided, as the contribution is entirely in the theoretical regret characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty (OFU) framework from UC-CURL (Agarwal et al., 2022b) and the epoch-based doubling structure from UCRL (Auer et al., 2008) into a quantum-classical hybrid MDP setting. The core quantum subroutine is the QBounded multivariate mean estimator (Cornelissen et al., 2022), which provides 1/n convergence versus the classical 1/√n rate. The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) the agent accesses a quantum transition oracle that returns superpositioned states rather than single classical samples, and (2) the regret analysis is martingale-free, circumventing the concentration inequalities that are standard in classical RL proofs. Prior quantum RL results (Ganguly et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2024) addressed episodic MDPs, while quantum bandit literature (Wang et al., 2021; Casalé et al., 2020) lacks state evolution, making this the first treatment of infinite-horizon average-reward RL in the quantum regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(1) regret bound breaks the classical Ω(√T) lower bound, demonstrating that quantum statistical estimation can fundamentally alter the convergence landscape of model-based RL. The momentum-based estimator (Eqs. 21–25) that fuses epoch-wise quantum estimates while respecting state-collapse constraints is a non-trivial technical contribution that could inform future quantum model-based learning. However, the result is entirely theoretical and hinges on a strong quantum transition oracle assumption that has no near-term hardware counterpart, and the paper provides no experimental validation. Open questions include whether parameterized quantum RL (as in Xu & Aggarwal, 2025 for discounted settings) can achieve similar gains for average-reward problems, and whether weaker oracle models (e.g., limited quantum coherence) preserve any speedup.
