## Problem

Extending classifier-free guidance (CFG) to video diffusion with variable-length history faces two fundamental obstacles: existing architectures (DiT, U-Net) only support fixed-size conditioning vectors, and the natural fix of framewise binary dropout during training performs poorly due to inefficient token utilization. The paper asks whether different portions of history—variable lengths, arbitrary frame subsets, and different image-domain frequencies—can serve as a flexible guidance signal for video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary-length set of history frames (any subset of previous frames, potentially at different noise levels) and generates future video frames, operating at 128×128 (Kinetics-600) or 256×256 (RE10K, Minecraft). A key distinction from standard image-to-video models is that the history can be any subset of frames at any positions, not just a fixed first frame or a fixed number of recent frames. In its most extreme demonstration, a single input image is rolled out autoregressively into an 862-frame navigation video, far exceeding the maximum training sequence length.

## Method

The Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) trains a standard full-sequence transformer (e.g., DiT) by assigning each frame an independent noise level k_t ∈ [0,1] rather than a uniform level, so the model learns to denoise any frame given any combination of clean and noisy neighbors. At sampling time, history frames are set to k=0 (clean) while generation frames are denoised at level k, enabling conditioning on arbitrary history subsets without architectural modification. On top of this, History Guidance composes weighted score estimates from multiple history windows (Temporal HG) and from history corrupted at intermediate noise levels (Fractional HG), which acts as a low-pass filter that preserves low-frequency motion consistency while freeing high-frequency details to remain dynamic. The training objective is shown to optimize a reweighted ELBO, and the score-composition strategy is justified via a maximum-likelihood interpretation of combining noisy score estimators.

## Results

On Kinetics-600 (128×128, 16-frame generation), DFoT trained from scratch achieves FVD 4.3±0.1, matching the industry-scale MAGVIT-v2 (4.3±0.1) and outperforming the same-architecture Standard Diffusion baseline (4.8±0.0) while supporting arbitrary-length history conditioning. The fine-tuned DFoT variant reaches FVD 4.7±0.0 using only 12.5% of the training cost. On the more challenging 64-frame sliding-window rollout, Fractional History Guidance reduces FVD from 208.0 (no guidance) to 170.4, surpassing SD (247.5) and Full-Sequence Diffusion (1040).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the same DiT architecture on Kinetics-600, DFoT (scratch, FVD 4.3) outperforms Standard Diffusion (SD, 4.8), Binary-Dropout Diffusion (BD, 6.4), and Full-Sequence Diffusion (FS, 95.5), with the gap over BD highlighting the inefficiency of framewise binary dropout for flexible history conditioning. Against industry-scale models trained with substantially more compute, DFoT matches MAGVIT-v2 (4.3) and Rolling Diffusion (5.2), and approaches W.A.L.T (3.3), while being the only method that supports truly arbitrary-length history. On the 64-frame sliding-window rollout (a setting where image-to-video models struggle), DFoT with Fractional History Guidance achieves FVD 170.4, beating SD (247.5) by ~31% and FS (1040) by ~6×. On RealEstate10K OOD history interpolation, DFoT with Temporal History Guidance maintains high-quality generation where all baselines produce incoherent or blurry frames. On Minecraft long-context generation, temporal history guidance improves FVD from 97.63 to 79.19, and on the Fruit Swapping imitation learning task, DFoT achieves an 83% success rate where baselines fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024), which introduced per-frame independent noise levels for causal state-space models, to non-causal transformers (DFoT), and generalizes Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from fixed-length conditioning to variable-length, arbitrary-subset history conditioning. It departs from standard video diffusion architectures (DiT, U-ViT) that inject conditioning via AdaLN or channel concatenation, which inherently constrain history to a fixed size, and shows that the natural fix—framewise binary dropout—performs poorly due to inefficient token utilization. The History Guidance family draws conceptual inspiration from compositional generative models (Du & Kaelbling, 2024; Liu et al., 2022) that combine scores from multiple conditioning sources, but applies this to the temporal and frequency axes of video history specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFoT's compatibility with existing DiT/U-ViT architectures and its ability to be obtained via lightweight fine-tuning (12.5% of training cost) make it a practical upgrade path for current video diffusion foundation models, potentially enabling flexible history conditioning in industry-scale systems. The 862-frame autoregressive video generation from a single image demonstrates a capability far beyond prior methods, with direct applications in robotics, simulation, and content creation. Open questions include whether the per-frame independent noise objective scales efficiently to very long sequences (the authors note decreased performance at 50 latent frames), whether more sophisticated frequency-band targeting can further improve dynamics, and how the method integrates with causal architectures for real-time generation.
