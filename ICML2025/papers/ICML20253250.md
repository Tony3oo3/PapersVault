## Problem

Finding valid Lyapunov functions for nonlinear dynamical systems remains an open challenge: existing neural network approaches suffer from poor interpretability and prohibitively expensive formal verification due to over-parameterization and nonlinear activations, while analytical methods like SOS scale poorly to local stability or non-polynomial dynamics. The paper asks whether a neural network can directly discover valid analytical (symbolic) Lyapunov functions from complex system dynamics, bypassing the need for large pre-training datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253250/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an autonomous nonlinear ODE system f(x) (up to 10 dimensions) expressed in analytical form, tokenized as pre-order traversal sequences of expression trees with SOS/EOS separators and base-10/scientific-notation coefficients, along with a bounded state space D. It outputs a symbolic Lyapunov function (e.g., V = ω₁² + ω₂² + (ω₂ − sin(δ₁) + sin(δ₂))²) with complexity ≤ 20 tokens, formally verified by the dReal SMT solver with tolerance ε=e^{-3} and precision δ=e^{-12} over D \ B_ε(0). The symbolic library is restricted to {+, −, ×, sin, cos, x_i}, and the candidate is automatically shifted so V(0)=0.

## Method

A symbolic transformer (2-layer encoder for dynamics, 3-layer for hierarchical tree state, 6-layer decoder) generates candidate Lyapunov expressions autoregressively, trained from scratch via a risk-seeking policy gradient (α=0.1) that optimizes only the top-quantile rewards rather than the mean, aligning the objective with the goal of finding at least one valid certificate. During training, SHGO global optimization identifies minimizers of V and −L_f V to localize likely violations, and counterexamples are fed back into the reward computation; a Genetic Programming module (DEAP) refines each batch of candidates, and the top-performing GP expressions serve as supervised targets through a reward-weighted cross-entropy expert guidance loss. This combination of adversarial verification, risk-seeking RL, and evolutionary expert guidance enables the framework to scale to 10-D systems and discover previously unknown local Lyapunov functions (e.g., for lossy 2-bus power systems) within hours on a single workstation.

## Results

The framework achieves 100% success rate on all 2-D and 3-D test systems (runtime 124–288 s) and 80% success on 6-D and 8-D systems (runtime 6,290–14,358 s), scaling to a 10-D polynomial system with 60% success in ~17.8 hours. All discovered Lyapunov functions pass formal SMT verification in 1–621 s. The method discovers previously unknown local Lyapunov functions for a 4-D lossy power system and a 6-D lossless 3-bus power system, which had no known analytical certificates in the control literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two neural Lyapunov baselines (ANLC and FOSSIL 2.0), the proposed method dominates on success rate: 100% vs. 53.3%/80% on 2-D, 100% vs. 46.7%/66.7% on 3-D, and 80% vs. 0% on both 6-D and 8-D, where both neural methods fail entirely due to verification bottlenecks from complex Lie derivatives. The neural baselines are faster on low-dimensional systems (1.4–7.7 s vs. 124–165 s) but cannot scale beyond 3-D. Against SOS (SOSTOOLS), the proposed method is slower on low-dimensional polynomial systems (97 s vs. 0.765 s on 2-D) but is the only method that succeeds on 6-D, 8-D, and 10-D locally stable polynomial systems where SOS is intractable. On non-polynomial systems, the proposed method is faster than SOS on the 3-D trig system (157 s vs. 6,163 s) though slower on the simple pendulum (288 s vs. 19.6 s). The pre-trained transformer of Alfarano et al. (2024) only succeeds on globally stable low-dimensional examples and fails on all locally stable benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deep symbolic regression (Petersen et al. 2020; Holt et al. 2023) and learning-based Lyapunov function construction (Chang et al. 2019; Edwards et al. 2024; Grande et al. 2023). It departs from neural Lyapunov approaches by generating interpretable analytical expressions via a symbolic transformer, and from Alfarano et al. (2024) by training from scratch with RL rather than relying on expensive pre-training on globally stable datasets. The risk-seeking policy gradient and genetic programming expert guidance are borrowed from the symbolic regression literature and repurposed here for the first time in the Lyapunov function discovery setting, while the SHGO-based counterexample generation is a novel application of global optimization to falsify Lyapunov candidates during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path to interpretable, formally verifiable stability certificates for high-dimensional (up to 10-D) non-polynomial systems where both SOS methods and neural Lyapunov approaches struggle with local stability. The discovery of previously unknown Lyapunov functions for lossy power system dynamics demonstrates concrete scientific value beyond benchmark performance. Key open questions include theoretical guarantees on completeness and convergence, extension to control Lyapunov functions for feedback design, and whether the per-system training paradigm can be augmented with pre-training to improve generalization across related systems.
