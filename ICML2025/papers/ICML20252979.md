## Problem

Normalizing Flows have long been a principled likelihood-based generative framework, yet they have fallen behind diffusion models and autoregressive approaches in both density estimation and sample quality, raising the question of whether NFs are fundamentally limited or simply lack the right architecture and training recipe to scale effectively.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a standard Gaussian noise vector z ~ N(0, I_D) (optionally conditioned on a class label) and produces a raw RGB image at pixel level (64×64, 128×128, or 256×256) via sequential inversion of the flow followed by a score-based denoising step. For likelihood estimation, it takes an image (with uniform dequantization noise) and outputs a bits-per-dimension score. The model operates directly on pixel patches without any VAE or tokenizer, converting an image of shape C×H×W into a sequence of N = HW/S² patches each of dimension D = CS².

## Method

TARFLOW stacks T causal Vision Transformer blocks on image patches, alternating the autoregression direction (forward/reverse) between layers, with each block performing a permutation, a causal Transformer pass, and an element-wise affine transformation parameterized by learnable μ and α functions. The key architectural insight is replacing MAF's per-dimension masked MLPs with a block-autoregressive causal Transformer, which enables powerful non-linear interactions across all patches while maintaining a parallel training graph and a simple, modular design that scales like a standard ViT. Training uses the standard MLE objective (Gaussian prior + log-determinant), and three generation techniques are introduced: (1) Gaussian noise augmentation (σ ≈ 0.05) during training to enrich the inverse model's support, (2) a training-free score-based denoising step via Tweedie's formula to remove the noise from raw samples, and (3) classifier-free-style guidance applied to the flow's μ and α predictions (or attention-temperature-based guidance for unconditional models) to improve mode-seeking at inference.

## Results

TARFLOW achieves 2.99 BPD on unconditional ImageNet 64×64, the first sub-3 BPD result for any model, beating the previous best (NFDM at 3.20 BPD) by 0.21 BPD and the prior best NF (Flow++ at 3.69 BPD) by 0.70 BPD. On conditional ImageNet 64×64, TARFLOW achieves 2.66 FID, surpassing iDDPM (2.92) and BigGAN (4.06) but trailing EDM (1.55) and ADM-dropout (2.09). On conditional ImageNet 128×128, TARFLOW reaches 5.03 FID, outperforming BigGAN-deep (5.70) but remaining behind Simple Diffusion (1.94) and RIN (2.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary likelihood benchmark (unconditional ImageNet 64×64 BPD), TARFLOW (2.99) decisively outperforms all prior methods: it beats the best diffusion/flow-matching model NFDM (3.20) by 0.21 BPD, the best autoregressive model Routing Transformer (3.43) by 0.44 BPD, and the best prior NF Flow++ (3.69) by 0.70 BPD — a margin far larger than typical incremental improvements in this field. On conditional ImageNet 64×64 FID, TARFLOW (2.66) outperforms iDDPM (2.92), BigGAN (4.06), IC-GAN (6.70), and both consistency models (4.70, 3.25), but remains behind EDM (1.55) and ADM-dropout (2.09). On conditional ImageNet 128×128, TARFLOW (5.03) beats BigGAN-deep (5.70) and CDM (3.52 is actually better — correction: TARFLOW loses to CDM 3.52, RIN 2.75, Simple Diff 1.94, and ADM-G 2.97) but outperforms BigGAN (8.70) and BigGAN-deep (5.70). On unconditional ImageNet 64×64 FID, TARFLOW (18.42) trails AGM (10.07), MFM (11.82), and IC-GAN (10.40), indicating that unconditional generation remains a weaker point relative to conditional settings. Overall, TARFLOW establishes a clear new state-of-the-art for NF models on likelihood and is competitive with (though not yet surpassing) the best diffusion models on FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

TARFLOW is explicitly positioned as a Transformer-based generalization of Masked Autoregressive Flows (MAF), replacing the per-dimension masked MLPs with causal Vision Transformers operating on image patches in a block-autoregressive fashion. The stacking of autoregressive flow blocks with alternating directions traces back to IAF, while the guidance mechanism is directly adapted from classifier-free guidance in diffusion models. The paper departs from the dominant coupling-based NF lineage (NICE, RealNVP, Glow, Flow++) and continuous NFs (Neural ODEs) by arguing that a purely autoregressive Transformer architecture, trained end-to-end with a simple MLE objective, is sufficient to unlock NFs' full potential without the complex interleaving of heterogeneous modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the prevailing narrative that normalizing flows have fundamentally plateaued relative to diffusion models, showing instead that the bottleneck was architectural rather than paradigmatic. The sub-3 BPD result on ImageNet 64x64 (2.99 vs. 3.20) and competitive FID scores (2.66 on conditional 64×64) demonstrate that NFs remain a viable, exact-likelihood generative paradigm. Open questions include sampling efficiency (the reverse pass is inherently sequential), whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (256×256 and beyond), and whether the Gaussian noise + score-based denoising pipeline generalizes to other modalities. The paper also leaves the optimal guidance schedule and the interplay between flow depth and Transformer width as areas for further exploration.
