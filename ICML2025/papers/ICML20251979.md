## Problem

Learning language-conditioned policies that generalize to unseen goals and states typically requires either large amounts of goal-annotated data, online environment interaction, or expert demonstrations—none of which are available in realistic offline settings. Standalone LLMs, despite strong language comprehension, fail at even simple multi-step decision-making tasks because their knowledge is ungrounded in environment dynamics. TEDUO addresses this gap by showing that generalizable language-conditioned policies can be learned entirely from unlabeled, potentially suboptimal state-action transitions and an unpaired list of natural language goals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled dataset of textual state-action-state triplets (e.g., 800k transitions in BabyAI) collected under an arbitrary policy with no reward signal, plus an unpaired set of natural language goal descriptions split into training and testing subsets. The output is a fine-tuned LLM (Llama-3-8B) that, given a natural language instruction and a textual state representation, produces a full sequence of discrete actions (e.g., [forward, turn_right, forward, pick_up]) rather than a single action per query. A key constraint is that environment states must be representable in text, and the action space must be discrete to match LLM tokenization.

## Method

TEDUO is a three-step pipeline that synergizes LLMs and offline RL. Step 1 uses LLM-automated hindsight labeling (via lightweight proxy reward networks trained on LLM-generated labels) and optional LLM-guided state abstraction to convert the unlabeled dataset into goal-conditioned, solvable abstract MDPs. Step 2 applies tabular Q-learning (or DQN) to each abstract MDP to obtain optimal goal-conditioned policies, improving over naive behavioral cloning. Step 3 distills the learned policies into a pre-trained LLM via supervised fine-tuning on synthetic (goal, initial abstract state, optimal action sequence) tuples, grounding the LLM's prior language knowledge in environment dynamics. The key insight is that LLMs serve a dual role: as cheap data enhancers in Step 1 (reducing labeling costs by orders of magnitude) and as flexible generalizers in Step 3, where their zero-shot capabilities enable transfer to novel goals and states that the tabular RL policies cannot reach.

## Results

On the BabyAI Synth benchmark (22×22 grids, 9 rooms), TEDUO with Llama-3-8B achieves 65% success rate on training goals/environments and 45% on novel goals and unseen environments, with an average episode length of 203 steps. This substantially outperforms vanilla Llama-3-8B (17%), Llama-3-70B (14%), and DeepSeek-R1 distilled to Llama-8B (32%). On the Webshop e-commerce environment, TEDUO-Llama-3-8B scores 52.1 (training) and 44.4 (testing) versus 8.4 and 13.8 for ReAct-Llama-3-8B and ReAct-Llama-3-70B respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEDUO Llama-3-8B decisively outperforms all non-fine-tuned LLM baselines: it achieves 65% success rate on training goals versus 17% (Llama-3-8B vanilla), 14% (Llama-3-70B vanilla), 16% (Llama-3-8B in-context+CoT), 21% (Llama-3-70B in-context+CoT), and 32% (DeepSeek-R1 distilled to Llama-8B). Against the BabyAI-IL-bot (GRU+CNN+FILM+LSTM trained via imitation learning on TEDUO's RL policies), TEDUO Llama-3-8B is slightly lower on in-distribution evaluation (65% vs. 69%) but dramatically superior on generalization: 45% vs. 16% on novel goals in unseen environments (a 29-point gap), and 55% vs. 15% on novel goals in the training environment (a 40-point gap). On the Webshop environment, TEDUO-Llama-3-8B scores 52.1/44.4 (training/testing) compared to 8.4/13.8 for ReAct-Llama-3-8B and 13.8 for ReAct-Llama-3-70B, a roughly 4–6× improvement. The key differentiator is generalization: while BabyAI-IL-bot overfits to the limited offline data (24% drop on new environments, 40% drop on new goals), TEDUO's LLM backbone retains zero-shot capabilities, suffering only a 12% drop on new environments and 8% on new goals.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEDUO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) LLM-based reward design and hindsight labeling (Kwon et al., 2023), which the paper adopts for goal-state identification but scales via lightweight proxy networks; (2) offline goal-conditioned RL, which prior work (Yang et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2022) addresses but typically requires labeled or expert data; and (3) LLMs as decision-making agents (ReAct, Voyager), which the paper shows fail without grounding in environment dynamics. The key departure is the sequential pipeline that uses LLMs in a dual role—as cheap data enhancers (state abstraction, hindsight labeling) feeding conventional offline RL, and then as the final generalizable policy via SFT—eliminating the need for labeled data, online interaction, or expert demonstrations that prior language-conditioned RL methods (Fu et al., 2018; Bahdanau et al., 2018; Brohan et al., 2023a) all require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to learn language-conditioned policies from purely unlabeled, non-expert offline data while generalizing to both novel goals and unseen states, shifting the bottleneck from data annotation to compute. The skill compositionality experiments (Section 5.3.1) provide evidence that SFT on diverse goal-conditioned policies teaches composable core skills rather than memorized trajectories, suggesting a path toward more flexible LLM agents. However, the approach is constrained to discrete action spaces and textual state representations, and absolute performance on test/test (45%) remains modest. Open questions include whether the pipeline extends to continuous control, multi-environment learning, and whether VLMs can handle non-textual observations.
