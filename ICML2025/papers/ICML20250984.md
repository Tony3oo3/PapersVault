## Problem

Existing learning-based multi-view image compression methods estimate inter-view disparities using 2D projection similarities (homography, patch matching, or cross-attention), which work for small stereo disparities but fail on the large, complex disparities encountered in wide-baseline multi-camera systems used in VR and autonomous driving. These approaches ignore the underlying 3D spatial geometry of the scene, limiting their ability to accurately align features across views with significant positional and angular differences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N calibrated multi-view RGB images along with their intrinsic and extrinsic camera matrices (obtained via SfM/COLMAP) and a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting model of the scene. During training, images are randomly cropped to 256×256 with correspondingly adjusted intrinsics. The system produces two parallel compressed bitstreams per view—one for the RGB image and one for the depth map—alongside reconstructed images and depth maps at the decoder. The depth map bitstream is a novel output that encodes geometric redundancy across views via a cross-view depth prediction module.

## Method

3D-LMVIC uses a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting model to generate per-view depth maps, then estimates pixel-wise disparity between adjacent views by back-projecting pixels into 3D world space and re-projecting into the reference view, with a validity mask that excludes occluded or out-of-bounds regions. The image compression backbone is an autoencoder with hyperprior and quadtree partition entropy models, augmented by an Image Context Transfer (ICT) module that warps multi-scale reference features using the estimated disparity and applies the mask before residual fusion. A separate depth map compression model employs a Cross-View Depth Prediction (CVDP) module to predict the current view's depth from the reconstructed previous depth, reducing geometric redundancy. A multi-view sequence ordering method defines a proven distance metric D_V(i,j) = ‖V_i V_j^{-1} − I‖_F and uses a greedy algorithm to maximize FoV overlap between adjacent views. Training minimizes a weighted rate-distortion loss over 4-view subsequences, jointly optimizing image and depth map reconstruction quality and estimated bitrates.

## Results

On the three multi-view datasets, 3D-LMVIC achieves BDBR reductions of −47.48% (PSNR) / −63.69% (MS-SSIM) on Tanks&Temples, −34.69% / −40.25% on Mip-NeRF 360, and −27.31% / −54.15% on Deep Blending, all relative to MV-HEVC. Compared to the strongest learning-based baseline BiSIC, 3D-LMVIC further reduces BDBR by 16.59% (PSNR) and 13.73% (MS-SSIM) on Tanks&Temples. In alignment experiments, the proposed 3D-GS-based disparity estimation achieves 18.14 PSNR and 0.8053 MS-SSIM, outperforming the best optical-flow baseline FlowFormer++ (18.08 / 0.7863).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-LMVIC outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the traditional MV-HEVC standard, it achieves 27–47% BDBR savings in PSNR and 40–64% in MS-SSIM. The 3D-GS compression method HAC performs far worse than MV-HEVC (positive BDBR of 294–674%), confirming that compressing the 3D scene representation is not a viable substitute for direct multi-view image coding. Among learning-based stereo codecs (HESIC, MASIC, SASIC), 3D-LMVIC leads by 15–50 percentage points in BDBR, reflecting the advantage of 3D geometric priors over 2D homography or patch-matching alignment. The strongest baseline BiSIC (ECCV 2024) is surpassed by 16.59% (PSNR) and 13.73% (MS-SSIM) on Tanks&Temples, with similar margins on the other two datasets. In the alignment sub-experiment, the proposed 3D-GS depth-based method (18.14 PSNR / 0.8053 MS-SSIM) edges out FlowFormer++ (18.08 / 0.7863) and clearly beats PWC-Net (17.59 / 0.7707), SPyNet (16.12 / 0.6289), COLMAP (14.32 / 0.7446), and MVSFormer++ (15.31 / 0.5544), validating that 3D geometric priors yield more accurate cross-view correspondence than 2D optical-flow or multi-view-stereo depth estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-based image compression lineage (Ballé et al., Minnen et al.) to the multi-view setting, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-view codecs (HESIC, MASIC, SASIC, LDMIC, BiSIC) that estimate inter-view disparities via 2D image-domain operations—homography, patch matching, or cross-attention. Instead, it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as a geometric prior: a pre-trained 3D-GS model provides per-pixel depth maps that enable 3D back-projection and re-projection for disparity estimation, a shift from 2D similarity to 3D spatial correspondence. The paper also introduces two auxiliary contributions absent from prior work: a dedicated depth map compression model with cross-view depth prediction, and a provably valid inter-view distance metric (Theorem C.3/C.6) that drives a greedy sequence ordering strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in wide-baseline multi-camera compression (VR, autonomous driving) where 2D alignment methods degrade, and the 16.6% BDBR improvement over the strongest learning-based baseline (BiSIC) on Tanks&Temples is meaningful for storage/transmission budgets. However, the pipeline requires a pre-trained 3D-GS model per scene and calibrated camera parameters from SfM, limiting applicability to uncalibrated, dynamic, or in-the-wild multi-view content—a limitation the authors acknowledge in their impact statement. Open questions include whether the 3D-GS prior can be learned jointly with the compression model (removing the offline SfM/3D-GS training step), how the method scales to very large view counts, and whether the geometric prior remains beneficial when depth estimation quality degrades in textureless or reflective regions.
