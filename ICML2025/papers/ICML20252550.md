## Problem

Existing sparse Newton methods for entropic-regularized optimal transport (e.g., SNS, SSNS) lack rigorous theoretical understanding of how Hessian sparsification affects eigenvalue structure and positive definiteness, and they degrade in performance when the true Hessian is dense (i.e., the transport plan is dense). This paper addresses both gaps by providing new eigenvalue analyses of sparsified Hessians and introducing a low-rank correction to handle dense cases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source distribution vector a ∈ R^n, a target distribution vector b ∈ R^m (both positive, summing to 1), a cost matrix M ∈ R^{n×m} (normalized to unit infinity norm), and a regularization parameter η, and produces the optimal transport plan T ∈ R^{n×m} via the dual variables (α, β). The problem is reduced to an unconstrained convex minimization over x = (α_1,…,α_n, β_1,…,β_{m-1})^T ∈ R^{n+m-1} with β_m fixed to zero, so the Hessian is of size (n+m−1)×(n+m−1). Experiments cover problem sizes up to n = m = 10000 and image-based OT with n = m = 784 (MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) or larger (ImageNet).

## Method

The SPLR quasi-Newton method approximates the Hessian at each iteration as B_k = H_Ω + au u^T + b v v^T + τI, combining three components: (i) a sparsified Hessian H_Ω that retains a fixed proportion ρ of the largest off-diagonal entries of T plus a minimal cross pattern (one full row and column) to guarantee positive definiteness via the Perron-Frobenius theorem; (ii) a rank-two low-rank correction determined by the BFGS secant equation y_k = B_{k+1} s_k, which compensates for dense Hessian structure that sparsification alone cannot capture; and (iii) a shift τI = min{τ_max, ‖g_k‖}I for numerical stability. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) proves that each incremental removal of a symmetric off-diagonal pair strictly decreases the condition number of the sparsified Hessian, implying that any valid sparsification scheme preserves positive definiteness and yields a smaller condition number than the full Hessian. The inverse B_k^{-1} is computed in O(n+m) time via the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula, reducing the search direction to a single sparse linear system solve. The density ρ is adaptively increased or decreased (×1.1 or ×0.99) based on whether the gradient norm decreased, and the Moré-Thuente line search enforces Wolfe conditions for global convergence.

## Results

The paper reports results through convergence plots (gradient norm vs. iteration count and vs. wall-clock time) rather than numerical summary tables. SPLR achieves the fastest convergence across all tested settings: synthetic problems at n=m=1000, 5000, and 10000; MNIST and Fashion-MNIST image pairs at n=m=784; and ImageNet class pairs. The method exhibits super-linear-like convergence in practice despite only a linear rate being theoretically guaranteed, and remains robust across regularization parameters (η=0.001 and η=0.01) and cost matrix norms (ℓ₁ and ℓ₂).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPLR outperforms all five baselines in every tested setting. Against the Sinkhorn algorithm (BCD) and APDAGD, SPLR converges dramatically faster, particularly at small η=0.001 where first-order methods require many more iterations. Compared to L-BFGS, SPLR achieves faster convergence by exploiting the problem-specific Hessian structure through sparsification. Against SSNS (Tang & Qiu, 2024), SPLR matches or exceeds performance in sparse-Hessian regimes (e.g., Synthetic II) but clearly wins in dense-Hessian regimes (e.g., Synthetic I with uniform cost, and ImageNet with η=0.01), where SSNS must retain many nonzeros and slows down. The full Newton method is infeasible at scale (not run for n≥5000) and suffers numerical instability at η=0.001, making it a weak practical competitor. The ablation study (Appendix A.1) confirms that the low-rank term is essential: removing it degrades SPLR to a pure sparse Newton method that is consistently slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse Newton line of work for entropic-regularized OT initiated by Tang et al. (2024, SNS) and Tang & Qiu (2024, SSNS), which approximate the dense Hessian via thresholding or off-diagonal sparsification. The key departure is twofold: (1) a new theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.4) proving that incremental sparsification strictly decreases the Hessian's condition number, providing guarantees that prior methods lacked; and (2) a rank-2 low-rank correction term derived from the BFGS secant equation, which compensates for the dense residual when the true Hessian is not well-captured by sparsity alone. The efficient inversion of the combined sparse-plus-low-rank matrix relies on the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula, a standard quasi-Newton tool repurposed here in the OT-specific Hessian structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Entropic-regularized OT is a core subroutine in many ML pipelines (domain adaptation, generative modeling, image comparison), and the slow convergence of Sinkhorn-type methods at small regularization remains a practical bottleneck. SPLR offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative that adapts to both sparse and dense transport plans, with the condition-number analysis providing a general template for designing flexible sparsification schemes. An open question the authors highlight is whether SPLR (or related second-order OT solvers) can achieve dimension-independent convergence rates analogous to the linear rate Carlier (2022) established for Sinkhorn, which would be a significant theoretical advance. The method is implemented in the open-source RegOT package, lowering the barrier to adoption.
