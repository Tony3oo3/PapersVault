## Problem

Existing tabular imputation methods either train under random masking or require complete data, ignoring the empirical missingness patterns that are often structured in real-world datasets. Additionally, they lack a straightforward mechanism to incorporate unstructured contextual knowledge (e.g., column names and descriptions) about semantic relationships between features, leaving a significant source of inductive bias untapped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250784/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CACTI consumes an incomplete tabular matrix with its binary missingness mask, plus per-column text metadata (names and optional descriptions). The text is processed once through a frozen language model (GTE-en-MLM-large) to produce fixed 1024-dim context embeddings that are linearly projected and concatenated with value embeddings. The model outputs a fully imputed matrix on the original feature scale (after inverting min-max normalization), evaluated via R², RMSE, and Wasserstein distance under MCAR, MAR, and MNAR regimes at 10–70% missingness.

## Method

CACTI is a transformer encoder-decoder masked autoencoder with two key innovations. First, Median Truncated Copy Masking (MT-CM) replaces random masking by sampling empirical missingness patterns from the dataset to construct training masks, then truncating each sample's observed feature sequence to the batch median so that null tokens are bounded at 50% and sequence lengths are uniform within a batch—enabling efficient transformer encoding while preserving realistic missingness structure. Second, context awareness embeds column names and descriptions via a language model and concatenates the resulting context vector (25% of embedding dimension) with the value embedding (75%) in both encoder and decoder, providing semantic inductive bias about feature relationships. The model is trained with a unified MSE reconstruction loss over both observed and masked values, and a theoretical analysis shows that copy masking approximates the true missingness distribution, making it a principled surrogate for the intractable full-data MAE objective.

## Results

CACTI achieves the best average R² across all 10 datasets at 30% missingness: 0.46 (MCAR), 0.47 (MAR), and 0.46 (MNAR) on the test split, outperforming the next best method (ReMasker) by 5.3%, 6.1%, and 13.4% respectively. On RMSE, CACTI records 0.64 (MCAR), 0.69 (MAR), and 0.67 (MNAR), and on Wasserstein distance 4.46 (MCAR), 1.94 (MAR), and 4.57 (MNAR). The paper reports an overall average R² gain of 7.8% over the next best method across all three missingness conditions, with the largest advantage under MNAR where structured missingness is most prevalent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CACTI outperforms all 13 baselines on R² across every missingness condition. The largest gap is against ReMasker (the closest competitor and primary masked-autoencoder baseline), where CACTI gains 13.4% R² under MNAR (0.46 vs. 0.40), 6.1% under MAR (0.47 vs. 0.44), and 5.3% under MCAR (0.46 vs. 0.44). Against DiffPuter (diffusion-based), CACTI leads by 9.5% R² under MCAR and 24.3% under MNAR. Against the best iterative method HyperImpute, CACTI gains 12.2% R² under MCAR and 17.9% under MNAR. Classical iterative methods (MissForest, ICE, MICE) and generative methods (GAIN, MIWAE, notMIWAE, Sinkhorn) trail CACTI by 20–46% in R². Notably, CACTI also beats its own ablation CMAE (without context) by ~2–5% R², confirming the contribution of context-aware embeddings, and CMAE alone already surpasses ReMasker, isolating the benefit of MT-CM over random masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

CACTI extends the masked autoencoder framework (He et al., 2021) to tabular imputation by building on ReMasker's (Du et al., 2024) transformer encoder-decoder architecture while replacing its random masking strategy with a novel median truncated copy masking (MT-CM) derived from AutoComplete's (An et al., 2023) naive copy masking. The key departure is that naive copy masking, which worked well with AutoComplete's shallow MLP, actually degrades performance when applied to transformer-based MAE due to high variance in observed feature counts across samples; MT-CM resolves this by capping sequence length at the batch median. The paper also introduces a second axis of novelty—context-aware embeddings from language models (GTE-en-MLM-large) applied to column names and descriptions—which is orthogonal to the masking strategy and draws on recent work incorporating textual context into tabular representation learning (Yin et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2024) but is the first to apply it specifically to imputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tabular imputation remains a pervasive practical problem, and CACTI's modular design (MT-CM applicable to any masked learning model; context embeddings integrable into any deep imputation framework) makes its components broadly reusable beyond this specific architecture. The 13.4% R² gain under MNAR is meaningful for real-world settings where missingness is structured and non-random, and the paper's thorough ablations and sensitivity analyses provide actionable guidance (e.g., masking rate ≥0.90, embedding size 64, 300–600 epochs). Limitations include evaluation on only 10 UCI datasets of modest size (up to ~47K samples), reliance on available column descriptions for context (many real datasets lack these), and the observation that context awareness provides only a modest ~5% gain that is not always statistically significant. Open questions include whether domain-specific language models (e.g., BioClinicalBERT) yield larger context gains in specialized fields, and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or very large tabular datasets.
