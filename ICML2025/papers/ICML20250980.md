## Problem

Knowledge Graph Foundation Models (KGFMs) have demonstrated empirical success in generalizing to novel KGs with unseen relations, yet their theoretical expressive power remains largely uncharacterized. The paper identifies that existing KGFMs rely exclusively on binary motifs (pairs of relations) to learn relation invariants, fundamentally limiting their ability to capture higher-order relational interactions. No prior work provides a rigorous framework for understanding how the choice of motifs governs a KGFM's capacity to distinguish between different relational structures.

## Input / Output

Given a KG G=(V,E,R) and a query link q(u,v), MOTIF produces a probability score for the existence of that link via a two-stage, query-conditioned encoding. The relation encoder computes representations for all relations r∈R conditioned on the query relation q, while the entity encoder computes node representations conditioned on both the source node u and query relation q. The LIFT operation transforms the KG into a relational hypergraph where hyperedges encode motif matches, enabling the relation encoder to capture interactions among k relations simultaneously rather than only pairs.

## Method

MOTIF is a three-step framework: (1) LIFT constructs a relational hypergraph where nodes are relation types and hyperedges encode matches of arbitrary graph motifs (not just binary paths); (2) a relation encoder (e.g., HCNet) performs conditional message passing on this hypergraph to produce relation representations; (3) an entity encoder (e.g., NBFNet variant) performs conditional message passing on the original KG using those relation representations. The key theoretical contribution is a two-stage Weisfeiler-Leman test that exactly characterizes MOTIF's link-separation power, together with a sufficient condition (absence of core-onto homomorphisms from existing motifs to a new motif) under which adding a motif provably increases expressiveness. The framework strictly subsumes ULTRA and INGRAM, and the paper demonstrates that augmenting ULTRA's 2-path motifs with 3-path motifs yields a provably more expressive model, validated empirically on 54 KGs across diverse domains including synthetic ConnectHub benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary benchmark of 51 KGs, MOTIF(𝓕₃^path) achieves 0.378 MRR and 0.537 Hits@10 in zero-shot inference, outperforming ULTRA (0.374 MRR, 0.529 H@10). In fine-tuned inference, MOTIF(𝓕₃^path) reaches 0.419 MRR and 0.569 H@10 versus ULTRA's 0.410 MRR and 0.563 H@10. In end-to-end training over 54 KGs, MOTIF(𝓕₃^path) achieves 0.409 MRR and 0.561 H@10, surpassing ULTRA's 0.394 MRR and 0.552 H@10. On the synthetic ConnectHub(k) datasets, ULTRA is stuck at 50% accuracy (random chance) while MOTIF with sufficiently expressive star motifs (m = k+1) reaches 100% accuracy, confirming the theoretical expressiveness gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ULTRA (Galkin et al., 2024), the dominant KGFM architecture. MOTIF(𝓕₃^path) consistently outperforms ULTRA across all evaluation settings: zero-shot (0.378 vs 0.374 MRR), fine-tuned (0.419 vs 0.410 MRR), and end-to-end (0.409 vs 0.394 MRR) over 51–54 KGs. The gap widens substantially on structurally challenging datasets: on Metafam, MOTIF achieves a 45% relative MRR improvement over ULTRA in zero-shot, and on WN-v2 (inductive on nodes, only 20 relations), MOTIF reaches 0.684 MRR versus ULTRA's 0.296 MRR. The paper also confirms Theorem 6.5 empirically: MOTIF(𝓕₂^path) performs comparably to ULTRA (0.370 vs 0.374 MRR zero-shot), validating that they share the same expressive power. Removing all motifs (MOTIF(∅)) causes catastrophic failure (0.082 MRR), demonstrating that relation invariants are essential for generalization to unseen relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOTIF generalizes the dominant KGFm paradigm—exemplified by ULTRA and INGRAM—which learns relation representations via message passing over a graph of relations built from binary motifs (e.g., head-to-tail, tail-to-head paths). The paper reframes this construction as a LIFT operation producing a relational hypergraph, enabling arbitrary k-ary motifs, and grounds the relation encoder in HCNet (hypergraph conditional message passing) rather than the NBFNet variants used by ULTRA. The expressiveness analysis builds on the relational Weisfeiler-Leman theory of Huang et al. (2023) and the hypergraph WL theory of Huang et al. (2024), extending these tools to characterize when adding a new motif strictly increases separation power. The framework strictly subsumes ULTRA and INGRAM, and provides a principled augmentation path for TRIX and RMPI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous expressiveness characterization of KGFMs, providing a concrete theoretical recipe (Theorem 6.4: a new motif P increases expressiveness iff no existing motif admits a core-onto homomorphism to P) that practitioners can use to design more powerful architectures. The empirical gains, while consistent across 54 KGs, are modest in absolute terms (e.g., 0.378 vs 0.374 MRR zero-shot), and the paper acknowledges a clear trade-off: richer motifs increase computational cost in both time and memory, limiting scalability to large real-world graphs. Open questions include whether the expressiveness hierarchy can be exploited more efficiently (e.g., via approximate or sampled motif matching) and whether the framework extends naturally to higher-arity knowledge bases beyond binary relations.
