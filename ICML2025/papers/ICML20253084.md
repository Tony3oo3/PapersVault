## Problem

SAR target scattering characteristics change significantly with azimuth and depression angles, yet training samples covering all viewing angles are rarely available in practice. Existing deep learning approaches for SAR ATR either rely on task-specific physical priors that limit generalizability or treat the angle-induced distribution shift as an afterthought, leaving models sensitive to unseen attitude angles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel SAR target image, pads it, and divides it into overlapping k×k patches (k=17) that are vectorized for dimensionality reduction. ΓPCA produces L=2 principal component feature maps at the same spatial resolution as the input, which are concatenated with the original image to form a 3-channel tensor fed into the backbone. The convolution kernels are fixed (no parameter updates during training), and Min-Max Scaling replaces standard normalization to preserve the Gamma-distributed statistics of SAR data.

## Method

The core contribution is ΓPCA, which extends the classical PCA mean-square-error objective to the Gamma distribution by formulating SAR pixel intensities within the exponential family framework and minimizing the deviance of natural parameters in a k-dimensional subspace. An MM algorithm iteratively solves for the center vector and orthonormal projection matrix, yielding a consistent projection that captures low-rank, angle-invariant structure shared across different viewing angles of the same target. The resulting projection matrix is reshaped into L fixed convolution kernels (17×17) that act as a parameter-free front-end filter, extracting principal component feature maps that are concatenated with the original image before standard backbone processing (ResNet, ViT, Swin, etc.). This design requires no additional learnable parameters or computational overhead beyond the initial kernel derivation, making it a plug-and-play module compatible with any backbone architecture.

## Results

On the MSTAR dataset with a challenging angle-robustness protocol (trained on only 25% azimuth at 17° depression, tested on full azimuth at 15° and 30° depression), ΓPCA-ViT achieves the best overall accuracy of 76.02% (A-R Test: 78.48%, AD-R Test: 70.67%), outperforming the ViT-B/16 baseline by 3.57% OA. ΓPCA-ResNet similarly improves over ResNet101 by 3.61% OA (67.23% vs. 63.62%). On the SAR-AIRcraft-1.0 dataset, ΓPCA consistently adds 1.0–1.3% OA over each backbone (e.g., 98.67% vs. 97.33% for ResNet101).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ΓPCA-ViT achieves the highest overall accuracy (76.02%) and the best A-R Test score (78.48%) among all 10 methods, beating the strongest single-test baselines: Swin-B (78.01% AD-R but only 61.58% A-R) and VisionLSTM-T (76.54% AD-R but only 55.25% A-R). The key advantage is balance: while Swin-B and VisionLSTM-T excel on the depression-variation test, they collapse on the azimuth-robustness test, whereas ΓPCA-ViT maintains strong performance on both (78.48% / 70.67%). Compared to its own backbone ViT-B/16, ΓPCA-ViT gains +0.36% on A-R and +10.56% on AD-R, indicating the Gamma-distribution PCA kernels are particularly effective under depression-angle shift. ΓPCA-ResNet improves over ResNet101 by +1.22% A-R and +9.01% AD-R. On the SAR-AIRcraft-1.0 dataset (no angle variation), all backbones already reach >97% OA, and ΓPCA adds a consistent 1.0–1.3% improvement, confirming generalizability beyond the MSTAR protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized PCA framework of Landgraf & Lee (2020) from the Gaussian assumption to the Gamma distribution, which is the empirically validated model for SAR pixel statistics (Li et al., 2011). The resulting ΓPCA projection matrix is then repurposed as a fixed, parameter-free convolution kernel, following the PCANet (Chan et al., 2015) paradigm of using PCA-derived filters as a front-end to deep networks. Conceptually, the paper departs from both physics-driven SAR ATR methods (attributed scattering centers, shadow/phase cues) that require domain expertise and are task-specific, and from standard deep learning approaches that treat angle-induced distribution shift as a data-scarcity problem rather than a distributional-mismatch problem. The key positioning is that ΓPCA is a distribution-aware, plug-and-play dimensionality-reduction module that can be prepended to any backbone (ResNet, ViT, Swin, etc.) without adding learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important and under-addressed gap: most SAR ATR methods are evaluated under single-angle or single-axis variation, while real deployments face simultaneous azimuth insufficiency and depression change. The parameter-free design (no extra FLOPs, no fine-tuning of the kernel) makes it attractive for deployment where compute is constrained. However, the improvements are modest (~3–4% overall accuracy on MSTAR), the AD-R test gains are inconsistent across backbones (ΓPCA-ResNet actually underperforms on the second training set's AD-R test), and the method is inherently tied to the Gamma-distribution assumption, limiting its transferability to non-SAR modalities. Open questions include whether the fixed-kernel approach can be adapted to handle more extreme angle gaps, whether the MM convergence behavior degrades with very few training samples, and whether the Min-Max normalization choice (motivated by distributional compatibility) is truly optimal versus learned normalization.
