## Problem

While preference feedback is widely used in language model fine-tuning, the statistical conditions under which it improves parameter estimation for continuous parametric distributions remain unclear. This paper asks whether and how preference labels—derived from the same unknown parameter as the data-generating distribution—can reduce estimation error beyond what samples alone provide, and whether deterministic (noise-free) preferences can break the standard Θ(1/√n) parametric rate.

## Input / Output

The learner observes n pairs of samples (X_i, Y_i) drawn i.i.d. from p_θ*⊗2, together with preference labels Z_i ∈ {±1, 0} generated by a parametric reward function r_θ (e.g., log p_θ) applied to each pair. Preferences can be deterministic (Z_i = sign(ℓ_θ*(X_i, Y_i))) or stochastic (Z_i sampled via a sigmoid link). The output is a point estimate θ̂ of the unknown parameter θ* in a bounded parameter space Θ ⊆ ℝ^k, with the key structural constraint that the preference model and the probability model share the same parameter.

## Method

The paper introduces three estimators of increasing sophistication. First, a preference-based M-estimator (SP MLE) augments the sample-only negative log-likelihood with a logistic classification loss on preferences, yielding a strictly lower asymptotic covariance than the sample-only MLE under an informativeness condition on the preference gradient. Second, for deterministic preferences, a DP MLE minimizes the sample-only log-likelihood subject to the hard constraint that all preference labels are correctly classified (i.e., Z_i·ℓ_θ(X_i, Y_i) ≥ 0 for all i), which for Gaussian and Laplace families reduces to a convex feasibility problem. The key insight is that deterministic preferences impose separability constraints that shrink the feasible parameter set at rate O(1/n) via a minimum operator over informative samples, rather than the averaging inherent in M-estimation. A matching Ω(1/n) minimax lower bound is established using Assouad's lemma with Hellinger distance and a restricted Bhattacharyya coefficient, confirming the accelerated rate is optimal up to dimension-dependent constants.

## Results

The paper establishes that preference-based estimators exploiting deterministic preferences achieve an accelerated estimation error rate of O(1/n) (Theorem 4.3), a significant improvement over the Θ(1/√n) rate of standard M-estimators (SO MLE, SP MLE). This rate is shown to be minimax optimal up to dimension-dependent constants via a matching Ω(1/n) lower bound (Theorem 5.3). Empirically, for Gaussian distributions with d=1, the C_n-based estimators (DP MLE, AE, RU, WE) clearly exhibit O(1/n) convergence while SO MLE and SP MLE follow O(1/√n). The asymptotic covariance gap between SP MLE and SO MLE is mild: approximately (0.16, 0.08) for Laplace and (0.17, 0.08, 0.10) for Gaussian distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six estimators: SO MLE (sample-only baseline), SP MLE (stochastic preference MLE), SP_det (deterministic preference M-estimator), DP MLE (MLE constrained to the feasible set C_n), AE (arbitrary point in C_n), and RU/WE (randomized uniform / worst-case in C_n). DP MLE is the standout performer: it dominates SO MLE for all n (Lemma 4.1, proven for Gaussian), achieves the accelerated O(1/n) rate, and consistently outperforms all other estimators in high dimensions (d=20, Figure 1b and Figure 2). In contrast, SP MLE, despite having lower asymptotic variance than SO MLE, actually performs worse than SO MLE at finite sample sizes (Figure 1), highlighting the gap between asymptotic and non-asymptotic behavior. The AE and WE estimators deteriorate quickly at small sample sizes when dimension increases, while DP MLE maintains its advantage. The covariance gap between SP MLE and SO MLE is empirically mild (≈0.16–0.17 for 1D), suggesting that the modest asymptotic variance improvement from stochastic preferences does not translate to meaningful finite-sample gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical parametric estimation theory (M-estimators, Fisher information, Cramér-Rao bounds) and the preference learning literature motivated by RLHF and DPO. While prior work on learning from pairwise comparisons (Bradley-Terry model, ranking) focused on tabular or discrete settings, and recent continuous-preference-learning papers (Zhu et al., 2023; Ge et al., 2024) studied linear utility functions, this paper is the first to characterize the statistical learning gap between preference-based and sample-only estimators for general continuous parametric distributions. It repurposes the 0-1 loss minimization perspective from classification to parameter estimation, showing that deterministic preferences impose hard geometric constraints analogous to the minimum operator in uniform distribution estimation, thereby breaking the standard parametric rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled statistical explanation for why preference feedback improves LLM fine-tuning beyond what supervised data alone can achieve, and identifies a regime (deterministic preferences) where the improvement is not merely a variance reduction but a fundamental rate acceleration from O(1/√n) to O(1/n). However, the results are limited to restrictive assumptions (positive density at zero, identifiability, linearization validity) verified only for Gaussian and Laplace families, and a dimensionality gap remains between the upper and lower bounds. Key open questions include extending the analysis to discrete distributions, closing the dimensionality gap, characterizing which reward models beyond log-likelihood satisfy the assumptions, and understanding how these results translate to the iterative self-play alignment paradigm used in practice.
