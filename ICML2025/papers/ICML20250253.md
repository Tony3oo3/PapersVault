## Problem

Constrained graph clustering seeks a partition minimizing the cut ratio between a MUST-LINK graph G and a CANNOT-LINK graph H, but existing Cheeger-type inequalities either require a third demand graph or relate the bound to the conductance of G, and practical spectral methods for this setting lack rigorous approximation guarantees. This paper closes that gap by establishing a new Cheeger-type inequality that bounds the optimal cut ratio purely in terms of spectral quantities of G and H, and by providing an efficient algorithm with matching guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes two weighted undirected graphs G and H on the same vertex set, where edge weights in G encode MUST-LINK constraint strengths and edge weights in H encode CANNOT-LINK constraint strengths. Before solving, G is preprocessed (scaled and augmented with self-loops) so that both graphs share an identical degree sequence, and H is modified with a single negative self-loop to render its signed Laplacian symmetric positive definite. The output is a bi-partition of the vertex set obtained by sweeping the eigenvector of a generalized eigenvalue problem.

## Method

The core result is a Cheeger-type inequality Φ_H^G ≤ 4√(λ₂(Δ_H^G)/λ₂(Δ^H)), proven via a sweep-set argument with a probability density 2|t| over the eigenvector range. To make the generalized eigenvalue problem computationally tractable, the algorithm adds a negative self-loop to H, converting its Laplacian into a signed Laplacian Δ_α^{H'} that is symmetric positive definite and invertible (Lemma 3.4), so the problem reduces to a standard eigenvalue solve via Cholesky decomposition rather than the QZ algorithm. The eigenvector of (Δ_α^{H'})^{-1}Δ^G is then thresholded using a sweep-set procedure to produce the final partition. This signed-Laplacian formulation avoids the extra tuning parameter required by prior flexible constrained spectral methods and guarantees all eigenvalues are real and non-negative (Lemma 3.5).

## Results

On the real-world Brittany Temperature Data (744 hourly observations), CC++ achieves 79.16–81.04% successful cluster separation across three temperature types, compared to 62.63–63.30% for standard spectral clustering (SC), a gain of roughly 16–18 percentage points. On synthetic Stochastic Block Model experiments (n=1000, p=0.2), CC++ maintains ARI above 0.5 as inter-cluster probability q approaches 0.17, while SC's ARI collapses from ~0.7 to near 0.1 between q=0.16 and q=0.18. In terms of runtime, CC++ scales comparably to SC (both grow modestly with n), whereas the original CC method's runtime rises sharply for larger graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard spectral clustering (SC, von Luxburg 2007), CC++ wins decisively on every dataset: on the Brittany temperature data it improves successful separation by 15.8–18.1 percentage points (e.g., 81.04% vs 62.90% for maximal temperature), and on the SBM it retains ARI > 0.5 at q≈0.17 where SC drops below 0.1. Against the Flexible Constrained (FC) method of Wang & Davidson (2010), CC++ matches or slightly exceeds ARI on the SBM across all tested graph sizes (n=250–1000) in Figure 6, while offering two practical advantages: it is parameter-free (FC requires a user-chosen β that can yield infeasible negative eigenvalues) and it comes with a provable Cheeger-type approximation guarantee that FC lacks. CC++ also matches or marginally beats the original CC method on the real-world data (81.04% vs 80.91% for maximal temperature) while running at a speed close to SC, whereas CC's runtime grows sharply with graph size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Cheeger inequality (Chung, 1997) to the constrained clustering setting, where the cut ratio is defined between two graphs G and H encoding MUST-LINK and CANNOT-LINK constraints. Unlike prior Cheeger-type results for constrained clustering (Cucuringu et al., 2016; Koutis et al., 2023), which bound the optimal cut in terms of λ₂(Δ_H^G) and a parameter of H (a third demand graph or the conductance of G), this work bounds Φ_H^G using λ₂(Δ_H^G) and λ₂(Δ^H) — the spectral properties of both constraint graphs. The practical implementation draws on Knyazev's (2017) signed Laplacian framework, repurposing a negative self-loop to render the constraint graph's Laplacian invertible and enabling Cholesky-based solvers in place of the more expensive QZ algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a gap between practical constrained clustering methods (which lack rigorous approximation guarantees) and theoretical results (which are not directly implementable due to computational intractability). The CC++ algorithm achieves runtime comparable to standard spectral clustering while significantly outperforming it in challenging regimes, making constrained clustering more accessible for real-world applications like microclimate segmentation. Open questions include extending the framework to multiway partitioning, optimizing the negative self-loop weight, and understanding how the approximation factor 4√(λ₂(Δ_H^G)/λ₂(Δ^H)) behaves in practice on very large or highly imbalanced graphs.
