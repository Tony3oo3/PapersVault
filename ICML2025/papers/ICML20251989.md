## Problem

Standard RLHF algorithms based on regularized maximum likelihood estimation are not robust to approximate clones: adding a near-duplicate alternative to the preference dataset can significantly shift the learned reward function. This is a practical concern because RLHF datasets are often unbalanced due to LLM-generated responses or adversarial manipulation, and the standard MLE's close relationship to Borda count (which is known to fail independence of clones) makes it inherently vulnerable to such redundancy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a pairwise preference dataset D over a finite set of alternatives M, where each alternative has a context vector in R^d (e.g., a text embedding), along with a specification of the full alternative space S (with finite volume |S|) used to compute Voronoi-based weights. It outputs a reward function r: M → R that assigns a scalar score to each alternative. Unlike the standard MLE, the weighted MLE requires the context vectors and the ambient space S to compute per-alternative weights, making it sensitive to the geometric arrangement of alternatives in context space rather than just the pairwise comparison counts.

## Method

The weighted MLE modifies the standard regularized MLE objective by multiplying each pairwise log-likelihood term by the product of Voronoi-cell weights w_M(x₁)·w_M(x₂) and each regularization term by w_M(x), where w_M(y) is the fraction of the alternative space S whose closest alternative in M is y. This down-weights alternatives that are near-duplicates of others (small Voronoi cells) and up-weights unique alternatives (large Voronoi cells), so that adding an approximate clone splits the weight between the original and the clone without substantially altering the objective. The paper proves the weighted MLE is robust to approximate clones (Theorem 4.2) via a three-step perturbation argument bounding the change in the optimal reward function by O(√ε) when a clone within distance ε is added, while showing the standard MLE fails this property (Theorem 3.2). The weighted MLE also preserves the M-estimator interpretation: its output is the unique solution to a system of equations equating the weighted average win rate to the empirical weighted average win rate, generalizing the Borda-count connection of the standard MLE.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 4.2, which proves that the proposed weighted MLE is robust to approximate clones (i.e., adding a near-duplicate alternative changes the reward function by at most O(√ε) where ε is the distance between the clone and original), while Theorem 3.2 proves the standard regularized MLE is not. In the case study using gpt-4o-mini-generated Paris descriptions with three annotator types (food, art, romance), the standard MLE shifts the highest-reward topic from romance (on 'Original') to art (on 'Clones'), whereas the weighted MLE keeps all three topic rewards roughly unchanged across both datasets. An impossibility result (Theorem 2.3) further shows that no RLHF algorithm can recover mean population rewards for all populations, even with just two BTL models and two alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed weighted MLE against the standard regularized MLE (the baseline from Siththaranjan et al., 2023) on the criterion of robustness to approximate clones. The weighted MLE wins decisively: it is provably robust (Theorem 4.2), while the standard MLE is provably not (Theorem 3.2). In the case study, the standard MLE's top-rewarded topic flips from romance to art when approximate clones are added to the dataset, a significant qualitative failure. The weighted MLE, by contrast, produces nearly identical mean rewards for all three topics (food, art, romance) on both the 'Original' and 'Clones' datasets, confirming the theoretical guarantee. The two methods are equivalent when alternatives are uniformly distributed over the alternative space, so the weighted MLE only differs (and improves) in the unbalanced/clone-heavy regime that is common in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Siththaranjan et al. (2023), who established the equivalence between the regularized MLE objective in RLHF and the Borda count voting rule, and on the social choice literature on independence of clones (Tideman, 1987; Conitzer et al., 2024). The key conceptual move is adapting the social choice axiom of independence of clones to the RLHF setting as 'robustness to approximate clones,' then showing that the Borda-count-equivalent regularized MLE inherits Borda count's known failure of this axiom. The proposed weighted MLE introduces Voronoi-diagram-based weights to down-weight near-duplicate alternatives, a technique that has no direct analogue in prior RLHF work but draws on geometric partitioning ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that standard RLHF (regularized MLE) is not robust to approximate clones, combined with a constructive fix (weighted MLE) that provably satisfies the property while preserving the MLE's interpretability as an M-estimator of weighted win rates, gives practitioners a principled way to handle unbalanced or adversarially duplicated datasets. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the theory assumes sufficient comparisons per pair (unrealistic for LLM-generated answers), the case study is a single synthetic prompt with LLM-simulated annotators, and the Voronoi weighting scheme is one of many possible choices. Open questions include how often approximate clones actually arise in production RLHF datasets, whether alternative weighting schemes perform better, and how to handle the common case where each LLM-generated answer participates in only one comparison.
