## Problem

Existing ToM evaluations for MLLMs rely on black-box QA tasks and unimodal inputs, making it impossible to distinguish genuine belief reasoning from pattern-matching, hallucination, or scenario-comprehension artifacts. No prior benchmark provides complete, controlled perceptual information from multiple agent perspectives in a multimodal (video + text) setting, and none examines the internal representations that underlie ToM-like behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251394/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7 sampled frames (4 key + 3 intermediate) from a 294×420-pixel 2D grid-world video alongside structured text annotations that include omniscient narrative, per-agent perspective descriptions (with perception masked behind closed doors), and belief statements. Internally, it extracts attention head activations at the final token position across all layers and heads (X ∈ R^{L×H×D}), which serve as the substrate for both probing and intervention. The output is a binary TB/FB belief classification for QA evaluation, and for the intervention step, a perturbed attention activation that shifts the model's reasoning direction toward the protagonist's perspective.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage interpretability pipeline: (1) linear probing with logistic regression on individual attention head activations to identify heads whose representations linearly separate correct from incorrect belief states, distinguishing perspective-separation-sensitive heads (from FB comparisons) and belief-reasoning-sensitive heads (from TB comparisons); (2) a training-free intervention that adds α·σ_l^h·θ_l^h to the output of the top-K most sensitive attention heads (after multi-head attention, before the output projection W^o), where θ is the probe weight direction and σ is the activation standard deviation along that direction. The key design insight is that in the 2D grid world, TB scenarios make the protagonist's perception identical to the omniscient view, so correct/incorrect belief pairs isolate pure belief reasoning, while FB scenarios with closed doors create genuine perspective divergence that isolates perspective separation. This allows the two ToM sub-competencies to be identified and targeted independently within a unified framework. The approach is validated on LLaVA-Next-Video-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B, showing consistent improvements across first-order and second-order TB, FB, and Both tasks without any parameter updates.

## Results

On the GridToM benchmark (multimodal setting), MLLMs exhibit a striking asymmetry: GPT-4o achieves 100% on first-order FB but only 6.2% on first-order TB, while Qwen2-VL reaches 97.0% FB but just 26.6% TB. The proposed training-free attention intervention (+α) substantially narrows this gap: LLaVA-Next-Video-7B improves from 53.2%→63.8% TB and 0.8%→22.0% Both (first-order), and Qwen2-VL-7B improves from 26.6%→60.4% TB and 15.4%→24.2% Both (second-order). Humans achieve near-perfect scores (≥99.8%) across all conditions, confirming dataset validity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven baselines, GPT-4o and Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro show extreme FB bias (100% FB) with near-zero TB accuracy (6.2% and 16.8%), indicating overreliance on false-belief patterns learned during training. DeepSeek-VL2-Small is the most balanced baseline (68.4% TB / 43.8% FB) but still far below human performance. In text-only settings, Doubao-1.5-Pro-32k achieves a perfect 100% on both first-order TB and FB, while LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct scores 0% TB / 100% FB, mirroring the multimodal asymmetry. The proposed +α intervention consistently outperforms the unmodified baselines on the two intervened models: LLaVA-Next-Video gains +10.6 pp on first-order TB and +21.2 pp on first-order Both, while Qwen2-VL gains +33.8 pp on first-order TB and +10.8 pp on second-order TB. The intervention does not degrade FB performance (Qwen2-VL FB stays at 97.4%), confirming that the steering direction selectively enhances perspective-sensitive reasoning without disrupting existing capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ToM evaluation literature (Sally-Anne-style QA benchmarks like MMToM-QA, OpenToM, and ToMi) in two key directions: from unimodal to multimodal (video + text) and from black-box output evaluation to interpretability-driven analysis of internal representations. Methodologically, it builds directly on the inference-time intervention framework of Li et al. (2024), adapting the activation-steering mechanism to the multimodal setting by probing attention heads with logistic regression and then shifting activations along the discovered direction. The 2D grid world environment draws on the MultiGrid/MiniGrid lineage used in prior ToM research (Ma et al., 2023; Oguntola et al., 2023), but adds explicit per-agent perceptual annotations that prior grid-world datasets lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretability-based evidence that MLLMs develop linearly decodable internal representations of multi-perspective belief states, moving the ToM debate beyond whether models 'pass' QA tasks to whether they genuinely represent others' mental states. The training-free intervention demonstrates a practical path to enhancing ToM without retraining, though it is validated on only two 7B MLLMs in a highly simplified 2D grid world. Key open questions include whether the discovered attention-head mechanisms generalize to larger or architecturally different models, whether the findings hold in more complex, open-ended social scenarios, and whether the TB/FB asymmetry (models excelling at false-belief but failing true-belief) reflects a fundamental limitation in how MLLMs encode perspective-taking.
