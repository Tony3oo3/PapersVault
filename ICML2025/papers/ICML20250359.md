## Problem

Optimization on the Pareto Set (OPS)—minimizing a user-specified preference function subject to weak Pareto optimality of a vector-valued objective—is a semivectorial bilevel problem that existing solvers handle only under strong convexity assumptions or with second-order derivatives. Preference-as-constraint formulations (e.g., PMTL, FERERO) converge to KKT points that need not be Pareto stationary, and linear scalarization cannot reach all Pareto-optimal solutions even after enumerating all weights. The paper asks whether a first-order, Hessian-free method can provably approximate necessary optimality conditions for OPS in nonconvex settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a vector-valued objective F(x) = (f_1, …, f_M) and a scalar preference function f_0(x) (e.g., the squared discrepancy between objective values or a user-defined equality constraint H(x)=0) over a closed convex set X ⊆ R^q. It outputs model parameters x* that are weakly Pareto optimal for F while minimizing f_0, effectively selecting a specific point on the Pareto front dictated by the preference. In practice, the objectives are differentiable losses (CTC, CPC, cross-entropy) evaluated on speech or image data, and the preference is encoded as a vector specifying desired trade-offs among objectives.

## Method

The key mechanism is a smoothed merit function v_{l,τ}(x) = −min_y [τ·LSE((f_m(y)−f_m(x))/τ) + (l/2)‖x−y‖²] that converts the vector-valued Pareto constraint into a single scalar constraint with an easily computable gradient via Danskin's theorem. A penalty-based reformulation replaces the constraint with a polynomial penalty p(x) = (v_{l,τ}(x)+τ ln M)^θ, yielding a single-level problem solvable by first-order methods. The FOOPS algorithm alternates an inner loop (K_t gradient steps on y to approximate the minimizer y*_{l,τ}(x)) with an outer loop (gradient step on x using the estimated penalty gradient), with scheduled step sizes and penalty parameter γ_t. Theoretically, the paper proves that v_{l,τ} satisfies a Hölderian error bound under subanalyticity of the objectives, establishes that stationary solutions of the penalty problem approximate KKT points of the constrained formulation under the KL inequality, and derives O(1/T) deterministic and O(1/√T) stochastic convergence rates.

## Results

On multi-patch image classification, FOOPS achieves the best hypervolume across all three datasets: 2.62×10⁻² on Multi-MNIST (vs. 1.95 for FERERO, 1.68 for LS), 8.32×10⁻² on Multi-Fashion (vs. 7.76 for FERERO, 6.77 for XM), and 4.80×10⁻² on Multi-F+M (vs. 3.89 for XM, 3.82 for FERERO). FOOPS also attains the highest accuracy on all three datasets (e.g., 0.33 on Multi-MNIST vs. 0.25 for FERERO; 0.72 on Multi-F+M vs. 0.53 for FERERO). On multi-lingual speech recognition (LibriSpeech + AISHELL-1), FOOPS-FT achieves an average WER of 5.1%, matching FERERO-FT, with a slight edge on Chinese (4.7% vs. 4.9%) but a small gap on English (5.7% vs. 5.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOOPS consistently outperforms all four baselines (LS, PMTL, EPO, XM) and the closest competitor FERERO on hypervolume across all three multi-patch image classification datasets, with the largest margin on Multi-MNIST (2.62 vs. 1.95 for FERERO, a 34% improvement). On accuracy, FOOPS leads by a clear margin on Multi-MNIST (0.33 vs. 0.25 for FERERO) and Multi-F+M (0.72 vs. 0.53 for FERERO), while the gap on Multi-Fashion is smaller (1.22 vs. 1.13). On multi-lingual speech recognition, FOOPS-FT ties FERERO-FT on average WER (5.1%) but trails slightly on English (5.7% vs. 5.4%), suggesting the method is less effective at precisely aligning with the fairness preference function f₀. The authors acknowledge that FOOPS is 'worse at aligning with preferences compared to other methods' (Figure 5), trading some preference alignment for superior Pareto-front coverage. On the synthetic Example 3.12, PMTL and FERERO fail to reach the Pareto front under certain initializations, while FOOPS successfully converges to preferred Pareto optimal solutions, validating the OPS formulation's advantage over constraint-based preference modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the merit function framework of Tanabe et al. (2024) for multiobjective optimization and the penalty-based bilevel optimization analysis of Shen et al. (2025), extending both to the semivectorial (vector-valued lower-level) setting. It departs from prior OPS methods (PB-PDO, PNG, TAWT, PMM) that either require second-order derivatives, assume convexity, or use linear scalarization (which provably fails to capture all Pareto-optimal solutions under nonconvexity). The key methodological departure is proving the Hölderian error bound for the smoothed merit function under subanalyticity rather than assuming it, and showing that KKT conditions for the bilevel problem can be approximated by first-order methods under the KL inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first first-order algorithm with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for the OPS problem under nonconvex objectives, removing the need for second-order derivatives that plagued prior approaches. The theoretical framework connecting subanalyticity, HEB, and KL inequality for the merit function is of independent interest to simple bilevel optimization. However, experiments reveal a trade-off: FOOPS achieves the best hypervolume but is notably worse at aligning solutions with user-specified preferences compared to FERERO and PMTL, suggesting the penalty-based approach may over-prioritize Pareto optimality at the expense of preference satisfaction. Stochastic variants and nonsmooth lower-level objectives remain open for future work.
