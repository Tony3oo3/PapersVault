## Problem

While the BBP phase transition for the largest eigenvalue of spiked Wigner matrices is well understood, the corresponding fluctuation results for entrywise-transformed spiked Wigner matrices—where a nonlinear function f is applied to each entry—were previously unknown. This gap is significant because entrywise transformations are used in practice to improve PCA by increasing the effective signal-to-noise ratio, yet the precise limiting distribution of the largest eigenvalue fluctuation after such a transformation had not been established.

## Input / Output

The method takes an N×N spiked Wigner matrix M = W + √λ xx^T (with W a Wigner matrix, x a unit spike vector, and λ the SNR) and applies an entrywise C³ function f to produce M̃_ij = N^{-1/2} f(√N M_ij). The output is a complete characterization of the largest eigenvalue μ₁(M̃): in the supercritical regime (effective SNR λ_e = λ(E[f'(√N W₁₂)])² > 1), the N^{1/2}-scaled fluctuation converges to a Gaussian with variance 2(λ_e−1)/λ_e; in the subcritical regime (λ_e < 1), the N^{2/3}-scaled fluctuation converges to the GOE Tracy-Widom law. Rigidity estimates (concentration at the correct fluctuation scale) are also provided in both regimes.

## Method

The core technical difficulty is that the Taylor expansion of the entrywise transformation yields not a rank-1 spiked Wigner matrix but a rank-2 spiked Wigner-type matrix, because the second-derivative term and the non-uniform variance of the noise entries (proportional to x_i²x_j²) are both of order N^{-1/2} and cannot be ignored. The proof proceeds by constructing a matrix interpolation (flow) H(t) from the approximating rank-2 spiked Wigner-type matrix to a target matrix with known spectral properties (a rank-2 spiked Wigner matrix in the supercritical case, a Wigner matrix in the subcritical case), then applying a Green function comparison argument along this flow. The key technical input is a local law for the resolvent of the Wigner-type matrices along the flow, obtained by showing they are asymptotically Wigner (the variance perturbation is O(N^{-3/2+2ε})), which allows the deterministic solution of the quadratic vector equation to be approximated by the semicircle Stieltjes transform. In the subcritical case, an additional eigenvalue sticking argument shows the spike does not move the largest eigenvalue beyond N^{-3/4}, reducing the problem to the Tracy-Widom limit of the pure noise matrix.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.5) establishes a BBP-type phase transition for the largest eigenvalue of a transformed spiked Wigner matrix: in the supercritical regime (effective SNR λ_e > 1), the rescaled fluctuation N^{1/2}(μ_1 − (√λ_e + 1/√λ_e)) converges to a centered Gaussian with variance 2(λ_e−1)/λ_e; in the subcritical regime (λ_e < 1), N^{2/3}(μ_1 − 2) converges to the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution. Theorem 2.6 provides matching rigidity (concentration) bounds of order N^{−1/2+ε} and N^{−2/3+ε} respectively. Numerical experiments with N=1024 and 5,000 samples confirm the theory: for non-Gaussian bimodal noise with optimal transformation (λ_e≈2.902 supercritical, λ_e≈0.363 subcritical) and for Gaussian noise with polynomial transformation f(x)=(x²+3x−1)/√11 (λ_e≈1.294 supercritical, λ_e≈0.350 subcritical), the empirical histograms closely match the predicted Gaussian and Tracy-Widom densities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no algorithmic baselines to compare against. The key comparison is with the prior work of Perry et al. (2018), which established the limit of the largest eigenvalue of transformed spiked Wigner matrices (the BBP transition at the level of the limit) and proved the approximation in (3) for the optimal transformation. The present paper extends this by proving the full fluctuation (Theorem 2.5) and rigidity (Theorem 2.6), which were previously unknown for the transformed model. The technical contribution is showing that the correct approximation is a rank-2 spiked Wigner-type matrix (not a rank-1 spiked Wigner matrix), due to the non-negligible second-derivative term in the Taylor expansion, and then applying a Green function comparison along a carefully constructed matrix interpolation. The numerical experiments in Section 5 serve as validation rather than comparison: the empirical distributions of the rescaled largest eigenvalue match the theoretical Gaussian (supercritical) and GOE Tracy-Widom (subcritical) predictions for both non-Gaussian and Gaussian noise settings, confirming the correctness of the effective SNR formula λ_e = λ(E[f′(√N W₁₂)])².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entrywise-transformed spiked Wigner matrix framework of Lesieur et al. (2015) and Perry et al. (2018), which established the limit of the largest eigenvalue and the effective SNR formula, by proving the full BBP-type fluctuation (Gaussian in the supercritical regime, GOE Tracy-Widom in the subcritical regime). The proof strategy relies on the local law for Wigner-type matrices from Ajanki et al. (2017), the eigenvalue sticking and rank-2 spiked Wigner matrix results of Knowles & Yin (2013) and Renfrew & Soshnikov (2013), and the resolvent-functional approximation technique of Lee & Schnelli (2018). A key technical departure from the rank-1 spiked Wigner matrix setting is that the Taylor expansion of the entrywise transformation forces a rank-2 spiked Wigner-type approximation, since both the first-derivative fluctuation term and the second-derivative expectation term are of order N^{-1/2} and cannot be neglected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result completes the BBP transition picture for transformed spiked Wigner matrices, providing the precise limiting fluctuation laws and rigidity estimates needed for finite-N hypothesis testing in improved PCA. The effective SNR formula λ_e = λ(E[f'(√N W₁₂)])² gives a concrete, computable criterion for selecting entrywise transformations that maximize detection power, with direct implications for signal-plus-noise inference in community detection, synchronization, and neural network feature learning. Open questions include extending the analysis to rectangular (spiked Wishart) models, removing the assumption that diagonal and off-diagonal noise entries share the same distribution, and treating the rank-k spike case, all of which the authors outline as future work.
