## Problem

Current cross-domain few-shot segmentation methods compute similarity by comparing full ViT features between support and query images, which implicitly cross-matches all ViT layers with equal weight. The paper identifies that this equal-weight cross-matching entangles rational pattern comparisons (e.g., wings vs. wings) with meaningless ones (e.g., bodies vs. wings), causing the model to bind co-occurring patterns together and severely harming cross-domain transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252376/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K support image-mask pairs and a single query image from an unseen target domain, operating in a meta-learning episodic format. Support features from L ViT layers are processed via mask average pooling to yield foreground and background prototypes, which are then cross-compared against query features to produce L²×2 score maps at patch resolution (n×n). These score maps are bilinearly upsampled to the original image resolution (h×w) and thresholded via argmax to yield a binary foreground/background segmentation mask.

## Method

The paper exploits the residual structure of ViT to decompose its output into L layer-wise components that all reside in the same feature space, then proposes a self-disentanglement and re-composition pipeline. The Orthogonal Space Decoupling (OSD) module concatenates all L layer features, projects them into a low-rank (r=8) orthogonal space with an explicit orthogonality regularization loss to enforce semantic independence, and projects back. The Cross-Pattern Comparison (CPC) module then computes L² cross-comparison score maps between support prototypes and query features, breaking the naive equal-weight matching. During source-domain training, score maps are composed with uniform weights; during target-domain finetuning, a lightweight Adaptive Fusion Weight (AFW) matrix of size L²×2 is introduced to learn domain-specific composition weights, enabling efficient adaptation without retraining the encoder.

## Results

On the CD-FSS benchmark (4 target domains: FSS-1000, DeepGlobe, ISIC2018, Chest X-ray), SDRC with ViT-Base achieves 63.22% average mIoU under 1-shot and 67.30% under 5-shot settings, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (APSeg, also ViT-Base) by 1.92% and 2.21% respectively. The method also surpasses the best ResNet-50-based methods (DRA at 60.86%/65.42%) by 2.36% (1-shot) and 1.88% (5-shot). Notably, SDRC achieves these gains with an encoder-only architecture and substantially lower FLOPs (18.86G) compared to PATNet (22.63G) and HSNet (20.11G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDRC outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all four target domains in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the closest ViT-based competitor APSeg (CVPR 2024), SDRC gains +1.92% (1-shot) and +2.21% (5-shot) average mIoU while using a lighter encoder-only architecture versus APSeg's SAM-based encoder-decoder. Against the best ResNet-50 methods, SDRC leads DRA (CVPR 2024) by +2.36%/+1.88% and ABCDFSS (CVPR 2024) by +2.55%/+2.33%. In the appendix, SDRC also surpasses disentanglement-based methods (InfoGAN +8.72%, DFR +5.25%), multi-layer fusion methods (FPN +7.35%, MMFuser +4.15%), and slot-attention methods (Slot-Attention +6.30%, DINOSAUR +5.37%) under 1-shot, confirming that structural ViT decomposition with learned cross-match weights is more effective than external disentanglement or fusion modules for cross-domain transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CD-FSS benchmark and episodic training framework established by PATNet (Lei et al., 2022), and adopts mask average pooling from SG-One (Zhang et al., 2020b) for prototype extraction. However, it departs conceptually from prior CD-FSS methods (APSeg, DRA, ABCDFSS, APM) that treat the ViT's final output as a monolithic feature to be refined or adapted; instead, it leverages the residual structure of ViT to decompose the output into per-layer components, identifying that equal-weight cross-layer comparisons in distance-based segmentation are the root cause of feature entanglement. The OSD module borrows orthogonal regularization (Xie et al., 2017) but repurposes it for semantic decoupling of ViT layer features, while the CPC and AFW modules are novel mechanisms for weighted re-composition of disentangled patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides an interpretable, structurally-grounded explanation for why cross-domain few-shot segmentation fails—namely, that meaningless cross-layer comparisons are entangled with meaningful ones at equal weight—which could inform future work on transformer-based transfer learning more broadly. The method is lightweight (OSD rank=8, AFW is 288 parameters for ViT-B) and avoids the heavy VAE-discriminator overhead of prior disentanglement approaches, making it practical for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the decomposition insight generalizes to architectures without residual connections in a shared feature space, whether the learned cross-match weights are stable across more diverse target domains, and whether the approach extends to multi-class or open-vocabulary segmentation settings.
