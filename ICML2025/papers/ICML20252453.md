## Problem

Existing epidemic forecasting methods fail to explicitly learn disease transmission mechanisms (e.g., SIR dynamics), cannot handle irregular or missing observation intervals common in real-world surveillance, and treat regional transmission graphs as static rather than evolving with the epidemic. These limitations prevent accurate continuous-time modeling of how local spatial spread patterns shift under global factors like lockdowns or vaccination campaigns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes regional infection time series (e.g., weekly case counts over a window T for N regions) and a geographic adjacency graph as input, and produces predicted infection counts at future horizons T+h. Crucially, disease states (susceptible, infectious, recovered) are treated as latent high-dimensional variables rather than requiring explicit observations, and the NCDE formulation handles irregular sampling intervals by constructing continuous control paths via interpolation, making the model robust to missing or unevenly spaced data points.

## Method

EARTH integrates two key modules: (1) EANO, which uses Neural Controlled Differential Equations to model continuous-time disease states S(t), I(t), R(t) as latent variables, with ODE functions structured after Network SIR that replace scalar transmission/recovery rates with learnable weight matrices and incorporate spatial neighbor aggregation; and (2) GLTG, which first identifies semantically similar regions via Dynamic Time Warping to extend the geographic graph, then applies a residual GNN to learn evolving global infection trend features, and finally uses these trends to generate time-varying local transmission graphs with asymmetric weights that guide EANO propagation. A multi-headed cross-attention mechanism fuses the global trend (as query) with local disease states (as key/value) to produce the final prediction, trained end-to-end with MSE loss.

## Results

EARTH achieves the best RMSE (R) and Peak Time Error (P) across all three datasets and all prediction horizons (h=5, 10, 15). On Australia-COVID, EARTH reaches R=156.8/P=30.12 at h=5, improving over the next-best EpiColaGNN (R=204.3/P=36.86) by ~23% in RMSE. On US-States, EARTH achieves R=243.2/P=67.43 at h=5 versus EpiColaGNN's R=286.1/P=83.38, and the gap widens at longer horizons (h=15: R=300.1 vs. 375.1). The advantage is most pronounced at h=15 on Australia-COVID, where EARTH (R=225.3) nearly halves the error of EpiColaGNN (R=886.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EARTH outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric (RMSE and Peak Time Error) across all three datasets and all three horizons. The closest competitor is EpiColaGNN (Liu et al., 2023), which EARTH beats by 23% RMSE on Australia-COVID h=5 (156.8 vs. 204.3) and by 20% on US-States h=5 (243.2 vs. 286.1). Among ODE-based methods, STG-NCDE (Choi et al., 2021) is the strongest baseline, but EARTH still improves RMSE by 45% on Australia-COVID h=10 (177.6 vs. 341.3) and by 26% on US-Region h=5 (1080 vs. 1284). Epidemic-specific methods like EINN (Rodríguez et al., 2023) and EpiGNN (Xie et al., 2022) perform well at short horizons but degrade sharply at h=15 (e.g., EpiGNN jumps to R=764.2 on Australia-COVID h=15 vs. EARTH's 225.3), indicating they fail to capture long-term epidemic evolution. General spatio-temporal methods (STGCN, ASTGCN, DCRNN) are consistently outperformed, confirming that epidemic-aware modeling with continuous-time dynamics is essential for accurate forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

EARTH sits at the intersection of three research threads: Neural ODEs for continuous-time modeling (Chen et al., 2018), Neural Controlled Differential Equations for irregular time series (Kidger et al., 2020), and compartmental epidemic models (SIR, Hethcote 2000; Network SIR, Balcan et al. 2009). Prior epidemic forecasting methods (EpiGNN, ColaGNN, EpiColaGNN) rely on static geographic graphs and discrete-time architectures, while ODE-based spatio-temporal methods (STGODE, STG-NCDE) lack explicit epidemic mechanisms. EARTH departs from both by embedding a learnable Network SIR structure inside an NCDE framework and introducing a Dynamic Time Warping-guided global trend signal that dynamically reshapes local transmission graphs during evolution — a combination not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practically critical gaps: irregular/missing observation intervals (common in early-stage outbreaks) and the dynamic evolution of regional transmission patterns (e.g., post-lockdown behavior changes). The SIR-inspired parameterization offers interpretability to public-health decision-makers that pure data-driven models lack. Open questions include scalability to very large region counts, whether the DTW-based global connections (computed once from history) remain valid under regime shifts, and whether the approach generalizes to diseases with fundamentally different transmission dynamics (e.g., vector-borne vs. respiratory). The model's reliance on a Runge-Kutta ODE solver at inference also raises real-time deployment concerns for rapid outbreak response.
