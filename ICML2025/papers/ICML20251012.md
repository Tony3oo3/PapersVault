## Problem

Conditional flow matching (CFM) trains flow-based generative models by regressing a neural network vector field against a conditional target, but this alone does not guarantee accurate learning of the probability path. The paper identifies that the divergence gap between the learned and exact vector fields can be substantial even when the vector field gap is small, leading to significant errors in density estimation and likelihood computation. This gap is particularly problematic for tasks requiring accurate sample likelihood, such as climate modeling and molecular dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as the starting distribution and produces samples from the target distribution across multiple modalities: 2D/3D densities, 1024-base-pair DNA sequences on the probability simplex, 60-step dynamical system trajectories in R^{Md}, and 64×64 video frames in VQGAN latent space. For guided generation, additional conditioning is provided as extra input to the vector field—event constraint functions for dynamical systems, promoter activity profiles for DNA design, and initial video frames for prediction. Beyond generated samples, the method also outputs negative log-likelihood estimates computed via the continuous change-of-variables formula, enabling direct comparison of density estimation quality.

## Method

The paper derives a PDE for the error ε_t = p_t − p̂_t between the exact and learned probability paths, showing it is driven by a forcing term involving both the vector field difference (u_t − v_t) and its divergence ∇·(u_t − v_t). Using Duhamel's formula, this yields a total variation bound TV(p_t, p̂_t) ≤ ½ L_DM(θ), where L_DM depends on the divergence gap. Since the unconditional divergence is intractable, the authors introduce a conditional divergence matching loss L_CDM that upper-bounds L_DM by exploiting the conditional continuity equation, and prove TV(p_t, p̂_t) ≤ ½ L_CDM(θ). The final training objective is the FDM loss L_FDM = λ₁ L_CFM + λ₂ L_CDM, which simultaneously matches the vector field and its divergence. For computational efficiency in high dimensions, the divergence is estimated via Hutchinson's trace estimator with a single extra backward pass, and a stop-gradient trick in the squared CDM loss prevents gradient flow through the already-matched vector field term.

## Results

FDM consistently outperforms FM across all benchmark tasks. On CIFAR-10, FDM(OT) reduces NLL from 2.99 to 2.85 and FID from 6.35 to 5.62. On the Lorenz dynamical system, FDM achieves the lowest TV distance (0.0306 vs. 0.0348 for FM and 0.0314 for Diffusion) and the best NLL (−14.361 vs. −13.190 for FM). On KTH video prediction, FDM improves FVD from 180 to 155.5 and PSNR from 30.4 to 31.2 over latent FM, while on BAIR it reduces FVD from 146 to 123±4.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDM beats standard FM (Lipman et al., 2023) on every task and metric: likelihood on checkerboard (2.53 vs. 2.38 ×10⁻² for OT), NLL/FID on CIFAR-10 (2.85/5.62 vs. 2.99/6.35), TV distance on DNA simplex (0.07 vs. 0.08 for Dirichlet), MSE on promoter design (2.59E-2 vs. 2.68E-2), TV distance on both dynamical systems, and FVD/PSNR on both video datasets. Against the diffusion model of Finzi et al. (2023), FDM achieves lower TV distance on all four dynamical-system conditions and substantially better NLL (−14.361 vs. −7.052 on Lorenz), while matching p(E) estimation. On DNA promoter design, FDM outperforms Bit Diffusion (3.95E-2), DDSM (3.34E-2), and the language model of Stark et al. (2024) (3.33E-2) in MSE. For video prediction, FDM surpasses Latent FM (Davtyan et al., 2023) on both KTH (FVD 155.5 vs. 180) and BAIR (FVD 123 vs. 146), and also beats SRVP, SLAMP, TriVD-GAN-FP, Video Transformer, LVT, and RaMViD on their respective benchmarks, while using far less memory (35 GB vs. 128–1024 GB on BAIR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional flow matching (CFM) framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden (2023) by identifying a fundamental gap: minimizing the CFM loss alone does not control the divergence of the learned vector field, which the authors show via a PDE error analysis is a critical source of probability path error. The proposed Flow and Divergence Matching (FDM) loss is conceptually related to higher-order score matching (Meng et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2022) and Fokker-Planck equation enforcement (Lai et al., 2023), but achieves comparable or better accuracy at significantly lower computational cost—requiring only one extra backward pass via Hutchinson trace estimation and stop-gradient. For DNA sequence generation, the paper adapts the Dirichlet flow classifier framework of Stark et al. (2024), penalizing the classifier's input gradient norm as a proxy for divergence matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PDE-based error characterization and TV bound provide a clean theoretical justification for why divergence matching matters, filling a gap in the FM literature where prior bounds relied on higher-order score functions or KL divergence. The practical impact is that FDM offers a drop-in improvement to any CFM training pipeline with minimal overhead, which is valuable for applications requiring accurate likelihood estimation (climate modeling, molecular dynamics, cyber-physical systems). Open questions include developing a principled method for choosing the hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂, extending the analysis to KL divergence control (the paper notes TV bounds do not imply KL bounds), and exploring the Schrödinger bridge setting.
