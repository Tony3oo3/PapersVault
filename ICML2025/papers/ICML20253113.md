## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently hallucinate non-existing objects in images, and existing remedies either require costly fine-tuning on curated datasets or API access to proprietary LLMs for post-generation correction. These approaches also fail to address the intrinsic causes of hallucination—insufficient visual context from the visual encoder and information distortion during cross-modal projection—leaving a gap for a training-free, API-free solution that targets these root causes at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., a captioning instruction or VQA question) as input to an existing LVLM. In addition to the LVLM's own visual tokens, object-level detections from external vision models (DETR and RAM++) are extracted, aggregated via intersection, and converted into a short textual guidance prompt (e.g., 'This image contains: [object list]'). The output is a text response—either an image caption or a VQA answer—generated autoregressively with guided decoding, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying LVLM.

## Method

MARINE is a training-free, API-free inference-time framework that applies classifier-free guidance to LVLM text generation using image-grounded visual signals. At each decoding step, two forward passes through the LLM are performed: one conditioned on the original visual tokens and prompt (unconditional branch), and one additionally conditioned on a textual guidance prompt derived from aggregated object detections of DETR and RAM++ (conditional branch). The final logits are a convex combination in log-space: log p̂ = γ·log p_cond + (1−γ)·log p_uncond, where γ (recommended 0.3–0.7) controls the strength of guidance. This mechanism acts as a controllable gate that suppresses tokens with low probability in the image-grounded branch, effectively filtering out hallucinated objects while preserving the LVLM's instruction-following ability and generation detail. The framework is model-agnostic, requiring only that the LVLM expose its logits, and adds only ~2× inference latency compared to greedy decoding.

## Results

Across five LVLMs (LLaVA, LLaVA-v1.5, MiniGPT-v2, mPLUG-Owl2, InstructBLIP), MARINE achieves the lowest average CHAIR_S (8.4%) and CHAIR_I (3.7%) while maintaining the second-highest Recall (44.5%), outperforming all baselines. On POPE (adversarial setting), MARINE attains the best average accuracy (79.9%) and F1 (80.4%), with a more balanced yes-ratio (51.1% vs. 67.0% for greedy). Inference latency increases by only 1.98× over greedy decoding, the lowest overhead among all compared methods (vs. 6.84× for LURE and 7.0× for OPERA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARINE outperforms all four baselines on both CHAIR and POPE metrics across all five LVLMs. Against Woodpecker (GPT-3.5 post-correction), the strongest baseline, MARINE improves average CHAIR_S by 1.9 points (8.4 vs. 10.3) and CHAIR_I by 2.0 points (3.7 vs. 5.7), while also achieving higher Recall (44.5 vs. 38.0); on POPE, MARINE gains 1.1% accuracy and 2.1% F1 over Woodpecker. VCD (visual contrastive decoding) is competitive on CHAIR_I (4.8 avg.) but lags MARINE by 1.1 points on CHAIR_S and 1.1% on POPE accuracy. OPERA (over-trust penalty beam search) performs similarly to VCD on CHAIR but is notably weaker on POPE (72.4% avg. accuracy vs. MARINE's 79.9%). LURE (fine-tuning-based) performs worst on CHAIR (36.2 avg. C_S, worse than greedy) and is not evaluated on POPE due to its inability to produce binary yes/no answers. MARINE also achieves the lowest inference latency overhead (1.98×) compared to LURE (6.84×), Woodpecker (3.59×), VCD (2.03×), and OPERA (7.0×).

## Relation to Prior Work

MARINE adapts the classifier-free guidance framework (Ho & Salimans, 2021; Sanchez et al., 2023) from single-modal LLMs to the multi-modal LVLM setting, using external image-grounded models (DETR, RAM++) as the conditioning signal rather than the textual prompt itself. This departs from prior hallucination-mitigation approaches that either fine-tune the LVLM on curated data (LURE, Liu et al.), rely on proprietary GPT APIs for post-hoc correction (Woodpecker), or modify decoding through input perturbation (VCD) or beam-search penalties (OPERA). The key conceptual move is treating object-level visual information from dedicated detectors as a 'guidance prompt' in logit space, enabling a training-free, API-free intervention that targets the two LVLM-specific root causes (insufficient visual encoding and cross-modal projection loss) without altering model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in MARINE's zero-training, zero-API design with only ~2× inference overhead, making it deployable in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings (e.g., medical imaging) where fine-tuning or GPT calls are infeasible. The finding that aggregating multiple open-source detectors via intersection approaches ground-truth oracle performance suggests a viable path to reducing hallucination without manual annotation. However, the method is limited to object-level hallucination, depends on the quality and coverage of external detectors, and the guidance strength requires manual tuning per model; extending to attribute-level or relational hallucinations, and exploring adaptive or learned aggregation strategies, remain open.
