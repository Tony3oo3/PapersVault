## Problem

Existing learnable interventions such as ReFT apply deterministic, pointwise transformations within the concept subspace, yet prior work indicates the concept space is continuous—meaning the neighborhood around an intervention point should also produce relevant effects. The paper asks how to effectively explore this surrounding region to achieve stronger high-level control over language model behavior with minimal parameter overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts in multi-choice QA format (commonsense) or word problems with chain-of-thought rationales (arithmetic) and produces a single selected choice or numerical answer. Interventions are inserted between transformer blocks at specific token positions (first and last 7 tokens), modifying hidden representations mid-forward-pass while the frozen LLaMA base model handles actual token generation. The stochastic intervention layers add no new input/output interface; they only alter internal activations.

## Method

The paper replaces deterministic MLP nodes in ReFT with stochastic nodes via the reparameterization trick: two separate networks independently learn the mean (μ) and log variance (log σ²) of a Gaussian, and the intervention output is computed as μ + σ ⊙ ε where ε ~ N(0, I), enabling gradient-based optimization through sampling while exploring the concept subspace neighborhood. A key empirical finding is that a mixed strategy—applying stochastic interventions only to the first 25% of layers while keeping later layers deterministic—yields the best performance, consistent with the data processing inequality suggesting early layers retain more task-relevant information. The method also introduces model-specific clamping based on adjacent layer weight statistics for numerical stability, and a temperature parameter τ at inference time that scales the learned variance to control the degree of stochasticity, with lower τ benefiting reasoning tasks and higher τ benefiting instruction-following.

## Results

On eight commonsense reasoning benchmarks, D-ReFT₂₅% (replacing only the first 25% of layers with stochastic nodes) achieves 89.1% average accuracy on Llama-3-8B, outperforming ReFT (86.6%), DoRA (85.2%), and LoRA (80.8%) while using only 0.039% of parameters. On seven arithmetic reasoning benchmarks, D-ReFT₂₅% reaches 77.4% average on Llama-3-8B versus ReFT's 76.6% and LoRA's 76.3%. The mixed strategy of stochastic early layers + deterministic later layers consistently outperforms full stochastic replacement (D-ReFT₁₀₀%), with gains of +2.5 to +3.0 points over pointwise ReFT across all four model sizes tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-ReFT₂₅% consistently outperforms all six baselines across every model size and both task families. Against its direct predecessor ReFT (Wu et al., 2024), D-ReFT₂₅% gains +2.0 to +2.5 points on commonsense reasoning (e.g., 89.1% vs. 86.6% on Llama-3-8B) and +0.8 to +1.4 points on arithmetic reasoning (77.4% vs. 76.6%), while using only ~50% more parameters (0.039% vs. 0.026%). Against DoRA (Liu et al., 2024), the gap is larger: +3.9 points on Llama-3-8B commonsense and +2.2 points on arithmetic, with DoRA using ~18× more parameters. LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) trails by 8.3 points on Llama-3-8B commonsense and 1.1 points on arithmetic. RED (Wu et al., 2024) and Prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) are further behind, with PrefT achieving only 64.6% on LLaMA-7B commonsense versus D-ReFT's 82.2%. Notably, D-ReFT₂₅% also demonstrates superior robustness: under random word-deletion perturbations, it retains stable accuracy while pointwise ReFT drops by ~30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ReFT (Wu et al., 2024), a representation fine-tuning method that inserts deterministic intervention nodes between transformer blocks, by replacing those nodes with stochastic ones that learn a Gaussian distribution via the reparameterization trick from VAEs (Kingma & Welling, 2014). The conceptual bridge is direct: just as VAEs replace deterministic autoencoder bottlenecks with stochastic sampling to learn latent distributions, D-ReFT replaces deterministic intervention MLPs with stochastic counterparts to explore the neighborhood of the concept subspace. The paper positions itself within the broader intervention-based interpretability literature (activation engineering, concept erasure) and the PEFT landscape (LoRA, DoRA, adapters), but its primary lineage is ReFT plus the reparameterization trick, making it a targeted architectural modification rather than a new paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple drop-in modification (stochastic nodes in the first ~25% of layers) yields consistent 1–3% gains over ReFT across 15 benchmarks and LLaMA model sizes, along with notably better robustness to input perturbations. The finding that optimal inference-time temperature depends on task type (deterministic for reasoning, stochastic for instruction following) offers a useful control knob for practitioners. However, the gains are incremental, the method is only validated on the LLaMA family, and the added variance parameters (though small) slightly increase the parameter count. Open questions include whether the early-layer stochasticity finding generalizes to non-LLaMA architectures, whether the learned variance has a meaningful interpretability signal, and whether the approach composes with other intervention types beyond ReFT.
