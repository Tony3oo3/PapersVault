## Problem

Existing optimal transport dataset distances (OTDD) require solving pairwise OT problems across all classes and data points, incurring O(n³ log n) time and O(n²) space complexity that becomes prohibitive for large-scale or frequent dataset comparisons. While sliced Wasserstein distances offer near-linear computation for general distribution comparison, extending them to the supervised dataset distance setting is non-trivial because labels are represented as distributions over features rather than simple vectors, making their projection to one dimension unclear.

## Input / Output

The method takes two labeled datasets of arbitrary size with features in any space (images, text embeddings, etc.) and labels that may be entirely disjoint across datasets, producing a single scalar distance. No model training, feature extraction pipeline, or label alignment is required—the method is both model-agnostic and embedding-agnostic. The computation is parameterized by the number of random projections L and the number of moments k, with the output being a Monte Carlo estimate of the expected 1D Wasserstein distance over random projection parameters.

## Method

The key innovation is Moment Transform Projection (MTP), which maps a label (a distribution over features) to a scalar by first projecting the distribution to one dimension via a feature projection (e.g., Radon transform or convolution) and then computing a scaled moment (divided by λ!) of the projected distribution. The data point projection combines the feature projection of x with k MTPs of the label distribution q_y, weighted by a random unit vector ψ, yielding a single scalar per data point. The s-OTDD is then defined as the expected 1D Wasserstein distance between the projected empirical distributions of two datasets, where the expectation is over random projection parameters (ψ, θ, λ, φ). This achieves O(L(n log n + dn)) time and O(L(d+n)) space complexity, independent of the number of classes, and is proven to be a valid metric on the space of distributions over feature-label-distribution pairs via injectivity of the data point projection.

## Results

On NIST transfer learning, s-OTDD (10,000 projections) achieves a Spearman correlation of ρ=0.40 with the performance gap, matching OTDD (Exact) and OTDD (Gaussian approximation) while substantially outperforming CHSW (ρ=0.16) and WTE (ρ=0.37). On text datasets, s-OTDD and OTDD (Exact) both achieve Pearson r=0.48 with the performance gap, but s-OTDD computes the distance in roughly one-tenth the time. In the data augmentation experiment on CIFAR-10/Tiny-ImageNet, s-OTDD attains a Spearman correlation of −0.74 with classification accuracy, slightly better than OTDD (Exact) at −0.70, while processing 10× more data points (50,000 vs. 5,000) in ~53×10³ seconds versus >74×10³ seconds for OTDD (Exact). On Split Tiny-ImageNet at 224×224 resolution, s-OTDD (500,000 projections) shows strong correlation with transfer performance gain, whereas OTDD is computationally infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OTDD (Exact) [Alvarez-Melis & Fusi, 2020], s-OTDD matches or slightly exceeds the correlation with downstream performance: equal Spearman ρ=0.40 on NIST, equal Pearson r=0.48 on text, and a marginally better Spearman of −0.74 vs. −0.70 in data augmentation, all while being significantly faster (≈10× speedup on text, ~1.4× faster on data augmentation despite 10× more data points). Against WTE [Liu et al., 2025], s-OTDD achieves a higher Spearman correlation on NIST transfer learning (ρ=0.40 vs. 0.37) and avoids the quadratic-in-classes MDS preprocessing that limits WTE's scalability. Against CHSW [Bonet et al., 2024], s-OTDD substantially outperforms on NIST transfer learning (ρ=0.40 vs. 0.16) and does not require the MDS-based label embedding step that introduces quadratic cost in the number of classes. In terms of computational time, s-OTDD scales near-linearly with dataset size and successfully processes datasets where OTDD (Exact), WTE, and CHSW crash due to memory limitations above ~30,000 samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs a sliced optimal transport version of the Optimal Transport Dataset Distance (OTDD) by introducing the Moment Transform Projection (MTP), which maps a label—represented as a conditional distribution over features—to a scalar via a scaled moment of its one-dimensional projection. Conceptually, it bridges the hierarchical OT structure of Alvarez-Melis & Fusi (2020) with the Radon-transform-based computational framework of sliced Wasserstein (Bonneel et al., 2015), while drawing on the hierarchical hybrid projection principle of Nguyen & Ho (2024) to ensure injectivity of the combined data-point projection. Unlike prior sliced-OT-based dataset distances (CHSW, WTE) that require MDS or Wasserstein embeddings of class labels, s-OTDD is embedding-agnostic and avoids any quadratic-in-classes preprocessing, making it the first formal dataset distance based purely on sliced optimal transport.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: s-OTDD scales to settings where OTDD is infeasible (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet at 224×224 resolution) and runs roughly 10× faster on text transfer learning while maintaining comparable correlation with the exact OTDD. The theoretical guarantees (metricity, injectivity under regularity conditions, O(L^{-1/2}) Monte Carlo convergence) provide a principled foundation. Open questions include understanding the gradient flow of s-OTDD for differentiable dataset optimization and adapting the method to continual learning, where task-similarity metrics are critical. A limitation is that the moderate correlations (ρ≈0.40 on NIST) suggest the distance captures transferability signal but not all of it, and the method still requires a large number of projections (10,000–500,000) for stable estimates.
