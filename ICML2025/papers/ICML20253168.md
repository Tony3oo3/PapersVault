## Problem

Diffusion models and Flow Matching produce high-quality samples but require many inference steps, and existing few-step alternatives—distillation methods and Consistency Models—suffer from training instability, mode collapse, and the need for careful tuning or pre-trained teacher networks. No single-stage training procedure existed that could learn a stable few-step generative model from scratch while guaranteeing distribution-level convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample ε ~ N(0, σ_d²I) (where σ_d is the data standard deviation) and an optional class label as input, and outputs a natural RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for ImageNet). At inference, a single application of the learned one-step sampler f_{s,t}^θ(x_t) maps a noisy interpolant x_t to a less-noisy x_s, and this can be iterated for 1, 2, 4, 8, or more steps. The network g_θ(x_t, s, t) is conditioned on both the source time t and target time s, enabling flexible step-count selection at inference without retraining.

## Method

IMM learns a one-step sampler that transforms the marginal interpolant distribution q_t(x_t) to q_s(x_s) for any s < t, using DDIM as a self-consistent, marginal-preserving interpolant. Training proceeds via inductive bootstrapping: for s < r < t, the model generates samples at time s from both x_t (current parameters θ) and x_r (stop-gradient parameters θ⁻), then minimizes the MMD between these two sets using a time-dependent Laplace kernel. This inductive construction—where the target distribution at s is itself produced by the model at a closer time r—guarantees convergence to the true data distribution (Theorem 1) and avoids the large distribution gap that makes naive objectives hard to optimize. The paper proves that Consistency Models are a single-particle, first-moment-matching special case of IMM, explaining their instability, and shows that using M particles (M ≥ 4) with MMD provides the repulsive forces and higher-moment matching needed for stable training. The entire procedure is single-stage, requires no pre-trained teacher, and uses standard architectures (DiT, DDPM++) with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, IMM achieves 1.99 FID with only 8 inference steps (w=1.5), surpassing DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID at 250 steps) and SiT-XL/2 (2.15 FID at 250 steps) using the same 675M-parameter architecture. On CIFAR-10, IMM achieves state-of-the-art 1.98 FID with 2-step generation for a model trained from scratch, outperforming EDM (2.05 FID at 35 steps) and all few-step-from-scratch baselines. At 16 steps, IMM reaches 1.90 FID on ImageNet, outperforming VAR's 2B variant. The method also scales well: FID decreases monotonically with both model size (DiT-S through DiT-XL) and inference steps (1, 2, 4, 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-256×256, IMM at 8 steps (1.99 FID) decisively outperforms DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID) and SiT-XL/2 (2.15 FID) which require 250 steps, achieving a 31× reduction in inference steps while improving quality. IMM also surpasses Shortcut models (3.80 FID at 128 steps) by a large margin at just 2 steps (3.66 FID). On CIFAR-10, IMM (1.98 FID, 2 steps) beats EDM (2.05 FID, 35 steps) and sCT (2.06 FID, 2 steps), and is competitive with CTM with GAN loss (1.87 FID, 2 steps) while using a single MMD-based loss without adversarial training. IMM slightly trails VAR-d20 (1.92 FID at 10 steps) on ImageNet, but VAR uses a 2B parameter model and fixed 10-step schedule, whereas IMM offers flexible step counts. The paper also shows that IMM's training is stable across embedding types (Fourier vs. positional) and noise conditioning scales, unlike Consistency Models which collapse with Fourier embedding at scale 16.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMM unifies and extends several threads: it builds on stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023) for the time-augmented distribution framework, uses DDIM (Song et al., 2020a) as a self-consistent interpolant, and employs MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) as the training divergence. Its most striking theoretical contribution is proving that Consistency Models are a single-particle, first-moment matching special case of IMM, which explains CMs' known training instability. Unlike diffusion distillation methods (DMD, SiD, SiM) that require a pretrained teacher, and unlike CMs/Shortcut Models that enforce point-wise consistency, IMM trains a single network from scratch via inductive bootstrapping with a distribution-level convergence guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMM demonstrates that a single MMD-based loss with no GAN, LPIPS, or distillation can match or beat the best few-step methods on both CIFAR-10 (1.98 FID, 2 steps) and ImageNet-256 (1.99 FID, 8 steps) while training stably across hyperparameters — a practical simplification that removes the two-stage distillation pipeline. The inductive convergence guarantee and the identification of why CMs are unstable (single-particle, first-moment only) provide a clearer theoretical foundation for the few-step generation landscape. Open questions include whether restart sampling and the continuous-time limit (subsuming continuous-time CMs) can push quality further, and whether the approach scales to video and audio generation.
