## Problem

While distribution shifts in tabular data have received substantial research attention with dedicated benchmarks (TableShift, Wild-Tab), feature shifts—the dynamic removal or addition of features between training and testing phases in open environments—remain largely unexplored. No systematic benchmark exists to evaluate how tabular models degrade when their input feature set changes, leaving a critical gap in understanding model robustness for real-world deployment where sensors fail, equipment is replaced, or new data sources emerge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250386/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 12 tabular datasets (binary classification, multi-class classification, and regression) from OpenML and Kaggle, each containing numerical and categorical features, and simulates feature-decrement scenarios by removing a user-specified proportion of features and imputing them with training-set mean values (or most-frequent category for categorical features). It produces performance metrics (accuracy and ROC-AUC for classification, RMSE for regression) along with the relative performance gap Δ, evaluated across 24 models from four categories at shift degrees from 0% to 100%, with results exportable as modified CSV datasets.

## Method

TabFSBench designs four controlled feature-shift scenarios—single shift (removing one feature at a time in ascending Pearson-correlation order), most/least-relevant shift (removing features in descending or ascending importance order), and random shift (uniformly sampling feature subsets at each shift ratio)—to systematically probe model robustness under feature decrement. Feature importance is quantified via Pearson correlation with the target, and shifted features are imputed with training-set means to avoid introducing artificial distribution shifts that would confound the evaluation. The benchmark evaluates 24 models spanning tree-based (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost), deep-learning (TabPFN, NODE, DANets, TabTransformer, FT-Transformer, and 12 others), LLM (Llama3-8B), and tabular LLM (TabLLM, UniPredict) categories, using adaptive hyperparameter optimization via Optuna with 100 trials per model-dataset pair. Key empirical findings include a linear relationship between the correlation sum of shifted features and performance degradation (ρ=0.74), and a positive correlation between closed-environment performance and feature-shift robustness, suggesting that improving baseline accuracy may transfer to open-environment resilience.

## Results

Across 12 tabular datasets and 22 models, TabFSBench reveals that tree-based models suffer the largest performance degradation under feature shifts: LightGBM and XGBoost lose 35–38% accuracy at 100% shift in binary classification, while CatBoost retains the best tree-based robustness (−33%). In contrast, NODE and tabular LLMs (TabLLM, UniPredict) show near-zero or even positive performance gaps in classification at moderate shift degrees (e.g., NODE +0.031 at 40% binary shift; UniPredict +0.080 at 100% binary shift). In regression, DANets and TabTransformer are most resilient (RMSE gap ≤0.011 at 100% shift), whereas tree-based models degrade by 1.4–1.7×. The paper further establishes a linear relationship (Pearson ρ = 0.74) between the sum of shifted features' correlations and model performance degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 22 evaluated models, no single model consistently dominates across all tasks and shift degrees. Tree-based models (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost) are the most vulnerable: at 100% feature shift in binary classification, XGBoost degrades by −37.5% accuracy and LightGBM by −35.3%, while CatBoost (−33.2%) is the most robust tree model. In classification, NODE and tabular LLMs (TabLLM, UniPredict) stand out for robustness—NODE achieves positive performance gaps up to +14.3% at 100% multi-class shift, and UniPredict shows +8.0% at 100% binary shift—though their closed-environment (0% shift) accuracy is not the best. In regression, DANets and TabTransformer are far more resilient than tree-based models, with RMSE gaps of only 0.011 and 0.006 at 100% shift versus 1.4–1.7 for LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost. Llama3-8B (general LLM) performs poorly under feature shifts (−35.7% at 100% binary shift), comparable to the worst tree-based models, while tabular-specific LLMs (TabLLM, UniPredict) are substantially more robust. CatBoost maintains the best average rank (4.8) across all settings, confirming its overall strength, but its robustness advantage over other tree models is modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by distinguishing feature shift (addition/removal of entire features) from distribution shift (changes in data distribution with fixed features), noting that much prior work conflates the two. It builds on the open-environments ML framework (Zhou, 2022) which identified feature space shifts as a core challenge, and on distribution-shift benchmarks like TableShift and Wild-Tab, but explicitly positions itself as addressing a gap those benchmarks leave open. The evaluation of LLMs and tabular LLMs (TabLLM, UniPredict, Llama3-8B) in a tabular feature-shift setting is novel, repurposing in-context learning and few-shot prompting paradigms from NLP to assess robustness under structural input changes rather than distributional changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Feature shifts from sensor failures, equipment upgrades, or incomplete measurements are a common real-world deployment challenge that has been largely unaddressed in tabular ML, and this benchmark provides the first systematic evaluation framework with practical APIs for the community. The finding that tabular LLMs exhibit positive performance gaps under feature decrement (unlike all other model categories) suggests a promising but underexplored direction, though the evidence is limited to classification tasks and small-scale models. Key open questions include: whether the closed-environment-to-feature-shift performance correlation has a theoretical foundation, how to design feature-importance-driven optimization that protects strongly correlated features, and whether the findings generalize to feature-increment scenarios and larger-scale tabular LLMs.
