## Problem

Kearns & Li (1988) established that the accuracy of the standard Bayes Optimal Classifier is Lipschitz-robust to malicious noise in the data distribution, but Konstantinov & Lampert (2022) showed that adding fairness constraints can break this robustness for deterministic classifiers. This paper investigates whether the Fair BOC can be made robust to adversarial distribution shift, and if so, what form of classifier achieves this robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250020/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete probability distribution over X × Z × Y (where Z = {A, D} encodes protected group membership and Y ∈ {0, 1} is the binary label) and produces a randomized Fair BOC — a classifier that is deterministic everywhere except possibly on a single point in the domain, where it randomizes to satisfy the fairness constraint. The robustness guarantee is expressed as a local Lipschitz bound on accuracy in terms of the TV distance between two distributions, with a Lipschitz constant that depends on the ratio of group masses (e.g., max(P(A), P'(A))/min(P(A), P'(A))), meaning the bound degrades when one group's mass becomes very small.

## Method

The paper proves that the randomized Fair BOC (characterized via mass-threshold classifiers over group-wise sorted cells from Agarwal & Deshpande, 2022) satisfies a local Lipschitz robustness property for Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity, and Predictive Equality. The proof technique decomposes an arbitrary distribution shift of TV distance ε into a sequence of elementary single-mass-transfer transitions (Lemma 1), then for each elementary transfer, tracks how the Fair BOC's threshold boundary shifts using the structure of score boundaries and merged cells. A key intermediate result (Claim 2) shows that any fixed hypothesis maintains comparable accuracy and fairness across close distributions, but the main theorem requires a more refined analysis because the Fair BOC itself changes between neighboring distributions. The randomized Fair BOC is shown to be nearly deterministic (randomized on at most one domain point), and the paper further establishes that computing the deterministic Fair BOC is NP-complete while the randomized version is computable in O(|X| log |X|) time, and that randomization can improve accuracy by up to 0.5 over the deterministic counterpart.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 1) proves that the randomized DP-Fair BOC satisfies a local Lipschitz robustness bound: |Acc(f_P^DP, P) − Acc(f_P'^DP, P')| ≤ ε(1 + max(P(A),P'(A))/min(P(A),P'(A)) + max(P(D),P'(D))/min(P(D),P'(D))), where ε = TV(P, P'). Analogous bounds are established for Equal Opportunity (Theorem 2) and Predictive Equality (Theorem 3). In contrast, Claim 1 shows the deterministic Fair BOC is non-robust, with accuracy gaps of Ω(1) between arbitrarily close distributions. Additionally, the randomized Fair BOC can outperform its deterministic counterpart by up to 0.5 − ε in accuracy (Claim 6, tight), while being computable in O(|X| log |X|) time versus NP-completeness for the deterministic version (Claim 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, but it positions its contributions against several concurrent and prior theoretical results. Compared to Blum et al. (2024), who construct a randomized closure PQ(H) of any hypothesis class and prove only a one-directional Lipschitz bound (Acc(f',P') ≥ Acc(f,P) − O(ε)), this paper proves a stronger two-directional (symmetric) Lipschitz guarantee for the full BOC hypothesis class, and the output classifier is randomized on at most one element rather than all elements. Compared to Xian & Zhao (2024) and Chen et al. (2024), which provide sensitivity analyses only for label or covariate shifts (not general adversarial shifts), this paper handles the strictly more general adversarial distribution shift setting and proves the excess risk vanishes as TV distance → 0, whereas Xian & Zhao's group-shift bound is a constant independent of shift magnitude. Compared to Chen et al. (2022), which shows fairness of a fixed hypothesis is stable under label/covariate shift (analogous to this paper's Claims 2/3/7), this paper tackles the harder problem of bounding the accuracy change of the optimal fair classifier itself, which can change significantly between neighboring distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Kearns & Li's (1988) Lipschitz robustness framework for the Bayes Optimal Classifier under malicious noise, Agarwal & Deshpande's (2022) characterization of the Fair BOC via mass-threshold classifiers, and Konstantinov & Lampert's (2022) demonstration that fairness constraints break robustness for deterministic classifiers. The key conceptual departure is showing that while the deterministic Fair BOC inherits the non-robustness identified by Konstantinov & Lampert, the randomized Fair BOC recovers a two-directional Lipschitz guarantee under adversarial (arbitrary) distribution shift. Compared to Blum et al. (2024), who construct a randomized closure PQ(H) for arbitrary hypothesis classes with only one-directional Lipschitzness, this paper works with the full BOC hypothesis class and achieves a stronger two-directional bound while randomizing on at most one domain element rather than all of them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first positive robustness guarantee for the optimal fair classifier under adversarial distribution shift, resolving a gap left by Konstantinov & Lampert's impossibility result. The finding that the randomized Fair BOC is nearly deterministic (randomized on at most one point) while being computable in polynomial time (vs. NP-complete for the deterministic version) makes the theoretical guarantee practically relevant. Open questions include extending to multi-class and regression settings, relaxed/approximate fairness, and experimental validation on real-world data; the Lipschitz constant also degrades when group masses become highly imbalanced, limiting applicability in extreme demographic settings.
