## Problem

User prompts for text-to-image generation are frequently underspecified, forcing users into frustrating cycles of trial-and-error prompt refinement while standard T2I models silently make implicit assumptions that misalign with user intent. This paper addresses the gap by proposing proactive T2I agents that actively ask clarification questions when uncertain and externalize their understanding as an interpretable, editable belief graph, rather than passively generating from an ambiguous prompt.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes an initial underspecified text prompt and, over multiple turns, receives user answers to clarification questions (multiple-choice with free-text fallback) plus direct edits to the belief graph through a visual interface. It produces a generated image via Imagen 3, a structured belief graph (entities with appearance probabilities and importance scores, attributes with value-probability distributions, and spatial relations between entities), and clarification questions with suggested answer options. The belief graph is both an internal reasoning structure and a user-facing editable representation, allowing users to directly manipulate the agent's assumptions about the scene.

## Method

The core mechanism is a graph-based symbolic belief state where nodes are entities (explicit, implicit, background) and edges are relations, each annotated with probability estimates and importance scores that jointly determine what to ask next. LLMs (Gemini 1.5) parse prompts into belief graphs via in-context learning with three specialized parsers (entity, attribute, relation), and the agent iteratively selects questions by combining uncertainty (entropy over value distributions) with importance scores. Three agent prototypes differ in question strategy: Ag1 computes a heuristic score (importance × entropy) to pick the highest-uncertainty element, Ag2 prompts an LLM with the full belief graph plus conversation history, and Ag3 relies solely on conversation history guided by explicit question-asking principles (no redundancy, uncertainty reduction, relevance, ease of answering). After each user response, the agent merges the interaction into a comprehensive prompt and re-parses it to update the belief graph, then feeds the refined prompt to Imagen 3 for image generation. Evaluation uses LLM self-play where a simulated user conditioned on a ground truth image answers the agent's questions over 15 turns, with alignment measured via VQAScore, DINOv2 cosine similarity, Davidsonian Scene Graph, and negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth belief state.

## Results

Across all three datasets (COCO-Captions, ImageInWords, DesignBench), the best agent (Ag3) achieves at least 2× higher VQAScore than the single-turn T2I baseline: 0.775 vs. 0.298 on COCO, 0.662 vs. 0.371 on ImageInWords, and 0.955 vs. 0.353 on DesignBench. Ag3 also leads on DSG text-text similarity (0.900–0.949), DINO image-image similarity (0.588–0.692), and NLL (257–447 vs. 321–520 for T2I). In human evaluation, agentic systems are ranked as the best image over single-turn T2I in over 80% of cases for both content and aesthetics across ~550 prompt-image pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a standard single-turn T2I model (Imagen 3) that generates an image directly from the initial underspecified prompt without any interaction. All three multi-turn agents (Ag1, Ag2, Ag3) substantially outperform this baseline on every metric across all three datasets. The improvement is most dramatic on VQAScore: Ag3 achieves 0.775 vs. 0.298 on COCO (2.6×), 0.662 vs. 0.371 on ImageInWords (1.8×), and 0.955 vs. 0.353 on DesignBench (2.7×). Among the agents, Ag3 (principle-prompted, using only conversation history and question-asking principles without explicit belief graph input) consistently dominates Ag2 (belief-prompted) and Ag1 (heuristic-score) on DSG, VQAScore, and NLL, while Ag2 edges out Ag3 on human content ratings for COCO and ImageInWords. The paper attributes Ag3's advantage to its more open-ended questions that elicit richer user information, whereas Ag1's hardcoded heuristic sometimes produces redundant or low-information questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical AI concepts—symbolic world representations and belief states (McCarthy & Hayes, 1969; Nilsson, 1986)—as an explicit, editable uncertainty representation for T2I agents, combined with LLM-driven question generation. It extends prior multi-turn T2I work (DialogGen, DSG-GAN) by introducing a structured belief graph that makes the agent's internal state transparent and user-editable, and by framing clarification as an active information-acquisition problem guided by importance scores and entropy. The evaluation methodology draws on Davidsonian Scene Graph (Cho et al., 2023) and VQAScore (Lin et al., 2024) for alignment metrics, while the simulated-user game for scalable evaluation is novel to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The editable belief graph offers a generalizable interface for AI transparency and user control that could extend beyond T2I to other generative tasks, and the scalable simulated-user evaluation pipeline addresses a real gap in assessing interactive agents. Key open questions include whether images can be generated directly from belief graphs (bypassing prompt text), whether VLMs can be fine-tuned on multi-turn image-text trajectories, and whether the simplified single-distribution importance scores generalize across diverse user populations. The prototype relies heavily on LLM quality for belief parsing and the approach has not been tested at scale or with production T2I models beyond Imagen 3.
