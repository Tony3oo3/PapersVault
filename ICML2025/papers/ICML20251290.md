## Problem

Existing weight-based test-time adaptation (TTA) methods mitigate weight degradation by averaging current weights with pre-trained source weights, but they implicitly assume time-invariant covariance in the weight distribution. In dynamic environments with evolving distribution shifts, the explicit gradient noise from unsupervised learning changes over time, making this assumption invalid and causing either instability (excessive covariance) or reduced adaptability (insufficient covariance). The paper identifies this neglect of time-varying covariance as a fundamental gap and proposes a principled, SDE-derived solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test samples (RGB images at ImageNet resolution or audio streams from TEDLIUM3) drawn sequentially from a shifting distribution, together with pre-trained source model weights (ViT-base, Swin, or D2V backbones). Only normalization layers (batch norm or layer norm, <1% of total parameters) are updated online at each discrete time step, producing an enhanced weight vector (the posterior mean) that yields class predictions. The update is a single-pass, per-sample operation: each incoming sample generates one gradient, one covariance estimate, and one Kalman-filtering correction, with no additional forward passes or sampling required.

## Method

The paper introduces a Bayesian weight enhancement framework that models the enhanced weight as a Gaussian posterior, unifying existing weight-based TTA methods (ROID, CMF, SAR) as special cases under time-invariant covariance. The transition weight distribution is derived from an SDE approximation of SGD via the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation with a Gaussian density approximation, yielding a linear-Gaussian state-space model solved through recursive Bayesian (Kalman) filtering. The key novelty is the Steady-State Adaptation (SSA) mechanism, which dynamically scales the effective learning rate by a factor α_k = √(σ_λ²/(η²σ_k²)) computed from the running gradient variance, enforcing the condition that the posterior covariance remains at a steady state. This increases the step size under low gradient noise (enhancing adaptability) and decreases it under high noise (preserving stability), directly addressing the instability that plagues TTA at elevated learning rates. Under the steady-state condition, the Kalman gain converges to a fixed scalar, so SSA seamlessly inherits the time-invariant covariance behavior of existing weight-based methods while adding the dynamic regulation they lack.

## Results

On ImageNet-C covariate shift (severity level 5), CMF+SSA achieves the best average error rate of 42.2% (±0.16), outperforming CMF (43.5%), ROID (44.4%), and all non-weight-based baselines (TENT 53.3%, EATA 50.2%, DeYO 47.1%). In the extreme label shift scenario (γ=0.0), CMF+SSA reaches 13.1% average error rate versus CMF's 14.4% and ROID's 17.1%. The method adds only 4.3% relative computational overhead over CMF while delivering consistent gains across all corruption types, datasets (D109, Rendition, Sketch), and model architectures (ViT, Swin, D2V).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA consistently improves both weight-based baselines: CMF+SSA reduces average error by 1.3 points over CMF (42.2% vs. 43.5%) on covariate shift, 2.3 points on label shift γ=0.1 (35.9% vs. 38.2%), and 1.3 points on label shift γ=0.0 (13.1% vs. 14.4%). ROID+SSA similarly improves over ROID by 0.5 points on covariate shift (43.9% vs. 44.4%). Both SSA-enhanced methods substantially outperform all sample-based baselines (TENT, LAME, RoTTA, SAR, EATA, DeYO), with the largest gap over LAME (70.7%) and TENT (53.3%). In the periodic long-term stability test (15 rounds), CMF+SSA maintains a stable 40.5% average while DeYO degrades from 47.1% to 60.0% and EATA from 50.2% to 42.3%. On the D109 cross-domain dataset, CMF+SSA achieves 41.5% average error, beating CMF (42.3%), ROID (43.9%), and all other methods. SSA also generalizes across architectures (ViT, Swin, D2V) and to a real-world speech recognition task (TEDLIUM3, 11.5% WER vs. CMF's 11.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies existing weight-based TTA methods (ROID, CMF, SAR) under a Bayesian weight enhancement framework, showing they are special cases that assume time-invariant covariance in the weight distribution. It then departs from this line by identifying the neglected time-varying covariance as a key limitation and deriving the Steady-State Adaptation (SSA) algorithm from the SDE approximation of SGD (Li et al., 2019; Malladi et al., 2022) combined with Bayesian filtering (Kalman gain). The covariance-aware learning rate adjustment (α_k) is the novel mechanism, replacing the fixed step size with a dynamic one that maintains steady-state posterior variance, effectively bridging stochastic optimization theory and practical TTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical grounding (SDE + FPK equation + Bayesian filtering) for what was previously a heuristic practice (averaging source and current weights), and the steady-state covariance balancing offers a clear mechanism for the stability-adaptability tradeoff in TTA. Practical impact is moderate: SSA is a lightweight plug-in (~1–4% overhead) that consistently improves ROID and CMF across datasets, scenarios, and architectures, and notably stabilizes performance at learning rates up to 15× the default. Open questions include whether the linear Gaussian weight distribution assumption holds under extreme shifts, whether the small-learning-rate requirement of the SDE approximation limits applicability, and whether the approach extends beyond normalization-layer-only adaptation to full model fine-tuning.
