## Problem

Classic rectified flow matching regresses a single velocity vector at each data-time space point using MSE loss, which averages over the inherently multi-modal 'ground-truth' velocities produced by random source-target coupling. This averaging forces curved, non-intersecting flow trajectories that are harder to integrate numerically and yield less accurate data distributions. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling this velocity multi-modality can improve both integration efficiency and generation quality.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a noise sample x_0 drawn from a known source distribution (unit Gaussian) and a latent z sampled from N(0,I), then integrates the ODE dx_t/dt = v_θ(x_t, t, z) from t=0 to t=1 to produce a sample from the target distribution. For conditional generation (ImageNet, CIFAR-10), a class label is additionally provided. Training additionally consumes a data sample x_1 and a random time t to compute the interpolated location x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t·x_1 and the ground-truth velocity x_1 - x_0. Experiments span 1D/2D synthetic data, 28×28 MNIST (grayscale), 32×32 CIFAR-10, and 256×256 ImageNet.

## Method

The core idea is to introduce a continuous latent variable z that conditions the velocity vector field, turning the pointwise velocity model from a uni-modal Gaussian into a Gaussian mixture p(v|x_t,t) = ∫ p(v|x_t,t,z) p(z) dz, thereby capturing multi-modal velocity directions at the same data-time location. Training follows a VAE paradigm: a recognition network q_φ(z|x_0, x_1, x_t, t) (a deep encoder outputting μ_φ and σ_φ) approximates the posterior, and the objective is the sum of the standard rectified flow MSE reconstruction loss E_z[‖v_θ(x_t,t,z) − (x_1−x_0)‖²] and a KL divergence D_KL(q_φ(·|x_0,x_1,x_t,t) ‖ N(0,I)). At inference, q_φ is discarded and z is sampled directly from the prior; the latent is fused into the velocity network via adaptive group normalization or bottleneck sum. The paper proves (Appendix A) that the marginal data distribution is preserved by extending the continuity-equation argument of Liu et al. (2023) to the latent-conditioned velocity field, and demonstrates that the learned flows exhibit significantly lower curvature (Table 1) and better FID scores across all tested scales.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, V-SiT-XL achieves FID-50K of 14.6 (400K steps) and 10.6 (800K steps), improving over SiT-XL's 17.2 and 13.1 respectively. With classifier-free guidance (cfg=1.5), V-SiT-XL reaches FID 4.91 (400K) and 3.22 (800K) versus SiT-XL's 5.40 and 3.43. On CIFAR-10, the best V-RFM variant (Model 3) achieves FID 27.46 at 5 NFEs and 3.478 with the adaptive Dopri5 solver, outperforming the I-CFM baseline (35.49 and 3.659). On MNIST, V-RFM with a 2D latent achieves FID 13.4 at 5 NFEs versus 26.9 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-RFM consistently outperforms the classic rectified flow baselines (Lipman et al. OT-FM and Liu et al. I-CFM) across all datasets and NFE settings; on CIFAR-10, the best V-RFM variant achieves FID 3.478 (adaptive) versus 3.659 for I-CFM, and on MNIST it reaches FID 4.6 at 200 NFEs versus 6.0 for the baseline. Against Tong et al.'s I-CFM (minibatch optimal transport), V-RFM wins at low NFEs (e.g., 27.46 vs. 35.49 at 5 NFEs on CIFAR-10) and matches or slightly exceeds at high NFEs. On ImageNet, V-SiT-XL improves FID by 2.6 points over SiT-XL at 400K steps (14.6 vs. 17.2) and by 2.5 points at 800K steps (10.6 vs. 13.1), with similar gains under classifier-free guidance. Against consistency flow matching (Yang et al.), V-RFM is superior at NFE ≥ 5 on CIFAR-10 and NFE ≥ 2 on MNIST, though consistency FM achieves the best FID at exactly 2 NFEs on MNIST (15.76 vs. 61.0 for V-RFM). The paper positions consistency models as orthogonal to its variational approach, noting that combining them is future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends rectified flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) by introducing a variational latent variable to model multi-modal velocity vector fields, directly borrowing the encoder-prior-KL structure from variational auto-encoders (Kingma & Welling, 2014). It departs from prior flow-matching improvements that target trajectory straightness (ReFlow, consistency models) by instead addressing the root cause of curved trajectories: the MSE loss's inability to represent ambiguous ground-truth velocities at the same data-time point. The variational formulation is positioned as orthogonal to consistency modeling and ReFlow, and distinct from two-stage approaches like diffusion autoencoders or latent-space score matching, which do not directly model velocity ambiguity in the data-time space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-modal velocity issue is fundamental to rectified flow matching and affects all downstream ODE integration quality; showing that a simple variational extension consistently improves FID across 1D synthetic data, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet 256×256 (e.g., 14.6 vs. 17.2 FID for SiT-XL at 400K steps) suggests the averaging problem is more impactful than previously appreciated. The latent variable also provides a natural controllability mechanism without additional conditioning signals. Open questions include combining variational flow matching with consistency models or distillation, scaling to larger latent spaces, and whether the approach transfers to other flow-matching variants beyond rectified flow.
