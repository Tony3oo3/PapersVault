## Problem

Post-hoc attribution methods are highly non-robust: imperceptible input perturbations can drastically alter attribution maps while leaving predictions unchanged, undermining their trustworthiness in safety-critical domains. Prior certification work provides only image-level similarity bounds that are too coarse, lack visual interpretability, and can be disproportionately affected by a small non-robust pixel subset. No existing method certifies individual pixels in attribution maps, leaving a gap in fine-grained, interpretable robustness guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and any black-box attribution method (no gradient access required) as input, along with a sparsification parameter K ∈ [50,100] defining the fraction of pixels considered important. It produces a per-pixel certified attribution map where each pixel is labeled '1' (robustly in top K%), '0' (robustly in bottom (100−K)%), or '⊘' (abstain), together with a certified ℓ₂ radius R within which the certified label is guaranteed to hold. The output is thus a binary segmentation-like map with a per-pixel robustness guarantee rather than a single image-level score.

## Method

The core insight is to reframe attribution as a segmentation task by sparsifying the continuous attribution map into a binary pixel-importance classification (top K% → 1, rest → 0), then applying Randomized Smoothing for segmentation (Fischer et al., 2021) to the resulting sparsified attribution function. For each pixel, the smoothed sparsified attribution estimates the probability that the pixel remains in the top K% (or bottom (100−K)%) under Gaussian noise ε ~ N(0, σ²I); if this probability exceeds a threshold τ, the pixel is certified as '1' or '0', otherwise it abstains. The certified radius R = σΦ⁻¹(τ) guarantees that the certified label is invariant to all ℓ₂ perturbations within that ball, with confidence 1−α via Monte-Carlo sampling (n=100 samples by default). This formulation is mathematically equivalent to smoothed segmentation, enabling direct application of existing certification theorems without modifying the underlying attribution method.

## Results

The paper evaluates 12 attribution methods on 5 ImageNet models (ResNet-18, W-ResNet-50-2, ResNet-152, VGG-11, ViT-B/16) using three proposed metrics: %certified (pixel-level robustness), Certified GridPG (robust localization), and a deletion-based faithfulness score, at a default certified radius R=0.10 with σ=0.15, τ=0.75, and n=100 samples. RISE achieves the highest %certified at the input layer across all models and radii, while LRP is the most robust backpropagation method. LRP, RISE, and Occlusion reach near-perfect Certified GridPG at K=10%, and LRP and RISE produce the steepest confidence drops in the deletion-based faithfulness analysis, confirming their certified pixels are truly class-relevant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 12 compared attribution methods, RISE dominates in certified robustness (%certified) at the input layer across all 5 models and all certified radii, outperforming LRP (the best backpropagation method) and all activation-based methods. LRP leads among backpropagation methods in both %certified and Certified GridPG at the input layer, while Grad, GB, and IntGrad show near-zero certification rates at the input due to sensitivity to input magnitude. At the final layer, Grad and GB exhibit high %certified but near-random Certified GridPG because they produce constant grid-like patterns from max-pooling, demonstrating that robustness does not guarantee localization. Activation-based methods (Grad-CAM, CAM, Grad-CAM++, Ablation-CAM, Layer-CAM) perform worse than random on input-layer localization but improve at the final layer, with CAM and Grad-CAM achieving near-perfect Certified GridPG at K=10%. RISE and LRP consistently strike the best tradeoff across all three metrics (robustness, localization, faithfulness) on both CNN and ViT architectures, while Occlusion is competitive at the final layer. On ViT-B/16 specifically, RISE maintains high robustness across both input and final layers where most other methods degrade, making it the most architecture-agnostic method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certified attribution line of work (Levine et al. 2019, Liu et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2022, Wang & Kong 2023/2024), which provides only image-level similarity bounds, by reframing sparsified attribution maps as segmentation outputs and applying Randomized Smoothing for segmentation (Fischer et al. 2021; Anani et al. 2024) to obtain per-pixel robustness guarantees. The key conceptual departure is the sparsification step that binarizes continuous attribution scores into top-K% / rest classes, making the high-dimensional attribution output amenable to the segmentation certification machinery. This transforms a previously intractable pixel-wise certification problem into a direct application of an existing theoretical framework, while the Monte-Carlo estimation procedure is inherited unchanged from the segmentation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first visually interpretable, per-pixel robustness certificates for any black-box attribution method, which is critical for safety-critical deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving) where a single misleading pixel attribution can have real consequences. However, the approach inherits limitations from randomized smoothing: certification is restricted to ℓ₂ balls, the sparsification step discards absolute attribution magnitudes, and the Monte-Carlo sampling is computationally expensive (100 noisy evaluations per image). Open questions include whether abstention rates can be reduced without sacrificing certified radius, whether the framework extends to other perturbation norms or to video/multi-modal settings, and whether the pixel-level guarantees compose meaningfully when multiple attribution methods are used in ensemble or disagreement-based pipelines.
