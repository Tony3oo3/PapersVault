## Problem

Existing graph anomaly detection methods focus exclusively on structural and attribute-level irregularities while ignoring geometric anomalies revealed through deviations in graph curvature. They also assume Euclidean representations, fail under heterophily, and do not leverage the rich geometric insights from Riemannian manifold learning, leaving curvature-driven anomalies undetected.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d_X}) and produces per-node anomaly scores computed as a weighted sum of three reconstruction errors (curvature, adjacency, and feature). The Ollivier-Ricci curvature is precomputed with δ=0.5 using a linear-time combinatorial approximation, and the product manifold is configured with total dimension d_P=48 and a task-specific signature (e.g., H^8×S^8×E^32) determined heuristically from the ORC distribution. The model also outputs the full reconstructed curvature, adjacency, and feature matrices, enabling interpretable attribution of each anomaly to geometric vs. structural vs. attribute causes.

## Method

CurvGAD decomposes anomaly detection into two parallel autoencoder pipelines sharing a mixed-curvature Chebyshev filter-bank encoder built on a product manifold (hyperbolic × spherical × Euclidean) using the κ-stereographic model for unified Riemannian operations. The curvature-equivariant pipeline reconstructs the Ollivier-Ricci curvature matrix via a Gaussian kernel decoder operating on geodesic distances between node embeddings, flagging geometric anomalies as high curvature reconstruction loss. The curvature-invariant pipeline first deforms the graph under discrete Ollivier-Ricci flow to uniformize edge curvatures, then reconstructs adjacency and features in a Euclidean manifold, isolating non-geometric anomalies. The filter bank with F=8 Chebyshev-order filters captures both low-pass (homophilic) and high-pass (heterophilic) spectral bands, and learnable attention weights aggregate across filters and manifold components. Training combines three Frobenius-norm reconstruction losses with a supervised cross-entropy classification loss, with learnable trade-off parameters balancing the contributions.

## Results

CurvGAD achieves the highest AUROC across all 10 node-level graph anomaly detection datasets, with gains of 1.05%–6.50% over the second-best method per dataset. The largest improvements appear on DGraph-Fin (+6.50% over AnomalyDAE), Elliptic (+4.79% over ADAGAD), and Tolokers (+4.61% over QGCN), all heterophilic or structurally complex networks. On homophilic datasets like Weibo and T-Finance, gains are smaller (1.05% and 2.23% respectively), consistent with curvature being less discriminative in those settings. The average absolute improvement over the strongest GAD baselines (ADAGAD, GADNR) is 4.25–5.69 percentage points in AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurvGAD outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset, with the most substantial margins on heterophilic and structurally complex graphs. Against the strongest dedicated GAD baselines, ADAGAD (4.25% avg. gain) and GADNR (5.69% avg. gain), CurvGAD's advantage is most pronounced on Elliptic (+4.12% over ADAGAD) and DGraph (+7.78% over GADNR), where curvature-driven anomalies dominate. Among Riemannian GNNs, κGCN and QGCN are the closest competitors on homophilic datasets (e.g., κGCN scores 97.87 on Weibo vs. CurvGAD's 99.04), but they degrade sharply on heterophilic graphs like Tolokers (79.64/81.46 vs. 85.22) and T-Social (85.65/73.32 vs. 89.23), confirming that single-curvature or pseudo-Riemannian spaces are insufficient. Reconstruction-based GAD methods (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE) lag significantly behind both GNN-based GAD models and CurvGAD, with DOMINANT showing the weakest overall performance (13.14% avg. gap), indicating that pure adjacency/feature reconstruction without geometric awareness is inadequate. Spectral methods (BernNet, ChebyNet) and conventional GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) are consistently outperformed by 5–13% on average, with the largest gaps on heterophilic datasets where low-pass filtering assumptions break down. HGCN and HGAT, despite leveraging hyperbolic geometry, underperform CurvGAD by 5–10% on average, as their constant-curvature constraint limits adaptability to mixed-geometry topologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

CurvGAD sits at the intersection of two previously disconnected research threads: (1) mixed-curvature Riemannian GNNs (Gu et al. 2019, κGCN, QGCN) that embed graphs in product manifolds for representation learning, and (2) reconstruction-based graph anomaly detection (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE) that flags nodes with high reconstruction error. The paper departs from both: it repurposes the κ-stereographic product manifold not merely as a representation space but as the *signal* for anomaly detection (reconstructing Ollivier-Ricci curvature itself), and it introduces a curvature-invariant pipeline via discrete Ricci flow that has no direct analogue in prior GAD work. The Chebyshev filter bank generalizes spectral GNNs (ChebyNet, BernNet) to mixed-curvature spaces, while the linear-time ORC approximation (Jost & Liu 2014) makes the geometric quantities tractable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing 'curvature-based geometric anomalies' as a distinct detection target, the paper opens a new axis of interpretability in GAD: anomalies can now be attributed to geometric irregularity (e.g., bottleneck hubs, cascade branching) rather than only to structural or attribute deviation. The dual-pipeline decomposition (equivariant vs. invariant) is a clean architectural principle that could transfer to other geometry-aware learning tasks. Open questions include: whether the heuristic manifold-signature selection (Algorithm 2) generalizes beyond the 10 tested datasets, how robust curvature-based anomaly scores are to adversarial edge perturbations, and whether the supervised classification loss can be fully replaced in large-scale unsupervised deployments without the modest 1.5% average drop becoming more pronounced.
