## Problem

Existing Multimodal Information Bottleneck (MIB) methods set regularization weights in an ad hoc manner, providing no theoretical guarantee that the optimal MIB (containing all task-relevant information while excluding all superfluous information) is actually achievable. Additionally, these methods assign fixed per-modality regularization weights, which can inadvertently exclude crucial but minor task-relevant information from weaker modalities when task-relevant information is imbalanced across modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multimodal inputs (e.g., audio+visual frames for emotion recognition, visual+acoustic+text for sentiment analysis, gene expression matrices+histology images for tissue detection) and produces a single compact MIB embedding ξ ∈ R^k. During training, each modality is first encoded into a sufficient representation z_i, then passed through a VAE to produce a stochastic bottleneck variable ζ_i before cross-attention fusion. At inference, the TRBs are disabled and only the trained encoders and OMF block generate the MIB embedding, which is then fed to a task-specific head for classification, regression, or SVDD-based anomaly scoring.

## Method

OMIB employs a two-phase training procedure: a warm-up phase where task relevance branches (TRBs) extract sufficient per-modality representations z_i by maximizing prediction accuracy under injected Gaussian noise, followed by a main training phase where an Optimal Multimodal Fusion (OMF) block fuses stochastic VAE-generated representations ζ_i via a Cross-Attention Network into the MIB embedding ξ. The key theoretical contribution is a derived upper bound M_u = 1/((1+r)(H(v₁)+H(v₂)−I(v₁;v₂))) on the redundancy regularization parameter β, which guarantees that upon convergence the MIB contains exactly the task-relevant information set {a₀, a₁, a₂} and no superfluous information. To handle imbalanced task-relevant information, a dynamic per-modality weight r is computed in closed form during training as r = 1 − tanh(ln(I(y;v₂|ξ)/I(y;v₁|ξ))), automatically reducing regularization on modalities with more remaining task-relevant information. The entire objective is optimized via a variational upper bound (Proposition 5.1) using the reparameterization trick for efficient gradient-based training.

## Results

On CREMA-D emotion recognition, OMIB achieves 63.6% accuracy, outperforming the best non-MIB baseline (Concat, 53.2%) by 8.9% and the best MIB baseline (E-MIB, 61.4%) by 3.6%. On CMU-MOSI sentiment analysis, OMIB leads all baselines across every metric: Acc7=48.6, Acc2=86.9, F1=87.1, MAE=0.709, and Corr=0.802. On the 10x-hBC anomalous tissue detection task, OMIB achieves a mean AUC of 0.754 and F1 of 0.737, surpassing the best MIB baseline (MMIB-Zhang) by 11.4% in AUC and 3.8% in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMIB consistently outperforms all seven MIB-based baselines (E-MIB, L-MIB, C-MIB, DMIB, MMIB-Cui, MMIB-Zhang, deep IB) and three non-MIB baselines (Concat, BiGated, MISA) across all three downstream tasks. On CREMA-D, OMIB (63.6%) beats the strongest MIB method E-MIB (61.4%) by 2.2 points and the strongest non-MIB method BiGated (58.4%) by 5.2 points. On CMU-MOSI, OMIB ties E-MIB on Acc7 (48.6) but leads on Acc2 (86.9 vs 85.3), F1 (87.1 vs 85.3), MAE (0.709 vs 0.711), and Corr (0.802 vs 0.798), with the largest gap over MMIB-Zhang (46.3 Acc7) at 2.3 points. On 10x-hBC anomaly detection, OMIB's mean AUC of 0.754 exceeds the next-best MMIB-Zhang (0.677) by 7.7 points, and its mean F1 of 0.737 surpasses DMIB (0.710) by 2.7 points. The ablation study confirms that each component (warm-up training, cross-attention fusion, OMF block, and dynamic weight r) contributes meaningfully, with removing the OMF block causing the largest drop (6.9% accuracy loss on CREMA-D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Multimodal Information Bottleneck (MIB) line of work initiated by Wang et al. (2019), Tian et al. (2021), Wan et al. (2021), and Mai et al. (2023), which all follow the paradigm of maximizing task-relevant mutual information while minimizing input-encoding mutual information via a regularization weight. OMIB departs from these methods in two key ways: it derives a theoretical upper bound on the regularization parameter β that guarantees achievability of the optimal MIB (a property no prior MIB method formally establishes), and it introduces a dynamically computed per-modality weight r that adapts to imbalanced task-relevant information across modalities. Architecturally, it combines a VAE-based variational approximation (following Alemi et al., 2017) with a Cross-Attention Network for fusion, and a warm-up phase with stochastic noise injection to pre-train modality-specific encoders for sufficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that optimal MIB is achievable within a computable β bound (via MINE estimation) addresses a fundamental gap in prior MIB methods that relied on ad hoc hyperparameter tuning, giving practitioners a principled starting point. The dynamic per-modality regularization directly tackles the practical problem of modality imbalance, where weak modalities carrying small but critical task-relevant information are otherwise suppressed. However, the theoretical analysis relies on a structured information decomposition (Assumption 5.3) that may not hold in real-world data, and the MINE-based estimation of entropy and mutual information for the β bound introduces additional estimation noise. Open questions include whether the guarantees extend to high-dimensional continuous data without the discrete information-set assumptions, how the method scales beyond three modalities in practice, and whether the warm-up phase's noise-injection strategy generalizes to modalities with very different signal-to-noise ratios.
