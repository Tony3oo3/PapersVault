## Problem

Existing unpaired point cloud completion methods rely on heuristic techniques (GAN-based latent mapping, cycle consistency, GAN inversion) without a rigorous theoretical formulation of the completion task. Moreover, real-world unpaired scenarios inherently exhibit class imbalance between incomplete and complete point cloud distributions, to which standard optimal transport is known to be vulnerable. This paper addresses both gaps by providing a principled OT-based formulation and demonstrating that the Unbalanced OT framework naturally handles class imbalance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single incomplete (partial) point cloud as input and produces a complete point cloud as output. During training, the source distribution is a 50/50 mixture of incomplete and complete point clouds (the 'mixture trick'), while the target is the complete point cloud distribution alone; at inference, only the incomplete point cloud is fed to the generator. The generator T_θ and potential v_φ share the USSPA backbone architecture, with the potential's final sigmoid layer removed to allow unbounded real-valued outputs.

## Method

UOT-UPC formulates unpaired point cloud completion as learning the Unbalanced Optimal Transport Map from the incomplete distribution μ to the complete distribution ν, using the semi-dual UOT formulation with InfoCD (a contrastive Chamfer distance) as the cost function. Training follows an adversarial max-min procedure: the generator T_θ minimizes c(x, T_θ(x)) − v_φ(T_θ(x)) plus a density loss, while the potential v_φ minimizes the softplus-regularized dual objective over both source and target samples. The UOT framework replaces exact marginal matching with f-divergence penalties (softplus convex conjugates), allowing the transport plan to softly rescale marginals and thereby remain robust to class imbalance between the two datasets. The paper provides a systematic cost-function analysis showing InfoCD outperforms l2, Chamfer distance, and forward Chamfer distance, with the theoretical ranking closely matching empirical completion quality.

## Results

On the USSPA benchmark (single-category), UOT-UPC achieves an average L1 Chamfer distance of 7.60×10⁻², outperforming the best prior unpaired method USSPA (8.56) and approaching the best paired method TopNet (7.07). In the multi-category setting, UOT-UPC attains the best F-score at 0.1% (19.25) and ties USSPA on cd^l1 (8.96). On the PCN synthetic dataset, UOT-UPC achieves an average cd^l1 of 7.87×10⁻², substantially outperforming USSPA (12.63) and all other unpaired baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOT-UPC outperforms all four unpaired baselines across both USSPA and PCN benchmarks. On USSPA single-category, it beats USSPA by ~11% in average cd^l1 (7.60 vs. 8.56), Cycle4 by ~34% (7.60 vs. 11.53), Unpaired by ~28% (7.60 vs. 10.47), and ShapeInv by ~64% (7.60 vs. 21.39). On PCN, the margin over USSPA widens to ~38% (7.87 vs. 12.63). Among paired methods, UOT-UPC (7.60) is competitive with Disp3D (7.78) and close to TopNet (7.07) on USSPA, despite not using any paired supervision. In the multi-category USSPA setting, UOT-UPC ties USSPA on cd^l1 (8.96) but achieves the best F-score at 0.1% (19.25 vs. 16.88), while its F-score at 1% (71.52) is slightly below USSPA (72.31). The class imbalance robustness experiments (Table 6) further show UOT-UPC maintaining stable performance (6.75–6.94) across imbalance ratios, while OT-UPC degrades severely (23.7–29.8) and USSPA shows higher variance (6.97–8.08).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the UOTM framework (Choi et al., 2023), originally developed for generative modeling via the semi-dual formulation of unbalanced optimal transport, to the task of unpaired point cloud completion. It departs from prior unpaired completion methods (USSPA, Cycle4, ShapeInv, Unpaired), which rely on heuristic GAN-based or cycle-consistency techniques without a rigorous transport-theoretic formulation, by casting the problem as learning a UOT Map with a carefully chosen cost function (InfoCD). The work also extends the Neural OT literature, which has predominantly used quadratic l2 costs, by providing a systematic analysis of point-cloud-specific cost functions and demonstrating their alignment with completion quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first theoretical formulation of unpaired point cloud completion as an optimal transport problem provides a principled foundation that prior heuristic methods lacked, and the demonstrated robustness to class imbalance (stable performance across varying ratios where standard OT degrades by 3–4×) addresses a practical pain point in real-world unpaired settings. Limitations include the adversarial training instability inherited from the GAN-like optimization, the restricted exploration of cost functions (only four candidates tested), and the modest overall SOTA margin over USSPA. Open questions include whether learnable or neural cost functions could further improve performance, and whether the UOT formulation generalizes to other unpaired 3D reconstruction tasks beyond completion.
