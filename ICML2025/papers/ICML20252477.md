## Problem

Implicit feedback recommendation suffers from missing negative feedback: unlabeled interactions may contain hidden positives, so treating them as negatives introduces bias. Existing unbiased estimators (RMF, UBPR, UPL) rely on propensity scores that are difficult to estimate accurately, are sensitive to small propensities, and can produce non-negative risk violations that destabilize training. The paper asks whether one can construct a theoretically unbiased recommendation loss that requires no negative samples and no propensity score estimation.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only positive user-item pairs and unlabeled user-item pairs (no explicit negatives), partitioned into mini-batches of positive samples x_p and unlabeled samples x_u. A matrix factorization backbone with user and item embedding look-up tables U and V produces interaction scores via dot product followed by sigmoid. The method additionally outputs an automatically estimated class prior κ̂_p = 1/w*, where w* is the mass weight minimizing the proximal transport cost, eliminating the need for manual prior specification. Negative feedback is used only at evaluation time for computing NDCG@k and Recall@k.

## Method

WeaklyRec reframes implicit feedback recommendation as a positive-unlabeled (PU) learning problem, deriving a surrogate risk R_weak(g) = κ_p E_{p_p}[ℓ̃(g(x))] + E_{p_u}[ℓ(g(x),−1)] that is provably unbiased for the ideal risk without any negative samples or propensity scores. To estimate the required class prior κ_p, the paper introduces Progressive Proximal Transport (PPT): it solves a family of proximal transport (PT) problems parameterized by a mass weight w ≥ 1, where PT relaxes the standard Kantorovich mass-preservation constraint to allow only a fraction 1/w of unlabeled samples to be matched to positive samples. As w decreases from a large initial value, the transport cost first drops (matching only concealed positives) then rises sharply when negative samples are forced into the matching, and the minimum-cost w* yields κ̂_p = 1/w*. The PT problem is reformulated as an augmented Kantorovich problem, enabling efficient solution via Sinkhorn or network-simplex solvers. The empirical risk enforces per-term non-negativity on ℓ̃ to prevent overfitting, and with logistic loss the surrogate risk is convex in the MF parameters, guaranteeing convergence under SGD.

## Results

On Yahoo! R3, WeaklyRec achieves NDCG@5 of 0.843, outperforming the best baseline DCF (0.826) and UPL (0.829). On Coat, it reaches NDCG@5 of 0.589 versus DCF's 0.566 and UPL's 0.539. On KuaiRec, WeaklyRec attains NDCG@5 of 0.488, surpassing the next-best UIDR (0.435) and CDR (0.412). In terms of Recall@5, WeaklyRec leads on all three datasets: 0.354 (Yahoo! R3), 0.425 (Coat), and 0.296 (KuaiRec), with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over the best baselines in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeaklyRec outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets on NDCG metrics. On Yahoo! R3, it beats the strongest baselines DCF (0.826) and UPL (0.829) by 1.7–2.1 points on NDCG@5, and leads on Recall@5 by 1.5–1.8 points over DCF (0.339) and UPL (0.346). On Coat, the margin is larger: WeaklyRec's NDCG@5 of 0.589 exceeds DCF (0.566) by 2.3 points and UPL (0.539) by 5.0 points; its Recall@5 of 0.425 surpasses DCF (0.386) by 3.9 points. On KuaiRec, WeaklyRec (NDCG@5 = 0.488) leads the next-best UIDR (0.435) by 5.3 points and DCF (0.466) by 2.2 points, while its Recall@5 of 0.296 exceeds DCF (0.288) and CDR (0.207). The propensity-weighting baselines (RMF, UBPR, CUBPR, SDR, CDR, UIDR) consistently underperform the heuristic DCF and the PU-based UPL, confirming the paper's argument that inaccurate propensity estimation is a key bottleneck. The ORACLE baseline, trained with true labels, is substantially outperformed by WeaklyRec on all datasets, demonstrating that the weakly supervised framework effectively compensates for the absence of negative feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeaklyRec reframes implicit feedback recommendation as a positive-unlabeled (PU) learning problem, building directly on the uPU risk estimator (du Plessis et al., 2014) and the broader weakly supervised learning framework (Sugiyama et al., 2022). It departs from the dominant propensity-score-based line of work (RMF, UBPR, CUBPR, UPL) by eliminating the need for propensity estimation entirely, instead relying on a class prior that is estimated via a novel Progressive Proximal Transport (PPT) method. The PPT method itself is grounded in the augmented Kantorovich problem formulation (Xu et al., 2020), repurposing optimal transport solvers (Sinkhorn, network simplex) for a task—class prior estimation in PU learning—that previously relied on f-divergence minimization with known positive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on propensity scores, WeaklyRec sidesteps the three practical failures of IPW-based methods (inaccurate propensity estimation, sensitivity to small propensities, and non-negativity violations) that have limited their real-world adoption. The PPT-based class prior estimation is a genuinely novel contribution to PU learning, offering a geometrically interpretable alternative to divergence-based estimators. However, the framework is limited to ID-based recommendation without user profiles, and the scalability of the LP-based PT solver to very large industrial-scale datasets remains an open question. The authors also note that harmonizing explicit and implicit feedback within this framework is a promising but unexplored direction.
