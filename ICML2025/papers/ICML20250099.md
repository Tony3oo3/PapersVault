## Problem

Existing math benchmarks for LLMs rely on fixed ground-truth answers that are increasingly saturated and cannot evaluate proof-based reasoning. Constructive proofs—where one must explicitly build a mathematical object satisfying given constraints—are common in olympiad mathematics and real applications, yet no public benchmark targets them with automated, objective verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem instance is a concretized natural-language statement (e.g., 'Find a 10×10 matrix of rank ≤ 3 with zero diagonal and positive off-diagonal entries') paired with formatting instructions that require the model to emit a complete explicit construction inside a \boxed{} environment—never a description or formula. The output is parsed by a specialized parser handling matrices, lists, and LaTeX, and checked by a hidden Python verifier that returns a boolean plus a diagnostic feedback string; models receive up to two parser-feedback rounds to fix formatting before final verification.

## Method

MATHCONSTRUCT encodes each of its 126 olympiad-sourced problems as a tuple (P, θ, V_θ): a symbolic statement P, concrete parameters θ, and an automated verification function V_θ, all in a single Python file. The key mechanism enabling robustness evaluation is systematic problem variation—each problem is instantiated with multiple parameter values (475 total instances), and a stricter 'robust accuracy' metric requires the model to solve every variation, mirroring how a human who knows the general construction solves all instantiations. Fourteen state-of-the-art LLMs are evaluated under both chain-of-thought and code-execution settings, with the best model (O3-MINI) reaching only 53.8% average and 34.9% robust accuracy, confirming that constructing valid mathematical objects remains a hard open problem for current models.

## Results

On MATHCONSTRUCT (475 problem instances from 126 constructive proof problems), the best model O3-MINI achieves 53.77% average accuracy and 34.92% robust accuracy, while the second-best O1 reaches 41.34% and 23.02% respectively. Among non-reasoning models, Gemini FLASH leads with only 11.57% average accuracy and 3.17% robust accuracy. Robust accuracy (requiring all variations to be solved) is consistently roughly half of average accuracy across all models, highlighting that models fail to generalize to parameter changes even when they solve the base problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 models across four provider families on MATHCONSTRUCT. OpenAI's reasoning models dominate: o3-mini leads at 53.77% avg accuracy, followed by o1 (41.34%) and o1-mini (25.46%), with o1 incurring a cost of $434—three times the combined cost of all other models. Google's Gemini FLASH-THINKING (27.05%) is competitive with o1-mini but trails o1 by 14 percentage points, while non-thinking Gemini FLASH (11.57%) is the best non-reasoning model. DeepSeek's R1-LLaMA-70B (19.51%) sits in the mid-range, outperforming all non-reasoning models but well behind the top reasoning models. All non-reasoning models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet/Haiku, LLaMA, Qwen) cluster below 6.35% average accuracy, confirming that constructive proof problems require explicit reasoning capabilities rather than pattern matching or memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATHCONSTRUCT carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: olympiad-level math benchmarks (MATH, OlympiadBench, Omni-MATH) that rely on fixed-answer comparison, and formal theorem-proving benchmarks (miniF2F, FIMO, PutnamBench) that require machine-checkable proofs in Lean. The paper identifies constructive proofs as a previously unexploited middle ground—hard enough to challenge LLMs, yet verifiable by simple programmatic checkers—drawing on the symbolic-variation idea from GSM-Symbolic and UTMath but applying it to the construction of complex mathematical objects (matrices, colorings, sequences) rather than numerical answers or code. It also connects to the broader ML-for-constructions literature (Wagner 2021, Bello et al. 2017) by repurposing the notion of 'find an object satisfying constraints' as an evaluation task rather than a generation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With existing math benchmarks saturated (state-of-the-art models solve ~87% of AIME 2024), MATHCONSTRUCT provides a future-proof evaluation axis: the best model (O3-MINI) reaches only 54% average accuracy and 35% robust accuracy, and the symbolic-variation design means the benchmark resists memorization and can be extended indefinitely. The robust-accuracy metric exposes a critical gap—models that solve one parameterization often fail on another—suggesting current LLMs perform pattern matching on specific instances rather than deriving general constructive procedures. Open questions include whether the gap will close with stronger reasoning models, whether the 126-problem scale is sufficient for reliable ranking, and whether the programmatic-verifiability constraint (which excludes geometry and many analysis problems) limits the benchmark's coverage of mathematical reasoning.
