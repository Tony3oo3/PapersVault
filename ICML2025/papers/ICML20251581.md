## Problem

LVLMs built on LLM backbones suffer from two inherited architectural flaws: causal self-attention produces a bimodal attention distribution that progressively neglects middle visual content as context lengthens (the 'lost in the middle' effect), and standard RoPE positional encoding causes remote decay and 2D spatial collapse when processing the expanded token sequences from dynamic high-resolution (DHR) image tiling. Existing single-path architectures (fully autoregressive LVLM-S or cross-attention LVLM-X) cannot simultaneously preserve visual grounding and long-context reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images processed via Dynamic High Resolution (split into multiple tiles plus a single thumbnail, yielding ~2k tokens per image when DHR is active) alongside text prompts, and generates free-form text responses. The image encoder (InternViT-300M) produces patch tokens that are projected into the LLM's embedding space (InternLM-1.8B backbone), with both the context and memory paths sharing identical image representations to ensure feature consistency.

## Method

CoMemo introduces a dual-stream architecture where image tokens are simultaneously fed into (1) a context path—concatenated with text tokens for standard autoregressive decoding—and (2) a memory path—processed through gated cross-attention mixin layers interleaved at a 1:4 ratio with transformer blocks, enabling text-conditioned retrieval of visual information independent of sequence position. To address positional encoding limitations under DHR, RoPE-DHR maps each tile patch's 2D coordinates to its corresponding thumbnail patch index, compressing the position space and preserving geometric relationships while mitigating remote decay. A three-stage training procedure (tune projector + mixin layers → freeze gate parameters to prevent cross-attention over-reliance → full fine-tuning) balances the two pathways, and position-aware cross-attention applies RoPE bidirectionally between sequence positions and visual token indices.

## Results

Under identical training data and 2B model size, CoMemo achieves 17.2%, 7.0%, and 5.6% relative improvement over the LVLM-S baseline on Caption, Long-Generation, and Long-Context tasks respectively. On the headline benchmarks, CoMemo scores 98.6 CIDEr on COCO captioning (vs. 79.1 for LVLM-S), 66.9 on LLaVABench long-generation (vs. 62.9), 34.2 on MM-NIAH-M long-context (vs. 26.7), and 54.6 on MileBench (vs. 52.1). CoMemo also leads on Math (50.0 MathVista, 17.0 MathVision) and General VQA (74.2 MMBench, 1904 MME, 36.0 MMVP), while trailing slightly on OCR tasks (73.6 ChartQA vs. 75.6, 72.6 TextVQA vs. 74.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InternVL-2 (2B, the direct LVLM-S equivalent trained on identical data), CoMemo wins on 15 of 19 benchmarks, with the largest gains in Caption (+19.5 CIDEr on COCO), Long-Context MM-NIAH-M (+7.5), and MMVP (+4.7), while losing marginally on AI2D (−0.1), ChartQA (−2.0), and TextVQA (−1.6). Compared to Qwen2-VL (2B), CoMemo trails on COCO captioning (98.6 vs. 108.2) and MME (1904 vs. 2326.8) but leads on MM-NIAH-M (34.2 vs. not reported) and MileBench (54.6 vs. not reported), reflecting its design focus on long-context robustness. Against InternVL-2.5 (2B), CoMemo achieves comparable or better scores on most tasks (e.g., 98.6 vs. 114 COCO is a loss, but 66.9 vs. not reported on LLaVABench, 54.6 vs. 50.1 MileBench). Versus the larger mPlug-owl3 (8B, LVLM-X) and LLaMA3.2-V (11B), CoMemo at 2B matches or exceeds them on Long-Generation (66.9 vs. 50.8/39.8 LLaVABench) and Multi-Image (43.5 vs. 50.3/39.8 BLINK), demonstrating that the dual-path architecture compensates for the smaller parameter budget. MiniCPM-V-2 (2.8B) is outperformed by CoMemo on Caption, Long-Generation, and Long-Context, confirming that the architectural advantage is not solely a function of model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMemo unifies two previously separate LVLM architectural paradigms: the LLaVA-style fully autoregressive path (image tokens concatenated with text) and the Flamingo-style cross-attention mixin layer path, into a single dual-stream model. It builds directly on InternVL2's Dynamic High Resolution (DHR) tiling strategy and pixel-shuffle technique, but departs from prior work by introducing RoPE-DHR—a thumbnail-anchored positional encoding that compresses the 1D position space of DHR tiles to preserve 2D spatial structure and mitigate remote decay. The paper explicitly contrasts with NVLM-H, which naively combined both paths without the balancing mechanisms (dual-path DHR allocation, three-stage training with gate freezing) that CoMemo identifies as necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete architectural remedy for the 'lost in the middle' problem in LVLMs, which is a well-documented but under-addressed limitation for long-context and multi-image scenarios. The three-stage training strategy and the finding that pathway balance is locked in during pretraining (Finding 4) offer practical guidance for anyone building dual-path multimodal models. However, OCR performance slightly degrades relative to LVLM-S, inference is ~20% slower, and the balancing challenge remains sensitive to pretraining duration—open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, whether the 1:4 mixin ratio is optimal, and whether the three-stage protocol is still necessary at 7B+ scale where the paper shows compressed encoding hurts OCR more.
