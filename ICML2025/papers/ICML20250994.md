## Problem

While sample-efficient exploration strategies for online RLHF have been studied, the potential of leveraging existing imperfect reward models—available from open-source models, LLM evaluators, or rule-based heuristics—to accelerate learning with fewer human annotations remains largely unexplored. The paper addresses how to transfer knowledge from such partially-aligned source rewards when their quality relative to true human preferences is unknown a priori.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250994/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a contextual bandit framework (prompts as states, responses as actions) and takes W source reward models of unknown quality, a reference LLM policy, and sequential human preference labels under the Bradley-Terry model as input. It outputs a fine-tuned LLM policy converging to the KL-regularized optimal policy, with adaptive transfer policy selection at each step. The empirical version (Alg. 3) is modular: it only requires win-rate comparisons between candidate policies and the current learning policy, making it pluggable into any policy optimization algorithm (DPO, IPO, XPO) without modifying their internals.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that KL-regularization in the RLHF objective induces a structural property: a policy's coverability of the optimal policy is upper-bounded by its sub-optimality gap (Lemma 3.1), so higher-value policies inherently provide better coverage for exploration, reconciling exploration and exploitation. TPO exploits this by alternating between online learning (fraction α) and transfer learning (fraction 1−α) in blocks, selecting transfer policies via optimistic value estimation for source policies and pessimistic estimation for a 'self-transfer' policy distilled from accumulated online data via offline RLHF (RPO). The self-transfer mechanism guarantees that after finite time, regret grows only as Õ(√T), independent of state/action space sizes or policy class complexity measures. The empirical version replaces expensive minimax value estimation with win-rate-based selection using a UCB multi-armed bandit, enabling efficient integration with DPO, IPO, or XPO as the underlying policy optimizer.

## Results

On the XSum summarization task with T5-small (80M), empirical TPO (Alg. 3) achieves 52.1–54.0% win rate against vanilla iterative-DPO (no transfer), 53.1–54.5% against purely exploiting the worst source reward (ROUGE-Lsum), and 49.5–50.6% against purely exploiting the best source reward (T5-Large) over 3 iterations. By iteration 3, TPO surpasses the best-source baseline (50.6% win rate vs. T5-Large), demonstrating that the self-transfer mechanism allows the method to escape the ceiling imposed by imperfect source reward models. Results are averaged over 3 random seeds with 95% confidence intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares empirical TPO against three baselines: (I) vanilla iterative-DPO without any transfer learning, (II) purely exploiting the worst-quality source reward (ROUGE-Lsum), and (III) purely exploiting the best-quality source reward (T5-Large). TPO consistently outperforms the no-transfer baseline by 2–4 percentage points in win rate across all iterations, confirming the benefit of transfer learning. Against the worst source (ROUGE), TPO wins by 1–3 points, showing the UCB-based policy selection avoids being misled by low-quality sources. Against the best source (T5-Large), TPO is competitive in early iterations (49.5–49.1%) and surpasses it by iteration 3 (50.6%), demonstrating that the self-transfer mechanism allows TPO to eventually exceed the ceiling of any fixed imperfect source reward model. The paper also reports that TPO's advantage over iterative-DPO holds when using IPO and XPO as the underlying policy optimization algorithm (details in Appendix I).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline RLHF framework of RPO (Liu et al., 2024) and the online RLHF algorithm XPO (Xie et al., 2024), combining them with a novel structural insight: KL-regularization in the RLHF objective induces a property where a policy's coverage of the optimal policy is bounded by its sub-optimality gap (Lemma 3.1). This property is unique to the regularized setting and has no analogue in pure-reward maximization RL, where near-optimal policies can have infinite coverage. The paper departs from prior transfer RL work (Golowich & Moitra, 2022; Huang & He, 2023) by showing that regularization eliminates the need for task-similarity assumptions, enabling a simple value-based transfer policy selection strategy. The empirical TPO is designed as a modular wrapper around existing policy optimization methods (DPO, IPO, XPO), positioning it as a plug-in enhancement rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that self-transfer learning (distilling a policy from one's own online data) achieves Õ(√T) regret independent of state/action space size is a strict improvement over all existing online RLHF bounds, which depend on structural complexity measures like Cov_∞(Π). The modular empirical TPO offers a practical path to improving DPO/IPO/XPO without architectural changes. However, empirical validation is limited to T5-small on XSum with simulated (not real) human preferences over only 3 iterations, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, real human feedback, or longer training horizons. The paper also leaves open extensions to Nash RLHF, prompt-wise (rather than policy-level) transfer, and settings where source reward models are adversarially misspecified.
