## Problem

Subgraph reasoning models have demonstrated strong empirical performance on inductive knowledge graph completion, yet their theoretical properties—specifically stability (output consistency under input perturbations) and generalization capability (training-to-test performance gap)—remain entirely unexplored. No prior work has established a formal relationship between these two properties for subgraph reasoning models, leaving it unclear why some models generalize better than others in the inductive setting.

## Input / Output

The model receives a knowledge graph G and a query triplet (h, r, t), from which a non-parametric subgraph extractor g produces a relation-labeled subgraph S containing the head and tail entities plus their k-hop neighborhood. The parametric SMPNN then computes a scalar score f_w(S) that is thresholded to predict whether the triplet is positive (+1) or negative (−1). Because the subgraph extractor is discrete and non-parametric, stability is defined solely with respect to the SMPNN's sensitivity to structural differences between extracted subgraphs, measured via the proposed Relational Tree Mover's Distance.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified Subgraph Message-Passing Neural Network (SMPNN) framework that subsumes GraIL, NBFNet, and RED-GNN by decomposing each into a subgraph extractor and a message-passing scorer with configurable MSG, AGG, UPD, GRD, and RD functions. To quantify structural differences between subgraphs, the authors define the Relational Tree Mover's Distance (RTMD), which computes an optimal transport distance over multisets of relational computation trees that mirror the message-passing computation at each layer, incorporating initial embeddings, edge relations, and query relations. They prove that any SMPNN with Lipschitz-continuous component functions is itself Lipschitz continuous with respect to RTMD, yielding an explicit upper bound on the Lipschitz constant η_f in terms of the per-function constants and the maximum graph degree. Finally, using a PAC-Bayesian argument extended to deterministic models, they derive a generalization bound for inductive KGC in which the expected risk discrepancy between training and inference KGs is inversely proportional to the stability C_f = 1/η_f, establishing that more stable subgraph reasoning models provably generalize better.

## Results

Using RTMD as an SVM kernel (exp(-0.1×RTMD)), the paper achieves label classification accuracies of 0.8204 (WNv3), 0.7743 (FBv1), and 0.8652 (NLv2) with L=2 layers, all substantially above the 0.5 random baseline. Across 48 trained subgraph reasoning models (GraIL, NBFNet, RED-GNN, and their variations), the Pearson correlation between stability (reciprocal of empirical Lipschitz constant) and generalization error is negative and statistically significant: −0.5759 (p=0.00019) on WNv3, −0.5134 (p=0.00031) on FBv1, and −0.4912 (p=0.00584) on NLv2, confirming that more stable models generalize better.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there is no single proposed method benchmarked against external baselines. Instead, the empirical validation compares 48 internally generated subgraph reasoning models (variants of GraIL, NBFNet, and RED-GNN with permuted component functions) against each other. The key finding is a consistent negative correlation between stability and generalization error across all three datasets (WNv3, FBv1, NLv2), with all p-values below 0.01 and 95% confidence intervals excluding zero. Additionally, the paper validates RTMD as a meaningful subgraph metric by showing that an SVM classifier using RTMD as an indefinite kernel achieves 77–87% accuracy in distinguishing positive from negative triplets, well above the 50% random baseline. The scatter plots in Figure 4 further confirm that score differences between SMPNN outputs are bounded by RTMD, empirically validating the Lipschitz continuity theorem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree Mover's Distance framework of Chuang & Jegelka (2022) from general graphs to relational subgraphs extracted from knowledge graphs, introducing RTMD to capture edge labels, query relations, and initial entity embeddings. It also builds on the PAC-Bayesian generalization bound for stochastic GNN classifiers from Ma et al. (2021), adapting it to deterministic subgraph reasoning models in the inductive setting. The SMPNN framework unifies existing models (GraIL, NBFNet, RED-GNN) under a common message-passing formalism, enabling a single stability analysis to cover all of them. Unlike Lee et al. (2024), which derived PAC-Bayesian bounds for transductive KG representation learning, this work addresses the inductive setting where training and inference KGs differ, making the expected risk discrepancy term central to the bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis connecting stability and generalization for subgraph reasoning models in inductive KGC, providing a principled design guideline: models with smaller Lipschitz constants (e.g., using mean rather than sum aggregation) should generalize better. The empirical validation across 48 model variants on three datasets confirms the theory, but the bounds remain somewhat loose and the analysis assumes bounded weight norms and entity representations. Open questions include whether the stability-generalization relationship holds in the fully inductive setting (unobserved relations), how expressive power interacts with stability, and whether the RTMD-based design principles can be directly exploited to improve state-of-the-art inductive KGC performance.
