## Problem

The phase transition threshold δ∞ for existence of unregularized M-estimators was previously characterized only for binary logistic regression (Candès & Sur, 2020), leaving open its form for other single-index models such as Poisson and binomial regression. Additionally, the nonlinear system governing the asymptotic behavior of the M-estimator was assumed to admit a unique solution for δ > δ∞, but this was proven only under the global null; outside that special case, existence remained a numerical observation without proof.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian covariates x_i ~ N(0, I_p), responses y_i generated through an unknown single-index model with convex loss ℓ_y, and operates in the proportional asymptotic regime n/p → δ. It produces an explicit closed-form threshold 1/δ∞ = inf_t φ(t) where φ is a convex function of a single scalar t involving expectations over (G, U, Y) with indicators for whether the loss is coercive, increasing, or decreasing. The output also includes a proof that the three-equation nonlinear system (γ, a, σ) has a unique solution if and only if δ > δ∞, which is a prerequisite for applying the Convex Gaussian Minmax Theorem in downstream asymptotic analyses.

## Method

For the phase transition (Theorem 2.6), the paper applies the Gaussian kinematic formula via conic geometry: the M-estimator fails to exist iff a certain cone C (defined by the sign structure of the loss on each observation) intersects a random (p−1)-dimensional subspace, and the statistical dimension of C concentrates to n/δ∞. For the system solution existence (Theorem 2.7), the key technical contribution is an infinite-dimensional convex optimization problem min_{(a,v)∈ℝ×ℋ} 𝓛(a,v) subject to 𝓐(v)≤0 in a Hilbert space of L² functions of (G,U,Y). The paper proves an exact equivalence between the nonlinear system and the KKT conditions of this problem, then shows the problem has a minimizer iff δ > δ∞: for δ ≤ δ∞, a feasible descent direction is constructed from the optimal point (t*, p*) of a related projection problem; for δ > δ∞, coercivity is established by decomposing v into a cone-constrained part and a remainder bounded by the one-sided/two-sided coercivity of ℓ_Y, with the critical factor (√(1−δ⁻¹) − ‖p*‖) becoming positive precisely on the δ > δ∞ side.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. Theorem 2.6 proves a sharp phase transition: as n/p → δ, the unregularized M-estimator exists with high probability if and only if δ > δ_∞, where δ_∞ is explicitly characterized by the convex minimization 1/δ_∞ = inf_t φ(t) over a loss-dependent function φ. Theorem 2.7 proves that the nonlinear system (2) governing the asymptotic behavior of the M-estimator admits a unique solution if and only if δ > δ_∞, filling a gap that was previously unproven outside the global null case. Numerical simulations for Poisson regression (n=1500) and Binomial regression (n=1000, q∈{1,2,4,8,16}) confirm that the theoretical threshold 1/δ_∞ accurately separates the existence and non-existence regions across varying signal strengths κ and aspect ratios p/n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that generalizes and extends prior results rather than comparing empirical methods. The critical threshold δ_∞ defined in equation (4) generalizes the δ_∞ = 2 result of Candès & Sur (2020) for binary logistic regression under the global null to arbitrary single-index models (Poisson, Binomial, etc.). The proof of Theorem 2.7 (existence of a unique solution to the nonlinear system iff δ > δ_∞) extends the global null result of Sur et al. (2019) to the general signal case, which was previously only observed numerically in Sur & Candès (2019). The infinite-dimensional Hilbert space optimization framework builds on Bellec & Koriyama (2023) for robust regression and Montanari et al. (2023) for max-margin classifiers, adapting their tools to handle the non-coercive loss functions that create the phase transition. The paper's Theorem 2.6 uses the Gaussian kinematic formula of Amelunxen et al. (2014) in the same spirit as Candès & Sur (2020), but extends the cone geometry argument to general loss functions beyond the binary logistic case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the phase transition framework of Candès & Sur (2020) from binary logistic regression to general single-index models (Poisson, binomial), deriving an explicit critical threshold δ∞ via the Gaussian kinematic formula of Amelunxen et al. (2014). The proof of the second main result—that the nonlinear system governing asymptotic behavior admits a unique solution if and only if δ > δ∞—relies on an infinite-dimensional convex optimization formulation in a Hilbert space, a technique pioneered by Montanari et al. (2023) and adapted from the robust regression setting of Bellec & Koriyama (2023). The paper departs from prior work by establishing a rigorous equivalence between the nonlinear system and a constrained infinite-dimensional minimization problem, then showing that the same geometric threshold δ∞ governs both the existence of the M-estimator and the solvability of the system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a significant theoretical gap: prior works applying the Convex Gaussian Minmax Theorem (Salehi et al. 2019, Loureiro et al. 2021) assumed the nonlinear system admits a unique solution without proof outside the global null case. By proving this assumption is valid exactly when δ > δ∞, the paper provides the missing prerequisite for a broad class of proportional asymptotic results in high-dimensional regression. The limitation is that the analysis is restricted to Gaussian covariates and strictly convex C¹ losses; extending to non-Gaussian designs or non-differentiable losses (where a different threshold δ_perfect emerges) remains open. The practical impact is primarily on the theoretical foundations of high-dimensional MLE theory rather than on algorithmic design.
