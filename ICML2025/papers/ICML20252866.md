## Problem

Existing multi-objective RL methods rely on linear scalarization, which restricts discovered solutions to the convex subset of the Pareto front and cannot precisely align the achieved trade-off with a user-specified preference direction. This makes trained agents uncontrollable: the same value vector is produced regardless of the preference input, and non-convex regions of the Pareto front remain undiscovered. The paper addresses both the limited Pareto coverage and the lack of fine-grained preference controllability in MORL.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the environment state and a continuous preference vector p ∈ R^m (a point on the probability simplex, p^T 1 = 1, p > 0) as input, and outputs a preference-conditioned action. During training, preferences are sampled uniformly from the simplex each episode; at test time, the agent generalizes to unseen preferences with 0-shot adaptation. The learned value vector v^{π_p} is expected to lie on the Pareto front at the point closest to the ray from the origin in direction p, measured by a custom similarity metric Ψ rather than cosine similarity.

## Method

PCRL trains a single preference-conditioned meta-policy π(a|s,p) by uniformly sampling preferences during training and updating the policy via a novel PreCo gradient manipulation. PreCo solves a min-norm problem (analogous to MGDA/SDMGrad) over the m objective gradients, but augments each objective gradient with a similarity gradient ∇Ψ(p, v^{π_p}) scaled by λ, where Ψ is a custom similarity function that penalizes deviation from the preference ray. This dual objective ensures the update direction both improves all objectives simultaneously (Pareto stationarity) and pulls the value vector toward the preference direction (controllability). The min-norm is solved at the policy level (batch-size B gradients) rather than the parameter level, making it memory-efficient for large models. Theoretical analysis under stochastic gradients proves O(mT^{-1/2}) convergence to Pareto stationary points and bounded similarity gradient norms, with λ growing as Θ(√T) to guarantee preference alignment.

## Results

In the 6D Fruit-Tree environment (the most challenging setting with a non-strictly convex Pareto front), PreCo achieves HV 15.61±0.75 and CS 0.78±0.03, outperforming all MOO baselines (EPO: 14.97/0.77, SDMgrad: 13.30/0.72, CAGrad: 4.93/0.60, LS: 5.74/0.72). In MO-Reacher with 4 highly conflicting objectives, PreCo reaches HV 33.11±6.29 and CS 0.906±0.002, substantially exceeding PDMORL (22.69/0.754) and CAPQL (20.56/0.760). On MO-Ant, PreCo achieves the best CS (0.990) and competitive HV (6.85×10⁶), confirming scalability to higher-dimensional continuous control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreCo consistently outperforms LS (Alegre et al., 2023) by a large margin: in 6D Fruit-Tree, HV 15.61 vs 5.74 (2.7×), and in MO-Reacher, HV 33.11 vs 15.66, with LS also showing uncontrollable behavior (same value regardless of preference). Against SDMgrad (Xiao et al., 2023), PreCo wins on both HV and CS across all dimensions (e.g., 6D Fruit-Tree: 15.61/0.78 vs 13.30/0.72; MO-Reacher: 33.11/0.906 vs 20.37/0.761). PreCo slightly edges out EPO (Mahapatra & Rajan, 2020) in Fruit-Tree (HV 15.61 vs 14.97) but dramatically outperforms it in MO-Reacher (HV 33.11 vs 9.87), where EPO's hard similarity constraint limits exploration. CAGrad (Liu et al., 2021) is the weakest baseline, with PreCo achieving 3.2× higher HV in 6D Fruit-Tree (15.61 vs 4.93) and 2.5× in MO-Reacher (33.11 vs 13.46), as CAGrad struggles with stochastic gradients. Against SOTA MORL methods, PreCo beats CAPQL (Lu et al., 2023) on HV in all environments (e.g., 6D Fruit-Tree: 15.61 vs 5.95) and PDMORL (Basaklar et al., 2023) on HV in both Fruit-Tree (15.61 vs 9.30) and MO-Reacher (33.11 vs 22.69), though PDMORL achieves higher CS in Fruit-Tree (0.89 vs 0.78) at the cost of much lower HV. PreCo with HER and Q-buffer also outperforms Q-Pensieve (Hung et al., 2023) in both Fruit-Tree (HV 15.61 vs 6.86) and MO-Reacher (HV 49.55 vs 47.36).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the min-norm gradient manipulation framework from MGDA (Désidéri, 2009) and the stochastic convergence guarantees of SDMGrad (Xiao et al., 2023), while incorporating the similarity-constraint idea from EPO (Mahapatra & Rajan, 2020) to achieve preference-specific solutions. It departs from the dominant Linear Scalarization paradigm in MORL (Yang et al. 2019, Xu et al. 2020, Alegre et al. 2023) by showing that LS is fundamentally restricted to the convex coverage set and cannot reach non-convex Pareto regions, and from CAPQL (Lu et al. 2023) by avoiding reward augmentation that causes information loss. The key conceptual departure is PreCo, which augments the standard min-norm problem with a novel similarity gradient term, unifying Pareto stationarity and preference alignment in a single update rule with theoretical guarantees under stochastic gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCRL provides the first MORL framework that systematically integrates advanced MOO algorithms (EPO, CAGrad, SDMGrad, PreCo) with a preference-conditioned policy, offering a principled alternative to LS-based methods that are known to fail on non-strictly convex Pareto fronts. The policy-level gradient computation (solving min-norm at batch size B rather than parameter size M) makes the approach memory-efficient and potentially applicable to multi-objective fine-tuning of large language models. Open questions include: how to handle unreachable preference regions on the Pareto front, whether curriculum learning over the preference distribution can improve sample efficiency, and whether the theoretical convergence rate O(m²ε⁻²) is tight or can be improved for high-dimensional objective spaces.
