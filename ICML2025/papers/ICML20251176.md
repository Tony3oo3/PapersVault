## Problem

Supervised classifiers used in density-ratio estimation (DRE)-based Bayesian optimization, such as BORE and LFBO, suffer from an over-exploitation problem: they become overconfident in small regions near known global solution candidates, causing the optimizer to get stuck in local optima. This paper addresses the gap by showing that incorporating unlabeled data points through semi-supervised learning can mitigate this overconfidence and better balance exploration and exploitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box objective function over a d-dimensional compact search space, a set of labeled data points (previously evaluated query points with function values), and unlabeled data points that are either sampled from a truncated multivariate normal distribution (via minimax tilting) or drawn from a predefined fixed-size pool of candidate solutions. At each iteration, a threshold ratio ζ (set to 0.33) splits labeled points into Class 1 (y ≤ y†) and Class 0 (y > y†), and the method outputs the next query point by maximizing the semi-supervised classifier's Class-1 probability, ultimately returning the best candidate found over T iterations.

## Method

DRE-BO-SSL replaces the supervised binary classifier in DRE-based BO with a semi-supervised classifier (label propagation or label spreading) that leverages unlabeled data points to reduce overconfidence in known regions. At each iteration, a similarity graph is constructed over labeled and unlabeled points using an RBF kernel with parameter β learned by minimizing the entropy of propagated labels; label propagation then iteratively assigns pseudo-labels to unlabeled points while constraining labeled points, or label spreading does so with a clamping factor α that allows labeled points to shift. The next query point is selected by maximizing an inductive class probability (a weighted average of propagated labels via RBF similarities) using multi-started L-BFGS-B with 1,000 restarts, with a random-selection heuristic for flat landscapes. The key insight is that unlabeled data points spread probability mass across the search space, making the classifier less overconfident in already-exploited regions and thereby encouraging exploration of wider areas satisfying p(x|y≤y†) ≥ p(x|y>y†).

## Results

The paper evaluates DRE-BO-SSL (with label propagation and label spreading) using simple regret (min observed function value minus global optimum) over 20 repeated runs with 5 initial points. On all four synthetic benchmarks (Beale, Branin, Bukin6, Six-hump camel), both DRE-BO-SSL variants achieve lower simple regret than every baseline in both the unlabeled-point-sampling and fixed-size-pool scenarios, including beating GP-based BO with EI and UCB. On Tabular Benchmarks (Naval, Protein, Parkinson's, Slice), NATS-Bench (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-16-120), and the 64D multi-digit MNIST search, DRE-BO-SSL consistently outperforms or matches the DRE-based baselines (BORE, LFBO with RF/GB/XGBoost/MLP), though GP-based BO is noted to be superior in some Tabular Benchmark cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GP-BO (EI/UCB with Matérn 5/2 kernel), DRE-BO-SSL achieves lower simple regret on all four synthetic benchmarks in both scenarios, demonstrating that the semi-supervised classifier better balances exploration and exploitation than the GP surrogate. However, on Tabular Benchmarks, the GP-based strategy outperforms DRE-BO-SSL in some cases, suggesting the advantage is problem-dependent. Against BORE (Tiao et al., 2021) and LFBO (Song et al., 2022) instantiated with random forests, gradient boosting, XGBoost, and MLPs, DRE-BO-SSL consistently achieves lower simple regret across all evaluated benchmarks (synthetic, Tabular, NATS-Bench, and 64D MNIST). The paper attributes this advantage to the semi-supervised classifier's reduced overconfidence in already-exploited regions, which allows it to consider a wider region satisfying p(x|y≤y†) ≥ p(x|y>y†) compared to the supervised classifiers used by BORE and LFBO. The gap is most pronounced at early iterations where supervised classifiers overfit to the small labeled set, and DRE-BO-SSL's use of unlabeled points (sampled via truncated multivariate normal or drawn from a fixed pool) mitigates this over-exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the density-ratio estimation (DRE)-based Bayesian optimization paradigm initiated by Bergstra et al. (2011) and formalized by BORE (Tiao et al., 2021) and LFBO (Song et al., 2022), which replace probabilistic regression surrogates with binary classifiers to estimate class probabilities as acquisition functions. The key departure is identifying an over-exploitation problem inherent to supervised classifiers in this setting and addressing it by substituting semi-supervised classifiers—specifically label propagation (Zhu & Ghahramani, 2002) and label spreading (Zhou et al., 2003)—which leverage unlabeled data to produce less confident predictions in already-explored regions. The method also draws on the cluster assumption from semi-supervised learning theory (Seeger, 2000) to justify sampling unlabeled points from a truncated multivariate normal distribution via minimax tilting (Botev, 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The over-exploitation diagnosis is a useful and well-motivated observation that explains why DRE-BO methods can get stuck in local optima at early iterations, and the semi-supervised remedy is a clean, low-overhead fix that practitioners can adopt by swapping the classifier. However, the approach inherits the cluster assumption and requires either access to a fixed-size pool or the ability to sample unlabeled points, limiting its applicability to continuous black-box settings without such structure. Open questions include whether a general likelihood-free framework (analogous to LFBO's utility-function formulation) can be defined for the semi-supervised case, whether theoretical guarantees from semi-supervised learning (e.g., Singh et al., 2008) transfer to the sequential BO setting, and whether the computational cost of label propagation/spreading scales acceptably for very large pools.
