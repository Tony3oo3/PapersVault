## Problem

Existing generative dataset distillation methods based on diffusion models require expensive fine-tuning with representative and diversity losses to produce diverse synthetic samples, yet still do not guarantee full mode coverage. Diffusion models naturally oversample the densest regions of the data distribution, leading to redundant synthetic datasets that underperform on downstream tasks. The paper asks whether a pre-trained diffusion model can be steered at sampling time to produce both diverse and representative samples without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained class-conditional diffusion model (DiT XL/2 or LDM, 256×256) trained on the target dataset, the original dataset (used only for K-Means mode discovery in VAE latent space), and per-class Gaussian noise for sampling. It produces a synthetic distilled dataset of IPC images per class (e.g., 10–100 images per class) with hard or soft labels, which is then used to train a target network from scratch. No fine-tuning of the diffusion model is required; the only computational overhead beyond standard sampling is the one-time K-Means clustering per class.

## Method

MGD³ is a three-stage, training-free sampling procedure. First, Mode Discovery runs K-Means (k=IPC) on the VAE-encoded original dataset per class to estimate N target modes in latent space. Second, Mode Guidance modifies the reverse diffusion noise prediction at each step by adding a directional signal g_t = (m_i − x̂_0^t) scaled by λ·σ_t, steering the denoising trajectory toward a specific mode m_i; each of the IPC samples per class is assigned a distinct mode. Third, Stop Guidance sets λ=0 after t_stop=25 steps (out of 50 total), transitioning to unguided denoising during the refinement stage to preserve image quality and class fidelity. The key insight is that mode-level diversity is best enforced during the semantic stage of denoising (20%–50%), while the refinement stage should remain unconstrained to avoid artifacts—achieving both diversity and representativeness without any gradient-based fine-tuning of the diffusion model.

## Results

On ImageNette (256×256), DiT+MGD³ achieves 66.4% top-1 accuracy at IPC-10, 71.2% at IPC-20, and 79.5% at IPC-50, surpassing the previous SOTA (MinMax Diffusion) by 4.4%, 4.4%, and 2.9% respectively. On ImageNet-1K at IPC-50, MGD³ reaches 60.2% (ResNet-18), 64.6% (ResNet-50), and 67.7% (ResNet-101), outperforming the prior best (EDC) by 2.2%, 0.3%, and 2.8%. The method also achieves 55.9% on ImageIDC (IPC-10) and 33.9% on ImageNet-100 (IPC-20, ResNetAP-10), both new SOTA results. Computationally, MGD³ generates a distilled ImageNet-100 IPC-10 dataset in 0.42 hours versus 10 hours for MinMax Diffusion and 100+ hours for IDC-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGD³ consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the primary diffusion-based SOTA, MinMax Diffusion (Gu et al., 2024), MGD³ gains 4.4% on ImageNette IPC-10 (66.4% vs. 62.0%) and 2.9% on ImageIDC IPC-20 (61.9% vs. 59.0%), while also improving the LDM backbone by 1.6–2.2 points. On ImageNet-1K IPC-50, MGD³ surpasses EDC (Shao et al., 2024b) by 2.2% on ResNet-18 (60.2% vs. 58.0%) and 2.8% on ResNet-101 (67.7% vs. 64.9%), and beats D4M (Su et al., 2024) by 5.0% on ResNet-18. Among generative-prior methods (GLaD, H-GLaD, LM3D), MGD³ leads by 6.4–12.1 points on ImageNet subsets A–E at IPC-10. The only near-tie is IDC-1 (Kim et al., 2022) on ImageNet-100 IPC-10 with ResNetAP-10 (25.7% vs. 25.8%), where MGD³ still edges ahead but at a fraction of the computational cost (0.42 h vs. 100+ h). Core-set selection baselines (Herding, Random) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 6–10 points on ImageNet-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative dataset distillation line of work (DIM, MinMax Diffusion, D4M) by replacing the fine-tuning step with a training-free sampling strategy. It builds directly on the DDPM reverse process and classifier-free guidance, adding a mode-directed guidance term (Eq. 6) that steers each sample toward a pre-identified cluster centroid in VAE latent space. The key departure from MinMax Diffusion (Gu et al., 2024) is that diversity is achieved through explicit mode targeting during sampling rather than through learned representative/diversity losses, and the Stop Guidance mechanism addresses the quality degradation that full-length guidance would cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation dramatically reduces the computational cost of dataset distillation (0.42 hours vs. 10+ hours for MinMax on ImageNet-100 IPC-10), making large-scale distillation practical in resource-constrained settings. The approach is also broadly applicable, working with both U-Net (LDM) and Transformer (DiT) backbones, DDPM and DDIM samplers, and even general-purpose text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion. Open questions include sensitivity to the mode discovery algorithm (GMM slightly outperforms K-Means in ablations), whether the fixed t_stop=25 generalizes across datasets and IPC values, and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or video distillation.
