## Problem

Most deep imbalanced learning research targets classification, leaving imbalanced regression underexplored despite its prevalence in real-world applications. Existing regression-specific methods either adjust only the final prediction loss (re-weighting, logit adjustment) or regularize features using intra-batch sample relationships, which biases representations toward majority targets and causes minority features to collapse. The paper asks how to construct a globally balanced and well-ordered feature space for regression that generalizes to underrepresented target regions.

## Input / Output

The method accepts diverse inputs—facial images (AgeDB, IMDB-WIKI), RGB indoor scene images (NYUD2), or sentence pairs (STS-B)—and outputs a single continuous scalar prediction (age, depth, or similarity score). Internally, the model comprises a feature encoder f mapping inputs to a d-dimensional feature vector z and a predictor g mapping z to the target; the key architectural addition is a set of C learnable proxy vectors in the same feature space, whose target values are pre-assigned as uniform quantiles of the target range (or K-means centers for multi-dimensional targets).

## Method

PRIME introduces C learnable proxies as synthetic anchors in the feature space, structured by two novel losses. The proxy loss L_proxy uses a t-SNE-inspired KL divergence between pairwise target similarities and pairwise feature similarities among proxies, plus a cosine-distance regularization term that spreads proxies proportionally to their target separation, preventing trivial low-dimensional solutions. The alignment loss L_align computes a softmax association vector between each sample feature and all proxies, then minimizes cross-entropy against a target-similarity-weighted distribution over proxies, effectively pulling features toward nearby-target proxies and away from distant ones. Because this alignment is structurally a classification over C proxy 'classes,' PRIME seamlessly integrates class-imbalance techniques (Proxy-wise Re-Weighting, Class-Balanced loss, LDAM margin) to further rebalance minority-target alignment. A theoretical analysis proves that minimizing the total loss bounds the generalization error under a balanced test distribution, with the bound scaling by a data-skewness constant C_D.

## Results

PRIME achieves state-of-the-art performance across four imbalanced regression benchmarks, with up to 9.0%, 2.0%, 4.5%, and 3.7% lower regression error on minority (Few) targets compared to prior methods on AgeDB-DIR, IMDB-WIKI-DIR, NYUD2-DIR, and STS-B-DIR, respectively. On NYUD2-DIR, PRIME+PRW achieves the best RMSE of 1.682 (Few) and 1.272 (All), outperforming ConR (1.784 Few) and Balanced MSE (1.761 Few). On AgeDB-DIR, PRIME+LDAM reduces Few-category MAE to 4.89, a substantial improvement over ConR (10.26) and HCA (10.13). The Spearman correlation between feature and label similarities reaches 0.828 in the Few category for PRIME, versus only 0.587 (RankSim) and 0.614 (ConR), confirming well-ordered representations even for underrepresented targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIME consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across the four datasets, with the largest gains in the Few (minority) category. Against the most direct representation-learning competitors, ConR (Keramati et al., 2024) and RankSim (Gong et al., 2022), PRIME reduces Few-category MAE on AgeDB-DIR from 10.26/10.45 to 10.13 (PRIME) and 4.89 (PRIME+LDAM), and improves Spearman ordinality correlation from 0.614/0.587 to 0.828. PRIME also surpasses the loss-adjustment methods (Balanced MSE, VIR, LDS/FDS from Yang et al., 2021) and the hierarchical classification approach HCA (Xiong & Yao, 2024), which reports only All-set results (RMSE 1.475 on NYUD2-DIR) versus PRIME's 1.292. Against the recent in-context learning method IM-Context (Nejjar et al., 2024), PRIME with the same CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone achieves MAE 5.47 (All) and 5.57 (Few) on AgeDB-DIR, versus 6.05/7.83 for GPT2-localized and 6.58/10.49 for PFN-localized. For general regression representation learning, PRIME outperforms HPN (Mettes et al., 2019), Ordinal Entropy (Zhang et al., 2023a), and Rank-N-Contrast (Zha et al., 2023) on AgeDB-DIR (MAE 7.09 vs. 7.38/7.33/7.27), and also beats the adapted ProxyNCA (Movshovitz-Attias et al., 2017) baseline (MAE 7.09 vs. 7.33), validating the learnable proxy design over fixed or non-learnable alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIME builds on the proxy-based metric learning lineage (ProxyNCA, Proxy Anchor Loss) and repurposes learnable prototypes for a fundamentally different setting: imbalanced regression. It departs from prior DIR representation methods (RankSim, ConR) that rely solely on intra-batch sample relationships—biased toward majority targets—by introducing global, learnable reference points that provide holistic supervision independent of batch composition. The SNE-inspired proxy loss and the alignment loss are novel formulations, but the conceptual bridge that proxy alignment reduces to a classification problem (enabling direct transfer of PRW, CB, and LDAM) is the paper's key positioning move, connecting two previously separate literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRIME provides a practical, modular framework that any existing imbalanced regression pipeline can adopt by simply adding two loss terms, and it opens a pathway for the entire imbalanced classification toolbox to be applied to regression. The theoretical generalization bound under balanced test criteria (Theorem 4.1) gives the approach a principled foundation that prior DIR methods lack. Open questions include how the number of proxies C should scale with target range or dimensionality, whether the approach extends naturally to multi-modal or structured targets beyond scalar depth, and whether the proxy-as-prototype equivalence holds under distribution shift at test time.
