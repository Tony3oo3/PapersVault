## Problem

Existing n-gram watermarking schemes for LLMs are vulnerable to watermark stealing, but prior work (Jovanović et al., 2024) required knowledge of the underlying hash function and relied on paraphrase tools that distort the original LM distribution. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a more general watermark stealing and removal framework that operates without hash-function knowledge and provides a provable bound on the gap between the post-removal and original LM distributions.

## Input / Output

The method requires API access to a watermarked LM and, in the gray-box setting (L1), top-k log-probabilities (e.g., top-20 from ChatGPT); in the black-box setting (L0), it estimates probabilities from multiple sampled generations. The core probing mechanism prompts the LM to randomly choose between two specified tokens, consuming one query per token pair per context. Outputs include the estimated watermark strength δ, the red and green token lists, the prefix n-gram length h, and a modified sampling distribution that either removes the watermark (preserving the original LM distribution up to a bounded TV distance) or applies the stolen watermark to a different LM for exploitation.

## Method

DE-MARK's core insight is random selection probing: by prompting the LM to choose uniformly between two tokens, the relative probability ratio R_{i,j} = log(P(T_i)/P(T_j)) becomes an unbiased estimator of ±δ when the tokens belong to different red/green lists (Theorem 4.1). Five sequential algorithms exploit this: (1) compute pairwise relative probability ratios across a target token set, (2) aggregate signed ratios into per-token scores to classify red vs. green membership, (3) identify the prefix n-gram length h by detecting where the consistency rate of token scores drops below a threshold as context length decreases, (4) estimate δ by averaging ratios from token pairs with extreme scores, and (5) refine the green list using δ-normalized adjusted scores. For watermark removal, DE-MARK applies an inverse logit adjustment (Eq. 2) to the watermarked distribution, and Theorem 4.2 provides a total-variation bound showing the removed distribution converges to the original as estimation errors ε₁ (list mismatch) and ε₂ (δ error) approach zero.

## Results

On the watermark removal task, DE-MARK reduces TPR@FPR=0.1% from 89–95% (watermarked baselines) to 6% on MMW Book Report and 9% on Dolly CW for Llama3.2-3B, while the median p-value increases from ~1e-6 to ~8e-2, rendering the watermark effectively undetectable. Text quality (GPT score) is preserved, remaining within 1 point of the watermarked baseline (e.g., 94.22 → 93.98 on MMW Book Report). On the ChatGPT case study, TPR@FPR=0.1% drops from 89.58% to 25.00% and median p-value from 7.61e-7 to 6.78e-3, confirming effectiveness on industry-scale LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE-MARK is evaluated against three n-gram-based watermarking schemes: the standard Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a) red-green list watermark, γ-reweight (Hu et al., 2023), and DiPmark (Wu et al., 2023b). Against the standard n-gram watermark, DE-MARK reduces TPR@FPR=0.1% from 89–95% to 6–9% across Llama3 and Mistral models, with median p-values rising from ~1e-6 to ~8e-2. Against γ-reweight (Table 11), TPR@FPR=0.1% drops from 44% to 5% (Llama3.1-8B) and from 42% to 15% (Llama3.2-3B) on MMW Book Report. Against DiPmark (Table 12), TPR@FPR=1% drops from 44% to 3% (Llama3.1-8B) and from 43% to 2% (Llama3.2-3B), showing DE-MARK generalizes across distortion-free watermark variants. In all cases, GPT scores remain within ~1 point of the watermarked baseline, confirming that removal does not degrade text quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DE-MARK extends the watermark stealing framework of Jovanović et al. (2024), which was the first to demonstrate that n-gram watermarks can be reverse-engineered via token frequency analysis. The key departure is that DE-MARK eliminates two critical requirements of the prior work: knowledge of the underlying hash function and a paraphrase tool for removal. Instead, it introduces random selection probing—a querying strategy that estimates the red-green list and watermark strength purely from top-k token probabilities—while providing a provable total-variation bound (Theorem 4.2) between the post-removal and original LM distributions. The paper also acknowledges concurrent work by Pang et al. (2024) and Zhang et al. (2024) but notes their experimental settings (detector-available, unigram-only) differ substantially from the detector-unavailable, n-gram setting addressed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: n-gram watermarking, the most widely deployed statistical watermark for LLMs, is vulnerable to removal even when the attacker has no knowledge of the hash function and only API-level access to top-k log-probabilities. The demonstration on ChatGPT confirms this vulnerability extends to industry-scale models, undermining watermarking as a reliable content-attribution mechanism. Key limitations include the requirement for top-k token probability access (gray-box setting) and the computational overhead of multiple queries. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-n-gram watermarking schemes (e.g., unigram, semantic-key watermarks), whether black-box settings without token probabilities can be addressed, and whether watermarking schemes can be redesigned to resist this class of probing attacks.
