## Problem

Linear sufficient dimension reduction (SDR) fails when the regression relationship between predictors and response is nonlinear, and existing nonlinear SDR methods (kernel-based GSIR, KSIR) struggle with large samples and complex inputs like images. Deep learning approaches to SDR have emerged but lack rigorous theoretical guarantees—specifically unbiasedness with respect to the central σ-field and explicit convergence rates—within the unified σ-field framework of Lee et al. (2013).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250326/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional predictor X ∈ R^p (tabular vectors or 2D images such as MNIST digits) and a scalar response Y ∈ R (continuous or categorical). The response is first discretized into binary variables via LVR (left-versus-right) or OVA (one-versus-another) slicing at h−1 or h(h−1)/2 cut points, and the network is trained separately on each binary problem. The output is a set of d low-dimensional nonlinear SDR functions (d determined by the ladle method), which can be used directly for 2D visualization or as features for downstream logistic/linear regression.

## Method

DPSVM generalizes the principal SVM objective by replacing the linear projection ψ^T X with a scalar-valued function f from a flexible function class F_n (a feedforward ReLU network with an expansion-contraction architecture), yielding the loss L_n(f,t) = λVar_n(f(X)) + λt² + (1/n)Σρ(−Ỹ_i{f(X_i) − E_n(f(X)) − t}), where ρ is a convex loss (hinge by default) and Ỹ is the binarized response. For each of the h−1 (LVR) or h(h−1)/2 (OVA) binary sub-problems, a separate network is trained; the final d SDR functions are the top d eigenvectors of the Gram matrix formed from the per-slice minimizers evaluated on the training data. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the population minimizer is unbiased (belongs to the central σ-field) without requiring linear conditional mean assumptions, and that for β-Hölder continuous targets, ReLU networks achieve the minimax-optimal rate n^{−2β/(p+2β)} up to a log n factor, leveraging VC-dimension bounds on the network class.

## Results

On the primary synthetic benchmark (n=500, p=10, 9 settings across 3 nonlinear models × 3 predictor distributions), DPSVM achieves the highest empirical distance correlation in 7 of 9 settings, with scores ranging from 0.71 to 0.81. The two exceptions are settings B-III and C-III, where DSRL achieves 0.87 and 0.89 respectively versus DPSVM's 0.75 and 0.81. On the MNIST image classification task (d=9 reduced dimensions), DPSVM with LeNet achieves 98.62% test accuracy, closely matching DDR (98.83%) and direct LeNet (98.99%), confirming effective nonlinear information extraction from 2D image inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSVM outperforms all six baselines (KPSVM, GSIR, KSIR, DDR, DSRL, GMDDNet) in 7 of 9 synthetic settings, with the largest margins over KSIR (e.g., 0.75 vs 0.15 on B-I) and DDR (e.g., 0.74 vs 0.57 on A-II). The two losses are to DSRL on settings B-III (0.75 vs 0.87) and C-III (0.81 vs 0.89), both involving correlated t-distributed predictors. DPSVM also consistently shows lower standard deviations than most baselines, indicating more stable estimation. On the MNIST image task, DPSVM (98.62%) is within 0.2–0.4% of DDR (98.83%) and direct LeNet (98.99%), while producing visually more discriminative 2D projections. On the CRIME regression dataset, DPSVM yields more visually coherent low-dimensional representations than DDR. The paper notes that DPSVM's robustness to outliers (introduced via 10% contamination in the synthetic design) is largely attributable to its LVR/OVA response-binarization step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Principal Support Vector Machine (PSVM) framework of Li et al. (2011) from linear and kernel-based nonlinear SDR to a general function-class setting, with ReLU neural networks as the primary instantiation. It is situated within the central σ-field formulation of Lee et al. (2013), which unifies linear and nonlinear SDR, and positions kernel PSVM as a special case of its more general objective. Unlike concurrent deep SDR methods (DDR, DSRL, GMDDNet) that rely on mutual information, distance covariance, or martingale difference criteria, DPSVM inherits the SVM hinge-loss structure and the response-binarization (LVR/OVA) slicing strategy, while replacing the RKHS with a flexible neural network class to handle large samples and non-vector inputs without kernel design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unbiasedness guarantee and minimax-optimal convergence rate (up to log n) for a deep neural network-based nonlinear SDR method within the central σ-field framework, filling a theoretical gap that other deep SDR approaches (DDR, DSRL, GMDDNet) have not addressed. Practically, the binarization-based objective confers robustness to heavy-tailed noise and outliers, and the method handles images and text natively without flattening or kernel selection. Key limitations include computational cost from repeated binarizations (typically 10 splits) and the difficulty of extending the quantile-based discretization to multivariate responses, which remains an open problem.
