## Problem

The quadratic attention complexity during the pre-filling stage makes long-context VLM inference prohibitively slow (minutes of Time-to-First-Token latency at 1M tokens). Existing dynamic sparse attention methods like MInference, designed for text-only LLMs, fail to exploit the unique grid-like sparse patterns arising from spatiotemporal locality in video inputs and cannot handle the modality boundaries that emerge in mixed video-text sequences, limiting their applicability to multi-modal settings.

## Input / Output

MMInference operates as a drop-in replacement for the attention computation in the LLM decoder of existing VLMs, consuming interleaved video frame tokens (e.g., 256 tokens per frame) and text tokens as input, and producing the same text response as full attention. It is training-free and requires no model modifications; the only offline step is a ~15-minute per-head pattern search on a single calibration sample. The method handles contexts up to 1M tokens (e.g., 6k video frames) and supports mixed-modality inputs where text segments are inserted at the document level across video frames.

## Method

MMInference identifies three distinct sparse attention patterns in VLMs and handles each via permutation-based transformations that convert sparse structures into dense blocks for hardware-efficient FlashAttention computation. For intra-modality attention, it introduces a Grid pattern (evenly spaced vertical and horizontal lines reflecting spatiotemporal locality) alongside A-shape and Vertical-Slash patterns, using row-and-column-wise permutation to gather the grid into contiguous blocks. For inter-modality boundaries, it classifies heads into Q-Boundary (row-wise permutation grouping same-modality queries) and 2D-Boundary (joint row-and-column permutation of Q, K, V by modality) types, enabling per-modality-pair sparse computation. A Modality-Aware Sparse Attention Search Algorithm performs offline per-head pattern selection across intra-modality, cross-modality, and inter-modality search spaces, optimizing for attention recall under a kernel-aware FLOPs budget. The entire pipeline is implemented with optimized Triton/FlashAttention kernels that perform dynamic loading and writing within the kernel to avoid explicit tensor transposition overhead.

## Results

On Llava-Video-7B (110 frames, ~20K tokens), MMInference achieves 57.6% average accuracy at 47.3% FLOPs, matching full attention (57.6% at 100% FLOPs) while using less than half the compute. On LongVILA-7B (256 frames, ~66K tokens), it reaches 55.4% average at 31.8% FLOPs versus 55.5% for full attention. At 1M-token context, MMInference delivers up to 8.3× end-to-end speedup over FlashAttention-2 and 1.7× over MInference, with a 12× kernel-level speedup from the Grid pattern alone. On the proposed MM-NIAH benchmark, MMInference maintains 91.3% recall versus 88.0% for MInference at 1M tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMInference matches or exceeds full attention accuracy across all three VLMs while using 32–47% of the FLOPs, and outperforms MInference (the closest competitor) at roughly half the compute: 57.6% vs. 57.5% avg on Llava-Video at 47.3% vs. 78.8% FLOPs, and 55.4% vs. 55.2% on LongVILA at 31.8% vs. 47.0% FLOPs. Static sparse patterns (SF-fixed, SF-strided, A-shape, Tri-shape) all degrade noticeably on multi-choice VQA (EgoSchema drops 2–6 points) and on VideoMME without subtitles, with A-shape suffering the largest loss (53.8% vs. 57.6% avg on Llava-Video). VisionZip, a token-compression baseline, collapses to 43.1% average on Llava-Video, confirming that aggressive token pruning is far more destructive than attention sparsification. On the long-context retrieval benchmarks (V-NIAH and MM-NIAH), MMInference stays within ~1% of full attention recall up to 1M tokens, whereas Tri-shape degrades sharply beyond 3.9K frames and MInference drops to 88.0% recall on MM-NIAH versus MMInference's 91.3%. The key differentiator is the modality-aware permutation: without inter-modality patterns, MMInference w/o Inter drops to 88.0% on MM-NIAH, matching MInference, confirming that the Q-Boundary and 2D-Boundary handling is essential for mixed-modality inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMInference extends MInference (Jiang et al., 2024), which introduced dynamic sparse attention with online index estimation for text-only LLMs, to the multi-modal setting. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a Grid pattern in VLM attention heads—arising from spatiotemporal locality in visual inputs—that is fundamentally different from the Vertical-Slash patterns dominant in text LLMs. The permutation-based hardware-efficient computation is inherited from PIT (Zheng et al., 2023), but is here applied to novel modality-aware row/column permutations that isolate intra-modality regions and handle Q-Boundary and 2D-Boundary structures. The paper also positions itself against static sparse patterns (A-shape, Tri-shape, Sparse Transformer) and visual token compression (VisionZip), showing that dynamic, modality-aware sparsity outperforms both at comparable FLOPs budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.3× pre-filling speedup at 1M tokens addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for long-context VLMs in robotics, autonomous driving, and healthcare, and the method is training-free with no model modifications, making it immediately applicable to existing pipelines. The modality boundary taxonomy (No-Boundary, K-Boundary, Q-Boundary, 2D-Boundary) provides a reusable analytical framework for understanding multi-modal attention structure. Open questions include generalization to audio-modal and end-to-end multimodal architectures (where boundary structure may differ), whether the offline per-model pattern search (~15 min) can be amortized across model families, and how the approach interacts with emerging KV-cache compression or speculative decoding techniques. The method also does not accelerate the auto-regressive decoding stage, which remains a separate bottleneck.
