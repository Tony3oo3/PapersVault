## Problem

Accurate species range estimates are only available for a small fraction of known species, and over half of the 373,000 species on iNaturalist have fewer than ten observations. Existing range estimation models like SINR and LE-SINR require retraining a per-species classifier for each new species and rely on relatively large observation counts, making them impractical for the majority of under-observed species. This paper tackles the underexplored few-shot regime where only a handful of presence locations are available and the target species is entirely unseen during training.

## Input / Output

At inference, FS-SINR accepts a variable-length unordered set of (lat, lon) context locations, optionally augmented with a text description (encoded by a frozen GritLM into a 4096-dim vector) and/or a species image (encoded by a frozen EVA-02 ViT into a 1024-dim vector). The model produces a single species embedding vector via a forward pass; this vector is then dot-producted with pre-computed location embeddings for all query points on Earth to yield a continuous presence-probability range map. No retraining or parameter updates are needed when new context locations or metadata are added, enabling interactive use.

## Method

FS-SINR replaces SINR's linear multi-label classifier head with a 4-layer Transformer encoder that processes an unordered set of input tokens: location embeddings from a shared pre-trained MLP encoder, optional text and image tokens from separate encoders, plus a CLS token and a register token. Learned token-type embeddings (5 types) let the Transformer distinguish token sources without positional encodings, preserving permutation invariance and variable-length flexibility. The species embedding is extracted from the CLS token output through a small 2-layer decoder MLP, and presence probability at any query location is computed as a sigmoid of the dot product between the query's location embedding and this species embedding. Training uses a batch-restricted variant of the AN-full loss (L_AN-full-b) that considers only species within the current batch rather than all species, since no per-species head exists. The location encoder is first pre-trained for 20 epochs on 1,000 examples per species, then all components are jointly fine-tuned on 100 examples per species with aggressive dropout (0.5 for text/image, 0.1 for locations) to ensure robustness to missing modalities at inference.

## Results

On the IUCN dataset with 10 context locations and range text, FS-SINR achieves 0.66 MAP, outperforming LE-SINR (0.62), SINR (0.57), Prototype SINR (0.61), and Active SINR (0.62). On the S&T dataset under the same setting, FS-SINR reaches 0.72 MAP versus LE-SINR's 0.69 and SINR's 0.64. In the zero-shot setting (no locations, range text only), FS-SINR scores 0.52 MAP on IUCN and 0.64 on S&T, beating LE-SINR (0.48 and 0.60 respectively). FS-SINR generates range estimates in 2% of LE-SINR's CPU time and 6% of its GPU time, since it requires only a single forward pass with no retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FS-SINR outperforms all six baselines across both IUCN and S&T datasets in both few-shot and zero-shot settings. Against SINR (Cole et al., 2023), FS-SINR gains 4–9 MAP points in the few-shot regime (e.g., 0.66 vs. 0.57 at 10 locations on IUCN) and 4–5 points zero-shot with range text (0.52 vs. 0.48 on IUCN). Against LE-SINR (Hamilton et al., 2024), the closest competitor, FS-SINR consistently leads by 2–4 MAP points in few-shot (e.g., 0.66 vs. 0.62 at 10 locations on IUCN) and by 4 points zero-shot with range text (0.52 vs. 0.48 on IUCN), while also eliminating the need for per-species retraining at inference. Prototype SINR (Snell et al., 2017) and Active SINR (Lange et al., 2023) both underperform FS-SINR by 4–8 MAP points in the few-shot setting, with Active SINR degrading sharply at higher context counts (0.36 at 1000 locations on IUCN vs. 0.68 for FS-SINR). Traditional machine learning baselines—Gaussian Process (Golding & Purse, 2016) and Random Forest (Breiman, 2001)—perform substantially worse than all deep learning approaches, confirming the advantage of the learned location encoder and Transformer architecture for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FS-SINR builds directly on the Spatial Implicit Neural Representations (SINR) framework of Cole et al. (2023), which learns a shared location encoder and per-species linear projections for multi-label range prediction, and on LE-SINR (Hamilton et al., 2024), which incorporated text descriptions but still required per-species logistic regression retraining at inference. The key departure is replacing the fixed per-species embedding with a Transformer-based encoder that processes a variable-length, unordered set of context locations (plus optional text/image tokens) in a single feedforward pass, producing a species embedding via a class token without any parameter updates. The loss is adapted from SINR's assume-negative-full objective to a batch-level variant that avoids enumerating all species, and the architecture borrows the register-token idea from Darcet et al. (2024) and the multi-modal conditioning pattern from recent language-driven SDM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because over half of the ~373,000 species on iNaturalist have fewer than ten observations, a method that produces reliable range estimates from a handful of points without retraining is practically critical for conservation planning and biodiversity monitoring. The feedforward design (2% CPU / 6% GPU time vs. LE-SINR) enables interactive exploration where a user can iteratively add context locations or metadata and see updated ranges in real time. Open questions include how to introduce stochasticity for calibrated uncertainty (the model is currently deterministic), how to incorporate confirmed absence data, and how to mitigate the strong geographic and taxonomic biases inherited from citizen-science training data, which the authors show degrade performance in South America, Oceania, and less-studied taxonomic groups.
