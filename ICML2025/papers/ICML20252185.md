## Problem

Standard conformal prediction calibration produces large prediction sets that are impractical for real-world use. Prior conformal training methods (ConfTr, CUT) integrate CP into classifier training but rely on stochastic approximation of quantiles from mini-batches, yielding a learning bound of Ω(1/s) in batch size s—far worse than the standard O(1/√n) bound—because batch-level quantiles carry large estimation error. The paper asks whether conformal training can achieve the standard O(1/√n) learning bound while directly minimizing prediction set size.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Caltech-101, iNaturalist) as input to a deep classifier (ResNet or DenseNet) and produces a prediction set—a subset of the K candidate classes—guaranteed to contain the true label with probability at least 1−α. During training, the classifier's softmax scores are converted into non-conformity scores via HPS, APS, or RAPS scoring functions; the output of interest is the size of the resulting prediction set, which DPSM explicitly minimizes while preserving valid marginal coverage.

## Method

DPSM formulates conformal training as a bilevel optimization problem: the lower level solves a quantile regression (QR) subproblem using the pinball loss to learn the (1−α)-quantile of non-conformity scores as a single scalar variable q, while the upper level minimizes the sigmoid-smoothed prediction set size conditioned on q. This decouples the quantile from stochastic mini-batch sampling, replacing the noisy batch-level quantile of prior SA-based methods with a learned parameter that converges to the true empirical quantile. A simple stochastic first-order algorithm alternates gradient updates on the classifier f (using classification loss plus conformal alignment loss on one data split) and on q (using QR loss on the other split). Theoretically, the paper proves the learning bound is O(1/√n), improving over the Ω(1/s) lower bound of SA-based conformal training, and empirically achieves a 20.46% average reduction in prediction set size over the best baseline across three datasets.

## Results

The headline metric is Average Prediction Set Size (APSS) at α=0.1, where smaller is better. DPSM achieves a 20.46% average reduction in prediction set size across three datasets (CalTech-101, CIFAR-100, iNaturalist) and two architectures (DenseNet, ResNet) compared to the best baseline. The largest gains appear on CalTech-101 (↓44.44% with DenseNet/HPS, ↓59.17% with DenseNet/APS) and iNaturalist (↓20.63% with DenseNet/HPS), while gains are more modest on CIFAR-100 (↓2.32%–4.82% with HPS). DPSM also maintains valid marginal coverage (≥90%) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSM is compared against two conformal training baselines: ConfTr (Stutz et al., 2021), which uses a stochastic approximation of the batch-level quantile to minimize differentiable prediction set size, and CUT (Einbinder et al., 2022), which penalizes deviation of conformity scores from uniformity. Against ConfTr, DPSM wins in all six HPS settings and five of six APS settings, with the largest margin on CalTech-101 (e.g., 0.90 vs. 4.10 APSS for DenseNet/HPS). Against CUT, DPSM wins in five of six HPS settings (e.g., 61.22 vs. 77.13 on iNaturalist/DenseNet) and four of six APS settings. The two exceptions where DPSM underperforms are minor: CIFAR-100/DenseNet with APS (2.64 vs. CUT's 2.41, an 8.71% increase) and iNaturalist/ResNet with APS (79.43 vs. CUT's 79.00, a 0.54% increase). Overall, DPSM achieves the best APSS in 10 of 12 configurations, yielding the reported 20.46% average improvement over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal training line initiated by ConfTr (Stutz et al., 2021) and CUT (Einbinder et al., 2022), which integrate conformal prediction into classifier training via stochastic approximation (SA) of batch-level quantiles. The key departure is formulating conformal training as a bilevel optimization problem where the lower level learns the quantile through quantile regression (pinball loss) rather than sampling it stochastically from mini-batches, and the upper level directly minimizes prediction set size conditioned on that learned quantile. This repurposes the well-studied QR objective as a constraint within a bilevel framework, a combination not previously applied to conformal training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution is the main advance: proving that SA-based conformal training suffers a learning bound of Ω(1/s) (lower bound) while DPSM achieves the standard O(1/√n) ERM bound, formally explaining why batch-level quantile estimation degrades the accuracy-efficiency trade-off. Practically, the 20.46% average reduction in prediction set size is meaningful for human-AI collaboration and medical diagnosis where smaller candidate sets are more actionable. However, the convergence guarantee for the practical stochastic algorithm is left as future work, and the improvement is highly dataset-dependent (44–59% on CalTech-101 vs. 2–8% on CIFAR-100), suggesting the method's benefit is most pronounced when the base classifier is less conformally aligned. Open questions include whether the bilevel-QR formulation extends to conditional coverage, regression, or online conformal settings.
