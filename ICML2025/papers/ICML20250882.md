## Problem

Existing evolving domain generalization (EDG) methods model only statistical correlations between data and targets across temporal domains, creating spurious shortcuts (e.g., relying on lighting rather than object presence) that degrade generalization to future domains. Moreover, static causal models assume time-invariant causal factors and mechanisms, failing to capture the continuous evolving structure and causal mechanism drifts inherent in non-stationary environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250882/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T sequential source domains, each containing N_t labeled samples (x, y) drawn from a time-conditional distribution P(x,y|t), spanning diverse modalities including RGB images (Caltran, Portraits, RMNIST), time-series signals (PowerSupply), and text (ONP). Domains are split into source (1/2), validation (1/6), and target (1/3) segments, and the model must predict class labels on K unseen target domains arriving sequentially in the near future. The output is a single class label per input, evaluated via worst-case and average accuracy across all future target domains.

## Method

SYNC introduces a time-aware structural causal model that decomposes causal factors into static (Z_c^st) and dynamic (Z_c^dy) components and adds a drift factor Z^d to model evolving causal mechanisms, with global (G) and local (L) variables governing factor generation. A sequential VAE with an LSTM-based encoder captures evolving patterns in dynamic factors, while a standard encoder handles static factors; Gumbel-Softmax maskers select causal dimensions from each. Training jointly optimizes an evolving-pattern loss (reconstruction + KL divergences), a mutual-information disentanglement loss between static and dynamic factors, and supervised contrastive losses that maximize cross-domain conditional MI (for static causal factors) and intra-domain conditional MI against static causal anchors (for dynamic causal factors). Theoretically, the paper proves that minimizing the overall objective yields the optimal causal predictor for each time domain, guaranteeing immunity to spurious factors under the global Markov and faithfulness conditions.

## Results

SYNC achieves the best overall worst-case accuracy of 63.4% and average accuracy of 73.1% across seven benchmarks (Circle, Sine, RMNIST, Portraits, Caltran, PowerSupply, ONP). Compared to the strongest EDG baselines, SYNC improves worst-case accuracy by 5.3% over MMD-LSAE (58.1%) and average accuracy by 1.2% over SDE-EDG (71.9%). Against causal DG methods, SYNC gains at least 7.6% in worst-case and 7.7% in average performance, highlighting the benefit of modeling dynamic causal factors and mechanism drifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SYNC outperforms all 20 baselines on overall worst-case accuracy (63.4%) and average accuracy (73.1%). Against the strongest EDG methods, SYNC improves worst-case accuracy by 5.3% over MMD-LSAE (58.1%) and average accuracy by 1.2% over SDE-EDG (71.9%), while also surpassing LSSAE by 7.2% in worst-case (56.2%→63.4%) and 3.4% in average (69.7%→73.1%). Compared to causal DG methods (IRM, IIB, iDAG), SYNC gains at least 7.3% in worst-case and 9.4% in average accuracy, demonstrating the critical role of modeling dynamic causal factors and mechanism drifts. Among non-causal DG baselines, SYNC leads ERM by 11.5% in worst-case and 9.2% in average, and outperforms the strongest non-causal method (DIVA) by 7.6% in worst-case. Notably, SYNC achieves the best worst-case accuracy on every individual dataset except ONP (where it ties with LSSAE at 64.6% vs. 64.5%), confirming consistent gains across diverse temporal shift scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the sequential VAE framework of LSSAE (Qin et al., 2022), which uses an LSTM-based encoder to capture evolving patterns across temporal domains, but departs from it by introducing a causal lens. While prior causal DG methods (IRM, IIB, iDAG) assume time-invariant causal structures and prior EDG methods (GI, DDA, DRAIN, SDE-EDG) ignore spurious correlations entirely, SYNC is the first to formalize a time-aware SCM that explicitly models both dynamic causal factors and causal mechanism drifts. The method combines the generative sequential architecture from LSSAE with information-theoretic disentanglement objectives and Gumbel-Softmax-based causal masking, bridging two previously separate research threads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode in EDG—spurious correlations (e.g., lighting confounding vehicle detection in Caltran)—that prior EDG methods do not handle, and provides a theoretical guarantee of recovering the optimal causal predictor per time domain. The ablation revealing that static causal factors primarily stabilize worst-case performance while dynamic causal factors drive average performance is a useful decomposition for practitioners. Open questions include whether the assumed SCM structure (causal sufficiency, faithfulness) holds in genuinely complex real-world settings, how the method scales to very long temporal sequences, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax masking remains effective at higher dimensionalities.
