## Problem

Vanilla LoRA applies a single set of low-rank matrices across all timesteps of the diffusion denoising process, yet diffusion models exhibit drastically different processing modes at different noise levels. This mismatch limits LoRA-tuned diffusion models: in domain adaptation they fail to fit the target distribution, and in post-pretraining they cause prompt misalignment that degrades the pre-trained model's generative capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253239/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and operates on a pre-trained diffusion model (SD1.5/SD2 at 512×512, PixArt-α at 512×512, SD3, or VideoCrafter2 for video), producing a generated image or video in the same format as the base model. During inference, the TimeStep LoRA experts are activated sequentially as the denoising timestep progresses, with the asymmetrical mixture router dynamically weighting context experts at each step based on both the current hidden state and the timestep embedding.

## Method

TSM introduces a two-stage paradigm for building an asymmetrical mixture of TimeStep LoRA experts. In the fostering stage, the T timesteps are divided into n intervals and a separate LoRA module is trained on noisy inputs within each interval, yielding n TimeStep LoRA experts that capture different noise levels. In the assembling stage, multi-scale interval divisions (e.g., n₁=8, n₂=4, n₃=2, n₄=1) produce m experts per timestep; the finest-granularity expert (smallest interval) acts as the core expert applied without gating to model the noise level directly, while coarser-granularity experts serve as context experts weighted by a time-dependent router G(z_t, t) = FC(z_t) + E(t). The core experts are frozen from stage 1 and only the lightweight router is trained in stage 2, keeping total trainable parameters comparable to vanilla LoRA while enabling adaptive multi-scale noise modeling.

## Results

On domain adaptation (T2I-CompBench), TSM achieves state-of-the-art scores across all six compositional categories for every tested architecture; for example, SD3+TSM reaches 83.45 (Color), 63.16 (Shape), 78.18 (Texture), 34.50 (Spatial), 31.81 (Non-Spatial), and 38.71 (Complex), surpassing SD3+Vanilla LoRA by up to 2.63 points on Spatial. On post-pretraining, TSM improves PixArt-α's Color score from 43.47 (LoRA) to 48.86 and VideoCrafter2's Inception Score from 15.06 (LoRA) to 18.08, while vanilla LoRA degrades most metrics. On model distillation (COCO2014, 4-step), TSM achieves FID 9.90 with only 68M trainable parameters and 3.7 A100 days, compared to FID 14.58 for DMD2+LoRA (67M params, 3.6 A100 days).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LoRA (the primary baseline), TSM consistently wins on every metric across all three tasks and all four architectures: on domain adaptation, SD3+TSM improves Spatial by +2.63 and Color by +1.04 over SD3+LoRA; on post-pretraining, TSM reverses the degradation caused by LoRA (e.g., PixArt-α Shape drops from 37.96 to 34.74 with LoRA but stays at 37.97 with TSM); on distillation, TSM reduces FID from 14.58 to 9.90. Against GORS (SD2+GORS, a prior LoRA-based domain adaptation method), TSM wins on Color (+11.79), Shape (+7.88), and Texture (+7.19) on SD2. Against DMD2+LoRA for distillation, TSM achieves a substantially lower FID (9.90 vs. 14.58) with nearly identical parameter count (68M vs. 67M) and training cost (3.7 vs. 3.6 A100 days). Against MoE LoRA (Li et al., 2024a), TSM 2-stage (asymmetrical) outperforms both MoE LoRA and the symmetrical TSM variant on most T2I-CompBench categories (Table 13). Against other accelerated diffusion methods (SLAM, DMD2, InstaFlow, etc.), TSM's FID of 9.90 is competitive with SLAM (10.06) and DMD2 (8.35) while using far fewer training resources (3.7 vs. 6 vs. 70 A100 days).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA-based fine-tuning of diffusion models by replacing the single shared low-rank adapter with multiple TimeStep LoRA experts, each trained on a distinct timestep interval. It draws on Mixture-of-Experts principles but departs from symmetric MoE designs (e.g., MixLoRA) by introducing an asymmetrical core-context hierarchy where the finest-granularity expert operates without gating while coarser experts are gated by a timestep-dependent router. The approach is conceptually related to timestep-aware denoising strategies like eDiff-I and DMP, but achieves timestep specialization through parameter-efficient LoRA modules rather than full expert networks or prompt tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode of LoRA in diffusion models: applying identical low-rank matrices across timesteps with fundamentally different noise levels degrades generation quality, particularly in post-pretraining where vanilla LoRA can substantially harm performance. The two-stage fostering-and-assembling paradigm is practical, generalizes across UNet/DiT/MM-DiT architectures and image/video modalities, and requires minimal additional parameters. Open questions include whether the multi-scale interval granularity (n=8 core, n=1 context) is truly optimal or task-dependent, whether the asymmetrical gating design extends to other MoE-based PEFT settings beyond diffusion, and whether the approach scales to the next generation of larger diffusion models with more timesteps.
