## Problem

Kronecker product structure in GP kernel matrices enables efficient computation on gridded data, but this structure is lost whenever any observation is missing from the Cartesian product grid—a common occurrence in real-world time series, climate data, and multi-task settings. Existing work either requires complete grids or falls back to sparse/variational approximations that introduce model error. The paper asks whether exact GP inference can retain Kronecker efficiency in the presence of missing values.

## Input / Output

The method requires data arranged on a partial Cartesian product grid (p spatial locations × q time steps or tasks) with a product kernel, where n ≤ pq observations are available and the remaining pq − n entries are missing. It produces exact GP posterior predictive means and variances computed from 64 pathwise-conditioned posterior samples, along with the full posterior distribution. The key constraint is that the kernel must factorize as k_S(s,s')·k_T(t,t'), and the data must be expressible as a subset of a complete grid rather than arbitrary points in a high-dimensional space.

## Method

The core contribution is expressing the observed n×n covariance matrix as an exact projection of a latent Kronecker product: K_XX = P(K_SS ⊗ K_TT)P^T, where P is a projection matrix that removes rows/columns corresponding to missing values. This is not an approximation—the original covariance is recovered exactly. Combined with iterative linear solvers (conjugate gradients), the Kronecker structure enables fast matrix-vector multiplication in O(p²q + pq²) time and O(p² + q²) space instead of O(n²), with projections implemented efficiently via zero-padding and slice indexing without explicitly instantiating P. Pathwise conditioning is extended with the same projections to draw exact posterior samples, and the paper derives asymptotic break-even points (Proposition 3.1) characterizing when the padding overhead outweighs the Kronecker benefit as the missing ratio γ increases.

## Results

On the Nordic Gridded Climate Dataset (up to 5M observations), LKGP achieves TestRMSE of 0.08–0.09 (temperature) and 0.24–0.28 (precipitation) across 10–50% missing ratios, substantially outperforming SVGP (0.21–0.23 / 0.70–0.72), VNNGP (0.13 / 0.46–0.58), and CaGP (0.18–0.19 / 0.61–0.63). LKGP also achieves the best TestNLL (e.g., −1.14 vs. −0.14 for SVGP on temperature at 10% missing) and requires the least runtime (9.9–28.7 min vs. 83–150 min for SVGP, 88–158 min for VNNGP, and 153–500 min for CaGP). On LCBench learning curve prediction (104K examples per dataset), LKGP achieves the best average TrainRMSE rank (1.17) and TestNLL rank (1.26) while being the fastest method (rank 1.00, ~1.5–2.4 min vs. 6.5 min for SVGP, 26 min for VNNGP, and 7 min for CaGP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVGP (Hensman et al., 2013), LKGP wins decisively on the climate dataset: TestRMSE is 2.6× lower for temperature (0.08 vs. 0.21) and 2.8× lower for precipitation (0.25 vs. 0.71) at 20% missing, with TestNLL improvements of ~1.0 nats. On LCBench, SVGP achieves slightly better TestRMSE (average rank 1.66 vs. 2.60 for LKGP), but LKGP produces the best TestNLL (rank 1.26 vs. 2.54) and runs ~4× faster. Against VNNGP (Wu et al., 2022), LKGP wins on all climate metrics (e.g., TestRMSE 0.08 vs. 0.13 for temperature) and is ~5× faster; on LCBench, VNNGP achieves the worst TestRMSE (rank 3.74) but LKGP still leads on TestNLL and runtime. Against CaGP (Wenger et al., 2024), LKGP dominates on climate data across every metric and missing ratio, with TestRMSE roughly 2–3× lower and runtime 5–50× faster; on LCBench, CaGP edges out LKGP on TestRMSE (rank 2.00 vs. 2.60) but LKGP wins on TestNLL (rank 1.26 vs. 3.00) and is ~3× faster. Overall, LKGP's exact GP inference (no inducing points or nearest-neighbor approximations) yields superior uncertainty calibration, while latent Kronecker structure provides the computational advantage that makes it the fastest method in every experiment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Kronecker-structured GP inference (Bonilla et al., 2007; Stegle et al., 2011) by introducing 'latent Kronecker structure'—expressing the observed covariance matrix as a projection of a full Kronecker product—thereby preserving exact GP inference when missing values break the Cartesian product assumption. It combines this algebraic insight with pathwise conditioning (Wilson et al., 2020; Maddox et al., 2021) and iterative linear system solvers (Gardner et al., 2018a; Wang et al., 2019) to achieve efficient inference without the approximations inherent in sparse/variational GPs (SVGP, VNNGP, CaGP) or structured kernel interpolation (SKI, SKIP). The approach departs from state-space formulations, which require stationary temporal kernels and scale poorly in spatial dimensions, and from High-order GPs, which require complete grids and infer latent variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables exact GP inference on datasets with up to five million observations under missing values, reducing MVM complexity from O(p²q²) to O(p²q + pq²) and space from O(p²q²) to O(p²+q²), while avoiding the underestimation of predictive variances and overestimation of noise that plague sparse GP approximations. The clean theoretical characterization of asymptotic break-even points (Proposition 3.1) gives practitioners a principled criterion for when latent Kronecker structure is beneficial. Key limitations include the requirement for a product kernel (assuming independence across S and T) and data living on a partial grid; open questions include multi-product generalizations, heteroskedastic noise, and whether the approach extends to non-grid data via artificial grid construction.
