## Problem

Existing multi-modal object ReID methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they perform cross-modal interaction only at high-level semantic feature spaces, neglecting the inherent pixel-to-pixel alignment between aligned multi-modal images, and they struggle to balance modality-shared and modality-specific representations within a single unified architecture—either losing modality-specific discriminative features in a shared backbone or incurring heavy parameter redundancy with separate per-modality networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-aligned RGB, NIR, and TIR image triples (resized to 256×128 for person ReID on RGBNT201 and 128×256 for vehicle ReID on RGBNT100/MSVR310) and produces a single object feature embedding per identity for ranking-based retrieval. A notable property is that at test time, if one or more modalities are missing, the FFM automatically reconstructs the absent modality's features from the available ones via conditional reconstruction rules, requiring no architectural changes or dedicated training for the missing-modality scenario.

## Method

MFRNet builds on a CLIP-pretrained ViT-base backbone and introduces two MoE-based modules. The Feature Fusion Module (FFM), placed before the ViT, performs pixel-level cross-modal interaction by regenerating each modality's patch features as a weighted combination of its own features and features generated from other modalities via sparse mixture-of-generators (simple two-convolution experts selected by a cosine-similarity router with top-1 gating). The Feature Representation Module (FRM), inserted at the final ViT layer, replaces the standard residual MLP with a MoE-enhanced RepAdapter (two 1×1 convolutions) that dynamically routes tokens to different experts, preserving modality-specific representations while sharing the backbone parameters. Training combines label-smoothing cross-entropy and triplet losses on the backbone with auxiliary importance and load-balancing losses to ensure uniform expert utilization across all MoE layers.

## Results

On RGBNT201 (multi-modal person ReID), MFRNet achieves 80.7% mAP and 83.6% R-1, surpassing the previous best (TOP-ReID) by 8.4% mAP and 6.9% R-1. On RGBNT100 (vehicle ReID), it reaches 88.2% mAP and 97.4% R-1, improving over RSCNet by 5.9% mAP. On MSVR310, MFRNet attains 50.6% mAP and 64.8% R-1, outperforming the best prior methods by 11.1% mAP and 9.6% R-1. Notably, MFRNet achieves these gains with only 57.1M parameters and 22.1G FLOPs, compared to 278.2M/34.5G for TOP-ReID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFRNet outperforms all 27 compared baselines across all three datasets. On RGBNT201, it surpasses the strongest prior multi-modal method TOP-ReID (72.3% mAP) by 8.4% mAP and 6.9% R-1, and beats EDITOR (66.5% mAP) and RSCNet (68.2% mAP) by 14.2% and 12.5% mAP respectively. On RGBNT100, MFRNet (88.2% mAP) exceeds RSCNet (82.3%) by 5.9% mAP and TOP-ReID (81.2%) by 7.0% mAP. On MSVR310, the gains are largest: MFRNet (50.6% mAP, 64.8% R-1) beats RSCNet (39.5% mAP) by 11.1% mAP and CCNet (55.2% R-1) by 9.6% R-1. All single-modal baselines (PCB, OSNet, TransReID, AGW, etc.) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of multi-modal fusion. Importantly, MFRNet achieves these results with 4.9× fewer parameters than TOP-ReID (57.1M vs. 278.2M) and 36% fewer FLOPs (22.1G vs. 34.5G), demonstrating that the sparse MoE design provides both accuracy and efficiency gains simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFRNet extends the sparse Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (Jacobs et al., 1991; Li et al., 2022) from NLP and domain generalization into multi-modal object ReID, deploying MoE in two distinct roles: generation experts for pixel-level cross-modal feature reconstruction (FFM) and representation experts for modality-specific feature extraction (FRM). The FFM is conceptually grounded in the RLE observation (Tan et al., 2024c) that cross-spectral transformation is locally linear with surface-dependent variation, motivating multiple simple generators over a single complex one. The FRM builds directly on RepAdapter (Luo et al., 2023), replacing its single adapter with a MoE-routed ensemble. A key departure from prior multi-modal ReID methods (TOP-ReID, EDITOR, RSCNet) is the insistence on pre-ViT pixel-level fusion rather than post-ViT semantic-level fusion, which the ablations show preserves fine-grained spatial alignment that higher-level representations lose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 57M-parameter model can outperform a 278M-parameter baseline (TOP-ReID) by 8.4% mAP on RGBNT201, suggesting that architectural efficiency in multi-modal ReID has been underexplored relative to brute-force parameter scaling. The finding that pre-fusion (before ViT) substantially outperforms post-fusion (after ViT) challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that high-level semantic fusion is the appropriate level for cross-modal interaction. However, the modality-missing results reveal significant degradation when two of three modalities are absent (e.g., 37.2% mAP for M(NIR+TIR) vs 80.7% full), and the framework has only been validated on RGB+NIR+TIR triplets, leaving open whether the MoE routing generalizes to arbitrary modality combinations or larger modality sets. The absence of a dedicated training procedure for modality-missing scenarios is a practical advantage, but the performance gap versus methods with explicit missing-modality training (e.g., DENet) in certain configurations warrants further investigation.
