## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) exhibits two poorly understood phenomena: robust overfitting, where test robust accuracy degrades after a peak while training robust loss continues to decrease, and the consistent benefit of soft-label methods like knowledge distillation. The underlying training dynamics of AT—specifically which features the model learns and when it abandons them—remain an open research problem.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a trained DNN classifier (decomposed into a feature extractor g and a linear classification layer W), a test dataset, and a perturbation bound ε. For each class, it crafts adversarial examples via untargeted PGD, computes per-sample attribution vectors as the element-wise product g(x+δ)⊙W[y], averages them per class, and produces a K×K cosine-similarity correlation matrix C. The scalar CAS score (sum of positive off-diagonal entries of C) quantifies overall cross-class feature usage at a given checkpoint.

## Method

The paper introduces a class-wise feature attribution framework that decomposes model features into cross-class (shared across multiple classes) and class-specific (exclusive to one class) components, measured via cosine similarity of averaged attribution vectors on adversarial examples. Empirically, across architectures (PreActResNet-18, DeiT-Ti), datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet), and perturbation norms (ℓ∞, ℓ₂), the model learns more cross-class features up to the best-robustness checkpoint, then progressively abandons them during robust overfitting. Theoretically, a synthetic 3-class model with decoupled cross-class and class-specific features proves that cross-class features are more sensitive to the robust loss (eliminated at smaller ε thresholds than class-specific features) yet monotonically improve robust classification probability, and that label smoothing raises the ε threshold at which cross-class features are discarded. This unified mechanism explains both robust overfitting (forgetting of cross-class features) and the advantage of soft-label training (preservation of cross-class features through softened targets).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ℓ∞-AT (ε=8/255, PreActResNet-18), the best checkpoint achieves 47.8% robust accuracy with CAS=25.6, while the last (overfitted) checkpoint drops to 42.5% RA with CAS=9.0, confirming that robust overfitting coincides with a 65% reduction in cross-class feature usage. Scaling ε from 2/255 to 8/255 increases the CAS gap between best and last checkpoints from 8.9 to 16.6, showing larger perturbations exacerbate cross-class feature forgetting. Knowledge distillation AT preserves cross-class features (CAS remains ~25.7 at best vs. 24.1 at last), explaining its 41%→48% robust accuracy improvement over vanilla AT on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed defense against competing baselines. Instead, it compares AT checkpoints against each other: the best-robustness checkpoint consistently uses more cross-class features (higher CAS) than the overfitted last checkpoint across all tested settings (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet; ℓ∞ and ℓ2 norms; CNN and DeiT-Ti Transformer). Comparing perturbation bounds, ε=8/255 produces a ΔCAS of 16.6 between best and last checkpoints versus only 8.9 for ε=2/255, confirming that larger ε drives more aggressive cross-class feature abandonment. Knowledge distillation AT is compared to vanilla one-hot AT: the KD-trained model shows minimal CAS change between best and last checkpoints (25.7→24.1) versus a dramatic drop in vanilla AT (25.6→9.0), explaining KD's mitigation of robust overfitting. Standard training (no adversarial perturbations) yields CAS values around 7–10, far below AT's >20, and shows no significant CAS change across epochs, consistent with the absence of robust overfitting in standard training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust/non-robust feature decomposition framework of Tsipras et al. (2019) by introducing an orthogonal axis: cross-class vs. class-specific features, and proposes a quantitative metric (CAS) to measure their usage via feature attribution correlation matrices. It positions itself within the growing literature on interpreting adversarial training dynamics (feature visualization, coverage analysis) but departs from prior work by providing a unified mechanistic explanation for two previously separate phenomena—robust overfitting (Rice et al., 2020) and the advantage of soft-label training (Chen et al., 2021)—through a single feature-forgetting mechanism. The theoretical analysis adapts the synthetic data model from Tsipras et al. but restructures it around the cross-class/class-specific distinction rather than the robust/non-robust split.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified explanation that robust overfitting stems from the model *forgetting* useful cross-class features (rather than merely memorizing training noise) reframes how practitioners think about AT dynamics and suggests concrete intervention points: preserving cross-class features through label smoothing, adaptive data sampling, or architecture design. Limitations include the highly simplified 3-class, 6-dimensional linear theoretical model, and the empirical evidence being primarily correlational (CAS tracks robustness but does not establish causation). Open questions include whether cross-class features can be explicitly regularized or preserved in deeper networks, whether the framework extends to non-visual or multi-task settings, and whether the observed dynamics hold under different optimization schedules or larger-scale training.
