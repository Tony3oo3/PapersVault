## Problem

Prior work on experimentation with interference has focused on the quality of final outputs (estimator MSE, p-values) while overlooking cumulative performance over the experiment horizon. This paper introduces the Multi-Armed Bandits with Interference (MABI) problem, where a learner must maximize cumulative reward over T rounds while each unit's reward depends on the treatments of all N spatially-located units, with interference decaying in distance and reward functions chosen adversarially.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251073/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N units with known fixed locations in a bounding box of size O(√N)×O(√N), a set of k arms, and a time horizon T. Each round the learner outputs an arm assignment Z_t ∈ [k]^N (one arm per unit) and observes bandit feedback Y_ut(Z_t) for every unit u. The reward functions are defined on the full hypercube [k]^N (not just [k]) and must satisfy a ψ-decaying interference property. The output is a sequence of arm assignments plus theoretical regret bounds: an optimal Õ(√(kT)) expected regret and a high-probability bound whose tail mass vanishes as N→∞.

## Method

The core policy (EXP3-HT-IX) integrates three components: (1) a Robust Random Partition (RRP) that starts from a uniform grid clustering and randomly reassigns boundary strips and quads to neighboring clusters, boosting the exposure probability from p^O(1) to Ω(p) and reducing variance from the specific clustering; (2) a Horvitz-Thompson estimator augmented with an implicit exploration (IX) parameter β in the propensity score, which truncates the estimator and interpolates between Leung's HT estimator for spatial interference and Kocák et al.'s IX estimator for adversarial bandits; and (3) the standard EXP3 multiplicative-weights update on the estimated rewards. Each round, the RRP is regenerated, arms are assigned to clusters independently according to the current exponential weights, and the HT-IX estimator provides unbiased (up to O(β) bias) estimates of the counterfactual mean reward for each arm. A nine-coloring of clusters ensures independence within color classes, enabling a Cramér-Chernoff concentration argument that yields the high-probability regret bound with tail mass vanishing in N.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax expected regret for MABI is Õ(√(kT)), matching a matching lower bound Ω(√(kT)) for any policy (Theorem 3.3). The proposed EXP3-IX-HT policy with Robust Random Partition achieves an optimal expected regret and, crucially, a high-probability regret bound whose tail mass vanishes as N→∞ (Theorem 4.6). Under no interference, the regret is (1 + √(1/N)·log(1/δ))·Reg_OPT w.p. 1−δ; under κ-neighborhood interference it becomes (1 + κ√(T/N)·log(1/δ))·Reg_OPT; and under power-law interference ψ(r)=O(r^{−c}) with c=2, the excess regret is Õ(√(kT)) + k·T^{9/10}/N^{2/5}·√(log(1/δ)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no standard benchmark baselines; the primary comparison is between switchback (SB) policies and the proposed clustered randomization (CR) policy. Theoretically, both achieve the same Õ(√(kT)) expected regret, but SB's tail mass is independent of N (P[Reg_SB − Reg_OPT > τ] ≤ e^{−τ}), while CR's tail mass decays as e^{−N^c·τ}, making CR substantially more robust for large N. In the synthetic experiments (Appendix E, N=T² and N=T³, T=10–50, 2-armed, 5-neighbor interference), CR consistently outperforms SB in 95th-percentile regret without sacrificing mean regret. The gap widens as N grows: when N=T³, the 95th-percentile regret of CR is lower than in the N=T² case, confirming the theoretical prediction that larger market size reduces estimation variance. The paper also notes that the lower bound (Theorem 3.3) shows no policy can beat Ω(√(kT)) in expectation, so the advantage of CR is purely in the tail behavior rather than the mean.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the EXP3-IX framework for adversarial bandits (Auer et al. 1995; Kocák et al. 2014; Neu 2015) and Leung's (2022) rate-optimal cluster-randomized designs for spatial interference in causal inference. The HT-IX estimator is the conceptual bridge, reducing to Leung's HT estimator when β=0 and to Neu's EXP3-IX estimator when N=1. The Robust Random Partition (RRP) is a novel design element that departs from both the uniform clustering in Leung (2022) and the switchback policies in Bojinov et al. (2023), specifically to handle the variance explosion that arises when arm probabilities become small under multiplicative weight updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for clustered switchback experiments, a policy class widely deployed at companies like DoorDash but previously lacking formal guarantees. The clean dichotomy—expected regret is N-independent (a negative result showing market size cannot help in expectation) while high-probability regret vanishes as N→∞—has direct practical implications since real platforms have N orders of magnitude larger than T. Open questions include extending the analysis to network-based (non-Euclidean) interference structures, incorporating context-dependent rewards, and tightening the gap between the upper and lower bounds in the high-probability regime.
