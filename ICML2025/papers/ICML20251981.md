## Problem

State-of-the-art image reconstruction relies on complex, heavily parameterized deep architectures that lack interpretability, convergence guarantees, and easy adaptability to new inverse problems. While classic Tikhonov regularization is principled and interpretable, its regularizers are hand-designed and do not capture the rich structure of natural images. The paper asks whether a data-driven regularizer can be built on the Tikhonov template while retaining the theoretical guarantees and simplicity of the classical approach.

## Input / Output

DEAL is trained on denoising (H = I) with AWGN of σ_n ∈ [0, 50] on 128×128 patches, but at inference it accepts any forward operator H and its adjoint H^T along with two scalar hyperparameters (model noise level σ and regularization strength λ) tuned on a small validation set. The iterative refinement starts from x_0 = 0 and terminates when the relative change falls below ε_out, producing a single reconstructed image of the same spatial dimensions as the unknown. The method handles grayscale, RGB, and multi-coil MRI data without architectural changes.

## Method

DEAL iteratively refines reconstructions by solving a sequence of quadratic problems: at each step k, it minimizes ‖Hx − y‖² + λ‖M(x_k)Wx‖², where W is a learned Multi-Conv filter bank (three 9×9 convolutions, 128 output channels, spectral-normalized) and M(x_k) is a spatially varying attention mask produced by a shallow CNN with learnable linear-spline nonlinearities. The key design choice is quadratic potentials ψ_c(·) = (·)², which makes each update a linear system A_k x_{k+1} = b solvable by conjugate gradient, in contrast to the LASSO subproblems required by the prior L1-based SAFI method. The attention mechanism is multiplicative (pointwise) rather than key-query based: M suppresses the regularization penalty at locations with strong filter responses (edges, textures), effectively preserving salient structures. Training uses the deep equilibrium framework in Jacobian-free mode, with a custom backward pass that avoids storing CG iterates by reformulating the gradient as another linear system solved by CG. Theoretical results establish uniqueness of each update, existence of a fixed point, and exponential convergence under a contractivity condition.

## Results

On grayscale denoising (BSD68, σ_n=25), DEAL achieves 29.16 dB PSNR, outperforming all spatially adaptive regularizers (SAFI 29.13, SARR 29.13, WCRR 28.68) and BM3D (28.61), while remaining within 0.32 dB of DRUNet (29.48) despite having 30× fewer parameters (0.85M vs. 32.64M). On color denoising (CBSD68, σ_n=25), DEAL reaches 31.31 dB, surpassing ProxDRUNet (31.14) and DnCNN (31.24). For 4-fold single-coil MRI reconstruction, DEAL achieves the best PSNR of 36.59 dB (PD), beating SAFI (36.43), SARR (36.25), and ProxDRUNet (36.20), while running ~30× faster than SAFI (14 s vs. 436 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAL outperforms all spatially adaptive regularizers (WCRR, SARR, SAFI) on denoising by 0.03–0.48 dB, confirming that iterative attention refinement over a single weight update yields measurable gains. Against the strongest deep baselines, DEAL trails DRUNet by 0.32 dB (gray, σ=25) and 0.38 dB (color, σ=25), but beats ProxDRUNet on color denoising (31.31 vs. 31.14) and is within 0.02 dB on grayscale. On 4-fold single-coil MRI, DEAL achieves the highest PSNR (36.59 dB PD) among all universal methods, surpassing SAFI by 0.16 dB and ProxDRUNet by 0.39 dB, while running in 14 s versus SAFI's 436 s. For superresolution with noise (s=2, σ_n=2.55), DEAL (27.99 dB) matches Prox-PnP (27.93) and DPIR (27.79) but substantially outperforms SwinIR (24.56), which degrades severely under kernel mismatch. Overall, DEAL provides a strong complexity–performance tradeoff: 0.85M parameters versus 32.64M for DRUNet-based methods, with competitive or superior results across all evaluated inverse problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEAL extends the fields-of-experts regularizer (Roth & Black, 2009) by replacing the L1 potentials used in SAFI (Pourya et al., 2024) with quadratic potentials, turning each iterative update into a linear system solvable by conjugate gradients. The spatially varying attention weights M are iteratively refined from the current reconstruction rather than computed once, and the entire scheme is interpreted as an infinite-depth network trained via the deep equilibrium framework (Bai et al., 2019). The filter architecture reuses the Multi-Conv block from Goujon et al. (2023), and all nonlinearities employ learnable linear splines (Bohra et al., 2020). Conceptually, the paper departs from the plug-and-play paradigm by explicitly modeling the regularizer and its spatial adaptivity, rather than relying on a black-box denoiser as a proximal operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEAL offers a principled bridge between classic Tikhonov regularization and deep learning, providing convergence guarantees (uniqueness, fixed-point existence, exponential convergence under contractivity) that are difficult to obtain for PnP or end-to-end methods. The adaptive-averaging interpretation—where each pixel is a structure-aware weighted average of the measurements—gives a level of interpretability absent in competing deep denoisers, and the method's universality (trained on denoising, deployed on SR/MRI/deblurring with only two scalar hyperparameters) is practically attractive. Limitations include training only on denoising, restriction to L2 data fidelity, and a very conservative Lipschitz bound that does not yet certify convergence in all regimes. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on task-specific data further improves performance and how to extend the framework to non-quadratic data-fidelity terms.
