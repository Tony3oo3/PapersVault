## Problem

Existing regret bounds for time-varying multiplayer games depend on measures of payoff variation (e.g., path length of Nash equilibria or variation of payoff matrices) that grow linearly with the horizon even when the underlying dynamics are fully deterministic and trivially predictable. This makes the guarantees vacuous in precisely the settings where agents could, in principle, forecast future payoffs. The paper asks how the quality of agents' predictions about future states of nature affects social welfare and convergence to equilibrium in uncoupled online learning.

## Input / Output

At each round t, each agent j observes covariates X_t^j, runs an online predictor to produce a predicted state of nature Ẑ_t^j from a finite set Z = {z_1,…,z_m}, and then selects a mixed strategy w_t^j ∈ Δ_K. After the joint action is realized, the agent receives the full cost matrix Φ^j(w_t^{-j}) ∈ R^{d×K} (encoding expected costs of each pure action given opponents' strategies) and the actual state Z_t, which are used to update both the strategy and the predictor. The key constraint is that the prediction must be made before the action is chosen, and the number of mispredictions L_T^j = Σ_t 1{Ẑ_t^j ≠ Z_t} directly enters the regret bound.

## Method

POMWU (Predictive Optimistic MWU) maintains one optimistic MWU instance per context z ∈ Z, each with its own weight vector ρ_z and optimistic matrix Ψ_z. At round t, the agent predicts Ẑ_t^j, retrieves the corresponding instance to compute the optimistic strategy, plays it, then updates only the instance indexed by the revealed state Z_t. The core theoretical contribution is a contextual RVU bound (Proposition 5) that decomposes regret along context-specific subsequences rather than the full timeline, yielding a bound that depends on the number of mispredictions L_T^j and the within-context variation of payoffs, rather than the total path length. Under realizability (bounded L_T^j via Littlestone-dimension-based online classification), the regret matches the O(T^{1/4}J^{1/2}) rate of static-game OMWU. A contextual Blum-Mansour reduction (Algorithm 2) converts the external regret guarantee into a swap regret guarantee at a factor-K cost, enabling convergence to contextual correlated equilibrium rather than merely coarse correlated equilibrium.

## Results

On the Sioux Falls routing problem (91 agents, 5 states of nature, T=2×10⁴ rounds), POMWU achieves sublinear average regret over agents, while the naive OMWU baseline (which ignores states of nature) exhibits linearly growing regret. The empirical average policy of POMWU converges to a 10⁻³-approximate coarse correlated equilibrium, as visualized by the stable agent distribution across network edges per context. The online logistic regression predictor's average error decreases slowly over time, and the proportion of regret attributable to mispredicted contexts grows, confirming that POMWU converges on each individual context. Theoretically, under realizability (bounded prediction errors L_T = O(1)), POMWU recovers the static-game guarantees of Syrgkanis et al. (2015): individual regret O(T^{1/4} J^{1/2}) and social cost within a price-of-anarchy factor of optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares POMWU against a naive OMWU algorithm that ignores the state of nature (i.e., treats the game as static). On the Sioux Falls routing experiment, POMWU clearly outperforms OMWU: POMWU's average regret over agents remains sublinear and bounded, while OMWU's contextual regret grows linearly with the horizon T due to its inability to adapt to different payoff structures across contexts. This gap is the central empirical finding, directly validating the paper's theoretical claim that prediction-aware learning recovers static-game performance when predictions are accurate. The comparison is qualitative (regret curves in Figure 2) rather than a single-point numerical benchmark, but the separation between sublinear and linear regret is unambiguous. No other algorithmic baselines (e.g., contextual bandit methods or social learning algorithms) are empirically compared, as the paper's contribution is primarily theoretical with a single illustrative experiment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual games framework of Sessa et al. (2021) and the optimistic MWU algorithm of Daskalakis et al. (2021) by introducing a prediction step: agents forecast the state of nature before acting, rather than observing it. It departs from the time-varying game literature (Zhang et al. 2022; Anagnostides et al. 2024), which bounds regret by variation measures (P_T, V_T) that become vacuous for predictable dynamics, by instead tying regret to prediction errors via a new contextual RVU bound. The Blum-Mansour reduction is extended to the contextual setting to bridge external and swap regret, and the Roughgarden smoothness condition is adapted to the contextual case for social welfare guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: existing time-varying game bounds grow linearly in T even for perfectly predictable payoff sequences, while the prediction-aware framework recovers static-game guarantees under bounded prediction errors (e.g., realizability). This is practically relevant as predictive models are ubiquitous in economic and traffic-routing applications. Open questions include extending to bandit feedback, infinite context spaces, and analyzing how collaborative/federated prediction among agents affects equilibrium convergence — the authors note that shared predictions implicitly coordinate policies even in uncoupled settings.
