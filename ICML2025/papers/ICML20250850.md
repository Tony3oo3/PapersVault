## Problem

Entropy minimization (EM) methods for test-time adaptation are computationally efficient but suffer from model collapse in continual test-time adaptation (CTTA), where the model converges to a trivial solution predicting a single class for all inputs. Existing stability-focused alternatives (consistency regularization with teacher-student frameworks) avoid collapse but require multiple models, numerous forward passes, and substantial memory, contradicting the efficiency goal of TTA. The paper addresses the gap between EM's efficiency and CR's stability in the CTTA setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled RGB target image (resized to 224×224 for ImageNet-based experiments, 384×384 for CIFAR-based experiments) and produces a class prediction over the source domain's label set. Internally, the input is processed three times per adaptation step: once unmasked and twice with progressively higher masking ratios (default 0%, 5%, 10%), where masking is applied to ViT patches identified as object-containing via self-attention scores. Only normalization layer parameters (0.03M) are updated, and the method requires just 3 forward passes per sample with a single stored model.

## Method

REM constructs an explicit difficulty ranking over predictions by exploiting ViT self-attention to identify and mask object-containing patches at increasing ratios, creating a mask chain where higher masking yields higher entropy and lower accuracy. Two complementary losses operate on this ranked structure: (1) a Masked Consistency Loss (MCL) that applies cross-entropy between predictions at different masking levels with stop-gradient on the less-masked prediction, indirectly reducing entropy while teaching the model to recover occluded content; and (2) an Entropy Ranking Loss (ERL) that enforces via a hinge loss that entropy of less-masked predictions remains below that of more-masked predictions, preventing overconfidence and background bias. The total loss is L_MCL + λ·L_ERL, and adaptation updates only normalization layers in a single model, preserving EM's computational efficiency (3 forward passes, no teacher model) while gaining CR-like stability against model collapse.

## Results

On ImageNet-to-ImageNetC under the CTTA protocol, REM achieves a mean classification error of 39.2% (16.6% relative gain over the source model), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Continual-MAE (42.5%) by 3.3% and closing the gap to the supervised upper bound (35.7%) to just 3.5%. On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C, REM reaches 9.4% mean error (18.8% gain), outperforming Continual-MAE (12.6%) by 3.2%. In terms of efficiency, REM requires only 0.03M trainable parameters, 3 forward passes, and 17 minutes of total computation, compared to 86.5M parameters, 12 forward passes, and 60 minutes for Continual-MAE—a 3.4× speedup with 0.03% of the trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REM outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the primary CTTA benchmarks. Against the closest CTTA competitors, REM beats Continual-MAE by 3.3% (39.2% vs. 42.5% mean error on ImageNetC) and ViDA by 4.2% (39.2% vs. 43.4%), while also surpassing SAR (45.2%), PETAL (52.3%), CoTTA (54.8%), and Tent (51.0%) by substantial margins. On CIFAR-10C, the advantage over Continual-MAE widens to 3.2% (9.4% vs. 12.6%), and on CIFAR-100C REM leads Continual-MAE by 3.0% (23.4% vs. 26.4%). In online TTA scenarios (imbalanced label shifts, batch size 1, mixed shifts, and domain shifts to ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch), REM consistently achieves the best mean accuracy among all EM-based methods (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO, MEMO, FOA), e.g., 65.1% vs. DeYO's 61.2% under imbalanced label shifts. The only method REM does not surpass is the supervised upper bound, which is expected since target labels are unavailable in TTA. Notably, REM achieves these gains while using only 0.03M trainable parameters and 3 forward passes per sample, compared to 86–94M parameters and 12 forward passes for CR-based methods like Continual-MAE and ViDA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established CTTA paradigms: entropy minimization (Tent, SAR, EATA) and consistency regularization (CoTTA, ViDA, Continual-MAE). It departs from EM by replacing the single entropy objective with a ranked structure built on explicit object masking, and departs from CR by eliminating the teacher-student framework in favor of a single model with self-attention-guided patch masking (borrowed from Token Merging and ViT distillation work). The key conceptual move is reframing the augmentation problem: rather than applying stochastic data augmentations to generate diverse views (as in CR), REM uses attention scores to identify and progressively mask object regions, creating an interpretable difficulty hierarchy that simultaneously serves as the basis for both a consistency loss and an entropy ranking constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the efficiency-stability gap in CTTA: REM achieves state-of-the-art accuracy (39.2% mean error on ImageNetC, within 3.5% of the supervised upper bound) while updating only 0.03M normalization parameters in a single model, compared to 86–93M parameters and multiple models in prior CR-based SOTA. This makes real-time, resource-constrained deployment of continual adaptation far more feasible. The main limitation acknowledged is the lack of theoretical guarantee that masking objects always increases entropy, and the authors note that domains with small original-vs-masked distribution gaps (e.g., brightness, JPEG) show weaker gains, suggesting adaptive loss weighting as a promising future direction. The plug-and-play applicability to CLIP, CNNs, and diverse ViT architectures broadens the method's relevance beyond the ViT-B/16 setting.
