## Problem

Data-based personalized learning methods implicitly assume balanced, high-quality annotated data, yet real-world educational benchmarks exhibit severe long-tail distributions that degrade LLM performance. The neural collapse phenomenon, which characterizes optimal feature geometry in vision, has never been extended to text-based LLMs or applied to the class-imbalance challenge in personalized learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250952/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text inputs—math word problems (TMWPL, 7 cognitive-dimension classes) or teacher-student dialogue utterances (PMTD, 8 dialogue-behavior classes)—and produces a single class label per input. Internally, the TC loss operates on hidden-layer text representations h_l(t_i) with a fixed-length norm constraint ‖h_l(t_i)‖₂ ≤ √T_W, and the final prediction is extracted by selecting the top-N generated tokens from the LoRA-fine-tuned LLM rather than a dedicated classifier head.

## Method

NCAL introduces a Text-modality Collapse (TC) regularization that explicitly drives cross-class text-embedding inner products toward the simplex ETF target value T_W·μ (where μ = −1/(N−1)), penalizing deviations via a pairwise squared-loss over all cross-class pairs in a batch. This TC loss is added to the standard task loss (L = L_Task + λ·L_TC) and optimized jointly with LoRA low-rank adapters (ΔW = BA, r ≪ min(d,k)) on frozen LLM weights, making the approach model-agnostic across architectures. The key theoretical insight is that under class imbalance, the inter-class repulsion term in the task gradient dominates for minority classes, distorting the feature geometry; the TC loss provides a frequency-independent corrective force that restores equiangular separation and prevents majority-class representations from monopolizing the embedding space. The method is validated across Chinese (Qwen2.5, DeepSeek) and English (Llama3.1, Vicuna) LLMs at 7B–16B scale, showing consistent 5–13 point accuracy gains over LoRA-only baselines under τ = 0.03–0.25 imbalance ratios.

## Results

On the TMWPL dataset, NCAL-Qwen2.5-Instruct (7B) achieves 74.86% accuracy and 75.58% Macro-F1, surpassing the 14B Qwen2.5-Instruct baseline (65.14% Acc) by nearly 10 points. On PMTD, the same 7B NCAL model reaches 70.31% accuracy and 67.43% Macro-F1, outperforming the 14B baseline (66.56% Acc) by over 3 points. NCAL-Llama3.1-Instruct (8B) also improves substantially over its base, reaching 69.43% Acc on TMWPL versus 61.43% for the unmodified Llama3.1-Instruct. Notably, the 7B NCAL model outperforms all larger baselines (up to 16B DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat), demonstrating that the TC regularization yields gains that exceed what additional parameters provide.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCAL consistently outperforms all baselines across both datasets and all model families. On TMWPL, NCAL-Qwen2.5-Instruct (7B) achieves 74.86% Acc, beating Qwen2.5-Instruct 14B (65.14%) by 9.72 points and DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat 16B (65.14%) by 9.72 points. On PMTD, the same model reaches 70.31% Acc versus 66.56% for Qwen2.5-Instruct 14B and 61.56% for DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat 16B. In the English-focused group, NCAL-Llama3.1-Instruct (8B) improves over Llama3.1-Instruct (8B) by 8.00 points on TMWPL (69.43 vs. 61.43) and by 1.88 points on PMTD (67.50 vs. 65.62), while also surpassing the larger Vicuna1.5 13B on both datasets. For math-oriented models, NCAL-Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct (7B) gains 7.15 points over Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct (7B) on TMWPL (74.86 vs. 67.71) and 3.43 points on PMTD (63.12 vs. 59.69). The paper also notes an interesting task-model interaction: reasoning-specialized models (Qwen2.5-Math, DeepSeek-Math) excel on the math classification task (TMWPL), while instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-Instruct) perform better on dialogue classification (PMTD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse phenomenon—originally characterized in vision for balanced datasets (Papyan et al., 2020) and subsequently explored in imbalanced learning, federated learning, and transfer learning—to text-based LLMs and the domain of personalized learning. It integrates a text-modality collapse (TC) regularization loss directly into the LoRA fine-tuning pipeline (Hu et al., 2021), positioning itself as the first work to impose simplex ETF structure on LLM text representations. The paper departs from prior NC research, which has been largely confined to linear classifiers in vision, by formulating the collapse constraint over transformer hidden states in a language model context and coupling it with parameter-efficient adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: educational benchmarks are inherently long-tail due to privacy constraints and expert-annotation costs, yet prior personalized learning work implicitly assumed balanced, high-quality data. The model-agnostic TC loss is simple to implement and yields substantial gains (e.g., 61→75 Acc on TMWPL), suggesting the ETF-regularization idea could transfer to other long-tail text classification settings. However, evidence is limited to two small Chinese math-education datasets, the theoretical analysis is largely heuristic rather than a formal guarantee, and it remains open whether the pairwise inner-product constraint generalizes to larger-scale or multilingual educational tasks, or to non-classification personalized learning objectives like knowledge tracing.
