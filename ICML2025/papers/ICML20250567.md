## Problem

MoE models face severe deployment challenges from their large parameter counts and heterogeneous expert activation patterns, yet existing mixed-precision quantization methods either optimize accuracy or performance in isolation and lack system-level support for executing precision-heterogeneous expert computations. No prior work jointly considers parameter sensitivity, expert activation dynamics, and hardware resources when allocating bitwidths, nor provides efficient GPU kernels for parallel mixed-precision Group-GEMM execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250567/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MxMoE takes as input the full-precision MoE model weights, a small calibration set (128 sequences of length 4096 from WikiText-2) used to compute per-linear-block quantization sensitivity and expert activation frequencies, and hardware specifications (supported quantization schemes, SM count, tile profiling data). It produces a per-linear-block mixed-precision quantized model (with GPTQ-quantized weights and dynamically quantized activations) along with an automatically generated CUDA kernel that fuses heterogeneous-precision GEMMs into a single parallel Group-GEMM execution stream with a precision-aware tile scheduler.

## Method

MxMoE is built on two empirical insights: linear blocks within an MoE layer exhibit significantly different quantization sensitivity (motivating linear-block-level rather than expert-level bitwidth allocation), and expert activation frequencies vary by over 10×, creating coexisting memory-bound and compute-bound operations within a single MoE block. The bitwidth allocation is formulated as an integer linear program that jointly minimizes a weighted product of quantization loss (Euclidean output perturbation per linear block) and estimated execution time (sum of pre-profiled tile costs divided by SM count), subject to a memory budget constraint, with a hyperparameter r controlling the accuracy–performance trade-off. After solving the ILP, weights are quantized via GPTQ following random Hadamard transformations (from QuaRot), and activations are dynamically quantized at runtime. For system support, MxMoE automatically generates mixed-precision Group-GEMM kernels by composing configurable CTA-level micro-kernels (each hand-tuned for a specific quantization scheme) under two constraints—uniform warp count across fused CTAs and shared-memory allocation at the maximum requirement—then applies a greedy tile-scheduling heuristic to minimize makespan across SMs, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of hand-writing a kernel per precision combination.

## Results

At 2.25-bit weight-only quantization, MxMoE achieves 2.4 lower Wikitext-2 perplexity than GPTQ on DeepSeekV2-Lite (7.01 vs. 8.49) and 2.4 lower on Qwen1.5-MoE (8.79 vs. 11.19), while also improving average benchmark accuracy by 4.0 and 6.3 points respectively. At 5-bit weight-activation quantization, MxMoE delivers up to 29.4% throughput improvement over W8A8 (QuaRot) while maintaining comparable perplexity to full-precision models (e.g., Qwen2-MoE: 6.25 vs. 5.84 baseline). Overall, MxMoE achieves up to 3.4× speedup over full-precision inference in compute-bound scenarios and 1.6–2.7× in memory-bound scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ* (uniform bitwidth, group-128 asymmetric), MxMoE consistently wins at 2.25-bit across all four models: perplexity improves from 8.49→7.01 (DeepSeekV2-Lite), 11.19→8.79 (Qwen1.5-MoE), 7.98→7.57 (Qwen2-MoE), and 5.69→5.63 (Mixtral), with corresponding average accuracy gains of 4.0, 6.3, 6.0, and 0.3 points. At 3.25-bit, MxMoE edges out GPTQ* on three of four models (e.g., DeepSeekV2-Lite PPL 6.08 vs. 6.18) but slightly trails on Qwen2-MoE (6.18 vs. 6.11), which the authors attribute to sensitivity-statistic inaccuracies amplified by inter-layer dependencies. Against QuaRot (uniform 4-bit weight-activation with Hadamard rotation), MxMoE at 5-bit average achieves dramatically lower perplexity on every model: 6.16 vs. 8.44 (DeepSeekV2-Lite), 7.01 vs. 18.44 (Qwen1.5-MoE), 6.25 vs. 110.66 (Qwen2-MoE), and 4.20 vs. 9.06 (Mixtral), while also delivering up to 29.4% higher throughput than the W8A8 uniform baseline at equivalent accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mixed-precision quantization lineage (HAQ, HAWQ) to the MoE setting, departing from prior MoE-specific quantization work (Li et al. 2024, Huang et al. 2024) that allocates bitwidths at the expert level by instead operating at the finer linear-block granularity. It builds directly on GPTQ for the underlying weight quantization and QuaRot's Hadamard transformations for outlier mitigation, but introduces a novel ILP-based co-design objective that jointly optimizes quantization loss and hardware execution time using roofline-derived cost models. On the systems side, it addresses a gap left by existing Group-GEMM implementations (CUTLASS, VLLM-Marlin-MoE) that cannot handle heterogeneous-precision expert computations, proposing an automated micro-kernel fusion and tile-scheduling framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (DeepSeek-V3, Mixtral, Qwen2-MoE) become the dominant paradigm for frontier LLMs, the gap between theoretical quantization gains and actual wall-clock speedup is a critical deployment bottleneck; MxMoE's algorithm-system co-design directly targets this gap. Key limitations include evaluation on a single GPU (RTX-4090), reliance on offline calibration (minutes to hours), and the tile-count >> SM-count approximation in the cost model. Open questions include whether cross-layer sensitivity metrics (acknowledged as a weakness for Qwen2-MoE at 3.25-bit) can close remaining accuracy gaps, and whether the kernel generation framework generalizes to emerging hardware with different precision support (e.g., FP4 on Blackwell).
