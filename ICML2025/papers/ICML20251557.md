## Problem

Existing neural audio watermarking methods face a fundamental trade-off: WavMark's fully shared architecture enables efficient joint detection and attribution learning but lacks robustness against diverse distortions, while AudioSeal's disjoint generator-detector design achieves strong detection (97.1% average) but collapses on attribution (39% average). No prior method simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art performance on both tasks, and none demonstrates non-trivial detection under generative editing transformations.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 16 kHz mono audio waveforms (1-second segments during training, 5-second clips during evaluation) and a 16-bit binary watermark message, producing a watermarked waveform (original plus a learned residual), a per-sample detection probability obtained by temporally averaging frame-level logits, and a 16-bit decoded message vector. The detector's message decoding head operates on a K×H latent state derived from the reconstructed waveform, attending over a shared 2K-entry embedding table to produce per-bit sigmoid logits.

## Method

XATTNMARK introduces a blended architecture with partial parameter sharing: the message conditioning module (a 2K-entry embedding table) is shared between the generator and detector, and the detector employs a cross-attention block that uses the reshaped embedding table as key-value pairs and the demodulated audio latent as query to retrieve message bits. A temporal modulation layer (linear projection W_M ∈ R^{K×t'}) replaces AudioSeal's mean-pooling-plus-repetition, distributing the K-bit message across the temporal axis rather than concentrating it in the frequency domain. For imperceptibility, a psychoacoustic-aligned TF masking loss computes per-tile masking energy using an asymmetric 2D decay kernel (200 ms forward / 20 ms backward temporal radii, critical-bandwidth-scaled frequency radii) and applies a TF-weighted ℓ₂ loss in the mel-spectrogram domain, allowing larger watermark energy in perceptually masked regions. Training uses supervised detection and message-decoding losses with adversarial multi-scale STFT losses, a curriculum schedule that defers quality-balancing gradients, and adaptive per-transformation sampling to accelerate convergence on harder edits.

## Results

XATTNMARK achieves state-of-the-art average detection accuracy of 99.19% (TPR 98.56%, FPR 0.19%) and average attribution accuracy of 93% across 16 standard audio transformations on MusicCaps. Under the challenging speed-change transformation, XATTNMARK maintains 99.5% detection accuracy while all baselines degrade to near random-guess levels (50–61%). Against zero-shot generative editing (AudioLDM2, Stable Audio), XATTNMARK is the only method that sustains non-trivial detection (91–94% accuracy) at all editing strengths, whereas all baselines collapse to 50–60%. In terms of perceptual quality, XATTNMARK achieves the best STOI score (1.000) and the lowest watermark residual loudness (−54.63 LUFS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

XATTNMARK decisively outperforms AudioSeal on attribution (93% vs. 39% average), closing the critical gap where AudioSeal's decoupled generator-detector design boosts detection but severely degrades message decoding. Against the traditional hand-crafted AudiowMark, XATTNMARK improves average detection from 92.9% to 99.19% while matching its 88% attribution (93% vs. 88%). Compared to WavMark, XATTNMARK achieves higher detection (99.19% vs. 91.8%) and attribution (93% vs. 87%), and critically maintains 99.5% detection under speed changes where WavMark drops to 50%. Against TimbreWM, XATTNMARK leads on both detection (99.19% vs. 92.5%) and attribution (93% vs. 80%). The most striking advantage appears under zero-shot generative editing (Table 2), where XATTNMARK sustains 91–94% detection across all strengths while all four baselines collapse to 50–60%, making it the only method that survives strong AI-based audio edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AudioSeal's disjoint generator-detector architecture but departs from it by introducing partial parameter sharing (a shared embedding table) between the generator and detector, bridging the gap between AudioSeal's fully-disjoint design and WavMark's fully-shared invertible architecture. The key architectural departure is replacing AudioSeal's mean-pooling message conditioning with a cross-attention mechanism that uses the shared embedding table as key-value reference for message decoding, combined with a temporal modulation layer that distributes the message across the time axis rather than collapsing it into a single frequency-domain vector. The psychoacoustic-aligned TF masking loss extends AudioSeal's coarse TF-Loudness penalty with per-tile asymmetric decay modeling grounded in auditory masking theory (critical bandwidth, upward spread of masking, forward/backward asymmetry).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the open problem of jointly achieving robust detection and accurate attribution in neural audio watermarking, which prior methods could only optimize one at the expense of the other. Its zero-shot robustness to generative editing (AudioLDM2, Stable Audio) at 91–94% detection accuracy—while all baselines collapse to near-random—marks the first demonstration that a watermark can survive diffusion-based audio editing, a critical capability as generative audio tools proliferate. However, attribution under generative editing and speed changes remains largely unsolved (3–4% accuracy), and the method's reliance on a proprietary detector raises practical deployment concerns. The psychoacoustic masking loss offers a reusable recipe for imperceptibility in other perceptual embedding tasks.
