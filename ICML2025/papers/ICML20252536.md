## Problem

It remains unclear whether vision-language models genuinely encode the semantic meaning of text embedded in images or merely treat it as another visual texture. While typographic attacks demonstrate that overlaid text can hijack classification, the internal mechanism—specifically at which network depth semantic understanding emerges versus texture-based pattern matching—has not been systematically characterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252536/cropped_page6_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images with text overlays rendered in random fonts (Roman, Courier, Times), colors (8 options), and sizes (20–120 pt) at arbitrary positions, drawn from 100 ImageNet-1k categories. Beyond standard class-label outputs, the analysis produces per-layer intrinsic dimension estimates (via TwoNN distance-ratio fitting) and per-layer linear probe accuracies for paronym-vs.-synonym binary classification, with probes applied at three sub-layers (Attn, c_fc, c_proj) within each of the 12 ViT blocks.

## Method

The paper introduces the ToT dataset with two controlled subsets: (1) images with semantically consistent, irrelevant, or nonsensical text overlays, and (2) 10 paronym-synonym word pairs that isolate orthographic form from semantic meaning. Using the TwoNN algorithm, it estimates Intrinsic Dimension at every layer of CLIP ViT-B/16, revealing a swell-shrink trajectory where early layers are dominated by texture-level orthographic features and true semantic differentiation appears only in the final block. Linear probes on paronym/synonym pairs confirm that mid-layer accuracy gains reflect orthographic disambiguation rather than semantic comprehension. For defense, the paper fine-tunes only the final block of the visual encoder on typographic attack data, showing that this targeted intervention effectively separates textural from textual representations and outperforms full-model fine-tuning and prior SOTA defenses across easy, medium, and hard attack scenarios.

## Results

On the ToT dataset's medium and hard defense tasks, fine-tuning only the final block (Ours_Hard) achieves 84.7% original-image accuracy and 81.7% Hard-Nons accuracy, substantially outperforming the CLIP baseline (82.3% Orig, 77.2% Hard-Nons) and SOTA defenses Prefix (75.5% Hard-Nons) and Disentangle (75.3% Hard-Nons). On the hard-level Consistent task, Ours_Hard reaches 35.0% versus 14.5% for CLIP, 10.6% for Prefix, and 12.9% for Disentangle. In cross-dataset evaluation on three public typographic attack benchmarks (easy level), the proposed method achieves the best average accuracy of 69.5%, compared to 64.5% (Prefix), 63.6% (Disentangle), and 46.8% (CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (the unmodified multimodal baseline), the proposed last-block fine-tuning (Ours_Hard) improves original-image accuracy from 82.3% to 84.7% while boosting Hard-Consistent accuracy from 14.5% to 35.0% and Hard-Irrelevant from 59.9% to 69.5%. Compared to Prefix (Azuma & Matsui, 2023), which fine-tunes only the language model, Ours_Hard wins on every hard-level metric (35.0% vs. 10.6% on Cons, 69.5% vs. 27.1% on Irr, 81.7% vs. 75.5% on Nons) and also on original accuracy. Against Disentangle (Materzyńska et al., 2022), which applies a linear transformation to bifurcate visual and textual streams, Ours_Hard dominates on all hard-level tasks (35.0% vs. 12.9% Cons, 69.5% vs. 13.8% Irr, 81.7% vs. 75.3% Nons). In the cross-dataset easy-level evaluation (Table 3), the proposed method achieves the best average (69.5%) over Disentangle (63.6%), Prefix (64.5%), and PAINT (55.0%), though it is marginally outperformed by Disentangle on the Disentangle-specific test set (73.3% vs. 77.8%). The vision-only models (ViT, DINOv2) in Table 1 confirm that text semantics have negligible impact on pure vision encoders, validating that the observed vulnerabilities are specific to multimodal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the TwoNN intrinsic dimension estimator (Facco et al., 2017) and the information bottleneck framework (Ansuini et al., 2019) from adversarial robustness and data geometry into a new analytical lens for understanding how vision-language models encode text in images. It departs from prior typographic attack defenses (Disentangle, PAINT, Prefix), which treat the vulnerability as a black-box alignment problem, by instead probing the internal representational dynamics layer by layer to identify *where* and *how* text is processed. The ToT dataset extends the controlled typographic attack paradigm by introducing paronym-synonym pairs that explicitly disentangle orthographic form from semantic meaning, a dimension absent from earlier attack benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that genuine semantic differentiation emerges only in the final transformer block—rather than being progressively constructed across depth—challenges a widely held assumption about hierarchical semantic formation in vision transformers and has direct implications for where to intervene for robustness. The practical consequence is a simple, effective defense (fine-tuning only the last block) that outperforms prior methods across easy, medium, and hard typographic attack scenarios while preserving original classification accuracy. Open questions include whether this late-emergence pattern persists in larger or decoder-based multimodal models (e.g., GPT-4V-class systems), whether the 10-word-pair paronym/synonym analysis generalizes beyond the tested vocabulary, and whether the final-block bottleneck can be addressed architecturally rather than through post-hoc fine-tuning.
