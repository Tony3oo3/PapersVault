## Problem

Standard K-Means and its kernel/fuzzy variants rely on explicit centroid computation, making them sensitive to initialization and outliers, limited to Euclidean or kernel-space distances that struggle with nonlinearly separable structures, and prone to producing unbalanced cluster sizes. No existing centerless K-Means formulation simultaneously unifies these variants under a single manifold-learning objective while enforcing cluster balance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{d×n}, a target number of clusters c, and a balance hyperparameter λ. It outputs a discrete one-hot cluster indicator matrix F ∈ R^{n×c} (each row assigns a sample to exactly one cluster). The distance matrix D ∈ R^{n×n} is computed via one of four strategies—Euclidean squared, K-nearest-neighbor, Gaussian kernel, or low-pass filtering—where the low-pass filtering variant additionally requires constructing an anchor graph T ∈ R^{n×r} via DAS sampling and a similarity matrix R = TΔ^{-1}T^T before applying the filter D_{ij} = a|H(jω)|² with ω = R_{ij}.

## Method

The core insight (Theorem 1) proves that the K-Means objective Σ‖x_i − u_k‖²F_{ik} is exactly equal to tr(G^T D G) where G = FP^{-1/2} and P is the diagonal cluster-size matrix, thereby eliminating all centroid variables and unifying K-Means, Kernel K-Means, and Fuzzy K-Means into a single centerless manifold-learning framework min_F tr(F^T D F (F^T F)^{-1}). A balanced regularization term −λ‖F^T‖_{2,1} is added, and Theorem 2 proves via Cauchy-Schwarz that maximizing this norm forces F to be discrete with equal cluster sizes n/c. Optimization proceeds by first-order Taylor expansion of the non-smooth ℓ_{2,1}-norm, yielding a row-wise coordinate descent update F_{ib} = 1{b = argmin_k (2F^T d_i − λ(h^i)^T)_k}, with an acceleration strategy that maintains cached values 2F^T D − λH^T to reduce per-iteration cost from O(n²c) to O(nc). Four distance metrics (Euclidean, KNN, Gaussian kernel, low-pass filtering) are plugged into D to adapt the framework to different data geometries, with the low-pass filter explicitly preserving high-similarity neighborhood structure for nonlinearly separable data.

## Results

The proposed low-pass filtering variant (Our-LF) achieves the strongest overall performance across the 10 benchmark datasets. On olivetti, Our-LF reaches 83.2% ACC / 81.8% NMI, nearly doubling the best baseline (K-sum at 42.3% ACC). On CMUPIE, Our-LF attains 63.4% ACC / 81.7% NMI versus 21.5% ACC for the next-best method (K-sum). On STL10, Our-LF scores 95.4% ACC / 89.7% NMI, outperforming RKM (94.2% ACC) and K-sum (92.2% ACC). The KNN and Gaussian-kernel variants also consistently surpass centroid-based baselines, with Our-KNN reaching 84.1% ACC on Pendigits and 75.5% ACC on USPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against five baselines: K-Means, KKM, RKM, CDKM, and the centerless K-sum / K-sum-x. Centroid-based methods (K-Means, KKM, RKM, CDKM) are consistently outperformed, with the gap most dramatic on CMUPIE (Our-LF 63.4% ACC vs. K-Means 19.7%) and olivetti (Our-LF 83.2% vs. K-Means 44.9%). The closest competitors are K-sum and K-sum-x, which also avoid centroid computation; however, Our-LF still leads on 8 of 10 datasets in ACC, with the largest margins on olivetti (+40.9 pts), CMUPIE (+41.9 pts), and USPS (+16.5 pts). On Pendigits, Our-K-ED (85.8% ACC) edges out K-sum (75.6%) by over 10 points. The only dataset where a baseline is competitive is PEAL, where K-sum achieves 87.7% ACC versus Our-KNN's 89.2% and Our-LF's 88.5%, a narrow margin. Overall, the low-pass filtering distance variant delivers the most consistent and largest improvements, particularly on datasets with nonlinear structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three classical K-Means variants (standard, kernel, and fuzzy) into a single centerless manifold learning objective via Theorem 1, building directly on the centerless clustering line initiated by Lu et al. (2023) and Pei et al. (2023, K-sum/K-sum-x). It departs from those prior centerless methods by incorporating an ℓ₂,₁-norm balanced regularizer (inspired by RKM and DBSC) and by framing the problem as label-guided manifold learning rather than pure pairwise distance minimization. The introduction of four distance metrics—particularly the low-pass filtering distance derived from anchor graph similarity—extends the framework beyond the Euclidean and KNN distances used in prior centerless work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elegant unification (showing K-Means, Kernel K-Means, and Fuzzy K-Means are all special cases of min tr(GᵀDG)) provides a clean theoretical lens for future centerless clustering research, and the low-pass filtering distance offers a practical tool for nonlinearly separable data without explicit kernel computation. However, the method's practical utility is tempered by per-dataset hyperparameter sensitivity (λ, K, σ, anchor ratio all require manual tuning) and the assumption that balanced cluster sizes are desirable, which may not hold for imbalanced real-world data. Open questions include scalability beyond the ~30K-sample datasets tested, automatic selection among the four distance metrics, and whether the balanced constraint can be relaxed to handle genuinely imbalanced cluster structures.
