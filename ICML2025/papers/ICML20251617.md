## Problem

Existing CLIP adaptation methods for video action recognition rely on cosine similarity (equivalent to Pearson correlation) between global tokens only, which captures solely linear dependencies and ignores local patch and word tokens encoding fine-grained spatio-temporal cues. This limits their ability to model the complex, nonlinear relationships between visual and textual elements that are critical for distinguishing actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251617/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (16 or 32 frames) processed by a ViT-B/16 visual encoder and text prompts (action category names, optionally enriched by GPT-4o) processed by the CLIP text encoder. Visual and textual token embeddings are first projected to a reduced dimension (d=192) via a linear layer before BDC computation. The output is an action class label via a softmax classifier on the video BDC representation, alongside a BDCorr similarity score in [0,1] for each candidate text category used in the contrastive objective.

## Method

BDC-CLIP replaces cosine similarity with Brownian Distance Covariance (BDC) for video-language alignment, leveraging all visual tokens ([CLS] + patch tokens) and all textual tokens ([EOS] + word tokens) rather than only global tokens. A video BDC adapter computes frame-wise BDC matrices from tokens weighted by their softmax similarity to the [CLS] token, then applies a temporal attention mechanism (queries/keys from [CLS] tokens, values from half-vectorized BDC matrices) to model inter-frame dynamics before averaging into a video-level BDC matrix. A text BDC adapter analogously computes a BDC matrix from tokens weighted by similarity to [EOS]. Alignment uses BDCorr, a normalized trace-based metric invariant to orthogonal, translational, and scaling transformations, which captures both linear and nonlinear dependencies without distributional assumptions. Training combines three losses: a BDC-based vision-language contrastive loss, a supervised vision classification loss on the video BDC representation, and the original CLIP backbone contrastive loss.

## Results

BDC-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot action recognition with 60.7% top-1 on HMDB-51, 86.9% on UCF-101, and 78.9% on K600 (with Weight-Space Ensembling), outperforming the runner-up TC-CLIP by 4.7%, 1.5%, and 0.8% respectively. In the few-shot setting (16-shot), it reaches 77.3% on HMDB-51, 98.5% on UCF-101, and 19.5% on SSv2. For fully-supervised K400 recognition, BDC-CLIP attains 86.5% top-1 accuracy (with WSE), surpassing TC-CLIP's 85.7%. The method also leads in base-to-novel generalization with harmonic means of 69.8% (HMDB-51), 92.5% (UCF-101), and 19.5% (SSv2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDC-CLIP outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every evaluation setting (zero-shot, few-shot, base-to-novel, and fully-supervised). The closest competitor is TC-CLIP (Kim et al., 2024), which BDC-CLIP surpasses by 4.7% on HMDB-51 zero-shot, 2.7%+ on HMDB-51 few-shot (all K values), 2.6% harmonic mean on HMDB-51 base-to-novel, and 0.8% top-1 on fully-supervised K400. Against the strong fine-tuning baseline ViFi-CLIP (Rasheed et al., 2023), BDC-CLIP gains 8.5% on HMDB-51 zero-shot and 4.3% on SSv2 16-shot. Among frozen-backbone methods (A5, Vita-CLIP, DiST, MoTED), BDC-CLIP leads by 1.5–15.4% depending on dataset and setting, confirming that the BDC alignment with fine-tuned encoders is substantially more effective than prompt/adapter approaches on frozen CLIP. On SSv2 (highly temporal), BDC-CLIP's advantage over TC-CLIP is largest (4.3% at 16-shot), suggesting the temporal BDC attention mechanism is particularly beneficial for motion-centric actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViFi-CLIP fine-tuning paradigm by replacing the standard cosine-similarity alignment between global [CLS]/[EOS] tokens with Brownian Distance Covariance (BDC) computed over all visual and textual tokens. It builds directly on the BDC metric from Szekely & Rizzo (2009) and its deep-learning adaptations (DeepBDC, BDC-Adapter), but departs from those unimodal applications by using BDC for cross-modal (vision-language) alignment and introducing a temporal attention mechanism over frame-wise BDC matrices. The key conceptual departure from the broader CLIP-to-video literature (ActionCLIP, X-CLIP, TC-CLIP, OST, FROSTER) is the argument that cosine similarity—equivalent to Pearson correlation—fundamentally cannot capture the nonlinear, non-Gaussian dependencies between video and text tokens, which BDC handles without distributional assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a principled statistical metric (BDC) can outperform the de facto standard (cosine similarity) for video-language alignment across zero-shot, few-shot, base-to-novel, and fully-supervised settings, with consistent 1–5% gains over strong baselines like TC-CLIP. The extension to few-shot image recognition (outperforming CLIP-LoRA by 1.9% average) suggests BDC-based alignment is a broadly useful tool beyond video. Limitations include modestly higher GFLOPS and lower throughput than competitors, and the method is currently tailored to action recognition; extending it to video-text retrieval or open-world detection, and exploring alternatives like mutual information, are noted as future directions.
