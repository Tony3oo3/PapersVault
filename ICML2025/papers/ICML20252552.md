## Problem

Existing distributionally robust policy learning methods (Si et al., 2023; Kallus et al., 2022) model the worst-case shift over the joint distribution of (X, Y), which is unnecessarily conservative when the decision maker knows the shift is confined to the conditional reward distribution Y|X (concept drift). The paper asks whether this additional structural knowledge can be exploited to learn a less conservative, more efficient robust policy, and provides a minimax-optimal algorithm with matching upper and lower regret bounds for general (possibly infinite) covariate spaces.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a logged dataset of n i.i.d. tuples (X_i, A_i, Y_i) where X ∈ R^d, A ∈ [M], and Y is a bounded scalar reward, along with a policy class Π and a KL-divergence radius δ that bounds the allowable shift in the conditional distribution Y(a)|X. It outputs a robust policy π̂: X → [M] maximizing the estimated worst-case policy value, plus a doubly-robust estimate of that value. Unlike prior work restricted to finite covariate spaces (Mu et al., 2022), the method handles general covariate spaces and potentially infinite policy classes, with the covariate distribution assumed invariant between training and target environments.

## Method

The key insight is to exploit the dual form of the KL-constrained DRO inner problem, which yields a loss function ℓ(x, y; α, η) = α·exp(−(y+η)/α − 1) + η + αδ whose minimizer (α*, η*) depends on both the context x and the policy π. A doubly-robust estimator is constructed via K-fold cross-fitting: nuisance parameters (propensity score, ERM optimizers, conditional mean) are estimated on off-fold data, and a de-biasing correction term removes the first-order bias from slow-converging nuisance estimates, yielding asymptotic normality at the √n rate. For policy learning, a critical computational shortcut replaces the infeasible task of solving the ERM problem for every π ∈ Π with solving it once per action a ∈ [M], reducing an infinite set of nuisance parameters to a finite one. The resulting algorithm achieves a regret upper bound of O(κ(Π)/√n) where κ(Π) is the Hamming entropy integral, complemented by a matching Ω(√(Ndim(Π)/n)) lower bound establishing minimax optimality.

## Results

On the simulated dataset, the proposed π̂_LN consistently outperforms the SNLN baseline across all KL-divergence radii (δ = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) and sample sizes (n = 7500–19500). At δ = 0.05 and n = 7500, π̂_LN achieves an empirical robust policy value of 0.2272 ± 0.002 versus 0.0554 ± 0.005 for SNLN — a roughly 4× improvement. On the real-world French unemployment counseling dataset, π̂_LN also shows higher robust policy values than SNLN across all tested sample sizes. Under the more realistic KL-sphere perturbation evaluation (Table 3), π̂_LN achieves worst-case rewards of 0.2075–0.2167 versus 0.1884–0.2020 for SNLN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is SNLN (Si et al., 2023, Algorithm 2), a distributionally robust policy learning method designed for joint distributional shifts, adapted here to the concept-drift setting via the KL chain rule. π̂_LN wins decisively on every configuration tested: on the simulated dataset at δ=0.05, it achieves a robust policy value of 0.2272 vs. 0.0554 for SNLN at n=7500 (a ~4× gap), and the advantage persists at larger n and across δ=0.1 and δ=0.2. On the real-world French unemployment counseling dataset (Figure 1), π̂_LN again shows higher robust policy values with comparable confidence intervals. Under the more realistic KL-sphere perturbation evaluation (Table 3), π̂_LN achieves worst-case rewards of 0.2075–0.2167 versus 0.1884–0.2020 for SNLN, confirming the advantage in a setting that mimics actual concept drift. The paper attributes SNLN's underperformance to its unnecessarily conservative uncertainty set, which includes adversarial covariate shifts that are infeasible under the known concept-drift structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributionally robust offline policy learning framework of Kallus et al. (2022) and Si et al. (2023), which model joint (X,Y) distributional shift, to the more specific and practically relevant setting of concept drift where only the conditional distribution Y|X changes. The ERM solution and convergence rate analysis for the dual variables (α*, η*) are inherited from Jin et al. (2022b) and Yadlowsky et al. (2022), while the doubly-robust cross-fitting structure follows the standard debiased machine learning paradigm. The key departure from Mu et al. (2022), who also separate covariate and conditional shifts, is the extension from finite covariate spaces to general (potentially infinite) covariate spaces, with a matching minimax lower bound that Mu et al. do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that knowing the source of distributional shift (concept drift vs. joint shift) yields a strictly smaller uncertainty set and thus less conservative, more efficient policies — a practically important insight for deployment scenarios where covariate distributions are stable but reward mechanisms evolve. The minimax optimal rate O(κ(Π)/√n) with a matching lower bound provides a clean benchmark for future work. Key limitations include the sensitivity to the choice of δ (which controls robustness degree without a principled selection rule), the restriction to discrete action spaces (the per-action ERM shortcut does not extend to continuous actions), and the assumption that the covariate distribution is exactly invariant. Extending to settings with both identifiable covariate shift and concept drift simultaneously is noted as future work.
