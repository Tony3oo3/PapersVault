## Problem

Two-stage Learning-to-Defer (L2D) systems route queries to the most cost-effective agent among a primary model and offline experts, but existing frameworks assume clean inputs and are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations that manipulate the rejector to misroute queries or overload specific agents. This paper presents the first comprehensive study of adversarial robustness in two-stage L2D, introducing both attack strategies and a principled defense with theoretical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251794/cropped_page14_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts either RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, or object-detection images on Pascal VOC) or tabular feature vectors (California Housing) as input. A fixed primary model and J offline experts produce predictions on the clean input, while the learned rejector r: X × A → R scores each agent and outputs an argmax assignment to one of J+1 agents. The rejector is the only component trained; all agent parameters remain frozen from offline training, and the adversarial perturbation is applied exclusively to the rejector's input at inference time.

## Method

SARD (Smooth Adversarial Regularized Deferral) builds a convex, differentiable surrogate for the adversarial true deferral loss by combining two terms per agent j: a scaled comp-sum cross-entropy loss Φ₀₁ᵘ(r/ρ, x, j) on the clean input, and a Lipschitz penalty ν·sup_{x′∈B_p(x,γ)}‖Δ̄_r(x′,j) − Δ̄_r(x,j)‖₂ that penalizes score-difference shifts under ℓ_p-bounded perturbations. The per-agent surrogates are weighted by the aggregated cost τ_j and summed over all agents, yielding a smooth adversarial deferral loss that upper-bounds the non-convex margin surrogate. Training minimizes the regularized empirical risk via projected gradient descent with T_a PGD steps per agent per sample, incurring a multiplicative overhead of (1+|A|T_a) over standard L2D. The paper proves Bayes-consistency and (R,G)-consistency for the surrogate family under the assumption that the rejector hypothesis class is symmetric and locally ρ-consistent, extending guarantees to the smooth SARD variant via the upper-bound inequality.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (classification), SARD achieves 49.8% accuracy under untargeted attacks versus 17.2% for the baseline (Mao et al., 2023a), a 2.9× improvement, while sacrificing only 5.8 points of clean accuracy (67.0% vs. 72.8%). On California Housing (regression), SARD maintains an RMSE of 0.17–0.18 across all adversarial conditions, whereas the baseline degrades to 0.29–0.41. On Pascal VOC (multi-task), SARD retains 39.0 mAP under untargeted attacks compared to 9.7 mAP for the baseline (Montreuil et al., 2025c), a 4× improvement, with clean mAP nearly matching (43.9 vs. 44.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mao et al. (2023a) on CIFAR-100, SARD trades 5.8 points of clean accuracy (67.0% vs. 72.8%) for massive robustness gains: 49.8% vs. 17.2% under untargeted attacks and 64.8% vs. 13.4% under targeted attacks on the weak expert M₃. Against Mao et al. (2024d) on California Housing, SARD matches the baseline exactly on clean RMSE (0.17) while remaining virtually unaffected by adversarial perturbations (0.17–0.18) compared to the baseline's degradation to 0.29–0.41. Against Montreuil et al. (2025c) on Pascal VOC, SARD is within 0.5 mAP on clean performance (43.9 vs. 44.4) but outperforms the baseline by 29.3 mAP under untargeted attacks (39.0 vs. 9.7) and by 22.3 mAP under targeted attacks on M₁ (39.7 vs. 17.4). In all three settings, SARD consistently wins on every adversarial metric while remaining competitive on clean performance, demonstrating that the robustness guarantee comes at a modest clean-accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads: two-stage Learning-to-Defer (building on Mao et al. 2023a for classification, Mao et al. 2024d for regression, and Montreuil et al. 2025c for multi-task) and adversarial robustness consistency theory (Bao et al. 2020; Awasthi et al. 2022, 2023; Mao et al. 2023b). The key departure is that prior L2D work assumes clean inputs and optimizes the rejector only for allocation accuracy, while prior adversarial robustness work targets prediction heads rather than routing mechanisms. Here, the authors transplant the comp-sum ρ-margin surrogate family into the deferral setting, introducing per-agent adversarial perturbations and a smooth convex surrogate (SARD) whose Bayes- and (R,G)-consistency are proven under a locally ρ-consistent hypothesis class assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of adversarial robustness for the L2D rejector, revealing that state-of-the-art two-stage L2D systems are catastrophically vulnerable (e.g., 72.8% → 17.2% accuracy under untargeted PGD on CIFAR-100). For safety-critical deployments in healthcare or finance where L2D is intended to guarantee reliable delegation, this vulnerability is a critical gap. Open questions include whether the locally ρ-consistent assumption holds for all practical architectures, how the multiplicative training overhead (1+|A|·T_a) scales to large expert pools, and whether the clean-performance trade-off (SARD is consistently 3–6 points worse on clean data) can be narrowed. The paper also leaves open the question of whether defenses at the individual-agent level (which can be deployed offline) complement or subsume system-level rejector robustness.
