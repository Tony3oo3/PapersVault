## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods operate under a static assumption that all views are already collected, while the sole streaming view clustering method (LSVC) fails to handle concept drift—the distribution imbalance between sequentially arriving view streams caused by different sensors or acquisition angles. This paper is the first to explicitly identify and address concept drift in the streaming view clustering setting, where outdated model knowledge misleads training on new views and causes persistent performance fluctuations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view data as a sequence of view feature matrices X^v ∈ R^{N×D^v}, where each view arrives one at a time and prior view data is inaccessible during training of the current view. The encoder maps each view to a fixed 256-dimensional latent space regardless of the input dimensionality D^v, and the final output is cluster labels obtained by applying K-means to the accumulated reconstructed feature representations H^v ∈ R^{N×d}. A persistent knowledge base of K prototype vectors (K = M×C, where C is the number of classes) is maintained across views and updated after each view is processed.

## Method

DSVC comprises a knowledge base and three modules operating per arriving view. The Knowledge Aggregation Learning (KAL) module uses an autoencoder (D^v-512-1024-512-256 encoder) to extract latent features, then a cross-attention mechanism between features and MLP-derived prototypes to aggregate discriminative prototype knowledge P^v and reconstruct cluster-friendly features H^v. The Distribution Consistency Learning (DCL) module computes a symmetric KL divergence between the softmax-normalized cosine-similarity distribution of the current view's prototypes and the historical knowledge base, explicitly aligning distributions to counteract concept drift (for the first view, it instead aligns latent and reconstructed features since the knowledge base is empty). The Knowledge Guidance Learning (KGL) module uses the aligned prototypes to compute per-sample assignment probabilities via softmax similarity and minimizes their negative log-likelihood, pulling features toward their nearest prototype and tightening cluster structure. After training, the current view's prototypes overwrite the knowledge base, and the total loss is L = αL_r + L_d + βL_g with view-specific trade-off parameters.

## Results

DSVC achieves the best or near-best ACC across all eight datasets, with headline results of 95.44% ACC on HandWritten, 86.69% on ALOI-10, 80.53% on ALOI-100, and 58.32% on ALOI-1000. The paper reports improvements of 0.23%–10.20% over the next-best method in ACC across the eight datasets, with the largest gain (10.20%) on ALOI-1000 where most baselines run out of memory. DSVC also exhibits notably lower standard deviations (e.g., ±0.32 on HandWritten vs. ±3.97 for DMCE), indicating more stable performance across random seeds. On the largest dataset (ALOI-1000, 110K samples, 1000 classes), only CPSPAN, DCP, and DSVC complete evaluation, with DSVC achieving 58.32% ACC vs. 48.12% (DCP) and 43.81% (CPSPAN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSVC outperforms all 13 baselines on 7 out of 8 datasets in ACC, with the closest competitors being DMCE (94.30%) and MVCAN (94.12%) on HandWritten, GDMVC (85.64%) and FMCSC (85.17%) on ALOI-10, and DCP (28.29%) on LandUse-21. On Scene-15, DSRL (45.53%) marginally edges out DSVC (45.33%) in ACC, though DSVC leads in NMI (43.13 vs. 40.11) and ARI (27.61 vs. 24.01). The sole prior streaming method, LSVC, is consistently outperformed by DSVC across all datasets and metrics (e.g., 95.44 vs. 88.69 ACC on HandWritten; 58.32 vs. O/M on ALOI-1000). On large-scale datasets (Stl10-fea, ALOI-100, YoutubeFace, ALOI-1000), most baselines either run out of memory or degrade severely, while DSVC achieves 63.41% ACC on Stl10-fea, 80.53% on ALOI-100, 25.09% on YoutubeFace, and 58.32% on ALOI-1000. The ablation confirms all three loss components (reconstruction, distribution consistency, knowledge guidance) contribute, with removing any single component causing 5–20% ACC drops depending on the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the streaming view clustering (SVC) paradigm introduced by LSVC (Li et al., 2024b), which was the sole prior method in this setting. While LSVC uses a consensus bipartite graph to fuse current and historical view knowledge, DSVC departs by introducing a prototype-knowledge-based architecture with cross-attention aggregation and a distribution consistency loss (symmetric KL divergence) to explicitly counter concept drift between view streams. The method repurposes autoencoder-based representation learning and attention mechanisms from the broader DMVC literature but reorganizes them around a knowledge-base update loop that processes one view at a time, distinguishing it from static multi-view clustering methods that assume all views are available simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of concept drift as a specific failure mode in streaming view clustering is a useful diagnostic that explains why prior lifelong-learning approaches (LSVC) exhibit unstable performance as views accumulate. The streaming design also offers a practical computational advantage, enabling processing of large-scale datasets (e.g., ALOI-1000 with 110K samples) where static methods run out of memory. However, the authors acknowledge that noisy views in real-world collection remain unaddressed, and the method's reliance on a fixed number of prototype knowledge (K) and hand-tuned trade-off parameters (α, β) per dataset limits its plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the distribution consistency mechanism generalizes to non-stationary class structures or to settings where the number of clusters changes over time.
