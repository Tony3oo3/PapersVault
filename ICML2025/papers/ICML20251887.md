## Problem

Neural causal discovery methods have improved in scalability and computational efficiency, yet their accuracy remains insufficient for real-world applications, and prior evaluations used inconsistent datasets and protocols that obscure the true state of the field. This paper identifies a fundamental limitation: unavoidable likelihood score estimation errors from finite data prevent distinguishing the true causal structure even for small graphs, and the faithfulness property—likely violated across any reasonable dataset size—directly undermines penalized-likelihood discovery methods.

## Input / Output

The benchmarked methods consume finite observational samples (tabular data) drawn from additive-noise SCMs over Erdős–Rényi DAGs with 5, 10, or 30 nodes, and produce a causal graph structure evaluated within the Markov Equivalence Class using ESHD_CP DAG and F1-Score_CP DAG. The paper additionally outputs a practical estimate of λ-strong faithfulness (λ̂) computed via partial Spearman correlations and F1-score threshold optimization, which serves as a per-dataset difficulty measure linking data properties to method performance.

## Method

The paper introduces three technical contributions. First, a unified benchmarking protocol that evaluates DCDI, SDCD, DiBS, and BayesDAG on identical synthetic datasets with consistent hyperparameter tuning and a standardized two-layer MLP (hidden dim 4) as the functional approximator. Second, a practical approximation of λ-strong faithfulness for nonlinear data: partial Spearman correlations are computed for all variable pairs across all conditioning sets, and λ̂ is defined as the threshold maximizing the F1-score of classifying node pairs as d-separated versus d-connected. Third, an exhaustive estimation approach (NN-opt) that trains a separate neural network for each variable–parent-set combination with random-seed bootstrapping over N independent initializations, minimizing approximation error to isolate the irreducible estimation error in likelihood score assignment. The paper then demonstrates that λ̂ decreases rapidly with graph size and density, and that method performance is strongly anti-correlated with λ̂ (Spearman ρ = −0.93 for NN-opt, ρ = −0.46 for benchmarked methods), establishing faithfulness as the critical bottleneck rather than architecture or optimization.

## Results

Across all three Erdős-Rényi graph classes, every benchmarked neural causal discovery method predicts more than half of the edges incorrectly. On the smallest graphs ER(5,1), the best method (DiBS) achieves ESHD_CP DAG of 2.6 (95% CI: 1.7–3.7) and F1-Score_CP DAG of 0.85, while on ER(30,2) even the best method (DCDI) yields ESHD_CP DAG of 45.9. The paper's controlled NN-opt experiment shows a strong anti-monotonic correlation (Spearman ρ = −0.93, p = 9e-4) between the λ-strong faithfulness measure and the minimum sample size needed for convergence, and the benchmarked methods show a similar but weaker correlation (ρ = −0.46, p = 4e-6). Critically, no method exhibits consistent improvement as observational sample size increases from 20 to 8,000, indicating that the data volume required for reliable recovery is far beyond practical scope.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four neural causal discovery methods—DCDI, BayesDAG, DiBS, and SDCD—under a unified protocol (identical datasets, standardized MLP approximators, consistent hyperparameter tuning). DiBS dominates on small graphs, achieving the lowest ESHD_CP DAG on both ER(5,1) (2.6 vs. 3.9–5.7 for others) and ER(10,2) (16.9, tied with DCDI), but collapses on ER(30,2) with an ESHD of 68.0 and F1 of only 0.23, the worst of all methods at that scale. DCDI is the strongest on the largest graphs (ER(30,2): ESHD 45.9, F1 0.73), outperforming BayesDAG (51.7) and SDCD (62.8) by a wide margin. SDCD consistently exhibits the highest ESHD_CP DAG across all three dataset sizes (5.4, 20.9, 62.8), making it the weakest method overall. BayesDAG occupies a middle position, never the best but never the worst, with its advantage on ER(5,1) (ESHD 3.9) narrowing to a disadvantage on ER(30,2). The key takeaway is that no single method wins across all scales, and all methods remain far from perfect recovery (ESHD = 0), with the ranking shifting as graph size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of λ-strong faithfulness from Zhang & Spirtes (2003) and Uhler et al. (2013)—which established that the fraction of λ-strong faithful distributions vanishes exponentially with graph size for linear SCMs—to nonlinear SCMs with neural network function approximators. It positions itself as a systematic diagnostic study of the four major neural causal discovery methods (DCDI, SDCD, DiBS, BayesDAG), all of which rely on penalized-likelihood objectives with differentiable acyclicity constraints descended from NO-TEARS, DAGMA, and NO-BEARS. The key departure from prior work is the argument that the bottleneck is not algorithmic (architecture, optimization, or regularization) but intrinsic to the data distribution: finite-sample estimation errors in likelihood scores make the true structure indistinguishable from alternatives when faithfulness is weakly satisfied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that neural causal discovery is fundamentally constrained by faithfulness violations rather than by methodological shortcomings—redirects the field's focus from incremental architectural improvements toward alternative assumptions (sparsity-based identifiability, interventional data, partial structure recovery) or entirely new paradigms. The unified benchmark with standardized hyperparameters and functional approximators provides a much-needed common reference point for comparing methods, which prior studies lacked. Open questions include whether interventional data can bypass the faithfulness requirement (as suggested by Lippe et al., 2022), whether real-world distributions are more or less faithful than the synthetic ER-graph-based distributions studied here, and whether alternative function classes beyond MLPs might be better suited to the task.
