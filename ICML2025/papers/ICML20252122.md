## Problem

Transformer-based Large Action Models (LAMs) achieve strong multi-task performance but suffer from quadratic inference complexity in sequence length, making them impractical for real-time robotics where control rates of 100–1000 Hz demand sub-10 ms inference. This paper asks whether modern recurrent architectures (xLSTM, Mamba) can replace Transformers as LAM backbones while preserving training-time parallelization and delivering linear-time inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each timestep contributes three tokens to the sequence: a state observation (grayscale 64×64 image for Atari/Procgen, or a zero-padded continuous vector up to 204 dimensions for Meta-World, DMControl, Composuite, and Mimicgen), a return-to-go scalar, and a reward scalar. Actions are deliberately excluded from the input to avoid the copycat problem in continuous control. The model outputs a single discrete action from a shared 2066-class vocabulary (18 discrete actions plus 8 continuous dimensions discretized into 256 bins each), predicted jointly by one shared head rather than autoregressively.

## Method

LRAM replaces the Transformer backbone with a modern recurrent architecture (xLSTM [7:1] or [1:0], or Mamba) that supports parallel training via chunked computation and recurrent inference by maintaining hidden states across timesteps, yielding O(1) per-step cost independent of context length. The sequence representation uses modality-specific encoders (IMPALA-CNN for images, linear layers for vectors and RTG/reward) and omits actions from the input, which the authors show mitigates the copycat problem in continuous control. Continuous actions are discretized into 256 uniformly-spaced bins per dimension and predicted jointly by a single shared action head, eliminating autoregressive decoding. Training follows a Decision Transformer-style behavior cloning objective (cross-entropy on next-action prediction conditioned on RTG) over 894M transitions from 432 tasks across 6 domains, with a context window of 50 timesteps. At inference, custom xLSTM kernels accelerate the recurrent pass, and the constant-time-per-step property enables arbitrarily long contexts without the KV-cache memory blowup that causes Transformers to run out of memory.

## Results

Across 432 tasks from 6 domains (Atari, Composuite, DMControl, Meta-World, Mimicgen, Procgen) with 894M total transitions, LRAM with xLSTM [7:1] outperforms both the DT Transformer baseline and Mamba in validation perplexity and normalized evaluation scores at all four model sizes (16M–206M), with the advantage growing at scale. At 206M parameters, xLSTM outperforms competitors on 3 of 6 domains. At inference, xLSTM achieves lower latency than DT at all context lengths (up to 25,600 timesteps) and handles batch sizes up to 128, whereas DT runs out of memory above batch size 64 due to KV-cache growth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Decision Transformer (DT) baseline, both xLSTM and Mamba achieve lower validation perplexity and higher normalized evaluation scores across all model sizes, with the gap widening substantially at 206M parameters. At inference time, xLSTM and Mamba maintain constant per-step latency regardless of context length, while DT's latency grows linearly with sequence length and it OOMs beyond ~25K timesteps at B=1; throughput for recurrent backbones also scales better with batch size (DT OOMs above B=64). Against Mamba specifically, xLSTM [7:1] performs comparably at 16M–110M but pulls ahead at 206M, which the authors attribute to the state-tracking capability of sLSTM blocks. Mamba is also slower than xLSTM at B=1 due to incompatibility with torch.compile, though the authors note this gap should close with compatible kernels. Overall, xLSTM [7:1] is the strongest backbone across performance, inference speed, and in-context learning (Dark-Room) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Large Action Model (LAM) paradigm established by the Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) and its multi-task successors (Multi-game DT, Gato, RT-1/RT-2, Octo) by replacing the Transformer backbone with modern recurrent architectures—specifically xLSTM and Mamba. The training formulation (RTG-conditioned next-action prediction via behavior cloning) is inherited directly from DT, while the architectural core draws on the xLSTM line of work (Beck et al., 2024) and the selective state-space model family (Gu & Dao, 2023). The key conceptual departure is motivated by the quadratic inference cost of self-attention, which is prohibitive for real-time robotics at 100–1000 Hz control rates, and the paper positions recurrent backbones as a drop-in replacement that preserves parallel training while enabling linear-time inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: robotics applications require sub-10 ms inference at high control rates, a regime where Transformer-based LAMs are currently impractical due to KV-cache memory growth and quadratic attention cost. By demonstrating that xLSTM and Mamba match or exceed Transformer performance across 432 tasks and 6 domains while offering constant-time-per-step inference, the paper provides a viable architectural path for deploying large action models on real robots. Key open questions remain: all experiments are in simulation (no real-robot validation), fine-tuning is limited to offline RL (online RL fine-tuning is envisioned but untested), and ICL is evaluated only in a grid-world setting. The released 894M-transition multi-domain dataset and data pipeline will lower the barrier for future multi-task LAM research.
