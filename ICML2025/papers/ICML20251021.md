## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-audio models (Tango, AudioLDM) achieve high audio fidelity but suffer from high inference latency due to iterative denoising over heavy attention-based backbones. The fast alternative, MAGNET, uses iterative mask-based parallel decoding on discrete tokens but produces inferior audio quality. The paper addresses how to combine the fidelity of continuous latent representations with the speed of parallel mask-based decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251021/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt, encoded into a conditioning sequence by concatenating CLAP and Flan-T5 embeddings (total length L=78, projected to dimension D). It produces 10-second audio clips by generating 256 continuous latent tokens (patched from AudioLDM's VAE latents of shape 256×16×8) in a latent space, then decoding them back to waveforms. Inference uses 100 diffusion sampling steps per decoding iteration and 64 decoding iterations by default, with classifier-free guidance (max scale 5.0) to balance text relevance and audio fidelity.

## Method

IMPACT integrates iterative mask-based parallel decoding with a latent diffusion model by replacing the heavy attention-based diffusion backbone with a lightweight MLP diffusion head (adopted from MAR), while a Transformer-based latent encoder (24–32 layers, MAE-style with masked-position dropping) provides contextual conditioning. Training proceeds in two phases: unconditional pre-training on ~5500 hours of AudioSet (no text, learning audio reconstruction from partially masked latents) followed by text-conditional training on 1200 hours of AudioCaps+WavCaps. During inference, decoding starts from a fully empty sequence; at each iteration, a cosine mask schedule determines how many positions to unmask, positions are selected randomly (since confidence scores are infeasible in continuous space), and the MLP diffusion head performs 100-step reverse diffusion sampling on only the newly unmasked positions, conditioned on the Transformer encoder's hidden representations. This design keeps the per-iteration compute cost low (MLP head vs. full attention U-Net) while preserving the quality benefits of continuous latent representations.

## Results

On the AudioCaps evaluation set, IMPACT base (32 decoding iterations) achieves state-of-the-art FAD of 1.07 and FD of 14.90, surpassing all public baselines including Tango (FAD 1.73), EzAudio-XL (FD 14.98), and AudioLDM2-full (FAD 2.17). The IMPACT large model attains the best IS score of 10.53 and a KL of 1.07, while subjective evaluations show IMPACT base (REL 4.31, OVL 3.51) outperforming Tango 2 (REL 4.13, OVL 3.37) and EzAudio-XL (REL 4.00, OVL 3.35). In terms of inference speed, IMPACT base with 32 decoding iterations achieves 11.2 s latency for a batch of 8, second only to MAGNET-S (6.9 s) and dramatically faster than Tango (182.6 s) or AudioLDM2-full (96.1 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMPACT decisively outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on FAD: IMPACT base achieves 1.07 versus Tango (1.73), Tango-full-ft (2.19), AudioLDM2-full (2.17), and even the non-public AudioLDM2-AC-large (1.42). On FD, IMPACT base (14.90) edges out EzAudio-XL (14.98) and substantially beats Tango (24.42) and Make-an-Audio 2 (16.23). Against the fastest discrete-token baseline MAGNET-S, IMPACT with only 4 decoding iterations already surpasses it on FAD (2.85 vs. 3.22), KL (1.17 vs. 1.42), and CLAP (0.321 vs. 0.287) while also achieving lower latency at all batch sizes (e.g., 7.75 s vs. 52.04 s at batch 64). The one metric where IMPACT trails is CLAP: Tango 2 reaches 0.375 and EzAudio-XL 0.387, compared to IMPACT's 0.364, though the authors attribute this to Tango 2's use of DPO and EzAudio's aggressive data filtering. On subjective text relevance (REL), IMPACT base (4.31) and large (4.39) both exceed Tango 2 (4.13) and EzAudio-XL (4.00), confirming that the CLAP gap does not translate to perceived quality differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMPACT sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: MAGNET's iterative mask-based parallel decoding for audio (operating on discrete tokens) and MAR's extension of that decoding strategy to continuous latent representations via a lightweight MLP diffusion head (demonstrated for image generation). The paper's core conceptual move is to transplant MAR's continuous-latent mask-decoding recipe into the audio domain, replacing the heavy attention-based diffusion backbones of AudioLDM and Tango with a small diffusion head while retaining AudioLDM's VAE for latent extraction. A key departure from both MAGNET and MAR is the introduction of an unconditional pre-training phase on large unlabeled audio (AudioSet), which the authors show is indispensable for competitive performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMPACT offers a practical recipe for low-latency, high-fidelity text-to-audio generation: 16 decoding iterations at 5.7 seconds already surpass all publicly available baselines on FAD and KL, and the model scales well to large batch sizes (8.26 samples/s at batch 64 vs. MAGNET-S's 1.23). The finding that naively replacing MAGNET's discrete tokens with continuous vectors—without the full diffusion-head framework—actually degrades performance significantly underscores that the diffusion modeling component is essential, not merely the continuous representation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer-form audio (beyond 10-second clips), whether the CLAP gap versus Tango 2 (which uses DPO) can be closed without sacrificing speed, and whether the unconditional pre-training requirement generalizes to other modalities.
