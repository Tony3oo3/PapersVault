## Problem

Extending 1D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) to video's spatio-temporal structure remains an open challenge: existing variants like M-RoPE and TAD-RoPE fail to simultaneously satisfy four essential properties—3D structure, appropriate frequency allocation, spatial symmetry, and temporal index scaling. The paper introduces the V-NIAH-D benchmark (V-NIAH with periodic distractors) to expose how suboptimal temporal frequency allocation causes models to be misled by semantically similar distractor frames, revealing a critical weakness in prior designs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video sampled at 2 fps (up to 128 frames, 144 tokens per frame) interleaved with text prompts, producing a text answer. Internally, each token is assigned a 3D position index (t, x, y) where the central patch of each frame sits at (t, t, t) and surrounding patches are offset by their spatial coordinates relative to the frame center. A hyperparameter δ scales the temporal index for video tokens relative to text tokens, decoupling the granularity of frame indices from token indices.

## Method

VideoRoPE restructures the 128-dimensional RoPE head into three groups: 48 interleaved dimensions for horizontal (x) and vertical (y) spatial positions at high frequencies, and 32 higher dimensions for the temporal axis at low frequencies (Low-frequency Temporal Allocation, LTA), which eliminates the periodic oscillations and hash collisions that plague M-RoPE's high-frequency temporal encoding. A Diagonal Layout (DL) places the entire input sequence along the 3D diagonal so that the central patch of each frame receives position (t, t, t), preserving spatial symmetry between preceding and following text tokens and maintaining the same index-increment pattern as vanilla RoPE between adjacent frames. An Adjustable Temporal Spacing (ATS) mechanism introduces a scaling factor δ (optimal at 2.0) that stretches the temporal index for video tokens, aligning the coarser frame-level granularity with the finer text-token granularity. The model is fine-tuned on ~1.3M QA pairs from LLaVA-Video-178k using Qwen2-VL-7B's vision encoder and Qwen2-7B's LLM backbone, with a 8192-token context window during training and evaluation up to 128k tokens at inference.

## Results

On long video understanding at 64k context (extrapolation beyond the 8k training range), VideoRoPE achieves 57.26 on LongVideoBench, 65.56 on MLVU, and 61.33 on Video-MME, outperforming M-RoPE by +2.9, +4.5, and +1.7 respectively. On long video retrieval, VideoRoPE reaches 91.11% accuracy on V-NIAH and 87.11% on V-NIAH-D, surpassing M-RoPE (78.67% and 74.67%) by +12.4 points in both settings. On video hallucination (VideoHallucer), VideoRoPE achieves 46.2 average score versus 34.3 for M-RoPE (+11.9), with the largest gain on temporal hallucination (+29.5 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoRoPE consistently outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark and context length. Against Vanilla RoPE (Su et al., 2024), which flattens video into 1D, VideoRoPE gains +3.2 on LongVideoBench-64k, +3.5 on MLVU-64k, and +3.0 on Video-MME-64k, and a massive +59.3 on V-NIAH (91.11 vs 31.78). Against TAD-RoPE (Gao et al., 2024), a 1D variant with temporal-aware indexing, VideoRoPE leads by +3.8 on LongVideoBench-64k, +4.8 on MLVU-64k, and +2.7 on Video-MME-64k, with +57.6 on V-NIAH. The most direct comparison is against M-RoPE (Wang et al., 2024a), the 3D RoPE used in Qwen2-VL: VideoRoPE improves by +2.9 on LongVideoBench-64k, +4.5 on MLVU-64k, +1.7 on Video-MME-64k, +12.4 on both V-NIAH and V-NIAH-D, and +11.9 on VideoHallucer. The largest margins appear on retrieval and hallucination tasks, where M-RoPE's high-frequency temporal allocation causes periodic oscillations that make it vulnerable to distractors and temporal confusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoRoPE is a direct 3D extension of the 1D RoPE mechanism (Su et al., 2024), building specifically on M-RoPE from Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024a), which already introduced temporal-spatial decomposition of rotary dimensions. The key conceptual departure is inverting M-RoPE's frequency allocation: where M-RoPE assigns high-frequency (low-dimension) rotary angles to the temporal axis, VideoRoPE assigns low-frequency (high-dimension) angles to temporal and interleaves high-frequency angles for spatial axes, motivated by the observation that high-frequency temporal encoding creates periodic 'hash collisions' that mislead the model. The paper also introduces the V-NIAH-D benchmark as a diagnostic extension of V-NIAH (Zhang et al., 2024d), adding periodic semantically-similar distractors to expose frequency-allocation vulnerabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temporal dimensions should use low-frequency (not high-frequency) rotary angles is a clean, actionable design principle for any video LLM using RoPE variants, and the V-NIAH-D benchmark provides the community with a practical diagnostic tool to evaluate positional encoding robustness. However, all experiments are conducted on a single architecture (Qwen2-VL-7B) with a single training dataset (LLaVA-Video-178k subset), leaving open whether the frequency-allocation principle generalizes across model scales, architectures, or training regimes. The diagonal layout and adjustable temporal spacing, while well-motivated, are relatively minor design choices whose individual contributions are modest in ablation, suggesting the core gain comes from the frequency allocation reversal alone.
