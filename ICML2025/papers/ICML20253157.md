## Problem

Diffusion distillation methods suffer from a fundamental limitation: mismatched step sizes and parameter counts between multi-step teachers and one-step students cause them to converge to different local minima, making instance-level imitation suboptimal. While adding a GAN loss to distillation has been shown empirically to help, the underlying mechanism remains unclear, and it is unknown whether a GAN objective alone—without any distillation loss—can suffice to convert a diffusion model into an efficient one-step generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained EDM diffusion U-Net (NCSN++ architecture for CIFAR-10/AFHQv2/FFHQ, ADM for ImageNet) and fine-tunes it into a one-step generator. At inference, a single random noise vector (optionally concatenated with a class embedding for conditional generation) is fed through the network once to produce a 64×64 RGB image. The D2O-F variant freezes 85.8% of convolutional parameters, tuning only normalization layers and skip connections, and achieves near-SOTA FID with as few as 0.2M training images (vs. tens to hundreds of millions for from-scratch training).

## Method

The paper proposes D2O (Diffusion to One-Step), which fine-tunes a pre-trained diffusion U-Net using exclusively a Projected GAN objective (with VGG16-BN and EfficientNet-lite0 as multi-scale feature extractors) and no instance-level distillation loss, arguing that the GAN objective aligns the generator with the overall data distribution rather than forcing it into the teacher's specific local minima. The key variant D2O-F freezes all convolutional weights (85.8% of parameters) and tunes only normalization layers and skip connections, demonstrating that the diffusion pre-training already encodes sufficient generative capability that a lightweight GAN fine-tuning can unlock. The paper further provides a frequency-domain analysis showing that diffusion inference processes low-to-high frequencies in a time-step-dependent manner, with deep U-Net blocks specializing in low frequencies and shallow blocks in high frequencies, offering a mechanistic explanation for why the pre-trained weights already contain one-step generative capacity.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, D2O-F achieves an unconditional FID of 1.54 (IS 10.10) and a conditional FID of 1.44 with only 5M training images, outperforming all prior one-step distillation methods. On ImageNet 64×64 (class-conditional), D2O-F reaches FID 1.16 with precision 0.75, surpassing SiD (FID 1.52), DMD2 (FID 1.51), and CTM (FID 1.73). On FFHQ 64×64, D2O-F achieves FID 0.85, and on AFHQv2 64×64, D2O achieves FID 1.23. Notably, D2O-F requires only 5M training images versus 100M–1000M for competing distillation methods while delivering superior FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2O-F (FID 1.54) substantially outperforms all prior one-step distillation methods on CIFAR-10 unconditional generation: CD (3.55), DMD (2.62), CTM (1.98), SiD (1.92), and StyleGAN2-ADA (2.92). On ImageNet 64×64, D2O-F (FID 1.16) beats SiD (1.52), DMD2 (1.51), CTM (1.73), and CD (6.20), and is competitive with the strongest baselines MSD (1.20) and DMD2 with longer training (1.26). On FFHQ, D2O-F (FID 0.85) and D2O (FID 1.08) both surpass SiD (1.55/1.71) and BOOT (9.0). A critical efficiency advantage: D2O-F uses only 5M training images on ImageNet versus 100M (DMD), 819M (sCD-S), and ~930M (SiD), while achieving the best FID. The paper also shows that adding a distillation loss (CD) to D2O slows convergence and does not improve over D2O-F, confirming that the GAN objective alone is sufficient. D2O-F slightly underperforms D2O on AFHQv2 (1.31 vs 1.23), the only dataset where the frozen variant is not best.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) as the pre-trained diffusion backbone, the Projected GAN discriminator (Sauer et al., 2022) as the adversarial training mechanism, and the Consistency Model parameterization (Song et al., 2023) for the generator's skip/output scaling. Its key departure from the distillation lineage (PD, CD, CTM, DMD, SiD) is the elimination of all instance-level teacher-student matching losses, relying exclusively on a GAN objective. The paper also reframes the relationship between diffusion and GAN training: rather than viewing GAN loss as an auxiliary regularizer for distillation (as in ADD, U-Gen, SDXL-Lightning), it argues the GAN objective alone is sufficient to 'unlock' generative capabilities already embedded in the diffusion-trained weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'generative pre-training' reframing offers a unifying lens that could reshape how the community approaches diffusion model acceleration, potentially making distillation losses unnecessary for a broad class of architectures. The 85% parameter-freezing result (D2O-F) is practically significant: it reduces fine-tuning cost by an order of magnitude and stabilizes GAN training, making one-step generation accessible with minimal compute. Open questions remain: whether the approach transfers to DiT architectures, higher-resolution datasets (e.g., COCO), and whether the preliminary frequency-domain analysis can be made into a predictive theory of which layers to freeze. The paper also notes that the distillation objective may still be useful for downstream tasks like classifier-free guidance or image editing, leaving room for hybrid approaches.
