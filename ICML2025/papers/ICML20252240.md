## Problem

Existing Multimodal LLMs for VideoQA operate as black boxes due to end-to-end training, unable to expose reasoning paths or indicate which video segments support the answer. Grounding-based methods partially address this by localizing temporal segments, but they still rely on opaque models without explicit step-by-step reasoning, especially for structurally complex questions. The paper tackles the gap of integrating modular, interpretable reasoning into Multimodal LLMs for VideoQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252240/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip (sampled at 1 fps) and a free-form text question as input. The Question Parser first decomposes the question into a tree-structured JSON policy specifying which of 7 small grounding modules to invoke and in what order. The Multimodal LLM then receives a composite input: temporally grounded frames, spatially cropped bounding-box regions, a globally pooled video representation, and an instruction prompt explaining each visual component. The output is a natural-language answer accompanied by the explicit reasoning path (the policy tree) and the localized temporal segments and spatial bounding boxes that serve as visual evidence.

## Method

MSR-ViR couples a Question Parser (Qwen2-7B) with a MoST-Grounding module containing 7 small modules (4 temporal, 3 spatial) that are dynamically assembled per policy: temporal modules (DetectAct via UniVTG, TemporalLocalize, TemporalBetween, TemporalTruncate) localize relevant video segments, while spatial modules (LocateObj via YOLO-World, SpatialLocalize, SpatialTruncate) crop relevant regions. The grounded frames plus a global video representation are fed to a Multimodal LLM (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2-VL, or LLaVA-Video) for answer generation. Training uses an Alternate Self-reflection Strategy: the Multimodal LLM is supervised-finetuned (cross-entropy loss) for 200 steps, then the Question Parser is optimized via DPO using the Multimodal LLM's loss as an implicit reward signal, where policies yielding lower loss are treated as preferred. This alternation refines both models jointly. The paper also proves an upper bound on computational complexity, showing the dominant overhead comes from the fixed-length Question Parser prompt rather than video length or resolution.

## Results

On NExT-QA, MSR-ViR_L achieves 74.9% average accuracy (Temporal 72.2, Causal 74.6, Descriptive 80.9), outperforming the best grounding-based baseline GCG (74.6%) and the direct baseline LLaVA-NeXT (73.1%). On STAR-sub, MSR-ViR_L reaches 71.0% average (Interaction 68.9, Sequence 73.1), surpassing LLaVA-NeXT (69.9%) and BLIP-2 (67.2%). In zero-shot long-form VideoQA, MSR-ViR_L achieves 61.2% on EgoSchema-sub and 46.0% on EgoSchema-full, while MSR-ViR_LV reaches 61.9% average on VideoMME. On grounded VideoQA (NExT-GQA), MSR-ViR_L attains the best mIoU (23.4) and IoU@0.5 (16.4) among comparable-size methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSR-ViR_L outperforms all grounding-based Multimodal LLM baselines: it beats GCG (74.6) by 0.3 on NExT-QA average, SeViLa (73.8) by 1.1, and TGB (72.1) by 2.8. Against its direct baseline LLaVA-NeXT, MSR-ViR_L gains +1.8 on NExT-QA and +1.1 on STAR-sub, with the largest margin on Temporal questions (72.2 vs. 69.5), confirming superior temporal understanding. On EgoSchema (zero-shot), MSR-ViR_L surpasses LLaVA-NeXT by +6.4 (subset) and +2.7 (full), and on VideoMME, MSR-ViR_LV improves over LLaVA-Video by +3.4 average, with gains concentrated on longer videos (Long: 52.6 vs. 49.3). On NExT-GQA, MSR-ViR_L achieves the best mIoU (23.4) and IoU@0.5 (16.4) among methods using comparable LLM sizes, beating SeViLa (21.7 mIoU, 13.8 IoU@0.5) and TGB (19.9 mIoU, 11.2 IoU@0.5), demonstrating more precise temporal grounding. The only methods exceeding MSR-ViR on NExT-GQA are those using significantly larger LLMs (LLoVi with GPT-4, MoReVQA with Palm-2), which the authors de-emphasize for fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: grounding-based VideoQA methods (TGB, SeViLa, GCG) that localize temporal segments before feeding frames to a Multimodal LLM, and modular VideoQA methods (MoReVQA, LLoVi) that decompose questions into sub-tasks executed by smaller models. The key departure is integrating the modular grounding network *inside* the Multimodal LLM pipeline rather than using a separate unimodal LLM for policy generation, and jointly optimizing both components via an alternate self-reflection strategy that uses DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) to refine the Question Parser based on the Multimodal LLM's loss signal. Prior modular methods relied on video captions as the only visual input to the policy-generating LLM, while prior grounding methods lacked explicit reasoning paths; MSR-ViR unifies both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in interpretability for VideoQA with Multimodal LLMs by providing both explicit tree-structured reasoning paths and spatially/temporally grounded visual evidence for each answer. However, the empirical gains are modest (+1.5–3.4 points over direct baselines), and the approach introduces roughly 2× inference overhead from the Question Parser, which limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the rule-based spatial localization modules (mirroring, cropping) can be replaced with learned components, whether the DPO-based policy refinement scales to more complex multi-hop reasoning, and whether the framework's benefits persist as Multimodal LLMs themselves improve in temporal understanding.
