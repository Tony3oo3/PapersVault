## Problem

Confidence-based pseudo-label filtering in semi-supervised semantic segmentation suffers from an inherent trade-off between true positive rate and false positive rate: high thresholds discard correct but low-confidence predictions, while low thresholds admit erroneous high-confidence ones. Existing methods that operate in the embedding space (e.g., contrastive learning) still rely on confidence scoring in the prediction space to select pseudo labels, failing to exploit the noise-resistant structure of the embedding space itself for scoring.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (513×513 crops for PASCAL, 801×801 for Cityscapes) processed by a teacher-student DeepLabv3+ network. The scoring operates on 256-dimensional pixel embeddings extracted from the ASPP module output before the classifier, computed over a batch of unlabeled images. The output is a per-pixel binary retention decision (use or ignore the teacher's pseudo-label) that gates the consistency regularization loss, rather than producing a segmentation mask directly.

## Method

AgScore constructs two reference sets from pixel embeddings: positive agents (N=64) sampled class-balanced from the top-1% confidence pixels, and negative agents (M=256) selected from bottom-1% confidence pixels via an orthogonal greedy strategy that maximizes cosine dissimilarity to already-selected agents, ensuring coverage of diverse error patterns. The score for a candidate pixel is a sum-softmax ratio of its cosine similarities to positive versus negative agents, capturing whether the pixel's embedding aligns with the homogeneous cluster of correct predictions or the scattered patterns of incorrect ones. A two-component GMM is then fit to the score distribution via EM, and pixels with posterior probability of correctness above 0.5 are retained for consistency training. A theoretical analysis shows the score ratio follows a log-normal distribution and that FPR at fixed TPR is a decreasing function of the number of negative agents, justifying the choice M > N.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC classic (ResNet-50, 1/16 partition with 92 labeled images), AgScore boosts FixMatch from 60.1% to 63.2% mIoU (+3.1%), UniMatch from 67.4% to 69.4% (+2.0%), and RankMatch from 71.6% to 73.1% (+1.5%). On Cityscapes (ResNet-101, 1/16), the gains are +3.4% for FixMatch (74.2→77.6) and +1.7% for UniMatch (76.6→78.3). On COCO (1/512 partition), UniMatch+AgScore reaches 33.9% mIoU vs. 31.9% for UniMatch alone (+2.0%). Improvements are consistently largest in the most label-scarce settings, confirming AgScore's strength in exploiting unlabeled data when labeled supervision is minimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgScore is integrated as a drop-in replacement for the confidence-thresholding pseudo-label filter in three frameworks: FixMatch, UniMatch, and RankMatch. In every configuration tested (ResNet-50/101, all partitions on PASCAL VOC, Cityscapes, and COCO), the AgScore-enhanced variant outperforms its base framework, with the largest gains (up to +3.9% mIoU on Cityscapes 1/16) occurring in the most label-scarce regime. Against other SOTA methods, RankMatch+AgScore (76.1% on PASCAL 1/16 R101) surpasses DAW (74.8%) and DDFP (74.9%), while UniMatch+AgScore (75.6%) also exceeds both. On the full-data PASCAL setting, UniMatch+AgScore (82.6% R101) and RankMatch+AgScore (82.6% R101) match or slightly exceed DDFP (81.9%) and DAW (81.5%). The paper does not report AgScore integrated into PCR, GTA-Seg, ReCo, AugSeg, NP-SemiSeg, PRCL, AEL, CFCG, Co-Train, PseudoSeg, PC2Seg, or CISC-R, so direct head-to-head comparisons against those methods are not available; however, the AgScore-enhanced baselines remain competitive with or above those methods' reported numbers on the shared benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dominant teacher-student semi-supervised segmentation paradigm (FixMatch, UniMatch, RankMatch) by replacing the standard confidence-threshold pseudo-label filter with an embedding-space scoring function (AgScore) that exploits homogeneous patterns among pixel features. It draws on DivideMix's GMM-based thresholding to convert the raw similarity score into a binary accept/reject decision, and introduces an orthogonal selection strategy for negative agents that has no direct precedent in prior pseudo-label filtering work. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prediction-space-only scoring tradition by arguing that the embedding space, which prior contrastive-learning methods (PCR, PC²Seg) already showed to be more noise-tolerant, contains additional discriminative structure (pattern consensus) that confidence alone cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The homogeneous-pattern observation provides a principled, interpretable rationale for why embedding-space scoring can outperform confidence thresholding, and the theoretical analysis (log-normal separability, monotonic FPR decrease with more negative agents) gives practitioners guidance on hyperparameter choices (N=64, M=256). However, the practical gains are modest (typically 0.4–3.1 mIoU) and the method is a drop-in replacement that does not alter the overall training pipeline, limiting its impact as a standalone contribution. Open questions include whether the homogeneous-pattern signal persists in transformer-based backbones, whether the orthogonal selection strategy generalizes to very high-class-count settings (e.g., ADE20K), and whether the GMM fitting step can be replaced by a learned threshold to reduce per-batch computational overhead.
