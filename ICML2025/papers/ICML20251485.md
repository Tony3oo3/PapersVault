## Problem

RL-based training for neural combinatorial optimization suffers from low sample efficiency due to sparse rewards, and the recent self-labeling learning (SLL) paradigm still discards all but the best sampled solution during training. This paper asks whether the natural preference ordering among multiple sampled solutions—determined by their objective values—can be exploited more fully to improve training efficiency without requiring expensive labels or explicit MDP formulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251485/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a COP instance as structured input: a disjunctive graph with processing times and machine assignments for JSP/FJSP, or 2D node coordinates for TSP. During training, B solutions (B−1 from stochastic sampling plus 1 from greedy rollout) are generated per instance, then K are uniformly selected and K−1 preference pairs are formed. At inference, the model outputs a complete solution as a sequence of actions—a schedule of operation-to-machine assignments (|y|=nm steps for JSP, nk for FJSP) or a node-visit tour (|y|=n steps for TSP)—constructed autoregressively with constraint masking.

## Method

BOPO introduces a preference optimization training paradigm with two problem-aware components. First, best-anchored preference pair construction: a hybrid rollout generates diverse and high-quality solutions, uniform filtering selects K representative solutions across the quality spectrum, and best-anchored pairing creates K−1 pairs each anchored to the best solution, prioritizing learning from high-quality examples while discouraging diverse suboptimal ones. Second, an objective-guided preference optimization loss based on the Bradley-Terry model, where the implicit preference is the length-normalized log-likelihood and the scaling factor β = g(y_l)/g(y_w) is derived directly from the objective values, acting as a natural curriculum that amplifies gradients for pairs with large quality gaps. This eliminates the need for a reference model (as in DPO), a reward model (as in RLHF), or fixed hyperparameters (as in SimPO), and is architecture-agnostic—demonstrated with a GAT+LSTM model (MGL) for scheduling and Transformer-based models (POMO, INViT) for TSP.

## Results

On JSP, BOPO achieves average optimality gaps of 7.5% (TA), 2.3% (LA), and 12.9% (DMU) with sampling rollout (B'=512), outperforming the SLL-based SLIM (7.8%, 2.5%, 13.1%) and all RL-based constructive methods. On TSP100, BOPO achieves a 0.04% gap with augmentation, compared to 0.14% for POMO and 1.17% for SLIM. On FJSP, BOPO reaches 5.0% (e-data), 3.4% (r-data), and 0.4% (v-data) gaps with B'=512 sampling, surpassing all RL baselines (DNN, HG, RS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOPO consistently outperforms the SLL-based SLIM across all three JSP benchmarks (e.g., 12.9% vs 13.4% greedy on TA; 6.7% vs 7.8% on LA) and all RL-based constructive methods (L2D, SchN, CL), with the gap widening under sampling rollout. On TSP, BOPO substantially surpasses POMO (0.37% vs 0.46% greedy on TSP100; 0.04% vs 0.14% with augmentation) and dramatically outperforms SLIM (0.04% vs 1.17% aug on TSP100), while matching DABL's supervised performance without requiring optimal labels. On FJSP, BOPO beats all RL baselines (DNN, HG, RS) by 1–3% gap across e-data, r-data, and v-data. BOPO is competitive with the RL-improvement method L2S_5k on TA (7.5% vs 7.4%) but requires ~4.8 min vs 4 hours of solving time. Against exact solvers (Gurobi, OR-Tools), BOPO achieves comparable gaps on smaller instances while being orders of magnitude faster (sub-second vs hours).

## Relation to Prior Work

BOPO adapts the preference optimization framework from LLM alignment (DPO, SimPO) to neural combinatorial optimization, replacing the reference model with an objective-guided adaptive scaling factor g(y_l)/g(y_w) derived directly from solution quality. It departs from the dominant RL paradigm (POMO, PPO) by eliminating the need for MDP formulation and reward baselines, and from the recent SLL paradigm (SLIM) by leveraging all sampled solutions in preference pairs rather than discarding all but the best. The best-anchored pairing strategy—always contrasting the best solution against suboptimal ones—is a COP-specific design choice not present in LLM preference optimization, where pairs are typically arbitrary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing preference optimization as a viable, architecture-agnostic training paradigm for NCO, BOPO offers a practical alternative to RL that avoids sparse-reward issues and MDP design while achieving higher sample efficiency. The comparison between BOPO and SLIM_MGL (identical model, different training) convincingly demonstrates that the training paradigm itself is a major performance driver, not just model architecture. A key limitation is the still-substantial rollout cost (B=256 solutions per instance), and the authors identify reducing this cost—via problem invariance, solution symmetry, or problem-specific heuristics during training—as the primary open direction.
