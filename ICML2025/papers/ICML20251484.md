## Problem

Binary Spiking Neural Networks (BSNNs) offer significant efficiency gains in forward propagation through binary weights, but their training still relies on full-precision latent weights and backpropagation through time, incurring memory overhead that contradicts the efficiency motivation. Existing online training methods for SNNs are designed for full-precision weights and do not exploit the binary sign-flipping structure, leaving a gap in memory-efficient training specifically for BSNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251484/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are processed as discrete spike trains over T time steps (2–32 depending on dataset): RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR) or 224×224 (ImageNet), event streams at 48×48×10 (DVS-CIFAR10), and 32×32 Mel-spectrograms (GSC audio). The network uses LIF neurons with binary (±1) weights in all intermediate layers while retaining full-precision weights in the first and last layers, producing a single class label per input. The key constraint is that all weight updates occur as discrete sign flips rather than continuous parameter adjustments, and memory usage must remain independent of the number of time steps.

## Method

BSO eliminates latent weights entirely by maintaining only a momentum vector M^l per layer, updated via exponential moving average of instantaneous gradients. At each time step, a binary weight w is flipped (sign inverted) only when the element-wise product w·M^l exceeds a threshold γ, ensuring updates align with gradient direction and preventing ascent-direction flips. The temporal-aware variant T-BSO adds a second-order moment v^l[t] (analogous to Adam's variance term) to make the threshold adaptive: γ·√(v^l[t]+ε) automatically relaxes in low-gradient regions and tightens in high-gradient regions, capturing heterogeneous gradient distributions across time steps. Both methods operate within an online training framework (OTTT/NDOT) that decomposes gradients into instantaneous spatial components, achieving time-independent memory. Theoretical analysis provides regret bounds of O(√T) for BSO and O(T^{3/4}) for T-BSO under bounded-gradient assumptions, with average regret vanishing as T→∞.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, T-BSO achieves 94.70% top-1 accuracy with 6 time steps (94.86% at T=4), using only 1.20 MB of model storage (30.8× smaller than full-precision baselines). On CIFAR-100, T-BSO reaches 74.27% (T=6), and on the neuromorphic DVS-CIFAR10 dataset it attains 81.00% (T=10), outperforming all other online-training and BSNN baselines. On ImageNet, T-BSO achieves 58.86% with a 4.22 MB model (20.7× compression), and on the audio GSC dataset it reaches 96.12% with a 1.26 MB model. Training memory remains constant (≈2.9–3.1 GB) regardless of time steps, versus BPTT's 20.3× memory growth from T=1 to T=35.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-BSO outperforms all BPTT-based BSNN baselines (CBP, Q-SNN) across every dataset: on CIFAR-10 it gains +1.50% over Q-SNN (94.70% vs 93.20%) and +3.19% over CBP (94.70% vs 91.51%); on CIFAR-100 it leads Q-SNN by +0.79% and CBP by +7.74%; on DVS-CIFAR10 it surpasses Q-SNN by +1.90% and CBP by +6.30%. Against online-training baselines, T-BSO beats OTTT on CIFAR-10 (+0.97%) and CIFAR-100 (+3.16%), but trails NDOT on CIFAR-10 (−0.20%) and CIFAR-100 (−2.34%), and falls short of SLTT on ImageNet (58.86% vs 66.19%) and DVS-CIFAR10 (81.00% vs 82.20%). On the audio GSC dataset, T-BSO achieves the highest accuracy (96.12%) while being the only method combining online training with 1-bit weights, outperforming STS-T by +0.94% and ATIF by +1.81% at a model size 5–35× smaller. The key differentiator is that T-BSO is the only method simultaneously supporting online training and binary weights, achieving 20–31× model compression without sacrificing accuracy on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: online training for SNNs (OTTT, NDOT, SLTT) which achieves temporal-independent memory but targets full-precision networks, and BSNN training (Q-SNN, CBP-QSNN, BS4NN) which exploits binary weights in forward propagation but relies on BPTT with latent weights in the backward pass. BSO departs from both by eliminating latent weights entirely, replacing continuous gradient updates with a momentum-driven sign-flip mechanism that is native to the binary weight paradigm. The temporal-aware extension (T-BSO) draws on adaptive optimization (Adam-like second-order moments) to modulate the flip threshold across time steps, a design choice not present in either parent line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in BSNN research: the binary weight representation that gives BSNNs their 30× compression advantage in inference is undermined during training by the need to store full-precision latent weights, neg much of the memory savings. By proving that only sign-flip decisions matter (not latent weight magnitudes), BSO makes the training memory orthogonal to time steps and eliminates latent weight storage, which is practically significant for edge and neuromorphic deployment. Open questions include the notable accuracy gap on ImageNet (58.86% vs. 66.19% for SLTT), whether the flip mechanism generalizes to ternary or multi-bit quantization, and whether the convergence guarantees (O(√T) and O(T^{3/4}) regret) translate to practical training stability at scale beyond the tested architectures.
