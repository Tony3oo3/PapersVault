## Problem

Existing source-free domain adaptation methods for SAM rely on fixed teacher-student self-training, which assumes the teacher consistently provides more reliable predictions than the student. Under severe domain shifts (e.g., medical imaging, camouflage, robotic scenes), this assumption breaks down as the student can surpass the teacher in capturing domain-specific features, limiting adaptation performance. The paper addresses this by replacing the static role assignment with a dynamic, bidirectional mutual learning mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a segmentation prompt (bounding box, five positive/five negative points, or a fitted polygon) and produces a binarized segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution. During training, each of the two collaborative encoders receives a differently augmented view of the input image (random color, brightness, contrast, shadows) to encourage complementary representations. Prompts are generated from ground-truth masks following the WeSAM protocol, and the final adapted model is selected as the better-performing of the two collaborative networks at the end of training.

## Method

The framework employs two independent SAM image encoders, each extended with a LoRA module and initialized with slight parameter perturbations (σ=0.001) from the frozen pre-trained SAM to break symmetry. At every training iteration, a knowledge-preserving metric computes the cosine similarity between each network's foreground feature representation and that of the frozen reference SAM; the network with higher similarity is dynamically assigned the teacher role, while the other becomes the student. Three losses drive the mutual learning: a direct alignment loss (focal + Dice) pulls the student's score map toward the teacher's, a reverse distillation loss pushes the teacher slightly away from the student to maintain diversity, and a triplet relationship loss enforces that the teacher's foreground features stay closer to the reference than the student's. A preservation loss additionally constrains both networks to remain close to the original SAM predictions, preventing catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.

## Results

The proposed Collaborative Mutual Learning framework achieves the highest average mIoU across all six target domains: 71.50 on COCO, 76.45 on Pascal VOC, 83.29 on Kvasir-SEG, 70.74 on CAMO, 64.66 on COD10K, and 84.02 on OCID. The GAIN metric (improvement over the frozen source SAM) reaches 17.32 on Kvasir-SEG and 13.74 on CAMO, indicating the largest relative improvements in the most challenging domain-shift scenarios. The method outperforms the closest competitor WeSAM by 1.97 (COCO), 2.74 (Pascal VOC), 7.26 (Kvasir-SEG), 3.45 (CAMO), 1.88 (COD10K), and 2.14 (OCID) in average mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. Against the closest competitor WeSAM (fixed teacher-student self-training), the gains are 1.97 mIoU on COCO, 2.74 on Pascal VOC, 7.26 on Kvasir-SEG, 3.45 on CAMO, 1.88 on COD10K, and 2.14 on OCID. WDASS is the second-strongest baseline, trailing by 2.16 (COCO), 2.03 (Pascal VOC), 12.43 (Kvasir-SEG), 5.10 (CAMO), 4.34 (COD10K), and 3.52 (OCID). TRIBE and SHOT show moderate adaptation but fall 5.46 and 3.87 mIoU behind on COCO respectively, with larger gaps on medical and camouflaged domains. TENT, Soft Teacher, and DePT all underperform or barely improve over the frozen source SAM on several datasets (e.g., DePT drops 11.29 mIoU on CAMO and 13.69 on COD10K), confirming that simple test-time entropy minimization or prompt tuning is insufficient for severe domain shifts. The largest absolute gains appear on Kvasir-SEG (medical) and CAMO (camouflage), where domain shift is most pronounced and the dynamic role-assignment mechanism provides the most benefit over fixed teacher-student paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends WeSAM (Zhang et al., 2024), which introduced a fixed teacher-student self-training paradigm for source-free SAM adaptation, by replacing the static role assignment with a dynamic mutual learning mechanism where two collaborative networks alternately serve as teacher and student based on a knowledge-preservation reliability metric. The architectural foundation is SAM's encoder-decoder-prompt structure, with LoRA-based fine-tuning of the image encoder for parameter efficiency, while the mutual learning strategy draws on adaptive mutual learning for domain adaptation (Zhou et al., 2023a). The loss design combines standard SAM distillation (focal + Dice) with a reverse distillation term for diversity and a novel triplet relationship loss that anchors both networks to the frozen pre-trained reference, distinguishing it from prior single-directional distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic role assignment addresses a genuine practical limitation of fixed teacher-student self-training under severe domain shift, where the assumed teacher superiority breaks down. However, the reliability metric (cosine similarity of foreground features to the pre-trained reference) is a heuristic that may not capture all failure modes, and the framework only adapts the image encoder via LoRA, leaving the mask decoder and prompt encoder frozen, which limits the ceiling of adaptation. Open questions include whether the mutual learning benefit persists with more than two networks, whether the approach transfers to SAM 2 or other foundation models, and whether the dynamic role switching is truly necessary versus the simpler explanation that two differently-augmented networks with shared losses already provide sufficient diversity.
