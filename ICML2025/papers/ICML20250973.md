## Problem

VAE-based single-cell methods cannot incorporate external gene knowledge (e.g., molecular sequences, textual annotations) because their fixed-length vector representations are incompatible with variable-length prior knowledge, while LLM-based foundation models are computationally expensive and poorly suited for tabular gene expression matrices. No existing framework efficiently bridges these complementary strengths to produce context-aware, biologically interpretable representations for both cells and genes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250973/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs: a scaled log-normalized scRNA-seq expression matrix (N cells × M genes, values clipped at ±10 after library-size normalization and z-score scaling) and precomputed static gene embeddings of dimension D from a single pretrained LLM (D ranges from 512 for scGPT to 5120 for ESM, depending on the source modality). It produces K-dimensional (K=40) context-aware cell and gene latent embeddings, plus reconstructed expression values computed as the dot product of cell and gene decoder hidden outputs plus a gene-specific bias, enabling downstream tasks like clustering, imputation, and gene module discovery.

## Method

sciLaMA extends the siVAE paired-VAE architecture by replacing the gene encoder's input from the transposed expression matrix with precomputed static gene embeddings from pretrained multimodal LLMs, so the gene encoder learns to adapt these fixed embeddings into context-specific representations rather than learning gene representations de novo. Training proceeds in three steps: (1) pretrain the cell VAE to reconstruct the expression matrix, (2) freeze cell parameters and pretrain the gene encoder/decoder to reconstruct expression via the paired dot-product output, and (3) jointly optimize all parameters with an additional alignment loss (γ=0.05) that enforces the latent-space dot product z_cell^T × z_gene to approximate the original expression value, ensuring interpretable dimension-to-dimension correspondence between cell and gene embeddings. This design keeps the gene encoder's input size fixed at D (the LLM embedding dimension) rather than scaling with the number of cells, making it tractable for large datasets while the lightweight adapter architecture runs ~25× faster than fine-tuning scGPT.

## Results

On the 5-dataset human pancreas benchmark (14,767 cells), sciLaMA achieves an average ARI of 0.522 and NMI of 0.745 for cell clustering, outperforming scVI-batch (ARI 0.447, NMI 0.718) and fine-tuned scGPT (ARI 0.483, NMI 0.704). For batch effect correction, sciLaMA reaches batchASW 0.865 and iLISI 0.238, surpassing the next-best methods by 16.26% and 96.69% respectively. On spatial transcriptomics gene imputation (leave-one-gene-out, 30 genes), sciLaMA achieves PCC 0.222 and JSD 0.283, outperforming the best baseline (gimVI) by 27.39% in PCC and 32.86% in 1-JSD. Training runtime is 759 s on a single A100 GPU, roughly 25× faster than fine-tuned scGPT (19,474 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

sciLaMA outperforms scVI-batch (Lopez et al., 2018) by 16.78% in ARI and 3.76% in NMI on cell clustering, and by 16.26% in batchASW for batch correction, confirming that LLM-derived gene priors add value beyond a standard VAE. Against fine-tuned scGPT (Cui et al., 2024), sciLaMA-scGPT wins on ARI/NMI (6.82% improvement) and batch integration (34.57% better batchASW/iLISI on average), while scGPT retains a marginal edge in silhouette width (ASW 0.650 vs 0.535); sciLaMA is also ~25× faster to train. Zero-shot foundation models (scGPT, CellPLM) are substantially outperformed, with sciLaMA achieving roughly 1.5× higher ARI/NMI. For gene imputation on OSM-FISH spatial data, sciLaMA beats scProjection (Johansen et al., 2023), gimVI (Lopez et al., 2019), uniPort (Cao et al., 2022), and Tangram (Biancalani et al., 2021) across all four metrics (PCC, SCC, JSD, RMSE), with the largest gap over Tangram (PCC 0.222 vs 0.130). The self-informed ablation sciLaMA(s.i.)—which uses the transposed expression matrix instead of LLM embeddings—consistently underperforms all LLM-informed variants, validating that external gene knowledge acts as a regularizer rather than merely adding parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

sciLaMA extends the siVAE paired-VAE architecture (Choi et al., 2023), which jointly learns cell and gene representations from scRNA-seq data alone, by replacing the gene encoder's input with precomputed static embeddings from pretrained multimodal LLMs (text, protein sequence, or single-cell foundation models). This positions it at the intersection of two lineages: the VAE-based single-cell representation learning tradition (scVI, scVAE) and the emerging use of LLM-derived gene embeddings in biological analysis (SATURN, GenePT, scELMo). The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating LLM embeddings as a frozen prior that a lightweight adapter contextualizes, rather than fine-tuning the LLM itself or using embeddings as auxiliary features in a separate pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a 25× computational speedup over fine-tuning scGPT while outperforming it on cell clustering and batch integration, making LLM-informed single-cell analysis accessible without foundation-model-scale compute. The finding that any structured prior knowledge (text, protein, or single-cell embeddings) consistently outperforms the self-informed baseline suggests the benefit is primarily regularizational rather than content-specific, which is an open question for understanding what biological information actually transfers. Limitations include dependence on precomputed embeddings (no dynamic gene-gene interaction modeling), evaluation limited to human/mouse datasets of moderate size (~15K cells), and the need for a reference scRNA-seq dataset for imputation tasks. Broader implications include a template for integrating any modality of prior knowledge into tabular biological data analysis through lightweight adapters.
