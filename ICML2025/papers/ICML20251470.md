## Problem

Preference data used for LLM alignment unavoidably contains 20–40% label noise, yet existing mitigations either only marginally reduce noise impact (label smoothing) or require an external teacher LLM for sample filtering, incurring high computational cost and potentially incorrect filtering. No prior method simultaneously achieves noise-tolerance and effective noisy-sample identification without relying on external models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251470/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy preference dataset of (query, response pair, binary preference label) tuples along with an SFT reference model, and produces a final aligned LLM after M iterative epochs. During training, the dataset itself evolves: each epoch filters out the top-ρ fraction of samples by ROPO loss and replaces them with newly sampled candidates via robustness-guided rejection sampling, so the effective training set shrinks in noise but retains query coverage. The only external model used is the frozen SFT reference; no separate reward model or teacher LLM is required.

## Method

ROPO formulates noisy preference learning as a constrained optimization over model parameters θ and per-sample binary weights w, solved by alternating noise-tolerant training and top-ρ loss-based sample filtering. The key technical contribution is a conservative gradient weight w_ropo that decreases when the implicit reward margin Δ is large (opposite to DPO's monotonically increasing w_dpo), yielding a loss decomposed into the standard DPO term plus a noise-aware term ℓ_na weighted α times higher. Theorems 3.4 and 3.5 prove that ℓ_na's optimal solution is invariant to label-flip noise η < 1/2 and that it separates noisy from clean samples under a far looser convergence requirement (δ < 1/2) than DPO (δ < (1−2η)/2 · (P*(c₁)+P*(c₂)−1)). A robustness-guided rejection sampling step then compensates for discarded queries by generating K new responses per filtered query, forming 2K candidate pairs, and retaining only the candidate with minimum ROPO loss, thereby preserving query diversity without external reward signals.

## Results

ROPO achieves the highest win rate across all three datasets (UFB, Alpaca Comparison, TL;DR) and all noise levels (0%, 20%, 40%) for both Mistral-7B and Llama-2-7B. The most dramatic gains appear on TL;DR: with Llama-2-7B, ROPO reaches 79.20% win rate at 40% noise versus DPO's 35.20%, a 44-point gap. Even without artificial noise, ROPO outperforms DPO by 16.0% on TL;DR (79.00% vs. 63.00% for Mistral-7B), indicating the datasets inherently contain noise. The performance advantage of ROPO over all baselines grows monotonically as the noise rate increases from 0% to 40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROPO outperforms all four baselines (DPO, IPO, rDPO, cDPO) across every dataset, model, and noise level tested. Against DPO, the gap is largest on TL;DR: at 40% noise with Llama-2-7B, ROPO achieves 79.20% vs. DPO's 35.20% (a 44-point margin), and even at 0% noise ROPO leads by 16.0 points (79.00% vs. 63.00% for Mistral-7B). Among the label-smoothing baselines, rDPO is the closest competitor under 20–40% noise (e.g., 84.72% vs. ROPO's 87.70% on UFB at 40% noise for Mistral-7B), but still trails ROPO by 2–5 points; the paper attributes rDPO's limited effectiveness to its requirement that the smoothing parameter ε exactly match the true noise proportion. cDPO performs comparably to rDPO under moderate noise but degrades more sharply at 40% noise. IPO is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly on TL;DR where it drops to 48.80% at 40% noise (Mistral-7B), well below ROPO's 75.80%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROPO builds directly on DPO by decomposing its loss into the original DPO term plus a new noise-aware term (ℓ_na) with a conservative gradient weighting strategy that suppresses updates on high-uncertainty samples. It departs from prior noise-robust alignment methods in two key ways: unlike label-smoothing approaches (rDPO, cDPO) that only marginally mitigate noise without reducing it, and unlike methods requiring an external teacher LLM for sample filtering (Gao et al., 2024), ROPO integrates noise-tolerance and active noisy-sample filtering into a single self-contained iterative loop. The iterative train-filter-rejection-sampling paradigm draws on the broader learning-from-noisy-labels literature (sample reweighting, robust loss design) but is the first to apply it in an end-to-end preference alignment setting without external models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Preference noise at 20–40% is ubiquitous in real-world datasets, and ROPO provides a practical, self-contained remedy that avoids the computational cost of external LLMs while offering clean theoretical guarantees (complete noise-tolerance of ℓ_na, provable distinguishability of noisy vs. clean samples). The finding that iterative training *improves* alignment performance under noise—while DPO degrades—validates the core design and suggests that data curation during training is more effective than post-hoc filtering. Open questions include how the method scales beyond 70B parameters, whether the symmetric-noise assumption in the theory holds for structured (asymmetric) annotation biases, and how sensitive performance is to the filtering ratio ρ when the true noise rate is unknown.
