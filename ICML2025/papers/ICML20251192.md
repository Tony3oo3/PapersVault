## Problem

GFlowNet training objectives for graph generation require accurate state transition probabilities, but multiple distinct actions can produce isomorphic graphs (the equivalent action problem), making exact computation intractable due to expensive graph isomorphism tests. Neglecting this issue introduces a systematic bias: atom-based generation under-samples highly symmetric graphs, while fragment-based generation over-samples symmetric fragments, both of which are prevalent in real molecules (over 50% of ZINC250k molecules have more than one symmetry).

## Input / Output

The method takes a reward function (a proxy model predicting molecular properties such as HOMO-LUMO gap or sEH binding energy) and an initial graph state, and produces molecular graphs sampled in proportion to the true reward. The key modification is that the reward signal is scaled by the order of the terminal graph's automorphism group (|Aut(G_n)|) for atom-based generation, or by |Aut(G)|/∏|Aut(C_i)| for fragment-based generation, where C_i are the constituent fragments. This scaling is computed once per trajectory using the bliss canonical labeling algorithm, rather than requiring per-transition isomorphism tests.

## Method

The core insight (Theorem 4.6) is that the ratio of forward to backward orbit-equivalent actions at any transition equals the ratio of the automorphism group sizes of the two graphs, |Aut(G)|/|Aut(G′)|. This telescoping property means that for the Trajectory Balance objective, all per-transition symmetry corrections collapse into a single factor |Aut(G_n)| at the terminal state, so the reward can simply be multiplied by this factor (Corollary 5.1). For Detailed Balance, a graph-level flow function F̃(G) = |Aut(G)|·F(G) satisfies the corrected balance condition (Theorem 5.2), again requiring only one automorphism evaluation per trajectory. For fragment-based generation, the correction additionally divides by the product of each fragment's automorphism group size to penalize symmetric fragments that create spurious forward equivalent actions (Theorem 5.3). The approach is exact, requires no per-transition isomorphism tests, and is compatible with both TB and DB objectives as well as flow-matching.

## Results

On the atom-based molecule generation task (QM9), Reward Scaling (Exact) improves diversity from 0.929 to 0.959 and uniqueness fraction from 0.93 to 1.0 over Vanilla GFlowNets, while maintaining comparable top-K reward (1.091 vs. 1.09). On the fragment-based task, Reward Scaling (Exact) achieves the highest top-K reward of 0.952 (vs. 0.941 for Vanilla) and top-K diversity of 0.151, with the approximate correction already reaching 0.949 top-K reward. In the synthetic graph environment, Reward Scaling achieves L1 errors comparable to exact Transition Correction and substantially lower than both Vanilla and the PE-based approximate method of Ma et al. (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: Vanilla GFlowNets (no symmetry correction) and the PE method of Ma et al. (2024) (approximate per-transition orbit correction via positional encoding). On the synthetic graph environment, Reward Scaling achieves L1 errors on par with exact Transition Correction and substantially outperforms both Vanilla and PE, with PE showing intermediate but still biased results. On the atom-based molecule task, Reward Scaling (Exact) wins on diversity (0.959 vs. 0.929) and uniqueness (1.0 vs. 0.93) while matching top-K reward, though the paper notes the improvement is modest due to the reward structure being negatively correlated with symmetry. On the fragment-based task, Reward Scaling (Exact) achieves the best top-K reward (0.952 vs. 0.941 Vanilla), and the paper highlights that Vanilla excessively samples symmetric fragments (5220 cyclohexane instances vs. 1042 for the corrected method). The PE method is not directly compared in the molecule generation experiments but is shown to be approximate and computationally more expensive than the single reward-scaling correction proposed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022; Bengio et al., 2023) by providing the first rigorous theoretical treatment of the equivalent action problem in graph generation, building on the orbit-stabilizer theorem from group theory. It departs from Ma et al. (2024), who first identified the bias and proposed approximate per-transition corrections via positional encoding, by showing that a single reward scaling by the automorphism group order at the trajectory end is both exact and sufficient for TB, DB, and FM objectives. The work also connects to autoregressive graph generation literature (Chen et al., 2021) on orbit sizes and equivalent transitions, but reframes the problem within the GFlowNet flow-consistency formalism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all prior GFlowNet results on symmetric molecules were systematically biased—penalizing symmetric graphs in atom-based generation while over-sampling symmetric fragments in fragment-based generation—is practically significant given that over 50% of ZINC250k molecules exhibit non-trivial symmetries. The fix is remarkably simple (multiply reward by |Aut(G)|), making it easy to adopt, but its correctness depends on the specific set of predefined graph actions and permutation-equivariant parameterization, limiting generality to novel action designs. Open questions include whether the correction extends to tasks with different symmetry structures, whether GNN expressiveness limitations (orbit collapse) interact with the correction, and whether the approach applies to non-graph compositional objects with analogous symmetries.
