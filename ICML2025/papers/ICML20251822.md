## Problem

Next-token prediction (NTP) is myopic for open-ended creative tasks that require an implicit, multi-step stochastic plan before generating output — the model must discover global higher-order patterns rather than local next-token regularities. Additionally, the standard mechanism for eliciting randomness (temperature sampling at the output layer) may be suboptimal compared to injecting noise at the input. The paper designs minimal, objectively quantifiable algorithmic tasks to expose these creative limits and test alternatives.

## Input / Output

The model receives a short task prompt plus (optionally) an arbitrary random seed prefix string; the knowledge graph or structural rules are stored entirely in model weights, not provided in context. For diffusion models, the input is a fully masked token sequence that is iteratively unmasked. Outputs are short coherent strings: sibling-parent node triplets (e.g., 'g,f,Y'), triangle vertex triplets, or adjacency lists that must resolve to a circle or line graph under some permutation. The key constraint is that the latent 'leap of thought' (which parent to pick, which permutation to use) is never explicit in the token sequence — the model must infer and plan it implicitly.

## Method

The paper introduces four minimal algorithmic tasks isolating combinational creativity (Sibling Discovery, Triangle Discovery: recall-and-search over an in-weights graph) and exploratory creativity (Circle/Line Construction: generate adjacency lists resolving to a fixed graph structure under a novel permutation). It then compares three training objectives — standard NTP, teacherless multi-token training (all tokens predicted simultaneously from the prompt alone), and discrete diffusion (SEDD) — on Gemma v1 (2B) and GPT-2 (86M) models, showing multi-token approaches achieve up to 5× higher algorithmic creativity and substantially less memorization. The core mechanistic argument is that NTP falls into 'Clever Hans cheats' (exploiting locally visible tokens rather than learning the global latent plan), making it data-inefficient by a factor proportional to graph degree. Orthogonally, the paper proposes seed-conditioning: training and testing with an arbitrary random prefix per example, which induces non-trivial diversity even under greedy decoding and matches or exceeds temperature sampling, sidestepping the 'cognitive overload' of computing a marginalized output distribution over multiple latent plans simultaneously.

## Results

On the Gemma v1 (2B) model, multi-token teacherless training achieves nearly a 5× improvement in algorithmic creativity (Eq. 1) over next-token prediction on the two discovery tasks (Sibling Discovery, Triangle Discovery), while also significantly reducing memorization. On the smaller GPT-2 (86M) model, the SEDD (90M) diffusion model outperforms NTP on 3 of 4 tasks (Circle Construction, Line Construction, Triangle Discovery), with only a mild loss on Sibling Discovery. Seed-conditioning (random input prefix) achieves algorithmic creativity comparable to temperature sampling even under deterministic greedy decoding, and consistently outperforms null-prefix baselines across all tasks and model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training/inference paradigms: standard next-token prediction (NTP), multi-token teacherless training (Bachmann & Nagarajan 2024; Monea et al. 2023), and discrete diffusion (SEDD, Lou et al. 2023). Multi-token teacherless training decisively wins over NTP on the larger Gemma v1 (2B) model, achieving ~5× higher algorithmic creativity on discovery tasks and substantially lower memorization; however, for the smaller GPT-2 (86M) model, teacherless training gains are absent (consistent with Gloeckle et al. 2024 finding that multi-token objectives are hard to optimize at small scale). The SEDD (90M) diffusion model beats NTP (GPT-2 86M) on Circle Construction, Line Construction, and Triangle Discovery, but is mildly worse on Sibling Discovery. Orthogonally, seed-conditioning (random input prefix) matches or exceeds temperature sampling in algorithmic creativity for both model sizes, and uniquely enables diverse generation under greedy decoding (temperature=0), a setting where standard NTP produces mode-collapsed output. The paper also notes that reversing the token order in Sibling Discovery (parent-first) partially closes the NTP gap, but the reversed NTP model still falls well short of the multi-token teacherless model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the argument of Bachmann & Nagarajan (2024) on the pitfalls of next-token prediction from a correctness gap (path-star task) to a diversity/creativity gap, and from many-token-lookahead to simpler 2-token-lookahead tasks where no token reordering rescues NTP. It draws on Boden's (2003) creativity taxonomy to design minimal algorithmic tasks isolating combinational and exploratory creativity, building on prior open-ended algorithmic task studies (Khona et al., 2024; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023b) but introducing a new 'algorithmic creativity' metric combining coherence, originality, and uniqueness. The multi-token alternatives (teacherless training, discrete diffusion) and the seed-conditioning technique connect to noisy-network exploration in RL (Plappert et al., 2017; Fortunato et al., 2017) and prompt-variation diversity work (Li et al., 2023; Naik et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, minimal test-bed for quantifying otherwise-elusive creative properties (originality, diversity) in language models, and offers a practical technique (seed-conditioning) that may serve as a diversity knob distinct from temperature sampling. However, the tasks are extreme caricaturizations capturing only computational aspects of creativity, the models studied are small (86M–2B), and the authors do not fully understand why seed-conditioning works or whether it generalizes to realistic data. Open questions include whether reasoning-enhancing methods (RL, CoT, test-time scaling) can address the identified NTP limitations, and whether the multi-token advantage persists at scale or with larger training sets.
