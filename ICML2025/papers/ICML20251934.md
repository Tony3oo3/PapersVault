## Problem

Most canopy height mapping studies predict tree height for a single year, despite temporal dynamics being essential for identifying carbon sources and sinks and assessing forest responses to drought, disease, or deforestation. No prior approach had combined 10 m spatial resolution with multi-year temporal coverage at continental scale, leaving a critical gap for large-scale forest monitoring and biomass estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251934/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatio-temporal volume per 100 km × 100 km UTM tile: 12 monthly Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance images (one best cloud-free image per month, all bands except B10, normalized to [0,1]) concatenated with a single Sentinel-1 SAR median composite (VV/VH, ascending/descending = 4 channels) repeated across all 12 time slices. The output is a single-channel 10 m resolution canopy height map per year (2019–2022), supervised by sparse GEDI rh_98 LiDAR measurements (~4% spatial coverage) constrained to [0, 100 m].

## Method

The core architecture is a 3D U-Net adapted from volumetric segmentation, where 3D convolutions operate over the 12-month Sentinel-2 stack plus the repeated Sentinel-1 composite, capturing both spatial and temporal dependencies; the encoder progressively collapses the temporal dimension from 12 slices to 1 at the bottleneck via skip connections with matching kernel sizes. Training uses a modified Huber loss with a per-track shift mechanism (up to 10 m) to absorb GEDI geolocation errors, computed only on labeled pixels given the extreme label sparsity (~0.15% per patch). The key design choice is feeding the full 12-month time series rather than a median composite, which preserves seasonal phenology (leaf-on/off transitions) and exploits Sentinel-2's ~4 m geolocation offset across months to recover sub-pixel structural detail. Multi-year training (2019–2022, 800,000 patches of 2.56 km × 2.56 km) with a linear warmup scheduler over 400,000 iterations, followed by a quadratic smoothing spline (parameter 5) for temporal consistency, yields the first 10 m resolution temporal canopy height map of Europe.

## Results

The proposed 3D-STACK-MULTIYEAR-L model achieves a MAE of 4.76 m, MSE of 74.28 m², and R² of 0.591 on 2020 GEDI labels (>7 m) across 1,500 validation points in Europe, outperforming all five compared canopy height products. This represents a 13% MAE improvement over the next-best method (Pauls et al., 5.46 m) and a 61% improvement over the only other multi-year European product (Turubanova et al., 12.39 m). The model also shows the strongest performance for tall trees (>20 m), achieving a mean error of approximately −5 m in the 35–40 m bin, where competing methods saturate or degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every metric (MAE, MSE, R²). Against the closest competitor, Pauls et al. (2024) using the same Sentinel-1/2 data at 10 m, the improvement is 0.70 m in MAE (13%) and 0.055 in R², attributed to the use of a full 12-month time series and 3D convolutions rather than a single composite. Lang et al. (2023), also Sentinel-2-based at 10 m, is beaten by 0.98 m in MAE. The higher-resolution products from Planet (Liu et al., 3 m; MAE 8.17 m) and Maxar (Tolan et al., 1 m; MAE 11.25 m) are substantially outperformed despite their finer spatial resolution, which the authors attribute to the availability of near-infrared and shortwave-infrared bands in Sentinel-2. Turubanova et al. (2023), the only other multi-year European canopy height product (Landsat, 30 m), is surpassed by a margin of 7.63 m in MAE (61% improvement), with the gap consistent across 2019–2021. Notably, the advantage is largest for tall trees (>20 m), where Tolan et al. and Turubanova et al. saturate below 25–28 m while the proposed model maintains accuracy up to 40–45 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 3D U-Net architecture (originally for volumetric medical image segmentation) to single-channel canopy height regression, building directly on Pauls et al. (2024) for the modified Huber loss and GEDI geolocation shift mechanism, and on Wolters et al. (2023) for the insight that multiple Sentinel-2 acquisitions exploit both temporal variation and sub-pixel geolocation offsets. The key departure from prior canopy height work is the deliberate use of a full 12-month Sentinel-2 time series processed by 3D convolutions rather than the standard median composite, combined with multi-year training to produce temporally consistent predictions. This positions the paper as the first to combine 10 m resolution, continental scale, and multi-year temporal coverage in a single deep learning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resulting 10 m temporal canopy height map of Europe (2019–2022) is the first product of its kind at this resolution, enabling direct observation of deforestation, growth, and stress responses at a spatial scale relevant to forest management and carbon accounting. The substantially improved tall-tree accuracy (critical given the power-law height–biomass relationship) addresses a known failure mode of prior maps that saturate around 25–35 m. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Europe, how it performs over longer time horizons where GEDI coverage becomes even sparser, and whether the 3D U-Net can be replaced by more parameter-efficient temporal architectures without losing the geolocation-offset benefit.
