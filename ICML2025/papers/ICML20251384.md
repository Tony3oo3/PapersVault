## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts LLMs suffer from large parameter counts and memory overhead that hinder deployment, yet existing compression methods (expert pruning, layer dropping) cause severe performance degradation under high compression ratios or require costly retraining. Directly applying SVD-based compression to MoE models leads to catastrophic collapse (perplexity exceeding 1000 on Mixtral-8×7B), because uniform decomposition ignores layer-wise sensitivity differences and fails to exploit redundancy among expert weight matrices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MoE model's expert weight matrices and a small calibration set (256 random samples from WikiText-2 by default) to collect activation statistics and router sampling frequencies. It outputs a compressed MoE model where each decomposed expert layer is replaced by a shared V-matrix (one per layer, selected by highest routing frequency) plus a small set of top-k U-matrices (typically k=2), reducing total parameters from N×m×n to approximately 2mr+rn per layer. The compressed model produces standard text tokens at inference with 1.2×–1.5× throughput gains on a single H800 GPU.

## Method

MoE-SVD applies activation-weighted SVD (scaling each expert weight matrix W by a Cholesky factor S of the activation Gram matrix before decomposition) to factorize expert FFN layers into U, Σ, V components. A selective decomposition strategy then uses a per-layer sensitivity metric S_L = Σ f_i·p_i·a_i—combining expert sampling frequency, principal rank of singular values, and activation outlier proportion—to automatically preserve sensitive layers (typically first and last) while decomposing less critical middle layers. To exploit inter-expert redundancy, the method shares a single V-matrix (from the most frequently routed expert) across all experts in a layer and trims U-matrices by retaining only the top-k (k=2) most frequently sampled ones, yielding the final expert function E_i(x) = (U_{i,1}Σ_{i,1} + U_{i,2}Σ_{i,2})V_s^T x. The entire pipeline is training-free, hardware-independent, and requires only a one-time calibration pass, achieving up to 60% parameter reduction with 1.5× inference speedup and minimal perplexity increase.

## Results

On Mixtral-8x7B at 20% compression, MoE-SVD achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 4.86 and 0.58 average reasoning accuracy, compared to the original model's 3.98 and 0.63. At 60% compression, MoE-SVD maintains a perplexity of 13.52 and 0.37 average accuracy, while SVD-LLM and ASVD collapse to perplexities exceeding 10,000. On Phi-3.5-MoE at 60% compression, MoE-SVD achieves 7.46 perplexity and 0.49 average accuracy versus 107.71 and 0.35 for ASVD. Inference speedup reaches 1.5x at 60% compression on a single H800 GPU, with model size reduced to ~40% of the original.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-SVD consistently outperforms all SVD-based baselines (ASVD, SVD-LLM) across every compression ratio, with the gap widening dramatically at higher compression: at 60% on Mixtral-8x7B, MoE-SVD achieves 13.52 WikiText-2 PPL versus 12,525 (ASVD) and 10,181 (SVD-LLM), and 0.37 average accuracy versus 0.30 for both. Against pruning-based methods, MoE-SVD beats Wanda (4.86 vs 4.72 PPL) and SparseGPT (4.86 vs 4.61) at 20% compression while achieving higher average accuracy (0.58 vs 0.57/0.56) and greater speedup (1.2× vs 1.04×/1.06×). LoSparse and MC-SMoE suffer catastrophic collapse at 20% compression (PPL > 900 and > 1300 respectively), making them non-competitive. MoE-Compression (He et al., 2024) is the closest competitor at 20% (6.12 PPL, 0.54 avg acc) but MoE-SVD still leads on both metrics. For quantization, MoE-SVD combined with 4-bit GPTQ matches or exceeds standalone 3-bit GPTQ in both memory (35.6 GB vs 33.4 GB) and perplexity (6.93 vs 6.22 on Mixtral-8x7B), demonstrating orthogonality with quantization approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SVD-based LLM compression (ASVD, SVD-LLM, FWSVD) to the MoE setting, where it identifies that vanilla SVD causes catastrophic performance collapse (perplexity >1000 on Mixtral-8×7B). It departs from MoE-specific pruning methods (NAEE, MoE-Compression, MoE-I2) that drop entire experts or require fine-tuning, and from expert-merging approaches (MC-SMoE) that operate on small-scale models. The key conceptual departure is the observation that standard layer-importance metrics (e.g., OWL) fail to transfer from dense to MoE architectures, motivating a new sensitivity metric combining singular value structure, activation outliers, and routing frequency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen2.5-Max) become the dominant scaling paradigm, training-free compression that preserves expert diversity while reducing memory and accelerating inference is practically critical. The 60% compression with 1.5× speedup and minimal performance loss is a strong practical result, and the method is orthogonal to quantization and parallelism strategies. Open questions include whether the V-matrix sharing assumption (high CKA similarity across experts) holds for more diverse or fine-grained expert designs, how the approach interacts with tensor parallelism at scale, and whether the calibration-data sensitivity metric generalizes to MoE models with significantly different routing dynamics.
