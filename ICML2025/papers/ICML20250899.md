## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for language models rely on fixed, manually tuned steering strengths determined through costly, model-specific hyperparameter sweeps, leading to understeering (insufficient correction) or oversteering (unnecessary degradation). No prior approach provides a principled, task-specific criterion for when to intervene and how strongly, nor does it guarantee non-degrading performance. MERA addresses this gap by formulating error-mitigation steering as a constrained optimization problem with a built-in abstention mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts and the corresponding LM residual stream activations extracted at a chosen token position (either the last prompt token or the 'exact' first matching label token) across all layers. It produces, for each token and layer, a binary steer-or-abstain decision and, when steering, a per-instance steering strength λ* that scales the probe weight vector w. The calibration step operates on a held-out set of 210–250 prompt-label pairs to select the threshold α from a discrete grid of 10 candidates, ensuring the final policy is either provably beneficial or fully abstained.

## Method

MERA trains L linear regression probes (one per layer) to predict the continuous model error E(a) from residual stream activations, rather than using contrastive pairs or binary classifiers. Steering is then formulated as min_v ||v||² subject to p̂(h+v) ≤ α, which for linear probes admits the closed-form solution λ* = max(0, (α − wᵀh)/||w||²), yielding selective intervention only when predicted error exceeds α and strength proportional to the residual error. A calibration step selects α* from K candidates on a held-out set using a Hoeffding-based confidence bound with a Bonferroni union bound, guaranteeing with probability ≥ 1−δ that the chosen threshold yields genuine accuracy improvement beyond a user-specified ε; if no candidate passes this test, MERA abstains entirely (λ*=0). This framework is direction-agnostic: the same optimization and calibration pipeline can be applied on top of contrastive means or logistic probe weights, turning unreliable fixed-strength baselines into safe, calibrated interventions.

## Results

MERA is evaluated using the Steering Performance Impact (SPI) metric, bounded in [-1, 1], where positive values indicate accuracy improvement and negative values indicate degradation. The best MERA results reach SPI of +0.52 (LLAMA-3.1-1B, YES/NO) and +0.44 (LLAMA-3.1-1B, SMS SPAM) in the 'last' evaluation mode. MERA consistently outperforms all baselines across 6 model families (LLAMA, GEMMA, QWEN; base and instruction-tuned) and 5 datasets, while abstaining (SPI = 0) when no statistically safe improvement exists. Base models benefit more than instruction-tuned variants, and MMLU subsets (4-class) remain the hardest to steer, with gains typically below +0.25 SPI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prompt-based baseline (BASE-x, 'Think before you answer'), MERA is consistently superior: BASE-x is highly unreliable, swinging from −0.79 SPI (SMS SPAM, LLAMA-IT) to +0.79 (SMS SPAM, LLAMA base), while MERA never degrades below 0. Against contrastive steering (BASE-μ₁₀₀), MERA-μ₁₀₀ transforms unreliable or negative results into consistent gains—for example, turning −0.05 SPI into +0.52 on YES/NO (LLAMA-3.1-1B) and −0.09 into +0.21 on MMLU-HS (QWEN-2.5-3B). Against probe-based steering (BASE-p̂), which is too weak to be useful (SPI ≈ 0 on most tasks), MERA makes the same probe direction effective by optimising the steering strength, achieving +0.64 SPI on SENTIMENT and +0.53 on YES/NO. Against logistic probe steering (BASE-p̂_LOG), which can catastrophically fail (−0.85 SPI on SENTIMENT, QWEN-2.5-3B), MERA-p̂_LOG prevents degradation entirely (SPI = 0), demonstrating its safety guarantee. The overarching finding is that MERA functions as a general-purpose wrapper: it improves every existing steering direction it is applied to, while guaranteeing non-degrading performance through its calibration-and-abstention mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probe-based activation steering framework (von Rütte et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2024) by replacing the use of probe weights as a fixed steering direction with a closed-form, error-proportional steering strength derived from a constrained optimization problem. It departs from the dominant practice of contrastive steering (Rimsky et al., 2024; Tan et al., 2024) and its reliance on expensive, model-specific hyperparameter sweeps for steering strength, instead introducing a calibration step with Hoeffding-based statistical guarantees that either certifies improvement or triggers abstention. The 'exact' token position strategy from Orgad et al. (2024) is adopted as the activation extraction point, and the framework is shown to be composable with existing steering directions (contrastive means, logistic probe weights), positioning MERA as a general-purpose calibration layer rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable non-degradation guarantee (steer only when improvement is statistically certified, otherwise abstain) addresses a practical safety concern that prior steering methods handle only heuristically, making MERA attractive for deployment settings where silent degradation is unacceptable. Key limitations include the inherently linear probe assumption (struggling with MMLU subsets where semantic class overlap is high), evaluation restricted to small models (1B–3B) and MCQA tasks, and the open question of whether task-specific steering impairs general generation or reasoning capabilities. Future directions include non-linear error estimators, extension to alignment objectives (toxicity, refusal), and investigating whether the calibration metric (accuracy vs. F1) affects steering balance under class imbalance.
