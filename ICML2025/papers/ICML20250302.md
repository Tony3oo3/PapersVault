## Problem

Sparse autoencoders decompose language model representations into interpretable latents, but inserting SAE reconstructions into the model causes a significant cross-entropy loss increase relative to the original model. Prior work has focused on improving SAE architectures (TopK, Gated, JumpReLU, e2e) to close this gap, but whether the language model itself can be optimized around a pre-trained SAE remained unexplored. This paper asks whether low-rank adaptation of the model can yield additional Pareto improvements in the sparsity-versus-loss frontier without retraining the SAE.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained language model (Gemma-2-2B/7B/27B, Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.1-8B) and a frozen SAE (TopK or JumpReLU) trained on cached activations, with the SAE inserted at a specific layer during the forward pass. It produces low-rank adapter weights (rank-r matrices A and B) added to MLP and attention sublayers, which modify the model's next-token probability distribution. For JumpReLU SAEs, only layers after the SAE are adapted to preserve average sparsity; for TopK SAEs, all layers can be adapted while maintaining the TopK constraint during training.

## Method

The core technique freezes both the SAE and the base model, then adds LoRA adapters of rank r to each MLP and attention sublayer, training only the adapter parameters to minimize KL divergence between the SAE-enhanced model's logits and the original model's logits. This avoids the expensive backward passes through the full language model required by e2e SAEs, since gradients flow only through the small adapter matrices, yielding 2–20× wall-clock speedups and 130× fewer model backward passes. The approach closes 30–55% of the cross-entropy loss gap with just 15M tokens of training, with larger gains at lower sparsities and on larger models. Notably, training adapters on just the single layer immediately after the SAE recovers ~88% of the full multi-layer benefit, suggesting the loss improvement mechanism is relatively local. The method also extends to multiple SAEs inserted simultaneously, reducing compound cross-entropy loss from ~7.8 nats (7 SAEs) to ~2.8 nats and enabling more faithful circuit analysis.

## Results

The method reduces the cross-entropy loss gap (L_SAE − L_BASE) by 30%–55% across Gemma Scope SAEs, with final gaps between 0.01 and 0.17 nats. On Gemma-2-2B, TopK+LoRA reaches a CE loss of 2.57 in 12 hours versus 243 hours for e2e SAEs (20× speedup), and on Llama-3.2-1B it reaches 2.70 in 70 hours versus 156 hours for e2e (2.2× speedup). For multiple SAEs on Llama-3.1-8B, compound CE loss drops from 7.83 to 2.78 nats (7 SAEs) and from 3.55 to 2.45 nats (3 SAEs). On SAEBench, the LoRA-adapted model improves SCR(max) from 0.448 to 0.526, TPP(max) from 0.372 to 0.412, and Sparse Probing (Top-1) from 0.732 to 0.760.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against e2e SAEs (Braun et al., 2024), TopK+LoRA achieves the same CE loss 3×–20× faster on Gemma-2-2B and 2×–10× faster on Llama-3.2-1B, while requiring 130× fewer model backward passes on Gemma-2-2B and 40× fewer on Llama-3.2-1B. However, e2e SAEs reach a slightly lower final CE loss on Llama-3.2-1B (2.67 vs. ~2.70), though not on Gemma-2-2B where TopK+LoRA wins. Against vanilla TopK SAEs (Gao et al., 2024), TopK+LoRA dominates the CE-loss-vs-training-time frontier on both models, reaching lower loss at every checkpoint. On SAEBench, the LoRA-adapted model outperforms the base TopK model on SCR, TPP, and Sparse Probing, while showing negligible differences on AutoInterp (0.830 vs. 0.832) and Absorption (0.210 vs. 0.205). On general language capabilities (MMLU, HellaSwag), the SAE+LoRA configuration matches or exceeds the original model without SAE across all Gemma sizes (2B, 9B, 27B), confirming no capability degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), a standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique, for a problem in mechanistic interpretability: reducing the cross-entropy loss gap when a frozen SAE is inserted into a language model. It departs from the dominant line of SAE architecture improvements (TopK, Gated, JumpReLU, e2e SAEs) that optimize the SAE itself, and from concurrent SAE+model training approaches (Lai & Heimersheim, 2024; Lai & Huang, 2024) that require training from scratch. The key conceptual shift is treating the model as the object of optimization rather than the SAE, freezing both and using low-rank adapters to close the KL divergence gap — a direction the authors identify as previously unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×–20× wall-clock speedup over e2e SAEs and the 130× reduction in backward passes make SAE-faithful model adaptation practical for existing large models (up to 27B), removing a major computational bottleneck in the MI pipeline. The multi-SAE result (7 SAEs dropping from 7.83 to 2.78 nats) opens a path toward more faithful circuit analysis that was previously infeasible. Open questions include whether the technique helps discover more faithful circuits, whether e2e SAEs have fully converged in the comparisons, and whether longer LoRA training (only 15M tokens used) would yield further gains. The finding that a single-layer adapter captures 88% of the full improvement suggests the loss-reduction mechanism may be simpler than expected, but this is demonstrated on one model.
