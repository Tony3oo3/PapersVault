## Problem

Existing algorithmic recourse methods only guarantee that a suggested action flips a model's prediction (h-validity) but cannot ensure the user's real-world outcome actually improves (h*-validity), since the true oracle h* is unknown. This gap is critical in high-stakes settings like loan approval, where a prediction change without genuine improvement degrades decision-making reliability. The paper tackles this by formulating recourse as a long-term online learning problem where the agent sequentially suggests actions and observes delayed feedback on whether they achieved improvement.

## Input / Output

In each round, the agent receives an instance x with h(x)=0 and a pre-constructed set of candidate actions A_t (perturbation vectors that shift x to one of K fixed recourse instances with h=1), along with past delayed rewards indicating whether previously suggested actions achieved h*-validity. The agent outputs a single recourse action a_t from A_t, balancing h*-validity probability against execution cost. The reward is binary (Bernoulli) and arrives after a stochastic delay D_t drawn from an unknown distribution, with unobserved rewards beyond a sliding window m censored to zero.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms for the ARLIM problem. The first (LinUCB) reduces the task to a contextual linear bandit under stochastic delayed feedback by mapping each candidate action to a feature vector φ_t(a) = E(a|x_t)·e_{π_t(l)}, where E is the execution probability (exponential in cost) and e is a one-hot indicator over recourse instances; it then applies OTFLinUCB with a sliding window to handle censored rewards, yielding a regret bound showing the mean expected reward converges to its upper bound as T grows. The second (BwOUCB) treats the task as contextual Bayesian optimization under stochastic delayed feedback, replacing the Gaussian process surrogate with a BwO forest (extremely randomized trees with bagging and oversampling) for scalability, and selects actions via a UCB score combining the forest's mean and variance estimates. The key distinction is that LinUCB requires known cost and execution probability but provides theoretical guarantees, while BwOUCB learns both E and I from data and is more robust to cost uncertainty at the cost of per-round retraining and no formal regret bound.

## Results

Across all three datasets (Credit, Diabetes, COMPAS), both proposed methods (LinUCB and BwOUCB) stably outperform the ProtoAR and TAP baselines in average improvement (h*-validity) under both noiseless and noisy cost evaluation settings. LinUCB achieves comparable average cost to the baselines while delivering higher improvement, and the mean expected reward (MER) of both proposed methods increases monotonically over 1000 rounds, consistent with the theoretical guarantee in Proposition 4.2. Under noisy cost evaluation (ξ=0.25), BwOUCB surpasses LinUCB in final-round MER on all datasets, and its performance is independent of the noise level since it does not require the executing probability E. In terms of running time, LinUCB is comparable to the baselines (~0.001s/round), BwOUCB is ~18x slower (~0.018s/round), and the GP-based CGPUCB is ~250x slower than BwOUCB (~4.5–5.0s/round).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProtoAR (Van Looveren & Klaise, 2021), both LinUCB and BwOUCB achieve consistently higher average improvement across all three datasets while LinUCB maintains comparable average cost, confirming that the online learning framework can identify more genuinely improvement-oriented actions than the static prototype-based method. Against TAP (Friedbaum et al., 2024), which uses a trained verifier to estimate trustworthiness, both proposed methods again achieve higher improvement; the paper notes that the quality of TAP's verifier hinders its accuracy, whereas the proposed methods learn directly from delayed feedback. Against CGPUCB (Verma et al., 2022), the GP-based CBO algorithm, BwOUCB achieves comparable or slightly better improvement and MER while being approximately 250x faster per round (0.018s vs. ~4.5–5.0s), validating the choice of the BwO forest as a scalable surrogate. Under noisy cost evaluation (ξ=0.25), BwOUCB outperforms LinUCB in final-round MER on all datasets, and as the noise level ξ increases further, LinUCB's advantage erodes while BwOUCB remains stable, making BwOUCB the preferred method when the cost function or user execution probability is uncertain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic recourse (AR) literature by shifting the objective from h-validity (changing a model's prediction) to h*-validity (improving the real-world outcome), a requirement first articulated by König et al. (2023) but previously addressed only via causal graphs or trained verifiers. The key methodological departure is formulating recourse as an online learning problem with stochastic delayed feedback, then reducing it to contextual linear bandit (CLB) and contextual Bayesian optimization (CBO) — repurposing the OTFLinUCB algorithm (Vernade et al., 2020a) and GP-UCB-SDF (Verma et al., 2022) for a new application domain. For the CBO variant, the authors replace the Gaussian process surrogate with a BwO forest (Kim & Choi, 2022) to address scalability, and construct candidate actions via prototype-based methods (Van Looveren & Klaise, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to ensure improvement-oriented recourse without requiring knowledge of the true oracle or causal structure, relying instead on sequentially observed delayed feedback — a practical assumption in settings like loan repayment monitoring. A significant open question is the long-delay regime: in applications where feedback arrives after years, the agent may lack sufficient data to learn effectively, and the authors acknowledge that exploiting intermediate observations would be non-trivial. Additional limitations include the assumption of a fixed classifier, complete action execution by users, and the prototype-based restriction on candidate actions for the CLB approach. The finding that the heuristic BwOUCB (without theoretical guarantees) matches or exceeds the theoretically-guaranteed LinUCB under noisy cost evaluation suggests that practical robustness may matter more than formal guarantees in deployment.
