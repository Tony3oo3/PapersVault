## Problem

LLMs are increasingly applied to mental health support, yet their deployment lacks structured ethical guidelines and evaluation frameworks that go beyond narrow technical accuracy. Existing studies predominantly rely on simulated data, assess only diagnostic correctness, and ignore critical dimensions like empathy, cultural sensitivity, and actionability, leaving a gap between LLM capabilities and safe, equitable real-world integration in a high-stakes clinical domain.

## Input / Output

The HAAS-e evaluation framework consumes text-based mental health conversations augmented with user interaction history, cultural context metadata, demographic information, and expert counselor reference responses. It produces four quantitative alignment scores—CES (semantic and emotional alignment with user concerns and expert evaluations), CSI (cultural appropriateness alignment), PAS (personalization alignment with interaction history), and ASA (conditional probability of user action given the LLM response)—alongside qualitative bias audit results and trustworthiness assessments. Alignment is computed via similarity metrics or reward scores in embedding space, with expert human ratings required for at least sampled responses to ensure quality.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary frameworks: SAFE-i (implementation) and HAAS-e (evaluation). SAFE-i structures deployment across three pillars—ethical data foundations (real-world data harvesting, demographic mosaic construction, regulatory compliance, expert-guided annotation), model engineering (open-source prioritization, domain adaptive fine-tuning/instruction-tuning, empathy-oriented prompt design, Tree-of-Thoughts and Chain-of-Thought reasoning with uncertainty-aware architectures for crisis escalation), and real-world integration (human-AI task specialization, personalized care orchestration, pilot safety-net deployment with toxicity detection, and structured feedback loops). HAAS-e operationalizes four evaluation criteria (trustworthiness, bias/ethics, empathy, helpfulness) into the four quantitative metrics above, validated through randomized controlled trials with real patient data, multi-method quantitative-qualitative assessment, adversarial red teaming by domain experts, and A/B testing across model architectures and prompting strategies. The key distinction from prior work is the unified, end-to-end coupling of implementation safeguards with multidimensional, human-centered evaluation metrics that translate therapeutic values into testable, reproducible scores.

## Results

This is a position paper that proposes the SAFE-i implementation guidelines and the HAAS-e evaluation framework rather than reporting empirical experimental results. The only quantitative illustration appears in Appendix A (a case study), where the Actionability and Safety Assessment (ASA) metric is demonstrated with a hypothetical pilot: 40 out of 50 users followed the LLM's hotline recommendation, yielding ASA ≈ 0.80. The other HAAS-e metrics (CES, CSI, PAS) are described qualitatively in the same case study without assigned numerical scores. No benchmark comparisons, ablations, or head-to-head model evaluations are presented.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not conduct direct empirical comparisons against baseline methods or competing frameworks. It references prior work (e.g., Fraser et al. 2023 on ChatGPT triage accuracy, Zack et al. 2024 on GPT-4 racial/gender biases, CRAFT-MD by Johri et al. 2025 for clinical LLM evaluation) to motivate the need for the proposed HAAS-e metrics, but does not benchmark SAFE-i or HAAS-e against these existing approaches. The paper's contribution is normative and structural—defining evaluation dimensions (trustworthiness, fairness, empathy, helpfulness) and four quantitative metrics (CES, CSI, PAS, ASA)—rather than demonstrating empirical superiority over prior baselines. Readers seeking quantitative validation of the proposed metrics would need to await follow-up empirical work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and extends the fragmented landscape of LLM applications in mental health (Woebot, SouLLMate, Serena, HAILEY) and prior evaluation efforts (CRAFT-MD, EPITOME, AlignScore) by proposing two unified frameworks—SAFE-i for implementation and HAAS-e for evaluation—that no single prior work has attempted. Rather than building on a specific method, it repurposes existing concepts (chain-of-thought reasoning, retrieval-augmented generation, red-teaming, RCTs) into a structured, domain-specific pathway for mental health LLMs, explicitly departing from the accuracy-centric evaluation paradigm that dominates prior clinical LLM studies. The paper positions itself against the 'assistive tool' framing of prior work, arguing for a 'co-creator' paradigm grounded in human-AI complementarity as demonstrated by HAILEY (Sharma et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical gap: mental health remains one of the least-deployed AI domains (48% adoption vs. 63–64% in imaging/EHR), and the absence of standardized, human-centered evaluation and implementation frameworks is a primary barrier. The four HAAS-e metrics (CES, CSI, PAS, ASA) are a concrete step toward moving beyond accuracy-only evaluation, but as a position paper they remain unvalidated at scale—no empirical study tests whether these metrics are reliably computable, discriminative, or stable across model architectures. Open questions include whether the frameworks can be operationalized consistently across diverse clinical settings, how they interact with evolving regulatory landscapes (HIPAA, GDPR, emerging AI-specific regulations), and whether the 'co-creator' framing meaningfully changes deployment practice versus remaining a rhetorical reframe.
