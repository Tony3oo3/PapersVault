## Problem

Computing explicit likelihoods P(X) for images remains an open challenge: diffusion models and GANs only provide score functions or implicit density estimates, not direct probability values. While CLIP has become the dominant dual text-image embedding, its latent space had never been analyzed from a probabilistic perspective or leveraged as a likelihood estimator. This paper is the first to compute direct likelihood functions for images and text prompts under the CLIP-learned distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250150/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images or free-form text captions, encodes them via CLIP ViT-L/14 into 768-dimensional embeddings, and applies a single pre-computed 768×768 whitening matrix (derived once from 5,000 MS-COCO validation samples) to produce a scalar log-likelihood score equal to the negative squared Euclidean norm in the whitened space. For the full-circle SLERP extension, two source embeddings and an angular parameter ω produce interpolated or extrapolated images at arbitrary positions along a circular path. The whitening matrix is modality-specific (separate matrices for images and text) and requires no gradient-based training at inference time.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free PCA whitening transform applied to CLIP's latent space: the covariance matrix Σ of a representative embedding set is eigendecomposed (Σ = VΛVᵀ), and the whitening matrix W = Λ^{-1/2}Vᵀ is applied to centered embeddings, yielding an isotropic space with identity covariance. The key insight is that in this whitened space, the 768 features are validated (via Anderson-Darling and D'Agostino-Pearson tests, with >98% of image features passing) to approximate i.i.d. standard normal variables, so the log-likelihood reduces to ℓ(x) = −½(d·log(2π) + ‖x‖²), making the squared norm a direct likelihood proxy. The transform is invertible (x = W⁻¹y + μ), preserving all existing CLIP-based applications, and the whitening matrix is computed once a priori with negligible memory overhead. The paper further extends SLERP to full-circle interpolation (t beyond [0,1]) in the whitened space, where embeddings remain within the natural distribution across all angles, unlike the raw CLIP space which produces structured noise outside the source-destination arc.

## Results

W-CLIP achieves an AUC of 0.999 for separating image captions from general text (Type) and 0.94 for separating original captions from noun-removed captions (Nouns), outperforming all five language/vision-language model baselines. Normality validation shows >98% of image features and >90% of text features pass both Anderson-Darling and D'Agostino-Pearson tests, confirming the i.i.d. standard normal approximation. Empirical norm statistics (mean 27.43, std 3.94 for images) closely match theoretical chi-distribution predictions (mean 27.7, std 3.96) with <1% relative deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W-CLIP outperforms all five baselines on both separation metrics. On Type separation (captions vs. general text), W-CLIP achieves 0.999 AUC versus 0.77–0.80 for LLMs (GPT-2, OPT, NEO) and 0.92–0.97 for VLMs (BLIP, GIT), with the largest gap over LLMs (~0.2 AUC). On Nouns separation (original vs. noun-removed captions), W-CLIP reaches 0.94 AUC, substantially exceeding the best VLM (GIT at 0.69) and all LLMs (0.43–0.58), indicating that CLIP's learned distribution captures semantic structure that autoregressive language models miss. The VLMs (BLIP, GIT) show intermediate behavior, partially separating text types due to their caption-based training, but still fall well short of W-CLIP's discrimination. W-CLIP's advantage is most pronounced for grammatical errors, where LLMs are nearly at chance (AUC ≈ 0.43–0.58) while W-CLIP achieves near-perfect separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the standard PCA whitening transform for a new application: converting CLIP's anisotropic latent space (known for its narrow cone geometry and modality gap) into an approximately isotropic, standard-normal space where the squared Euclidean norm directly yields a log-likelihood score. It builds on CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as the embedding backbone and extends the SLERP interpolation of Ramesh et al. (2022) to full-circle SLERP in the whitened space. Conceptually, it departs from prior image likelihood methods (diffusion models, GANs, EBMs) that only provide score functions or implicit likelihoods, and from prior CLIP analyses that studied geometric properties without a probabilistic interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, single-matrix approach to image likelihood scoring is practically attractive: it requires no additional training, is computationally trivial at inference, and provides semantic-level (not patch-level) probability estimates. However, the experiments are explicitly preliminary, the normality approximation holds for ~98% of image features but only ~90% of text features, and the 'likelihood' is really a norm-based ranking rather than a calibrated probability. Open questions include whether this generalizes to other contrastive embedding spaces, whether the scores can be calibrated to true probabilities, and whether the domain-shift sensitivity (a limitation) can be mitigated for broader deployment.
