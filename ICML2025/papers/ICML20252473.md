## Problem

Existing weakly supervised video anomaly detection methods rely on a frame-wise classification paradigm followed by manual post-processing to aggregate predictions into temporal segments, which produces fragmented and incomplete anomaly intervals. The paper addresses this by hypothesizing that anomaly scores should exhibit local consistency and explicitly enforcing this prior during training to learn complete event boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed RGB videos (sampled into T snippets) with only video-level labels (binary anomaly flag plus one of C anomaly categories), extracting frame features via pre-trained encoders (I3D, C3D, or CLIP ViT-B/16). At inference, coarse-grained output is a per-frame anomaly confidence (averaging the category-agnostic and category-aware scores), while fine-grained output is a set of anomaly instances with start/end timestamps, category, and confidence, obtained via two-step thresholding (r_cls=0.1, r_ano=0.2) followed by NMS at IoU 0.5.

## Method

LEC-VAD employs a dual-structured framework with a category-agnostic binary branch and a category-aware multi-class branch. Visual features are enhanced by a local transformer with constrained attention windows followed by a GCN for global temporal modeling, while text features from CLIP are enriched via cross-attention with video features and a momentum-updated memory bank of category prototypes to compensate for the brevity of anomaly category descriptions. The key novelty is a Gaussian Mixture Prior (GMP) mechanism: for each temporal position, a shared FC layer predicts per-category Gaussian kernels (mean and variance) whose weighted mixture (weighted by category probabilities) produces a locally smooth constraint score that regularizes the binary anomaly scores via an L2 loss. An additional L1 loss enforces consistency between the category-agnostic and category-aware scores, and the total objective combines BCE, cross-entropy, GMM, and regularization terms.

## Results

On coarse-grained WS-VAD, LEC-VAD achieves 88.47 AP (I3D) and 86.56 AP (CLIP) on XD-Violence, and 89.97 AUC (CLIP) on UCF-Crime, all setting new state-of-the-art. On fine-grained WS-VAD, the gains are most dramatic: LEC-VAD reaches 35.14 AVG mAP on XD-Violence (vs. 27.36 for ReFLIP, a 28.4% relative improvement) and 13.56 AVG mAP on UCF-Crime (vs. 9.62 for ReFLIP, a 41.0% relative improvement). Notably, the advantage grows with stricter IoU thresholds (e.g., 37.5% relative gain at IoU=0.5 on XD-Violence), confirming that the Gaussian mixture prior yields more complete event boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEC-VAD outperforms all 31 compared baselines across both datasets and both granularity levels. On coarse-grained XD-Violence, it surpasses the strongest I3D-based method PE-MIL (88.05 AP) by 0.42 AP and the strongest CLIP-based method ReFLIP (85.81 AP) by 0.75 AP; on UCF-Crime it beats ITC (89.04 AUC, CLIP) by 0.93 AUC. The most striking advantage appears in fine-grained localization: on XD-Violence, LEC-VAD's 35.14 AVG mAP exceeds ReFLIP (27.36) by 7.78 points and ITC (26.83) by 8.31 points, while on UCF-Crime it leads ReFLIP (9.62) by 3.94 points. The margin widens at higher IoU thresholds (e.g., +6.46 at IoU=0.5 on XD-Violence vs. +9.54 at IoU=0.1), directly validating the paper's claim that the Gaussian mixture prior produces more complete, less fragmented anomaly intervals. Among CLIP-based methods specifically, LEC-VAD consistently ranks first, outperforming STPrompt (88.08 AUC), VadCLIP (88.02 AUC), and TPWNG (87.79 AUC) on UCF-Crime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based WS-VAD line (VadCLIP, ITC, ReFLIP, TPWNG) by introducing a dual-structured framework that jointly encodes category-agnostic and category-aware anomaly semantics, departing from prior methods that typically rely on a single classification head. It builds directly on VadCLIP's local-transformer-plus-GCN temporal modeling pipeline and AutoLoc's outer-inner scoring for instance confidence, but adds two novel mechanisms: a memory bank-based prototype learning strategy to enrich concise text descriptions, and a Gaussian mixture prior-based local consistency constraint that enforces smoothness in predicted anomaly scores. The key conceptual departure from prior WS-VAD work is the explicit hypothesis that frame-level predictions should exhibit local consistency, bridging the gap between classification-based training and localization-based inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial fine-grained improvements (28–41% relative mAP gains over ReFLIP) suggest that enforcing local smoothness via learnable GMM masks is an underexploited and effective strategy for boundary precision in weakly supervised temporal localization. However, the coarse-grained gains are modest (0.4–1.7 absolute AP/AUC), and the method is evaluated on only two datasets with relatively small test sets, leaving open whether the GMM prior generalizes to longer videos, more diverse anomaly taxonomies, or open-vocabulary settings. The memory bank mechanism for text enrichment is a lightweight but potentially transferable idea for other vision-language tasks where textual prompts are inherently concise. A key limitation is that the method still relies on post-processing (thresholding + NMS) for instance extraction, and the GMM components are learned per-category, which may not scale gracefully to very large category spaces.
