## Problem

During later stages of supervised training, models memorize atypical samples, which improves ID accuracy but causes overconfident predictions on OOD data, degrading OOD detection. Existing remedies like Unleashing Mask directly forget these atypical samples via gradient ascent, sacrificing ID generalization and capping the model's OOD detection potential. The paper asks how to learn from atypical samples in a way that simultaneously preserves ID performance and restores reliable uncertainty estimation for OOD detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-200) and produces a K-way class prediction plus a scalar OOD uncertainty score (MSP, Energy, or KNN-based) used to flag samples as ID or OOD. During training, pseudo-outliers are generated from validation images via rotation, downsample-then-upsample distortion, and Gaussian noise at 10 intensity levels, serving as a proxy OOD set for teacher model selection without requiring realistic OOD data.

## Method

PSKD is a progressive self-distillation framework where the student model is jointly trained with cross-entropy on ID labels and a self-distillation loss against a teacher model selected from its own training history. The teacher is chosen by maximizing AUROC on pseudo-outliers, and is updated whenever the student surpasses it, creating a progressive refinement loop. The distillation loss combines KL divergence on temperature-scaled softmax outputs (response-level) and L2 distance on penultimate-layer features (feature-level), weighted by a factor α. A cosine-annealed weight λ gradually increases the distillation contribution over training epochs, preventing early over-reliance on an undertrained teacher. The key mechanism is that the teacher's soft predictions serve as uncertainty-embedded targets, replacing one-hot labels and teaching the model to assign higher uncertainty to atypical samples, thereby reducing overconfidence on OOD inputs without discarding any training data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Energy+PSKD achieves 91.71% AUROC (near-OOD) and 94.56% AUROC (far-OOD) with 95.14% ID accuracy, improving over vanilla Energy (87.58%/91.21%) by ~4 and ~3.4 points respectively. On the large-scale ImageNet-200 benchmark, T2FNorm+PSKD reaches 88.22% average AUROC and 39.91% average FPR95, outperforming T2FNorm alone (88.06%/40.45%) while maintaining 86.74% ID accuracy. PSKD consistently outperforms the closest competitor UM/UMAP across all three benchmarks while preserving or improving ID classification accuracy (e.g., 95.14% vs. 95.06% on CIFAR-10 with Energy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSKD (as a plugin) consistently outperforms vanilla scoring methods: Energy+PSKD improves near-OOD AUROC from 87.58% to 91.71% and far-OOD AUROC from 91.21% to 94.56% on CIFAR-10, while MSP and ODIN are substantially weaker baselines. Against the most relevant competitor UM/UMAP (which forgets atypical samples via gradient ascent), PSKD achieves comparable or slightly better OOD detection (e.g., 91.71% vs. 91.00% near-OOD AUROC with Energy on CIFAR-10) while preserving ID accuracy (95.14% vs. 95.06% for UMAP; UM drops to 92.33%). Among training-time regularization methods, T2FNorm+PSKD edges out T2FNorm+UMAP on near-OOD FPR95 (25.72% vs. 26.78%) and ID accuracy (95.06% vs. 94.69%), and OE+PSKD surpasses OE+UMAP on far-OOD AUROC (96.58% vs. 96.37%) and ID accuracy (94.75% vs. 94.63%). On ImageNet-200, PSKD's gains are more modest but consistent: Energy+PSKD reduces average FPR95 from 47.55% to 44.38% and T2FNorm+PSKD achieves the best average AUROC (88.22%) among all compared methods. PSKD also outperforms outlier-based methods (OE, MixOE, DOE) when combined, e.g., OE+PSKD achieves 96.58% far-OOD AUROC vs. OE's 96.00% on CIFAR-10, confirming its orthogonal, plugin-compatible nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSKD sits at the intersection of self-distillation (Zhang et al., 2019; Hinton et al., 2015) and OOD detection training strategies. It directly responds to Zhu et al. (2023)'s Unleashing Mask/UMAP, which showed that models lose OOD detection capability in later training stages due to memorization of atypical samples and proposed gradient-ascent-based forgetting as a remedy. PSKD departs from this 'forget' paradigm by instead learning from atypical samples through uncertainty-embedded soft targets provided by a self-selected teacher from training history, combining response-level KL divergence and feature-level L2 distillation. The progressive teacher selection mechanism, guided by pseudo-outlier-based AUROC evaluation, distinguishes it from static self-distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSKD offers a practical plugin that simultaneously improves both ID accuracy and OOD detection without the trade-off inherent in forgetting-based methods, making it broadly composable with existing OOD scoring and training-time regularization methods. A key limitation is that gains on large-scale ImageNet-200 are noticeably smaller than on CIFAR, suggesting the approach's effectiveness depends on the model's intrinsic OOD capability relative to dataset complexity. Open questions include whether the progressive selection mechanism is essential versus a single well-chosen teacher, whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities or very large transformer models, and whether the pseudo-outlier-based teacher selection can be replaced by cheaper proxies without performance loss.
