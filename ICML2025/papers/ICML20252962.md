## Problem

LLM-based software engineering agents exhibit high variance across attempts, with best-case performance far exceeding average-case, yet powerful guided search methods like MCTS are inapplicable in non-serializable environments (e.g., Docker containers) where intermediate states cannot be saved, restored, or branched. This paper asks what guided search strategies remain effective under such forward-only execution constraints and whether they can close the gap between peak and average agent capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252962/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a GitHub issue as the task, a base LLM policy (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-72B or GPT-4o), and a learned critic model (LLaMA3.1-70B-based) that scores partial trajectories via a scalar action-value head. The agent interacts with a SWE-agent Docker container through shell commands under an 'until submitted' regime (up to 10 retries), and the final output is a code patch (diff) selected from N candidate trajectories by the critic's terminal action-value. The critic conditions on the policy identity via a system-prompt token, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen policies like GPT-4o.

## Method

The paper introduces two guided search operators that require only forward progression through the environment: (1) 1-step lookahead, which at each step samples K candidate actions from the base policy, scores them with the critic Q(s,a), and commits to the argmax action; and (2) trajectory selection, which generates N complete trajectories and selects the one with the highest critic-scored terminal action-value, generalizing outcome reward models to arbitrary sparse-reward environments. The critic is trained with TD(λ) targets (λ=0.7 optimal) on ~80,000 bootstrapped trajectories collected through iterative policy fine-tuning, and the two methods compose by applying 1-step lookahead within each of the N trajectory-selection runs. This combination doubles the base policy's success rate, achieving 40.8% on SWE-bench Verified (SOTA for open-weight models) and transferring to GPT-4o with comparable gains despite the critic having no GPT-4o-specific training data.

## Results

On the full SWE-bench Verified benchmark, the proposed method (Qwen-based policy + 1-step lookahead with K=8 + trajectory selection with N=15) achieves 40.8% success rate, establishing a new state-of-the-art among open-weight model systems. On the Verified-50 subset, combining both search strategies yields a 2.6× improvement over the base Qwen policy (41.7% vs. 16.2% in the Default regime), and the technique transfers to GPT-4o, boosting its success rate from 22.0% to 40.0% with 1-step lookahead + trajectory selection (N=5). Performance scales favorably with test-time compute: increasing trajectory selection candidates from N=5 to N=10 raises success rate from 36.5% to 41.7% (Default regime).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three open-weight baselines on SWE-bench Verified. Against SWE-Gym (32.0%), which uses trajectory selection with N=32 runs but no step-level guidance, the paper's combined 1-step lookahead + trajectory selection (N=15) achieves 40.8%, an 8.8-point gain while using fewer trajectory candidates. Against SWE-Fixer (30.2%), a dedicated fine-tuned model for GitHub issue resolution, the improvement is 10.6 points. Against Lingma Agent + Lingma-SWE-GPT-72B (25.0%), the gap is 15.8 points. Notably, the paper's approach also demonstrates transferability to a proprietary model (GPT-4o), where 1-step lookahead + trajectory selection (N=5) lifts success rate from 22.0% to 40.0% on Verified-50, a result not reported by any of the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) guided test-time search for LLM agents, which has been explored extensively in serializable settings (MCTS, beam search, best-first search) primarily for mathematical reasoning, and (2) software engineering agents operating in Docker containers (SWE-agent, SWE-bench). The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'non-serializable environments' as a constraint that rules out tree-search methods requiring state rollback or branching, and the demonstration that the simpler 1-step lookahead policy improvement operator (from Sutton & Barto) combined with trajectory-level selection (a generalization of ORMs from Cobbe et al.) is sufficient to recover most of the benefit. The sample-based action selection follows Hubert et al.'s approach for large action spaces, and the bootstrapping data collection pipeline extends SWE-Gym's outcome-supervision framework to also produce step-level critic training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× success-rate improvement (16.2% → 40.8% for the open-weight model) is practically significant for software engineering agents, where the gap between best-case and average-case performance has been a persistent reliability bottleneck. The non-serializability framing is broadly applicable beyond Docker (any stateful, forward-only execution environment), and the transferability to GPT-4o without domain-specific critic training data suggests the approach generalizes. Open questions include: how to mitigate the observed value hacking in the critic, whether replay-based serialization (despite its correctness issues) could unlock MCTS-level gains, and whether the methods scale further with more test-time compute or require fundamentally different search structures.
