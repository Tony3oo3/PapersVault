## Problem

Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) like DPO and SimPO suffer from likelihood displacement—where preferred response probabilities are undesirably reduced—and reward over-optimization, both of which degrade alignment performance. While prior work identified these failure modes, no method had systematically explored how the shape of the implicit reward function (most DAAs use a log reward) governs the intensity of these effects. This paper asks whether changing the reward shape beyond the log form can provide fine-grained control over likelihood displacement while improving alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference triplets (prompt x, preferred response y_w, dispreferred response y_l) where responses are regenerated on-policy from instruct models and ranked by a reward model (PairRM or ArmoRM). Training produces a fine-tuned LLM policy π_θ that generates text responses to prompts. The key constraint is that the reward function is length-normalized by |y|, and the method operates without a reference model during training, unlike DPO.

## Method

AlphaPO introduces an α-parameterized reward function r_α(y;x) = β(1 − π_θ(y|x)^(−α/|y|))/α derived from α-divergence, combined with length normalization and a margin term γ in the Bradley-Terry objective. In the limit α→0, this recovers SimPO's log reward, making SimPO a special case; specific values α=1 and α=−1 yield inverse-linear and linear reward shapes. Gradient analysis reveals that the gradient magnitude |∂ℓ/∂v| is non-monotonic in α: for positive margins, large positive α causes super-exponential gradient decay (regularization), while for negative margins, large positive α causes gradient explosion (aggressive correction). This means α acts as a dial controlling whether the optimizer is conservative or aggressive in adjusting preferred/dispreferred likelihoods, with small positive α (0.1–0.25) empirically yielding the best alignment performance by reducing over-optimization while preserving margin growth.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0 (AE2) length-controlled win rate (LC), AlphaPO achieves 45.37% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct and 33.03% for Mistral-7B-Instruct (PairRM data), representing a 7–10% relative improvement over SimPO (42.05% and 29.71%) and a 15–50% relative improvement over DPO (39.18% and 21.96%). On Arena-Hard, AlphaPO matches or slightly exceeds SimPO (34.6% vs. 32.8% WR for Llama-3, 21.7% vs. 21.5% for Mistral). On HellaSwag and TruthfulQA, AlphaPO also outperforms SimPO across both Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B settings (e.g., 0.7694 vs. 0.7576 on HellaSwag for Llama-3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaPO consistently outperforms SimPO on AE2 LC for Llama-3-8B (45.37% vs. 42.05%, +7.9% relative) and Mistral-7B (33.03% vs. 29.71%, +11.2% relative) under PairRM data, and also for Llama-3 under ArmoRM data (53.01% vs. 51.66%). Against DPO, the gains are larger: +15.9% relative on AE2 LC for Llama-3 (45.37% vs. 39.18%) and +50.1% for Mistral (33.03% vs. 21.96%). On Arena-Hard, AlphaPO edges out SimPO for Llama-3 (34.6% vs. 32.8%) and Mistral (21.7% vs. 21.5%), while DPO is slightly ahead on Llama-3 (34.1% vs. 34.6% is a near-tie). For Gemma-2-9B, AlphaPO achieves the best AE2 LC (74.13% vs. 73.72% SimPO) but trails on AE2 WR (65.89% vs. 67.36%) and AH WR (57.7% vs. 59.0%), which the authors attribute to GPT-judge length bias. On language modeling benchmarks (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA), AlphaPO outperforms SimPO in all four model-setting combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlphaPO extends SimPO by replacing its fixed log reward with a parameterized α-divergence-based reward function, while retaining SimPO's length normalization and margin term. The α-divergence idea is borrowed from f-DPO (Wang et al., 2024a), but AlphaPO departs from f-DPO in three key ways: it applies length normalization (critical for SimPO's gains over DPO), imposes no restrictions on α (f-DPO requires α∈(0,1) for normalization constant cancellation), and demonstrates that non-zero α improves generalization where f-DPO found only α=0 (standard DPO) effective. Conceptually, the paper positions reward function shape as a fourth tuning dimension alongside learning rate, β, and γ, arguing that the shape—not just the magnitude—of the reward governs the aggressiveness of likelihood displacement during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for alignment practitioners: a single additional hyperparameter (α) provides fine-grained control over the margin-vs-likelihood-displacement trade-off, yielding 7–10% relative improvement over SimPO and 15–50% over DPO on AlpacaEval 2.0 across Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B. The gradient analysis (Theorems 3.1–3.2, Corollary 3.3) gives a clean theoretical account of why different α values produce non-monotonic training dynamics, which could inform future DAA design. Open questions include whether reward shape tuning transfers to RLHF-based pipelines (where the reward is learned rather than parameterized), whether the optimal α generalizes to larger model scales, and how the approach interacts with iterative or multi-epoch training beyond the single-epoch regime used here.
