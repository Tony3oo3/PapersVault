## Problem

Approximate machine unlearning methods that degrade model performance on forget samples (via wrong-label fine-tuning, gradient ascent, or boundary shifting) fail to replicate the behavior of retrained-from-scratch models: they cannot simultaneously reduce prediction confidence on forget samples while preserving test accuracy, because forcing a model to misclassify natural in-distribution data causes catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies that prior methods overlook the fact that retrained models treat forget samples as unseen data with the same confidence profile as test samples, and that this behavior can be achieved by exploiting the model's own learned distribution rather than contradicting it.

## Input / Output

AMUN takes a trained CNN classifier (ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 at 32×32, or VGG19 on Tiny ImageNet at 64×64), a forget set D_F of 10% or 50% of training images with their true labels, and optionally the remain set D_R. It first runs PGD-50 (ℓ₂-norm bounded, starting from a small ε and doubling until misclassification) to generate the closest adversarial example for each forget sample, then fine-tunes the model for 10 epochs on D_F ∪ D_A (or D_R ∪ D_F ∪ D_A when D_R is available). The output is a set of updated model parameters whose prediction confidence on D_F matches that of a model retrained on D_R, verified by SOTA membership inference attacks (RMIA) achieving AUC at chance level.

## Method

The key insight is that adversarial examples of training samples, while misclassified by the model, still belong to the distribution the model has learned, so fine-tuning on them with their (wrong) predicted labels does not cause catastrophic forgetting—instead it locally shifts the decision boundary near each forget sample, reducing confidence on the original sample without disturbing global behavior. AMUN (Algorithm 1) finds the minimum-ε adversarial example for each forget sample to maximize localization, then fine-tunes the model on the union of forget samples and their adversarial counterparts. The paper proves an upper bound (Theorem 4.1) on the parameter distance between the AMUN-unlearned model and the gold-standard retrained model, showing that lower Lipschitz constants, smaller adversarial perturbations, and higher-quality attacks all tighten the bound. Ablation studies confirm that replacing adversarial examples with random perturbations, random labels, or original samples with wrong labels causes significant accuracy degradation, validating that the specific use of in-distribution adversarial examples with their mispredicted labels is essential.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, AMUN achieves an Average Gap of 0.62±0.05 (10% forget) and 0.33±0.03 (50% forget) when the remain set D_R is accessible, compared to the best prior method SalUn (1.44 and 1.36 respectively). The FT AUC from the SOTA RMIA membership inference attack drops to 50.18±0.36 (10%) and 49.99±0.18 (50%), meaning the attack cannot distinguish forget samples from test samples better than random guessing. When D_R is unavailable, AMUN+SalUn achieves an Avg. Gap of 1.77±0.06 (10%) and 2.00±0.17 (50%), still substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: SalUn at 4.11 and 4.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMUN outperforms all six baselines on the Avg. Gap metric in both the 10% and 50% forget settings, with the largest margins over GA (4.28 vs. 0.62 at 10%) and BS (3.48 vs. 0.62 at 10%; 32.01 vs. 0.33 at 50%, where BS catastrophically fails). The closest competitor is SalUn (Fan et al., 2024), which achieves an Avg. Gap of 1.44 (10%) and 1.36 (50%) with D_R access, roughly 2.3x worse than AMUN. When D_R is unavailable (Table 2), the gap widens dramatically: AMUN+SalUn achieves 1.77 (10%) and 2.00 (50%) versus SalUn's 4.11 and 4.40, while GA collapses to 68.53 at 10% (near-random accuracy). RL (Golatkar et al., 2020) and BS (Chen et al., 2023b) both fail to reduce the FT AUC below ~61% without D_R, indicating the model retains strong memorization of forget samples, whereas AMUN keeps it near 52%. On Tiny ImageNet with VGG19, AMUN similarly dominates, achieving an Avg. Gap of 5.17 (with D_R) versus 11.24 for SalUn and 52.67 for GA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes adversarial examples—a concept central to adversarial robustness—for the machine unlearning task, departing from prior approximate unlearning methods that rely on fine-tuning with wrong labels, gradient ascent, or boundary shifting. While adversarial robustness literature fine-tunes on adversarial examples with corrected labels to improve robustness, AMUN is the first to exploit fine-tuning on adversarial examples with their mispredicted (wrong) labels to selectively reduce confidence on forget samples. The method also builds on the observation that retrained-from-scratch models exhibit similar prediction confidence on the forget set and test set (both unseen), a characterization that prior unlearning methods either overlooked or addressed incorrectly by directly degrading performance on forget samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMUN provides a practical, computationally efficient unlearning method that makes SOTA membership inference attacks (RMIA) perform at chance level, addressing a critical gap where prior approximate methods left detectable traces of training data. The method's effectiveness in the harder setting without access to the remain set makes it more deployable under real privacy constraints. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models or LLMs, whether it can provide formal privacy guarantees beyond MIA resistance, and whether the utility-loss bounds can be tightened. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet) and moderate architectures (ResNet-18, VGG19).
