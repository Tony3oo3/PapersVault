## Problem

The theoretical understanding of when diffusion model probability flow converges to training samples (memorization) versus more general points on the data manifold (generalization) remains incomplete, particularly because practical deep architectures resist closed-form analysis. This paper asks: for a theoretically tractable denoiser, what determines whether probability flow converges to a single training point, a sum of training points, or a general boundary point of an implicit data manifold?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252974/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The denoiser h(y) takes a noisy observation y = x + ε (with ε ~ N(0, σ²I)) and outputs a clean estimate; the probability flow ODE is initialized at a random point y_T from a high-variance Gaussian and sequentially applies S denoisers at decreasing noise levels to produce a final sample y_0. The score flow variant uses a single denoiser at a fixed noise level and iterates to convergence. In experiments, the ambient dimension is d=30 with orthonormal training points, and convergence is assessed by L∞ distance (threshold 0.2) to predicted stationary points such as training points, virtual points (sums of training points), or hyperbox boundary points.

## Method

The paper analyzes the probability flow and score flow ODEs of shallow ReLU denoisers with skip connections trained to minimum ℓ² representation cost, using Tweedie's identity to estimate the score as (h*(y) − y)/σ². For orthogonal training points, the paper proves that the stable stationary points of the score flow are exactly the vertices of a hyperbox whose vertices are all partial sums of training points, including 'virtual training points' that are sums of multiple training points. The key mechanistic insight is that the diffusion time scheduler in probability flow induces 'early stopping': while score flow always converges to a hyperbox vertex, probability flow can converge to a non-vertex boundary point when the total diffusion time T falls below a critical threshold τ(y_T, ρ_T). Results are extended to obtuse simplex datasets, and simulations using the Augmented Lagrangian method to enforce exact interpolation confirm the theory, showing that memorization (convergence to training points) decreases as the number of training samples grows.

## Results

For orthogonal training data in d=30 with K=300 hidden units, 98.6% of predicted virtual training points (sums of pairs) are confirmed as stable stationary points via fixed-point iterations, with 429, 3,390, and 6,965 stable points found for pair, triplet, and quadruplet combinations respectively. In the full diffusion sampling (500 random initializations from N(0,100I)), the score flow converges almost exclusively to virtual points or hyperbox boundaries, while the probability flow shows a higher fraction converging to training points due to the large-noise regime bias. Generalization improves monotonically with training set size N: as N increases from 10 to 30, a larger percentage of samples converge to virtual points and hyperbox boundaries rather than individual training points, reducing memorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method baselines; the primary comparison is between the score flow (fixed noise level) and the probability flow (scheduled noise reduction) of the same minimum-norm denoiser. The score flow converges only to hyperbox vertices (training points or virtual points), while the probability flow additionally converges to non-vertex boundary points due to early stopping induced by the diffusion time scheduler σ_t=√t. In simulations, the probability flow produces a higher fraction of training-point convergences than the score flow, because the large-noise denoisers at the start of sampling attract samples toward the training-point mean before the low-noise regime takes over. The paper also compares training with and without weight decay: without weight decay, probability flow converges only to training points or boundary points (no virtual points), while with weight decay (or the Augmented Lagrangian minimum-norm training), virtual points emerge as stable attractors. Increasing N from 10 to 30 shifts the convergence distribution away from individual training points toward virtual points and boundary points, consistent with prior empirical findings by Kadkhodaie et al. (2024) on geometry-adaptive generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Zeno et al. (2023), which derived explicit minimum-norm shallow ReLU denoiser solutions for analytically tractable dataset geometries (orthogonal points, obtuse simplex, equilateral triangle). Here, those closed-form denoisers are plugged into the probability flow ODE and a simpler score flow ODE to study their sampling dynamics, extending the function-space characterization to a dynamical-systems analysis. The work also connects to the broader memorization-in-diffusion-models literature (Carlini et al., 2023; Somepalli et al., 2023; Kadkhodaie et al., 2024) by providing a theoretical mechanism—early stopping induced by the variance schedule—that explains why diffusion models both reproduce training samples and generate novel combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical account of the memorization–generalization duality in diffusion models: the diffusion time scheduler acts as an implicit early-stopping mechanism that allows probability flow to land on the hyperbox boundary (generalization) rather than only at vertices (memorization), while the score flow at fixed noise always converges to vertices. The identification of 'virtual training points' (sums of training samples) as stable stationary points provides a geometric explanation for the semantic-composition behavior observed empirically in Stable Diffusion. Key limitations include the restriction to a single hidden layer, specific dataset geometries, and the assumption of full d-dimensional ball interpolation; extending the analysis to variance-preserving schedules, deeper architectures, and realistic data manifolds remains open.
