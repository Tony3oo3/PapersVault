## Problem

Hypergraph Neural Networks (HNNs) are increasingly deployed for modeling higher-order relationships, yet their security under adversarial manipulation remains largely unexplored. Existing injection attack methods designed for pairwise graphs fail to account for hypergraph-specific structural properties, and naive injection strategies on hypergraphs cause large homophily shifts that make attacks detectable. This paper is the first to study node injection attacks specifically tailored for HNNs, addressing both effectiveness and stealth.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a strict black-box setting, receiving only the node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ R^{|V|×|E|} without any model querying. It produces a modified incidence matrix H' (block-structured with original H, cross-connection blocks I_e and I_v, and a new-node/new-hyperedge block Ĥ) and a modified feature matrix X', where original nodes' features and edges remain untouched. Key hyperparameters include the injection ratio α (fraction of new nodes), the number of selected hyperedges k, perturbation magnitude η, perturbation budget B, and homophily-preservation weight λ.

## Method

HyperNear is a four-step injection framework: (1) feature generation creates adversarial node features by adding small perturbations η to existing node features; (2) randomized hyperedge selection avoids bias and aids concealment; (3) fully-connected injection places new nodes into selected hyperedges to maximize influence; and (4) gradient-guided structural update splits the injected hyperedge to maximize classification loss. The key differentiator is a homophily-preserving adversarial objective: the overall loss is L_atk = L_cls − λ·L_FHH, where L_FHH penalizes squared changes in feature-based homophily (FHH) before and after the attack, ensuring perturbations align with existing structural patterns. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.1) establishes that hypergraph multi-hop aggregation amplifies small topological perturbations, explaining why both effectiveness and stealth require careful design. The method is evaluated across eight HNN backbones (UniSAGE, UniGCN, UniGAT, UniGIN, HyperGCN, HGNN, ED-HNN, UniGCNII) on five datasets, demonstrating strong transferability and unnoticeability.

## Results

HyperNear achieves the lowest classification accuracy (i.e., strongest attack) across all five datasets and four victim HNNs. On UniSAGE, it reduces accuracy from 74.13% to 60.24% on Cora-CA (−13.89 pts), from 67.44% to 49.35% on Cora (−18.09 pts), and from 74.31% to 55.31% on Pubmed (−19.00 pts). The attack also transfers well to four additional HNN architectures (HyperGCN, HGNN, ED-HNN, UniGCNII), degrading accuracy by 10–27 points depending on the model and dataset. Homophily ratio remains largely unchanged post-attack (e.g., Cora-CA: 0.6129→0.5416 for naive attacks vs. minimal shift for HyperNear), confirming stealth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperNear substantially outperforms both baselines across all datasets and victim models. Against NDA (Zhang et al., 2024), HyperNear achieves 11–19 points lower accuracy on UniSAGE (e.g., 60.24% vs. 71.70% on Cora-CA; 49.35% vs. 64.96% on Cora), and similar margins on UniGCN/UniGAT. Against FGA (Chen et al., 2018), the gap is comparable: 11–19 points lower on UniSAGE (e.g., 60.24% vs. 71.58% on Cora-CA; 55.31% vs. 71.57% on Pubmed). The Random baseline is essentially ineffective and sometimes even improves model accuracy (e.g., 76.36% vs. 75.74% clean on UniGCN/Cora-CA), confirming that naive injection without structural awareness is counterproductive. Both NDA and FGA are adapted from pairwise-graph settings and fail to exploit hypergraph-specific higher-order structure, whereas HyperNear's homophily-preserving injection strategy is purpose-built for hypergraphs, yielding consistent and large accuracy drops (10–27 points) across all eight evaluated HNN architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the node injection attack paradigm from traditional graphs (Zou et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024) to the hypergraph setting, where higher-order hyperedge structures introduce unique amplification effects. The theoretical vulnerability analysis builds on the general hypergraph convolution framework of Chien et al. (ICLR 2022), while the homophily-preserving stealth constraint is adapted from the unnoticeability principles in graph injection attacks (Chen et al., ICLR 2022). The key departure is that prior injection attack work targets pairwise graphs, and no prior work formalizes or executes injection attacks specifically on hypergraph neural networks in a black-box setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As HNNs are increasingly deployed in sensitive domains (healthcare, finance, social networks), this is the first systematic characterization of their vulnerability to stealthy node injection, providing both a threat model and a baseline for future defense research. The paper acknowledges that formal theoretical guarantees on detectability remain open, and that existing graph-based defenses do not transfer to hypergraphs due to fundamental differences in connectivity structure. A natural next step is designing hypergraph-specific robustness mechanisms, which the authors identify as an important and promising direction.
