## Problem

Existing Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) methods assume structured domain changes with uniform durations (CSC), but real-world environments exhibit dynamic patterns where domains recur with varying frequencies and durations. When evaluated under this more realistic Continual Dynamic Change (CDC) setting, SOTA methods like ViDA suffer severe degradation (e.g., error rate jumps from 43.4% to 52.1% on ImageNet-C) due to convergence failures on brief domain exposures, catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge, and negative transfer between dissimilar domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253039/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step, DPCore receives an unlabeled batch of 64 RGB images from an unknown target domain and uses pre-computed source feature statistics (mean and standard deviation of [CLS] features from just 300 unlabeled source examples, computed once offline). It outputs a class prediction (or segmentation mask for Cityscapes-to-ACDC) and an updated prompt coreset consisting of (prompt, statistics) pairs. No labels, no source data access, and no warm-up on the full source dataset are required during adaptation—only the 300-example statistics are needed, and the method remains effective even when source data is entirely unavailable.

## Method

DPCore introduces L=8 learnable 768-dim prompt tokens into a frozen ViT-Base and manages them through a dynamic coreset inspired by Online K-Means. Visual Prompt Adaptation (VPA) learns each prompt from scratch by minimizing the L2 distance between the target batch's feature statistics (mean, std) and the source statistics, requiring no warm-up. The Prompt Coreset stores (prompt, statistics) pairs representing learned domain groups, and the Dynamic Update mechanism decides per batch whether to refine existing prompts or create a new one: it computes a softmax-weighted prompt from all core elements, and if this weighted prompt sufficiently reduces the source-target domain gap (ratio ≤ ρ=0.8), it performs a single refinement step and updates all core elements proportionally to their weights; otherwise, it learns a new prompt from scratch and appends it to the coreset. This multi-prompt strategy learns from homogeneous domain groups rather than individual batches, enabling robust adaptation even when domains appear briefly or in arbitrary order, while using only 0.08M trainable parameters (99% fewer than ViDA) and 64% less computation time.

## Results

On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C (CSC setting, severity 5), DPCore achieves a 39.9% mean error rate, a +15.9% improvement over the source model (55.8%), surpassing the previous SOTA C-MAE (42.5%) by 2.6% and ViDA (43.4%) by 3.5%. In the more challenging CDC setting, DPCore maintains 42.1% error rate while ViDA degrades sharply to 52.1% and Tent falls below the source model (57.1% vs. 55.8%). On Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation, DPCore achieves 62.5% mIoU in CSC (+5.8% over source) and 61.0% in CDC (+4.3%), outperforming ViDA by 0.6% and 1.8% respectively. DPCore uses only 0.08M learnable parameters (99% fewer than ViDA's 93.70M) with 1.8× relative computation time vs. ViDA's 2.8×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPCore outperforms all 11 baselines on ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C in both CSC and CDC settings. In CSC, DPCore (39.9%) beats the closest competitors C-MAE (42.5%) and BDG (42.8%) by 2.6% and 3.1% respectively, and the previous SOTA ViDA (43.4%) by 3.5%. The gap widens dramatically in CDC: DPCore (42.1%) surpasses ViDA (52.1%) by 10.0%, while Tent (57.1%), CoTTA (57.3%), and EcoTTA (55.3%) all fall below the unadapted source model (55.8%). On Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation, DPCore leads ViDA by 0.6% mIoU in CSC (62.5% vs. 61.9%) and by 1.8% in CDC (61.0% vs. 59.2%), while Tent and EcoTTA degrade below source in both settings. Notably, DPCore achieves these gains with only 0.08M learnable parameters versus ViDA's 93.70M (89× fewer) and 1.8× relative computation time versus ViDA's 2.8×, while also requiring only 300 unlabeled source examples for statistics rather than the full source dataset needed by ViDA, VDP, DePT, and EcoTTA for warm-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPCore extends the Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) paradigm introduced by CoTTA (Wang et al., 2022a) by replacing the single-model parameter-update strategy with a multi-model prompt coreset inspired by Online K-Means. It builds on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., 2022) for lightweight adaptation and on marginal distribution alignment (Ben-David et al., 2006) as the adaptation objective, but departs from prior prompt-based TTA methods (DePT, VDP) by eliminating the need for source-data warm-up and by dynamically managing multiple prompts rather than updating a single set. The paper also introduces the Continual Dynamic Change (CDC) setting as a deliberate generalization of the Continual Structured Change (CSC) assumption used by all prior CTTA work, where domains recur with varying frequencies and durations rather than appearing once in fixed-length blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDC setting exposes a practical gap: methods that work well under structured domain sequences (ViDA, CoTTA) degrade substantially when domains shift rapidly and unpredictably, a pattern more representative of real deployment (e.g., autonomous driving through tunnels, fog, and rain). DPCore's 99% parameter reduction and 64% computation-time savings relative to ViDA make CTTA more viable on edge devices, and the theoretical order-independence guarantee (under well-separated cluster assumptions) provides a principled justification for the coreset design. Open questions include whether the well-separatedness assumption holds in practice for highly overlapping domains, whether the unbounded coreset growth is manageable in truly open-ended streams, and whether the approach extends to settings where batch-level domain homogeneity is violated.
