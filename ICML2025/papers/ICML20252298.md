## Problem

Existing reasoning optimization for LLMs has been confined to mathematical problems and image-based inputs, leaving general video understanding—where audio, visual, and text modalities interact—largely unexplored. Multimodal LLMs that process video with audio tend to lose step-by-step reasoning ability and directly output answers, limiting their applicability to tasks requiring logical inference over complex audio-visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252298/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips at 2 fps (max 60 frames) with paired audio, processed separately by a SigLIP visual encoder and a Whisper-Large-v3 audio encoder (via a 0.2s-window Q-Former producing 150 tokens per 30s), then temporally interleaved before feeding a Qwen2-7B LLM. Output is a chain of reasoning steps each terminated by an <end_of_step> token, followed by a final answer (5-way multiple choice for Academic/StandUp, or yes/no for synthetic video detection). The synthetic video detection task is entirely zero-shot, never seen during training.

## Method

video-SALMONN-o1 is trained in two stages: (1) a multi-stage SFT pipeline (audio aligner from scratch, then LoRA on modality aligners) using 150k normal QA pairs plus 30k reasoning-intensive QA pairs generated by Gemini-1.5-pro with GPT-4o quality checks; and (2) process DPO (pDPO), a step-level pairwise preference optimization that avoids absolute score prediction. pDPO identifies the most video-dependent error-prone steps via contrastive step selection—computing length-normalized per-token KL divergence between original and perturbed video inputs—then performs pairwise rollouts on the top-T selected steps to construct preference pairs. The pDPO loss uses a Bradley-Terry model over step-level preferences with soft labels calibrated by rollout estimation noise, and is integrated with full-path PPRM for complementary granularity. The paper also introduces RivaBench, a 4,000+ QA benchmark across standup comedy, academic presentations, and synthetic video detection with human expert annotations.

## Results

video-SALMONN-o1 (pDPO) achieves 65.6% on VideoMME, 82.3% on NExT-QA, 76.7% on RivaBench StandUp, 48.3% on RivaBench Academic, and 17.8% F1 (87% precision / 13% recall) on RivaBench SynthDec. Compared to the LLaVA-OneVision baseline, it gains 7.4% on VideoMME, 2.9% on NExT-QA, 9.5% on StandUp, and 2.5% on Academic, while achieving zero-shot synthetic video detection (17.8% F1) where all other open-source models score 0%. The pDPO training adds a further 2.7–8.1% over the SFT-only model across benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-OneVision (the primary open-source visual baseline), video-SALMONN-o1 (pDPO) wins on every benchmark: +7.4% on VideoMME (65.6% vs 58.2%), +2.9% on NExT-QA (82.3% vs 79.4%), +9.5% on StandUp (76.7% vs 67.2%), +2.5% on Academic (48.3% vs 45.8%), and +17.8% F1 on SynthDec (17.8% vs 0.0%). Against the other audio-visual baselines (video-SALMONN and Video-LLaMA 2.1), the gains are even larger, with 22+ point improvements on VideoMME and 29+ points on NExT-QA over video-SALMONN. Compared to proprietary models, video-SALMONN-o1 (pDPO) surpasses Gemini-1.5-pro on StandUp (76.7% vs 75.8%) and NExT-QA (82.3% vs 79.2%), and beats GPT-4o on StandUp (76.7% vs 63.3%) and NExT-QA (82.3% vs 81.7%), but trails both on Academic (48.3% vs 67.1% and 60.0%) and VideoMME (65.6% vs 75.0% and 71.9%). On SynthDec, GPT-4o (visual-only) achieves the highest F1 at 34.1%, followed by Gemini-1.5-pro at 23.6%, while video-SALMONN-o1 reaches 17.8%—still a significant zero-shot capability that no other open-source model possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) from sequence-level to step-level preference modelling for multimodal reasoning, building on the PPRM formulation from LLaMA-Berry (Zhang et al., 2024a) and the Monte Carlo rollout-based step annotation from Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024b). Architecturally, it inherits the interleaved audio-visual synchronization module and multi-stage SFT pipeline from video-SALMONN 2 (Tang et al., 2024a). The key departure from prior reasoning-optimization work is the shift from mathematical problems with image inputs to general video understanding where audio-visual-text interactions must be jointly reasoned over, and the introduction of contrastive step selection to focus compute on video-dependent error-prone steps rather than uniform step annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source model to demonstrate that RL-based reasoning optimization (pDPO) transfers from math to general audio-visual video understanding, and the zero-shot synthetic video detection capability, while modest in absolute F1 (17.8%), suggests that enhanced reasoning can yield emergent perceptual-detection abilities without task-specific training. RivaBench fills a gap by providing expert-annotated, reasoning-intensive video QA that existing benchmarks (VideoMME, NExT-QA) do not cover. Open questions include whether pDPO and contrastive step selection scale to larger backbones or longer videos beyond the 60-frame/2fps limit, and whether the 3–8% gains over LLaVA-OneVision persist as base models improve. The finding that SFT models still prefer direct answers over reasoning (Table 3) highlights that exposure bias in long-sequence generation remains a fundamental challenge for multimodal CoT.
