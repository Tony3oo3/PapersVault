## Problem

Standard statistical estimation assumes i.i.d. observations, but many practical tasks (stock prices, sensor readings, network traffic) involve a time-varying ground truth where the distribution shifts over time. Prior work (Mazzetto and Upfal, 2023) addressed this via adaptive moving windows based on local stability, but this approach is limited to piecewise-constant structure and requires O(log n) ERM oracle calls for classification. The paper asks whether a more general, sparsity-based characterization of nonstationarity can yield sharper point-wise error guarantees and better computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a reverse-indexed noisy observation sequence y_n, …, y_1 (where index 1 is the most recent time point) and an orthonormal wavelet transform matrix W, then produces a single point estimate θ̂_1 of the latest ground truth with a high-probability point-wise error bound. Unlike moving-average methods, no explicit window size is maintained; the relevant data portion is implicitly selected through the wavelet coefficient structure. For the classification extension, the input is a sequence of covariate-target pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from potentially different distributions D_i, and the output is a hypothesis f̂ whose excess risk on D_1 is controlled via a single ERM call.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) applies an orthonormal wavelet transform to the observation sequence, soft-thresholds the resulting coefficients at level λ = 2σ√(2 log(log n/δ)), and inverse-transforms to reconstruct the signal, returning the coordinate corresponding to the most recent time point. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that only log₂(n) wavelet coefficients affect the reconstruction at the target index, so the point-wise error is bounded by a weighted sum of min(|β_i|, λ) over those coefficients—directly linking estimation quality to the sparsity of the true wavelet coefficients. Using Haar wavelets recovers the variational bounds of Mazzetto and Upfal (Theorem 2), while higher-order Daubechies or CDJV wavelets capture complex trends (polynomial, piecewise-smooth) with sparser representations, yielding potentially sharper rates. For binary classification under temporal shift, the wavelet-based loss estimator replaces the O(log n) ERM calls of prior work with a single ERM call, inflating the excess risk bound only by O(log n) while preserving differentiability of the surrogate loss objective.

## Results

On synthetic Doppler signals with known noise standard deviation, the proposed DB8 wavelet estimator achieves MSE of 0.007±0.0002 at noise level 0.2, compared to 0.511±0.0013 for the AVG baseline (Mazzetto & Upfal, 2023) — a ~73× improvement. On the Dubai Land Department real-world dataset (192 months of apartment sales), HAAR achieves test MSE of 0.0722±0.0006 under a 79%-1% train-validation split, outperforming ARW (0.0790±0.0005) when validation data is scarce. The method also requires only a single ERM oracle call for binary classification under distribution shift, versus O(log n) calls in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AVG (Mazzetto & Upfal, 2023), the wavelet methods dominate on synthetic data: on the Doppler signal, DB8 reduces MSE from ~0.51 to 0.007–0.129 across noise levels, while HAAR reduces it to 0.068–0.155. On the Random (piecewise-constant) signal, all methods perform comparably when the noise standard deviation is unknown, with AVG and ARW slightly edging out HAAR/DB8 at some noise levels due to the signal's favorable structure for window-based averaging. Against ARW (Han et al., 2024), the proposed methods win decisively on Doppler signals (e.g., DB8 at 0.0204 vs. ARW at 0.3844 MSE at noise 0.2, unknown std dev) but on the Dubai real-data task, HAAR (0.0722) narrowly beats ARW (0.0790) only under the 79%-1% split, while ARW slightly wins under the 75%-5% split (0.0719 vs. 0.0736). Against Aligator (Baby et al., 2021), the wavelet methods outperform on Doppler signals (DB8 at 0.0204 vs. Aligator at 0.234 at noise 0.2) but Aligator remains competitive on Random signals. A key computational advantage is that the proposed ERM-based classifier requires exactly one oracle call versus O(log n) for Mazzetto & Upfal, at the cost of an O(log n) inflation in the excess risk bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the adaptive estimation problem under temporal distribution shift (first addressed by Mazzetto and Upfal, 2023, via local-stability windowing) through the lens of classical wavelet soft-thresholding (Donoho, 1995), showing that the latter already recovers the same optimal point-wise error guarantees while offering a more general sparsity-based framework. It further builds on the log-loss decomposition of Achille and Soatto (2018) to formally characterize how distribution shift degrades model performance, and draws a black-box connection to the total-variation denoising literature (Mammen and van de Geer, 1997; Tibshirani, 2014) to uncover previously unknown minimax optimality of existing algorithms. The key departure from prior work is replacing the explicit adaptive window-size mechanism with an implicit, wavelet-driven selection of relevant data, and reducing the number of ERM oracle calls from O(log n) to one for classification under drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wavelet-sparsity perspective subsumes and generalizes the local-stability notion, potentially yielding sharper error rates for signals with complex (non-piecewise-constant) trends, as demonstrated empirically with Daubechies wavelets. The black-box reduction from point-wise estimation bounds to TV-denoising optimality is a clean theoretical contribution that retroactively certifies several existing algorithms as minimax optimal. Open questions include principled wavelet selection for a given task, the interaction between the implicit windowing and changepoint detection, and whether the framework extends beyond univariate scalar estimation to multivariate or structured settings. The practical impact is moderate: the methods are computationally efficient and applicable to real data (Dubai housing), but the problem setting (scalar time-series estimation) is narrower than the broader distribution-shift challenges in deep learning.
