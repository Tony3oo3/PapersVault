## Problem

Hierarchical clustering algorithms are widely used but their stability under random data perturbations remains poorly understood. While prior work identified sensitivity to adversarial changes, it is unclear whether theoretically grounded hierarchical k-median algorithms like CLNSS exhibit low average sensitivity (i.e., small expected output change when a random point is deleted). This paper fills that gap by providing the first rigorous average sensitivity analysis for hierarchical k-median clustering in Euclidean space.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a point set P ⊆ R^d of size n, a target number of clusters k, and a privacy parameter ε > 0. It produces an ordered sequence of n centers c_1, …, c_n and a nested sequence of partitions P_1, …, P_n where P_k is a k-partition obtained by successively splitting clusters. The sensitivity is measured as the Earth Mover's Distance between the output distributions on P and on P with one point removed, with the distance between two clusterings defined via the minimum symmetric difference over all bijections of cluster labels.

## Method

The proposed algorithm (Algorithm 2) modifies the CLNSS pipeline by replacing its deterministic greedy center selection with the exponential mechanism from differential privacy. After applying a random shift and constructing a 2-RHST, each iteration t samples a center c_t with probability proportional to exp(−COST_T(P, x ∪ S_{t-1})/λ), where λ is drawn uniformly from [ε·COST_T(P, x̄_t ∪ S_{t-1})/(6 ln n), ε·COST_T(P, x̄_t ∪ S_{t-1})/(3 ln n)]. The selected center labels its highest unlabelled ancestor in the tree, and all points are reassigned to their closest labelled ancestor. This yields an average sensitivity of O(k ln n / ε) while preserving an expected approximation ratio of O(d · log Λ · (1+ε)^k) with probability at least 1 − k/n². The paper further proves Ω(n) lower bounds on the sensitivity of single linkage and the deterministic CLNSS variant, and shows that single linkage achieves low sensitivity O(2m) on well-clusterable datasets where inter-cluster distances dominate intra-cluster diameters.

## Results

The proposed Algorithm 2 achieves an expected k-median cost of O(d·log Λ·(1+ε)^k)·OPT(P,k) with average sensitivity O(k ln n / ε) for any k ∈ {1,…,n} (Theorem 3.1). On synthetic worst-case instances, single linkage and deterministic CLNSS exhibit average sensitivity of Ω(n), while the proposed algorithm maintains low sensitivity. On real-world datasets (Diabetes, Wholesale, WDBC, Digits) with k=4, the proposed algorithm (ε=1) achieves lower average sensitivity than CLNSS, complete linkage, average linkage, and Ward's method, while its k-median cost is competitive—slightly higher than Ward's method and complete linkage but lower than single linkage, average linkage, and CLNSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CLNSS algorithm (Cohen-Addad et al., 2021), the proposed method achieves significantly lower average sensitivity on both synthetic worst-case instances (Fig. 2b) and real datasets (Fig. 5), while incurring only a modest increase in k-median cost. Against single linkage, the proposed algorithm substantially outperforms on k-median cost (single linkage produces significantly higher costs, as shown in Figs. 4 and 9), though single linkage appears to have lower sensitivity on real datasets—explained theoretically (Lemma G.2) and empirically by the well-clusterable structure of those datasets. Against Ward's method and complete linkage, the proposed algorithm slightly underperforms on k-median cost but achieves lower average sensitivity. Against average linkage, the proposed algorithm wins on both k-median cost and average sensitivity. The deterministic variant of CLNSS (Algorithm 1) is shown to have Ω(n) average sensitivity (Lemma 4.2), confirming that the probabilistic exponential mechanism is essential for stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLNSS algorithm (Cohen-Addad et al., 2021) for hierarchical k-median clustering on 2-RHST trees, replacing its deterministic greedy center selection with the exponential mechanism from differential privacy (McSherry & Talwar, 2007) to achieve provable low average sensitivity. It departs from prior robustness studies (Balcan et al., 2014; Eriksson et al., 2011) that focused on adversarial perturbations or outlier removal, instead adopting the average sensitivity framework (Peng & Yoshida, 2020; Varma & Yoshida, 2021) that measures expected output change under random point deletion. The paper also distinguishes itself from Hara et al. (2024), which studies average sensitivity of hierarchical clustering but under sparsest-cut/Dasgupta cost criteria without approximation guarantees, whereas this work targets the k-median objective with explicit utility bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a hierarchical k-median clustering algorithm with simultaneous guarantees on both approximation quality and average sensitivity, filling a gap where prior algorithms either lacked stability guarantees or lacked objective-function-based analysis. The Ω(n) lower bounds for single linkage and deterministic CLNSS establish that instability is inherent to these methods on adversarial instances, while the complementary result (Lemma G.2) showing single linkage is stable on well-clusterable data reconciles theory with practice. Key limitations include the O(n³) running time and the (1+ε)^k degradation in approximation ratio, which becomes problematic for large k; open questions include whether the sensitivity-utility trade-off can be tightened and whether the approach extends to other hierarchical objectives (e.g., Dasgupta cost) or to dynamic/streaming settings.
