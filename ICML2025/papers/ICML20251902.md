## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for generating unrestricted adversarial examples (UAEs) primarily use diffusion models as strong priors to purify noise, rather than fully leveraging the learned real-data distribution to guide adversarial feature generation, which risks producing unnatural results. Additionally, most methods are rigidly designed for a single attack scenario (e.g., from-noise or from-reference), limiting their overall effectiveness and flexibility across diverse attack conditions.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an optional 224×224×3 reference image, a ground truth label, a target classifier, and a pre-trained diffusion model; for regional or style attacks it additionally takes a binary mask and/or a style reference image. When no reference image is provided, a conditional diffusion model first generates low-level features of the target class from pure Gaussian noise before DiffAdvMAP takes over. The output is a single unrestricted adversarial example image that misclassifies under the target model while preserving natural appearance, with the reconstruction constraint (via a customizable function Ω and mask m) controlling which image attributes are preserved or modified.

## Method

DiffAdvMAP formulates UAE generation as a Bayesian MAP problem: it approximates the posterior distribution of adversarial latent codes by combining the diffusion model's learned real-data prior with an adversarial constraint (logit difference ≤ c) and a reconstruction constraint (m ∘ Ω(x̂₀) = m ∘ Ω(x̃)), then samples from this posterior via greedy optimization at each reverse diffusion step. A key efficiency mechanism is the 'destruction and construction' strategy, which first applies t* forward diffusion steps to destroy high-level semantics of the reference image, then runs only a truncated t*-step reverse process to regenerate adversarial high-level features while preserving low-level structure, dramatically reducing generation time. A one-step estimation f_θ^t(x_t) approximates the final image from any intermediate latent code, avoiding the need to run the full reverse process at each optimization iteration. The reconstruction constraint's function Ω and mask m are swappable across scenarios (identity for global, LAB-space conversion for color, style-score for style transfer, region mask for partial editing), giving the framework its flexibility without architectural changes.

## Results

On the ImageNet-compatible dataset (1,000 images, 224×224×3) with Inception V3 as surrogate model, DiffAdvMAP achieves 100% white-box attack success rate, the best FID (61.2) and tied-best LPIPS (0.127) among all baselines, while completing generation in 6.0 s per sample. Transfer attack success rates reach 42.8% (ResNet-50), 48.6% (MobileNet V2), and 30.3% (Swin-B). Against defense methods, DiffAdvMAP maintains the highest white-box ASR under R&P (46.8%) and NRP (93.3%), and ranks top-2 across all seven defense strategies tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two classical unrestricted attacks, DiffAdvMAP dominates: cAdv (91.7% white-box, FID 65.7) and ReColorAdv (98.4%, FID 63.4) are outperformed on white-box ASR, FID, LPIPS, and all three transfer targets, though ReColorAdv is faster (3.86 s vs 6.0 s). NCF is the only baseline that beats DiffAdvMAP on transferability to ResNet-50 (47.4% vs 42.8%) and MobileNet V2 (53.8% vs 48.6%), but it lags substantially on white-box ASR (82.6% vs 100%), FID (70.9 vs 61.2), and LPIPS (0.383 vs 0.127). Among diffusion-based methods, Diff-PGD (83.8% white-box, FID 65.9) is clearly outperformed on every metric, while DiffAttack (86.1%, FID 62.3, 28.2 s) is beaten on white-box ASR, FID, Swin-B transferability (30.3% vs 25.4%), and runtime (6.0 s vs 28.2 s), though DiffAttack edges ahead on ResNet-50 transfer (39.4% vs 42.8% — actually DiffAdvMAP wins here too). In the from-noise setting (Table 1), DiffAdvMAP improves transfer ASR over AdvDiff by roughly 2× (e.g., 30.0% vs 10.5% to ResNet-50) with comparable FID (~43–44). In the white-box robust-model setting (Table 4), DiffAdvMAP matches AdvDiffuser's near-100% ASR while achieving dramatically lower LPIPS (0.006–0.012 vs 0.03–0.05), lower FID (6.83–13.20 vs 20.9–27.2), and ~20× faster generation (~4 s vs ~90 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM deterministic sampling framework and the CW attack's logit-difference constraint, but departs conceptually from prior diffusion-based adversarial attack methods (DiffAttack, AdvDiff, Diff-PGD, AdvDiffuser) that treat diffusion models as strong priors for purifying noise or guiding latent codes. Instead, DiffAdvMAP reframes UAE generation as Bayesian inference: it explicitly derives and samples from the posterior distribution of adversarial examples conditioned on adversarial and reconstruction constraints, using the diffusion model's learned prior over natural data. The destruction-and-construction strategy, which truncates the generation process to preserve low-level features while regenerating adversarial high-level features, draws on image editing techniques (SD-Edit, DiffEdit) but applies them to the adversarial setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Bayesian framework provides a principled way to handle multiple attack scenarios (from noise, global similar, regional, color/style) through a single reconstruction constraint, which is more flexible than prior methods designed for specific conditions. The practical runtime reduction (~7×) from the truncated generation process makes diffusion-based unrestricted attacks more deployable. Open questions include whether the one-step approximation degrades under more complex or multi-objective constraints, whether the approach extends to video or 3D adversarial generation, and whether the posterior formulation generalizes beyond diffusion models to other generative architectures. The method still requires a pre-trained diffusion model and a target classifier, limiting its applicability in fully black-box settings.
