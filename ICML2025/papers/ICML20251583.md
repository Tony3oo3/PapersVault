## Problem

In the DSP framework for neural theorem proving, LLMs face a dilemma: generating rough proof sketches produces intermediate conjectures too hard for off-the-shelf ATPs, while generating detailed sketches introduces formalization errors. Previous methods apply automation tools at only a single granularity level or only when explicitly invoked, failing to exploit their full power across the hierarchical structure of a proof.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251583/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a formal theorem statement in Isabelle/Isar (or Lean 4), an informal problem statement, and an informal proof draft as input. The LLM (deepseek-math-7b-base) generates a full formal proof proposal, which is then parsed into a sequence of proof steps and processed through the ITP. The output is a verified formal proof that passes the ITP's verification check, with all sorry gaps filled by built-in heuristics (auto, simp, fastforce, arith, etc.) and Sledgehammer, or by the ERP module's recursive completion queries.

## Method

ProofAug operates bottom-up on a full proof proposal: it first identifies the Maximal Compatible Semi-Proof (MCSP), the finest-grained semi-proof (with sorry steps) that still passes ITP verification, by walking through proof steps and replacing failed proof methods or entire proof...qed blocks with sorry. It then iteratively applies a composite <ATP> method (8 Isabelle heuristics + Sledgehammer) to each sorry gap; when an ATP call fails, the algorithm recursively coarsens the semi-proof by replacing the enclosing block with a single sorry, thereby avoiding wasted computation on sub-goals that are already known to be unsolvable. An optional Efficient Recursive Proving (ERP) module integrates tree-search: when ATP fails at a conjecture, the system sends a targeted proof-completion prompt to the LLM conditioned on the verified partial proof and current state, adding new search nodes only for failed blocks in the original MCSP. This plug-and-play design works with any tree-search algorithm and requires no fine-tuning, achieving 66.0% pass rate on curated miniF2F-test with at most 2100 model queries per problem.

## Results

On miniF2F-test (Isabelle), ProofAug with deepseek-math-7b-base achieves 52.5% pass rate with 100 model queries, outperforming the DSP baseline (49.2%) by 3.3%. With the ERP module and 0-shot prompting, performance reaches 56.1% at 500 queries. Using a mixed prompting strategy, the paper achieves 61.9% at ~1400 queries on the original miniF2F-test and 66.0% at ~2100 queries on the curated version, surpassing the previous Isabelle SOTA (Subgoal-XL: 56.1% at 16384 queries) with roughly 8× fewer queries. In Lean 4, ProofAug improves Kimina-Prover Preview-Distill-1.5B pass@1 from 44.3% to 50.4% on miniF2F-test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DSP baseline using the same model (deepseek-math-7b-base), ProofAug consistently improves pass rate: +7.8% at 1 query (36.5% vs 28.7%), +4.1% at 10 queries (44.7% vs 40.6%), and +3.3% at 100 queries (52.5% vs 49.2%). Compared to the previous Isabelle SOTA, Subgoal-XL (56.1% at 16384 queries), ProofAug with mixed strategy achieves 61.9% at only ~1400 queries—a 5.8% absolute gain with roughly 8× fewer model queries. ProofAug also outperforms LEGO-Prover (50.0% at 100 queries) and Lyra (51.2% at 200 queries) at the same or lower query budgets. The ERP module adds a further 1.6% gain over ProofAug alone at 500 queries (56.1% vs 54.5%), whereas RMaxTS shows less than 0.2% advantage over naive retrying at 3200 queries. In the Lean 4 setting, ProofAug improves Kimina-Prover Preview-Distill-1.5B from 44.3% to 50.4% pass@1, demonstrating cross-system generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the DSP (Draft, Sketch, and Prove) framework by inverting its logic: rather than generating a proof sketch with placeholders for ATPs to fill, ProofAug generates a full proof and then performs bottom-up fine-grained structural analysis to find the Maximal Compatible Semi-Proof (MCSP), progressively coarsening the proof structure when ATPs fail. It also borrows the proof-completion prompting strategy from RMaxTS (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5) to build its Efficient Recursive Proving (ERP) module, but avoids RMaxTS's inefficiency by first validating the reasoning path via structural analysis before expanding the search tree. The unified view in Section 2.1 provides a conceptual taxonomy that positions all existing generative-LM theorem proving methods (single-pass, proof-step, and hybrid) under a common three-step framework, motivating modular optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a dramatic sample-efficiency gain: 66.0% pass rate on curated miniF2F-test with ≤2100 model queries versus 56.1% for the previous Isabelle SOTA (SubgoalXL) requiring 16384 queries, an ~8× reduction in compute. As a plug-and-play module, ProofAug can be integrated with any tree-search algorithm and has been demonstrated on both Isabelle and Lean 4, suggesting broad applicability across proof systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to IMO-level or research-level theorems, how it interacts with expert iteration and online learning paradigms, and whether the bottom-up structural analysis generalizes to procedural proof languages (Coq, HOL Light) that lack explicit block structures.
