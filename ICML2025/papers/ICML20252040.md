## Problem

Despite the widespread use of boxplots in exploratory data analysis, no differentially private boxplot had been directly studied. A naive construction using a single private quantile algorithm for all quantiles fails: state-of-the-art algorithms like JointExp, PrivateQuantile, and ApproxQuantile are inconsistent for extreme quantiles (needed for whiskers), while the unbounded algorithm, though consistent for extremes, performs poorly on inner quantiles (needed for the box). The paper asks whether a hybrid private boxplot can simultaneously achieve optimal estimation of location and scale while remaining consistent for skewness and tails.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a univariate dataset of n real numbers, a total privacy budget ε, data bounds [a, b] (which can be set loosely), and a buffer parameter λ_n = n^{-1/4}. It outputs a 7-tuple (õ_ℓ, ℓ̃, ξ̃_{1/4}, ξ̃_{1/2}, ξ̃_{3/4}, ũ, õ_u) representing noisy lower outlier count, lower whisker, private Q1, private median, private Q3, upper whisker, and noisy upper outlier count. The privacy budget is allocated via sequential composition: 3ε/16 each to the two unbounded calls for min/max, ε/2 to a single JointExp call for the three inner quantiles simultaneously, and ε/16 each to the two Laplace-mechanism outlier counts.

## Method

DPBoxplot's core design insight is to match each boxplot component to the private quantile algorithm best suited for it: JointExp (an exponential-mechanism-based algorithm) estimates Q1, median, and Q3 jointly for the box, achieving minimax-optimal sample complexity under a mild density condition (bounded below near the quantile, bounded above everywhere); the unbounded algorithm (a sequential exponential-search procedure) estimates the extreme quantiles for whiskers, where it is proven weakly consistent while JointExp is proven inconsistent. A buffer parameter λ_n = n^{-1/4} governs whisker selection: the IQR-based whisker is retained unless the extreme quantile estimate deviates by more than λ_n × 100% in magnitude, preventing the higher-variance extreme estimates from overriding the more stable IQR-based values. Outlier counts are released via the Laplace mechanism with sensitivity 1. The paper establishes matching upper and lower bounds (up to log factors) on sample complexity for inner quantiles via a new minimax lower bound using the differentially private Fano inequality, and proves consistency of whiskers and outlyingness numbers for their population counterparts.

## Results

In simulations across five distributions (normal, skew-normal, uniform, beta, Airbnb) with ε=1 and n ranging from 1,000 to 100,000, DPBoxplot's average error in all four metrics (location, scale, skewness, tails) converges to zero and closely tracks the non-private boxplot error, indicating that sampling error dominates over privacy-induced noise. The naive boxplots built from JointExp, ApproxQuantile, or PrivateQuantile exhibit persistent inconsistency in the skewness metric (whisker error does not vanish) for skewed and uniform distributions, confirming the theoretical inconsistency result (Lemma 4.1). The naive unbounded boxplot, while consistent for extreme quantiles, shows substantially larger errors in location and scale compared to DPBoxplot. Theoretically, DPBoxplot achieves minimax-optimal sample complexity for the box (location and scale) up to logarithmic factors (Theorems 4.2 and 4.4), and the whiskers and outlyingness counts are weakly consistent (Theorem 4.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPBoxplot is compared against four naive private boxplots, each constructed by applying a single quantile algorithm to all required quantiles. Against the JointExp-naive, ApproxQuantile-naive, and PrivateQuantile-naive baselines, DPBoxplot clearly wins on skewness and tails: these three algorithms are provably inconsistent for extreme quantiles (q=1/n and q=1) when the input bounds do not exactly match the support, causing whisker error that does not vanish with increasing n. Against the unbounded-naive baseline, DPBoxplot wins on location and scale, since the unbounded algorithm performs worse than JointExp for inner quantiles (quartiles and median) on Gaussian-like data, as confirmed by the matching upper and lower bounds in Theorems 4.2 and 4.4. Overall, DPBoxplot 'retains the best of both worlds' by combining JointExp (optimal for the box) with unbounded (consistent for the whiskers), and in the Airbnb case study (n=40,738, ε=1), the private boxplots reveal the same qualitative patterns as their non-private counterparts, with discrepancies attributable to small subgroup sample sizes (e.g., only 9 shared-room listings in Staten Island).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two existing differentially private quantile algorithms—JointExp (Gillenwater et al., 2021) for inner quantiles and unbounded (Durfee, 2023) for extreme quantiles—into a novel hybrid boxplot construction, motivated by the discovery that no single algorithm handles all quantile levels needed for a boxplot. It builds on the broader DP visualization literature (private histograms, scatterplots, heatmaps) but is the first to directly study boxplots, a widely used yet previously unaddressed visualization. The theoretical contribution extends the private quantile estimation literature by proving inconsistency of JointExp/PrivateQuantile/ApproxQuantile for extreme quantiles, establishing consistency of unbounded for extreme quantiles, and providing a new minimax lower bound for private quantile estimation under general distributional assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the DP visualization toolkit by providing a theoretically grounded, practically usable private boxplot that performs comparably to its non-private counterpart. The inconsistency result for extreme quantiles is a useful negative finding that applies beyond boxplots to any application requiring private estimation of distributional tails. Key open questions include optimal privacy budget allocation for iterative/sequential exploratory workflows, extension to multivariate or high-dimensional settings, and whether the hybrid algorithm-selection strategy generalizes to other composite visualizations. The Airbnb case study demonstrates practical viability but also reveals that small subgroup sample sizes remain a fundamental challenge for private visualization.
