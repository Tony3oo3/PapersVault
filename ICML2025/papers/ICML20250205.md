## Problem

Known tractable probabilistic models (d-DNNFs, SPNs, DPPs, etc.) can all be expressed as polynomial-size uniform finally multilinear arithmetic circuits (UFMACs), raising the question of whether UFMACs are maximally expressive for tractable marginalization. This paper answers negatively: it exhibits simple functions with polynomial-time marginalization that admit no UFMAC representation, assuming FP ≠ #P, revealing a gap between tractable marginalization and known circuit-based models.

## Input / Output

The paper defines a hierarchy of three marginalization problems over functions f_n: {0,1}^n → Q. MAR(f) takes a partial assignment m ∈ {0,1,*}^n and returns the sum over all completions; HMAR(f) additionally takes a Hamming weight k and restricts the sum to assignments of weight k; VMAR(f) takes an arbitrary rational point x ∈ Q^n and returns the multilinear polynomial p evaluated there. The key structural observation is that each query level corresponds to evaluating the multilinear polynomial on an increasingly large domain ({0,1}^n ⊆ {0,1/2,1}^n ⊆ Q^n), and the classes satisfy PVM ⊆ PHM ⊆ PM with UFMAC ⊆ PVM.

## Method

The paper formalizes UFMACs as a unified circuit model and proves they support tractable Hamming weight marginalization (via a network polynomial transformation that converts a UFMAC into a circuit whose univariate evaluation encodes weight-restricted sums) and virtual evidence marginalization (via direct polynomial evaluation). To separate PM from PHM, the authors construct a function f_aff built from width-3 affine (XOR) constraints and use CSP dichotomy theorems: #CSP is tractable for affine relations (giving f_aff ∈ PM via Gaussian elimination), while #k-ONES is #P-hard for width-3 affine relations (giving HMAR(f_aff) #P-hard via a reduction that encodes width-3 formulas as evidence and uses balancing variables to track Hamming weight). Finally, a completeness result shows that in the real RAM model, any polynomial-time algorithm for VMAR(f) implies the existence of a real RAM UFMAC for f, establishing UFMACs as complete for virtual evidence marginalization.

## Results

The paper establishes a strict hierarchy of tractable marginalization classes: UFMAC ⊆ PVM ⊆ PHM ⊆ PM ⊆ FP, and proves that UFMAC ⊊ PM (assuming FP ≠ #P) by exhibiting the function f_aff which is tractable for standard variable marginalization (via Gaussian elimination on affine constraints) but #P-hard for Hamming weight marginalization (via reduction from #k-ONES on width-3 affine relations). Additionally, the paper proves a completeness result: in the real RAM model, UFMAC = PVM, meaning any function with a polynomial-time real RAM for virtual evidence marginalization necessarily has a uniform finally multilinear arithmetic circuit. As a corollary, d-DNNF is strictly less succinct than EADT (unless PH = Σ₂).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, datasets, or quantitative performance comparisons. The 'comparisons' are between complexity classes and representation languages: UFMAC (the unified circuit model encompassing DPPs, PGCs, SPNs, d-DNNF, etc.) is shown to be strictly contained in PM, meaning known tractable models do not capture all functions with tractable marginalization. The separating function f_aff is representable in EADT/AFF (polynomial size) but not in d-DNNF (which would imply PH = Σ₂), partially resolving an open succinctness question from Koriche et al. (2013). The paper also positions UFMAC as complete for virtual evidence marginalization in the real RAM model, generalizing known algorithms for bounded-treewidth Bayesian networks and syntactically multilinear circuits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the unified circuit framework of Broadrick et al. (2024b), which showed that diverse tractable probabilistic models (DPPs, SPNs, d-DNNFs, PGCs) can be expressed as uniform finally multilinear arithmetic circuits (UFMACs). It departs from that line by asking whether UFMACs are *complete* for tractable marginalization, and answers negatively by introducing a hierarchy of stronger marginalization tasks (Hamming weight, virtual evidence) and using CSP dichotomy theorems (Creignou & Hermann 1996; Creignou et al. 2010) to construct a separating function. The real RAM completeness proof additionally relies on Strassen's (1973) division-elimination result to convert real RAM arithmetic into circuit form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative answer to the central question—that known tractable models do not characterize all functions with tractable marginalization—suggests room for yet more expressive-efficient tractable models, reframing the design space for probabilistic inference. The completeness result (UFMACs are complete for virtual evidence marginalization in the real RAM model) provides a clean positive characterization of exactly what UFMACs can do, which is useful for understanding the boundary of current tractable inference. Two open questions remain: whether the PVM ⊆ PHM inclusion is strict, and whether sequential (P-hard) marginalization problems exist, both of which could further refine the complexity landscape for tractable inference.
