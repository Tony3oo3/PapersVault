## Problem

Multi-Instance Learning for Whole Slide Image classification requires processing bags of variable length (hundreds to thousands of patch instances), which cannot be stacked into uniform mini-batches. This non-stackability forces sequential single-bag processing, causing prohibitively long training times and unstable gradient estimates from non-IID single-bag statistics. No prior method achieves lossless batch processing of variable-length MIL data while simultaneously improving both speed and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes pre-extracted WSI patch feature vectors organized as variable-length bags (e.g., 512-dim ResNet-50, 1024-dim DS-MIL multiscale, or 1536-dim UNI2 features), where each bag contains an arbitrary number of instances with a single bag-level binary label. Rather than stacking raw data, DPGS distributes bags across parallel sub-processes that each produce a gradient tensor; the system outputs a bag-level class prediction alongside compressed gradient tensors and compressed weight deltas transmitted between training nodes and the master server.

## Method

DPGS assigns variable-length bags to P parallel sub-processes that independently compute forward/backward passes and send their gradients to a master node, which averages them to produce a global update—proven mathematically equivalent to standard mini-batch training with mean loss. DMGC (Deep Model-Gradient Compression) layers dual sparsity on top: top-k gradient sparsification with local error-feedback accumulation (sub-threshold residuals are retained and added to the next round's gradient), and weight-delta compression that transmits only the parameter change rather than full weights. This joint compression achieves 99.2% communication reduction at a 99.99% gradient discard rate while preserving convergence, and the multi-round local accumulation creates a virtual batch of size P·b·T that naturally satisfies linear learning-rate scaling rules without manual adjustment.

## Results

On TCGA-Lung Multiscale, DPGS+DMGC accelerates ABMIL convergence by 31.8× (636.3 s → 20.0 s) while improving accuracy from 92.38% to 93.90%. On Camelyon16-ImageNet-Pretrain, ACMIL gains 6.40% accuracy (81.31% → 87.71%) with a 44.7× speedup (2735.3 s → 61.2 s). Across all six MIL backbones and three datasets, DPGS+DMGC consistently outperforms the Classic, Padding, and Sampling baselines in both accuracy and convergence time. DMGC further reduces communication by up to 99.2% (117.5× compression ratio) at a 99.99% gradient discard rate while maintaining identical accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPGS+DMGC dominates all three stacking baselines (Classic, Padding, Sampling) on every dataset and MIL backbone. Against Classic (sequential single-bag processing), DPGS+DMGC achieves 3–45× faster convergence and 0.5–9.3% higher accuracy; for example, TransMIL on C16-Multiscale improves from 93.80% to 95.23% while cutting time from 304 s to 115 s. Against Padding, DPGS+DMGC is 3–8× faster (e.g., ABMIL on C16-ImageNet: 9040 s → 92 s) and matches or exceeds accuracy, whereas Padding's zero-filling inflates computation and destabilizes convergence. Against Sampling, DPGS+DMGC recovers the accuracy lost by random instance dropping (e.g., TransMIL on C16-Multiscale: 89.93% → 95.23%) while remaining 20–200× faster. For gradient compression, DMGC outperforms DGC (Lin et al. 2017) by 1.73× in convergence speed under 20 Mbps bandwidth (ABMIL: 142 s vs. 249 s) with identical accuracy (93.83%), and achieves a 117.5× communication compression ratio at 0.01% keep rate versus DGC's single-gradient sparsification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Gradient Compression (DGC) framework of Lin et al. (2017) into DMGC, which additionally compresses weight deltas rather than full parameters, and couples it with a novel gradient-stacking mechanism (DPGS) that resolves the fundamental incompatibility between variable-length MIL bags and standard mini-batch training. It departs from prior distributed training systems (DistBelief, Megatron-LM, DeepSpeed) by arguing their batch-uniformity assumptions are structurally misaligned with MIL's heterogeneous bag lengths, and from prior MIL methods (ABMIL, TransMIL, CLAM) by treating them as frozen architectures whose training loop is replaced rather than modified. The theoretical equivalence proof between gradient stacking and mini-batch averaging positions DPGS as a drop-in replacement for standard SGD in MIL pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31× training speedup and simultaneous accuracy gains (up to 9.3%) address a practical bottleneck that has constrained WSI analysis to single-bag sequential processing, making large-scale histopathology training feasible on modest hardware. The virtual-batch equivalence result (Section 3.3.1) provides a clean theoretical justification for why gradient accumulation under DMGC preserves the linear learning-rate scaling rule, reducing the need for ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the synchronous design's communication plateau (observed beyond 4 nodes at 1% keep rate) can be overcome via asynchronous updates, whether the approach generalizes to other variable-length structured data beyond WSI, and how performance scales with the increasingly large foundation-model feature extractors (e.g., UNI2) that produce higher-dimensional instance representations.
