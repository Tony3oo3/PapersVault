## Problem

Existing theoretical results establishing minimax-optimal nonparametric regression risk rates for over-parameterized neural networks trained by gradient descent require specific distributional assumptions on the covariate, such as spherical uniform distribution or restrictive density conditions. This paper asks whether sharp risk rates can be achieved without any distributional assumption on the covariate, as long as it lies on the unit sphere, and provides a confirmative answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252841/cropped_page12_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs consisting of feature vectors on the unit sphere S^{d-1} (drawn i.i.d. from an arbitrary continuous distribution P) and noisy response labels y_i = f*(x_i) + w_i where w_i are i.i.d. sub-Gaussian with variance proxy σ_0². The target function f* is assumed to lie in the RKHS ball H_K(μ_0) of the ReLU NTK. The output is a trained two-layer ReLU network function f_t at step t of GD, with the theoretical guarantee being a population risk bound E_P[(f_t - f*)²] ≲ ε_n², where ε_n is the critical population rate of the NTK, and a characterization of the early-stopping time T̂ ≍ ε_n^{-2}.

## Method

The paper trains a two-layer ReLU network (only the first layer is optimized; second-layer weights are randomly initialized and fixed) by GD with a constant learning rate η ∈ [1,2) and early stopping at T̂ ≍ ε_n^{-2}. The key technical novelty is a new decomposition of the network function at any GD step as f_t = h_t + e_t, where h_t belongs to the RKHS H_K with bounded norm and e_t has a small L^∞ norm controlled by the network width m, replacing the common f_t − f* = (f_t − f_t^{NTK}) + (f_t^{NTK} − f*) decomposition that necessitates distributional assumptions. Uniform convergence of the finite-width network to the NTK is established throughout training (not just at initialization), and local Rademacher complexity is employed to tightly bound the Rademacher complexity of the function class comprising all possible network functions reachable by GD. The network width lower bound m ≳ d^{8/3}/ε_n^{80/3} depends only on d and ε_n, avoiding the need to lower-bound the minimum eigenvalue of the empirical NTK matrix, which in prior works required additional data distribution assumptions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) establishes that an over-parameterized two-layer ReLU network trained by GD with early stopping achieves a nonparametric regression risk of O(ε_n²), where ε_n is the critical population rate of the NTK, with no distributional assumption on the spherical covariate. Under the polynomial eigenvalue decay rate λ_j ≍ j^{-d/(d-1)}, this yields the minimax optimal rate O(n^{-d/(2d-1)}). The network width requirement is m ≳ d^{8/3}/ε_n^{80/3} (or m ≳ n^{80α/(3(2α+1))} d^{8/3} under polynomial EDR), which depends only on d and n rather than the minimum eigenvalue of the empirical NTK matrix. The stopping time is characterized as T̂ ≍ ε_n^{-2} ≍ n^{d/(2d-1)}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper achieves the same minimax optimal risk rate O(n^{-d/(2d-1)}) as Hu et al. (2021), Suh et al. (2022), and Li et al. (2024), but without any distributional assumption on the covariate (only requiring it to lie on the unit sphere), whereas Hu and Suh require the spherical uniform distribution and Li et al. require a restrictive polynomial decay condition on the density. Compared to Kuzborskij & Szepesvári (2021), which also requires no distributional assumption, this paper achieves a strictly better (minimax optimal) rate by eliminating the σ₀² noise floor term and the slow O(n^{-2/(2+d)}) dimension-dependent term. The network width lower bound m ≳ n^{80α/(3(2α+1))} d^{8/3} is substantially smaller than the m ≳ n^{24}(log m)^{12} implied by Li et al. (2024) and the m/log³m ≳ 2^{20}n^{24} required by Suh et al. (2022) for a two-layer network. Additionally, this work uses a constant learning rate η = Θ(1) in GD, whereas Li et al. (2024) requires gradient flow (η→0) and Hu et al. (2021) requires learning rates of order o(n^{-(3d-1)/(2d-1)}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel regression analysis of Raskutti et al. (2014) to over-parameterized two-layer neural networks trained by GD with early stopping, showing the NN achieves the same sharp O(ε_n²) risk rate as the classical kernel method. It departs from prior NN-based nonparametric regression works (Hu et al. 2021, Suh et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024) by eliminating all distributional assumptions on the spherical covariate, using a novel decomposition f_t = h_t + e_t (RKHS component plus small L∞ error) instead of the standard comparison to the NTK kernel regressor. The proof leverages local Rademacher complexity (Bartlett et al. 2005) to tightly bound the function class of all GD-iterates, and adapts uniform convergence results from Yang & Li (2025) for the NTK approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sharp nonparametric regression risk rate for over-parameterized neural networks trained by GD that requires no distributional assumptions on the covariate beyond lying on the unit sphere, filling a gap between kernel regression theory and finite-width NN analysis. The result also resolves open questions about the stopping time characterization (T̂ ≍ ε_n^{-2}), provides a much smaller width lower bound depending only on d and ε_n (rather than the minimum eigenvalue of the empirical NTK), and shows a constant learning rate η = Θ(1) suffices. Limitations include the requirement that the target function lies in the RKHS ball H_K(μ_0), the restriction to spherical input space, and the fact that only the first layer is trained. Open questions include extending the distribution-free analysis to bounded (non-spherical) input spaces and to deeper networks with all layers trained.
