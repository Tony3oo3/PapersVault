## Problem

Diffusion-based re-ranking methods for instance retrieval suffer from two coupled failures: positive similarity signals decay over multiple propagation steps, reducing discriminative power beyond local neighborhoods, and errors in the k-nearest-neighbor graph construction propagate throughout the diffusion process. Existing manifold ranking approaches lack a principled mechanism to bridge distant instances while preserving the reliability of local neighborhood information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251666/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-processing re-ranking step on pre-extracted d-dimensional global image features (from models such as R-GeM, DOLG, or CVNet) for a query and a gallery set of up to 1M images. It produces a re-ranked gallery list by computing a final distance that is a weighted combination (controlled by θ) of the original Euclidean feature distance and a manifold-aware thermodynamic transition cost. Only the top-k candidates (e.g., k=5000) are re-ranked, keeping execution time under 3 seconds.

## Method

LPMT comprises three stages. First, Bidirectional Collaborative Diffusion (BCD) extends a reference k-NN graph into a set of m graphs with varying connectivity (k scaled by [1/√2, 1, √2]) and jointly optimizes both the smoothed similarity matrix F and the graph combination weights β via an alternating fixed-point scheme with a closed-form β update derived from KKT conditions. Second, Locality State Embedding (LSE) maps each instance to an n-dimensional probability distribution over the graph nodes using k-reciprocal neighborhoods and the BCD similarity matrix, emphasizing reciprocal neighbors via a parameter κ to enforce local consistency. Third, Thermodynamic Markovian Transition (TMT) defines a multi-state stochastic process governed by the master equation, where two distant distributions are connected through a sequence of intermediate states each confined to a local region; the minimum transition flow cost (equivalent to a sum of 1-Wasserstein distances along the path) serves as the manifold distance, which is then fused with the Euclidean distance for final ranking.

## Results

Using R-GeM features, LPMT achieves mAP of 84.7% on ROxf (Medium) and 67.8% on ROxf (Hard), outperforming the previous best method CAS by 4.0% and 3.0% respectively. On RPar, LPMT reaches 93.0% (Medium) and 84.1% (Hard), surpassing CAS by 2.0% and 3.4%. The method also delivers consistent gains on the large-scale +1M variants (e.g., 64.8% vs. 61.6% on ROxf+1M Medium) and with alternative feature extractors DOLG and CVNet, where it improves ROxf Hard from 62.6% to 78.2% (DOLG) and from 62.1% to 75.2% (CVNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPMT consistently outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every dataset and protocol. Against the strongest prior method CAS (Luo et al., ICML 2024), LPMT gains +4.0 mAP on ROxf Medium (84.7 vs. 80.7), +3.0 on ROxf Hard (67.8 vs. 64.8), +2.0 on RPar Medium (93.0 vs. 91.0), and +3.4 on RPar Hard (84.1 vs. 80.7). Among query expansion methods, the best (LAttDBA+LAttQE) reaches only 74.0/54.1 on ROxf M/H, trailing LPMT by 10.7/13.7 points. Diffusion-based methods (DFS, RDP, EIR, EGT) cluster around 72–80 mAP on ROxf Medium, all well below LPMT's 84.7. Context-based (STML, ConAff) and learning-based (GSS, SSR, CSA) methods similarly fall 4–10 points short. The advantage is most pronounced under the Hard protocol and on the +1M distractor settings, where LPMT's locality-preserving transition maintains discriminative power that other methods lose over longer propagation paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPMT extends the diffusion-based re-ranking lineage (Zhou et al. 2003; Iscen et al. 2017; Bai et al. 2019a; Luo et al. 2024) by replacing point-based similarity propagation with a distribution-based thermodynamic transition. The BCD component generalizes Bidirectional Similarity Diffusion (Bai et al. 2019a; Luo et al. 2024) from a single graph to an automatically weighted ensemble of multi-scale graphs, while the TMT component imports the master equation from stochastic thermodynamics (Seifert 2012; Van Vu & Saito 2023) and connects it to Wasserstein optimal transport as a computable equivalent. The k-reciprocal encoding strategy (Zhong et al. 2017) is repurposed within LSE to define local regions for distribution construction, bridging context-based re-ranking with the thermodynamic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPMT addresses a well-identified failure mode of diffusion-based re-ranking—positive signal decay beyond local neighborhoods—by constraining each transition step to a local region and using multi-state paths to bridge distant instances. The thermodynamic framing provides a principled, entropy-linked distance metric that outperforms total variation and other standard distributional distances by 5–7% in ablations, suggesting the manifold geometry is better captured through flow dynamics than static divergence measures. However, the O(n³) complexity (even reduced to O(k³) for top-k re-ranking) remains a practical barrier for very large-scale databases, and the method's dependence on a pre-trained feature extractor limits its applicability in end-to-end learning pipelines. Open questions include whether the thermodynamic transition cost can be approximated more efficiently at scale and whether the multi-state path formulation generalizes to other retrieval or clustering tasks.
