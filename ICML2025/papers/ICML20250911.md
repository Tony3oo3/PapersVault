## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for Contrastive Representation Learning (CRL) assume that input tuples are drawn i.i.d. from a tuple distribution, but practical training is limited to a fixed pool of labeled data points that must be recycled across tuples to create sufficient training pairs. This recycling invalidates the tuplewise independence condition, leaving a gap between theory and practice. The paper provides the first generalization analysis for CRL under this non-i.i.d. regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed pool of N labeled data points (x_j, y_j) drawn i.i.d. from a joint distribution over X × C, a bounded contrastive loss ℓ: R^k → [0, M] (e.g., N-pair/logistic loss), and a class of representation functions F: X → R^d. Tuples are constructed by selecting anchor-positive pairs from the same class and k negatives from other classes, with data points reused across tuples; training then evaluates the empirical risk over M sub-sampled tuples drawn from this pool. The output is a high-probability excess risk bound of order O(1/√Ñ) for the full U-Statistics minimizer and O(1/√Ñ + 1/√M) for the sub-sampled minimizer, where Ñ scales as N/|C| for small k and N/k for large k under balanced class distributions.

## Method

The core technical contribution is formulating the population unsupervised risk as a weighted combination of class-wise U-Statistics, where each class's U-Statistics averages the contrastive loss over all valid tuples whose anchor-positive pair belongs to that class. The non-i.i.d. dependence structure is handled via the U-Statistics decoupling technique (de la Peña & Giné), which rewrites each class-wise U-Statistics as an average over independent blocks formed by permuting and pairing 2-element blocks from the class with k-element blocks from the complement, enabling application of McDiarmid-type concentration inequalities. The generalization gap is decomposed into two deviations: between the sub-sampled empirical risk and the U-Statistics (controlled by the Rademacher complexity of the loss class ℓ∘F restricted to the tuple set), and between the U-Statistics and the population risk (controlled by concentration of the decoupled blocks). The resulting bounds show that under balanced class distributions, sample complexity scales as O(|C|/N) for small k and O(k/N) for large k, and the sub-sampling regime adds only an O(1/M) penalty, confirming that increasing the number of recycled tuples recovers the full U-Statistics performance. The results are instantiated for linear maps (constrained by spectral norm and 2,1-norm) and deep neural networks (constrained by layer-wise spectral norms and Lipschitz activations) via the Dudley entropy integral on L2-covering numbers.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical generalization bounds rather than empirical accuracy numbers. Theorem 5.1 shows the excess risk of the U-Statistics minimizer scales as O(1/√Ñ) where Ñ = N·min(ρ_min/2, (1−ρ_max)/k), yielding O(√(|C|/N)) for small k and O(√(k/N)) for large k under balanced classes. Theorem 5.2 adds an O(1/√M) term for the sub-sampled regime, showing the gap between sub-sampled and full U-Statistics minimizers vanishes as M grows. Empirically on MNIST (n=100 and n=10000), sub-sampled (non-i.i.d.) tuples consistently outperform independent tuples, with the advantage most pronounced at n=100 where recycling data rapidly surpasses the i.i.d. regime. Synthetic experiments confirm linear scaling of sample complexity with both k and |C|, matching the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there are no traditional method-vs-method accuracy comparisons. Instead, the paper compares three training regimes on MNIST: (1) independent (disjoint) tuples, (2) sub-sampled non-i.i.d. tuples with recycled data, and (3) all possible valid tuples. The sub-sampled regime outperforms the independent-tuple regime on both supervised (linear classifier on learned representations) and unsupervised (contrastive loss) metrics, with the gap being largest at n=100 labeled examples where data recycling is most beneficial. At n=100, the sub-sampled regime closely approximates the all-tuples regime, empirically validating Theorem 5.2's prediction that the performance gap vanishes as M increases. Theoretically, the paper's bounds for balanced classes behave similarly to prior i.i.d. results (Arora et al. 2019; Lei et al. 2023; Hieu et al. 2024) when expressed in terms of total labeled data points N rather than tuple count n, though the paper notes a square-root dependency on |C| for small k that prior works avoid by assuming i.i.d. tuples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CRL generalization analysis lineage initiated by Arora et al. (2019) and sharpened by Lei et al. (2023), which both assumed i.i.d. input tuples. The key departure is recognizing that practical CRL training recycles data from a fixed labeled pool, invalidating tuple independence. To handle this, the authors import U-Statistics decoupling techniques from de la Peña & Giné (1998) and Clémençon et al. (2008), adapting the order-2 bipartite ranking framework to the (k+2)-order structure of contrastive tuples with class-conditional validity constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a gap between CRL theory and practice by providing the first generalization bounds under the non-i.i.d. data-recycling regime that dominates real-world training. However, the bound carries a square-root dependency on the number of classes |C| (via ρ_min), which the authors acknowledge is potentially overly pessimistic for long-tail distributions; they conjecture a joint-concentration U-Statistics formulation could resolve this but identify it as a major open technical challenge. The sub-sampling result (Theorem 5.2) provides a practical justification for increasing the number of recycled tuples when the labeled pool is large, though empirical validation is limited to MNIST and synthetic Gaussian data.
