## Problem

Non-contrastive self-supervised learning is prone to four distinct failure modes—representation, dimensional, cluster, and intracluster collapses—that existing heuristic strategies (momentum encoders, stop gradients, asymmetric projectors) do not universally prevent. The paper asks whether there exist sufficient conditions on the loss function and projector design that provably avoid all four collapses simultaneously, without relying on ad-hoc tricks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of randomly augmented RGB images (e.g., 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces, through a backbone encoder (ResNet-8/18 or ViT-small) followed by a two-layer projector, (i) normalized f-dimensional embeddings and (ii) a probability vector of length c over a frozen dictionary of Rademacher-distributed codes. The temperature τ of the softmax is not a free hyperparameter but is analytically fixed as τ = f/(√n · log((1−ε(c−1))/ε)), and the dictionary size c is deliberately chosen much larger than the embedding dimension f (e.g., c = 16384 with f = 128) to exploit quasi-orthogonality from hyperdimensional computing.

## Method

CPLearn combines a two-term loss—cross-entropy between augmented-view code probabilities (invariance) and cross-entropy between a uniform prior and the batch-mean code distribution (prior matching)—with a projector that maps backbone representations through a linear layer, batch normalization, L2-norm (or tanh), and a temperature-scaled softmax over a frozen f×c Rademacher dictionary W. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that at the joint minimum of the loss, each embedding aligns with a single dictionary code up to a vanishing spurious term, which yields a diagonal embedding covariance (decorrelation, preventing dimensional collapse) and a block-diagonal adjacency matrix (clustering, preventing intracluster collapse), while the matched-prior condition ensures all codes are used (preventing cluster collapse) and the hard assignment prevents representation collapse. The quasi-orthogonality of W (W^T W ≈ fI in expectation) is inherited from hyperdimensional computing, allowing c ≫ f without requiring an orthogonal basis, and the large dictionary size monotonically improves both clustering NMI and linear-probing accuracy across all tested datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CPLearn with an overcomplete dictionary (c=16384) achieves 0.35 NMI for clustering and 67% linear probing accuracy, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. On CIFAR-100, CPLearn(16384) reaches 0.59 NMI and 40% accuracy, versus 0.28 NMI and 39% for GEDI-no-gen (best clustering baseline) and 28% for Barlow Twins (best linear baseline). On SVHN, CPLearn(16384) scores 0.29 NMI and 75% accuracy, matching Barlow Twins' 76% linear accuracy while far exceeding its 0.06 NMI clustering score. The method is the only one to systematically improve both tasks by increasing dictionary size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPLearn(16384) wins on both clustering NMI and linear probing accuracy across all three datasets, beating the strongest baselines by wide margins: +0.23 NMI over GEDI-no-gen on CIFAR-100 clustering, +12 points over Barlow Twins on CIFAR-100 linear accuracy, and +0.24 NMI over Self-Classifier(16384) on SVHN clustering. Barlow Twins is the strongest linear-probing baseline (76% on SVHN, 65% on CIFAR-10) but is the weakest on clustering (at most 0.10 NMI everywhere). SwAV is consistently the worst performer on both metrics. GEDI-no-gen is the closest clustering competitor (0.33 NMI on CIFAR-10) but trails CPLearn by 0.02 NMI and 1 point on linear accuracy. Self-Classifier(16384) is highly unstable (variance of 0.18-0.21), suggesting the overcomplete dictionary setting is not well-suited to its Sinkhorn-based design, whereas CPLearn benefits monotonically from larger c.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPLearn unifies two previously separate families of non-contrastive SSL—feature decorrelation (Barlow Twins, VICReg) and cluster-based methods (SwAV, DINO)—into a single objective and projector design, departing from the standard reliance on heuristics like momentum encoders, stop-gradient, and asymmetric heads. The loss function (cross-entropy invariance plus KL prior matching) builds directly on the author's earlier GEDI work (Sansone 2023), but here the generative term is dropped and the invariance-plus-prior-matching pair is shown to be jointly sufficient for collapse avoidance. The projector's frozen Rademacher dictionary introduces the first formal connection between SSL and hyperdimensional computing (Kanerva 2009), replacing learned prototypes with quasi-orthogonal bipolar codes whose expected inner products satisfy W^T W = fI on average.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principal contribution is a provable, heuristic-free training recipe: if the loss is minimized and the projector uses a large frozen Rademacher dictionary, all four collapse modes are avoided by construction, and embeddings are simultaneously decorrelated and clustered without explicit covariance computation or Sinkhorn layers. This simplifies the training pipeline (no EMA teacher, no stop-grad, no centering) and provides a certificate (the loss lower bound) for collapse avoidance. Key limitations include the storage cost of large dictionaries (c=16384+), the assumption of infinite backbone capacity in the theory, and the fact that the HC compositional properties remain unexploited. Open questions include whether the bipolar code structure can be leveraged for efficient storage and whether the framework extends to non-uniform priors or compositional reasoning tasks.
