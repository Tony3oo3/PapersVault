## Problem

Interpretable-by-design models that rely on concept embeddings (e.g., IP-OMP, CBMs) suffer non-vanishing performance degradation when those embeddings deviate from ground-truth latent features, a situation that is the norm when embeddings come from pretrained models like CLIP. Existing remedies introduce additional black-box learning modules that undermine the interpretability guarantee, and no prior work provides theoretical convergence guarantees for refining concept embeddings while preserving interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet/CUB-200/Places365) and a fixed set of n textual concepts (generated by GPT-3) as input. Both images and concepts are projected into a shared CLIP latent space; the method then produces a predicted class label, a sparse concept code vector s (obtained via hard-thresholding of D^T x rather than exact top-k selection, enabling parallelization), and the refined concept embedding matrix D^(T). A tunable constraint radius ρ (fixed at 0.1 in experiments) governs how far refined embeddings may deviate from their initial CLIP values, directly trading off accuracy against interpretability.

## Method

CCR optimizes concept embeddings via gradient descent on a differentiable surrogate of the IP-OMP estimator, subject to a column-wise ℓ2-norm constraint ‖ΔD‖_{1,2} ≤ ρ that keeps refined embeddings in a small neighborhood of their initialization. The surrogate exploits the closed-form IP-OMP solution under column-orthogonality (sum of top-k inner products), and a concept dispersion step is applied to CLIP embeddings to approximate the required orthogonality. For image classification, a linear classifier is trained on the sparse codes with cross-entropy loss, and each gradient step is followed by normalization and projection onto the constraint ball. Theoretically, under a sparse generative model, the paper proves CCR achieves zero population loss while the embedding matrix converges geometrically to the ground-truth latent feature matrix, simultaneously improving both accuracy and interpretability. Empirically, CCR outperforms CLIP-IP-OMP and lf-CBM on four of five benchmarks with roughly a 10× reduction in training time (≈2 h vs. ≈33 h on ImageNet).

## Results

CCR outperforms both CLIP-IP-OMP and lf-CBM on four of five benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, Places365), with the sole exception being CUB-200 where lf-CBM benefits from a ResNet-18 backbone specifically trained on that dataset. On ImageNet (~1.2M images), CCR achieves higher accuracy than CLIP-IP-OMP (k≈50) while reducing training time from ~33 hours to ~2 hours (a ~16× speedup), and from ~50 hours for lf-CBM. The average concept embedding deviation (ACED) remains small (0.041–0.097) across all datasets, confirming that interpretability is preserved. CCR also consistently outperforms its own baseline (no concept refinement, η_D=0) across all benchmarks, validating the necessity of the constrained refinement step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP-IP-OMP (Chattopadhyay et al., 2024), CCR wins on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Places365 in prediction accuracy while using the same CLIP encoder and GPT-3-generated concept set. The computational advantage is dramatic: on ImageNet, CLIP-IP-OMP requires ~33 hours (k=50) or ~6 hours (k=10, dropping accuracy to ~63%), whereas CCR completes in ~2 hours at k≈49 with higher accuracy. Against lf-CBM (Oikarinen et al., 2023), CCR wins on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Places365, but loses on CUB-200 where lf-CBM's ResNet-18 backbone (trained specifically on CUB-200) gives it an advantage over the general-purpose CLIP encoder used by CCR. The CCR baseline without concept refinement (η_D=0) is consistently outperformed by full CCR across all five datasets, confirming that the constrained embedding optimization is the key driver of the accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the IP-OMP framework of Chattopadhyay et al. (2024), which established a connection between Information Pursuit and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit for interpretable prediction. The key departure is showing that the exact-knowledge assumption on latent feature vectors (Assumption 2.4) is violated in practice by CLIP-generated embeddings, and proposing Constrained Concept Refinement (CCR) to correct this within a bounded neighborhood. Methodologically, CCR draws on dictionary learning (Mairal et al., 2011) and unrolled optimization, but distinguishes itself by imposing a hard constraint on embedding deviation to preserve interpretability, rather than introducing additional black-box modules as prior CBM extensions do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCR provides the first convergence guarantee for updating concept embeddings in an explainable AI setting, showing that zero training loss and progressive interpretability improvement can be achieved simultaneously under a generative model. The ~10× computational speedup over CLIP-IP-OMP on ImageNet (2 hours vs. 33 hours) makes interpretable classification more practical at scale. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (column-orthogonal ground-truth dictionary, full-rank, specific generative model), the hyperparameter ρ controlling the accuracy-interpretability trade-off remains underexplored, and the framework is currently tied to the IP-OMP architecture, leaving open whether similar constrained refinement applies to other interpretable-by-design models.
