## Problem

In operator learning for PDEs, passive (i.i.d.) data collection strategies are limited to a convergence rate of at most ~1/n, and the paper shows this rate is in fact a non-vanishing lower bound regardless of the covariance kernel's eigenvalue decay. The paper asks whether active data collection—where the learner chooses which input functions to query—can break this barrier and achieve arbitrarily fast error convergence for linear solution operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252268/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives a continuous covariance kernel K defining a family of centered distributions over L²(𝒳), and has black-box access to the solution operator 𝓕 through an ε-approximate oracle (e.g., a PDE solver with discretization error ε). The output is a finite-rank linear operator F̂_n = Σᵢ wᵢ ⊗ φᵢ that maps any input function v to an approximate solution F̂_n(v), with error measured in expectation under the L²-Bochner norm over the distribution family 𝒫(K).

## Method

The key insight is to use the Karhunen-Loève decomposition: the learner computes the eigenfunctions {φᵢ} of the covariance kernel K via the Fredholm integral equation, then actively selects the first n eigenfunctions as source terms (membership query model) and queries the oracle to obtain wᵢ = 𝒪(φᵢ). The estimator is the natural least-squares solution F̂_n = Σᵢ wᵢ ⊗ φᵢ, which is well-defined because the φᵢ are orthonormal. The proof decomposes the risk into an irreducible term ε²Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ λᵢ (oracle error) and a reducible term ‖𝓕‖²_op Σᵢ>ₙ λᵢ (truncation error), showing the latter vanishes at a rate determined by eigenvalue decay—enabling arbitrarily fast polynomial or even exponential rates for kernels like the fractional inverse Laplacian or RBF. Complementarily, the paper constructs a sparse distribution (rare large spikes along eigenfunction directions) that yields a non-vanishing lower bound ‖𝓕‖²_op λ₁/2 for any passive i.i.d. strategy, establishing a fundamental separation between active and passive learning.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that the active linear estimator achieves error bounded by ε²Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ λᵢ + ‖F‖²_op Σᵢ>ₙ λᵢ, where λᵢ are eigenvalues of the covariance kernel. For the fractional inverse of shifted Laplacian kernel with 2γ > d, the reducible error decays at rate n^{-(2γ/d - 1)}, which can be made arbitrarily fast by choosing large γ (e.g., n^{-1.5} for 2D Navier-Stokes parameters, n^{-3} for 1D Burgers). In contrast, Theorem 4.2 proves a non-vanishing lower bound of (1/2)‖F‖²_op λ₁ for any passive strategy, regardless of eigenvalue decay. Empirically, on both Poisson and Heat equations (64×64 grid, 100 test samples), the active linear estimator consistently outperforms both the passive linear estimator and FNO across all sample sizes, with the gap widening as n grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed active linear estimator against two baselines: (1) a passive linear (least-squares) estimator trained on i.i.d. samples, and (2) the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) trained on passive data. On the Poisson equation (Figure 1–2), the active linear estimator achieves substantially lower mean-squared relative error than both the passive linear estimator and FNO at every sample size, with the advantage growing as n increases — consistent with the theoretical n^{-1.5} rate versus the passive 1/n rate. On the Heat equation (Figure 4), the same pattern holds: the active linear estimator converges faster than both passive baselines. Notably, FNO trained on actively collected data performs poorly (Figures 5–6 in Appendix), suggesting that the benefit of active data collection is specific to the linear estimator that exploits the eigenfunction structure. The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 4.2) further establishes that no passive strategy can achieve a vanishing error, providing a fundamental separation between active and passive approaches that is not captured by the empirical comparisons alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the operator learning framework established by Kovachki et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2021), which study learning PDE solution operators from i.i.d. input-output pairs under the L^p-Bochner norm. The key departure is replacing the passive (i.i.d.) sampling paradigm with an active membership-query strategy that exploits the Karhunen-Loève decomposition of the input distribution's covariance kernel. Unlike Kovachki et al. (2024a), whose active analysis still yields only n^{-1/2} Monte Carlo rates via standard ERM, and Boullé et al. (2023), who require Hilbert-Schmidt norm control and Green's function structure, this work achieves arbitrarily fast polynomial or exponential rates using only the operator norm and black-box oracle access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-vanishing lower bound for passive learning (Theorem 4.2) is a strong negative result: no amount of i.i.d. data can overcome the fundamental information bottleneck when the input distribution has sparse eigenfunction support. This reframes data collection as a first-class design choice in operator learning rather than a fixed sampling procedure. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear operators (e.g., via RKHS of operators or PCANet architectures), handling unknown covariance kernels, and whether the active strategy's advantage persists under distribution shift between training and deployment. The practical scope is currently limited to linear operators with known kernels, though the Poisson and Heat equation experiments validate the theory in standard PDE settings.
