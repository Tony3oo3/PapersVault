## Problem

Imitation from Observation (IfO) methods for cross-dynamics RL assume that expert state distributions are similar across different environment dynamics, but this breaks down when certain states become inaccessible under some dynamics (e.g., high-speed driving states in dense traffic). Blindly imitating such inaccessible states misleads the learning policy, and standard imitation learning also imposes a performance ceiling and requires optimal demonstrations, limiting applicability to general reward-based RL settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations from multiple dynamics (vector states in MuJoCo/MetaDrive, or multi-scale CNN-processed map observations in the fall-guys game), environment reward signals, and optionally expert state trajectories. It produces a policy (action distribution over discrete or continuous action spaces) and an augmented reward signal where log(discriminator output) is added to the environment reward at each timestep. The method is designed as a plug-in module: it operates on top of existing RL algorithms (PPO, MAPLE, ESCP) by modifying only the reward computation step, requiring no changes to the base algorithm's architecture or update rules.

## Method

ASOR restricts state distribution imitation to 'globally accessible states'—states with nonzero visitation probability under all considered dynamics—formalized via an F-distance regularized policy optimization constraint. Theoretically, instantiating the F-distance with JS divergence (infinite samples) or network distance (finite samples) yields performance lower bounds with linear dependence on the effective horizon 1/(1−γ), tighter than the quadratic bounds of prior imitation learning analyses. Practically, the F-distance is estimated via a GAN-like discriminator trained to classify a subset of high-value, high-visit states (D_P, selected using state values and pseudo-counts from ensemble disagreement or RND) against remaining states (D_Q). The trained discriminator's log-output is then added to the environment reward as an augmentation term, transforming the constrained optimization into a standard reward-maximization problem solvable by any existing RL algorithm. This reward augmentation mechanism makes ASOR a general add-on module applicable to both offline and online cross-dynamics RL pipelines.

## Results

On offline MuJoCo benchmarks (D4RL format with 3 dynamics), MAPLE+ASOR achieves the highest average score of 0.45, outperforming MAPLE+SRPO (0.40), MAPLE+DARA (0.37), MAPLE (0.36), CQL (0.25), and IfO methods BCO/SOIL (0.11). In the large-scale fall-guys-like game, PPO+ASOR achieves a total reward of 0.554 and success rate of 0.387, substantially exceeding PPO (0.329, 0.361) and PPO+SRPO (0.337, 0.376). In online MuJoCo and MetaDrive tasks, ESCP+ASOR achieves the best performance across all environments, with particularly large gains in Walker2d and Ant where state accessibility changes significantly under dynamics shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPLE+ASOR outperforms all 7 offline baselines in Table 1, with the largest margins over IfO methods (BCO, SOIL at 0.11 avg) and MOPO (0.04 avg), and a 0.05 improvement over the strongest cross-domain baseline MAPLE+SRPO (0.40). In the fall-guys-like game (Table 3), PPO+ASOR beats PPO+SRPO by 0.217 in total reward (0.554 vs 0.337) and reduces the unnecessary jump rate from 0.040 to 0.029, demonstrating that restricting regularization to globally accessible states avoids imitating suboptimal jumping behaviors. In online MuJoCo/MetaDrive tasks (Fig. 2), ESCP+ASOR surpasses ESCP, CaDM, OSI, SAC, and GARAT across all environments, with the most pronounced gains in Walker2d and Ant where state accessibility shifts dramatically. In Minigrid (Fig. 6), PPO+ASOR substantially outperforms PPO, PPO+SRPO, SOIL, and GAIfO, particularly when the bottom lava block is at Row 5 where SRPO fails to recognize inaccessible states. The paper notes that ASOR provides only marginal improvement in HalfCheetah online tasks because the agent does not 'fall over,' meaning state accessibility remains largely unchanged under dynamics shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-dynamics policy learning line of work (ESCP, CaDM, SRPO, DARA) by identifying a fundamental flaw in the IfO assumption that expert state distributions are similar across dynamics: state accessibility can break under dynamics shift, making some expert states misleading. Theoretically, it builds on the F-distance framework from Arora et al. (2017) and the GAN discriminator mechanism from Goodfellow et al. (2014), but departs from the closest prior work SRPO (Xue et al., 2023a) by replacing the strong 'homomorphous MDP' assumption (1-0 accessible) with the milder M-Rs accessible MDP formulation, yielding tighter linear-horizon performance bounds. Practically, it reframes state distribution imitation as a GAN-like reward augmentation module rather than a standalone imitation objective, making it composable with existing RL algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of globally accessible states as the correct restriction for cross-dynamics imitation addresses a real failure mode in IfO methods that prior work overlooked, and the F-distance regularization framework provides the first theoretical guarantees with linear (rather than quadratic) effective-horizon dependency for policy performance under dynamics shift. The practical ASOR module is attractive because it requires only a discriminator network and a pseudo-count mechanism, making it a low-cost add-on to PPO, MAPLE, or ESCP. Limitations include the assumption of a static reward function (inapplicable to multi-reward tasks), weaker bounds for adversarial HiP-MDPs with large Rs, and reliance on proxy visitation measures (RND, ensemble disagreement) that may be unreliable in very high-dimensional or stochastic environments. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks where the reward function itself changes across dynamics, and how the method scales when the set of globally accessible states becomes a very small fraction of the full state space.
