## Problem

Existing unsupervised multi-object segmentation methods either rely on reconstruction objectives that fail to scale to complex real-world images, or group similar pretrained features that produce under-segmentation (e.g., merging nearby objects into one). The core gaps are an unclear definition of objectness and the absence of an effective mechanism to discover individual objects in unseen scenes, particularly in crowded images where all baselines collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252863/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene image at arbitrary resolution and produces per-object bounding boxes and binary segmentation masks. Internally, the objectness network operates on 128×128 patches, and the multi-object reasoning module iteratively refines anchor-based proposals (5 scales × 3 aspect ratios) without any learnable parameters. The authors also introduce COCO*, an augmented COCO validation set with 197 additional object categories manually annotated to address under-annotation in the original benchmark.

## Method

unMORE is a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, an objectness network (ResNet50 for binary existence + DPT-large with two CNN heads for center and boundary fields) is trained on ImageNet using rough masks from VoteCut/DINO-v2 to learn three explicit object-centric representations: a binary existence score, a center field of unit vectors pointing to the object centroid, and a normalized signed boundary distance field. In stage 2, a network-free multi-object reasoning module initializes anchor-based proposals and iteratively refines them through three steps: existence checking (discard low-confidence patches), center reasoning (a 5×5 anti-center kernel detects multi-object regions and triggers proposal splitting), and boundary reasoning (border-based expansion/contraction using the gradient of the distance field to converge on tight bounding boxes). Optionally, discovered objects serve as pseudo labels to train a single Cascade Mask R-CNN detector, yielding the full unMORE system.

## Results

On the COCO* validation set, unMORE (full pipeline with one trained detector) achieves AP50^box = 32.6, AP^box = 18.0, AP50^mask = 29.6, and AP^mask = 15.5, outperforming the best prior method CuVLER (AP50^box = 28.0, AP^box = 15.5) by 4.6 and 2.5 points respectively. The network-free discovery variant unMORE_disc achieves AP50^box = 19.1 and AP^mask = 9.5 without any trained detector, already surpassing all direct-discovery baselines. In zero-shot evaluation across 6 additional datasets (COCO20K, LVIS, VOC, KITTI, Object365, OpenImages), unMORE achieves the highest AP on nearly all metrics, e.g., AP50^box = 25.9 on COCO20K and 26.7 on KITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

unMORE clearly outperforms all six baselines on COCO* across both direct discovery and trained-detector settings. Among trained-detector methods, unMORE beats CuVLER (the strongest baseline) by +4.6 AP50^box, +2.5 AP^box, +5.2 AP50^mask, and +2.9 AP^mask, and beats CutLER by +6.6 AP50^box and +3.7 AP^mask. UnSAM, despite predicting 332 objects per image, achieves only 10.2 AP50^box due to severe over-segmentation, while unMORE achieves 32.6 with just 100 predictions. In the direct-discovery setting (no trained detector), unMORE_disc surpasses VoteCut (the best feature-similarity method) by +8.3 AP50^box and +4.9 AP^mask, and dramatically outperforms DINOSAUR (+17.1 AP50^box) and FOUND (+14.7 AP50^box). The advantage grows substantially on crowded images (≥10 GT objects), where baselines collapse (e.g., VoteCut drops to 8.8 AP50^box) while unMORE_disc maintains 18.0. In zero-shot transfer, unMORE achieves the highest AP on all 6 external datasets, confirming generalization beyond COCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in unsupervised multi-object segmentation: slot-based reconstruction methods (SlotAtt and variants) that fail to scale to real-world images, and self-supervised feature distillation methods (TokenCut, CutLER, CuVLER) that group similar DINO/v2 features but suffer from under-segmentation in crowded scenes. Rather than learning a detection head or relying on feature similarity, unMORE explicitly defines three object-centric representations (existence score, center field, boundary distance field) trained on ImageNet, then applies a deterministic, network-free reasoning algorithm to discover objects. The boundary distance field concept is repurposed from shape reconstruction (DeepSDF), and the bootstrapping pipeline follows CuVLER's VoteCut for generating rough training masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The network-free multi-object reasoning module is a practical and elegant contribution: it eliminates the need for a learned detector in the discovery stage, reducing training complexity while achieving SOTA across six benchmarks. The method's particular strength on crowded images (≥5 objects) where all baselines collapse addresses a real failure mode in current unsupervised segmentation. Limitations include dependence on natural-image object priors (domain gap for medical/satellite imagery) and reliance on rough VoteCut masks for training. The authors suggest extending the framework to large-scale 2D image generation as a future direction, and the released COCO* dataset with augmented annotations is a useful community resource for more accurate evaluation of unsupervised discovery.
