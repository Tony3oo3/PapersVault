## Problem

Residual connections in deep Transformers suffer from representation collapse in later layers and create a bottleneck for cross-layer communication, limiting the formation of sophisticated circuits spanning distant layers. Existing dense connection approaches (DenseNet, DenseFormer) use static weights shared across all sequence positions and input streams, which severely limits their expressive capacity in sequence models where per-position and per-stream differentiation is critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252729/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model takes a text sequence of up to 4096 tokens and produces next-token probability distributions; for vision, it takes 16×16 image patches and produces ImageNet-1k class labels. The key structural difference from standard Transformers is that each layer's input is not a single tensor but four separate tensors (query, key, value, residual streams), each computed as a dynamic weighted aggregation of all preceding layers' outputs at that position, rather than simply the previous layer's output.

## Method

MUDDFormer replaces standard residual connections with Multiway Dynamic Dense (MUDD) connections: (1) the single input to each Transformer block is decoupled into four streams (Q, K, V, R), each fed by its own Depth-wise Aggregate (DA) module; (2) each DA module generates position-specific connection weights via a lightweight MLP — GELU(RMSNorm(X_i)W₁)W₂ + a_i — that computes per-position, per-layer weights dynamically conditioned on the current hidden state, then performs a weighted sum over all preceding layers' outputs; (3) FFN hidden dimensions are re-allocated to grow linearly from 0.5D_f to 1.5D_f with depth to match the increasing information available to upper layers. The overall mechanism is equivalent to depth-wise multi-head (4-head) attention across layers, expanding cross-layer communication bandwidth far beyond the single residual stream while adding only ~0.2% parameters and ~0.4% FLOPs.

## Results

On 300B-token Pile pretraining, MUDDPythia-2.8B achieves 6.29 Pile perplexity, matching Pythia-6.9B (2.46× compute) while adding only 0.23% parameters and 0.4% FLOPs. On 11 downstream benchmarks, MUDDPythia-2.8B reaches 55.0% average zero-shot accuracy (vs. 53.1% for Pythia-2.8B and 55.1% for Pythia-6.9B), and 57.0% five-shot accuracy (vs. 54.1% and 56.4% respectively). With the stronger Transformer++ architecture, MUDDFormer-2.8B surpasses Pythia-12B in five-shot settings (58.4% vs. 57.2% avg accuracy). In scaling-law experiments (405M–1.4B, 7B–26B tokens), MUDDFormer-834M matches the loss of Transformer++ trained with 1.89× compute, and the gain over Transformer++ remains stable or grows with scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (Llama-style architecture with RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm), MUDDFormer consistently outperforms across all tested scales (405M–1.4B), with the 834M model matching Transformer++ trained with 1.89× compute; the gap widens at larger scales. Compared to DenseFormer (static dense connections) and Hyper-Connections (dynamic but non-all-to-all cross-layer weights), MUDDFormer achieves lower validation loss at every model size in the scaling experiments, with the advantage growing from 405M to 1.4B. Against the ablated DDFormer (dynamic but single-stream dense connections), MUDDFormer's multiway design provides a further perplexity reduction (10.83 → 10.77 at 405M), confirming that stream-specific aggregation matters. At the 2.8B scale on 300B tokens, MUDDPythia-2.8B ties Pythia-6.9B on Pile perplexity (6.29) and nearly ties on average downstream accuracy (55.0 vs. 55.1), while the Transformer++-based MUDDFormer-2.8B exceeds Pythia-12B in five-shot accuracy (58.4% vs. 57.2%). In the vision experiment, MUDDViT-S/16 (22M params) outperforms both ViT-S/16 (76.0% → 78.1% top-1 at 300 epochs) and the 1.72× larger ViT-M/16 (77.9%). In the MoE comparison, MUDDFormer-405M achieves ~50% of the loss reduction obtained by a 4.4× larger 1.8B-A405M MoE model, and MUDD provides greater benefit for MoE (Δloss −0.0641) than for dense (Δloss −0.0596) models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense-connections lineage from DenseNet (Huang et al., 2017) through DenseFormer (Pagliardini et al., 2024) and Hyper-Connections (Zhu et al., 2025) by introducing two key departures: (1) connection weights are generated dynamically per sequence position via a lightweight MLP (analogous to depth-wise self-attention), rather than being static scalars shared across positions; and (2) the single residual stream is decoupled into four independent streams (query, key, value, residual), each with its own dedicated aggregation module. The multiway design draws conceptual inspiration from Highway Networks' input-dependent gating, while the dynamic weight generation is explicitly framed as a simplified form of depth-wise multi-head attention, distinguishing MUDD from both the static-weight approaches (DenseFormer, DLCL) and the expansion-based Hyper-Connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MUDDFormer achieves 1.8×–2.4× compute efficiency gains with only 0.23% extra parameters and 0.4% extra FLOPs, making it a highly practical drop-in architectural upgrade for existing Transformer pipelines. The demonstrated ability of a 2.8B MUDD model to rival a 12B baseline in five-shot in-context learning suggests that cross-layer information flow, not raw parameter count, is a key bottleneck for emergent capabilities. Open questions include whether the gains persist at 70B+ scale, whether kernel fusion can close the remaining 10–16% training/inference speed gap, and how MUDD interacts with other within-layer innovations (linear attention, SSMs, MoE routing) that are increasingly adopted in frontier models.
