## Problem

Deep learning predictors for mutational effects on protein-protein binding energy (ΔΔG_bind) have consistently underperformed computationally expensive empirical force-field methods like FoldX and Flex ddG, largely because experimental binding data covers fewer than 350 distinct interfaces. This paper addresses the data scarcity by exploiting the thermodynamic identity that binding energy equals the difference between the complex's folding energy and the sum of its partners' folding energies, thereby enabling transfer from the much larger folding stability dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single backbone structure (from the bound complex), a wild-type sequence, and a mutant sequence as input, and outputs a scalar ΔΔG_bind prediction in kcal/mol. The same backbone structure is used for all six sub-systems (complex and two monomers for both wild-type and mutant), reflecting the assumption that backbone changes are negligible upon binding and mutation. ProteinMPNN's stochastic decoding order and backbone noise are fixed identically between wild-type and mutant evaluations (antithetic variates) and averaged over 20 Monte Carlo samples to reduce prediction variance.

## Method

STAB-DDG parameterizes binding energy via the StaB identity: b_θ(s_A:B) = f_θ(s_A:B) − f_θ(s_A) − f_θ(s_B), where f_θ(s) = log p_θ(s) is the log-likelihood from a pre-trained ProteinMPNN inverse-folding model, and ΔΔG_bind is the difference of b_θ between mutant and wild-type. This construction guarantees antisymmetry, mutational path independence, and expressivity—properties not simultaneously satisfied by prior DL predictors. Training proceeds in two sequential fine-tuning stages: first on 776K Megascale folding stability measurements (using Δf_θ as the predictor), then on 7K SKEMPIv2.0 binding measurements (using Δb_θ), with per-complex loss normalization. Variance reduction via antithetic variates (fixing stochasticity between wild-type and mutant) and Monte Carlo ensembling over 20 samples further reduces prediction error at both training and inference time.

## Results

On the SKEMPIv2.0 test split (80 complexes, 1,491 mutants), STAB-DDG achieves a per-interface Spearman of 0.45±0.04 and overall Spearman of 0.53±0.05, matching FoldX (0.48±0.03 per-interface) and outperforming all prior deep learning methods (best DL baseline: Surface-VQMAE at 0.33 per-interface Spearman). An ensemble of STAB-DDG and FoldX achieves state-of-the-art performance with 0.53±0.03 per-interface Spearman and 0.78±0.04 AUROC. STAB-DDG runs ~1,000× faster than FoldX (0.2 s/mutation on GPU vs. 210 s/mutation on CPU). On the yeast surface display case study, sequential fine-tuning on folding stability then binding data progressively improves per-interface Spearman from 0.09 (zero-shot) to 0.30 (full STAB-DDG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAB-DDG (per-interface Spearman 0.45) is the first deep learning method to statistically match FoldX (0.48) and Flex ddG (0.42) on the SKEMPIv2.0 benchmark, whereas all six supervised DL baselines (Surface-VQMAE 0.33, Prompt-DDG 0.27, RDE-Net 0.28, DiffAffinity 0.25, PPIformer 0.20, ProMIM 0.15) fall well below both force-field methods. The STAB-DDG + FoldX ensemble (0.53 per-interface Spearman, 0.78 AUROC) surpasses every individual method tested. Notably, STAB-DDG zero-shot (0.43) already outperforms all supervised DL baselines without any binding-energy fine-tuning, and the stability fine-tuned variant (0.40) is competitive with FoldX on per-interface metrics. On the yeast surface display dataset, STAB-DDG improves per-interface Spearman from 0.09 (zero-shot) to 0.30 (full model), with the folding-stability fine-tuning step contributing most of the gain. On the small TCR mimic dataset (30 measurements), STAB-DDG yields an inconclusive Spearman of 0.13±0.39, likely due to the difficulty of predicting effects on flexible antibody loops and the limited sample size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ProteinMPNN (Dauparas et al., 2022) as a pre-trained inverse-folding model, repurposing its sequence log-likelihoods as a proxy for folding free energy via the thermodynamic identity ΔG_bind = ΔG_fold(complex) − ΔG_fold(A) − ΔG_fold(B). While Jiao et al. (2024) arrived at a similar decomposed predictor form, STAB-DDG is the first to explicitly interpret this decomposition in terms of folding energy, enabling fine-tuning on the much larger Megascale folding stability dataset (776K measurements vs. 7K binding measurements). The paper also departs from prior DL predictors (PPIformer, Surface-VQMAE, Prompt-DDG, etc.) by formally proving that its StaB parameterization satisfies all three thermodynamic properties—antisymmetry, mutational path independence, and expressivity—simultaneously, a combination no other method achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAB-DDG is the first deep learning predictor to match FoldX accuracy while being over 1000× faster (0.2 s vs. 210 s per mutation), making large-scale in silico screening of binding specificity feasible for therapeutic design. The transfer-learning strategy—leveraging copious folding stability data to address binding data scarcity—offers a general recipe applicable to other data-limited biophysical prediction tasks. However, the problem remains open: the TCR mimic case study (Spearman 0.13 ± 0.39) shows that flexible antibody loops defeat current methods, and the model's reliance on a single static backbone structure limits accuracy on flexible interfaces. Future work on modeling backbone dynamics and scaling to larger, more diverse binding datasets is needed.
