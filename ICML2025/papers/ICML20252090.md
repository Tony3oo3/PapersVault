## Problem

Despite the success of scaling laws in supervised learning and language modeling, value-based off-policy RL has been considered unpredictable at scale due to community lore about its pathological training dynamics (overfitting, plasticity loss, distribution shift). This paper asks whether data and compute requirements for value-based TD-learning RL can be predicted from small-scale runs, enabling reliable extrapolation to larger budgets without running the expensive large-scale experiment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes empirical data from grid searches over UTD ratio σ, batch size B, and learning rate η (spanning at least an order of magnitude in σ, e.g., σ ∈ {1,2,4,8} for DMC/OAI Gym or σ ∈ {1/1024,...,1/65536} for IsaacGym), along with a linear budget constraint F = C + δ·D. It produces closed-form power-law fits for optimal hyperparameters B*(σ), η*(σ), data requirement D_J(σ), compute requirement C_J(σ), and optimal UTD σ*(F_0), all of which extrapolate to unseen σ values and budget levels. Returns are preprocessed via isotonic regression and normalized to [0,1000] before fitting.

## Method

The core insight is that in value-based RL, batch size B and learning rate η are NOT correlated with each other (unlike supervised learning) but are each inversely proportional to the UTD ratio σ, following power laws B*(σ) ∝ σ^{-α_B} and η*(σ) ∝ σ^{-α_η}. This decoupling arises because high σ causes overfitting (countered by smaller B) and plasticity loss (countered by smaller η) through distinct mechanisms. With these hyperparameter fits, the paper shows that data requirement D_J(σ) follows a single power law with an offset D_J^min, while compute C_J(σ) is a sum of two power laws in σ, together defining a Pareto frontier between data and compute. The optimal UTD for a given total budget F_0 then follows its own power law σ*(F_0) ∝ F_0^{-α_σ}, enabling prediction of optimal resource allocation and hyperparameters for arbitrarily large budgets from small-scale fits. The approach is validated on SAC (OAI Gym), BRO (DMC), and PQL (IsaacGym) across 12 continuous-control tasks.

## Results

The paper establishes that data requirements follow a power law D_J(σ) ≈ D_J^min + (β_J/σ)^α_J, with α_J ≈ 0.74 (DMC), 0.69 (OAI Gym), and 0.87 (IsaacGym). The optimal UTD ratio for a given budget follows σ*(F_0) ≈ (β_σ/F_0)^α_σ with α_σ ≈ 0.53 (DMC/OAI Gym) and 0.57 (IsaacGym). Pareto frontier extrapolation achieves 7.8% error toward larger compute and 10.6% error toward larger data regimes, validating that small-scale fits reliably predict large-scale resource requirements across SAC, BRO, and PQL algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a scaling-law study rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing RL algorithms. The key empirical comparison is between the proposed hyperparameter prediction workflow (fitting B*(σ) and η*(σ) as power laws) and the standard practice of tuning hyperparameters at a single UTD value (σ=2) and reusing them across all σ values. On OpenAI Gym, the proposed approach improves data efficiency for all σ values other than σ=2, with the gap widening substantially at larger σ (e.g., σ=16), demonstrating that accounting for the B–σ and η–σ dependencies is critical for reliable extrapolation. The paper also shows that the critical batch size heuristic from supervised learning (McCandlish et al., 2018) does not correlate with empirically optimal batch sizes in value-based RL (Figure 11), motivating the UTD-dependent power law formulation instead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling-law methodology from supervised learning (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2023) to off-policy value-based RL, introducing the updates-to-data (UTD) ratio σ as a third critical hyperparameter alongside batch size and learning rate. It departs from the supervised-learning assumption that B and η are inversely correlated, showing instead that both are independently inversely correlated with σ due to RL-specific overfitting and plasticity-loss dynamics. The data-compute Pareto frontier controlled by σ is a formulation unique to RL, where data collection and gradient updates are coupled through the replay buffer, and distinguishes this work from prior RL scaling studies focused on on-policy methods (Hilton et al., 2023) or offline model-size scaling (Springenberg et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the widespread community belief that value-based deep RL exhibits 'pathological' and unpredictable behavior at scale, providing a concrete empirical workflow to predict large-budget RL training from small runs. The practical impact is significant for RL practitioners who must allocate data and compute resources, as the Pareto frontier and budget-extrapolation results enable planning without running expensive large-scale experiments. Key limitations include the study of only three hyperparameters (excluding model size, weight decay, and reset frequency), relatively small network architectures, and 12 tasks across 3 domains, leaving open whether the power-law forms hold at much larger scales or with modern architectures. Theoretical grounding for the observed power laws remains an open question.
