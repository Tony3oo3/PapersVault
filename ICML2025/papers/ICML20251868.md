## Problem

Previous propagation-of-chaos (PoC) results for mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) yield a particle approximation error of O(λ/(αN)), where the log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) constant α can be exponentially small as the regularization coefficient λ→0, making the bound vacuous in practice. This paper removes the exponential dependence on α from the particle approximation term while simultaneously achieving a faster optimization convergence rate than prior work that already eliminated α (Nitanda 2024; Chewi et al. 2024).

## Input / Output

For the MFNN experiments, the method takes input-label pairs (binary classification on concentric circles, or k-multi-index regression in d=100 dimensions) and trains M independent networks of N neurons each via noisy gradient descent, then merges them into a single MN-neuron network whose output is compared against an N∞=10000-neuron reference. For the LoRA application, the input is a pre-trained Llama2-7B or Llama3-8B model plus M=8 independently trained LoRA parameter pairs (A_j, B_j) of rank N=32; the output is a single merged (k×d) weight matrix ΔW = (1/MN)Σ b_j^i a_j^⊤ that requires no additional inference memory due to the linearity of the activation.

## Method

The core technical contribution is an improved defective log-Sobolev inequality (Lemma 3.7) built on a new uniform directional LSI assumption (Assumption 3.2) imposed on conditional Gibbs distributions ν_{i|-i}(·|x^{-i}). By connecting these conditional distributions to the proximal Gibbs distribution via the Bregman divergence of F_0 (Eq. 8) and bounding the Bregman term by B/N² (Assumption 3.4), the defective LSI yields a particle approximation error of B/N that is independent of the LSI constant α. This leads to the main PoC theorem (Theorem 3.8): the objective gap decomposes into O(1/N) particle error plus exp(−2αλt) optimization error, where α is the (larger) uniform directional LSI constant rather than the smaller LSI constant on μ_*^{(N)} used in prior work. The PoC result is then translated into point-wise and uniform model approximation bounds (Theorems 4.3, 4.7) using Han's inequality to control inter-particle correlations and Dudley's entropy integral for uniformity. Finally, a PoC-based ensemble method merges M independently trained networks, exploiting inter-network independence to reduce the variance term from O(1/N) to O(1/(MN)) while the bias term scales as O(1/√(MN)), yielding an optimal M = λ^{1/3}K^{1/3} under a fixed total-neuron budget K.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning benchmarks, PoC-based merging of 8 LoRA parameters (N=32 each) outperforms the best single LoRA (N=32) by +4.05% average accuracy on Llama2-7B (82.74% vs. 78.69%) and by +2.04% on Llama3-8B (87.30% vs. 85.26%). Under the same total parameter budget (MN=256), PoC-merge with M=8, N=32 substantially outperforms a single LoRA of rank 256 by +21.46% on Llama2-7B (82.74% vs. 61.28%) and +2.31% on Llama3-8B (87.30% vs. 84.99%). The theoretical contribution establishes that the particle approximation error in MFLD is O(1/N) independent of the LSI constant, with optimization rate exp(−αλt), improving upon prior bounds that carried an exponential dependence on the regularization coefficient λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PoC-based model merging (averaging M=8 independently trained LoRA parameters of rank N=32) against two LoRA baselines: (1) the best single LoRA parameter selected among 8 runs ("LoRA(32, best)"), and (2) a single LoRA of rank 256 matching the total parameter budget MN=256 ("LoRA(256)"). PoC-merge wins on both Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B across nearly all 8 commonsense reasoning benchmarks. The advantage is most dramatic on Llama2-7B, where PoC-merge gains +4.05% over the best single LoRA and a striking +21.46% over the same-budget LoRA(256), suggesting that the ensemble's variance reduction from independent training is far more effective than simply increasing rank. On Llama3-8B, the gains are more modest (+2.04% over best single, +2.31% over same-budget), likely because the larger base model already generalizes well from a single fine-tune. The theoretical PoC result (Theorem 3.8) underpins these gains by showing that merging M independent networks reduces the particle approximation error from O(1/N) to O(1/(MN)) in the leading term, with an optimal ensemble size M ∝ λ^{1/3}K^{1/3} under a fixed total neuron budget K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the propagation-of-chaos (PoC) framework for Mean-Field Langevin Dynamics established by Chen et al. (2022) and Suzuki et al. (2023a), which bounded the particle approximation error as O(λ/(αN)) with an LSI constant α that can be exponentially small. By introducing a uniform directional LSI on conditional Gibbs distributions (Assumption 3.2) and leveraging the Bregman divergence structure of the mean-field objective, the authors remove the α-dependence from the particle term (achieving O(1/N), matching Nitanda 2024) while simultaneously obtaining a faster optimization rate exp(−2αλt) than the uniform-in-N LSI approach of Nitanda (2024) and Chewi et al. (2024). The model ensemble application and its translation into point-wise/uniform approximation bounds extend the PoC literature beyond objective-gap analysis into practical model-merging guarantees, and the LoRA experiment repurposes the ensemble theory for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the exponential-in-1/λ dependence from the particle approximation term has direct practical implications: it eliminates the need for an exponential number of neurons in learning setups like k-parity problems (Suzuki et al., 2023b), making mean-field training more tractable at small regularization. The PoC-based ensemble result provides the first theoretical guarantee for merging independently trained MFNNs, and the empirical LoRA experiment (where M=8 networks of rank 32 outperform both the best single rank-32 LoRA and a single rank-256 LoRA under the same total parameter budget) suggests the ensemble principle may generalize beyond the mean-field theory. A key limitation is that the theory cannot explain the asymptotic behavior as λ→0, and the KL-divergence-based correlation control may be overly conservative; the authors identify replacing KL with a smaller metric as a promising future direction. The LoRA validation, while encouraging, is limited to two model families and eight commonsense-reasoning benchmarks.
