## Problem

Leading fixed-backbone protein sequence design methods (e.g., ProteinMPNN) do not explicitly model sidechain conformations during sequence generation, instead implicitly reasoning about critical sidechain interactions from backbone geometry and amino acid labels alone. This limits their ability to capture nuanced intermolecular interactions—Van der Waals contacts, hydrogen bonds, pi-stacking—that depend on precise sidechain atom distances and angles, and which determine protein stability and binding affinity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252660/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein backbone structure (Cα coordinates), a partially masked amino acid sequence, and partially masked sidechain coordinates in atom37 format (37×3 per residue, with ghost atoms set to Cα for absent atom types) as input. It produces a full amino acid sequence, full-atom sidechain coordinates, and a per-atom predicted sidechain error (pSCE) in Ångströms. Sidechain coordinates are internally represented in a local backbone frame (defined by N, Cα, C) to ensure SE(3) invariance, and are transformed back to global coordinates after the diffusion trajectory completes.

## Method

FAMPNN uses a hybrid MPNN-GVP encoder with three components—an invariant backbone encoder, an invariant full-atom encoder with expanded inter-atomic distance featurization, and an equivariant GVP encoder capturing unit-vector orientations between Cα and all sidechain atoms—to produce node embeddings that jointly condition both sequence and sidechain prediction. Training combines a categorical cross-entropy loss for sequence identity with a per-token Euclidean diffusion loss (variance-exploding EDM scheme) for sidechain coordinates, where a lightweight MLP denoiser is conditioned via AdaLN on the node embedding, noise level, and amino acid one-hot. Sampling follows a MaskGIT-style iterative unmasking procedure: starting from fully masked sequence and sidechains, the model predicts all tokens in parallel at each step and progressively unmasks a subset until the full sequence and structure are generated. A separate confidence module, trained with stop-gradient on diffusion rollouts, predicts per-atom packing error (pSCE) binned from 0–4 Å, providing an interpretable quality metric for both packing and design.

## Results

On the CATH 4.2 test set, FAMPNN achieves 49.66% single-step sequence recovery, surpassing ProteinMPNN (45.96%) and GVP (39.47%), while remaining competitive with PiFold (51.66%) and Frame2Seq-Ensemble (49.11%). For sidechain packing on CASP15, FAMPNN (0.0 Å) achieves state-of-the-art 0.690 Å all-atom RMSD (0.350 core / 0.789 surface), outperforming FlowPacker (0.765 Å) and LigandMPNN (0.788 Å). On zero-shot protein fitness prediction, FAMPNN achieves 0.39 Spearman correlation on SKEMPIv2 (vs. 0.27 for ProteinMPNN, 0.29 for ESM-IF1) and 0.58 on the leakage-corrected SKEMPI-PPI split (vs. 0.44 for the best supervised baseline PPIFormer). Self-consistency (scTM) on RFdiffusion de novo backbones is competitive with ProteinMPNN across all lengths (e.g., 0.968 vs. 0.964 at length 100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAMPNN outperforms ProteinMPNN (45.96%) and GVP (39.47%) on CATH 4.2 sequence recovery by 3.7 and 10.2 percentage points respectively, and beats Frame2Seq (46.53%) by 3.1 pp, though it trails PiFold (51.66%) and ESM-IF1 trained on AlphaFoldDB (51.60%) by ~2 pp. On sidechain packing (CASP15), FAMPNN (0.0 Å) achieves the best all-atom RMSD of 0.690 Å, beating FlowPacker (0.765 Å), LigandMPNN (0.788 Å), ChromaDesign (0.810 Å), and 3DCNN (0.897 Å), with particularly strong core-residue packing (0.350 Å vs. 0.434–0.489 Å for others). For self-consistency on de novo backbones, FAMPNN (0.3 Å) matches or slightly exceeds ProteinMPNN at shorter lengths (scTM 0.968 vs. 0.964 at length 100) but falls behind at length 500 (0.545 vs. 0.578). On protein fitness, FAMPNN substantially outperforms both ProteinMPNN and ESM-IF1 on SKEMPIv2 (0.39 vs. 0.27/0.29 Spearman) and SKEMPI-PPI (0.58 vs. 0.41/0.18), and modestly leads on stability benchmarks (e.g., S669: 0.41 vs. 0.30/0.31; FireProt: 0.55 vs. 0.50/0.47). On antibody-antigen binding (CR9114, CR6261), FAMPNN consistently outperforms both baselines (e.g., CR6261-H1: 0.56 vs. 0.46/0.43), though the gap narrows on the G6 dataset (0.29 vs. 0.28/0.13).

## Relation to Prior Work

FAMPNN extends the ProteinMPNN architecture (invariant MPNN encoder/decoder) and GVP equivariant layers into a hybrid full-atom encoder, while adopting the iterative unmasking sampling scheme from MaskGIT and the variance-exploding EDM diffusion framework for continuous sidechain coordinate generation. The key departure from prior fixed-backbone sequence design methods (ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF1, Frame2Seq) is the explicit joint modeling of discrete sequence identity and continuous sidechain conformation per token, rather than implicitly reasoning about sidechain interactions through backbone geometry alone. The closest prior work—3DCNN (which co-generates sequence and sidechains but requires expensive MCMC), AttnPacker (sidechain packing with auxiliary sequence prediction), and LigandMPNN (sidechain conditioning without co-generation)—each addresses only part of this joint problem, and none uses the lightweight per-token diffusion + cross-entropy combined objective proposed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that full-atom conditioning provides a scaling benefit—disproportionately larger gains when more structural context is available (15% improvement at 90% context vs. 8% at 0%)—suggests that as design workflows incorporate richer structural information (e.g., from co-folding or ligand context), full-atom models will pull further ahead of backbone-only methods. The zero-shot outperformance of supervised models on the leakage-corrected SKEMPI-PPI split is practically significant for protein-protein interaction design. Open questions include extending the framework to ligand conformation modeling, concurrent backbone design, and alignment to experimental fitness data; the paper also notes that full-atom conditioning did not improve antibody-antigen binding prediction, likely due to inaccurate homologous input structures, highlighting a sensitivity to structural quality that remains unresolved.
