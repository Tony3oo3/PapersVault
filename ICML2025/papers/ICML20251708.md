## Problem

Prior causal reasoning benchmarks for language models evaluate whether models can extract causal relations or answer individual causal queries, but none systematically assess whether models can reason about how causal measures compose across graph components. This paper identifies that gap by defining compositional causal reasoning (CCR)—the ability to infer how local causal quantities propagate and combine through causal graphs—and provides the first framework for exhaustively measuring it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a causal DAG satisfying four structural assumptions (single root, single leaf, at least one cutpoint, no unobserved confounders) and transforms it into a commutative cut tree (CCT) that collapses biconnected components into single edges. LMs are queried via series of factual and counterfactual yes/no text prompts (based on a CandyParty math word problem); their natural-language responses are parsed into Boolean vectors by a separate LM, then treated as Monte Carlo samples from interventional distributions to compute 1000 PNS estimates per quantity. The output is a structured error report: per-quantity external validity (RAE vs. ground truth), per-path internal consistency (equivalent compositions compared against each other), and a four-way reasoner taxonomy classification (VC, VI, IC, II) per model.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) proves that under monotonicity, the PNS for a root-leaf pair composes multiplicatively across biconnected components of the causal DAG, extending the classical product-of-coefficients heuristic from linear ATE to nonlinear PNS. This compositionality enables a systematic evaluation procedure (Algorithm 1) built on the CCT: every path from root to leaf in the CCT corresponds to a distinct composition of local PNS values, and all such compositions must equal the global PNS and each other. The algorithm computes three error types—quantity-wise external validity (η), composition-wise external validity (ε), and composition-wise internal consistency (γ)—using a chosen metric (relative absolute error). The taxonomy of reasoners (VC/VI/IC/II) then classifies models by whether their estimates match ground truth and whether theoretically equivalent compositions agree, revealing qualitatively distinct failure modes invisible to external validity alone. Empirically, seven LMs (Llama 2/3/3.1/3.1-Math, Phi-3-Mini, GPT-4o, o1) are evaluated with and without chain-of-thought prompting, with only o1 achieving full VC reasoning on the test graph.

## Results

On the CandyParty CCR task (n=1000 PNS estimates per quantity per model, RAE threshold 0.1), only o1 achieved valid-consistent (VC) reasoning, correctly implying that the commutative cut tree C_XY commutes. GPT-4o with CoT reached near-valid-inconsistent (near-VI) status with 75.1% of estimates near-valid, >77% mean external validity on local quantities, and >95% on compositions, yet still scored 0% validity on the global PNS_XY. All other models (Llama 2, 3, 3.1, 3.1 Math, Phi-3-Mini, and GPT-4o without CoT) fell into the invalid-inconsistent (II) category, with Phi-3, Llama 2, Llama 3, and GPT-4o (no CoT) failing to exceed 3% validity for any quantity. CCR errors increased monotonically with shortest path distance and total mediating variables for all models except o1 and GPT-4o with CoT, though GPT-4o with CoT still showed >10× higher RAE for six mediators versus three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates seven LM architectures on a single CCR task, classifying each into one of four reasoner categories (VC, VI, IC, II). o1 (Jaech et al., 2024) is the only model to achieve full VC reasoning, placing mass in the valid-consistent quadrant and correctly implying CCT commutativity. GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023) with CoT prompting transitions from II to near-VI, showing the greatest sensitivity to prompt formulation: 75.1% near-valid estimates and >95% composition validity, but still failing on the global PNS_XY (internally inconsistent). All open-source models—Llama 2 (Touvron et al., 2023), Llama 3 and 3.1 (Dubey et al., 2024), Llama 3.1 Math (Toshniwal et al., 2024), and Phi-3-Mini (Abdin et al., 2024)—remain firmly in the II category, with errors not decreasing monotonically with model size or Llama version. The paper notes that even this low-complexity CCR task (8-node DAG, 3 indirect paths) represents a significant challenge for smaller and open models, while o1's complete reasoning validates the framework's correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual data simulation methodology of González & Nori (2024) and Hüyük et al. (2025), which treat LMs as sources of interventional/counterfactual samples for computing causal quantities, by adding a compositional dimension: it asks not just whether a model computes the right causal quantity, but whether it correctly infers how those quantities compose across graph components. The theoretical backbone draws on classical path-tracing and mediation analysis (product-of-coefficients heuristics) and the PrC literature (Tian & Pearl, 2000), but the specific evaluation task—systematically checking commutativity of causal measure compositions via a new graphical tool (the commutative cut tree)—is absent from prior causal reasoning benchmarks like CLADDER or Corr2Cause, which evaluate single-quantity correctness without probing compositional structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, scalable way to diagnose *how* LMs fail at causal reasoning (via the VC/VI/IC/II taxonomy and internal consistency metrics) rather than merely *whether* they fail, which is a richer diagnostic than the binary correct/incorrect scoring used in most prior benchmarks. The finding that only o1 achieved valid-consistent reasoning on an 8-node graph, and that errors grow monotonically with mediation depth for all other models, suggests compositional causal reasoning remains a significant open gap. Key limitations include evaluation on a single toy problem, restriction to ATE/PNS under monotonicity, and graphs with cutpoints; extending to other estimands, nonlinear settings, and larger DAGs is left to future work.
