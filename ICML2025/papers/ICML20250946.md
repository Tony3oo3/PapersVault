## Problem

Token-based multimodal protein language models (e.g., DPLM-2) suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) vector quantization of 3D structures into discrete tokens discards fine-grained geometric details, and (2) the language model's direct prediction of index-based structure tokens is highly inaccurate (8.6% accuracy on CAMEO) because small bit-level changes produce drastically different indices. The paper systematically identifies these pitfalls and elucidates a design space spanning generative modeling, architecture, representation learning, and data to overcome them.

## Input / Output

The model consumes amino acid sequences (20-residue vocabulary) and 3D backbone coordinates (4 atoms per residue: N, Cα, C, O) jointly as multimodal input, or operates unconditionally for co-generation. Structure is discretized via an LFQ tokenizer into K-bit binary tokens per residue, which the language model predicts bit-by-bit rather than as a single 2^K-way index. At inference, predicted bits are decoded through a structure decoder (optionally refined by a residual diffusion module or flow-matching sampler) to recover continuous backbone coordinates. The model also supports inverse folding (structure-conditioned sequence generation) and representation extraction for downstream tasks.

## Method

The paper builds on DPLM-2's absorbing discrete diffusion framework and introduces four complementary design axes. First, bit-based discrete modeling replaces 2^K-way index prediction with K independent binary classifications, providing finer-grained supervision that dramatically improves structure token accuracy (from 8.6% to 26.4% index accuracy on PDB). Second, a lightweight residual diffusion module (ResDiff) recovers the quantization residuals z_cont − z_quant conditioned on LM hidden states, refining local atomic geometry. Third, a hybrid flow-matching approach composes the structure encoder, PLM, and decoder into a data-space denoiser, enabling direct coordinate-level sampling with 10× fewer steps. Fourth, GeoDPLM injects geometric inductive biases via PairFormer-inspired structure attention (pair representations with transition layers) and SeqStruct attention, while REPA aligns PLM hidden states to ESMFold's folding-trunk representations for smooth, high-dimensional structural learning signals. The recommended setting (Geo + Bit-based) reduces folding RMSD from 5.52 to 2.37 on PDB with only 650M parameters, outperforming 3B baselines.

## Results

The best 650M-parameter model (combining GeoDPLM, bit-based modeling, flow matching, REPA, ResDiff, and folding SFT) reduces folding RMSD from 5.52 to 2.37 on the PDB date split and from 7.70 to 5.85 on CAMEO 2022, while improving TMscore to 0.927 and 0.844 respectively. The recommended setting (Geo + Bit-based modeling without SFT) achieves RMSD 2.55 / TMscore 0.925 on PDB date split while preserving unconditional generation diversity (0.900). On CAMEO 2022, the full model reaches RMSD 5.85 / TMscore 0.844, outperforming the 3B DPLM-2 baseline (5.98 / 0.844) with only 650M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the PDB date split, the 650M model with all improvements (RMSD 2.37) outperforms ESMFold 3B (2.84) by 0.47 Å and DPLM-2 3B with folding SFT (3.15) by 0.78 Å, while also surpassing ESM3 1.4B (4.90) and MultiFlow (15.64). On CAMEO 2022, ESMFold 3B retains a lead (RMSD 3.99 vs. 5.85), but the 650M model still outperforms ESM3 1.4B (6.33), DPLM-2 650M baseline (7.70), and DPLM-2 3B with SFT (5.98). MultiFlow is dramatically outperformed on both benchmarks (17.84 vs. 5.85 on CAMEO). The recommended Geo+Bit setting (without SFT) achieves RMSD 2.55 on PDB date split—nearly matching the full model—while preserving unconditional generation diversity (0.900 vs. 0.525 with FM), making it the practical choice for multimodal co-generation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPLM-2, a multimodal diffusion protein language model that jointly models amino acid sequences and structure tokens via absorbing discrete diffusion, and systematically explores its design space across four axes: generative modeling, architecture, representation learning, and data. It adapts several ideas from adjacent fields—bitwise autoregressive modeling from image synthesis (Infinity), representation alignment from vision (REPA), flow-matching-based sampling from AlphaFlow, and geometric pair-representation modules from AlphaFold3's PairFormer—into the protein language model setting. The paper departs from prior multimodal PLMs (ESM3, DPLM-2) by identifying that the core bottleneck is not the tokenizer's reconstruction quality but the language model's inability to predict index-based structure tokens, and by showing that finer-grained bit-level supervision and geometric inductive biases are the more effective levers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 650M-parameter multimodal PLM can match or exceed 3B specialized folding models (ESMFold) on structure prediction, while retaining generative and representation-learning capabilities, suggests that unified multimodal protein models are becoming practically competitive with task-specific architectures. The paper also reveals that reconstruction accuracy of the structure tokenizer is a poor proxy for downstream generation quality, redirecting research effort toward language-model-side improvements rather than tokenizer refinement. Key open questions include whether these design choices scale beyond 3B parameters, how to incorporate physical/energy-based constraints, and whether the discrete-token paradigm can ultimately be replaced by direct atomic-coordinate modeling in a unified framework.
