## Problem

Existing watch-time prediction methods ignore the multimodal distribution properties of watch-ratio within the same video duration bucket and suffer from instance representation confusion, where the model cannot effectively differentiate between distinct user behavior patterns (e.g., 'scroll', 'like', 'very like'). This limits prediction accuracy across diverse deep recommendation architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250446/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is model-agnostic and takes standard tabular recommendation features (user_id, video_id, author_id, duration_level, and modality-specific features) as input to any deep recommendation model, producing a continuous predicted watch-time value. Unusually, the method also consumes the ground-truth watch-ratio distribution (ratio of watch-time to video duration) across duration buckets offline to generate discrete prototype vectors, which then serve as calibration references during training. At inference, the OT module is removed and prediction is computed as a linear combination of prototype similarities fed into a regressor.

## Method

ProWTP is a two-stage calibration framework. In Stage 1, a Hierarchical VQ-VAE (HVQ-VAE) encodes sampled near-Gaussian sub-distributions of the watch-ratio into a codebook of shape C×K×d, where the hierarchical structure first selects the closest cluster via target attention and then indexes the nearest prototype vector within that cluster, capturing the multimodal structure with equal-means/varied-variances semantics. In Stage 2, the alignment between instance representations and prototypes is formulated as a Semi-relaxed Unbalanced Optimal Transport (SUOT) problem where the marginal constraint on the prototype side is relaxed via an L2 penalty, reformulated as a weighted Lasso regression solved by a regularization path algorithm. Two auxiliary losses—assignment loss (cross-entropy between transport plan and softmax similarities) and compact loss (contrastive clustering of instances under the same prototype)—further tighten the instance space around prototypes, reducing representation confusion and improving prediction accuracy.

## Results

ProWTP achieves the best performance across all four metrics (RMSE, MAE, XAUC, XGAUC) on all three datasets. On WeChat, it reaches RMSE 28.47, MAE 19.84, XAUC 0.6183, and XGAUC 0.5730; on KuaiRand-Pure, RMSE 40.45, MAE 24.43, XAUC 0.7290, XGAUC 0.7048; and on Short-video, RMSE 28.67, MAE 10.69, XAUC 0.5929, XGAUC 0.5731. The most notable gains are in XAUC and XGAUC (ranking metrics), where ProWTP improves over the strongest baseline CWM by 0.0068–0.0081 in XAUC and 0.0049–0.0076 in XGAUC across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProWTP outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The strongest competitor is CWM (Zhao et al., 2024), which uses counterfactual reasoning to mitigate duration bias; ProWTP beats it by 0.31 RMSE on WeChat (28.47 vs. 28.78), 0.30 on KuaiRand-Pure (40.45 vs. 40.75), and 0.33 on Short-video (28.67 vs. 29.00), with XAUC gains of 0.0068, 0.0081, and 0.0081 respectively. TPM (Lin et al., 2023), the tree-based progressive regression model, is the second-strongest baseline on WeChat and Short-video but is surpassed by ProWTP by 0.38–0.35 RMSE and 0.0076–0.0098 XAUC. D2Q (Zhan et al., 2022) addresses duration bias via quantile regression but lags ProWTP by 0.65–1.20 RMSE. WLR (Covington et al., 2016) and OR (Crammer & Singer, 2001) are the weakest baselines, with ProWTP improving RMSE by 1.77–1.92 and 0.51–0.97 respectively. DVR (Zheng et al., 2022), which uses adversarial learning for debiasing, is consistently outperformed by ProWTP by 0.44–0.51 RMSE and 0.0074–0.0107 XAUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research threads: (1) VQ-VAE-based discrete representation learning (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), which is repurposed here as a hierarchical variant (HVQ-VAE) to generate prototypes from label distributions rather than from pre-trained instance representations as in prior prototype learning (Snell et al., 2017; Chang et al., 2022); and (2) unbalanced optimal transport (Chapel et al., 2021), specifically the semi-relaxed variant (SUOT), which is adapted to the mini-batch recommendation setting where strict mass conservation on the prototype side is infeasible. The paper departs from existing WTP methods (WLR, D2Q, TPM) that focus on loss design or label transformation by instead targeting instance representation confusion through distribution-level calibration, positioning itself as a model-agnostic regularizer rather than a task-specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of instance representation confusion as a root cause of WTP inaccuracy, supported by a mathematical proposition and t-SNE visualizations, provides a useful diagnostic lens for the broader recommendation literature. The model-agnostic design (applicable to any deep recommender) and the observation that watch-ratio distributions are stable over months make the approach practically deployable. However, all experiments use only MLP as the base model, leaving open whether the calibration benefit persists with more expressive architectures (e.g., transformers, multi-tower models). The offline HVQ-VAE stage also assumes distributional stationarity, and the paper's own stability analysis (Fig. 10) covers only ~10 months of a single platform, so robustness to regime shifts (e.g., new content categories, policy changes) remains untested.
