## Problem

Classical clustering of bandits (CB) algorithms improve multi-user contextual bandit performance by sharing data across similar users, but they require absolute numerical rewards as feedback. In many real-world settings such as recommendation systems and LLM prompt optimization, only pairwise preference feedback (which of two items is better) is available. Classical dueling bandit algorithms handle preference feedback but cannot exploit cross-user collaboration, leaving significant performance gains untapped. This paper introduces the first clustering of dueling bandit algorithms that combine both capabilities.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the system receives a user index i_t drawn uniformly from u users and a set of K context vectors (arms) in R^{d'} with ||x||_2 ≤ 1. The agent outputs a pair of arms (x_{t,1}, x_{t,2}) to present to the user, who returns a binary preference y_t ∈ {0,1} modeled via the Bradley-Terry-Luce logistic function. The algorithm also maintains and updates a dynamic graph over all users whose connected components encode the inferred clustering structure, with edges deleted when estimated reward functions diverge beyond a confidence threshold.

## Method

Both COLDB (linear) and CONDB (neural network) maintain a complete undirected graph over all users and iteratively delete edges to refine the clustering. Each round, the arriving user is assigned to its connected component, and a shared reward model is estimated via MLE over all historical preference data from that cluster: a common preference vector θ̄_t for COLDB, or a ReLU neural network trained with NTK-based regularization for CONDB. The first arm is selected greedily by maximizing the estimated reward, while the second arm is chosen via an upper confidence bound that balances exploration against the first arm. After observing the binary feedback, the individual user's reward estimate is updated, and edges to users whose estimates now differ by more than a data-dependent threshold f(T_{i,t}) + f(T_{ℓ,t}) are removed. Theoretical analysis shows that after O(u log T) rounds the graph correctly partitions users, and the subsequent regret scales as O(d√(mT)/κ_μ) for COLDB and O((√d̃/κ_μ + B√(λ/κ_μ))√(d̃mT)) for CONDB, where m is the number of clusters and d̃ is the NTK effective dimension.

## Results

The paper evaluates COLDB and CONDB on both synthetic and MovieLens datasets (200 users, K=20 arms, d=20 features). In the synthetic experiments, COLDB with m=2 clusters achieves substantially lower cumulative regret than with m=5 clusters, confirming the theoretical prediction that fewer clusters (more users per cluster) yield better performance. CONDB with a non-linear (square) reward function similarly shows improved regret when m=2 versus m=5. On the MovieLens dataset (m=5 clusters), both COLDB and CONDB consistently outperform their independent single-user baselines across all time horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: LDB_IND (independent Linear Dueling Bandits per user, from Bengs et al. 2022) for the linear setting, and NDB_IND (independent Neural Dueling Bandits per user, from Verma et al. 2024) for the non-linear setting. COLDB significantly outperforms LDB_IND in cumulative regret on both the synthetic dataset (for both m=2 and m=5) and the MovieLens dataset (m=5), with the gap widening over time as the clustering structure is learned. CONDB similarly achieves significantly smaller cumulative regret than NDB_IND on both synthetic and MovieLens experiments with the square reward function. In both cases, the improvement is most pronounced when m=2 (i.e., when more users share the same cluster), consistent with the theoretical regret bound scaling as O(d√(mT)) for COLDB and O((√d̃/κ_μ + B√(λ/κ_μ))√(d̃mT)) for CONDB. The paper does not report specific numerical regret values in tabular form; results are presented as cumulative regret curves (Figures 1 and 2) showing clear and consistent separation between the proposed methods and their independent baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: online clustering of bandits (Gentile et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2024a), which uses a dynamic graph to partition users into clusters that share data, and contextual dueling bandits (Bengs et al., 2022; Saha, 2021; Verma et al., 2024), which replaces numerical rewards with binary preference feedback under the Bradley-Terry-Luce model. The graph-based edge-deletion mechanism for cluster inference is inherited directly from the CB literature, while the UCB-based arm selection and BTL likelihood formulation come from the dueling bandit line. The neural extension (CONDB) further builds on the NTK-based analysis framework of Zhou et al. (2020) and Zhang et al. (2021), adapting the effective-dimension machinery of Verma et al. (2024) to the multi-user clustering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: in recommendation systems and LLM prompt optimization, users naturally provide pairwise preferences rather than absolute scores, yet prior multi-user bandit methods assumed numerical feedback. The regret bounds formally quantify the benefit of collaboration (scaling with number of clusters m rather than total users u) while capturing the additional difficulty of preference feedback through the 1/κ_μ factor. Open questions include how the methods behave under weaker cluster-separation assumptions, non-uniform user arrivals, or adversarial corruptions, and whether the graph-based clustering can be replaced by more scalable approaches for very large user populations (millions of users).
