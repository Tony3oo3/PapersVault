## Problem

Probabilistic Circuits (PCs) offer exact tractable inference but have been limited in scale because their sum blocks use dense matrices with quadratic cost in hidden size. Prior work exploited either sparsity (arbitrary pruning/growing) or tensorized operations, but no method combined structured sparsity with efficient tensorized computation, preventing PCs from scaling to competitive model sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252998/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text, the method consumes character sequences (Text8, vocab 27, chunks of 256) or SentencePiece tokens (LM1B, vocab 8192, packed sequences of length 128). For images, it operates on 8×8×3 = 192-dimensional patches extracted from ImageNet32/64 after a lossless YCoCg-R color transform, rather than full images. Outputs include exact log-likelihoods (bits-per-character or bits-per-dimension), generated samples, and marginal probabilities computed via a single graph traversal.

## Method

The key insight is that multiplying two compatible PCs produces sum blocks whose weight matrices are Kronecker products, which can be materialized as interleaved sums and permutations — exactly the structure of Monarch matrices. The paper replaces dense sum-block matrices with generalized Monarch matrices parameterized by 3D tensors, reducing per-block cost from O(h²) to O(h^{3/2}) for a 2-layer construction, and further to O(h·c·log_c(h)) by choosing the factorization base c to interpolate between Butterfly (c=2) and standard Monarch (c=√h). Training uses stochastic mini-batch EM, with a principled initialization obtained by multiplying smaller dense PCs and then untieing the resulting shared parameters. The architecture is instantiated as a Monarch-HMM for language modeling and a Monarch-HCLT (hidden Chow-Liu tree) for image patches, both trained end-to-end without task-specific optimization.

## Results

On Text8, Monarch-HMM achieves 1.57 BPC, outperforming the dense HMM baseline (1.69 BPC) and all other PC baselines (SparsePC 2.60, NPC2 3.17), while closing the gap to diffusion models (SEDD ≤1.47). On LM1B, Monarch-HMM achieves 190.34 perplexity versus 320.78 for HMM, a 41% improvement. On ImageNet32/64, Monarch-HCLT achieves 4.01/3.74 bpd (lossy) and 4.62/4.33 bpd (lossless), setting a new state-of-the-art among probabilistic circuits and surpassing LVD-PG (4.06/3.80 lossy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monarch-HMM decisively outperforms all PC baselines: it improves over HMM by 0.12 BPC on Text8 (1.57 vs 1.69) and by 130.44 perplexity on LM1B (190.34 vs 320.78), and beats LVD-PG by 0.05/0.06 bpd on ImageNet32/64 (lossy) and QPC by 0.45/0.68 bpd (lossless). Against non-PC generative models, Monarch-HMM surpasses both flow baselines (IAF/SCF 1.88, Argmax Coup Flow 1.80) and D3PM (≤1.61) on Text8, but remains behind SEDD (≤1.47) by approximately 0.1 BPC. On ImageNet, Monarch-HCLT outperforms all PC-based methods (LVD, LVD-PG, QPC) on both lossy and lossless metrics, yet still trails neural density estimators: VDM achieves 3.72/3.40 bpd (lossless) versus Monarch's 4.62/4.33, a gap of roughly 0.9 bpd. The paper also demonstrates that Monarch-HMM is 200× faster at inference than diffusion models (0.017s vs 3.60s per 256-character sample) while retaining exact tractability for marginal computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: tensorized probabilistic circuits (Peharz et al., 2020; Vergari et al., 2021) and structured sparse matrices for efficient linear algebra (Dao et al., 2022). The key conceptual insight is that circuit multiplication naturally produces Kronecker-product structures in sum blocks, which, when explicitly materialized and parameter-untilted, yield exactly the Monarch matrix construction. This departs from prior PC scaling efforts (Dang et al., 2022) that exploited arbitrary learned sparsity through pruning/growing, which is incompatible with tensorized execution, by instead imposing a structured sparsity pattern that remains fully parallelizable. The HMM architecture for character-level language modeling (Zhang et al., 2023) serves as the base model, with Monarch matrices replacing its dense transition matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the tractable probabilistic modeling community: Monarch parameterization enables scaling hidden sizes by orders of magnitude (2^15 → 2^19 for HMMs) while achieving better FLOPs-normalized performance, and it provides a principled initialization strategy via circuit multiplication. However, the paper identifies a clear memory bottleneck from caching hidden states during backpropagation that limits how much sparsity can be exploited in practice, and the image modeling results still lag substantially behind neural baselines (VDM, Glow), partly due to the lack of an effective homogeneous architecture analogous to HMMs for images. Open questions include whether sparsifying the hidden state representation itself (beyond the weight matrices) can overcome the memory wall, and whether the improved scaling unlocks meaningful gains in downstream PC applications like controllable generation and causal inference.
