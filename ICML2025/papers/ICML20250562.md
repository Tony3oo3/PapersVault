## Problem

Class distribution mismatch (CDM) arises when training data class distributions diverge from target task requirements. Existing approaches address CDM exclusively in semi-supervised settings, requiring labeled data that is often unavailable or expensive to obtain, and they perform poorly on open-set tasks where unknown and new classes must be grouped into a unified 'other' category. No unsupervised method has been explored for CDM, leaving a gap in both applicability and open-set classification performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and a predefined list of K known-class names formatted as text prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a [CLASS]'). No ground-truth labels are used at any point during training. The output is a WideResNet-28-2-based classifier that performs either K-way closed-set classification among known classes or (K+1)-way open-set classification where unknown and new classes are consolidated into a single 'other' category, with the open-set probability computed by combining K binary classifiers with a closed-set head via Eqs. (10)–(11).

## Method

UCDM constructs positive-negative training pairs entirely from unlabeled data using a frozen Stable Diffusion 2.0 model. For positive instances, a randomly sampled seed image is noised via the forward diffusion process and then denoised with a class-conditional prompt (σ_t=1 for diversity), yielding an image that embeds the target semantics while retaining the seed's visual structure. For negative instances, the paper introduces conditional DDIM inversion (Theorem 3.1), which progressively moves the latent away from the target class's log-likelihood, followed by an unconditional DDIM reverse (Theorem 3.2) that recovers visual fidelity while preserving the erased semantics. A confidence-based labeling mechanism then iteratively assigns pseudo-labels to high-confidence real images using both other-probability-driven and known-probability-driven scores, incorporating them into training with matched generated counterparts. The classifier is trained with a composite loss (Eq. 12) combining open-set binary cross-entropy on positive-negative pairs, a known-class maximization constraint, and a closed-set cross-entropy term, all without any human annotation.

## Results

On the open-set task (the paper's primary evaluation), UCDM achieves a balance score of 94.6 on CIFAR-10 (60% mismatch), with 94.0% known-class, 100.0% unknown-class, and 100.0% new-class accuracy—far exceeding the next-best method (OpenMatch at 47.7 balance score). On the more challenging Tiny-ImageNet (60% mismatch), UCDM reaches 22.9 balance score (15.8% known, 94.9% unknown, 95.4% new), while OpenMatch achieves only 3.0. On the closed-set task, UCDM attains 95.6% on CIFAR-10 (60% mismatch) and 32.3% on Tiny-ImageNet, competitive with or exceeding all semi-supervised baselines that use 40 labels per class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four SCDM_CT baselines (DS³L, UASD, CCSSL, T2T) achieve 0% accuracy on unknown and new classes in the open-set task, as they are designed only for closed-set classification and cannot identify out-of-distribution samples. Among SCDM methods, IOMatch degenerates on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet by classifying nearly all samples as 'other' (0% known-class accuracy, 100% unknown/new accuracy), yielding a balance score of only 9.0. MTCF is highly inconsistent, sometimes achieving 98.7% unknown accuracy but 0% known accuracy (CIFAR-100, 20% mismatch). OpenMatch is the closest competitor, achieving a balance score of 47.7 on CIFAR-10 (60% mismatch) and 3.0 on Tiny-ImageNet (60% mismatch), but UCDM surpasses it by 46.9 and 19.9 balance points respectively, while also achieving higher known-class accuracy (94.0% vs. 47.4% on CIFAR-10; 15.8% vs. 10.8% on Tiny-ImageNet). Critically, UCDM achieves these results without any labeled data, whereas all baselines require 40 labels per known class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the class distribution mismatch (CDM) literature, which has been addressed exclusively through semi-supervised methods (SCDM_CT for closed-set and SCDM for open-set tasks), into a fully unsupervised setting where no ground-truth labels are available—only a predefined set of class names. Methodologically, it repurposes DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) in a novel way: conditional DDIM inversion is used to *erase* a semantic class from a seed image (creating negatives), while conditional DDIM sampling *adds* the target class (creating positives). Two theorems formally justify that this inversion-reverse pipeline removes class semantics while preserving visual structure, providing a theoretical foundation absent from prior diffusion-based data augmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any labeled data, UCDM removes a significant practical barrier in CDM scenarios where annotation is costly or infeasible, and it achieves 100% unknown-class accuracy on CIFAR-10—something no prior semi-supervised method matches. However, known-class accuracy on Tiny-ImageNet remains modest (15–22%), suggesting the approach struggles when the number of known classes grows and visual discrimination becomes harder. The authors acknowledge that limited prompt variability constrains positive-instance diversity, and integrating a large language model for richer prompts is identified as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet) and whether the reliance on a frozen pretrained diffusion model limits adaptability to domain-specific data.
