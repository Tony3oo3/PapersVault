## Problem

Finding effective 2:4 semi-structured sparsity masks for LLMs is challenging because existing methods (Wanda, SparseGPT) rely on local, layer-wise heuristic criteria (Hessian scores, activation magnitudes) that cannot leverage global cross-layer feedback. The rigid, non-differentiable nature of mask selection further prevents end-to-end optimization, causing premature commitment to suboptimal pruning decisions.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained LLM weights and a small calibration set (~100–400 C4 samples) as input, and produces a Boolean 2:4 sparsity mask of the same shape as the weight tensor. Crucially, no weight updates occur after the mask is determined: the final pruned model is simply W₀ ⊙ Mask, meaning the retained weights are exactly the original pretrained values. The optimization operates on a continuous weight vector W (not a separate mask variable), with the mask extracted at the end via magnitude-based projection.

## Method

ProxSparse reformulates the discrete mask-selection problem as a continuous regularized optimization over the weight vector, using two structure-inducing regularizers: Reg_{2:4} (a sum of cubic absolute-value terms that is zero iff the 4-element block is 2:4 sparse) and Reg_{W₀} (a weighted L² penalty that discourages deviation from pretrained values, with stronger penalty for larger weights). The optimization is solved via proximal gradient descent: a standard SGD/Adam step on the differentiable part (loss + Reg_{W₀}), followed by a proximal operator on Reg_{2:4}. The proximal operator is solved by EnumALM, an alternating minimization scheme that iterates coordinate-wise soft-thresholding with adaptively chosen thresholds, running 10× faster than projected gradient descent and 100× faster than interior-point methods. This end-to-end formulation allows gradient feedback to flow across all layers simultaneously, enabling the mask to evolve gradually with 'wiggling room' rather than committing abruptly, which the paper shows is critical for avoiding suboptimal local solutions.

## Results

ProxSparse achieves the best Wikitext PPL and average zero-shot accuracy across all 7 evaluated LLMs (Mistral-v0.1-7b, Mistral-v0.3-7b, Qwen2.5-14b, OpenLlama-7b-v2, Llama-2-7b, Llama-2-13b, Llama-3.1-8b) under 2:4 semi-structured sparsity. Headline improvements include a 35% PPL reduction on Llama-2-7b (8.51 vs. Wanda's 11.42), a 28% PPL reduction on OpenLlama-7b-v2 (9.91 vs. 13.81), and a 20% average accuracy gain on Mistral-v0.1-7b (52.7% vs. 44.1%). ProxSparse also outperforms SparseGPT (which updates weights) by up to 18% in PPL, and delivers 1.26× end-to-end inference speedup with 29.5–37.3% memory reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxSparse wins against all 7 baselines on every model tested. Against Wanda (the strongest no-weight-update baseline), ProxSparse reduces PPL by up to 35% (Llama-2-7b: 8.51 vs. 11.42) and improves average zero-shot accuracy by up to 20% (Mistral-v0.1-7b: 52.7% vs. 44.1%). Against SparseGPT (which updates weights to minimize reconstruction error), ProxSparse still achieves lower PPL by up to 18% (Llama-2-7b: 8.51 vs. 10.30) and higher average accuracy, demonstrating that global mask learning outweighs local weight reconstruction. Against magnitude pruning, ProxSparse wins by large margins (e.g., PPL 8.51 vs. 54.74 on Llama-2-7b). Against ADMMPrune, ProxSparse achieves lower PPL (8.51 vs. 9.67 on Llama-2-7b; 8.68 vs. 9.06 on Mistral-v0.3-7b) and higher average accuracy. Against OWL and AlphaPrune (which use layer-varying sparsity ratios), ProxSparse outperforms on both PPL and accuracy, with the authors noting that varying ratios are less effective under fine-grained 2:4 patterns. Against MaskLLM (which requires ~100,000 calibration samples), ProxSparse achieves substantially better PPL with only 100–400 samples (e.g., 9.91 vs. >13 on Llama-2-7b at 100 samples), positioning the two methods as complementary across different data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProxSparse builds directly on the 2:4-sparsity regularizer and proximal operator formulation from Kübler et al. (2025), replacing their Interior Point Method solver with a faster alternating minimization (EnumALM) that achieves better solutions at 100× lower cost. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant layer-wise heuristic paradigm of SparseGPT (Hessian-based) and Wanda (activation-aware magnitude), which solve local per-layer optimization problems, by reframing mask selection as an end-to-end regularized optimization with global gradient feedback. It also contrasts with MaskLLM, another learning-based approach, by operating with ~1000× fewer calibration samples (~100 vs. ~100,000) and using proximal gradient descent rather than Gumbel-Softmax sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since 2:4 sparsity is natively supported by Nvidia Ampere and later GPUs, a method that produces high-quality masks without post-pruning weight updates has direct deployment value for LLM serving. The consistent 20–35% PPL and ~20% zero-shot accuracy gains over SOTA baselines across seven models from four families make this a practically significant improvement for model compression. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to denser patterns (4:8, 1:8) where the regularizer's search space grows, whether the surprisingly weak dependence on the frozen-weight regularizer (λ₂) indicates an implicit constraint from the sparsity regularizer, and how the method scales to models with tens of billions of parameters.
