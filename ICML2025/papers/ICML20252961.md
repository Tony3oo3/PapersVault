## Problem

CLIP's distributed multimodal representations remain difficult to interpret, and existing sparse autoencoder methods face a fundamental trade-off: L1-regularized ReLU SAEs suffer from activation shrinkage that underestimates feature magnitudes, while TopK SAEs impose rigid fixed-sparsity constraints that misalign with the natural concept density across CLIP's embedding space. No prior SAE architecture can simultaneously optimize reconstruction fidelity and sparsity without compromising one for the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252961/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-pooled CLIP embeddings (768-dim for ViT-L/14, 512-dim for ViT-B/16) from either the image or text modality, which are first centered by per-modality mean and scaled so that E[||x||₂] = √n. The encoder produces a sparse activation vector of dimension d = expansion_rate × n (e.g., 6144, 12288, or 24576 for ViT-L/14), and the decoder reconstructs the original embedding. At inference, all TopK constraints are removed, leaving only ReLU gating so the model adaptively selects the number of active features per input without a fixed k.

## Method

MSAE replaces the single TopK or L1 sparsity mechanism with a sequence of h nested TopK operations at progressively increasing k values (powers of 2 up to d), computing h latent representations z₁, …, zₕ per input and summing their reconstruction losses weighted by coefficients αᵢ. Two weighting strategies are evaluated: uniform (αᵢ = 1) and reverse (αᵢ = h−i+1), where reverse weighting emphasizes sparser reconstructions and yields higher L₀ sparsity with minimal fidelity loss. The nested structure guarantees TopK₁ ⊆ TopK₂ ⊆ … ⊆ TopKₕ, creating a coherent hierarchy from coarse to fine-grained concepts. At inference, TopK is discarded entirely, allowing the ReLU-gated full-dimension representation to be used for concept manipulation, similarity search, and bias analysis without any fixed sparsity budget.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with CLIP ViT-L/14 (expansion rate 8), MSAE (RW) achieves 0.997 cosine similarity, 0.007 FVU, and 82.9% L0 sparsity with only 4 dead neurons, while MSAE (UW) reaches 0.999 CS and 0.002 FVU at 74.8% sparsity. The paper's headline claim of 0.99 CS with <0.1 FVU at ~80% sparsity is realized by the RW variant. MSAE also extracts over 120 validated semantic concepts from CLIP's representation space, enabling concept-based similarity search and gender bias detection on CelebA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReLU SAE (λ=0.003), MSAE (RW) achieves substantially higher sparsity (82.9% vs. 64.9% L₀) at a small fidelity cost (FVU 0.007 vs. 0.004, CS 0.997 vs. 0.998), while also improving CKNNA alignment (0.809 vs. 0.781) and avoiding the activation shrinkage inherent to L₁ regularization. Against TopK SAE (k=256), MSAE (RW) achieves lower FVU (0.007 vs. 0.011) and fewer dead neurons (4 vs. 296), though TopK retains a sparsity advantage (90.0% vs. 82.9% L₀) and slightly better CKNNA (0.874 vs. 0.809). Against BatchTopK (k=256), MSAE (RW) matches or exceeds fidelity (FVU 0.007 vs. 0.010) while reducing dead neurons from 919 to 4, though BatchTopK achieves marginally higher L₀ (88.2% vs. 82.9%). The key differentiator is the Pareto frontier: MSAE is the only method that simultaneously improves on both sparsity and fidelity relative to any single baseline configuration, and it is the only architecture where performance consistently improves with higher expansion rates across all metrics. In the progressive recovery experiment, MSAE combines TopK's efficient high-sparsity performance with ReLU's continuous scaling, surpassing TopK's plateau beyond k=10 while maintaining near-TopK quality at the highest sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSAE adapts the Matryoshka representation learning principle (Kusupati et al., 2022) to the sparse autoencoder setting, replacing the single TopK sparsity constraint of Gao et al. (2025) with a nested sequence of TopK operations at increasing k values. This departs from both the ReLU SAE lineage (Bricken et al., 2023), which suffers from L1-induced activation shrinkage, and the fixed-k TopK SAE, which imposes rigid sparsity that misaligns with natural concept density. For CLIP-specific interpretability, the paper builds on Rao et al. (2024) for automated concept naming via decoder-to-vocabulary cosine matching, and on Bhalla et al. (2024) for cross-modal embedding normalization during SAE training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSAE provides a practical, well-evidenced tool for decomposing CLIP's multimodal embeddings into interpretable monosemantic features, enabling downstream applications like concept-controlled retrieval and bias auditing that were previously difficult with less flexible SAE variants. The consistent Pareto frontier improvement across two CLIP architectures, three expansion rates, and both modalities strengthens confidence in the architectural choice. Key open questions include whether the multi-granularity training overhead can be eliminated via parallelized CUDA kernels, whether the approach extends to other VLMs (SigLIP, LLaVA), and how to handle complex or compositional concepts that fall outside a fixed vocabulary. The finding that text-trained SAEs generalize better cross-modally than image-trained ones is a useful practical insight for multimodal interpretability pipelines.
