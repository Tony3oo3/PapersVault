## Problem

Existing safety alignment methods teach LLMs to refuse harmful queries by instinct (System 1 thinking), making them brittle against jailbreak attacks that obscure the initial refusal-triggering tokens. This reliance on direct refusals also creates a safety-performance trade-off, as aggressive refusal training degrades helpfulness, truthfulness, and robustness. The paper asks whether equipping models with deliberate, safety-aware chain-of-thought reasoning (System 2 thinking) can simultaneously improve jailbreak resistance and preserve general capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text query and produces a structured multi-step reasoning trace where each step is enclosed in <|Reasoning_step|>…<|/Reasoning_step|> tokens with a title and detailed analysis, followed by a final answer block <|Output|>…<|/Output|> containing either a helpful response or a concise refusal. During test-time scaling, a separately trained process reward model scores partial reasoning trajectories (same-depth node pairs from the SI-MCTS search trees) to guide Best-of-N or Beam Search selection over N full-trajectory candidates, adding roughly 2–4× inference latency depending on the search budget.

## Method

STAIR is a three-stage framework. First, the model is SFT-trained on ~20k GPT-4o-generated structured CoT examples to internalize the reasoning format. Second, an iterative self-improvement loop (K=3) uses Safety-Informed MCTS: the model generates reasoning steps as search nodes, rollouts to final answers are scored by a self-rewarding mechanism, and a theoretically derived reward R(H,S)=S·H+2S (proven to satisfy safety-as-priority, dual monotonicity of helpfulness, and degeneration to single-objective MCTS) backpropagates safety information to internal nodes; step-level DPO is then performed on threshold-sampled preference pairs from the search trees. Third, a process reward model trained via Bradley-Terry on the same SI-MCTS trees enables test-time scaling through Best-of-N or Beam Search, improving both safety and helpfulness as compute increases.

## Results

On StrongReject (average goodness score over PAIR and PAP-Misrepresentation jailbreaks), STAIR-DPO-3 achieves 0.8798 on LLaMA-3.1-8B and 0.8486 on Qwen-2-7B, outperforming the best baseline (SACPO at 0.7264) by 0.15 on LLaMA. Simultaneously, STAIR improves AlpacaEval winning rate against GPT-4 by 13.11 points on LLaMA (38.66% vs. 25.55% base) and 6.25 points on Qwen (26.31% vs. 20.06% base), while most baselines degrade helpfulness. With test-time scaling via Beam Search, STAIR reaches a StrongReject average of 0.9391, comparable to Claude-3.5 (0.9359).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAIR-DPO-3 outperforms all baselines on StrongReject for both LLaMA (0.8798 vs. SACPO's 0.7264, DPO's 0.5054, Self-Rewarding's 0.4633) and Qwen (0.8486 vs. SACPO's 0.5577). Against SACPO, which is the strongest safety-focused baseline, STAIR gains 0.15 on StrongReject while SACPO severely degrades SimpleQA (0.74% vs. STAIR's 6.38%) and AlpacaEval (20.44% vs. 38.66%). Standard DPO and SFT baselines suffer significant safety-performance trade-offs: DPO drops AlpacaEval to 15.26% (vs. 25.55% base) while only reaching 0.5054 on StrongReject. Self-Rewarding achieves high XsTest refusal (99%) but fails to generalize to jailbreaks (StrongReject 0.4633), confirming that direct-refusal training is insufficient. With test-time scaling (Beam Search), STAIR reaches 0.9391 average StrongReject, surpassing GPT-4o (0.3772), Qwen-Max (0.3728), and DeepSeek-R1 (0.3052), and matching Claude-3.5 (0.9359). Against open-source o1-like models (LLaMA-o1, Skywork-o1, OpenO1, DeepSeek-r1-Distilled), STAIR-DPO-3 achieves substantially higher jailbreak resistance (e.g., 0.7919 on PAIR vs. 0.2987–0.4441 for o1-like models) while maintaining competitive GSM8k scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAIR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) safety alignment via preference optimization (DPO, SACPO, Safe RLHF), which the paper argues is insufficient because it teaches models to refuse by instinct (System 1) rather than through deliberative risk analysis; (2) MCTS-based reasoning enhancement for LLMs (Vodopivec et al., 2017; Wan et al., 2024), adapted here into SI-MCTS with a theoretically grounded safety-informed reward; and (3) self-rewarding and iterative self-improvement (Yuan et al., 2024; Pang et al., 2024), used to eliminate external annotation. The key conceptual departure from Deliberative Alignment (Guan et al., 2024) is that STAIR does not require a pre-existing powerful reasoning model—it bootstraps safety-aware reasoning from a standard instruction-tuned LLM via structured CoT SFT followed by iterative step-level DPO on SI-MCTS-generated data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that an 8B open-source model can reach safety performance comparable to Claude-3.5 (0.94 vs. 0.94 average goodness on StrongReject) while simultaneously improving helpfulness, truthfulness, and robustness—directly addressing the long-standing safety-performance trade-off. The theoretical characterization of the safety-informed reward (Theorem 2.1) provides a principled design space, and the finding that even the simplest valid form R = S·H + 2S performs well lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger or multimodal models, how it interacts with emerging reasoning models that already have strong System 2 capabilities, and whether the ~30-hour training cost on 8 A800s is prohibitive for frequent re-alignment as new jailbreak strategies emerge.
