## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on machine unlearning suffer from three critical limitations: they require pre-defined attack targets (making them inflexible), they must be re-executed from scratch for each new target (making them inefficient for sequential requests), and they are unstable due to the non-convexity of loss functions causing unpredictable parameter trajectories. This paper is the first to address all three issues simultaneously, enabling target-agnostic, on-demand, and stable adversarial attacks on deployed MU models.

## Input / Output

The attacker injects a poisoning dataset constructed from clean instances by sampling group centers and generating perturbed samples (e.g., Gaussian-distributed) around them within a fixed MU budget (5–20% of training data). At attack time, the attacker submits unlearning requests specifying which poisoned samples to remove, and the model's parameters shift toward an attacker-chosen target w_t. The output is a manipulated model that misclassifies inputs into a target class (targeted) or any wrong class (untargeted), with the attack remaining dormant (ASR = 0) until the unlearning request is processed.

## Method

DDPA operates in two stages. In the poisoning stage, it first applies a convex polyhedral approximation (via Carathéodory's theorem) to identify group centers whose neighborhoods are near-convex on the original loss function, minimizing the deviation between the original and convex loss to ensure stable gradient behavior during unlearning. These group centers are then mapped to parameter-space 'thrust vectors' via the conjugate algorithm, and a maximum regular simplex is constructed over them using an optimization objective that jointly maximizes pairwise vertex distances, centers the centroid at the origin, and enforces John's-ellipsoid regularity conditions. In the attack stage, the attacker selects a target parameter w_t, computes the deviation Δw = w_t − w, and issues unlearning requests for a subset of thrust vectors whose combined removal direction approximates −Δw, steering the model parameter toward w_t. Theoretical results (Theorems 3.1–3.4) prove the John ellipsoid of the constructed simplex is its inscribed ball, quantify the regularity measure φ(V), and derive the proportion ρ of the effective Gaussian parameter space occupied by the simplex, guaranteeing coverage for arbitrary target directions.

## Results

On VGG-16 + CIFAR-100 (5% unlearned), DDPA achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all five unlearning algorithms: 92.0% (First-Order), 91.0% (Second-Order), 88.0% (Unroll-SGD), 89.4% (Amnesiac), and 89.7% (SISA), while maintaining 0% ASR before unlearning (high stealth). Across three model-dataset combinations, DDPA achieves an average ASR increase of 22.74% (VGG16-CIFAR100), 16.07% (ResNet18-Tiny-ImageNet), and 21.45% (LLaMA-3B-SST-2) over baselines. In the target-agnostic setting with 20 potential targets, DDPA reaches 88.3% targeted ASR and 91.6% untargeted ASR, while the best competing method drops to as low as 5.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPA outperforms all nine baselines on every unlearning algorithm. On First-Order unlearning, DDPA (92.0%) exceeds the strongest baseline UBA-Inf (89.0%) by 3.0 points; on Second-Order, it edges out SSCSF (90.0%) by 1.0 point; on Unroll-SGD, it beats SSCSF (86.93%) by ~1.1 points; on Amnesiac, it surpasses SSCSF (88.0%) by 1.4 points; and on SISA, it leads RMBMU (86.74%) by ~3.0 points. The gap is most pronounced against EVMUS (79.2–81.8% ASR) and BAU (70.8–80.0%), which struggle with non-convex loss landscapes and target-specific design. In the target-agnostic evaluation (20 potential targets), DDPA maintains 88.3% targeted ASR while all baselines collapse to 5.9–50%+, confirming that pre-defined-target methods cannot adapt without re-poisoning. DDPA also achieves the lowest running time for multi-attack scenarios (2–5 sequential unlearning requests) since it reuses a single pre-constructed poisoning dataset, whereas baselines must rebuild poisoned data per target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on machine unlearning initiated by Zhang et al. (2023, BAU) and subsequent works (AwoP, MUECPA, SSCSF, UBA-Inf, RMBMU, DABF, AdvUA, EVMUS), all of which require pre-defining attack targets during the poisoning phase. The key departure is the introduction of target-agnostic, on-demand attacks via a geometric framework: convex polyhedral approximation (grounded in Carathéodory's theorem) to stabilize non-convex loss landscapes, and regular simplex construction (grounded in John's theorem) to maximize parameter-space coverage. The 'thrust vector control' analogy from aerospace engineering provides an intuitive mechanism for dynamically steering model parameters toward arbitrary targets at unlearning time, rather than committing to a single target at poisoning time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As machine unlearning systems are increasingly deployed to satisfy GDPR/CCPA compliance (e.g., Stable Diffusion's artist opt-out), demonstrating that a single poisoning dataset can support arbitrary, on-demand attacks across multiple unlearning requests raises the bar for defense design. The target-agnostic flexibility gap (88.3% ASR vs. 5.9% for baselines forced to distribute across 20 targets) suggests that defenses must account for unknown attack objectives, not just known triggers. Open questions include whether the Gaussian parameter-space assumption generalizes to very large or non-convex architectures, whether the convex polyhedral approximation remains tractable in high-dimensional settings, and whether practical defenses (e.g., unlearning verification, influence-function auditing) can neutralize the simplex-based parameter manipulation.
