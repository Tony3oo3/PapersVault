## Problem

While mechanistic interpretability has established that high-level concepts are encoded as linear directions in transformer activations and can steer generation, no general method exists to implement logical implication (conditional behavior) directly into pretrained models. Models can internally detect concepts like hallucination or toxicity but frequently fail to translate that detection into appropriate generation behavior, and traditional fine-tuning or prompting is opaque, data-hungry, and prone to off-task regression. The paper asks whether one can hand-engineer interpretable, neuro-symbolic logic circuits into transformer representations to enforce 'if concept P is present, behave according to Q' with minimal data and compute.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained transformer (Mistral 7B Instruct v0.2), small contrastive datasets of 100 examples each defining the condition concept P and the target behavior Q, and produces a modified model with a single LIMS circuit inserted at the attention output projection of one transformer block (layer 17 of 32). The circuit operates at every token position during inference but is calibrated on the last-token representation, and the entire extraction and insertion process requires only forward-pass inference compute (no backpropagation), peaking at ~19 GB versus ~270 GB for DPO fine-tuning. The m-LIMS variant merges the circuit into existing weight matrices with zero architecture change, enabling drop-in deployment.

## Method

LIMS extracts a sensing vector p via contrastive mean difference between P and ¬P hidden states (with projective removal of ¬P-aligned components) and a steering vector q via mean difference between Q and ¬Q∩P states, both at the last-token position of the attention output projection space. It then constructs a conditional circuit f_{q,p}(x) = q·σ(p^T h(x) − b_p) that adds the steering vector q to the residual stream only when the sensing perceptron activates, and inserts this as an additive term into the layer's attention output projection. The m-LIMS variant replaces the nonlinear gating with the linear outer product q·p^T added to the projection matrix W, making the circuit mergeable into existing parameters. The approach is grounded in a formal neuro-symbolic logic over concept-predicates (Definition 3.1) and the linear representation hypothesis (Definition 3.2), and Proposition 3.3 provides a decoupled statistical model that predicts full LIMS performance from independently measurable sensing and steering accuracies, enabling interpretable risk estimation from as few as 100 training examples.

## Results

Using only 100 training examples on Mistral-7B-Instruct, LIMS achieves 83.0% accuracy on HaluEval (vs. 53.3% base), 79.6% on SQuAD 2 (vs. 61.8% base), and 85.0% on AdvBench (vs. 61.7% base), while maintaining MT-Bench open-ended generation scores at 7.30–7.33 (base: 7.30). The mergeable variant m-LIMS reaches 94.8% on AdvBench and 84.7% on HaluEval. On the SQuAD 2 task, LIMS with 500 examples (81.4%) outperforms DPO with 20,000 examples (81.3%), demonstrating extreme data efficiency. For automatic chain-of-thought steering on GSM8K, LIMS recovers 72.2% of the COT-prompt performance (normalized) versus 55.5% for the base model, while selectively extending reasoning tokens only for math inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), LIMS trails on HaluEval (83.0% vs. 98.9%) and AdvBench (85.0% vs. 99.8%), but DPO exhibits severe task overfitting: its MT-Bench open-ended generation score drops from 7.30 to 6.72 on HaluEval and 6.93 on AdvBench, while LIMS maintains scores at or above the base model (7.30–7.33). On SQuAD 2, LIMS (79.6% with 100 examples; 81.4% with 500) substantially outperforms DPO (61.9%), and even LIMS with 500 examples edges out DPO with 20,000 examples (81.4% vs. 81.3%). LIMS also requires far less memory (peak 18.9 GB vs. DPO's 270.2 GB on HaluEval) and does not require backpropagation through the model. Compared to 10-shot prompting, LIMS wins on all three QA tasks (HaluEval 83.0 vs. 50.5, SQuAD 2 79.6 vs. 78.6, AdvBench 85.0 vs. 52.6), with 10-shot prompting actually degrading below the base model on HaluEval and AdvBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering paradigm (Turner et al., 2024) and the linear representation hypothesis formalization (Park et al., 2023) by introducing a *conditional* steering circuit that implements logical implication (P→Q) rather than unconditional vector addition. Unlike prior steering work that applies a fixed vector to all inputs, or hallucination-specific circuits (Wang et al., 2024b) that the authors explicitly frame as a special case, LIMS formalizes a general neuro-symbolic logic with decoupled sensing (perceptron) and steering components, enabling composable multi-rule programs. The method also departs from memory editing approaches (Meng et al., Mitchell et al.) by operating on high-level behavioral concepts rather than discrete factual associations, and from neuro-symbolic methods that treat the transformer as a black box by operating directly on internal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is extreme data and compute efficiency: LIMS requires only 100 labeled examples and inference-level compute (no backpropagation) to induce large behavioral shifts, outperforming DPO with 20,000 examples on SQuAD 2. The decoupled interpretability framework—where sensing accuracy and steering success can be independently measured and used to predict overall performance from training data alone—offers a diagnostic tool absent in fine-tuning or prompting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Mistral-7B and a single middle layer, whether more complex multi-hop logic scales reliably, and whether the m-LIMS mergeable variant's slight interpretability loss is acceptable for production deployment. The paper also leaves open whether coupling LIMS circuits across modalities or between different pretrained models can compose agent capabilities effectively.
