## Problem

Prior work in the weak-strong oracle model (Bateni et al., 2024) achieved only constant-factor approximations (>10) for standard k-clustering, and no prior work addressed fair k-median clustering under this model. The paper asks whether one can construct (1+ε)-approximate fair coresets with near-optimal strong oracle query complexity, bridging the gap between expensive exact distance queries and cheap but unreliable weak oracle estimates while enforcing disparate-impact fairness constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n points in a metric space where pairwise distances are accessible only through a weak oracle (exact with probability 2/3, adversarially wrong with probability 1/3, drawn once) and a strong oracle (deterministically exact but costly), along with group labels specifying (α,β)-fairness constraints per point. It outputs a weighted coreset of size Õ(Λ·k²/ε²) that preserves fair k-median costs within a (1+ε) factor for all center sets of size ≤k, using Õ(Λ·k) strong oracle point queries and Õ(Λ·nk) weak oracle queries. The coreset weights are estimated from sample counts rather than exact ring sizes, since the algorithm cannot afford strong queries on all points.

## Method

The core technique combines heavy-hitter ring sampling with recursive peeling: after obtaining an O(1)-approximate clustering via the weak oracle, points are partitioned into logarithmic-distance rings around each center, and in each iteration the algorithm samples O(k·polylog(n)/ε³) points, uses strong oracle queries to determine their exact ring assignments, identifies 'heavy' rings (those with ≥Ω(k·polylog(n)/ε³) samples), and builds weighted coresets only for those rings. Light rings are either charged to adjacent heavy rings (for the unconstrained case) or uniformly sampled from the full peeled set (for the fair/assignment-preserving case). The peeling step removes all points within the outermost heavy ring's radius using a distance estimator from Bateni et al. (2024), and the process recurses until all points are processed in O(log²n) iterations. For fairness, the algorithm samples uniformly from all peeled points rather than only heavy rings, requiring a white-box re-proof of assignment-preservation lemmas from Cohen-Addad & Li (2019) to handle point sets spanning multiple rings.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is the first (1+ε)-coreset for fair k-median clustering in the weak-strong oracle model, with coreset size Õ(Λ·k²/ε²) and Õ(Λ·k) strong oracle queries (Theorem 1). For the assignment-preserving (Λ=1) case, the algorithm uses O(k log⁴n) strong queries and O(nk log³n) weak queries (Theorem 2), which is optimal up to polylog(n) factors given the Ω(k) lower bound from Bateni et al. (2024). For general (k,z)-clustering without fairness, the coreset size is Õ(k²/ε³) with Õ(k²/ε³) strong queries (Theorem 3). Empirically, on the Adult (~50K instances) and Credit Card (~30K instances) datasets, the proposed fair coreset of size 100 consistently achieves lower relative cost than a uniform-sampling baseline across all values of k, using only ~200 strong oracle queries (10% of the subsampled data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its fair coreset algorithm against a single baseline: uniform random sampling with re-weighting (selecting 100 points uniformly at random from the Meyerson-subsampled dataset of ~2000 points). On both the Adult and Credit Card datasets, the proposed algorithm's coreset consistently outperforms the uniform baseline in relative fair k-median cost across all tested values of k (Figure 1). The improvement is achieved while using only ~200 strong oracle queries, which constitutes just 10% of the subsampled data size. The paper notes that for larger datasets, the fraction of strong oracle queries relative to dataset size is expected to decrease further. No comparison is made against other fair clustering coreset methods (e.g., Huang et al. 2019, Schmidt et al. 2019, Bandyapadhyay et al. 2024) since those methods assume full distance access and do not operate in the weak-strong oracle model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of fair clustering coresets (Huang et al. 2019; Braverman et al. 2022; Cohen-Addad & Li 2019) and the weak-strong oracle model introduced by Bateni et al. (2024), which previously achieved only constant-factor (>10) approximations for standard k-clustering with no fairness guarantees. The technical approach adapts the ring-sampling framework of Chen (2009) with a novel heavy-hitter sampling and recursive peeling mechanism to handle the oracle constraints, while the fairness guarantees are obtained by white-boxing the assignment-preserving coreset machinery of Cohen-Addad & Li (2019) and Braverman et al. (2022) to work with non-ring point sets. The reduction from assignment-preserving coresets to fair coresets follows Huang et al. (2019), and the weak coreset and distance estimator primitives are taken directly from Bateni et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first (1+ε)-coreset for fair k-median clustering in the weak-strong oracle model, closing a gap where prior work offered only constant-factor approximations without fairness. The near-optimal query complexity (matching the Ω(k) lower bound up to polylog factors) makes the result practically relevant for settings where expensive similarity models (e.g., large-scale embeddings) must be queried sparingly. Open questions include the optimal k-dependence for general (k,z)-clustering coresets, extending the approach to other fairness notions beyond disparate impact, and whether the white-box adaptation of ring-sampling lemmas can be generalized to reduce the coreset size further. The experimental validation on two tabular datasets is preliminary, and the algorithm's practical utility at scale remains to be demonstrated.
