## Problem

The KV cache in Transformer-based LLMs scales linearly with sequence length, creating a severe memory bandwidth and capacity bottleneck during the decode phase. Existing KV cache compression methods—whether permanent eviction or dynamic selection—fail to accurately predict the top-k important KV tokens under low token budgets, resulting in substantial accuracy drops compared to an oracle top-k attention scheme. The paper identifies that no single-stage, single-dimension approach can simultaneously achieve high compression ratios and maintain accuracy.

## Input / Output

RocketKV operates on the KV cache produced during the prefill phase of a text prompt, applying compression exclusively during the decode phase without modifying prefill attention. The user specifies a target compression ratio (or equivalently a token budget per attention group), and the method adaptively decomposes this across its two stages. The output is generated text identical in format to full-KV inference, but with up to 400× fewer KV tokens fetched per attention operation. For GQA models, the token budget is defined per attention group rather than per head, and the method is fully compatible with FlashAttention and tensor parallelism.

## Method

RocketKV is a training-free, two-stage KV cache compression framework. Stage 1 performs coarse-grain permanent eviction using SnapKV (with a larger pooling kernel size of 63 and per-group token selection for GQA) to remove low-importance KV tokens from the input prompt. Stage 2 applies a novel Hybrid Sparse Attention (HSA) mechanism that estimates top-k KV indices via two-dimensional reduction: it first groups keys into consecutive pages storing element-wise max/min values (inspired by Quest), then selects only k₁ heads with the largest |q| magnitude (inspired by SparQ) to compute approximate per-page attention scores, and finally fetches the original K/V vectors at the top-k₂ predicted indices for exact sparse attention. An adaptive compression decomposition formula r = min(0.2 + 0.06·log₂(c), 0.8) splits the overall compression ratio c into c^r for Stage 1 and c^(1−r) for Stage 2, with HSA further splitting its share evenly between sequence and head dimensions. A multi-turn variant, RocketKV-MT, retains all KV tokens in memory across turns (sacrificing storage savings) while still restricting each turn's decode to the dynamically selected subset, achieving near-oracle accuracy in multi-turn settings.

## Results

RocketKV achieves up to 400× KV cache compression with negligible accuracy loss: on LongBench (Llama3.1-8B-Ins), it scores 51.1% average at a token budget of 256 (vs. 52.2% Full-KV, only 1.1% drop) and 100% on Needle-in-a-Haystack at the same budget. In efficiency experiments on an NVIDIA A100, RocketKV delivers up to 3.7× end-to-end decode speedup and 32.6% peak memory reduction compared to Full-KV. For multi-turn scenarios, RocketKV-MT reaches 50.5% average on SCBench at token budget 16384, nearly matching the oracle Exact-TopK (50.8%) and Full-KV (49.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RocketKV consistently outperforms all four baselines (DuoAttention, SnapKV, Quest, SparQ) across LongBench, NIAH, and RULER at every token budget tested. At the most aggressive budget of 256 tokens on LongBench (Llama3.1-8B-Ins), RocketKV scores 51.1% versus SnapKV's 42.6%, SparQ's 25.7%, Quest's 17.8%, and DuoAttention's 37.6%—a gap of 8.5–33.3 points. On NIAH at 256 tokens, RocketKV achieves 100% while Quest and SparQ collapse to ~10% and DuoAttention to 20%, though SnapKV remains competitive at 98.3%. SparQ is the strongest single-dimension baseline at higher budgets (e.g., 51.1% LongBench at 1024 tokens) but still trails RocketKV (52.1%) and degrades sharply on Mistral-7B-Ins-v0.2. For multi-turn SCBench, the original RocketKV lags behind SparQ at budgets ≥8192 (35.2% vs. 38.3% at 8192), but the RocketKV-MT variant closes this gap entirely, reaching 49.6% at 8192 and 50.5% at 16384—within 0.3 points of the oracle Exact-TopK.

## Relation to Prior Work

RocketKV sits at the intersection of two established lines of KV cache compression research: permanent eviction methods (SnapKV, H2O, Ada-KV) that reduce both storage and bandwidth, and dynamic selection methods (Quest, SparQ, Loki) that reduce bandwidth but retain full storage. Rather than choosing one category, RocketKV composes them into a two-stage pipeline—adopting SnapKV for coarse-grain eviction and proposing Hybrid Sparse Attention (HSA) for fine-grain selection that performs two-dimensional reduction across both sequence and head dimensions, in contrast to the single-dimension approximations used by Quest (sequence) or SparQ (head). The adaptive compression decomposition mechanism, which splits a target compression ratio across stages and dimensions via a log-scaled formula, is a novel scheduling contribution not present in prior single-stage methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 400× compression with negligible accuracy loss, 3.7× end-to-end speedup, and 32.6% peak memory reduction, all training-free and compatible with FlashAttention, tensor parallelism, and disaggregated serving. However, several limitations remain open: RocketKV-MT sacrifices all storage savings in multi-turn settings (retaining full KV history), the demonstrated speedups use a Python implementation that the authors acknowledge could be further improved with custom CUDA kernels, and accuracy degrades at sequence lengths beyond a model's effective context window. A key open question is whether the two-stage decomposition generalizes to even higher compression ratios or to models with more aggressive attention variants (MLA, sliding-window), and whether the adaptive split factor can be learned rather than heuristically set.
