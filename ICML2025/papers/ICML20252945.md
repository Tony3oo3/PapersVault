## Problem

Existing physical adversarial attacks on face recognition systems—patches, visible light projections, and infrared emitters—suffer from conspicuousness, weak perturbation energy, and poor multi-angle robustness. In particular, IR-based methods lack sufficient photon energy for strong attacks, IR glasses cannot support attacks from multiple viewing angles, and IR directed at the face poses safety risks. This paper addresses the gap of an invisible, high-energy, and angle-robust physical adversarial attack on FR systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252945/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a face image captured by a standard RGB camera (iPhone 13 with Sony IMX772 CMOS) and a set of UV light parameters—distance (25–50 cm), voltage/power (1–5 V), wavelength (365/395/415 nm), and 2D position on the hat surface—as input. It produces optimized UV attack parameters that, when physically deployed on a knitted hat, cause the target FR model to misclassify the attacker for one of four goals (dodging, DoS, untargeted or targeted impersonation). The UV images are generated digitally via interpolation between physically captured UV emission frames, so the attacker never needs white-box access to the victim model.

## Method

UVHat combines three technical components. First, an interpolation-based UV simulation uses the VIDIM video-interpolation model (cascaded diffusion) to synthesize UV emission images for arbitrary (distance, power, wavelength) combinations by interpolating between a small set of physically recorded UV frames grouped by wavelength. Second, a hemispherical UV modeling strategy approximates the hat as a hemisphere and computes the effective UV power at any point on the curved surface by scaling with the cosine of the emission angle relative to the camera axis, correcting for the geometric attenuation that flat-surface methods ignore. Third, an actor-critic reinforcement learning agent searches the joint parameter space (distance, power, discrete wavelength, 2D position) in a black-box setting, using a weighted composite reward that encodes position validity, goal-specific classification probabilities, and entropy for DoS, thereby identifying the optimal physical deployment without gradient access to the target model.

## Results

UVHat achieves attack success rates (ASR) of 64–100% across four FR models (ArcFace, FaceNet, CosFace, MobileFace) on LFW and CelebA, substantially outperforming both baselines. Dodging attacks yield the highest ASR, reaching 100% on FaceNet/LFW and 91% on FaceNet/CelebA. DoS attacks peak at 89% (FaceNet/LFW), untargeted impersonation at 93% (FaceNet/LFW), and targeted impersonation at 69% (FaceNet/LFW). FaceNet is consistently the most vulnerable model, while ArcFace and CosFace show similar, lower susceptibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVHat dominates both internal baselines across every attack goal, model, and dataset. MaxUV (highest-power UV at closest distance) achieves at most 66% ASR (DoS on FaceNet/LFW) and drops below 41% in all other settings, confirming that brute-force UV intensity favors DoS but fails at dodging or impersonation. 2DUV (random placement ignoring curvature) never exceeds 13% ASR, demonstrating that without hemispherical modeling and RL-based optimization, digital-domain simulations do not transfer to the physical world. In the Appendix A comparison (Table 6), UVHat also outperforms the patch-based AdvHat (Komkov & Petiushko, ICPR 2021; 70–74% ASR) and the IR-glasses method (Wang et al., CCS 2024; 92% at 0° only), achieving 96–99% ASR across −15°/0°/15° angles. The paper's own IRHat variant (IR emitters replacing UV) scores only 54–57%, confirming that UV's shorter wavelength and higher photon energy produce substantially stronger adversarial perturbations than IR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical adversarial attack lineage on face recognition from patches → visible light projections → infrared (IR) emitters to a new modality: ultraviolet (UV) light. It departs from prior IR-based methods (e.g., Agile glasses, invisible mask) on three axes: (1) UV photons carry higher energy than IR at comparable wavelengths, enabling stronger perturbations; (2) the hat-mounted geometry provides multi-angle coverage unlike glasses-based IR emitters; and (3) a hemispherical surface model accounts for emission-angle-dependent intensity falloff on curved surfaces, a factor ignored by flat-surface IR approaches. The optimization step repurposes an actor-critic RL framework and the VIDIM video-interpolation diffusion model (originally for frame synthesis) to search and simulate UV parameters in a black-box setting, rather than relying on gradient-based or heuristic search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UVHat demonstrates that a practically deployable, invisible, multi-angle physical attack can achieve >90% ASR on dodging and >80% on DoS/untargeted impersonation across four mainstream FR models, raising the bar for what FR systems must defend against. The hemispherical modeling and RL-based parameter search are transferable to other curved-surface, multi-parameter physical attack scenarios. Key open questions include: whether standard camera UV filters (common in consumer hardware) fully neutralize the attack, how defenses scale when attackers vary wavelength/power/position simultaneously, and whether the close-range limitation (<50 cm) is sufficient for all FR deployment contexts. The paper also highlights a tension: higher UV power improves DoS but degrades targeted impersonation, suggesting no single parameter setting is universally optimal.
