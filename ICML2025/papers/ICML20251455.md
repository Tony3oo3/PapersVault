## Problem

Prior egocentric privacy research has focused almost exclusively on the privacy of third-person subjects captured in first-person footage, largely overlooking the unique privacy threats posed to the camera wearer themselves. It remains unclear how much demographic, identity, and situational information about the wearer can be recovered from their own egocentric videos, and whether off-the-shelf foundation models are sufficient to mount effective attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251455/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes synchronized ego-exo video pairs (8 frames per clip by default) from Ego-Exo4D (5,625 clips, 839 participants, 131 scenes) and Charades-Ego (4,000 clips, 112 participants), with demographic labels (gender, race, age) crowd-sourced via Amazon Mechanical Turk from exocentric views. Demographic tasks output categorical labels; identity, scene, and moment tasks output ranked retrieval lists evaluated by HR@k. The Retrieval-Augmented Attack additionally takes an external unlabeled exocentric video pool as auxiliary input, retrieving top-M matching clips to augment the egocentric prediction.

## Method

EgoPrivacy formulates seven tasks across three privacy categories (demographic classification, identity retrieval, and situational retrieval) and evaluates them under four escalating threat-model capabilities, from zero-shot foundation models to fine-tuned and retrieval-augmented attackers. For retrieval tasks, a joint ego-exo embedding space is learned via supervised contrastive learning (SupCon), where positive pairs are defined by shared identity (individual privacy) or synchronized space-time (situational privacy), unifying both task types under a single loss. The key novel contribution is the Retrieval-Augmented Attack (RAA), a two-stage 'retrieve-then-predict' strategy: an ego-to-exo retriever first identifies the top-M most similar exocentric clips from an external pool, and a voting ensemble (hard or weighted soft) aggregates the egocentric prediction with predictions from the retrieved exocentric clips. Experiments show RAA consistently improves demographic attack accuracy by 2–16% over ego-only baselines, and that even zero-shot models achieve 70–80% accuracy on gender, race, and age without any task-specific training.

## Results

On the EgoPrivacy benchmark (Ego-Exo4D ID test set), zero-shot foundation models already compromise demographic privacy substantially: CLIP H/14 achieves 78.6% gender, 60.0% race, and 73.5% age accuracy on egocentric video without any training data. Fine-tuning pushes these to 96.0% (gender), 88.6% (race), and 69.3% (age) for CLIP, approaching the exocentric upper bound. For identity and situational privacy, fine-tuned models reach 86.5% HR@1 on ego-to-ego identity retrieval, 51.5% on ego-to-exo identity retrieval, 89.3% on scene retrieval, and 17.7% on moment retrieval (VideoMAE-L). The proposed Retrieval-Augmented Attack (RAA) consistently improves demographic accuracy by 4–16% over the direct egocentric attack, with the largest gain (16.1%) observed for VideoMAE-B on gender.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates seven foundation models as attack models: CLIP H/14, EgoVLPv2, VideoMAE B/14, VideoMAE L/14, LLaVA-1.5 13B, VideoLLaMA2 7B, and VideoLLaMA2 72B. CLIP H/14 and EgoVLPv2 are the strongest on fine-tuned demographic attacks (96.0% and 92.0% gender accuracy respectively), while VideoMAE-L achieves the best moment retrieval (17.7% HR@1) and scene retrieval is near-saturated at ~89% for CLIP and EgoVLPv2. EgoVLPv2 consistently outperforms CLIP on zero-shot ego-to-ego identity retrieval (4.85% vs. 0.92% HR@1), suggesting egocentric pre-training helps, though both remain near chance without fine-tuning. RAA provides the largest relative gains for weaker models: VideoMAE-B gains +16.1% on gender and LLaVA-1.5 gains +12.9% on race, while already-strong models like EgoVLPv2 see only +0.6% on race, confirming that RAA is most effective when the direct egocentric signal is weak. On OOD (Charades-Ego), all fine-tuned models drop 10–30% in accuracy, whereas zero-shot models retain their performance, indicating overfitting during fine-tuning but also confirming that even minimal-capability attacks remain effective across distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of egocentric privacy research, which has focused on protecting third-person subjects visible in first-person footage (face blurring, image degradation, anonymization), by shifting the lens to the camera wearer's own privacy. Prior egocentric privacy benchmarks (FPSI, EVPR, IITMD) were small (6–32 subjects), covered only identity retrieval, and lacked demographic or scene annotations; EgoPrivacy is the first to unify demographic, individual, and situational privacy into a single large-scale (731 subjects, 123 scenes) benchmark with OOD evaluation. The Retrieval-Augmented Attack (RAA) repurposes ego-to-exo cross-view retrieval—previously studied for knowledge transfer and video generation—as a privacy amplification mechanism, a framing absent from prior cross-view work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that off-the-shelf zero-shot foundation models recover wearer demographics at 70–80% accuracy from faceless egocentric video challenges the implicit assumption that first-person framing provides inherent privacy, and underscores an urgent need for wearer-specific defenses in wearable camera systems. The RAA result—that the retrieval model need only cluster similar-attribute identities (82%+ demographic agreement) rather than find the correct person (50.31%) to boost attack accuracy—reveals a subtle and previously unexamined vulnerability in cross-view retrieval. Key limitations include reliance on perceived (not self-reported) demographic labels, inherited demographic imbalances from Ego-Exo4D/Charades-Ego, and the absence of any proposed defense; open questions include how to preserve task utility while mitigating these leakage channels and whether the findings generalize to longer, more diverse recordings.
