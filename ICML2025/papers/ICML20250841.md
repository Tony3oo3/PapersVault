## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning is typically addressed by mitigation strategies, but it remains unclear which specific examples are most susceptible to being forgotten. Most replay-based continual learning methods fill their buffers via uniform random sampling, treating all previously seen examples equally, despite evidence that forgetting is not uniform across examples. This paper asks whether the learning speed of individual examples predicts their forgetting susceptibility and whether this insight can be exploited to improve replay buffer composition.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification tasks (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, TinyImageNet at 64×64) split into sequential tasks, producing class labels as output. The key additional input is a per-example boolean classification matrix tracked across training epochs, which requires only one float per example in memory (a running mean) and is computed during the standard forward pass. The output of SBS is a filtered replay buffer: a subset of examples from the completed task, from which the CL algorithm samples uniformly during subsequent task training.

## Method

Speed-Based Sampling (SBS) is a drop-in replacement for uniform replay buffer sampling in any buffer-based continual learning method. During training on task t, SBS records whether each example is correctly classified at every epoch, yielding a learning speed score (the fraction of epochs with correct classification, Eq. 1). At the end of the task, SBS removes the q% quickest-learned and s% slowest-learned examples from the task's dataset, then samples the replay buffer uniformly from the remaining middle range. The core empirical finding is that quickly learned examples are already robust to forgetting (so replaying them wastes buffer capacity), while the slowest examples are too complex to benefit from limited replay; the optimal buffer focuses on moderately fast examples. Hyperparameters q and s can be tuned via a lightweight RotNet auxiliary task or set to a default of 20%/20%, which consistently outperforms random sampling across all tested datasets, buffer sizes, and CL algorithms (DER, DER++, GEM, A-GEM, ER, RPC, FDR, X-DER).

## Results

SBS achieves the highest mean test accuracy across all six dataset/buffer-size combinations in Table 1. Headline numbers: on CIFAR-10-5 with a 10k buffer, SBS reaches 89.17% (vs. 82.4% for random sampling, a +6.77 pt gain); on CIFAR-100-20 with a 1k buffer, SBS scores 55.43% (vs. 51.75% random); on TinyImageNet-2 with a 10k buffer, SBS attains 63.16% (vs. 61.48% random). Even the fixed-hyperparameter variant (SBS-fix, q=s=20%) consistently outperforms all other sampling strategies without any tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBS outperforms all five compared sampling baselines across every dataset and buffer-size combination. The largest margin is on CIFAR-10-5 with a 10k buffer, where SBS (89.17%) beats the next-best method LARS (84.93%) by 4.24 points and GSS (84.13%) by 5.04 points. With small 1k buffers, Herding is the strongest competitor (e.g., 54.00% on CIFAR-100-20 vs. SBS's 55.43%), reflecting its bias toward typical, quickly-learned examples that are most useful when storage is limited. GSS, which prioritizes gradient-diverse (slower-learned) examples, performs best among baselines with large 10k buffers (72.90% on CIFAR-100-20) but still trails SBS by 1.9 points. Max Ent and IPM consistently underperform random sampling in most settings, confirming that neither entropy-based nor structure-preserving selection is a reliable general-purpose strategy. Notably, even SBS-fix with default q=s=20% beats every tuned baseline in all six scenarios, indicating the gains are not an artifact of hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simplicity bias literature (Shah et al., 2020; Gissin et al., 2020) into the continual learning domain, reframing it as a 'reverse simplicity bias' where complex (slowly-learned) examples are preferentially forgotten during task transitions. It departs from prior model-centric analyses of forgetting (Nguyen et al., 2020; Ramasesh et al., 2021) by adopting a data-centric, behavioral perspective that tracks per-example learning speed. The proposed Speed-Based Sampling strategy is positioned against purpose-built replay buffer selection methods (Herding, GSS, LARS, IPM) and curriculum/self-paced learning paradigms, distinguishing itself by operating purely through selective replay without modifying training order or data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBS offers a drop-in replacement for uniform sampling in any replay-based CL method, consistently improving performance across DER, GEM, A-GEM, and other algorithms without architectural changes, making it immediately practical for the CL community. The finding that the optimal buffer composition is independent of the subsequent task simplifies hyperparameter selection in multi-task pipelines. Key limitations include poor applicability to single-epoch/stream settings where learning speed is ill-defined, and the reliance on a RotNet auxiliary task for hyperparameter tuning, though the authors show q=s=20% works broadly. Open questions include whether learning speed can be incorporated more directly into loss functions or gradient-based CL methods, and how to approximate example complexity in the single-pass regime.
