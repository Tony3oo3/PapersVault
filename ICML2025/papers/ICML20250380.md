## Problem

VLMs consistently fail at basic spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., identifying 'behind' or 'under' relationships between two objects), yet the internal mechanism behind these failures remains unexplored. This paper investigates how vision and text tokens interact within the model's attention layers during spatial reasoning, revealing that image tokens receive only ~10% of total attention despite comprising ~90% of the input sequence, and that the geometric distribution of that attention—not its quantity—is the critical factor determining correctness.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a standard VLM (LLaVA-1.5/1.6 or Qwen2-VL) taking an RGB image (encoded into 576 tokens via a 24×24 patch grid by CLIP) and a multiple-choice text question about spatial relationships (4 or 6 options such as left/right/on/under/behind/front). The output is a single spatial relationship label. The intervention is applied at inference time to the attention logits of the last input token directed toward image tokens in intermediate transformer layers, requiring no retraining or additional forward passes beyond a single confidence computation.

## Method

ADAPTVIS is a training-free, confidence-aware temperature scaling of image attention. The key insight is that the model's generation confidence (output token probability) serves as a reliable proxy for whether its current attention pattern over image patches is trustworthy: high confidence indicates familiar spatial relationships (e.g., 'left', 'right') where the attention is generally correct but imprecise, while low confidence indicates unfamiliar relationships (e.g., 'on', 'under') where attention is misallocated. When confidence exceeds a threshold β, the method multiplies image attention logits by α₁ > 1 to sharpen the distribution and reinforce focus on the correct objects; when confidence is below β, it multiplies by α₂ < 1 to smooth the distribution and broaden the context window for exploration. This intervention is applied uniformly across all heads and layers to the attention of the final input token toward image tokens, achieving up to 50 absolute point improvements on spatial reasoning benchmarks with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the WhatsUp benchmark, ADAPTVIS achieves up to 50.0 absolute points improvement over LLaVA-1.6 on Controlled_A (48.2% → 98.2% accuracy) and 24.6 points on LLaVA-1.5 (60.3% → 84.9%). On the VSR benchmark, ADAPTVIS improves F1 score by 11.2 points for LLaVA-1.5 (51.3% → 62.5%) and 9.9 points for LLaVA-1.6 (29.4% → 39.3%). The method also generalizes to Qwen2-VL, yielding a 10.7-point gain on VG_two_obj (56.22% → 66.95%) while maintaining performance on general QA benchmarks like POPE and GQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAPTVIS substantially outperforms both baselines across all datasets. Compared to VCD (Leng et al., 2024), which provides only 0–1.7 point gains on LLaVA-1.5 and 0–4.3 points on LLaVA-1.6, ADAPTVIS achieves 24.6–50.0 point improvements on synthetic subsets (Cont_A, Cont_B) and 0.6–7.3 points on real-image subsets. DoLa (Chuang et al., 2023) performs even more modestly, with gains of at most 1.9 points (F1 on VSR) and occasionally degrading performance (e.g., −0.7 on COCO_two for LLaVA-1.5, −0.3 on COCO_two for LLaVA-1.6). The gap is most dramatic on synthetic data where the model is less familiar with spatial relationships: ADAPTVIS lifts Cont_A Set Accuracy from 0.0% to 57.0% (LLaVA-1.6), while VCD reaches only 10.9% and DoLa remains at 0.0%. On VSR, ADAPTVIS matches SCALINGVIS for LLaVA-1.5 (65.0 vs. 64.9 EM) but clearly surpasses both baselines for LLaVA-1.6 (62.7 EM vs. 59.3 DoLa and 58.8 VCD), with a 9.9-point F1 gain over baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-hoc attention steering paradigm from PASTA (Zhang et al., 2023) to vision-language models, removing the need for manual segment specification or per-head validation. It situates itself within the broader VLM hallucination-mitigation literature (VCD, DoLa, activation-based decoding) but departs by targeting the geometric distribution of attention over image tokens rather than output logits or contrastive signals. The mechanistic interpretability framing—tracing attention across layers to diagnose spatial reasoning failures—builds on attention-pattern analyses in LLMs (lost-in-the-middle, attention sinks) but applies it to the text-vision interaction in VLMs, a less explored angle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that image tokens receive only ~10% of attention despite comprising ~90% of the input sequence provides a concrete diagnostic for VLM spatial failures, and the confidence-based adaptive intervention (ADAPTVIS) offers a training-free, low-cost remedy. However, the large gains (up to 50 points) are concentrated on synthetic benchmarks with clean backgrounds; on real-world COCO and VG subsets, improvements are modest (1–7 points), and the method still requires a validation set to tune the confidence threshold β. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to 3D spatial reasoning, long-horizon geometric tasks, and whether the underlying bottleneck is truly attentional or rooted in the CLIP encoder's representational limitations.
