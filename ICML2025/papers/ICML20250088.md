## Problem

Current synthetic video datasets primarily replicate real-world scenarios, leaving impossible, counterfactual, and anti-reality video concepts entirely unexplored. No existing benchmark evaluates whether video generation models can faithfully follow prompts to create physically or logically impossible content, or whether video understanding models can reason about temporal dynamics and world knowledge in such out-of-distribution videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation evaluation, the benchmark provides 260 structured text prompts (IPV-TXT) organized by a 4-domain/14-category taxonomy, which are fed to T2V models to produce videos scored on visual quality and prompt fidelity. For understanding evaluation, 902 curated videos (IPV-VID) at a 1:1 synthetic-to-real ratio are presented to Video-LLMs, which must output a binary judgment (AI-generated or real), a 4-way multiple-choice selection of the impossible phenomenon, or a free-text explanation of what makes the video impossible. The MCQA distractors are specifically designed to share visual elements with the video to prevent simple grounding shortcuts.

## Method

IPV-BENCH is built on a hierarchical taxonomy spanning Physical Laws (mechanics, thermal, optics, fluid, material, conservation), Biological Laws (capability, morphology, anthropomorphism), Geographical Laws (climate, terrain), and Social Laws (commonsense, magical effects, historical anomalies), developed through iterative LLM-assisted and crowdsourced refinement. The video dataset combines 2,600 clips generated by 10 T2V models, 155 web-collected AI videos, and 650 real-world videos retrieved from OpenVid via CLIP, all filtered and annotated for visual quality and semantic plausibility. Three hierarchical understanding tasks (Judgment, MCQA, OpenQA) assess increasing difficulty, with MCQA distractors generated by GPT-4o under strict constraints and OpenQA scored via a justification-then-score protocol using GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 as evaluators. For generation, the novel IPV-Score metric computes the joint percentage of videos that are simultaneously high-quality and faithful to the impossible prompt, with an automated surrogate using VBench sub-metrics and a three-step GPT-4o chain-of-thought judgment achieving 0.80 Spearman correlation with human annotations.

## Results

On the IPV-TXT generation benchmark, the best model (Mochi 1) achieves an IPV-Score of only 37.3%, meaning it produces a high-quality video that faithfully depicts the impossible event in fewer than 4 out of 10 prompts; proprietary models like Hailuo (36.2%) and Kling (26.7%) trail behind, while LTX scores just 10.0%. On the IPV-VID understanding benchmark, the top open-source model LLaVA-Next reaches 86.4% MCQA accuracy but only 34.4% on OpenQA, while GPT-4o leads on OpenQA at 49.1% (Claude-evaluated: 45.1%). Human annotators achieve 94.0% MCQA and 82.7% OpenQA, revealing a large gap between current models and human-level understanding of impossible videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among video generation models, Mochi 1 leads with an IPV-Score of 37.3%, narrowly ahead of Hailuo (36.2%), while Sora (25.2%) and Kling (26.7) show strong visual quality (98.8% and 98.8%) but weak prompt following (15.3% and 21.5%), illustrating a quality-vs-faithfulness trade-off. LTX (10.0%) and Luma (14.3%) perform worst overall, with Luma's prompt-following score of 17.1% being the lowest among all models. For video understanding, LLaVA-Next dominates MCQA at 86.4% (vs. GPT-4o's 79.7% and Gemini's 84.4%), but GPT-4o substantially outperforms all others on OpenQA (49.1% vs. LLaVA-Next's 34.4% and Gemini's 42.5%), indicating that open-ended reasoning about impossibility remains the hardest task. Video-LLaVA is near random on MCQA (26.8% vs. 20% random), while Qwen2-VL achieves the best Judgment accuracy (76.2%) yet lags on MCQA (71.4%). Across all models, the Physical domain is consistently the most challenging, and temporal-reasoning videos score 10–20 points lower than spatial ones, confirming that temporal dynamics remain the key bottleneck for impossible video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing 'impossible videos'—counterfactual and anti-reality content—as a systematic evaluation axis for both video generation and understanding. While prior benchmarks like VideoPhy and PhyGenBench evaluate whether generated videos obey physical laws, and understanding benchmarks like MV-Bench and TempCompass test on real-world videos, none explicitly target content that *violates* real-world laws. The taxonomy draws conceptual inspiration from physical commonsense benchmarks but extends the scope to biological, geographical, and social domains, and the video collection repurposes existing T2V models (Sora, Kling, HunyuanVideo) and the OpenVid dataset in a novel counterfactual framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical and actionable gap: current video models, even state-of-the-art ones like GPT-4o and Sora, struggle substantially with impossible content, with the best generation model achieving only 37.3% IPV-Score and most understanding models scoring near-random on open-ended identification of impossibilities. The finding that temporal dynamic reasoning—not just world knowledge—is the primary bottleneck, and that image-based models outperform dedicated video models on temporal tasks, points toward a concrete research direction for next-generation video architectures. Open questions include whether the taxonomy is exhaustive, how results generalize beyond the specific 902-video set, and whether the GPT-4o-based OpenQA evaluation introduces systematic bias.
