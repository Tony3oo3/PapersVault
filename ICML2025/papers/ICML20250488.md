## Problem

Existing RL benchmarks (Atari, DMC, ProcGen, Distracting Control Suite) conflate representation learning with policy optimization and dynamics modeling, making it impossible to systematically evaluate how well agents learn visual representations in isolation. No prior benchmark allows precise, independent control over visual complexity while holding environment dynamics, action space, and reward structure constant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent observes a single composite RGB image (84×84 pixels, normalized to [0,1]) depicting a 3×3 grid of image patches drawn from a sampled pool, and outputs one of four discrete actions (UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT) to slide a tile into the empty cell. The observation is a POMDP: the agent never sees the internal tile arrangement and must infer puzzle state purely from pixels. Visual diversity is controlled by the pool size p (number of distinct source images sampled per training run), while grid dimensions (H×W) provide a secondary scaling axis that expands the state space without changing observation resolution or action space.

## Method

SPGym reformulates the classic 8-tile sliding puzzle as a visual RL benchmark with two orthogonal difficulty knobs: image pool size (scaling |Ω| while fixing |S|, A, P, R) and grid dimensions (scaling |S| while fixing observation shape and action space). The reward is a dense, normalized Manhattan-distance signal between current and target tile positions, with a +1 terminal reward on puzzle completion and −1 penalty for invalid moves. The paper's empirical contribution evaluates PPO, SAC (with seven representation-learning variants: RAD, CURL, SPR, DBC, AE, VAE, SB), and DreamerV3 across pool sizes 1–100, using 3-layer CNN encoders with mirrored deconvolutional decoders and MLP heads. Key findings include a strong correlation (r = −0.81) between linear-probe accuracy of frozen encoders and sample efficiency, universal near-zero success on hard out-of-distribution images, and the counterintuitive result that sophisticated auxiliary objectives (contrastive, self-predictive, bisimulation) often underperform simple data augmentation (grayscale + channel shuffle) in this setting.

## Results

The primary metric is million environment steps to reach 80% success rate (lower is better). DreamerV3 achieves the best overall performance at 0.42M (pool 1), 1.23M (pool 5), and 1.44M (pool 10) steps, while SAC+RAD is the best model-free method at 0.24M, 0.42M, and 0.82M steps respectively. Standard PPO is the weakest at 1.75M, 7.80M, and 9.73M steps. Notably, sophisticated auxiliary objectives (CURL, SPR, VAE, SB) consistently underperform both standard SAC and the simple data augmentation approach (RAD), with SPR and VAE failing entirely at pool size 10 (10M steps without reaching 80% success).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2025) is the strongest method overall, outperforming all PPO and SAC variants across every pool size, with particularly stable scaling (1.44M steps at pool 10 vs. 9.73M for PPO). Among model-free methods, SAC+RAD (Laskin et al., 2020a) is the clear winner at 0.24M/0.42M/0.82M steps, beating standard SAC (Christodoulou, 2019) by 27–60% and dramatically outperforming all auxiliary-objective variants. In contrast, CURL (Laskin et al., 2020b) underperforms standard SAC at pool 5 (1.56M vs. 0.91M) and degrades sharply at pool 10 (5.24M). SPR (Schwarzer et al., 2020) and VAE (Yarats et al., 2021b) are the worst SAC variants, both failing to reach 80% success within 10M steps at pool 10. DBC (Zhang et al., 2021) and AE (Yarats et al., 2021b) offer only marginal improvements over standard SAC. The Simple Baseline (Tomar et al., 2023) also fails at pool 10. PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with in-distribution pretraining improves from 1.75M to 0.95M at pool 1 but still trails SAC+RAD by 4×, and out-of-distribution pretraining provides only modest gains. The key finding is that simple data augmentation (RAD) outperforms all sophisticated representation learning objectives in this setting, and DreamerV3's world-model architecture provides the most robust scaling with visual diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPGym carves new ground by repurposing the classic sliding tile puzzle as a controlled visual RL benchmark, departing from prior benchmarks (Atari, DMC, ProcGen, Distracting Control Suite) that either conflate representation learning with policy optimization and dynamics, or introduce task-irrelevant distractors. It extends the puzzle-based benchmarking direction of Estermann et al. (2024) by replacing discrete state observations with image patches drawn from arbitrarily large pools, while keeping transition dynamics, action space, and reward function fixed. The evaluation protocol draws on the standard visual RL representation learning toolkit (RAD, CURL, SPR, DBC, AE/VAE, DreamerV3) but positions them as out-of-the-box diagnostics rather than co-designed components of the environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal near-zero success on completely unseen images (Hard OOD) and the counterintuitive finding that sophisticated auxiliary objectives (CURL, SPR, DBC, VAE) underperform simple data augmentation (RAD) expose a memorization-vs-generalization gap that current visual RL methods cannot bridge, even when trained on pools of up to 100 images. SPGym provides a minimal, controlled stress test that isolates visual diversity as the sole scaling axis, making it a practical diagnostic tool for future representation learning research. Key limitations include only 5 seeds per configuration, out-of-the-box hyperparameters that may understate peak method performance, and evaluation restricted to a single puzzle task family, leaving open whether the memorization failure generalizes to other structured visual tasks.
