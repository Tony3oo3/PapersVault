## Problem

Domain randomization in sim-to-real RL typically relies on manually specified or simple parametric sampling distributions (Gaussians, independent betas, uniform intervals) that cannot capture multimodality or inter-parameter dependencies in the environment space. This limits the breadth of environments a policy can be robust to, and provides no principled mechanism for detecting when a real-world state falls outside the policy's training distribution. The paper asks whether a flexible neural sampling distribution can be learned jointly with the policy to maximize both coverage and solvability, and whether the resulting distribution can serve as an out-of-distribution detector for multi-step planning.

## Input / Output

The RL actor consumes a history of the 10 previous end-effector poses and velocities (estimated via finite differencing) plus force measurements, and outputs 3-DOF translational and 1-DOF rotational end-effector pose offsets. The critic additionally receives the privileged environment parameter vector ξ (e.g., grasp pose offsets in x, y, yaw) that the actor never sees. In the multi-step planning extension, RGB-D images are fed into Bayes3D to produce a full posterior over the 3-DOF object pose (x, y, yaw), which is then checked against the flow-derived precondition. The final system output is a sequence of high-level skills (insertion policies and inspection actions) rather than a single continuous action.

## Method

GoFlow jointly optimizes a PPO actor-critic policy and a neural spline flow (3 layers, 64 hidden features, 8 bins, implemented in Zuko) that parameterizes the domain randomization distribution p_φ(ξ). The flow is updated by maximizing a composite objective: expected policy reward under p_φ, plus α·entropy(p_φ), minus β·KL(p_old‖p_φ) for self-paced stability. A critical design choice is that both the reward and entropy terms are estimated via importance sampling from a uniform distribution u(ξ) over the full parameter space rather than from p_φ itself, which prevents the learned distribution from collapsing onto narrow high-reward regions. The asymmetric actor-critic architecture passes ξ to the critic but not the actor, ensuring the deployed policy relies only on observable state. For multi-step planning, the learned flow and privileged value function jointly define belief-space preconditions: a belief b passes if E_b[1_{V_ψ(s,ξ)>J_T} · 1_{p_φ(ξ)>ε}] > η, where ε restricts evaluation to regions the flow has adequately sampled. A BFS planner then sequences insertion policies and a camera-inspection action (which reduces pose uncertainty) to satisfy preconditions before executing each skill.

## Results

GoFlow achieves the highest domain coverage (proportion of the parameter space where the policy exceeds a task-specific reward threshold) across all six simulated environments, with particularly large margins on Quadruped (0.275 vs. 0.086 for NoDR) and Humanoid (0.274 vs. 0.195 for FullDR). On the real-world gear insertion task with a Franka Emika robot, GoFlow achieves a 9/10 success rate compared to 6/10 for FullDR and 5/10 for all three learned baselines (LSDR, DORAEMON, ADR). The method also demonstrates graceful degradation as parameter ranges are widened, while simpler parametric baselines (LSDR, DORAEMON) collapse to near-zero coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoFlow outperforms all three learned domain randomization baselines (LSDR, DORAEMON, ADR) and both fixed baselines (FullDR, NoDR) on every one of the six simulated environments. The advantage is most pronounced in environments with inter-parameter dependencies or off-center feasible regions: on Quadruped, GoFlow achieves 0.275 coverage versus 0.086 (NoDR) and 0.029 (DORAEMON); on Humanoid, it reaches 0.274 versus 0.195 (FullDR) and 0.176 (LSDR). On the Gears domain, GoFlow is the only method achieving non-trivial coverage (0.035), while LSDR and FullDR both score 0.000. In the real-world gear insertion experiment, GoFlow's 9/10 success rate significantly exceeds FullDR (6/10) and all learned baselines (5/10 each), confirming that the more expressive normalizing-flow distribution translates to better sim-to-real robustness under pose uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoFlow extends the line of learned domain randomization methods (LSDR's multivariate Gaussians, ADR's expanding uniform intervals, DORAEMON's independent beta distributions) by replacing their parametric sampling distributions with neural spline flows, which can model multimodality and inter-parameter dependencies. The actor-critic architecture with privileged information to the critic follows the asymmetric design of Pinto et al. (2018), and the multi-step planning extension repurposes the learned flow as an out-of-distribution detector within a belief-space planning framework (Kaelbling & Lozano-Perez, 2013), using Bayes3D for probabilistic pose estimation to update beliefs. The key departure from prior work is the use of importance sampling from a uniform distribution (rather than from the flow itself) to estimate reward and entropy terms, preventing distributional collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that normalizing flows capture inter-parameter dependencies and multimodality in the feasible region—where simpler parametric distributions fail—provides a principled upgrade path for practitioners already using learned domain randomization. The belief-space planning extension, while using a simple BFS planner, opens a concrete path toward integrating short-horizon RL skills into long-horizon manipulation under partial observability. Key limitations include training instability of the flow (mitigated by increasing β at the cost of sample efficiency), manual threshold tuning (ε, η) that is environment-specific, and the need for uncertainty quantification of the neural value function to remove the ε parameter. The real-world validation, while compelling (9/10 vs. 5–6/10), is limited to a single gear-insertion task with 10 trials.
