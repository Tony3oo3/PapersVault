## Problem

Causal discovery in time series has traditionally relied on stationarity assumptions, but real-world systems exhibit nonstationary dynamics driven by hidden state variables (e.g., mood, collision events, gene activation). Existing approaches either model nonstationarity through noise distributions, time-varying effects with fixed structure, or discrete latent regimes, but none provide a unified framework with identifiability guarantees for the 'states determined' and 'states recurrent' cases where hidden states depend on observations or historical events.

## Input / Output

SDCI takes a multi-variable time series x_{1:T} ∈ R^{Nd} (N variables, T time steps) and produces a conditional summary graph G^{CS}_{1:K} consisting of K directed graphs (one per state value), a set of edge-type labels W = {w_{i,j,k}} specifying which of n_ε interaction functions governs each i→j edge at each state k, and posterior distributions over latent state sequences s_{1:T}. In the determined case, states are computed exactly from current observations; in the recurrent case, a bidirectional RNN-GNN approximates the state posterior. The method also supports forecasting by generating future x_{t+1} given the inferred structure and states.

## Method

SDCI introduces the conditional summary graph as a compact representation that scales linearly with K rather than exponentially with K^N (as in regime-dependent graphs), and establishes partial identifiability of edge-type labels under unique indexing of outgoing structure and function types. The generative model uses a two-stage GNN interaction-aggregation architecture where each pair (i,j) interacts via a state-dependent function f_{w_{i,j,k}} selected from a finite set of n_ε edge-type functions, and a permutation-invariant aggregation g combines all incoming interactions. Training optimises an ELBO objective with Gumbel-softmax reparameterisation for the discrete latent variables (edge labels and states), using a GNN-based encoder (adapted from ACD) for edge-type inference and a bidirectional RNN-GNN for recurrent state smoothing. The key theoretical contribution is a three-step identifiability proof: (i) regime-dependent graph identifiability via Gaussian mixture identifiability (Yakowitz & Spragins), (ii) transfer to conditional summary graph via the multi-state dependence structure, and (iii) edge-type permutation equivalence via partial derivative analysis of the aggregation function.

## Results

On the Gene Regulatory Network (GRN) benchmark (DynGen, N=49 genes, T=50 steps, 4 regimes), SDCI-Determined achieves the best F1 score of 0.347 and AUROC of 0.936, substantially outperforming all baselines. SDCI-Recurrent follows with F1 0.276 and AUROC 0.855, still well ahead of the strongest baseline ACD (F1 0.187, AUROC 0.714). On the Springs and Magnets fixed-graph setting, SDCI achieves perfect structure estimation (F1 ≈ 1.0) for both determined and recurrent states. On NBA player trajectory forecasting, SDCI achieves lower long-term MSE than ACD, VRNN, and NRI-NPM, with GNN-based methods overall outperforming the RNN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDCI-Determined dominates all six baselines on the GRN benchmark, achieving F1 0.347 vs. ACD's 0.187 (the strongest baseline) and Rhino's 0.081, while also matching Rhino's AUROC (0.936 vs. 0.933). SDCI-Recurrent (F1 0.276) also clearly beats ACD (0.187) and all other baselines, though it lags behind SDCI-Determined due to the exponential cost of exact state inference in high-dimensional settings. SDCI-Observe (F1 0.182) is roughly on par with ACD (0.187), suggesting that observed state information provides limited additional advantage when gene deactivation is gradual. On the Springs and Magnets variable-graph setting, SDCI achieves the highest summary graph F1 score, while N-GC surprisingly outperforms ACD despite its simplicity. On NBA player trajectories, SDCI slightly outperforms ACD in long-term forecasting MSE, and both GNN-based methods (ACD, SDCI) clearly beat the non-causal VRNN baseline, with the 'no-edge' interaction mechanism reducing error accumulation over time.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDCI extends Amortized Causal Discovery (ACD; Lowe et al., 2022) from stationary to conditionally stationary time series by introducing per-variable latent states that modulate the causal graph, and draws on the two-stage interaction-aggregation framework from Neural Relational Inference (Kipf et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2021) for efficient GNN-based implementation. Theoretically, it builds on Markov Switching Models (Hamilton, 1989) and the Gaussian mixture identifiability result of Yakowitz & Spragins (1968), but departs from prior nonstationary causal discovery work (HMM-ICA, CtrlNS, R-PCMCI) by not requiring knowledge of the number of regimes K and by allowing states to depend on observations (determined/recurrent cases) rather than being independent or globally shared. The conditional summary graph is a new compact representation that avoids the exponential blowup of regime-dependent graphs while retaining state-specific causal information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability guarantee for conditional summary graphs without pre-specifying K addresses a fundamental gap in regime-switching causal discovery, enabling principled model selection for the number of states. Practically, SDCI demonstrates that causality-driven modeling improves trajectory forecasting (NBA) and gene regulatory network inference (DynGen) over both causal and non-causal baselines, suggesting that explicit causal structure learning can serve as an inductive bias for prediction. Key open questions include the consistency of the variational estimator (acknowledged as unresolved), the exponential computational cost of exact inference in the recurrent state case, and whether the identifiability results extend to state distribution recovery. The method's reliance on first-order Markov transitions and Gaussian noise limits its applicability to more complex temporal dependencies.
