## Problem

Existing text-to-song generation methods decompose the task into sequential stages (vocal synthesis followed by accompaniment generation), causing error accumulation, cumbersome pipelines, and suboptimal global coordination between vocals and instruments. Additionally, no publicly available dataset with paired audio, lyrics, and captions exists, severely limiting research in this area. The paper asks whether a single-stage autoregressive model can jointly model vocals and accompaniment with quality competitive with multi-stage approaches.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three conditioning signals: lyrics tokenized into phoneme-like tokens via a 6681-vocabulary VoiceBPE tokenizer, a free-form textual description (instruments, genre, mood, timbre, tempo, singer gender) encoded by a frozen FLAN-T5, and an optional 3-second reference vocal clip processed by a frozen MERT encoder for zero-shot voice cloning. Outputs are 16 kHz audio at 50 Hz frame rate, up to 30 seconds, in either mixed mode (a single blended track) or dual-track mode (separate vocal and accompaniment stems for post-production). All three conditions are concatenated along the temporal dimension and injected via cross-attention rather than prepended to the token sequence.

## Method

SongGen is a 24-layer autoregressive transformer decoder (1024 hidden size) that predicts discrete audio tokens from X-Codec (8 RVQ codebooks, 1024 codes each) using the codebook-delay pattern. In mixed mode, an auxiliary vocal-token prediction head (trained only, not used at inference) counteracts the model's bias toward the higher-energy accompaniment, significantly improving vocal clarity. In dual-track mode, the paper explores parallel and interleaving token patterns; the interleaving (A-V) variant—where accompaniment tokens precede vocal tokens at each frame—achieves the best quality by enabling lower transformer layers to capture inter-track interactions while higher layers refine intra-track characteristics. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (modality alignment → voice-free dropout → high-quality fine-tuning on a 100K filtered subset) combined with progressive codebook loss weight scheduling that prioritizes early codebooks before balancing. The authors also build an automated 540K-clip / 2000-hour dataset from MSD, FMA, and MTG-Jamendo using Demucs source separation, VAD-based segmentation, dual-Whisper lyric transcription with edit-distance filtering, and CLAP-score caption validation.

## Results

On the MusicCaps test set (326 English-voiced samples), SongGen's Mixed Pro model achieves FAD=1.71, KL=0.69, CLAP=0.35, and PER=40.58, while the best dual-track variant (Interleaving A-V) achieves FAD=1.87, KL=0.69, CLAP=0.35, and PER=39.46. In human evaluation, Mixed Pro scores 3.96/5 on Overall Quality and 4.01/5 on Harmony, compared to 3.39 and 2.97 for the multi-stage baseline. The single-stage model also generates a 30-second sample in 18.04 s on an A800 GPU, versus 42.85 s for the multi-stage pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SongGen is compared against three baselines: a two-stage autoregressive model (vocal-then-accompaniment), fine-tuned Parler-TTS, and the commercial product Suno (human eval only). Against the multi-stage baseline, SongGen's Mixed Pro wins on FAD (1.71 vs. 2.18), CLAP (0.35 vs. 0.29), and all four aesthetics metrics (e.g., PQ 7.19 vs. 6.69, CE 6.77 vs. 6.39), as well as human-evaluated Overall Quality (3.96 vs. 3.39) and Harmony (4.01 vs. 2.97). The multi-stage model retains a slight edge only on PER (38.80 vs. 40.58) and SECS (74.04 vs. 73.78), attributable to its first stage being trained exclusively on clean vocal targets. Parler-TTS, despite being a strong TTS system, is far outperformed on every metric (FAD 4.13 vs. 1.71; OVL. 2.58 vs. 3.96), confirming that song generation is substantially harder than speech synthesis. Compared to Suno, SongGen achieves higher text-relevance (REL. 3.86 vs. 3.31) and supports voice cloning, though Suno still leads on Overall Quality (4.28 vs. 3.96) and Harmony (4.33 vs. 4.01) in human evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SongGen builds directly on the MusicGen (Copet et al., 2024) autoregressive codec language model paradigm, adopting its codebook-delay pattern and drawing inspiration from its stereo-channel modeling for the parallel dual-track pattern. It departs from the dominant multi-stage song generation pipeline (Melodist, MelodyLM) that sequentially generates vocals then accompaniment, instead proposing a unified single-stage transformer that jointly models both tracks. For conditioning, it repurposes existing components—MERT for voice embeddings, FLAN-T5 for text, and VoiceBPE for lyrics—into a cross-attention framework, while the X-Codec tokenizer (Ye et al., 2024) is selected over Encodec/DAC specifically for its semantic-aoustic hybrid design suited to the higher complexity of sung vocals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first fully open-source single-stage text-to-song generation model with a released 540K-clip annotated dataset and preprocessing pipeline, SongGen establishes a reproducible baseline that the community has lacked. The key open questions it surfaces include: whether single-stage joint modeling scales to full-length songs (currently capped at 30 seconds), whether higher-fidelity song-specific codecs can close the 16 kHz gap, and whether the token-pattern insights (interleaving > parallel, accompaniment-before-vocal ordering) generalize to longer contexts and more tracks. The finding that a simple auxiliary vocal prediction head substantially improves vocal clarity in mixed mode offers a practical recipe that may transfer to other multi-source generation settings.
