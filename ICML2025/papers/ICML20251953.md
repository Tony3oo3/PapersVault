## Problem

Protective perturbations (adversarial examples) have been proposed to prevent unauthorized customization of latent diffusion models, but their robustness has been evaluated primarily through purification-based adaptive attacks that are computationally expensive and introduce distributional uncertainty. This paper asks whether the robustness of these protections can be assessed from a model adaptation perspective, and identifies that the core mechanism by which adversarial examples protect data is the distortion of latent representations in the autoencoder.

## Input / Output

The method takes protected image samples (512×512 RGB, perturbation budget δ=16/255) and a text prompt as input. It produces lightweight LoRA adapters (rank 128 for CAT-both, rank 256 for CAT-en/de) attached to the latent encoder and/or decoder, which are detachable at inference time so the original model remains unaffected. During customization, the protected image is first encoded through the adapted encoder to obtain a realigned latent representation, which is then used by the denoising module; the final generated image is decoded through the adapted decoder back to pixel space.

## Method

CAT applies contrastive adversarial training to the latent autoencoder of a pre-trained LDM (Stable Diffusion v2.1), using a contrastive adversarial loss defined as the MSE between the decoded reconstruction and the original protected image. LoRA adapters are inserted into all convolutional and attention layers of the encoder and decoder, and only these adapter weights are updated (batch size 4, lr 1×10⁻⁴, 1000 steps) while all base weights remain frozen. The key insight is that adversarial examples distort latent representations, so realigning them via the adapted encoder allows the diffusion denoising module to learn from the protected samples as if they were clean. Three settings are evaluated: CAT-both (adapters on encoder and decoder), CAT-en (encoder only), and CAT-de (decoder only), with CAT-de serving as a control that confirms latent distortion is the critical factor. The approach is parameter-efficient and avoids the optimization-heavy purification step required by prior adaptive attacks.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, CAT-both improves Face Similarity Score (FSS) from a baseline of 0.160–0.497 to 0.572–0.678 across nine protective perturbation methods, with the largest gains observed for Mist (0.160→0.572) and SDST (0.175→0.594). On VGGFace2, CAT-both raises FSS from 0.035–0.456 to 0.486–0.607, and CAT-en achieves the highest FQS in most settings (e.g., 0.712 for Mist, 0.740 for Glaze). CAT-de (decoder-only adapters) shows negligible improvement, confirming that latent encoder distortion is the primary mechanism of protection. In style mimicry on WikiArt, CAT-en achieves CLIP-IQA scores of 0.621–0.730 versus baseline scores of 0.285–0.532.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT is compared against two purification-based adaptive attacks: Noisy-Upscaling (NU, Hönig et al. 2025) and Gaussian Filtering (GF). On CelebA-HQ FSS, CAT-both outperforms both NU and GF across all nine protective perturbation methods, with margins ranging from +0.003 (SDS(+): 0.602 vs. 0.599) to +0.210 (SDS(−): 0.678 vs. 0.468). On VGGFace2 FSS, CAT-both or CAT-en wins in 7 of 9 settings; the two exceptions are Mist (NU achieves 0.566 vs. CAT-both 0.557) and SDS(−) (GF achieves 0.593 vs. CAT-both 0.570). Overall, CAT achieves comparable or superior performance to both purification baselines without requiring per-sample optimization, making it more computationally efficient. The paper also notes that purification-based methods carry the risk of altering original image content, whereas CAT adapters are detachable and leave the base model unaffected at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a departure from purification-based adaptive attacks (IMPRESS, GrIDPure, Noisy-Upscaling) by reframing the robustness evaluation of protective perturbations as a model adaptation problem rather than a data cleaning problem. It builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the parameter-efficient mechanism, repurposing low-rank adapters from their original role in diffusion model customization to instead modify the latent autoencoder. The core insight—that adversarial protective perturbations work primarily by distorting latent representations—extends qualitative observations in Xue et al. (2024) and Cao et al. (2024) into a systematic quantitative and qualitative validation across nine protection methods, and then leverages this insight to target the autoencoder specifically rather than the full diffusion pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight, detachable adapter trained for only 1000 steps can significantly degrade the effectiveness of nine diverse protective perturbation methods, reinforcing a growing consensus that current protections against unauthorized LDM customization are fragile. The practical advantage over purification-based attacks is notable: CAT avoids per-sample optimization and does not require knowledge of the original data distribution, making it more scalable. However, the evaluation is limited to Stable Diffusion v2.1 and relatively small datasets (5 identities per dataset), and the paper does not explore whether end-to-end protections (e.g., AdvDM targeting the full diffusion process) might be more resilient than encoder-targeted ones. Open questions include whether protections can be designed to be robust against adaptive model modification, and whether the findings generalize to newer architectures like SDXL or DiT-based models.
