## Problem

Existing active learning methods for PDE surrogate modeling always acquire entire trajectories from the expensive numerical solver, even though states within a trajectory are highly correlated (reducing diversity) and only certain time steps carry the most information. This wastes the solver budget on redundant queries, limiting how many distinct initial conditions can be explored under a fixed acquisition cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial condition u^0 (a 1D or 2D spatial field, e.g., 256 points for 1D equations or 32×32 for 2D) and a committee of M Fourier Neural Operator surrogate models trained to predict state-to-state transitions. It outputs a boolean sampling pattern S of length L specifying which time steps to query to the numerical solver versus approximate with the surrogate, and ultimately a trained surrogate model. The FNO predicts differences between adjacent time steps rather than absolute states, and data is normalized by the initial dataset's mean and standard deviation over all temporal and spatial dimensions.

## Method

STAP introduces a selective time-step acquisition framework where, for each chosen initial condition, only a subset of time steps is simulated with the expensive numerical solver while the surrogate model approximates the remaining steps, reducing per-trajectory cost from L solver calls to ‖S‖ calls. The key acquisition function measures variance reduction between all pairs of committee members: it computes how much the disagreement between two models' rolled-out trajectories shrinks when one model is 'updated' with the other's predictions at the selected time steps, generalizing Query-by-Committee to partial trajectories. The sampling pattern S is optimized via a greedy bit-flip mutation procedure (T=100 iterations, ε=0.1) over the O(2^L) combinatorial space, maximizing the cost-weighted acquisition value a(u^0,S)/‖S‖. STAP operates as an add-on to existing batch AL base methods (SBAL, QbC, LCMD) that select initial conditions, reducing the optimization space from O(|P|^B) to O(2^L) per initial condition while preserving the diversity and representativeness properties of the base method.

## Results

SBAL+STAP achieves the lowest log RMSE across all five benchmark PDEs, improving over the strongest baseline SBAL by 0.286 on Burgers (−3.674 vs −3.388), 0.118 on KdV (−0.088 vs 0.030), 0.074 on KS (−0.349 vs −0.275), 0.064 on INS (−0.525 vs −0.461), and 0.059 on CNS (2.363 vs 2.422). Notably, STAP also reduces the 99%, 95%, and 50% quantiles of log RMSE simultaneously, a property the authors note is rare for active learning algorithms. On the KS equation, where no other baseline significantly improves over Random sampling, SBAL+STAP still achieves a clear margin (−0.349 vs −0.258 for Random).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBAL+STAP outperforms all four baselines (Random, QbC, LCMD, SBAL) on every one of the five PDE benchmarks. The strongest baseline is SBAL, which STAP improves upon by 0.06–0.29 in log RMSE depending on the equation, with the largest gain on Burgers (0.286) and the smallest on CNS (0.059). QbC is consistently outperformed, with particularly large gaps on KdV (0.354) and CNS (0.481). LCMD is the weakest structured baseline, performing worse than Random on KS (0.046 vs −0.258) and INS (−0.320 vs −0.422), and STAP improves over it by 0.21–0.88 across equations. The paper further shows that STAP's advantage is not merely in average error: it simultaneously reduces the 99%, 95%, and 50% quantiles of log RMSE, whereas baselines like LCMD improve the top quantiles but fail to reduce the median error below Random.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active learning framework for PDE trajectory prediction established by Musekamp et al. (2024), which always acquires full trajectories from a numerical solver. The key departure is introducing selective time-step acquisition: rather than querying all L time steps, the method uses a committee-based acquisition function (a generalization of Query-by-Committee) to determine which subset of time steps to simulate with the expensive solver, filling the rest with surrogate model predictions. STAP is designed as a modular add-on to existing batch AL methods (SBAL, QbC, LCMD), inheriting their diversity and representativeness guarantees while reducing the per-trajectory cost. The connection to multi-fidelity active learning is also drawn, where the sampling pattern acts as a fidelity selector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for scientific computing: by reducing the number of expensive solver calls per trajectory, STAP enables exploration of more diverse initial conditions within the same budget, which is the core bottleneck in PDE surrogate modeling. The finding that STAP improves all error quantiles (99%, 95%, and 50%) simultaneously is notable, as most AL methods only improve tail quantiles. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional PDEs or non-autoregressive surrogate architectures, how to handle the occasional simulation instability from out-of-distribution synthetic inputs more systematically, and whether the greedy bit-flip optimization over sampling patterns scales to very long trajectories (large L).
