## Problem

Existing work on online robust reinforcement learning is limited to CRMDPs with TV-distance uncertainty sets and relies on restrictive structural assumptions (fail-states or vanishing minimal values) that eliminate the information deficit between nominal and worst-case environments. This paper asks: under what general conditions can provably efficient online learning be achieved for RMDPs with arbitrary f-divergence-based uncertainties (TV, KL, χ²) without such structural assumptions, and what is the fundamental sample complexity?

## Input / Output

The agent interacts online with a tabular nominal MDP (finite state space S, action space A, horizon H) over K episodes, receiving state observations and rewards at each step. It is parameterized by either an uncertainty radius ρ (CRMDP) or a regularizer β (RRMDP) that controls the allowed distributional shift. The output is a robust policy mapping states to actions, along with estimated robust Q-functions and value functions at each timestep. A key structural requirement is the supremal visitation ratio C_vr (the worst-case ratio of nominal to worst-case visitation measures), which must be polynomial in H, S, and A for sublinear regret to be achievable.

## Method

ORBIT (Online Robust Bellman Iteration) is a value-iteration-based algorithm that, in each episode, performs backward Q-function updates using the dual formulation of the robust Bellman optimality equation (with problem-specific closed forms for TV, KL, and χ² divergences) plus an optimism bonus term scaled by the inverse square root of visitation counts. After computing the greedy policy from the optimistic Q-estimates, the agent executes it in the nominal environment and updates empirical transition and reward estimates. The central theoretical contribution is the supremal visitation ratio C_vr, which quantifies the information deficit between nominal and worst-case dynamics; the paper proves matching upper and lower regret bounds of Õ(C_vr^{1/2}·S^{3/2}·A^{1/2}·H²·√K) (for TV/KL), showing C_vr is both necessary and sufficient for sample-efficient online learning, and that unbounded C_vr makes the problem exponentially hard.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that ORBIT achieves regret Õ(C_vr·S²AH² + C_vr^{1/2}·S^{3/2}·A^{1/2}·H²·√K) for CRMDPs with TV and KL divergences, and Õ((1+H√S/(ρC_MP))·(C_vr·SAH + C_vr^{1/2}·S^{1/2}·A^{1/2}·H·√K)) for χ², matching the lower bound Ω(C_vr^{1/2}·√K) in the dominant terms. For RRMDPs, the TV bound improves by a factor of O(√S) over the constrained setting. In experiments on a simulated RMDP, all robust policies (TV, KL, χ²) outperform the non-robust baseline once the environment perturbation exceeds 0.6, while the non-robust policy's performance drops drastically. On Frozen Lake (H=25, K=1000), ORBIT converges consistently and outperforms the non-robust version across most perturbation levels P_perturb ∈ {0, 0.05, ..., 0.3}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between ORBIT (robust) and a non-robust baseline (standard optimistic value iteration with TV regularization set to β=10000, effectively eliminating robustness). On the simulated RMDP (H=3, S=5, A=5), the non-robust policy's average reward drops sharply as the perturbation rate q increases, while all three robust variants (TV, KL, χ²) maintain stable performance; specifically, when q > 0.6, all robust policies clearly outperform the non-robust one. On Frozen Lake (8×8 grid, H=25), ORBIT outperforms the non-robust version in most perturbation settings (P_perturb ∈ {0, 0.05, ..., 0.3}), with the gap widening as perturbation increases. The paper also compares CRMDP vs. RRMDP formulations: RRMDP-TV achieves a tighter bound (smaller by O(√S)) and lower training time due to closed-form dual solutions, while CRMDP-χ² has the highest computational cost due to its more complex inner optimization. No comparison against other robust RL algorithms (e.g., from Liu & Xu 2024a or Lu et al. 2024) is provided, as those are limited to TV-distance with fail-state assumptions that do not apply to the general f-divergence setting studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online RMDP learning framework established by Liu & Xu (2024a) and Lu et al. (2024), which were restricted to TV-distance uncertainty sets with restrictive structural assumptions (fail-states or vanishing minimal value). It generalizes to arbitrary f-divergences (TV, KL, χ²) for both constrained (CRMDP) and regularized (RRMDP) formulations, building on the robust Bellman optimality equations from Iyengar (2005) and Yang et al. (2023), and the optimism principle from Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2011). The key conceptual departure is identifying the 'information deficit' as the fundamental obstacle in online RMDPs and characterizing it through the supremal visitation ratio, rather than relying on structural assumptions that constrain the direction of distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish provably sublinear regret for online RMDPs with KL and χ² divergences, and to show that the supremal visitation ratio is a tight (matching upper and lower bounds) characterization of exploration difficulty. The hard-instance construction showing exponential hardness when C_vr is unbounded provides a clear boundary for when online robust RL is feasible. Limitations include the tabular, finite-horizon, (s,a)-rectangular setting; open questions include extending to function approximation, infinite horizons, and non-rectangular uncertainty sets, as well as understanding whether the supremal visitation ratio can be estimated or bounded in practice without full knowledge of the worst-case dynamics.
