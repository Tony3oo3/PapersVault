## Problem

The HiPPO initialization, which underpins most state space model dynamics, fundamentally assumes observed data is noise-free. In practice, measurements are contaminated by sensor noise, environmental variability, or inherent stochasticity, causing HiPPO-initialized SSMs to perform suboptimally on noisy real-world signals. This paper asks: can we derive an SSM initialization that explicitly accounts for measurement noise without changing model structure or increasing computational cost?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a univariate scalar sequence (e.g., a raw audio waveform of 8000 samples at 8 kHz for Free Spoken Digits or 16000 samples for Speech Commands) and outputs a 10-class label. The signal is processed by a 4-layer LSSL/UnLSSL architecture with N=128 state dimensions, 4 latent channels, and a linear encoder/decoder. The UnHiPPO initialization is computed once at setup time by running the Kalman filter over all integer steps up to t_max=1000, after which the SSM parameters are fixed during training.

## Method

The paper reinterprets HiPPO as a linear stochastic control problem where the signal acts as a noise-free control input, then reformulates it so the signal becomes noisy observations of a latent system, enabling posterior inference via a Kalman filter. To handle the fact that Legendre polynomial reconstructions diverge rapidly outside their domain, the authors derive a regularized dynamics matrix A_R using a pseudo-inverse that enforces linear extrapolation of the reconstruction. The resulting UnHiPPO dynamics (Eq. 24) combine the regularized transition with a Kalman gain, producing a single linear recurrence that implicitly filters observation noise. The σ² hyperparameter controls filtering strength, and the transition matrix is computed via a closed-form matrix exponential exp(log(t_{k+1}/t_k) A_R) for numerical stability. This initialization is applied to the LSSL architecture (yielding UnLSSL) by selecting time-varying UnHiPPO matrices at log-uniformly spaced time points, preserving the multi-timescale structure of the original LSSL while adding built-in noise robustness at no additional runtime cost.

## Results

On the Speech Commands (SC10) dataset with ρ=10⁻¹ Gaussian noise, UnLSSL achieves 79.3% classification accuracy using the closed-form matrix exponential discretization and 79.8% with backward Euler. The primary results (Figures 6–8) are reported as accuracy differences (UnLSSL − LSSL) across a sweep of noise levels ρ, showing that UnLSSL consistently outperforms LSSL at moderate-to-high noise while incurring a small penalty at very low noise. The σ² hyperparameter in the Kalman filter controls the denoising strength, and the closed-form discretization is only 50% slower than the trapezoidal approximation (2.8 ms vs. 1.8 ms for N=256), a one-time initialization cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is LSSL (Gu et al., 2021), the standard HiPPO-initialized Linear State Space Layer. UnLSSL (the UnHiPPO-initialized variant) outperforms LSSL in classification accuracy at moderate and high noise levels on both FSD and SC10, with the accuracy gap widening as the test noise ρ increases beyond the training noise ρ′ (Figure 6). When training and test noise match (Figure 7), UnLSSL shows a slight accuracy drop at very low noise (ρ near 0) but a clear and growing advantage as ρ increases, confirming that the built-in Kalman-filter denoising is beneficial precisely when the data is noisy. At the specific setting of SC10 with ρ=10⁻¹, UnLSSL reaches 79.3–79.8% accuracy depending on discretization, while the trapezoidal and forward Euler discretizations of the UnHiPPO matrix diverge on the long 16 000-step sequences, making only the closed-form and backward Euler options viable. Overall, UnLSSL wins on noise robustness without any architectural change or additional runtime cost, at the expense of a marginal accuracy loss in the near-noise-free regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HiPPO framework (Gu et al., 2020) and its application to LSSL (Gu et al., 2021) by identifying a fundamental assumption—noise-free observations—that limits practical applicability. The key conceptual move is reinterpreting HiPPO as a linear stochastic control problem (Aström, 1970) where data enters as a noise-free control signal, then reformulating it as a system with noisy observations of a latent state, which naturally yields a Kalman-filter-based initialization. This positions the work alongside related SSM robustness efforts (Yu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024; Agarwal et al., 2023) but distinguishes itself by deriving the modification directly from HiPPO's polynomial projection theory rather than proposing an alternative parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSMs (Mamba, S4, etc.) become the dominant architecture for long-sequence modeling, the noise-free assumption in their initialization is a real practical limitation for sensor data, audio, and financial signals. The UnHiPPO initialization offers a drop-in replacement that adds configurable noise filtering without architectural changes or runtime overhead, making it broadly applicable. However, the authors note they could not find a structured (O(N)) parametrization for the regularized or uncertainty-aware matrices, limiting efficiency gains for large state dimensions, and the σ² hyperparameter's scale (≈10¹⁰) makes it difficult to interpret as a direct noise variance, complicating practical tuning.
