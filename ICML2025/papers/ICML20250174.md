## Problem

Existing generative models for wireless channels (predominantly GAN-based) require large site-specific high-quality training datasets, cannot generalize to different system configurations (antenna counts, subcarrier spacings) without retraining, and produce samples that lack physical interpretability and consistency. This paper addresses these gaps by combining the physics-related compressibility of wireless channels with sparse Bayesian generative modeling (SBGM) to learn distributions of underlying physical channel parameters from imperfect observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy, potentially compressed channel observations y = Ah + n collected during normal AP/BS operation, where A is a known measurement matrix (identity for narrowband SIMO, a random selection matrix extracting M entries for OFDM) and n is AWGN with per-sample SNR drawn uniformly between 5–20 dB. It produces sparse complex-valued parameter vectors s in a physically interpretable domain (angular grid for SIMO, delay-Doppler grid for OFDM), which are then mapped to full channel realizations h = Ds via a system-specific dictionary. The grid resolution S controls the approximation error between s and the true continuous physical parameters, and the dictionary D encodes the system configuration, enabling generalization to new configurations by swapping D without retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a physics-informed prescribed generative framework where the channel is represented as h = Ds with s approximately sparse in a physically meaningful dictionary, and the distribution p(s) is learned solely from compressed noisy observations y = ADs + n. Two implementations are provided: CSVAE (a VAE whose decoder outputs conditional variances γ_θ(z) parameterizing a zero-mean diagonal-covariance Gaussian on s) trained via variational inference with closed-form posterior moments, and CSGMM (a GMM with zero means and diagonal covariances) trained via an extended EM algorithm. The key theoretical insight is that SBGM's structural constraint E[s|z]=0 and Cov(s|z)=diag(γ(z)) translates through the dictionary to E[h|z]=0 and a (block-)Toeplitz covariance for h, which exactly matches the physical requirement that stationary wireless channels conditioned on side information not containing path-loss phases must have zero mean and Toeplitz covariance. This makes the prescribed approach physically consistent by construction and sparsity-promoting, in contrast to the implicit AmbientGAN baseline which imposes neither constraint and thus remains an ill-posed learning problem. The framework generalizes to new system configurations by replacing the dictionary D at inference time while retaining the learned p(s), since the parameter grid G is configuration-independent.

## Results

On the Modified 3GPP SIMO dataset (16 antennas, 256 grid points, 10,000 training samples), CSVAE produces the power angular profile that best matches ground truth, while CSGMM and M-SBL yield sharper profiles with more peaks, and AmbientGAN significantly overestimates the angular spread. On QuaDRiGa 5G-Urban (M=30 pilots, S_t=S_f=40), CSVAE and CSGMM achieve the best nMSE and cosine similarity ρ_c across all training set sizes, with CSGMM closely approaching ground-truth autoencoder performance even at small N_t. On QuaDRiGa 5G-Rural (N_t=3,000), M-SBL performs worst overall, and while AmbientGAN shows good performance when the delay/Doppler bounds θ̄ and τ̄ are tight, its performance drops significantly as these bounds increase, whereas CSGMM and CSVAE maintain strong performance due to their sparsity-inducing and physically consistent regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is M-SBL (Wipf & Rao, 2007), which fits a single Gaussian to the sparse representation s (equivalent to CSGMM with K=1). On QuaDRiGa 5G-Rural, M-SBL performs worst among all methods in both nMSE and cosine similarity, while CSVAE and CSGMM clearly outperform it. On 5G-Urban, M-SBL performs consistently well (suggesting the true p(s) has Gaussian characteristics), but CSVAE and CSGMM still achieve the best reconstruction metrics. The implicit AmbientGAN for s improves with more training samples but does not outperform M-SBL on 5G-Urban, and its angular spread is significantly overestimated on the Modified 3GPP dataset. A black-box AmbientGAN trained to directly output h performs significantly worse than all physics-informed methods. The key differentiator is that CSGMM and CSVAE maintain strong performance even when the delay/Doppler grid bounds θ̄ and τ̄ are enlarged (weakening the implicit regularization), whereas AmbientGAN degrades substantially, demonstrating the advantage of explicit sparsity and physical consistency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Sparse Bayesian Generative Modeling (SBGM, Böck et al. 2024b) — originally designed for solving inverse problems in compressive sensing — as a generative framework for wireless channels, and adapts AmbientGAN (Bora et al. 2018) with a physics-informed forward mapping. The key conceptual departure from prior GAN-based channel modeling (which operates as a black box on raw channel realizations) is learning the distribution of physical channel parameters (delays, DoAs, Dopplers) through a sparse, dictionary-based representation, thereby shifting from analytically oriented to physically oriented channel models. The theoretical justification draws on Böck et al. (2024a), who showed that under stationarity, wireless channels conditioned on side information exhibit zero mean and Toeplitz covariance — a structure that SBGM's conditional zero-mean, diagonal-covariance model enforces exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for ML-aided wireless communications: the method learns site-specific channel distributions from noisy, compressed pilot observations collected during normal operation, eliminating the need for expensive dedicated measurement campaigns, and generalizes to new antenna configurations or subcarrier spacings without retraining. The foundation-modeling perspective (one trained model serving channel estimation, prediction, and precoder design) is a compelling direction. Open limitations include inability to model non-stationary channel trajectories, sensitivity to off-grid parameter mismatches, and constraints on OFDM pilot patterns (regular patterns degrade performance). Whether the prescribed SBGM advantage over implicit GANs persists in more complex MIMO-OFDM settings with larger parameter spaces remains to be validated.
