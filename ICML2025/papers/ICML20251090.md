## Problem

Geometry-only point cloud registration methods rely solely on 3D geometric information for correspondence estimation, leaving them vulnerable in low-overlap or repetitive-pattern scenarios. While RGB-D registration studies have shown that color and texture cues significantly enhance descriptor distinctiveness, pure point cloud registration has no access to the corresponding RGB images. This paper asks whether generative models can synthesize the missing color information from point clouds alone to improve registration accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of geometry-only source and target point clouds (no RGB) as input, converts them to depth maps via a camera intrinsic matrix, and produces a vertically stacked cross-view RGB image pair (2H×W) that is geometrically aligned with both point clouds. From these images, it extracts color descriptors (via DINOv2 or Stable Diffusion feature maps projected back to point cloud space) and fuses them with existing geometric descriptors via weighted concatenation, or alternatively constructs 6D XYZ-RGB color point clouds. The final output is the estimated rigid transformation (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ ℝ³) aligning the two point clouds.

## Method

The core contribution is Match-ControlNet, a matching-specific variant of ControlNet that generates pairwise images with both 2D-3D geometric consistency and cross-view texture consistency. For geometric consistency, it uses ControlNet's depth-conditioned generation with depth maps derived from the point clouds. For texture consistency, it introduces coupled conditional denoising: the source and target noisy latents are vertically concatenated into a single [2H′, W′, d] tensor, so the UNet's self-attention naturally establishes cross-view dependencies enabling mutual texture message passing without any architectural modification. A coupled prompt (instructing the model to generate two vertically stacked images from different viewpoints of the same scene) further guides consistent texture generation in a zero-shot manner. Optionally, few-shot fine-tuning (~3K RGB-depth pairs) refines only ControlNet's learnable encoder copy. The generated images are then encoded by DINOv2 or Stable Diffusion, projected to point cloud space, and fused with geometric descriptors via weighted concatenation (ω=0.5 default) or used to form XYZ-RGB color point clouds for color-aware registration.

## Results

On ScanNet (50-frame separation), Generative FCGF^SD achieves 94.4% rotation accuracy at 45° threshold (vs. 87.5% for FCGF), reducing mean rotation error from 19.4° to 8.4° and mean Chamfer distance from 100.7 mm to 66.0 mm. On 3DMatch (40-frame separation), Generative FCGF^SD reaches 98.1% rotation accuracy at 45° (vs. 94.8% for FCGF) with mean rotation error dropping from 9.4° to 4.5°. The generative variants consistently outperform their base descriptors across all three metrics (rotation, translation, Chamfer) on both datasets, with the largest gains observed on ScanNet where overlap is lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's generative variants consistently outperform all 8 baselines. Against FCGF, Generative FCGF^SD improves rotation 45° accuracy by +6.9% on ScanNet (94.4% vs. 87.5%) and +3.3% on 3DMatch (98.1% vs. 94.8%), while halving mean rotation error. Against Predator, Generative Predator^SD gains +6.1% rotation accuracy on ScanNet and +2.8% on 3DMatch. Against GeoTrans, Generative GeoTrans^SD improves by +6.5% on ScanNet and +2.9% on 3DMatch. The method also surpasses the end-to-end RegTR (94.4% vs. 94.1% rotation 45° on ScanNet) and the traditional FPFH by a wide margin (+21.1% rotation accuracy). Notably, Generative ColorPCR with synthesized RGB outperforms the original ColorPCR using real RGB data (93.2% vs. 88.9% rotation 45° on 3DMatch), demonstrating that generated color can even surpass real color due to reduced calibration errors and consistent lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges 2D generative models (ControlNet, Stable Diffusion) with 3D point cloud registration, a task previously addressed exclusively through geometric descriptors (FCGF, Predator, GeoTransformer) or real RGB-D data (ColorPCR, PointMBF). The key architectural departure from ControlNet is the coupled conditional denoising scheme—vertically stacking source and target latents so that self-attention naturally enables cross-view texture message passing—combined with a coupled prompt that guides consistent pairwise generation without any fine-tuning. The zero-shot geometric-color fusion step repurposes pretrained vision foundation models (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) as feature extractors on the generated images, a use case not previously explored in the 3D registration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play generative paradigm offers a practical path to enrich geometry-only registration with color cues in settings where RGB is unavailable (e.g., LiDAR-only capture), and the consistent gains across FCGF, Predator, and GeoTransformer on both ScanNet and 3DMatch validate its generality. However, the approach assumes known camera intrinsics for depth-map projection, incurs significant inference cost from diffusion sampling, and the moderate improvements (typically 2–7% in accuracy) may not justify the overhead in latency-sensitive applications like SLAM. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to outdoor/large-scale scenes, dynamic environments, or registration without any camera model, and whether the few-shot fine-tuning requirement can be eliminated entirely for truly domain-agnostic deployment.
