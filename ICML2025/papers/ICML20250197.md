## Problem

Prior empirical work showed that linearly combining scores from multiple diffusion models can produce out-of-distribution compositions (e.g., oil paintings of a specific dog, or multiple objects in a scene), but the theoretical foundations remain incomplete: it is unclear what it formally means for a composition to be 'correct,' under what conditions linear score combination provably achieves the desired result, and why it sometimes fails. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a formal definition of composition success and proving sufficient conditions for its achievability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the score functions ∇log p_i(x) from k pretrained conditional diffusion models plus a background model score ∇log p_b(x), all evaluated at the same noisy input x_t during reverse diffusion. The compositional score is computed as ∇log p_b(x_t) + Σ_i(∇log p_i(x_t) − ∇log p_b(x_t)), which is then fed into a standard reverse-diffusion sampler (DDPM, DDIM, or Predictor-Corrector) to produce composed images. The key design choice is the background distribution p_b: using an empty-scene background (rather than the unconditional) is critical for length-generalization, and the method requires no knowledge of the coordinate partition a priori.

## Method

The paper introduces Projective Composition (Definition 4.1), which specifies that a composed distribution p̂ must match each p_i when projected through an associated feature extractor Π_i, formalizing what 'correct composition' means without requiring a joint distribution. The central technical result (Theorem 5.3) proves that when the input distributions satisfy a Factorized Conditionals structure (Definition 5.2)—each p_i is 'active' on a disjoint coordinate mask M_i while matching the background p_b elsewhere—the composition operator C[p_b, p_1, …, p_k] = p_b(x)·∏_i p_i(x)/p_b(x) yields a projective composition, and standard reverse-diffusion sampling correctly generates from it because the diffusion noising process commutes with the composition operator. The result extends to arbitrary feature spaces via a reparameterization-equivariance property of C (Theorem 6.1), though sampling can become intractable in nonlinear feature spaces due to poor Lipschitz constants of the composed distribution (Lemma 6.3). A practical heuristic based on CLIP embedding orthogonality (Lemma 7.1) is provided to predict whether a new set of concepts will compose successfully.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative results come from CLEVR length-generalization experiments (Table 1), where 100 samples are generated per configuration and manually scored for objects in correct locations. The single-object empty-background composition (satisfying Factorized Conditionals) achieves near-perfect length-generalization: 99/100 samples contain all 3 objects, 98/100 for 4 objects, and 97/100 for 6 objects. In stark contrast, the Bayes composition (unconditional background) degrades rapidly, with only 32/100 samples containing 2 objects at N=2 and 77/100 samples containing 0 objects at N=4. The Bayes-cluttered variant (1–5 object distributions) also generalizes well, achieving 100/100 correct for N≤4 and 98/100 for N=5–6, supporting the theory that cluttered settings approximately satisfy Factorized Conditionals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so it does not benchmark against external methods but rather compares three internal composition strategies on the same CLEVR task. The single-object empty-background composition (which satisfies Factorized Conditionals) dramatically outperforms the standard Bayes composition: at N=4, the empty-background method places 98/100 objects correctly while the Bayes method places 0 objects in 77/100 images. The Bayes-cluttered variant (using 1–5 object distributions) performs comparably to the empty-background method for N≤4 (100/100 correct) and remains strong at N=6 (98/100), confirming the paper's theoretical prediction that cluttered settings approximately satisfy Factorized Conditionals. The paper also provides a qualitative comparison on SDXL (Figure 6) showing that composing entangled concepts ("dog" + "horse") fails while composing disentangled concepts ("dog" + "red hat") succeeds, consistent with the orthogonality heuristic from Lemma 7.1. No external baselines (e.g., other composition methods from Du et al. 2023 or Liu et al. 2022) are directly benchmarked, as the paper's goal is theoretical understanding rather than proposing a new method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first systematic theoretical analysis of the linear score-composition method for diffusion models introduced by Du et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2022), which had been used empirically without a clear formal account of when or why it works. It departs from the standard 'Bayes composition' framing (using the unconditional distribution as reference) by showing that a distinct 'background' distribution is essential for out-of-distribution generalization, and formalizes the correct target via a new notion called Projective Composition. The reparameterization-equivariance property of the composition operator (Lemma 6.2) and the non-smoothness result (Lemma 6.3) are new theoretical tools that distinguish this operator from simpler alternatives like the pointwise product.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the compositional generation literature by explaining, with formal guarantees, why some score compositions length-generalize while others fail, and provides a practical CLIP-orthogonality heuristic for predicting composition success before running expensive sampling. A key open question is sampling: Theorem 6.1 guarantees projective composition exists in feature space, but reverse-diffusion (and even Predictor-Corrector) may fail to sample it due to the composition operator's poor Lipschitz constant, leaving a gap between existence and tractable generation. The theory also clarifies that the common choice of the unconditional distribution as background is suboptimal for clean OOD settings, which has direct implications for how practitioners should configure their composition pipelines.
