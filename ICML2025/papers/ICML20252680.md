## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) have not been effectively applied to long-term time-series prediction due to two critical gaps: existing spike encoding methods treat input sequences monolithically, inflating hidden dimensions and undermining SNN energy efficiency, and Spiking Transformers lack proper positional encoding, causing self-attention to fail at capturing sequential structure. Additionally, no comprehensive SNN benchmark exists across the standard long-sequence forecasting datasets (ECL, Weather, ETT, Traffic, Exchange).

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time-series input x^{1:L} ∈ R^{L×D} (look-back window L=96, D variates) and produces a predicted future sequence ŷ^{1:H} ∈ R^{H×D} for horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Internally, the input is segmented into N patches, each mapped through a shared linear layer and temporally upsampled by a factor T_s (4 or 16 depending on dataset dimensionality) via zero-order hold before being converted to binary spike trains. At inference, the decoder reconstructs continuous outputs by averaging MLP predictions across T_s synchronized spike groups, effectively downsampling the high-resolution spike representation back to the target temporal resolution.

## Method

SpikF is an attention-free SNN architecture with three stages: (1) a Spiking Patch Encoder that segments the input into patches, applies a shared learnable linear projection + batch normalization + LIF neurons to produce sparse binary spike trains, reducing computational burden versus monolithic encoding; (2) a Spiking Frequency Selector (SFS) that groups patches, applies a Spiking FFT (S-FFT) to obtain frequency components, generates a binary spiking selector via a linear+BN+LIF pathway with cross-group spiking max-pooling, performs element-wise Hadamard selection of frequency bins, and reconstructs via grouped S-iFFT followed by another LIF layer to denoise; (3) an MLP Decoder with a temporal synchronization mechanism that upsamples the prediction target to match spike resolution, processes T_s groups independently, and averages at inference. The key insight is that FFT rotation factors inherently encode positional information, so S-FFT naturally captures sequential dependencies without explicit positional encoding, while the sparsity of spike trains makes S-FFT/S-iFFT operations scale with the firing rate α rather than full sequence length, yielding 3.16× lower total energy consumption than iTransformer.

## Results

SpikF achieves an averaged 1.9% reduction in MAE compared to the best baseline (iTransformer) across eight long-term forecasting datasets (ECL, Weather, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Traffic, Exchange), with the largest gain of 6.1% MAE reduction on ETTm1. In terms of energy efficiency, SpikF reduces total energy consumption by 3.16× and operational energy by 75.05% relative to iTransformer on the ECL dataset. On the short-term Solar-Energy benchmark, SpikF outperforms both iTransformer and iSpikformer by 2.8% and 2.3% in RSE, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikF outperforms iTransformer (the strongest Transformer-based baseline) on 6 of 8 datasets in both MSE and MAE, with the most notable gains on ETTm1 (6.1% MAE reduction) and ETTh2 (3.2% MAE reduction), but trails iTransformer on ECL (MAE 0.275 vs 0.270) and Traffic (MAE 0.296 vs 0.282). Against PatchTST, SpikF wins on all 8 datasets in MAE, with advantages ranging from 0.5% (ETTm2) to 17.5% (ETTh2). Compared to linear baselines (DLinear, RLinear), SpikF achieves substantially lower errors across all datasets, e.g., 10.4% lower MAE than DLinear on ETTh1. Against TCN-based models (TimesNet, SCINet), SpikF consistently outperforms, with SCINet showing the largest gap (e.g., 40.4% higher MAE on ETTh2). In the extended ETT benchmark (Table 12), SpikF surpasses the second-best SNN model (iSpikformer) by 1.4% MSE and 0.7% MAE, and beats the best FFT-based ANN model (FITS) by 3.4% MSE and 3.6% MAE. On the short-term Solar-Energy task, SpikF achieves the lowest RSE across all prediction horizons, outperforming iSpikformer by up to 4.7% (at H=6) and iTransformer by up to 8.7% (at H=96).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikF adapts PatchTST's patch-based segmentation into a spiking context (Spiking Patch Encoder) and replaces the self-attention mechanism with a Spiking Frequency Selector built on S-FFT, drawing from the frequency-domain time-series literature (FEDformer, FITS, FilterNet). It departs from the Spiking Transformer line of work (Spikformer, iSpikformer) by arguing that positional encoding in spiking self-attention is fundamentally inadequate—evidenced by Spikformer underperforming simpler SNNs like SpikeTCN—and instead leverages the positional information inherently embedded in FFT rotation factors. The training framework follows standard STBP with surrogate gradients on the SpikingJelly platform, and the LIF neuron model is standard in the SNN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpikF establishes the first comprehensive SNN benchmark across all major long-term prediction datasets (ECL, Weather, ETT, Traffic, Exchange), filling a gap that has limited SNN research in this domain. The 3.16× total energy reduction versus iTransformer while achieving a 1.9% MAE improvement makes it practically relevant for edge deployment and energy-constrained forecasting. However, the ablation reveals that the Spiking Frequency Selector contributes almost no accuracy gain over its removal (0.441 vs 0.440 MSE on ETTh1), raising questions about whether the frequency selection mechanism is truly necessary or whether the patch encoding and S-FFT alone suffice. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, real neuromorphic hardware deployment, and whether the frequency selection generalizes to highly non-stationary or multi-scale data.
