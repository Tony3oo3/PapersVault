## Problem

Identifying which genes were the targets of a biological perturbation (e.g., CRISPR knockdown, drug treatment) from transcriptomic data is critical for drug target discovery and cell engineering, but remains unsolved. Direct causal discovery is intractable at the scale of thousands of genes with only tens of samples per intervention, and existing methods rely on incomplete, noisy external knowledge graphs that do not generalize across cell lines or perturbation types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252689/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of single-cell transcriptomics datasets (observational and interventional), each represented as a matrix of log-normalized gene expression counts, and reduces them to summary statistics: a global N×N pairwise correlation matrix and T=100 local FCI edge-type estimates over random subsets of k=5 variables. The output is a per-node binary prediction (probability of being an intervention target) that accommodates multi-target perturbations, alongside predicted causal graphs for both datasets. On real data, N≈1000 candidate genes (top differentially expressed) are considered per perturbation.

## Method

CDN comprises two jointly trained modules: a causal structure learner (based on the SEA architecture) that maps each dataset to a predicted causal graph using axial attention over N×N×T×d input tensors, and a differential network that takes the pair of graph representations (combined via concatenation or subtraction, augmented with node-level mean/variance statistics) and classifies each node as an intervention target. The key insight is that differences between the two predicted graphs and their summary statistics are informative of which variables' conditional independencies changed, and this mapping can be learned end-to-end via amortized supervised training. Training proceeds in three stages: initialization from pretrained SEA weights, joint training on 8640 synthetic datasets with diverse causal mechanisms and hard/soft interventions, and finetuning on real Perturb-seq data using only the target loss. The graph prediction loss (L_G) provides an auxiliary supervision signal that stabilizes training and improves target prediction, while the target loss (L_I) is a per-node binary cross-entropy that handles multi-target interventions.

## Results

On five Perturb-seq datasets (seen cell line setting), CDN(Perturb-seq) achieves normalized rank 0.92–0.98 and Spearman ρ 0.67–0.83, substantially outperforming the best baseline PDGRAPHER (rank 0.48–0.54, ρ 0.20–0.41) and the statistical DGE baseline (rank 0.79–0.89, ρ 0.45–0.60). In the unseen cell line setting, CDN(Perturb-seq) retains rank 0.91–0.97 with minimal degradation. On synthetic benchmarks (N=20, E=40), CDN achieves mAP 0.82–0.90 for hard interventions and 0.76–0.97 for soft interventions, compared to the best baselines DCI (mAP 0.55–0.63) and MB+CI (mAP 0.47–0.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDN outperforms all nine baselines across both biological and synthetic evaluations. On Perturb-seq data, CDN(Perturb-seq) achieves rank 0.92–0.98, roughly doubling the best deep learning baseline PDGRAPHER (rank 0.48–0.54) and substantially exceeding GENEPT (0.31–0.52), GEARS (0.45–0.56), LINEAR (0.45–0.51), and MLP (0.45–0.53). The statistical DGE baseline is the strongest competitor (rank 0.79–0.89), but CDN still leads by 0.03–0.14 in rank and 0.17–0.38 in Spearman ρ. On synthetic benchmarks, CDN (mAP 0.82–0.90) dominates all causal discovery methods: DCI (mAP 0.55–0.63), MB+CI (0.47–0.79), BACADI (0.10–0.37), DCDI (0.15–0.27), and UT-Igsp (0.10–0.23). Notably, no deep learning perturbation model (GEARS, GENEPT, PDGRAPHER) exceeds the simple DGE statistical test, highlighting that current perturbation-effect predictors are not yet useful as scoring functions for target identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDN builds directly on the amortized causal discovery framework of SEA (Wu et al., 2025), repurposing its axial-attention architecture as a supervised graph predictor rather than a standalone causal discovery tool. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate literatures: causal discovery with unknown interventions (DCDI, BACADI, UT-IGSP) and perturbation target prediction in genomics (PDGRAPHER, GEARS, DCI). The key departure is treating the *difference* between two predicted causal graphs as the primary signal for target identification, rather than predicting the full graph or relying on external knowledge graphs, and doing so within a single end-to-end supervised framework trained on thousands of synthetic datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDN offers a practical path to reducing the combinatorial search space in perturbation experiments, which is a major bottleneck in drug target discovery and cellular reprogramming. However, significant limitations remain: the method assumes causal sufficiency (no latent confounders), cannot handle cyclic or time-resolved dynamics, and shows substantially weaker performance on chemical perturbations (3/6 targets in top 100) compared to genetic ones. The finding that no existing deep learning perturbation model outperforms simple differential gene expression (DGE) on Perturb-seq data is a useful negative result that tempers enthusiasm for complex architectures in this setting. Open questions include extending to latent confounding, bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap via self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled single-cell data, and scaling to dynamic systems.
