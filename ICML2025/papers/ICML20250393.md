## Problem

CAD sketch generation faces two fundamental challenges: the heterogeneity of primitive parameterizations (lines, circles, arcs, and points each have distinct parameter sets) and the permutation invariance of primitives (any ordering encodes identical geometry). Prior autoregressive models struggle with both, and existing discrete diffusion methods (categorical/multinomial) cannot represent blended class labels, making gradual information destruction impossible and severely limiting generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise seed and iteratively denoises it over T=2000 steps to produce a 2D CAD sketch encoded as a 16×20 matrix, where each row is a composite primitive containing one-hot class labels (LINE, CIRCLE, ARC, POINT, NONE), a construction-aid flag, and the full parameter set for all four primitive types simultaneously. At inference, the final class label is extracted via argmax and only the corresponding parameters are retained, while continuous parameters are weighted by rescaled type probabilities to recover a standard sketch representation.

## Method

SketchDNN introduces Gaussian-Softmax diffusion, a novel discrete diffusion paradigm where Gaussian noise is injected in log-space and projected back onto the probability simplex via softmax, enabling superposition (blended class labels) that standard categorical diffusion cannot support. This is combined with standard Gaussian diffusion for continuous parameters, creating a joint discrete-continuous diffusion process where each primitive is independently noised. The denoiser is a diffusion transformer (DiT) with positional encodings omitted to ensure permutation equivariance, and the model predicts the clean data point x₀ rather than the added noise. A variance schedule augmentation (Eq. 8) is proposed to correct the distortion introduced by the softmax projection, ensuring the argmax of the noisy label follows the intended categorical distribution. Training uses a reconstruction loss with MSE for continuous variables and cross-entropy for discrete variables, with timestep-dependent weighting of the MSE term.

## Results

On the SketchGraphs dataset, SketchDNN achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 7.80 (vs. 16.04 for Vitruvion) and NLL of 81.33 bits/sketch (vs. 84.80 for Vitruvion and 88.22 for SketchGen). In terms of sample diversity, SketchDNN attains a recall of 0.266, substantially exceeding Vitruvion's 0.176, though at the cost of slightly lower precision (0.246 vs. 0.294). The Gaussian-Softmax diffusion paradigm is the dominant factor: replacing it with standard categorical diffusion (SketchDNN Cat.) degrades FID to 148.93 and NLL to 106.10 bits/sketch, performing worse than both autoregressive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchDNN outperforms Vitruvion (Seff et al., 2022) on both NLL (81.33 vs. 84.80 bits/sketch) and FID (7.80 vs. 16.04), while also achieving higher recall (0.266 vs. 0.176) at the expense of slightly lower precision (0.246 vs. 0.294). Against SketchGen (Para et al., 2024), SketchDNN achieves a lower NLL (81.33 vs. 88.22 bits/sketch), though SketchGen is not evaluated on FID/precision/recall in this paper. The ablation with categorical diffusion (SketchDNN Cat.) reveals that the Gaussian-Softmax superposition mechanism is the primary driver of improvement: without it, the model performs worse than both autoregressive baselines on every metric (FID 148.93, NLL 106.10). The permutation-invariance ablation (SketchDNN Pos.) shows a smaller but consistent benefit, with NLL increasing from 81.33 to 83.03 and FID from 7.80 to 10.26 when positional encodings are added. A latent diffusion baseline (VAE + diffusion in latent space) also underperforms significantly (FID 93.34), confirming that data-space diffusion is better suited to the structured, heterogeneous nature of CAD primitives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm for CAD sketch generation (Vitruvion, SketchGen) by introducing the first data-space diffusion model for this task. It builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) for continuous variables and the Diffusion Transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2022) for the denoiser, but critically departs from Multinomial/Categorical diffusion (Hoogeboom et al., 2021) by proposing the Gaussian-Softmax distribution—a simplex-constrained relaxation that enables superposition of class labels. The variance schedule augmentation (Eq. 8) is a novel correction not present in prior discrete diffusion work, addressing the distortion introduced by the softmax projection on injected noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gaussian-Softmax diffusion paradigm offers a general-purpose mechanism for modeling discrete variables with gradual information destruction, potentially applicable beyond CAD to any domain requiring blended discrete-continuous generation (the authors suggest text generation). However, the current model only generates geometric primitives without constraints, limiting its practical utility for fully parametric CAD. Open questions include whether conditional generation can mitigate the observed failure modes (incoherent sketches, endpoint gaps), whether the x_0-prediction advantage over noise prediction generalizes to other structured domains, and whether the superposition mechanism transfers to other heterogeneous discrete-continuous tasks.
