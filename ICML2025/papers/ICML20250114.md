## Problem

Existing certified unlearning methods require access to the original training data to compute Hessians and other statistics for the Newton update, an assumption that breaks down when source data is unavailable due to privacy constraints, storage limitations, or regulatory restrictions. No prior work provides certified unlearning guarantees in this source-free setting, leaving a gap between the theoretical guarantees of certified unlearning and the practical reality of organizations that no longer retain their training data.

## Input / Output

The mechanism consumes the trained model parameters w*, the forget set D_u (m samples to remove), the Hessian of a surrogate dataset H_{D_s} (n_2 samples from a potentially different distribution ν), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs updated model parameters ŵ'_r = ŵ_r + n, where n is Gaussian noise with variance σ²I calibrated to the statistical distance between source and surrogate distributions. Notably, the method never touches the original source data D; all second-order information is derived from the surrogate, and the noise scale Δ explicitly depends on TV(ρ‖ν) or its KL-based upper bound.

## Method

The approach performs a single-step second-order Newton update where the retain Hessian is estimated from the surrogate as Ĥ_{D_r} = (n·H_{D_s} − m·H_{D_u})/(n−m), then adds Gaussian noise with σ = (Δ/ε)√(2 ln(1.25/δ)). The key theoretical contribution is bounding ‖w*_r − ŵ_r‖₂ ≤ Δ, where Δ decomposes into a term from the Newton approximation error (2γLm²/α³n₁²) and a term scaling with TV(ρ‖ν) between source and surrogate distributions. For practical deployment without knowing TV exactly, the paper approximates KL(ν‖ρ) by decomposing it into a conditional-divergence term (estimated via log-likelihood ratios of two classifiers) and an input-marginal term (estimated via SGLD sampling from the classifier's implicit energy-based model combined with the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation). This yields a fully source-free pipeline: the surrogate Hessian drives the update, and the model itself drives the noise calibration.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ξ=100), Unlearn(-) achieves 77.2% test accuracy and 77.3% forget accuracy, matching Retrain (76.9%/77.8%) and Unlearn(+) (77.3%/77.2%). On StanfordDogs (ξ=100), Unlearn(-) reaches 75.6% test and 72.0% forget accuracy, comparable to Retrain (76.0%/71.1%) and Unlearn(+) (75.7%/72.2%). MIA scores remain near 50% (random chance) across all methods and datasets, confirming effective unlearning. The required noise variance σ increases with distributional divergence (lower ξ or higher ζ), but utility is preserved within 0.5–1% of the retrained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three approaches: Retrain (exact retraining from scratch on retain data), Unlearn(+) (certified unlearning with full source data statistics), and the proposed Unlearn(-) (certified unlearning using only a surrogate dataset). Across all real-world datasets (CIFAR-10, StanfordDogs, Caltech-256) and all Dirichlet concentration levels (ξ=13, 36, 100), Unlearn(-) matches Retrain and Unlearn(+) within 0.5–1% on test, retain, and forget accuracies. MIA scores for Unlearn(-) stay within 1–2% of 50% (random chance), on par with both baselines, confirming that the surrogate-based noise calibration preserves certified indistinguishability. Relearn Time (RT) values are comparable across methods, indicating similar degrees of effective forgetting. The MNIST→USPS and USPS→MNIST cross-dataset experiments (Table 7) further confirm that Unlearn(-) generalizes to genuinely different surrogate distributions, achieving 91.1% test accuracy (M→U) and 90.4% (U→M) versus 91.4%/91.1% for Retrain. The key practical finding is that the proposed noise scaling (FS1(-)) is necessary: applying Unlearn(+) noise to Unlearn(-) (FS2(-)) consistently yields lower forget scores, demonstrating that the surrogate-aware calibration is essential for certified guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certified unlearning framework established by Sekhari et al. (2021) and Guo et al. (2019), which rely on single-step second-order Newton updates combined with Gaussian noise calibrated to an upper bound on the model difference. The key departure is removing the assumption that the source (retain) data is accessible: instead of computing the Hessian over the true retain set, the paper estimates it from a surrogate dataset and scales the noise by the total variation (or KL) distance between source and surrogate distributions. For practical KL estimation without source data, the paper borrows energy-based modeling (Grathwohl et al., 2019) and the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation, and validates on mixed-linear networks (Golatkar et al., 2021) to satisfy convexity assumptions for neural architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap: organizations subject to GDPR/CCPA often cannot retain original training data, making all existing certified unlearning methods inapplicable. The theoretical bound explicitly quantifying how surrogate-source distributional distance degrades (or preserves) certification is a useful design principle for practitioners choosing surrogate data. However, the guarantees require knowledge of the exact TV/KL distance, which in practice must be heuristically approximated, yielding 'potentially weaker but still meaningful' guarantees as the authors acknowledge. Open questions include extending the framework beyond convex/smooth loss settings to general deep networks, tightening the gap between the heuristic KL estimate and the true distance, and understanding how the approach behaves under adversarial or highly skewed surrogate distributions.
