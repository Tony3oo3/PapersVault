## Problem

RL policies are brittle because they pick up spurious correlations between high-dimensional sensory inputs and actions, failing under slight state variations. Existing information-theoretic regularizers like RPC and LZ-SAC only enforce consistency on specific aspects of behavior (state representations or actions separately) rather than on the complete trajectory. This paper asks whether maximizing the total correlation across entire state-action trajectories can induce simpler, more robust, and more compressible behavior.

## Input / Output

The agent receives either a robot state vector (e.g., 9-dim for Finger Spin) or stacked RGB image frames (3×84×84×3 with random shift augmentation) and outputs a continuous action vector bounded to [-1,1]. A learned 3-layer FCN encoder (256-256-60) maps observations to a 30-dim (or 50-dim for image tasks) latent representation z, which is parameterized as a diagonal Gaussian. The policy remains Markovian, conditioning only on the current state rather than a history, while the auxiliary prediction models consume a fixed-length history (8 steps for state-vector tasks, 3–5 for image tasks) via an LSTM module.

## Method

MTC-RL extends the standard RL objective by adding α·C(z₁;a₁;…;a_{T-1};z_T), the total correlation over the trajectory of latent states and actions. Since this quantity is intractable, the authors derive a variational lower bound using a history-based latent dynamics model q_η(z_{t+1}|z_{1:t},a_{1:t}) and a history-based action prediction model q_χ(a_t|z_{1:t},a_{1:t-1}), both parameterized as LSTM + 3-layer MLP. The lower bound decomposes into per-step log-ratios that act as an information-regularized reward r* replacing the original reward inside SAC's Q-function update. The trade-off coefficient α is not fixed but optimized via dual gradient ascent against a target information constraint I_p, automatically adapting the strength of the consistency pressure. The prediction models are trained by maximum likelihood on the replay buffer, while the encoder and policy are pulled toward the history-based predictions through the KL terms in the regularized reward, all within a standard off-policy SAC loop with dual Q-networks and target networks.

## Results

On eight DeepMind Control Suite tasks at 1M environment steps, MTC achieves the best or near-best score on 7 of 8 tasks, with particularly large margins on Hopper Stand (933 vs. 683 for SAC), Quadruped Walk (944 vs. 738 for SAC), and Cheetah Run (874 vs. 811 for SAC). In zero-shot robustness evaluations, MTC attains the highest aggregated normalized reward across all three perturbation types (observation noise, action noise, and mass scaling) compared to all baselines. On six image-based PlaNet tasks, MTC also leads with an average reward of 939 on Walker Walk (vs. 917 for RPC and 897 for CURL), and on Metaworld manipulation tasks it achieves the highest average success rate across all eight tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTC outperforms SAC on 5 of 8 DMC tasks (Hopper Stand, Finger Spin, Cheetah Run, Quadruped Walk, Walker Run), with the largest gap on Quadruped Walk (+206 points) and Hopper Stand (+250 points), while SAC edges out MTC only on Walker Stand (985 vs. 983). Against RPC, MTC wins on 6 of 8 tasks, with particularly large margins on Hopper Stand (933 vs. 568) and Quadruped Walk (944 vs. 842); the original RPC implementation (RPC-Orig) performs substantially worse, scoring only 20 on Acrobot Swingup. MTC dominates LZ-SAC and SPAC across nearly all tasks, with SPAC showing the weakest performance overall (e.g., 136 on Finger Spin, 347 on Walker Run). In robustness experiments, MTC achieves the best aggregated normalized reward under observation noise, action noise, and mass changes, outperforming all baselines including SAC. On image-based PlaNet tasks, MTC surpasses CURL, SAC-AE, and SAC on 5 of 6 tasks, and on Metaworld manipulation tasks it achieves higher average success rates than both RPC and SAC across all eight tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of information-theoretic regularization in RL from single-transition mutual information objectives (InfoMax, CURL, EMI) and sequence-level objectives that target either state consistency (RPC) or action consistency (LZ-SAC) to a unified trajectory-level objective: maximizing total correlation over the joint sequence of latent states and actions. The key conceptual departure is that total correlation captures all pairwise and higher-order dependencies within a trajectory simultaneously, rather than decomposing the problem into separate state and action regularizers. The paper further shows that RPC's regularizer can be derived as a special case of the total correlation formulation, providing a unifying theoretical perspective. Practically, the method is built on SAC with LSTM-based history models for the variational lower bound, and uses dual gradient ascent to adapt the regularization coefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a simple, task-independent regularizer that consistently improves robustness to observation noise, action noise, dynamics mismatch, and spurious correlations across diverse continuous control tasks, without requiring task-specific perturbation design. This suggests that encouraging compressible, periodic behavior is a broadly effective inductive bias for robustness. Key limitations include the fact that the variational lower bound is always negative (a sum of negated KL divergences) and thus cannot estimate the true total correlation, and the history-based LSTM models add computational overhead. Open questions include whether tighter or alternative bounds can better capture the true total correlation, whether the approach scales to high-dimensional real-world settings beyond simulation, and whether the benefits persist under more severe distribution shifts.
