## Problem

Variational diffusion models for generative tasks assume equal dimensions between latent variables, making them inapplicable to multilayer matrix factorization (MMF), where latent dimensions strictly decrease across layers. The only VI-based solution for MMF in the literature is the VAE, which relies on deep neural networks per layer and suffers from high-variance stochastic approximation as depth grows. This paper asks whether the computationally efficient, per-layer structure of diffusion VI can be adapted to the dimension-reducing geometry of MMF.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix Y ∈ R^{d0×L} (e.g., hyperspectral pixels with d0 = 156–285 spectral bands, or flattened grayscale/RGB images) and produces a low-dimensional representation X_T ∈ R^{dT×L} with dT ≪ d0, along with the factor matrices A_1, …, A_T. For hyperspectral unmixing, X_T is constrained to the unit simplex (one row per endmember, one column per pixel); for representation learning, X_T is non-negative and bounded in [0,1]. The dimension schedule is fixed (e.g., [64,32,16,8,dT] for hyperspectral or [256,128,64,32,16] for images), and the latent prior is application-specific: Dirichlet for simplex, Beta for non-negative bounded, or Gaussian for ICA.

## Method

The core contribution is a dimension-reducing diffusion variational process where each step t maps x_{t-1} ∈ R^{d_{t-1}} to x_t ∈ R^{d_t} (d_t ≤ d_{t-1}) via x_t = √a_t U_t^T x_{t-1} + √(1−a_t) e_t, with U_t semi-orthogonal. The generative model's intermediate-layer function is structured as f_{t,θ}(x_t) = B_t x_t + C_t ρ(A_t x_t), and B_t, C_t are analytically fixed to match the diffusion posterior mean μ_{t,φ}(x_t, x_0), so that the per-layer ELBO term reduces to a simple squared-error loss ‖U̅_{t-1}^T x_0 − ρ(A_t x_t)‖² weighted by the noise schedule. This eliminates the need for deep networks at each layer (unlike hierarchical VAEs), requiring only the factor matrices A_t and a one-layer network for the final-layer prior-dependent distribution (Dirichlet or Beta). The overall ELBO decomposes into T+1 per-layer terms, each amenable to stochastic approximation, and is optimized jointly with a semi-orthogonality regularizer on U_t using Adam.

## Results

On hyperspectral abundance estimation (MSE, lower is better), DRD-VI achieves the best average MSE on 3 of 4 datasets: SAMSON (0.328), JASPER (0.305), and URBAN (0.677), outperforming all baselines including the deep network MiSiCNet (0.461, 0.518, 0.955) and the VAE-based VASCA (0.401, 0.634, 0.785). On APEX, MiSiCNet (0.413) outperforms DRD-VI (0.609). For low-dimensional representation learning, DRD-VI achieves the best clustering accuracy on CMU PIE (0.615 vs. 0.395 for Deep Semi-NMF) and the best NMI on Fashion-MNIST (0.601) and CMU PIE (0.792), while remaining competitive with DMF and Deep Semi-NMF on other datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRD-VI consistently outperforms the VAE-based VASCA across all four hyperspectral datasets (e.g., 0.328 vs. 0.401 on SAMSON, 0.305 vs. 0.634 on JASPER), confirming the advantage of the diffusion-based variational process over standard VAE. Against the deep convolutional baselines CNNAEU and MiSiCNet, DRD-VI wins on SAMSON, JASPER, and URBAN but loses on APEX where MiSiCNet achieves 0.413 vs. DRD-VI's 0.609. Compared to the classical SISAL and probabilistic PRISM methods, DRD-VI achieves substantially lower MSE on all four datasets. For low-dimensional representation learning, DRD-VI clearly dominates on CMU PIE (Accuracy 0.615, NMI 0.792, ARI 0.479 vs. next-best Deep Semi-NMF at 0.395/0.677/0.304), but DMF and Deep Semi-NMF remain competitive or superior on Fashion-MNIST accuracy (0.620 vs. 0.588) and Silhouettes NMI (0.535 vs. 0.532). Overall, DRD-VI is the strongest method on the primary hyperspectral unmixing task and highly competitive on representation learning, with its lightweight per-layer design offering a practical advantage over deep-network-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the variational diffusion model framework (Kingma et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020) to the multilayer matrix factorization (MMF) setting, where latent variables have unequal dimensions across layers—a structural mismatch that prevents direct application of standard diffusion VI. The key departure is a dimension-reducing forward process where semi-orthogonal projection matrices U_t gradually reduce dimensionality, with each diffusion layer aligned to one MMF layer. This contrasts with VAE-based MMF (which uses full neural networks for the variational process) and hierarchical VAEs (which employ deep networks per layer), and differs from prior dimensionality-reduction-for-diffusion work (Rombach et al., 2022; Jing et al., 2022) that applies DR outside the diffusion process rather than embedding it within.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-layer light-weight operations (linear projections plus a single activation) make training substantially easier than VAE or HVAE-based MMF, while the ELBO decomposition yields closed-form or near-closed-form terms amenable to stochastic approximation. The approach naturally handles structured latent priors (simplex, bounded non-negative) through appropriate q_γ choices, broadening applicability beyond Gaussian latent spaces. Open questions include whether the log-inequality approximation in Eq. (24) remains tight for very deep MMF stacks, how the method scales to hundreds of layers, and whether the semi-orthogonality constraint on U_t is truly necessary or merely a convenient initialization.
