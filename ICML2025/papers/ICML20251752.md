## Problem

Prior membership inference attacks (MIAs) against LLMs assume the adversary can query the model or its logits, but in practice only the synthetic data is released, leaving the privacy risk of that data largely unquantified. Additionally, canaries designed for model-based attacks—high-perplexity, out-of-distribution sequences—are shown to be sub-optimal for detecting leakage in synthetic text, because such sequences, while memorized, have limited influence on in-distribution generation.

## Input / Output

The data-based adversary receives only the synthetic dataset (text sequences with class labels) and the target canary record, with no access to the fine-tuned model or its logits. Membership signals are computed by either training a 2-gram model on the synthetic corpus and evaluating the canary's sequence probability, or by computing Jaccard or embedding cosine similarity between the canary and the top-k (k=25) most similar synthetic sequences. These signals are then combined via RMIA as the ratio of the target signal to the mean across 4 reference models, producing a per-canary membership score from which ROC AUC and TPR at low FPR are derived.

## Method

The paper introduces two data-based MIA methodologies—an n-gram model and a similarity metric—adapted to the RMIA scoring framework of Zarifzadeh et al. (2024), requiring only 4 reference models fine-tuned on the same dataset with canaries inserted. The key technical insight is that canaries optimal for model-based attacks (fully out-of-distribution, high perplexity) are sub-optimal for data-based attacks: as canary perplexity increases, its influence on synthetic data decreases, reducing detectability. To address this, the paper proposes hybrid canaries with an in-distribution prefix of length F followed by a high-perplexity suffix, exploiting the greedy nature of autoregressive decoding (top-p sampling) so the familiar prefix anchors generation while the suffix ensures memorization. The 2-gram model consistently outperforms similarity-based signals, and the optimal prefix length is intermediate (0 < F < max), outperforming both fully in-distribution and fully out-of-distribution canaries. Finally, fine-tuning with DP-SGD at ε=8 reduces the strongest data-based MIA to random-guess AUC (0.5), confirming differential privacy as an effective defense while preserving downstream utility.

## Results

Data-based MIAs that rely solely on synthetic text (no model access) achieve ROC AUC of 0.74 on SST-2, 0.68 on AG News, and 0.77 on SNLI, substantially above the random-guess baseline of 0.5. The 2-gram model consistently outperforms Jaccard and embedding-similarity variants across all datasets. In contrast, model-based MIAs (with logit access) reach near-perfect AUC (0.89–0.999), revealing a large gap: canaries must be repeated ~8× more often in training for data-based attacks to match model-based performance. When DP-SGD (ε=8) is applied during fine-tuning, the strongest data-based MIA drops to AUC ≈ 0.5, confirming differential privacy as an effective defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between model-based MIAs (adversary with logit access to the fine-tuned LLM) and the proposed data-based MIAs (adversary with only access to the synthetic dataset). Model-based RMIA achieves near-perfect AUC (0.89–0.999) across all datasets, while the best data-based method (2-gram) reaches 0.68–0.77, a gap of roughly 0.2–0.4 AUC. The paper quantifies this gap by showing that a canary must be repeated ~8× more often in training for the data-based attack to match the model-based attack's performance at the same repetition count. Among data-based methods, the 2-gram model consistently outperforms both Jaccard similarity and Sentence-BERT cosine similarity by 0.05–0.15 AUC, indicating that membership signal resides in subtle n-gram co-occurrence shifts rather than lexical or semantic resemblance. When DP-SGD (ε=8) is applied, all data-based attacks drop to AUC ≈ 0.5 (random), confirming that formal privacy guarantees effectively neutralize the leakage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RMIA scoring framework (Zarifzadeh et al., 2024) from model-based to data-based membership inference, where the attacker only observes synthetic text output rather than model logits. It departs from the standard canary-based auditing paradigm (Carlini et al., 2019; Meeus et al., 2024c) by showing that high-perplexity, out-of-distribution canaries—optimal for model-based MIAs—are suboptimal when only synthetic data is released, and proposes a hybrid in-distribution-prefix / out-of-distribution-suffix canary design instead. The work also connects to the synthetic tabular data privacy literature (Stadler et al., 2022; Yale et al., 2019) but is the first to study this setting for text generated by fine-tuned LLMs, where the generative process introduces unique challenges in both signal extraction and worst-case canary identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard canary design choices for LLM privacy auditing actively undermine data-based attack effectiveness means that existing auditing pipelines may significantly underestimate the privacy risk of releasing synthetic text. The 8× repetition gap between model-based and data-based vulnerability highlights that synthetic data is a meaningfully weaker (but non-trivial) leakage channel, and the confirmation that DP-SGD (ε=8) neutralizes the attack while preserving utility provides a practical mitigation path. Open questions include whether the canary design insights generalize to larger models, different decoding strategies, or multi-turn generation, and whether the 2-gram signal persists at scale with much larger synthetic corpora.
