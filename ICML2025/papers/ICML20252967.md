## Problem

TABPFN, a transformer-based in-context learning model for tabular classification, achieves strong performance on small datasets but is fundamentally limited by the quadratic complexity of self-attention, preventing it from scaling to datasets with hundreds of thousands or millions of samples. The paper asks whether scalable attention alternatives can preserve the in-context learning capability that makes TABPFN effective while enabling efficient processing of large, high-dimensional tabular data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252967/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an entire tabular dataset in a single forward pass: training samples (features + labels) and test samples (features only) are embedded via MLPs and concatenated into one prompt, with non-causal attention ensuring training positions interact freely while test positions attend only to training positions. Output is a class probability distribution per test sample, projected from the transformer output at each test position. The system handles up to ~1K features and 100 classes natively, with random projection reducing higher-dimensional inputs to 1K features; for regression, targets are discretized into 10 or 100 uniform bins and treated as classification.

## Method

TABFLEX replaces TABPFN's softmax attention with non-causal linear attention (using the elu(·)+1 kernel feature map), reducing per-position complexity from O(n) to O(1) by precomputing two global statistics (Σφ(k_j)·v_j and Σφ(k_j)). The paper first demonstrates that causal alternatives (Mamba/SSMs) are fundamentally unsuitable for ICL because they cannot freely attend to all training samples, then shows non-causal linear attention preserves accuracy while cutting runtime by >2×. Three specialized models are trained for different regimes: TABFLEX-S100 (1152-length prompts, 100 features, 10 classes), TABFLEX-L100 (50K-length, 100 features, 10 classes), and TABFLEX-H1K (50K-length, 1K features, 100 classes), selected via a simple conditional rule on sample size and dimensionality. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that the straightforward two-line PyTorch implementation of linear attention already achieves O(ND) HBM accesses—matching the optimized FlashLinearAttention—eliminating the need for custom kernel engineering.

## Results

On 57 small tabular datasets (≤1250 samples), TABFLEX achieves a median AUC of 0.96 (mean 0.89) with a median runtime of 0.29s per 1000 instances, matching TABPFN's 0.97 median AUC while delivering a >2× speedup (0.29s vs. 0.82s). On the TabZilla hard benchmark (36 datasets), TABFLEX is one of only three methods (with TABPFN and XGBoost) to complete all datasets, and on the poker-hand dataset (1,025,009 samples) it achieves 0.84 AUC in just 4.88s, compared to 0.72 AUC in 15.36s for TABPFN and 0.54 AUC in 504.52s for the 5th-best baseline. TABFLEX also achieves a 1.5× speedup over XGBoost (0.29s vs. 0.49s median per 1000 instances) while maintaining higher AUC (0.96 vs. 0.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TABFLEX nearly matches TABPFN on small datasets (0.96 vs. 0.97 median AUC) while running 2.8× faster (0.29s vs. 0.82s per 1000 instances). Against XGBoost, TABFLEX achieves higher AUC (0.96 vs. 0.91) and is 1.7× faster (0.29s vs. 0.49s). On the TabZilla hard benchmark, TABFLEX substantially outperforms TABPFN on large/high-dimensional datasets — e.g., on poker-hand (1M+ samples) TABFLEX reaches 0.84 AUC in 4.88s versus TABPFN's 0.72 AUC in 15.36s, and on nomao it achieves 0.99 AUC vs. TABPFN's 0.76. Among all 25 baselines, only LightGBM is faster (0.25s) but with significantly lower AUC (0.83). On the fine-grained synthetic comparison, TABFLEX outperforms XGBoost in both accuracy and inference time for feature dimensions below 800, with XGBoost overtaking only at 800+ features. TABFLEX also outperforms sliding-window attention by large margins (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.48 AUC on poker-hand), confirming that non-causal linear attention is the right architectural choice for ICL-based tabular learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABFLEX is a direct architectural extension of TABPFN, replacing its quadratic-complexity softmax attention with non-causal linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) to enable scaling to millions of samples. The paper systematically rules out state-space models (Mamba) as an alternative, showing that their inherent causality fundamentally impairs in-context learning performance—a finding consistent with recent theoretical work showing ICL analyses assume non-causal attention. It also connects to the FlashAttention/FlashLinearAttention literature by proving that a straightforward PyTorch implementation of non-causal linear attention already achieves the same O(ND) HBM access as the optimized causal FlashLinearAttention, eliminating the need for further IO-aware engineering. Concurrent scaling efforts (TuneTables, MixturePFN, LoCalPFN, TabPFNv2) address the same bottleneck via prefix-tuning, mixture routing, retrieval, or improved low-data accuracy, whereas TABFLEX tackles it purely through the attention mechanism itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making ICL-based tabular classification feasible at the million-sample scale (poker-hand: 1M+ in ~5 s) with a 2× speedup over TABPFN and 1.5× over XGBoost while maintaining competitive AUC, TABFLEX removes the primary practical barrier to deploying prior-data-fitted networks on real-world datasets. The negative result that SSMs/Mamba are unsuitable for ICL is a useful caution for the broader community exploring sub-quadratic sequence models for few-shot or in-context tasks. Open questions include scaling beyond ~2K features, a principled (non-discretization) extension to regression, and whether the linear-attention swap transfers to the improved TabPFNv2 architecture. The paper also sketches preliminary results on image classification (MNIST, CIFAR-10), hinting at cross-modal applicability of the ICL + linear-attention recipe.
