## Problem

GenAI systems that answer every query immediately reduce user engagement with human-driven forums (e.g., Stack Overflow), which in turn starves GenAI of the high-quality, community-generated data it needs for retraining—a negative feedback loop. No prior work has formally studied whether GenAI should strategically withhold or degrade responses to certain queries, and under what conditions such 'selective response' can simultaneously improve GenAI revenue and user social welfare.

## Input / Output

The model is parameterized by an instance tuple ⟨a, γ, r, β, w^s⟩ specifying the accuracy function, discount factor, revenue function, user sensitivity, and forum utility, over a horizon of T rounds. The algorithm (ASR) additionally takes a finite action set A ⊂ [0,1] and an approximation parameter ε, and outputs a discrete strategy vector x ∈ A^T specifying the response proportion per round. The welfare-constrained variant (Theorem 4.7) additionally requires a welfare threshold W and is restricted to γ = 1, returning a strategy that approximately maximizes revenue while keeping welfare above W.

## Method

The paper models the GenAI–Forum ecosystem as a sequential game over T rounds where users choose platforms via a softmax over expected utilities, and GenAI's quality depends on cumulative data generated by Forum users. The central technical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that any τ-selective modification (reducing the response level in round τ) strictly increases data and user proportions in all subsequent rounds, establishing a compounding effect. For revenue maximization, the ASR algorithm discretizes the data state space into O(T/ε) levels and runs backward dynamic programming over (round, discretized-data) states, achieving an additive O(εL_r T²) approximation in O(T²|A|/ε) time. A welfare-constrained variant reduces the problem to a Weight-Constrained Shortest Path on a layered MDP graph, solved via a PARS-MDP reduction (Ben-Porat et al., 2024) in O(T²|A|/ε² · log(T|A|/ε)) time. Finally, the paper derives threshold-based sufficient conditions (involving thresholds B and C from Theorem 5.1) under which a regulator's one-round selective-response intervention provably increases social welfare while bounding the revenue gap.

## Results

The paper establishes that selective response can Pareto-dominate the always-responding strategy, improving both GenAI revenue and user welfare simultaneously (Observation 3.1). In a concrete example (T=10, a(D)=1-e^{-0.3D}, γ=0.9, r(p)=p², β=10, w^s=0.5), a strategy that withholds responses for the first 4 rounds yields ~5% higher revenue and ~7.6% higher welfare than always responding. The Revenue Price of Always Response (RPAR) is unbounded (Proposition 3.2), while the Welfare Price of Always Response (WPAR) approaches 2 (Proposition 3.3). The ASR algorithm (Theorem 4.4) achieves an additive O(εL_r T²) approximation to optimal revenue with runtime O(T²|A|/ε), and the welfare-constrained variant (Theorem 4.7) runs in O(T²|A|/ε² · log(T|A|/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the always-responding strategy x̄ = (1,1,...,1), which serves as the natural default for GenAI. Selective response strategies can strictly dominate this baseline on both revenue and welfare (Pareto improvement), or trade one for the other depending on instance parameters. The RPAR metric (ratio of optimal revenue to always-responding revenue) is unbounded, meaning that in instances with steep sigmoid revenue functions, always responding can be arbitrarily worse than the optimal selective strategy. The WPAR metric is bounded by 2, showing that always responding can at most halve social welfare relative to the optimum. The Price of Anarchy (PoA) is also unbounded (Theorem B.1), indicating that GenAI's revenue-maximizing strategy can be arbitrarily worse for social welfare than the welfare-optimal strategy. No competing algorithmic baselines are compared, as this is the first paper to formalize the selective response problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing 'selective response'—strategically withholding or degrading GenAI answers—as a mechanism design problem in a GenAI-forum ecosystem. Conceptually, it draws on information design (Bergemann & Morris, 2019) and algorithmic deferring (Mozannar & Sontag, 2020), but departs from the closest prior work (Taitler & Ben-Porat, 2025, 'Braess's paradox of GenAI'), where GenAI decides *when to train* rather than *which queries to answer*. Technically, the welfare-constrained revenue maximization reduces to the PARS-MDP framework (Ben-Porat et al., 2024), while the unconstrained case uses a novel discretized dynamic programming approach (ASR) with nontrivial approximation analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal evidence that GenAI can Pareto-dominate the always-responding strategy—improving both its own revenue and user welfare simultaneously—by strategically redirecting users to human forums to generate training data. This challenges the default engineering assumption that GenAI should answer every query and offers a principled framework for regulators to design one-round interventions that improve welfare with bounded revenue impact. Key limitations include the single-GenAI assumption (multi-platform settings may trigger a tragedy of the commons), the non-strategic Forum, and reliance on a Lipschitz condition on the accuracy function; extending the constrained algorithm to continuous strategies and relaxing Assumption 2.1 are identified as open problems.
