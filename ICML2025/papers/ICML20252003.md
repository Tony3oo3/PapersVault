## Problem

Continual Instruction Tuning (CIT) of Large Foundation Models suffers from catastrophic forgetting: gradient-based updates inevitably drive parameters toward new datasets, destroying previously learned knowledge. While Exponential Moving Average (EMA) naturally preserves old parameters, a fixed EMA weight cannot adapt to the ever-changing nature of sequential instruction datasets, leading to an out-of-balance between stability and plasticity. Existing solutions either expand model architecture (causing memory explosion) or apply regularization penalties that constrain plasticity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For multimodal settings, the method consumes RGB images paired with natural language instructions (e.g., 'Answer the question using a single word or phrase') and produces text responses or bounding box coordinates. For LLM settings, it processes text-only instructions from dialogue tasks. A key operational detail is that instructions are converted to TF-IDF token vectors and matched against a stored codebook via cosine similarity to determine which parameter set to initialize, meaning the same instruction template across tasks can trigger parameter reuse rather than expansion. The number of parameter groups is approximately 50% of the number of tasks, making it a limited expansion strategy.

## Method

The core mechanism derives a self-adapting EMA weight β_t at every training iteration by formulating an ideal state (zero loss change on new data, zero parameter change on old data), expanding the loss via Taylor series, and solving the resulting constrained optimization with Lagrange multipliers, yielding β_t = (L'(θ_t)+1)/((θ_t−θ_{t-1}*)·L''(θ_t)). Two approximations make this tractable: the Hessian is replaced by a finite-difference of consecutive gradients, and an L1-norm aggregation produces one β_t per module layer rather than per parameter. An instruction grouping strategy using TF-IDF vectors and cosine similarity against a codebook decides whether to retrain an existing parameter set or create a new one, limiting expansion to semantically novel instructions. The method is model-agnostic, applied here with LoRA adapters on LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL, and T5, and achieves the best trade-off between Avg.ACC, Forgetting, and New.ACC across all benchmarks.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark with LLaVA-7B, the proposed method achieves 64.64 Avg.ACC, 1.93 Forgetting, and 66.33 New.ACC across eight multimodal instruction datasets. This represents a +3.85 improvement in Avg.ACC and a −3.49 reduction in Forgetting over the strongest baseline (EProj), while also achieving the highest New.ACC among all methods. On LLaVA-13B, the method reaches 65.10 Avg.ACC with only 2.31 Forgetting, and on Qwen-VL it attains 55.64 Avg.ACC with 1.62 Forgetting, confirming generalization across model architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on all three metrics simultaneously on LLaVA-7B. Against the closest competitor EProj (He et al., 2023), it gains +3.85 Avg.ACC (64.64 vs. 60.79), reduces Forgetting by 3.49 points (1.93 vs. 5.42), and improves New.ACC by 0.79 (66.33 vs. 65.54). PGP (Qiao et al., 2024a) and MT (Zhu et al., 2024) achieve competitive New.ACC (67.09 and 66.00) but suffer substantially higher Forgetting (12.94 and 14.03), indicating poor stability. Regularization-based methods EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) and LWF (Li & Hoiem, 2017) reduce forgetting relative to LoRA Fine-Tune but at the cost of severely degraded plasticity (New.ACC 54.68 and 52.58). MoELoRA (Chen et al., 2024a) and the LoRA Fine-Tune baseline (Hu et al., 2022) are the weakest, with Forgetting of 25.71 and 37.29 respectively. Notably, the method also surpasses the Multi-Task upper bound (57.18 Avg.ACC) by 7.46 points, demonstrating that sequential continual tuning with the proposed EMA strategy can exceed joint training performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Exponential Moving Average (EMA) — a standard optimization technique — as the core parameter update mechanism for Continual Instruction Tuning, departing from the gradient-based regularization approaches (EWC, LWF, PGP) that dominate the CIT literature. It builds on model-expansion methods like EProj and MoELoRA but replaces their learned routing or MoE gating with a lightweight TF-IDF-based instruction grouping strategy that requires no extra trainable parameters. The theoretical contribution derives a dynamic EMA weight via Taylor expansion of the loss and Lagrange multipliers, providing a principled (if heavily approximated) alternative to the fixed β used in prior EMA-based continual learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for CIT that achieves the best trade-off between stability and plasticity across LLaVA-7B/13B and Qwen-VL, with the notable property of positive backward transfer on the LLM benchmark — something most non-replay methods fail to achieve. Its lightweight instruction grouping (TF-IDF + cosine similarity) avoids the memory explosion of per-task parameter expansion, making it deployable in resource-constrained settings. Limitations include testing only on image+text modalities, reliance on several loose approximations in the β derivation (Eq. 14–15), and the empirical fallback to β=0.99 when the computed value exceeds (0,1), which somewhat undermines the theoretical motivation. Extending to audio/video modalities and validating the dynamic weight derivation at larger scale remain open.
