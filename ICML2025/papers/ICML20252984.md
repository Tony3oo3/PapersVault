## Problem

Existing Riemannian optimization methods for low-multilinear-rank tensor completion exclusively use the canonical metric inherited from the ambient Euclidean space, leaving the potential of tailored metrics unexploited. The paper asks whether a data-driven preconditioned Riemannian metric can accelerate convergence of Riemannian gradient descent while preserving near-optimal O(n^{3/2}) sampling complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sparse 3rd-order tensor with entries observed independently at probability p (Bernoulli model), the target multilinear rank r=(r1,r2,r3), and a spikiness parameter ν (set slightly above the true Spiki(X*)). It outputs a dense completed tensor of multilinear rank r via iterative Riemannian updates. A spectral initialization step (Algorithm 2) first estimates factor matrices from the diagonally-deleted Gram matrix of the observed matricizations, then applies trimming and HOSVD to produce X^0 on the manifold.

## Method

PRGD replaces the canonical tangent-space metric with a data-driven inner product ⟨Y,Z⟩_{W_t} = ⟨Y ×_{i=1}^3 G_{t,i}, Z⟩, where each diagonal matrix G_{t,i} = (ε_t I + P_diag(M_i(G^t) M_i(G^t)^T))^{1/6} is built from the current Euclidean gradient's mode-i slice norms. Computing the Riemannian gradient under this non-canonical metric requires a novel tangent-space parameterization (Lemma 3.2) using G_{t,i} as gauge matrices, which restores orthogonality among the four tangent components and yields the closed-form projection P̃_{T_X} = W_t^{-1/2} · P_{T_X̂} · W_t^{1/2} (Lemma 3.4). Each iteration then follows the standard RGD loop—preconditioned gradient step, entrywise trimming for incoherence, and HOSVD retraction—with per-iteration cost O(n^3 r) identical to vanilla RGD. Theoretically, PRGD achieves linear convergence with a 0.351 contraction factor independent of the condition number under near-optimal sampling, and empirically delivers ~10× speedup over RGD and 3–4× over ScaledGD.

## Results

On synthetic tensor completion (n=100–200, r=5, OS=10–20), PRGD achieves approximately a 10× runtime speedup over RGD and a 3–4× speedup over ScaledGD, while maintaining the same per-iteration computational complexity as RGD. On real video inpainting (10% observed pixels), PRGD consistently attains the highest PSNR across all three test videos and all tested multilinear ranks (r=50, 60, 70), e.g. 28.25 dB on Tomato (r=70) vs. 27.50 dB (RGD) and 27.15 dB (ScaledGD). The theoretical guarantee establishes linear convergence with a contracting factor of 0.351 and near-optimal sampling complexity O(n^{3/2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRGD is compared against two baselines: RGD (Cai et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023) and ScaledGD (Tong et al., 2022). On synthetic data, PRGD wins decisively on both runtime and iteration count, achieving ~10× speedup over RGD and 3–4× over ScaledGD at OS=10–20; the advantage over RGD is most pronounced at low sampling levels (OS=10) and narrows as OS increases toward 100, where RGD approaches PRGD's iteration count. On noisy data (σ from 5×10⁻⁴ to 10⁻¹), PRGD remains faster and more robust than both baselines. On real video inpainting, PRGD achieves the highest PSNR in all 9 settings (3 videos × 3 ranks), with margins of 0.1–0.75 dB over RGD and up to 1.83 dB over ScaledGD (e.g., Hall Monitor r=60: 28.34 vs. 28.06 vs. 26.25 dB). The per-iteration cost of PRGD is the same order as RGD (O(n³r)), so the speedup comes entirely from fewer iterations rather than cheaper individual steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian optimization framework for low-multilinear-rank tensor completion (Cai et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023) by replacing the canonical ambient metric with a data-driven metric derived from the current gradient. The preconditioner is conceptually a diagonal, single-step simplification of Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018), adapted to the Riemannian setting via a novel tangent space parameterization that restores orthogonality of components under the new inner product. The key departure from prior RGD work is the demonstration that the metric choice on the fixed-multilinear-rank manifold is not fixed by the ambient space, and that a carefully designed non-canonical metric can yield substantial convergence acceleration at negligible additional per-iteration cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× speedup over standard RGD with identical per-iteration complexity and near-optimal O(n^{3/2}) sampling complexity makes PRGD a practically attractive option for large-scale tensor completion, as demonstrated on real video inpainting tasks. The tangent space parameterization framework is general and could be extended to preconditioned Riemannian conjugate gradient or Gauss-Newton methods, as the authors note. Open questions include whether entrywise (ℓ∞) convergence can be established to eliminate the trimming step theoretically, whether the approach extends robustly to noisy/outlier-corrupted data with guarantees, and whether similar data-driven metrics benefit other tensor formats (Tensor-Train, Tensor-Ring).
