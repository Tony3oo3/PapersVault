## Problem

Paraphrasing-based text watermarks offer a larger action space than synonym-substitution methods, but it remains unclear how to inject an imperceptible yet reliably detectable multi-bit signal while preserving the original text's semantic meaning. Existing approaches either use fixed-length codes, lack multi-bit support, or suffer significant performance degradation under common perturbations such as word substitution and sentence-level paraphrasing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252768/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an original text and a user-chosen binary watermark message, producing a paraphrased watermarked text where each sentence encodes one bit. The decoder takes any text, segments it at the sentence level, and outputs a variable-length binary string whose length equals the number of sentences (approximately one bit per 23 tokens for 128-token inputs). This variable-length design contrasts with fixed-length watermark schemes, allowing more information to be embedded in longer texts.

## Method

The core architecture pairs two LLM paraphrasers (θ₀, θ₁) that are fine-tuned to produce semantically equivalent but distinguishably different paraphrases, with an LM-based binary text classifier serving as the decoder. During encoding, the two paraphrasers alternate token-by-token based on the current watermark bit determined by a sentence-level segmentor; during decoding, the classifier assigns a bit to each sentence. Training uses a co-training loop: the decoder is optimized with standard cross-entropy loss, while the encoder is fine-tuned with PPO where the decoder itself acts as the reward model (combined with a similarity reward to preserve paraphrase fidelity). A critical initialization step jointly SFTs both paraphrasers on a paraphrasing dataset while penalizing their JS divergence, ensuring they start with a meaningful behavioral difference before the RL loop begins.

## Results

On the C4 RealNewsLike dataset (128-token texts), the proposed method achieves 95.63% bit accuracy with 5.57 bits per text, 0.9981 detection AUC, 98.0% TPR@FPR=1%, and 0.8739 sentence similarity (all-mpnet-base-v2). With five paraphraser repeats, detection AUC scales to over 0.9999 and bit accuracy exceeds 99%. The method also generalizes well to OOD datasets, maintaining AUC above 0.97 on five of six out-of-distribution benchmarks (HH, PKU, Reward, UltraF, FineWeb, Pile).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baseline families on every reported metric. Against RemarkLLM (Zhang et al., 2024), the gap is largest on bit accuracy (95.6% vs. 76.6% for 4-bit) and AUC (0.998 vs. 0.786), while also achieving higher fidelity (0.874 vs. 0.810). Against KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), the method improves AUC from 0.865 (zero-bit) / 0.833 (multi-bit) to 0.998 and TPR@1% from 25.9% / 22.9% to 98.0%. Against KTH (Kuditipudi et al., 2023), the advantage is most pronounced in the multi-bit setting, where KTH drops to 0.678 AUC and 61.3% bit accuracy versus the proposed 0.998 AUC and 95.6% bit accuracy. Against Waterfall (Lau et al., 2024), the closest competitor at κ=1 achieves 0.939 AUC and 62.4% TPR@1%, still trailing the proposed method by ~6 AUC points and ~36 TPR points, while the proposed method also maintains higher similarity (0.874 vs. 0.842). Notably, the proposed method is the only one that simultaneously achieves >95% bit accuracy, >0.99 AUC, and >0.87 similarity, whereas all baselines sacrifice at least one of these dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-training framework of Xu et al. (2024), which showed that a text classifier can serve as a PPO reward model to fine-tune an LLM for watermark detection, by applying it to the paraphrasing-based text watermarking setting with a dual-paraphraser encoder. It departs from prior paraphrasing watermarks (RemarkLLM, AWT) that use fixed-length codes and Gumbel-softmax token selection, and from LLM-output watermarks (KGW, KTH) that modify token sampling, by instead training two LLM paraphrasers to produce semantically distinct outputs at the sentence level, initialized with a JS-divergence penalty to ensure behavioral difference. The variable-length, sentence-level multi-bit encoding generalizes the fixed-length formulations of earlier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that a small (1.1B) model pair, fine-tuned with PPO co-training, can achieve near-perfect detection (>0.9999 AUC with repetition) while maintaining high fidelity and stealthiness, making multi-bit text watermarking practical at low compute cost. However, robustness under sentence-level paraphrasing remains the weakest link (AUC drops to ~0.86–0.93), and the fundamental vulnerability that a sufficiently strong paraphraser can erase the watermark signal is acknowledged but not resolved. Open questions include whether the co-training approach scales to longer documents, whether the dual-paraphraser design can be replaced by a single model with conditional generation, and how the method performs under adaptive attacks where the adversary knows the decoder.
