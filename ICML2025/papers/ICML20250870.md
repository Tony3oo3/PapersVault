## Problem

Conventional discrete diffusion models use dimensionally independent (product) denoisers for scalability, which requires hundreds of sampling steps to approximate the data distribution because they ignore correlations between dimensions (e.g., pixel-to-pixel or token-to-token dependencies). This paper asks: can we distill a many-step product-model teacher into a few-step student that explicitly captures these dimensional correlations, and what loss functions make this distillation theoretically sound?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250870/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained discrete diffusion teacher (product model) and produces a student mixture model that generates samples in fewer steps. For images, inputs are 32×32×3 pixel values (256 discrete levels per channel) on CIFAR-10 or 16×16=256 VQ tokens (codebook size 1024) on ImageNet 256×256, optionally conditioned on a class label. For text, inputs are sequences of up to 1024 GPT-2 tokens (vocabulary 50257) on OpenWebText, optionally conditioned on a 50-token prompt. The student model adds a single continuous conditioning variable λ~Unif([0,1]) to the teacher architecture, increasing sequence length by one token in transformer settings.

## Method

Di4C introduces a mixture model p(x_s|x_t) = E_λ[∏_d p(x_s^d|x_t; λ)] that represents dimensional correlations as a convex mixture of product distributions indexed by a random variable λ, remaining computationally tractable (Proposition 1 proves universality). Two loss functions drive distillation: a distillation loss (KL between teacher and student at a small time δ) and a consistency loss (KL between the composition p^θ_{s|u}∘p^ψ_{u|t} and p^θ_{s|t}), which forces the student to internalize multi-step teacher compositions in fewer steps. Theoretically, Theorem 1 establishes that N-step product-model sampling achieves O(1/N) total variation error (with a matching Ω(1/N) lower bound), while Theorem 2 proves the Di4C losses upper-bound the TV distance between the N-step teacher output and the 1-step student output. Practically, the consistency loss is computed via Monte Carlo sampling with a dimensionally independent control variate to reduce variance, and the student is initialized from teacher weights with zero-initialized λ-conditioning subnetworks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (discretized Gaussian diffusion), the Di4C student model substantially improves FID over the teacher at 10 steps (20.64 vs. 32.61) and 20 steps (9.77 vs. 12.36), while the teacher retains a slight edge at 40 steps (8.01 vs. 9.66). On ImageNet 256×256 (masked diffusion), 4-step Di4C sampling matches the FID-IS performance of the 8-step MaskGIT teacher, achieving an approximate 2× speed-up. On OpenWebText (masked diffusion language modeling), one round of Di4C on SDTT-6 yields improvements comparable to one additional round of SDTT distillation, and two rounds of Di4C achieve >2× speed-up while maintaining or improving generative perplexity and diversity (Self-BLEU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Campbell et al. (2022) continuous-time discrete diffusion teacher on CIFAR-10, the Di4C student wins decisively at 10 steps (FID 20.64 vs. 32.61, a ~37% reduction) and at 20 steps (FID 9.77 vs. 12.36), but the teacher is marginally better at 40 steps (FID 8.01 vs. 9.66), suggesting Di4C's benefit is concentrated in the few-step regime where dimensional correlations matter most. Against the Besnier & Chen (2023) MaskGIT teacher on ImageNet 256×256, the 4-step Di4C model closely matches the 8-step teacher's FID-IS curve across all CFG coefficients, effectively halving the sampling cost without quality loss. Against the Deschenaux & Gulcehre (2025) SDTT language model baselines on OpenWebText, Di4C-distilled models (sdtt-6+di4c, sdtt-6+di4c²) consistently achieve lower Self-BLEU (higher diversity) than sdtt-7 at the same number of steps while maintaining comparable or better generative perplexity, and two rounds of Di4C match the teacher's 64- or 128-step performance in under 32 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

Di4C extends the consistency-type distillation framework (Song et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024) from continuous-state to discrete-state diffusion, identifying the specific obstacle that product models ignore dimensional correlations. It builds on the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework of Campbell et al. (2022) and the masked diffusion paradigm of MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) and MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), proposing a mixture-of-product architecture and two novel loss functions (distillation + consistency) tailored to the discrete setting. The approach also generalizes the multi-round distillation spirit of SDTT (Deschenaux & Gulcehre, 2025) through iterated Di4C training, and connects to the Gaussian mixture denoising idea of Li et al. (2024) in the continuous domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of why discrete diffusion fundamentally requires many steps (O(1/N) TV error with a matching lower bound) and proves that its proposed losses can in principle distill to one step, giving practitioners a principled path to faster discrete sampling. The mixture model adds negligible latency (≤5%) while enabling correlation capture, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing discrete diffusion pipelines. However, empirical results achieve only ~2× speedup rather than the theoretical one-step limit, leaving open how to better optimize the λ-conditioning architecture and training schedule to close this gap. The method's generality across U-Net/Transformer backbones and image/text modalities suggests broad applicability, but scaling to larger discrete state spaces (e.g., higher-resolution VQ tokens) remains untested.
