## Problem

While computing optimal decision trees from scratch is well understood, the algorithmic complexity of optimally pruning a given tree has received scant attention. This paper initiates a rigorous study of two fundamental pruning operations—subtree replacement and subtree raising—revealing the surprising asymmetry that replacement is polynomial-time solvable while raising is NP-complete, and then provides a near-complete parameterized complexity classification to delineate tractable from intractable regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled training dataset (E, λ) over d features with two classes, a binary decision tree T for that dataset, and integer parameters k (number of inner nodes to prune) and t (upper bound on resulting misclassifications). It produces a yes/no feasibility answer for the pruning problem, and in the empirical component, the full Pareto front mapping every achievable number of pruned nodes to the minimum possible classification error. The trees are assumed reasonable (no empty leaves, each leaf labeled by the majority class), and the domain size D per feature is bounded by the number of distinct thresholds appearing in T plus two.

## Method

For subtree replacement, the paper improves upon Almuallim's O((n+ℓ)s) DP to O((n + min{k², t²})·s) by indexing the recurrence on the smaller of k or t, and further accelerates example classification to O(d log²s) per example via heavy-light decomposition. For subtree raising, the key algorithm is a bottom-up DP over the tree where each entry is indexed by the current node, remaining pruning budget, and the two strongest surviving cuts (leftmost and rightmost thresholds) in every feature, yielding O(D^{2d}·s³) time; this is tightened to O((k+1)^{2d_T}·s³) for the exactly-k variant by restricting thresholds to the k+1 strongest cuts per feature. The hardness side establishes W[1]-hardness and ETH-based lower bounds for k, ℓ, d, and d+ℓ, plus paraNP-hardness of DTRAIS≥ even at k=0, via reductions from κ-Independent Set, κ-Hitting Set, and Multicolored Clique. A proof-of-concept implementation on 26 PMLB benchmark instances confirms that standard C4.5 heuristics achieve near-optimal tradeoffs between pruned nodes and errors.

## Results

Theoretically, DTREP (subtree replacement) is solvable in O((n + min{k², t²}) · s) time, improving on the prior O((n+ℓ)s) quadratic-in-tree-size bound. DTRAIS (subtree raising) is NP-complete, but admits an XP algorithm running in O(D^{2d} · s³) time and an FPT algorithm for DTRAIS= parameterized by k+d running in O((k+1)^{2d_T} · s³) time. Experimentally, on 26 of 40 benchmark datasets (solved within 64 GB / 24 h), the C4.5 raising heuristic achieves the optimal number of pruned nodes in 22/26 cases and the minimum possible errors in 22/26 cases, with gaps of at most 2 nodes or 1 error in the remaining instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its exact DP algorithm (based on Theorem 4.2) against the raising and replacement heuristics of WEKA's C4.5 implementation (Quinlan, 1993) on 26 solvable instances from the PMLB benchmark. The raising heuristic matches the optimal number of pruned nodes (k_rais = k*) on 22 of 26 instances and achieves the minimum possible errors (t_rais = t*) on 22 of 26 instances; the four suboptimal cases show gaps of at most 2 additional prunable nodes or 1 fewer error. The replacement heuristic is slightly weaker: on heart-statlog it prunes only 27 nodes versus the raising heuristic's 31 (matching k*), and on haberman it prunes 71 versus 74. Overall, the heuristics achieve near-optimal tradeoffs between pruned nodes and classification errors, with the paper noting that in many cases more nodes could be pruned without significantly worsening accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameterized complexity analysis of decision tree learning (Ordyniak & Szeider, 2021; Staus et al., 2025; Komusiewicz et al., 2023a) from tree construction to the previously under-studied problem of optimally pruning a given tree. For subtree replacement, it improves upon Almuallim's (1996) quadratic-time dynamic programming algorithm to achieve linear dependence on tree size when the pruning budget or error bound is small, using heavy-light decomposition (Sleator & Tarjan, 1983) for faster classification. For subtree raising, it leverages the witness tree enumeration technique of Komusiewicz et al. (2023b) to obtain an XP algorithm, and positions its hardness results within the W[1]/W[2] hierarchy and ETH framework standard in parameterized complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete parameterized complexity classification of subtree raising provides a precise map of when efficient pruning algorithms exist (e.g., FPT for k+d on the exactly variant, XP for D^{2d_T}·|I|^{O(1)}), which directly informs algorithm design for interpretable ML. The experimental finding that standard C4.5 heuristics achieve near-optimal Pareto tradeoffs despite the underlying NP-completeness is practically reassuring but also raises the open question of whether other local operations (e.g., partial subtree reconstruction) could yield more substantial improvements over heuristics. Key open problems include parameterized tractability of ensemble pruning beyond the NP-hardness for two trees, and the unresolved FPT/XP status of combinations like d+t+ℓ.
