## Problem

Existing fair graph generation methods address only structural bias (over-connectivity within sensitive groups) while overlooking feature bias, where generated non-sensitive attributes exhibit distributional disparities across subgroups. Since feature and structural biases are mutually reinforcing through GNN message passing, mitigating only one leaves the other unaddressed, producing synthetic graphs that still propagate discrimination in downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252602/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FDGen consumes an attributed graph (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, binary adjacency A, and binary sensitive attribute vector S) and produces a synthetic graph with reconstructed node features and adjacency. The sensitive attribute S is preserved throughout training and sampling (initialized from its original distribution at inference), so the model learns how sensitive attributes correlate with structure without amplifying undesirable biases. Node representations are internally decomposed into N_c disentangled channels, with one channel identified as sensitive-related and the remainder treated as sensitive-irrelevant for fairness enforcement.

## Method

FDGen builds on the GRAPHARM autoregressive diffusion framework, where a learned node-ordering network sequentially masks nodes in the forward pass and a GNN-based denoiser reconstructs them in the reverse pass. The key innovation is a multi-channel GAT-style aggregator that decomposes each node embedding into N_c latent-factor channels via dot-product attention, enforced to be mutually independent by a distance-covariance regularizer. A channel-level discriminator then identifies which single channel encodes the sensitive attribute (guaranteed unique by Theorem 4.4), allowing the fairness regularizer to apply MMD alignment only to the sensitive-irrelevant channels. The fairness regularizer L_T combines an MMD term (L_f) aligning non-sensitive representations across groups with a structural term (L_g) penalizing discrepancies between predicted and ground-truth inter/intra-group edge counts. The final training objective augments the diffusion variational lower bound with L_f, L_g, the disentanglement loss, and the discriminator loss, with theoretical upper bounds (Theorems 4.2–4.3) linking representation discrepancy to demographic parity in downstream predictions.

## Results

On the Cora dataset, FDGen-GCN achieves 80.05% node classification accuracy while attaining the best fairness metrics among all methods: ΔDP of 13.88% and ΔEO of 11.95%. This represents a 0.28 percentage point improvement in ΔDP over the closest fairness-aware baseline (FairGen at 14.16%) and a 1.40 point improvement in ΔEO over FairGen (13.35%). In graph generation quality (Figure 2), FDGen achieves comparable or better MMD scores for degree distribution and clustering coefficient than baselines while significantly reducing fair degree distribution and fair clustering coefficient disparities across subgroups on all four datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Photo, Computer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDGen outperforms all five baselines on both fairness metrics (ΔDP and ΔEO) while maintaining competitive accuracy. Against the strongest fairness-aware baseline FairGen, FDGen reduces ΔDP from 14.16% to 13.88% and ΔEO from 13.35% to 11.95%, while also improving accuracy from 79.54% to 80.05%. Compared to FairWire (which addresses only structural bias via a diffusion-based regularizer), FDGen achieves a 0.88 point lower ΔDP and a 1.70 point lower ΔEO, demonstrating the benefit of simultaneously mitigating feature bias. Against FG²AN and FairAdj, which use adversarial training and adjacency modification respectively, FDGen leads by 4.78 and 3.25 points on ΔDP, and by 2.10 and 2.01 points on ΔEO. In terms of generation quality (Figure 2), FDGen matches or exceeds GRAPHARM on MMD for degree distribution and clustering coefficient across all four datasets, while substantially reducing the proposed fair degree distribution and fair clustering coefficient metrics—confirming that the fairness gains do not come at the cost of graph fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FDGen builds directly on the autoregressive diffusion framework of GRAPHARM (Kong et al., 2023) for graph generation, extending it with a fairness-aware training objective. It departs from prior fair graph generation methods (FairWire, FairGen, FG2AN, FairAdj) that address only structural bias by introducing feature bias as a distinct and co-equal concern, using multi-channel GAT disentanglement with distance covariance to isolate sensitive-irrelevant representations. The theoretical grounding via MMD-based representation discrepancy bounds (Theorems 4.2–4.3) connects the disentanglement mechanism to demographic parity, providing a principled justification for the fairness regularizer that prior fair graph generation work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying feature bias as a separate axis of unfairness in graph generation is a useful conceptual contribution that opens a new research direction beyond structural parity. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments use only binary sensitive attributes, four relatively small datasets (≤14K nodes), and a single downstream classifier (GCN), leaving open whether the disentanglement-based approach scales to multi-attribute settings, heterogeneous feature types, or large-scale graphs. The modest numerical gains over the strongest fairness baselines (e.g., ΔDP improving from 14.16 to 13.88 on Cora) suggest the theoretical framework is sound but the empirical advantage is not yet decisive.
