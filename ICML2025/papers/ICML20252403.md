## Problem

Replay-based online continual learning methods suffer from a stability gap (sudden accuracy drops at task boundaries) and loss of plasticity, because first-order optimizers treat all parameter directions equally and cannot distinguish between directions that cause forgetting and those that enable fast adaptation. Existing approaches either penalize plasticity to prevent forgetting or project gradients to avoid interference, but none provide a principled mechanism to simultaneously maximize both stability and plasticity at every step of a nonstationary stream.

## Input / Output

At each step the method consumes a tiny minibatch of 10 new images from the nonstationary stream plus 10 samples drawn from a reservoir-sampling replay buffer (2000 samples for CIFAR-100/CLEAR, 4000 for TinyImageNet), with no access to task identity, boundaries, or length. The model (Slim-ResNet18 or ResNet18) produces class predictions and updated parameters; it must be inference-ready at every iteration. The only additional signal beyond the data is the number of new classes per experience, used solely to schedule the buffer-weighting parameter λ.

## Method

OCAR formalizes each OCL step as a second-order joint optimization over new and replay data with an explicit KL-divergence constraint on the buffer, solved by preconditioning the gradient with the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) rather than using it as a quadratic penalty. The FIM is approximated via block-diagonal K-FAC with exponential moving averages, adapted for non-i.i.d. streams by resetting the last-layer G-factor when the classifier shape changes. A Tikhonov damping term τ (initialized at the learning rate and increased per step) caps acceleration in low-curvature directions, while a scheduled λ up-weights the buffer FIM as information content grows. The resulting update δ* = −α(F_N + (1+λ)F_B + τI)⁻¹(∇_N + ∇_B) acts as a metric-adapted step in the information-geometric manifold, accelerating learning in non-interfering directions while constraining movement in directions that would shift predictions on past data.

## Results

On Split-CIFAR100 (20 tasks), OCAR achieves 48.2% Average Anytime Accuracy (AAA) and 25.0% Worst-Case Accuracy (WC-Acc), substantially outperforming the previous SOTA LPR (42.5% AAA, 19.3% WC-Acc) and basic ER (36.6% AAA, 12.5% WC-Acc). On Split-TinyImageNet, OCAR reaches 38.3% AAA and 17.4% WC-Acc, again beating LPR (34.9% AAA, 16.2% WC-Acc). On the domain-incremental Online CLEAR benchmark, OCAR achieves 75.3% final accuracy and 73.9% AAA, a large margin over LPR (65.2% Acc, 63.5% AAA). OCAR also obtains the best Probed Accuracy (46.2% on CIFAR100, 38.3% on TinyImageNet), surpassing even the i.i.d. upper bound on CIFAR100 (45.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCAR outperforms the previous SOTA LPR (ICML 2024) on all continual metrics: on Split-CIFAR100 it gains +5.7 points in AAA (48.2 vs 42.5) and +5.7 in WC-Acc (25.0 vs 19.3); on Online CLEAR the margin is even larger at +10.4 points in final accuracy (75.3 vs 65.2) and +10.4 in AAA. Against basic ER, OCAR improves AAA by +11.6 points on CIFAR100 and +4.4 on TinyImageNet, while WC-Acc more than doubles on CIFAR100 (25.0 vs 12.5). On final accuracy (Acc), OCAR leads all base methods on CIFAR100 (34.9 vs LPR's 33.3) but trails ER-ACE on TinyImageNet (21.7 vs 23.6), which the authors attribute to ER-ACE's artificially low plasticity. The combination OCAR-ACE surpasses even the i.i.d. upper bound on CIFAR100 (35.6 vs 35.3) and achieves the best WC-Acc (26.5) across all methods. OCAR also beats EWC+ER and NGD+ER in a controlled convex experiment, showing lower cumulative loss on both current and past data, confirming that using the FIM as a preconditioner (rather than a quadratic penalty) is more effective in nonstationary settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCAR extends the Experience Replay optimization framework by reformulating it as a second-order problem with an explicit KL-divergence stability constraint, then approximating the resulting Hessian/FIM via K-FAC. Conceptually it sits at the intersection of Natural Gradient (using the FIM as a preconditioner rather than a quadratic penalty as in EWC) and replay-based OCL, but departs from both: unlike NG it is derived for non-i.i.d. streams with a buffer-weighted Fisher, and unlike projection-based methods (GEM, LPR) it remains fully compatible with rehearsal. The Tikhonov damping analysis and the λ scheduling tied to buffer information content are novel practical contributions not present in prior second-order CL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled optimization-based explanation for the stability gap and loss of plasticity observed in OCL, and shows that a single second-order preconditioner can simultaneously address both, rather than requiring separate stability and plasticity mechanisms. The OCAR-ACE combination matching i.i.d. accuracy on Split-CIFAR100 is a strong practical signal that the optimization framing is not merely theoretical. Open questions include whether dynamic adaptation of α and τ (rather than fixed schedules) can further close the gap, whether the approach scales to larger architectures or longer streams, and whether the class-count information needed for λ scheduling can be fully eliminated in truly task-agnostic settings.
