## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs frequently over-refuse benign pseudo-harmful prompts, degrading user experience and model utility. Existing mitigations are either data- and compute-intensive (full-model fine-tuning) or brittle under distribution shifts (fixed steering vectors applied uniformly at inference time), leaving a gap for an efficient, robust method that provides fine-grained, query-adaptive control over the refusal mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250457/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ACTOR consumes only raw text queries (harmful, benign, and pseudo-harmful) without requiring any generated responses or reference answers for training, making it a response-free procedure. It produces updated weights for a single transformer layer (e.g., layer 13 for Llama-2-7b-chat), which when applied to the base model yields a calibrated model that generates text responses with reduced over-refusal while preserving safety on genuinely harmful inputs.

## Method

ACTOR first identifies a safety-critical middle layer via silhouette-score clustering of last-token activations, then extracts a refusal vector as the difference-in-means between harmful and benign query representations at that layer. The key insight is that a uniform shift along this vector causes model breakdown; instead, ACTOR applies a 'just-enough' individualized shift whose magnitude is proportional to each query's projection onto the refusal vector, formalized as a Projection-Calibrated Refusal Direction Loss (cosine similarity between current and target activations). Training fine-tunes only the single target layer using this loss, iteratively re-computing the refusal vector as parameters update, and requires no response generation—only forward-pass activations—making it both compute-efficient (~4 min vs. 15 min for SFT on H100) and data-efficient (effective with as few as 10 pseudo-harmful queries).

## Results

ACTOR achieves the highest average Compliance Rate (90.74%) and Tradeoff Score (94.88) on Llama-2-7b-chat, improving over the default model's 61.53% CR by 47.47 percentage points while maintaining a 99.03% Safety Score on AdvBench. On Gemma-7b-it, ACTOR reaches 77.01% Avg CR and 84.75 Avg TS, outperforming all baselines. Training with ACTOR takes only 4 minutes on an H100 versus 15 minutes for SFT, and general model capabilities (MMLU, MT-Bench, WikiText2 perplexity) remain within 1% of the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTOR outperforms all five baselines on both average Compliance Rate and Tradeoff Score for both Llama-2-7b-chat and Gemma-7b-it. The inference-time methods Safe-Decoding and DRO are dramatically worse on compliance (24.63% and 46.32% Avg CR vs. ACTOR's 90.74% on Llama-2-7b-chat), indicating that decoding-level interventions cannot adequately address over-refusal. Self CD (82.59% Avg CR, 89.18 TS) and SCANs (80.40% Avg CR, 89.82 TS) are closer competitors but trail ACTOR by 8+ points on Avg CR; SCANs in particular suffers a severe drop on OR-Bench (40.52% vs. 76.28%), revealing brittleness under distribution shift. Surgical (86.39% Avg CR, 92.90 TS) is the strongest baseline but still lags ACTOR by ~4.4 points on Avg CR and ~2 points on TS. On safety, all methods maintain high AdvBench scores (>92%), with ACTOR at 99.03% for Llama-2-7b-chat, confirming that compliance gains do not come at the cost of safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACTOR sits at the intersection of representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023a; Arditi et al., 2024) and localized representation fine-tuning (REFT, Wu et al., 2024; LoFiT, Yin et al., 2024), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike inference-time steering methods (SCANS, Surgical) that apply a fixed refusal vector at test time, ACTOR bakes the correction into a single layer's weights via a projection-calibrated cosine loss, making the intervention query-adaptive rather than uniform. Compared to full-model SFT or other training-based over-refusal mitigations (DRO, Safety Patching), ACTOR is response-free and updates only one layer, drawing its supervision signal from internal activations rather than generated outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains—4× faster training than SFT, operation with as few as 10 pseudo-harmful queries, and robustness to shifts in the harmful-query distribution—make ACTOR a lightweight post-hoc fix that model providers could deploy without re-running full alignment pipelines. However, the method is strictly white-box (requiring activation access), depends on an existing safety-alignment mechanism to define the refusal direction, and has only been validated on 7B–13B models. Open questions include whether the single-layer, single-direction assumption holds for larger or more complex architectures, how the approach behaves in multi-turn dialogue where over-refusal and genuine safety concerns interleave, and whether jointly curating training data and fine-tuning (rather than optimizing against fixed benchmarks) closes the gap between benchmark compliance and real-world safety.
