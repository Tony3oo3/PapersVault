## Problem

Expert parallelism in MoE models requires All-to-All communication to route tokens to distributed experts, creating a sequential dependency between communication and computation that accounts for up to 50–60% of total execution time. Existing optimizations such as pipelining and hierarchical All-to-All only partially overlap these operations because the MoE module's input is inherently the current layer's output, forcing communication to wait for computation to finish. The paper asks whether the MoE architecture itself can be restructured to break this dependency and enable full communication-computation overlap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer inputs: 192×192 RGB images for vision classification (ImageNet-1K) and text sequences (up to 2K tokens) for language modeling. The key structural difference is that the gate-routed experts consume the preceding layer's intermediate representation (specifically the output of the Attention sub-layer, 'Pos-2') via a shortcut connection, while the shared expert consumes the current layer's intermediate representation as usual. Outputs are standard: ImageNet class labels for vision and next-token predictions for language, with the MoE output being the sum of the shared expert result and the gate-routed expert result (optionally weighted by a coefficient gating network).

## Method

ScMoE replaces the standard top-2 MoE with two independently executed components: a top-1 gate-routed expert that processes the preceding layer's representation through a shortcut connection, and a shared expert (dense MLP) that processes the current layer's representation. Because the gate-routed expert's input is available one layer earlier, its All-to-All dispatch and combine operations are fully decoupled from the current layer's computation, enabling them to run on a separate CUDA stream in parallel with the current layer's Attention, Shared Expert, and MLP operations. An adaptive scheduling strategy then selects the optimal position for expert computation among four candidate slots within the shared-expert stream to maximize the overlap window, achieving up to 100% communication overlap when communication time does not exceed the computation duration. Theoretical analysis confirms that the residual structure of the shortcut connection preserves gradient propagation independent of network depth, and empirical results show ScMoE matches or exceeds top-2 MoE quality while delivering 1.49× training and 1.82× inference speedups in high-communication-overhead settings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (SwinV2-MoE-S, 8×A30-PCIe), ScMoE (Pos-2) achieves 79.38% top-1 accuracy (vs. 79.33% for standard top-2 MoE) with a 1.43× training and 1.66× inference speedup; the Pos-3 variant reaches 1.49×/1.82× speedup at 79.20% accuracy. On language benchmarks (8×A800-NVLink), ScMoE attains the highest average zero-shot score of 38.69 on GPT2-MoE (vs. 37.86 for top-2) and 38.96 on LLaMA2-MoE (vs. 38.39), with 1.12×/1.17× and 1.14×/1.21× training/inference speedups respectively. In the multi-node 16×A800 scenario, ScMoE achieves complete communication overlap, yielding a 24% speed improvement over shared-expert MoE and 43% over pipelined top-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard top-2 MoE (Shazeer et al., 2017), ScMoE matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (79.38% vs. 79.33% on ImageNet-1K; 38.69 vs. 37.86 avg on GPT2-MoE; 38.96 vs. 38.39 on LLaMA2-MoE) while delivering 1.43–1.49× training and 1.66–1.82× inference speedups in the high-communication 8×A30-PCIe setting, and 1.12–1.14× training / 1.17–1.21× inference speedups in the low-communication 8×A800-NVLink setting. Against the shared-expert MoE (Rajbhandari et al., 2022), ScMoE trades a marginal 0.15% accuracy drop on ImageNet (79.38% vs. 79.53%) for a substantially larger speedup (1.43× vs. 1.24× training), and on language tasks ScMoE actually surpasses shared-expert MoE on both accuracy (38.69 vs. 38.58; 38.96 vs. 38.82) and speed. Against Tutel's pipelined top-2 MoE (Hwang et al., 2023), ScMoE's overlapping strategy provides a 42% speed improvement in the 8×A30-PCIe scenario and a 43% improvement in the 16×A800 two-node scenario, because the shortcut connection decouples All-to-All communication from the sequential data flow, enabling up to 100% overlap with computation rather than the partial overlap achievable by pipelining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparsely-gated MoE architecture (Shazeer et al., 2017), the shared-expert variant introduced by DeepSpeed-MoE (Rajbhandari et al., 2022), and expert parallelism as formalized in GShard (Lepikhin et al., 2021). It departs from prior communication-optimization work (hierarchical All-to-All, pipelining in Tutel) by taking an architectural rather than purely scheduling-based approach: a shortcut connection from the preceding Transformer layer decouples the gate-routed expert computation from the sequential data flow, enabling the two independent streams (shared expert on current-layer tokens, routed experts on preceding-layer tokens) to be scheduled in parallel. This reframes the All-to-All bottleneck as an overlap problem solvable by restructuring the MoE module itself, rather than by finer-grained token chunking or multi-level communication trees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

All-to-All communication consuming ~50% of MoE training time is a well-recognized bottleneck, and ScMoE's ability to achieve up to 100% communication-computation overlap with a minimal architectural change (one shortcut connection) offers a device-agnostic, drop-in improvement compatible with existing parallelism stacks. The finding that adjacent-layer representations have near-unit cosine similarity provides a mechanistic explanation for why the shortcut preserves model quality, and the DGMoE analysis clarifies the boundary conditions (distinct expert selection is necessary for top-2-equivalent quality). Open questions include whether the Pos-2 configuration remains optimal at much greater depth, whether the parameter-sharing interpretation (one MoE module serving two layers) can be pushed further for parameter efficiency, and how the approach interacts with emerging MoE variants like fine-grained expert routing or expert-level parallelism beyond All-to-All.
