## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for generative models rely on per-step losses that excessively modify intermediate generation trajectories, degrading overall model quality, and are often tied to specific architectures (e.g., cross-attention modules) that are absent in newer rectified flow DiT models. Moreover, prior methods lack robustness, as erased concepts can be reintroduced through adversarial prompts. The paper addresses the need for a minimally intrusive, model-agnostic, and adversarially robust concept erasure framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained rectified flow model (FLUX.1-Schnell, 12B parameters) and a small set of text prompts (as few as 20) specifying target concepts to erase, along with neutral prompts from GCC3M to preserve. It produces a modified model with a binary neuron mask applied element-wise to FFN and normalization layer weights, resulting in a model that generates images free of the target concept while maintaining CLIP alignment, FID, and SSIM scores close to the original. The mask is learned via continuous relaxation with Hard-discrete sampling and binarized post-training.

## Method

The core insight is formulating concept erasure as an optimization over only the final generation output distribution rather than per-step intermediate outputs, yielding a minimalist objective that minimizes the posterior probability of target concepts while constraining KL divergence for neutral concepts. For rectified flow models, this objective is upper-bounded by MSE terms between the modified model's full ODE trajectory output and either the original model's output (preservation) or the null-prompt output (erasure), enabling end-to-end backpropagation through all generation steps. To keep memory constant regardless of step count, the method employs step-wise gradient checkpointing that stores only step outcomes and recomputes forward passes during backpropagation. For robustness, instead of fine-tuning weights, the method learns a binary mask over neurons in FFN and normalization layers using Hard-discrete sampling, effectively ablating connectivity associated with the target concept. A prompt filtering step selects training prompts that produce consistent backgrounds with distinct foregrounds, improving the precision of which neurons are masked.

## Results

On the FLUX.1-Schnell model, the proposed minimalist concept erasure achieves the lowest detection rate (ACC↓) across all three concept categories: 43% for inappropriate objects, 10% for IP characters, and 1% for art styles, substantially outperforming all baselines. The method also best preserves model utility, achieving the highest SSIM (0.45–0.51) and competitive CLIP scores (0.29–0.31) and FID (41.5–44.4) compared to the original FLUX (FID 40.4, CLIP 0.31). Against four adversarial attacks on nudity, the method reduces attack success rates to 4–19% (e.g., 19% on Ring-A-Bell K77 vs. 91% for EAP), demonstrating strong robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023), CA (Kumari et al., 2023), SLD (Schramowski et al., 2023), EAP (Bui et al., 2024), and FlowEdit (Kulikov et al., 2024). The proposed method wins decisively on erasure effectiveness (ACC) across all three categories, reducing detection rates to 43%/10%/1% versus the best baseline (CA at 11% for IP characters, ESD at 4% for art styles). On model preservation, the method achieves the best SSIM in all categories (0.45/0.51/0.41) and matches or slightly trails EAP on CLIP and FID (e.g., FID 43.6 vs. EAP 42.7 for objects). Against adversarial attacks (Table 2), the method dominates all baselines by a wide margin: 19% ASR on Ring-A-Bell K77 vs. 91% for EAP, and 0.4% on MMA-Diffusion vs. 7.6–8.5% for all baselines. EAP is the closest competitor on preservation metrics but fails catastrophically on robustness (54% ASR on normal prompts vs. 4% for the proposed method). CA and SLD are incompatible with the DiT architecture of FLUX and show the worst preservation (FID 71.8–96.5), while FlowEdit provides no preservation metrics and moderate erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant per-step concept erasure paradigm (ESD, CA, SLD, EAP) that adjusts intermediate noise predictions at every generation step, instead formulating erasure solely over the distributional distance of final outputs. It builds on rectified flow theory to derive a tractable end-to-end loss, borrows the neuron-masking idea from prior pruning work (Yang et al. 2024a, Fang et al. 2024) as a robustness mechanism, and establishes a formal equivalence between the erasure objective and RLHF-style alignment with a moving reward. The method is positioned as model-agnostic, extending naturally from rectified flows to diffusion models, and is validated on the 12B-parameter FLUX DiT architecture where cross-attention-based methods are inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robust concept erasure in large-scale generative models is a critical AI safety problem, and the paper's demonstration that connectivity ablation dramatically outperforms weight fine-tuning against adversarial prompts (0.4% vs. ~8% ASR on MMA-Diffusion) addresses a widely acknowledged vulnerability. The minimalist formulation—supervising only the final output rather than every intermediate step—offers a cleaner theoretical foundation and lower variance in Monte-Carlo estimation. Key limitations include single-GPU training, no post-masking fine-tuning, and evaluation on only one model architecture (FLUX.1-Schnell), leaving open whether the robustness advantage generalizes to other DiT or UNet-based models at scale.
