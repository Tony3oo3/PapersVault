## Problem

Most vision-based trackers treat tracking as a discrete image-matching problem, relying solely on visual discriminability and ignoring the temporal coherence inherent in video. While recent SOTA trackers incorporate temporal mechanisms, they require heavy sequential training that substantially increases computational and memory demands, limiting their ability to perceive long trajectories. The paper asks whether heavy sequential training is truly necessary for capturing temporal relations in tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a template image, a search image cropped from the current frame, and a historical trajectory of T=30 predicted bounding boxes (normalized to the current search region's coordinate system). Each 2D corner coordinate is encoded via Gaussian-based spatial positional encoding into a C-dimensional motion token matching the baseline visual feature dimension (768 for OSTrack/ARTrack-B, 256 for SeqTrack-B). The output is a single predicted bounding box for the current frame, produced by the baseline tracker's head operating on the fused visual-motion embedding.

## Method

The plug-and-play Motion Prompt Tracking (MPT) module consists of three components: (1) a motion encoder that maps T-frame trajectories into 2T motion tokens plus three learnable representative tokens (top-left, bottom-right, and weight confidence), using Gaussian spatial PE, learnable point embeddings, and a non-linear temporal PE initialized via the Nyquist frequency (α=7.23) to emphasize recent frames; (2) a lightweight 2-layer Transformer fusion decoder with motion self-attention, visual-to-motion cross-attention, and motion-Rep-to-visual cross-attention with dense spatial maps; and (3) an adaptive weight mechanism that dynamically blends the fused feature with the original visual feature, supervised by the IoU between motion-predicted and ground-truth boxes. Training employs prompt learning with all baseline parameters frozen, using only frame-level fine-tuning (5 hours on 2×A100, 6G memory) with DiMP-18-generated trajectories augmented via CutMix and sparse sampling, avoiding the sequential training and multi-GPU memory requirements of prior temporal trackers.

## Results

On VOT2018, integrating MPT into OSTrack-B256 boosts EAO from 0.287 to 0.406 (+10.2% average across all five baselines) and reduces the robustness metric R from 1.204 to 0.473 (+57.3% improvement). On VOT2020 and VOT2022, MPT yields average EAO gains of +1.5% and +2.4% respectively. On LaSOT_EXT, SeqTrack-L384+MPT reaches 50.4 AUC, competitive with sequential-training methods like ODTrack-B384 (52.0) while requiring only 5 hours of training on 6G GPU memory versus 26+53 hours on 50G for ARTrack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPT consistently outperforms all frame-level baselines (MixFormer, ROMTrack, OSTrack, SeqTrack) on VOT2018 by large margins: ARTrack-B256+MPT achieves 0.469 EAO vs. ARTrack-B256's 0.399, and SeqTrack-L384+MPT reaches 0.461 vs. SeqTrack-L384's 0.319. On VOT2020/2022, the gains are more modest (+1.5%/+2.4% EAO) but MPT-augmented trackers remain competitive with sequential-training SOTA methods like ODTrack and ARTrackV2 on LaSOT_EXT (50.4–50.9 AUC vs. 52.0–53.9 for ODTrack/ARTrackV2). On LaSOT and TrackingNet, improvements are smaller (+0.4–0.6% AUC), reflecting that MPT's primary benefit is robustness in hard scenarios rather than peak accuracy. The method is substantially more training-efficient than sequential-training baselines: 5 hours and 6G memory for OSTrack+MPT versus 26+53 hours and 50G for ARTrack, while adding only 13M parameters and ~8 FPS overhead. MPT also complements existing temporal trackers: ARTrack-B256+MPT improves over ARTrack-B256 alone on all three VOT benchmarks, confirming that motion prompts and autoregressive temporal modeling are complementary rather than conflicting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant vision-based tracking paradigm that treats tracking as a discrete image-matching problem, and against recent sequential-training approaches (SLT, ARTrack, ODTrack) that incorporate temporal information through heavy autoregressive or multi-template mechanisms. It draws on prompt learning from NLP and vision to propose a plug-and-play motion prompt module that encodes historical bounding-box trajectories via Gaussian spatial encoding, learnable point embeddings, and a non-linear temporal positional encoding (initialized per Nyquist frequency). The module is integrated into three architecturally distinct trackers (OSTrack, SeqTrack, ARTrack) with all baseline parameters frozen, departing from the sequential-training requirement that prior temporal trackers impose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that temporal tracking benefits can be obtained with frame-level prompt learning (5 hours, 6 GB GPU) rather than sequential training (26+53 hours, 50 GB), making temporal cues accessible to practitioners with limited compute. The plug-and-play design means any existing vision-based tracker can be upgraded without retraining its backbone. However, the paper acknowledges that tracking accuracy remains slightly below sequential-training SOTA, and that extremely poor-quality trajectories (IoU < 0.2) can mislead the motion module—a limitation that the adaptive weight mechanism only partially mitigates. An open question is whether the motion prompt paradigm can be extended to capture more complex dynamic relations (e.g., non-rigid motion, multi-object interactions) without reverting to heavier temporal processing.
