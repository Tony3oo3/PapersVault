## Problem

Despite the ubiquity of self-attention in modern architectures, its theoretical underpinnings remain fragmented: existing analyses target isolated problems without a unifying lens, largely ignore out-of-distribution (length) generalization, and impose restrictive assumptions on model parameters. This paper asks whether a single, domain-agnostic framework can simultaneously explain how self-attention represents, learns, and generalizes pairwise interactions across diverse settings (multi-agent RL, genomics, vision, language).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252406/cropped_page20_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of L entity embeddings stacked as rows of X ∈ R^{L×d}, where each row is the embedding of a discrete entity (agent position, allele, pixel patch, or word) drawn from a finite domain S. It produces a per-entity output matrix Y ∈ R^{L×d₂} whose i-th row aggregates the pairwise interaction effect of all other entities on entity i. A key structural property is length-agnosticism: the same parameters C and W^V must work for any sequence length L, which is precisely what the out-of-distribution generalization theorem guarantees.

## Method

The paper proves that a single-layer linear self-attention (omitting the softmax, so SA^lin(X) = (XCX^T)XW^V) with embedding dimension d = |S| exactly represents any pairwise interaction function y_i = Σ_j f(X(i),X(j)) w_{X(j)}, and that this is parameter-efficient (Θ(|S|²) vs. Ω(L²|S|²) for a fully connected net). Under a mild 'training data versatility' condition (full-rank count matrices per domain element), gradient flow on MSE converges to zero training error, and the resulting solution generalizes to both unseen in-distribution examples and unseen sequence lengths. Building on this theory, the authors introduce two new modules: HyperFeatureAttention, which computes a Hadamard product of multiple attention matrices (one per feature dimension) to capture couplings of feature-level interactions with only Θ(M) parameters instead of Θ(exp(M)), and HyperAttention, which replaces the rank-d attention score matrix with a d×d×d tensor to model three-way (or n-way) entity interactions. Empirical validation on a colliding-agents grid environment confirms the theoretical parameter recovery, and small-scale OpenWebText benchmarks show that HFA and HA reduce perplexity relative to standard self-attention under matched GPT-3-small hyperparameters.

## Results

On the 1-layer, 1-head, 256-token OpenWebText benchmark (GPT-3-small hyperparameters), HyperAttention (order 3) achieves a validation perplexity of 51.26, substantially outperforming standard Self-Attention (62.28) and HyperFeatureAttention (60.22). The no-sharing variant of HyperAttention further reduces perplexity to 48.50. On the 3-layer, 1024-token benchmark, the hybrid HFAv2 (2 SA heads + 2 order-3 HFA heads) attains the best perplexity of 27.75, compared to 28.70 for standard SA and 27.97 for HFA alone.

| HyperFeatureAttention | 4 | 60.22 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Self-Attention (Vaswani et al., 2023), evaluated under identical GPT-3-small hyperparameters (d_model=768, vocab 50257, same optimizer and LR schedule). In the 1-layer, 256-token setting (Table 1), HyperAttention (order 3) reduces perplexity from 62.28 to 51.26 (≈18% relative improvement), and the no-sharing variant further drops it to 48.50 (≈22% improvement). HyperFeatureAttention (order 4) offers a more modest gain, reaching 60.22. In the 3-layer, 1024-token setting (Table 2), the improvements are smaller but consistent: HFA (order 3) achieves 27.97 vs. SA's 28.70, and the hybrid HFAv2 reaches 27.75. All proposed modules match or exceed SA in parameter count and computational cost (HFA retains Θ(L²) complexity; HA scales as Θ(L³) without low-rank tricks), confirming that the perplexity gains stem from richer interaction modeling rather than additional capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational self-attention mechanism of Vaswani et al. and the linear attention formulation of Katharopoulos et al., using the latter as a tractable analytical proxy for softmax attention. It departs from prior Transformer theory (which the authors catalog as targeting isolated problems, ignoring OOD generalization, and imposing restrictive parameter assumptions) by proposing a unified 'interacting entities' lens that simultaneously covers multi-agent RL, genotype-phenotype mapping, vision, and time series. The two novel modules—HyperFeatureAttention (Hadamard-coupled per-feature attention) and HyperAttention (n-way tensor attention)—extend the representational scope beyond pairwise interactions, building on but generalizing the Kronecker-attention ideas of Alman & Song and the circuit-theoretic observations of Elhage et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable, domain-agnostic characterization of what a single self-attention layer represents (pairwise interaction functions), how it converges under gradient flow, and why it generalizes to unseen sequence lengths—filling gaps the authors identify in prior theoretical work. The introduction of HFA and HA opens a concrete architectural direction for capturing feature-coupled and higher-order dependencies, with small-scale OpenWebText benchmarks showing consistent perplexity reductions over standard attention. Key limitations temper the impact: all theory is for linear (non-softmax) attention with d=|S| and noise-free data, and the empirical validation of the new modules is limited to GPT-3-small-scale runs on a single dataset. Extending the framework to softmax attention, deeper stacks, and noisy/real-world data remains open, as does scaling HFA/HA validation to larger models and diverse tasks.
