## Problem

Real-world traffic signal control (TSC) systems are vulnerable to adversarial sensor attacks (Gaussian noise, U-rand, MAD, MinQ) and physical sensor damage causing missing data, yet existing defenses address only one attack type and rely on offline-trained static models that cannot adapt to real-time traffic dynamics. There is no unified, online, plug-and-play framework that simultaneously recovers from multiple noise attacks and missing data without altering the underlying TSC algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251429/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the noisy or partially masked traffic state vector (Efficient Pressure and Running Vehicle features per intersection), the full TSC state trajectory τ_{t-1}^s, the previous TSC action a_{t-1}, and a binary sensor mask matrix m indicating which directional sensors are damaged. It outputs a recovered state ŝ_t in the same tabular format as the original observation, which is then passed unchanged to any existing TSC algorithm (CoLight, MaxPressure, etc.) for signal phase selection. The diffusion model operates on 12- or 24-dimensional state vectors with 4-dimensional actions, using 100 diffusion timesteps for recovery.

## Method

RobustLight introduces a Dynamic State Infilling (DSI) agent that trains an improved conditional diffusion model online via actor-critic RL, using the TSC agent's replay buffer for continuous adaptation. The actor is a U-Net that performs the diffusion reverse process as a policy, guided by a custom beta schedule (β_i = e^{-(b/(i+a+c))}) tuned for small-to-medium noise scales, and is optimized with a non-Markov loss that balances current and future timestep errors. At inference, two algorithms leverage the trained diffusion model: Denoise runs the full reverse process to remove adversarial noise, while Repaint (adapted from image inpainting) alternates between sampling known sensor dimensions from the forward process and denoising unknown dimensions via the conditional model, iteratively reconstructing missing state entries. The entire framework is plug-and-play: it sits between the sensors and the TSC agent, recovering data in real time without modifying the TSC algorithm's architecture or training.

## Results

RobustLight improves Average Travel Time (ATT, lower is better) by up to 50.43% across all tested TSC algorithms under adversarial noise and sensor damage. On JiNan₁ with Gaussian noise (scale 3.5), Advanced-MpLight's ATT drops from 356.86 to 302.16 (−15.3%), and on HangZhou₁ the same attack reduces Advanced-CoLight's ATT from 480.38 to 327.98 (−31.7%). Against the native Diffusion Linear-Beta baseline, RobustLight achieves 294.57 vs. 324.04 ATT on JiNan₁ (Gaussian 3.5), and against LSTM it achieves 294.57 vs. 892.0, a 67% reduction. Under 25% sensor damage on JiNan₁, Advanced-CoLight's ATT improves from 732.37 to 680.37 with the Repaint algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional FixedTime baseline (Webster, 1958), RobustLight-enhanced RL algorithms consistently achieve lower ATT (e.g., 294.57 vs. 428.11 on JiNan₁), confirming that the diffusion-based recovery preserves the advantage of adaptive control even under attack. Compared to the native Diffusion Linear-Beta model (Ho et al., 2020), RobustLight's improved beta schedule and conditional U-Net yield lower ATT in every tested scenario (e.g., 294.57 vs. 324.04 on JiNan₁ Gaussian 3.5; 327.98 vs. 480.53 on HangZhou₁ Gaussian 3.5). The LSTM baseline (Sun et al., 2021) performs dramatically worse, with ATT values 3–4× higher (892.0 vs. 294.57 on JiNan₁), indicating that sequence models cannot capture the multi-modal state distribution needed for recovery. Against DiffLight (Chen et al., 2024), RobustLight wins on U-rand noise (304.34 vs. 310.92 on JiNan₁; 297.34 vs. 361.31 on HangZhou₁) and sensor damage (304.13 vs. 366.05 on JiNan₁; 306.56 vs. 372.53 on HangZhou₁), while DiffLight edges ahead on Gaussian noise for JiNan₁ (289.45 vs. 294.57) and MinQ for JiNan₁ (321.98 vs. 323.25). Among the TSC algorithms themselves, Advanced-CoLight benefits most from RobustLight under strong attacks (e.g., 480.38→327.98 under Gaussian 3.5 on HangZhou₁), while MaxPressure shows the smallest relative gain, suggesting that simpler policies are less sensitive to state corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobustLight sits at the intersection of three research threads: denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020) for generative data recovery, the Repaint inpainting paradigm (Lugmayr et al., 2022) adapted from image completion to tabular state imputation, and diffusion-based RL policies (Yang et al., 2023) for actor-critic control. Unlike prior robust RL work (Zhang et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2022) that trains policies to be perturbation-resistant, or TSC-specific defenses (Lin et al., 2017; Mei et al., 2023) that use MLPs or interpolation for a single failure mode, RobustLight positions the diffusion model as a plug-and-play data-recovery layer that sits *between* the sensor and any existing TSC algorithm, handling both adversarial noise and missing data simultaneously without modifying the downstream controller. The improved beta schedule and non-Markov loss represent a departure from standard DDPM training, tailoring the diffusion process to the small-to-medium noise regime typical of sensor attacks rather than the full-noise-to-data generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a unified, model-agnostic defense for deployed TSC systems that face heterogeneous sensor failures (Gaussian noise, MAD, MinQ, physical damage) without requiring retraining of the traffic controller itself. However, the real-time viability is questionable: MAD and MinQ attacks require 240 diffusion steps with inference times of 3.8–5.3 seconds per recovery, far exceeding the sub-second decision cycles of TSC, and the authors only gesture toward DDIM acceleration as a fix. The framework also assumes reliable attack detection (validated at 60–80% detection rates via TP-FDS), introducing a dependency on a separate anomaly-detection pipeline whose failure modes are not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the non-Markov loss and custom beta schedule generalize beyond the specific noise scales tested, and whether the approach scales to the 192-intersection New York network without the proposed subregion decomposition.
