## Problem

DDIM Inversion suffers from a fundamental discrepancy between the noise estimated during inversion and the noise expected during sampling, leading to poor reconstruction quality. Existing remedies like ReNoise and Fixed-Point Iteration address this through iterative optimization at each time step, which is computationally expensive (3× slower) and can fail catastrophically (e.g., producing black images) when the underlying diffusion model lacks high precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.4, taking an RGB image (encoded to a 4×64×64 latent tensor) and a text prompt as input, and producing a noise tensor z_T as output after T=50 inversion steps. The output noise tensor, when fed back through the standard DDIM denoising process, reconstructs the original image. EasyInv is designed as a plug-and-play module (Algorithm 1) that can be inserted into any existing inversion algorithm at selected time steps without modifying the base inversion logic.

## Method

EasyInv's core insight is that the initial latent state z_0* carries the most information about the original image, and errors accumulate as noise terms are introduced at each inversion step. Rather than iteratively refining the noise ε_t* at each step (as fixed-point iteration methods do), EasyInv periodically blends the current latent with the previous one at strategically chosen steps (0.05T < t̄ < 0.25T) using the rule z_t̄* = η·z_t̄* + (1−η)·z_{t̄−1}*, with η=0.5. This is formally derived as a simplified Kalman filter where the Kalman gain is fixed empirically, treating the previous latent as a 'measurement' and the current DDIM-inverted latent as a 'prediction.' The rescaling preserves the noise distribution pattern of standard DDIM inversion while reducing its magnitude, effectively increasing the weight of the initial latent state throughout the process. Because no iterative optimization is needed, EasyInv maintains the same 5-second runtime as vanilla DDIM Inversion while achieving better SSIM (0.646 vs 0.621) and competitive LPIPS scores.

## Results

On COCO 2017 (2,298 images) with SD V1.4, EasyInv achieves LPIPS 0.321, SSIM 0.646, and PSNR 30.189 while completing inversion in 5 seconds — matching the speed of vanilla DDIM Inversion. This is a ~3× speedup over ReNoise (16s) and Fixed-Point Iteration (14s) while achieving the best SSIM among all methods. In downstream editing tasks (Table 3, PNPinversion benchmark), EasyInv+DirectInv improves over DirectInv alone in 5 out of 7 metrics across P2P, MasaCtrl, P2P-Zero, and PnP editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyInv outperforms vanilla DDIM Inversion (Couairon et al., 2023) on all three quality metrics (LPIPS 0.321 vs 0.328, SSIM 0.646 vs 0.621, PSNR 30.189 vs 29.717) at the same 5-second runtime, confirming that the Kalman-filter-inspired blending adds quality without extra cost. Against ReNoise (Garibi et al., 2024), EasyInv wins on SSIM (0.646 vs 0.641) and is 3.2× faster (5s vs 16s), but ReNoise retains a slight edge on LPIPS (0.316 vs 0.321) and PSNR (31.025 vs 30.189). EasyInv decisively beats Fixed-Point Iteration (Pan et al., 2023) on every metric (LPIPS 0.321 vs 0.373, SSIM 0.646 vs 0.563, PSNR 30.189 vs 29.107) while being 2.8× faster. In the downstream editing benchmark (Table 3), EasyInv+DirectInv improves over DirectInv (Ju et al., 2024) on structure distance, LPIPS, MSE, and SSIM for P2P, MasaCtrl, P2P-Zero, and PnP tasks, and also outperforms Null-Text Inversion, Negative-Prompt Inversion, and StyleDiffusion on most background-preservation and CLIP-similarity metrics when paired with the same editing backends.

## Relation to Prior Work

EasyInv builds directly on the DDIM Inversion framework (Couairon et al., 2023), which established the baseline of reversing the denoising process to recover an initial noise tensor. It departs from the dominant line of iterative refinement work—ReNoise (Garibi et al., 2024) and Fixed-Point Iteration (Pan et al., 2023)—which repeatedly add and denoise noise at each step to converge on a more accurate ε_t*. Instead of optimizing the noise estimate, EasyInv reframes the problem through a (simplified) Kalman filter lens, proposing a single-pass aggregation of the current and previous latent states to reinforce the initial latent's influence without any iterative loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: EasyInv matches or exceeds ReNoise on SSIM and LPIPS while running at the same speed as vanilla DDIM Inversion (~3× faster than iterative methods), and it degrades more gracefully on less precise models like SD-V1-4. The acknowledged 'overdenoising' limitation (overly smooth outputs) and the empirical, non-principled choice of η and the step range 0.05T < t̄ < 0.25T leave room for a more theoretically grounded formulation of the Kalman gain. An open question is whether the Kalman filter analogy can be made rigorous enough to auto-tune η per timestep rather than relying on a fixed constant.
