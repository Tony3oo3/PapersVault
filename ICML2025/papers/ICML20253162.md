## Problem

Deep learning-based blind image quality assessment (BIQA) for authentically distorted images requires large labeled datasets, but annotation is expensive and most authentic distortion databases only provide scalar MOS labels without score distributions. Existing semi-supervised BIQA methods (SSLIQA, SS-IQA) rely on multiple network branches, additional training datasets, or unavailable score distributions, limiting their practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes authentically distorted RGB images (resized to 256×256, randomly cropped to 224×224 patches) as input and produces a scalar MOS quality score in [1,100]. Internally, the network outputs a 100-dimensional probability vector over discrete score levels {1,...,100}, which is converted to a scalar via dot product with the level vector [1,2,...,100]. Training uses a mix of labeled images (with MOS) and unlabeled images from the same or different authentic distortion datasets.

## Method

CPL-IQA is a single-branch semi-supervised framework (ResNet101 + FC layer mapping 2048→256 dims) trained in two stages. A preprocessing step converts scalar MOS labels into 100-dim vector labels via entropy minimization, enabling label propagation (which natively handles vector/multi-label data) in a regression setting. Stage 1 trains the network supervised with EMD loss on these vector labels. Stage 2 alternates between (a) Label Optimizing—constructing a kNN graph (k=10) in the 256-dim feature space and propagating labels via the closed-form solution P*=(1−γ)(I−γG̃)⁻¹V—and (b) Model Training with L1 loss weighted by per-sample confidence scores derived from the entropy of each pseudo-label. This confidence weighting down-weights unreliable pseudo-labels, and the entire pipeline requires no auxiliary branches, no extra datasets, and no score distribution information.

## Results

On KonIQ-10K (1:3:1 split, 20% labeled / 60% unlabeled / 20% test), CPL-IQA achieves PLCC 0.873 and SRCC 0.845, outperforming the best supervised method MUSIQ (PLCC 0.864, SRCC 0.838) and the best prior semi-supervised method SSLIQA (PLCC 0.867, SRCC 0.841). In cross-data experiments, CPL-IQA attains PLCC 0.777 on LIVE-C and PLCC 0.772 on NNID, surpassing all competing methods by at least 0.026 PLCC on LIVE-C. The method uses only a single ResNet101 branch with 10 total training epochs, requiring no auxiliary datasets or multi-branch architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPL-IQA outperforms all 25 baselines on the primary KonIQ-10K benchmark. Among semi-supervised competitors, it beats SSLIQA by +0.006 PLCC / +0.004 SRCC, SS-IQA by +0.019 / +0.016, and Semi-IQA by +0.009 / +0.006, while using a single network branch versus the two-branch architectures of SSLIQA and SS-IQA. Against the strongest supervised methods, CPL-IQA edges out MUSIQ (+0.009 PLCC, +0.007 SRCC) and GraphIQA (+0.011 PLCC, tied SRCC at 0.845), despite training on only 20% labeled data. The gap over unsupervised methods is substantial: Re-IQA is surpassed by +0.091 PLCC and +0.041 SRCC, and CONTRIQUE by +0.105 / +0.070. In cross-data evaluation (Table 2), CPL-IQA leads on both LIVE-C (PLCC 0.777 vs. Causal-IQA's 0.742) and NNID (PLCC 0.772 vs. SSLIQA's 0.771), with the LIVE-C margin exceeding 10% relative improvement over the second-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical label propagation framework (Zhou et al., 2003) from classification to the regression setting of blind image quality assessment, a non-trivial extension since standard LP relies on cosine-similarity manifold structures that distort regression label distributions. It draws on NIMA's observation that training with MOS score distributions outperforms scalar MOS labels, and uses entropy minimization to convert scalar MOS into vector labels that satisfy LP's multi-label requirement. Unlike prior semi-supervised BIQA methods (SSLIQA, SS-IQA) that require dual-branch architectures and auxiliary datasets, CPL-IQA operates on a single ResNet101 backbone with no extra inputs, positioning itself as a more standardized and lower-cost semi-supervised paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the annotation burden for authentic-distortion IQA, where labeling is expensive and large databases do not exist. The confidence-weighted pseudo-label mechanism provides a principled way to handle noisy propagated labels, and the cross-dataset generalization (LIVE-C PLCC 0.777, >10% above runner-up) suggests the learned features transfer well. However, improvements over SSLIQA on the primary KonIQ-10K benchmark are modest (0.873 vs 0.867 PLCC), and the method's dependence on a well-conditioned kNN graph in 256-dim feature space may limit scalability to very large unlabeled pools. Open questions include whether the entropy-minimization label conversion is truly optimal versus other distributional assumptions, and whether the approach extends to other regression tasks beyond IQA.
