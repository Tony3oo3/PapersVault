## Problem

Rule-based planners are efficient and interpretable but fail to generalize to long-tail driving scenarios, while learning-based planners generalize well but suffer from high computational cost and lack interpretability. Existing hybrid approaches (e.g., fast-slow paradigms) treat the learning-based planner as mere guidance rather than a full alternative, failing to exploit the complementary strengths of both planner types in a scenario-aware manner.

## Input / Output

The system consumes estimated perception data (vehicles, pedestrians, lanes, traffic signs), HD map information, a start-end point pair, and the ego vehicle's historical trajectory. The diffusion proposal generator is deliberately non-conditional (no scene-feature conditioning), operating only on the noised future trajectory and historical trajectory to sample multimodal proposals. Output trajectories are represented as (x, y, θ) sequences over T timesteps, refined through LQR-based simulation to satisfy bicycle-model dynamics before scoring.

## Method

SAH-Drive integrates PDM-Closed (rule-based, high-frequency) with a non-conditional diffusion proposal generator (learning-based, low-frequency) under a scenario-aware hybrid paradigm. A dual-timescale decision neuron governs planner switching: short-term plasticity is implemented via a score-based rule (comparing planner scores against thresholds) and a scenario-based rule (detecting 'trapped' states or sustained excellent PDM performance), while long-term plasticity uses a score-adapted STDP mechanism where planner scores replace spike times and the negative reciprocal of score difference replaces temporal offset. A proposal number regulator dynamically halves or doubles the diffusion sample count based on the best diffusion trajectory score relative to a threshold, and a trajectory fusion module blends the top diffusion and PDM proposals via exponential score weighting to mitigate aggressive diffusion outputs. The diffusion model is trained only on nuPlan Mini with a self-attention encoder-decoder, requiring far less data than fully learning-based planners.

## Results

On the interPlan long-tail benchmark, SAH-Drive achieves a closed-loop score of 64, outperforming the next-best method Diffusion-ES (57) by 7 points and the SOTA rule-based planner PDM-Closed (42) by 22 points. On Test14-Hard (long-tail), SAH-Drive scores 83 (reactive) / 78 (non-reactive), surpassing Diffusion-ES (77/77) and PDM-Closed (75/65). On regular-scenario splits (Val14, Test14-Random), SAH-Drive delivers near-SOTA performance (90/89 and 87/86) while PDM-Closed remains slightly ahead (92/93 and 91/90). The model requires only 284k parameters (0.0355% of STR2's 800M) and is trained solely on the 7.96 GB nuPlan Mini dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAH-Drive decisively outperforms all learning-based baselines on long-tail scenarios: it beats Diffusion-ES (57) by 7 points on interPlan and by 6/1 points on Test14-Hard (R/NR), and surpasses PlanTF (33), Pluto (48), and Diffusion Planner (24) by 16–40 points on interPlan. Against the SOTA rule-based planner PDM-Closed, SAH-Drive trades a small margin on regular scenarios (90 vs 92 on Val14-R, 87 vs 91 on Test14-Random-R) for a large gain on long-tail scenarios (64 vs 42 on interPlan, 83 vs 75 on Test14-Hard-R). Compared to the hybrid HybridLLMPlanner (53 on interPlan), SAH-Drive improves by 11 points while using a far smaller model (284k vs LLM-scale parameters). The ablation confirms the hybrid design is essential: using only the rule-based planner drops interPlan to 40 (−37.5%), and using only the learning-based planner drops it to 62 (−3.1%), with the rule-based variant failing catastrophically on overtaking (9 vs 78) and accident zones (0 vs 44). The diffusion model trained on just nuPlan Mini (7.96 GB) outperforms STR2 trained on the full nuPlan dataset, validating that the rule-based planner internalizes most regular-scenario knowledge, freeing the learning-based component to focus on long-tail generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid planner line initiated by DriveVLM-Dual and DualAD, which combined rule-based and learning-based planners in a fast-slow hierarchy where the learning-based component merely guides the rule-based one. SAH-Drive departs from this by proposing a scenario-aware paradigm where both planners operate as full trajectory generators and a dual-timescale decision neuron (score-based rule, scenario-based rule, and an STDP-inspired neuron adapted from Markram et al.) dynamically selects between them. The learning-based component is a non-conditional diffusion model derived from Diffusion-ES (removing evolutionary search) and the rule-based component is PDM-Closed, with the denoising diffusion framework of Ho et al. providing the generative backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 284k-parameter diffusion model trained only on nuPlan Mini can, when paired with PDM-Closed under the SAH paradigm, outperform an 800M-parameter model (STR2) on long-tail scenarios, suggesting that hybrid architectures can dramatically reduce data and compute requirements for generalization. A notable open question is raised by the finding that increasing training data (nuPlan Mini → +Singapore → +Boston) actually degrades interPlan performance, hinting at a capacity-data mismatch that the authors do not fully resolve. The stated limitation—that diffusion-sampled trajectories do not strictly obey physical dynamics and require LQR simulation for feasibility—points toward future work on generative models that natively produce physically-constrained trajectories.
