## Problem

Standard autoregressive time-series models require observations at a fixed frequency, forcing costly imputation or resampling for irregularly sampled data, while neural ODE/SDE approaches that handle irregularity are difficult to scale to large spatio-temporal datasets. No prior work had formulated time-series forecasting as direct supervised learning of a continuous time-shift operator, which would naturally accommodate irregular sampling and enable super-resolution forecasting without the adjoint sensitivity machinery of neural ODEs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250478/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the system's history over a configurable look-back window [t_p, t] as a function (not a fixed-length sequence), so observations can be irregularly spaced in time and space without any imputation step. It produces future values over a prediction window (t, t_f] at an arbitrary output resolution, enabling super-resolution forecasting where the output grid in both space and time can be finer than the input grid. For spatio-temporal problems, the input is a sequence of spatial field snapshots (e.g., 32×32 or 72×72 grids) and the output is a sequence of predicted fields at potentially higher resolution.

## Method

The paper introduces Khatri-Rao Neural Operators (KRNOs), a neural operator architecture whose integral transform layers use non-stationary kernels with a component-wise product structure across the time and spatial dimensions. When quadrature nodes lie on a product grid, this structure causes the full kernel matrix to inherit a Khatri-Rao (block-wise Kronecker) product form, reducing the matrix-vector product cost from O(N²) to O(N^{1+1/d}) with O(N^{2/d}+N) memory, while preserving exact kernel evaluations and non-stationarity. Each one-dimensional component kernel is parametrized by a small MLP (3 hidden layers) with a cosine input feature transform, and the full KRNO stacks 3 such integral layers (20 channels each) with MLP lifting/projection layers. This yields a model with only ~146K parameters on the shallow water problem (vs. 2.5M for FNO-3D) while achieving superior L² error, and it avoids the adjoint sensitivity computations required by neural ODE/SDE training.

## Results

On the shallow water spatio-temporal benchmark, KRNO achieves L² relative errors of 0.00145 (ρ), 0.01497 (u), and 0.01459 (v) using only 146K parameters — 6% of FNO-3D's 2.46M and 0.15% of LOCA's 94.5M — while outperforming both. On irregularly sampled time-series, KRNO achieves the best MSE on MIMIC (1.57×10⁻²) and USHCN (4.95×10⁻¹), and the best MAE on USHCN (3.06×10⁻¹). On MuJoCo, KRNO achieves 0.007±0.002 test MSE on regular data and maintains strong performance under 30–70% sample dropout, outperforming all Neural SDE and NCDE baselines. Across 39 test cases, KRNO achieves top-1 performance on 17 and top-3 on 30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KRNO outperforms FNO-3D and LOCA on the shallow water problem across all three field variables, using only 6% of FNO-3D's parameters and 0.15% of LOCA's (e.g., 0.01497 vs. 0.02606 L² error for u). On irregular time-series, KRNO beats T-PatchGNN (the strongest baseline) on MIMIC MSE (1.57 vs. 1.69) and USHCN MSE/MAE (4.95/3.06 vs. 5.00/3.08), but loses to T-PatchGNN on Human Activity MSE (2.85 vs. 2.66) and MAE (3.46 vs. 3.15). On MuJoCo, KRNO achieves 0.007 MSE on regular data, a 46% improvement over the best Neural SDE variant (Neural LNSDE at 0.012) and a 68% improvement over LEAP (0.022), while remaining robust under 70% sample dropout (0.0115 vs. 0.014 for Neural LNSDE). On M4, KRNO is best on Weekly (sMAPE 6.934 vs. KNF's 7.254) and competitive on Daily (3.086 vs. KNF's 2.990), but trails on Quarterly (10.503 vs. Nbeats-I+G's 9.212). On Crypto and Player Trajectory, KRNO is consistently second-best after KNF (e.g., 6.95 vs. 6.91 weighted RMSE on Crypto; 1.25 vs. 1.16 RMSE on Player Trajectory), outperforming FedFormer, LEM, VARIMA, and MLP baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural operator framework (FNO, GNO, MGNO) by introducing a new architecture—KRNO—that exploits the Khatri-Rao product structure originally developed for scalable GP regression (Saatci, 2011; Wilson et al., 2014) to achieve near-linear scaling with exact, non-stationary kernel evaluations, a combination no prior neural operator achieves. Conceptually, it departs from both discrete-lag autoregressive models and single-state flow-map learning by formulating forecasting as supervised learning of a continuous time-shift operator, drawing on shift-operator theory from functional analysis (Marchenko, 2006) and recent Koopman-operator connections (Zhen et al., 2022). Unlike neural ODE/SDE approaches for irregular data, the operator-theoretic formulation avoids adjoint sensitivity computations while retaining continuous-time flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled path to handling irregularly sampled observations, super-resolution forecasting in both space and time, and significant parameter efficiency (6% of FNO-3D parameters on shallow water) without sacrificing accuracy, which addresses practical pain points in deployment. Open questions include the lack of a continuity proof for the spatio-temporal operator on PDEs (deferred to future work), the limited theoretical analysis of the time-shift operator's spectral properties and its precise relationship to the Koopman operator, and whether the Khatri-Rao product assumption on the kernel is restrictive for highly anisotropic or non-separable dynamics. The forced-system extension (Appendix A) is outlined but not empirically validated, leaving a clear path for future work on control and exogenous-input settings.
