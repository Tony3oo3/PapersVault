## Problem

Visual autoregressive (VAR) models rely on vector quantization to cast continuous visual data into discrete tokens, introducing information loss that upper-bounds generation quality and limits the model's ability to capture fine-grained continuous variations. Without a finite vocabulary, explicit likelihood estimation over continuous spaces is intractable, making standard cross-entropy training infeasible. Prior continuous AR approaches are either constrained to predefined distribution families (GIVT's Gaussian mixtures) or require multiple denoising iterations per token (diffusion loss), limiting expressiveness or inference speed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label encoded as 64 class tokens prepended to the decoder sequence and generates a 16×16 grid of continuous-valued tokens (from a KL-16 tokenizer with stride 16), which are decoded into 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. Generation follows a masked autoregressive schedule: tokens are revealed in random order over 64 steps, progressively reducing the masking ratio from 1.0 to 0 via a cosine schedule. Each token is predicted in a single forward pass through the MLP generator, unlike diffusion-based per-token prediction that requires multiple denoising iterations.

## Method

The paper introduces a Continuous VAR framework grounded in strictly proper scoring rules, where the training objective is the negative of a chosen score function evaluated at each autoregressive step. They instantiate this with the energy score (α=1), which is likelihood-free and only requires the ability to sample from the model distribution, enabling an expressive implicit generative architecture. The model is a masked autoregressive Transformer (decoder-only, ViT-style) where the standard softmax output layer is replaced by a small MLP generator that takes a 64-dimensional uniform random noise vector as input and uses adaptive layer normalization (shift, scale, gate) to inject noise into residual blocks with SiLU-activated FFNs. The energy loss |x₁−y|ᵅ + |x₂−y|ᵅ − |x₁−x₂|ᵅ incentivizes proximity to the target while preserving diversity between independent samples. Key training techniques include a reduced learning rate (×0.25) for the MLP generator, temperature scheduling (τ_train=0.99 in the last 50 epochs, τ_infer=0.7), and classifier-free guidance with linearly increasing scale.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, EAR-H (937M) achieves a FID of 1.97 with classifier-free guidance, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (2.27), VDM++ (2.12), and LlamaGen-3B (2.18) while using fewer parameters. The smaller EAR-B (205M) already reaches FID 2.83, surpassing GIVT (3.35) and MaskGIT (6.18). In inference speed, EAR produces a high-quality image in roughly 1 second on a single A100 GPU, compared to ~10 seconds for the closest comparable method MAR at similar quality, since EAR requires only a single forward pass per token rather than multiple denoising iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAR-H (937M) achieves FID 1.97 with guidance, beating diffusion baselines DiT-XL/2 (2.27), VDM++ (2.12), and LDM-4 (3.60), as well as VQ-based AR models LlamaGen-3B (2.18) and VAR-d30 (1.92, with 2× more parameters). Against the closest continuous-token AR method MAR (943M), EAR-H trails on FID (1.97 vs 1.55) but is roughly 10× faster at inference since it needs only one forward pass per token rather than multiple denoising steps. EAR-B (205M) already outperforms GIVT (304M, FID 3.35) and MaskGIT (227M, FID 6.18) with guidance, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. The paper also shows that continuous tokenization with the energy loss consistently outperforms discrete VQ tokenization with cross-entropy at matched model size (Figure 3), and that EAR's inference speed/quality trade-off curve dominates MAR's across all tested diffusion step counts (Figure 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies prior continuous visual autoregressive methods—GIVT (logarithmic score via GMM) and MAR (Hyvärinen score via per-token diffusion)—under a single theoretical framework grounded in strictly proper scoring rules, then introduces the energy score as a likelihood-free alternative that avoids both GMM expressiveness limits and multi-step denoising. Architecturally, it departs from the standard Transformer by replacing the softmax output layer with a small MLP generator that implicitly represents the predictive distribution through sampling, drawing on GAN-style noise injection via adaptive layer normalization. The continuous KL-16 tokenizer and masked autoregressive generation strategy are adopted directly from Li et al. (2024), while the core training objective is the energy score from Székely (2003).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~10× inference speedup over MAR (≈1 s vs ≈10 s per image) while achieving competitive FID (1.97 vs 1.55 with guidance), making continuous-token autoregressive generation viable for latency-sensitive applications. The scoring-rule framework is general in principle—any strictly proper score can serve as the objective—opening a menu of training signals beyond energy and Hyvärinen scores. Open questions include whether the quality gap to MAR closes with further scaling, whether the framework extends cleanly to video/audio, and whether the MLP generator's implicit distribution can be made more interpretable or constrained. The paper is limited to class-conditional ImageNet generation, so text-to-image and other conditional settings remain unexplored.
