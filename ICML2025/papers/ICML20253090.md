## Problem

The Bradley-Terry reward model, the standard approach in RLHF, imposes a total ordering on preferences via scalar rewards, making it fundamentally unable to represent intransitive or cyclic human preferences. Supervised pair preference models (PairRM/PairPM) can capture such structures but require O(K²) pairwise evaluations for K responses, hindering scalability in test-time ranking and verifier settings. The paper asks whether there exists a principled preference model that is both fully expressive for arbitrary (including intransitive) preference structures and computationally efficient at O(K) query complexity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a prompt-response pair (x, y) as text input, processed through a standard autoregressive LM backbone (e.g., Gemma-2B-it or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). It produces a 2k-dimensional L2-normalized preference embedding vector v_{y|x} for each response, from which pairwise preference scores are computed via a fixed skew-symmetric operator, yielding preference probabilities in (0,1). The architecture splits into two heads: an eigenvalue scale gate that produces context-dependent non-negative scaling factors {λ_l(x)} from the prompt alone, and an eigenvector embedding head that produces response-specific embeddings conditioned on both prompt and response.

## Method

The core mechanism embeds each response into a 2k-dimensional latent space and computes pairwise preference scores as v_i^T D(x) R^≻ D(x) v_j, where R^≻ is a block-diagonal skew-symmetric operator (k copies of the 2×2 rotation matrix [[0,-1],[1,0]]) and D(x) is a prompt-conditioned block-diagonal scaling matrix with blocks √λ_l(x)·I₂. This construction guarantees skew-symmetry (s(y_i≻y_j) = −s(y_j≻y_i)) and magnitude preservation, and Theorem 4.4 proves it can represent any real skew-symmetric preference matrix, making it fully expressive for intransitive and cyclic preferences. When k=1, the model reduces exactly to the Bradley-Terry model, establishing GPM as a strict generalization. For policy optimization, GPO replaces the win-rate objective of SPPO with the preference score s directly, using an iterative multiplicative-weights-style update that increases the log-probability of responses with high average preference scores, with a proven convergence rate of O(1/√T) (Theorem 5.1).

## Results

On RewardBench, GPM (embedding dim 6) with Gemma-2B-it achieves 82.29% average accuracy, a +7.44% improvement over the BT reward model (74.85%); with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPM (dim 8) reaches 91.90% vs. BT's 90.56% (+1.34%). On the CyclicPreference benchmark, GPM achieves 100% accuracy across all four cyclic datasets, while the BT model scores only 50.0–62.9% (near random-guess level). On AlpacaEval 2.0 (GPT-4-turbo evaluator), GPM-integrated GPO consistently outperforms BT-RM-based GPO in Win Rate across all model sizes and iterations (e.g., 48.25% vs. 44.20% for 2B at iteration 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Bradley-Terry (BT) reward model, which is in fact a special case of GPM when the embedding dimension is set to 1. On RewardBench, GPM consistently outperforms BT RM: with Gemma-2B-it, the best GPM configuration (dim 6) improves average accuracy from 74.85% to 82.29% (+7.44%), with the largest gains in Chat (+12.29%) and Chat-Hard (+12.29%); with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPM (dim 8) improves from 90.56% to 91.90% (+1.34%), with the most notable gain in Chat (+5.03%). On the CyclicPreference benchmark, the advantage is dramatic: GPM achieves 100% accuracy on all four cyclic datasets while BT RM scores only 50.0–62.9%, essentially at random-guess level, confirming that BT's scalar-reward structure fundamentally cannot represent intransitive preferences. On downstream alignment (AlpacaEval 2.0), GPM-integrated methods (both SPPO and GPO) consistently beat BT-RM-based methods in Win Rate across all model sizes (2B, 8B) and iterations (1–3), with margins ranging from +0.72 to +8.31 percentage points depending on the setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry reward model (the standard scalar-reward formulation in RLHF) by replacing the scalar with a 2k-dimensional embedding and a fixed skew-symmetric operator, drawing directly on the 'disc game' / 'spinning top' decomposition from game theory (Balduzzi et al., 2018; Czarnecki et al., 2020). It departs from supervised pairwise preference models (PairRM, PairPM) that require O(K²) evaluations by achieving O(K) query complexity through per-response embeddings. The proposed General Preference Optimization (GPO) is derived from SPPO (Wu et al., 2024b) but replaces the win-rate objective with the unbounded preference score s(y≻y'), connecting it to a multiplicative-weights-update analysis for convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine expressiveness gap: BT models impose a total ordering that cannot represent intransitive or cyclic preferences, which are documented in human judgment. The clean theoretical result (any skew-symmetric matrix is representable) and the O(K) complexity make this a practical alternative for test-time scaling with verifiers. However, the empirical gains are modest (7.44% on RewardBench for 2B, 1.34% for 8B), the cyclic-preference experiment uses only 216–363 instances (essentially memorization), and the length-bias issue in GPO-aligned models remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the learned embedding dimensions correspond to interpretable preference axes and whether the approach scales to very large candidate sets in reasoning tasks.
