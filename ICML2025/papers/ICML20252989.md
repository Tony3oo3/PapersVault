## Problem

Existing ad hoc teamwork (AHT) methods rely on online interaction with the environment and a fixed set of pre-trained teammates, making them infeasible when simulators are unavailable or teammate policy spaces are too large to enumerate. Extending AHT to an offline setting introduces three compounding challenges: limited dataset coverage of diverse teammate behaviors, partial observability without the ability to compensate via active exploration, and the inadequacy of a scalar return-to-go signal to capture rapidly changing teammate intentions.

## Input / Output

At deployment, the ego agent receives only its own local observation (a 5×5 window in grid environments or a kitchen state in Overcooked) and must produce a discrete action (directional movement, collect, or interact). Internally, the high-level module encodes this local observation into a stochastic latent team context, predicts a scalar TA-RTG, and decodes a TA-Goal — a concatenated one-hot vector of all agents' discretized observations k steps ahead (k is a tunable hyperparameter, optimal values ranging from 2 to 6 depending on environment). The low-level causal Transformer consumes a sliding window of K=30 past (TA-Goal, observation, action) triples and outputs the next ego action via negative log-likelihood.

## Method

TAGET is a two-level hierarchical sequence model. The high level uses a VAE-style dual-encoder design: a team context encoder f_φ takes all agents' observations to produce a latent h, while a proxy encoder f_θ takes only the ego's local observation to produce z, with a hybrid reverse-KL/forward-KL regularization forcing z to approximate h under partial observability. A TA-RTG predictor (MSE-trained) conditions on h to estimate future team return, and a TA-Goal decoder (cross-entropy-trained) maps (h, TA-RTG) to the predicted global state at t+k. The low level is a 2-layer causal Decision Transformer (d=64, 1 head, K=30) that replaces the conventional RTG token with the predicted TA-Goal as its conditioning prompt, learning to generate actions via supervised sequence modeling. A trajectory mirroring pre-processing step reorders each multi-agent episode so every agent in turn serves as the ego, multiplying effective training data by the number of agents and exposing the policy to multiple decision-making perspectives.

## Results

TAGET achieves the highest average return across all six environment configurations (three environments × two teammate-set sizes). Headline values include 63.2 on PP-4, 0.140 on LBF-4, 0.98 on Overcooked-4, 62.9 on PP-8, 0.080 on LBF-8, and 0.77 on Overcooked-8. The paper reports an average improvement of 37.83% over the best-performing baseline in each setting, with the largest gains on Overcooked-4 (+113.04% over CQL) and LBF-8 (+45.45% over DT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAGET outperforms all six baselines across every environment and teammate-set size. Against the strongest baselines, the margins are: +2.93% over MADT on PP-4 (63.2 vs 61.4), +37.25% over Prompt-DT on LBF-4 (0.140 vs 0.102), +113.04% over CQL on Overcooked-4 (0.98 vs 0.46), +8.01% over MADT on PP-8 (62.9 vs 62.4), +45.45% over DT on LBF-8 (0.080 vs 0.055), and +20.31% over Prompt-DT on Overcooked-8 (0.77 vs 0.64). The gains are most pronounced in Overcooked, where the mandatory role-splitting between agents makes dynamic teammate adaptation critical, and in LBF-8, where the larger unseen teammate set exposes the fragility of baselines that overfit to training-time behaviors. DT and Prompt-DT plateau at lower returns because they lack explicit mechanisms to model changing teammate policies, while ODITS-off and LIAM-off show high variance and occasional near-zero returns (e.g., ODITS-off scores 0.000 on LBF-8), indicating poor generalization to unseen teammates. CQL and MADT, though stronger than the DT variants, still trail TAGET because they do not incorporate teammate-aware goal prediction to adapt in real time.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAGET extends the Decision Transformer framework by replacing the scalar return-to-go conditioning signal with a hierarchical teammate-aware sub-goal (TA-Goal), drawing on variational autoencoder-style regularization to infer global team context from local observations. It departs from prior AHT methods (ODITS, LIAM) that require online interaction with the environment and pre-trained teammates, instead formulating the problem entirely in the offline regime. The trajectory mirroring strategy repurposes multi-agent trajectory data from a data-efficiency perspective, while the overall architecture borrows the high-level/low-level decomposition from hierarchical RL and the sequence-modeling paradigm from offline RL (DT, Trajectory Transformer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving AHT into the offline setting, TAGET addresses a practical bottleneck: many real-world multi-agent scenarios (autonomous driving, rescue robotics) lack access to interactive simulators but do have recorded interaction data. The key open question is whether the hierarchical goal-based approach generalizes beyond the small grid-based benchmarks (10×10 or 20×20) to larger, continuous, or partially stochastic environments where sub-goal prediction becomes significantly harder. The paper also leaves open how well the method scales when the number of teammates or the joint policy space grows substantially, and whether the TA-Goal mechanism remains informative in environments with very sparse or delayed rewards.
