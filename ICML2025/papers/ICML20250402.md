## Problem

Linear bound propagation verifiers scale to large networks but produce surprisingly loose bounds under ℓ₂-norm perturbations because they cannot capture inter-neuron coupling, while SDP-based verifiers naturally model this coupling but suffer cubic O(n³) complexity that limits them to small models. No existing method achieves both the tightness of SDP relaxations and the scalability of bound propagation for ℓ₂ adversaries on networks with tens of thousands of neurons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a feedforward ReLU network with weight matrices W^(k), a center point x̂ ∈ ℝⁿ, and an ℓ₂ perturbation radius ρ, the method computes a valid lower bound on the robustness margin min_{x ∈ B₂(x̂,ρ)} cᵀf(x) for each pair of classes. The ℓ₂ ball at each intermediate layer z^(k) is propagated via spectral norms of the weight matrices (ρ^(k) = ‖W^(k)‖₂⋯‖W^(1)‖₂·ρ), and the only additional optimization variable is a single scalar λ^(k) ≥ 0 per layer, solved jointly with the standard α parameters via projected gradient ascent (300 Adam iterations).

## Method

SDP-CROWN derives a new linear lower bound offset h(g, λ) = −½(λ(ρ²−‖x̂‖₂²) + (1/λ)‖φ(g,λ)‖₂²) by taking the SDP relaxation of the ReLU activation under an ℓ₂ ball constraint, applying positive/negative splitting, and solving the resulting dual in closed form. This offset replaces the standard bound propagation offset h(α) = −ρ‖min{g(α),0}‖₁, switching from an ℓ₁ to an ℓ₂ norm that captures inter-neuron coupling, while adding only one scalar parameter λ per layer. The bound is proven to be up to √n tighter than per-neuron bounds in the zero-centered case (Theorem 5.2) and is exactly tight for the SDP relaxation (Theorem 5.3). In practice, it integrates seamlessly into α-CROWN by simply substituting the new offset at each backward pass step, preserving the O(n) per-layer cost of bound propagation while approaching the tightness of full SDP solvers on models with 65k neurons and 2.47M parameters.

## Results

On the largest model ConvLarge (CIFAR-10, 65k neurons, 2.47M parameters) under ℓ₂ perturbations (ρ=8/255), SDP-CROWN achieves 63.5% verified accuracy in 73s, compared to 2.5% for α-CROWN (47s), 5% for β-CROWN (289s), and 47.5% for the naive Lipschitz baseline LipNaive (1.2s). On ConvSmall (MNIST, ρ=0.3), SDP-CROWN reaches 81.5% (12s) versus 0% for α-CROWN and 16–19.5% for β-CROWN/GCP-CROWN/BICCOS. SDP-CROWN consistently outperforms all bound-propagation baselines by 10–60 percentage points across all model/dataset combinations, while remaining 1–4× faster than branch-and-bound methods and 100×+ faster than SDP-based BM-Full (which is infeasible beyond ~1k neurons).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDP-CROWN dominates all bound-propagation baselines (α-CROWN, β-CROWN, GCP-CROWN, BICCOS) by 10–60 percentage points in verified accuracy across every model/dataset combination; the gap is most dramatic on large models where α-CROWN drops to 0–2.5% while SDP-CROWN maintains 63–80%. Against the Lipschitz-based baselines, SDP-CROWN is consistently tighter: on ConvLarge (CIFAR-10) it achieves 63.5% vs. 47.5% for LipNaive, and on ConvSmall (MNIST) 81.5% vs. 77.5%, confirming that global Lipschitz bounds remain overly conservative even for 1-Lipschitz-trained networks. LipSDP (SDP-based Lipschitz estimation) is infeasible on ConvLarge (MNIST) and ConvLarge (CIFAR-10) due to cubic scaling, while SDP-CROWN handles both in under 90s. The full SDP verifier BM-Full achieves the highest accuracy on the smallest model (53% vs. 32.5% on MLP/MNIST) but becomes computationally infeasible on all models with more than ~1k neurons, whereas SDP-CROWN scales to 65k neurons. LP-All (LP relaxation) is also infeasible on large models and yields lower accuracy than SDP-CROWN where it can run (9% vs. 32.5% on MLP/MNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established but incompatible paradigms in neural network verification: linear bound propagation (LiRPA, α-CROWN, β-CROWN) and semidefinite programming relaxations (Brown et al., 2022; Chiu & Zhang, 2023). The key conceptual move is deriving a linear bound from the SDP dual that captures ℓ₂-norm inter-neuron coupling through a single scalar parameter λ per layer, rather than the full n×n coupling matrix required by SDP. This departs from prior bound propagation methods, which relax ℓ₂ balls into ℓ∞ boxes and thereby lose the coupling structure, and from prior SDP methods, which preserve coupling but incur cubic complexity. The resulting SDP-CROWN is a drop-in extension of α-CROWN's pipeline, adding one optimizable parameter per layer while preserving linear-time scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical blind spot: state-of-the-art bound propagation verifiers (α-CROWN, β-CROWN, BICCOS) perform catastrophically under ℓ₂ perturbations on large networks, yielding verified accuracies of 2.5–6% that are worse than a naive per-layer Lipschitz product (47.5%). SDP-CROWN closes most of the gap to the PGD upper bound (63.5% vs 72.5% on ConvLarge) while scaling to 65k neurons where SDP is infeasible. Limitations include the √n tightness guarantee holding only for the zero-centered single-ReLU case, and the general-network improvement being empirical rather than analytically bounded. Open questions include whether the ellipsoid and intersection extensions in the appendix can further close the gap, and whether analogous SDP-derived bounds exist for non-ReLU activations or mixed-norm settings.
