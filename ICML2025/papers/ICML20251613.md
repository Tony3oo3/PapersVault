## Problem

Prior last-iterate convergence analyses of shuffling gradient methods (Liu & Zhou, 2024b) treat each epoch as a single proximal step, yielding rates for nonsmooth (strongly) convex objectives that are no faster than Proximal GD and thus fail to capture the benefit of random permutations. This paper asks whether the Random Reshuffle (RR) and Single Shuffle (SS) strategies can be proved to converge strictly faster than Proximal GD in the nonsmooth setting, and whether the resulting rates match known lower bounds for the suffix average.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a finite-sum objective f(x) = (1/n)Σf_i(x) with each f_i convex and G_i-Lipschitz on dom ψ, a proper closed convex regularizer ψ (possibly μ-strongly convex), an initial point x₁ ∈ dom ψ, and a stepsize schedule η_t. The index i(t) is generated by a permutation-based shuffling strategy (RR, SS, or IG), and the output is the last iterate x_{T+1} with a high-probability or expected function value gap bound E[F(x_{T+1}) − F_⋆]. A notable feature is that the results hold for arbitrary T ∈ ℕ rather than requiring T = Kn for integer K, and the rates track the fine-grained dependence on individual Lipschitz constants through the arithmetic mean G_{f,1} and root-mean-square G_{f,2}.

## Method

The key insight is that prior analyses miss randomness by verifying convergence whenever each epoch contains a full permutation of [n], regardless of how indices are ordered. The paper instead abstracts the randomness as the marginal condition i(t) ≡_D Uniform[n] for all t, then introduces a novel sufficient lemma (Lemma 5.1) that guarantees last-iterate convergence provided the bias term |E[f_{i(t)}(x_s) − f(x_s)]| is bounded by Φ·η_s for all s ≤ t, where Φ quantifies the dependence structure. For RR, the authors show Φ = Θ(√n · G_{f,1} · G_{f,2}), while for SS, Φ = Θ(√T · G_{f,1} · G_{f,2} + (T/n) · G_{f,2}²). Plugging these into the lemma with optimized stepsizes yields rates improved by up to Θ(n^{-1/4}) over Proximal GD for RR in the general convex case and Θ(n^{-1/2}) in the strongly convex case, and for SS a critical epoch threshold K_⋆ = nG_{f,1}²/G_{f,2}² below which the rate strictly beats Proximal GD. In the constrained case (ψ = I_C), a sharper SS bound is obtained that dominates both the new Theorem 4.5 and the prior Liu & Zhou (2024b) rate for all K.

## Results

For the Random Reshuffle (RR) scheme in the general convex case (μ=0), the paper achieves a last-iterate rate of Õ(GD*/(n^{1/4}√K)), improving upon the prior best O(GD*/√K) by up to a factor of Θ(n^{-1/4}). In the strongly convex case (μ>0), RR achieves Õ(μD*²/(n²K²) + G²/(μ√(nK))), improving the prior Õ(μD*²/K² + G²/(μK)) by up to Θ(n^{-1/2}). For Single Shuffle (SS), the paper shows improved rates over Proximal GD when K ≤ K* = nG_{f,1}²/G_{f,2}², and in the constrained case (ψ=I_C) achieves Õ(√(G_{f,1}G_{f,2})D*/(n^{1/4}K^{1/4}) ∧ G_{f,1}D*/√K) which dominates both prior bounds for all K. The suffix average under RR matches the lower bound Ω(1/(n^{1/4}√K)) from Koren et al. (2022) up to logarithmic factors, giving the first (nearly) optimal result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Liu & Zhou (2024b, ICML), the paper's RR results are strictly better in both the general convex case (improvement of up to Θ(n^{-1/4}) in the rate) and the strongly convex case (improvement of up to Θ(n^{-1/2})), providing the first concrete evidence that RR provably beats Proximal GD. For SS, the paper's rates dominate Liu & Zhou (2024b) when K ≤ K* = nG_{f,1}²/G_{f,2}² (which equals n when all G_i are equal), and in the constrained case (ψ = I_C) the sharpened Theorem 4.6 rate Õ(√(G_{f,1}G_{f,2})D*/(n^{1/4}K^{1/4}) ∧ G_{f,1}D*/√K) is faster than both the paper's own Theorem 4.5 and Liu & Zhou (2024b) for all K. Against Koren et al. (2022, NeurIPS), the paper's Corollary 4.3 provides the first last-iterate (and suffix-average) convergence rate matching their lower bound Ω(1/(n^{1/4}√K)) up to logarithmic factors, closing a gap that was previously only established for the full average iterate under the restricted setting ψ = I_C. The paper also extends all results to arbitrary T ∈ ℕ (not just T = Kn) and to general convex ψ (not just indicator functions), which neither baseline handles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the last-iterate convergence analysis of shuffling gradient methods initiated by Liu & Zhou (2024b), who established the first such results but proved nonsmooth rates no better than Proximal GD. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the prior analysis—treating each epoch as a single proximal update—fundamentally missed the randomness in index selection. The authors instead adopt an analytical template inspired by Proximal SGD (Liu & Zhou, 2024a), abstracting the index process to any random variable marginally uniform on [n], and introduce a novel sufficient condition (Lemma 5.1) that controls the bias term |E[Ω_t(x_s)]| to recover the randomness benefit. The suffix-average result also closes a gap with the lower bound from Koren et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first concrete theoretical evidence that Random Reshuffle and Single Shuffle are provably faster than Proximal GD in the nonsmooth convex regime, resolving an open question explicitly raised by prior work. The suffix-average rate under RR matching the known lower bound (up to log factors) is a notable closure. However, significant gaps remain: the rates still do not match Proximal SGD, the SS improvement is conditional on the time horizon being below a critical threshold K*, and the strongly convex SS case lacks a bound better than both the new and prior results. Whether the RR rate O(n^{1/4}/√T) is tight for the last iterate (as opposed to the suffix average) remains an open conjecture.
