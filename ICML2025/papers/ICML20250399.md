## Problem

Existing video generation models for embodied scenarios primarily perform conditional simulation without understanding current or predicted states, limiting them to fixed-frame, single-round predictions that cannot detect flawed outputs or generalize to OOD scenarios. This undermines their utility as world simulators that must iteratively refine predictions, handle multi-step task decomposition, and adapt to diverse embodied environments without human intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250399/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial RGB observation frame and a natural-language task instruction (e.g., "pick up the book") as input. It produces 16-frame video chunks (2 seconds each) alongside text descriptions or QA answers, and through the autoregressive reflection loop, iteratively extends the video by feeding the last 1+t frames back as conditioning for the next generation round until the VLM judges the task complete. The VLM operates within a 2048-token budget using ChatUniVi's parameter-free token clustering to compress video representations without additional computational overhead.

## Method

EVA integrates a 7B VLM (CLIP ViT-L/14 + Vicuna-v1.5) and a 1.5B latent diffusion VDM (Dynamicrafter-based) via a cross-attention generation adapter that aligns VLM hidden features with VDM text embeddings through linear transformation, trained with diffusion denoising loss. The core mechanism is Reflection of Generation (RoG): after each 16-frame chunk, the VLM evaluates task completion and issues one of three commands—extend, regenerate, or output—enabling iterative self-correction and adaptive sequence length without fixed horizons. For multi-domain generalization, an Ensemble-LoRA system applies domain-specific low-rank adapters (human-egocentric, real-robot, simulation-robot) selected by a task-token-controlled softmax gating mechanism inspired by mixture-of-experts. Training follows a three-stage paradigm: (1) video-caption alignment on COCO/CC3M, (2) embodied QA instruction tuning on 500K EVA-Instruct pairs from Open-X-Embodiment, Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, and CALVIN, and (3) joint generation-adapter and Ensemble-LoRA fine-tuning to maximize cross-domain generation quality.

## Results

On the How-To task, EVA achieves an EVA-Score of 81.67 (language: 85.51, video: 77.83), outperforming the two-stage EVA-2Stage (79.42) and the best VLM+VDM baseline Qwen2+EVA-Gen (55.44). On the Next-Step task, EVA scores 70.85, again surpassing EVA-2Stage (68.74) and Qwen2+EVA-Gen (51.55). On RT-1 robot planning, EVA achieves 84% in-domain success (42/50) and 80% OOD success (12/15), compared to AVDC's 58% and 47% respectively. On CALVIN simulation with CMLP2, EVA+RoG reaches 90/157 on the rotate task versus 48/157 without RoG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA dominates all zero-shot VLM baselines on the Action-Description task: it scores 62.63 under GPT-4o evaluation versus GPT-4o itself (33.19), Qwen2 (29.58), LLAVA-NI (26.94), LLAVA-NV (25.56), LLAVA-O (22.35), MiniCPMv2 (17.63), and ChatUniVi (9.03). In the two-stage RoG setting (Tables 2–3), EVA consistently outperforms both Qwen2+EVA-Gen and LLAVA-O+EVA-Gen on all video quality metrics (SC, BC, MS, FVD) and the combined EVA-Score, with the largest gap on Next-Step (70.85 vs. 51.55 and 35.47). Against the video-generation-only baseline Dynamicrafter (DC), EVA-Gen improves GCE from 80.96 to 86.83 and reduces FVD from 362.56 to 177.28. On RT-1 robot planning, EVA surpasses AVDC by 28 percentage points in-domain (84% vs. 58%) and by 33 points OOD (80% vs. 47%), with the RoG reflection mechanism contributing 7 additional OOD successes over the no-RoG variant. On CALVIN, EVA+RoG+CMLP2 nearly doubles the rotate-task success rate (90/157 vs. 48/157) and substantially improves lightbulb (32/41 vs. 10/41) and open-drawer (25/41 vs. 11/41) tasks over the w/o-RoG+CMLP2 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video generation (Dynamicrafter, VideoCrafter, Stable Video Diffusion) and embodied world models (RoboDreamer, Enerverse, AVDC, Unisim, VLP), but departs from both by introducing a reflection mechanism inspired by chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs. Prior embodied video predictors treat generation as a one-shot conditional simulation without an understanding loop, while prior VLMs lack the ability to iteratively verify and extend their own predictions. RoG carves new ground by modularly combining any VLM and VDM with an explicit reflect-extend-regenerate loop, and by decomposing embodied video anticipation into four evaluable meta-tasks (Action-Description, How-To, Finish-Thinking, Next-Step) that no prior benchmark has jointly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVA-Bench (125 curated samples across egocentric, real-robot, and simulated-robot domains) fills a gap in evaluation for embodied video prediction, and the RoG framework offers a practical recipe for making video generators self-correcting and task-aware without retraining the full pipeline. The OOD RT-1 results (12/15 vs. 7/15 for AVDC) and CALVIN simulation gains (e.g., 32/41 vs. 10/41 on lightbulb with CMLP2) suggest the reflection loop meaningfully improves generalization, though the small benchmark size and reliance on human evaluation limit statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the chunk-wise autoregressive extension scales to truly long-horizon multi-task sequences, whether the Ensemble-LoRA gating generalizes to unseen domains without per-domain fine-tuning, and whether the reflection mechanism can operate in real time for closed-loop robot control.
