## Problem

Role-playing language agents for established characters suffer from two persistent gaps: existing datasets are either LLM-synthesized (compromising authenticity) or limited to two-character dialogues lacking rich context, and current evaluation methods rely on single-turn interactions with pre-defined questions or biased LLM judges that fail to assess complex multi-character portrayals. No prior work provides both authentic, multi-character dialogue data with comprehensive contextual information and an evaluation protocol grounded in those authentic scenes.

## Input / Output

The model receives a role-playing instruction (scenario description, target character's profile and motivation, profiles of other participants, optionally plot summaries) concatenated with the conversation message history, and generates the target character's next utterance structured across three orthogonal dimensions: speech (verbal output), action (physical behaviors in parentheses, e.g., tool-use-like triggers), and thought (invisible internal cognition creating information asymmetry). For evaluation, the multi-agent system produces a full simulated conversation (up to 20 turns) with a next-speaker-prediction model selecting speakers from characters plus an environment role, and LLM critics output penalty-based scores (100 minus 5×sum of flaw severities, length-corrected) across four dimensions: anthropomorphism, character fidelity, storyline quality, and storyline consistency.

## Method

The core technical contribution is given-circumstance acting (GCA), adapted from Stanislavski's acting methodology, which trains and evaluates LLMs by having them sequentially portray each character in authentic multi-character book conversations. For training, each sample designates one character's utterances as optimization targets while all other messages and the role-playing instruction serve as inputs; models are fine-tuned on LLaMA-3.1 (8B and 70B) using ~0.1B tokens of authentic dialogue data augmented with Tulu-3 for general capability retention, with diverse instruction templates and optional inclusion of inner thoughts, other characters' profiles, and plot summaries. For evaluation, GCA builds a multi-agent system where the actor LLM instantiates an RPLA per character (with motivations and thoughts hidden from other agents), simulates the conversation under the same setting, and then penalty-based LLM critics identify specific flaw instances against detailed rubrics using the original conversation as reference, with length correction (λ=1.5) to mitigate the natural accumulation of flaws in longer simulations. The dataset itself is curated via an LLM-based pipeline that chunks 771 books, extracts plots/conversations/character data in JSON, unifies character aliases, and generates profiles, yielding 17,966 characters and 29,798 conversations with speech, action, thought, and environment messages.

## Results

On the primary COSER Test (GCA evaluation), CoSER-70B achieves an average LLM-judged score of 59.06%, competitive with GPT-4o (59.95%), while substantially outperforming all open-source models and its LLaMA-3.1-70B baseline (55.49%). On N-gram metrics, CoSER-70B achieves BLEU 10.10 and ROUGE-L 14.78, exceeding the second-best by 58% on BLEU. On existing multi-choice benchmarks, CoSER-70B reaches 93.47% on LifeChoice (surpassing GPT-4o's 75.96% by 23 points), 75.80% on InCharacter (BFI), and 64.49% on CroSS-MR, achieving state-of-the-art or matching performance across all three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSER-70B matches or surpasses GPT-4o on the primary GCA evaluation (59.06% vs 59.95% average) and dominates on N-gram fidelity metrics (BLEU 10.10 vs 5.90, ROUGE-L 14.78 vs 12.11), indicating more authentic reproduction of original dialogue. On LifeChoice, CoSER-70B achieves 93.47% accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (75.96%) by 23 points and LLaMA-3.1-70B (86.48%) by 7 points. On InCharacter, CoSER-70B (75.80%) is within 0.74 points of GPT-4o (76.54%) and surpasses LLaMA-3.1-70B (72.16%). Both CoSER models substantially outperform their LLaMA-3.1 baselines (CoSER-70B: +3.57 avg; CoSER-8B: +3.46 avg), while Higgs-Llama-3-70B, fine-tuned on LLM-synthesized dialogues, actually underperforms the unmodified LLaMA-3.1-70B, confirming the value of authentic book-sourced data over synthetic alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RPLA research line initiated by Park et al. (2023) and Shao et al. (2023), but departs fundamentally from prior datasets that rely on LLM-synthesized dialogues or limited two-character interactions. It draws on Stanislavski's given-circumstance acting methodology to frame both training and evaluation as multi-character sequential performance within authentic book scenes, rather than single-turn Q&A. The evaluation protocol extends LLM-as-a-judge approaches by introducing penalty-based scoring with detailed rubrics, original dialogue references, and length correction, addressing known biases in prior LLM-judged RPLA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSER provides the first large-scale, authentic, multi-character role-playing dataset (17,966 characters, 771 books) with open-source SOTA models, filling a critical gap where prior work was limited to small synthetic or single-source datasets. The GCA evaluation protocol offers a more comprehensive and controlled assessment than single-turn benchmarks, though it still depends on LLM judges whose reliability is only partially validated (68.6% alignment with humans for GPT-4o). Open questions include scaling beyond English literary fiction, handling real-world individual personas with privacy constraints, and whether the multi-agent simulation evaluation generalizes to interactive, open-ended user scenarios rather than bounded book scenes.
