## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving KNN classification frameworks suffer from severe online communication inefficiency due to two issues: (1) they require communicating secret shares or encrypted elements for every Euclidean square distance computation, and (2) they use sequential bubble protocols that demand O(kn) communication rounds to select the k nearest neighbors. These bottlenecks make such frameworks impractical for large-scale datasets and wide-area network deployments.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input an m-dimensional attribute vector from a user (shared via masked secret sharing), a horizontally distributed dataset of n labeled samples held by multiple data owners (shared via MSS for attributes and ASS for labels), and pre-generated Euclidean triples (two secret-shared vectors and a secret-shared scalar). It outputs a single predicted class label reconstructed by the user from additive secret shares sent by the two computation parties. All computation is performed over the ring Z_{2^64} under a semi-honest, non-colluding two-party model.

## Method

Kona introduces two communication optimizations on top of two-party additive and masked secret sharing. First, it designs novel Euclidean triples—correlated randomness consisting of secret-shared vectors [[u]] and [[v]] plus a secret-shared scalar [[w]] where w=(u-v)²—that enable both computation parties to locally compute their shares of the Euclidean square distance with zero online communication, eliminating the O(nm) communication size of prior methods. Second, it proposes a divide-and-conquer bubble protocol (DQBubble) that replaces sequential pairwise comparisons with parallel compare-and-swap operations over halved sublists, reducing the communication rounds for k-nearest-neighbor selection from O(kn) to O(k log n). Together, these optimizations reduce total online communication size from O(nm+kn) to O(kn) and rounds from O(kn) to O(k log n), while preserving identical classification accuracy to plaintext KNN.

## Results

Kona achieves identical classification accuracy to scikit-learn across all eight datasets (e.g., 0.9722 on Mnist, 0.9667 on Iris, 0.9011 on Spambase). In terms of efficiency (k=5), Kona reduces online communication size by 1.10× (Iris) to 3121.25× (PEMS-SF) and communication rounds by 16.72× (Iris) to 5783.21× (Dota2 Games) compared to SecKNN. In WAN settings, Kona achieves 14.36×–232.60× runtime speedups, and in LAN settings, 1.15×–11.36× speedups. The gains scale with dataset size (more samples → larger round reduction) and dimensionality (more attributes → larger size reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SecKNN (Li et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art FSS-based secure KNN framework, Kona dominates on every efficiency metric across all eight datasets. Communication size is reduced by 1.10×–3121.25× (largest on high-dimensional PEMS-SF with 137,710 attributes), communication rounds by 16.72×–5783.21× (largest on large-sample Dota2 Games with 102,944 samples), and WAN runtime by 14.36×–232.60×. Even in the LAN setting where computation (not communication) becomes the bottleneck, Kona still achieves up to 11.36× speedup on PEMS-SF. Against scikit-learn (plaintext KNN), Kona ties exactly on classification accuracy for all eight datasets (e.g., 0.9722 on Mnist, 0.9667 on Iris), confirming that the privacy-preserving protocols introduce no accuracy loss. The ablation study further confirms that each optimization (Euclidean triples for size, DQBubble for rounds) independently contributes to the overall gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of MPC-based privacy-preserving KNN classification frameworks (Sun & Yang 2020, Liu et al. 2019, SecKNN/Li et al. 2023) by targeting the two dominant sources of online communication overhead: secure distance computation and k-NN selection. The Euclidean triples concept is directly inspired by the input-independent but function-dependent correlated randomness technique of Ben-Efraim et al. (2019), repurposed here to eliminate all online communication for Euclidean square distance. The divide-and-conquer bubble protocol applies a standard parallel comparison strategy to the sequential bubble sort used in prior work, reducing round complexity from O(kn) to O(k log n) without changing communication size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 1.1×–3121× communication size and 16.7×–5783× round reductions make privacy-preserving KNN classification practical for large-scale, high-dimensional real-world datasets (e.g., 100k+ samples, 137k attributes) under WAN conditions, where prior frameworks were prohibitively slow. Key limitations include the semi-honest security model (no malicious-party resistance), the two-party computation assumption (though extensible), and reliance on Euclidean square distance (though adaptable). Open questions include extending to malicious security, supporting vertically distributed datasets natively, and whether the function-dependent preprocessing approach generalizes to more complex distance metrics or other MPC-based ML tasks beyond KNN.
