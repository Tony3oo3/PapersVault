## Problem

LLM-based embodied agents struggle to adapt to unseen domains without extensive data collection or retraining. Existing approaches either rely on in-context adaptation (inefficient due to searching large information volumes) or static model integration (lacking flexibility to expand beyond learned domains). The paper addresses the gap of enabling dynamic, test-time composition of multiple domain-specific world models into a fixed LLM reasoning policy for robust cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252905/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives the current environment state (represented as a list of object-relation triples in VirtualHome or text observations in ALFWorld) along with a natural language task instruction, and produces a single action from a fixed action vocabulary (e.g., walk, grab, open, put, switchon) at each timestep. Object-wise states are extracted via an object detection model Φ_D and embedded via a language/vision embedding model Φ_E, which are used both for prototype-based world model retrieval and as inputs to the world models. The reasoning model (Llama-3.2-3B) and world models (Llama-3.2-1B) operate on text, with the compound attention connecting intermediate layers [13,27] of the reasoning model to layers [7,15] of each world model.

## Method

WorMI introduces a dual-stage test-time composition: (1) prototype-based world model retrieval, where k-center clustering on object-wise state embeddings produces compact prototypes (~0.1% of total embeddings) per domain, and Wasserstein distance between prototype sets selects the top-K most relevant world models; and (2) world-wise compound attention, a hierarchical cross-attention module with a world-level layer (query from the reasoning model, keys/values from projected world model outputs) that integrates multiple world representations, followed by a reasoning-level layer that aligns the integrated representation back to the LLM. The compound attention is meta-learned via a MAML-style inner/outer loop over random subsets of world models, so it learns a general integration strategy that adapts to any combination of world models with few gradient steps. This design allows seamless implantation and removal of world models at test time while keeping both the LLM reasoning model and individual world models frozen.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation on VirtualHome (unseen tasks & unseen scenes), WorMI achieves 66.12% success rate (SR) and 15.17 pending steps (PS), outperforming the strongest baseline SayCanPay (45.71% SR, 19.04 PS) by 20.41% in SR and 3.87 steps in PS. On ALFWorld under the same setting, WorMI reaches 51.67% SR and 20.18 PS versus SayCanPay's 39.66% SR and 23.86 PS. In few-shot (average over 1/5/10 shots), WorMI further extends its lead, achieving 77.51% SR in VirtualHome and 58.22% SR in ALFWorld, surpassing SayCanPay by 26.58% and 19.16% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorMI consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting in both VirtualHome and ALFWorld. Against the strongest baseline SayCanPay (Hazra et al., 2024), WorMI gains 20.41% SR in zero-shot VirtualHome (unseen/unseen) and 26.58% SR in few-shot VirtualHome (average), while also reducing pending steps by 3.87 and 4.98 respectively. Compared to LLM-Planner (Song et al., 2023), which relies on in-context learning, WorMI achieves substantially higher SR (66.12% vs. 21.45% in zero-shot VirtualHome unseen/unseen) and lower PS (15.17 vs. 27.73), indicating that attention-driven world model fusion is far more effective than retrieving textual examples. The zero-shot planner ZSP (Huang et al., 2022a) and the fine-tuned LLM+FT baseline are both significantly outperformed, with LLM+FT reaching only 42.82% SR in the hardest VirtualHome setting versus WorMI's 66.12%. Notably, WorMI's advantage grows with LLM scale (Table 4), reaching 71.18% SR with Llama-3.2-11B, while LLM-Planner degrades sharply with smaller models, highlighting the robustness of the world model implanting approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorMI sits at the intersection of LLM-based embodied agents (SayCan, SayCanPay, LLM-Planner) and model composition/merging (CALM, LLM-Blender), but departs from both in a key way: rather than retrieving raw in-context examples or fusing two fixed models, it dynamically composes a variable subset of pre-trained domain-specific world models into a frozen LLM reasoning model at test time. The meta-learning training procedure follows first-order MAML (Nichol, 2018), while the hierarchical cross-attention (world-level integration followed by reasoning-level alignment) is a new architectural contribution not present in prior model-merging or embodied-agent work. The prototype-based retrieval using k-center clustering and Wasserstein distance over object-wise embeddings is also novel, providing a bounded, computationally efficient alternative to full set-distance matching for model selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play 'implanting' paradigm—where domain-specific world models can be added or removed without retraining the reasoning model—offers a practical path toward scalable embodied agents that accumulate knowledge over time, addressing a core limitation of both in-context adaptation (data overhead) and fixed model integration (rigidity). The demonstration of graceful performance degradation upon model removal (a 'machine unlearning' effect) and the robustness to up to 33% adversarial world models are practically relevant for deployment. Key open questions include scaling beyond simulation environments, reducing the computational overhead of multi-model inference, and whether the approach transfers to truly open-world settings with unbounded domain diversity. The preliminary multi-modal results (VLM as reasoning model) suggest extensibility but remain under-optimized.
