## Problem

FTRL is widely considered unsuitable for dynamic environments because its accumulated gradient state grows unboundedly, causing iterates to become 'lazy' and fail to track moving comparators. No prior work established dynamic regret guarantees for FTRL over compact sets with the minimax-optimal O(√((1+P_T)T)) rate, nor with full dependence on prediction accuracy E_T. This paper asks whether FTRL can recover such guarantees and identifies the precise mechanism—unbounded state growth decoupled from iterates—that hinders dynamic regret.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a compact convex set X ⊂ ℝ^n (with ‖x‖ ≤ R) and receives, at each slot t, an L-Lipschitz convex cost f_t and a prediction f̃_t of the current cost before committing to x_t. The set constraint is modeled as an indicator function I_X(x) appended to each cost, so the update solves a composite objective over ℝ^n followed by a Euclidean projection. The output is a sequence of feasible actions {x_t} with dynamic regret bounds of the form O((1+P_T)√E_T) or O((1+√P_T)√E_T) depending on the regularization strategy, where all terms vanish under perfect predictions.

## Method

The core mechanism is a pruning rule that selects the normal-cone subgradient g_t^I ∈ N_X(x_t) to reset the accumulated state p_{1:t} whenever the unconstrained iterate x_t^uc falls outside X, ensuring ‖p_{1:t}‖ ≤ Rσ_{1:t-1} + ε_t rather than growing linearly in t. This transforms the FTRL update from a 'lazy' aggregation of all past gradients into a bounded-memory process whose state norm is tied to the (optimistically chosen) regularization σ_{1:t} ∝ √E_t. The update also incorporates the next-step prediction f̃_{t+1} directly into the minimization objective, avoiding the cyclic dependency that plagues prior optimistic OMD analyses. Four regularization strategies are provided: P_T-agnostic, P_T-known (achieving the minimax-optimal √P_T rate), P_T-unknown with online estimation, and AdaFTRL-style recursive regularization that applies only the minimal necessary strong convexity at each step.

## Results

OptFPRL achieves dynamic regret R_T = O((1 + √P_T)√E_T) under prior knowledge of P_T (Theorem 3.2), matching the minimax-optimal rate Ω(√((1+P_T)T)) while being fully modulated by the cumulative prediction error E_T. In the P_T-agnostic setting (Theorem 3.1), the bound degrades gracefully to O((1+P_T)√E_T). A key property is that under perfect predictions (E_T = 0), the regret is exactly zero regardless of P_T, and the hybrid term H_T = Σ ε_t‖u_{t+1}−u_t‖ ensures P_T is always multiplied by prediction error rather than appearing independently.

|---|---|---|---|
| P_T-agnostic (Thm 3.1) | σ_{1:t} ∝ √E_t | O((1 + P_T)√E_T) | No prior info needed |
| Known P_T (Thm 3.2) | σ_{1:t} ∝ √(E_t/P_T') | O((1 + √P_T)√E_T) | Minimax-optimal rate |

| Recursive (Thm 3.4) | σ_t ∝ δ_t (AdaFTRL-style) | O((1 + P_T)√E_T) | Minimal regularization |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is purely theoretical and does not run empirical experiments, so comparisons are against prior regret bounds. Against the OMD-based optimistic dynamic regret of Jadbabaie et al. (2015), which gives O((P_T+1)√(E_T+1)) without path observability, OptFPRL improves the P_T dependence from linear to √P_T when P_T is known. Against Scroccaro et al. (2023), which resolves the cyclic dependency issue but yields O((1+P_T)(1+√D_T)), OptFPRL avoids the cyclic issue entirely (no need to linearize predictions at x_t) and achieves the tighter √P_T rate. Against the only prior FTRL dynamic regret result (Ahn et al., 2024: O(P^{1/3}T^{2/3})), OptFPRL provides the first data-dependent FTRL bound with √P_T dependence. The impossibility result of Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2022) showing standard FTRL fails for constant P_T is resolved by the pruning mechanism, which bounds ‖p_{1:t}‖ ≤ Rσ_{1:t−1} + ε_t rather than allowing linear growth in t.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FTRL framework (McMahan, 2017) and its optimistic variant (Mohri & Yang, 2016) to the dynamic regret setting, directly addressing the impossibility result of Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2022) that showed standard FTRL cannot achieve sublinear dynamic regret. The key departure from prior work is identifying that the bottleneck is not FTRL's projection style but the unbounded growth of its state vector, and resolving this through a simple pruning mechanism that selects subgradients from the normal cone. The analysis also incorporates AdaFTRL-style recursive regularization (Orabona & Pal, 2018) and builds on the optimistic dynamic regret framework of Jadbabaie et al. (2015) and the minimax-optimal bounds of Zhang et al. (2018), but achieves these results natively within FTRL rather than through OMD or meta-learning frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing open question about whether FTRL can achieve meaningful dynamic regret guarantees, and in doing so provides a principled interpolation between 'lazy' and 'agile' iterates through the pruning mechanism. The zero-regret-under-perfect-predictions property (all terms including P_T modulated by E_T) and the absence of cyclic dependency (unlike OMD-based optimistic dynamic regret) make this a cleaner theoretical tool. Open questions include extending the pruning mechanism to unbounded domains, delayed feedback, and memory-constrained settings where FTRL is often the framework of choice, as well as exploring more flexible pruning strategies that go beyond the binary prune/don't-prune rule analyzed here.
