## Problem

Generating ultra-long sequences (up to 100K tokens) with LLMs is prohibitively slow—taking nearly 5 hours for 100K tokens on LLaMA3.1-8B—yet existing speculative decoding methods (TriForce, MagicDec) are designed for short outputs (64–256 tokens) and fail when extended to 100K due to KV cache budget constraints. The paper identifies three compounding bottlenecks: frequent model reloading (I/O-bound), unbounded KV cache growth during drafting, and severe repetitive degeneration at extreme lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (2K–8K tokens from PG-19) and a frozen target LLM, and produces a lossless generated sequence of up to 100K tokens. Unusually, the 'draft model' is the target model itself operating with a partial KV cache (budget |B|) plus three lightweight linear layers, so no separate draft LLM is trained or loaded. Verification uses tree-based attention over a [1,3,3,3] candidate tree, and the final output is guaranteed identical to the target model's autoregressive sampling.

## Method

TokenSwift is a self-speculative decoding framework with three integrated mechanisms. (1) Multi-token self-drafting: three residual-connected linear layers (h_i = f_i(h_{i-1}) + h_{i-1}) appended to the target model's last hidden state produce 4 logits in one forward pass, reducing model reload frequency; token reutilization further retrieves top-k frequent n-grams from a running frequency table to serve as additional draft candidates. (2) Dynamic KV cache management: a fixed-budget partial cache preserves the first |S| KV pairs (attention sinks) and dynamically evicts/replaces the remaining |B|−|S| pairs based on QK^T importance scores (adapted for GQA by summing within query groups), recalculating importance when eviction reaches the sink boundary. (3) Contextual penalty: a windowed penalty (θ on the most recent W=1024 tokens) is applied during both drafting and verification to suppress repetition, combined with random selection among multiple valid n-gram branches to further enhance diversity. Only ~50M parameters (the three linear layers) are trained via supervised learning on 8K tokens of PG-19, while the target LLM remains frozen.

## Results

TOKENSWIFT achieves over 3× speedup over autoregressive (AR) generation at 100K tokens across all tested models and architectures. On LLaMA3.1-8b (GQA) with 4096-token prefix, it reaches 3.27× speedup with 0.84 acceptance rate; on YaRN-LLaMA2-7b-128k (MHA) it reaches 3.23× with 0.90 acceptance rate. The speedup grows with generation length, from ~2× at 20K to ~3.3–3.4× at 100K. On Qwen2.5-14B, TOKENSWIFT reduces 100K-token generation from 474.6 minutes (AR) to 142.2 minutes, saving 5.54 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOKENSWIFT decisively outperforms both baselines on speedup across all settings. Against Medusa* (retrained with TOKENSWIFT's verification), TOKENSWIFT achieves 3.23× vs. 0.99× on YaRN-LLaMA2-7b (MHA) and 3.27× vs. 1.45× on LLaMA3.1-8b (GQA) at 100K/prefix 4096 — Medusa* actually fails to accelerate on the MHA model at longer prefixes. Against TriForce* (improved with dynamic KV updates), TOKENSWIFT achieves 3.23× vs. 2.14× on MHA and 3.27× vs. 0.92× on GQA at the same setting; notably, TriForce* achieves higher raw acceptance rates (0.96–0.99) but its speedup collapses on GQA because it lacks repetition penalty and suffers from KV cache bloat. The gap widens with generation length: at 20K, TOKENSWIFT leads TriForce* by ~0.5×, but at 100K the lead grows to over 1× on GQA. TOKENSWIFT's advantage is attributed to token reutilization (n-gram retrieval) and dynamic KV cache management, which become increasingly effective as the generated sequence grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenSwift extends the speculative decoding paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) by combining Medusa-style multi-token drafting with TriForce-style KV cache compression, but departs from both by introducing dynamic KV cache updates (building on attention sinks, Xiao et al., 2024) and n-gram token reutilization to handle the fundamentally different regime of ultra-long (100K token) generation rather than the long-prefix/short-output setting that prior methods target. The contextual penalty mechanism is a windowed refinement of the penalized sampling idea from Keskar et al. (2019), adapted to the observation that in 100K-token sequences the generated vocabulary nearly saturates, making global penalties ineffective. The paper positions itself as the first framework to achieve lossless acceleration at 100K-token generation lengths, where existing methods like TriForce and MagicDec either fail or yield negligible speedup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing 100K-token generation from ~5 hours to ~90 minutes on LLaMA3.1-8B makes o1-style reasoning, agentic workflows, and long-form creative writing feasible in development and deployment settings. However, evaluation is confined to PG-19 (a book corpus), and the method requires training additional linear heads per model, limiting plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the speedup holds for multi-GPU inference of 400B+ models where computation (not memory) becomes the bottleneck, and whether the n-gram reutilization strategy generalizes to domains with low lexical repetition (e.g., code, mathematics). The trade-off between diversity (contextual penalty) and speedup also remains a tunable knob without a principled selection criterion.
