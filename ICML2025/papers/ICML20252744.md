## Problem

Existing analyses of adaptive optimization with structured preconditioners (AdaGrad, Shampoo, AdaGrad-Norm) are case-by-case, lacking a unified framework that explains when and why different preconditioner structures succeed. The prevailing assumption that less structured (more expensive) preconditioners like full-matrix AdaGrad inherently yield better convergence is unchallenged, and no general sufficient condition exists for the operator monotonicity property required to derive clean regret bounds for arbitrary preconditioner families.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a sequence of convex (or stochastic convex) loss functions, a convex parameter set X ⊆ R^d, and a matrix subalgebra K ⊆ M^d that defines the preconditioner cone H = K ∩ S_+^d. It produces parameter iterates via a mirror-descent-style update with the optimal preconditioner H_t = argmin_{H∈H} ⟨M_t, H^{-1}⟩ + η²Tr(H), along with a regret bound factored as ||X||_H · ||g_{1:T}||_H and, for smooth stochastic settings, a convergence rate involving H-smoothness. The subalgebra structure (scalar, diagonal, full-matrix, Kronecker-factored, or layerwise direct sums) is the key design knob that simultaneously controls both the domain diameter and the adaptive gradient norm.

## Method

The paper introduces the notion of well-structured preconditioner sets (H = K ∩ S_+^d for a matrix subalgebra K containing I_d), proving that this algebraic closure under addition and multiplication is a sufficient condition for the preconditioner map P_H(M) to be operator monotone, thereby enabling a single unified regret analysis for all such families. The resulting bound decomposes into a domain term ||X||_H and a gradient term ||g_{1:T}||_H, revealing an intrinsic trade-off: enlarging H (less structure) shrinks the gradient norm but inflates the domain diameter, so more structured preconditioners can yield strictly better bounds. Applying this to the Kronecker-factored subalgebra R^{d_L×d_L} ⊗ I_{d_R}, the paper derives a regret bound for one-sided Shampoo that is always no worse than two-sided Shampoo and can be smaller by a factor of d, and identifies a class of quadratic losses where one-sided Shampoo achieves O(1/T) convergence while full-matrix AdaGrad only achieves O(min(d_L,d_R)·d_R/T). Experiments on a linear regression problem with structured Hessian confirm that one-sided Shampoo outperforms full-matrix AdaGrad, diagonal AdaGrad, and AdaGrad-Norm in practice.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that one-sided Shampoo achieves a worst-case convergence rate of 1/T on a class of quadratic losses, strictly outperforming AdaGrad-Norm (min(d_L,d_R)/T), diagonal AdaGrad (d_L·d_R/T), and full-matrix AdaGrad (min(d_L,d_R)·d_R/T). The regret bound for one-sided Shampoo is also always no larger than that of two-sided Shampoo, and can be smaller by a multiplicative factor of min(d_L,d_R) in the worst case. In experiments on a linear regression problem with d=1000 parameters over 100 steps, one-sided Shampoo achieves the lowest last-iterate and average-iterate loss, while full-matrix AdaGrad performs the worst.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaGrad-Norm (Streeter & McMahan, 2010), one-sided Shampoo achieves a strictly better worst-case convergence rate of 1/T versus min(d_L,d_R)/T, and in experiments on the d=1000 linear regression problem, one-sided Shampoo reaches substantially lower loss after 100 steps. Against diagonal AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011), the gap is even larger: 1/T versus d_L·d_R/T in the worst case, and AdaGrad performs poorly in the experiments. Against full-matrix AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011), one-sided Shampoo is theoretically superior (1/T vs. min(d_L,d_R)·d_R/T) and empirically the worst performer, directly challenging the conventional wisdom that more adaptivity (larger preconditioner set) always helps. Against two-sided Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018), the paper proves that the one-sided variant's regret bound is always no larger and can be smaller by a factor of min(d_L,d_R); in experiments, one-sided Shampoo also outperforms the original two-sided Shampoo on both last-iterate and average-iterate loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AdaReg meta-algorithm of Gupta et al. (2017), which provided a unified *derivation* of adaptive optimizers (AdaGrad, AdaGrad-Norm, Shampoo) but only a case-by-case *analysis*. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'well-structured preconditioner sets' (intersection of a matrix subalgebra with the PSD cone) as a sufficient condition for operator monotonicity, thereby enabling a truly unified regret and convergence analysis across all these algorithms. It also builds on the original two-sided Shampoo analysis of Gupta et al. (2018) to derive a strictly improved bound for the one-sided variant, and positions against the broader literature on Kronecker-factored and structured preconditioners (K-FAC, CASPR, LARS/LAMB) that frame structured methods as approximations to full-matrix AdaGrad.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption in adaptive optimization that less structured (more expensive) preconditioners necessarily yield better convergence, showing instead that the trade-off between domain diameter and adaptive gradient norm can favor cheaper, more structured methods. The unified framework via matrix subalgebras provides a principled tool for designing new preconditioners with guaranteed analysis, and the compositional properties (direct sums, Kronecker products, rotations) suggest a systematic path toward layerwise or blockwise optimizers. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single linear regression setup with a specific Hessian structure, and the non-convex deep learning regime—where these optimizers are most relevant—remains unaddressed. Open questions include identifying which subalgebras are most useful for real neural network training and extending the analysis to non-convex or non-smooth settings.
