## Problem

Pixel-space diffusion models are computationally expensive and difficult to scale to high resolutions, typically requiring an auxiliary VAE (e.g., SD-VAE trained on 9M images) to reduce dimensionality. The paper argues that pixel space contains inherent redundancy and that a near-lossless frequency-domain representation could provide a more compact space for diffusion modeling, enabling high-resolution generation without any VAE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes isotropic Gaussian noise in the DCT coefficient space (optionally conditioned on a class label for ImageNet 64) and produces a full RGB image via inverse DCT after the reverse diffusion process. Images are processed at resolutions from 32×32 to 512×512, with the DCT block size B and number of eliminated high-frequency coefficients m* varying by resolution (e.g., B=8, m*=46 for 512×512). Each Transformer token packs 4 Y-blocks, 1 Cb-block, and 1 Cr-block (6(B²−m*) coefficients total), with coefficients ordered low-to-high frequency via zigzag scanning.

## Method

DCTdiff replaces pixel-space diffusion with diffusion over DCT frequency coefficients: images are converted to YCbCr, chroma-subsampled 2×, split into B×B blocks, transformed by 2D type-II DCT, and tokenized for a ViT-based diffusion model (UViT or DiT). Three key design factors make this effective: (1) Entropy-Consistent Scaling, which rescales all coefficients by the DC-component bound of Y blocks to preserve distribution shape; (2) SNR Scaling, which adjusts the noise schedule to counteract the faster forward perturbation caused by DCT's energy concentration in low frequencies; and (3) Entropy-Based Frequency Reweighting, which prioritizes modeling of high-entropy low-frequency coefficients. The paper further proves that image diffusion is equivalent to spectral autoregression (Theorem 5.1) and derives a DCT upsampling theorem (Theorem 5.2) that outperforms bilinear/bicubic interpolation. This enables seamless scaling to 512×512 without a VAE, matching or beating SD-VAE-based latent diffusion at 1/4 the training cost.

## Results

DCTdiff consistently outperforms pixel-based UViT across all datasets and NFEs. At NFE=100 with DDIM, DCTdiff achieves FID-50k of 5.02 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 6.23 UViT), 8.69 on ImageNet 64 (vs. 10.65), and 8.22 on FFHQ 128 (vs. 13.87). At 512×512 resolution without any VAE, DCTdiff reaches FID 7.07 on FFHQ 512 and 8.76 on AFHQ 512, beating UViT(latent) with SD-VAE (10.89 and 10.86 respectively) while using only 1/4 the training cost (133 vs. 575 GFLOPs). Training convergence is up to 2.5× faster than pixel-based UViT (e.g., 300k vs. 750k steps on FFHQ 128).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-based UViT, DCTdiff wins on every dataset at every NFE with both DDIM and DPM-Solver solvers, with the largest gains at low NFE (e.g., FID 6.81 vs. 21.48 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=20 with DDIM) and on higher-resolution FFHQ 128 (FID 8.22 vs. 13.87 at NFE=100). The only exception is CelebA 64 with DPM-Solver, where UViT slightly edges out DCTdiff (1.57 vs. 1.71 at NFE=100), which the authors attribute to UViT's default training parameters being particularly well-suited to that dataset. Against UViT(latent) using SD-VAE at 512×512, DCTdiff achieves substantially better FID (7.07 vs. 10.89 on FFHQ 512; 8.76 vs. 10.86 on AFHQ 512 at NFE=100) while requiring only 133 GFLOPs per forward pass versus 575 for UViT(latent), i.e., roughly 1/4 the training cost. Against pixel-based DiT, DCTdiff also wins on both CelebA 64 (FID 3.84 vs. 5.11) and FFHQ 128 (FID 11.16 vs. 12.81) at NFE=100 with the DDPM sampler.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the UViT and DiT Transformer architectures for diffusion modeling but fundamentally departs from pixel-space diffusion by performing the entire generative process in the DCT frequency domain, inspired by JPEG compression. It extends the line of frequency-domain image modeling (DCTransformer, Fourier Image Transformer, wavelet-based tokenizers) by showing that DCT is not merely an auxiliary module but a viable primary representation for end-to-end diffusion. The theoretical contribution bridges diffusion and autoregressive paradigms by proving that forward diffusion in pixel space is equivalent to spectral autoregression, connecting to VAR's coarse-to-fine generation and Dieleman's empirical observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DCTdiff enables 512×512 image generation without a VAE at roughly 1/4 the training cost of latent diffusion, removing a major engineering bottleneck (training a 9M-image VAE with compound losses). The frequency prioritization property opens a design axis for both generative and discriminative tasks, and the DCT upsampling theorem offers a principled alternative to interpolation-based super-resolution. Open questions include scaling to 1024×1024, designing frequency-oriented Transformer architectures, and extending the approach to video where temporal redundancy may further benefit from frequency-space modeling.
