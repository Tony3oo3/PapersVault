## Problem

Fully amortized policy-based Bayesian experimental design methods (e.g., DAD) train a design policy once offline and deploy it as a fixed network, leaving no mechanism to integrate real experimental data during deployment. This rigidity causes suboptimal design decisions when observed data deviates from the simulated training histories, particularly under model misspecification or prior mismatch, and the high-dimensional history space makes it increasingly difficult for a single pre-trained policy to cover all possible experimental trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-length experimental history of design-outcome pairs (ξ_i, y_i), a generative model p(θ)p(y|θ,ξ), and posterior samples from p(θ|h_τ) obtained via importance sampling or other inference schemes. It produces the next design ξ_t at each step and, at refinement points, an updated step policy π_τ fine-tuned to maximize the remaining EIG. The policy architecture uses a permutation-invariant encoder that sums per-pair embeddings into a fixed-dimensional summary vector, which a decoder then maps to the next design, handling variable history lengths without retraining.

## Method

Step-DAD introduces a semi-amortized PB-BED framework built on a key decomposition (Proposition 3.1) showing that total EIG splits into EIG accumulated up to an intermediate step τ plus the expected remaining EIG conditional on the history h_τ, making the optimal policy for steps τ+1 to T independent of the earlier policy. At each refinement point, Step-DAD executes an 'infer-refine' procedure: it fits the posterior p(θ|h_τ) using standard inference (e.g., importance sampling), then fine-tunes the existing DAD policy via stochastic gradient ascent on the sPCE lower bound of the remaining EIG, treating the posterior as an updated prior. Rather than training a new policy from scratch, it leverages the fully amortized π_0 as initialization and performs lightweight fine-tuning (e.g., 2.5K steps), preserving most of the offline training investment. The approach extends naturally to multiple refinement steps via a refinement schedule {τ_0, τ_1, …, τ_K}, trading off deployment-time computation against design quality with diminishing returns.

## Results

Step-DAD consistently outperforms the fully amortized DAD baseline across all three experimental domains, with total EIG improvements of ~10% on source location finding (7.759 vs 7.040), ~40% on hyperbolic temporal discounting (6.711 vs 4.778), and ~36% on the CES model (13.879 vs 10.181). Notably, Step-DAD with a reduced 10K-step pre-training budget matches or exceeds DAD trained for the full 50K steps, achieving better results with nearly 5× fewer total training steps. The method also demonstrates robustness to prior perturbations where DAD's EIG drops to near zero, while Step-DAD maintains positive information gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Step-DAD outperforms DAD (Foster et al., 2021) across all three experiments, with the largest relative gain on hyperbolic temporal discounting (6.711 vs 4.778 EIG, ~40% improvement). Against the psychology-derived baselines on the discounting task, Step-DAD substantially exceeds Kirby (2009) (6.711 vs 1.861) and Frye et al. (2016) (6.711 vs 3.500), as well as the problem-specific Greedy/BADapted approach of Vincent and Rainforth (2017) (6.711 vs 4.454). On the CES model, Step-DAD (13.879) also surpasses the traditional Greedy(vPCE) baseline from Foster et al. (2019) (13.333), while DAD itself underperforms even the Step-Static baseline (10.181 vs 13.010) due to training difficulties from the censored likelihood. The consistent pattern is that semi-amortized refinement at mid-experiment (τ ≈ T/2) yields the largest gains, as sufficient data has accumulated to inform the posterior while enough steps remain to exploit the refined policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully amortized policy-based Bayesian experimental design (PB-BED) framework introduced by DAD (Foster et al., 2021) by introducing a semi-amortized variant that periodically refines the design policy during the experiment using an 'infer-refine' procedure. The theoretical foundation rests on a decomposition of total EIG (Proposition 3.1) showing that the optimal policy for remaining steps depends only on the current history, enabling targeted mid-experiment updates. Conceptually, Step-DAD bridges the gap between traditional adaptive BED (which performs greedy per-step posterior inference and EIG optimization) and fully amortized PB-BED (which fixes the policy after offline training), retaining the non-myopic policy-based formulation while adding test-time adaptability. The method is compatible with existing EIG estimators (sPCE, InfoNCE, NWJ) and policy architectures (DeepSets, transformers) from the DAD/iDAD lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Step-DAD addresses a practical vulnerability of fully amortized PB-BED: when real experimental data deviates from the simulated training distribution (due to model misspecification or prior mismatch), the fixed policy has no mechanism to recover, as demonstrated by DAD's EIG collapsing to zero under prior perturbation. The finding that Step-DAD with 10K pretraining steps plus 2.5K finetuning matches or exceeds DAD trained for 50K steps suggests substantial compute savings for practitioners. Open questions include how to optimally choose the refinement schedule (number and timing of updates), whether the approach scales to very long experimental horizons where history dimensionality grows, and how to handle settings where even the limited test-time compute budget is unavailable. The CES experiment, where DAD underperforms the simpler Step-Static baseline due to censored-likelihood discontinuities, also raises questions about when policy-based approaches are preferable to simpler two-stage static designs.
