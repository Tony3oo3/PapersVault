## Problem

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) have become a leading tool for extracting interpretable concepts from vision models, but the paper reveals a fundamental instability: identical SAEs trained on the same data with different random seeds produce sharply different dictionaries, with only ~50% concept overlap. This undermines SAEs' reliability as an interpretability protocol, since the 'concepts' discovered may be arbitrary artifacts of initialization rather than stable properties of the model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes element-wise standardized activations from pretrained vision models (DINOv2, SigLIP, ViT, ConvNeXt, ResNet50) computed over ImageNet (~1.28M images, yielding 60M–250M tokens per epoch depending on architecture). It produces an overcomplete sparse dictionary (5× the feature dimension, e.g., 3840 atoms for DINOv2's 768-d features) and corresponding sparse codes. The dictionary is a plug-and-play decoder parameterization that slots into any existing SAE architecture (TopK, JumpReLU, vanilla), so the encoder and sparsity mechanism remain unchanged.

## Method

The core idea is to constrain the SAE decoder (dictionary) to the convex hull of the data, drawing on Archetypal Analysis (Cutler & Breiman, 1994). Concretely, the dictionary is parameterized as D = WC where W is a row-stochastic matrix (each row a probability distribution over data points) and C is a compact set of K-Means centroids distilled from the full activation matrix, making the optimization tractable at scale. Each dictionary atom is thus a convex combination of real data points, geometrically anchoring concepts to the data manifold and preventing out-of-distribution directions. A relaxed variant (RA-SAE) adds a small deviation term Λ with norm constraint ||Λ||² ≤ δ, permitting mild excursions beyond the convex hull to match standard SAE reconstruction quality while preserving stability. The paper further introduces two evaluation benchmarks — a Plausibility score measuring alignment with the classifier's true decision directions, and a Soft Identifiability benchmark testing whether the SAE disentangles synthetic object mixtures — to rigorously assess dictionary quality beyond reconstruction error.

## Results

On DINOv2 with a 90%-sparse, 2000-concept overcomplete dictionary, RA-SAE achieves R²=89.34 (nearly matching TopK SAE's 89.52) while dramatically improving stability from 0.542 to 0.927 and reducing the OOD score from 0.551 to 0.060. On the Plausibility Benchmark, RA-SAE (δ=0.01) recovers classification directions with scores of 0.4045 (ConvNeXt), 0.6136 (ResNet), and 0.3821 (ViT) at 32k dictionary size, roughly doubling the TopK SAE baseline (0.1684, 0.3125, 0.2939 respectively). On the Soft Identifiability Benchmark, RA-SAE achieves 0.94–0.96 average accuracy across DINOv2, ResNet, SigLIP, and ViT, outperforming all SAE baselines (0.80–0.83) and classical methods (0.76–0.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TopK SAE baseline (Gao et al., 2025), RA-SAE matches reconstruction fidelity (R² 89.34 vs. 89.52) while improving stability by 71% (0.927 vs. 0.542), reducing OOD score by 89% (0.060 vs. 0.551), and cutting negative interference from 135.7 to 0.012. The JumpReLU SAE (Rajamanoharan et al., 2024) shows the same pattern: RA-SAE trades a negligible 0.58 R² points for a 72% stability gain and 89% OOD reduction. The Vanilla SAE (Bricken et al., 2023) is outperformed on every metric except dead codes, with RA-SAE achieving 5.4 points higher R² and 30% better stability. Against classical methods, RA-SAE dominates on reconstruction (89.34 vs. 67.43 for Semi-NMF, 55.48 for Convex-NMF) while matching their stability (0.927 vs. 0.925/0.933) and improving OOD (0.060 vs. 0.430/0.087). On the Soft Identifiability Benchmark, RA-SAE (0.94–0.96) substantially outperforms K-Means (0.76–0.77), Sparse PCA (0.80–0.83), and all SAE variants (0.80–0.83), confirming superior disentanglement of ground-truth generative factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct lineages: the Archetypal Analysis framework of Cutler & Breiman (1994), which constrains dictionary atoms to the convex hull of data, and modern Sparse Autoencoders (TopK SAE, JumpReLU SAE) that have become the dominant scalable approach to concept extraction in vision models. The key conceptual move is repurposing the archetypal convex-hull constraint as a plug-and-play decoder parameterization for SAEs, rather than jointly optimizing codes and atoms as in classical AA. A mild relaxation term (RA-SAE) and a K-Means-based distillation of the data matrix address the scalability gap that made full archetypal analysis intractable at the scale of modern vision model activations (250M+ tokens per epoch).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instability finding (cosine stability ~0.5 for standard SAEs) is a significant reliability concern for the interpretability community: if re-running training on the same model and data yields largely different concept dictionaries, downstream analyses built on those concepts are not reproducible. The convex-hull fix is simple, well-motivated geometrically, and empirically restores stability to near-classical-method levels (~0.93) without sacrificing reconstruction quality, making it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the K-Means distillation step is optimal, how the relaxation parameter δ should be chosen in practice, and whether the approach generalizes to language model interpretability where SAEs are increasingly deployed. The plausibility and soft-identifiability benchmarks introduced here also fill a gap in the evaluation methodology for dictionary learning methods.
