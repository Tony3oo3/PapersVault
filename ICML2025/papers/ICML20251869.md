## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are restricted in scope: entropy-based objectives only support classification, alignment-based methods require access to source data, and consistency-based methods rely on heavy geometric augmentations (random cropping, resizing) that destroy object locations and sizes, making them infeasible for fine-grained tasks like 3D object detection and semantic segmentation. The paper seeks a single, fully TTA framework that is simultaneously task-agnostic (classification and regression), architecture-agnostic (CNN and transformer), and applicable across image-, object-, and pixel-level predictions without altering the original training process or requiring source data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB test image in an online streaming fashion and produces task-specific predictions: class labels (via KL-divergence-aligned logits), pixel-level segmentation masks, or 3D bounding boxes (coordinates, dimensions, depths, and angles via L1-aligned regression heads). Only norm-layer affine parameters (and a lightweight projector for classification) are updated per sample, keeping the adaptation overhead to one additional forward-backward pass per image. For dense prediction tasks, the confidence-aware selection operates at the pixel level, averaging the similarity loss only over selected pixels where the strong view is more confident.

## Method

SPA performs active weak-to-strong self-bootstrapping: the original image (strong view) provides the supervision target, and the model is optimized to make consistent predictions on a deteriorated view (weak view), with gradients stopped on the strong-view prediction to enforce unidirectional learning. The key technical contribution is a pair of geometry-preserving augmentations derived from Fourier-domain analysis of how domain shifts affect radially averaged power spectral density (RAPSD): (1) random low-frequency amplitude masking, which exploits the high RAPSD at low frequencies to create large information-power differences and thus rich learning signals while preserving object geometry, and (2) Gaussian noise injection, which enhances high-frequency RAPSD to supply learning signals where amplitude masking would be ineffective. An active selection mechanism gates optimization to only those samples (or pixels) where the strong view's prediction confidence exceeds the weak view's, filtering unreliable supervisions. The two augmentations are applied separately to generate two distinct weak views, and the method is plug-and-play, combining additively with entropy minimization or feature-statistics alignment objectives.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5) with ViT-Base, SPA achieves 70.1% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline CMF (69.2%) and the strongest entropy-based method EATA (67.8%) by 0.9% and 2.3% respectively. On 3D monocular detection (KITTI-C, MonoFlex), SPA achieves 33.8/11.9/9.5 AP₃D|R40 for Car/Pedestrian/Cyclist, surpassing MonoTTA (32.4/9.5/8.9) by up to 2.4% on Pedestrian. On segmentation (Cityscapes→ACDC, Segformer-B5), SPA achieves 59.2% mIoU in continual TTA, beating CoTTA (58.5%) with only 2 augmentations vs. CoTTA's 29, and reaches 60.7% when combined with CoTTA as a plug-and-play module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA outperforms all 16 compared baselines on ImageNet-C, achieving 70.1% average accuracy vs. CMF (69.2%), ROID (68.0%), and EATA (67.8%), the three strongest baselines. Against entropy-based methods (TENT 59.6%, EATA 67.8%, SAR 62.7%, DeYO 66.0%), SPA gains 2.3% over the best (EATA) while also supporting regression tasks they cannot handle. Compared to the consistency-based CoTTA (61.7%), SPA's geometry-preserving Fourier augmentations yield a substantial 8.4% improvement, demonstrating that structure-preserving deteriorations provide far richer learning signals than random cropping/resizing. Against ActMAD (66.3%), SPA achieves 3.8% higher accuracy without requiring access to source data statistics. On 3D monocular detection, SPA surpasses MonoTTA (the task-specific SOTA) by 1.4% on Car and 2.4% on Pedestrian AP₃D|R40, benefiting from its ability to align both classification and regression heads. On segmentation, SPA alone (59.2% mIoU) beats CoTTA (58.5%) with only 2 augmentations vs. 29, and as a plug-and-play module (SPA+CoTTA) pushes mIoU to 60.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPA extends the self-bootstrapping paradigm from BYOL/DINO into a test-time adaptation framework, but departs from prior consistency-based TTA methods (MEMO, CoTTA) in two key ways: it uses unidirectional weak-to-strong learning rather than bidirectional consistency between two augmented views, and it replaces heavy geometric augmentations (random crop, resize) with Fourier-domain operations (low-frequency amplitude masking and high-frequency noise injection) that preserve spatial structure. This design is motivated by a novel RAPSD analysis showing that domain shifts manifest differently across spatial frequencies, justifying frequency-specific augmentation choices. Unlike entropy-based TTA (TENT, EATA, SAR) which is restricted to classification, or alignment-based methods (ActMAD) which require source data, SPA is task-agnostic and source-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA provides a practical, plug-and-play TTA module that unifies adaptation across classification, segmentation, and 3D detection without requiring source data or architecture-specific modifications, addressing a real deployment concern where diverse tasks coexist. The Fourier-domain analysis offers a principled (if empirical) justification for augmentation design that could inform future TTA and self-supervised learning work. A notable limitation is that SPA's gains on CLIP cross-dataset generalization are modest compared to OOD robustness, since the text encoder remains unadapted; extending the framework to jointly adapt vision-language models is an open direction. The method also requires careful tuning of noise injection γ for dense prediction tasks, limiting its fully hyperparameter-free appeal.
