## Problem

Gradients backpropagated through the softmax operation in Transformer attention are scaled by the softmax Jacobian, which is proportional to attention probabilities. In large language models, ~80% of attention probabilities fall below 10⁻³ and ~20% below 10⁻⁷, causing severe gradient saturation that leads to inefficient learning of parameters preceding attention layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250572/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LASER operates on the same inputs and produces the same outputs as standard dot-product attention: text sequences (up to 1024 tokens), image patches (ViT-S/16 on ImageNet-1k), or speech audio (Conformer on Librispeech), yielding next-token logits, class labels, or transcriptions respectively. The only modification is internal to the attention computation—the value matrix V is exponentiated before the weighted sum and the result is log-transformed—so the external interface, causal masking, and multi-head structure remain unchanged.

## Method

LASER (LogArithm of Summed Exponentials of Representations) replaces standard attention softmax(QKᵀ)V with log(softmax(QKᵀ)exp(V)), which admits a log-sum-exp structure. The paper analytically shows that the LASER Jacobian in the limiting case (exp(v₁)≫exp(v₂)) approaches (1−α) rather than α(1−α) as in standard attention, yielding substantially larger gradient signals when attention probabilities are small. To avoid numerical overflow from exp(V), a log-weighted-sum-exp trick subtracts the column-wise maximum m from V before exponentiation and adds m back after the log, requiring only input/output modifications to the attention function without altering the underlying implementation (e.g., FlashAttention). The approach is validated across decoder-only LLMs (234M–7.7B params), BERT (2.2B), ViT-S/16, and Conformer, showing up to 1.7% relative test-loss improvement in pretraining and 1.44% average downstream accuracy gain.

## Results

On a 7.7B parameter autoregressive language model trained on 44B tokens, LASER achieves an average downstream accuracy of 53.97% versus 52.53% for standard attention (1.44% absolute improvement), with the largest gains on BoolQ (+6.0%) and RTE (+3.61%). On the 2.2B model, LASER achieves 63.39% average one-shot accuracy versus 62.34% for standard attention, and 44.01% versus 42.35% on SuperGLUE finetuning (1.65% improvement). For pretraining test loss on C4, LASER achieves 2.595 vs. 2.641 on the 301M model (1.74% relative improvement), and LASER reaches a target loss of 2.347 with 15.65% fewer parameters per the scaling law fit. Cross-modal results include 1.15% absolute error reduction on ViT-S/16 ImageNet (24.17% vs. 25.32%) and 0.2% WER reduction on Conformer/Librispeech.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard dot-product attention (Vaswani et al., 2017), LASER consistently wins: 1.74% relative test-loss improvement on the 301M C4 pretraining, 1.05% average accuracy gain on 2.2B one-shot evaluation, 1.44% on the 7.7B model, and 1.65% on SuperGLUE finetuning. Temperature-tuned standard attention (Standard+temp) narrows the gap slightly (62.49% vs. 63.39% average) but does not close it, and per-dimension temperature scaling for standard attention (62.56%) still trails LASER (63.39%). Against QK-Normalization (Dehghani et al., 2023), LASER+QK-Norm achieves 23.72% validation error on ViT-S/16 ImageNet versus 24.55% for Standard+QK-Norm, and 0.0808 vs. 0.0829 validation WER on Conformer/Librispeech, confirming the improvement is orthogonal to normalization. Against the Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2024), Diff+LASER achieves 51.52% average downstream accuracy versus 50.59% for DiffTransformer alone (~1% improvement), with the largest gains on MultiRC (+2.31%) and RaceM (+1.67%), indicating LASER's gradient-flow benefit composes with other attention modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Transformer attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) by identifying a gradient bottleneck in the softmax Jacobian and proposing LASER, which applies attention in exponential value space (exp(V)) with a log output. It is conceptually distinct from efficiency-focused attention variants (Linear Attention, Performer, FlashAttention) and from distribution-sharpness work (Veličković et al., 2024; QK-Normalization in Dehghani et al., 2023), which address expressiveness or training instability rather than gradient flow. The numerical stabilization via the log-weighted-sum-exp trick draws on the classical log-sum-exp trick (Blanchard et al., 2019), and the paper further demonstrates compatibility with the Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2024) by applying LASER to each of its two attention terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~80% of attention probabilities in LLMs are below 10⁻³, causing the softmax Jacobian to suppress backpropagated gradients, reframes a long-standing vanishing-gradient concern as a specific, addressable bottleneck in the attention layer itself. The practical significance is that LASER requires only input/output transformations (exp on V, log on output) without modifying the underlying attention kernel, making it drop-in compatible with optimized implementations like FlashAttention. Open questions include whether the theoretical advantage (derived in a limiting case where exp(v₁) ≫ exp(v₂)) holds uniformly across all attention patterns, whether gains compound at scales beyond 7.7B parameters, and how LASER interacts with emerging attention variants (e.g., sliding-window, grouped-query). The modest 1–2% improvements, while consistent across text, vision, and speech, leave room for whether the gradient-flow argument fully explains the observed gains or whether other architectural effects contribute.
