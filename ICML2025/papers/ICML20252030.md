## Problem

Robust active task sampling (RATS) enables efficient risk-averse policy training by using a risk predictive model to amortize expensive policy evaluation, but the state-of-the-art method MPTS suffers from a concentration issue that collapses performance when enlarging the candidate batch, requires careful UCB hyperparameter tuning, and lacks a versatile theoretical framework. This paper addresses these gaps by providing an infinitely-many-armed bandit (i-MAB) formulation, diagnosing the concentration problem theoretically, and proposing a simpler acquisition principle that avoids over-exploitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional task identifiers (e.g., mass, velocity, target position vectors in R^d) and, in few-shot Meta-RL, K support rollouts per task. The risk predictive model amortizes evaluation by predicting adaptation risk (negative average rollout returns) for any candidate task via a single stochastic forward pass, enabling screening of up to 64B candidates at negligible cost relative to environment interaction. The output is a zero-shot adapted policy (DR) or a meta-initialized policy requiring few gradient steps for fast adaptation (Meta-RL), evaluated via CVaR_α across validation and OOD task distributions.

## Method

PDTS frames the RATS pipeline as a task-selection MDP and constructs an infinitely-many-armed bandit where each arm is a feasible task subset, establishing MPTS as a UCB-guided special case. The key practical innovation replaces MPTS's multi-pass UCB acquisition with posterior sampling (one stochastic forward pass per candidate task) combined with a diversity regularization term that penalizes pairwise identifier distances within the selected subset, solving a max-sum diversification problem via approximate submodular algorithms. This synergy between stochastic optimism and diversity coverage prevents the Top-B concentration issue (formally proven in Proposition 3.3) and enables enlarging the candidate batch to 64B without performance collapse, achieving nearly worst-case optimization (Proposition 3.4). The risk predictive model itself is a variational autoencoder with a latent variable summarizing optimization history, trained via streaming variational inference with a β-VAE objective, and reused unchanged from MPTS.

## Results

On Meta-RL benchmarks (Reacher, Walker2d, HalfCheetah), PDTS outperforms all baselines by more than 15% in CVaR_0.9 return on all tasks except HalfCheetahMassVel. On physical robotics DR, PDTS exceeds ERM by over 8% in CVaR_0.9 across all benchmarks, reaching up to 73% improvement on LunarLander, while also achieving 2.4× training acceleration on Pusher and 1.3× on LunarLander. On the PEARL backbone (Table 2), PDTS achieves 993±26 CVaR_0.95 return on HalfCheetahBody and 1296±41 on HalfCheetahMass, surpassing MPTS (945±26, 1209±45), RoML (855±35, 1197±59), and vanilla PEARL (847±42, 1118±51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDTS consistently outperforms ERM in adaptation robustness (CVaR_0.9) while matching its average return, with gaps up to 73% on LunarLander. Against DRM and GDRM, PDTS dominates in both robustness and average performance, as these methods suffer from limited exploration capacity under fixed batch sizes. Compared to MPTS (the prior SOTA RATS method), PDTS avoids the concentration-induced performance collapse that MPTS exhibits at B_hat=8B, and PDTS's advantage grows with larger candidate sets (B_hat=64B). On the PEARL backbone, PDTS beats RoML by ~14% and vanilla PEARL by ~17% in CVaR_0.95 return on HalfCheetahBody, confirming its plug-and-play effectiveness across meta-learning backbones. In OOD zero-shot evaluation on LunarLander, PDTS shows the smallest performance degradation, particularly in the most challenging OOD range τ∈[1.0, 4.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MPTS (Wang et al., 2025), which introduced the robust active task sampling (RATS) paradigm and its risk predictive model for amortized task difficulty evaluation. The key departures are twofold: theoretically, the authors abstract RATS as a task-selection MDP and construct an infinitely-many-armed bandit (i-MAB) as a versatile analytical tool, showing MPTS is a special UCB-guided solution; practically, they replace MPTS's UCB acquisition with posterior sampling (Russo & Van Roy, 2014) and add diversity regularization (Borodin et al., 2017) to fix a concentration pathology that MPTS exhibits at larger candidate pool sizes. The risk predictive model architecture (a neural-process-style encoder-decoder with variational inference) is reused unchanged from MPTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The i-MAB formulation provides a reusable theoretical scaffold for analyzing and designing future RATS methods beyond PDTS and MPTS, and the concentration diagnosis (Proposition 3.3) explains a previously unexplained failure mode that limits the scalability of active task sampling. The simultaneous improvement in robustness and training speed (up to 2.4× acceleration) suggests that exploration-aware task selection can be a free lunch rather than a robustness-efficiency tradeoff. Limitations include reliance on the risk predictive model's accuracy and the smoothness assumption on adaptation risk, and open questions include whether more expressive surrogate models or stronger robust optimization techniques within the i-MAB framework can further close the gap to true worst-case guarantees.
