## Problem

Existing agnostic-to-realizable reductions for PAC learning either fail to preserve the representation of the output function (David et al., 2016's compression-based approach produces functions outside the hypothesis class) or do not extend to multiclass PAC learning with unbounded label spaces (Hopkins et al., 2022). This paper resolves the open question posed by Hopkins et al. by providing a simple, representation-preserving reduction that works in the unbounded-label multiclass setting and more generally in a new Unified PAC Learning framework.

## Input / Output

The reduction takes as input a realizable PAC learner A (mapping labeled samples to predictors in H), a concept class C, a hypothesis class H, and a labeled sample S of instance-output pairs. It splits S into a training portion V and a validation portion T, runs A on every subset of V to produce a finite hypothesis set H_V ⊆ H, and returns the member of H_V with minimum empirical risk on T. The critical output constraint is that the returned predictor is always an element of H (the same space the realizable learner outputs), preserving data-dependent properties such as geometric margin, rather than merely belonging to some larger function class.

## Method

The core algorithm is a three-line agnostic-to-realizable reduction: (1) partition the sample S into V and T; (2) run the realizable learner A on all subsets of V to form the finite collection H_V; (3) return the hypothesis in H_V with lowest empirical error on T. The key insight is that H_V is guaranteed to contain a hypothesis within ε/2 of the optimal concept's risk (by applying the realizable learner's guarantee to the realizable subset of V, whose size is controlled via a Chernoff bound over the unknown fraction μ* of realizable points), and then a standard uniform concentration bound over the finite set H_V on T converts this into the full agnostic guarantee. The representation-preserving property follows directly because every element of H_V is an output of A and hence lies in H. The resulting sample complexity is Õ((d^{3/2} + log(1/δ))/ε³) for multiclass learning with DS dimension d, and the framework extends to list learning, multilabel learning, partial concept classes, and Massart/Tsybakov noise models with improved rates.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 4.1) establishes that if a Unified PAC Learning instance is realizable PAC learnable with sample complexity M^RE(ε,δ), then it is agnostic PAC learnable with sample complexity O(1/ε² · (max_{μ∈[ε/2,1]} M^RE(ε/(2μ),δ/3)/μ + log(1/δ))). For multiclass learning with finite DS dimension d, this yields an agnostic sample complexity of Õ((d^{3/2} + log(1/δ))/ε³) (Corollary 4.3). Under Massart noise (Theorem 5.3), the bound improves to Õ((d^{3/2} + log(1/δ))/(Δε²)), and under Tsybakov noise (Theorem 5.5) to Õ(a(d^{3/2} + log(1/δ))/ε^{3−α}). A lower bound (Theorem 6.1) shows an Ω(1/ε²) gap between optimal realizable and agnostic sample complexity is unavoidable for representation-preserving reductions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the compression-based reduction of David et al. (2016), which achieves a 1/ε² dependence in the agnostic setting but does not preserve the representation of the output hypothesis (i.e., the output may not belong to the hypothesis class H). This paper's Algorithm 1 is representation-preserving but incurs an additional 1/ε factor, yielding 1/ε³ for multiclass learning. The lower bound in Theorem 6.1 demonstrates that this 1/ε gap is sometimes unavoidable: for a multiclass instance with m constant functions over n points, the realizable sample complexity is O(1) while the agnostic sample complexity is Ω(1/ε²), confirming the extra factor cannot be eliminated in general. Compared to Hopkins et al. (2022), whose three-line algorithm handles binary and bounded-label settings, this paper's modification extends the reduction to unbounded label spaces (multiclass, list, and multilabel learning), resolving their open question. Under noise models (Massart and Tsybakov), the representation-preserving reduction achieves the same 1/ε² or 1/ε^{3−α} dependence as the compression approach, showing the 1/ε penalty is specific to the fully agnostic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agnostic-to-realizable reduction program initiated by Hopkins et al. (2022), whose three-line algorithm provably fails for unbounded-label multiclass learning (demonstrated via the 'stars and sets' counterexample in Appendix A). It also departs from the compression-based approach of David et al. (2016), which was the only prior tool for agnostic learnability in unbounded-label settings but does not preserve the representation of the realizable learner's output. The key technical contribution is a simple modification—running the realizable learner on all subsets of a validation split followed by ERM over the resulting finite hypothesis set—that overcomes the failure mode of Hopkins et al. while maintaining representation preservation. The framework is further grounded in the DS dimension characterization of Brukhim et al. (2022) and the list learnability characterization of Charikar & Pabbaraju (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first representation-preserving agnostic-to-realizable reduction for multiclass PAC learning with unbounded label spaces, resolving an explicit open question from Hopkins et al. (2022). The 'Unified PAC Learning' abstraction yields new equivalences (e.g., realizable and agnostic multilabel learnability) and provides a template for future reductions. The lower bound (Theorem 6.1) showing an Ω(1/ε²) gap between realizable and agnostic sample complexity for representation-preserving learners clarifies the cost of this property. A key open question remains whether proper sample compression schemes of sublinear size exist, which would close the gap between the 1/ε³ rate of this paper and the 1/ε² rate achievable via compression.
