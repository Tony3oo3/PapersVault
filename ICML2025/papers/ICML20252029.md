## Problem

MLM-based non-autoregressive translation models (CMLM, GLAT, DAT) introduce a training-inference gap: during training they condition on sampled target tokens, but at inference they must produce the full translation from source alone. Simply annealing the sampling rate to zero during training causes significant performance degradation, meaning NATs cannot fully realize their potential under their own inference conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source sentence X as input and produces a complete target translation Y in a single forward pass. During inference, the decoder receives the source encoder hidden states plus quantized representations from the Semantic Quantization Space (SQS) as conditioning signals—no target tokens are provided. A length predictor determines the output length m, and the Softcopy mechanism initializes m decoder positions from the source representations before parallel token generation.

## Method

AEQA-NAT introduces a pre-aligned Semantic Quantization Space (SQS), a K×D codebook (K=2048) initialized via mBART's multilingual encoder and updated with exponential moving averages, which serves as a shared discrete representation space for both source and target. The key mechanism replaces target tokens in the Glancing Sampling Strategy with quantized SQS vectors, so the model never requires explicit target information during training, eliminating the training-inference gap. Training optimizes three losses jointly: a translation likelihood loss L_NAT over the full target sequence, a semantic quantization alignment loss L_SQA that enforces consistency between encoder and decoder outputs in SQS space (via commitment and cross-lingual alignment terms), and a length prediction loss. An Aligned Reordering (AR) step uses a softmax alignment matrix between quantized representations and ground-truth tokens to reorder decoder inputs into the correct target syntactic order before generation.

## Results

On WMT14 EN→DE (raw data), AEQA-NAT with Aligned Reordering achieves 26.82 BLEU at 17.0× speedup, outperforming DAT (26.57 BLEU, 13.9×) and GLAT (18.94 BLEU, 14.2×). The AEQA-DAT variant (extending AEQA to the directed acyclic architecture) reaches 27.03 BLEU on EN→DE and 33.89 BLEU on RO→EN (KD), surpassing the autoregressive Transformer baseline (27.37/34.00) by less than 1 BLEU while maintaining 9.1× speedup. The performance gap between raw-data and knowledge-distilled training is reduced to just 0.29 BLEU for AEQA-NAT, compared to 0.81 for DAT and 7.24 for Vanilla NAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEQA-NAT with Aligned Reordering outperforms GLAT by 7.88 BLEU on WMT14 EN→DE raw data (26.82 vs. 18.94) and by 1.25 BLEU on EN→RO (32.75 vs. 26.38), while achieving a higher speedup (17.0× vs. 14.2×). Against DAT, AEQA-NAT w/Reordering wins on EN→DE raw (26.82 vs. 26.57) and EN→RO raw (32.75 vs. 32.71) but trails on DE→EN raw (29.61 vs. 30.68) and RO→EN raw (32.07 vs. 33.25); however, the AEQA-DAT variant reverses this, beating DAT on all four benchmarks (e.g., 30.94 vs. 30.68 on DE→EN, 33.56 vs. 33.25 on RO→EN). On knowledge-distilled data, AEQA-NAT w/NPD remains within 0.84 BLEU of DAT on DE→EN (30.53 vs. 31.37) and 1.26 BLEU on RO→EN (32.59 vs. 33.85), while matching DAT exactly on EN→RO (32.79). The autoregressive Transformer retains a small edge (≤1 BLEU) over AEQA-DAT on all benchmarks, but at 1.0× speedup versus 9.1×. Critically, AEQA-NAT reduces the raw-to-KD performance gap to 0.29 BLEU (vs. 0.81 for DAT and 7.24 for Vanilla NAT), indicating substantially weaker dependence on knowledge distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE vector quantization mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the Glancing Transformer's adaptive sampling strategy (Qian et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from the MLM-based NAT training paradigm (CMLM, GLAT) that conditions on explicit target tokens during training. Rather than using a latent variable network as in latent-GLAT, AEQA-NAT introduces a pre-aligned Semantic Quantization Space (SQS) via mBART that jointly encodes source and target representations, enabling training-inference parity without auxiliary prediction networks. The framework is also extended to the DAT architecture (Huang et al., 2022c), showing that the SQS mechanism is orthogonal to and complementary with directed acyclic graph decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the training-inference gap without requiring target information at inference, AEQA-NAT reduces the raw-data-to-distilled-data performance gap from 7.24 BLEU (Vanilla NAT) to 0.29 BLEU, substantially lowering the practical barrier to deploying NAT models without a teacher model. The 17x speedup while matching AT quality makes this practically relevant for latency-sensitive applications. Open questions include whether the mBART pre-alignment step is strictly necessary or whether the SQS could be learned end-to-end, whether the approach generalizes to other sequence generation tasks beyond translation, and how it scales to larger model sizes or low-resource language pairs.
