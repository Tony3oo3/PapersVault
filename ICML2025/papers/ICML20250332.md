## Problem

Estimating nested expectations—where the outer integrand depends on an intractable inner conditional expectation—is a core computational bottleneck in Bayesian optimisation, option pricing, and health economics. Existing estimators (NMC, NQMC, MLMC) achieve rates of O(Δ^{-4}), O(Δ^{-2.5}), and O(Δ^{-2}) respectively, but none explicitly exploit the smoothness of the integrands f and g, leaving a gap when those functions are sufficiently regular.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T outer points θ drawn from Q, N inner points x drawn from P_θ for each θ, the corresponding function evaluations g(x,θ), the outer function f, and requires closed-form kernel mean embeddings for both Q and each P_θ (obtainable via a change-of-variable trick that maps to uniform distributions). It outputs a single point estimate of the nested expectation I, with the number of total function evaluations N×T governed by the smoothness parameters s_X, s_Θ and dimensions d_X, d_Θ. A key practical constraint is that the Gram matrices at both stages must be inverted, giving worst-case O(TN³ + T³) cost, though this can be reduced to O(TN + T) with precomputed weights.

## Method

NKQ is a two-stage nested kernel quadrature estimator: Stage I applies a KQ estimator (kernel ridge regression with a Sobolev kernel k_X) at each outer point θ_t to approximate the inner conditional expectation J(θ_t) from N inner samples; Stage II applies a second KQ estimator (with kernel k_Θ) to the T noisy values f(Ĵ_KQ(θ_t)) to approximate the outer expectation. The key theoretical insight is that the Stage II observation noise induced by Stage I estimation error is of the same order as the Stage I error itself, so the standard noiseless KQ convergence rate is preserved at both stages, yielding an overall bound of Õ(N^{-s_X/d_X} + T^{-s_Θ/d_Θ}) with sub-exponential tail guarantees. By setting N = Õ(Δ^{-d_X/s_X}) and T = Õ(Δ^{-d_Θ/s_Θ}), the total cost becomes Õ(Δ^{-d_X/s_X - d_Θ/s_Θ}), which strictly improves upon MLMC's O(Δ^{-2}) whenever d_X/s_X + d_Θ/s_Θ < 2. The regularization parameters λ_X and λ_Θ are chosen to decay as N^{-2s_X/d_X}(log N)^{(2s_X+2)/d_X} and T^{-2s_Θ/d_Θ}(log T)^{(2s_Θ+2)/d_Θ} respectively, balancing approximation and estimation error while maintaining numerical stability.

## Results

NKQ achieves a convergence rate of Õ(Δ^{-d_X/s_X - d_Θ/s_Θ}) function evaluations to reach error Δ, which is strictly faster than all existing methods when d_X/s_X + d_Θ/s_Θ < 2. In the finance risk management experiment, NKQ achieves an empirical rate of r̂=1.90 (i.i.d.) and r̂=1.91 (QMC), matching the theoretical rate r=2, while NMC achieves r̂=2.97 (theory r=3) and NQMC achieves r̂=2.74 (theory r=2.5). In the synthetic 1D experiment, NKQ requires only O(Δ^{-1}) total evaluations versus O(Δ^{-3}) for NMC, meaning NKQ needs roughly 100× fewer samples at Δ=0.01. In Bayesian optimization, NKQ achieves the lowest NMSE across all three benchmark functions (Dropwave, Ackley, Cosine8) under a fixed wall-clock budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NKQ outperforms all three baselines across every experiment. Against NMC (Rainforth et al., 2018), NKQ wins decisively: in the finance experiment NKQ's empirical rate of r̂≈1.90 is nearly 1.6× faster than NMC's r̂≈2.97, and in the synthetic experiment the gap is even wider (rate 1 vs. 3). Against MLMC (Giles and Goda, 2019), NKQ consistently achieves lower mean absolute error at every cost level in both the finance and health economics (EVPPI) experiments, and in Bayesian optimization NKQ reaches the lowest NMSE at every wall-clock time. Against NQMC (Bartuska et al., 2023), NKQ matches or slightly exceeds the theoretical rate (r̂=1.91 vs. r=2.5 for NQMC in the finance setting), while NQMC actually underperforms its own theory (r̂=2.74 vs. r=2.5) due to unbounded domains violating its assumptions. Notably, NKQ also outperforms MLMC in the high-dimensional health economics problem (d_X=17, d_Θ=2) where MLMC's exponential sample allocation across levels becomes impractical.

## Relation to Prior Work

NKQ extends the kernel quadrature (KQ) framework—previously applied to single-level integration—to the nested expectation setting by composing KQ estimators at both the inner and outer levels. The theoretical analysis builds directly on kernel ridge regression generalization bounds from Fischer and Steinwart (2020), adapted to the noiseless (quadrature) regime, and on the Sobolev-space equivalence of Matérn kernels. Conceptually, NKQ departs from NMC, NQMC, and MLMC by being the first method whose convergence rate explicitly depends on the smoothness of the integrands f and g, rather than treating them as black-box functions with only Lipschitz or bounded-derivative assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NKQ provides a principled, smoothness-aware alternative to the dominant NMC/MLMC/NQMC toolkit for nested expectations, with a proven rate that can be substantially faster than O(Δ^{-2}) when integrands are smooth and dimensions are moderate. The paper also identifies a clean technical result (Remark 4.1) showing that Stage II observation noise does not degrade the KQ rate, resolving a concern raised by Cai et al. (2023) for noisy KQ. Open questions include establishing minimax lower bounds for genuinely two-stage problems, improving the sub-optimal MLKQ (NKQ+MLMC) rate, and extending NKQ to its Bayesian counterpart for adaptive sample selection in expensive-evaluation settings.
