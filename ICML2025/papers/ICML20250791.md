## Problem

Current CQA benchmarks (FB15k237, NELL995) appear to measure complex multi-hop reasoning, but the paper shows that up to 98% of test queries can be reduced to single-link prediction because training links are present in their reasoning trees. This skews the evaluation distribution so heavily toward easy answers that reported SoTA performance is inflated and does not reflect true multi-hop reasoning capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250791/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a first-order logical query (with anchor entities, relation names, and operators like conjunction, disjunction, negation, and existential quantification) over a multi-relational KG, plus the training graph G_train, and produces a ranked list of candidate answer entities scored by MRR. Unusually, evaluation is stratified by decomposing each query type into subtypes based on how many links in the reasoning tree are missing (full-inference) versus already present in G_train (partial-inference), revealing that the 'overall' MRR is dominated by the easiest subtype. The new benchmarks additionally enforce equal sampling across all hardness subtypes per query type and introduce harder 4p and 4i structures.

## Method

The paper formalizes a 'reasoning tree' for each query-answer pair and classifies pairs as full-inference (all links missing) or partial-inference (some links in G_train), showing that standard benchmarks are overwhelmingly partial-inference and reducible to 1p. To demonstrate that exploiting training links explains SoTA performance, the authors build CQD-Hybrid, a minimal modification of CQD that assigns the maximum score to links observed in G_train during greedy variable assignment, which outperforms more sophisticated non-hybrid baselines on the old benchmarks. The core deliverable is three new benchmarks (FB15k237+H, NELL995+H, ICEWS18+H) that sample equal numbers of QA pairs for every hardness subtype per query type, filter out non-existing links in union queries, and add 4p/4i types. ICEWS18+H further replaces the arbitrary random train/test split with a temporal split (80% past links for training, 10% validation, 10% future test), making even 1p queries substantially harder and better reflecting real-world KG growth.

## Results

On the existing FB15k237 benchmark, up to 98.3% of complex query answer pairs (e.g., 2i1p, 2u1p) can be reduced to simple 1p link prediction, with only 0.1–4% requiring full inference. When evaluated on the new balanced benchmarks (FB15k237+H, NELL995+H, ICEWS18+H), MRR drops dramatically: for example, QTO's MRR on 3i queries falls from 54.6 on FB15k237 to 10.1 on FB15k237+H, and on 2i1p from 24.7 to 13.5. On the new benchmarks, no single method achieves SoTA across most query types, in stark contrast to the old benchmarks where QTO dominated. ICEWS18+H (temporal split) is substantially harder than both FB15k237+H and NELL995+H, with even 1p MRR dropping to 12–17 vs. 40–60 on the other two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the old FB15k237/NELL995 benchmarks, QTO and CQD-Hybrid achieve the highest overall MRR for most query types (e.g., QTO: 24.7 on 2i1p, CQD-Hybrid: 24.0), confirming that exploiting training links via memorization or explicit retrieval yields SoTA. However, when stratified by hardness, all methods—including QTO and CQD-Hybrid—suffer large drops on full-inference QA pairs (e.g., QTO's 2i1p MRR falls from 24.7 overall to 8.1 on full-inference only). On the new balanced benchmarks, the competitive landscape shifts: CLMPT leads on 2i1p (14.9) and 2u1p (14.2) for FB15k237+H, while CQD leads on 2i (11.3) and 3i (12.8); for NELL995+H, CLMPT dominates 2i (22.7), 3i (25.0), 2i1p (24.4), and 2u1p (22.0), while CQD leads on 1p (60.4) and 2p (9.6). No single method is SoTA across more than 2–3 query types on the new benchmarks, in contrast to QTO's dominance on the old ones. CQD-Hybrid consistently outperforms its non-hybrid counterpart CQD (e.g., 2i1p: 12.9 vs. 11.5 on FB15k237+H; 3i: 57.8 vs. 51.8 on NELL995), validating that explicit use of training links provides a meaningful but insufficient advantage for truly complex queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CQA task formulation and query-type taxonomy established by Xiong et al. (DeepPath, 2017) and Ren et al. (Query2Box, 2020), and on the neural query answering methods of Arakelyan et al. (CQD, 2021/2023) and Bai et al. (QTO, 2023). Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'reasoning trees' to show that query type does not uniformly determine difficulty: when training links are present in the reasoning path, a nominally complex query (e.g., 2i1p) collapses to a simpler one (e.g., 1p). The new benchmarks modify the query-generation algorithm of Ren et al. (2020) to balance partial- and full-inference pairs, and the CQD-Hybrid solver is a minimal extension of CQD that explicitly scores training links at maximum, echoing the explicit graph-matching component of QTO and FIT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that up to 98% of 'complex' CQA queries reduce to single-link prediction fundamentally challenges the field's narrative of progress and motivates re-evaluation of all prior SoTA claims on FB15k237 and NELL995. The new balanced benchmarks (FB15k237+H, NELL995+H, ICEWS18+H) are likely to become the standard for future CQA work, and the ICEWS18+H temporal split highlights that realistic data splits are substantially harder than random ones. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-target-variable queries, inductive settings with unseen entities/relations, and DAG-structured queries, all of which the authors note as future work. A practical limitation is that the benchmarks still assume a single target variable, which excludes a large class of real-world KG queries.
