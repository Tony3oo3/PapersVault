## Problem

Existing crystal generative models rely on continuous coordinate-based representations (diffusion, flow matching) that do not formally condition on space group symmetry, making consistent generation of symmetrically valid structures difficult. Since over 98% of experimentally stable materials in MP-20 have unique Wyckoff representations—meaning space group symmetry and composition almost completely determine the structure—there is a natural discrete, compressed representation that prior generative models have underexploited.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single space group number as input and autoregressively generates a sequence of (element, site symmetry, enumeration) triplets, each triplet encoding one Wyckoff position occupancy. The discrete Wyckoff representation is then converted to a 3D crystal structure via pyXtal sampling followed by relaxation with CHGNet or DiffCSP++. For property prediction, the same Wyckoff representation (without atomic coordinates) is fed through the encoder to produce scalar outputs such as formation energy, band gap, or thermal conductivity. The space group itself is encoded as a flattened 15×10 one-hot matrix of symmetry elements per axis, making the embedding transferable across space groups rather than a simple categorical index.

## Method

WyFormer represents each crystal as an unordered set of tokens where each token fuses a chemical element with its Wyckoff position's site symmetry and enumeration, replacing the conventional Wyckoff letter with a universal site-symmetry descriptor that is consistent across all 230 space groups. The architecture is a Transformer encoder with no positional encoding, making it formally permutation-invariant; element, site-symmetry, and enumeration embeddings are concatenated and mixed by a linear layer before multi-head attention. Training uses a cascade autoregressive scheme (predict element, then site symmetry, then enumeration) with random masking of the target token part, and permutation invariance is enforced by shuffling the Wyckoff triplet order and randomly selecting among equivalent Wyckoff representations at each epoch. The model is extremely compact (~150k parameters) and trains on the full MP-20 dataset in a single GPU memory pass using SGD without batching. For property prediction, Wyckoff positions are additionally encoded via spherical harmonics descriptors of their symmetry operations, yielding competitive accuracy without any atomic coordinates.

## Results

On MP-20, WyFormer+DiffCSP++ achieves 22.2% S.U.N. (stable, unique, novel) and 21.1% S.S.U.N. (symmetric, stable, unique, novel) on 105 DFT-relaxed structures, outperforming the best baseline (DiffCSP at 22.2% S.U.N. but with 36.6% P1 structures). WyFormer generates 186 novel unique symmetry templates (vs. 10 for DiffCSP++ and 74 for CrystalFormer), with only 1.46% P1 structures and a Space Group χ² of 0.212, closely matching the 1.7% P1 rate in MP-20. For property prediction without atomic coordinates, WyFormer achieves 25 meV formation energy MAE and 234 meV band gap MAE on MP-20, competitive with full-structure models like PotNet (19/204 meV), and the best thermal conductivity MAE (2.20) on AFLOW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WyFormer decisively outperforms DiffCSP++ (Jiao et al., 2024b) on S.U.N. (22.2% vs. 14.4%) and template novelty (186 vs. 10), while also matching its space group distribution (χ²=0.212 vs. 0.255); DiffCSP++'s low template diversity causes it to repeat structures. Against CrystalFormer (Cao et al., 2024), WyFormer achieves higher S.U.N. (22.2% vs. 20.1%), far more novel templates (186 vs. 74), and better space group fidelity (χ²=0.212 vs. 0.276), though CrystalFormer has a slightly lower P1 fraction (0.91% vs. 1.46%). SymmCD (Levy et al., 2024) is the closest competitor, achieving 20.7% S.U.N. and 101 templates, but WyFormer leads on all symmetry and novelty metrics. WyCryst (Zhu et al., 2024) is far behind at 5.5% S.U.N. with poor symmetry reproduction (P1=4.79%, χ²=0.710). DiffCSP and FlowMM, which cannot be conditioned on space group, produce 36.6% and 44.3% P1 structures respectively—unrealistically asymmetric—while WyFormer stays near the natural 1.7% rate. On property prediction, WyFormer (25 meV energy, 234 meV band gap) is competitive with coordinate-based models like PotNet (19/204) and Matformer (21/211) despite using no atomic coordinates, and achieves the best thermal conductivity MAE (2.20) on AFLOW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wyckoff-position-based representation for materials (Goodall et al. 2020, 2022; Jain & Bligaard 2018) into a generative setting, departing from the VAE approach of WyCryst (Zhu et al. 2024) by adopting a permutation-invariant autoregressive Transformer without positional encoding. It differentiates from concurrent Transformer-based work (CrystalFormer, Cao et al. 2024) by fusing element and Wyckoff position into single tokens, using universal site-symmetry descriptors instead of space-group-dependent Wyckoff letters, and explicitly handling the coset-representative ambiguity of the Euclidean normalizer. Compared to diffusion/flow-matching crystal generators (DiffCSP, FlowMM, SymmCD), WyFormer replaces gradual continuous refinement with fast discrete autoregressive sampling over a compressed symmetry space, and complements rather than replaces coordinate-based methods by providing symmetry-constrained initial structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Wyckoff symmetry information alone (without atomic coordinates) yields property predictions competitive with full-structure models challenges the assumption that precise coordinates are necessary for many material property tasks, with implications for high-throughput screening and inverse design. The four-orders-of-magnitude inference speed advantage over CSP methods makes rejection-sampling-based crystal structure prediction practically viable. Open questions include whether the over-representation of P1 (asymmetric) structures in generated samples reflects a genuine discovery or a systematic flaw in convex-hull-based stability estimation, and whether WyFormer can be extended to enforce stoichiometry constraints for true CSP. The paper also leaves open the integration of generation and property prediction in a single model and the adaptation of spherical-harmonic-encoded WPs for direct structure generation.
