## Problem

Existing LLM-based image generation methods either introduce domain-specific inductive biases (e.g., low-to-high resolution sequencing, diffusion loss, random token permutation) or adopt LLMs without systematically exploring the design space. This paper asks whether a vanilla autoregressive language model, without any vision-specific architectural modifications, can achieve near state-of-the-art image generation simply by making the right choices across tokenization, modeling, scanning, vocabulary, and sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet class label as the conditioning signal (not a free-form text prompt) and generates a full RGB image. At 256×256 resolution, the image is tokenized into a 16×16 grid of 256 spatial positions, each producing two sub-tokens (e.g., 2-10 or 2-12 decomposition), yielding a 512-token sequence. At 512×512, the sequence extends to 1024 tokens via fine-tuning from the 256×256 checkpoint. The model can also generate unfixed-size 'streaming' images by continuing to predict tokens beyond the trained receptive field.

## Method

ELM uses a BAE (binary autoencoder) tokenizer with Bernoulli sampling to discretize images into binary codes, replacing the standard VQGAN codebook. The binary code at each spatial position is split into two subcodes (e.g., a 20-bit code becomes two 10-bit subcodes), which reduces the per-position vocabulary from 2^20 to 2×2^10, lowering prediction complexity and embedding memory. A LLaMA-2-style autoregressive transformer with row-major raster scanning predicts the two sub-tokens at each position sequentially. Training uses standard next-token cross-entropy with classifier-free guidance; inference employs a linearly increasing CFG scale and a high top-k threshold (roughly 0.5× vocabulary size) to preserve the randomness needed for realistic image textures. The paper demonstrates that this combination of design choices—BAE over VQGAN, AR over MLM, two-way code decomposition, and high-randomness sampling—yields FID 1.54 at 256×256 and 3.29 at 512×512 on ImageNet without any domain-specific inductive bias.

## Results

On 256×256 ImageNet, ELM-2B (1.9B params) achieves an FID of 1.54, IS of 332.69, Precision of 0.81, and Recall of 0.60, matching or surpassing all compared methods. ELM-XL (757M) reaches FID 1.79 with IS 332.99, and ELM-L (315M) achieves FID 2.17. On 512×512 ImageNet, ELM-XL (2-12) fine-tuned from 256×256 achieves FID 3.29 and IS 321.21 in only 250k steps, outperforming MaskGIT (FID 7.32) but slightly trailing DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELM-2B (FID 1.54) achieves the best or near-best FID among all compared methods on 256×256 ImageNet, edging out MAR-H (1.55) by 0.01 and substantially outperforming VAR-d30-re (1.80), SiT-XL/2 SDE (2.06), DiT-XL/2 (2.27), LlamaGen-3B (3.05), LlamaGen-XXL (3.09), MaskGIT (6.18), and VQGAN (18.65). Against diffusion models, ELM-2B improves FID by 0.52 over the strongest diffusion baseline (SiT-XL/2 SDE) and by 0.73 over DiT-XL/2. Among autoregressive baselines, ELM-2B beats LlamaGen-3B (3.1B params) by 1.51 FID with fewer parameters (1.9B vs 3.1B), and outperforms VAR-d30 (2.0B) by 0.43 FID. On 512×512 ImageNet, ELM-XL (FID 3.29) trails DiT-XL/2 (3.04) by 0.25 but surpasses MaskGIT (7.32) by 4.03 FID, demonstrating efficient transfer from 256×256 pre-training with only 250k fine-tuning steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of AR-based image generation work (LlamaGen, VAR, MAR) but deliberately departs from their reliance on domain-specific inductive biases—such as VAR's low-to-high resolution sequencing, MAR's diffusion loss, or random token permutation. Instead, it systematically ablates the vanilla AR design space (tokenizer, scan pattern, vocabulary decomposition, sampling) to show that proper choices within a standard LLaMA-2 architecture suffice for near-SOTA performance. The key methodological departure is replacing VQGAN with BAE (binary autoencoder) as the image tokenizer and introducing a two-subcode decomposition strategy that refines the look-up-free quantization idea from Yu et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that image tokens are near-uniformly distributed (far more random than text tokens) reframes how we should think about applying language modeling objectives to vision, suggesting that next-token prediction may not be the optimal training signal for image generation. The practical demonstration that BAE outperforms VQGAN across reconstruction and generation, combined with the code decomposition strategy, provides concrete design guidance for building unified multimodal AR models. An open question the paper acknowledges is whether the non-converging training loss indicates a fundamental mismatch between classification-based objectives and highly random visual token distributions, pointing toward alternative training signals (e.g., diffusion loss as in MAR) for future work.
