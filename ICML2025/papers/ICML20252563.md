## Problem

Existing CPT scaling laws (e.g., D-CPT, CMR) can only predict the final validation loss for a given configuration, leaving the learning dynamics throughout the CPT process uncharacterized. No prior work provides a unified, step-level description of how general-domain and downstream-domain losses co-evolve under varying learning rate schedules, replay ratios, and loss potentials, making it impossible to trace or optimize intermediate training behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252563/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM checkpoint (or an open-source model with unknown PT details, handled via proxy datasets and fitted parameters), the PT and CPT datasets, a specified learning rate schedule (constant, cosine, or WSD), and hyperparameters such as peak LR, replay ratio, batch size, and sequence length. It outputs a closed-form loss prediction at every CPT step for both D_pt and D_cpt validation sets, plus derived optimal hyperparameters (loss potential, peak LR, replay ratio, turning length) for a user-specified balance coefficient between general and downstream performance. For out-of-domain validation sets, the loss is predicted as a linear combination of the D_pt and D_cpt curves with dataset-specific coefficients that are independent of training hyperparameters.

## Method

The key insight is that the CPT loss curve acts as a transfer curve between two 'hidden' PT curves: one obtained by continuing to train on D_pt and another by training from scratch on D_cpt. The total loss is decomposed into (i) a base scaling law with LR annealing (inheriting the S_1/S_2 formulation from Tissue et al., 2024) that captures the trajectory the model would follow without distribution shift, and (ii) a power-law distribution shift term ΔL(t) = B·(1 − (1 + E·S_1^cpt)^(−β)) that is empirically shown to be independent of the transfer starting point and parameterized by the forward area S_1^cpt rather than raw step count, thereby generalizing across arbitrary LRS. The combined equation (Eq. 4) uses separate annealing coefficients C_1 and C_2 for the PT and CPT phases to account for different data distributions. Extensions incorporate model size via N^γ scaling on the annealing terms, replay ratio via exponential modulation of both the shift and annealing components, and OOD prediction via a linear-combination trick that reduces to the same balance optimization problem.

## Results

The proposed CPT scaling law (Eq. 4) achieves R² values of 0.9944 (D_pt) and 0.9993 (D_cpt) with Huber losses of 0.0016 and 0.0021 respectively, demonstrating near-perfect fitting across constant, WSD, and cosine learning rate schedules. The law successfully predicts loss curves for unseen LRS (e.g., predicting cosine from WSD-fitted parameters) and generalizes across model sizes (106M–1.7B), batch sizes (1M–8M tokens), and sequence lengths (4K–8K). Key quantitative findings include: PT models with higher loss potential consistently achieve lower D_cpt validation loss; the distribution shift term follows a power-law form independent of transfer starting points; and the replay ratio modulates distribution shift exponentially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform a direct head-to-head numerical comparison against competing CPT scaling laws (D-CPT, CMR, Tissue et al.) in a unified benchmark table. Instead, it positions its contribution as a strict superset: while D-CPT (Que et al., 2024) and CMR (Gu et al., 2024) predict only the final loss for a given replay ratio, the proposed Eq. 4 predicts the full loss trajectory at every CPT step. Compared to Tissue et al. (2024), which describes LR-annealing dynamics in single-dataset pre-training, this work extends the formulation to handle distribution shift when the training data changes mid-training. The paper demonstrates that its simpler original format (fewest parameters) matches or slightly outperforms more complex variants (LR-weighted coefficient, S₂ power) in fitting quality, with R² ≥ 0.994 across all configurations. For open-source models with unknown PT details (e.g., LLaMA3.2-1B), the proposed proxy-dataset and parameter-fitting approach achieves near-perfect prediction of CPT loss curves, a scenario where prior scaling laws are not directly applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling law with learning rate annealing from Tissue et al. (2024) to the continual pre-training setting by adding a distribution shift term that captures the transition between PT and CPT data distributions. It also generalizes the D-CPT law (Que et al., 2024) and CMR law (Gu et al., 2024), which predict only the final CPT loss, into a formulation that predicts validation loss at every intermediate training step under arbitrary learning rate schedules. The conceptual framing of CPT as a 'transfer curve' between two hidden PT loss curves, with the gap governed by a power-law distribution shift independent of the transfer starting point, is the paper's core analytical contribution beyond prior CPT scaling laws.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scaling law provides practitioners with a predictive tool for optimizing CPT hyperparameters (loss potential, peak LR, replay ratio, training steps) toward specific goals such as balancing general and domain performance, and the finding that higher-loss-potential PT models yield better CPT outcomes challenges the default practice of releasing fully annealed checkpoints. Key limitations include the empirical (non-theoretical) derivation, experiments restricted to 106M–1.7B parameter models, and assumptions about open-source model training details that may not hold universally. Open questions include whether the power-law distribution shift form and the loss-potential advantage persist at 7B–70B scale, and whether a rigorous theoretical derivation from optimization dynamics is possible.
