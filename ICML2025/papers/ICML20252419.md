## Problem

Existing generalization analyses for message-passing GNNs rely on VC dimension with a trivial 1-WL-based discrete pseudo-metric where graphs are either equivalent or entirely dissimilar, yielding vacuous bounds irrelevant to practice. These analyses also overlook architectural variations like aggregation functions and assume the impractical 0-1 loss. The paper asks how refined notions of graph similarity, combined with specific aggregation schemes and practical loss functions, can produce tighter, more informative generalization error bounds for MPNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252419/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a class of graphs (unlabeled n-order graphs, bounded-degree labeled graphs, or Otter trees), an MPNN architecture class (order-normalized sum, sum, or mean aggregation with FNN readout), and a bounded loss function (cross-entropy or MAE) as inputs. It produces a generalization error bound of the form L_ℓ · L_FNN · γ̄⁻¹(2ε/n²) + M·√(4N(G,d,ε)log(2)+2log(1/δ))/|S|, where the covering number N is computed under the chosen pseudo-metric (Tree distance with cut norm, or Forest distance) and the radius ε is a free parameter trading off the two terms. For labeled graphs, the bound further depends on the number of 1-WL equivalence classes m_{n,d,q,L} and a degree-dependent constant b(d,q,L).

## Method

The paper extends the robustness-based generalization framework of Xu & Mannor (2012) by introducing fine-grained pseudo-metrics on graphs that align with MPNN computation: the Tree distance (matching 1-WL expressivity for sum aggregation) and the Forest distance (matching 1-WL up to L iterations, and shown equivalent to the Tree Mover's Distance of Chuang & Jegelka, 2022). The key technical step is proving equicontinuity (for unlabeled graphs) or Lipschitz continuity (for labeled graphs) of MPNNs with respect to these pseudo-metrics, which converts the robustness condition into a covering-number-based generalization bound. For mean aggregation, the authors define a new 1-MWL algorithm and a corresponding mean-Forest distance that precisely captures the distinguishing power of mean-aggregation MPNNs. The resulting bounds strictly improve upon Morris et al. (2023a) because the continuous pseudo-metric allows arbitrary real-valued covering radii rather than the binary 0/1 distances of the trivial 1-WL metric, and the analysis covers cross-entropy and MAE losses rather than only 0-1 loss. Empirically, the bounds are validated on MUTAG, NCI1, MCF-7H, and OGBG-MOLHIV, showing the predicted covering numbers and generalization gaps match observed behavior.

## Results

On four graph classification datasets (MUTAG, NCI1, MCF-7H, OGBG-MOLHIV), the empirical generalization gap ranges from 0.002±0.028 (MCF-7H) to 0.344±0.095 (MUTAG). The paper's theoretical bound using the optimal ε matches the empirical gap in order of magnitude across all datasets (e.g., 0.060 bound vs. 0.053 gap on NCI1; 0.015 bound vs. 0.007 gap on OGBG-MOLHIV). The bound with ε=0 is consistently looser but still within the same order, confirming the covering-number-based analysis captures real generalization behavior. The Tree distance covering bound (Theorem 10) is shown to be tighter than simply using the total number of 1-WL equivalence classes m_n as the covering number.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between different generalization bound formulations rather than model accuracy benchmarks. The paper's Tree distance–based bound (Theorem 10) is strictly tighter than the trivial 1-WL discrete pseudo-metric bound from Morris et al. (2023a), which treats graphs as either identical or maximally distant, yielding vacuous bounds. The Forest distance covering bound (Proposition 16, using m_{n,d,q,L}/(k+1)) improves over simply equating the covering number to the total number of 1-WL indistinguishable graphs m_n, as confirmed empirically in Figure 10. On the Otter tree class, the covering number bound decreases exponentially with radius (Proposition 12), in contrast to the linear decay for general n-order graphs (Theorem 10). The paper does not compare against competing MPNN architectures on downstream task accuracy; its contribution is the tightness and structure of the generalization bound itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robustness-based generalization framework of Xu & Mannor (2012) and Kawaguchi et al. (2022) to graph neural networks, replacing the trivial 1-WL discrete pseudo-metric used by Morris et al. (2023a) with refined graph distances (Tree distance, Forest distance) that capture structural similarity. The Forest distance is shown to be equivalent to the Tree Mover's Distance of Chuang & Jegelka (2022), providing a simpler combinatorial definition while preserving the Lipschitz properties needed for generalization bounds. The work also introduces 1-MWL, a new isomorphism heuristic characterizing mean-aggregation MPNNs, and builds on the uniform continuity results of Böker et al. (2023) for sum-aggregation architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generalization analysis of MPNNs that simultaneously accounts for non-trivial graph structure, multiple aggregation functions (sum, mean, order-normalized), and practically relevant loss functions (cross-entropy, MAE), yielding strictly tighter bounds than prior VC-dimension-based analyses. Key limitations remain: the analysis is restricted to discretely-labeled graphs with bounded degree, does not cover common architectural choices like normalization layers or skip connections, and does not explain why gradient-based optimization converges to generalizing solutions. Open questions include extending to real-valued vertex features, connecting the covering-number geometry to optimization dynamics, and determining whether the linear covering-number decay is tight or can be improved.
