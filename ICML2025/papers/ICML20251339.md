## Problem

Estimating counterfactual outcomes for spatial-temporal data remains underexplored: existing spatial-temporal causal frameworks either ignore temporal carryover effects (Christiansen et al., 2022) or lack explicit methods for computing propensity scores over high-dimensional spatial point patterns (Papadogeorgou et al., 2022). Classical kernel-based approaches further suffer from poor generalization under complex data patterns. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a deep-learning-based estimator that handles both the high-dimensionality of spatial treatments and the temporal persistence of treatment effects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial-temporal point pattern series—binary grids of treated/outcome locations at each time step, along with covariate point patterns—organized as 3D tensors (e.g., 100×100×192 in synthetic experiments). A user-specified counterfactual intervention distribution F_H, parameterized by M intensity functions h_j controlling treatment density over the last M periods, defines the 'what-if' scenario. The output is the expected count of outcome events within a target region ω under that intervention, averaged over time, with theoretical guarantees of consistency and asymptotic normality as T→∞.

## Method

The estimator extends inverse probability weighting (IPW) from Marginal Structural Models to the spatial-temporal setting, where the weight is the product of counterfactual probabilities over M periods divided by propensity scores, multiplied by a spatial intensity function. To handle the intractable high-dimensionality of point-pattern treatments (2^100 possible configurations for 100 locations), a dimension-reduction map R(Z_t) collapses each treatment to its event count, and a CNN regresses this count on historical information to estimate the Poisson parameter governing the propensity score. The counterfactual probability is computed by sampling from the specified intervention Poisson process and estimating its mean. A Transformer encoder-decoder (8 blocks, 16 heads) models the outcome intensity function λ(s) via a combined MLE + VAE objective that maximizes the Poisson point-process likelihood while regularizing the latent representation with a KL divergence term, enabling the network to capture long-range spatial dependencies in the outcome pattern.

## Results

On synthetic spatial-temporal data (T=32, 48, 64 time steps), the proposed Transformer-based estimator achieves the lowest Relative Error Rate (RER) across all intervention settings (c=3–7, M=1 and M=3), consistently outperforming four baselines. On real-world Colombian conflict/forest-loss data (2002–2022), estimated forest loss events range from approximately 20.5 (M=1, c=3) to 28.0 (M=7, c=7), demonstrating a monotonic increase with both conflict duration (M) and intensity (c). The method runs in under 10 minutes per experimental setting on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: MSMs (Robins et al., 2000), RMSNs (Lim, 2018), Causal Forest (Wager & Athey, 2018), and a Linear Regression (LR) method. In synthetic experiments (measured by RER), the proposed Transformer-based estimator consistently achieves the lowest error across all time lengths (T=32, 48, 64) and intervention settings (M=1, 3; c=3–7), outperforming all four baselines. The advantage is most pronounced at longer time horizons and higher intervention intensities, where classical statistical methods (MSMs, LR) and the RNN-based RMSN degrade more noticeably. An ablation replacing the Transformer with a GRU-based RNN confirms the Transformer backbone is superior in most settings. On real data, the baselines are not directly compared (only the proposed method's estimates are reported in Table 1), but the monotonic trend in estimated forest loss with conflict duration and intensity aligns with prior literature on conflict–deforestation relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-temporal potential outcomes framework of Papadogeorgou et al. (2022) by replacing their kernel-based estimation with a deep learning architecture (CNN for propensity scores, Transformer for intensity functions) and by generalizing the intervention model to allow diverse, time-varying treatment distributions rather than a single fixed one. The IPW-based estimator is directly inherited from Marginal Structural Models (Robins et al., 2000), adapted here to the spatial-temporal setting where treatments and outcomes are high-dimensional point patterns. The key departure from prior spatial-temporal causal inference (Christiansen et al., 2022; Papadogeorgou et al., 2022) is the explicit modeling of temporal carryover effects through multi-period intervention windows and the provision of a concrete, scalable method for computing propensity scores when both treatment and covariates are high-dimensional.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to our knowledge, the first deep learning framework with formal statistical guarantees (consistency, asymptotic normality) for counterfactual outcome estimation on spatial-temporal point process data, filling a gap between classical spatial-temporal causal inference and modern deep learning methods that ignore spatial structure. The real-data application to conflicts and forest loss in Colombia provides a concrete, policy-relevant demonstration, though the conclusion is consistent with prior ecological literature. Key limitations include the reliance on the unconfoundedness assumption (unmeasured confounders are excluded), the Poisson point process assumption (though robustness is partially tested), and the restriction to discrete point pattern data rather than general spatial-temporal stochastic processes, which the authors identify as future work.
