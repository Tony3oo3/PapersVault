## Problem

Forward KL divergence (FKLD) and reverse KL divergence (RKLD), the two standard objectives in logit-based knowledge distillation, represent extreme and unbalanced cases of two mode-concentration effects: hardness-concentration (focusing on classes with large teacher-student error) and confidence-concentration (focusing on classes where the student is already confident). FKLD is too weak on both effects, causing the student to spread probability mass uniformly and fail to concentrate on the target class; RKLD is too strong on both, collapsing the student distribution to a near one-hot vector and discarding the soft-label information that motivates distillation. No existing divergence provides a principled, independently tunable mechanism to balance these two effects.

## Input / Output

For instruction-following, the method consumes a text prompt and a reference output sequence, computing the α-β-divergence at each token position over the full vocabulary (50,257 dimensions for GPT-2) between the teacher's and student's autoregressive distributions. For image classification, it operates on C-dimensional class probability vectors (e.g., 100 for CIFAR-100) produced by vision backbones such as ResNet, WRN, VGG, or CLIP ViT. In both settings the loss is computed purely on the logit-level distributions—no intermediate features or generated sequences are required—so the method is a drop-in replacement for the standard KL term in the KD objective.

## Method

ABKD replaces the standard KL divergence in the distillation loss with the α-β-divergence D_AB^(α,β)(p‖q_θ), which subsumes FKLD (α=1, β=0), RKLD (α=0, β=1), Hellinger distance, β-divergence, and squared Euclidean distance as special cases. The key theoretical tool is the log mass ratio LogR, shown to be proportional to the logit gradient, which decomposes into a hardness-concentration term |p(y)^α − q(y)^α|/α (controlled by α: smaller α penalizes hard-class errors more aggressively) and a confidence-concentration term q(y)^β (controlled by β: larger β sharpens focus on high-confidence classes). Unlike the α-divergence, which forces β = 1−α and thus couples the two effects along a one-dimensional submanifold, the α-β-divergence allows independent tuning of both hyperparameters across a two-dimensional space. The training objective is ℓ = ℓ_CE + λ·D_AB^(α,β)(p‖q_θ), requiring no additional parameters or sampling overhead beyond standard KD, and the framework can also be plugged into existing distillation methods (DKD, TTM, LSD) by simply swapping their loss functions.

## Results

On five instruction-following datasets (Dolly, Self-Instruct, Vicuna Eval, Super-NaturalInstructions, Unnatural Instructions), ABKD achieves ROUGE-L scores of 25.65, 13.47, 16.06, 26.47, and 29.32 when distilling GPT-2 XL (1.5B) into GPT-2 (0.1B), outperforming the best prior method (DISTILLM) by up to 3.31 points. The paper reports consistent improvements of 0.81–3.31 ROUGE-L over both FKLD and RKLD across all five datasets. On CIFAR-100 vision tasks, ABKD serves as a plug-and-play loss replacement that further boosts DKD, TTM, and LSD across 9 teacher-student architecture pairs (e.g., WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2, ResNet-110→ResNet-20). Training speed matches vanilla KD since only the loss function is modified, unlike SGO-based methods that require 1.6–7× longer training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABKD outperforms all NLP baselines across the five instruction-following datasets in the GPT-2 XL→GPT-2 (0.1B) setting: it beats KD/FKLD (Hinton 2015) by 1.85–10.33 ROUGE-L, SeqKD (Kim & Rush 2016) by 1.37–5.73, MiniLLM/RKLD (Gu et al. 2024a) by 1.03–5.02, GKD/JSD (Agarwal et al. 2024) by 1.16–7.91, and DISTILLM (Ko et al. 2024) by 0.33–3.53. The largest gains appear on Super-NaturalInstructions (+2.95 over DISTILLM) and Unnatural Instructions (+3.53 over DISTILLM), where the teacher's soft-label information is most informative. For vision tasks on CIFAR-100, ABKD acts as a loss-function replacement that improves DKD (Zhao et al. 2022), LSD (Sun et al. 2024), and TTM (Tian et al. 2019) across all 9 architecture pairs tested (e.g., ABDKD > DKD, ABLSD > LSD, ABTTM > TTM). In the base-to-new CLIP setting (ViT-L/14→ViT-B/16), ABKD-rectified methods achieve competitive or superior harmonic-mean accuracy compared to SFT prompt-tuning baselines CoCoOp (Zhou et al. 2022), MaPLe (Khattak et al. 2023a), and PromptSRC (Khattak et al. 2023b), particularly on new-class generalization. Notably, ABKD matches vanilla KD training speed since it introduces no extra sampling or trainable parameters, unlike SGO-based methods (GKD, DISTILLM, MiniLLM) that require 1.6–7× more training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit-based knowledge distillation paradigm introduced by Hinton (2015) by replacing the standard FKLD or RKLD objective with the α-β-divergence from Cichocki et al. (2011), a general family that subsumes both KL divergences, Hellinger distance, and β-divergence as special cases. It departs from prior attempts to balance FKLD and RKLD (e.g., weighted-sum divergence, JSD) by showing those composite metrics suffer from gradient instability or vanishing gradients, and from the α-divergence by demonstrating that the constraint α+β=1 unnecessarily couples the hardness- and confidence-concentration effects. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical lens of tracking probability mass allocation via log mass ratio, which reveals FKLD and RKLD as two extremes of the same underlying mechanism rather than fundamentally different behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABKD provides a principled, drop-in replacement for the KD loss that requires no extra parameters, no student-generated outputs, and matches vanilla KD training speed, making it immediately adoptable across architectures and tasks. The hardness-concentration / confidence-concentration framework gives practitioners a theoretically grounded guide for selecting α and β based on output dimensionality (e.g., small α for high-dimensional vocabularies, small β for low-dimensional classification). Open questions include whether the framework extends to feature-based or intermediate-layer distillation, whether the hyperparameter sensitivity observed in practice can be further reduced via adaptive scheduling, and whether the analysis generalizes to continuous (non-softmax) output distributions.
