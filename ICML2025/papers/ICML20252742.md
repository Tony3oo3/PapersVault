## Problem

State-of-the-art imitation learning methods like Diffusion Policy directly model optimal actions from expert demonstrations, making them unable to leverage the more readily available suboptimal trajectories (failed rollouts, play data) or action-free data (kinesthetic demos, human videos). Prior attempts to use such data rely on restrictive assumptions like reward labels, optimality annotations, or pretraining-then-finetuning pipelines that don't improve planning capability. The paper asks whether a modular design can natively exploit all three data types—optimal, suboptimal, and action-free—within a single imitation learning framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (e.g., from a wrist-mounted Zed camera) and robot proprioception at each timestep, encoding them into a 16-dimensional latent vector via a frozen VAE. At inference, the planner forecasts a dense temporal sequence of H_p future latent states, and the IDM then extracts H_a actions between consecutive latent pairs; only the first H_a actions are executed before re-observing and re-planning (receding-horizon control). The output is thus a short action chunk per planning cycle, with the planning horizon typically longer than the execution horizon to enable reactive replanning.

## Method

LDP decomposes the control problem into three independently trained components operating over a learned latent space: (1) a β-VAE trained with image reconstruction loss to produce compact 16-dim latent embeddings; (2) a diffusion-based planner using Diffusion Policy's Conditional U-Net architecture that denoises a chunk of future latent states conditioned on the current latent embedding (image latent concatenated with proprioception); and (3) a lightweight diffusion-based inverse dynamics model (MLPResNet from IDQL) that denoises the action between a pair of consecutive latent states. The key architectural insight is that because the planner forecasts states rather than actions, it can be trained on action-free demonstrations, while the IDM, which only needs state-action pairs, can be trained on suboptimal in-domain data. Dense forecasting of the full latent trajectory (rather than sparse subgoals) enables fast closed-loop replanning in latent space, avoiding the computational cost and instability of video generation while retaining the reactivity needed for precise manipulation.

## Results

On four simulated robotic manipulation tasks (Robomimic Lift, Can, Square, and ALOHA Sim Transfer Cube) in a low-demonstration regime, LDP achieves 0.65 average success rate using only expert demos, outperforming Diffusion Policy (0.51). When augmented with both action-free and suboptimal data, LDP reaches 0.95 average success rate (1.00 on Lift, 0.98 on Can, 0.83 on Square, 0.97 on ALOHA Cube). On the real-world Franka Lift task, LDP+Action-Free achieves 79.3% success rate versus 69.6% for Diffusion Policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), LDP wins on all four simulated tasks using only expert demos (0.65 vs. 0.51 average), with the gap widening to 0.95 vs. 0.51 when LDP additionally leverages action-free and suboptimal data that DP cannot use. The DP-VPT variant, which relabels action-free data via an IDM before training DP, improves DP to 0.59 average but still trails LDP+Action-Free+Subopt by 36 percentage points. Against UniPi (Du et al., 2023), LDP achieves a dramatic margin: UniPi-OL+Action-Free scores only 0.11 average and UniPi-CL+Action-Free scores 0.18, while LDP reaches 0.65–0.95. The paper attributes UniPi's weakness to the difficulty of generating visually consistent video frames for precise manipulation (e.g., grasping), whereas LDP's latent-space planning avoids this bottleneck. On the real-world Franka Lift task, LDP+Action-Free (79.3%) outperforms DP (69.6%) by nearly 10 points, confirming the advantage transfers beyond simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LDP builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) by training a β-VAE to compress images and then diffusing over the resulting latent embeddings, rather than over raw pixels or actions. The planner directly adapts the Conditional U-Net architecture from Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), while the inverse dynamics model borrows the lightweight MLPResNet from IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure from prior modular approaches (UniPi, Black et al.) is planning over a compact learned latent space with dense temporal forecasting rather than over high-dimensional video frames or sparse subgoals, and the explicit separation of data sources: the planner consumes action-free demonstrations while the IDM consumes suboptimal trajectories, something no prior imitation learning method handles natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in robot learning: expert demonstrations are expensive to collect, while suboptimal rollouts and action-free data are abundant but unusable by standard behavior cloning. By showing that a simple modular decomposition (latent planner + IDM) can exploit both data types simultaneously, LDP offers a scalable recipe for data-efficient robot learning. Limitations include reliance on a VAE that may not learn control-optimal features, additional inference cost from diffusing over states, and validation only on single-task manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-task or language-conditioned settings, whether better representation objectives (beyond β-VAE) improve the latent space, and whether recent diffusion advances (DiT, flow matching) further close the gap in large-data regimes.
