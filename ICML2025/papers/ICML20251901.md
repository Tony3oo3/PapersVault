## Problem

Hierarchical clustering and overlapping clustering are two well-studied paradigms in graph analysis, yet their natural hybrid—hierarchical overlapping clustering (HOC), where clusters at different levels may overlap—has no established cost function, theoretical analysis, or scalable algorithm. This paper initiates the study of HOC on graphs by formulating the first cost function for this setting and providing approximation guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251901/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected weighted similarity graph G=(V,E,w) and an integer k bounding the width (longest anti-chain of non-leaf nodes) of the output structure. It produces a k-HOC graph D, a directed acyclic graph whose n leaf nodes correspond to graph vertices, whose single root has out-degree 0, and whose non-root nodes have parent sets forming anti-chains. The belonging factors are normalized so that each edge's mass is distributed across its minimal common ancestors summing to 1, and the width constraint k limits the number of incomparable bottom-level clusters.

## Method

The core cost function H^D(G) generalizes Dasgupta's HC objective by replacing the unique LCA of each edge with a set of minimal common ancestors (MCAs) on the HOC DAG, weighted by edge-to-node belonging factors derived from multiplicative node-to-node belonging factors along paths. The algorithm recursively applies a 2-OC local search that partitions a subgraph into three sets (A, B, C) where B is the overlap, optimizing a surrogate cost w(E)−w(A,C)+xw(B) via pairwise node exchanges until no swap improves the cost by more than (1+ε/n²). At each iteration of the k-HOC loop, the cluster with the largest relative dual-cost benefit Δ(X) is selected for splitting, building a binary tree of internal nodes that is then simplified by merging identical leaf sets and removing redundant edges. A speed-up variant replaces random initialization with RatioCut spectral partitioning and swaps pairwise exchanges for batch migration of the top-ℓ nodes with the largest cost increment, achieving a constant approximation factor 2/(3√6)−Θ((1+ε)/n) for the dual k-HOC problem while running in O(kε⁻¹n⁴ log m) time.

## Results

On OSBM synthetic datasets with embedded HOC structures, the speed-up algorithm achieves NMI nearly 1.0 on dense graphs (size 5000) and above 0.95 on sparse graphs (size 10^5) at both hierarchy levels, while running in ~80s (dense) and <15min (sparse). On real datasets, the algorithm runs on a single laptop in <12min on Youtube (~1.1M nodes, ~3M edges), which is only ~20% of the 55–75min runtime of cm+improve executed on a server cluster. The cost function also yields lower HOC cost than both the non-overlapping Dasgupta baseline and OHC'20 on OSBM, confirming the benefit of overlapping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines. Against OHC'20 (Jeantet et al., 2020), a density-based agglomerative method adapted to similarity graphs via spectral embedding, the proposed method achieves lower HOC cost and higher NMI (≈1.0 vs. lower values) on OSBM, while OHC'20 fails to terminate within one hour on sparse graphs of size 10^4. Against cm+improve (Orecchia et al., 2022), a nearly-linear-time O(log n) approximation for overlapping bipartition, the proposed speed-up algorithm is roughly 5× faster on real datasets (e.g., <12 min on a laptop vs. 55–75 min on a server for Youtube), attributed to the simpler local-search strategy and the hyperparameter-free cost function. The paper also includes a non-overlapping Dasgupta baseline (its own algorithm with B always empty), confirming that overlapping consistently reduces cost relative to the non-overlapping counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of graph clustering research—hierarchical clustering (Dasgupta's cost, 2016) and overlapping clustering (belonging factors from Nepusz et al., 2008; Nicosia et al., 2009)—into a single hybrid framework called HOC. The cost function extends Dasgupta's LCA-based objective by replacing the unique LCA with a set of minimal common ancestors (MCAs) and introducing normalized edge-to-node belonging factors, while the dual objective mirrors Moseley & Wang's (2017) dual HC cost. Methodologically, it departs from the cut-matching-and-improve approach of Orecchia et al. (2022) in favor of a simpler local search over a surrogate cost, trading theoretical tightness for practical scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal cost function and approximation algorithm for hierarchical overlapping clustering on graphs, filling a gap where HC and OC were only studied in isolation. The practical result—that a simple local search on a single laptop runs ~5× faster than a nearly-linear-time algorithm on a server cluster—suggests that algorithmic simplicity can outperform theoretical sophistication in graph partitioning at scale. Open questions include approximation guarantees for the primal k-HOC problem with k > 2, alternative belonging factor definitions, and whether the width constraint is the most natural way to control cluster complexity in HOC graphs.
