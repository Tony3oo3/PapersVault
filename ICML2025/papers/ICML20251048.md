## Problem

Existing projection-based LP dimensionality reduction methods either use random matrices that fail to capture the subspace of good solutions, or learn a single shared projection matrix that generalizes poorly across heterogeneous LP instances with different sizes and parameters. The paper addresses how to generate instance-specific projection matrices that adapt to each LP's structure while handling variable problem sizes, enabling high-quality feasible solutions with significantly reduced computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes LP parameters (objective vector c ∈ R^N, constraint matrix A ∈ R^{M×N}, constraint vector b ∈ R^M) as input, where N and M can differ across instances. It outputs a non-negative projection matrix P ∈ R^{N×K} (K < N) via a single forward pass; the reduced LP (P^T c, AP, b) is then solved by any LP solver, and the recovered solution x̃ = Py* is guaranteed feasible for the original LP. The projection matrix rows are produced by a shared fully-connected network with softmax normalization per reduced variable, and the model's parameters are independent of N and M, enabling size-agnostic inference.

## Method

The architecture uses stacked PELP (Permutation Equivariant Linear Programming) layers that process elements of c, A, and b with parameters shared across all positions, incorporating average pooling over rows and columns to gather cross-element information while maintaining permutation equivariance on variables and invariance on constraints. A projection generator then applies max pooling over the constraint dimension and a shared FCN to produce each row of P. Training is formulated as bilevel optimization: the inner loop solves projected LPs with a solver, and the outer loop maximizes the expected objective ratio by updating network parameters. Gradients are computed analytically via the implicit function theorem using only the primal and dual optimal solutions of the projected LP (∂u/∂P = cy*^T − A^T λ* y*^T), avoiding backpropagation through the solver path. The paper also provides a generalization bound via pseudo-dimension analysis, showing Pdim(F_N^u) = O(WL log(U+NK) + WK log(LMK)), which guarantees the empirical-to-true performance gap shrinks with training data size.

## Results

On packing problems with 100,000 variables (K=50), the proposed method achieves an objective ratio of 0.924 while solving in 0.055s, compared to 23.487s for the full LP solver (Full). Across all three problem types (Packing, MaxFlow, MinCostFlow) with K=50, the method achieves 89%–99% objective ratio while being orders of magnitude faster than solving the original LPs. The method also generalizes to LPs of different sizes than those seen during training, maintaining high objective ratios when the number of variables is close to or smaller than the training size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method substantially outperforms all baselines on objective ratio. Against Rand (Akchen & Mišić, 2024), which randomly selects K variables, the method achieves 0.924 vs. 0.230 objective ratio on 100K-variable packing problems—a nearly 4× improvement. Against PCA and SharedP (Sakaue & Oki, 2024), which learn a single shared projection matrix across all LPs, the method achieves 0.924 vs. 0.066/0.070, demonstrating that instance-specific projections are critical for heterogeneous LPs. The FCNN baseline (a fully-connected network without permutation equivariance/invariance) scores 0.257, confirming the importance of the PELP architecture's symmetry properties. The Direct baseline (predicting solutions without projection) achieves only 0.014 objective ratio and violates constraints in 10%–48% of test instances, whereas the proposed method always produces feasible solutions. In terms of computational time, the method (0.055s) is 425× faster than Full (23.487s) while retaining 92.4% of the optimal objective value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven projection framework of Sakaue & Oki (2024), which trained a single shared projection matrix across all LP instances, to the more general setting of generating instance-specific projection matrices via a neural network. The theoretical analysis builds directly on the pseudo-dimension bounds for neural networks in data-driven algorithm design established by Cheng et al. (2024), adapting their framework to the LP projection setting. Architecturally, the paper introduces PELP layers that enforce permutation equivariance on variables and invariance on constraints, distinguishing it from prior GNN-based LP solvers (Chen et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2024) that use equivariance for solution prediction rather than projection generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver-agnostic, feasibility-guaranteeing nature of the approach makes it a practical drop-in accelerator for any LP solver, and the ability to handle heterogeneous LPs of varying sizes with a single trained model is a meaningful step toward general-purpose LP acceleration. Key limitations include: the theoretical bound is proven only for fully-connected networks (not the PELP architecture actually used), all experiments are on synthetic LPs rather than real-world instances, and training requires LPs similar to the test distribution (cross-type generalization degrades sharply). Open questions include extending the analysis to permutation-equivariant networks, applying the method to MILPs, and integrating it with predict-then-optimize frameworks.
