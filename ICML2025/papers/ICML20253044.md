## Problem

Existing multimodal Theory-of-Mind (ToM) methods face a fundamental scalability barrier: structured Bayesian workflows with ToM-specific priors cannot handle increasing multi-step planning complexity, while inference-time scaling (CoT, o1-style reasoning) plateaus as task demands grow. Additionally, multimodal ToM reasoning requires broad social and world knowledge that correlates strongly with model scale, yet prior approaches relied on small LMs (≤7B) for likelihood estimation, limiting generalization. The paper asks whether ToM reasoning can be made scalable by combining structured Bayesian decomposition with large-model world knowledge without expensive post-training of the large model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames from the VirtualHome household simulator (averaging ~1,462 frames per video representing ~36 human actions) alongside textual scene descriptions, which are converted via scene-graph generation and LLM-based text parsing into unified symbolic predicates (e.g., In(pear, basket), walk to cabinet). These symbolic representations encode states, beliefs, goals, and actions at discrete time steps. The output is a Bayesian posterior comparison of competing hypotheses about the agent's goal and belief, yielding goal inference (what the agent intends) and belief inference (where the agent thinks objects are located), with action likelihood estimates computed at each step by the large LM policy model.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable Bayesian inverse planning (BIP) framework that decomposes multimodal ToM reasoning into modular stepwise Bayesian updates (state transitions, belief updates, action likelihoods), making multi-step inference tractable. The key architectural innovation is a weak-to-strong control mechanism: a small LM (4B–8B) is post-trained via instruction tuning and DPO-style preference optimization to specialize in ToM likelihood estimation, and at inference time the ratio π^E/π^N (post-trained small LM divided by naive small LM) multiplicatively redirects the policy distribution of a much larger LM (70B–405B) that serves as the primary policy model, leveraging its extensive world knowledge without any post-training on the large model. Theorem 1 provides a KL-divergence bound showing the proxy-tuned large model approximates a directly fine-tuned model within O(η²) error, establishing that the small LM need only provide a coarse gradient approximation. This design achieves 81.3% accuracy on MMToM-QA multimodal tasks (4.6% over prior best) and generalizes to unseen scenarios (fairy tales, ancient Egyptian, outer space, etc.) while allowing the weak controller to be downsized to 4B with minimal performance loss.

## Results

On the multimodal MMToM-QA benchmark, the proposed method with Llama3.1-405B achieves 81.3% overall accuracy (87.1% belief inference, 76.9% goal inference), a 4.6% improvement over the best prior baseline (BIPALM w/ Llama2-7B at 76.7%). In the text-only setting, the same model reaches 77.8% overall, outperforming BIPALM w/ GPT-J-6B (71.7%) and SymbolicToM w/ GPT-4 (63.0%). On the MuMA-ToM real-world social interaction benchmark, the method performs competitively with GPT-4o-based LIMP while using only open-source models. The gap to human performance (93.0% multimodal) narrows to 11.7 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all prior baselines on the multimodal MMToM-QA benchmark. Against the closest competitor, BIPALM w/ Llama2-7B (76.7% overall), the proposed method gains 4.6 percentage points (81.3%), with the largest gains in belief inference (87.1% vs 80.3%) and goal inference (76.9% vs 73.3%). Compared to BIPALM w/ GPT-J-6B (75.3%), the improvement is 6.0 points. The method substantially surpasses general-purpose multimodal models: GPT-4V (44.0%), LLaVA-7B (43.5%), and Video-Llama2-13B (40.2%) all fall 37+ points behind, highlighting the critical role of the structured Bayesian ToM workflow. In the text-only setting, the method (77.8%) also beats BIPALM w/ GPT-J-6B (71.7%), SymbolicToM w/ GPT-4 (63.0%), and GPT-4 (48.0%). The method remains 11.7 points below human performance (93.0%), with the largest gap in goal inference subtask 2.3 (75.5% vs 83.3%). On the MuMA-ToM real-world benchmark, the method matches GPT-4o-based LIMP while using only open-source Llama models, avoiding expensive API costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian Inverse Planning (BIP) framework for Theory-of-Mind (Baker et al., 2017) and the multimodal BIPALM planner (Jin et al., 2024) by replacing their small-LM likelihood estimators with a weak-to-strong control mechanism that redirects large LMs (up to 405B) at inference time. The post-training stage combines instruction tuning with a DPO-modified preference objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) to specialize small LMs as ToM policy estimators, while the inference-time ratio π^E/π^N borrows conceptually from activation steering and decoding-time reweighting literature but applies it within a structured Bayesian planning loop. The key departure from prior BIP work is that the large LM is never directly fine-tuned; instead, its pretrained world knowledge is progressively redirected by the small LM's learned behavioral shift, avoiding the instability and cost of post-training 70B+ models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that 4B-parameter controllers (even width-reduced Minitron variants) can effectively guide 70B–405B policy models across unseen scenarios offers a cost-efficient path to scalable ToM reasoning without the 50–64 GPU fine-tuning cost of large models. However, the approach remains tied to structured symbolic representations from a single simulator (VirtualHome), and the Bayesian framework requires pre-parsed states, beliefs, and goals, limiting applicability to open-ended, unstructured video. Open questions include whether the weak-to-strong ratio generalizes beyond the POMDP-structured setting, whether the KL divergence bound in Theorem 1 holds empirically at scale, and whether the method extends to multi-agent or real-world social interaction without heavy symbolic preprocessing.
