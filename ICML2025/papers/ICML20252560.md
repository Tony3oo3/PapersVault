## Problem

Spatio-temporal forecasting for geographical scientific data is hindered by severe data scarcity: individual studies collect observations from few entities over short time spans, leaving target entities with insufficient history for existing methods. While heterogeneous datasets from different studies (varying granularities, regions, and variables) contain complementary information about the same underlying system, no framework exists to integrate them into unified representations for entities that are entirely absent from any training dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252560/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical multivariate time series for target entities (e.g., 2 years of daily CO₂ emissions) alongside heterogeneous auxiliary datasets: grid-based observations at two resolutions (1 km and 1°) and entity-based records at country, province, or city granularity from different scientific sources. Critically, the test entities are completely disjoint from all pretraining entities (E^train ∩ E^test = ∅), so the model must infer their representations purely from spatial structure. The output is a forecasted multivariate time series for the target entities plus a learned interaction graph (adjacency matrix) derived from Segment Quadtree node embeddings, which can be plugged into any downstream time-series or spatio-temporal model.

## Method

SQGEF introduces a Segment Quadtree, a hierarchical spatial data structure where each node is recursively divided into four quadrant children (NW, NE, SW, SE), enabling a single representation to encode entities at any granularity from the full region down to individual grid cells. Two complementary learning procedures train the quadtree: Hierarchical Grid-Based Learning re-partitions grid datasets at multiple levels and computes mean embeddings over each node and its children to build cosine-similarity adjacency matrices, while Geographical Entity-Based Learning traverses the quadtree to collect nodes overlapping each entity's binary boundary mask and aggregates their embeddings weighted by 1/2^i to produce a level-aware entity vector. Training proceeds sequentially (grid datasets first, then entity datasets) with multiple downstream models sharing the graph weights, and at inference the trained quadtree is queried with test-entity boundaries to construct the interaction graph, which is jointly fine-tuned with the target model. A Rademacher complexity analysis shows the quadtree fuses only O(log k) node embeddings versus O(k) for naive grid partitioning, yielding a strictly tighter generalization bound.

## Results

SQGEF-enhanced models achieve the best performance across all three forecasting tasks (China Province, Europe Country, China City). On China Province, TimesNet w/Seg attains the lowest MSE of 0.2743 (vs. 0.4503 without SQGEF, a 39% reduction), while FEDformer w/Seg reaches 0.2941 MSE. On Europe Country, AGCRN w/Seg achieves the best MSE of 2.0247 (vs. 2.2722 baseline). On the extremely data-scarce China City task (only 37 temporal points), FEDformer w/Seg achieves the best MSE of 6.2043, though SQGEF degrades two spatio-temporal baselines (MTGNN, GWNet) due to noise from under-represented cities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQGEF consistently improves time-series baselines across all three datasets: TimesNet benefits most on China Province (MSE 0.4503→0.2743, −39%), FEDformer improves from 0.3588→0.2941, and Autoformer from 0.3242→0.2934. Among spatio-temporal baselines, AGCRN shows the largest gain on Europe Country (MSE 2.2722→2.0247, −11%) and China Province (0.9951→0.7061, −29%), while GWNet improves modestly (0.3953→0.3732 on China Province). MTGNN benefits on China Province (0.5926→0.4978) and Europe Country (2.1919→2.1395) but is the only baseline that degrades on China City (7.1258→7.2951 MSE), as do GWNet (6.8174→7.1551), because the obscure cities in that dataset are overwhelmed by surrounding larger-city signals in the quadtree. Overall, SQGEF wins on 17 of 19 available (method × dataset) comparisons, with the two losses confined to the extreme data-scarcity China City setting for spatio-temporal models that rely on dense relational graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the quadtree data structure from spatial data management (Kothuri et al., Yin et al.) for a new role: hierarchical geographical entity representation in spatio-temporal forecasting. It departs from existing spatio-temporal methods (AGCRN, MTGNN, GWNet) that rely on target entities' own historical data by instead integrating heterogeneous auxiliary datasets (grid and entity-based) to construct interaction graphs for entities absent from training. The framework is model-agnostic, plugging into both time series models (Informer, FEDformer, TimesNet) and spatio-temporal models, rather than proposing a new end-to-end architecture. The theoretical contribution via Rademacher complexity provides a formal justification for why the O(log k) node fusion in the quadtree yields tighter generalization bounds than O(k) grid-level fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in scientific computing where expensive data collection limits both the number of observed entities and the temporal span, making standard spatio-temporal methods inapplicable. The model-agnostic design (works as a plug-in enhancement for any forecasting model) and the ability to represent previously unseen geographical entities make it practically useful for environmental monitoring and resource management. However, the authors acknowledge that obscure, under-represented entities (e.g., small Chinese cities) suffer from noise in their learned representations, and the approach is inherently tied to entities with well-defined geographical boundaries. Open questions include whether the hierarchical quadtree generalizes to non-geographical hierarchical structures and how to handle the boundary between 'obscure' and 'well-represented' entities.
