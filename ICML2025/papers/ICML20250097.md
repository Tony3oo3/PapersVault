## Problem

In self-supervised contrastive learning, negative pairs are sampled from the entire dataset, inevitably including semantically similar samples (false negatives) that are incorrectly pushed apart, degrading learned representations. Existing remedies are either local (mini-batch top-k, unreliable for small batches) or global but expensive (full-dataset clustering at fixed epochs). The paper addresses the gap of detecting false negatives globally across the dataset with per-iteration cost independent of dataset size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes augmented image views (unimodal) or augmented image-text pairs (bimodal) encoded by a backbone network (ResNet-50 or a 12-layer transformer for text). It produces two outputs: the learned encoder representations for downstream tasks, and a per-anchor threshold vector λ ∈ ℝⁿ maintained throughout training. The thresholds are initialized to 1 (no false negatives) and updated only for anchors appearing in the current mini-batch, so the per-iteration cost is O(B²) regardless of dataset size n. A warm-up phase of standard contrastive training (e.g., 70 epochs) is required before GLOFND activates, ensuring embeddings are sufficiently structured for meaningful similarity-based thresholding.

## Method

GLOFND learns a per-anchor threshold λ_i by solving a convex quantile optimization problem (minimizing να + (1/|R|)Σ(r−ν)₊ over ν ∈ [−1,1]) via stochastic subgradient descent, where the subgradient is estimated from the mini-batch similarities alone. This makes the threshold track the (1−α)-quantile of the anchor's similarity distribution over the full dataset without ever computing all pairwise similarities. The modified contrastive loss then excludes from the negative set any sample whose similarity to the anchor exceeds λ_i, effectively filtering false negatives on the fly. The overall problem is a stochastic bilevel optimization (thresholds at the lower level, encoder weights at the upper level), but the hypergradient of λ with respect to w is ignored, following the MAML convention. A moving-average estimator (inherited from SogCLR) approximates the partition function over the filtered negative set, enabling small-batch training. The hyperparameter α controls the fraction of negatives treated as false negatives, adapting to the desired semantic granularity of the learned representations.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 semi-supervised linear evaluation, GLOFND achieves 63.36% average top-1 accuracy (vs. 61.66% for SogCLR and 62.60% for SogCLR+FNC), with the largest gain at 0.1% labeled data (37.38% vs. 34.94%). For false negative identification, GLOFND reaches 53.10% F1-score, substantially outperforming FNC's 36.42%. In transfer learning across 8 datasets, GLOFND achieves 64.31% average accuracy (+1.64% over SogCLR). In bimodal CLIP-style training on CC3M, GLOFND improves the Datacomp average from 24.87 to 25.37 (SogCLR) and from 24.76 to 25.37 (FastCLIP), while adding only ~2% per-epoch computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SogCLR (Yuan et al., 2022), GLOFND consistently improves all downstream metrics: +1.70% average semi-supervised accuracy, +1.64% transfer learning accuracy, and +0.50% Datacomp in the bimodal setting, while adding only 2% per-epoch overhead (435s vs. 427s). Against FNC (Huynh et al., 2022), GLOFND achieves a 16.68% higher F1-score in false negative identification (53.10% vs. 36.42%) and outperforms it by 0.76% in semi-supervised and 0.91% in transfer learning; in the bimodal setting, FNC actually degrades both SogCLR (24.87→24.55) and FastCLIP (24.76→24.63) Datacomp scores, whereas GLOFND improves both to 25.37. Against FastCLIP (Wei et al., 2024), GLOFND raises the Datacomp average from 24.76 to 25.37 and IN&Variants from 19.08 to 19.38, while FNC degrades both metrics. Statistical significance testing (paired t-test) confirms GLOFND's improvements over SogCLR are significant at the 1% level on 100% and 1% labeled data, and significant on 6 of 8 transfer learning datasets (vs. only 2 of 8 for FNC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the false-negative discovery problem in contrastive learning from the local (mini-batch) setting addressed by FNC (Huynh et al., 2022) and WCL (Zheng et al., 2021) to a global (dataset-wide) setting, while avoiding the expensive full-dataset clustering required by IFND (Chen et al., 2022). The core mechanism draws on the quantile-optimization formulation of Ogryczak & Tamir (2003) to learn per-anchor thresholds via SGD, and is integrated as a drop-in module into SogCLR's global contrastive loss (Yuan et al., 2022) and CLIP-style bimodal losses (Radford et al., 2021). Unlike prior methods that fix a top-k count or manually schedule a similarity threshold, GLOFND treats the threshold as a learned parameter updated alongside the encoder, making it adaptive to the evolving embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

False negatives are a well-recognized source of representation degradation in contrastive learning, yet existing remedies are either batch-limited (missing global structure) or computationally prohibitive at scale. GLOFND's 2% overhead and batch-size independence make it a practical, scalable addition to any similarity-based CL pipeline, and its demonstrated compatibility with unimodal, bimodal, and semi-supervised settings broadens its applicability. Open questions include whether the detected false negatives can be exploited more productively than simple filtering (e.g., as soft positives), whether α can be individualized per sample, and whether the approach transfers to non-contrastive SSL objectives. The paper acknowledges that benefits depend on the proportion of false negatives in the pretraining data and the granularity of the downstream task.
