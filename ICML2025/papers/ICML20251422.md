## Problem

Offline preference alignment methods such as DPO, KTO, Step-DPO, and Step-KTO were each designed to address a specific limitation (paired vs. unpaired data, answer vs. step-level annotations, token-level credit assignment) but lack a unified theoretical understanding. This paper asks whether these diverse algorithms can be derived from a single probabilistic principle, and whether a principled formulation can simultaneously handle all data settings without additional computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251422/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-answer pairs (x, y) with binary correctness labels c ∈ {0,1} at either answer-level (one label per response) or step-level (a label per token, derived from MCTS Q-values or LLM-as-a-judge). It operates on unpaired data by default, though paired data can be decoupled into individual samples. The output is a fine-tuned autoregressive transformer (7B-scale, LoRA rank 64) that generates text answers, with an auxiliary scalar value head g^θ(x, y_{<t}) providing per-token correctness estimates usable for inference-time search.

## Method

PIPA reformulates preference alignment as maximum likelihood estimation of the label likelihood log p^θ(c|x,y) subject to a prior constraint, rather than as offline RL. Two variants are derived: PIPA-M constrains the marginal p^θ(y|x) to match a frozen prior model, while PIPA-N constrains the negative conditional p^θ(y|x,c=0) to the prior. Via Bayes' rule, the label likelihood decomposes into a learnable next-token model f^θ(y_t|x,y_{<t}) and a learnable value head g^θ(x,y_{<t}), with the prior model in the denominator, yielding a simple per-token log-likelihood loss that requires no paired data and no KL estimation step. DPO and KTO are proven to be special cases under different prior choices and loss functions. The token-level factorization p(c|x,y)=∏_t p(c_t|x,y_{≤t}) seamlessly extends the framework to step-level annotations, and the jointly trained g^θ provides natural token-level credit assignment without any additional training stage.

## Results

PIPA achieves 3–10% absolute improvement over all baselines on GSM8K and MATH across four data settings (paired/unpaired × answer/step-level) and two base models (DeepSeek-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B). The best PIPA-N configuration reaches 80.97% on GSM8K and 52.78% on MATH (unpaired, step-wise, DeepSeek), while PIPA-M peaks at 74.91% GSM8K and 53.26% MATH (paired, step-wise, Qwen). These gains are achieved without additional training stages or computational overhead relative to DPO or KTO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIPA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every setting. Against DPO (paired, answer-wise, DeepSeek), PIPA-N improves GSM8K from 68.39 to 80.29 (+11.9 pts) and MATH from 46.94 to 52.32 (+5.4 pts); against IPO the gains are similar (+11.2 GSM8K, +2.4–5.4 MATH). Against KTO (unpaired, answer-wise, DeepSeek), PIPA-N lifts GSM8K from 76.04 to 80.97 (+4.9 pts) and MATH from 46.72 to 52.22 (+5.5 pts). In step-wise settings, PIPA-M surpasses Step-DPO by +10.6 pts on GSM8K (74.91 vs 66.11, Qwen) and +4.9 pts on MATH (53.26 vs 48.38), and outperforms Step-KTO by +3.4 pts GSM8K and +7.5 pts MATH (Qwen, paired step-wise). Notably, PIPA-N tends to lead on DeepSeek (self-alignment) while PIPA-M leads on Qwen (cross-model alignment), and neither variant is uniformly dominant.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIPA repositions offline preference alignment from the dominant offline-RL framing (used by DPO and KTO) to a maximum-likelihood-estimation problem with prior constraints, showing that DPO and KTO are special cases that differ only in which probability they pin to a prior (marginal vs. negative conditional) and what loss they use. PIPA-N is derived directly from KTO by replacing its heuristic KL-based class-ratio assumption with a learnable value head g^θ, while PIPA-M changes the prior constraint from the negative conditional to the marginal distribution. The framework also subsumes step-level variants (Step-DPO, Step-KTO) by simply decomposing the label autoregressively at the token level, something prior methods handled ad hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified MLE-with-priors lens gives practitioners a principled way to design new alignment losses by choosing different prior constraints, rather than deriving each variant from a separate RL or prospect-theoretic argument. The jointly trained value model g^θ, which learns a per-token correctness probability, opens a concrete path toward inference-time search (e.g., beam search guided by learned step values) that neither DPO nor KTO provides. However, the evaluation is confined to 7B math-reasoning models on GSM8K and MATH with a single offline dataset, so generalization to broader tasks, larger scales, and iterative/online settings remains untested. The finding that step-level annotation helps MATH but can hurt GSM8K, and that training separate 'accurate' priors did not improve over the SFT model, are useful negative results that temper expectations for the framework's flexibility.
