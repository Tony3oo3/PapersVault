## Problem

Existing analyses treat Noisy SIGNSGD's privacy guarantee as identical to the underlying Gaussian mechanism via the post-processing property of differential privacy, ignoring that the sign(·) operation discards magnitude information and should amplify privacy. Additionally, prior work (Jang et al., 2024) claims logistic noise outperforms Gaussian noise for sign-based compressors based on sign-flipping probability, but this metric is misleading for heterogeneous data where vanilla signSGD (zero sign-flipping) fails to converge. The paper makes the privacy amplification of sign(·) rigorous and re-evaluates the Gaussian-vs-logistic comparison under a proper estimation-error criterion.

## Input / Output

Each worker computes per-example gradients on a mini-batch, clips them to a bounded norm c, adds independent Gaussian noise N(0, σ_DP²·I), and transmits only the element-wise sign (1 bit per coordinate, yielding 32× communication reduction over full-precision gradients). The server aggregates via either coordinate-wise averaging (Scheme I) or majority voting (Scheme II) and updates the global model. The privacy guarantee is expressed as an f-DP tradeoff function, which is tighter than the (ε,δ)-DP bound obtained by naively applying post-processing to the Gaussian mechanism.

## Method

The paper derives the exact f-DP tradeoff function of a generic NoisySign compressor using the Neyman-Pearson lemma, showing the sign(·) step yields a piecewise-linear tradeoff function that dominates the Gaussian mechanism's tradeoff function (i.e., strictly better privacy at every type-I error rate). In the high-dimensional limit, the G-NoisySign mechanism converges to μ-GDP with μ = 2C/(σ√(π/2)), a √(π/2) improvement over the vanilla Gaussian mechanism, which via the GDP composition rule translates to π/2 ≈ 1.57× more training steps at the same overall privacy budget. The paper further shows that, at matched privacy, G-NoisySign has a smaller cubic estimation error than L-NoisySign (|Δ_G| = |x|³/(6σ²) vs. |Δ_L| = |x|³/(12s²)), making Gaussian noise more suitable for heterogeneous distributed learning. Convergence is established for both averaging and majority-voting aggregation under standard smoothness, bounded-variance, and bounded-gradient assumptions, yielding O(1/μ_T) neighborhood convergence that matches state-of-the-art DP-SGD rates.

## Results

On Fashion-MNIST (α=0.1, 100 workers), G-NoisySign achieves 45.24% test accuracy at the strictest privacy budget (μ=0.04) and 79.57% at μ=1.6, closely matching the classic Gaussian mechanism (DP-SGD) at 43.78% and 79.90% respectively. On CIFAR-10 (α=1, 30 workers), G-NoisySign reaches 40.28% at μ=0.8 and 66.82% at μ=16, versus 40.77% and 67.53% for DP-SGD. Across all settings, Noisy SIGNSGD with either G-NoisySign or L-NoisySign attains performance within ~1–2% of the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism while reducing per-coordinate communication from 32 bits to 1 bit (a 32× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classic Gaussian mechanism (DP-SGD, Abadi et al. 2016), G-NoisySign achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at the strictest privacy budgets (e.g., 45.24% vs. 43.78% at μ=0.04 on Fashion-MNIST) and is within 0.3–0.7% at looser budgets, confirming that discarding gradient magnitudes does not meaningfully degrade the privacy-utility tradeoff. Against L-NoisySign (Jang et al. 2024), G-NoisySign shows a modest advantage at high-privacy settings on Fashion-MNIST (45.24% vs. 42.52% at μ=0.04), consistent with the paper's theoretical result that G-NoisySign has a smaller estimation error than L-NoisySign under the same privacy guarantee. The majority-vote aggregation variants (G-NoisySign-VOTE, L-NoisySign-VOTE) incur a small additional degradation of less than 2% in most settings. The paper's key theoretical contribution is that the sign(·) compressor provides a √(π/2) ≈ 1.25× privacy amplification over the raw Gaussian mechanism, meaning G-NoisySign allows ~π/2 ≈ 1.57× more training steps for the same overall privacy budget—a benefit that Jang et al.'s logistic-noise analysis did not capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signSGD line of work (Bernstein et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020b) by rigorously quantifying the privacy amplification effect of the deterministic sign(·) operator through the f-DP framework (Dong et al., 2021), rather than relying on the post-processing property that would suggest no privacy gain. It directly departs from Jang et al. (2024, ICML), which recommended logistic noise over Gaussian noise for sign-based compressors based on sign-flipping probability, by showing that once the privacy amplification from discarding magnitudes is properly accounted for, Gaussian noise actually yields smaller estimation error under the same privacy guarantee. The analysis also connects to the broader privacy-amplification-by-compression literature (Chen et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2023) but specializes to the sign-based compressor and provides tight scalar-case guarantees via the Neyman-Pearson lemma.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that G-NoisySign achieves a √(π/2) privacy improvement over the vanilla Gaussian mechanism (translating to ~1.5× more training steps at the same privacy budget) while maintaining 32× communication efficiency provides a concrete, theoretically grounded recipe for privacy-preserving distributed learning at scale. The correction to Jang et al.'s recommendation is practically important for practitioners choosing noise distributions for sign-based federated learning. Open questions include the impact of gradient clipping on convergence (deferred to future work), whether the privacy amplification from majority voting on the server side can be further quantified, and whether the analysis extends to more complex compression schemes beyond the sign operator.
