## Problem

Despite LoRA's widespread adoption for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, its optimization dynamics remain poorly understood: it is unclear which subspaces the low-rank adapters converge to and how gradient updates evolve over training. This lack of theory has led to heuristic variants (e.g., LoRA-GA, LoRA-SB) that misidentify the correct alignment subspace and can fail to converge. The paper asks whether a single full-gradient step at initialization contains enough information to both characterize and exploit the correct subspace structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained weight matrix W^natural, a small sampled batch B (as few as 8 samples in experiments) to compute the one-step full gradient G^natural, and standard LoRA hyperparameters (rank r, alpha, learning rate). It produces the fine-tuned weight W^natural + (α/√r)·A_T·B_T, where A_T ∈ R^{d×r} and B_T ∈ R^{r×k} are the trained low-rank adapters. Notably, the one-step gradient is computed once at initialization and then discarded; all subsequent training uses only the downstream batches with standard AdamW, so the memory and time overhead over vanilla LoRA is negligible (e.g., 21.7 GB vs. 21.6 GB on MetaMathQA).

## Method

The paper proves that under gradient descent with standard LoRA initialization, B_t is confined to the right top-r* singular subspace of the one-step full gradient G^natural from the first step, while A_t aligns with the left top-r* singular subspace after O(log d / λ_{r*}(G^natural)) steps. Leveraging this, LoRA-One performs a spectral initialization: it computes the SVD of G^natural and sets A_0 = √γ·[Ũ_{G^natural}]_{:,1:r}·[S̃_{G^natural}^{1/2}]_{1:r} and B_0 = √γ·[S̃_{G^natural}^{1/2}]_{1:r}·[Ṽ_{G^natural}]_{:,1:r}^T, achieving subspace alignment immediately and ensuring ‖A_0B_0 − Δ‖_F is small. The paper then establishes linear convergence of ‖A_tB_t − Δ‖_F under this initialization for both linear and nonlinear (ReLU) models, and shows that adding preconditioners (right-multiplying gradients by (B_tB_t^T)^{-1} and (A_t^T A_t)^{-1}) removes the dependence on the condition number κ(Δ). The resulting algorithm (Algorithm 1) uses this spectral init followed by standard AdamW training, and a preconditioned variant (LoRA-One-P, Algorithm 2) further improves robustness to suboptimal learning rates.

## Results

On the GLUE subset (T5 base, rank 8), LoRA-One achieves 88.73% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (82.72%), LoRA-GA (87.77%), and LoRA-Pro (88.44%). On NLG benchmarks with LLaMA 2-7B, LoRA-One reaches 60.44% on GSM8K (direct), 47.24% on MMLU, and 28.66% PASS@1 on HumanEval, surpassing LoRA by 1.18, 1.51, and 2.81 points respectively. Training time and memory costs are nearly identical to standard LoRA (e.g., 6h23m/21.7GB vs 6h20m/21.6GB on MetaMathQA). On small-scale NLU tasks (CoLA, MRPC), the one-step spectral initialization alone matches full LoRA performance in under one second.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-One (88.73% avg) outperforms all seven baselines on the GLUE subset, with the largest gains over standard LoRA (+6.01 pts) and LoRA+ (+3.78 pts). Against the closest competitor LoRA-Pro (88.44%), LoRA-One wins on SST-2 (94.53 vs 94.19), CoLA (82.04 vs 81.94), and MRPC (87.83 vs 86.60), while LoRA-Pro edges ahead on MNLI (86.03 vs 85.89) and QNLI (93.42 vs 93.37); the paper attributes this to LoRA-Pro computing full fine-tuning gradients at every step at significant extra FLOP and memory cost (43.87 GB vs 21.7 GB). On NLG benchmarks (Table 3), LoRA-One dominates LoRA-GA across all four tasks (e.g., 55.88% vs 46.07% on GSM8K-CoT, 28.66% vs 26.95% on HumanEval), and also beats LoRA on every task. In the toy linear experiments (Figure 4), both LoRA-GA and LoRA-SB fail to reach global minimizers under under-ranked and over-ranked settings, while LoRA-One converges to lower risk. On full MetaMathQA (395K, 4 epochs, Figure 5), LoRA-One consistently leads LoRA, LoRA+, and LoRA-GA across all epochs, with LoRA-GA showing only marginal gains over the other two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by providing the first rigorous characterization of how LoRA's gradient descent dynamics align with the singular subspaces of the one-step full fine-tuning gradient, using a Schur decomposition approach that decouples the spectral behavior of A_t and B_t individually—something prior symmetrization techniques from matrix sensing (Stöger & Soltanolkotabi, 2021; Soltanolkotabi et al., 2023) could not achieve. It positions itself against gradient-alignment-based variants like LoRA-GA (Wang et al., 2024) and LoRA-Pro (Wang et al., 2025), showing that LoRA-GA's initialization actually targets the wrong singular subspace for B, and against preconditioned matrix factorization methods (Zhang et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2021) by incorporating preconditioners into the LoRA update to eliminate condition-number dependence. The one-step gradient sufficiency result connects to a broader line of work showing that a single gradient step captures meaningful feature structure (Ba et al., 2022; Moniri et al., 2024), but applies this insight specifically to low-rank adapter initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded initialization (Spectral-init) that achieves subspace alignment immediately rather than after O(log d / λ_{r*}) steps, and a preconditioned variant (LoRA-One-P) that is robust to suboptimal learning rates—both practically useful for practitioners tuning LoRA hyperparameters. However, the theory is established for linear models and single-layer ReLU networks under Gaussian/sub-Gaussian data, leaving open whether the alignment and convergence guarantees extend to the multi-layer, multi-head architectures of real LLMs. The correction of LoRA-GA's subspace misalignment is a useful clarification for the community, but the empirical gains over strong baselines (1–3 points) are moderate, and the low-rank assumption on Δ (Rank(Δ) = r* < min{d,k}) may not always hold in practice. Open questions include whether the spectral initialization principle generalizes to multi-layer interactions and whether the preconditioning benefit persists under Adam-family optimizers at scale.
