## Problem

The sparse variational GP (SVGP) framework assumes the variational posterior has the form q(f,u) = p(f|u)q(u), where p(f|u) is the conditional GP prior. While this structural constraint enables O(NM²) computations, it causes underfitting bias—such as overestimating noise variance—when the number of inducing points is limited relative to the training set. The paper asks whether this particular form is truly necessary for scalability and demonstrates that it can be relaxed to obtain tighter bounds without increasing computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard GP regression inputs (training data X, y, kernel hyperparameters, noise variance, and M inducing points) and produces predictive means and variances at test points along with a tighter evidence lower bound. The key addition is N per-data-point variational parameters v_i that are analytically optimized in closed form, requiring only the diagonal elements of K_ff − Q_ff. For non-Gaussian likelihoods these reduce to a single scalar v, and the entire method requires only a minor code modification to existing SVGP implementations—replacing a per-point trace term with a per-point log term.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the conditional GP prior p(f|u) in the variational distribution with a more general q(f|u) whose covariance is (K_ff − Q_ff)^{1/2} V (K_ff − Q_ff)^{1/2}, where V is a diagonal matrix of N positive variational parameters. The key insight is that V approximates the inverse (I + (1/σ²)(K_ff − Q_ff))^{-1} by zeroing out off-diagonal elements, making q(f|u) a closer approximation to the true posterior p(f|u, y). Both the KL divergence and the likelihood expectation decompose into per-data-point terms computable in O(NM²) time, enabling analytic optimization over each v_i. Substituting the optimal v_i* = (1 + (k_ii − q_ii)/σ²)^{-1} yields a collapsed bound where the trace regularization term −(1/2σ²)tr(K_ff − Q_ff) is replaced by the strictly tighter −(1/2)Σ log(1 + (k_ii − q_ii)/σ²), exploiting the inequality log(a+1) ≤ a. For non-Gaussian likelihoods, a spherical V = vI maintains O(NM²) tractability, and stochastic minibatch training requires only the same per-point log substitution in the uncollapsed bound.

## Results

On 8 large-scale regression datasets (ranging from 25k to 1.3M points), SVGP-new with M=2048 inducing points achieves test log-likelihoods of 0.286 (Kin40k), -0.938 (Protein), 1.051 (KeggDirected), 0.703 (KEGGU), -0.651 (3dRoad), -1.190 (Song), -0.060 (Buzz), and 1.421 (HouseElectric), consistently outperforming the standard SVGP bound. On medium-size datasets (~10k points), SGPR-new achieves test log-likelihoods of 0.998 (Pol) and 2.511 (Bike) with M=2048, approaching the exact GP values of 1.089 and 3.105 respectively. For Poisson regression on NYBikes with only M=8 inducing points, SVGP-new achieves -9.713 test log-likelihood versus -36.397 for standard SVGP, a dramatic improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVGP-new consistently outperforms the standard SVGP bound (Hensman et al., 2013) across all 8 large-scale regression datasets, with the largest gains on Kin40k (0.286 vs 0.237) and Buzz (-0.060 vs -0.063). On medium-size datasets, SGPR-new beats both SGPR (Titsias, 2009) and SGPR-artemev (Artemev et al., 2021), e.g., achieving 0.998 vs 0.958 vs 0.976 on Pol and 2.511 vs 2.337 vs 2.356 on Bike with M=2048. Against the stronger baselines ODVGP (Salimbeni et al., 2018) and SOLVE-GP (Shi et al., 2020), SVGP-new is competitive or superior on most datasets, though SOLVE-GP retains an edge on KeggDirected (0.973 vs 1.051 is actually SVGP-new winning) and KEGGU (0.680 vs 0.703 is SVGP-new winning). For Poisson regression, SVGP-new dramatically outperforms SVGP with few inducing points (M=8: -9.713 vs -36.397 on NYBikes), approaching the full GP value of -5.061, while standard SVGP at M=8 is far from the full GP. The improvement requires only a minor code modification (replacing a linear trace term with a log term) at no additional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the sparse variational GP (SVGP) framework of Titsias (2009) and its stochastic extension by Hensman et al. (2013), which impose the conditional GP prior p(f|u) as a fixed factor in the variational distribution. The key departure is showing that this assumption, long considered fundamental, can be relaxed for training by replacing p(f|u) with a more general q(f|u) parameterized by N extra diagonal variational parameters that better approximate the true posterior covariance p(f|u,y). The resulting collapsed bound is strictly tighter than both the original SVGP bound and the spherical-constraint bound of Artemev et al. (2021), which is shown to be a special case of the proposed method. The approach is also positioned relative to Bui et al. (2017)'s power EP framework, with which it shares structural similarities but differs in the specific regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that GP practitioners can reduce underfitting bias in hyperparameter estimation (particularly noise variance) with only a one-line code change in existing sparse GP implementations, while maintaining identical O(NM²) computational cost and stochastic optimization compatibility. The extension to non-Gaussian likelihoods (Poisson, Bernoulli) broadens applicability beyond conjugate regression. Open questions include whether the method extends cleanly to multi-output GPs, deep GPs, and uncertain-input models, and whether the tighter bound improves theoretical convergence guarantees for sparse variational inference. The paper also notes concurrent work by Bui et al. (2025) arriving at similar approximations, suggesting this is a timely direction in the field.
