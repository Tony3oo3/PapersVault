## Problem

Existing stochastic zeroth-order optimization methods require carefully tuned step sizes (depending on Lipschitz constants and iteration budgets) and smoothing parameters (depending on target accuracy), making them sensitive to hyperparameter choices. While parameter-free methods like DoG have been developed for first-order optimization, extending them to the zeroth-order setting is non-trivial because both the step size and the smoothing parameter must be made adaptive while preserving optimal dependence on problem dimension.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only stochastic function evaluations at two points per iteration (x_t + μ_t v_t and x_t − μ_t v_t), an initial point x₀ in a compact convex set, a small initial movement r_ε ∈ (0, D_X], and a total iteration budget T. It outputs a weighted iterate average x̄_{τ_T} where the averaging weights are r̄_k/r̄_t and the index τ_T is selected to maximize the cumulative weight ratio, rather than simply returning the final iterate. The key constraint is that no gradient information is available—only function values from a stochastic zeroth-order oracle.

## Method

POEM builds on projected SGD with two adaptive mechanisms: a step size η_t = r̄_t/√G_t (where r̄_t is the maximum distance from the initial point and G_t is the cumulative squared finite-difference norm, inspired by DoG) and an adaptive smoothing parameter μ_t = r̄_t√(d/(t+1)) that is generally larger than those in prior methods, improving numerical stability. The gradient is estimated via a two-point finite difference along a random direction on the unit sphere, with the smoothing parameter appearing in the denominator. The theoretical analysis decomposes the optimality gap into weighted regret, gradient noise, and smoothing noise, establishing a near-optimal SZO complexity of Õ(dL²D_X²/ε²) that matches the known lower bound, with the initialization r_ε affecting rates only logarithmically. For unbounded domains, the paper shows that an ideal fully parameter-free algorithm is provably impossible, requiring at least an overestimate of the Lipschitz constant.

## Results

POEM achieves a near-optimal stochastic zeroth-order oracle (SZO) complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dL^2D_{\mathcal{X}}^2/\epsilon^2)$ for bounded domains, matching the lower bound established by Duchi et al. (2015). For unbounded domains, the complexity improves to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dL^2s_0^2/\epsilon^2)$, which is better than the $\mathcal{O}(d^2L^2s_0^2/\epsilon^2)$ rate of Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017). The initialization parameter $r_\epsilon$ affects convergence only through a logarithmic factor $\log_+(D_{\mathcal{X}}/r_\epsilon)$. In numerical experiments on mushrooms, a9a, and w8a, POEM converges faster than both TPGE and TPBCO under their theoretical parameter settings, while matching the performance of their well-tuned variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POEM is compared against three existing stochastic zeroth-order methods: TPGE (Duchi et al., 2015), TPBCO (Shamir, 2017), and RSNSO (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017). In terms of theoretical complexity, POEM matches the near-optimal $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dL^2D_{\mathcal{X}}^2/\epsilon^2)$ rate of TPGE and TPBCO while being the only parameter-free method in the comparison. In numerical experiments on mushrooms, a9a, and w8a, POEM converges faster than both TPGE-T and TPBCO-T (theoretical parameter settings) and achieves performance comparable to their well-tuned empirical variants (TPGE-E, TPBCO-E). A key practical advantage is that POEM exhibits significantly greater stability across parameter settings: when $r_\epsilon \leq R = 1$, varying $r_\epsilon$ over $\{10^{-7}, \ldots, 10^2\}$ has negligible impact on the final function value, whereas baselines are highly sensitive to their $1/L$ tuning parameter. For unbounded domains, POEM's $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dL^2s_0^2/\epsilon^2)$ rate strictly improves upon RSNSO's $\mathcal{O}(d^2L^2s_0^2/\epsilon^2)$ by removing one factor of $d$.

## Relation to Prior Work

POEM extends the parameter-free optimization paradigm from first-order methods (notably DoG by Ivgi et al., 2023a, which schedules step sizes via distance-over-gradients) to the zeroth-order setting, where one must additionally adapt the smoothing parameter in the finite-difference gradient estimator. The randomized smoothing framework and gradient norm bounds are inherited from Shamir (2017), while the weighted-regret analysis and concentration inequalities follow the DoG toolkit. The key conceptual departure is that zeroth-order methods introduce a second tunable hyperparameter (the smoothing parameter μ_t) and a dimension-dependent noise structure that have no first-order analogue, requiring a joint adaptation strategy rather than a simple step-size schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POEM is the first parameter-free zeroth-order method to achieve near-optimal stochastic zeroth-order oracle complexity, removing the need to tune both step size and smoothing parameter—practically important in bandit optimization, adversarial training, and black-box settings where gradient access is unavailable. The impossibility result for unbounded domains (Theorem 5.6) draws a sharp theoretical boundary: without a bounded domain, no fully parameter-free zeroth-order algorithm can achieve near-optimal rates, which clarifies the role of domain geometry in adaptive optimization. Open questions include extending POEM to minimax, bilevel, and finite-sum problems, and whether the bounded-domain requirement can be relaxed with weaker assumptions than a Lipschitz overestimate.
