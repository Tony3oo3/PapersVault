## Problem

LoRA constrains optimization to a low-rank subspace, and a minimum that appears flat in this restricted space can still be sharp when viewed in the full parameter space where merged weights actually operate at inference. Existing sharpness-aware approaches like SAM are impractical for the full parameter space because they double training cost and require O(m×n) extra memory to store perturbations, contradicting the parameter-efficiency goal of LoRA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251356/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained model (T5, CLIP ViT, Llama 2, SDXL) and optimizes only low-rank matrices A and B per linear layer, with task-specific inputs (text sequences, 224×224 RGB images, or text prompts for generation). At inference, the low-rank update ΔW = BA is merged into the pre-trained weights W, so the output is a standard model with no architectural change. The only additional state stored during training is a random seed and per-filter L2 norms (O(m) values per layer), not the full perturbation matrix.

## Method

Flat-LoRA replaces the SAM min-max objective with a Bayesian expected loss: min_{A,B} E_{ε_W ~ N(0, σ²/n · ||W'_i,:||²)} L(W + BA + ε_W), which smooths the loss landscape in the full parameter space without requiring an extra gradient step. The perturbation is generated per-filter with variance proportional to the filter's squared L2 norm and inversely proportional to the input dimension n, ensuring the forward-pass variance increase (by a factor of 1+σ²) is independent of layer width. A key theoretical insight is that LoRA-SAM's effective perturbation on W reduces to c·(∇_W L)·AᵀA when B is small, meaning it only optimizes sharpness within the column space of A—a tiny subspace—whereas Flat-LoRA perturbs the full merged weight matrix. Memory overhead is O(m) per layer (storing seeds and filter norms) versus O(m×n) for SAM, and training time remains 1× that of vanilla LoRA rather than 2×.

## Results

On Llama 2-7B fine-tuned with LoRA (r=8), Flat-LoRA achieves 60.65% on GSM8K (+3.18% over LoRA's 57.47%), 27.93% PASS@1 on HumanEval (+3.08% over 24.85%), and 6.16 on MT-Bench (+0.20 over 5.96). On T5-Base GLUE tasks, Flat-LoRA (r=16) improves average accuracy by 0.57% over LoRA, with the largest gains on smaller datasets (CoLA +1.19%, MRPC +0.94%). On CLIP ViT-B/32 image classification, Flat-LoRA (r=8) surpasses both LoRA (r=16) and full fine-tuning by 0.28% on average. Out-of-domain robustness on CIFAR-100-C improves from +1.38% at corruption level 1 to +3.56% at level 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flat-LoRA consistently outperforms standard LoRA across all evaluated tasks and model scales, with the largest gains on Llama 2-7B (+3.18% GSM8K, +3.08% HumanEval) and smaller datasets (CoLA +1.19%, MRPC +0.94% at r=16). Against SAM applied to the full parameter space (LoRA+SAM on W), Flat-LoRA achieves comparable or better accuracy (83.61 vs 83.67 on CoLA, 89.59 vs 89.26 on MRPC) while requiring only O(m) additional memory and 1× training time versus O(m×n) memory and 2× time. Against SAM applied to LoRA parameters only (LoRA+SAM on A,B), Flat-LoRA clearly wins (83.61 vs 83.31 on CoLA, 89.59 vs 88.98 on MRPC), confirming that optimizing sharpness in the LoRA subspace is insufficient. Among LoRA variants (PiSSA, LoRA-GA, DoRA, AdaLoRA, LoRA+), Flat-LoRA leads on both CoLA (83.61) and MRPC (89.59), and the flat-loss objective can be stacked on top of these variants for further gains (e.g., Flat-DoRA reaches 89.99 on MRPC). Against the most recent methods (oBAR, nBAR, LoRA-Pro, GaLore, CoRDA), Flat-LoRA is competitive: CoRDA edges ahead on MRPC (89.76 vs 89.59) but Flat-LoRA leads on CoLA (83.61 vs 83.38), while LoRA-Pro is slightly ahead on MRPC (89.23) but behind on CoLA (83.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: low-rank adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and flat-minima optimization (SAM, Entropy-SGD, random weight perturbation). The key conceptual departure is showing that applying SAM to LoRA parameters (LoRA-SAM) only optimizes sharpness within the column space of A, a small subspace of the full parameter space, and thus cannot guarantee flatness of the merged weights used at inference. Rather than adopting SAM's min-max formulation (which doubles training cost and requires O(m×n) extra memory for full-space perturbations), the authors replace the adversarial maximization with a Bayesian expected loss under filter-wise Gaussian perturbations, drawing on the RWP literature (Bisla et al., 2022) but adapting it specifically to the LoRA setting with seed-based storage for memory efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for PEFT practitioners: Flat-LoRA adds only ~0.12 GB memory and ~11 minutes of training time over vanilla LoRA while consistently improving in-domain and out-of-domain generalization across NLU, vision, LLM, and diffusion-model tasks. The insight that LoRA's restricted optimization space can produce solutions that are sharp in the full parameter space reframes how one should think about PEFT quality — it is not enough to find a good solution in the adapter space; the merged weights must sit in a flat region. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other PEFT paradigms (e.g., adapters, prefix tuning), how to automatically select the perturbation strength σ per layer or per model scale, and whether the benefits persist under extremely low-rank regimes (r=1) for very large models.
