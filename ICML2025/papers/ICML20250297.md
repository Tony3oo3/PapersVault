## Problem

Prior adaptive weighting algorithms for differentially private partition selection (e.g., PolicyGaussian, GreedyUpdate) are inherently sequential and require in-memory processing, making them infeasible for datasets with hundreds of billions of entries. The only known parallelizable approach—basic uniform weighting—is lossy because it overallocates weight to high-frequency items, missing opportunities to boost less frequent items closer to the privacy threshold. This paper asks whether an adaptive, non-uniform weighting scheme can be designed that is both amenable to massively parallel implementation and provably superior to basic weighting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a collection of user sets (user-item pairs) over an unbounded item universe, privacy parameters (ε, δ), and a degree cap Δ₀ that triggers random subsampling of any user set exceeding Δ₀ items. It outputs a privatized subset of the union of all user sets along with a noisy weight vector over the universe; however, the noisy weight vector is only materialized on items in the true union and is never released as a final output—it serves as an internal signal for the two-round variant. The entire computation requires constant parallel rounds and linear work in the total number of user-item pairs, fitting the Massively Parallel Computation model without assuming the input fits in a single machine's memory.

## Method

MAD (MaxAdaptiveDegree) adaptively reroutes overallocated weight from high-frequency items to less frequent ones while preserving the same ℓ₂ and novel ℓ∞ sensitivities as basic uniform weighting, thus using identical noise σ and threshold ρ. Concretely, each adaptive user first sends a small uniform weight 1/|S_u| per item; items exceeding an adaptive threshold τ = ρ + βσ are truncated, and the excess is returned to users proportionally; users then reroute a carefully chosen fraction α of this excess back to their items, and finally add the remaining 1/√|S_u| − 1/|S_u| uniformly. This design provably stochastically dominates basic weighting: every item below τ gains weight, while items above τ retain at least τ before noise. MAD2R extends this to two rounds by splitting the privacy budget, removing items found in round 1, discarding items whose noisy round-1 weight upper bound falls below the round-2 threshold, and biasing round-2 weights downward for items whose noisy round-1 lower bound already exceeds the threshold—thereby concentrating the ℓ₂ budget on harder-to-output items.

## Results

The primary metric is the number of items output (output size) under user-level DP with ε=1, δ=10⁻⁵, and Δ₀=100. Among parallel algorithms, MAD2R achieves the best output size on 7 of 9 datasets, with gains of up to 9.5% over DP-SIPS (e.g., 3,369 vs 3,076 on IMDb). On the largest dataset (Common Crawl, 800 billion entries), MAD2R outputs 29.4M items versus 15.7M for Basic and 28.3M for DP-SIPS. On smaller datasets where sequential baselines are feasible, MAD2R outputs at least 86% of the items produced by the best sequential algorithm (PolicyGaussian).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Basic (Korolova et al., 2009), MAD always achieves a higher or equal output size across all 9 datasets, consistent with the proven stochastic dominance guarantee (e.g., 4,162 vs 4,062 on Reddit; 15.8M vs 15.7M on Common Crawl). Against DP-SIPS (Swanberg et al., 2023), MAD2R wins on 7 of 9 datasets with improvements up to 9.5% (IMDb: 3,369 vs 3,076) and 3.7% on Common Crawl (29.4M vs 28.3M); the two exceptions are Higgs (where MAD single-round wins) and Clueweb (where DP-SIPS edges ahead by 0.6%). Against the sequential baselines PolicyGaussian (Gopi et al., 2020) and GreedyUpdate (Carvalho et al., 2022), MAD2R is consistently competitive but does not match the best sequential result on any small dataset—PolicyGaussian leads on 6 of 7 smaller datasets, while GreedyUpdate is highly dataset-dependent (best on Higgs and Finance, worst on IMDb and Wiki). The key practical advantage is scalability: sequential algorithms cannot complete on Clueweb (43B entries) or Common Crawl (800B entries) within 16 hours, whereas all parallel algorithms finish within 4 hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-and-threshold framework for differentially private partition selection introduced by Korolova et al. (2009), which uses basic uniform weighting and is the only prior approach amenable to massively parallel implementation. It departs from sequential greedy weighting algorithms (Gopi et al. 2020, Carvalho et al. 2022) that achieve better utility but require in-memory, single-machine processing. The closest prior work is DP-SIPS (Swanberg et al. 2023), which iterates basic weighting across multiple rounds; MAD2R refines this by leveraging noisy weights from prior rounds to bias the adaptive reweighting in subsequent rounds, a capability enabled by a generalized sensitivity analysis of the meta-algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MAD and MAD2R scale to datasets with 800 billion user-item pairs (three orders of magnitude beyond prior sequential work), making private partition selection feasible for real-world deployments like vocabulary extraction and search query analysis at web scale. The provable stochastic dominance over basic weighting provides a clean theoretical guarantee that the adaptive reweighting never hurts utility. Open questions include closing the remaining gap between parallel and sequential algorithms (sequential methods still outperform on smaller datasets due to longer adaptivity chains) and developing quantitative competitive-ratio bounds rather than the current ordinal dominance results.
