## Problem

Existing safe offline RL methods can only mitigate but not fully avoid the out-of-distribution problem in constrained settings, and they rely on inaccurate discounted approximations of safety constraints that create a many-to-one mapping between cumulative costs and discounted values, leading to false positives/negatives in constraint checking. Additionally, fixed constraint thresholds fail to adapt to the dynamic cost budget that evolves over a trajectory's horizon.

## Input / Output

The method takes the environment state and a safety constraint threshold as joint inputs to all value functions, Q-functions, and the policy network. During training, the threshold is randomly sampled from a predefined set {small, middle, high, ∞}; during testing, it is computed dynamically as the remaining cost budget scaled by the remaining horizon (L_t = C_t^b · T/(T−t)), enabling the policy to adapt its aggressiveness based on how much cost has already been incurred. The output is a continuous action sampled from the learned policy.

## Method

C2IQL extends IQL to constrained settings (CIQL) by applying CPQ's constraint-penalized shielding to the reward Q-value and using expectile regression to avoid OOD, then derives the cost value function update under the same implicit policy via a theorem that generalizes IDQL's implicit policy formulation (Theorem 1). To fix the inaccurate discounted constraint, a 5-layer MLP cost reconstruction model is trained to map multiple discounted cost Q-values (computed with m different discount factors) back to the non-discounted cumulative cost, resolving the many-to-one mapping problem. A constraint-conditioned mechanism incorporates the threshold as an input to all networks, allowing the agent to allocate cost budgets dynamically across the horizon. Policy extraction uses expectile-weighted regression rather than standard AWR, with a separate temperature parameter κ₂ balancing behavior cloning against value maximization.

## Results

On the Bullet-Safety-Gym benchmark (DSRL datasets, normalized cost threshold = 1), C2IQL achieves an average normalized reward of 0.74 and average normalized cost of 0.76 across 8 tasks (4 robots × 2 task types), making it the best-performing safe method. The closest safe competitor, CDT, achieves 0.71 reward and 0.91 cost. Among unsafe baselines, BCQ-Lag reaches 0.69 reward but violates the constraint (cost 1.05), while VOCE is the worst violator (cost 1.80). FISOR is the safest (cost 0.06) but overly conservative, achieving only 0.31 reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2IQL outperforms all safe baselines on average reward: it beats CDT (0.74 vs 0.71), WSAC (0.74 vs 0.54), COptiDICE (0.74 vs 0.53), CPQ (0.74 vs 0.41), and FISOR (0.74 vs 0.31). On cost, C2IQL (0.76) is also lower than CDT (0.91), WSAC (0.97), COptiDICE (0.80), and CPQ (0.83), though FISOR is more conservative (0.06) at the cost of significantly lower reward. Three baselines—BCQ-Lag (cost 1.05), BEAR-Lag (cost 1.49), and VOCE (cost 1.80)—violate the safety constraint entirely. The closest competitor is CDT, which achieves comparable reward but with 20% higher cost; Figure 2 further shows C2IQL flexibly adapts across different threshold levels while CDT fails on small thresholds and cannot maximize reward on large thresholds. On the additional SafetyGymnasium tasks (Table 6), C2IQL again achieves the best safe performance (avg reward 0.56, cost 0.64) over CDT (0.67 reward but 0.67 cost with violations on some tasks) and FISOR (0.49 reward, 0.27 cost).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends IQL's OOD-avoidance mechanism to constrained settings by combining CPQ's shielding (constraint-penalized) update with IQL's expectile-regression value estimation, yielding CIQL. It then leverages IDQL's theoretical framework to derive an implicit-policy-based update for the cost value function, eliminating the need to explicitly extract the policy (which would reintroduce OOD risk). Two further departures from prior SORL work are the cost reconstruction model—which corrects the well-known mismatch between discounted and non-discounted cumulative costs—and a constraint-conditioned mechanism borrowed from goal-conditioned RL that allows a single trained model to adapt to varying safety thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, previously under-addressed weaknesses in SORL: the inaccuracy of discounted constraint approximations and the rigidity of fixed thresholds. The cost reconstruction model is a practical, lightweight solution that could be adopted by other value-based safe RL methods, and the constraint-conditioned design enables deployment under multiple safety budgets without retraining. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively simple continuous-control benchmarks (Bullet-Safety-Gym, SafetyGymnasium), the need for pre-training the reconstruction model per environment, and the value-based formulation's restriction to continuous action spaces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or discrete-action safe RL and whether the reconstruction model generalizes across environments with different cost structures.
