## Problem

DPO and its variants (IPO, f-DPO, GPO) all rely on a reparameterization trick that induces an implicit reward, and the paper proves this structure causes two fundamental failures: (1) the minimizer cannot preserve the reference policy in regions where it is already optimal while simultaneously improving in weak regions, and (2) no QPO loss can satisfy the Strong Interpolation Criteria, instead converging to a degenerate delta policy rather than the BT-optimal policy as the regularization parameter vanishes. These are not hyperparameter issues but structural artifacts of the reparameterization itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference tuples {y_w, y_l, x} sampled from the reference policy π_ref (not the trainable policy), making all sampling independent of θ and amenable to standard SGD. The compositional variant additionally accepts unlabeled prompt-only data for its unsupervised KL term. The regression variant requires the ground-truth preference distribution p*, but Proposition 4.1 shows this can be replaced by a closed-form computable equivalent, so in practice only the labeled pairs and π_ref are needed. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy π_θ that, by construction, interpolates smoothly between the BT-optimal policy and π_ref as λ varies.

## Method

EXPO introduces two explicit loss functions outside the QPO family. The compositional loss ℓ_EXPO^c combines a supervised KL divergence between the ground-truth preference distribution p* and the policy-induced preference p_θ (reducing to log(1 + π_θ(y_l|x)/π_θ(y_w|x))) with an unsupervised forward-KL regularization λ·KL[π_ref‖π_θ]. The regression loss ℓ_EXPO^r directly penalizes squared deviation of p_θ from a weighted average of p* and p_ref, with λ∈[0,1] controlling the blend. Neither loss requires reparameterization or an implicit reward link to RLHF. The key theoretical results are that EXPO minimizers provably preserve π_ref where it equals the BT-optimal π* while improving elsewhere (Proposition 4.2), and satisfy the Strong Interpolation Criteria (Proposition 4.3), both of which are impossible for any QPO loss (Theorems 3.1 and 3.6). Empirically, EXPO outperforms DPO, IPO, and f-DPO on win rates (Anthropic HH, IMDb, AlpacaEval 2) and produces more diverse outputs.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2 (Llama-3-8B), EXPO(reg) achieves a 14.64% win rate and 20.32% length-controlled win rate, outperforming DPO (14.62% WR, 16.71% LC) and IPO (11.16% WR, 13.31% LC). On the Anthropic HH dataset (Pythia 2.8B), both EXPO variants show significant win-rate improvements over DPO and IPO, evaluated on both helpfulness and harmlessness. In controlled synthetic experiments, EXPO is the only method that converges to the BT-optimal policy π* (satisfying the Strong Interpolation Criteria) and preserves optimal policies in strong regions while improving in weak regions, whereas DPO, IPO, and f-DPO all converge to the degenerate mode π^δ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, EXPO(reg) achieves a comparable raw win rate on AlpacaEval 2 (14.64% vs 14.62%) but a substantially higher length-controlled win rate (20.32% vs 16.71%), and wins on Anthropic HH win rates for both helpfulness and harmlessness. Against IPO, EXPO dominates on AlpacaEval 2 (14.64% vs 11.16% WR; 20.32% vs 13.31% LC) and in synthetic interpolation tests where IPO converges to the degenerate π^δ rather than the BT-optimal π*. Against f-DPO (Jensen-Shannon), EXPO wins in synthetic preservation and interpolation tests; the authors note they omit f-DPO from real-world comparisons since prior work shows reverse-KL (i.e., DPO) is the best f-divergence choice. Against SimPO and KTO (both outside the QPO family), EXPO remains the top performer on Anthropic HH even though SimPO and KTO benefit from additional tunable hyperparameters (margin offset, prospect-theoretic coefficients) that EXPO does not require. In diversity metrics on Anthropic HH, EXPO variants consistently outperform DPO across normalized entropy, self-BLEU, and distinct-n scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the DPO lineage (Rafailov et al., 2024; Azar et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a; Tang et al., 2024), which all rely on a reparameterization trick to induce an implicit reward from the policy ratio log[π_θ/π_ref]. EXPO rejects this mechanism entirely, instead constructing explicit loss functions (a compositional KL-based objective and a regression-to-weighted-average objective) that directly target the BT-optimal policy without any implicit reward or reparameterization. The paper first unifies DPO, IPO, GPO, and f-DPO under a single QPO framework, then proves that this entire family shares structural limitations (inability to preserve optimal policies, failure to satisfy strong interpolation criteria) that EXPO avoids by design. Conceptually, EXPO is closer in spirit to the original RLHF objective (separate reward maximization + KL regularization) but made tractable for SGD without RL, rather than being a variant of the DPO reparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that all QPO-family losses (encompassing DPO, IPO, GPO, f-DPO) provably degrade performance in regions where the reference model is already optimal while improving elsewhere is a significant theoretical result that reframes how practitioners should interpret DPO's regularization behavior. Practically, EXPO offers a drop-in replacement with a single hyperparameter that consistently outperforms DPO/IPO/f-DPO on win rates and diversity metrics, and its compositional variant can naturally incorporate unlabeled prompt-only data. Open questions include whether EXPO's advantages persist at larger model scales (experiments use Pythia 2.8B and Llama-3-8B), whether the BT-optimal policy assumption is always the right target, and how EXPO compares to online/iterative preference optimization methods that the paper acknowledges may still be superior in some settings.
