## Problem

Diffusion-based imitation learning policies excel at modeling multi-modal action distributions from demonstrations but remain vulnerable to distribution shift and compounding errors. Existing approaches to fine-tune diffusion policies with RL (e.g., policy gradient on denoising steps, Q-weighted regression) suffer from training instability, low efficiency, or require significant architectural modifications. The paper asks how to stably and efficiently integrate RL fine-tuning into diffusion policies without these pathologies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot state observations (proprioceptive vectors or RGB images encoded by a ResNet-18 backbone), paired expert demonstration trajectories used as OT keypoints, and online transition data stored in a replay buffer. It outputs a single robot action (or action chunk) per decision step, produced by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise via a reverse SDE (DDIM solver, T=20 steps) conditioned on the current state, followed by a compatibility-based resampling step that selects among L=8 candidate actions. During training, the score model is updated via denoising score matching on VP-SDE forward noising, while the OT dual potentials are updated via stochastic gradient ascent on the L2-regularized dual objective.

## Method

OTPR reframes policy optimization as an L2-regularized optimal transport problem where the Q-function serves as the transport cost and the policy is the transport map from the state distribution to the action distribution, establishing an equivalence between the optimal OT map and the optimal RL policy (Proposition 4.1). The L2-regularized OT dual yields a compatibility function H(s,a) that defines a soft state-action coupling, replacing the hard Dirac-delta coupling of standard conditional score matching. Masked OT incorporates expert demonstration pairs as keypoints via a binary mask and Jensen-Shannon divergence cost, ensuring expert pairs are always matched in the transport plan. During online fine-tuning, the algorithm iteratively: (1) updates OT dual potentials u,v via stochastic gradient ascent, (2) generates L candidate actions from the diffusion policy and resamples one using normalized H scores (compatibility-based resampling), (3) updates the Q-network via IQL, and (4) updates the score model via H-weighted denoising score matching. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.3) proves that the H-weighted DSM objective has the same gradient as standard conditional score matching, and an upper bound on the Wasserstein distance between generated samples and the true conditional OT plan is derived.

## Results

On Franka-Kitchen, OTPR achieves the highest post-fine-tuning scores across all three sub-tasks: 92 on Kitchen-Complete-v0 (up from 61 offline), 79 on Kitchen-Mixed-v0 (up from 59), and 93 on Kitchen-Partial-v0 (up from 42). On RoboMimic, OTPR reaches 99 on Can-State and 98 on Square-State, substantially outperforming the next-best method IBRL (64 and 50 respectively). On LIBERO-Long (10 tasks), OTPR improves the pre-trained Diffusion Policy success rate on all 10 tasks, e.g., from 0.65 to 0.85 on Task 1 and from 0.10 to 0.35 on Task 9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTPR dominates all baselines on both Franka-Kitchen and RoboMimic. On Kitchen-Complete-v0, OTPR (92) outperforms Cal-QL (57) by 35 points and IBRL (25) by 67 points; on Kitchen-Partial-v0, OTPR (93) beats Cal-QL (84) by 9 points and IBRL (15) by 78 points. On RoboMimic, OTPR (99/98) far exceeds IBRL (64/50), while Cal-QL and RLPD fail entirely (score 0). In the diffusion-policy-specific fine-tuning comparison (Figure 2), OTPR consistently outperforms IDQL, DQL, and DPPO in both convergence speed and final performance, with the latter three exhibiting notable training instability especially in sparse-reward settings. On LIBERO-Long, OTPR improves over the pre-trained Diffusion Policy (DP) on all 10 tasks, with the largest gains on Task 9 (0.10→0.35) and Task 1 (0.65→0.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

OTPR builds on the Diffusion Policy architecture (Chi et al., 2023) for visuomotor control and the OT-guided conditional score-based diffusion framework (Gu et al., 2023), extending both to the RL fine-tuning setting. It departs from prior diffusion-policy fine-tuning methods (IDQL, DPPO, DQL) that frame denoising as an MDP or use Q-function importance sampling, instead reframing the policy as an L2-regularized optimal transport map with the Q-function as transport cost. The masked OT mechanism (Gu et al., 2022) is repurposed to use expert state-action pairs as keypoints that constrain the transport plan, and the L2-regularized OT dual (Dessein et al., 2018) provides the compatibility function H that replaces the hard Dirac-delta coupling in standard conditional score matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OTPR provides a theoretically grounded bridge between imitation learning and RL for diffusion policies, with Proposition 4.1 establishing equivalence between the optimal transport map and the optimal policy under Q-cost. The compatibility function H addresses a practical mini-batch bias problem that plagues standard score-matching training on unpaired data, and the masked OT leverages expert demonstrations as structural constraints without requiring them to be perfectly optimal. Limitations include evaluation on only three simulation benchmarks with IQL as the sole RL backbone, and the OT dual estimation adds computational overhead (two additional neural networks for u and v). Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional continuous action spaces, real-robot deployment, and whether the theoretical Wasserstein bounds in Section 5 are tight enough to be practically informative.
