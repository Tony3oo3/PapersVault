## Problem

Standard DNN-based optimization proxies for problems like ACOPF require 10⁴+ labeled instances and long training times, making them impractical when system parameters change or compute is limited. Moreover, existing confidence bounds (e.g., Hoeffding's inequality) are too loose to be useful for constraint-satisfaction guarantees with limited validation data. This paper tackles both gaps simultaneously: learning effective proxies under low-data, low-compute constraints while providing tight, practically meaningful probabilistic error bounds.

## Input / Output

The model takes per-bus active and reactive power demand vectors as tabular input and produces four output groups—generator real/reactive power setpoints, voltage magnitudes, and voltage angles—via four separate sub-networks. Uniquely, the BNN outputs a posterior prediction matrix (PPM) of 500 weight-sampled predictions per test input, from which both a point estimate (via Selection via Posterior) and a predictive variance are derived. The predictive variance feeds directly into Bernstein-based confidence bounds on per-bus voltage errors, requiring no additional labeled validation data beyond the M test samples already used for empirical error computation.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-supervised 'Sandwich' BNN that alternates between supervised SVI updates on labeled data (minimizing prediction cost) and unsupervised SVI updates on unlabeled data augmented with a feasibility pseudo-label F(y,x)=0, where F combines squared equality gaps and ReLU-rectified inequality gaps. This feasibility-based data augmentation enforces constraint satisfaction without requiring additional labeled instances or projection steps. At inference, Selection via Posterior (SvP) scans the 500-column PPM and selects the weight sample minimizing the maximum equality gap, improving feasibility at negligible computational cost. For confidence bounds, the paper uses the Theoretical Bernstein inequality with 2× the Mean Predictive Variance (MPV) as an upper bound on total variance in error, yielding bounds roughly 6× tighter than Hoeffding's and 2× tighter than the empirical Bernstein bound, validated empirically across 57-, 118-, 500-, and 2000-bus ACOPF instances.

## Results

On the 118-bus ACOPF problem (case118) with 512 labeled samples and 600 s training time, the proposed Sandwich BNN SvP achieves a 1.484% optimality gap and a maximum equality gap of 0.089 pu, compared to 1.565% gap and 1.284 pu max equality gap for the best DNN baseline (LD+MAE) — a ~14× reduction in equality constraint violation. On the 57-bus case (case57), the same method achieves 0.928% gap and 0.027 pu max equality gap versus 3.709% and 0.221 pu for LD+MAE (~8× reduction). The method also achieves zero mean inequality gap across all test cases, and the theoretical Bernstein confidence bound using 2×MPV yields a maximum voltage error of 0.010 pu on case118, far tighter than Hoeffding's 0.064 pu bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Park & Van Hentenryck (2023) baselines (Naïve MAE, Naïve MSE, MAE+Penalty, MSE+Penalty), the Sandwich BNN SvP achieves a 14–43× lower maximum equality gap on case118 (0.089 vs. 1.463–3.780 pu) while maintaining a comparable or lower optimality gap (1.484% vs. 1.563–1.638%). Against the Fioretto et al. (2020) Lagrangian Duality baseline (LD+MAE), the Sandwich BNN SvP reduces the maximum equality gap by ~14× (0.089 vs. 1.284 pu) and the mean equality gap by ~4.6× (0.018 vs. 0.083 pu) on case118, with a slightly lower optimality gap (1.484% vs. 1.565%). The one metric where the DNN baselines retain an edge is maximum inequality gap on case118 (0.000 vs. 0.008 pu), which the authors note can be eliminated by adding a standard bound-repair layer. On the larger 500-bus and 2000-bus cases, the BNN methods maintain an order-of-magnitude advantage in max equality gap over all DNN baselines, with the gap widening as system size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Bayesian Neural Network framework (Jospin et al., 2022) for constrained optimization proxy learning, a domain previously dominated by DNN-based supervised and self-supervised methods (Park & Van Hentenryck, 2023; Donti et al., 2021; Fioretto et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is the 'sandwich' semi-supervised training that alternates between supervised cost minimization and unsupervised feasibility enforcement via a feasibility-based data augmentation scheme, adapting the BNN data augmentation idea from Sharma et al. (2024) from image classification to constraint satisfaction. For confidence bounds, the paper moves beyond the standard Hoeffding inequality to a Theoretical Bernstein bound using the BNN's Mean Predictive Variance as a proxy for total variance in error, drawing on Audibert et al. (2007) and Sridharan (2002).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical tightness of the Bernstein-based confidence bounds (0.010 pu vs. 0.064 pu for Hoeffding on case118) makes probabilistic guarantees on voltage constraint satisfaction actionable for grid operators, something prior DNN proxies could not provide without extensive labeled validation. The 10× reduction in maximum equality gap over DNN baselines under a strict 10-minute/512-sample budget is well-evidenced across four system sizes (57 to 2000 buses), though the BNN architecture is intentionally unoptimized and the hypothesis α·MPV ≥ V_e (with α=2) is validated empirically rather than proven. Open questions include whether the sandwich training generalizes beyond ACOPF to other non-convex constrained problems, whether the SvP selection criterion can be made more principled for inequality constraints, and whether the approach scales to even larger networks where the 600-second training budget becomes insufficient.
