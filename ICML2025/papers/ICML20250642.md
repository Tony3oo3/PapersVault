## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks suffer from O(T) time complexity because LIF neurons must be simulated sequentially across T timesteps, making training prohibitively slow for long temporal tasks. Existing acceleration strategies either sacrifice accuracy through timestep reduction or gradient truncation, require specific architectural modifications, or rely on assumptions (e.g., removing the reset mechanism) that limit generality. The paper addresses the open problem of achieving architecture-independent parallel training that preserves the full dynamics of standard LIF neurons.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard SNN inputs—event-based vision streams (DVS-CIFAR10, DVS-Gesture), RGB images (ImageNet-100, Sequential CIFAR processed column-by-column over 32 timesteps), and graph node features (Amazon Photos via DRSGNN)—and produces class labels. The key operational detail is that all T timesteps of synaptic current are processed simultaneously as a vector rather than sequentially, with the decay matrix Λ encoding the temporal coupling. The output spike vector s* is obtained at the fixed-point equilibrium and passed to the readout layer, making the method a drop-in replacement for any LIF-based SNN without altering the network architecture.

## Method

FPT reformulates the sequential LIF recurrence into a fixed-point equation u = -V_th(Λ-I)H(u-V_th) + Λc, where Λ is a lower-triangular decay matrix capturing exponential memory across all T timesteps. The discontinuous Heaviside function is replaced by a sigmoid surrogate S_α, and the system is solved via K iterations (typically K=3) that process all timesteps in parallel, reducing time complexity from O(T) to O(K). A convergence proof via the Banach fixed-point theorem shows the mapping is a contraction when V_th·L_α·λ(1-λ^{T-1})/(1-λ) < 1, and the condition is decoupled from T. The forward pass uses a larger α_f for accurate approximation while the backward pass uses α_b = α_f/3 for stable gradients, and the reset mechanism is fully preserved—unlike PSN (K=1) or PSU (K=2), which are shown to be special cases of FPT that lose biological fidelity.

## Results

On DVS-CIFAR10, FPT achieves 85.50±0.22% accuracy (best run: 85.70%), surpassing the strongest baseline LocalZO (81.87%) by over 3.6%. On ImageNet-100, FPT reaches 83.27±0.16% (best: 83.48%), outperforming LocalZO (81.56%) by ~1.7%. On DVS-Gesture, FPT achieves 98.38±0.17% (best: 98.61%), comparable to LocalZO (98.43%). In efficiency experiments on Amazon Photos (DRSGNN), FPT is over 10× faster than BPTT at T=128 (5.58 ms vs. 74.04 ms per batch) with no statistically significant accuracy difference (p > 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPT clearly outperforms all timestep-shrinkage and online-training baselines on DVS-CIFAR10: it beats SEENN (82.70%) by ~2.8%, SLT (81.46%) by ~4%, and OTTT (76.63%) by ~8.9%. Against the strongest general-purpose baseline LocalZO, FPT wins on DVS-CIFAR10 (+3.6%) and ImageNet-100 (+1.7%) but is marginally lower on DVS-Gesture (98.38% vs. 98.43%, within one standard deviation). On the sequential CIFAR benchmarks, FPT with a learnable decay matrix (89.53% on Seq-CIFAR10, 64.50% on Seq-CIFAR100) surpasses PSN (88.45%, 62.21%) and IPSU (87.28%, 59.76%), confirming that the reset mechanism and K=3 iterations are critical. In the BPTT efficiency comparison on Amazon Photos, FPT matches BPTT accuracy (p > 0.05 at all T) while reducing per-batch training time from 74.04 ms to 5.58 ms at T=128, a >13× speedup. T-RevSNN and SSF, which require architectural modifications or specific assumptions, are outperformed by FPT on both accuracy and generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the fixed-point iteration framework from deep equilibrium models (Bai et al., 2019) and implicit deep learning (El Ghaoui et al., 2021) to reformulate LIF neuron dynamics as a parallelizable fixed-point mapping, a connection not previously explored in SNNs. It unifies existing parallel spiking neuron models—PSN (Fang et al., 2023) and PSU/IPSU (Li et al., 2024)—as special cases corresponding to K=1 and K=2 iterations, while retaining the reset mechanism that those models discard. Unlike prior training-acceleration approaches (OTTT, SLTT, T-RevSNN, SSF) that sacrifice temporal fidelity through gradient truncation or architectural modifications, FPT achieves O(K) complexity while preserving the full LIF dynamics and requiring no network architecture changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPT offers a practical path to scaling SNN training to long temporal horizons (T≥512) with up to 100× GPU speedup, addressing a key bottleneck for event-based vision and other long-sequence neuromorphic applications. Its architecture-independence and compatibility with sequential inference on neuromorphic hardware make it a drop-in replacement for BPTT in existing SNN pipelines. Open questions include memory overhead from storing all T timesteps simultaneously (O(LT) space), inability to handle non-decaying neurons (e.g., IAF), and whether the fixed-point formulation extends to more complex neuron models with multiple compartments or dendritic dynamics.
