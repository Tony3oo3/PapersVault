## Problem

Non-hierarchical Vision Mamba (Vim) models suffer from severe overfitting and even model collapse at large scale, leaving a noticeable accuracy gap with ViT on ImageNet-1K (e.g., MambaMLP-L at 84.5% vs. MAE-L at 85.9%). While architectural modifications such as registers or autoregressive pre-training have partially addressed this, the training-side regularization strategies specific to Vim's sequential, non-causal token structure remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images patchified into 1-D token sequences (e.g., 196 tokens at 224×224 with 14×14 patches), with a [CLS] token prepended for classification. For dense prediction downstream tasks, the non-hierarchical backbone is paired with a multi-scale adapter to produce FPN-compatible feature maps for UPerNet (segmentation) or Mask R-CNN (detection/instance segmentation). The shuffle regularization is applied only during training and is fully deactivated at inference, so the deployed model consumes and produces outputs identically to a vanilla Vim.

## Method

SLWS is a four-step per-layer regularization: (1) assign each layer ℓ a shuffle probability p_ℓ = (ℓ/L)·P_L that grows linearly with depth, (2) sample a Bernoulli variable b_ℓ to decide whether to shuffle, (3) apply a random permutation to the input token sequence before the Mamba block, and (4) restore the output sequence to its original order via the inverse permutation so that subsequent layers see tokens in canonical order. The design rationale is that deeper layers encode higher-level semantics and can tolerate greater positional perturbation, while shallower layers must preserve low-level spatial sensitivity. Combined with a masked feature distillation (MFD) pre-training pipeline that uses frozen CLIP vision encoders as semantic-rich teacher tokenizers and a lightweight self-attention decoder, the full SLWS+MFD framework achieves 87.6% on ImageNet-1K with MambaMLP-H, establishing a new SOTA for Vision Mamba models. The method introduces only O(L log L) overhead per layer, requires no architectural changes, and is entirely removed at inference time.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed MFD+SLWS pipeline achieves 87.6% top-1 accuracy with MambaMLP-H (662M params), establishing a new state-of-the-art for non-hierarchical Vision Mamba models and surpassing ViT-H trained with MAE (86.9%). At the large scale, MambaMLP-L with MFD+SLWS reaches 86.7%, outperforming both the ARM pre-training baseline (84.5% at 1600 epochs) and ViT-L MAE (85.9%) within just 300 epochs. In supervised training, SLWS alone boosts Vim-B from 79.8% to 82.7% (+2.9%) and prevents the previously collapsing Vim-L (284M) from diverging, achieving 82.9%. On ADE20K segmentation, MambaMLP-L with MFD+SLWS reaches 53.8 mIoU, matching ViT-L[MAE] (53.6), and on COCO detection, MambaR-L with SLWS achieves 48.9 AP^b, outperforming ViT-Adapter-L (48.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MFD+SLWS pipeline decisively outperforms the ARM pre-training baseline across all scales: MambaMLP-B improves from 83.2% to 84.3% (+1.1%), MambaMLP-L from 84.5% to 86.7% (+2.2%), and MambaMLP-H from 85.0% to 87.6% (+2.6%), all within 300 epochs versus ARM's 800–1600 epochs. At the huge scale, MambaMLP-H (MFD, S.) at 87.6% surpasses ViT-H MAE (86.9%) by 0.7% and matches ViT-L MaskDistill (87.6%). In supervised training, SLWS provides consistent gains: Vim-B improves by 2.9% (79.8→82.7%), Vim-M by 1.9% (80.9→82.8%), and MambaMLP-L by 1.5% (81.4→82.9%), while MambaR-B sees a marginal +0.1% (83.0→83.1%). Critically, SLWS prevents the Vim-L (284M) model from collapsing entirely, enabling stable training at 82.9%. On dense prediction, MambaR-Adapter-B with SLWS (49.3 mIoU on ADE20K) outperforms ViT-Adapter-B (48.8) by 0.5%, and MambaR-L with SLWS (48.9 AP^b on COCO) edges out ViT-Adapter-L (48.7) and ViT-L (45.7). The method does not outperform ViT-L MaskDistill (87.6%) or BEiTv2 (87.3%) at the large scale, but closes the gap substantially compared to prior SSM-based results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a training-methodology contribution for non-hierarchical Vision Mamba (Vim) models, departing from prior Vim improvements that focus on architectural modifications (scanning strategies, registers, MLP branches) or autoregressive pre-training pipelines (ARM, MAP). The core mechanism is inspired by stochastic depth's Bernoulli gating, repurposed to randomly permute token sequences rather than drop residual connections, while the MFD pre-training pipeline adapts the masked feature distillation framework (Peng et al., 2023) with a frozen CLIP teacher in an MAE-style auto-encoder design. Unlike hierarchical SSM variants (VMamba, U-Mamba) that incorporate spatial downsampling, SLWS is explicitly incompatible with such architectures, confining its applicability to plain, non-hierarchical Vim stacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical blocker for the Vim community: large non-hierarchical Vim models (e.g., 284M Vim-L) collapse during supervised training without adequate regularization, and SLWS resolves this with negligible overhead (<2% throughput loss). The combination of MFD pre-training with SLWS narrows the gap to MAE-pretrained ViTs (87.6% vs. 85.9% for ViT-L), suggesting that Vim's underperformance was partly a training-stability issue rather than a fundamental architectural limitation. Open questions include whether the linear layer-dependent probability schedule is optimal, whether the approach transfers to hierarchical Vim variants or other SSM backbones, and whether the reliance on a frozen CLIP teacher for MFD limits scalability to larger or domain-specific settings.
