## Problem

Existing learning-based approaches to Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs) rely on either supervised learning from feasible solutions (expensive to generate for NP-complete problems, and ambiguous when multiple solutions exist) or reinforcement learning (requiring large compute budgets and expert-designed reward signals). This paper asks whether a Transformer can learn to improve CSP solutions without either labeled solutions or RL, by training directly on constraint-violation signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a current (possibly infeasible) variable assignment tokenized as value embeddings, multi-dimensional absolute positional encodings for variable indices (e.g., row and column for Sudoku), a binary constraint graph encoded as relative positional encodings in attention, and a learned update-set embedding marking which variables are selected for modification in the current step. It outputs a refined one-hot assignment over each variable's domain for the selected subset, leaving unselected variables unchanged. The model is trained for a single refinement step but deployed iteratively at test time, with the output of each step fed back as the next input until a feasible solution is found or an iteration limit is reached.

## Method

ConsFormer is a standard multi-layer Transformer (self-attention + FFN) that performs one step of stochastic local search: a random subset of variables is selected for update (probability p), and the model predicts new values for only those variables. The key architectural insight is encoding the CSP structure through positional encodings—multi-dimensional APE captures variable indices (e.g., grid position in Sudoku), while the binary constraint graph is injected as RPE into attention logits, either masking or penalizing attention between unconstrained variable pairs. Training is fully self-supervised: discrete constraints (ALLDIFFERENT, inequality, cardinality) are approximated by differentiable continuous penalties (e.g., sum of absolute deviations from 1 for ALLDIFFERENT), and the loss is a weighted sum of squared penalties over the predicted assignment, requiring no feasible-solution labels or reward engineering. Gumbel-Softmax provides a differentiable proxy for discrete value selection at the output. At test time, the single-step model is applied repeatedly, and the iterative nature allows harder or out-of-distribution instances to be solved simply by increasing the number of refinement steps.

## Results

On Sudoku, ConsFormer achieves 100% solve rate on in-distribution instances (SATNet dataset, 31–42 missing values) and 65.88% / 77.74% on harder OOD instances (RRN dataset, 17–34 missing values) with 2k / 10k iterative refinement steps, outperforming the best prior neural method (Du et al., 2024) at 62.1%. On Graph Coloring with k=10 colors, ConsFormer (52.60% in-distribution, 11.92% OOD) surpasses OR-Tools (52.41%, 10.25%) under a 10-second budget. On Nurse Scheduling, ConsFormer matches OR-Tools at 100% solve rate for both in-distribution and OOD instances. On MAXCUT (GSET), ConsFormer achieves a 0.31%–1.27% average gap to best-known cut values, competitive with but slightly behind ANYCSP (0.02%–0.42%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsFormer outperforms all four supervised neural baselines on OOD Sudoku: Du et al. (2024) achieves 62.1%, Yang et al. (2023) 32.9%, Palm et al. (2018) 28.6%, and Wang et al. (2019) 3.2%, versus ConsFormer's 65.88% (2k iters) and 77.74% (10k iters). Notably, Yang et al.'s recurrent Transformer degrades with more test-time iterations (32.9% → 14.0%), while ConsFormer improves, attributed to its single-step training enabling better iterative generalization. On Graph Coloring, ConsFormer is competitive with OR-Tools on in-distribution instances (81.00% vs. 83.08% for k=5; 52.60% vs. 52.41% for k=10) and surpasses OR-Tools on OOD k=10 instances (11.92% vs. 10.25%), while decisively outperforming the RL-based ANYCSP (34.83% vs. 47.33% for k=5 OOD; 0.00% vs. 11.92% for k=10). On MAXCUT, ConsFormer (0.31%–1.27% gap) trails ANYCSP (0.02%–0.42%) but substantially outperforms OR-Tools (1.84%–3.38%) and other learned methods like ECORD (0.11%–1.53%) on larger instances (|V|≥3K: 1.27% vs. 1.53% for ECORD). On Nurse Scheduling, ConsFormer ties OR-Tools at 100% solve rate, though the authors note this problem has a large feasible solution space making it relatively easy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsFormer carves new ground by combining self-supervised learning with differentiable constraint penalties in a Transformer framework for CSPs, departing from both supervised approaches (SATNet, RRN, Yang et al. 2023) that require feasible solutions as labels and RL-based methods (ANYCSP) that need expert-designed reward functions. Conceptually, it repurposes constraint-based local search (Hentenryck & Michel, 2009) by replacing hand-crafted heuristics with a learned single-step refiner, and it adapts Transformer positional encodings in a novel way: APE encodes variable indices while RPE encodes the binary constraint graph. Unlike prior recurrent Transformer work (Yang et al. 2023, Dehghani et al. 2019) that shares weights across training-time layers, ConsFormer introduces recurrency only at test time, training for a single improvement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-supervised learning with differentiable constraint penalties is a viable paradigm for NP-complete CSPs, eliminating the need for expensive label generation or complex RL reward engineering. The clean separation between single-step training and iterative test-time deployment offers a practical scaling mechanism for OOD generalization that is absent in fixed-step supervised or RL baselines. Key limitations include the assumption of a fixed constraint structure (parametric constraints like SAT clauses with negations are unsupported) and the restriction to a small set of constraint types (ALLDIFFERENT, inequality, cardinality). Open questions include whether a general continuous approximation exists for arbitrary constraints, how to integrate ConsFormer into neuro-symbolic solvers with backtracking, and whether the approach scales to problems with heterogeneous constraint structures.
