## Problem

Denoising diffusion models require hundreds of sequential neural network evaluations to generate a single sample, making inference computationally expensive. While speculative sampling has proven effective for accelerating LLM inference by drafting tokens with a cheap model and verifying them in parallel, it was previously limited to discrete sequences and could not be directly applied to the continuous, vector-valued Markov chains underlying diffusion models. The core technical obstacle is that the standard adjusted rejection distribution r(x) ∝ max(0, q(x)−p(x)) is intractable to sample from in continuous state spaces, rendering the naive LLM rejection mechanism computationally useless for diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise N(0, Id) as the initial state and produces samples exactly distributed according to the target diffusion model's discretized Markov chain. For image generation, this yields 32×32×3 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64×3 (LSUN) RGB images; for robotics, it produces 16-step action sequences of dimension 2 (PushT). A key property is that the output is an exact sample from the target distribution (not an approximation), while the number of target model evaluations (NFE) is reduced by roughly 2×. In the Langevin diffusion extension, the input is an expensive energy function E(x) plus a cheap proxy Ê(x), and the output is a sample from the unnormalized target π(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)).

## Method

The paper adapts speculative sampling to diffusion models by replacing the intractable continuous rejection distribution with reflection maximal coupling (Bou-Rabee et al., 2020), which operates on the fact that both draft and target transition kernels are Gaussians with identical covariance but different means. Upon rejection, instead of sampling from r(x) ∝ max(0, q(x)−p(x)), the new state is computed deterministically as a reflection of the rejected draft state across the midpoint between the two means: Y = m^q + σ(Id − 2ee^T)Z. This requires only one target evaluation per step and is provably optimal (maximizes the probability of accepting each draft state). For drafting, the paper proposes a 'frozen target' strategy that reuses the score evaluated at the start of a window of L steps, requiring only one target call per window to generate all L draft states in parallel—no separate draft model training is needed. The algorithm then verifies all L draft states in parallel against the target model and accepts them sequentially until the first rejection, at which point the remaining draft states are discarded and a new window begins.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best speculative sampling configuration (ε=0.25, τ=2.0) achieves FID 2.34 with only 35.4 NFE, compared to FID 2.45 at 100 NFE for the full target model and FID 4.32 at 30 NFE for the reduced-step target — a 65% reduction in function evaluations while matching or improving quality. On the PushT robotics task, speculative sampling with L=10, K=10 achieves a reward of 0.903±0.007 with just 5.05 NFE, matching the target model's 0.901±0.008 at 10 NFE. On LSUN, similar ~2× NFE reductions are reported. The method produces exact samples from the target distribution (τ=1) or near-exact samples with marginally improved FID (τ>1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares speculative sampling against three baselines: (1) the full target model at 100 steps, (2) the target model at reduced 30 steps, and (3) the draft model alone. Speculative sampling clearly wins against the reduced-step target: at 35 NFE it achieves FID 2.34 versus FID 4.32 for the 30-step target, a 46% FID improvement. Against the full 100-step target (FID 2.45), speculative sampling matches or slightly improves FID (2.34) while using only 35% of the function evaluations. The draft model alone is far inferior (FID 17–188 depending on ε), confirming that the verification step is essential. On PushT, speculative sampling at 5 NFE achieves reward 0.903±0.007, slightly exceeding the 5-step target (0.876±0.008) and matching the 10-step target (0.901±0.008) at half the cost. The frozen draft strategy consistently outperforms the independent draft strategy across all settings, aligning with LLM speculative decoding findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative sampling—originally developed for discrete autoregressive LLMs (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023)—to continuous-state denoising diffusion models (Song et al., 2021). The key technical departure from prior continuous-state adaptations (Wang et al., 2024) is the use of reflection maximal coupling (Bou-Rabee et al., 2020) to handle the adjusted distribution at rejection, replacing an inefficient rejection-sampling approach that degrades as the draft model improves. The frozen draft model strategy, requiring no additional training, draws inspiration from Medusa-style multi-head drafting in LLMs (Cai et al., 2024), and the method is positioned as complementary to existing acceleration techniques like distillation, improved integrators, and Picard-iteration parallel sampling (Shih et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a training-free, drop-in acceleration for any diffusion model that halves NFE while preserving exact sampling from the target distribution—a property that distillation and reduced-step samplers sacrifice. This makes it particularly attractive for settings where the target model is fixed and retraining is infeasible. Limitations include inapplicability to deterministic samplers (without adding noise) and increased memory overhead from parallel verification calls, and the paper itself notes that unlike memory-bound LLMs, diffusion models already exploit parallelism, so the practical latency gains in serving settings remain an open question. The extension to Langevin diffusions for MCMC opens a broader avenue for accelerating expensive energy evaluations in scientific computing.
