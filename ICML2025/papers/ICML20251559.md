## Problem

Existing shapelet-based time series classification methods rely on hard sparsification—selecting a subset of discriminative subsequences and discarding the rest—which risks excluding beneficial shapes and ignores the varying contributions of individual shapelets to classification performance. This binary keep/discard strategy leads to poor capture of class patterns in regions with subtle inter-class differences while omitting many informative regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a univariate time series of length T and converts it into M overlapping subsequence embeddings of fixed length m (selected from {4,8,16,24,32,48,64,0.1T,0.8T} via validation) using a 1D CNN with learnable positional embeddings. After soft sparsification at rate η=50%, the number of shapes is reduced to J×η+1, where J is the original count. The output is a class label produced via conjunctive pooling (attention-weighted mean of shape embeddings passed through a linear classifier), along with per-shape attention scores that provide interpretability by highlighting discriminative regions.

## Method

SoftShape replaces hard shapelet selection with a soft sparsification mechanism: a parameter-shared gated attention head scores each shape embedding's classification contribution, the top-η shapes are scaled by their scores to form soft shapes, and the remaining low-score shapes are fused into a single embedding, preserving all subsequence information while reducing computational cost. A soft shape learning block then processes these sparsified shapes in two parallel pathways: (1) an MoE router activates k=1 class-specific MLP experts per shape to learn intra-shape local temporal patterns, and (2) a shared Inception CNN module treats the sparsified shapes as a sequence to learn inter-shape global temporal dependencies. The two pathways are combined residually, stacked over L=2 layers, and trained with cross-entropy loss plus MoE load-balancing and importance losses. The key distinction from prior work is that soft weighting (rather than binary selection) retains and differentiates all subsequence information, while the MoE+shared-expert dual pathway captures both class-specific local patterns and cross-shape global structure.

## Results

On the 128 UCR time series datasets, SoftShape achieves an average test accuracy of 93.34%, outperforming the strongest baselines TSLANet (92.05%) and InceptionTime (91.81%). SoftShape attains the best average rank of 2.72 and wins on 53 of 128 datasets. Wilcoxon signed-rank test P-values confirm statistically significant improvements over all 19 baselines (all P < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoftShape outperforms all 19 baselines on average accuracy across the 128 UCR datasets, with the closest competitors being TSLANet (92.05%, a 1.29-point gap) and InceptionTime (91.81%, a 1.53-point gap). Among non-deep-learning methods, MR-H (89.72%) and RDST (88.97%) are the strongest, yet SoftShape still leads by 3.6 and 4.4 points respectively. Transformer-based baselines (TST, PatchTST, Medformer) and foundation models (GPT4TS, UniTS) trail significantly, with average accuracies between 77.5% and 85.9%. SoftShape wins on 53 individual datasets, compared to 31 for TSLANet and 29 for both InceptionTime and MR-H. All P-values from the Wilcoxon signed-rank test are below 0.05, confirming that the performance gains are statistically significant rather than due to chance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shapelet-based TSC lineage (Ye & Keogh, 2009; Hills et al., 2014) by replacing the hard sparsification paradigm—where only top-ranked subsequences are retained—with a soft sparsification mechanism that weights all shapes by learned gated-attention contribution scores (Ilse et al., 2018; Early et al., 2024) and merges low-score shapes into a single fused embedding. The intra-shape learning module repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts router (Shazeer et al., 2017; Riquelme et al., 2021), previously applied to time-series forecasting and foundation models, for class-specific shapelet specialization, while the inter-shape module borrows the Inception convolutional architecture (Ismail Fawaz et al., 2020) as a shared expert. The shape embedding layer follows the patch-tokenization paradigm (Nie et al., 2023; Eldele et al., 2024), and the final classification uses conjunctive pooling from MIL (Early et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoftShape addresses a practical tension in interpretable TSC: hard shapelet selection discards potentially useful subsequences and ignores varying shapelet importance, while using all subsequences is computationally prohibitive for long sequences. The finding that 50% soft sparsification is statistically indistinguishable from no sparsification (p > 0.05) provides a concrete efficiency lever for practitioners. A key limitation is that the model handles only univariate time series and does not model inter-variable relationships, which the authors flag as future work. The interpretability via MIL attention scores is a meaningful advantage over black-box deep TSC models in safety-critical domains like healthcare, though the visualization approach itself is not novel.
