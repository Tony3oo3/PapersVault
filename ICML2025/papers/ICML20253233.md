## Problem

LLMs are increasingly used to generate synthetic datasets, but accurately measuring the diversity of these datasets remains under-explored. Existing methods suffer from inherent limitations: n-gram metrics capture only textual form, reference-based methods require costly reference data, and transformation-based methods like VendiScore incur O(n³) eigenvalue computation that limits scalability. The paper addresses three specific challenges: holistic per-sample analysis, satisfaction of principled diversity axioms, and reduced computational cost.

## Input / Output

DCScore takes a set of diversity-sensitive components (e.g., input text for classification tasks, excluding labels) from a synthetic dataset, along with a user-chosen embedding function (e.g., SimCSE, Sentence-BERT) and kernel function (inner product, RBF, or polynomial). It outputs a single scalar diversity score equal to the trace of an n×n probability matrix, bounded between 1 (all samples identical) and n (all samples distinct). The method is applied per batch of samples sharing a generation context, with results averaged across batches.

## Method

DCScore reframes diversity evaluation as an n-classification task where each sample constitutes its own category. An embedding function Φ maps each diversity-sensitive component to a d-dimensional representation, and a kernel function computes an n×n similarity matrix K whose entries serve as classification logits. A softmax with temperature τ converts K into a probability matrix P, where P[i,i] represents the probability that sample i is correctly classified into its own category. The final diversity score is tr(P) = Σ P[i,i], so highly distinguishable samples contribute more to the overall score. Theoretically, DCScore satisfies four axioms (effective number, identical samples, symmetry, monotonicity) and achieves O(n²·O_kernel + n²) complexity versus VendiScore's O(n²·O_kernel + n³), with the advantage most pronounced for non-linear kernels.

## Results

DCScore achieves Spearman's ρ correlations of 0.9961 (zero-shot, 13B text classification) and 0.9974 (few-shot, 70B story completion) with the generation temperature τg, matching or exceeding VendiScore in most settings. Against human judgment, DCScore reaches ρ = 0.9040 (Story-Few) and 0.8798 (Text-Zero), confirming alignment with subjective diversity assessments. On computational cost, DCScore reduces evaluation time from 449.14 s to 117.06 s (RBF kernel, 64k samples on SST2) and from 295.14 s to 90.36 s (vs. FKEA-Vendi at 64k), a ~3× speedup over efficiency-improved baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VendiScore (Dan Friedman & Dieng, 2023), DCScore ties or slightly leads in τg correlation (e.g., 0.9974 vs. 0.9961 on few-shot 70B story completion) while delivering a substantial computational advantage: at 64k samples with an RBF kernel on SST2, DCScore takes 117 s versus VendiScore's 449 s (~3.8× faster). Against Distinct-n (Li et al., 2015), DCScore matches or exceeds it in 7 of 8 settings, with the largest gap on zero-shot 70B text classification (0.9779 vs. 0.9870, where Distinct-n actually edges ahead slightly). K-means Inertia (Du & Black, 2019) is clearly the weakest baseline, showing a negative correlation (−0.1143) on zero-shot 13B text classification and correlations as low as 0.7104 on few-shot text, whereas DCScore maintains ρ ≥ 0.9792 across all settings. Against the efficiency-improved FKEA-Vendi and FKEA-RKE (Ospanov et al., 2024), DCScore is roughly 3× faster at 64k samples (90.36 s vs. 295.14 s on SST2), confirming its scalability advantage for large synthetic datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCScore repurposes the transformation-based diversity evaluation paradigm (embedding + kernel matrix) from VendiScore and K-means Inertia, but replaces the high-complexity summarization step (eigenvalue decomposition or clustering) with a softmax-based n-classification task where each sample is its own category. Conceptually, it draws on Quine's observation that diversity is fundamentally about distinguishing differences, and on Leinster & Cobbold's axiomatic framework for principled diversity metrics. The paper carves new ground by specifically targeting LLM-generated synthetic datasets and introducing the notion of 'diversity-sensitive components'—only evaluating the subset of samples whose diversity actually impacts downstream model performance—rather than treating all generated text uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs become the primary tool for synthetic data generation, a scalable and principled diversity metric is essential for data quality control, augmentation assessment, and mode collapse detection. DCScore's O(n²) summarization complexity (vs. O(n³) for VendiScore with general kernels) makes it practical for the increasingly large synthetic datasets in production, and its satisfaction of four diversity axioms provides theoretical grounding that prior heuristic metrics lack. Key limitations include inapplicability to multimodal data (e.g., image-text pairs) and sensitivity to identical content in early portions of generated text, which can reduce correlation with human judgment. Open questions include extending DCScore to multimodal settings and exploring whether the classification perspective generalizes to other evaluation tasks beyond diversity.
