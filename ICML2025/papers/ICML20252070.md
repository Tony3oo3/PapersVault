## Problem

Robust reinforcement learning under the average reward criterion, which optimizes long-term performance under model uncertainty, remains largely unexplored due to the non-contracted Bellman operator, high-dimensional solution space, and convergence instability inherent to average reward MDPs. While prior work established that robust discounted value functions converge to robust average reward as the discount factor approaches 1, no practical guidance existed for selecting a specific discount factor that guarantees a desired level of policy optimality, leaving the reduction approach theoretically motivated but algorithmically intractable.

## Input / Output

The framework requires the robust optimal bias span H (or any valid upper bound, e.g., derived from mixing time or diameter estimates) and an accuracy target ε, from which the reduction discount factor is set to γ = 1 − ε/H. In the model-based setting, the algorithm consumes N nominal transition samples per (s,a) pair drawn from the generative model, along with the uncertainty set specification (divergence type D and radius R), and outputs an ε-optimal stationary policy for the robust average reward. In the scalable setting, it additionally takes predefined feature functions φ(s) ∈ ℝ^d and produces a policy parameterized by a weight vector w_T, with the critic error ε_c determined by the representation power of the chosen function class.

## Method

The core contribution is a reduction framework (Theorem 3.4) proving that any ε_γ-optimal policy for the robust DMDP with discount factor γ = 1 − ε/H is also O(ε)-optimal for the robust AMDP, under a standard compactness and unichain assumption. This is established by bounding the span of the discounted value function via the robust optimal bias span, showing that the convergence error between (1−γ)V_γ and the average reward is controlled by Sp((1−γ)V_γ) ≤ ε. Two algorithmic instantiations follow: a model-based algorithm (Algorithm 1) that estimates the nominal kernel, constructs an empirical uncertainty set, and solves the reduced robust DMDP via value iteration, achieving near-optimal sample complexity Õ(SAH²/ε²) for TV and χ² divergence sets; and a scalable robust natural actor-critic algorithm (Algorithm 2) with linear function approximation that bypasses the non-uniqueness and Span-semi-norm contraction instability of the average reward Bellman equation by operating entirely in the discounted setting, converging in Õ(H²ε⁻³) samples without additional assumptions on the feature basis.

## Results

The reduction framework (Theorem 3.4) shows that setting γ = 1 − ε/H transforms any ε_γ-optimal robust DMDP policy into an O(ε)-optimal robust AMDP policy, with suboptimality bounded by (8 + 5ε_γ/H)ε. For data efficiency (Theorem 4.4), the model-based algorithm requires Õ(SAH²/ε²) samples under TV and χ² divergence uncertainty sets, matching the minimax lower bound up to the H factor. For scalability (Theorem 5.1), the reduction robust natural actor-critic algorithm achieves Õ(H²ε⁻³) total sample complexity with linear function approximation. Empirically, on the Garnet problem (20 states, 8 actions, R=0.2), the reduction method converges to the optimal robust average reward as γ→1, and on Walker2d-v3 and Hopper-v3, performance stabilizes at large discount factors, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two direct robust AMDP baselines: Robust Value Iteration (RVI, Wang et al. 2023d) and Robust Relative Value Iteration (RRVI, Wang et al. 2023e). In Figure 9 (Garnet problem, 20 states, 8 actions, TV/CS with R=0.2, reduction factor γ=0.99), the proposed reduction method obtains a better policy within the same number of iterations, achieving state-of-the-art performance in robust average reward optimization. Compared to the Blackwell discount factor approach of Grand-Clément & Petrik (2024), this paper's framework is strictly more general (requiring only compactness and unichain assumptions rather than ℓ₁/ℓ_∞ norms and rational kernels) and yields polynomial rather than exponentially large sample complexity. The reduction framework also outperforms direct non-robust reduction methods in the neural network approximation experiments (Figures 5–6), showing greater robustness under model uncertainty in Walker2d-v3 and Hopper-v3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymptotic convergence result of Wang et al. (2023d) and Grand-Clement et al. (2023), which showed that robust discounted RL converges to robust average reward as γ→1, by providing a concrete, finite-accuracy discount factor choice (γ = 1 − ε/H) that guarantees near-optimality under the average reward criterion. The sample complexity analysis builds on the robust DMDP literature (Panaganti & Kalathil 2022; Shi et al. 2023) and the non-robust AMDP reduction framework (Wang et al. 2022; Zurek & Chen 2023), while the scalable algorithm extends the robust natural actor-critic method of Zhou et al. (2024) to the average reward setting. Unlike Grand-Clément & Petrik (2024), which bounds the Blackwell discount factor under restrictive rational-kernel assumptions yielding exponential sample complexity, this work operates under the milder unichain assumption and achieves polynomial, near-optimal complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-sample complexity characterization and the first scalable (function-approximation-based) algorithm for robust RL under average reward, filling a significant gap between the well-studied discounted setting and the practically important but theoretically challenging average reward setting. The reduction framework is universal—any robust DMDP algorithm can be plugged in—making it a practical tool for long-term performance optimization under model uncertainty. Key limitations include the requirement to know (or upper-bound) the robust optimal bias span H, the unichain assumption, and the generative-model setting for the sample complexity results; extending to offline/online settings and tightening the H-dependence to match non-robust lower bounds are natural open directions.
