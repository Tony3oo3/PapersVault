## Problem

Existing Bayesian personalized federated learning methods construct global distributions through heuristic aggregation—arithmetic averaging of posterior parameters or product approximations—which introduces strong biases, obscures the distinctive characteristics of heterogeneous local data, and yields global models with poor generalization. No prior framework provides a principled hierarchical Bayesian mechanism that jointly infers a global posterior over the parameters of personalized posteriors while preserving local personalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 grayscale for FMNIST, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or 60-dimensional synthetic vectors) and maintains a Bayesian neural network whose weights are represented by a diagonal-covariance Gaussian posterior (mean and variance vectors of dimension M = |W_k|). The server outputs a global posterior parameter vector θ̂_g = (μ̂_g, σ̂_g) regularized by a Gaussian–Half-Normal hyper-prior, which is broadcast back to clients as a local regularization target. The model is decomposed into a shared base component and a client-specific head component, with distribution parameters concatenated as θ_k = θ_{k;base} ⊕ θ_{k;head}.

## Method

The core contribution is a conjugate hyper-prior p(θ_g|α) = N(μ_g | 0, σ₁²I) · Half-Normal(σ_g | σ₂) placed over the parameters of each client's personalized posterior, enabling a single hierarchical Bayesian model that is jointly inferred via stochastic variational inference. The ELBO objective is decomposed into a server-side term (KL between each client's variational posterior and the global posterior, plus KL against the hyper-prior, yielding L₂ regularization on μ̂_g and σ̂_g) and a client-side term (expected negative log-likelihood plus KL against the global posterior), optimized in alternating block-coordinate fashion. For communication efficiency, the global variational distribution is a Dirac delta, so the server transmits only point estimates θ̂_g. Theorem 4.1 proves that each client's posterior mean is a precision-weighted convex combination of its local MLE and the global mean, with the shrinkage coefficient β_k = σ_k²/(σ_k² + σ̄²) automatically giving more weight to the global estimate for clients with less data or noisier labels. Setting the hyper-prior regularization coefficients λ₁ = λ₂ = 0 recovers pFedBayes and pFedVEM as special cases, confirming the framework's generality.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (100 clients), the proposed method achieves 74.7% PM accuracy and 63.8% GM accuracy, outperforming the strongest Bayesian PFL baseline pFedVEM (71.9% PM, 60.1% GM) by 2.8% and 3.7% respectively. On CIFAR-100 (100 clients), the gains are even larger: 62.2% PM and 55.4% GM versus pFedVEM's 56.2% PM and 52.3% GM. On Fashion-MNIST (100 clients), the method achieves 91.6% PM and 85.9% GM, matching or slightly exceeding pFedVEM (91.4% PM, 85.6% GM). The method also demonstrates rapid convergence, reaching competitive performance at approximately half the communication rounds required by baselines on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all Bayesian PFL baselines on Global Model accuracy. On CIFAR-10 (50 clients), it achieves 65.9% GM accuracy, surpassing pFedVEM (56.0%) by 9.9%, pFedBayes (52.0%) by 13.9%, and pFedME (56.6%) by 9.3%. On CIFAR-100 (100 clients), the GM advantage over pFedVEM is 3.1% (55.4% vs 52.3%), while over pFedBayes it reaches 14.1% (55.4% vs 41.3%). For Personalized Model accuracy, the method leads pFedVEM by 5.6% on CIFAR-100 (50 clients: 66.6% vs 61.0%) and by 3.9% on CIFAR-10 (50 clients: 77.1% vs 73.2%). Against non-Bayesian FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold), the method's GM accuracy is substantially higher on CIFAR-100 (56.0% vs 51.7%/48.4%/47.2% at 50 clients), while on the simpler FMNIST dataset the GM gap narrows to ~1–2%. Against non-Bayesian PFL baselines (FedPer, FedRep, PerFedAvg), the method's PM accuracy is higher on CIFAR-100 (66.6% vs 49.7%/50.9%/52.1% at 50 clients) but comparable on FMNIST (92.2% vs 91.4%/91.5%/88.7%). The ablation study confirms that the hyper-prior regularization (λ₁=λ₂=5) is critical: removing it (λ₁=λ₂=0) degrades CIFAR-100 GM from 56.0% to 53.1%, recovering the arithmetic aggregation of pFedBayes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian personalized federated learning (PFL) paradigm established by pFedBayes (Zhang et al., 2022) and pFedVEM (Zhu et al., 2023), which both rely on heuristic aggregation (arithmetic mean or product) of personalized posterior parameters to form a global distribution. The key departure is the introduction of a conjugate hyper-prior (Gaussian × Half-Normal) over the parameters of the personalized posteriors, enabling a principled hierarchical Bayesian inference that jointly computes global and local posteriors via stochastic variational inference. The authors demonstrate that pFedBayes and pFedVEM are recovered as special cases when the hyper-prior regularization coefficients are set to zero, providing a unifying theoretical lens. The local BNN optimization follows Bayes-by-Backprop (Blundell et al., 2015), and the base-head architecture is adopted from pFedVEM and FedRep (Collins et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical formulation offers a principled mechanism for balancing personalization and global robustness, with a clean convergence theorem (Theorem 4.1) showing that clients with less data are naturally 'shrunk' more toward the global mean—providing an interpretable account of how statistical strength is shared. The consistent global model improvements (up to 5.5% on CIFAR10, 3.2% on CIFAR100) over strong Bayesian PFL baselines across multiple datasets and client counts are practically meaningful. However, the full-Bayesian approach incurs roughly 4× the local training time of non-Bayesian methods (90s vs 21s per round on CIFAR10), and experiments are limited to relatively small-scale datasets (up to CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet with 200 classes). Open questions include whether the Dirac approximation for the global posterior and the Half-Normal prior on variance are optimal, how the framework scales to deeper architectures or larger datasets, and whether the computational overhead can be reduced through approximate inference.
