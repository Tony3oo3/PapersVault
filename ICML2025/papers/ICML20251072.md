## Problem

Autoregressive generation of continuous speech tokens is hindered by a fundamental tension: causal-attention language models impose unidirectional dependencies that conflict with the strong inter-frame correlations in continuous representations, while approaches that repurpose LM parameters for diffusion (e.g., ARDiT, Transfusion) incur prohibitive computational costs. The paper asks whether one can autoregressively predict continuous tokens with high generation quality and reasonable computational demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251072/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a concatenation of prompting text, target text (converted to phoneme embeddings via a lookup table), and a 3-second prompt audio (encoded to 40 Hz VAE latents of dimension 64 at 24 kHz) as prefix context, then autoregressively generates target speech latents patch by patch. The final output is a 24 kHz waveform produced by a BigVGAN-based VAE decoder from the generated 40 Hz latent sequence. A binary stop classifier at the LM output determines when generation terminates.

## Method

DiTAR employs a divide-and-conquer patch-based architecture: an aggregation encoder compresses each patch of P continuous tokens (default P=4) into a single vector, a causal-attention language model performs inter-patch next-token prediction on these aggregated embeddings, and a bidirectional diffusion transformer (LocDiT) generates the next patch conditioned on the LM output. Historical context patches are fed to LocDiT as prefix inputs, reframing the task as outpainting and substantially improving quality. For training, a conditional flow-matching loss with velocity prediction is used, and LM guidance is implemented by randomly replacing the LM output with a zero vector during training, then applying a CFG-like interpolation at inference requiring only two diffusion-head evaluations. For inference, the paper introduces a new temperature definition (τ ∈ [0,1]) as the time point at which noise is injected along the reverse ODE trajectory, enabling fast ODE-based sampling that balances diversity and determinism without the many steps required by SDE solvers.

## Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean A, DiTAR (0.6B, NFE=10) achieves the best WER of 1.78% (vs. 1.81% for NaturalSpeech3 and 2.14% for Voicebox), SIM of 0.64, and UTMOS of 4.15, while requiring only ~2.75 TFLOPs—roughly 3–43× less than competing NAR systems. On LibriSpeech test-clean B, DiTAR achieves WER 2.39%, SIM 0.67, and UTMOS 4.22, outperforming F5TTS (WER 2.42%, UTMOS 3.88) and E2TTS (WER 2.95%, UTMOS 3.56). On the multilingual Seed-EN and Seed-ZH benchmarks, DiTAR (1B, 280k hours) achieves the lowest WER (1.685% and 1.023% respectively), surpassing Seed-TTS, CosyVoice 2, F5TTS, and MaskGCT on robustness, though trailing Seed-TTS on speaker similarity (0.735 vs. 0.790 on Seed-EN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTAR achieves the best WER across all evaluated benchmarks, outperforming the strongest NAR baselines: on LibriSpeech A it beats NaturalSpeech3 (1.78% vs. 1.81%) and Voicebox (1.78% vs. 2.14%); on LibriSpeech B it beats F5TTS (2.39% vs. 2.42%), E2TTS (2.39% vs. 2.95%), and MaskGCT (2.39% vs. 2.72%); on Seed-EN it beats Seed-TTS (1.685% vs. 1.733%) and F5TTS (1.685% vs. 1.83%); on Seed-ZH it beats Seed-TTS (1.023% vs. 1.178%) and MaskGCT (1.023% vs. 2.273%). On speaker similarity, DiTAR is competitive but trails the best NAR systems: SIM 0.64 vs. NaturalSpeech3's 0.67 on LibriSpeech A, and 0.735 vs. Seed-TTS's 0.790 on Seed-EN. On naturalness (UTMOS), DiTAR leads all baselines on LibriSpeech B (4.22 vs. F5TTS 3.88, E2TTS 3.56) and is second only to NaturalSpeech3 on subset A (4.15 vs. 4.30). The most striking advantage is computational efficiency: DiTAR requires ~2.75 TFLOPs versus 37–117 TFLOPs for NAR systems (F5TTS, E2TTS, MaskGCT), a 13–43× reduction, while also achieving lower latency (0.066 s vs. 0.37 s at batch size 1) due to its autoregressive structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTAR directly addresses a limitation identified by Li et al. (2024a), who showed that attaching a diffusion head to a causal-attention language model yields poor generation quality due to the conflict between unidirectional dependencies and the strong inter-frame correlations in continuous tokens. Rather than repurposing LM parameters for diffusion (as in ARDiT or Transfusion, which inflate compute), DiTAR introduces a divide-and-conquer decomposition: a causal LM handles inter-patch prediction while a separate bidirectional diffusion transformer (LocDiT, building on the DiT architecture of Peebles & Xie, 2023) handles intra-patch generation. The continuous token representation follows the LDM-style VAE latent space (Rombach et al., 2022), the training objective is conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), and the proposed LM guidance adapts classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to the hybrid LM+diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiTAR demonstrates that autoregressive continuous-token generation can match or exceed non-autoregressive diffusion systems in zero-shot TTS quality while reducing FLOPs by 3–43×, offering a practical path to high-fidelity speech synthesis at a fraction of the inference cost. The patch-based decomposition (causal LM for long-range context, bidirectional DiT for local detail) is a general architectural principle that could transfer to other continuous-modal generation tasks such as video or music. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form generation without error accumulation, how it performs in low-resource or multilingual settings beyond the tested English/Chinese data, and whether the VAE bottleneck limits ultimate fidelity. The temperature-as-noise-introduction-time definition for continuous-valued LMs is a clean conceptual contribution, but its interaction with more complex sampling strategies (e.g., multi-step SDE solvers) remains unexplored.
