## Problem

In offline preference-based RL, humans struggle to label clear preferences between highly similar trajectory segments, producing ambiguous queries that reduce labeling efficiency and limit real-world applicability. While prior work addressed this in online PbRL, the offline setting—where the agent must learn from a fixed dataset with a limited feedback budget—remains unsolved, and existing query selection strategies (e.g., maximum disagreement) do not explicitly account for segment distinguishability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset D (no reward signals) from Metaworld or DMControl, along with preference feedback on segment pairs (σ₀, σ₁, p) where p ∈ {0, 1, no_cop} and segments are H=50 timesteps long. It produces three outputs: fixed-dimensional trajectory embeddings via a BDT encoder, a reshaped set of selected query pairs (via rejection sampling that biases toward clearly distinguished segments), and a final policy trained with IQL on the relabeled dataset. The 'no_cop' label for indistinguishable segments is a key input signal that drives the contrastive training objective.

## Method

CLARIFY trains a Bi-directional Decision Transformer encoder via two contrastive losses: an ambiguity loss L_amb that pushes apart embeddings of clearly distinguished segment pairs while pulling together ambiguous ones, and a quadrilateral loss L_quad that models preference relations between pairs of queries as quadrilaterals, expanding effective training data from O(n) to O(n²) and preventing representation collapse. A rejection sampling procedure then reshapes the query distribution using density estimates ρ_clr and ρ_amb over embedding distances, increasing the proportion of unambiguous queries sent to the (non-ideal) teacher. Theoretical analysis provides a positive separation margin guarantee (Proposition 5.1) and convex separability of preference signals (Proposition 5.2), ensuring the embedding space supports reliable margin-based query filtering. The full training loop alternates between embedding updates, reward model updates (Bradley-Terry), and query selection, before a final IQL policy optimization on the relabeled dataset.

## Results

On the offline PbRL benchmark (skip rate ε=0.5), CLARIFY achieves the best performance among all preference-based methods on 7 out of 9 tasks, with notable gains on dial-turn (77.50% vs. 57.33% for OPRL), handle-pull-side (95.00% vs. 83.92% for OPRL), and drawer-open (83.50% vs. 72.67% for OPRL). On cheetah-run, CLARIFY (617.31) even surpasses the IQL upper bound trained with ground-truth rewards (607.46). CLARIFY also selects significantly more clearly-distinguished queries (e.g., 76.33% clarity on dial-turn vs. 46.95% for MR and 31.67% for OPRL), and in human experiments achieves 87.08% labeling accuracy vs. 66.67% for OPRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLARIFY outperforms all five PbRL baselines (MR, OPRL, PT, OPPO, LiRE) on the majority of tasks under both skip rates. Against OPRL (the strongest baseline using reward ensembles and max-disagreement query selection), CLARIFY gains 20.17 points on dial-turn (77.50 vs. 57.33), 10.83 on drawer-open (83.50 vs. 72.67), and 11.08 on sweep-into (68.00 vs. 61.00). Against LiRE (listwise reward estimation), CLARIFY shows the largest gap on handle-pull-side (95.00 vs. 52.07) and hammer (26.75 vs. 16.20). OPPO, which optimizes policy directly in an embedding space, performs worst overall (near-zero on several tasks), while MR and PT degrade severely under the skip mechanism. In the real human-labeler experiment on walker-walk, CLARIFY achieves 420.75 episodic return vs. 265.91 for OPRL, with 87.08% labeling accuracy vs. 66.67%, confirming the advantage transfers beyond scripted teachers.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLARIFY extends the offline PbRL literature by addressing the ambiguous query problem—where humans cannot reliably distinguish between highly similar trajectory segments—which was previously only tackled in online settings by Mu et al. (2024). Methodologically, it repurposes contrastive learning (previously applied to image-based RL representations and temporal distance learning) for trajectory embedding in preference-based settings, using a Bi-directional Decision Transformer (Furuta et al., 2021a) as the encoder backbone. The two proposed losses (ambiguity loss and quadrilateral loss) depart from standard Bradley-Terry reward modeling by operating directly in embedding space, and the rejection-sampling query selection mechanism is a novel application of density-ratio weighting to preference query design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ambiguous query bottleneck is a practical barrier to deploying PbRL in real-world settings (robotics, LLM alignment), and CLARIFY provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution with provable margin separation and convex separability guarantees. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively simple robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks (Metaworld, DMControl), and the non-ideal teacher model, while validated by human experiments, may not fully capture the complexity of real human decision-making at scale. Open questions include whether the contrastive embedding approach generalizes to high-dimensional or language-based preference settings, and whether the rejection sampling strategy remains effective as the preference dataset grows large.
