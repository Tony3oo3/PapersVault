## Problem

Existing cross-modal person re-identification methods are restricted to retrieval between fixed modality pairs (e.g., text-to-RGB or sketch-to-RGB), making them unable to exploit multiple modalities simultaneously when they are available. In practical surveillance scenarios, operators often receive information from several modalities at once, yet no unified framework supports flexible retrieval across arbitrary combinations of text, sketch, infrared, and RGB.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252291/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts any subset of three query modalities (text descriptions of 77 tokens, sketch images, infrared images) and retrieves matching pedestrians from an RGB gallery (images resized to 384×128). Missing modalities in a query are automatically filled with learnable embedding vectors, so the network always processes a unified four-modality representation. Output is a TopK ranked list of gallery matches, evaluated under seven distinct retrieval configurations (single-modality and all pairwise/triple combinations).

## Method

FlexiReID builds on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 dual-encoder backbone and introduces two key modules. First, the Adaptive Expert Allocation Mixture-of-Experts (AEA-MoE) replaces fixed Top-K routing with a threshold-based mechanism: experts are activated in descending confidence order only until the cumulative confidence exceeds a set threshold, so simple inputs activate fewer experts while complex ones activate more; an entropy-based adaptive loss prevents the gating network from diffusing probability mass across too many experts. Second, the Cross-Modal Query Fusion (CMQF) module performs cross-attention where each modality's features serve as queries attending to the other modalities' keys and values, then concatenates and mean-pools through a shared transformer block to produce a single fused representation per retrieval mode. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a bi-directional Similarity Distribution Matching (SDM) loss summed across all seven retrieval modes, plus the adaptive loss, while only the AEA-MoE adapters, CMQF parameters, and learnable embeddings are updated.

## Results

On CUHK-PEDES, FlexiReID achieves R@1 of 69.20% (T→R), 84.92% (S→R), 85.26% (IR→R), and 88.23% (T+S+IR→R), with mAP of 62.47%, 79.21%, 79.43%, and 82.63% respectively. On ICFG-PEDES, it reaches SOTA R@1 of 61.34% (T→R), 79.28% (S→R), and 82.03% (IR→R). On SYSU-MM01 (All-Search), the full-modality T+S+IR→R task achieves R@1 of 71.3% and mAP of 69.5%, outperforming the best single-modality IR→R baseline (MPANet, R@1=70.6%). The multi-modal combinations consistently outperform any single-modality retrieval, with T+S+IR→R surpassing the best single-modality result by 3.3% R@1 on CUHK-PEDES.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the T→R task, FlexiReID (R@1=69.20 on CUHK-PEDES) is slightly behind CSKT (69.70) and CFine (69.57) but surpasses UNIReID (68.71) and all earlier methods; on ICFG-PEDES it achieves SOTA R@1 of 61.34%, beating UNIReID (61.28) and CFine (60.83). For S→R, FlexiReID achieves SOTA across all three datasets (84.92/79.28/66.79 R@1), outperforming UNIReID (84.87/77.47/65.80) and DALNet (83.03/77.28/64.68). On IR→R, FlexiReID (85.26/82.03/75.36 R@1) surpasses SDCL (84.57/81.36/74.67) and GUR (82.06/80.31/73.42) on all datasets. The combined-modality tasks (T+S→R, T+IR→R, S+IR→R, T+S+IR→R) have no direct baselines except UNIReID for T+S→R, where FlexiReID leads by 1.18% R@1 on CUHK-PEDES and 1.65% on ICFG-PEDES. Overall, FlexiReID achieves SOTA or near-SOTA on every single-modality task and is the only method supporting all seven retrieval combinations, with multi-modal fusion consistently yielding 2–4% R@1 gains over the best single-modality result.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiReID builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture (ViT-B/16) as a frozen backbone, extending it with an Adaptive Expert Allocation Mixture-of-Experts (AEA-MoE) mechanism that modifies the standard Top-K sparse routing of Shazeer et al. by dynamically adjusting the number of activated experts via a confidence threshold. The adapter structure follows AdaptFormer, and the training objective adopts the Similarity Distribution Matching (SDM) loss from Jiang & Ye. The key departure from prior cross-modal ReID work (UNIReID, SketchTrans+, GUR, etc.) is the shift from fixed modality-pair retrieval to a unified framework supporting seven arbitrary modality combinations, with a Cross-Modal Query Fusion (CMQF) module and learnable embedding features to handle missing modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flexible retrieval formulation addresses a genuine practical gap: real surveillance systems often receive heterogeneous information (a sketch from a witness, an IR snapshot, a text description) simultaneously, yet prior methods force the user to pick a single modality pair. However, a significant limitation is that the CIRS-PEDES dataset's additional modalities are synthetically generated (StyleGAN3 for sketches, InfraGAN for IR, GPT-4 for text) from RGB images, so the cross-modal gap is artificially narrowed compared to genuinely independent data sources. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to truly diverse, non-derived modalities, whether the adaptive routing advantage over simpler strategies (hash routing achieves 83.11 vs. 83.90) persists at scale, and how the approach handles genuinely missing or noisy modalities in deployment.
