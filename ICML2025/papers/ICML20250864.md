## Problem

SNN supervised learning is constrained by a trade-off: STBP training achieves ultra-low latency but suffers from slow training, high GPU memory, and accuracy gaps with pretrained ANNs, while ANN-SNN Conversion preserves ANN-level accuracy but requires high time latency due to serial LIF neuron computation. Parallel spiking neurons offer O(T) parallel computation but have only been explored within STBP training, leaving their potential for conversion-based learning unexploited.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained ANN (QCFS-based or vanilla ReLU-based) along with a calibration dataset (one epoch of training data), and produces a converted parallel SNN with per-layer parameters: synaptic weights W, parallel conversion matrix Λ_PC ∈ R^{T×T}, per-step bias vectors b^l ∈ R^T, and pre/post thresholds. The converted SNN accepts standard RGB images and outputs class labels, with inference completing in O(T) charging operations plus O(log T) spike-firing decisions per layer rather than T serial steps.

## Method

The paper derives a parallel conversion matrix Λ_PC that maps cumulative spike firing counts predicted by a QCFS activation function onto parallel spiking neuron firing decisions, proving the mapping is lossless when T = T̃ and expectation-preserving when T ≠ T̃ (Theorem 4.1). A key insight is the sorting property: if a parallel neuron fires at step x, it fires at all subsequent steps, enabling binary search to locate the first firing step in O(log T) and reducing total charging from O(T²) to O(T) via a Hadamard-product reformulation. For general activation functions or training-free ReLU conversion, a Distribution-Aware QCFS (DA-QCFS) function with learnable per-channel shift and scale factors is calibrated iteratively via a greedy layer-wise error correction procedure before the final lossless parallel conversion step.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-34, the method achieves 72.90% top-1 accuracy at T=4 (with error calibration) and 74.32% at T=8, matching the pretrained ANN accuracy of 74.30%. On CIFAR-10 with VGG-16, it reaches 95.50% at T=4, surpassing the ANN baseline of 95.43%. For training-free conversion on ImageNet-1k ResNet-101, the method achieves 76.42% at T=32 (only 0.96% below the 77.38% ANN), while prior methods require T=64 to approach comparable accuracy. Inference speed is accelerated by 19–38× over serial IF neuron inference at T≥32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1k ResNet-34, the method at T=8 (74.32%) matches the ANN accuracy and outperforms COS (72.66% at T=10) by 1.66% while using fewer time-steps, and surpasses QCFS (69.37% at T=32) by 4.95% with only 1/4 the latency. Against STBP methods (RecDis 67.33%, Dspike 68.19%, GLIF 67.52%, TAB 67.78% at T=4–6), the parallel conversion with calibration achieves 72.90% at T=4, a 4.7–5.6% improvement without any gradient-based training. On ImageNet-1k VGG-16, the method at T=8 (73.92%) exceeds SNM (64.78% at T=32) by 9.14% and Burst (70.61% at T=32) by 3.31%, all with 4× lower latency. For training-free conversion (Table 4), the method at T=16 on ResNet-34 (68.04%) beats TBC at T=32 (59.03%) by 9.01% and SNNC-AP at T=32 (64.54%) by 3.50%, while at T=32 on ResNet-101 it reaches 76.42% versus SNNC-AP's 64.54% at the same latency. On CIFAR-10 VGG-16, the method at T=4 (95.50%) surpasses SRP (94.47% at T=6) by 1.03% with fewer time-steps, and on CIFAR-100 ResNet-20 at T=8 it achieves 69.62% versus SRP's 53.96% at T=6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously independent research threads in SNN learning: ANN-SNN Conversion (Bu et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2015), which maps pretrained ANN activations to spike firing rates via QCFS functions, and parallel spiking neurons (Fang et al., 2023), which were designed exclusively for STBP training. The key conceptual departure is establishing a provably lossless mathematical mapping between the parallel spiking computation and the QCFS activation function, then extending it via DA-QCFS to handle arbitrary activation functions (including vanilla ReLU) and mismatched simulation/inference time-steps. The authors position this as a 'third path' for SNN supervised learning, distinct from both STBP (high training cost) and serial ANN-SNN Conversion (high inference latency).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19–38× inference speedup over serial LIF neurons, combined with theoretical losslessness guarantees, makes ultra-low-latency SNN deployment (4 timesteps on ImageNet-1k) practically viable for neuromorphic hardware where serial computation is the bottleneck. The training-free conversion pipeline (ReLU → ClipReLU → DA-QCFS → parallel spiking) lowers the barrier to SNN adoption by eliminating the need for QCFS-specific pretraining. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-CNN architectures (Transformers, point clouds), whether the O(log T) binary-search inference translates to actual hardware speedups on neuromorphic chips, and how the method behaves under distribution shift or adversarial inputs where the uniform-distribution assumption underlying the losslessness proof may break down.
