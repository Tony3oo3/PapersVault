## Problem

Vision Transformer prompt tuning (learnable registers) is a standard training strategy, but its effect on cross-domain few-shot learning transferability has been unexplored. This paper discovers that learnable registers actually harm target-domain performance by absorbing source-specific domain information, while random registers consistently improve it—a counterintuitive finding that contradicts the usual assumption that learned prompts should generalize better.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 84×84 RGB images as input, processed through a DINO-pretrained ViT-S backbone with a CLS token, image patch tokens, and register tokens appended to the input sequence. During source-domain training on miniImageNet (64 base classes), clustered image patches are replaced with Gaussian-noise registers; during target-domain evaluation, 1-shot or 5-shot episodes are sampled from CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, or ChestX, and classification is performed via Euclidean distance to class prototypes computed from support-set features.

## Method

The paper identifies that learnable registers overfit to source-domain patterns (focusing on class-irrelevant regions like backgrounds), increasing loss-landscape sharpness and reducing transferability, while random registers act as a form of sharpness-aware minimization by perturbing attention maps to find flatter minima. Building on this, REAP (Random Registers Enhanced Attention Perturbation) improves efficiency by clustering semantically similar image patches via cosine similarity and replacing entire clusters with random Gaussian-noise registers, rather than requiring hundreds of additional register tokens to achieve comparable perturbation. A two-stage training strategy is used: the source-domain stage applies REAP to encourage domain-agnostic feature learning, while the target-domain stage switches to learnable registers for few-shot adaptation, exploiting their ability to absorb target-specific domain information. The approach requires only 16 additional random registers (versus GPU-memory-limited counts in the naive formulation) and achieves state-of-the-art results across all four target domains in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings.

## Results

REAP achieves the best average accuracy across all four target domains in every evaluation setting. In the standard 1-shot without fine-tuning setting, REAP reaches 54.89% average accuracy (23.62% ChestX, 37.21% ISIC2018, 74.69% EuroSAT, 84.04% CropDiseases), outperforming the closest competitor AttnTemp (54.12%). In the 5-shot setting, REAP achieves 66.75% average without fine-tuning and 68.38% in the transductive setting, consistently leading all baselines. The method also generalizes across backbones, improving DINO-ViT-Base from 64.74% to 65.87% and CLIP from 58.17% to 60.93% average in 5-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REAP consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every evaluation setting (1-shot/5-shot, with/without fine-tuning, transductive). The closest ViT-based competitor is AttnTemp (Zou et al., NeurIPS-24), which REAP surpasses by +0.77% average in 1-shot (54.89 vs. 54.12) and +0.13% in 5-shot (66.75 vs. 66.62) without fine-tuning, and by +0.89% in the 1-shot transductive setting (58.12 vs. 57.23). CD-CLS (Zou et al., NeurIPS-24) is the second-closest ViT baseline, trailing REAP by 1.21% in 1-shot and 0.65% in 5-shot. Among CNN-based methods, LDP-net (Zhou et al., CVPR-23) achieves the highest 5-shot average at 61.29%, still 5.46% below REAP. Earlier baselines such as MEM-FS (Walsh et al., TIP-23), StyleAdv (Fu et al., CVPR-23), FLoR (Zou et al., CVPR-24), and DAMIM (Ma et al., AAAI-25) all trail REAP by 1.6–3.6% in the 1-shot setting. The gap is most pronounced on ISIC2018 (skin lesions), where REAP gains +2.29% over AttnTemp in 1-shot, suggesting the attention-perturbation mechanism is particularly effective for highly domain-shifted medical imaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the register token concept from Darcet et al. (2024) and the visual prompt tuning paradigm of Jia et al. (2022), but departs sharply from the standard assumption that learnable prompts should be optimized during source-domain training. It reframes the role of registers through the lens of sharpness-aware minimization (Foret et al., 2021), arguing that random registers act as an implicit attention perturbation that flattens loss landscapes, while learnable registers absorb source-domain-specific information that increases sharpness. Within the CDFSL literature (Tseng et al. 2020, Fu et al. 2023, Zou et al. 2024a), this is the first work to identify prompt learning as a specific source of cross-domain degradation and to propose a register-based remedy grounded in loss-landscape geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a standard, widely-adopted training technique (learnable prompt tuning) actively harms cross-domain transfer while a seemingly naive randomization helps is a useful diagnostic for practitioners working with ViT-based transfer. The SAM interpretation provides a principled framework for understanding *why* the effect occurs, potentially guiding future regularization strategies beyond CDFSL. Limitations include evaluation on only four target domains with a single source (miniImageNet), reliance on CKA similarity as an indirect proxy for domain-agnostic information, and the fact that the REAP method's cluster-dropping heuristic is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include whether the random-register-as-SAM interpretation holds for deeper architectures or non-ViT backbones, and whether the two-stage strategy (random in source, learnable in target) generalizes to other transfer settings.
