## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods either rely on task-specific fixed patterns (sliding windows, attention sinks) that don't generalize across model types, or are designed exclusively for language models, making them inapplicable to diffusion-based image and video generation. A universal, training-free sparse attention operator that simultaneously guarantees speedup and end-to-end accuracy across diverse models and sequence lengths remains elusive, primarily because accurately predicting sparse regions and keeping prediction overhead minimal are in tension.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in replacement for the attention operator, consuming Q, K, V matrices in FP16 (or 8-bit quantized via SageAttention) of shape N×d at sequence lengths ranging from 4.5K (image models) to 128K (language models). For 3D visual tokens (T×H×W×d) in video/image diffusion models, an optional Hilbert Curve permutation reorders tokens before attention to increase adjacent-token similarity, with an inverse permutation applied to the output. The output is the standard attention matrix O of shape N×d, which feeds directly into the downstream generation pipeline without any architectural modification.

## Method

SpargeAttn implements a two-stage online filter within FlashAttention's tiled computation. In Stage 1, blocks of Q and K with high intra-block cosine similarity are compressed to single representative tokens; a compressed attention map is computed on these tokens, and a TopCdf selection (keeping blocks whose cumulative attention score reaches threshold τ) determines which Q_i K_j^T and P̃_ij V_j products to skip, while non-self-similar 'fix blocks' are always computed to preserve critical information. In Stage 2, a warp-level sparse online softmax check skips the P̃_ij V_j product when the local rowmax of S_ij falls below the running global max m_ij by more than a threshold λ, incurring zero extra overhead. The entire pipeline is integrated into SageAttention's per-block 8-bit quantization framework, and hyperparameters (τ, θ, λ) are auto-tuned per layer via grid search under a relative L1 error budget, making the method fully training-free and plug-and-play.

## Results

SpargeAttn achieves 2.5×–5× speedup over full attention across language, image, and video models while matching or slightly exceeding full-attention end-to-end quality. On Llama3.1 (128K), it reaches 708.1 TOPS (vs. 156.9 for full attention) at 54% sparsity, with WikiText perplexity of 6.020 (vs. 6.013 full) and NIAH retrieval accuracy of 0.909 (vs. 0.907 full). On video models, it achieves 507.9 TOPS on CogvideoX (17K) and 582.4 TOPS on Mochi (22K), with CLIPSIM and VQA scores within 1–2% of full attention. End-to-end generation latency drops from 1897s to 1037s on Mochi (1.83× speedup) and from 52s to 29.98s on Llama3.1 (128K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpargeAttn is compared against two dynamic sparse attention baselines—MInference (Jiang et al., NeurIPS 2024) and FlexPrefill (Lai et al., ICLR 2025)—and against SageAttention (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025) for end-to-end latency. On Llama3.1 (128K), SpargeAttn achieves 708.1 TOPS, which is 3.0× faster than FlexPrefill (240.6 TOPS at 50% sparsity) and 5.1× faster than MInference (140.1 TOPS at 50% sparsity), while matching or exceeding full-attention quality on all four metrics. At comparable sparsity (~50%), MInference degrades WikiText perplexity from 6.013 to 10.631 and NIAH from 0.907 to 0.832, whereas SpargeAttn maintains 6.020 and 0.909. On video and image models, the gap widens: FlexPrefill drops VQA-a from 80.4 to 1.5 on CogvideoX and FID from 166 to 444 on Flux, while SpargeAttn stays within 2–3% of full attention. For end-to-end latency, SpargeAttn (1037s on Mochi) is 1.5× faster than SageAttention alone (1544s) and 1.83× faster than the original model (1897s).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpargeAttn builds directly on FlashAttention's tiling and online softmax framework and integrates with SageAttention's 8-bit per-block quantization, combining sparsity and quantization as orthogonal acceleration axes. It departs from the dominant line of sparse attention work in two key ways: (1) unlike pattern-based methods (H2O, InfLLM, StreamingLLM, DitFastAttn) that exploit task-specific structures like sliding windows or attention sinks, and (2) unlike dynamic methods (MInference, FlexPrefill) that compress tokens uniformly and are designed primarily for LLMs, SpargeAttn uses selective token compression guided by intra-block self-similarity and a warp-level online softmax filter, making it model-agnostic. The Hilbert Curve permutation for visual tokens is a novel preprocessing step not present in prior sparse attention work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free sparse attention demonstrated to accelerate language, image, and video generation models simultaneously without end-to-end metric loss, addressing a gap where prior methods were either model-specific or degraded quality on non-LLM architectures. The 2.5×–5× speedup over dense attention with plug-and-play deployment (no retraining, no architectural changes) makes it immediately practical for production inference pipelines. Open questions include whether the per-layer hyperparameter search (τ, θ, λ) scales to very large model families, whether the Hilbert Curve permutation generalizes to non-3D visual layouts or other modalities, and whether the approach remains effective as sequence lengths push beyond 128K or into multi-million-token regimes.
