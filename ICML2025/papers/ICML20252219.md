## Problem

Generating diffusion model samples that satisfy specific user preferences (e.g., prompt fidelity, aesthetic quality, or rare attributes like toxicity) remains challenging. Existing solutions—fine-tuning, best-of-n sampling, and gradient-based guidance—are respectively expensive and reward-locked, computationally wasteful, or restricted to differentiable rewards on continuous-state models. The paper asks whether a single, flexible, training-free framework can steer both continuous and discrete diffusion models with arbitrary reward functions at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion variants at 860M–2.6B parameters, or text diffusion models SSD-LM/MDLM), a conditioning signal (text prompt or class label), and an arbitrary reward function (e.g., ImageReward, GPT-2 perplexity, a toxicity classifier). It outputs k particle samples at the terminal denoising step, from which the highest-reward sample is selected; for images this yields 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images, and for text, sequences of length 50 tokens. A key design choice is that intermediate rewards are evaluated at the model's denoised estimate x̂_t rather than the noisy state x_t, and the number of particles k (typically 2–8) and resampling schedule are the primary compute knobs.

## Method

FK steering applies Feynman-Kac interacting particle systems (FK-IPS) to the denoising trajectory of a diffusion model. It generates k parallel diffusion paths (particles), scores them at selected intermediate timesteps using potential functions G_t built from intermediate rewards r_φ(x_t), and resamples particles via multinomial selection to up-weight trajectories likely to yield high-reward terminal samples. The potentials must satisfy a product constraint (∏G_t = exp(λr(x_0))) so that the terminal particle distribution consistently approximates the tilted target p_θ(x_0)exp(λr(x_0)). The paper introduces three potential families (max, sum, difference), three intermediate-reward strategies (evaluated at the denoised state, many-sample log-sum-exp, and a learned regressor), and interval resampling to balance exploration and compute. The framework is training-free, requires no reward gradients, and unifies prior particle-based methods (TDS, SVDD) as special cases while extending them to discrete-state models and non-differentiable rewards.

## Results

On the GenEval prompt fidelity benchmark, FK steering with k=4 particles improves all tested Stable Diffusion models over their base versions, with SDv2.1 (0.8B) reaching 0.62 GenEval and 1.01 ImageReward, outperforming the fine-tuned SDXL DPO (2.6B) at 0.58 GenEval. For text diffusion, FK steering SSD-LM with a GPT2 perplexity reward reduces PPL from 23.2 to 11.0 and improves CoLA from 68.3 to 80.0, surpassing GPT2-Medium (PPL 14.1, CoLA 87.6) on perplexity. In toxicity red-teaming, FK steering MDLM with k=8 particles and many-sample intermediate rewards increases toxicity rate from 0.3% to 64.7%, far exceeding best-of-8 (3.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FK steering with k=4 outperforms gradient guidance (Bansal et al., 2023) on both GenEval (0.54 vs 0.45 for SDv1.5) and ImageReward (0.89 vs 0.66), while sampling 6× faster (8.1s vs 55s when combining FK with gradient guidance). Against DPO fine-tuning (Wallace et al., 2024), FK steering SDv2.1 achieves 0.62 GenEval versus 0.58 for SDXL DPO, and FK steering SDXL DPO further reaches 0.67, showing that FK steering composes with fine-tuning. Compared to DDPO (Black et al., 2023), FK steering with just k=2 already surpasses the fine-tuned model's GenEval score (0.59 vs 0.43 for SDv1.4). For text generation, FK steering SSD-LM with a GPT2 perplexity reward achieves PPL 11.0, beating GPT2-Medium (PPL 14.1) and outperforming best-of-4 (PPL 13.6) with the same number of samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes prior particle-based diffusion steering methods—specifically the Twisted Diffusion Sampler (TDS, Wu et al., 2023a) and Soft Value-Based Decoding (SVDD, Li et al., 2024)—by showing both are special cases of Feynman-Kac interacting particle systems (FK-IPS, Moral, 2004). The key departure is a systematic expansion of the design space: multiple potential functions (difference, max, sum), multiple intermediate reward estimators (denoised-state, many-sample, learned), and flexible proposal generators (base model, gradient-guided), applied to both continuous and discrete diffusion models with arbitrary (including non-differentiable) rewards. This positions FK steering as a strict generalization that subsumes prior methods while enabling settings they could not handle, such as discrete-state text diffusion with non-differentiable toxicity classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FK steering offers a training-free, plug-and-play alternative to fine-tuning (DDPO, DPO) and gradient guidance for aligning diffusion models with user preferences, with the practical implication that inference-time compute can substitute for both model scale and training compute. The finding that a steered 0.8B model outperforms a fine-tuned 2.6B model challenges the assumption that larger models are always preferable for prompt fidelity. Open questions include how the method scales to very large particle counts, whether optimal potential and reward choices are task-dependent in predictable ways, and whether the approach extends to video and 3D generation where intermediate reward estimation is more expensive.
