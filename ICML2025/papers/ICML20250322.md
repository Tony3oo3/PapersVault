## Problem

In many decision-making applications (bail, lending, hiring), outcome labels are only observed for individuals who received a particular decision, creating severe selection bias that prevents standard supervised learning from generalizing to the full population. Prior work on this selective label problem relies on heuristic approaches or focuses on evaluating fixed models rather than learning new classifiers, and does not provide a principled characterization of when classification risk is identifiable from the observed data. This paper formalizes the problem using an instrumental variable framework with multiple heterogeneous decision-makers, establishing both the power and fundamental limits of this structure for risk identification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data where each observation includes observable features X, a multi-valued decision-maker assignment Z ∈ [J] (J ≥ 2), a binary decision D ∈ {0,1}, and a selectively observed label Y that is NaN when D=0. The output is a multi-class classifier t: X → [K] trained to minimize worst-case misclassification risk over the full population, along with explicit partial identification bounds [l_k(x), u_k(x)] on the conditional class probabilities η_k*(x) for each class k. The key structural requirement is that decision-maker assignment Z must satisfy IV conditions (relevance, independence, exclusion restriction) relative to the decision D and unobserved features U.

## Method

The paper formalizes decision-maker assignment Z as an instrumental variable for the binary decision D, then derives two identification results: (1) under a No Unmeasured Common Effect Modifiers (NUCEM) assumption, the conditional class probabilities η_k*(X) are point-identified via a conditional covariance ratio Cov(DY_k, Z | X)/Cov(D, Z | X), and (2) under only mild boundedness assumptions, tight partial identification bounds are obtained as max/min over decision-maker strata of E[DY_k + a_k(1-D) | X, Z=z]. Both settings reduce to a unified cost-sensitive classification objective with weight functions w_k(x) that differ between point and partial identification. The proposed Unified Cost-sensitive Learning (UCL) algorithm estimates these weights via cross-fitting (L-fold splitting to avoid overfitting bias), then minimizes a softmax-based surrogate risk R_exp(h,w) = E[Σ_k w_k(X) · exp(h_k(X))/Σ_p exp(h_p(X))] using standard gradient-based optimization, with calibration bounds guaranteeing that minimizing the surrogate controls the true cost-sensitive risk.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic FICO dataset (10,459 home loan applications, binary label, J=10 decision-makers), the paper reports testing accuracy across 50 replications for α ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.9} under both NUCEM and UC selection models (Figures 2–3). The PartialLearning method consistently outperforms the SelectedSample and SelectedSample-IPW baselines, with the accuracy gap widening as the selection bias parameter α increases. Notably, PartialLearning also outperforms PointLearning even under the NUCEM model where point identification holds, attributed to the greater stability of estimating conditional expectations versus conditional covariance ratios. FullSample (oracle with all labels observed) remains the upper bound that no method reaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two proposed methods (PointLearning and PartialLearning) against three baselines: SelectedSample (standard ERM on the labeled subsample), SelectedSample-IPW (inverse propensity weighting to correct for observed-feature selection bias), and FullSample (oracle benchmark with complete labels). Both proposed methods outperform SelectedSample and SelectedSample-IPW across all α values and both selection models, with PartialLearning achieving the largest gains as selection bias intensifies (α = 0.9). The IPW correction provides only marginal improvement over plain SelectedSample, confirming that unobserved confounders U cannot be addressed by adjusting for X alone. PartialLearning dominates PointLearning in practice despite the latter being theoretically applicable under NUCEM, because estimating the conditional covariance ratio Cov(DY_k, Z | X)/Cov(D, Z | X) is empirically noisier than estimating the conditional expectations needed for the partial-identification bounds. No method closes the gap to the FullSample oracle, reflecting the irreducible information loss from unobserved labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the selective label problem within a principled instrumental variable (IV) framework, departing from the heuristic 'contraction' and imputation approaches used by Kleinberg et al. (2018), Lakkaraju et al. (2017), and De-Arteaga et al. (2018, 2021). It extends the binary-IV identification results of Wang & Tchetgen (2018) and Cui & Tchetgen Tchetgen (2021) to multi-valued instruments, and generalizes the partial identification bounds of Balke & Pearl (1994) and Manski (1990) to multi-class outcomes. Unlike prior ITR learning work (Cui & Tchetgen Tchetgen 2021; Pu & Zhang 2021) restricted to binary IVs and binary treatments, this paper handles multi-valued IVs and multi-class classification, unifying point and partial identification into a single cost-sensitive learning formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental limitation: even with a valid IV from multiple decision-makers, exact identification of classification risk requires the strong NUCEM assumption, which is often implausible in practice. This clarifies the power and limits of the multi-decision-maker structure in a way prior heuristic approaches could not. Open questions include practical verification of NUCEM, the width of partial identification bounds in high-dimensional settings, and whether the unified cost-sensitive learning framework extends to online or sequential decision-making. The finding that the simpler PartialLearning method outperforms PointLearning even when point identification holds (due to estimation stability of conditional expectations vs. variance ratios) is a useful practical caveat.
