## Problem

Automated computational phenotypes (ACPs) offer a fast alternative to labor-intensive gold-standard labels, but naively substituting them introduces bias in downstream inference. Existing prediction-powered inference (PPI) methods either assume the distributional difference between labeled and unlabeled populations is fully known or do not account for covariate shift in the labeling mechanism. This paper addresses how to validly and efficiently incorporate ACPs into semi-supervised estimation under covariate shift, where the labeled subset is selected based on baseline characteristics rather than at random.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled data (Y, X, Ŷ) of size n and unlabeled data (X, Ŷ) of size N, where the ACP Ŷ is produced by a black-box model (ML, NLP, or LLM) that may depend on additional unseen features Z beyond X and need not share the same scale or format as Y. The output is a d-dimensional parameter estimate β for a target characteristic of the unlabeled population (e.g., a regression coefficient or mean) along with asymptotically valid confidence intervals for any linear combination v^T β. Nuisance functions (density ratio, conditional score means) are estimated via K-fold cross-fitting with super learner ensembling GLMs, random forests, kernel SVMs, and XGBoost.

## Method

The paper derives the efficient influence function (EIF) for estimating β under covariate shift with ACPs by decomposing the semiparametric tangent space into orthogonal components corresponding to the labeling probability, labeled/unlabeled covariate distributions, ACP distribution, and outcome distribution. The resulting estimator (equation 5) is a cross-fitted, doubly robust estimating equation that combines three terms: a weighted residual on labeled data, a correction term involving the difference between ACP-conditioned and marginal score means, and a marginal score mean on unlabeled data. A key theoretical finding is that the efficiency gain from ACPs arises exclusively from the unlabeled data (Proposition 3.5), and the gain is always non-negative, vanishing only when Ŷ is a deterministic function of X. The estimator achieves semiparametric efficiency under rate double robustness conditions (products of nuisance convergence rates o(M^{-1/2})), permitting flexible ML-based nuisance estimation without Donsker restrictions.

## Results

The primary metric is the length of 95% confidence intervals for regression coefficients (shorter is better). Across three real-world datasets (Income, Politeness, Wine), the proposed method achieves the largest CI length reduction of approximately 58% relative to PPI (e.g., 0.0008 vs. 0.0014 for the Age coefficient on Income data). On the UF Health System Diabetes dataset, incorporating ACPs yields consistently shorter confidence intervals and identifies self-paid insurance as a significant diabetes risk factor that the method without ACPs fails to detect. In synthetic experiments, the Asymptotic Relative Efficiency (ARE) exceeds 1 in all settings except when the ACP carries no predictive signal (α=0) or is fully determined by X (ζ=1), confirming the theoretical guarantee that ACPs never reduce efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against three prediction-powered inference baselines: PPI (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a), PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b), and RePPI (Ji et al., 2025). Against PPI, the proposed method consistently wins on every variable across all three datasets, with CI length reductions ranging from 41.55% (Wine/Price) to 57.14% (Income/Age). Against PPI++, the proposed method wins on 6 of 8 variable-dataset combinations, with the largest gains on Income/Age (66.67% of PPI++ CI length) and Politeness/Hedge (71.70%), but is marginally worse on Wine/New York (102.45%). Against RePPI, the pattern is similar: wins on 6 of 8 combinations, with the largest advantage on Income/Age (66.67%) and Politeness/Hedge (73.77%), while being slightly worse on Wine/California (101.95%) and Wine/New York (102.55%). Overall, the paper reports that the proposed method is 'generally a lot more efficient than PPI, PPI++, and RePPI, with the largest reduction in confidence interval length reaching approximately 60%,' with only minor losses to PPI++/RePPI on a few Wine region variables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-powered inference (PPI) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023a) to a semi-supervised setting under covariate shift, where the labeling mechanism depends on features rather than being purely random. While PPI required the distributional difference between labeled and unlabeled data to be known a priori, this paper estimates it from data using a density ratio, making the approach feasible in practice. The estimation strategy draws on the doubly robust / double/debiased ML literature (Chernozhukov et al., 2018) with cross-fitting, and the efficiency analysis follows the semiparametric influence function framework. The paper also connects to the broader surrogate variable and label-noise literature in biostatistics, positioning ACPs as a flexible form of auxiliary prediction that need not share the same scale or format as the ground-truth outcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous guarantee that incorporating ACPs never reduces estimation efficiency and identifies precisely where the gain originates: ACPs for the unlabeled data, not the labeled data. This is practically important for EHR and clinical research settings where gold-standard labels are expensive but ML-generated phenotypes are abundant. An open question the authors flag is how to integrate uncertainty quantification measures that typically accompany ACP predictions (e.g., from LLMs or ensemble models) into the inference pipeline. The untestable assumption (3) — conditional independence of (Y, Ŷ) and R given X — remains a practical limitation, though the authors note it is analogous to the standard covariate shift assumption already widely adopted in the field.
