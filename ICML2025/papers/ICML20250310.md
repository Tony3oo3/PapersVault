## Problem

Semantic tokens derived from self-supervised speech models (e.g., HuBERT) capture linguistic content but neglect paralinguistic attributes like prosody, energy, and spectral characteristics, resulting in generated speech with reduced naturalness. Existing remedies such as adding hand-engineered pitch features are suboptimal because pitch alone cannot represent the full range of paralinguistic information, and selecting appropriate features requires careful manual design. The paper asks whether these continuous speech attributes can be learned end-to-end within an autoregressive generative framework without any manual feature extraction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a mel-spectrogram speech prompt (first 3 seconds from LibriSpeech dev sets, 80 filter-banks) and generates a 10-second mel-spectrogram continuation, which is then converted to waveform via a pre-trained HiFi-GAN vocoder. At each autoregressive time step, the model jointly predicts a discrete semantic token (from a k=200 HuBERT k-means vocabulary) and a 4-dimensional continuous variational feature vector. An utterance-level encoder additionally produces a static embedding from a randomly cropped 2–4 second segment to capture speaker and environment information shared across the utterance.

## Method

The core contribution is a VAE with an autoregressive prior integrated into a token-based speech language model. A neural encoder q_φ maps the mel-spectrogram to a diagonal Gaussian posterior over continuous variational features Z^c, which complement the discrete semantic tokens Z^d; the prior p_ψ(Z^c) is modeled by a transformer-based autoregressive model enhanced with a time-wise normalizing flow (4 affine coupling layers with FiLM conditioning) for greater expressiveness. The decoder p_θ(X|Z^d, Z^c) is a DDPM diffusion model (1000 training steps, U-Net architecture) conditioned on both token and variational feature embeddings. The training objective decomposes into three terms—reconstruction (O_rec), variational feature prediction loss (L_kl^c), and semantic token prediction loss (L_kl^d)—balanced by hyperparameters β and γ with a linear warm-up schedule for β to mitigate posterior collapse. This design lets Z^c automatically learn paralinguistic attributes (prosody, energy, spectral details) that complement the linguistic content in Z^d, eliminating the need for hand-engineered features while keeping the parameter overhead under 1%.

## Results

On speech continuation (the primary task), the proposed variational approach achieves the highest naturalness MOS (N-MOS 3.60±0.10) and meaningfulness MOS (M-MOS 3.45±0.09) among all compared methods, approaching ground-truth levels (N-MOS 3.89, M-MOS 3.94). For reconstruction, the method reduces F0-RMSE from 43.90 (Token-LM) to 16.56 and CER from 10.19 to 4.35, while adding only ~2M parameters (<1% overhead). On language modeling metrics (sWUGGY/sBLIMP), the proposed method scores slightly below Token-LM (60.48/56.56 vs. 61.75/58.31), which the authors attribute to the model allocating capacity to acoustic information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Token-LM (Lakhotia et al., 2021), the proposed method wins decisively on naturalness (N-MOS 3.60 vs. 3.19, +0.41) and meaningfulness (M-MOS 3.45 vs. 3.24, +0.21), while trailing slightly on sWUGGY (60.48 vs. 61.75) and sBLIMP (56.56 vs. 58.31). Against Token-LM + Pitch (Kharitonov et al., 2022), the proposed method achieves a large naturalness gain (N-MOS 3.60 vs. 3.08, +0.52) and meaningfulness gain (M-MOS 3.45 vs. 3.29), with near-parity on sWUGGY/sBLIMP. Against Token-LM + Acoustic (Defossez et al., 2024), the proposed method dominates on every metric: N-MOS 3.60 vs. 3.03, M-MOS 3.45 vs. 2.75, sWUGGY 60.48 vs. 56.23, and sBLIMP 56.56 vs. 52.03, indicating that directly feeding RVQ acoustic tokens into the autoregressive model degrades linguistic quality. Against SpeechTokenizer-LM (Zhang et al., 2024, Table 5), the proposed method again wins on both N-MOS (3.61 vs. 3.33) and M-MOS (3.68 vs. 3.26), confirming the framework generalizes across different semantic token extraction strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-based generative spoken language model framework (Lakhotia et al., 2021; Borsos et al., 2023; Maiti et al., 2024) by integrating a variational autoencoder with an autoregressive prior—a combination previously explored in computer vision (Vahdat & Kautz, 2020; Zhu et al., 2020) and applied to TTS with separate optimization (Sun et al., 2020). The key departure from prior work is the joint, end-to-end training of the VAE encoder, the autoregressive model, and the decoder, where the learned continuous variational features automatically capture paralinguistic attributes (prosody, emotion, speaker style) that hand-engineered features like pitch (Kharitonov et al., 2022) or discrete acoustic tokens (Defossez et al., 2024) handle suboptimally. Unlike Moshi-style approaches that add acoustic tokens to the autoregressive sequence, this method augments the input representation with a low-dimensional continuous latent, keeping the autoregressive model's parameter budget nearly unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 4-dimensional continuous latent, learned end-to-end, can capture paralinguistic information (emotion, speaker identity) that discrete semantic tokens fundamentally cannot, as evidenced by the large gap in emotion recognition accuracy (91.6% vs. 57.5%). This eliminates the need for manual feature engineering and selection of paralinguistic attributes, offering a more principled and generalizable path for natural speech synthesis. However, the approach remains sensitive to the loss-balancing hyperparameters β and γ, has only been validated on English audiobook data with relatively small models (~220M parameters), and the authors acknowledge that cross-lingual generalization and scaling behavior are open questions. The finding that adding acoustic tokens improves reconstruction but degrades continuation quality also raises broader questions about how much information an autoregressive model can effectively process at each step.
