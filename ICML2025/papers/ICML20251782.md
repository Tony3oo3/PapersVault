## Problem

Existing diversity measures (average pairwise distance, Vendi Score, DPP determinant, Energy, etc.) all exhibit counterintuitive behavior: they can assign higher scores to clearly less diverse configurations or produce degenerate optima. The paper asks whether a diversity measure can simultaneously satisfy three natural axioms—monotonicity, uniqueness, and continuity—and if so, whether such a measure can be computed efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251782/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a multiset of n objects (duplicates allowed) together with an n×n pairwise distance matrix D satisfying non-negativity, symmetry, and the consistency condition that d_ij = 0 implies the two objects have identical distances to all others; the triangle inequality is not required. The output is a single real-valued diversity score. The function must be permutation-invariant, so only the multiset of pairwise distances matters, not the labeling of objects. The number of objects n is fixed throughout the analysis.

## Method

The paper first formulates three axioms for a reliable diversity measure: strict monotonicity in all pairwise distances, uniqueness (replacing a duplicate with a novel element must increase diversity), and continuity. It then proves via explicit counterexamples that every known measure violates at least one axiom. To show the axioms are jointly satisfiable, the authors construct two measures: MultiDimVolume, which sums over k the maximum product of pairwise distances over all k-element submultisets (analogous to a k-dimensional volume), and IntegralMaxClique, which integrates over a distance threshold t the total edge-weight of the maximum-weight maximum clique in the thresholded graph. Both are proven NP-hard via reduction from maximum clique. The paper further establishes two impossibility results: any measure with uniqueness and continuity must be invariant to which elements are duplicated (Proposition 6.1), and no additive decomposition of the form F(distances from x₁) + G(rest) can satisfy all three axioms (Proposition 6.2), ruling out a natural class of efficient update schemes.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that none of the 13 existing diversity measures satisfies all three proposed axioms (monotonicity, uniqueness, continuity) simultaneously. The two newly constructed measures, MultiDimVolume and IntegralMaxClique, are the first to satisfy all three axioms, but both are proven NP-hard to compute (reducible from maximum clique). Among existing measures, only Average/SumAverage, RKE, and Species(q) satisfy monotonicity; only Unique satisfies uniqueness; and all measures except #Circles(t) and Unique satisfy continuity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven compared baselines fail at least one of the three axioms, while both proposed measures satisfy all three. Energy(γ) from Velikonivtsev et al. (2024) fails both monotonicity and uniqueness because it takes the value −∞ in the presence of any duplicate, making it insensitive to all other distances. #Circles(t) from Xie et al. (2023) is the only existing measure that fails all three axioms and is also NP-hard. HamDiv from Hu et al. (2024) fails monotonicity (insensitive to distances not on the shortest Hamiltonian circuit) and uniqueness (two configurations with different diversity profiles can share the same HamDiv value), and is NP-hard. Vendi Score from Friedman & Dieng (2023) fails monotonicity (moving a point further away can decrease the score) and uniqueness (replacing a point with a duplicate can increase the score). DPP from Wilhelm et al. (2018) fails monotonicity (increasing a pairwise similarity can increase the determinant) and uniqueness (all-coincident objects have the same diversity as any other configuration). RKE from Jalali et al. (2023) satisfies monotonicity and continuity but fails uniqueness (making a point a duplicate of another can increase RKE). Species(q) from Leinster & Cobbold (2012) similarly satisfies monotonicity and continuity but fails uniqueness for all tested q values. The proposed measures 'win' on axiom satisfaction but 'lose' on computational feasibility, as both are NP-hard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying and extending the axiomatic analyses of diversity measures scattered across prior work (Velikonivtsev et al. 2024, Xie et al. 2023, Friedman & Dieng 2023, Leinster & Cobbold 2012) into a single framework with three axioms: monotonicity, uniqueness, and continuity. While prior works each proposed subsets of these properties, this paper is the first to add continuity as a necessary axiom and to systematically show that every known measure (Average, Diameter, Bottleneck, Energy, #Circles, Vendi Score, DPP, RKE, Species(q), HamDiv) fails at least one. The two constructed measures (MultiDimVolume, IntegralMaxClique) repurpose graph-theoretic concepts (maximum clique, k-dimensional volume analogs) in a novel way to satisfy all three axioms, though at NP-hard computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the widely-used diversity measures in image generation, recommender systems, and molecule design all have fundamental theoretical flaws, providing practitioners with a clear diagnostic for when a particular measure is safe to use (e.g., Energy for optimization but not comparison). The central open problem—whether a polynomial-time diversity measure satisfying all three axioms exists—remains unresolved, and the negative result (Proposition 6.2) ruling out additive decompositions constrains the design space for future efficient measures. The practical impact is indirect but significant: it reframes diversity measurement as a constrained optimization problem rather than an ad-hoc metric selection task.
