## Problem

Current test-time scaling for LLM reasoning relies on external verifiers or reward models to guide search, creating costly two-player systems that do not internalize search capabilities into a single model. This paper asks whether a single LLM can be trained to perform autoregressive search—self-reflection and self-exploration of alternative strategies—without any external guidance at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252171/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a math problem statement as input and produces an autoregressive COAT reasoning trajectory where each step begins with a meta-action token (<|continue|>, <|reflect|>, or <|explore|>) followed by reasoning text. The trajectory is generated step-by-step, and the model can pause mid-reasoning to verify prior steps, identify errors, or abandon the current path and explore an alternative solution, ultimately terminating with a final answer. Evaluation uses greedy decoding with zero-shot pass@1 accuracy.

## Method

Satori introduces the Chain-of-Action-Thought (COAT) reasoning format, which augments standard CoT with special meta-action tokens that let the model decide when to continue, reflect, or explore alternatives. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: (1) a small-scale format tuning stage (10K samples) using behavior cloning on demonstration trajectories synthesized by a multi-agent framework (Generator, Critic, Reward Model), and (2) a large-scale self-improvement stage using PPO with a Restart-and-Explore (RAE) strategy inspired by Go-Explore, where the model restarts from intermediate steps of both correct and incorrect past trajectories with a reflect token appended. The reward function combines rule-based correctness, an Outcome Reward Model (trained via Bradley-Terry preferences) for dense signal, and reflection bonuses that reward correcting past mistakes and penalize unnecessary revisions. Iterative self-improvement alternates RL rounds with SFT distillation of the current policy back into the base model to escape local optima.

## Results

Satori-Qwen-7B achieves an average of 62.6% across five math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, OlympiadBench, AMC2023, AIME2024), outperforming the same-base-model instruct variant Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (59.9%) despite using only 10K format-tuning samples versus large-scale SFT. After iterative self-improvement (Round 2), Satori reaches 64.4% average, with notable gains on harder benchmarks (AMC2023: 72.5%, AIME2024: 23.3%). On out-of-domain tasks, Satori achieves 60.4% average across six diverse reasoning benchmarks, surpassing Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (52.5%) by a large margin despite being trained exclusively on math data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (same 7B base model), Satori wins on four of five math benchmarks (MATH500: 85.6 vs 83.6, OlympiadBench: 46.6 vs 41.6, AMC2023: 67.5 vs 62.5, AIME2024: 20.0 vs 16.7) while trailing slightly on GSM8K (93.2 vs 95.2), achieving a higher overall average (62.6 vs 59.9). Satori also substantially outperforms the 70B OpenMath2-Llama3.1-70B on MATH500 (85.6 vs 71.8) and AIME2024 (20.0 vs 13.3), demonstrating that the 7B model with COAT+RL surpasses a 10x larger model trained with conventional SFT. Against the larger QwQ-32B-Preview, Satori remains competitive but trails on all benchmarks (e.g., AIME2024: 20.0 vs 50.0, MATH500: 85.6 vs 90.6), confirming that scale still matters for the hardest problems. On out-of-domain tasks, Satori (60.4% avg) beats Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (52.5%) by ~8 points, particularly on BoardgameQA (61.8 vs 51.3) and CRUXEval (42.5 vs 28.0), while still trailing QwQ-32B-Preview (71.9%). The paper also shows that large-scale format tuning (300K SFT) without RL underperforms the 10K FT + 300K RL recipe, validating the RL-driven self-improvement approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting by introducing meta-action tokens (reflect, explore, continue) to form Chain-of-Action-Thought (COAT), enabling a single LLM to perform autoregressive search without external verifiers. It departs from the dominant two-player test-time scaling paradigm (LLM + reward model) and from prompting-based search methods (Tree of Thoughts, Reflexion) by internalizing search through a two-stage training recipe: small-scale imitation learning on synthesized COAT trajectories followed by large-scale PPO with a Restart-and-Explore (RAE) strategy inspired by Go-Explore. The high-level strategy of minimal cold-start SFT plus large-scale RL closely parallels DeepSeek-R1, but Satori operates at 7B scale, uses a distinct multi-agent data synthesis pipeline, and introduces the RAE mechanism and iterative distill-then-RL loop as differentiating methodological contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model trained with only 10K format-tuning samples plus large-scale RL can outperform a same-base instruct model trained with far more supervision, and that the resulting self-correction and self-exploration capabilities transfer to out-of-domain tasks (logic, code, commonsense) despite math-only training. The clean ablation showing that 300K supervised fine-tuning underperforms 10K FT + 300K RL provides practical guidance for post-training budgets. Open questions include whether the COAT format and RAE strategy scale to much larger models, whether additional meta-actions (e.g., decomposition, verification) yield further gains, and whether the iterative self-improvement loop eventually plateaus or suffers from model collapse.
